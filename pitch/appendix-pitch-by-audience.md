# Appendix — Pitch the same TAM + competition story to three different audiences

Same numbers. Same competitors. Different frame depending on who's listening. Read the room before opening with the wrong one.

---

## To a retail OPERATOR (CMO, VP Email, Head of CX)

**Frame TAM as "stuff already on your line item."**

> *"You're already paying Litmus $10K, Persado $80K, Contentsquare $120K, Qualtrics $40K. None of those tells you what a named 62-year-old shopper actually thinks of yesterday's campaign. We're a $30K line item that adds the one thing your $250K stack is missing — and the artifacts come pre-decked, so you stop redrawing slides."*

**Frame competition as "your existing tools, plus the lens they don't have."**

| You already buy | What it does | What's still missing |
|---|---|---|
| Litmus | Render QA | "Did Walker care?" |
| Persado | Subject-line copy | "How did Walker feel about it?" |
| Klaviyo dashboards | Aggregate CTR | "Why did the BOGO bomb with Boomers?" |
| Contentsquare | Post-click replay | "What did the inbox do *before* he clicked?" |
| Qualtrics | Quarterly survey | Real-time, every campaign, in voice |

**Why we win for them:** etell turns scattered analytics into a person you can ask. The CFO asks "is the email program working?" — they want a Walker quote, not a 12-tab dashboard.

**Best opening question:** *"Last campaign you launched — could you tell me, by name, who liked it and who didn't?"*

## To an INVESTOR (pre-seed angel, retail-vertical fund)

**Frame TAM as "wedge math, not Brinker chart."**

> *"$540B martech TAM is a vanity number. The real wedge is **2,000 brands × $30K = $60M ARR** in year-3 reach. That's enough to be a venture-scale outcome on its own; the expansion to site + app + ads + support takes ACV to $80–120K and the wedge to $160–240M ARR without adding a customer."*

**Frame competition as "structural moat = time-on-persona."**

> *"Litmus can ship AI critique in 6 months. They can't ship 18 months of accumulated Walker-on-Skechers history in any timeframe. The data compounds; the moat widens with every audit."*

**The two questions investors ask + the answers:**

1. *"Why won't Mailchimp / Klaviyo just bolt this on?"*
   → Feature conflict. They'd be critiquing the output of their own AI copy generator. Strategic blocker, not capability blocker.

2. *"Why hasn't Persado already done this?"*
   → They generate copy from a brand voice model. We narrate from a reader-perspective model. Different dataset, different model architecture, different go-to-market. They'd have to rebuild the asset to compete.

**Best opening question:** *"Name a company today that tells a brand what one specific shopper, by name, thought of last Tuesday's email."* (There is no answer.)

## To a STRATEGIC BUYER (Klaviyo, Mailchimp, Litmus, Contentsquare M&A)

**Frame TAM as "tuck-in revenue + data graph asset."**

> *"In a $30K ACV × 2,000-brand wedge, etell on its own is a $60M ARR business. Inside your suite, the ACV doubles because we attach to existing accounts. The bigger asset is the **persona reaction graph** — 18+ months of named-reader reactions across 50+ brands. That's the dataset to train the next generation of your AI personalization, and it's not buyable from anyone else."*

**Frame competition as "everyone competes for the seat; we're the only one with the asset."**

> *"You can build a render-QA tool in a quarter. You cannot build a persona-memory dataset retroactively. Acquiring etell at year 2 is a 3-year time-to-market shortcut on the data layer."*

**Best opening question:** *"What would you do if Walker — with 1,000 audited campaigns in his memory — was a feature inside your product tomorrow?"*

## Read-the-room cheat sheet

| Signal in the room | Lead with |
|---|---|
| Investor asks about competitive moat first | Slide 08a (Why we win), then 08 |
| Operator pulls out their current tool stack | Operator frame above + slide 03a (capabilities) |
| Strategic buyer asks about exit comps | Strategic frame above + appendix-investor-targets |
| Anyone says "isn't this just Litmus + AI?" | Differentiation answer from slide 08 + the time-on-persona moat from 08a |

If you can only memorize one line per audience, memorize the **best opening question** for each — that's the line that flips the room from "explain it to me" to "tell me how you do that."
