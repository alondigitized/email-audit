# etell investor deck — how to use this

This directory holds a slide-by-slide markdown draft of the etell pitch deck plus supporting materials for founder prep. Each `NN-*.md` file is one slide; paste the content into your tool of choice (Google Slides, Keynote, Pitch.com, Figma Slides).

## Ground rules baked into every slide

1. **No Skechers affiliation.** etell is Alon's personal side project. His Skechers role is biographical credibility only — never customer traction, never institutional backing. If any slide drifts into that framing, fix it before sending.
2. **No invented metrics.** Every number in the deck is either verifiable in the live product or labelled as a projection.
3. **No invented customers or LOIs.** Pre-revenue, no external users yet — say so plainly on slide 09.

## Recommended audience

**Primary: strategic angels (retail / CX operators) + retail-vertical pre-seed funds.**

Why: pre-revenue + nights-and-weekends won't clear the bar for a generalist SaaS seed fund, but the founder-market-fit story plays *stronger* in front of retail operators and funds that back domain-expert builders. Strategic angels also unlock design-partner intros, which is what actually moves this to the next round.

**Secondary: martech/CX vertical VCs** (Bloomberg Beta, Notation, Craft) once you have 2-3 design partner conversations in flight.

**Not yet: generalist seed VCs.** Come back when there's paid traction.

## Suggested flow for a 10-minute pitch

| Minute | Slides | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | 00 title, 01 problem | Frame the pain |
| 1–2 | 01a prospect-questions | "Here are the questions on your whiteboard right now" |
| 2–3 | 02 insight | "Why this is suddenly answerable" |
| 3–4 | 03 product, 03a capabilities | Persona narrative + what we actually ship |
| 4–5 | 04 how-it-works, 05 moat | Demystify the tech; land the data-compounds point |
| 5–7 | 06 market, 07 GTM | Size of prize + how we get there |
| 7–8 | 08 competition, 08a why-we-win, 09 traction | Position vs alternatives + structural moat + honest state |
| 8–9 | 10 team, 11 vision | Why us + how big this can get |
| 9–10 | 12 ask, 13 close | Concrete ask + next step |

**Optional: lead with prospect-questions** in front of an operator audience (they self-identify with the questions before you've even shown the product). Lead with the original problem slide in front of investors — they want the framing first.

## Appendices (keep in back pocket, don't present)

- `appendix-risks.md` — risks you'll surface voluntarily when asked
- `appendix-qa-prep.md` — scripted answers to the questions you'll actually get
- `appendix-investor-targets.md` — starting list of investors to research
- `appendix-metrics.md` — the factual backup for every number in the deck
- `appendix-pitch-by-audience.md` — TAM + competition framing tailored to operator / investor / strategic buyer

## Review order before sending to any real investor

1. Slides 00–03 — elevator pitch and problem. If these three don't land for a trusted non-technical peer, don't look at the rest.
2. Slide 09 (traction) — the place first-time founders over-claim. Re-read it skeptically.
3. Slide 12 (ask) — replace the $500K–$1M range with whatever number matches your actual burn plan and appetite.
4. `appendix-qa-prep.md` — read it aloud. Practice the top five answers.
5. Dry-run the full deck in 10 minutes with one retail operator and one former investor before any real meeting.

## Legal reminder

Review your Skechers employment agreement with a startup attorney (Gunderson, Cooley, and Wilson Sonsini all offer free first meetings) before raising outside capital. Specifically check:
- Moonlighting / outside-activity clauses
- IP assignment scope (does work created on personal time, on personal equipment, using personal brand subscriptions, vest in etell — or is there any ambiguity?)
- Non-compete clauses

This isn't legal advice — it's the two-hour conversation that keeps a promising pre-seed from blowing up on day one.
