# Homepage snapshot · www.buybuybaby.com · 2026-05-19
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is very much my store — I literally came here to build a registry and the first thing I see is a gorgeous wood-slat crib draped in a knit blanket surrounded by stuffed animals, captioned "Dreamy Neutrals." That is my entire Pinterest board. This homepage is doing a solid job telegraphing "elevated, safe, nursery-obsessed" to a first-time mom who has opinions about aesthetics. The brand is clearly pushing a neutral-palette nursery moment right now, which feels timely and right.

## What's working

- **"Dreamy Neutrals" hero** — that headline copy + the crib image is speaking *directly* to me. It's not generic "Shop Baby Gear," it's a specific aesthetic story I'm already telling myself.
- **"welcome rewards+" in the header** — I notice it immediately and it tells me there's a loyalty program worth investigating before I spend several hundred dollars here.
- **The crib photography** — genuinely beautiful, warm lighting, textured materials, layered styling. This looks like a brand that gets my taste, not a warehouse catalog.
- **Category tiles below the fold** — "Hello, Little Bugs!" with a baby playing is charming and immediately scannable; these are fast-entry points to browsing without hunting through nav.
- **"The Toddler Shop" secondary nav** — even though it's partially obscured, I can tell there's organized navigation beyond just the hero, which is reassuring for a shopping trip this complex.

## What's weak

- **Zero deal hook above the fold** — I cross-shop Babylist and Target. If there's no offer surfaced up top, I have no reason not to just go check Target's registry discount (typically 15% off) instead.
- **Sign In flyout overlapping content** — the login dropdown is visibly layered over the secondary nav, cutting off category labels. On mobile this is legitimately annoying and makes the brand look unpolished.
- **No registry CTA anywhere above the fold** — I am *literally here to build a registry* and the word "registry" is not visible in this screenshot. That's a significant miss.
- **No person in the hero** — the nursery is gorgeous but there's no mom or baby in frame. "Hello, Little Bugs!" tile has a baby, but the main hero is product-only. Aspirational lifestyle content with a real person would add warmth.
- **No trust signals** — no "4.7 stars from 12,000 reviews," no "free returns," no "safe + non-toxic" badge. For a safety-obsessed first-time mom who reads every recall note, this is a gap.

## Recommendations

- **Surface a registry offer in the hero or a persistent top banner** — something like "Build your registry today — get 15% off a completion purchase" above the hero or in a slim strip. One line. That's what I'm comparing you against on Babylist and Target.
- **Add a "Start Your Registry" CTA button** alongside or below "Shop Now" in the hero — this is the highest-intent action for your audience and it's invisible.
- **Fix the Sign In flyout** — it's covering nav on mobile. Either collapse it after 2 seconds or don't auto-open it; the login state shouldn't obscure page content on first load.
- **Swap the pure-product crib shot for a lifestyle version** — same neutral palette, but include a mom (30ish, relaxed, maybe in a nursing outfit) interacting with the crib. Keeps the aesthetic, adds the human warmth.
