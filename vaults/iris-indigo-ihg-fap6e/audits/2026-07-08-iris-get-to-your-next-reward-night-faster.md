# Iris, get to your next Reward Night faster
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- This is IHG One Rewards' fifth buy-points email in roughly two weeks — following June 22, June 29, July 1, and July 6 sends that all ran the same 100% bonus offer with nearly identical creative. The one meaningful change here is the subject line: instead of leading with "100% more points," IHG pivots to aspirational framing — "get to your next Reward Night faster" — which is smarter positioning. It acknowledges that a committed loyalty traveler isn't buying points for points' sake; they're buying toward a stay.
- The email body, however, is pixel-for-pixel the same three-step treatment we've seen four times already: hero image of a hotel terrace, BUY NOW, three-column process (Buy → Get 100% → Start Planning), BUY NOW again, brand logo grid, wall of legal text. The July 16 deadline is visible in the body, which adds real urgency, but nothing in the copy acknowledges that you've already seen this offer four times. That's an omission a subscriber will feel even if they can't articulate it.
- At this point the audience is either already bought in or fully tuned out. The subject line refresh was the right call; the body needed to match it with sharper copy or a closer tie to a specific destination or redemption milestone.

## What's working

- **Subject line pivot is the right move.** "Get to your next Reward Night faster" is benefit-led and aspiration-adjacent — meaningfully different from the four prior sends that led with the points bonus.
- **Hero image is strong.** An aspirational outdoor terrace/pool setting at golden hour puts the redemption vision front and center before any copy.
- **Two clear CTAs in orange.** "BUY NOW" buttons are visible, high-contrast, and appear above and below the three-step section — no hunting required.
- **July 16 deadline is in the body.** Time-bounded urgency is explicitly stated, which the prior sends also had but still earns credit.
- **Three-step structure is scannable.** Buy → Get 100% → Start Planning reads in under five seconds.

## What's weak

- **Fifth identical body in two weeks.** There's zero acknowledgment of the campaign's cadence — no "last chance," no "you've been eyeing this," nothing that makes this send feel distinct from the July 6 one.
- **"BUY NOW" is a weak CTA verb for the body.** Given the subject's shift to Reward Night framing, the button should say "Get My Bonus Points" or "Start Earning" — something that closes the loop on the aspiration set up by the subject.
- **No current points balance or redemption milestone.** The email doesn't tell Iris how far away her next Reward Night is or how many points the 100% bonus would add to her account. This is the most compelling personalization hook available and it's absent.
- **Dense legal block at the bottom.** The wall of fine print after the brand logo grid is visually heavy and breaks the clean close established by the brand grid.
- **Brand logo grid dilutes focus.** Showing 16+ hotel brands at the bottom is informational but not conversion-driving; it's a habit from older emails that fills space without helping the click.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a redemption milestone to the hero.** "You're X points away from your next Reward Night — this offer gets you there" is the single highest-leverage personalization change available. It converts the offer from abstract to concrete.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA to match the subject's aspiration.** Change "BUY NOW" to "Claim My Bonus Points" or "Get to My Next Reward Night" — close the loop the subject opens.
- 3. **Acknowledge the cadence.** Add a one-liner like "Offer ends July 16 — this is your final reminder" to give this send a distinct identity versus the previous four.
- 4. **Trim the brand logo grid or remove it.** On the fifth send, every subscriber already knows IHG has 16 brands. Use that vertical space for a redemption inspiration image (a specific property, a city) tied to the persona's travel preferences.
- 5. **Surface a specific buy tier prominently.** "Buy 10,000 points, get 10,000 free — starting at $X" is a sharper hook than the current generic "up to 200,000" range.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Last chance: double your points before July 16`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Buy points now, earn 100% more — offer ends July 16`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `One purchase could cover your next free night — here's how`

## Technical Audit — IHG One Rewards: "Iris, get to your next Reward Night faster"

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has one confirmed CAN-SPAM compliance failure (missing body unsubscribe link per QA scan), one malformed destination URL, and pervasive missing alt text across 30+ images. Authentication status is unverifiable through the relay. The two "broken" font preconnect URLs are QA tool false positives.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**False positives (do not action):**
- `https://fonts.googleapis.com` and `https://fonts.gstatic.com` flagged as 404 — these are `rel="preconnect"` hints, not navigable URLs. The actual font resource is `fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat:...`, which loads correctly. QA probe artifact.

**Real issues:**

- **Malformed privacy URL (403):** `https://www.ihg.com/content/gb/en/customer-care/privacy_statement.` — trailing period is a syntactic error. Will 403/404 for all recipients. Must be corrected to remove the period.

- **SSO gateway link (403):** `https://ssogateway.points.com/SSOGateway/gateway.do?ssoProduct=buy_fasttrack&ssoSource=ihg&channel=SILK&ssoData=801a1cdf...` — 403 on probe is expected for a session-bound, tokenized SSO link. However, the encoded `ssoData` blob appears to be a static value in this sample. Verify that the `ssoData` parameter is rendered per-recipient at send time, not hardcoded at template level.

- **29 tracking links unverifiable:** All CTAs pass through click-redirect infrastructure (domain: `image.points-mail.com` or equivalent). Destination health cannot be confirmed without live redirect resolution.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Alt text failures — 30 images missing `alt` attribute:**

- Spacer GIFs (`spacer-IHG-email-template-new-2024-update.gif`, ~26 instances) hosted at `s3.amazonaws.com/points.com-marketing-images/...` — should carry `alt=""` (explicitly empty) so screen readers skip them. Absent `alt` causes readers to announce the filename.
- Content images missing descriptive alt:
  - `Shopping-cart-icon_1-IHG-email-template-new-2024-u...` (s3.amazonaws.com)
  - `star-icon_2-IHG-email-template-new-2024-update.png` (s3.amazonaws.com)

  - `603f2770-6bfd-45f6-8c2f-652bb772a282.png` (image.points-mail.com)

These two CDN-hosted PNGs have opaque filenames — their purpose cannot be determined without rendering; alt text is required.

**Image URL truncation:** Several S3 image URLs are truncated in the QA log (ending mid-filename). Verify full paths resolve correctly at send time — S3 key names with spaces (URL-encoded as `+`) can break in some email clients.

**MSO/VML conditionals:** Present and correctly structured (`<!--[if gte mso 9]>...<!--[if !mso]><!-->`). No issue.

**Viewport meta:** `width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0 ` — trailing space after `1.0` is benign but sloppy; no functional impact.
