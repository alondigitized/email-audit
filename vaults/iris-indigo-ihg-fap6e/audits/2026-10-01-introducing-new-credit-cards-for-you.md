# Introducing new credit cards for you 💳
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-01**
## Executive summary

- This is another credit-card acquisition send from IHG, which has pushed the Chase co-brand pitch for months. Today's version is a product-lineup email. A teal hero reads "Introducing our new cards, offers up to 200,000 bonus points" over a poolside sunset image with a "someday, SOONER" script overlay. Below it are three stacked card tiles: Premier at 180,000 points, Premier Credit Card at 200,000, and the base card at 125,000. A small-business tile closes the body.
- The layout is clean and the hierarchy works, with one card per block and a button under each. But the email is a credit-card ad with no stay offer, no destination and no member benefit. The headline "up to 200,000" conflicts with the first tile's "180,000", and the 200,000 sits two tiles down, so the top-line number is hard to match to a card. The three near-identical LEARN MORE buttons give no reason to pick one. The long legal block and the brand-logo wall at the bottom add weight.
- Compared with the 2026-09-20 "Where would you go with 140K bonus points?" send and the 2026-09-10 "Earn 140K bonus points" send, this is the same acquisition play with a new "new cards" framing and bigger numbers. A subscriber who has seen this pitch many times will likely skip it. I'd call it competent but fatigued.

## What's working

- The hero is attractive and on-brand, with a clear "up to 200,000 bonus points" headline in large type.
- The three tiles share one structure of card image, bold points figure, one-line benefit and button. That makes them easy to scan.
- Each tile has one button, so the click action is unambiguous.
- The "new" angle (Introducing new cards) gives a reason to send beyond the older 140K creative.
- The small-business tile reaches a different segment.

## What's weak

- "Up to 200,000" in the hero doesn't match the first tile's 180,000. The 200,000 tile is the second one, and the base card is only 125,000.
- All three buttons say "LEARN MORE", so there is no card-specific verb and no sense of what to choose.
- The copy gives no comparison of annual fee, earn rates or perks, so the reader can't pick a card.
- No deadline or urgency is visible, and the body copy mentions a "limited time" without giving a date.
- The legal and disclosure block at the bottom is long and dense. The brand logo wall is also long and distracts from the offer.
- It is a credit-card pitch from a loyalty-program sender, and it follows many similar sends. That is poor cadence.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace three "learn more" tiles with a single side-by-side comparison, with fee, bonus points and top perk for each card. Add one recommended card with a clear "Apply now" button.
- 2. Match the hero number to the first card shown. Lead with the 200,000 card, or say "up to 200,000" and put that card first.
- 3. Add a real deadline (a specific "offer ends" date) next to the points number.
- 4. Make the buttons specific: "Get 200,000 points", "Compare cards", "Apply for Premier".
- 5. Cut the logo wall and shorten the footer so the offer isn't buried.
- 6. Vary the creative from the repeated 140K sends and show what the points buy, such as a free night.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email has a malformed open-tracking pixel (an unencoded date in the URL), content placed before the DOCTYPE, and an `http://` image. Header-based compliance and authentication checks could not be verified because AgentMail didn't capture the headers. The source is truncated, so body-level findings are limited to what is visible.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Malformed open pixel.** The `src` is `http://mi.ihg.com/p/up/2b47e4754a78cfd7/o.gif?mi_u=695150425&mi_ecmp=542789&10/1/2026 12:00:00 AM`.
  - The trailing `10/1/2026 12:00:00 AM` is an unencoded date/time with spaces and slashes, and it has no `key=` name.
  - The QA parser read `2026 12:00:00 AM` as the alt text. That means the spaces split the attribute value, so `12:00:00` and `AM` likely parse as stray attributes.
  - Some clients will truncate or reject the URL, so opens could be undercounted.
- **Non-HTTPS pixel.** The same `mi.ihg.com` pixel loads over `http://`. This risks blocking or mixed-content warnings in some clients.
- **Second pixel.** `https://pxl.mon-trk.com/954cb38e-4a43-4730-b327-6b102c213a01/542789/0037y00002BeqgfAAB` carries a Salesforce-style Contact ID (`0037…` prefix) in the path. It is a per-recipient identifier exposed in an image URL, and the pixel has no `alt`.
- **Skipped links.** 24 tracking links were not probed because they are click-redirect domains. Their destinations and validity are unverified.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Content before the DOCTYPE.** `<div style="display:none;"> </div>` and the ADA comment blocks sit before `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN"...>`. Content ahead of the doctype can trigger quirks mode in some renderers and web-based clients.
- **Broken CSS selector.** `ReadMsgBody { width: 100%; }` is missing the leading `.`. As written it targets a `<ReadMsgBody>` element, which doesn't exist, so the rule does nothing.
- **Invalid CSS value.** `max-height: auto` (in `img`, `img .header`) is not valid CSS and will be ignored.
- **Unescaped ampersand.** `<title>IHG Hotels & Resorts</title>` should use `&amp;`.
- **Missing alt text.** Both tracking pixels (see section 2) lack `alt`. Use `alt=""` plus `width="1" height="1"` so screen readers skip them.
- **Web font.** The Google Fonts `<link>` is stripped by many clients. Montserrat falls back to Arial/Helvetica, so this is acceptable.
- **ADA blocks.** The "ADA ALT Block" and "ADA Aria-label_Landmark Block" comments are empty placeholders. Nothing is injected, so they add no accessibility value.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No unrendered merge tokens (`{{…}}`, `%%…%%`, `[FNAME]`) appear in the visible source. The date in the `mi.ihg.com` pixel (`10/1/2026 12:00:00 AM`) is a rendered token that was not URL-encoded (see section 2). The truncated body prevents a full check.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058).** Both are missing in the captured data. QA notes the relay may strip them, so this is unconfirmed rather than a confirmed sender defect.
- **`Authentication-Results`.** It was not captured, so SPF/DKIM/DMARC status is unknown. The sender is `IHGOneRewards@mc.ihg.com`, a subdomain, so alignment can't be assessed.
- **Body unsubscribe link and postal address.** Neither is in the truncated source, so they can't be confirmed or denied.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not verifiable. The 24 click-tracking links were skipped, and the visible source shows no UTM parameters or final landing URLs. The campaign ID `542789` appears in both pixels, which suggests consistent campaign tagging in the tracking layer.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Fix the `mi.ihg.com` pixel. URL-encode the date (`10%2F1%2F2026%2012%3A00%3A00%20AM`) or give it a parameter name, and serve it over `https://`.
2. Add `alt=""`, `width="1"` and `height="1"` to both tracking pixels.
3. Move the hidden `<div>` and the ADA comment blocks inside `<body>`, after the DOCTYPE.
4. Change `ReadMsgBody` to `.ReadMsgBody`, remove `max-height: auto`, and escape `&` in the title.
5. Stop putting raw CRM IDs (`0037y00002BeqgfAAB`) in pixel URLs. Use an opaque token instead.
6. For the unverified items, inspect the raw `.eml` in a real inbox to confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results`. Resolve a sample of the redirect links to confirm UTM and landing-page alignment.
