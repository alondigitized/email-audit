# Extra points are calling, Iris ☎️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-29**
## Executive summary

- IHG One Rewards is back with an almost pixel-identical buy-points promotion seven days after the June 22 "💯 Special offer: Get 100% more points, Iris" send. The email itself is well-executed — clean three-step explanation, strong orange CTAs, and the headline "This is your chance for 100% more points" lands without ambiguity. The hotel rooftop hero image sets aspirational context without feeling off-brand.
- The problem is repetition. A loyal IHG member who opened the June 22 email will experience this as a near-copy: same mechanic, same creative structure, same three-step layout, same CTA. That déjà vu will tank open rate and erode the promotion's credibility — if 100% bonus points were urgent last week, why are they still here this week? The subject line ("Extra points are calling, Iris ☎️") doesn't help — it soft-pedals the actual offer rather than differentiating this send from the prior one.
- The email would score higher if the cadence were fresh or the creative signaled progression (e.g., "last days to get 100%"). As it stands, a committed loyalty traveler might open it out of habit, but won't feel urgency to click.

## What's working

- **Strong headline with a concrete number** — "100% more points" is prominent and doesn't require scrolling to find.
- **Dual orange CTAs** ("BUY NOW") bookend the explainer copy, giving two clear action moments.
- **Three-step structure** (Buy → Get 100% more → Start planning) makes the mechanic scannable and easy to follow.
- **Brand logo grid** at the bottom reinforces the breadth of the IHG portfolio and reminds the reader where those points can be used.
- **Personalization in subject** — "Iris" in the subject line is a small but real signal of segmentation.

## What's weak

- **Repeat creative fatigue** — the June 22 send was structurally identical; this email looks like it was re-queued, not refreshed.
- **Subject buries the offer** — "Extra points are calling" is a soft hook that doesn't state 100%, buy-points, or any deadline; a member scanning their inbox has no reason to prioritize this over the prior send.
- **No deadline or urgency visible in the hero** — the offer timer or end date (if it exists) isn't prominent in the upper fold.
- **CTA copy is generic** — "BUY NOW" is a blunt call to transact; something like "Buy Points — Get 100% Back" would carry the benefit into the button itself.
- **Hero image is aspirational but abstract** — rooftop pool with no brand signage doesn't connect the dream to a specific bookable hotel.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Inject deadline urgency into the hero** — if the promotion ends July 4 (or any specific date), make that the headline, not supporting copy. "Last chance: 100% bonus points through July 4" outperforms "This is your chance."
- 2. **Differentiate from the June 22 send** — change the hero image to a different property type (a resort, a city hotel, a Kimpton), swap to a second-tier brand in the grid, or add a "Offer ends in X days" countdown. Signal that time has moved.
- 3. **Move the offer into the CTA copy** — "Buy Points, Double Them" or "Buy Points → Get 100% Back" converts the button from a transaction command into a benefit statement.
- 4. **Add a minimum urgency anchor** — even one line like "Offer valid through July 6" in the hero block would change the behavioral calculus.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 100% points bonus ends soon, Iris`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Double your IHG points before [date], Iris`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Buy points now and we'll match every single one — through [date] only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `100% bonus means your next stay costs half the points. Here's how.`

## Technical Audit — IHG One Rewards "Extra points are calling" (2026-07-01)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has a confirmed CAN-SPAM compliance failure (no body unsubscribe link detected), is missing required bulk-sender headers (`List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`), and has pervasive missing alt attributes across 30+ images. Two SSO destination links return 403 and one privacy URL has a trailing-period encoding defect.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**False positives in QA scan:**
- `https://fonts.googleapis.com` and `https://fonts.gstatic.com` flagged as broken (404) — these are `<link rel="preconnect">` DNS hints in `<head>`, not navigable URLs. Not real failures.

**Real issues:**

| Severity | URL | Finding |
|----------|-----|---------|
| FAIL | `https://www.ihg.com/content/gb/en/customer-care/privacy_statement.` | Trailing period in URL — likely a template encoding bug causing a 403/redirect failure |
| FAIL | `https://ssogateway.points.com/SSOGateway/gateway.do?ssoProduct=buy_fasttrack&ssoSource=ihg&channel=SILK&ssoData=…` | 403 on buy-points CTA; `ssoData` token may be expired or recipient-bound and invalid at crawl time — needs live recipient verification |
| FAIL | `https://ssogateway.points.com/SSOGateway/gateway.do?ssoProduct=gift_fasttrack&…` | Same 403 issue on gift-points CTA |

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Alt text — 30+ violations:**
Every spacer GIF (`spacer-IHG-email-template-new-2024-update.gif`, repeated ~28 times) has no `alt` attribute at all — it should be `alt=""` to signal decorative intent to screen readers. Three content images also lack alt text:

- `0612cf26-ec2e-4e9c-acd2-ad88a584c4d0.png` (image.points-mail.com)
- `603f2770-6bfd-45f6-8c2f-652bb772a282.png` (image.points-mail.com)
- `Shopping-cart-icon_1-IHG-email-template-new-2024-u…` (s3.amazonaws.com)
- `star-icon_2-IHG-email-template-new-2024-update.png` (s3.amazonaws.com)

Content images require descriptive alt text; spacers require `alt=""`.

**Other rendering notes:**
- `<title></title>` is empty — some clients (Samsung Mail, Outlook web) display this in tab titles; consider "IHG One Rewards — Double Points Offer."
- `p { Margin: 0px !important; }` — capital-M `Margin` is non-standard CSS. It works in Outlook via the MSO rendering engine but is technically invalid. Low risk, already deployed pattern.
- Preheader padding uses `&#8199;&#847;` (hair space + word-joiner) interleaved with `&shy;` — an aggressive cloaking technique. Renders correctly in major clients but may trigger spam heuristics in aggressive filters.
- Google Fonts load is correctly wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!-->` to suppress in Outlook. No issue.
