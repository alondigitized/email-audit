# Iris, every stay just got more rewarding
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-25**
## Executive summary

- This is the IHG credit card acquisition email again, and the subject hides it. "Every stay just got more rewarding" sounds like a loyalty-program update. The render is a Premier vs. Premier Select card pitch. The teal hero reads "OUR NEW PREMIUM CARDS / OFFERS UP TO 150,000 BONUS POINTS" over a "someday SOONER" lifestyle photo.
- The body is competent. Two card columns show 140,000 and 150,000 points, the $150 and $350 annual fees, and red "LEARN MORE" buttons. Below that, a long comparison grid covers 24X/28X earn rates, Elite status, 5X and 6X categories, the anniversary free night, and "Explore more benefits" links. The hierarchy is clean, but the email runs very long. A reader has to scroll past a dense table before reaching the second half of the pitch.
- This follows the 10-01, 10-08 and 10-29 credit-card sends in your history, and it lands one day after the 11-24 Cyber sale email. A subscriber who has seen this offer several times gets no new reason to open it. The subject doesn't say "credit card" or give a number, so it's the weakest part of the send. Overall it's a solid mid-tier email in a repetitive cadence.

## What's working

- The hero states the offer plainly: "UP TO 150,000 BONUS POINTS" in large white type on teal.
- The side-by-side Premier and Premier Select columns make the choice easy to compare, with the point totals and annual fees in the same positions.
- The red "LEARN MORE" buttons match the brand's other sends and are easy to find.
- There are no render problems. Images load, text doesn't overlap, and the grid is aligned.
- The lifestyle photo adds some warmth above an otherwise dense, text-heavy layout.

## What's weak

- The subject gives no hint that this is a credit card offer, so it doesn't match the content.
- "Learn more" is a weak CTA for a 150K-point offer, and the email repeats it in several places.
- The comparison grid below the cards is long and dense, with tiny icons and a lot of fine print ("Offer Details", "Pricing & Terms", spend caps).
- The spend requirements ($3,000 and $4,000 within three months) sit in small type under the points headline, which makes the offer feel less honest.
- This is the same credit card creative as 10-01, 10-08 and 10-29, with nothing new for this send.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the offer and the product in the subject, and keep "Iris" for personalization. This is the biggest lift available.
- 2. Replace "LEARN MORE" with "Get 150,000 Points" or "Compare the Cards".
- 3. Cut the comparison grid to the top three perks and link out to the rest.
- 4. Move the spend requirement up next to the points number.
- 5. Pause the card sends for a week, or rotate the creative, so subscribers don't see the same offer every few days.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email has valid personalization in the subject line and uses ESP tracking redirects. The QA data shows 15+ images without `alt` attributes, a malformed HTTP tracking-pixel URL, and markup that is out of order before the DOCTYPE. The header-based compliance checks (List-Unsubscribe, Authentication-Results) are inconclusive because the relay didn't capture the headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Malformed open-tracking pixel:** `http://mi.ihg.com/p/up/2b47e4754a78cfd7/o.gif?mi_u=695150425&mi_ecmp=571235&11/25/2026 12:00:00 AM`
  - The query string contains an unencoded date-time with literal spaces and slashes, and it has no `key=` name. This looks like a merge field or date token rendered into the URL without encoding.
  - Strict clients or proxies may truncate the URL or reject it, which would under-count opens.
  - It is also served over `http://` (see Rendering).
- **Contact ID in a pixel path:** `https://pxl.mon-trk.com/954cb38e-4a43-4730-b327-6b102c213a01/571235/0037y00002BeqgfAAB`. The final segment is a Salesforce Contact ID (`0037y…` prefix) exposed in a plain URL. It is a stable identifier visible to anyone who sees the source or forwards the email. This is an observation, not a functional break.
- **Unverified links:** 27 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the QA probe, so destination validity and redirect chains are unverified.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text, 15+ `<img>` tags.**
  - Affected images:
    - `…/m/1/18eba6ec-…jpg`
    - `…/m/1/486d3934-…png`
    - `…/m/1/e3691fe8-…png`
    - `…/m/1/46206e90-…png` (2×)
    - `…/m/10/437f94d8-fece-409a-8591-32f0b6ae72ad.png` (about 10×)
    - the two tracking pixels
  - The repeated `437f94d8…png` is probably an icon or spacer. If it's decorative, it needs `alt=""`. If it carries meaning, it needs descriptive text.
  - The "ADA ALT Block" comments at the top of the source are empty, so no alt remediation was applied.
  - Tracking pixels should also carry `alt=""`.
- **Mixed content:** `http://mi.ihg.com/.../o.gif` is non-HTTPS and may be blocked or rewritten by clients, which would suppress that open signal.
- **Markup before DOCTYPE:** `<div style="display:none;"> </div>` and comments appear before `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC …>`. Content before the DOCTYPE can trigger quirks mode in some renderers. This is only a risk, not a confirmed failure.
- **Invalid CSS selector:** `ReadMsgBody { width: 100%; }` is missing the leading `.` and targets a nonexistent element, so the Outlook.com fix doesn't apply.
- **Unescaped ampersand:** `<title>IHG Hotels & Resorts</title>` should use `&amp;`. This is minor.
- **Web font dependency:** Montserrat is loaded via `<link>` to Google Fonts and wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`. The `Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif` fallback is present, so this is fine.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The subject renders the name correctly ("Iris, every stay…"), and no unresolved `%%…%%`, `{{…}}`, or `${…}` tokens were reported by QA.
- The only token problem is the unencoded date in the `mi.ihg.com` pixel URL (see Section 2).
- The truncated source doesn't allow a full scan of the body.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** Both are not found. QA notes they may not be captured by the AgentMail relay, so this is unconfirmed. If they are absent at the sender, Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements are not met.
- **Authentication-Results:** Not found, so SPF/DKIM/DMARC status is unknown. The sender domain `mc.ihg.com` could not be checked for alignment.
- **Body unsubscribe link and postal address:** Not visible in the truncated source, so they can't be assessed.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Cannot be assessed. All 27 click links are tracking redirects that weren't resolved, so the UTM parameters and landing-page destinations are unknown.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add `alt` to all 15+ flagged images. Use `alt=""` for decorative images, spacers and pixels, and descriptive text for content images.
2. Fix the `mi.ihg.com` pixel:
   - URL-encode the date.
   - Give it a parameter name.
   - Serve it over HTTPS.
3. Move the DOCTYPE to the first line of the document and put the hidden preheader `<div>` inside `<body>`.
4. Correct `ReadMsgBody` to `.ReadMsgBody` and escape `&` in `<title>`.
5. Confirm at the raw-message level that `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` are present, and that SPF, DKIM and DMARC pass and align for `mc.ihg.com`.
6. Resolve the 27 redirect links in a follow-up probe to verify the destinations and UTM parameters.
7. Consider removing the raw Salesforce Contact ID from the `pxl.mon-trk.com` path in favor of an opaque token.
