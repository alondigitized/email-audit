# Homepage snapshot · www.ihg.com · 2026-07-19
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- I opened IHG.com and got a cookie consent wall before I could see anything. The actual homepage is completely obscured — I can barely make out what looks like a beautiful overwater-resort scene peeking behind the modal, and the header shows the IHG logo with a "Sign in / Join for free" link, but that's genuinely all I have. Right now this reads less like a hotel brand and more like a compliance checkbox exercise. For someone who opens IHG.com to dream-browse Six Senses and Kimpton properties, this is a brutal first impression.

## What's working

- **The resort background image**: What little I can see — turquoise water, wooden overwater structures, tropical blue sky — is genuinely evocative of Six Senses or an IHG luxury property. If that's what I'd see without the modal, we'd be talking.
- **"Sign in / Join for free" in the header**: At least the hook to return or join One Rewards is persistent in the nav — it survives the modal.
- **Clean modal rendering**: The consent dialogue itself is legible, buttons are clearly labeled, three-tier structure (Continue / Manage Preferences / Opt-Out) respects user choice. Better than most.

## What's weak

- **The cookie wall is the entire viewport**: No amount of beautiful brand imagery survives a full-screen text wall as the first thing I read. The luxury positioning evaporates instantly.
- **Zero brand voice in the consent copy**: "We use cookies and other tracking technologies, including session replay tracking" — cold, legal, off-brand for a company selling Six Senses retreats and Kimpton vibes.
- **No loyalty acknowledgment before the gate**: If I'm already a One Rewards member, there's nothing here that says "welcome back, Iris." The header CTA could say "Sign in to see your member rates" but it doesn't.
- **The resort scene is barely legible**: The background image is obscured enough that it might as well not exist. What should be the emotional hook is a ghost.

## Recommendations

- **Move cookie consent to a slim bottom banner** instead of a full-page takeover — let the homepage breathe and the luxury imagery lead. Compliance doesn't require nuking the experience.
- **Rewrite the consent copy in brand voice**: Something like "Before you explore, a quick note on how we personalize your journey" — still compliant, actually sounds like a hotel that cares about hospitality.
- **Surface a One Rewards member greeting in the header**: "Sign in to unlock member rates" is one tiny copy change that activates the loyalty hook for returners before they even see the homepage.
- **Make the background image visible even with the banner**: If you must show a consent notice, use an overlay approach that keeps 60-70% of the hero imagery legible — I should feel the brand while I'm consenting, not after.
