# Homepage snapshot · www.ihg.com · 2026-05-19
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- Before I can even see what IHG wants to sell me, I'm staring at a full-screen cookie consent wall in corporate red-and-white. The booking widget is there — dates already populated for tonight — but any hero imagery, lifestyle content, or loyalty hooks are completely buried. This feels less like a brand experience and more like a legal department's homepage.

## What's working

- **Sign in / Join for free** — clean placement in the top nav; low-friction entry point for someone already in the loyalty program
- **Booking widget with today's dates pre-populated** — smart UX shortcut; it knows I might be looking for tonight
- **Three-button cookie consent** — CONTINUE, MANAGE PREFERENCES, OPT-OUT is actually more honest than most brands' dark-pattern "Accept All / Reject All buried in settings" approach

## What's weak

- **Cookie modal as first impression** — this is not a brand moment. A full-screen takeover before I see a single hotel photo is a conversion killer, especially on mobile where real estate is precious
- **Zero lifestyle content visible** — nothing here signals Hotel Indigo, Kimpton, Six Senses, or anything else in the premium-lifestyle tier of the portfolio; the page is functionally blank behind the modal
- **No loyalty signal in the booking widget** — even with the modal up, there's room in the header to show a "your points: X" hook for signed-in members
- **"TRAVEL ADVISORY" banner** — sits above everything in amber-warning style; before I even get to the cookie wall I'm primed for friction, not aspiration

## Recommendations

- **Shrink the cookie consent to a bottom banner** (à la GDPR-compliant slimline bars) so the hero is immediately visible; the legal disclosure doesn't need to own the full viewport
- **Lead the header with a loyalty tier hook** — "Welcome back, Iris" or even just a points balance chip next to the Sign In CTA so IHG One Rewards members feel recognized before they dismiss the modal
- **Inject the current Accelerate offer or 4th-night-free messaging into the booking widget** — even one line under the date picker ("Book 4 nights, pay for 3 with One Rewards") would surface the reason I'd book direct vs. Booking.com
- **Swap the travel advisory to a collapsible drawer** — if it's a real safety notice it should be accessible, but it shouldn't be the first visual impression competing with brand identity
