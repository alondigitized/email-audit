# Iris, 20,000 points is a really big deal 🔥
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-28**
## Executive summary

- This is the third send of the same 20,000-point offer. It follows "how's 20,000 points sound?" (09-28) and "🛎️ Ding ding! Your 20,000 points offer ends soon." (10-05, 7/10). The offer is still one clear idea: register, stay 2 nights, get 20,000 bonus points. The deadline has moved to November 12, which gives the repeat a reason to exist. The subject names the recipient and puts the offer in the first words.
- The body is easy to follow. A "LIMITED TIME OFFER" banner sits over a bold "EARN 20,000 BONUS POINTS" headline. A single "Register in 1-click" button follows, then a hero photo of a Crowne Plaza property, then "Earn in 3 easy steps" and a repeat of the button. That is good discipline compared with the credit-card sends of 10-01 to 10-22.
- The rest drags it down. After the second CTA there is a long logo wall of about 20 brands and a very long block of terms and conditions. The copy also says "register by November 12, but you only have 60 days after you register to stay", which is slightly confusing. It is a solid, familiar loyalty send, but it is the third nudge on one offer, so it needs a fresher hook to feel like more than a reminder.
- My view of the render was small, so I could not read the fine print or any button wording beyond the headline copy.

## What's working

- A single offer, "20,000 bonus points when you stay 2 nights", sits in the headline.
- The "Register in 1-click" button is high-contrast and appears twice, above and below the steps.
- The "Earn in 3 easy steps" block makes the mechanic simple: register, book, stay.
- The Nov 12 registration deadline and the 60-day stay window give a real time frame.
- The subject uses the recipient's first name and a single emoji.

## What's weak

- The offer is the third send in a month with the same 20,000-point hook, so it risks reading as fatigue.
- The logo wall of about 20 brands and the long T&Cs make the lower half of the email heavy and push the app-download block out of focus.
- The hero photo is a generic hotel exterior and says nothing about where or why to stay.
- There are two deadlines (register by Nov 12, stay within 60 days of registering), which is harder to take in than one date.
- "By clicking, you are agreeing to the campaign's terms & conditions" appears twice, which adds friction and makes the offer feel a little less clean.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put one concrete date in the subject and first line, such as "Register by Nov 12", and state the 60-day stay window once. This lifts open and click more than any design change.
- 2. Remove or collapse the logo wall and move the T&Cs behind a link. End the email at the second CTA and the app-download block.
- 3. Replace the generic hotel hero with a destination or trip-type image matched to the recipient's past stays or location.
- 4. Say what the points are worth, for example "enough for a free night at select hotels".
- 5. Space this offer out from the 10-05 send, or change the creative so it doesn't read as a repeat.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 20,000 points are waiting, Iris`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stay 2 nights within 60 days of registering. Takes one click.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Register by Nov 12, then book your next 2-night stay.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's markup is mostly standard for an ESP-built template, with dark-pixel tracking and ADA comment blocks. The confirmed defects are a malformed, non-HTTPS open-tracking pixel URL and content ahead of the DOCTYPE. Header-based compliance and authentication checks could not be verified from this capture.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Malformed tracking pixel URL.** The pixel `http://mi.ihg.com/p/up/2b47e4754a78cfd7/o.gif?mi_u=695150425&mi_ecmp=550736&10/28/2026 12:00:00 AM` has three problems:
  - It contains raw spaces and slashes (`10/28/2026 12:00:00 AM`) in the query string, with no `key=` and no URL-encoding. This looks like an unencoded date merge value. Clients and proxies may truncate, reject, or mis-resolve the URL, so opens may be under-counted.
  - It uses `http://` (see Rendering).
  - The QA tool parsed the tail of the URL as the image's alt text (`2026 12:00:00 AM`), which confirms the URL is broken.
- **Second pixel:** `https://pxl.mon-trk.com/954cb38e-4a43-4730-b327-6b102c213a01/550736/0037y00002BeqgfAAB` is HTTPS and well-formed. Its path ends in what looks like an 18-character Salesforce Contact ID (`0037y00002BeqgfAAB`). That is a persistent recipient identifier in a URL, which is a privacy consideration if the email is forwarded or rendered in a shared context.
- **36 tracking/redirect links were skipped** from the HTTP probe, so their destinations and status codes are unverified. This is a coverage gap, not a confirmed defect.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Content before the DOCTYPE.** `<div style="display:none;"> </div>` and the ADA comment blocks precede `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN"...>`. Content ahead of the doctype can trigger quirks mode in some clients and is invalid markup.
- **Missing alt text on both tracking pixels.** QA flagged `pxl.mon-trk.com/.../0037y00002BeqgfAAB` and the `mi.ihg.com/.../o.gif` pixel. For 1×1 tracking images, an explicit `alt=""` is the correct fix, since a missing alt attribute can cause screen readers to announce the filename or URL.
- **Non-HTTPS image.** The `o.gif` pixel loads over `http://`. It may be blocked or rewritten by clients that enforce HTTPS, and it can trigger mixed-content handling in webmail.
- **Ineffective CSS selector.** `ReadMsgBody { width: 100%; }` is missing its leading dot. As written it targets a nonexistent `<ReadMsgBody>` element, so the Outlook.com rule has no effect. The impact is minor.
- **Unescaped ampersand.** `<title>IHG Hotels & Resorts</title>` should use `&amp;`. The impact is minor.
- **Web font dependency.** The Montserrat `<link>` to `fonts.googleapis.com` is stripped by Gmail and some other clients. The `Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif` fallback in the stylesheet covers this, so it is not a defect.
- The `html lang="en"` attribute and the viewport meta tag are present.
- The source is truncated, so I can't assess alt text on content images, table roles, or the rest of the markup.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved tokens (`{{ }}`, `%%...%%`, `[FirstName]`) are visible in the supplied source or the subject. The subject's "Iris," merged correctly.
- The `o.gif` pixel has a bare date value with no parameter name. If it came from a merge field, it was inserted unencoded, which is the same defect as in Section 2.
- The body is truncated, so I can't check the merge fields in it.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058)** were not found. The QA tool notes that the AgentMail relay may strip them. This is unconfirmed, so I'm not asserting that the sender omitted them. Check the raw message headers at the origin. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require one-click unsubscribe.
- **Authentication-Results** is absent, so SPF, DKIM, and DMARC cannot be assessed. The From domain `mc.ihg.com` is a sending subdomain, but its alignment is unverified.
- **In-body unsubscribe link and physical postal address** are not visible in the truncated source. They are unverified, not missing.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Not assessable. 36 links were skipped as click-redirect domains, and the supplied source shows no resolved destination URLs or UTM parameters.
- Both pixels carry campaign ID `550736` (`mi_ecmp=550736` and the path segment in the `pxl.mon-trk.com` URL). This is consistent tagging across the two pixels.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Fix the `mi.ihg.com` pixel URL.** Encode the date value, give it a parameter name (for example `&mi_dt=10%2F28%2F2026%2012%3A00%3A00%20AM`), or remove it. Serve the pixel over HTTPS.
2. **Add `alt=""`** to both tracking pixels, along with `width="1" height="1"` and `style="display:block"`.
3. **Move the DOCTYPE to the first line** of the document. Put the hidden div and the ADA comments inside `<body>`.
4. **Correct the `.ReadMsgBody` selector** and escape `&` in `<title>`.
5. **Inspect the original message headers** outside the relay for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and `Authentication-Results`, to settle the compliance and deliverability warnings.
6. **Resolve the 36 redirect links** with a redirect-following probe or from the full HTML. This would confirm the destinations, UTM parameters, and the in-body unsubscribe link.
7. **Review the Salesforce Contact ID in the pixel path** to decide whether a recipient identifier should appear in a URL.
