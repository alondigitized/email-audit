# Free Shipping on Orders $75+
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- This is Bombas's transactional-ish shipping incentive email — the functional kind brands send when they want basket-building without offering a percent-off discount. The creative is minimal: big "FREE SHIPPING" headline in Bombas's signature chunky serif, a cropped sock-leg lifestyle shot, three gender-segment CTAs, and a quick trust-block reiterating the offer. It's clean and on-brand, but it's also almost entirely generic — there's no product, no specific item to covet, no urgency.
- The timing is telling. After a run of editorial catalog emails (sock heights, quarter socks, compression colors, running colors), this feels like a forced yield — something to send on a Thursday before the July 4th weekend that doesn't demand new creative. The $75 threshold is reasonable for Bombas but the email gives you no compelling reason to hit it. There's no "here's what to buy to get there" product module, no curated bundle, nothing that pulls you toward an actual transaction.
- The result is a functional but forgettable send. It confirms Bombas ships free at $75 — information a subscriber probably already knows — and then asks them to shop with no additional pull. A weak email in an already email-heavy week.

## What's working

- **Headline hierarchy is immediately legible** — "FREE SHIPPING" dominates the fold in large, bold type; the offer is understood in under a second.
- **Three-way gender segmentation CTAs** (Shop Women / Shop Men / Shop Kids) are clear, well-styled dark buttons above the fold — low friction for the right segment.
- **Trust badges (One Purchased One Donated, 100% Happiness Guaranteed)** are visible and reinforce brand equity at a glance.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, images load, text is crisp, no broken elements.
- **Offer copy is honest and specific** — "$75+" threshold is stated clearly in both the headline block and the body copy.

## What's weak

- **No product is featured** — the hero image is just a cropped sock leg; there's nothing to want. Shipping incentive emails work best when they show you what to buy.
- **Zero urgency** — no expiration date, no "this weekend only," no countdown. The offer could last forever, so there's no reason to act now.
- **Repeats known information** — any Bombas subscriber who's ordered before already knows about the $75 free-shipping threshold. This email teaches nothing new and creates no new desire.
- **"SIGNED. SEALED. DELIVERED." headline is cute but empty** — it adds another word-mark but contributes no call to action or persuasive content.
- **Four-email-in-a-week cadence fatigue** — this is the third or fourth promotional email in under five days; the marginal open rate on this one was almost certainly lower than any of its predecessors.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a curated product module** — show 3–4 specific products (with prices) that get you to $75. The email currently has zero merchandise. Every free-shipping email should answer "here's what to buy to get there."
- 2. **Add a credible deadline** — "Free shipping this weekend only" or "through July 5" tied to the July 4th holiday would create actual urgency. Right now there's none.
- 3. **Reframe around July 4th gifting** — the holiday is tomorrow; this could have been "Stock up before the 4th" with summer-relevant product callouts. The seasonal angle was entirely missed.
- 4. **Cut the "SIGNED. SEALED. DELIVERED." section** — it takes up space, adds no persuasive content, and pushes the product (if there were any) further down.
- 5. **Subject refinement** — the current subject is purely functional; a small upgrade with urgency and/or a product hook would meaningfully lift opens.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free shipping this weekend — stock up on summer faves`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last chance for free shipping before the 4th 🇺🇸`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Orders $75+ ship free — here's what to grab before July 4th.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Build your bundle: these picks get you to $75 fast.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Bug confirmed — missing CSS class selector dot (600px breakpoint):**

```css
/* In @media only screen and (max-width:600px) */
mobile-third { width: 33% !important; max-width: 33% !important; }
/* Missing leading dot — this rule matches nothing */
```

The sibling `.mobile-third` rule in the 480px breakpoint is correctly prefixed. The 600px rule is dead code. Any element using `class="mobile-third"` at tablet width will not render at the intended 33%.

**Bug confirmed — duplicate `xmlns` attribute on `<html>` tag:**

```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" ... xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" ...>
```

Duplicate attribute is invalid XML/XHTML. While most email clients parse past it, strict parsers may drop the element or throw a warning.

**Dark mode `.dark-mode-show` set to `display: block`:**

```css
.dark-mode-show { display: block !important; }
```

If this class is applied to `<table>`, `<tr>`, or `<td>` elements (common in email), forcing `display: block` will break table layout in dark mode. Should use `display: table`, `display: table-row`, or `display: table-cell` depending on context. Needs verification against where the class is actually applied in the full source.
