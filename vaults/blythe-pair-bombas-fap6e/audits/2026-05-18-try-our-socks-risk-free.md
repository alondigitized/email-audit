# Try Our Socks, Risk-Free
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a guarantee/reassurance email — Bombas is pre-empting common first-purchase objections with four tiled scenarios: wrong size, wrong color, dryer ate one, just not feeling it. The "100% HAPPINESS 0% HASSLE" hero makes the emotional promise, and the four-tile grid below makes it tangible. The copywriting on the tiles ("DRYER GOT HUNGRY?") is genuinely good. The 20% off code COMFORT20 sits in a thin green top banner and is repeated in the bottom CTA block — but the code is identical to the one from the welcome email two days ago. A subscriber who opened that email gets the same promo with different wrapping, which deflates the sense of value.
- The structural problem is focus. Three CTAs (Shop Women / Shop Men / Shop Kids) split attention without directing the reader anywhere specific. No product is featured, no deadline exists, and the offer is never the visual star — the guarantee messaging is. That's a legitimate strategy for a nurture email, but it means this send converts mostly on brand warmth, not on offer urgency.
- Bottom line: clean execution of a stock retention playbook. The guarantee tile copy earns its keep, but recycled COMFORT20, zero urgency, and a split three-way CTA leave real conversion on the table for day three of a welcome sequence.

## What's working

- **"DRYER GOT HUNGRY?" tile** — memorable, specific, and earns a smile. The four-tile grid makes the abstract guarantee feel concrete.
- **Top banner doubles as preheader** — "Get 20% Off Your First Purchase with Code COMFORT20" leads the email and would show in the inbox preview, complementing the "Risk-Free" subject nicely.
- **Clean render, zero bugs** — layout is tight, images load, no overlapping elements.
- **Bottom CTA block redundancy** — "Grab a Pair Risk-Free" at the bottom restates the offer for scroll-through readers who missed the top banner.

## What's weak

- **Same COMFORT20 code as May 16's welcome email** — three days in, the "discount" is indistinguishable from the default onboarding offer. No freshness, no reward signal.
- **Three competing CTAs dilute action** — Shop Women / Shop Men / Shop Kids asks the reader to make a decision that should have been pre-answered by segmentation.
- **No product shown** — the entire email is about the guarantee but features no specific sock, no colorway, no bestseller. Bombas's product is the point; keeping it abstract is a missed click driver.
- **Zero urgency** — no deadline, no countdown, no inventory signal. The risk-free framing actually undercuts any urgency: if there's no risk, there's no reason to act now.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make one product the hero, not the guarantee** — show the #1 bestselling style in the hero image with a "try it risk-free" callout underneath. The guarantee becomes the reason to act, not the entire email.
- 2. **Collapse to one CTA based on gender/segment** — if you know whether the subscriber is female or male, send one "Shop Women" button. Three-way split creates micro-friction.
- 3. **Add a deadline** — even a soft one. "Code expires Sunday" doubles as urgency and scarcity without feeling manipulative.
- 4. **Retire COMFORT20 or rename it** — if this is the third email with the same code, call it something new (TRYME20, FIRSTPAIR20) so it feels like a distinct offer, not a recycled coupon.
- 5. **Surface one piece of social proof** — the May 17 email had strong customer quotes. One of those in the footer ("I never knew I could love a sock") would bridge the send cadence and reinforce the guarantee claim.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your First Pair Is Risk-Free (+ 20% Off)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Sock That Comes With a Promise`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Didn't love them? We'll replace, exchange, or refund — no questions.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Wrong size, wrong color, or the dryer ate one. We've got you.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Bug — missing CSS class selector dot (confirmed):**
In the `@media only screen and (max-width:600px)` block:
```css
mobile-third{width:33%!important;max-width:33%!important}
```
The leading `.` is absent. This rule targets the element type `mobile-third` (which doesn't exist), not the class `.mobile-third`. The one-third column layout will **never apply on mobile**. Compare to the correctly prefixed `.mobile-half` immediately above it.

**Google Fonts via `@import` (confirmed):**
```html
<style>
  @import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Poppins:...&display=swap);
</style>
```
Gmail (web and iOS/Android app) strips `@import` directives from `<style>` blocks. Poppins will silently fail for all Gmail recipients, falling back to the default sans-serif. The MSO fallback (`font-family:Arial,Helvetica,sans-serif`) is correctly wired for Outlook via conditional comment.

**Empty first preheader div (confirmed):**
```html
<div style="color:#f3f3f3;display:none!important;font-size:1px;line-height:1px;
            max-height:0;max-width:0;mso-hide:all!important;opacity:0;
            overflow:hidden;visibility:hidden"></div>
```
This hidden div contains no text. The technique is sometimes used to suppress default preview text in certain clients, but here the div is entirely empty with no apparent function. Likely a leftover template artifact.

**No issues found:**
- `role="article"`, `aria-roledescription="email"`, `aria-label="Email from Bombas"`, `lang="en"`, `dir="ltr"` all correctly set on wrapper `<div>`.
- `<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">` present.
- `<meta name="color-scheme" content="light dark">` and `@media (prefers-color-scheme:dark)` block with `.darkbg`, `.darkcolor`, `.darkBorder` utilities are present.
