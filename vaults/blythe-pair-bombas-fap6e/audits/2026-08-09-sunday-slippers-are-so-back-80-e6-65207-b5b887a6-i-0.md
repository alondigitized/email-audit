# Sunday Slippers Are So Back
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-09**
## Executive summary

- Bombas returns to its slipper franchise with "Sunday Slippers Are So Back," and it's a clean, single-category launch that follows the exact same disciplined playbook the brand has run all week — hero image, two sub-lines (Original Sunday gingham, Sunday Plush leopard), gender-split CTAs throughout. It's well-lit, cozy, on-brand, and easy to scan on mobile without any layout breakage.
- The catch: this is a slipper email in a week that just ran the 8/2 "Everything New" send featuring cherry-embroidered slippers and a four-way sock push. Slippers aren't a new idea to this inbox, and "so back" as a hook only lands if the subscriber remembers slippers left in the first place — most won't. Compared to 8/6's compression launch or 8/5's dress-sock relaunch, which introduced genuinely new product lines, this is closer to a restock notice dressed up as a launch.
- Solid, safe, on-brand — but it's the least differentiated send in this stretch, and the "Kids" nav link with zero kids product in the body is a real miss for a family-forward brand.

## What's working

- Hero image is warm and inviting — two pairs of feet in gingham/plaid slippers against wood paneling reads instantly as "cozy Sunday."
- "MAKE WAY FOR SUNDAY" is a strong, punchy hero headline with a "Limited Edition" kicker for urgency.
- Clear two-product structure (Original Sunday, Sunday Plush) each with its own CTA, consistent with the brand's proven single-category discipline.
- "They're (finally) back, and will (probably) sell out again quickly" on Sunday Plush is a nice, low-key urgency line that feels honest rather than pushy.
- Trust badges (One Purchased One Donated, 100% Happiness Guaranteed) reinforce brand credibility at the footer.

## What's weak

- Sunday Plush only has a "Shop Women" button — no men's CTA, which breaks the gender-parity pattern the rest of the email (and recent Bombas sends) established.
- "Kids" appears in the top nav but no kids slippers appear anywhere in the body — a dead-end category link.
- "SUNDAY NEVER LOOKED LESS SCARY" is a confusing tagline that doesn't connect to anything else in the email — no visual or copy setup makes "scary" make sense.
- This is the second slipper-adjacent send in a week (after 8/2's cherry-embroidered slipper push), so cadence is repetitive for anyone who opened that one too.
- No visible price or specific discount anywhere — this is a product launch, not a promo, so click intent relies entirely on product appeal rather than an offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a "Shop Men" or "Shop Kids" button under Sunday Plush so the CTA structure stays consistent and doesn't silently exclude segments who scrolled that far already engaged.
- 2. Cut or explain "Sunday Never Looked Less Scary" — either build supporting visual/copy around it or replace with a line that reinforces the cozy/restock story.
- 3. Either add a kids slipper module or drop "Kids" from the nav for this send so the category promise matches the content.
- 4. Since there's no discount, lean harder into scarcity/limited-edition framing in the subject to justify urgency for a repeat product.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Gingham & Leopard: New Sunday Slipper Colors`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Sunday Slippers Just Got a Refresh`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New gingham and leopard prints on our best-selling slippers — limited edition.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sold-out Sunday Plush is finally back in stock.`
- 5. Space out slipper content from the 8/2 roundup send — if slippers are recurring inventory, consider folding restocks into a broader multi-category email instead of a dedicated send twice in one week.

## Full review
## 1. Overview

Bombas returns to its slipper franchise with "Sunday Slippers Are So Back," and it's a clean, single-category launch that follows the exact same disciplined playbook the brand has run all week — hero image, two sub-lines (Original Sunday gingham, Sunday Plush leopard), gender-split CTAs throughout. It's well-lit, cozy, on-brand, and easy to scan on mobile without any layout breakage.

The catch: this is a slipper email in a week that just ran the 8/2 "Everything New" send featuring cherry-embroidered slippers and a four-way sock push. Slippers aren't a new idea to this inbox, and "so back" as a hook only lands if the subscriber remembers slippers left in the first place — most won't. Compared to 8/6's compression launch or 8/5's dress-sock relaunch, which introduced genuinely new product lines, this is closer to a restock notice dressed up as a launch.

Solid, safe, on-brand — but it's the least differentiated send in this stretch, and the "Kids" nav link with zero kids product in the body is a real miss for a family-forward brand.

## 2. What worked

- Hero image is warm and inviting — two pairs of feet in gingham/plaid slippers against wood paneling reads instantly as "cozy Sunday."
- "MAKE WAY FOR SUNDAY" is a strong, punchy hero headline with a "Limited Edition" kicker for urgency.
- Clear two-product structure (Original Sunday, Sunday Plush) each with its own CTA, consistent with the brand's proven single-category discipline.
- "They're (finally) back, and will (probably) sell out again quickly" on Sunday Plush is a nice, low-key urgency line that feels honest rather than pushy.
- Trust badges (One Purchased One Donated, 100% Happiness Guaranteed) reinforce brand credibility at the footer.

## 3. What didn't

- Sunday Plush only has a "Shop Women" button — no men's CTA, which breaks the gender-parity pattern the rest of the email (and recent Bombas sends) established.
- "Kids" appears in the top nav but no kids slippers appear anywhere in the body — a dead-end category link.
- "SUNDAY NEVER LOOKED LESS SCARY" is a confusing tagline that doesn't connect to anything else in the email — no visual or copy setup makes "scary" make sense.
- This is the second slipper-adjacent send in a week (after 8/2's cherry-embroidered slipper push), so cadence is repetitive for anyone who opened that one too.
- No visible price or specific discount anywhere — this is a product launch, not a promo, so click intent relies entirely on product appeal rather than an offer.

## 4. What I'd change

1. Add a "Shop Men" or "Shop Kids" button under Sunday Plush so the CTA structure stays consistent and doesn't silently exclude segments who scrolled that far already engaged.
2. Cut or explain "Sunday Never Looked Less Scary" — either build supporting visual/copy around it or replace with a line that reinforces the cozy/restock story.
3. Either add a kids slipper module or drop "Kids" from the nav for this send so the category promise matches the content.
4. Since there's no discount, lean harder into scarcity/limited-edition framing in the subject to justify urgency for a repeat product.
   - **Subject Alt A:** `Gingham & Leopard: New Sunday Slipper Colors`
   - **Subject Alt B:** `Your Sunday Slippers Just Got a Refresh`
   - **Preheader Alt A:** `New gingham and leopard prints on our best-selling slippers — limited edition.`
   - **Preheader Alt B:** `Sold-out Sunday Plush is finally back in stock.`
5. Space out slipper content from the 8/2 roundup send — if slippers are recurring inventory, consider folding restocks into a broader multi-category email instead of a dedicated send twice in one week.
