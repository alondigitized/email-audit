# Everything New for Summer
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- This is Bombas's summer new-arrivals catalog email — a broad "everything new" sweep across Women, Men, and Kids. The hero delivers fast: "THE BIG SUMMER REFRESH" in chunky type with a trio of sock/sneaker imagery that's bright and seasonal. But it stalls almost immediately. Below the fold, the email collapses into a near-empty void — a two-line teaser sentence, a "GO WILD" headline, and three category nav links that feel like placeholders rather than a real product story. There is no offer, no specific product, no price, no urgency. It's a homepage, not a campaign.
- Coming the day after the MARVEL collab drop (2026-07-10, 8/10) and a full week of strong sends, this email is the cadence hangover. The creative quality of the hero is fine, but the email has nothing to sell — no anchor product, no discount, no reason to click today versus tomorrow. "Everything New for Summer" is the least specific thing Bombas could say.
- **Judgement:** A clean but hollow catalog push. The hero earns its keep; everything below it is filler. Misses its potential by not picking one hero product and committing to it.

## What's working

- **Hero headline is on-brief:** "THE BIG SUMMER REFRESH" is clear, season-appropriate, and reads in a single glance.
- **Three-way CTA split is clean:** "Shop Women / Shop Men / Shop Kids" immediately beneath the hero gives every segment a clear entry point.
- **Imagery is bright and product-forward:** The sock-on-foot photography is attractive and communicates the product category without ambiguity.
- **Navigation bar echoes the CTAs:** Women / Men / Kids in the top nav reinforces segmentation — a small but good structural decision.

## What's weak

- **No offer whatsoever:** After a week of 10–20% discounts and pack deals, this email arrives with nothing — no percent off, no dollar threshold, no free shipping.
- **"GO WILD" is dead copy:** The sub-headline below the fold is vague to the point of meaninglessness — it connects to nothing visible.
- **No hero product:** There's no single item anchoring the email. "Everything new" is the enemy of conversion.
- **The body is nearly empty:** Below the hero, there's a two-line sentence, a vague CTA set, and a footer. This email has almost no content to justify opening it.
- **Render bug — two icon badges are unreadable:** The bottom badge row shows indistinct blobs (appears to be "B Corp Certified" and "100% Happiness Guaranteed" icons) but the text is too small to read at email widths.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero product and show it.** "Everything new" is a trap. Choose the single best new summer arrival — a colorway, a pack, a fabric innovation — and make the email about that. The rest can live in a supporting grid below.
- 2. **Add an offer or at minimum a reason-to-click-today hook.** After days of 10–20% mechanics, this email offers nothing. Even "Free shipping on new arrivals this week" would anchor it.
- 3. **Replace "GO WILD" with something that says what you're selling.** It could be "5 new styles just dropped" or "New colors across every category." Tell, don't tease.
- 4. **Expand the body with at least one product grid.** Three or four product tiles with names and prices turn a catalog teaser into an actual email.
- 5. **Tighten the subject line to something specific.** "Everything New for Summer" is the lowest-energy possible framing. Reference the actual product.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Just in: our biggest summer lineup yet`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New colors, new styles — across every category.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop Women, Men & Kids — all new arrivals inside.`
