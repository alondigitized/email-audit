# New Compression Colors Are Hot Right Now
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, well-organized product launch email announcing new colorways for Bombas's Everyday Compression line. The creative is structured around two feature callouts — "Medium Support" (15-20 mmHg) and "Comfort Cushion" — each with its own gender-split CTAs and product photography. That's a smart editorial pattern: you're educating the subscriber on what makes compression worth buying, not just shouting colors at them.
- The problem is the subject line — "New Compression Colors Are Hot Right Now" — sets up an expectation of vibrant, seasonal color story, but the email opens on muted neutrals (beige, blue-grey, dusty pink). The creative doesn't deliver on the "hot" heat the subject promises. The new-color product shots are small thumbnails anchored at the bottom of each section, so the actual newness gets buried under the benefit copy.
- Overall this is a competent product announcement that undersells the visual hook it's trying to make. Better than last week's string of pure-catalog sends (June 27, June 29 both hit 5/10) but doesn't reach the urgency benchmark of the June 28 Low Stock email that earned a 7.

## What's working

- Dual gender CTAs ("Shop Women" / "Shop Men") appear consistently throughout — frictionless segmentation at every decision point.
- The benefit hierarchy is clear: Medium Support → spec (15-20 mmHg) → Comfort Cushion → tactile descriptor. Real reasons to buy, not just "quality socks."
- Product thumbnails at the bottom of each section include labeled variant names, giving the subscriber something specific to click toward.
- The dark footer section "Less Stressin' More Compression" with the trust-badge icons (Zero Gravity Sole, 100% Happiness Guarantee, etc.) reinforces value without cluttering the body.
- No render bugs visible — images load, text is legible, layout holds.

## What's weak

- The "hot new colors" premise is immediately undercut by a palette of neutrals and muted blues — the hero image doesn't say "new colors," it says "same socks."
- Dual hero text "NEW COLORS / EVERYDAY COMPRESSION REFRESH" competes with itself; the eye doesn't know which line is the headline.
- No offer or incentive anywhere in the email — pure editorial with zero discount, following two other non-discount sends this week (June 29, June 27). The cadence is starting to feel like catalog fatigue.
- Product thumbnails with variant names are tiny and easy to miss — the actual new colors, which are the email's entire premise, get the least visual real estate.
- "Shop Women / Shop Men" CTA copy is generic and repeated verbatim across three sections with no variation — by the third instance it reads as boilerplate.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the color itself.** Drop the "refresh" headline framing and open with a tight 3-4 color swatch row. Let the new colorways be the hero — they're the product news, not a footnote under benefit callouts.
- 2. **Tighten the hero headline to one line.** "NEW COLORS. EVERYDAY COMPRESSION." stacks two competing messages. Pick one — probably "New Colors Drop: Everyday Compression" as a single scannable line.
- 3. **Add a lightweight incentive or urgency note.** After three pure-editorial sends this week with no offer, even "Free shipping on your first compression pair" or a low-stock nudge would break the fatigue pattern.
- 4. **Make the product thumbnails bigger.** If new colors are the news, show them at a size where the subscriber can actually evaluate the palette.
- 5. **Vary the CTA copy across sections.** First CTA: "Shop New Colors" → second: "Find Your Support Level" → third: "Shop the Full Compression Range." Reduces boilerplate feel.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Fresh colors for all-day compression`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your compression socks just got a color upgrade`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New seasonal hues in 15–20 mmHg support — finally, socks worth looking at`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Medium support. Comfort cushion. New colors you'll actually want to wear.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Bug — Missing CSS class selector dot (confirmed):**

In the `@media only screen and (max-width:600px)` block:

```css
/* BROKEN — no leading dot, selector never matches */
mobile-third { width: 33% !important; max-width: 33% !important; }

/* Correct form used elsewhere in same block */
.mobile-third { width: 33% !important; }
```

The un-dotted rule targets an HTML element named `mobile-third` (which doesn't exist), so any three-column layout will fail to collapse on mobile. The `.mobile-half` rule directly above it is correctly dotted — this is a typo, not an intentional pattern.

**Minor — Duplicate `xmlns` on `<html>`:**

```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" ... xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">
```

The namespace is declared twice. Redundant but harmless in practice; parsers use the first declaration.

**Minor — `<o:allowpng>` not self-closed:**

```xml
<o:allowpng>
<o:pixelsperinch>96</o:pixelsperinch>
```

Should be `<o:allowpng/>`. Non-self-closed VML elements can trip some Outlook XML parsers, though modern Outlook versions are tolerant.
