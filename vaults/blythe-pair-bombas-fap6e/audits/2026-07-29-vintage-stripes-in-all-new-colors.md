# Vintage Stripes? In All New Colors?
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-29**
## Executive summary

- This is Bombas's Vintage Stripes colorway refresh — the same retro-striped crew/quarter/half-calf silhouettes they've been running through the summer, now in a new seasonal palette (dusty rose, terracotta, sage, navy). It's essentially a sibling to the 2026-07-25 "New Colors in All-Time Favorites" send: zero discount, zero urgency, pure new-arrival merchandising. The execution is tighter than that one, though — the hero banner immediately states the news ("NEW COLORS, VINTAGE STRIPES"), the three-way Women/Men/Kids CTA row up top does real segmentation work, and the sock-stack photo under the hero is a nice concrete preview of the palette.
- Where it falls short is the same place every non-discount Bombas launch falls short: there's no reason to act now. No price, no bundle math, no countdown — just "these exist, in new colors." Compared to the brand's own discount sends (7/18's 20%-off blitz scored 8/10), this reads as a lower-urgency catalog update dressed up with strong visual design.
- Judgment: solid, on-brand execution that will get looked at by existing Bombas customers but won't create new purchase pressure. A middle-of-the-pack send for this brand.

## What's working

- The hero clearly states the news in one glance ("Just Dropped / NEW COLORS, VINTAGE STRIPES") with an immediate three-button Women/Men/Kids split for fast segmentation.
- The striped sock-stack strip right under the hero gives a concrete, appetizing preview of the actual colorway before any scrolling fatigue sets in.
- Section headers ("Classic Quarters, New Colors" / "Half Calf, Fresh Hues") organize the email by silhouette, each with its own lifestyle photo and matching product-tile pairs — easy to scan.
- The kids' callout ("Little Feet, Big Comfort") extends the launch to the full family lineup instead of just adults, matching the top-nav Women/Men/Kids structure.
- Trust badges at the footer (one purchased/one donated, 100% happiness guaranteed) reinforce the brand's mission-driven positioning without cluttering the main content.

## What's weak

- No discount, price anchor, or urgency anywhere in the email — it's a pure "these are new" message, which per brand history (7/25 send) tends to underperform Bombas's own discount sends.
- CTA copy is generic across the board ("Shop Women," "Shop Men," "Shop Now") — none references the Vintage Stripes product specifically, so the CTA doesn't reinforce the hero's news.
- No social proof, ratings, or fit/sizing detail on any product tile — just product shots and names, which limits confidence for a first-time buyer of this colorway.
- The email is long (multiple full silhouette sections stacked vertically) with no single strongest hero product called out — attention gets diffused across quarters, half-calf, and kids sections equally.
- This is now the third or fourth "new colors, no discount" send in recent weeks (7/16, 7/25, 7/27 all similar), and cadence fatigue is a real risk for anyone opening consecutively.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete hook to the hero — even a soft one like "Back for a limited run" or bundle pricing ("4-Packs from $36") would give the send a reason to act now, not just to notice.
- 2. Make CTA copy specific to the product: swap "Shop Women" for "Shop Vintage Stripes — Women's" so the button reinforces the hero news instead of reading as boilerplate nav.
- 3. Pick one hero SKU (e.g., the Half Calf 4-Pack) and lead with it above the fold, then let quarters/kids follow as secondary sections — reduces the "everything is equally important" scroll fatigue.
- 4. Add a single social-proof line ("4.8★ from 12,000+ reviews") near the first product grid — currently zero trust signal exists on any individual product.
- 5. Space this out from the 7/25 and 7/27 sends in the calendar, or bundle it with a real incentive next time to break the launch-after-launch pattern.
