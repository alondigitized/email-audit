# Up to 20% Off Kids’ Packs
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Bombas' "back to school" pivot into kids' packs — a genuinely different audience from the "Build Your Own Pack" and "Fall Shop" sends earlier this week (2026-09-13, 2026-09-12), and structurally it's the most organized send in this run: four clean age-tier bands (Youth, Junior, Toddler, Baby), each color-coded with an icon, an age/size callout, and a dedicated Shop CTA. The hero — kids mid-stride with a hula hoop, "Up to 20% Off Pack-to-School Savings" — is a decent pun and a clear discount, but it's competing with "Back to School" fatigue that every retailer runs in September, and the vague "up to 20%" (no tier-by-tier pricing shown) undercuts urgency.
- The real issue is depth over speed: this is a very long, very scrollable email (four full category modules) with no single hero product or single CTA to rally around, and by the time a reader reaches "Shop Kids" at the bottom they've made four separate stop/scroll decisions already. It's well-organized but exhausting, and it assumes the reader has kids in multiple age brackets rather than helping a parent of one age group find their section fast.

## What's working

- Age-tiered segmentation (Youth/Junior/Toddler/Baby) with explicit age + size ranges removes guesswork for a parent shopping for a specific kid.
- Color-coded blocks (blue, yellow, purple, green) create visual rhythm and make scanning/skipping to the right section easy.
- Each tier pairs an ankle-sock and calf-sock pack side by side with real product photography, not just icon renders.
- "One Purchased, One Donated" and "100% Happiness Guaranteed" trust badges reinforce the brand's known social-good positioning near the footer.

## What's weak

- No visible price or per-tier discount breakdown — "Up to 20%" in the hero is the only number in the whole email; a parent can't tell which pack qualifies for what.
- Four back-to-back category modules with four CTAs dilutes urgency; there's no single hero offer to anchor a click.
- "Is It Time For Recess Yet?" as a section header right before "Shop Kids" is a non-sequitur that doesn't connect to sock shopping or urgency.
- No deadline or expiration on the "Up to 20%" offer, so it reads as evergreen rather than time-bound.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real number next to the hero CTA (e.g., "20% Off Packs of 4+") so the reader doesn't have to guess which discount applies to them — this is the single biggest lift available.
- 2. Cut the email to two tiers max above the fold (e.g., lead with the most common purchase — Youth/Toddler — and let "Shop All Kids" absorb Junior/Baby) to reduce scroll fatigue.
- 3. Add a deadline ("Ends Sunday") near the hero to convert "Up to 20% Off" from a standing offer into an urgent one.
- 4. Replace "Is It Time For Recess Yet?" with copy that ties back to the offer or savings, not a disconnected tagline.
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% Off Kids' Packs — Back to School Starts Here`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Pack Their Backpack, Save on Socks: Up to 20% Off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop by age — Baby to Youth, packs starting at [price].`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Four sizes, one sale: save up to 20% on kids' packs.`
