# The Bombas Guide to Summer Sports
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- Bombas goes back to their editorial catalog playbook one day after dropping their first explicit discount ("Save Up to 20% On Summer Packs," June 21). The email opens with a strong hero — "LET THE GAMES BEGIN" over what looks like a tennis court — then flows into four clean sport-specific blocks (Golf, Hiking, Running, All Sport), each with feature bullets and a yellow "Shop Now" CTA. The "Choose Your Player" gamified section at the bottom is a personality touch that's distinctly Bombas.
- The structural problem is timing and framing. A subscriber who just got a 20%-off promo yesterday is now being shown full-price catalog browsing with no offer. The natural question — "where's the deal?" — goes unanswered. This email would have landed better before the discount email, not after it.
- The gender targeting ("Shop Men's Performance") is clear in the hero, and the sport segmentation creates useful shelf organization, but the breadth (golf to all-sport) dilutes the sense that this was sent *to you*. Competent, clean, but low conversion leverage.

## What's working

- Hero is bold and immediate — "LET THE GAMES BEGIN" communicates the sport theme in one glance
- Sport-specific product blocks (Golf, Hiking, Running, All Sport) give the email a logical, browsable structure
- Feature bullets under each category are specific and useful (blister defense, sweat-wicking, strategic cushioning)
- Yellow CTA buttons are consistent and visually pop against the dark backgrounds
- "Choose Your Player" section adds personality and a subtle prompt to self-segment

## What's weak

- No offer — comes one day after a 20%-off promo, making this feel like a step backward in the funnel
- "Shop Now" button copy is generic across all four sport blocks — each could say the category name
- No social proof anywhere — no review counts, no bestseller badges, no "top-rated" callouts
- Sport segmentation is so broad (golf through all-sport) that it reads as mass-market, not targeted
- The "Choose Your Player" icons at the bottom are unclear — they look like small silhouettes with no legible labels at screenshot resolution

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hook offer** — even a light one ("Pack savings still active for 24 more hours" or "Members save 20% on all performance styles") gives subscribers a reason to click now vs. never. Yesterday's promo primed them; use that momentum.
- 2. **Rewrite sub-section CTAs** — "Shop Golf Socks" / "Shop Running Socks" / "Shop Hiking Socks" instead of four identical "Shop Now" buttons. Specificity lifts click rate.
- 3. **Add a bestseller badge or star rating** to at least one product in each sport block — buyers want permission to pick the safe choice.
- 4. **Segment this email by stated sport preference** — a running-focused subscriber shouldn't have to scroll past golf to find their section. Even a reordering by likely interest would help.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Bug — missing CSS selector dot (broken responsive rule):**

In the `@media only screen and (max-width:600px)` block:
```css
.mobile-half { width: 50% !important; max-width: 50% !important; }
mobile-third { width: 33% !important; max-width: 33% !important; }   /* ← no dot */
```
`mobile-third` is missing the leading `.` and will never match any element. The corrected selector (`.mobile-third`) appears later in a separate `@media screen and (max-width:480px)` block, so the 600px breakpoint layout is silently broken for any element using that class.

**Bug — unclosed VML element:**

Inside `<!--[if gte mso 9]>`:

<o:officedocumentsettings>
