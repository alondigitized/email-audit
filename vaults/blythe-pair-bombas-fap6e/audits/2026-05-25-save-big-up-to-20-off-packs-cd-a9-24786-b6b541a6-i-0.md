# Save Big: Up to 20% Off Packs
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- This is Bombas's second consecutive pack-sale email in two days. May 24's "Up to 20% Off Trending Packs" just ran, and this is the same offer with a reshuffled headline and tweaked subject line. The creative itself is competent: the stacked-sock hero is visually appealing, the three-way gender split CTA handles the household targeting efficiently, and the "GO AHEAD, GET COMFORTABLE" secondary section gives use-case browsers a reason to stay engaged. The "MORE SOCKS, MORE SAVINGS" headline is punchier than anything in the recent run.
- The problem is strategic, not executional. A subscriber who opened yesterday already saw this deal. There's no new hook, no deadline, no featured product — just the same floating "up to 20% off" with category-level shop buttons. Six emails in under ten days, and this is the second near-identical pack send. The creative team delivered; the send-decision didn't.
- Verdict: well-produced template, badly timed. The lift from sending again the next day is real, but so is the unsubscribe pressure.

## What's working

- "MORE SOCKS, MORE SAVINGS" is the strongest headline in this recent run — benefit-led, rhythmic, and more memorable than the May 24 "Save on Popular Packs" version.
- The three-way gender split (Women / Men / Kids) immediately after the hero handles the household buyer cleanly and opens three separate click paths without fragmentation.
- The "GO AHEAD, GET COMFORTABLE" secondary section adds a use-case browse layer that gives non-deal-motivated openers something to engage with.
- Mission callout ("MORE PURCHASED, MORE DONATED") appears in the footer without dominating — a better balance than the May 16 welcome email where it buried the 20% offer.
- Layout is clean and renders without obvious bugs at mobile width.

## What's weak

- Near-duplicate of the May 24 pack sale — same offer percentage, same pack focus, no meaningful creative evolution or new angle.
- "Shop Man" in the primary CTA row is inconsistent with "Shop Women" and "Shop Kids" — a visible copy error on the most prominent buttons.
- No deadline anywhere — "up to 20% off" with no end date makes this read like a permanent sale rather than a reason to act today.
- The "up to" hedge is doing a lot of work with nothing to anchor it — no hero pack name, price point, or "X pairs for $Y" unit to make the offer feel real.
- Secondary product tiles are quite small at mobile width, making individual use-case categories hard to distinguish.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline or kill the repeat send entirely.** If May 24's pack email underdelivered, the fix is a new angle ("Last 48 hours," a featured bestseller callout, a specific customer story), not the same creative with a new subject. Without a deadline this send adds fatigue without urgency.
- 2. **Feature a specific hero pack.** Pick the top-selling pack by name, show the price and pair count, and make that the primary CTA. "The Ankle Run 6-Pack — now $X" converts better than "Shop Women."
- 3. **Fix "Shop Man" → "Shop Men."** Plural inconsistency in a primary CTA button is a visible polish failure.
- 4. **Use the preheader to add information, not echo the subject.** "Up to 20% off packs" appears in both the subject and likely the preheader — that's a wasted impression. The preheader slot should give a specific name, pair count, or deadline.
- 5. **Throttle cadence.** This is email six in under ten days with two back-to-back near-identical sends. Even strong subscribers will start ignoring or unsubscribing.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Packs Sale Ends Tuesday — 20% Off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Sock Drawer Needs a Restock`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The Ankle Run 6-Pack is the move — grab it while the discount holds.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Pick a pack, save 20%. Offer ends Tuesday.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Meta tags and `<title>` placed inside `<body>`**
After the `<div itemscope>` block and the MSO conditional comment, the following tags appear outside `<head>`:
```html
<title>Bombas</title>
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width,initial-scale=1" />
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no" />
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light dark" />
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light dark" />
```
`<head>` already closes before `<body>` opens. These tags are technically in the document body. Most email clients tolerate this, but Outlook desktop and strict-parsing clients may ignore `viewport`, `color-scheme`, and `X-UA-Compatible` directives, degrading mobile scaling and dark-mode behavior. Move all `<meta>` and `<title>` into `<head>`.

**[MEDIUM] CSS class selector missing leading dot**
Inside `@media only screen and (max-width:600px)`:
```css
mobile-third { width: 33% !important; max-width: 33% !important; }
```
Should be `.mobile-third { ... }`. The missing `.` means this rule targets a literal `<mobile-third>` element (nonexistent) rather than `.mobile-third` class nodes. Any element using `class="mobile-third"` will not receive the responsive width override on mobile.

**[LOW] Duplicate `xmlns` attribute on `<html>`**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" ... xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" ...>
```
Duplicate attribute — second declaration is a no-op but indicates a template copy-paste artifact. Remove one.

**No issues found:** `role="article"`, `aria-roledescription="email"`, `aria-label`, `lang="en"`, and `dir="ltr"` are all correctly applied to the wrapper div. Dark-mode CSS (`.dark-mode-hide`, `.darkbg`, `.darkcolor`) and corresponding meta tags are present and consistent.
