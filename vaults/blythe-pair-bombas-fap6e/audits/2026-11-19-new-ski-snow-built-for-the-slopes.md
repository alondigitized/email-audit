# New Ski & Snow Built for the Slopes
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-19**
## Executive summary

- This is a product-launch send, not a promo, and it is a welcome change after a week of "25% off" and "40% off packs" emails (11/15 through 11/18). The "MOUNTAIN TIME" hero shows a snowboarder carving, a lime "NEW" badge on a tall merino sock, and three navy Shop Women, Shop Men and Shop Kids buttons. The creative is confident, the lime-on-charcoal palette is distinctive, and the category story (ski, snowboard, hiking) is clear.
- The weakness is that there is no offer anywhere. Following the 11/18 "Final Hours" send, a subscriber who has been trained to expect a deadline and a percentage gets none. Nothing in the render says whether the sitewide 25% still applies. Nothing shows price, reviews, or urgency either. The email also repeats itself: each category gets a lime-button banner, then a product row, and the product row has no buttons or prices, so the layout is long and the path to click is muddy.
- Judgement: well made and on-brand, but a mid-pack send for conversion. It reads as a catalog. It would work better with one line saying how the sale applies to these socks.

## What's working

- The hero is clear and emotional. The action shot, "MOUNTAIN TIME" in heavy type and the "NEW" badge land the story in a second.
- The lime-on-charcoal banners create strong visual rhythm and make the Shop buttons easy to find.
- The category split is tidy: Mid-Cushion Ski & Snowboard, Full-Cushion Ski & Snowboard, and Hiking, each with a one-line benefit ("Stay-Up Tech", "blister defense").
- The lead-in copy, "soft, temp-regulating Merino Wool yarns", states the material benefit plainly.
- The footer trust badges (One Purchased One Donated, 100% Happiness Guaranteed) are clean and on-brand.

## What's weak

- No offer, price, or deadline is visible. After four straight sale sends, this one gives no reason to buy today.
- The product rows show socks with captions but no price, no buttons and no reviews. Their only link is the banner button above them.
- The email is long, and the product shots are small and loosely spaced, with large white gaps between modules.
- The closing "THIS IS PEAK COMFORT" with three blue underlined links is weak. The link styling looks off next to the lime buttons and the hero's navy buttons.
- Offering Shop Women, Shop Men and Shop Kids on a hero for ski socks, when only the Full-Cushion banner lists Kids, adds some doubt about which CTA is relevant.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a one-line offer strip under the hero, for example "Still 25% off sitewide" or a loyalty perk, and put the deadline next to it. This single change would lift the email most.
- 2. Make each product row clickable with price, a star rating and a "Shop" link, so shoppers can go straight from the image to a product page.
- 3. Cut the length. Merge the banner and product row into one module per category and drop the duplicate buttons.
- 4. Add one proof point, such as a review count or "tested on the slopes", beneath the hero copy.
- 5. Restyle the closing links to match the lime button system, and show only the audiences that apply to each category.
