# Pick Your Faves: 10% Off Custom Packs
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- This is Bombas's "Build Your Own Pack" email — a mix-and-match custom pack mechanic with 10% off when you hit 4+ pairs. The hero does its job quickly: "BUILD YOUR OWN PACK" in the brand's chunky type, a photo of three socks, and a "Save 10%" badge are all above the fold. The setup is clear enough.
- But the execution is thin. Below the hero, the body copy is a gentle four-option question ("Casual? Sporty? Dressy? Compressy?") followed by duplicate CTAs — and then the email just ends. There's no actual product showcase, no style tiles, no individual styles to spark desire. The "pick your faves" premise promises variety but delivers nothing to pick from. After Bombas's genuinely excellent "4-Packs of July" send five days ago (which showed off four specific pack categories with product imagery), this email lands flat by comparison.
- The 10% discount is also a step down from the 20% that ran through July 4th weekend. That's defensible as post-holiday repricing, but the creative doesn't compensate with any richer product content to justify the weaker offer. Verdict: structurally sound, visually underpowered, conversion-limited.

## What's working

- **Hero is clean and legible** — "BUILD YOUR OWN PACK" reads immediately at mobile sizes, no visual clutter.
- **"Save 10%" badge is visually distinct** — the red circular badge on the hero image is the clearest offer signal in the email, effectively draws the eye.
- **Body copy is playful** — "Casual? Sporty? Dressy? Compressy?" is on-brand and light; "Compressy?" is a fun Bombas-voice moment.
- **Dual CTAs are clear** — "Shop Women" / "Shop Men" appear twice (hero and body), segmenting intent cleanly.
- **Trust badges are visible** — "One Purchased, One Donated" and "100% Happiness Guaranteed" in the footer provide reassurance.

## What's weak

- **No product shown below the hero** — the "pick your faves" promise implies variety; there's nothing to pick from. The email asks you to imagine and then act on imagination alone.
- **10% feels thin after 20%** — the July 4th send ran 20% off 4-packs; this is the same mechanic at half the discount, with no framing to justify the change.
- **"PICK FOUR FAVES" section is empty** — the bolded subhead arrives and then immediately hands off to two text links. No grid, no styles, no imagery to anchor the choice.
- **Email is very short** — opens wide, closes fast. There's a real risk the recipient sees this as a lazy retread of last week's packs email.
- **No urgency signal** — no deadline, no "limited styles," nothing to create forward pressure.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a style grid below "PICK FOUR FAVES"** — show 4-6 actual sock styles (one each of Casual/Sporty/Dressy/Compressy) with individual names and links. The headline promises a choice; the body has to deliver one. This is the single change that would lift CTR the most.
- 2. **Add a time-bound hook** — even a vague "this weekend only" or "offer ends Sunday" would create the urgency this email completely lacks.
- 3. **Reframe the 10% in the copy** — acknowledge the step-down gracefully: "Mix your own four and still save — no holiday required." Makes it feel like a feature, not a consolation.
- 4. **Replace the text "SHOP WOMEN / SHOP MEN" links** with two small image tiles showing women's vs. men's pack previews — cheaper to produce than a full grid but still gives the eye something to land on.
- 5. **Preheader should not be the SMS sign-up banner copy** — the "Sign up for texts" bar at top is likely bleeding into preview text for some clients.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Build a 4-Pack, Save 10% — Pick Any Style You Want`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Mix & Match: 10% Off When You Build Your Own Pack`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Casual, sporty, dressy, or compressy — mix any 4 pairs and save.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `4 pairs, your choice, 10% off. Pick what fits your week.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Bug (silent, functional):** Missing `.` on `.mobile-third` selector at `@media only screen and (max-width:600px)`:

```css
/* BROKEN — targets a non-existent <mobile-third> HTML element */
mobile-third { width: 33% !important; max-width: 33% !important; }

/* CORRECT — appears later in the same stylesheet */
.mobile-third { width: 33% !important; }
```

Any element using `class="mobile-third"` will not collapse to one-third width on mobile. Effect depends on how many layout columns use this class.

**Bug (markup invalidity):** Duplicate `xmlns` attribute on `<html>`:

```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" ... xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" ...>
```

Duplicate attributes are invalid in both HTML and XML. Most parsers silently drop the second, but strict renderers may reject.

**Minor — incorrect inline CSS property:** `<html style="... supported-color-schemes: light dark;">` — `supported-color-schemes` is not a valid CSS property. The correct signal is the `<meta name="supported-color-schemes">` tag, which is present. The inline style is a no-op but adds noise.

**Minor — MSO element not self-closed:**

```xml
<o:allowpng>   <!-- should be <o:AllowPNG/> -->
```

Well-formed XML requires self-closing for void elements. Outlook's XML parser is lenient here, but it's worth correcting.

**`u+.body` Gmail hack is scoped inside a width breakpoint:**

```css
@media screen and (max-width:480px) {
  u+.body { min-width: 100% !important; max-width: 100% !important; }
}
```

This Gmail Dark Mode / width fix is typically applied unconditionally. Scoping it inside `max-width:480px` means it only fires on narrow viewports and won't fix Gmail rendering at tablet/desktop widths.
