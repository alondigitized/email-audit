# The Compression Socks You Need
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-10**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, well-organized category send for compression socks. "COMPRESSION SOCK ROLL CALL" sits over a close crop of pink floral knee-highs, with "Fresh Florals" as a badge. Three use-case bands follow: Everyday, Merino Wool Blend and Travel. Each band has a spec block (15-20mmHg, a feature, "Great For:") and a Women's / Men's product row below it. The spec blocks are the strongest idea here. They answer the question a compression buyer actually has, which is which pair suits their situation.
- It is also another no-offer product send. It follows 11/09 "Back In Stock: The Best Golf Socks" and 11/06 "Seamless Cami Is Finally Back". Bombas has been in educational-catalog mode for days, and this one adds no price, no urgency and no deadline. The three "15-20mmHg" blocks are identical, so the bands differ only in a single feature and a "Great For" line. The hero offers only two generic Shop buttons. Nothing links to a specific pair.
- Verdict: a good explainer with weak conversion mechanics. It will hold attention for a reader already in the compression category, but nothing pushes a click today.

## What's working

- The "Great For: long shifts / chilly weather / travel days" lines tie each product to a real use case.
- The three-band layout is easy to scan. The image and spec panels alternate sides, which keeps the rhythm moving.
- The hero is bold, and the Shop Women / Shop Men buttons are visible at the top.
- The product rows are clean, with 3-pack shots, full product names and the mmHg rating in each caption.
- The badges ("Fresh Florals", "New Colors", "Selling Fast") add light freshness and scarcity cues, and the Wirecutter badge on the Everyday socks adds credibility.

## What's weak

- There is no offer, price or deadline anywhere in the email, so nothing gives a reason to click today.
- The three spec panels repeat "15-20mmHg," so the differences between them are small.
- The product captions have no prices and no links that signal a specific destination, and there are no star ratings or review counts.
- The closing "FEEL THE RELIEF" CTA uses small blue underlined text links, which look weaker than the hero buttons.
- The hero buttons are a flat grey-beige that blends into the photo background and has low contrast.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete hook, such as a price, a bundle deal or a clear "Bundle & save" line, ideally in the hero. Bombas has run three or more no-offer sends in a row, and one price signal would lift the click score the most.
- 2. Put a star rating and review count, plus a price, under each product caption. Wirecutter appears only on one product, so extend that kind of proof to the others.
- 3. Make the hero CTA bolder and more specific, for example a solid dark or red "Shop Compression" button.
- 4. Use a clear primary action per band, such as "Shop Everyday 3-Pack," rather than only a generic Shop Women / Shop Men pair at the bottom.
- 5. Add a short size or fit note near the bottom (calf range, who it's for), since the email is educational.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's HTML source has no confirmed structural defects, but the automated QA found 2 failing links (HTTP 400, both SMS-signup CTAs) and 7 images without alt text. The 33% pass rate is partly a probing artifact, since 6 links returned 429 and 4 were never probed. Header-based checks (List-Unsubscribe, SPF/DKIM) could not be evaluated because the AgentMail relay did not expose the headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **2 failing links (400):** both are SMS-signup CTAs on `clicks.bombas.com/f/a/...`.
  - "Get Texted Exclusive Offers" (`.../9l2bXI935Gkc6B56-2_xqg~~/...`)
  - "Sign up for texts to stay up to date on holiday drops and sales. Sign Up →" (`.../QgJQAslFqSvvAayrluUjzw~~/...`)
  - A 400 on a tracked redirect may mean the click-tracking redirect rejected the probe, or that the destination is an SMS deep link (`sms:`). The QA data can't tell which, so treat these as unverified. They should be tested manually on a real device.
- **6 links returned 429** ("Shop Women", "Shop Men", "BOMBAS" logo, "Fresh Florals. COMPRESSION SOCK ROLL CALL", the "EVERYDAY. 15-20mmHg…" tile, and the floral image link). Rate limiting by the click-tracking domain, not breakage, is the likely cause. They are unverified.
- **4 links were unprobed** because probing hit its 70 s time budget, and **25 were skipped** by design. Coverage is about 12 of 41 links, so the 33% pass rate understates link health.
- **Click-tracking wrapper:** all links go through `clicks.bombas.com/f/a/<token>~~/AAQRxRA~/...`. These are very long (~700+ chars), which is normal for this ESP. There is also a tracking pixel at `clicks.bombas.com/q/xR11qFUiZ72m9rQ6G8oZng~~/...`.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **7 images are missing alt text.** Four are floral/sea-rougemix/sea-green/chai-grey layflat product images served from `assets.bombas.com/image/fetch/c_scale,w_600,q_auto/https://images.ctfassets.net/...`.
  - `11837-floralmix-adult-female-layflat-1-transparent`
  - `11836-searougemix-adult-male-layflat-1-transparent`
  - `12124-sea_green_mix-adult-femalemale-knee_high-lay...` (flagged twice)
  - `11495-chai_grey_mix-adult-femalemale-knee_high-lay...` (flagged twice)
- **Linked image with no accessible name:** the floral image is wrapped in a link. Its QA link text is `[image: 11837-floralmix-...]`, so screen readers get no meaningful name for that link, and when images are blocked the link is empty.
- **Tracking pixel:** the `clicks.bombas.com/q/...` image also lacks alt. This is benign if it is a 1×1 pixel, but `alt=""` is preferable.
- **Duplicate attribute:** the `<html>` element declares `xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml"` twice. It is harmless in most clients but invalid markup.

2. Add descriptive `alt` text to the 6 product images (e.g. "Floral mix compression socks"). Use `alt=""` on the tracking pixel.
3. Make the linked floral image's link self-describing through alt text.
4. Re-run link probing with slower pacing or a longer time budget to resolve the 6 429s and 4 unprobed links.

6. Resolve the redirect chains to confirm UTM tagging and that landing pages match each tile.
