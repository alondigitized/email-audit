# Save 10% with Custom Packs
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-31**
## Executive summary

- This is Bombas's pack-builder promotional email — a clean, well-structured send that asks subscribers to customize a 4+ pair pack and save 10% in the process. The hero executes well: bold "BUILD YOUR OWN PACK" headline, a punchy "Save 10%" badge, and a dual-gender CTA split that Bombas has used effectively before. The layout is tight, the brand voice is intact ("Casual? Sporty? Dressy? Compressy." lands), and there are no render issues.
- The problem is the number in the badge. On May 24th Bombas sent "Up to 20% Off Trending Packs" and followed it on May 25th with "Save Big: Up to 20% Off Packs." Any subscriber who saw either of those — and Bombas's cadence makes it very likely they did — is now looking at a 10% offer on the same format just six days later. The discount has been cut in half. There's no framing to justify the regression: no "build your perfect mix" angle, no exclusive SKU access, no member-only spin. It just reads as a weaker offer.
- The creative team did their job. The offer strategy undid it.

## What's working

- Hero layout is compact and complete — headline, discount badge, product image, and two CTA buttons all land above the fold with no scroll required.
- "Casual? Sporty? Dressy? Compressy." is the best line in the email — on-brand, fun, and actually merchandises the versatility angle of mix-and-match packs.
- Dual "Shop Women / Shop Men" split is present at both CTA tiers, giving two click opportunities without adding visual clutter.
- The One Purchased / One Donated and 100% Happiness Guaranteed trust marks reinforce brand values without eating significant real estate.
- Clean render — no broken images, no layout breakage, no lorem ipsum.

## What's weak

- The 10% offer is a visible step-down from the 20%-off pack emails sent May 24-25; there's no framing to explain or justify the difference.
- No deadline or urgency mechanism anywhere in the email — nothing to motivate acting today vs. next week.
- No specific product featured — the hero shows three generic socks, but there's no "pick from these 12 styles" or standout hero SKU to anchor a click.
- "PICK FOUR FAVES" as a section header is vague — it doesn't tell you what the four faves are or why they matter.
- The top banner ("Sign up for texts to stay up to date on drops and sales") almost certainly leaks as the inbox preview text, wasting that real estate entirely.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Raise or reframe the offer.** If 10% is the floor, add a mechanic that earns it — "Save 10% + free shipping on any custom pack" or "Members save an extra 5% on top." Without a meaningful delta from the May 24-25 20%-off sends, this email will feel like a downgrade to anyone paying attention.
- 2. **Add a deadline.** Even a soft one: "through Sunday" or "this week only" is enough to create a reason to act now. There is no urgency signal anywhere in this email.
- 3. **Feature two or three specific sock styles in the hero.** Replace the generic product-shot trio with the three top-sellers for the pack builder — ankle, calf, and a wild-card style. Subscribers click things they can envision buying.
- 4. **Fix the preview text.** The SMS sign-up banner is almost certainly leaking into the inbox preheader. Write an explicit preheader along the lines of "Mix any 4+ pairs and save 10% — casual, sporty, dressy, you pick."
- 5. **Rename "PICK FOUR FAVES" to something with product specificity.** "Start with these bestsellers →" or "Top pack-builder picks →" gives the section a reason to exist.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Build your own pack — save 10%`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Mix & match 4+ pairs, save 10% today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Casual, sporty, dressy — pick any combo. Save 10% on 4+ pairs through Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your pack, your pick. Save 10% when you build 4+ pairs — socks you'll actually reach for.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bug — missing CSS class selector dot:**
In the `@media only screen and (max-width:600px)` block, the `.mobile-third` rule is written without the leading `.`:

```css
/* BUG: parsed as element selector, never applies */
mobile-third { width: 33% !important; max-width: 33% !important; }

/* Should be */
.mobile-third { width: 33% !important; max-width: 33% !important; }
```

Any element with `class="mobile-third"` will not receive its mobile width on narrow viewports — likely causing layout breakage in any three-column section on mobile.

**Duplicate `.mobile-half` definition** in the same 600px block — two identical rules. Second rule overrides first; no functional impact but indicates a copy-paste artifact.

**Duplicate `xmlns` attribute on `<html>`:**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" ... xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml"
```
Technically invalid XML; XHTML parsers must treat duplicate attributes as a parse error. Browsers and most email clients recover silently, but it should be cleaned up.
