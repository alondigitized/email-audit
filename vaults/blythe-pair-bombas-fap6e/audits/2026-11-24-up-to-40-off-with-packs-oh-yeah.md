# Up to 40% Off With Packs, Oh Yeah
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-24**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, well-organized Black Friday packs send, and it nearly repeats Bombas's 11/22 "Oooh, Up to 40% Off Packs". The hero says "UP TO 40% OFF PACKS" in heavy navy type over a photo of three feet in colorful socks, with Shop Women, Shop Men and Shop Kids buttons directly under it. The eye lands on the offer first. A short line below adds "25% off everything else".
- The body works as a category catalog: Socks, Underwear and T-Shirts, each with a banner and Shop Women / Shop Men buttons. Each banner is followed by four product tiles with names. The black background makes the product shots look sparse and floaty, and nothing on the tiles shows a price, a savings amount or a rating. The "Four Purchased, Four Donated" banner near the bottom is an on-brand mission beat but sits too late to matter.
- Overall it's competent but familiar. A subscriber who got the 11/22 packs email, the 11/23 "25% Off All Kids' Gifts" send and three earlier 25%-off sends will see little new. This is the fifth or sixth Black Friday email in four days, with no new hook beyond a "Peak comfort" angle.

## What's working

- The hero offer, "UP TO 40% OFF PACKS", is huge, high-contrast and the first thing you read.
- Shop Women, Shop Men and Shop Kids buttons sit directly under the hero, so the path to shop is clear.
- The "25% off everything else" line fits the brand's sitewide Black Friday message.
- The Socks, Underwear and T-Shirts banners make the long email easy to scan.
- Product tiles are named with pack counts ("8-Pack", "4-Pack", "3-Pack"), which reinforces the packs offer.
- The mission banner is a distinctive brand moment.

## What's weak

- Product tiles show no prices, no "was/now" pricing and no ratings, so the 40% promise is never made concrete.
- Product images float on a black field with a lot of dead space, and the tiles don't link visibly to specific products.
- The "up to" language hides which packs get 40% and which get less.
- The email repeats the 11/22 packs send almost word for word, and fatigue is likely.
- The "See Offer Details" fine print is tiny and sits near the bottom, and the footer sections are crowded.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add pack pricing to every tile, such as "8-Pack $XX, was $XX", plus a star rating. This single change would do the most for click-through.
- 2. Lead with the one best-selling pack and the exact discount, instead of "up to" language.
- 3. Put "Ends Monday at midnight" or similar in the hero so the deadline is credible.
- 4. Cut the Underwear and T-Shirts sections or move them lower, so the socks hero stays the story.
- 5. Brighten the background behind the product tiles for contrast, and make the tiles clickable "Shop this pack" links.
- 6. Make the offer details more visible.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Stock up: socks 40% off in packs`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your sock drawer's Black Friday deal`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 25% off everything else. Ends Monday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `8-packs, 4-packs and 3-packs, all marked down`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Duplicate attribute on root:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" lang="en" dir="ltr" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" ...>` declares `xmlns` twice. That is invalid under the XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype. HTML-mode clients ignore it, but any strict XML parser would reject it.
- **Very small mobile text:** `.font13` drops to `font-size: 11px` at ≤570px, `10px` at ≤480px, and `9px` at ≤470px. `.font13Pad` is `9px` at ≤480px. These sizes are below common legibility guidance (roughly 13–14px minimum), and `line-height` equals `font-size` (e.g. `9px/9px`), which risks clipped ascenders and descenders. I can't tell which elements use these classes, since the body is truncated.
- **Conflicting duplicate rules:** `.mobile-half` and `.mobile-third` are each defined twice in the `max-width:600px` block. `.mobile-third` gets `max-width: 33%` in one rule and `width: 33%` in the other, versus `33.33%` earlier. This is harmless but redundant.
- **Second hidden div lacks `mso-hide`:** the padding-hack div uses `display:none; max-height:0; overflow:hidden` without `mso-hide: all`. The first preheader div has it. Some Outlook versions may show the `&nbsp;&zwnj;` padding.
- **Generic title:** `<title>Bombas</title>` does not describe the message. This is minor.
- **Positives confirmed in source:**
  - `role="article"`, `aria-roledescription="email"`, `aria-label="Email from Bombas"`, and `lang`/`dir` are present on the wrapper.
  - The `color-scheme` meta and `prefers-color-scheme` rules are in place.
  - `-webkit-text-size-adjust` is set.
  - The `u+.body` Gmail fix is paired with `class="body"`.

Image alt text, table roles, and dark-mode image swaps (`.dark-mode-hide` / `.dark-mode-show`) can't be verified from the truncated source.
