# You’re Invited to The Early Holiday Sale
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-13**
## Executive summary

- This is a teaser, not a sale email. The scalloped "THE EARLY HOLIDAY SALE / Starts Saturday" card is bold and legible, and the terracotta and cream palette feels like Bombas and like the season. But it never says what the sale is. There's no percent, no dollar amount, and no named product. "Our biggest savings" is a claim, not an offer.
- The second half is weaker. "WHAT'RE YOU GRABBIN' FIRST?" sits over a plain blue "SHOP NOW" link with no products beneath it. Below that are four badge tiles (One Purchased One Donated, 100% Happiness Guaranteed, texting, Help Center) that take up as much space as the hero. Compared with the Nov 11 "Take Me to Cozy Town" send (7/10), which had real products and a clear idea, this one has less to click on.
- Judgement: good brand polish, thin substance. It works as a "save the date" for Saturday, but it gives a subscriber little reason to act tonight.

## What's working

- The hero is a clear hierarchy: "You're Invited" leads into a huge "THE EARLY HOLIDAY SALE" with "Starts Saturday" under it.
- The salmon "Fill Your Cart" button contrasts well against the terracotta block and is easy to find.
- "Starts Saturday" plus "fill up your cart tonight" creates a believable early-access feeling.
- Nothing is broken. The render is clean, with no overlap or missing images.
- The trust badges (Happiness Guaranteed, One Purchased One Donated) reinforce the brand's values.

## What's weak

- No visible discount anywhere. "Biggest savings" gives the reader nothing concrete to want.
- The lone maroon slipper silhouette floats in a lot of empty cream space and shows no real product, model, or sock.
- "WHAT'RE YOU GRABBIN' FIRST?" asks a question, but no products follow it. The only answer is a default-blue "SHOP NOW" link that looks unstyled next to the polished hero.
- The top bar's SMS signup pitch competes with the main message, and it is the first text a reader sees.
- Bombas has sent something nearly every day (Nov 7–12). A teaser with no offer adds to that fatigue.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the offer in the hero. For example, "Up to 25% off sitewide, starts Saturday." If the number can't be shared yet, tease a specific one, such as "Early access: our best socks and slippers, up to X% off."
- 2. Replace the blank slipper with three or four real hero products (socks, slippers, a gift set) in a linked row, so the click goes to a specific product and not the homepage.
- 3. Cut the "WHAT'RE YOU GRABBIN' FIRST?" block, or turn it into Women / Men / Kids / Gifts buttons that match the main CTA style.
- 4. Move the SMS signup below the hero, or make it a real early-access hook: "Text us for first dibs Saturday."
- 5. Condense the four badge tiles into a single trust strip.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Early access: holiday sale starts Saturday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your early look at our biggest sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fill your cart tonight, savings unlock Saturday. Socks, slippers and gifts inside.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Get a jump on our biggest savings before they go public.`

## Technical Audit

# Technical Audit: Bombas, "You're Invited to The Early Holiday Sale"

## 1. Technical Summary
Click tracking and structure are mostly sound. The one confirmed failure is the two SMS-signup links returning HTTP 400, and the HTML source was truncated, so footer and compliance checks are incomplete. The 33% pass rate overstates the damage, because most of the "failures" are 429 rate limits or unprobed links.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Two SMS-signup links return 400** from the click tracker (`clicks.bombas.com/f/a/...`):
  - "Get Texted Exclusive Offers" (`/f/a/HB2Z6nf55CvlEzfZIz9hfg~~/...`)
  - "Sign up for texts to stay up to date on holiday drops and sales. Sign Up →" (`/f/a/2VrxC4dTSTdN8yuhDeyq9A~~/...`)
  - A 400 from a tracking host often means the redirect was rejected, for example because of an HTTP method or header mismatch, an expired token, or bot filtering. It may be a probe artifact rather than a user-facing break.
  - Both links share the same second path segment, `98jojH9G_CyJTLfW...`, so they likely point to the same destination.
  - Treat this as **unconfirmed but high priority**, since both hit one conversion path. Verify with a real-browser click.
- **Four probed links returned 429** (hero "You're Invited…", "BOMBAS", "SHOP NOW", "ONE PURCHASED ONE DONATED", "100% HAPPINESS GUARANTEED"). That is rate limiting from probing, not a defect. The tool itself marks these "may be valid".
- **Five links were never probed** because of the 70s time budget, for example `/f/a/H8np8VNm5PRh0VV6IdC5ew~~/...` and `/f/a/lkMTVjYEJTBTkjjmCASxDA~~/...`. Their status is unknown.
- Every link is wrapped in a click-tracking redirect, so final destinations can't be checked from the source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text** on one image: `src=https://clicks.bombas.com/q/cogUTZef5fNF6ItvwWbTzw~~/AAQRxRA~/4YqezZukDI...`. The `/q/` path suggests a tracked image or pixel. If it is a 1×1 tracking pixel, use `alt=""`. If it is visible content, it needs real alt text.
- **Duplicate attribute on `<html>`**: `xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml"` appears twice. This is invalid markup. Most clients tolerate it, but it can break strict XML parsing.
- Positives confirmed in the source:
  - `lang`, `dir`, `role="article"`, and `aria-label` are set.
  - Viewport and `color-scheme` meta tags are present.
  - An MSO conditional block and responsive media queries are present.
  - A hidden preheader is present.
  - The `<title>` is present, though generic ("Bombas").

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unrendered tokens (`{{ }}`, `%%`, `[FIRST_NAME]`) appear in the visible source. The source was truncated, so the body past the header and the footer weren't inspected.

## 5. Compliance
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found.** The QA tool notes the AgentMail relay may strip these headers, so this is **unverified, not confirmed missing**. Check the raw headers in a direct-to-mailbox test. Gmail and Yahoo require one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders.
- **`Authentication-Results` was not found.** SPF, DKIM, and DMARC status is unknown for the same relay reason. The From domain `hello.bombas.com` can be checked with a DNS lookup.
- The physical address and visible unsubscribe link weren't in the truncated source. The QA run says an unsubscribe link was included in probing, but its result isn't listed.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
Can't be assessed. All destinations sit behind opaque `clicks.bombas.com` redirects, and the QA data doesn't include resolved final URLs or UTM parameters. The plain-text part is 4188 of 5103 characters URLs (82%), mostly long tracking URLs. This isn't a defect by itself, but it makes the text version hard to read.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Click both SMS-signup links in a real browser and confirm they resolve. If they return 400, fix them first, since they are a conversion path.
2. Re-run link QA with a longer time budget, lower concurrency, or a delay between requests. This resolves the 429s and the 5 unprobed links.
3. Add `alt=""` or descriptive alt text to the `/q/` image, depending on whether it is a pixel.
4. Remove the duplicate `xmlns` on `<html>`.
5. Capture raw headers from a direct send, not the relay, to confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC.
6. Have the QA pipeline follow redirects and record the final URL and UTM parameters. Also report 429s separately from failures in the pass-rate summary.
