# Save Up to 20% On Summer Packs
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Bombas's first explicit discount email in the recent batch — all those editorial sends from June 13–20 have been building catalog awareness, and now they're activating with a "20% off packs" promo. The hero is clean: single offer, single headline, three gender-segmented CTAs. That's the strongest part of the email.
- But it has an identity crisis. Below the promo hero sits a full "GO AHEAD, GET COMFORTABLE" editorial block — four lifestyle product tiles with no discount messaging, no pack context, no urgency. Then a "PACK IT IN" header re-routes to the same three gender-segmented links already in the hero. It's two emails stitched together and the seam shows. Unlike the tight editorial focus of the June 14 "Lil' Guide" send (which bundled Pack Savings alongside new releases in a coherent hub), this one splits its register and neither half reinforces the other.
- The 20% offer is real and visible, but the structural messiness and total absence of a deadline mean this will convert well below its potential.

## What's working

- Hero is unambiguous: "UP TO 20% OFF PACKS" in bold white lands immediately as the visual anchor.
- Gender segmentation is effective — three parallel CTA buttons (Shop Women / Shop Men / Shop Kids) give every recipient a fast, relevant path.
- Brand execution is clean: no render bugs, consistent Bombas typography, and the summer color palette holds throughout.
- Subject line is efficient at 30 chars — direct, no spam signals, names the offer and category.

## What's weak

- The "GO AHEAD, GET COMFORTABLE" editorial block undercuts the promo — four lifestyle product tiles with "Shop [category]" links that never mention the 20% offer or packs; it reads like a separate browse email grafted on.
- No deadline anywhere in the visible email — "up to 20% off" with no expiry gives zero reason to act today vs. next week.
- The "PACK IT IN" section at the bottom duplicates the hero CTA structure (same Shop Women / Men / Kids segmentation) without adding product, pricing, or urgency context — pure scroll waste.
- "Up to 20% off" floats without evidence — no pack is shown at a specific before/after price, so the qualifier feels soft rather than concrete.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Replace or reframe "GO AHEAD, GET COMFORTABLE" for the promo.** Swap the four editorial tiles for three pack-specific modules — Women's Pack / Men's Pack / Kids' Pack — each showing the bundle contents, the regular price crossed out, and the discounted price. That turns the secondary section into conversion depth rather than browse drift.
- 2. **Add a deadline.** "Offer ends Sunday" or "3 days only" in the hero or a thin banner below it would materially lift urgency without being misleading.
- 3. **Show actual savings on one anchor pack.** "Women's 6-Pack: $65 → $52" makes the "up to 20%" claim tangible. Right now the offer is real but feels abstract.
- 4. **Cut the "PACK IT IN" block.** The hero already has the three shop buttons — the redundant second set adds scroll distance and no information.
- 5. **Retest the subject with a deadline hook:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% Off Packs — This Weekend Only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Summer Packs, 20% Off — Ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Six-packs, multi-packs, and more — all discounted now. Shop your category.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your favorite everyday socks finally on sale. Stock up before the deal ends.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — broken CSS class selector (mobile layout):**

In the `@media only screen and (max-width:600px)` block, the rule is written as:

```css
mobile-third { width: 33% !important; max-width: 33% !important; }
```

The leading `.` is missing, making this an element selector targeting a non-existent `<mobile-third>` HTML tag. It will never fire. A corrected `.mobile-third` rule does appear later in the same block (`@media screen and (max-width:480px)`), but the broken rule at the 600px breakpoint means any `.mobile-third` elements collapse to their default width between 481px–600px.

**Confirmed issue — duplicate `xmlns` attribute:**

```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" ... xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" ...>
```

`xmlns` is declared twice on `<html>`. While most parsers silently ignore the duplicate, it is malformed XML and may trigger strict-mode parse errors in some enterprise mail gateways or preprocessors.

**No issues found:**
- `role="article"`, `aria-roledescription="email"`, `aria-label="Email from Bombas"`, `lang="en"` are all correctly applied on the wrapper `<div>`.
- MSO conditional comments and `<o:PixelsPerInch>` are well-formed.
- Preheader spacer pattern (two hidden divs: one with copy, one with `&nbsp;‌` padding characters) is correct standard practice.

**Cannot audit:** `alt` text on images, link text on CTAs, color contrast ratios — all below the truncation point.
