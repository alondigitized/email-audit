# New Suede Slip-On Shoe Colors Are Here
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Bombas revisiting the Saturday Suede Slip-On a week after launching it (2026-08-15, "Meet the New Suede Moccasin Slipper," 6/10) — but the subject line oversells it as "colors" news when the hero banner literally says "NEW SATURDAY SUEDE SLIP-ONS," which reads as a second launch beat, not a colorway refresh. The creative itself is strong: consistent warm, earthy fall palette across all four modules, real lifestyle photography of the shoe in use (on carpet, gravel, wood floor), and a tidy feature callout structure (multi-layer cushion, water resistant, outside outsole) that actually tells you something about the product.
- The problem is message discipline. Subject promises "colors," hero says "new," and nowhere in the visible render do I actually see the three colorways named or swatched as a "new colors" moment — I see three shoes in a stack shot at the top and that's it. It's a good slipper email wearing a mismatched subject line. Against the brand's last two weeks (a genuine restock on 8/21, a real launch on 8/15, several colorway drops in between), this lands as competent mid-pack: fine production, muddled framing.

## What's working

- Hero image (three suede tones fanned out) is a clean, immediate color-story visual even if the subject/headline text undersells it.
- Fall-appropriate copy ("See that around the corner? It's fall.") ties the seasonal moment to the product without being cheesy.
- Feature icons (Multi-Layer Cushion, Water Resistant, Outside Outsole) give concrete product detail instead of generic lifestyle fluff.
- Every module has a working, high-contrast CTA button (Shop Women / Shop Men), so the path to purchase is never ambiguous.
- Consistent warm color grading across all four hero images makes the email feel like one cohesive shoot, not a stitched-together template.

## What's weak

- No visible product name/swatch labeling for the actual colors (e.g., "Olive," "Blush," "Chestnut") — a "new colors" email should show and name the colors, not just imply them in a stack photo.
- No price, no offer, no discount anywhere in the render — this is a pure product-awareness send with zero purchase incentive.
- "SLIP ON, STEP OUT" footer CTA block is text-link only (blue underlined text) sitting awkwardly below four image-heavy modules — visually the weakest CTA in the email.
- Kids isn't represented despite the top nav listing Women / Men / Kids and the footer badges pushing a broader lifestyle message — the whole email is Women/Men only.
- Redundant structure: four near-identical "photo + feature icons + two buttons" blocks in a row is a lot of scroll for one product with no size/fit guidance visible.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the subject/hero mismatch first — either show the actual named colorways with swatches under the hero (this is what "new colors" implies) or change the subject to match what's actually being sold ("The Saturday Slip-On Is Back for Fall").
- 2. Add a price or intro offer — a $-off or "just dropped, $X" line would give Click Likelihood a real reason beyond curiosity.
- 3. Cut one of the four repeating hero modules — "Feels Like Staying In" and "While You're Out and About" cover overlapping ground (comfort + durability); merge into one module to shorten the scroll.
- 4. Replace the text-link "SLIP ON, STEP OUT" footer CTA with a proper button to match the visual weight of every other CTA in the email.
- 5. Add sizing/fit note (true to size? runs small?) near the product shots — that's the one concrete detail missing that would reduce purchase hesitation.
