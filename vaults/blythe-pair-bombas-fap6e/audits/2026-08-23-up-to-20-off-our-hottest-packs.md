# Up to 20% Off Our Hottest Packs
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-23**
## Executive summary

- This is Bombas doing what Bombas does best: a broad multi-pack roundup, but this time actually organized around a real segmentation logic — "Daily Drivers," "Dress to Impress," "Get Cozy" — each with its own hero banner and two matched 4-packs. It's the most structurally coherent send in this recent stretch, cleaner than the "Summer Best-Sellers" grab-bag (2026-08-16, 5/10) because the groupings map to actual use-cases instead of just being "here's what's popular."
- The catch: the subject promises "Up to 20% Off Our Hottest Packs" but nowhere in the visible render — hero, section banners, or product tiles — is a percentage, price, or discount actually shown. That's a bait-and-switch gap between subject and body that will cost clicks once people scroll looking for the deal and don't find it.
- It's a solid organizational upgrade over last week's roundups, but the missing discount visibility caps it below where the concept deserves to land.

## What's working

- Three-way segmentation (Daily Drivers / Dress to Impress / Get Cozy) gives the grid actual narrative structure instead of a flat product wall.
- Color-coded banners (yellow, olive, blue) create visual rhythm and make the email easy to scan while scrolling.
- Hero product trio at top (checkered ankle sock, fruit-print pack, ribbed crew) is well-shot and on-brand.
- Two clear top-of-email CTAs ("Shop Women" / "Shop Men") sit right under the hero, no scrolling required.
- Footer trust badges (One Purchased One Donated, 100% Happiness Guaranteed, B Corp) reinforce brand credibility for a repeat subscriber.

## What's weak

- The "Up to 20% Off" promise from the subject is never restated with an actual number anywhere in the visible body — no strikethrough price, no "20% off" badge on any product tile.
- No urgency or end-date signal for the sale, so "hottest packs" reads as generic timing rather than a real, expiring promo.
- Six products span three lifestyle categories with no way to tell price or fit differences at a glance — social proof, reviews, or price comparison are entirely absent.
- This is the fourth-plus multi-pack/restock email in two weeks (Farmer's Market 8/19, Ragg Colors 8/17, Summer Best-Sellers 8/16); cadence fatigue is a real risk for anyone reading these back to back.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the discount on the product tiles themselves — a "20% off" badge or a struck-through price under at least the hero trio — so the subject's promise is visible without hunting.
- 2. Add a deadline ("Ends Sunday" / "This week only") to the hero banner to convert "hottest packs" into a real urgency trigger.
- 3. Cut from six products to four — the three-banner structure is good, but each themed block competing for attention dilutes all three.
- 4. Vary the CTA copy per section (e.g. "Shop Daily Drivers") instead of repeating generic "Shop Women"/"Shop Men" at top and bottom.
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% Off Packs — Daily, Dressy, or Cozy`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Fall Sock Drawer, Sorted (20% Off)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Everyday, dress, and cozy 4-packs — mix and match, ends soon.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Three ways to upgrade your drawer, all discounted this week.`
