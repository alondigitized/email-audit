# Up to 20% Off on 4-Packs 💥
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-04**
## Executive summary

- This is Bombas swinging their best pitch of the week on the holiday it was made for. "THE 4-PACKS OF JULY" is a genuinely good pun — it surfaces the product, the occasion, and the offer in a single scannable hero, and the red-white-and-blue star motifs keep it seasonal without being garish. After six straight emails that ranged from editorial catalog to a Marvel teaser, this is the first send in the sequence with an actual percentage discount. The prior-day setup (July 3 "Free Shipping on $75+") was threadbare at 4/10, but it primed the basket — and this email is the payoff.
- The structural weakness is the same one Bombas keeps fumbling: no deadline. "Up to 20% off" with zero end-date language means the urgency of a holiday that literally expires at midnight is left completely untapped. The "up to" qualifier also hedges the promise — without anchoring it to a floor price or showing what a full 20% looks like on a real pack, it reads as covering-their-bases marketing copy rather than a confident offer.
- Still, this is the most complete send in the seven-day stretch. Clean hierarchy, segmented sections, no render bugs, and timing that will land when people are relaxed and in a spending mindset.

## What's working

- **"4-Packs of July" headline** — rare good brand wordplay that ties product, occasion, and discount into one line without trying too hard.
- **Segmented browse paths** — Women's / Men's / Kids' packs each get their own image, label, and CTA, so any household type sees something immediately relevant.
- **"UP TO 20% OFF" badge in the hero** — placed high enough that it's the second thing the eye hits after the headline; not buried.
- **Seasonal design coherence** — red/cream/blue palette with star decorations lands the holiday frame without overwhelming the product imagery.
- **"A day to grill, chill, and save"** — casual, seasonal copy that earns the preview slot without repeating the subject.

## What's weak

- **No deadline** — a July 4th sale that doesn't say "today only" or "ends midnight" wastes the most valuable urgency signal in the send.
- **"Up to 20% off" is a hedge** — no floor price, no minimum-purchase threshold, no pack price shown; subscribers can't know if their item qualifies.
- **Generic "Shop Now" CTAs inside each section** — after the good "Shop Women / Shop Men / Shop Kids" buttons in the hero, the repeated "Shop Now" below each section regresses to boilerplate.
- **Seven consecutive daily emails** — this is the seventh send in seven days from Bombas. Cadence fatigue is real; a meaningful portion of the list will open this with "again?" as their first instinct.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add "today only" or "ends tonight" to the hero** — this one edit captures the entire July 4th urgency that the email is built around but never states. Could go right under the headline or as a badge ribbon.
- 2. **Anchor the price** — show the pack starting price and the savings: "4-Packs from $42 — now $33" makes 20% concrete. "Up to" alone is weak.
- 3. **Replace "Shop Now" segment CTAs with product-specific copy** — "Shop Women's Packs" / "Shop Men's Packs" keeps the specificity from the hero and improves CTR.
- 4. **Consider dropping the Friday-before-holiday free-shipping email next year** — the July 3 send (4/10) did little and cannibalized the holiday narrative. Save the slot for a teaser with a countdown.
- **Subject Alt A:** `4-Packs of July — 20% off today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your July 4th sock deal is live 🇺🇸`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Women's, Men's & Kids' 4-packs at 20% off — sale ends tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Grill, chill, save. 4-packs are 20% off through midnight.`
