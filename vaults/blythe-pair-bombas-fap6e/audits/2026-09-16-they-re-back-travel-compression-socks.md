# They’re Back: Travel Compression Socks
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-16**
## Executive summary

- This is Bombas running a "back in stock" beat for the first time this run — a clean break from the discount-pack sends (2026-09-13, 2026-09-06/07) and the trending/social-proof angle (2026-09-09). "BACK IN STOCK" plus "COMPRESSION FOR TRAVEL" is the sharpest, most single-minded hero this brand has shown recently — no scattered categories like the Fall Shop send (2026-09-12), just one product, one benefit, one urgency hook (scarcity, not discount).
- The body does real work explaining a product most subscribers don't understand: easy on/off, medium compression level, thermoregulating yarns. That's genuinely useful specificity this history has been missing — most prior sends leaned on style/social proof, not function.
- The miss is the CTA structure: two immediate "Shop Women / Shop Men" buttons under the hero, then a third "Shop Now" pointing to the general Travel Shop — none links to the compression sock product itself, so the click funnel dilutes right when interest is highest.

## What's working

- Single clear hero offer: "BACK IN STOCK" badge + bold "COMPRESSION FOR TRAVEL" headline, unmissable above the fold.
- Sold-out scarcity framing ("sold out in a flash are finally back") gives credible urgency without discount language.
- Three benefit blocks (easy on/off, medium compression, thermoregulating) give concrete product education absent from most prior sends.
- Consistent Bombas visual brand — muted travel palette, yellow luggage motif, icon system — no render bugs.
- Gender-split CTAs (Shop Women / Shop Men) right under the hero support quick self-segmentation.

## What's weak

- No product photo of the actual sock on its own — every shot is a partial leg/ankle crop, so shoppers can't see color/pattern options.
- CTA leads to homepage-level "Shop Women/Men," not a compression-sock-specific landing page, adding friction between interest and purchase.
- Bottom "Explore the Travel Shop" broadens scope right when the email should be funneling to one SKU family.
- No price or member-pricing signal anywhere in the email, so undecided shoppers have no economic hook to click on.
- No social proof (reviews/ratings) despite the product being pitched as a proven, popular restock.

## Recommendations

- 1. Change both primary CTAs ("Shop Women"/"Shop Men") to route directly to the compression sock product page, not the general shop — this is the single biggest click-lift opportunity given the tight hero framing.
- 2. Add a price or "from $X" line near the hero — right now there's zero economic anchor for a considered purchase.
- 3. Swap one benefit-block photo for a clean flat-lay/product shot of the actual sock colorways so buyers can see what they're choosing.
- 4. Add a one-line review snippet or star rating under the hero ("4.8★ — 12k travelers agree") to reinforce the "sold out fast" claim with proof.
