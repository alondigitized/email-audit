# How to Spot and Avoid Fake Bombas
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-06**
## Executive summary

- This is a plain, text-only brand-protection notice, and it is a break from Bombas's recent sends. Since 10/24 the audited emails have been product drops (11/06 "Seamless Cami Is Finally Back", 11/05 "Incoming: New Cozy Socks", 11/04 ski socks) or the 10/31 "up to 20% off" send. This one has no product, no offer and no hero image. It reads like a personal note, which suits the topic. The tone is on-brand ("way less comfy products", "Stay safe and cozy out there").
- The content is useful and trust-building. It tells shoppers to check the URL, look for the blue-checkmark @bombas TikTok Shop, and reply if they spot an imposter. It also ties authenticity back to the one-purchased, one-donated mission, which gives people a reason to care. But it is a service email and will not drive revenue. The only visual is a lone blue speech-bubble graphic that looks like a stranded icon, and the real CTAs are inline text links in a bulleted list.
- Verdict: a good, honest, low-commercial-value send. It is worth sending once, but it is under-designed and misses the chance to steer a newly alert shopper toward the official site.

## What's working

- The voice is warm and human ("Hey," and a sign-off of "Stay safe and cozy out there"), and it fits the brand's tone.
- The guidance is concrete: check the URL, look for the blue checkmark and the @bombas handle, or shop at bombas.com or in a Bombas retail store.
- The email links authenticity to the one-purchased, one-donated mission, which gives readers a reason to care.
- The footer is tidy. It has the One Purchased/One Donated and 100% Happiness Guaranteed badges, Get Texted and Help Center tiles, social icons and the B Corp mark.
- The layout is clean, with generous spacing and a readable type size.

## What's weak

- No button. The key actions (go to bombas.com, see authorized retail partners, see retail stores) are small blue underlined text links in the body. One of them is a bare "http://www.bombas.com".
- The blue speech-bubble graphic at the top is a large empty icon that adds nothing. It reads as a missing image or leftover template art.
- The email is a wall of text, with no visual example of what a real versus fake ad or TikTok Shop page looks like.
- The yellow "Sign up for texts… holiday drops and sales" banner sits above a fraud warning and competes with it. It also repeats the "Get Texted Exclusive Offers" tile in the footer.
- Because the email warns about fraud, the "Women / Men / Kids" nav and the footer text-signup prompt feel like an afterthought, and the email gives readers no easy next step.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one clear button, such as "Shop the Official Site" or "See Authorized Retailers", after the bullets. This is the single biggest lift because it turns a purely informational email into one with a click path.
- 2. Replace the empty speech bubble with a simple graphic. A checkmark badge or a "Real vs. Fake" side-by-side would work, and the TikTok verified-handle screenshot would help most.
- 3. Shorten the bullets to three tight lines and bold the URL and the @bombas handle so they can be scanned.
- 4. Remove the plain "http://www.bombas.com" link and use a single branded link.
- 5. Move the SMS banner below the content, or drop it for this send, so the safety message leads.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email renders with sound structure (lang/dir, role="article", color-scheme meta), but 2 of the 12 probed links, both SMS signup CTAs, returned HTTP 400, and the greeting has an empty name slot ("Hi ,"). Authentication and unsubscribe headers could not be verified because of the relay.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Broken CTAs (FAIL, 400):**
  - "Get Texted Exclusive Offers" → `https://clicks.bombas.com/f/a/OLULi1mFOzXKJSs6AGCPRQ~~/AAQRxRA~/0ZqqNRvS…`
  - "Sign up for texts to stay up to date on holiday drops and sales. Sign Up →" → `https://clicks.bombas.com/f/a/lPI2axN7c0diGJcDLp91iA~~/AAQRxRA~/0ZqqNRvS…`
  - Both wrapped URLs share the same encoded destination prefix (`0ZqqNRvSvq4UEW_27tVmcs6qR8ACSSdpHI4zgJeOpC…`), which points to a common bad destination or a malformed redirect. A 400 can also come from a bot-rejecting endpoint, so confirm with a real client before treating it as definitely broken.
- **Rate-limited (429), not failures:** "BOMBAS", "Bombas", "ONE PURCHASED ONE DONATED" and "100% HAPPINESS GUARANTEED" all returned 429 from `clicks.bombas.com`. The QA marks them "may be valid", and they are unverified.
- **Unprobed:** 4 links were not checked because the 70s probe budget ran out (`…/1QUnUPEGpBsFOvxTYQXERA~~`, `…/XG_Wk4yasptKS2Olj1o_xg~~`, `…/33cnRjd1p56M4yf5j6cF2A~~`, `…/RWEItRHrRXzcbqcEuEl8VA~~`). They are neither pass nor fail.
- The 22% pass rate reflects the probing limits (429s, skipped links), not 78% broken links. Only the 2 SMS CTAs are confirmed failures.
- Every link goes through the first-party tracking domain `clicks.bombas.com`. That is consistent with the sender domain, and no mixed domains were observed.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Duplicate attribute:** the `<html>` tag declares `xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml"` twice. That is invalid markup, and strict XHTML parsers could reject it. Browsers and most email clients tolerate it.
- **Missing alt text (WARN):** the image `https://clicks.bombas.com/q/dbZKHjg55Zwh8S5b6ILIug~~/…` has no alt. Its `/q/` path suggests a tracking or open pixel, so the impact is low. If it is a content image, it needs alt text.
- **Positives seen in the source:** `lang="en" dir="ltr"`, `role="article"` with `aria-label`, `<title>`, viewport meta, `prefers-color-scheme` handling, and an MSO conditional block.
- **Not assessable:** the HTML is truncated, so body-level checks (alt on other images, table roles, link text) can't be confirmed.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Empty greeting slot (FAIL):** the greeting renders as "Hi ," with no first name. No visible raw tokens such as `{{first_name}}` were reported, so the blank is probably a missing-value fallback. A default such as "Hi there," would avoid it.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** both are not found (WARN). The QA notes the AgentMail relay may strip these headers, so this is unconfirmed. Re-check against the raw message at the original receiving mailbox.
- **Authentication-Results:** not found (WARN). SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown, again expected through the relay.
- **Body unsubscribe link:** the QA states an unsubscribe link is always probed, and no failure was reported for it. The physical postal address is not visible in the truncated source, so I can't confirm it.
- **Plain-text part:** 4916 of 7009 characters (about 70%) are tracking URLs (WARN). That raises spam-filter risk slightly.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- The wrapped URLs hide the destination parameters, so UTM tagging and landing-page alignment can't be verified from the source or the QA data.
- The only continuity finding is the two SMS CTAs returning 400, which breaks the path from email to signup.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Fix or re-point the two SMS signup links ("Get Texted Exclusive Offers" and "Sign Up →"). Verify the destination in a real browser and in the ESP's link preview.
2. Add a fallback for the first-name merge field (for example "Hi there,") or suppress the greeting when the value is empty.
3. Remove the duplicate `xmlns` attribute on `<html>`.
4. Add `alt=""` to the tracking pixel, or a descriptive alt if it is a content image.
5. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` on the raw message at the original inbox, and check SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment for `hello.bombas.com`.
6. Re-run link QA with a longer probe budget and rate-limit backoff to cover the 4 unprobed links and the 429 responses.
7. Check that UTM parameters resolve correctly by following the redirects on the tracking links.
