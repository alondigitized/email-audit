# New Back-to-School Styles Are In
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-20**
## Executive summary

- This is Bombas' first genuine back-to-school send in this stretch, and it's a clean pivot away from the summer sock-restock treadmill — new hero art, a new kid-focused segment, and a real product taxonomy (Youth Dinner Party, Vintage Stripes, Junior/Toddler Eye Spy) instead of another "colorway just dropped." The layout is dense but organized: each product duo gets its own color-blocked banner with age range called out clearly, which is genuinely useful for a parent buyer who needs to know 6-10 vs. 3-6 vs. 1-3 at a glance.
- The problem is this email isn't built for me. I'm not shopping for kids — Youth, Junior, and Toddler socks sit entirely outside my category, so no amount of clean execution converts intent here. The donation callout ("350,000 pairs to classrooms") is a nice values-add but it's buried at the bottom after seven product blocks, which is a lot of scroll for anyone, parent or not.
- Solid seasonal execution for the right subscriber, essentially irrelevant for this one. Judgment: on-brand and well-organized, but zero personal relevance drags it down hard.

## What's working

- Hero art is distinctive and on-theme — "Back to School Style" with the yarn-monster/backpack visual is a real seasonal moment, not a reused banner.
- Age ranges (Ages 6-10, 3-6, 1-3) are printed directly on each banner, which is genuinely helpful shopping information visible at a glance.
- Color-blocked sections (purple/blue/orange/yellow) make it easy to visually segment product lines while scrolling.
- The donation program ("up to 350,000 pairs...to classrooms and educators") is a credible, specific value-driven CTA distinct from a pure sales pitch.
- No render bugs — images, text, and buttons all display cleanly.

## What's weak

- Entire email is Youth/Junior/Toddler product — no adult or "Women"/"Men" category content despite those nav links existing at the top.
- No discount, percentage off, or price anywhere in the email — it's a pure new-arrival/catalog push with no urgency.
- Seven near-identical product-banner blocks in a row makes for a very long, repetitive scroll before reaching the donation message.
- Subject and preview don't hint at any specific hook (new product, restock, or offer) — it reads generically compared to prior sends like "Ultra Strong Socks Are Back."
- The donation angle, arguably the most compelling story in the email, is the very last content block instead of being pulled forward.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this send — a subscriber with no kids' purchase history shouldn't get an all-kids catalog email; this should be suppressed or replaced with the adult BTS equivalent (if one exists) for non-parent segments.
- 2. Move the "350,000 pairs donated" message up near the hero — it's the most differentiated, shareable story in the email and currently reads as an afterthought.
- 3. Add a concrete offer or bundle deal (e.g., "Mix & Match 3-Packs") — right now there's no price/discount signal anywhere, which is unusual for a catalog-style send.
- 4. Cut the product banners from seven down to three or four hero items with a "Shop All Kids" link, rather than making every SKU a full-width block.

- **Subject Alt B:** `New Kids' Styles + We're Donating 350K Pairs`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Toddler to tween, we've got the size covered`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Every pair purchased helps a student in need`
