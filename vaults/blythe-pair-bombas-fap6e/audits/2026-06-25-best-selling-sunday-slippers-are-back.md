# Best-Selling Sunday Slippers Are Back
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- A clean, focused restock announcement for Bombas's Sunday Slipper. The hero is bold and legible — dark navy background, stacked white type, a floating product shot labeled "Cool Smoke" — and the dual gender CTAs (Shop Women / Shop Men) are immediately actionable. For what it is, the email executes well.
- The problem is what it isn't: there's no price, no discount, no deadline, and no social proof beyond some tiny, unreadable badges below the fold. The entire email is asking you to go browse a product you may have seen before, with no hook beyond "they're back." For the fifth consecutive email day from Bombas (June 21 discount → June 22 Summer Sports → June 23 Flower Shop → June 24 Studio Grippers → June 25 this), subscriber fatigue is real.
- Verdict: a competent browse email that would work much harder with a price anchor, a restock-urgency message ("only X left"), or a member benefit callout. As-is it's a 7 across the board — functional but not compelling.

## What's working

- **Bold hero with instant legibility** — "SUNDAY SLIPPERS ARE BACK" in stacked display type against a dark background is unmissable.
- **Product specificity in hero** — "Cool Smoke" colorway is called out by name right under the product image; a specific thing to shop for.
- **Dual-gender CTA split** — "Shop Women" / "Shop Men" buttons sidestep the need for segmentation and reduce friction for any subscriber.
- **"Best-Selling" in the subject** — bakes social proof into the open trigger without a separate copy element.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, no broken images, no overlapping text, proportional on the mobile render width shown.

## What's weak

- **No offer** — no price point, no discount, no free-shipping callout. After the June 21 "Save Up to 20% On Summer Packs" send, returning to zero-offer browse mode is a downgrade.
- **Slippers in late June is off-season** — mid-summer is when this category loses its urgency; no copy acknowledges or overcomes that.
- **Social proof badges are unreadable** — the small icons below the "WE'RE SO BACK" section are too small to parse at render size; they add no effective weight.
- **No restock urgency** — "Are Back" implies limited supply but the email never makes that argument. No "limited restock," no "only 3 sizes left," no deadline.
- **Fifth email in five days** — the cadence history (June 21–25) means any subscriber who tracks their inbox is already fatigued.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a restock-scarcity message** — "Limited restock — grab them before they sell out again" is the honest payload of an "Are Back" announcement. Use it. One line under the hero copy is enough.
- 2. **Drop a price anchor** — even just "Starting at $XX" gives subscribers a reason to click now instead of later.
- 3. **Make the social proof legible** — if those badges say "100% Satisfaction Guarantee" or carry a rating, move that line to visible body copy instead of tiny unreadable icons.
- 4. **Acknowledge the season** — "perfect for early mornings, late nights, and air-conditioned offices" or similar earns the summer relevance the product doesn't naturally carry.
- 5. **Rest the cadence** — skipping or delaying one send after five consecutive days would lift open rates on the next one more than any subject-line tweak.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Missing CSS class dot (broken responsive selector):**
In the `@media only screen and (max-width:600px)` block, the `.mobile-third` rule is written without its leading dot:

```css
mobile-third { width: 33% !important; max-width: 33% !important; }
```

This selector is invalid and never fires. Any element using `class="mobile-third"` will not reflow on mobile. The sibling `.mobile-half` and `.em_full_img` rules are correctly dotted.

**Issue 2 — `dark-mode-show` uses `display: block`:**
```css
.dark-mode-show { display: block !important; }
```
If this class is applied to inline or `<span>`-level elements (common for swapping dark/light logo variants), forcing `block` will break flow. Should be `display: inline-block` or matched to element type.

**Issue 3 — Duplicate `xmlns` attribute on `<html>`:**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" ... xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" ...>
```
`xmlns` appears twice. Strict XML parsers (some Outlook variants) may reject or warn; harmless in most clients but indicates a template merge error.

**Issue 4 — `<o:allowpng>` missing close:**
```xml
<o:allowpng>
<o:pixelsperinch>96</o:pixelsperinch>
```
`<o:allowpng>` is not self-closed or explicitly closed. Should be `<o:allowpng/>`. Most MSO versions tolerate this but it is malformed MSO XML.

**Issue 5 — `<title>` is generic:**
`<title>Bombas</title>` — some clients (Gmail app, certain Outlook versions) surface `<title>` as fallback label text. Should match the subject line or be descriptive: e.g., `Best-Selling Sunday Slippers Are Back`.
