# NEW: Welcome Back to Sesame Street
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-03**
## Executive summary

- This is the Sesame Street collab launch that the 2026-09-25 teaser ("A Fuzzy, New Collab is Coming Soon") promised, and it delivers on it. The hero is strong. Two pairs of kid-sized legs wear lime-green and red character socks, "Limited Edition" is stamped on the image, and "A Sunny Day Starts With Something Soft" sits over a dark "Shop Now" button. Below it, the Sesame Street x Bombas lockup and the four character illustrations make it clear what this is within a second.
- The body is a long, well-ordered scroll: Comfort (ankle and half-calf packs), Little Monsters (youth gift boxes), Grippy (toddler and baby), then slippers, then the donation message. Each block pairs a lifestyle tile with two product tiles. It's tidy and on-brand, but it's a catalog, not a campaign. There's no price, no offer, no ship-by date, no deadline and no review. The only urgency is the "Limited Edition" badge, and it doesn't say how limited. The email is also about 5,000px tall with seven or so product links and a single "Shop Now" button in the hero, so the reader has to scroll a long way to find a specific product.
- Overall it's a polished, charming brand moment with weak conversion mechanics. It beats the teaser because it has product, but it still matches the 5-6 band of the recent sends.

## What's working

- The hero is immediate and warm. The kid-leg crop, the "Limited Edition" badge and the Sesame Street x Bombas lockup tell the story at a glance.
- The "C is for Comfort" and "G is for Grippy" blocks use the show's alphabet conceit, so the copy has a clear voice.
- Every block follows the same rhythm of lifestyle tile, then two product tiles, then named products, which is easy to scan.
- The product names are specific (Adult Ankle 4-Pack, Baby Gripper 0-6 Months), so shoppers can tell which size and age each is for.
- The donation block ("For every pair purchased, another is donated") ties the collab to Bombas's core promise and adds an emotional reason to buy.
- I see no broken images or overlapping text. The render is clean.

## What's weak

- No price, no offer and no deadline appear anywhere. The "Limited Edition" badge is the only urgency, and it has nothing behind it.
- There is one button, in the hero. After that the CTAs are product images and a small blue "SHOP THE COLLAB" text link near the bottom, which is easy to miss.
- The email is very long, with four lifestyle blocks, seven product tiles and no reviews or ratings. Product tiles are small and lack price and color information.
- The product tiles are small, with tiny caption text that's hard to read on mobile.
- The slipper section ("Craving Something Cozy?") is aimed at women, but the adult sock tiles above it are unspecified. A recipient who isn't a parent, a kid or a Sesame Street fan has little reason to continue.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a price or value signal to the hero or the product tiles, such as "From $X" or a bundle price, plus a clear launch window ("Available through Oct 31" or "While supplies last").
- 2. Put a second CTA after the first block, then repeat it at the slippers. Use a verb plus a noun, such as "Shop the Gripper Sets".
- 3. Shorten the scroll. Cut it to three or four product groups, and lead with the best sellers or gift boxes, since holiday gifting is the strongest angle.
- 4. Add star ratings or a "most gifted" tag on the tiles to give social proof.
- 5. Make the product captions larger and add prices.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Sesame Street x Bombas is here`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Soft socks + slippers, Sesame Street style`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Limited-edition socks, grippers and slippers, with a pair donated for every pair you buy.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Cookie Monster slippers and Big Bird socks, from baby to adult.`
