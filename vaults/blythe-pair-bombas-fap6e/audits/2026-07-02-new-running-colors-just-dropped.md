# New Running Colors Just Dropped
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- This is Bombas's second "new colors" launch email in three days — compression on June 30, running today. The structure is nearly identical to that send: dark hero with bold headline, three feature-section pillars (LOCK IN LEVEL UP / STAY FRESH MILE AFTER MILE / CUSHIONED FOR IMPACT), alternating product grids, and dual gender CTAs throughout. Visually it's clean and well-executed — the hero reads instantly, the color palette is cohesive, and the feature naming is punchy enough to carry the scroll.
- The problem is the same one that cost the June 30 compression email: there is no offer. No discount, no free shipping callout, no member perk — just "new colors exist, go shop." For a subscriber who has already received six Bombas emails in seven days, "new colors" alone isn't enough friction to overcome inbox fatigue. The running category also drifts slightly from a walking-centric persona, which erodes relevance at the margin.
- The email does its job as a product announcement but won't win the inbox battle. It's a solid 7 execution on a weak creative brief.

## What's working

- **Hero reads instantly.** "NEW RUNNING COLORS" in oversized bold type over a dark action-shot background — eye lands on the headline with zero effort.
- **Dual-gender CTAs are persistent.** "Shop Women" / "Shop Men" orange buttons appear at multiple scroll depths, reducing the distance to a click.
- **Three feature pillars add editorial depth.** "Lock In Level Up," "Stay Fresh Mile After Mile," and "Cushioned for Impact" break up what would otherwise be a pure grid dump and reinforce performance positioning.
- **Consistent branding.** Typography, color, and voice are all on-brand Bombas — builds trust in a crowded inbox.
- **No render bugs.** Clean layout, images loading, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **No offer.** There's no price, no discount, no free shipping threshold, no "members get early access" — just new colorways. That's a very light reason to click today vs. tomorrow vs. never.
- **Cadence is hitting fatigue.** Six Bombas emails since June 27, two in the "new colors" format in three days. This one risks getting auto-sorted or ignored.
- **Running vs. walking audience mismatch.** The feature copy ("Up your mileage," "Stay Fresh Mile After Mile") is pitched at runners — a walker persona doesn't see themselves in this.
- **No social proof anywhere.** Not a single review quote, star rating, or "bestseller" badge — a colorway launch is exactly the send where "our most-reviewed run sock just got new colors" would convert better.
- **"TAKE YOUR MARK..." footer CTA** is weak closer copy — vague and feels like leftover sports-metaphor wordplay.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with a hook, not just newness.** Reframe the email around a reason-to-buy today: "Our #1 Run Sock Just Got a Summer Refresh" with a "4.8 stars, 12K reviews" social proof pull — converts "new colors" from a catalog update into a validated drop.
- 2. **Add a price-reducing incentive.** Even a free-shipping-on-run-socks bar or a 15%-for-members teaser gives subscribers a forward-looking reason to click now. Without it, the email is asking for maximum click effort with minimum reward signal.
- 3. **Tighten the walker/runner bridge.** Copy like "Whether you're clocking miles or steps, these socks bring the same cushion" brings a broader active audience into the frame rather than speaking exclusively to runners.
- 4. **Swap "TAKE YOUR MARK..." for a specific deadline.** "New colors sell fast — our last drop sold out in 48 hrs" creates honest urgency backed by brand history.
- 5. **Subject and preheader:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your fave Run Sock just got new colors`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New colorways dropped — but they go fast`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fan-fave Run Socks refreshed with bold summer hues`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New colors for our most-cushioned, most-reviewed runner`
