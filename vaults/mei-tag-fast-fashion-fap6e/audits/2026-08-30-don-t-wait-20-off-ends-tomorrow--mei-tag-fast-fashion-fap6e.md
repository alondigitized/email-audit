# Don't wait: 20% off ends tomorrow
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-30**
## Executive summary

- Okay so it's the same "20% off Members" offer H&M ran on 08-29 — literally the same headline, same six outline buttons, same font treatment — but this time they slapped "ends tomorrow" urgency on the subject line and called it a new send. My eye actually did catch on "Fall jeans are made of these" under the offer, which is a decent line, but then it's just... category buttons again. Women, Men, Kids 2-8Y, Kids 9-14Y, Sign In, Become a Member — that's not a trend story, that's a sitemap.
- The hero shot of the girl in the white poplin shirt and dark denim is genuinely cute and directional, I'll give them that, and it's paired with actual jeans styling below the fold (the wide-leg olive cargo look, the black-on-black denim jacket combo). But there's zero indication of what "20% off" actually saves me on a specific item, no price shown anywhere, and the whole thing reads as a recycled template with a new deadline bolted on.
- I'm not clicking this one. I already saw basically this exact email yesterday and gave it a 4. Adding "ends tomorrow" to the subject doesn't fix the fact that the body still has no editorial hook. Not forwarding, not opening tomorrow's version either if it looks like this again.

## What's working

- The hero image of the poplin shirt + dark wash denim look is styled well and feels current, not stocky.
- "Fall jeans are made of these" is a decent trend-adjacent line — better than yesterday's flat "20% off your order" alone.
- Model diversity across the sections (the hero, the "New Classics" guy, "Instant Hits" girl) reads more representative than H&M's Welcome email, which was just monochrome nothing.
- Section labels (Best of Trends, New Classics, Mini Favorites, Instant Hits) at least attempt a trend narrative instead of pure category dump.

## What's weak

- This is basically a reskin of the 08-29 "20% off for Members" email — same offer, same buttons, just a new deadline slapped on. That's lazy cadence and I notice.
- No actual price or dollar amount shown anywhere — just "20% OFF YOUR ORDER" in a wall of text, no visual hierarchy pulling my eye to a number.
- Six stacked outline buttons (Women/Men/Kids 2-8Y/Kids 9-14Y/Sign In/Become a Member) before any actual product — that's five scrolls of decision fatigue before I see a single item.
- "Ends tomorrow" urgency is only in the subject line — nowhere in the actual email body is there a countdown or deadline reinforcing it, so it feels like bait.
- Zero social proof, zero styling tips, zero "how to wear it" — just image, headline, button, repeat four times.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the redundant offer block** — since this is a repeat of yesterday's exact promo, either escalate the discount (like the 08-26 flash sale did, going from the standard 20%/$125 to 25% off everything) or don't resend it. Recycling the same 20% two days running reads as filler, not urgency.
- 2. **Put the deadline in the body, not just the subject** — a literal "ENDS TOMORROW 11:59PM" banner near the top would make the urgency feel real instead of clickbait.
- 3. **Cut the six-button wall down to 2-3** — Women/Men/Kids as one row, drop Sign In and Become a Member from prime real estate, they belong in the footer.
- 4. **Show a price** — "20% off" on a $69.99 pair of jeans landing at $55.99 is way more clickable than an abstract percentage.
- 5. Compare to ASOS's Lioness drop — that email opened with a full editorial moment (cargo culottes, kitten mules, a girl strutting down cobblestone) before ever mentioning a discount. H&M could borrow that "show the vibe first" instinct instead of leading with buttons.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
Campaign email fails automated QA at a 16% pass rate; all 12 probed CTA/nav links return HTTP 403, and compliance/deliverability headers required for inbox authentication and one-click unsubscribe are absent.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All 12 probed links resolve through the `t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=...` redirector and return **403 Forbidden**, including every "Shop now"/"SHOP NOW" CTA, the header "H&M" logo link, and the **Unsubscribe** link itself (`...&p4=3326930539&p5=9be06746af74de7271d9dcc8d83f5742`). Since the redirector itself fails, this is likely an infra/ESP-side issue (auth-walled redirect domain, expired signed params, or QA prober being blocked) rather than a per-link authoring error — but it means **100% of tested destinations are unreachable** in this sample.
- Tracking parameter structure is consistent across links (`did`, `rid`, `erid`, `p1`–`p3` campaign/message IDs), so the tracking schema itself appears intact — the failure is at resolution, not construction.
- 61 of 73 total links were deprioritized/skipped by the prober (footer, social, utility) — coverage is CTA-focused, not exhaustive.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 12 `<img>` elements are missing `alt` text, including tracking pixels (`t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=...`) and all product imagery (`cq5dam.web.1400.1400.jpeg` / `cq5dam.web.750.750.jpeg` variants across 6 unique product images, each duplicated at two resolutions). Screen readers will announce these images with no descriptive fallback.
- Mobile-responsive CSS (`@media (max-width:699px)`) and Outlook/Yahoo-specific overrides (`mso-hide`, `#MessageViewBody`, `@media screen yahoo`) are present, indicating standard multi-client defensive coding — no structural rendering issues found in the visible markup.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The embedded JSON-LD (`schema.org/EmailMessage` / `DiscountOffer`) has **empty values** for `subjectLine`, `discountCode`, `availabilityEnds`, `image`, and `url` — these structured-data fields are unpopulated, which will suppress or degrade any Gmail/Outlook promotional markup (e.g., no discount chip or countdown rendering in-inbox) even though the visible HTML presumably renders a code/date.
- No visible unresolved merge tags (e.g. `{{first_name}}`) found in the truncated source.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header not found** — may be a relay-capture limitation (AgentMail) rather than a true absence; cannot confirm compliance either way from this data.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found** (RFC 8058) — one-click unsubscribe via mail client cannot be confirmed as supported.
- The in-body **Unsubscribe link is broken (403)** — this is a confirmed, concrete compliance risk: if the mailto/header-based path is also unavailable, a recipient has no working unsubscribe mechanism.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)

This is a vendor-specific tracking scheme, not a defect, but it means landing-page attribution can't be validated against typical UTM conventions from this data.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Investigate the 403s on `t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=...` as priority one** — confirm whether this is a QA-prober access issue (e.g., bot-blocking, geo/IP restriction, missing session) vs. a genuinely broken redirect service; if genuine, every CTA and the unsubscribe link are non-functional for recipients.
2. Add descriptive `alt` text to all 12 flagged images, particularly the 6 product images (currently relying on filename-only identification like `cq5dam.web.1400.1400.jpeg`).
3. Populate the empty JSON-LD fields (`subjectLine`, `discountCode`, `availabilityStarts`/`Ends`, `image`, `url`) to enable Gmail/Outlook promotional inbox markup.
4. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are present at the true SMTP layer (not just via this relay's capture) to confirm RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe compliance.
