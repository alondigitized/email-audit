# The main event: 20% off 'til tomorrow
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-04**
## Executive summary

- OK so we are genuinely doing this again. This is the fourth H&M email this week with a 20% off hook — I've seen "stars, stripes & 20% off," "20% off + the long weekend starts now," "20% off red, white & all new," and now "the main event: 20% off 'til tomorrow." My nervous system has fully tuned this number out. The subject line's urgency is real — "'til tomorrow" is a clean deadline — but when every email is a main event, nothing is.
- That said, the email itself is not a disaster. The hero is big and readable, the July 4th framing is contextually correct, and buried in the scroll there's a genuinely better offer: "UP TO 70% OFF + NEW STYLES ADDED" with "breezy sets, mini dresses & more." That copy is doing real work. The 70% section has the specific language I actually want in the hero. Instead H&M led with the weaker number and hid the better deal below the fold.
- I would not click today. I've hit my H&M fatigue ceiling — I'd close this and open the ASOS tab instead, because ASOS just stacked 30% extra on top of an existing 70% sale and that math hit different. I would not forward this to a friend. I would not mark it as anything useful. I'd let it die in my inbox.

## What's working

- The "20% OFF" hero is enormous and immediately readable — no scrolling, no squinting, zero ambiguity about the offer.
- "HAPPY FOURTH OF JULY" framing lands for today specifically; this at least feels like it was meant for July 4th unlike Zara who runs "Woman | Discover what's new" on the same week every month regardless of calendar.
- The women's model reads Gen Z — the casual summer silhouette and color palette match where my head is at in July.
- Subject is 37 characters, mobile-perfect, and "'til tomorrow" is a credible, clean deadline with no exclamation points stacking.
- The 70% sale section copy — "breezy sets, mini dresses & more" — is the most relevant, specific language in the entire email, and it's sitting in a visually punchy red block.

## What's weak

- "ALMOST everything" is doing a lot of quiet damage — my eye goes straight to the fine print wall at the bottom and I know whatever I actually want is excluded.
- The 70% off sale (the legitimately exciting offer) is buried below four category boxes and two product images. It should be in the hero or at minimum swapped in position.
- The membership acquisition banner at the very top treats me like a stranger — I'm clearly a subscriber, and "BECOME A MEMBER" as the first thing I read makes me feel like a cold lead, not a customer.
- No trending item or specific product linked — ASOS would surface "most saved this week" or "trending now" edits; H&M gives me category buttons to a homepage.
- Fourth near-identical offer this week; by now I'm genuinely not processing the discount percentage anymore.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the hero offer** — lead with "UP TO 70% OFF SALE + EXTRA 20% OFF EVERYTHING ELSE." Stack the deal, don't split it into two separate email moments.
- 2. **Pull one specific trending product into the women's hero block** — "our most-saved mini this summer" or even just a single hero product image with a price. Give me a reason to click *this* rather than "shop women."
- 3. **Bury the exclusions differently** — instead of "ALMOST everything," say "20% off, including new arrivals — see exclusions" with a tappable link. Cleaner, more honest, and doesn't make me assume my cart is going to be hit.
- 4. **Collapse or segment the membership acquisition banner** — for known subscribers, replace with something loyalty-adjacent: early access, member-only drops, whatever. Stop treating me like a stranger on email four of the same week.
- 5. **Add one urgency reinforcement in the email body** — the subject says "'til tomorrow" but the body doesn't repeat it. A simple "Offer ends July 5 at midnight" line under the hero CTA would close the loop.
- **Subject Alt A:** `70% off sale + 20% off the rest — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call: double deal, ends midnight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Breezy sets, mini dresses & summer-ready styles — all marked down, all day.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The sale got bigger. Shop before it's gone — offer ends tonight.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading — confirmed broken in Outlook:**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
Outlook (all versions), Yahoo Mail, and some Android clients strip `@import` inside `<style>` blocks. Custom fonts will silently fall back to system defaults — but only if the stack includes a web-safe fallback. If the fallback is missing from the font stack, Outlook will render in Times New Roman.

**`alt` text on images** — not verifiable from truncated HTML. Flag for full-source check.

**`.prevent-auto-links` pattern** — correctly implemented to suppress iOS auto-detection of phone numbers/addresses. No issue.

**`#MessageViewBody` overrides** — Outlook-specific block is present and structurally correct.
