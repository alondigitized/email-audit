# Summer hiking with H&M MOVE
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- Okay so H&M has been in my inbox every single day this week — shorts at $12.49, 50% off escalation, the denim editorial, the minimalist "keep it simple" moment — and now we pivot to... hiking? Like, H&M MOVE hiking-essentials functional-sportswear catalog energy? I didn't see that coming, and not in a good way.
- Here's the thing — gorpcore is absolutely a TikTok trend right now, and if H&M had SAID that, framed this as "the gorpcore era is here, here's how we do it," I would have stopped scrolling. But this email doesn't know it's sitting on a trend. It positions these pieces as literal gear for literal hiking. The headline is "SUMMER HIKING ESSENTIALS" and the CTA is "SHOP NOW" and the products are called things like "SLIM FIT REFLECTIVE SPORTS TOP WITH..." — that's product catalog copy, not trend editorial copy. ASOS just dropped a Rina Lipa collab email that FELT like it owned the cultural moment. This feels like H&M's logistics department built the email after the trend team went home.
- No price anchor, no urgency, no cultural framing. I'd mark it as read and keep it moving.

## What's working

- **The hero image is actually pretty.** Outdoor landscape, female model in a cap and activewear, clean composition — aesthetically it fits the gorpcore moment even if the copy doesn't know it.
- **Model diversity in the product grid.** I can see different skin tones and aesthetics across the product shots, which reads right for H&M's Gen Z audience.
- **Sub-brand clarity.** "H&M MOVE" is visibly called out — I know what I'm shopping before I even scroll.
- **Layout is clean and renders without bugs.** No broken images, no overlapping text, the hierarchy from hero → products → shop-by flows logically.

## What's weak

- **Zero price anchor.** Yesterday's "Peak-summer shorts from $12.49" had me opening immediately. Today? Just "SHOP NOW" into the void. At minimum throw me a starting price in the hero.
- **"SHOP NOW" is the laziest CTA in the world.** ASOS uses "Shop Rina Lipa," Zara uses "Explore Pre-Owned" — something with a noun. "Shop Now" tells me nothing about what I'm clicking into.
- **Gorpcore is RIGHT THERE and they missed it entirely.** The whole outdoor-chic, trail-to-street aesthetic is all over TikTok and this email calls it "hiking essentials." That is a positioning miss of the highest order.
- **Product names are SO utilitarian.** "REGULAR FIT TRACK SHORTS WITH POCKETS" is a warehouse description. Zara would call it something that makes you feel like you found it. This makes me feel like I'm ordering workwear off a spec sheet.
- **Seventh email in seven days — I'm starting to fatigue.** The daily cadence H&M has been running is getting exhausting, and when one of those sends isn't bringing me a deal or a clear trend moment, it just registers as noise.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Reframe gorpcore as the trend lead.** The copy should open with the cultural hook — "gorpcore is your new weekend uniform" or "the trail aesthetic just got an upgrade" — not "designed for harmony with landscapes" functional gear language.
- 2. **Add a price anchor to the hero.** Even "from $19.99" gives me a reason to click. Yesterday's $12.49 short email hit harder because I knew what I was walking into.
- 3. **Make the CTA specific.** "Shop MOVE" or "See the trail edit" over "SHOP NOW." Give the button a noun.
- 4. **Product naming needs a creative pass.** The product grid names are SEO-first, not brand-first. One editorial rename — "the cap that makes you look like you hiked there" — would do more work than all the "SLIM FIT REFLECTIVE" labeling.
- 5. **Cadence warning:** With 7 sends in 7 days, this one needed to pull real weight. A functional sub-brand email with no deal and no trend angle was not the send to drop mid-streak.
