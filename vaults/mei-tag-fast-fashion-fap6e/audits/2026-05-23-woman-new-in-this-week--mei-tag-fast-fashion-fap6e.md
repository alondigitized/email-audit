# Woman | New in this week
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- Okay so Zara came back with another "Woman | New in this week" — and honestly? After the misfire of the Man email on May 17 and the Kids send before that, it's a relief to actually be in the right tab this time. The editorial collage at the top is giving classic Zara energy — candid, sun-drenched, multiple silhouettes — and I kept scrolling because the sections felt like flipping through a lookbook I actually cared about.
- But here's the thing: this email is doing the same structural move as May 16 "Woman | Discover what's new." Same multi-block editorial grid, same tiny label CTAs ("SEE," "DRESSES," "SHIRTS"), same no-price philosophy. Where it earns its keep is "THE ITEM" — the asymmetric ring swimsuit section breaks the grid, goes full editorial-magazine with the large display font, and gives me something specific to want. That one moment is actually TikTok-brief-worthy. The rest is just Zara doing Zara.
- Would I click? Yes — straight to the swimsuit. Would I forward it? Only to one friend who's specifically been hunting one-shoulder swimsuits for summer. Would I buy today? Probably not without seeing a price first.

## What's working

- **THE ITEM feature** — the asymmetric ring swimsuit callout is the single best thing in this email. Large display text, solo model shot, specific product name. This is how you make one thing feel unmissable in a scroll.
- **TRF Collection block** — crochet and boho cuts are everywhere on TikTok right now; including TRF (Zara's youth line) signals they know their audience is in this email.
- **Hero collage** — the multi-model, street-style-adjacent top image feels like an Instagram carousel I'd save. Models look age-appropriate and stylish.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, everything laid out the way it's supposed to be. After I've seen how badly some H&M sends brick on mobile, this is worth noting.
- **Season relevance** — swimwear, lingerie, spring collections. Timing is right for late May.

## What's weak

- **No pricing anywhere** — I get that Zara doesn't do sales, but ASOS just sent me their leopard print edit on the 22nd and I could see exactly what it cost from the body. Zara's pretending price doesn't exist while I'm literally on three tabs comparing.
- **Subject is a recycled structure** — "Woman | New in this week" versus the May 16 "Woman | Discover what's new" are functionally the same email in my inbox. If I wasn't paying close attention I'd think it was a resend. H&M pulled the same move twice in a week and it tanked the vibe.
- **THE ITEM is buried** — arguably the strongest product in the email and I have to scroll past six other collection blocks to reach it. On mobile that's a lot of thumb work.
- **CTAs are barely CTAs** — "SEE" is not a call to action. "DRESSES" is a noun. Even "FIND IT" for the swimsuit is barely pulling weight. ASOS writes "Shop the edit" and it still destroys Zara's minimal link labels.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface THE ITEM to the top half** — put the asymmetric swimsuit as the hero or second block. It's the most specific, most scroll-stopping thing here. Don't make me earn it.
- 2. **Put at least one price anchor in the email** — even one product with "from $X" does something. ASOS and H&M both show pricing and it drives click behavior. Zara's editorial purism is costing them conversions against anyone who compares tabs.
- 3. **Differentiate the subject from the last Woman drop** — "New in this week" vs. "Discover what's new" look identical to inbox scanners. Name a trend or a product.
- 4. **Elevate the TRF and lingerie section** — these are the most Gen-Z-relevant categories here; they're currently the same visual weight as everything else.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Woman | The asymmetric swimsuit is here`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New in: the ring swimsuit + TRF drop`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `THE ITEM this week is the one everyone's going to copy`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Swimwear, TRF, lingerie — all dropping now`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Viewport meta uses semicolons instead of commas as delimiters:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width; initial-scale=1.0; maximum-scale=1.0; user-scalable=no;" />
```
Per spec, `content` values must be comma-separated. Most mobile clients are lenient, but this is technically malformed and may break on strict parsers.

**Zoom is disabled** via both `maximum-scale=1.0` and `user-scalable=no` — WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text) failure. Prevents low-vision users from scaling text.

**Invalid HTML nesting in preheader block:**
```html
<div style="mso-hide:all;">
  <tr>
    <td ...>
```
A `<div>` is placed between `<table>` and `<tr>` with the `<tr>` as a direct child of the `<div>`. This is invalid in both XHTML 1.0 Transitional and HTML5. Outlook will suppress it via `mso-hide:all`, but other clients may produce broken table structure or ignored rows.

**Preheader padding uses heavy invisible-character sequences** (`&#8199;&#847;` repeated 30+ times). Aggressive use of zero-width joiners and hair spaces is a known spam-filter signal in several anti-spam rulesets (e.g., SpamAssassin `ZERO_WIDTH_JUNK`).
