# Up to 70% off Sale
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- Okay so I open this and immediately — seventy percent. That's the number. After ASOS hit me with the XTRA30 code email two days ago (July 18) and the Rina Lipa drop five days before that, they've been in my inbox literally every other day, and now they're going full nuclear with the biggest sale claim I've seen from them all month. And honestly? It lands. The hero is clean, the offer is loud, and "SHOP WOMEN'S" is right there above the fold. I don't have to hunt for anything.
- The category tiles — TOPS UNDER £5, DRESSES UNDER £10 — are the part that actually made me stop scrolling. That's useful. That's how I shop sale: by what I can justify spending on a trend that might last two months. The editorial grid underneath is gorgeous too; diverse models, summer whites, that lacy cactus-garden shot at the bottom right felt like a Pinterest board I'd actually save.
- The one thing nagging me: this is the fifth ASOS email in nine days. I'm not quite at unsubscribe mode but I'm watching. And "up to 70% off" with a tiny asterisk on "limited time only" is the oldest trick in the discount playbook. I'd click the DRESSES UNDER £10 tile immediately, but I'd do it knowing ASOS is running a classic clearance burn, not because this email made me feel like they *knew* me.

## What's working

- **The 70% number does the heavy lifting.** Bigger than H&M's "up to 50%" from earlier this week — that's a real competitive advantage and I clocked it instantly.
- **Price-category tiles are genuinely useful.** TOPS UNDER £5 and DRESSES UNDER £10 are the kind of specificity that makes me open a new tab, not just scroll past.
- **Editorial model diversity is solid.** Lighter-skinned woman in linen, darker-skinned woman rocking a black mini with red sandals — the grid felt like my For You page, not a legacy brand catalog.
- **"YOU DESERVE TO SAVE ON THESE" hits right.** Empowering without being cringe. ASOS knows its voice.
- **No clutter, no render bugs.** Clean two-column grid, readable hierarchy — zero of the broken-comma chaos from the July 11 disaster.

## What's weak

- **Fifth email in nine days is starting to feel like a pusher, not a brand.** The July 18 XTRA30 email was fine; the Rina Lipa send on July 15 was genuinely exciting. But this cadence is taxing my goodwill.
- **"Limited time only \*" with an asterisk is lazy.** No deadline. No "ends midnight Sunday." H&M literally wrote "ends tonight" in their subject line — that's how you do urgency. ASOS's version is just hand-waving.
- **No personalization anywhere in the body.** "You deserve to save on these" is cute copy but it's the same for every subscriber. They have my purchase history — show me the dresses section, not a generic women's grid.
- **"DOWNLOAD THE APP" lime CTA below the fold.** Wrong place, wrong time. I'm already in the email; sell me the dress, not the app download.
- **Pricing is in GBP** — not a killer for me since I know ASOS is UK-based, but if I'm comparing to Shein or H&M US pricing, the mental conversion friction is real.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the asterisk for an actual deadline.** "Ends Sunday midnight" or "48 hours left" — one of those two. H&M's "ends tonight" subject on their 50% email created more urgency than this entire send.
- 2. **Pull in one personalized signal.** Even "Based on your recent saves" above the dress grid would make this feel like ASOS sent *me* an email, not blasted a list.
- 3. **Lead the category tiles with the biggest-value one.** DRESSES UNDER £10 should be first — that's the price-per-trend sweet spot for a Gen Z sale browser. Tops under £5 is cool but dresses drive the cart.
- 4. **Kill the DOWNLOAD THE APP block mid-email.** Move it to the footer or send it in a dedicated onboarding moment. Right now it's eating real estate from the brand logos section, which is actually more useful to me.
- 5. **Dial back the send cadence.** One more ASOS email this week and I'm filtering it to Promotions permanently.

**Issue — `font-size: 0em` on table elements:**

This "ghost text" fix suppresses whitespace nodes in Outlook but will silently hide any text content placed directly inside `<td>` without an inner `<p>` or `<span>`. Requires verification that all body copy is wrapped.

**Issue — mobile breakpoint is 460px, not 600px:**
```css

```
Standard email container width is 600px. Devices with viewport widths between 461–600px (e.g., large Android phones in portrait, some foldables) will receive the desktop layout without triggering the mobile stack. Consider adding a second breakpoint at `max-width: 600px` for layout-critical rules.
