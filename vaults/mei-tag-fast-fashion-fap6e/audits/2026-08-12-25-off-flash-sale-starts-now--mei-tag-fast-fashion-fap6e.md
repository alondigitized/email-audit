# 25% off flash sale starts now
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-12**
## Executive summary

- Okay so it's another sitewide percentage-off email — 25% off, midnight deadline, "beat the back-to-school rush." That's it, that's the whole hook. Yesterday it was denim, day before that a flat 15-20% off, now this. Three sends in four days and they're all wearing the same red-white-bold-caps uniform. Nothing here says "trend," it just says "sale," and the WOMEN/MEN/KIDS/HOME button grid up top is the exact "shove the category nav in my face before I even see a product" move I called out on the denim email yesterday.
- The models are fine — I actually like the long slate-blue dress on the woman, and I do care whether H&M got the maxi trend before Zara or after — but the email gives me zero context on that. It's just "here's a person in clothes, buy clothes." Compare that to Santos Bravos' live-from-LA email from a few days back, which felt like an actual moment happening, not a discount code with a stock photo attached. Even ASOS's Lioness drop had a specific styled "reason" for the fit. This is just percentage-off wallpaper.
- Would I click? Maybe, on autopilot, because 25% off sitewide is a real number and I do need back-to-school stuff. Would I forward it to a friend? No — there's nothing here worth screenshotting into the group chat.

## What's working

- 25% off sitewide with a hard "until midnight ET" deadline is a real, clear, honest offer — no vague "up to."
- The "back-to-school rush" line at least tries to give the sale a moment instead of being generic.
- Category buttons (Women/Men/Kids/Home) are big, tappable, and easy to hit on a phone.
- The dress on the woman model and the styling on the kid's fit are genuinely decent — not embarrassing content.

## What's weak

- I've now gotten three near-identical H&M blasts this week (denim Tuesday, this flash sale) — the cadence is wearing me down and it's starting to read as spam-adjacent even without ALL CAPS everywhere.
- Zero trend framing — no "first to drop," no "as seen on," nothing that tells me why THIS 25% off matters vs. any other retailer's 25% off today.
- The layout is 90% navigation (two separate category grids, app download, store finder, socials) and maybe 10% actual "here's the thing to buy" — I have to scroll past a wall of category tiles before anything feels like a real hero moment.
- Fine print block at the bottom is genuinely huge — exclusions on sale, designer collabs, gift cards, final sale items — that's the "buried exceptions" energy that makes an offer feel less honest than it looks at first glance.
- No urgency visual (no countdown, no banner) to match the "until midnight" copy — it's just stated in plain red text, easy to skim past.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with ONE hero product/trend moment above the category grid — pick the maxi dress, tell me it's the trend piece, THEN hit me with 25% off. Right now the offer and the product are disconnected.
- 2. Cut the redundant second "SHOP BY" image grid — I already got Women/Men/Kids/Home as buttons at the top, showing it twice as photos is filler that pushes real content down.
- 3. Add a visible countdown or "sale ends in Xh" chip near the headline so "until midnight ET" actually feels urgent instead of just stated.
- 4. Trim the T&Cs preview or at least don't make it the single longest block of text in the email — one line ("some exclusions apply, details below") reads more honest than a wall of legalese.
- 5. Space out sends — this is the third H&M email in my inbox this week doing some version of "% off," and it's actively lowering my open rate on the brand.

## Technical Audit

1. Technical Summary
Broken tracking links across the board and missing unsubscribe compliance headers are the critical issues; personalization tokens render correctly.

2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All 12 probed CTA/nav links resolve through `t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=...` redirector and return 403: header nav (H&M logo, Women, Men, Kids, Home — both instances), primary CTA ("Shop now" ×2), membership banner link, and the Unsubscribe link itself.
- All broken links share the same tracking payload structure (`id`, `did=1715131006`, `rid=3326930539`, `erid=...`, `p1=DM5935449`, `p2=...`, `p3=20260812`), indicating the redirector/ESP tracking domain itself is rejecting requests — not an isolated bad link. This points to a systemic issue with the `t19.email.hm.com` redirect service (auth/campaign-token expiry or geo/bot blocking) rather than individual destination URLs.
- Two CTA links carry zero-width/invisible characters in the anchor text (`‌ ﻿Shop now` — contains U+200C ZWNJ and U+FEFF BOM), likely leftover from template merge logic; doesn't break rendering but is worth flagging as markup hygiene.
- 52 additional (non-CTA) links were present but deprioritized/skipped by the probe — footer, social, and utility links were not verified and status is unknown.

3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 9 images missing `alt` text: 4 product images (`cq5dam.web.1400.1400.jpeg` / `.750.750.jpeg` variants under `image.hm.com/.../us138_s4---ca0074_s4/`, each duplicated at two resolutions) and tracking pixels served from `t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=...`. Missing alt attributes degrade screen-reader experience and fallback rendering when images are blocked.
- Extensive mobile-responsive CSS (`@media (max-width:699px)`) and Outlook/Yahoo-specific rules (`mso-hide`, `#MessageViewBody`, `@media screen yahoo`) are present, indicating multi-client rendering was accounted for at build time; no structural markup issues observed in the truncated source.

4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tokens (e.g. `{{first_name}}`, `%%TOKEN%%`) found in the visible HTML/QA output.
- The `ld+json` `EmailMessage`/`DiscountOffer` schema block has several empty string fields (`subjectLine`, `availabilityEnds`, `discountCode`, `image`, `url`) — these are structured-data placeholders that appear unpopulated at send time, which will affect Gmail annotations/promo markup rather than on-page personalization.

5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- Unsubscribe link is present in the markup but returns a 403 per the QA probe — a functionally broken unsubscribe link is a CAN-SPAM risk if reproducible outside the scan environment.
- `List-Unsubscribe` header not detected — no evidence of RFC 2369 header-based unsubscribe.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not detected — no evidence of RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe support.
- `Authentication-Results` header not found — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from available data.
- Note: header absence is flagged by QA as possibly an artifact of the AgentMail relay not capturing/forwarding these headers, not necessarily proof they're absent at origin — treat as unconfirmed rather than a confirmed failure.

6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- No standard UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are present on any link; H&M uses a proprietary redirector scheme (`id`, `did`, `rid`, `erid`, `p1`–`p5`) instead. Continuity/attribution can't be assessed from this data since all links 403 before reaching a landing page — destination URL and parameter alignment are unverifiable.
- Plain-text version is 87% URL content (518/596 chars), which is a deliverability/spam-filter risk flag independent of the destination links' validity.

7. Recommendations
- Priority 1: Investigate why `t19.email.hm.com` redirector is returning 403 for all 12 probed links, including Unsubscribe — this is a compliance-blocking and revenue-blocking issue, not cosmetic.
- Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are actually set at the sending MTA (verify outside the AgentMail relay, e.g. via raw header capture at another mailbox) given CAN-SPAM/Gmail bulk sender requirements.
- Add `alt` text to the 4 product images at minimum; tracking-pixel alt text is lower priority but cheap to fix.
- Strip stray zero-width characters (U+200C, U+FEFF) from CTA anchor text.
- Populate or remove the empty `ld+json` structured-data fields to avoid malformed rich-snippet rendering in Gmail.
