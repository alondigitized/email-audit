# The Scandi edit
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- Okay so ASOS drops a Scandi edit the day after they hit me with the face + body beauty email, and honestly? The hero stopped my scroll for a second — white linen coords, clean silhouette, the kind of outfit that's all over my saved folder right now. But then I kept reading and realized: there's no deal here. This is purely editorial, which ASOS can pull off, but they just ran a 25% off Premier send literally four days ago and now I'm getting a mood board? The energy whiplash is real.
- The "Scandi edit" concept is actually decent timing — we're right at Midsommar, Scandinavian aesthetic is having a moment on FYP, and the hero model nails the vibe. But the email never tells me WHY I should care right now. No urgency, no offer, no "these 12 pieces drop today." It's like ASOS wanted to do a trend piece but forgot to give me a reason to click today vs. next Saturday.
- I'd mark it read and probably come back to it when I'm doing my three-tab compare on the weekend. Wouldn't forward to a friend — there's nothing to say "you need to see this."

## What's working

- **Hero model is dead-on.** All-white, relaxed Scandi silhouette, sunglasses, black bag — that's my Pinterest board in one shot. Demographic match felt genuine, not forced.
- **"SHOP SCANDI BRANDS" CTA is specific enough.** Verb + noun, branded angle. Better than "Discover more" which Zara did in their summer edit and I ignored it immediately.
- **The brand tie-ins mid-email are a nice touch.** Specific brand CTAs (NO. 21 level, Charlotte Tilbury crossover) give the editorial credibility — feels curated, not just a dump of inventory.
- **"YOUR NEXT HYPED / BACK IN STOCK" panel is smart.** That FOMO angle actually gets me. Back-in-stock restocks on trending pieces is the one thing that makes me open fast.
- **Visual layout is clean, no bugs.** The two-column grid reads well even at screenshot size, no broken images, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer.** After the Premier 25% off email four days ago and the 20% face + body email yesterday, dropping a full-price editorial with no hook feels like a bait-and-switch in the other direction. H&M at least announced their vacation wardrobe editorial with a "no sale, just vibes" energy intentionally — ASOS doesn't own that lane clearly enough for this to feel intentional.
- **No urgency anywhere.** Nothing time-bounded. No "Midsommar week only," no "while stock lasts" on the back-in-stock section specifically. That back-in-stock section had the perfect setup for urgency and didn't use it.
- **The Charlotte Tilbury crossover is confusing.** I'm here for Scandi fashion and suddenly there's a "get set: glaze" beauty panel? The June 18 email was the beauty email. Mixing it in here dilutes the editorial concept and makes the hierarchy feel scattered.
- **No personalization.** The tennis heritage email felt like ASOS knew I was into the Wimbledon moment. This just feels like a blast to everyone on their list. Stranger energy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Tie the editorial to a real cultural moment.** Midsommar is June 21 — that's right there. "The Scandi edit (and yes, we timed this for Midsommar)" would've felt intentional and shareable. A single line of context in the hero copy turns mood board into moment.
- 2. **Add a scarcity/urgency layer to the back-in-stock section.** "Only a few left" or "restocked — moving fast" right under that panel would've made me click immediately. That section earned urgency and wasted it.
- 3. **Cut the Charlotte Tilbury panel or save it for the next beauty send.** The Scandi concept is strong; the beauty crossover breaks the edit's coherence. ASOS just ran a dedicated beauty email — let this one breathe.
- 4. **Give me at least one concrete offer.** Even free express shipping on Scandi brands this week would've made this feel like a reason to shop today vs. eventually.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Midsommar-ready (shop the Scandi brands)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Clean lines, cool-girl coords — and one restocked piece you keep missing`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Scandi brands, back-in-stock drops, and the hero outfit you need this week`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — `-webkit-text-size-adjust` conflict:**
- `body` sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`
- The `*` rule sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%`

The `none` value on `body` suppresses iOS auto-scaling globally. Because `*` is lower specificity than a direct element selector in some clients, the `none` on `body` wins and can make small text illegible on iOS Mail without a zoom gesture. Standard practice is `100%` on both.

**Non-standard mobile breakpoint:**
- Media query fires at `max-width: 460px`. Industry standard is 600px. Devices 461–599px wide (many Android phones, small tablets) receive the desktop layout with no responsive adjustments.

**Cannot audit** — `alt` attributes on images, heading hierarchy, color contrast ratios, and VML fallback rendering require the full source.
