# Need some inspo?
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so ASOS hits me with "Need some inspo?" and honestly I almost didn't open it because... that's the laziest possible thing you can ask someone who's already on your list. I'm comparing Shein, ASOS, Zara, and H&M in three tabs every Saturday — I have *plenty* of inspo, I need a *reason*. The email itself is just a product grid dump. No cohesive theme, no trend story, no offer. Compare this to the Scandi edit from June 19, which had an actual aesthetic point of view — white linen coords, a clear vibe — and this feels like ASOS cleaned out a staging folder and hit send.
- The one thing that actually made me pause was the "YOUR MOST HYPED" back-in-stock section. That's the only moment where ASOS is treating me like someone with a purchase history, not a cold visitor. But it's buried halfway down and tiny.
- I would not click. I would not forward this. I'd mark it read and close the tab.

## What's working

- **Diverse casting** — multiple skin tones, different body types in the grid. ASOS has always done this better than Zara (who literally sent me a menswear lookbook twice this month), and they kept that up here.
- **"YOUR MOST HYPED" back-in-stock hook** — the copy "Missed the first few rounds? Your sold-out faves are back in stock!" is genuinely smart. That's the kind of FOMO that actually moves me.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, nothing janky. The grid loads cleanly and the yellow "DOWNLOAD THE APP" banner pops.
- **Right gender/demo** — at least ASOS sent me women's fashion. Zara's "Man | Summer in Style" is still living rent-free in my head as a cautionary tale of zero segmentation.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is doing nothing** — "Need some inspo?" is 17 characters of zero commitment. No trend name, no offer, no urgency. They ran "The Scandi edit" three days earlier and that immediately told me what I was getting. This told me nothing.
- **No offer anywhere** — after June 15 gave me 25% off thousands of styles and June 18 gave me 20% off face + body, this email has literally no discount or hook. The escalation is going the wrong direction.
- **No theme = no scroll pull** — a maxi dress, a bikini top, sunglasses, mules, a knit top, denim shorts, a grey ribbed top, a floral wide-leg — what is the edit? H&M's "active edit" at least had a lane. This is just "here are things we sell."
- **"SHOP OUR TOP BRANDS" logos are tiny** — I can barely read them. Brand collab potential is wasted when the logos are stamp-sized at the bottom.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name the trend in the subject line.** "Need some inspo?" could be "The Y2K mules are back" or "New in: crochet season is here" — anything that signals I'm getting trend intel, not a catch-all blast.
- 2. **Give the email a single editorial theme.** Pick one: resort, going-out, minimal summer basics. The Scandi edit worked because everything felt intentional. This grid feels like a leftover sale.
- 3. **Surface the "Most Hyped" section to the top.** That back-in-stock hook is your strongest engagement signal — put it in the hero or at minimum above the fold on mobile.
- 4. **Add a lightweight offer** — even free shipping or a Premier-member callout would give me a reason to tap now vs. later (or never).
- 5. **Blow up the brand logos** — if you're using New Balance or Havaianas as social proof, make them visible enough to actually function as social proof.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your most-hyped styles are back`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New in: the summer edit you actually want`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Crochet tops, mules, and the sold-out pieces everyone's been asking about.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Back in stock + fresh drops you haven't seen yet.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<meta>` tags inside `<body>/<p>` — confirmed structural defect.**
The document opens as `<html><body>` with no `<head>`, and meta tags are emitted inside a `<p>` element:
```html
<p>
  <meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8">
  <meta charset="utf-8">
  <meta name="viewport" ...>
  ...
</p>
```
`<meta>` is a void element belonging in `<head>`; placing it inside `<p>` is invalid per the HTML spec. Most email clients tolerate this (they ignore `<head>` anyway), but strict parsers may drop the viewport directive, breaking mobile scaling on some clients.

**Duplicate charset declarations** — `http-equiv="Content-Type; charset=UTF-8"` and `<meta charset="utf-8">` coexist. Redundant but harmless.

**Empty preheader div** — The hidden preheader `<div>` at the top of `<body>` contains no text:
```html
<div style="display:none !important;..."></div>
```
With no preheader copy, inbox clients (Gmail, Apple Mail) fall back to pulling the first visible rendered text as preview text, which is unpredictable and likely unintended.

**MSO conditional targets `gt mso 15` only** — The VML block uses `<!--[if gt mso 15]>` (strictly greater than MSO 15), excluding Outlook 2013 (MSO 15). If VML CTAs are load-bearing for Outlook rendering, Outlook 2013 users will miss them. Standard practice is `[if gte mso 9]` or `[if mso]`.
