# 20% off bedding, duvet sets & more
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- OK so first reaction: H&M slid into my inbox with a BEDDING email and I'm like — did I accidentally subscribe to H&M Home? Because I'm here for the Zara-versus-H&M runway-speed wars, not thread counts. The hero is actually kind of cute in a muted-olive, "my bedroom is my TikTok backdrop" way, and the 20% off headline is clean enough that I clocked it immediately. But the whole email screams "your mom's Saturday shopping list," not mine.
- There's also a "JUST LANDED" fashion section at the bottom that should have been the WHOLE email — or at least the first thing I see. Instead it's buried under six duvet sets like a footnote. And shoutout to whoever approved "BEERSUCKER TWIN DUVET COVER SET" twice in the product grid — seersucker, babe, s-e-e-r. That's the kind of typo that makes me screenshot and send to the group chat, not click add to cart.
- I'd mark this as read and move on. Maybe glance at the "JUST LANDED" fashion block on my way out. I would not forward this to a friend unless I was roasting it.

## What's working

- **20% off is front-loaded and unambiguous** — the hero hierarchy does its job, I didn't have to hunt for the offer
- **The olive-green bedroom aesthetic is actually TikTok-adjacent** — "coquette dorm room" energy is having a moment; the styling is better than I expected from a sale email
- **"Dorm to king" sizing copy** is a subtle nod at the back-to-school audience and it landed more naturally than most BTS copy I've seen
- **"JUST LANDED" pivot at the bottom** saves the email from being a total fashion miss — at least they remembered I subscribed for clothes
- **Tax-free days banner at top** is additive info that pairs well with a 20% off sale — double-savings framing is smart even if executed quietly

## What's weak

- **Wrong category entirely for a trend-chaser** — ASOS just sent me a "Fringe is in" editorial email that felt like it was MADE for me; this feels like H&M accidentally CC'd me on their home décor list
- **"BEERSUCKER" typo appears TWICE** in the product grid — seersucker is a real fabric, this is a real spelling error, and it undercuts credibility in an email that's already asking me to trust product quality
- **Body copy is generic** — "give your bedroom the refresh it deserves" is what every home brand writes in Q3; it's not H&M's voice and it's definitely not mine
- **"FIND A STORE" CTA** makes no sense in a mobile email for a 24-year-old in 2026 — that button is wasted space that creates decision split and buries SHOP NOW
- **H&M has been in my inbox every single day this week** — after the sale blitz of "Today only: 15% off sitewide" and the "New arrivals: Back to school," pivoting to bedding with no acknowledgment of the topic switch feels tone-deaf, not editorial

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the "JUST LANDED" fashion content** if you're emailing a fashion subscriber — put bedding as a secondary module, not the hero. Segmenting home shoppers from fashion shoppers would fix this entirely at the list level.
- 2. **Kill the "FIND A STORE" button** — replace it with something like "Shop Dorm Sets" to lean into the back-to-school angle that's actually landing right now
- 3. **Fix the BEERSUCKER typo** — both instances — before this email goes anywhere near a live send again
- 4. **Add a deadline to the offer** — "20% off through Sunday" or "tax-free weekend only" would give me a real reason to act now instead of swiping past
- 5. **Make the hero copy more voice-forward** — something like "your room, re-done. 20% off all bedding" over "give your bedroom the refresh it deserves"

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS `@import` failure (high severity)**

```html
<style>
  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
</style>
```

`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is stripped by Gmail (web and Android) and ignored by Outlook on Windows. Both font families (`northstar`, `oces`) will fail to load for the majority of recipients. The email will fall back to system fonts throughout. Fonts should be loaded via `<link>` tags or inlined — though most email clients block external font loading entirely; system-font fallbacks in inline CSS are the safe path.

**Responsive CSS strategy** — The `#optimize-css` block uses `@media (max-width:699px)` with class-based overrides (`.is-hidden-mobile`, `.is-hidden-desktop`, etc.). This approach works on modern clients but will produce desktop-only layout on Outlook 2016–2021, which ignores media queries entirely. Not a new issue for H&M, but noted.

**Alt text** — Cannot assess; image tags are not visible in the truncated source.
