# 25% off flash sale starts now
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- Okay H&M is genuinely testing my patience right now. Yesterday it was "new sale price drops from $6.99" — which felt like a markdown announcement after a week-long 20% off tunnel — and now we have a *flash sale* bumped to 25% off until midnight. The escalation is real, but so is the fatigue. At this point I can't tell where last week's sale ended and this one started; it's just one long blurry discount hallway.
- That said, the hero actually has urgency I'll respect: bold red "25% OFF UNTIL MIDNIGHT ET" is impossible to miss, and the midnight deadline is more credible than a vague "limited time." The problem is that's where the excitement stops. I open the email and there's nothing to *look at* — six labeled category boxes, some thumbnail-sized lifestyle shots, and an app download pitch. No model I want to be, no product I want to click, just a department-store directory.
- I'd click through on FOMO alone — midnight is midnight — but I wouldn't forward this to a friend. It's functional, not exciting.

## What's working

- **25% OFF + midnight ET** in the hero is real urgency — my thumb would hesitate before swiping past
- The bump from 20% (last week's endless tunnel) to 25% actually *feels* like news, even if barely
- "Fresh summer fits are on the rise" at least nods to trend language — more effort than the July 7th Campus Shop email gave me
- Clean render, no broken images, easy to scan top to bottom without zooming

## What's weak

- **No hero product or model** — just a grid of six labeled category buttons. Zara does this recycled-format thing and I call it out every time; H&M is committing the same sin here
- **SHOP BY section is useless on mobile** — the lifestyle thumbnails are postage-stamp sized, nothing inspires me to tap anything specific
- **BABY, KIDS, HOME in the first-fold CTA grid** — I'm 24, I do not have children or a mortgage. Those categories kill the "this was made for you" feeling immediately
- **Zero personalization** — this email would hit my mom's inbox identically. ASOS at least nods to what I've browsed recently
- **Cadence is exhausting** — this is somewhere around email number six or seven in what feels like a continuous sale tunnel. The subject says "flash" but nothing about my inbox history makes this feel urgent or rare

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with a trend-specific hero product** — pick one silhouette that's actually moving right now (mesh tops, barrel leg, whatever's blowing up on FYP) and make that the hero with 25% off called out on top. ASOS's "Must-have summer dresses" from this same week opened with an editorial dress shot that made me want to scroll — H&M had the same opportunity and left it on the table.
- 2. **Segment the CTA grid** — for a women's subscriber, ditch BABY, KIDS, HOME above the fold. Show me WOMEN and BEAUTY, full stop.
- 3. **Add a countdown or "X hours left" visual** — the midnight deadline is great copy but a live-style "8 HOURS LEFT" banner would physically stop the scroll.
- 4. **Show 3-4 specific products with crossed-out prices** — yesterday's email had "$6.99" and that felt tangible. Today's email gives me a percentage and a wave at the entire store. Price anchoring with before/after numbers converts.
- 5. **Fix the preheader** — there's no visible preheader copy in this render, which means the inbox is either showing "view in browser" junk or nothing. Wasted real estate.

## Technical Audit — H&M Flash Sale Email

### 1. Technical Summary
The email is structurally sound for major clients (Outlook, Apple Mail, Gmail) with appropriate meta tags and MSO fallbacks, but the JSON-LD structured data block contains stale/unpopulated fields that will suppress Gmail Promotions tab annotations, and `@import` font loading is blocked by most email clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues
**Cannot fully assess** — HTML is truncated before body links appear. From what is visible:
- No UTM parameters are confirmed present or absent on any CTA yet.
- The `PromotionCard` schema block has `"url":""` (empty string) — if this is not populated server-side before send, Gmail's native promo card will fail to render a deep link.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility
**`@import` font loading will be stripped**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
Gmail, Outlook, and Yahoo all strip `@import` inside `<style>` tags. These imports will silently fail; confirm system font fallbacks are defined.

**No `lang` attribute on `<html>`**
```html
<html style="border:0;margin:0;outline:0;padding:0">
```
Missing `lang="en-US"` (or locale equivalent). Required for screen readers and affects spell-check behavior in some clients.

**Duplicate CSS rules in mobile breakpoint** — `.cta-link-mobile-border-ns`, `.set_font_interactive_standalone_link_default`, and `.set_font_heading_xlarge` are each declared twice within the same `@media (max-width:699px)` block. No functional breakage, but adds parse overhead and is a maintenance flag for the template.

**`#MessageViewBody` selector** — Present for Outlook Web App targeting. Confirm this isn't the only path to show/hide `.hide-webkit` / `.show-webkit-table` elements; Gmail ignores `#MessageViewBody`.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**Stale date in JSON-LD DiscountOffer**
```json
"availabilityStarts":"2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"
```
This is an 8-year-old hardcoded timestamp. Either a copy-paste artifact or an unfilled template slot. It will not cause a delivery failure, but it suppresses correct Gmail Promotions tab rendering.

**Multiple empty JSON-LD fields** — `"subjectLine":""`, `"availabilityEnds":""`, `"discountCode":""`, `"description":""`, `"image":""`, `"url":""` in the DiscountOffer and PromotionCard blocks. Any field left as an empty string rather than omitted signals an unpopulated template to inbox providers.

**Unsubscribe link** — `.footer-unsubscribe` CSS class exists, suggesting a footer unsubscribe is present, but it is beyond the truncation point. Cannot confirm CAN-SPAM physical address or one-click List-Unsubscribe header (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) from available source.
