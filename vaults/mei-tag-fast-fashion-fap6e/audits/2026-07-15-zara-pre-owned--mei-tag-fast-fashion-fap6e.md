# ZARA PRE-OWNED
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- Okay so Zara Pre-Owned is genuinely one of the more interesting things Zara does — resale, thrifting, circularity vibes — it's a whole Gen Z language and I respect that they built a program around it. When this landed I was like, okay, this is *different* from the seven Men's and Kids emails I've been drowning in lately. The hero is editorial and the crochet moment is real — crochet is everywhere right now, and the "Crochet, Revisited" framing is the right idea.
- But then I scroll down and there it is: Men Collection. Kids Collection. Folded right into a Pre-Owned send that opened with so much potential. We've been here before. Multiple times. July 11 they sent me the full Men's editorial. July 9 it was Kids. I get it, Zara is a multi-category brand, but I do not need to be reminded every single email. The dilution is real and it chips away at what could have been a tight, community-feeling Pre-Owned drop.
- I'd read this. I'd maybe click the women's crochet section out of genuine curiosity because pre-owned pricing on trend pieces is actually a good deal. I would not forward it. And I'd feel slightly annoyed scrolling past three male models and a kid before I hit the footer hashtag.

## What's working

- **Hero editorial is strong** — woman in a colorful crochet top in a styled chair reads high-fashion, not discount-bin. Correct aesthetic energy for a resale program trying to feel premium.
- **"Crochet, Revisited" is trend-aware** — crochet is genuinely having a moment and naming it directly signals that Zara is tracking what's happening, not just clearing inventory.
- **#ZARAPREOWNED in the footer** — hashtag community-building is a nice touch; it frames this as a movement, not just a sale.
- **Shoes & Accessories and Summer Shorts sections** — the product layout within the women's sections looks organized and shoppable, multiple items visible in a clean grid.
- **No visible render bugs** — email looks clean, images loaded, layout isn't broken.

## What's weak

- **Men Collection and Kids Collection are in here again** — this is the fourth or fifth consecutive Zara email where I have to scroll past content that has nothing to do with me. The Pre-Owned concept had real focus; the multi-category padding killed it.
- **"NOT NEW BUT STILL COOL!" lands a little clunky** — the exclamation copy reads like someone trying too hard to make secondhand feel exciting. ASOS's Rina Lipa email didn't have to convince me the collab was cool — it just *was*. The defensive framing here undercuts the hook.
- **No price signal anywhere** — Pre-Owned's whole value proposition is *better prices on real pieces*. If a crochet top that retailed for $79 is now $32, show me that. I see no numbers, no "up to X% off original," nothing.
- **Subject line "ZARA PRE-OWNED" is flat** — it's the name of the program, not a reason to open. Compare to H&M's "The best denim of summer" — at least that tells me what I'm getting.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut Men and Kids entirely from this send** — Pre-Owned is a destination concept; it deserves its own segmented list. If they have men's pre-owned shoppers, send them their own email. The dilution is the biggest brand-consistency miss.
- 2. **Add a price anchor in the hero** — even one line: "From $12. Seriously." or "Your $89 crochet top — now $29." That converts Pre-Owned from a vibe to a reason to click.
- 3. **Rewrite the hero subhead** — drop the defensive "NOT NEW BUT STILL COOL!" energy. Let the product speak.
- 4. **Lean into the social/resale community angle** — a stat like "12,000+ pieces added this week" or "join 2M Pre-Owned shoppers" makes the hashtag footer feel earned instead of tacked on.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Crochet. Pre-owned. Yours for less.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The pre-owned drop you actually want`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Trend pieces, real prices — crochet & summer styles now live`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the looks without the markup — crochet, shorts, accessories`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Google Fonts stripped by major clients**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Roboto+Condensed:400,700&display=swap..." rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail, Yahoo Mail, and Outlook all strip external `<link>` tags. No system-font fallback is declared alongside this request. Recipients on those clients will render in the client's default serif/sans-serif — not Roboto Condensed. Fix: add a CSS fallback stack (`'Roboto Condensed', Arial, sans-serif`) in inline styles or a `<style>` block declaration.

**[HIGH] Preheader hidden from all Outlook versions**
```html
<!--[if !mso 9]><!-->
<div style="mso-hide:all;">
  <span style="display: none !important; ...">New second-hand arrivals…</span>
```
The `<!--[if !mso 9]><!-->` conditional excludes Outlook 2007 (mso 9) entirely. For all other Outlook versions, the `mso-hide:all` on the wrapper div then suppresses it. Net effect: **no Outlook client renders the preheader preview text.** Outlook users see a blank preview pane. Fix: move the preheader `<span>` outside the `mso-hide` div, or use a standard MSO-safe preheader pattern without the `mso-hide` wrapper.

**[MEDIUM] `user-scalable=no` violates WCAG 1.4.4**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width; initial-scale=1.0; maximum-scale=1.0; user-scalable=no;" />
```
Disabling pinch-to-zoom on mobile fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `user-scalable=no` and `maximum-scale=1.0`.

**[LOW] Viewport meta uses semicolons as separators**
The same tag uses `;` between properties instead of `,`. Technically invalid per spec; most parsers tolerate it, but it should be corrected.

**[LOW] Invalid HTML — `<div>` as direct child of `<tr>`**
The preheader `<div>` is placed directly inside a `<tr>` with no `<td>` wrapper. This is invalid HTML and may trigger quirks-mode rendering in strict parsers. Wrap in `<td>`.

**[LOW] Duplicate CSS rule**
In the `@media only screen and (max-width: 640px)` block, `.bot40 {padding-bottom: 40px!important;}` is declared twice. The duplicate is dead code — remove one.
