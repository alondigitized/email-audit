# Summer styles to wear on repeat
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- Okay so after five consecutive 20% off emails — literally five in a row, I counted — H&M just casually slides into my inbox with a full-price lookbook called "Silhouettes of Summer" like we weren't just in a sale tunnel together all week. The editorial photography is genuinely pretty. Hero shot is a tall, airy silhouette in a white slip dress that hits the Gen Z minimalist-meets-elevated-basics trend hard. It *looks* expensive in a way H&M doesn't always pull off.
- But here's the thing — there is not a single offer in this email. No percent off, no free shipping, no "members only" price. After being trained all week to expect 20% off anything I click on, switching to full-price editorial with no incentive is a whiplash moment. It feels like H&M forgot to attach the coupon. ASOS has been running "further reductions" all week and Zara's lookbook at least has the mystique of never discounting. H&M is stuck in the middle — too discount-conditioned to sell me on a no-offer editorial, but the offer cadence this week already burned my attention down to nothing.
- I'd mark this as read. I would not click. I would not forward it — if anything I'd send it to a friend as a "lol they forgot the promo code."

## What's working

- **Hero image is genuinely on-trend.** The white slip dress + editorial crop is exactly the silhouette I've been seeing all over my FYP this week. H&M actually looked like *they* own this aesthetic for once.
- **"Silhouettes of Summer" as a concept is strong.** It gives the email a mood rather than just dumping a product grid — Zara does this but rarely labels it this clearly.
- **Product photography is clean and consistent.** The linen cami, floral dress, and layered jacket looks all feel like they belong in the same editorial shoot. No chaos in the grid.
- **Model diversity is present.** At least two different models visible, both young, both on-demographic for me. That's table stakes but H&M doesn't always deliver it.
- **Layout is tight with no visible render bugs.** On mobile-width this reads fast — hero → products → shop by category. Nothing breaking, no orphaned text.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer after five offer emails is brand whiplash.** H&M spent the entire week training me that their emails = discounts. A full-price editorial drop with no transition explanation feels incomplete, like the promo code fell off.
- **Product price text is microscopic.** I can see item names but the actual prices are unreadably small in the grid. That's the one thing I want to know before I click anything.
- **"SHOP NOW" is doing too much heavy lifting.** Every CTA in this email is the same generic label. After all those items — cami top, floral dress, trousers — there's no "Shop the Silhouette" or "Get the Look" moment. It's copy-paste buttons.
- **Subject gives me nothing to open for.** "Summer styles to wear on repeat" sounds like SEO copy, not a trend alert. Zara's bad subject lines have at least trained me to open out of FOMO. This one just… sits there.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Bridge from the sale era to the editorial era explicitly.** Lead with something like "The sale's over. These are worth full price." Own the transition instead of pretending the discount week didn't happen — your subscribers remember, and a little self-awareness actually builds trust.
- 2. **Make prices readable.** If you're going full-price editorial, lean into it — show the price proudly next to the item name in a size I can actually read. Hiding it makes me more suspicious, not less.
- 3. **Differentiate the CTAs.** "Shop the Look" or "Get the Silhouette" per product cluster is shareable and trend-language aligned. "SHOP NOW" four times is just noise.
- 4. **Add at least one trend hook in the body copy.** "The slip dress silhouette is everywhere right now" — one line like that validates my TikTok algorithm and makes H&M feel like they're *setting* the trend, not just cataloguing clothes.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Linen cami, floral midi, wide-leg trousers — the summer formula.`
