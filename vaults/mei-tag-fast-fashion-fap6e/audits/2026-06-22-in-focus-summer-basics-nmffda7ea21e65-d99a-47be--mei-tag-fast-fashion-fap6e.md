# In focus: Summer basics
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- Ugh. H&M is in my inbox *again* — this is genuinely the seventh send from them in about six days, and my thumb is already pre-conditioned to swipe past before I've even registered what the email says. "In focus: Summer basics." Basics. That word is doing a lot of heavy lifting and I'm not here for it. The June 19th "cool for the summer" 60% off send had a concrete offer that made me stop — this one is asking me to shop full price for a crinkled dress and a cardigan with zero discount, zero urgency, and zero trend energy whatsoever.
- The red tank in the hero is actually cute — the color pops hard against the white layout and it reads fast on mobile. But this is the same editorial-no-offer formula they ran on June 21st's "active edit," and when it's your fifth straight no-offer send in a row, the creative feels like a template swap before I've even scrolled. H&M keeps swinging between sale-hammer (60% off, 60% off) and editorial (here are some clothes, besties) with nothing in between, and "summer basics" lands squarely in the "I'll look at this never" pile.
- I would not click. I would not forward this. I'd mark it read and go open ASOS, where their recent "60% off Sale starts now" hero told me everything I needed to know in under a second without making me decode editorial vibes.

- The red tank model is a genuinely strong hero — that color contrast reads instantly and it's the clearest visual signal in the whole email
- Individual product names are visible on each tile ("RUFFLE-TRIMMED CARDIGAN," "CAP-SLEEVED CRINKLED DRESS") with "New Arrival" badges — more info than most of their editorial sends give me
- Price points are surfaced on the product tiles so I don't have to click through just to know if I can afford it
- Clean render — no broken images, no overlapping copy, layout holds together on mobile

## What's weak

- "Summer basics" is literally the opposite of why I follow H&M — I want trend signals, not a meditation on wardrobe foundations
- No offer anywhere in the body — "SALE" in the nav is navigation, not a promo, and full-price basics with zero incentive is a hard sell in a week where they ran 60% off four days ago
- This is the seventh email in six days and the creative template is identical to June 21st's "active edit" — H&M is officially running on copy-paste energy
- "SHOP NOW" as a CTA four times in a row tells me nothing — where am I going, what am I getting, why now?
- The "SHOP BY" grid at the bottom (WOMEN / MEN / KIDS / HOME / BEAUTY) is homepage UI crammed into an email — I'm already in their women's send, I don't need to re-sort myself

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with a trend hook instead of a category label.** Linen and tank tops are genuinely having a TikTok moment right now — say that. "The tank that's everywhere rn" or "linen isn't a vibe, it's a uniform this summer" lands completely differently than "basics."
- 2. **Add one actual incentive.** Even "free shipping on new arrivals this week" changes the calculus. The June 19th 60% off send pulled because there was something concrete at stake — this one has nothing.
- 3. **Make every CTA specific.** "Shop the cap-sleeve dress → " beats "SHOP NOW" every single time. I want to know what I'm clicking into.
- 4. **Reduce cadence or segment harder.** Seven sends in six days is the fast lane to unsubscribes. At 3x/week, each email gets actual real estate in my brain instead of blurring together.
- 5. **Cut the SHOP BY footer grid.** Use that space for a fit note, a sizing callout, a TikTok reference — anything that adds purchase context.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Font @import stripping (high risk)**
Both font stylesheets are loaded via `@import` inside `<style>` blocks:
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
Gmail and Outlook strip `@import` directives; custom fonts will silently fall back to system defaults in all major clients. This is expected behavior for email and likely intentional, but confirms custom typography will not render in Gmail/Outlook.

**Incomplete `format-detection` meta**
```html
<meta content="telephone=no" name="format-detection"/>
```
Only telephone auto-linking is suppressed. iOS Mail and some Android clients also auto-link dates, addresses, and email addresses. The complete suppression directive is:
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no, date=no, address=no, email=no"/>
```
