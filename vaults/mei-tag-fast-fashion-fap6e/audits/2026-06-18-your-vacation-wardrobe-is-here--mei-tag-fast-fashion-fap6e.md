# Your vacation wardrobe is here
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- Okay so H&M slides into my inbox for the **fifth time in four days** and this time the vibe is full editorial — no sale, no discount, just "here are two dresses, bestie." After Tuesday's "up to 60% off" and Monday's "up to 60% off (but make it app-exclusive)," showing me a $59.99 balloon-sleeved dress at full price feels like a personality reset. The whiplash is real and I clocked it.
- The "VACATION EDIT" headline is doing something right — "edit" is native vocabulary, it doesn't feel corporate. And the drawstring balloon-sleeved dress is genuinely cute, that silhouette is all over my For You Page right now. But the hero copy promises me linen pants and swimwear and then shows me... a crêpe skirt and a formal evening dress? That's a lie of omission. I came in for the promise and left disappointed by the product page.
- I would not click and I would not forward. The Anitta email from three days ago? I sent that to two friends. This one I'm marking as read and scrolling past. The one thing saving it from being a full unsubscribe trigger is that balloon dress.

## What's working

- **"VACATION EDIT" framing lands.** Using "edit" is TikTok-native curation language — feels intentional, not corporate buzzword.
- **The balloon-sleeved dress is actually trend-relevant.** Big dramatic sleeves are everywhere right now, and putting it against that moody dusk backdrop gives it an editorial moment.
- **Clean visual hierarchy.** Logo → hero → products → categories. My eye doesn't fight with the layout, which is more than I can say for every email in my inbox.
- **Specific product names and prices are shown.** "Gathered Crêpe Skirt $46.99" — I know what I'm clicking into. Compare to Zara's recent home edit where I had zero idea what anything cost until I landed on a product page.
- **No broken images, no render issues.** The email just works, which honestly feels rare.

## What's weak

- **Full prices after two "60% off" conditioning emails is a major vibes mismatch.** H&M trained me to expect a deal this week and then ghosted the discount. That's not a treat, that's bait-and-switch energy.
- **Hero copy promises linen pants and swimwear, products deliver a crêpe skirt and a formal dress.** The copy and the product selection are living in different emails.
- **Both models are white and blonde.** It's 2026. ASOS's last email I audited had a full cast that actually looked like the people who buy fast fashion — this feels like a step backward, especially for a Gen Z-targeting send.
- **"SHOP NOW" as the CTA is so lazy.** ASOS says "Shop the Look," Shein will write "Grab Yours Now." "Shop Now" tells me nothing about why I should click this one vs. every other "SHOP NOW" button.
- **The email copy describes products that aren't in the email.** "Breezy linen pants and standout swimwear" — where? I scrolled and saw none of them.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the actual products the copy promises.** If you're writing "linen pants and swimwear," put linen pants and swimwear in the product tiles. The crêpe skirt and balloon dress should live in a different edit framing.
- 2. **Add at least a soft incentive.** After two back-to-back sale emails, a "free shipping on orders over $40" line would bridge the gap between sale mode and editorial mode without cheapening the aesthetic.
- 3. **Diversify the model cast.** This is the most obvious fix. One swap in casting makes this feel like it's for me, not a 2015 Scandinavian catalog.
- 4. **Rename the CTA to be section-specific.** "Shop the Vacation Edit" on the hero, "Shop This Look" under each product. ASOS does this and it makes every click feel intentional.
- 5. **Fix the copy-product mismatch** or anchor the headline more tightly. Either show me the swimwear or don't promise it.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` for web fonts (confirmed issue)**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
Outlook (all versions) ignores `@import` entirely. Fonts will silently fall back. This is expected behavior for Outlook-targeted emails, but if brand font fidelity matters in desktop Outlook, these should be served via `<link>` in a conditional MSO comment or inlined as `font-face` declarations.

**`viewport` meta missing `initial-scale`**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width"/>
```
Should be `width=device-width, initial-scale=1` to prevent iOS Safari from auto-zooming on narrow layouts.

**Client-specific selector coupling**
```css
#MessageViewBody .hide-webkit { display: block !important; }
```
`#MessageViewBody` is a Gmail webmail DOM ID. This selector is Gmail-only and will have no effect in other clients, which is fine as long as the fallback display state is correct for non-Gmail. No bug if the hide/show logic degrades gracefully.

**Alt text**: Cannot verify — image tags are in the truncated body. Flag for full-source check.
