# Stars, stripes & 20% off sitewide
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- OK so H&M really dressed up yesterday's offer in a flag costume and called it a new email. I saw the 20% off angle literally yesterday — the "long weekend starts now" send — and here we are again, same number, different theme. The hero model in the blue off-shoulder ruffle top is genuinely cute, I'll give them that — coastal summer energy is tracking right now — but the stars and stripes framing exists only in the subject line. Crack open the email and there's zero red, white, or blue editorial direction. It's like they wrote a patriotic subject and then forgot to brief the design team.
- The thing that actually gave me pause: subject says "sitewide," body says "almost everything." Those are not synonyms and I know it. "Almost everything" is H&M's escape clause for collabs, new arrivals, and anything actually worth buying. Small bait, but I clocked it.
- I'd scroll FOR HER, maybe save the blue top to a wishlist. Click to buy today? Probably not. Forward to a friend? No — she's getting this exact email herself. Mark as read.

## What's working

- The blue off-shoulder ruffle hero image is on trend — coastal Europe summer is having a TikTok moment and this hits that beat
- "20% OFF ALMOST EVERYTHING" lands immediately after the hero — the offer hierarchy is clean at the top of the email
- The FOR HER / FOR HIM / FOR THEM swimlane structure is useful — I can skip straight to my section without scrolling past men's cargo shorts
- The member benefit banner at the very top is a quiet but consistent nudge that's slowly working on me
- Including the "UP TO 70% OFF" sale callout below reminds me the bigger discount is still alive — useful context even if it creates two competing offers in one email

## What's weak

- Subject says "sitewide," email says "almost everything" — first trust crack before I even scroll
- This is the second 20% off email in 24 hours; the novelty ceiling is zero at this point, same as when ASOS recycled their "Guess who's back?" energy right after their 70% off peak
- The 4th of July angle stops at the subject line — no Americana editorial, no red/white/blue palette, no holiday-coded looks in the hero; Zara would have committed to the aesthetic even if Zara also sends the same subject title twice a month
- FOR HIM and FOR THEM take up real estate that does nothing for me — a women-only send would hit sharper
- No single trend piece spotlit — just category buckets, which is the same lazy structure H&M used in their welcome email back when they first got my address

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the "sitewide" vs "almost everything" split.** Pick one and honor it. The subject is the contract; the body breaking it immediately costs trust that takes five good emails to rebuild.
- 2. **Give me one hero product with a name and a link.** The off-shoulder top in the hero is the right move — take it all the way, name the piece, link directly to the PDP, tell me if it's selling out. ASOS does this better even on their worst sends.
- 3. **Commit to the holiday creative.** If you're going stars and stripes in the subject, bring that energy into the email — a linen white set, a denim short moment, a red-lip editorial. Right now the design team and the CRM team are not talking to each other.
- 4. **Collapse to a single offer.** Two competing discounts (20% off + 70% off sale) in one email means I trust neither. Send the 20%-off story clean; let the sale section live on its own dedicated send tomorrow.
- 5. **Dial back the cadence.** Five emails in four days is the pacing of a brand that's panicking, not one that's confident. Even Zara — who I've called out for sending the exact same "Woman | Discover what's new" subject twice in two weeks — doesn't stack this fast.

- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your summer wardrobe just got cheaper. Shop before it ends.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading will fail in major clients:**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` tags is not supported by Outlook (all versions), Yahoo Mail, or older Android Mail. These clients will fall back to system fonts silently, but it means brand typography is absent in a significant portion of the audience. Font stacks in inline `font-family` declarations are the fallback; whether those are set is not visible in the truncated source.

**Protective meta tags present** — `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` and `format-detection=telephone=no` are both included. Viewport meta is present. No issues here.

**Mobile breakpoint at 699px** — standard for H&M's template system; no anomaly.

**`alt` text on images** — cannot confirm from truncated source. Flag for manual check.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Stale `availabilityStarts` in JSON-LD DiscountOffer:**
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"
```
This date is from 2018 and is clearly a template default that was never replaced. Gmail uses this to determine offer validity window in the Promotions Tab. A date 7+ years in the past may cause the offer to render as expired or be deprioritized.

**Multiple empty JSON-LD fields indicate incomplete structured data population:**
```json
{ "@type": "EmailMessage",   "subjectLine": "" }
{ "@type": "DiscountOffer",  "availabilityEnds": "", "discountCode": "", "description": "" }
{ "@type": "PromotionCard",  "image": "", "url": "" }
```
`subjectLine` should match `"Stars, stripes & 20% off sitewide"`. `discountCode` should contain the promo code if one exists. `PromotionCard.image` should point to the hero image for Gmail Promotions Tab rendering. All are empty, meaning Promotions Tab annotations will either not render or render with missing content.

**No visible merge tokens** in the truncated header/head block — personalization tokens (first name, etc.) are expected in the body, which is not visible.
