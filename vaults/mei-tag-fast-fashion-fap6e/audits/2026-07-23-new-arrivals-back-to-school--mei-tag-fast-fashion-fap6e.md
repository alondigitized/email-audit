# New arrivals: Back to school
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- Okay so H&M just sent me a "new arrivals" email after absolutely torturing my inbox all week with sale content. The 50% off sirens, the "ends tonight," the "today only 15% off" — and now they want to come in clean with a fresh drop? The audacity is real, and honestly? I respect the pivot a little. The varsity jacket moment in the hero is genuinely cute and the BTS timing is on point for late July. This is the exact wave Shein and ASOS are both riding right now so the category instinct is solid.
- BUT. Midway through the email there's a full "GOLDEN SUMMER" section dropped in — headless mannequin, yellow crochet knit, completely different energy — and it kills the whole BTS narrative. It's like they couldn't decide which campaign to send and just... taped both together. That's a Zara move, and not in a good way. Zara can get away with the moody mannequin editorial because their whole brand is that. H&M doing it feels like a borrowed aesthetic mid-email.
- I'd probably scroll through once and bounce. I wouldn't click on the Golden Summer thing. I might click the varsity grid if the prices are right. I would not forward this — I'd DM my friend the individual ASOS BTS link before I'd share this whole thing.

## What's working

- **Varsity jacket hero is the right trend at the right time.** Collegiate/letterman is having a serious moment and H&M's model styling looks authentically Gen Z, not like they sourced it from a 2019 mood board.
- **BTS timing is sharp.** Late July is exactly when I start thinking about the transition haul. They're not late.
- **Layout is clean — no bugs, no broken images.** Everything rendered properly, hierarchy is obvious: hero → product grid → editorial shot → secondary story. Easy to scroll.
- **Young, diverse model casting in the product grid.** The three-model row beneath the hero looks like actual people, which I appreciate vs. the typical samey poses.
- **Product thumbnails with pricing visible.** Even small, I can see items and their price points, which helps me decide whether to open the full shop.

## What's weak

- **The "GOLDEN SUMMER" section completely breaks the email's focus.** BTS and Golden Summer are two different emotional modes. This is not a pivot — it's a crash landing into a different campaign. ASOS would have sent these as two separate emails, both better for it.
- **No offer whatsoever after a week of discounts.** After training me to expect deals, showing up with full-price new arrivals and no incentive is a whiplash I didn't ask for. Even a "free shipping on new arrivals" signal would've landed.
- **"SHOP NOW" is doing nothing for me.** It's the laziest CTA possible. "SHOP THE DROP" or "SHOP BTS LOOKS" gives me context. "SHOP NOW" just says you filled the button field.
- **The headless mannequin for Golden Summer reads Zara-lite and not in a complimentary way.** Zara does the cold, sculptural mannequin thing and it works because it's their whole brand language. H&M doing it as a mid-email section feels like they're copying the vibe without the authority.
- **Cadence fatigue is real here.** I've had five H&M emails this week. I barely opened this one.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut Golden Summer from this email entirely.** Send it as its own send next week. One campaign, one story, one click intent. This is the highest-leverage fix.
- 2. **Add a soft offer or exclusive hook for new arrivals.** Even "first to shop before it sells out" framing or a loyalty-member early-access badge would give me a reason to act now rather than maybe-later.
- 3. **Rewrite the CTA copy.** "SHOP BTS LOOKS" or "SHOP THE DROP" gives the button a job. "SHOP NOW" is an afterthought.
- 4. **Add one social proof signal.** A "trending on TikTok" callout on the varsity jacket or even a sticker-style "just dropped" badge would make this feel like real momentum instead of a catalog page.
- 5. **Subject line needs urgency or FOMO.** "New arrivals" is informational, not motivational.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Varsity era is here — shop before it goes`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The BTS haul just dropped at H&M`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Letterman jackets, collegiate hoodies + more new arrivals just in`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh drop for the back-to-school transition — shop the new arrivals now`

## Technical Audit — H&M "Back to School" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard table-based layout with Gmail Promotions tab JSON-LD annotations and responsive CSS. Several JSON-LD fields contain stale or empty values, and two `@import` calls for external fonts will silently fail in the majority of email clients.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` for external fonts will be blocked**
```html
<style>
  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
</style>
```
`@import` inside `<style>` is stripped or ignored by Outlook (all versions), Yahoo Mail, and several mobile clients. If the CSS cascade doesn't fall back to a declared `font-family` stack, text will render in the client's default serif/sans. Confirm that inline `font-family` fallbacks exist on all text elements.

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`**
```html
<html style="border:0;margin:0;outline:0;padding:0">
```
No `lang="en"` (or locale-appropriate value). Screen readers cannot infer the content language; WCAG 2.1 SC 3.1.1 failure.

**`display:inline` on `.is-hidden-desktop` at mobile breakpoint**
```css
.is-hidden-desktop { display: inline !important; ... }
```
Using `display:inline` to un-hide block-level elements (tables, `<td>`) produces unpredictable layout in email clients. The pattern should use `display:block` or `display:table` depending on the element type.

**Positive findings:**
- `<meta name="x-apple-disable-message-reformatting"/>` present — correct
- `<meta content="telephone=no" name="format-detection"/>` present — correct
- `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width"/>` present — correct
- `charset=UTF-8` declared — correct

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Stale hardcoded date in JSON-LD**
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"
```
This date is ~7.5 years in the past. It appears to be a leftover default from template creation. Gmail uses this field to render Promotions tab deal cards; an expired `availabilityStarts` with no `availabilityEnds` may suppress the annotation or display incorrect deal timing.

**Multiple empty JSON-LD fields that should be populated or omitted**
```json
{ "@type": "EmailMessage", "subjectLine": "" }
{ "@type": "DiscountOffer", "discountCode": "", "availabilityEnds": "", "description": "" }
{ "@type": "PromotionCard", "image": "", "url": "" }
```
- `subjectLine` should match the actual subject (`"New arrivals: Back to school"`) or be omitted.
- `PromotionCard.url` and `PromotionCard.image` are empty strings — Gmail will ignore or reject the annotation. If this email is not intended to carry a deal card, the `DiscountOffer` and `PromotionCard` blocks should be removed entirely rather than left as empty shells.
- Empty `discountCode` and `description` on `DiscountOffer` will prevent Gmail from rendering a deal badge.
