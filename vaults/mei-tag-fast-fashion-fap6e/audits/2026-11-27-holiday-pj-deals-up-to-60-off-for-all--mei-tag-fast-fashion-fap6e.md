# Holiday PJ deals: Up to 60% off for all!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-27**
## Executive summary

- Okay H&M, this is the thirteenth Black Friday-season email in about two weeks and I'm on autopilot. The first thing I see is a free shipping banner, then "UP TO 60% OFF SITEWIDE" in big bold type. The pajama hero is a woman in red-and-cream striped PJs, and she's actually my demo. That's the first time in a while an H&M hero looked like it was meant for me instead of a toddler (11/26 sweaters) or a loaf of bread (11/26 "EVERYTHING" email).
- It still isn't a trend email. PJs and slippers are cozy basics, not a TikTok moment. Nothing here says "this is the thing right now." It reads like a sale being dressed up as a theme. I'd skim it, maybe tap the PJ hero, and forward it to nobody.

## What's working

- The hero is a woman my age in matching PJs, with a plaid set and fluffy slippers right under it. That's the first time in this run of sends that it feels aimed at me.
- "UP TO 60% OFF SITEWIDE" is huge and impossible to miss. The discount is the loudest thing in the email.
- The "Settle In / Comfort Mode / Time for Bed" section headers are short and have some personality.
- The free shipping over $50 and member banner sits at the very top, so I see it before anything else.
- The "Shop by" tiles at the bottom make it easy to jump to womenswear.

## What's weak

- It's a very long scroll. The men's, baby and kids sections push the womenswear well away from the top, and by the second half I'm done.
- The CTAs are thin outlined buttons that all look alike ("Pajamas," "The Cozy Shop," "Beauty"). None of them says what I'd get, and on a phone they're small.
- This is the 13th near-identical 50-60% off send since 11/24, including the "Up to 50% off the season's bestsellers" email, so the urgency doesn't feel credible.
- There's no trend angle at all. Zara's "40% OFF selected items" was just as plain but at least didn't pretend to be a moment. ASOS's Lioness drop made me want the outfit.
- The fine print is a huge wall of tiny text, and it's the only place the exclusions and the deadline are spelled out.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one hero that sells a trend. The striped PJ set is the best candidate, with a specific price like "Matching PJ sets from $X." Then cut the men's and baby sections from this send and use a separate segment for them.
- 2. Use verb-plus-noun buttons like "Shop PJ sets" and "Shop slippers," and make them filled instead of outlined.
- 3. State the deadline in the hero, like "Ends Sunday 11:59pm," instead of burying it in the footer.
- 4. Cut the cadence. One strong send beats thirteen near-identical ones, which is how Shein trains me to open nothing.
