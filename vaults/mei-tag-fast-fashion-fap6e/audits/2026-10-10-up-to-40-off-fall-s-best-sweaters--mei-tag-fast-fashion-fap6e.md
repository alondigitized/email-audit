# Up to 40% off fall's best sweaters
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-10**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is a clean sale email, but it isn't a trend email. The hero is a girl in a cream mock-neck knit vest, which looks good. Then it's "THE FALL EVENT: UP TO 40% OFF SITEWIDE", and I've seen that exact banner already. It's the same offer as the 10/8 "The Fall Event: Up to 40% off sitewide" send, and the 10/9 "The new utility" email had the same red banner. That's three days in a row of H&M telling me about 40% off.
- On a Saturday three-tab compare, this loses to ASOS. The ASOS Lioness drop had a point of view, with camo cargo culottes and kitten mules. Zara's ORIGINS email was a menswear lookbook I couldn't use, but it at least felt like an editorial moment. H&M here feels like it's chasing the sweater trend. I'd skim it, maybe tap one cardigan, and not forward it.

## What's working

- The hero model is on-brand for me and the cream knit looks cozy. It reads fall right away.
- Prices are right there under each product, with red sale prices next to strikethroughs. Around $20-$32 is my range for a trend piece.
- The 3x2 product grid is easy to scan on my phone. Each tile has a name and a price, and the models look like people my age.
- The models are a mix of skin tones and styling. The polo and turtleneck shots feel less stiff than H&M's usual catalog look.
- Two buttons, "Shop Now" and "Find a Store", sit under the headline. The layout is clean and nothing is broken.

## What's weak

- The subject says sweaters, but the hero says "sitewide". The copy ("luxe textures, relaxed cuts, refined details") is generic. Nothing says what's trending or why I need it now.
- It's the third 40% off email in a few days. Compare the 10/7 "The Night Out Edit", which I'd actually screenshot. The discounts have crowded out the trend moments.
- The fine print is a wall of text at the bottom, and it's the only place the deadline (10/13) appears. Up top, there's no urgency at all.
- The grid repeats products. There are two fine-knit cardigans and two loose-fit mock turtlenecks, so it feels padded rather than curated.
- "Shop Now" is the generic CTA. The "Shop by" section (Men, Kids, Home, Beauty) wastes space on a sweater send.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the deadline and the trend angle in the hero. Something like "Ends Monday" next to a named trend ("the quiet-luxe knit") would give the offer a reason to exist. Right now it's only a discount.
- 2. Cut the repeated products and make the first row the hero pick, with a tap-through straight to that product.
- 3. Replace "Shop Now" with "Shop the knits" or "Get the cardigan".
- 4. Drop "Shop by" Men/Kids/Home/Beauty from a sweater email. Add a short "styled 3 ways" strip or a "most-saved" label instead.
- 5. Show a member perk or a free-shipping threshold above the fine print.
- 6. Space out the 40% sends. Alternate with editorial emails like "The Night Out Edit".

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cardigans, mock necks and polos from $19.99. Sale ends 10/13.`

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **All 12 probed links returned 403.** Every one is a `https://t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=hbddcc45e-…&did=1418729587&rid=3326930539&erid=…&p1=DM6124643&p2=…&p3=20261010` redirect. This covers "Shop now", "Find a store", the logo link ("H&M"), the image links and "Unsubscribe".
- **Likely cause:** the ESP redirector is rejecting the automated probe, not breaking for real recipients. The same host returns 403 on the unsubscribe link, which must work for real users. Treat this as unconfirmed. The final destinations were never reached, so landing-page health is unknown.
- **Duplicate link slots.** "Shop now" and "Find a store" each appear twice, with ids `…f869e639`/`…f869e63b` and `…f869e63a`/`…f869e63c`. These are probably separate desktop and mobile variants.

- **Probe coverage was thin.** Only 12 links were probed and 55 skipped (footer, social and utility links). Those 55 are untested.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `alt` text on 16 images.** Every pair is the same asset served at two sizes (`cq5dam.web.1400.1400.jpeg` and `cq5dam.web.750.750.jpeg`) for desktop and mobile. 1. Re-probe the redirect links with a realistic user agent, or whitelist the prober. This tells you whether the 403s are a prober artifact or a real redirector failure. Prioritize the unsubscribe link and the main "Shop now" CTAs.
