# The early fall outerwear edit
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-15**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "JACKET REQUIRED" as a headline is actually kind of a mood — it's giving editorial rather than "SHOP NOW 50% OFF SITEWIDE," and I respect that. But then it just... shows me a checkered shacket on one model, a women's/men's/kids grid, and randomly detours into H&M HOME living room furniture halfway through? Like I did not open a fashion email to get served a beige couch.
- There's zero pricing anywhere, zero "this is the trend right now" framing beyond one line of copy, and the HOME section genuinely killed my momentum — I was mid-scroll looking for the actual outerwear drop and got a modernist living room instead. Compare that to H&M's own tennis-core send from 9/11 which at least committed to one aesthetic all the way through.
- Would I click? Maybe, on the "JACKET REQUIRED" hero shot alone because the styling is kind of cool. Would I forward it to a friend? No — nothing here screams "this is THE jacket" enough to be worth the text.

## What's working

- The "JACKET REQUIRED" headline has actual voice — it reads like a person wrote it, not a merchandising calendar.
- Hero image styling (the tied-collar shacket) is genuinely editorial, not catalog-flat.
- Women/Men/Kids 2-8Y/Kids 9-14Y grid is clean and easy to scan on mobile.
- Two clear "SHOP NOW" CTAs above the fold-ish area.

## What's weak

- The HOME furniture section completely derails the "outerwear edit" premise — feels like two different emails stapled together.
- No price, no % off, no "new drop" urgency anywhere — just vibes and a button.
- No mention of trend context (is this THE jacket of the season, or just... a jacket) — Zara's Kids "New" email at least names specific collabs and moments even if I don't care about kids stuff.
- Kids sections eating prime real estate in an "outerwear edit" for a 24-year-old is dead weight for me specifically.
- Cadence fatigue — this is my 5th H&M email this week and the third with zero concrete offer after two days straight of "15%/20% off" math.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the HOME section entirely from this send — it dilutes the outerwear premise and reads like a merged campaign, not a curated edit.
- 2. Add a concrete hook — "new drop," "back in stock," or a price point on the hero jacket — ASOS's Lioness email at least felt like a specific product moment; this feels like stock photography with a headline slapped on.
- 3. Move Women/Men above the Kids rows since this subject line has zero kids framing.
- 4. Lean into trend language in the body copy — one line about "the trench is back" or naming the actual fabric/silhouette trend would sell the "required" urgency the headline promises.
