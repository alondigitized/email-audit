# Last day: Up to 50% off your summer wardrobe
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- Okay so H&M just sent me their *sixth* "up to 50% off" email in six days and honestly the only thing that saved this one from an instant archive is the "LAST DAY" badge at the top. That's the first genuinely new piece of information they've given me since May 20. The sale-fatigue is real — at this point I have zero emotional response to "50% off sitewide" because it's been in my inbox every single morning. The May 24 tops & shorts send already felt like scraping the bottom of the barrel; this one is at least honest that the run is ending.
- What I see in the email is a clean, editorial-style layout — "SEEKING SUN," "ON DECK," "BUILT FOR FUN," "A LITTLE R&R" — split across four category blocks, followed by a Member Giveaway for an H&M Studio Resort Bundle that is genuinely the only novel thing here. The summer models in the women's section look age-appropriate and the styling is fine. But then I'm scrolling past a Kids section, a Home section, a Beauty section, and a massive block of fine print before I get out. For a "last day" urgency play, this email asks me to do way too much work.
- I would not click. I might scan the member giveaway section because "H&M Studio Resort Bundle" is actually cute framing, but the main offer? I've seen it too many times. I would not forward this. Mark as read, maybe unsubscribe.

## What's working

- **"LAST DAY" hero badge** — this is the first subject line variation in the whole campaign that introduced real urgency; it actually made me look twice versus the May 22 "kick-start summer" and May 24 "tops & shorts" sends
- **Clean visual hierarchy** — "UP TO 50% OFF SITEWIDE" lands immediately above the fold, no guessing what the email is about
- **Women's model styling** — the "SEEKING SUN" section shows a white dress look and a denim moment, both feel current and the models read young/Gen Z
- **Member Giveaway section** — "H&M Studio Resort Bundle" with a sign-in CTA is genuinely differentiated content; it's the only thing in this email that isn't a carbon copy of the previous five sends
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, images load, nothing is broken

## What's weak

- **Cadence is destroying trust** — this is email six in six days on the same offer; "last day" urgency only works if the previous five days weren't also screaming urgency
- **CTAs are just category names** — "WOMEN," "MEN," "KIDS 2-8Y" are destinations, not reasons to click; compare this to ASOS who at least ties CTAs to a specific trend hook (even their chaotic "Need some inspo?" had a style direction)
- **Zero trend language** — no barrel jeans, no linen, no Y2K, no TikTok-trending silhouette callout; the copy says "new dresses, easy shorts and beach essentials are calling" which could have been written in 2018
- **Kids and Home sections dilute the message** — I'm 24 with no kids and no interest in throw pillows right now; Zara at least gets roasted when they send me kids content, and H&M is doing the same thing here by making me scroll past "BUILT FOR FUN" kids blocks to find anything I'd buy
- **Pre-header banner feels utilitarian** — "NEED AN ITEM SOONER? BUY ONLINE, PICK UP IN STORE" is a logistics message, not a hook; if that's leaking into my inbox preview it's doing zero work

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the last-day send by category** — if they know I'm in women's fashion, cut the kids/home/beauty blocks entirely and double down on the trending summer women's styles; ASOS at least pretends to know what I shop
- 2. **Name one specific trending item in the hero** — "barrel leg jeans, ribbed tanks, linen sets — last day at 50% off" beats "summer wardrobe" every time for a trend-chaser like me
- 3. **Give the member giveaway its own email** — the Studio Resort Bundle callout is genuinely interesting content that's getting buried below four category blocks; it would convert better standalone
- 4. **Tighten the CTA copy** — "Shop women's summer" beats "WOMEN" every time; add a verb
- 5. **Replace the pre-header with offer reinforcement** — "Sale ends tonight — dresses, denim, swimwear all included" is infinitely better than "NEED AN ITEM SOONER?"

### 1. Technical Summary

The send infrastructure and core HTML structure are sound, but the email contains a critically stale JSON-LD annotation block with an 8-year-old hardcoded date and multiple empty schema fields, plus a CSS `@import` font-loading pattern that silently fails in Outlook and several other major clients.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading — high severity**

```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```

CSS `@import` inside `<style>` blocks is not processed by Outlook (2007–2021+), Samsung Mail, or Lotus Notes. Web fonts silently fall back to system fonts in these clients, which can break typographic layout if the design depends on specific metrics. The correct pattern for email is `<link rel="stylesheet">` in `<head>` or inlined `@font-face` declarations, though even those are ignored by Outlook.

**Missing `lang` attribute — medium severity**

```html
<html style="border:0;margin:0;outline:0;padding:0">
```

No `lang` attribute is present on the root element. Screen readers cannot infer document language; this is a WCAG 2.1 §3.1.1 (Language of Page) failure. Should be `<html lang="en">` (or locale-appropriate value).

**`format-detection` meta — no issue**
`<meta content="telephone=no" name="format-detection"/>` is correctly set.

**Apple Mail reformatting suppression — no issue**
`<meta name="x-apple-disable-message-reformatting"/>` is present.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**JSON-LD annotation block — high severity**

The `<script type="application/ld+json">` block contains multiple unfilled fields and one hardcoded stale date:

| Field | Value | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| `availabilityStarts` | `"2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"` | Hardcoded 2018 date — never templated |
| `availabilityEnds` | `""` | Empty — merge token not resolved |
| `discountCode` | `""` | Empty — merge token not resolved |
| `description` (DiscountOffer) | `""` | Empty |
| `subjectLine` (EmailMessage) | `""` | Empty — subject line not injected |
| `image` (PromotionCard) | `""` | Empty |
| `url` (PromotionCard) | `""` | Empty |

Gmail uses the `DiscountOffer` and `PromotionCard` schema for Promotions Tab rendering (deal badge, image thumbnail, expiry countdown). With `availabilityStarts` set to 2018 and `availabilityEnds` empty, Gmail's promotions enrichment either ignores the annotation entirely or renders an expired deal indicator — both outcomes suppress visibility in the Promotions tab.

The 2018 date indicates this annotation block was copied from an old template and the dynamic fields were never wired to the ESP merge system.
