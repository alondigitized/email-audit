# Kids | Once upon a party
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-16**
## Executive summary

- Zara Kids again. I'm 24, and this is the second kids or baby send I've gotten from them in about a week, after the 11/08 capsule. The confetti hero is cute and the editorial feel is real, but every model is under 14. I'm not the audience and I'm not who this email is trying to reach.
- It's all black, with beautiful photography and no words. There's no price, no button and no "shop now". Even if I had a niece to shop for, I'd have nothing to tap except TikTok in the footer. I'd mark it as read and move on. I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- The confetti hero with the sequin dress is a genuinely fun, party-season image, and it's the one frame that made me stop scrolling.
- The black and white editorial styling feels expensive and consistent, like the 11/12 SRPLS lookbook.
- The group shot with the bow-tie kids has more range in who's pictured than most Zara sends, which I noticed.
- The age labels under each image (Girl / 6-14 years, Baby / 0-18 months) are clear, so a parent would know where they land.

## What's weak

- Everything is kids and babies. Nothing is for a 24-year-old woman, and nothing is trend-led.
- There's no CTA anywhere. No button, no price, no offer, and no product links I can see.
- The only tappable-looking things are the social links and "Download our app" at the bottom, which is a weak ending for a campaign.
- The black background and tiny gray captions are hard to read on a phone.
- After "This week's arrivals" (menswear, 11/15) and the kids capsule on 11/08, this is a third "not for me" send in two weeks. My Zara cadence feels random.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment it. Send this to people who've browsed or bought kids' items and give me the women's party edit, like the 11/14 "Trending now: Party" send that actually worked on me.
- 2. Add a real CTA with a clear button, like "Shop kids party looks", and put it right under the hero.
- 3. Put prices or "from $X" on the hero looks so there's something concrete.
- 4. Make the images shoppable, with each look linked to its product.
- For comparison, H&M's "Up to 40% off premium knits" is annoying in cadence but at least has a clear offer. ASOS's Lioness drop gave me a campaign mood plus products I could shop. Shein would have put a price on every frame. Zara is the prettiest of the group here and the least useful.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email has a 38% QA pass rate. The one hard failure is the missing plain-text part (0 chars). Other findings are missing alt text on every image (16 flagged), an invalid, zoom-blocking viewport meta tag, and compliance items that can't be confirmed because headers and the footer weren't captured. ## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 12 material links were probed and 4 were skipped (deprioritized as footer, utility or social). No broken or redirecting links were reported, so the 4 skipped links are unverified.
- The open-tracking pixel is served first-party from `https://news.zara.com/pub/as?_ri_=…` (Responsys). It has no `alt` attribute and was flagged as "as". It should carry `alt=""` and `width="1" height="1"`.

- Per-link tracking parameters couldn't be assessed, because the `href` values fall in the truncated portion of the source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on 16 images:**
  - Hero images `img_01.jpg` and `img_02_en.jpg` through `img_06_en.jpg` (all under `/2026_11_16_Norte_ki…`).
  - Footer images `latredes142_black.jpg` (×2), `rrss1b_black.jpg` through `rrss6b_black.jpg`, and `download_b_en.jpg`.
  - With images off or in a screen reader, the body and the social and app-download links have no accessible text.
- **Invalid viewport meta:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width; initial-scale=1.0; maximum-scale=1.0; user-scalable=no;">`.
  - Semicolons are used as separators where the spec calls for commas.
  - `user-scalable=no` and `maximum-scale=1.0` block pinch-zoom, which fails WCAG 1.4.4.
- **Invalid markup:** `<div style="mso-hide:all;">` is a direct child of `<table>`, wrapping `<tr>`. A `div` is not allowed there, and parsers may hoist it out of the table.
- **Link contrast:** the global rule `a { color: #999898; }` gives about 2.8:1 contrast on the `#ffffff` background, below the WCAG AA 4.5:1 minimum. This applies unless inline styles override it, which I couldn't see.
- **Other:**
  - The Google Fonts `<link>` in `<head>` is unsupported in many clients and falls back silently.
  - `<title>` is the generic "Zara".
