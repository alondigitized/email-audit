# Up to 20% off school staples
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- Okay so "school staples" and the second I see "WOMEN / MEN / GIRLS 2-8Y / BOYS 2-8Y / GIRLS 9-14Y / BOYS 9-14Y" as the FIRST thing after the offer banner, I already know this isn't for me — it's a category-navigation email dressed up as a promo, and I'm not the target audience, I'm just on the list. Denim wrap shirt, oversized printed tee, collins super baggy jeans — fine, those are actually kind of cute and on-trend, but they're buried under four sections of kids' backpacks and "parent-approved" motif water bottles before I even get there.
- Compare this to H&M's own "Contrast Dressing" send from 08/04 — that one had actual editorial energy, a full styled outfit story. This is the opposite: it's a spreadsheet with product photos, sorted by shopper demographic, and I happen to be scrolling past the parenting aisle to get to my aisle. Zara's "This week's selection" (3/10, all menswear) at least stayed in its lane and let me bail immediately — this one wastes my scroll pretending to have something for everyone.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I'm already mid-scroll and land on the Collins baggy jeans or the wide-leg jeans — those are legit trend pieces. Would I forward it? No. This reads like a back-to-school circular, not a trend drop, and I'm not forwarding a Target-coded backpack grid to my friends.

## What's working

- "15% OFF $80 OR 20% OFF $100" is a real, specific tiered offer — no vague "save big" nonsense.
- Collins Super Baggy Jeans and Wide-Leg Regular Waist Jeans are genuinely on my radar right now — baggy/wide-leg is the exact silhouette conversation I care about.
- Clean grid layout, no overlapping text or broken images — visually it's not a mess, just misdirected.
- "New Arrival" tags on a few pieces at least signal freshness instead of just clearance dumping.

## What's weak

- Category nav (Women/Men/Girls/Boys by age) up top makes this feel like a department store insert, not a fashion drop — zero trend-first energy.
- Four of six product sections (No-Brainers men's basics, Parent-Approved kids, Classroom-Ready kids) are completely irrelevant to me — I'm scrolling past backpacks and joggers to find two jean options.
- No urgency — no countdown, no "ends Sunday," just "WEEKEND DEAL" sitting there with no deadline pressure, unlike the 08/07 email that at least had a live weekend frame.
- Product names are truncated ("Brushed-Insi...", "Drawstring-D...") — looks sloppy, like nobody proofed the render.
- This is basically the third "up to X% off" H&M send I've seen this week (08/07's 15/20% denim deal is nearly identical math) — cadence is getting repetitive with zero new creative angle.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this by segment — send me a Women-only version led with the jeans and tees, skip the kids/backpack sections entirely. A trend shopper doesn't want to scroll past school supplies.
- 2. Lead the hero with the actual product (baggy jeans, wide-leg denim) instead of a nav bar of shopper categories — show me the drop before you show me the store map.
- 3. Fix the truncated product names before send — "Brushed-Insi..." looks broken and cheap.
- 4. Add a real deadline ("Ends Sunday 11:59pm") to the weekend deal banner — right now it has zero urgency.
- 5. Differentiate this from last week's 08/07 denim promo — same discount tiers, same vibe, no new hook.

# Technical Email Audit — H&M "Up to 20% off school staples"

## 1. Technical Summary
The email uses a standard ESP redirect-tracking architecture (t19.email.hm.com), but every probed material link — including the unsubscribe link — returns HTTP 403, making the send effectively non-functional for click-through and opt-out.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **12/12 probed material links return 403 Forbidden**, including both "Shop now" CTAs, the "H&M" logo link, all category links (Women, Men, Girls, Boys), and the footer "Unsubscribe" link. Example: `https://t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=hbc9eea1f-eca2-44e9-90d0-f9dc86250347,6639bb91,f7b3ceed&did=1715059601&rid=3326930539&erid=MzMyNjkzMDUzOQ%3D%3D&p1=DM5925800&p2=0982dd0aa7b117bf086dee6ed4af7d0c&p3=20260808`
- This is consistent across every redirect ID (`f7b3ceed`, `f7b3ceef`, `f7b3ceec`, `f7b3cf25`–`f7b3cf2c`, `f7b3cfc9`), suggesting the redirect domain/token itself is rejecting requests (e.g., expired campaign token, bot/UA filtering, or an IP block) rather than an issue with individual destination pages.
- Link anchor text contains injected zero-width/invisible characters (U+200C zero-width non-joiner, U+FEFF BOM) around visible text, e.g. `"‌  ﻿Shop now"`. These are likely artifacts of personalization/tracking pixel injection and can affect screen-reader pronunciation and text-based spam filters.
- 161 non-material links (footer/utility/social) were deprioritized and not probed — findings above are not exhaustive of the full link set.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **31 images missing `alt` text**, spanning both product imagery (`image.hm.com/assets/hm/...jpg`) and tracking-pixel/redirect images served from `t19.email.hm.com/r/...`. Each product image appears twice (at `imWidth=344` and `imWidth=322`), indicating responsive/retina variants are both missing alt attributes.
- Client-specific CSS resets are present (`#MessageViewBody`, `@media screen yahoo`, `mso-hide`), indicating standard Outlook/Yahoo/Gmail compatibility handling — no issues found here.
- Mobile breakpoint styles (`@media (max-width:699px)`) are defined via `#optimize-css` with extensive `!important` overrides — no structural issues found in the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The `application/ld+json` schema.org block has multiple **unresolved/empty merge fields**:
  - `"subjectLine":""` (EmailMessage)
  - `"discountCode":""` and `"availabilityEnds":""` (DiscountOffer)
  - `"image":""` and `"url":""` (PromotionCard)
- `"availabilityStarts":"2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"` is a stale/hardcoded date from 2018, inconsistent with the current send date (2026-08-08) — likely a template default that was never overwritten by the merge process.
- These empty values suggest the Gmail Promotions annotation (which relies on this JSON-LD for the "Shop" chip/discount display) will render incorrectly or be suppressed by Gmail, independent of the visual template.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Unsubscribe link returns 403** (see §2) — functionally broken opt-out mechanism.
- `List-Unsubscribe` header not detected — cannot confirm one-click unsubscribe support at the header level (may be a relay-capture limitation rather than a true absence; flagged as WARN, not FAIL).
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) header not detected — one-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender rules) cannot be confirmed as supported.
- `Authentication-Results` header not found, so SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status cannot be verified from available data.
- Given the broken unsubscribe link, this is the most significant compliance risk in this send — combined with the missing headers, there is no confirmed working opt-out path.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- All destination links route through the ESP redirect domain (`t19.email.hm.com/r/`) with proprietary tracking parameters (`id`, `did`, `rid`, `erid`, `p1`–`p5`) rather than standard `utm_source`/`utm_medium`/`utm_campaign` query params. No UTM parameters are present in the sampled URLs, so campaign attribution on landing pages (if GA/analytics expects UTMs) cannot be confirmed from this data.
- Landing page alignment cannot be verified at all — since every redirect returns 403, the actual destination URLs (and whether they match the category/CTA labels like "Women," "Men," "GIRLS 2‑8Y") are unreachable and unconfirmed.

3. Populate the empty JSON-LD merge fields (`subjectLine`, `discountCode`, `availabilityEnds`, `image`, `url`) or remove the schema block if Gmail Promotions annotations aren't being used for this send.
4. Correct the stale `availabilityStarts` date (2018) in the structured data.
5. Add `alt` text to all 31 flagged images for accessibility and to preserve context when images are blocked by default in mail clients.
