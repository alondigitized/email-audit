# Kids | Discover what's new this week
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- Okay so Zara has now done this to me twice in ten days. The Home email on May 6th was already a hard miss — I don't want throw pillows and ceramic crabs — and now I'm getting the Kids drop. I am twenty-four. I do not have children. I barely have houseplants. The editorial grid is doing what Zara always does — clean, airy, minimal white space, nice summer palette — but every single model in this email is a toddler or a grade schooler. There is no version of this that lands for me.
- The "this week" hook in the subject didn't alarm me because Zara sends me stuff constantly and I reflexively open it, but the second I see "Kids |" I already know I've been mis-segmented. Again. This is a segmentation failure wearing a nice linen outfit.
- I would not click, I would not forward this to a friend (unless I'm mocking it in a group chat), and I'm starting to wonder if Zara's list hygiene is as bad as a mid-tier fast fashion brand. That's not a compliment.

## What's working

- **Zara's editorial visual DNA is intact** — the grid of lifestyle shots with natural light and minimal props is exactly their brand, even in the kids category.
- **The summer palette is on-point** — bright stripes, that cobalt blue dress, the knit green tank on a kid — all feel current, not last season.
- **No render bugs** — images load cleanly, layout holds, nothing is broken or pixelated.
- **Subject line is short and honest** — 36 characters, no screaming caps, no fake urgency stacking. If you're a Zara Kids shopper, the subject does exactly what it needs to.

## What's weak

- **I am not a Zara Kids customer and they know this** — the same segmentation failure they pulled with Zara Home on May 6th, just in a different category. Two wrong-section sends in ten days is a pattern, not a fluke.
- **Zero offer anywhere in the email** — no price, no percent off, no "new arrivals from X" hook. The entire email is editorial images with tiny captions. Even H&M's "Today only: 20% off in-app" (which I found annoying) at least gave me a reason to care.
- **The only CTA is "Download Our App"** — that's it. That's the whole ask. Not "shop the drop," not "see the full kids collection" — just a generic app download banner at the bottom. I'm already on your app, Zara.
- **No product detail** — the captions under each image are too small to read without zooming. I can't tell what any of these items are called, what they cost, or if they come in different colorways.
- **Demographic signals are aggressively mismatched** — every model is a child. There's not even a parent model styled alongside the kids to create any crossover aspiration.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the segmentation before sending again.** If my account history shows zero kids browsing, zero kids cart activity, and a "Woman" category preference — don't send me the Kids weekly drop. This is the second time and it's eroding my trust in the brand faster than any bad design choice could.
- 2. **Add at least one offer signal.** Even a "free shipping on kids orders over $X" or "new-in, this week only" feels like a reason to click. Right now this is a lookbook with no door.
- 3. **Make the CTA about shopping, not app downloads.** "Shop the Drop" or "See All New Kids" is infinitely more compelling than a generic app prompt to someone who's already subscribed to your emails.
- 4. **Increase caption legibility.** If you're going to use a grid format, either size the product labels up or commit to a hover/tap reveal. As it stands, I genuinely cannot read what any of these items are.
- 5. **Reference the cadence in the copy.** The May 8th "Woman | The New" send felt like a magazine. Lean into that more consistently — if the editorial angle is the brand voice, make the weekly kids drop feel like a curated pull, not a content dump.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Viewport meta: semicolons used as delimiters (spec violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width; initial-scale=1.0; maximum-scale=1.0; user-scalable=no;" />
```
The `content` attribute of `<meta name="viewport">` requires comma-separated key-value pairs, not semicolons. Some WebKit-based clients may parse this incorrectly, breaking responsive layout. Fix: replace `;` with `,`.

**`user-scalable=no` + `maximum-scale=1.0` — WCAG 1.4.4 failure**
Both properties disable pinch-to-zoom, which is a Level AA accessibility failure affecting users with low vision. Remove both properties; the responsive CSS already handles layout at `max-width: 640px`.

**External Google Fonts stylesheet will be blocked by most clients**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Roboto+Condensed:400,700&display=swap&subset=cyrillic,cyrillic-ext" rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail (web and app), Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo strip or block `<link>` tags. Roboto Condensed will silently fall back to the system sans-serif stack in the majority of the audience. This is low-severity if the fallback stack is acceptable, but the stylesheet load is wasted and adds a DNS lookup. Either inline the `@font-face` declarations (where supported) or remove the `<link>` entirely.

**Invalid `line-height: auto` in CSS**
```css
.heightmobile { height: auto!important; line-height: auto!important; }
```
`auto` is not a valid value for `line-height`. This declaration will be ignored by all compliant CSS parsers. Remove it to avoid confusion; no functional replacement is needed since `line-height` inherits correctly without it.

**Structurally invalid preheader markup — `<tr>` outside `<table>`**
```html
<!--[if !mso 9]><!-->
<div style="mso-hide:all;">
  <tr>
    <td ...>
```
A `<tr>` is a direct child of a `<div>`, which is invalid in both HTML and XHTML. Browsers recover silently, but this can cause unpredictable rendering in older email clients. The preheader content should be wrapped in its own `<table role="presentation">`.
