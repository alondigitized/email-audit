# 25% off your entire order
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- OK so H&M has officially entered exhaustion territory in my inbox — this is the sixth email in five days if I'm counting right, and after the Summer Shop send yesterday and the Pride send before that and "The Refined Edit" before THAT, my brain kind of just sees the H&M logo and pre-calculates "promo." The subject line "25% off your entire order" did make me stop, ngl — a flat percentage off is the kind of thing I actually screenshot and send to my group chat. So I opened it.
- And then: Women. Men. Kids 2-8. Kids 9-14. Home. Beauty. Full department store layout. The whole family. I'm 24 and I do not have a 9-year-old. I do not need the split kids age bands taking up a third of my screen. This is a mass blast dressed up as a weekend deal and the targeting shows.
- The offer is genuinely good — 25% off $125 with free shipping is a real haul incentive. But the email doesn't give me any reason to care about WHAT I'm buying. Zara's stripes editorial from a few days ago actually made me want specific pieces. ASOS at least used the word "trending." This email just says: here's money off everything, good luck. I'd click Women out of habit. I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- **Hero offer lands immediately** — "25% OFF $125 + FREE SHIPPING" is the biggest text on the screen, no hunting required.
- **"WEEKEND DEAL" framing** adds a time signal even without a hard deadline — it reads like it won't survive to Monday.
- **The Women's model is on-point** — olive cargo-ish pants + white tank is literally everywhere on my FYP right now; it doesn't feel like a stock image from 2022.
- **Clean render** — everything loaded, no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds up.
- **H&M is a real brand I actually shop** — no credibility tax to pay before I trust the offer.

## What's weak

- **Kids 2-8 and Kids 9-14 split** takes up huge real estate and signals immediately that this email wasn't built for me.
- **No specific product or trend hook** — this is a category grid, not a "you need to see this before it sells out" moment.
- **Zero trend language** — "so many new styles to save on" is generic copy that could have been written in 2019. Nothing about what's first, what's new, what's moving.
- **$125 minimum is easy to miss** — it's in the hero subtext ("25% OFF $125+") but written small enough that I'd probably get to checkout confused.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — six H&M emails in five days. The last send that landed well was the Summer Shop yesterday; this one feels like it's eating that goodwill.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the list.** A 24-year-old female should never see Kids 2-8 in her weekend deal email. A Women-only version with three or four actual product images would perform better than this six-department grid.
- 2. **Set a hard deadline.** "Weekend Deal" is soft. "Ends Sunday 11:59 PM" creates actual FOMO. Right now I can tell myself I'll come back later — and I won't.
- 3. **Lead with one trend-forward hero product.** Zara's stripes editorial gave me a visual I wanted. ASOS's trending section at least named specific categories. H&M's own Summer Shop send yesterday was more editorial than this — go back to that playbook.
- 4. **Own the $125 threshold.** Either surface it as a feature ("Big haul? 25% off everything over $125") or tighten the minimum so it doesn't feel like a bait-and-switch at checkout.
- 5. **Cool the send frequency.** After five emails in four days, a sixth dilutes everything. The Summer Shop email would have hit harder as a standalone if I hadn't already been fatigued.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Fill your cart — 25% off + free shipping`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ends Sunday — women's, denim, tops, and more all included.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your big haul moment: new styles, 25% off, free shipping.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` for custom fonts will silently fail.**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is stripped by Gmail (web and app), Apple Mail iOS (partial), and Outlook all versions. Font will fall back to client default — no declared `font-family` fallback stack is visible in the provided CSS, meaning fallback behavior is undefined.

**No `lang` attribute on `<html>` element.**
```html
<html style="border:0;margin:0;outline:0;padding:0">
```
Missing `lang="en"` (or locale-appropriate value). Screen readers cannot determine language; some spam filters also flag this absence.

**`<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">` syntax error** — correct attribute is `content="telephone=no"`, but the tag is written as `content="telephone=no" name="format-detection"` with attributes reversed. While browsers/clients are tolerant of attribute order, this deviates from spec and could cause parse issues in strict clients.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unrendered merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `*|FNAME|*`) are visible in the provided source. Body content is truncated, so this cannot be fully confirmed — recommend a full-source scan.

### 5. Compliance

**JSON-LD `DiscountOffer` fields are unpopulated:**
```json
{
  "@type": "DiscountOffer",
  "description": "",
  "discountCode": "",
  "availabilityStarts": "2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00",
  "availabilityEnds": ""
}
```
- `discountCode: ""` — for an email whose subject line is a promotional offer, an empty discount code is a data integrity gap (may not be CAN-SPAM relevant but is a deliverability/Promotions tab signal issue).
- `availabilityStarts: "2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"` — **hardcoded stale date from 2018**. This is a template artifact that was never parameterized. Gmail's Promotions tab parsing uses this field; a 7-year-old start date could suppress or mis-rank the annotation.
- `availabilityEnds: ""` — empty; offer has no machine-readable expiry.

**JSON-LD `EmailMessage.subjectLine` is empty:**
```json
{"@type":"EmailMessage","subjectLine":"","@context":"http://schema.org/"}
```
Should match the email subject `"25% off your entire order"`. This prevents Gmail structured snippet rendering.

**Unsubscribe mechanism** — `.footer-unsubscribe` class is referenced in the responsive CSS, indicating a footer unsubscribe element exists. Cannot verify the actual link href or one-click List-Unsubscribe header from the truncated source. **Recommend confirming `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are present in the sending infrastructure** — required for bulk senders under Gmail/Yahoo 2024 requirements and CAN-SPAM.
