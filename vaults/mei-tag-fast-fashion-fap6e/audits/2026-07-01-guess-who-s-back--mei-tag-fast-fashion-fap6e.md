# Guess who's back?
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- Okay so ASOS just did the most disorienting thing possible — after five days of stacking sale discounts that had me genuinely excited (70% off two days ago!), they show up on July 1st going "Guess who's back?" and it's... restocked items? At full price? I had to re-read the sub-headline twice because my brain was looking for a sale number and couldn't find one. The subject reads like a pop culture tease and the email delivers... a back-in-stock module. That whiplash is real.
- The visuals are actually fine — four young women in solid summer looks, lavender slip dress especially catching my eye, models that look like people I follow on TikTok. The layout is clean, no bugs, renders well. But there is literally zero urgency or offer here, and after the sale cadence ASOS ran all last week, this feels like they forgot to attach the discount code.
- Mark as read. I'm not clicking through to shop full price when I know ASOS was at 70% off 48 hours ago. I'd maybe mention the slip dress to a friend in a voice note but I wouldn't forward this email.

## What's working

- **Models are on point for me** — young, diverse, the lavender slip and the red top/white pants both hit current aesthetics I'm actively tracking
- **Layout is clean** — hero headline → sub-copy → CTA → product grid flows naturally, no visual clutter
- **"SHOP BACK IN STOCK" CTA** is more specific than their usual "Discover More" and actually tells me what I'm clicking into
- **No render issues** — images load, text is legible, buttons are tappable, the neon green app download button is ugly but it's not broken

## What's weak

- **Zero price signal after a week of sale emails** — ASOS trained me to expect a discount number in every email and now there's nothing; the cognitive dissonance kills intent immediately
- **"Guess who's back?" tells me nothing** — compared to Zara's June 28 email "SALE | Today at 9 pm ET online" which gave me a time, a channel, and a category in one line, this subject is just vibes with no payload
- **No specific products called out** — just a grid of looks; I can't tell if "they're back" means a specific collab, a specific item I wishlisted, or just random restocks
- **The "DOWNLOAD THE APP" block in neon yellow-green** looks bolted on and breaks the neutral aesthetic the rest of the email has going
- **No urgency copy** — "back in stock" without "selling fast" or "limited quantities" removes the only natural urgency this concept had

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with scarcity** — "Back in stock — and going fast" with even a soft countdown or "limited restock" badge would activate the FOMO that makes back-in-stock emails actually work
- 2. **Name at least one item** — "Your lavender slip is back" or "The red linen set everyone wanted" turns a generic browse into a targeted reason to click; H&M's recent "Your next great outfit is here" email failed for exactly this same generic reason
- 3. **Acknowledge the sale pivot** — even one line like "These are from outside the sale — and they're worth it" validates that you know I'm in sale mode and gives me permission to care about full price
- 4. **Kill or relocate the app download block** — it reads as a separate campaign stuck inside this one, dilutes the message
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Your wishlist just got restocked`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `The slip dress is back (finally)`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Sold-out faves are back — and these go fast`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Limited restock just dropped. Don't sleep.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`prefers-reduced-motion` missing.**
The `@keyframes ticker` animation and `@supports` block have no `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` override. Violates WCAG 2.1 SC 2.3.3 (Animation from Interactions). Fix:
```css
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  .ticker { animation: none !important; }
  .fallback { display: block !important; }
}
```

**Mobile breakpoint too narrow.**
`@media screen and (max-width:460px)` leaves viewports 461–479px on the desktop layout. Standard threshold is `480px` (or `600px` for simpler stacks).

**`FuturaPT-Heavy` declared `font-weight: 600`.**
"Heavy" conventionally maps to 800–900. Declaring it at 600 (semi-bold) means weight-based CSS synthesis may render lighter than intended. Should be `font-weight: 800`.

**SVG font format declarations are dead code.**
Both `@font-face` blocks include `url("…FuturaPT-Book.svg") format("svg")`. SVG fonts lost browser support ~2016. No functional impact but should be pruned.

**Image alt text** — cannot audit; HTML truncated before `<body>` content.

The legacy `http-equiv` form is unnecessary when `<meta charset>` is present. Remove the former.
