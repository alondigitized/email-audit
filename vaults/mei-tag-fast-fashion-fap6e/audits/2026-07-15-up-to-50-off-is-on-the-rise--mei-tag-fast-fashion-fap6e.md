# Up to 50% off is on the rise
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- H&M has been in my inbox every single day this week — and I mean *every day*. July 11, 12, 13, 14, and now today is the fifth consecutive send. So when I saw "Up to 50% off is on the rise" drop in, my thumb was already hovering on archive before I even opened it. The subject is doing something vaguely clever with "on the rise" — like the discount is a trend? — but honestly it just reads confused. A discount doesn't rise. Trends rise. Those are different things and this email doesn't know what it wants to be.
- Inside it's the standard H&M summer sale template: big bold percentage hero, five category nav buttons, lifestyle shots, dense fine print. Then below the scroll there's a member giveaway — a $250 gift card back-to-school bundle — which is actually the most interesting thing in here. But I had to *scroll* to find it. They buried the differentiated piece and led with a generic offer I've already seen three times this week. The July 13 "The art of keeping it simple" email was stunning — editorial, confident, no discount desperation. This is the hangover after that.
- I would mark this as read. Not clicking, definitely not forwarding. If ASOS had a Rina Lipa collab email hitting the same day, this doesn't even compete.

## What's working

- **Hero offer is instant and unambiguous** — "UP TO 50% OFF ALMOST EVERYTHING" in giant bold type does exactly one thing right: you know the sale on first glance
- **"Peak summer savings" framing** is seasonally accurate and at least not recycling the exact same 70% off language from the July 11 flash-sale email
- **Member giveaway section lands well visually** — the "MEMBER GIVEAWAY" label, the $250 gift card callout, clean SIGN IN / JOIN NOW buttons all look sharp and are the one genuinely differentiated moment in this send
- **No render bugs** — email loaded clean, no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum

## What's weak

- **BABY and HOME in the category nav** — I'm 24, I do not have a baby, I do not want a throw pillow, and seeing those right next to WOMEN screams mass blast with zero segmentation; Zara Pre-Owned felt more personally curated than this
- **"Almost everything" hedges the hero offer** — "up to 50% off ALMOST everything" kills the excitement before it starts; own the sale or don't
- **"On the rise" subject line is muddled** — it tries to borrow trend language but discount percentages don't "rise," trends do; the phrasing signals a brand that wants to sound Gen Z without actually being Gen Z
- **Member giveaway is buried** — the only thing that separates this from the previous four H&M emails this week is below the fold and requires a scroll to find
- **No trend hook anywhere** — as someone who tabs between Shein, Zara, and ASOS comparing who dropped the trend first, there is nothing in this email that tells me what's *trending right now*; it's just "stuff is cheaper"

## Recommendations

- 1. **Flip the structure — lead with the giveaway** — "Member access: win $250 + shop 50% off" as the hero makes me feel like a subscriber who gets something, not just another address on a list
- 2. **Cut BABY and HOME from the category nav for this segment** — five categories for a trend-chaser is noise; WOMEN + maybe BEAUTY is plenty; ASOS would have segmented this already
- 3. **Rewrite the subject to actually mean something:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `50% off peak summer — shop it before it's gone`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Member giveaway + 50% off drops today`
- 4. **Fix the preheader so it adds information, not body text overflow:**
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Win a $250 gift card + dress the season for half price`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The back-to-school bundle giveaway is live — plus half off everything`
- 5. **Give me a trend anchor** — one sentence like "trending now: linen sets, wide-leg denim, and Y2K accessories — all in the sale" would make this email actually useful for how I shop

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` for web fonts — not supported in most clients**

```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```

`@import` inside `<style>` is stripped by Gmail, Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo Mail. Fonts will silently fall back to system fonts in those clients. This is expected behavior in email HTML but worth logging — if brand typography is critical, web-safe fallback stacks should be validated.

**Duplicate CSS rules in mobile media query**

Four rule blocks are duplicated verbatim inside `@media (max-width:699px)`:

```css
.cta-link-mobile-border-ns{border:1px solid #000!important}
.cta-link-mobile,.set_font_interactive_standalone_link_default{line-height:17px!important;font-size:14px!important}
.flexible-elements-secondary-text-block-headline,...{width:327px!important}
.flexible-elements-secondary-text-block-headline-text{font-size:24px!important;line-height:28px!important}
```

No rendering impact (last rule wins and they're identical), but adds dead weight to the style block.

**Alt text, image counts, dark mode** — cannot audit; HTML truncated before `<body>` content.
