# Up to 30% extra off Sale
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-04**
## Executive summary

- Okay so ASOS just walked back into sale territory three days after the "Guess who's back?" email made me think the whole thing was wrapping up. Now it's 30% extra off — on top of the 70% that's been running since June 29 — with a code (MORE) and a Monday 9am cutoff. The math is genuinely stacked if you dig into it. And the email itself is clean: hot pink hero, no broken images, a grid of models wearing actual summer trends I'd want right now.
- But I've gotten five sale emails from ASOS in nine days. Five. The June 28 email was "Up to 25% extra off all Sale." The June 29 email escalated to "now up to 70% off." Then July 1 was "Guess who's back?" — which I took as the sale closing — and now July 4th is *more* extra percent off. At this point the cadence isn't building urgency, it's building numbness. I feel like ASOS is the store that keeps texting you "last chance" every other day until you block the number.
- That said — I'd click SHOP WOMEN'S. The patterned wide-leg pants on the bottom-left model are exactly what I've been seeing all over my For You page, and knowing they're on a stacked sale closes the loop. I would not forward this. My friends have definitely already gotten it in their own inboxes.

## What's working

- The "UP TO 30% EXTRA OFF SALE" hero in that magenta reads instantly — zero hunting required, first second of open
- Code "MORE" paired with "Ends Monday 9am" is the full decision package: what to do + when it expires, no ambiguity
- The model grid earns its space — patterned wide-leg pants, linen coordinates, Birkenstock-adjacent sandals; these are actual TikTok-trending shapes right now, not clearance shelf filler
- "YOUR STYLE, FOR LESS" in the hot-pink band repositions "sale" as editorial taste, not desperation — that's the move Zara wishes it could make
- SHOP WOMEN'S / SHOP MEN'S split is efficient; I'm not clicking through a gender-ambiguous homepage to find my section

## What's weak

- Five sale emails in nine days has completely collapsed the urgency signal — this "extra off" doesn't feel like a reward, it feels like haggling
- After July 1's "Guess who's back?" strongly implied the sale was ending, relaunching another extra-off less than 72 hours later is disorienting; the brand story is broken
- Zero trend language — as someone who specifically tracks which retailer ships the trend first, I can't tell if any of this is new-in or old inventory someone needs to move
- The hero subtext (the fine print under the headline) is too small to read at phone scale — the 70% + 30% stack math is the actual hook and it's invisible without pinch-zooming
- Bershka and Pull&Bear got their own headline email on June 25 and drove real excitement; here they're just tiny logos in the footer with no editorial context

## Recommendations

- 1. **Reset the cadence visually** — same percentage sale, new creative frame; show one hero trend (the patterned wide-leg pants from the grid) with a "ASOS had this first" callout; Zara's "Woman | Discover what's new" emails at least feel like curation even when they're repetitive
- 2. **Surface the stack math in the hero** — "Already up to 70% off. Now take 30% more. Code: MORE." should be readable at arm's length, not hidden in 10px subtext
- 3. **Give the Bershka/Pull&Bear logos actual copy** — June 25's email showed ASOS can own a trend story around Inditex brands; a single line like "Bershka is in the sale" in the grid would earn clicks from people who didn't open that email
- 4. **One trend anchor** — even "this week's most-saved styles" above the grid signals curation vs. clearance; H&M's July 4th emails are failing at this exact beat too, ASOS could own it
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 30% extra moment ends Monday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The wide-legs + linen sets everyone's buying — 30% extra off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stack on top of 70% off. Code: MORE. Ends Monday 9am.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The trends you've been watching just got cheaper.`
