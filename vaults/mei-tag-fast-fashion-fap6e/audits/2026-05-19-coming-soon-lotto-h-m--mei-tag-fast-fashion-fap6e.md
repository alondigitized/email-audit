# Coming soon: LOTTO H&M
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- Okay so H&M keeps serving me soccer content and I am genuinely divided. The Lotto collab is *legitimately* interesting — Lotto is having a moment, I've seen the logo on vintage pieces all over TikTok, and the heritage angle could be so good. But then I open this email and it's. All. Men. Every single model. From the hero image to both product cards to the third editorial shot — it's a dude holding a soccer ball, two dudes in jerseys, a dude with a red bag. I'm staring at a menswear lookbook that wandered into my inbox by mistake.
- This is the third soccer-coded send in five days — May 15 was "soccer-inspired styles are goals," May 18 was the @nassnyc collab, now this. I actually liked both of those because they felt like they were gesturing at me. This one doesn't. It feels like H&M signed the Lotto deal and then handed the brief to the menswear team without telling the women's team anything.
- I would not click. I might mark-as-read and look up the collab separately later on TikTok, where I'll probably find the women's pieces exist. I would not forward this to a friend — I'd send them a TikTok instead.

## What's working

- **The collab itself is on-trend.** Lotto logos are circulating on vintage resale and the soccer-girl aesthetic is still very much alive on my FYP. The brand choice is smart.
- **"Launching May 21" is specific and creates real urgency** — a date lands better than "limited time only." I know exactly when to check back.
- **The collaboration lockup (© Lotto H&M) is clean** — the dual-logo treatment reads premium and I know exactly what I'm looking at.
- **"VIEW THE COLLECTION" is a clear, unambiguous CTA** — no "discover" or "explore more" vagueness here.
- **No render bugs.** Layout is clean, images load, the hierarchy flows top-to-bottom without breakage.

## What's weak

- **Every single model is male.** For a "coming soon" teaser at a brand I shop as a woman, this is the single biggest miss. ASOS's "What's hot this summer" email at least showed a range of bodies and genders. This doesn't even try.
- **Two identical product cards back-to-back.** Both read "LOOSE-FIT SOCCER JERSEY / $44.99 / New Arrival" — same label, only the colorway differs. That's lazy editing and it makes the product feel thin.
- **Third soccer-themed send in five days is too much.** The cadence is beating a single trend into the ground. May 15 + May 18 + May 19 = overexposure. My thumb is getting tired.
- **No discount, no promo, no hook beyond "it exists."** A "coming soon" email needs something to make me care beyond the announcement — early access sign-up, members-first preview, anything. Right now it's just "FYI a thing is happening."
- **"50 years of soccer heritage" copy reads masculine and archival** — which is fine for a campaign, but it doesn't invite me in as a 24-year-old woman who just wants the cute jersey.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with a female model or at minimum a mixed cast.** This is the single change that would most lift this email for me. Even one image swap makes it feel like I'm invited.
- 2. **Add an early-access or waitlist hook.** "Be the first to shop on May 21 — save your spot" with a single input field would give me a reason to act *today* instead of closing the tab.
- 3. **Cut one of the duplicate jersey cards.** Show the two colorways in a side-by-side grid instead of stacking two near-identical product tiles — it reads richer and takes up less scroll.
- 4. **Differentiate this send from the May 18 @nassnyc email.** That one was more editorial and social-native. This one could own the "collab heritage" angle harder with one piece of brand story content — a quick reel screengrab, a "why Lotto" callout — rather than just product tiles.
- 5. **Shorten the body copy block.** "H&M's latest collaboration brings soccer culture to life..." is five lines of marketing text that nobody reads. Cut it to two punchy sentences max.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Lotto x H&M drops May 21 — here's your first look`

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Soccer heritage meets H&M — shop the full collection Thursday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Loose jerseys, retro branding, limited run — May 21 in stores and online`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading will fail in Outlook (all versions) and some webmail clients.**

```html
<style>
  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
</style>
```

`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is not supported by Outlook (Word rendering engine) and is blocked by several webmail clients that strip or sandbox `<style>` tags. Fallback system fonts should be explicitly declared in every font-stack rule (e.g., `font-family: 'NorthStar', Arial, sans-serif`). If the CSS already does this, the visual degradation is acceptable — but it should be confirmed.

**`<script>` tag in `<head>`** — the `application/ld+json` block is schema markup, not executable JS, so it is safe. However, aggressive spam filters can flag `<script>` presence regardless of type. Low risk but worth noting.

**Alt text** — truncated source prevents full audit. Must verify all `<img>` tags carry meaningful `alt` attributes (not empty strings) per accessibility and deliverability best practices.
