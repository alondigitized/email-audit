# Summer-to-fall favorites from $9.99
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- Day seven of H&M being in my inbox and I genuinely can't tell what day it is anymore. Yesterday's TGIF send led with 50% off as its whole hero moment, and now today we're back to... also 50% off sitewide, just repackaged with a "summer-to-fall" subject line. The $9.99 anchor in the subject actually made me pause — I thought we were getting a flash deal on a specific item, maybe a specific dress or a capsule drop. Then I opened it and the hero is screaming "PEAK-SUMMER SAVINGS, UP TO 50% OFF SITEWIDE," which is the same offer I've already seen multiple times this week. The subject and the email aren't even telling the same story.
- What's really losing me is the email is trying to dress itself as a curation ("favorites") but it's basically H&M's entire catalog in one scroll — women, men, dresses, tees, and then a full third of the email is GIRLS 2-8Y and BOYS 2-8Y buttons. I am 24 and childless. Zara's "This week's selection" was vague and got dragged in my head for it, but at least it wasn't suggesting I shop for a second-grader. This is giving: summer clearance for the whole family, not summer-to-fall edit for me.
- I'd skim it, maybe drift into the women's section, but this is a dead-eyed click, not an excited one. Would not forward.

## What's working

- The **$9.99 price anchor in the subject** is genuinely the strongest thing here — it's specific, it's scannable, it actually made me curious before I opened.
- **"UP TO 50% OFF SITEWIDE"** in the hero is clear and unambiguous — no mystery about whether there's a real offer.
- The **"ONE-&-DONE DRESSES"** section copy is cute and the model styling leans dressy-casual which feels right for late summer.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no layout explosions, no lorem ipsum. After the ASOS comma merge-tag disaster I'm relieved.

## What's weak

- The subject says "from $9.99" but the email pivots to "UP TO 50% OFF SITEWIDE" — those are two completely different value propositions and neither pays off the other. One's a price floor, one's a discount ceiling. Pick one.
- **Kids content eats a third of this email** — GIRLS 2-8Y, BOYS 2-8Y, GIRLS 8-14Y, BOYS 8-14Y. If H&M has any click data on me, they know I have never once clicked a kids category button. This is a batch-and-blast send wearing a curation disguise.
- The **preheader is "NEED AN ITEM SOONER? BUY ONLINE, PICK UP IN-STORE"** — that's operational noise. It doesn't build on the subject at all and it's the first thing I'd see next to the subject line in my inbox.
- This is the **same 50% off offer I saw yesterday** in the TGIF email, and the day before that in the "Up to 50% off is on the rise" send. H&M has gone to the 50% off well so many times this week that it genuinely doesn't feel like a deal anymore.
- **CTAs are just category labels** — "WOMEN," "MEN," "GIRLS 2-8Y" — there's no pull, no verb, no urgency. ASOS's "Extra 30% off selected Sale styles" at least gave me a code (XTRA30) and a specific action. This is just... a nav bar.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment out the kids content.** If I've never touched a kids category, remove it from my version of this send. ASOS has done better segmenting — even their mid-week sale emails keep it womenswear-forward for me. A 24-year-old getting a back-to-school family grid is a quick disengagement spiral.
- 2. **Commit to the $9.99 angle.** If the subject promises it, show me a specific item at that price in the hero — a dress, a tee, a bag. Right now the hero says 50% off but the subject says $9.99 and they never meet.
- 3. **Fix the preheader.** The in-store pickup line is doing nothing. It needs to carry the "summer-to-fall" edit angle or tease a specific category.
- 4. **Rewrite the CTAs with verbs.** "WOMEN" is not a CTA. "Shop the $9.99 edit" or "See what's trending now" — give me a reason to tap, not just a label.
- 5. **Break the 50% off loop.** Yesterday was 50% off. Today is 50% off. If tomorrow is also 50% off, I'm unsubscribing or just filtering. A free-shipping threshold or a flash window on a specific category would do something different.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` for web fonts — high impact**

```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```

`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is stripped or ignored by Outlook (all versions), Samsung Mail, and several Android clients. No fallback font stack is declared in the import rule itself, so these clients will fall back to the browser/client default rather than a controlled system font.

**`@media screen yahoo` pseudo-query**

```css
@media screen yahoo { .show-webkit-table { display: none; } }
```

This is a non-standard Yahoo Mail targeting hack. It has no effect in any standards-compliant renderer and only works in specific older Yahoo Mail versions. If this rule is load-bearing for layout, it should be verified against current Yahoo Mail behavior.

**Alt text and table structure** — cannot audit without full HTML.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

The JSON-LD block contains multiple unfilled template slots:

```json
{"@type":"EmailMessage","subjectLine":"","@context":"http://schema.org/"}
```
→ `subjectLine` is empty. Should be `"Summer-to-fall favorites from $9.99"` to match the envelope subject.

```json
{
  "availabilityEnds":"",
  "discountCode":"",
  "@type":"DiscountOffer",
  "description":"",
  "availabilityStarts":"2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"
}
```
→ `availabilityEnds`, `discountCode`, and `description` are empty strings — unfilled placeholders.
→ `availabilityStarts` is hardcoded to **2018-12-31**, roughly 7.5 years in the past. This is a stale template default that was never updated for this send.

```json
{"image":"","@type":"PromotionCard","promotionCardLayout":"SingleImageLayout","url":""}
```
→ Both `image` and `url` on `PromotionCard` are empty.

**Net effect:** Gmail's Promotions Tab annotation system reads this JSON-LD to render deal cards and images in the tab view. With empty `subjectLine`, `image`, `url`, and a past `availabilityStarts`, no annotation will render — the email appears as a plain entry with no deal card.
