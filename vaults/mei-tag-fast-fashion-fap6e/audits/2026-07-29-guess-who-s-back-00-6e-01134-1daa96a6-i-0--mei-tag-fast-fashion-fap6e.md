# Guess who's back?
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-29**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "restock" energy but zero "restock" info — no product names, no sizes, no "these sold out in 6 hours" urgency copy, just six pretty photos and a "SHOP NOW" button. My gut reaction is: this is a good idea (FOMO on sold-out faves is genuinely a strong hook for me) executed like a lookbook, not a restock alert. Compare this to ASOS's own "Fringe is in" send from 7/22 — that one leaned all the way into editorial mode with zero discount language and it worked because the styling did the talking. This one is trying to be both "urgent restock" and "editorial mood board" and lands in the middle, doing neither fully.
- The models are diverse and the pieces themselves are actually covetable — the orange sequin set and that yellow ruched dress are giving main character energy — but I have literally no idea what any of this costs, what sizes are back, or how fast it'll sell out again. Would I click? Probably, out of curiosity, since I do like the pieces shown. Would I forward to a friend? Only if I already recognized one of the exact items from before, which I can't confirm here since nothing's labeled.

## What's working

- The hook itself — "sold-out faves are back" — is a genuinely good trigger for someone like me who missed drops the first time.
- Model diversity across the six photos is solid, different skin tones and hair textures, feels like ASOS's usual range.
- The orange sequin dress and pink polka-dot halter are strong hero-worthy pieces, exactly the kind of loud, trend-forward pick I'd screenshot for a group chat.
- Clean grid layout, no render bugs, everything loads and lines up fine on mobile.

## What's weak

- Zero product names, prices, or "sold out in X hours" specificity — this is the laziest version of a restock email; H&M's office-core send at least named products even if the vibe was off.
- No urgency or scarcity messaging ("back for 48 hours," "these won't last twice") despite the whole premise being about scarcity — huge missed beat.
- Six unlabeled product shots means I can't tell which items click to what, so I'd have to guess-tap and hope.
- "SHOP OUR TOP BRANDS" section with Mango, Bershka, Topshop feels like a bolted-on afterthought that dilutes the restock focus — Zara's cluttered "SRPLS" send had the same problem of cramming too much into one email.

## Recommendations

- 1. Label every product tile with name + price — this is the single biggest miss. A restock email that hides prices kills the impulse-buy moment entirely.
- 2. Add real urgency copy under the hero — "back for a limited time" or a countdown, since scarcity is literally the entire premise of this email.
- 3. Cut the "Shop Our Top Brands" logo row — it's not what I opened this email for and just adds scroll fatigue before I even get to the app download CTA.
- 4. Add a tiny social-proof line under 1-2 hero pieces ("sold out in 48hrs last time") — ASOS has the data, use it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your sold-out faves are back (for now)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `They sold out. Now they're not.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The pieces you missed — restocked, not reordered twice`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sizes are limited. Again.`
