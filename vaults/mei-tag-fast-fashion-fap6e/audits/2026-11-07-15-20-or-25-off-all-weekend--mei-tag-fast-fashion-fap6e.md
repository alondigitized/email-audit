# 15%, 20% or 25% off all weekend
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-07**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction is that this is the third "spend more, save more" email from H&M in four days. On 11/06 it was "Up to 25% off your order starts now," and the 11/04 flash sale had the same 25% hook. By Saturday I'm numb to it. The offer is clear: 15% off $75, 20% off $100, 25% off $125, plus free shipping. But the hero is a little boy in a red "Jolly Time" sweater. I'm 24 and I do my three-tab compare every Saturday. This didn't make me feel like H&M had the trend first. It felt like a family holiday catalog.
- The rest is a grid of Women, Men, Baby, Kids, Home and Sport, with no product names, no prices and nothing I can tell is trending. Zara at least shows me a look, even if it keeps sending menswear. ASOS's Lioness drop gave me a whole moody vibe I could screenshot and send to the group chat. This is a coupon with pictures. I'd skim it, maybe tap Shop Now if I needed basics, and I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- The tiered offer is huge and bold in the hero, so I get it in two seconds.
- Free shipping is stacked right under the tiers, which is a real perk and not hidden.
- The "all weekend" deadline matches the footer's 11/08 end date, so the urgency is believable.
- The red sweater and red hoodie give the email a cohesive holiday color story that's easy to scan.
- Two clear buttons, Shop Now and Find a Store, sit right under the offer.

## What's weak

- The hero is a kid in a sweater. I'm not buying kids' clothes, so the biggest image has nothing to do with me.
- There's zero trend signal: no "what's trending," no TikTok-style look, no new drop. Shein and Zara would at least frame something as new.
- The grid labels (Women, Men, Baby, Kids, Home, Sport) are generic. Nothing is linked to a specific product or price, so there's no reason to tap one over another.
- The black banner at the top, "Become a member for exclusive Black Friday access + 10% off," competes with the main offer. The same banner was in the 11/04 email, so it's stale.
- The "Shop by" block repeats the same categories again, which makes the email feel padded.
- The fine print is a wall of tiny text, and the spend thresholds are before-tax with lots of exclusions.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with women's trend pieces, like a styled outfit with a price, instead of a kid's sweater. Keep the tier math in the headline.
- 2. Cut the duplicate "Shop by" grid and use that space for 3-4 specific products with prices.
- 3. Drop the member banner or replace it with something new, since I've seen it all week.
- 4. Add a countdown, like "Ends Sunday 11:59pm," to make the deadline feel real.
- 5. Space out sends. Three offer emails in four days trains me to wait for the next one.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your holiday fit, up to 25% off till Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Gifts + going-out fits count toward your total. Ends 11/08.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The tiers are live all weekend. Free shipping included.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email renders from a table-based template with responsive CSS, but the automated QA run cannot confirm that any tracked link resolves. All 12 probed links returned 403, and the unsubscribe headers and authentication results are missing from the capture. Most images have no alt text, and the JSON-LD block is empty.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **All 12 probed links returned 403** (16% pass rate). They are all on `t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=h78ecf624-…`: "Shop now" (×4), "Find a store" (×2), the "H&M" logo, four image links and "Unsubscribe".
  - Every failure comes from the same redirector host, and the 403 is uniform. That points to bot or WAF blocking of the probe, not 12 separate broken links.
  - I can't confirm real-user breakage from this data, and the final destinations were never resolved.
  - 56 links were skipped. Footer, social and utility links were not verified.
- **Duplicate tracked links per module.** Each product has a separate 1400px and 750px image link (for example `…,55138b91,f8b35fca` and `…,55138b91,f8b35fcb`). The source uses `is-hidden-mobile` and `is-hidden-desktop` classes to swap them, so this is a desktop/mobile variant pattern. Click counts will split across the two link IDs unless they are aggregated.
- **Zero-width characters in link text.** Two "Shop now" links (`…f8b35faa`, `…f8b35fac`) have link text of `"‌  ﻿Shop now"`, which carries ZWNJ and BOM characters. These can make link text read oddly in assistive tech and in text-based link matching.
- **Opaque tracking only.** There are no direct destination URLs in the captured links.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on 16 of the images flagged by QA.**
  - Hero and module images are `cq5dam.web.1400.1400.jpeg` and `cq5dam.web.750.750.jpeg`, served from `image.hm.com/content/dam/hm/regional-local-activities/ame/2026-ame/wk-45/…`.
  - Each of these is also wrapped in a link, so the link's accessible name falls back to the filename. This shows as `[image: cq5dam.web.1400.1400.jpeg]` in the link text.
  - The tracking pixel/image `t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=…,1` is also flagged. A decorative or tracking image should carry `alt=""` explicitly.
- **Invalid or non-standard CSS.**
  - `.is-hidden-desktop{… line-height:auto!important …}` is invalid, because `auto` is not a valid `line-height` value.
  - `@media screen yahoo { … }` is a malformed media query and will be ignored in most clients.
- **Web fonts via `@import`.** The `<style>` block imports `fabric.hmgroup.com/…/fonts.css` and `s1-cdn.hm.com/…/oces.css`. Gmail and several other clients drop `@import`, so those clients fall back to system fonts. This is acceptable only if the fallback stack is defined.
- **The source is truncated.** I can't evaluate the font-stack fallbacks, `role="presentation"` on layout tables, `lang` on `<html>`, or `<title>`.
