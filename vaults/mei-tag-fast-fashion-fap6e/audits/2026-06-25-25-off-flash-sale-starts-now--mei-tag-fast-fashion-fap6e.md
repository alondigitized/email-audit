# 25% off flash sale starts now
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- Okay so H&M hit my inbox again — I've genuinely lost count but this has to be somewhere around the ninth or tenth email from them in a single week, and my thumb was already pre-programmed to swipe left before I even read it. What stopped me was "flash sale starts now" — there's a hard deadline baked in (12AM ET) and I could feel the fomo even through the fatigue.
- Opening the email, the **25% OFF UNTIL 12AM ET** lands fast and clean — no confusion about what's happening. But then I scroll and I hit Baby, Kids, Home, Beauty all in a grid. I'm 24, I rent a studio, I do not need children's clothes in my flash sale email. That's not even aspirational for me — it just signals that H&M blasted their entire list without a second thought about who I actually am.
- I'd probably click into Women if I was already on my phone, but I wouldn't forward this to anyone — I'd be too embarrassed to send another H&M email after the seven they got from me this week. Mark as read, maybe revisit at lunch.

## What's working

- The **25% OFF UNTIL 12AM ET** typographic hierarchy is clean — my eye landed on the number before anything else, which is literally the only job this hero section has
- **"Flash sale starts now"** in the subject is a genuine step up from the soft "summer basics" energy from June 22 — there's consequence here
- The **30-character subject line** sits perfectly on mobile, no truncation, no spam triggers
- The **"up to 60% off sale price"** callout in the subtext is a bonus signal — the markdown goes deeper than just the 25%, which makes me more curious about the sale rack
- H&M's editorial grid aesthetic is still clean and consistent — this doesn't feel like a discount bin, which matters

## What's weak

- **Baby, Kids, Home, Beauty** in a flash sale email aimed at me is dilution — a women's-only send would've felt targeted instead of lazy
- Zero trend specificity — "new summer styles" tells me nothing about whether H&M is the one setting the summer look or just chasing it, which is the exact thing I compare brands on
- **Cadence is cooked** — this is the ninth or tenth H&M send in a week; even a hard-deadline flash sale feels routine at this point
- The **"SHOP BY" lifestyle section** is pure filler — three more editorial shots after the product grid adds zero information
- No social proof, no TikTok signal, no "trending now" badge — compare this to how ASOS literally name-dropped Bershka and Pull&Bear in their *subject line* to signal trend authority

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this send** — strip Baby, Kids, Home for the 18-30 female segment; if I never buy kids' clothes, showing me a kids photo grid is just noise that slows my path to checkout
- 2. **Name a trend in the hero copy** — "Barrel leg, crochet, micro-sets — 25% off until midnight" beats "new summer styles" in every A/B test imaginable
- 3. **Add one specific trend product** with a TikTok or "trending" signal — ASOS named Bershka and Pull&Bear in their subject and I genuinely clicked. H&M needs that energy somewhere above the fold inside the email
- 4. **Add a countdown timer widget** — the 12AM deadline is doing real work as text, but a live countdown would actually stress me out (in a good way)
- 5. **Pull back the cadence** — even skipping one send this week would've made this flash sale land as an event instead of another inbox item to dismiss

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Web font `@import` — will silently fail in most clients:**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is stripped by Gmail (desktop and mobile), iOS Mail, and Outlook. The email will fall back to system fonts in the majority of clients. This is expected for email but worth confirming fallback stacks are defined inline on text elements.

**Duplicate CSS declarations in `@media (max-width:699px)` block:**
The following selectors appear twice in the mobile query, which is harmless but indicates a template generation defect:
- `.department-title` (font-size/line-height re-declared)
- `.department-link` (font-size/line-height re-declared)
- `.cta-link-mobile-border-ns` + `.set_font_interactive_standalone_link_default` (two identical pairs)
