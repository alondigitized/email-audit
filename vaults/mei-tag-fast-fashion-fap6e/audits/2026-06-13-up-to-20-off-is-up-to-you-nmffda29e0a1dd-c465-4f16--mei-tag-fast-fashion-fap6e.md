# Up to 20% off is up to you
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- H&M slides in on a Friday with "Up to 20% off is up to you" and the first thing I see when the email opens is a Father's Day banner. H&M — I have received three Father's Day emails from you already this month. June 9th was the breaking point for me, and here it is again, greeting me before the actual offer even registers. That's not how you make me feel like this email was built for me.
- When I finally get past the preheader injury, the hero is a tiered spend discount — 15% off $80, 20% off $100 — which, okay, but now I'm doing math? Compare this to ASOS's tennis heritage send last week, which just showed me what was trending and made the case for itself. That email had one cultural read and a clear point of view. This email is a math problem wrapped in three audience segments (women, men, kids) with a "WEEKND DEAL" pun that's trying harder than it should.
- The women's "Standouts" section is actually cute. The draped jersey top, the denim drawstring shorts — that's real summer energy, and the $9.99 tee is peak H&M value. But surrounded by men's linen shirts and a kids soccer set, it gets diluted fast. I'd probably scroll the women's grid, maybe click a product, but I'm not building a $100 cart to unlock 20% off. Mark as read.

## What's working

- The women's "Standouts" grid has a clean, editorial pull — the styling on the jersey top and denim shorts is summer-appropriate and looks like something I'd actually wear
- Prices are visible right on every product tile, which is exactly how I shop — I compare price-per-trend across three tabs and I need numbers up front
- "New Arrival" tags scattered throughout the grid signal first-mover opportunity, which matters to me more than the discount honestly
- The overall email renders clean — no broken images, no overlapping text, no phantom layout gaps (Zara's "Sartorial Reframed" email this month was more art-directed but had less product clarity than this)
- The summer color palette — pinks, whites, sandy neutrals — is genuinely seasonal and doesn't feel like it was copy-pasted from April

## What's weak

- The Father's Day banner is sitting at the very top of this email like an uninvited guest — H&M already sent me three Father's Day emails (June 5, 7, and 9) and I am not buying for a dad, I have never been buying for a dad
- The 15%/$80 or 20%/$100 tiered structure is a math problem, not an offer — I'm not calculating cart thresholds in my head on a Friday, ASOS would have just said "20% off, here's what's hot"
- Three audiences in one email (women, men, kids) means nothing feels curated for me — the June 11 day-to-night email from H&M scored 9/10 in my head precisely because it felt like it was made for one person
- "Up to 20% off is up to you" as a subject line is neither a pun nor a clear offer — it occupies this awkward middle ground where it's too cute to be direct and too vague to create urgency
- No dedicated CTA button under the hero offer — the category tabs (WOMEN / MEN / KIDS) are the only navigation, which means extra friction between reading the offer and acting on it

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the Father's Day preheader.** The email is a weekend deal on summer styles. Having a Father's Day gift banner as the first visible text in the email is misaligned for anyone who isn't shopping that category — which is most of the women's list.
- 2. **Simplify the offer to one number.** Lead with 20% off and let the spend threshold be the fine print, not the hero. "20% off your summer haul this weekend" is one idea that's easy to act on immediately.
- 3. **Add a CTA button under the offer block.** "Shop the deal" or "Shop women's" immediately under "15% OFF $80 OR 20% OFF $100" removes a navigation step and anchors intent before the product grid.
- 4. **Give the women's section its own send.** The June 11 day-to-night email worked because it had editorial focus. The men's and kids sections dilute this email for anyone on the women's segment.
- 5. **Commit to the Weeknd angle or cut it entirely.** "Maybe you wanna?" is clever if you know what you're referencing, but it's buried in small copy and feels like an easter egg that was supposed to be the hook. Either make it the subject line hook or replace it with something that lands for someone who didn't catch it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Weekend deal: 20% off your summer haul`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New arrivals + 20% off — this weekend only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $80 get 15%, spend $100 get 20%. New summer drops inside.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Women's new arrivals — draped tops, denim shorts, and a weekend deal.`
