# 0FR. PARIS and ZARA
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-03**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "0FR. Paris and Zara" — a Sydney Brae Thomas collab, pop-up at 8 Rue des Filles du Calvaire, curated vintage-meets-archive vibe. It LOOKS gorgeous, I'll give it that — flat-lay of trinkets, moody Seine editorial shots, the whole "we shot this on film in an actual Parisian apartment" energy. But by the third scroll of the same two models sitting by the river in slightly different jackets, I'm checking out. This is giving Zara's "Woman | This week's selection" energy all over again — pretty pictures, zero reason to actually tap anything.
- There's no price anywhere. No "shop now for X." Just a Paris pop-up address and dates I'll never make it to because I don't live there. It's an ad for a physical event dressed up as a shoppable email, and that's a mismatch for someone like me who's opening this on my phone during a commute wanting to know what's new and how much it costs.
- Would I click? Maybe once, out of curiosity for "Discover The Collection." Forward to a friend? Only if she's the one friend who's actually flying to Paris this month. Otherwise this is a scroll-and-archive.

## What's working

- The "Discover The Collection" button is at least visible and specific-ish, sitting right under the flat-lay hero.
- Model diversity is actually decent here — different ethnicities represented in the Seine shots, which Zara doesn't always nail (their "Woman" email last week was pretty homogenous).
- The collab framing (Sydney Brae Thomas x Zara, Est. 1996 nod) gives it a "this is exclusive, not just another drop" hook, similar to how ASOS's Lioness collab worked for me.
- Product grid at the bottom (Woman & Man, Kids, Home) at least breaks things into shoppable categories once you get there.

## What's weak

- Zero prices, zero percent off, zero urgency beyond a pop-up date range I can't act on remotely — this is the same complaint I had about "Woman | This week's selection."
- Way too long. I counted like 8+ full-bleed editorial shots before even hitting product tiles. My thumb gets tired before my wallet gets interested.
- The product grid at the bottom has no names, no prices, just tiny thumbnails — I have to tap blind to find out what anything costs.
- This reads like a Pinterest mood board, not an email trying to sell me something today.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price or "from $X" on at least the hero collection CTA — I'm not clicking into a mystery collab with no price anchor.
- 2. Cut the editorial shots in half. Three river shots of the same two models is one too many — H&M's Kelly Wearstler collab email hooked me in one hero shot, not eight.
- 3. Add product names/prices under the bottom grid thumbnails so I'm not tapping blind.
- 4. If the pop-up is Paris-only, segment this send — don't make a US/non-Paris subscriber scroll through store hours for a shop they can't visit.
