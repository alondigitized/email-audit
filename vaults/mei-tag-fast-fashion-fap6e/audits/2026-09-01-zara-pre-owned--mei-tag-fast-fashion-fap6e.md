# ZARA PRE-OWNED
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-01**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "ZARA PRE-OWNED" — this is actually kind of interesting? Resale/thrift energy is very much a thing my algorithm feeds me nonstop, so a big brand doing curated secondhand is on-trend. But then I scroll and it's... numbered product grids like a lookbook (001 TRENCH, 002 POLO TOP) with zero prices, zero condition notes, zero "how worn is this actually," and by the time I hit Skirts and the Man/Kids Collection tiles I've completely forgotten this was supposed to feel special or limited.
- This is the fourth Zara email in a week and the third with literally no dollar figure anywhere in it — following "Woman | This week's selection" (4/10, also zero prices) and "Into The Process IX" (3/10, menswear editorial). Zara has a pattern now: gorgeous typography, zero commerce info. Compare that to ASOS's Lioness drop, which at least had a clear "this is THE drop" moment even without heavy discounting — Zara Pre-Owned just lists items like a museum placard.
- Would I click? Maybe out of curiosity for the resale angle since I haven't seen H&M or ASOS run this exact play yet — that's a genuine white space. But would I forward to a friend? No, because there's nothing to actually show them — no price, no "this specific piece is $40," nothing to react to. Marking as read.

## What's working

- The "ZARA PRE-OWNED" hero in that orange block type actually pops — first thing my eye hits, no notes.
- Resale/vintage-adjacent concept is genuinely on-trend for my age group and nobody else in my inbox (H&M, ASOS) is doing a dedicated secondhand push right now, so points for being first here.
- "#zarapreowned" hashtag is a smart touch — feels like they want this to be a moment, not just a sale email.
- Model diversity in the hero and skirts section (the streetstyle shot crossing the crosswalk) feels current, not stock-photo stiff.

## What's weak

- Not one single price anywhere in this entire email — I genuinely can't tell if "pre-owned" means $15 or $150, and that's the whole point of resale shopping for me.
- No condition grading (like new/good/worn) which is THE thing that makes or breaks a secondhand purchase decision — Depop and thredUP live and die by this info.
- The numbered grid (001, 002, 003...) reads more like an inventory sheet than a curated drop — kills the "special find" feeling resale should have.
- Random detour into Skirts, then Man Collection and Kids Collection tiles — this started as a focused pre-owned concept and turned into a full catalog dump by the end, same problem as their "Woman" email.
- CTA is basically nonexistent — no "SHOP NOW" button anywhere I can see, just images to tap.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price or price range on every single product tile — this is the single biggest miss, resale shopping is priced-shopping and right now this email gives me nothing to react to.
- 2. Add condition tags (Like New / Gently Worn / Vintage) next to each item — makes it feel curated instead of catalog-dumped.
- 3. Cut the Man/Kids Collection tiles from this send — keep it laser-focused on the Pre-Owned Woman drop, spin the rest into its own email.
- 4. Add one clear CTA button ("SHOP PRE-OWNED") near the top instead of relying on tap-anywhere images.
- 5. Lead with the streetstyle crosswalk photo higher up — it's the most scroll-stopping image in the whole email and it's buried two-thirds down.
