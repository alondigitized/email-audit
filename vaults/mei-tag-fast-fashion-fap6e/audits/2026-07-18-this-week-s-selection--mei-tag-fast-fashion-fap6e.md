## What's weak

- **Wrong audience, again** — every single model is male, every category (Printed Edit, Man, Knits, Shirts, Faux Suede) is men's. This is the second Man send to my inbox in a week. ASOS would never do this — they segment by what I actually click.
- **"SEE" is the laziest CTA on earth** — Like... see what? Compared to H&M's TGIF email that at least said "Up to 50% off ALL of this," Zara's "SEE" tells me nothing about what I'm walking into.
- **No offer, zero urgency** — No discount, no deadline, no "free shipping ends Sunday," nothing. H&M gave me 50% off in a subject line. ASOS gave me XTRA30. Zara gave me vibes. Vibes don't ship.
- **"This week's selection" subject is gender-invisible** — At minimum, the July 11th Men's email said "Man" in the subject. I could dodge it. This one set me up to expect women's content and delivered men's fashion. That's a trust erosion moment.
- **App download CTA at the bottom feels tacked-on** — It's the last thing in the email and it's clearly a template footer that has nothing to do with the editorial. Doesn't land.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put gender in the subject line** — "Man | This week's selection" or just tag it so I know before I open. The July 11th email at least did me that courtesy. This is a regression.
- 2. **Replace "SEE" with something that earns the click** — "Shop the Printed Edit," "Explore New Greece," anything with a noun. "SEE" is a placeholder, not a CTA.
- 3. **Add one concrete offer or hook** — Even if it's not a discount, "exclusive early access" or "new in: 47 pieces" gives me a reason to click that "vibes" doesn't.
- 4. **Stop sending men's content to womenswear-active subscribers** — Behavioral segmentation fix. I have never once clicked a men's item. ASOS figures this out. Zara's sending the same blast to everyone.
- 5. **If this IS intentional cross-shopping (buy for a partner), say so** — "Shop for him" is a frame that at least makes sense for a women's audience. The current email just ignores that I exist.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

```html

```

Two distinct campaign codes are embedded in the same rendered HTML — the base variant (`Norte_man_weekly_18_07`) and the B-test variant (`testb_18_07`). If both are parsed by the ESP's analytics layer, click/open attribution will be split or double-counted. **External Google Fonts request:**

This request will be blocked or stripped in Gmail (web and app), Outlook (all versions), and Apple Mail in certain privacy modes. No explicit `font-family` fallback is declared in the stylesheet — the `a` rule and body rule lack a `font-family`, meaning rendering will silently fall back to client defaults rather than a specified fallback stack.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`user-scalable=no` + `maximum-scale=1.0` (confirmed):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width; initial-scale=1.0; maximum-scale=1.0; user-scalable=no;" />
```
This combination prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Violates WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). iOS 10+ ignores `user-scalable=no` in Safari, but it remains a compliance flag and affects Android WebView-based clients.

**Semicolon delimiters in viewport meta:** The `content` value uses semicolons (`;`) as separators instead of the spec-required commas. Widely tolerated in practice but technically malformed.

**Invalid DOM nesting in preheader block (confirmed):**
```html
<table ...>
  ...
  <!--[if !mso 9]><!-->
  <div style="mso-hide:all;">
    <tr>   ← div directly wraps tr, outside any tbody/thead
      <td ...>
```
A `<div>` wrapping `<tr>` directly is invalid in both HTML and XHTML. Most rendering engines will recover, but this can cause preheader content to be misplaced or duplicated in strict parsers (Litmus notes this pattern causes issues in older Yahoo Mail).

**`role="presentation"` on tables:** Correctly applied. No issue.
