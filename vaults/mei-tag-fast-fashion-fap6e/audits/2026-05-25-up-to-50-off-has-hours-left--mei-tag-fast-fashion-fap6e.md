# Up to 50% off has *hours* left
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- So H&M just landed in my inbox a SECOND time today. Already got "Last day: Up to 50% off your summer wardrobe" this morning, and now this — same sale, same energy, same creative, just with "FINAL HOURS" swapped in for "LAST DAY." At this point I have genuinely lost track of how many times they've told me this 50% off is ending. The denim email on the 23rd was actually good — specific product, real TikTok moment, felt like they knew what was trending. This? This is just a sitewide wallpaper with a countdown that stopped feeling real three emails ago.
- The hero image is a woman in a neutral cream summer set, clean and fine, but it's giving brand lookbook from 2021, not summer 2026 viral moment. Nothing about this email tells me what's trending, what I should actually buy, or why H&M found it first. ASOS at least tries to spark "inspo" — this is just "give us your money before midnight" without any editorial soul.
- Mark as read. Don't click. Definitely not forwarding it — I already sent someone the denim one two days ago and I'm not going back to the same well.

## What's working

- **"FINAL HOURS" badge** above the headline earns its spot — it's the one piece of new information in this whole send.
- **Free shipping callout** for H&M Members in the top banner is a clean, upfront loyalty touch — doesn't make you dig for it.
- **Visual hierarchy** does its job: FINAL HOURS → 50% OFF SITEWIDE → hero image → category grid, all reading top to bottom without confusion.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, no broken images, text is legible at this size.
- **Summer hero image** is on-season and the model styling (female, summer casual) matches my demographic at least broadly.

## What's weak

- **Second email of the day from the same brand with the same promo** — this is the trust-erodering move that makes me check if unsubscribing is worth the friction.
- **"UP TO 50% OFF SITEWIDE" with BABY and KIDS prominently in the category grid** — I'm 24 with no kids, why am I staring at a BABY button as one of six primary choices?
- **Zero trend language** — nothing about what's actually blowing up right now, no barrel jeans, no linen sets, no "TikTok made me buy it" energy. Zara Home sent me patio furniture this week and that was a miss; this is different but equally untargeted.
- **SHOP BY section with tiny circular thumbnails** — those images are so small they communicate nothing about actual product. ASOS at least shows me a full editorial shot in their category breaks.
- **No specific product linked** — it all routes to category-level pages. After seven days of the same sale, I need a reason to click, not a map of the website.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the second send entirely or make it a genuinely different angle.** If you already sent "Last day" this morning, the evening send has to earn its spot — a specific "here are the 10 pieces flying out the door" edit would do it. Copy-pasting with "FINAL HOURS" doesn't.
- 2. **Swap the hero for a trending product or outfit moment**, not a neutral lookbook shot. Name the trend: "The linen coord everyone's screen-recording right now — final hours."
- 3. **Collapse BABY, KIDS, HOME, BEAUTY** into a single "More categories" link if you're emailing a fashion-forward solo shopper segment. Six equal-weight CTAs for wildly different audiences is unfocused.
- 4. **Give me one specific product in the SHOP BY section** with a real image — something I can screenshot and send to a friend.
- 5. **Fix the preheader** — the preview is almost certainly leaking the terms asterisk text right now.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Hours left: the summer pieces actually selling out`
- **Subject Alt B:** `50% off ends tonight — here's what's actually flying`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `These styles won't wait — and neither should you.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your final chance on the summer pieces worth grabbing.`
