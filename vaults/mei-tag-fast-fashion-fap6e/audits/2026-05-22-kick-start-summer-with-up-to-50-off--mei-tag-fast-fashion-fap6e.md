# Kick-start summer with up to 50% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- H&M just served me an almost identical email to the one I got two days ago — the May 20 "Up to 50% off sitewide starts now" send. Same offer, different subject, same energy. My inbox is already tired of it.
- The layout is clean and I'll give them credit for that. The red wide-leg trousers on the women's model stopped me for half a second because that exact silhouette is genuinely all over my FYP right now. The colorful printed shirt in the Men's shot has real energy too. But the email doesn't *know* any of this — it's presenting trend pieces like catalog SKUs, no context, no "this is the moment." And then it pivots to BABY, KIDS, HOME tiles like I suddenly became someone's mom? Hard pass on that whole section.
- Compared to ASOS's leopard print edit from today — which at least picked a specific trend and committed to it — this H&M send is scatter-shot. And compared to Zara's Bad Bunny email earlier this week: yes, the execution there was weak, but at least Zara was building around a cultural moment. This is just "sale." I'm marking this as read. The offer clearly isn't going anywhere given they've already run it twice this week.

## What's working

- The red wide-leg trousers on the women's model are actually the move right now — H&M happened to shoot a trending silhouette and it lands
- "LONG WEEKEND, BIG DEALS" gives the sale an occasion frame that at least implies urgency, even without an explicit deadline
- The colorful abstract-print shirt in the Men's section is genuinely bold and trend-forward, not the safe-dad polo I expect
- 50% off sitewide is front-loaded in the hero — zero ambiguity, zero scrolling required to understand the offer
- Renders clean: no broken images, no overlapping text, readable without zooming on my phone

## What's weak

- This is the second near-identical sitewide-sale email in three days — the May 20 send already killed the surprise, and repeating it this fast makes the urgency feel manufactured
- BABY, KIDS, HOME category tiles are visual noise for me — they signal immediately that H&M isn't speaking to a 24-year-old, they're carpet-bombing every segment at once
- The in-store "SEE WHAT'S NEW IN-STORE" section is an entire vibe pivot I did not ask for — I'm not driving to a mall, I'm shopping at midnight on my phone
- The preheader ("NEED AN ITEM SOONER? BUY ONLINE, PICK UP IN-STORE") reads like a logistics announcement, not hype — it's working against the sale energy before I even open
- No trend language anywhere — ASOS at least names the trend (leopard print, embellished pieces); H&M just says "sunlit hues" which could be a 2012 Pottery Barn catalog

## Recommendations

- 1. **Strip the Baby/Kids/Home tiles.** This should be a Women's-first email with maybe a Men's secondary. Every irrelevant category tile is a signal that H&M isn't talking to me specifically.
- 2. **Name a trend.** "Wide-leg moment," "resort linen," "printed shirting" — anything that sounds like TikTok found it first. Right now this email could have been sent in 2018.
- 3. **Add a hard deadline in the hero.** "Ends Monday" in the 50% off headline would do more for urgency than anything else on this page.
- 4. **Kill the in-store section or make it earn its space.** If it stays, it should at least tease exclusive in-store drops — not just "feel our products." Nobody is coming in to feel the products.
- 5. **Match the hero imagery to the May 21 Studio Resort standard.** That email had an editorial shot that made me screenshot it. This one is a standing-pose catalog image. The brand clearly has range — use it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Wide-leg, printed, linen — 50% off this long weekend`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your FYP just went on sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ends Monday — grab the trend pieces before they sell out`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `50% off sitewide, long weekend only — the red trousers are calling`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**External font imports will fail in most clients:**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` is blocked by Outlook (all versions), Yahoo Mail, and older Android clients. No fallback font stack is declared in the visible CSS — if these imports fail, the client's default serif or sans-serif is applied.

**Non-standard media query:**
```css
@media screen yahoo { ... }
```
This is a Yahoo Mail hack, not a W3C media feature. It functions as intended but will silently no-op in any client that doesn't recognize it — confirm test coverage on Yahoo.

**`alt` attributes** — not visible in truncated source; cannot confirm coverage on product images.

**`x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` meta** — present and correct.
