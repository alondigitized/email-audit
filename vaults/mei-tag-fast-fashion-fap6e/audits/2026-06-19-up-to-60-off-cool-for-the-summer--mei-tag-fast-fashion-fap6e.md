# Up to 60% off = cool for the summer
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- Okay so H&M is in my inbox *again* with the same 60% off energy they've been running since June 16 — that's four sends in four days with the same offer, just slightly reworded each time. The "cool for the summer" subject line has a cute cultural wink (that Nicki/Doja lyric definitely lives in my brain) and I'll admit it made me stop for half a second. But the first thing I see when I open is a Father's Day banner pushing gifts for him — and I'm a 24-year-old woman who opened a summer sale email. That mismatch kills any "they know me" feeling instantly.
- The bones are solid: the SALE UP TO 60% OFF headline is big and unambiguous, the FOR HER styling is actually cute (that oversized brown tee, the denim moment), and the email renders cleanly without a single broken image. But it's doing way too many jobs — Father's Day, women's, men's, kids', home, in-store, download the app. It's less of an email and more of a circular dropped in my inbox.
- Would I click? Honestly, maybe — out of muscle memory more than excitement. The 60% is real money and I trust H&M on basics. But I wouldn't forward this to anyone. ASOS gave me the Scandi edit last week with an actual point of view; this is just "all the things, discounted." Mark as read energy.

## What's working

- The SALE UP TO 60% OFF headline lands immediately — big, unambiguous, readable without zooming on mobile.
- "Cool for the summer" in the subject is a low-key culture nod that earned a pause from me — it doesn't feel like a boomer writing summer promo copy.
- The FOR HER section is actually styled well — the oversized brown tee and relaxed denim silhouette is the kind of low-effort summer look my FYP is full of right now.
- TEES and SHORTS as CTA buttons are specific enough that I know exactly where I'm landing — not just "shop now" into the abyss like Zara keeps doing.
- The email renders completely clean — no broken images, no overlapping text, visual hierarchy from hero to category sections is logical.

## What's weak

- The Father's Day banner is the very first thing in the email — gift shop for him, date for men's items — inside what's supposed to be a women's summer sale email. Hard segmentation miss.
- This is literally the fourth iteration of the same 60% off message since June 16. The creative changed slightly but the offer is verbatim the same. The cadence is exhausting and eroding the urgency.
- FOR HIM and FOR THEM sections eat up prime scroll real estate — I'm not shopping for my hypothetical kids or my brother's graphic tees right now.
- Zero trend narrative — no call-out of what's *actually* hot right now, what sold out, what just dropped. ASOS names the aesthetic (Scandi edit); H&M just says "stuff is cheap."
- The in-store section (SEE WHAT'S NEW IN-STORE) feels completely out of place — I'm shopping at midnight on my phone, not driving to a mall.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Strip the Father's Day banner** from the women's send entirely — or at minimum drop it below the fold. Don't open a female-targeted summer sale with a gifts-for-him promo.
- 2. **Add one trend story to the hero** — "Linen moments, oversized cotton, the off-duty summer look — up to 60% off" is a reason to click, not just a discount. ASOS did this with the Scandi edit; H&M needs a named aesthetic.
- 3. **Segment out FOR HIM and FOR THEM** from the women's variant — women's subscribers don't need to mentally manage three audiences in one scroll.
- 4. **Put a deadline on the 60% off** — the Father's Day banner has a cutoff date (06/19) but the main sale is open-ended. No deadline = I'll come back tomorrow = I never will.
- 5. **Collapse the bottom half** — SEE WHAT'S NEW IN-STORE + SHOP BY grid + DOWNLOAD APP + STORES & SERVICE all stacked is too much. Pick two max.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` for external fonts — will fail silently in most clients**

```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```

`@import` inside `<style>` is stripped by Gmail, Apple Mail (some versions), and all Outlook/Exchange clients. Fallback fonts must be specified via `font-family` stack; if they are not, system defaults will render instead. This is a known anti-pattern in email HTML.

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`**

```html
<html style="border:0;margin:0;outline:0;padding:0">
```

No `lang="en"` (or locale equivalent). Screen readers cannot infer language; this fails WCAG 2.1 SC 3.1.1 and will be flagged by accessibility audits.

**CSS media query `line-height:auto` is invalid**

```css
.is-hidden-desktop { line-height: auto!important; ... }
```

`line-height: auto` is not a valid CSS value (valid: `normal`, a number, a length). Most email clients will ignore this declaration; the intended behavior (restoring collapsed elements) may not trigger reliably on Android clients.

**`img` alt attributes** — cannot confirm from truncated source; flag for manual check.
