# Score 30% off in-store all weekend
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-01**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so H&M followed up "Campus favorites from $6.99" (7/31) with... more back-to-school, except now it's leaning HARD into family/kids energy — a toddler in a jersey top, two little kids in "Chelsea" and "94" jerseys for the tax-free section. I'm 24 with no kids. This is not my email. The 30% off is real and the layout is clean, but the second I saw those kid models I mentally filed this as "mom email" and kept scrolling.
- The product grid itself is fine — cable-knit sweater, off-shoulder sweatshirt, twill pants — nothing screams trend to me, it screams "fall basics restock." Compare that to what ASOS did with the adidas Originals lookbook (7/30 and 7/31 back to back) — even repeating it twice in two days, at least that's a co-sign, a "this is the trend and we have it" flex. This is just... inventory.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I'm already thinking about a new sweater, since the price cuts are legit and visible. Would I forward it? No. This is the eighth-ish email from H&M in like two weeks and it's the least "for me" one yet — I'd mark it read and move on.

## What's working

- The 30% off headline is impossible to miss — big, bold, right under the fold.
- Prices are shown with the strikethrough original next to the sale price, so the math is done for me (no mental gymnastics).
- Two clear CTAs — "Shop Now" and "Find a Store" — so it doesn't force me to pick between browsing and in-store, that's smart for a "weekend only" push.
- Clean render, no broken images or overlapping text — better than that Zara "This week at Zara" send that was just a men's editorial with zero relevance to me.

## What's weak

- The models skew way younger (little kids) or way more "campus mom" than trend-forward 24-year-old — this reads like a family back-to-school flyer, not a Saturday trend-check email.
- Zero trend language anywhere — no "TikTok's obsessed with," no "as seen on," nothing that tells me H&M got this first vs. copied it from Zara or Shein.
- The fine print at the bottom is a lot — "cannot be used for gift card purchases," "no adjustments," "final sale cannot be returned or exchanged" — that's the kind of buried exclusion that makes a 30% off feel less generous once you actually read it.
- Cadence is rough — this is the same "back to school" beat H&M has hit in "Straight A's in back to school style," "Up to 30% off your back-to-school checklist" (7/30), and now this. I'm fatigued on the theme itself.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap at least half the models for people my age in styled-out fits — right now this reads like a family circular, not a trend drop. Show me the sweater on someone who looks like they picked it, not someone who got dressed for a school photo.
- 2. Cut the fine print or move the worst exclusions ("final sale," "no adjustments") somewhere less prominent — it undercuts the offer's honesty.
- 3. Give the hero product page a "why now" trend hook — even one line like "the cardigan everyone's restocking for fall" would do more for me than "cardigans, hoodies, jackets you have to see to believe."
- 4. Space out the back-to-school sends — five-plus emails on the same theme is Forever 21 spam territory, not a "your inbox, curated" feeling.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your fall wardrobe reset, up to 30% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New arrivals + markdowns, in-store and online`
