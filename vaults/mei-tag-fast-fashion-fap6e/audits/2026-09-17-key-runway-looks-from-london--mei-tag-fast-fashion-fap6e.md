# Key runway looks from London
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-17**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is actually kind of a flex from H&M — backstage runway shots from the actual London show, not another studio flatlay grid. The "H&M&YOU&I" thing is a little cringe as wordplay but the imagery has real texture: sheer ruffled corset dress, that cream ruffle-appliquéd jacket with the tiny hair bow, the wet-look boots shot pile — it reads editorial, not catalog. But here's my issue: there's no price context, no "shop this trend before it's everywhere," and zero urgency. It's basically a lookbook with a shop button slapped on, which is cute for vibes but doesn't push me to actually buy today.
- Compared to yesterday's H&M send ("Start the season in SculptMove™"), this is a way better creative direction — moody backstage energy over gym-catalog grid — but it has the same core problem as Zara's Kids drop I reviewed: gorgeous photography, no reason to act *now*. ASOS's Lioness drop still wins this specific "runway energy" lane for me because that one had an actual narrative (a girl strutting somewhere specific) plus it felt more "for me" as a 24-year-old than boots-and-corsets-at-fashion-week backstage shots that could be for anyone 22-45.
- Would I click? Maybe, to screenshot the corset dress for my group chat. Would I forward it? To my one friend who's into runway-inspired stuff, sure, as a "look what H&M's doing" — not because I'm buying anything.

## What's working

- The backstage/showroom photography (visible racks, exposed lighting rigs) feels authentically "we were there" not staged, which is rare for H&M and beats their usual clean studio shots.
- Individual product call-outs with names and prices (Balloon-Skirt Corset Dress $99, Ruffled Maxi Dress $379, Knee-High Boots $89.99, Ruffle-Appliquéd Jacket $139) at least let me price-check without clicking through.
- The runway framing ("Key runway looks from London") gives H&M a trend-first flex that Zara Kids and ASOS's category emails don't really attempt — this feels closer to "we were at the show" than "we made a lookbook."
- Two CTA placements (top and bottom "SHOP THE RUNWAY") means I don't have to scroll back up.

## What's weak

- $379 for a "Ruffled Maxi Dress" from H&M is a genuine double-take — that's not fast-fashion pricing, and there's zero context for why (special collab? limited capsule?) so it just looks like a typo or a trap.
- No urgency, no "shop before it sells out," nothing that says this is time-limited — runway capsules move fast in real life and the email doesn't communicate that.
- Model casting is all one look — pale, similar hair, similar age-adjacent styling — doesn't feel like it's speaking to a diverse Gen Z audience the way ASOS's Lioness shoot did.
- "H&M&YOU&I" as hero copy is trying too hard and doesn't tell me anything about the actual drop.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add urgency/scarcity language since runway capsules genuinely do sell out — "capsule drops [date]" or "selling fast" would match how this stuff actually moves.
- 2. Justify or flag the $379 dress price immediately (e.g., "Runway Exclusive" tag) so it doesn't read as a mistake next to $99-$139 pieces.
- 3. Diversify the model casting across the four product shots — right now it's visually monotone for a "London fashion week" story.
- 4. Cut "H&M&YOU&I" and replace with something that names the trend, e.g. "Straight from the runway: London's key looks."
