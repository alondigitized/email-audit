# In focus: The Knitwear Edit
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-15**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one is actually pretty. It's a clean knitwear editorial with the cream scarf-collar sweater hero and the dark turtleneck cardigan shots. After a week of the red "up to 40% off" banner, it finally looks like H&M has taste again. It's the same energy as their "H&M Moves New York" send on 10/14, which I liked.
- But it's calm to the point of being sleepy. The only "offer" is a tiny line at the very top about Halloween, which is a weird mismatch with a fall knitwear story. Nothing here says trend or "everyone's wearing this." Zara Pre-Owned had way more of a point of view, and ASOS's Lioness drop felt like an actual moment. This just feels like a lookbook.
- Would I click? Maybe a quick tap on the cream sweater. Would I forward it to a friend? No. I'd mark it read and go check Zara for the same sweater.

## What's working

- The hero is a strong, moody editorial shot. The oversized cream knit collar is exactly the cozy layered look I'm saving right now.
- The "KNITWEAR EDIT" headline is big and clear, and the "fall is the season of layering" copy is short enough to actually read.
- Prices are visible right under the products ($49.99, "New Arrival"), so I don't have to tap to find out if it's affordable.
- The models look my age and the styling feels current, not mom-core.
- The "Fall Beauty" block with the berry nail polish at $6.99 is a cute surprise, and I'd probably add it.

## What's weak

- The Halloween "up to 50% off" line sits tiny and gray at the top, and it has nothing to do with knitwear. It's confusing and easy to miss.
- Two of the three product shots are labeled "Button-detail turtleneck cardigan" at $49.99. It looks like a copy-paste mistake, and I can't tell if they're two colors or one product shown twice.
- Only one CTA, "Shop Now," repeats, and none of them are product-specific. I can't tell which sweater is which.
- The legal fine print at the bottom is a giant wall of text. It makes the "50% off" feel buried and sketchy.
- I see nothing about shipping speed, sizing, or what's trending. Shein and Zara would at least tease "just dropped" or "selling fast."

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the offer and the content match. Either lead with a real knitwear promo ("Knits from $24.99") in the hero, or drop the Halloween banner entirely.
- 2. Fix the duplicate product label. Show three distinct pieces with distinct names and colors.
- 3. Replace the generic "Shop Now" buttons with specific ones like "Shop the cream knit" or "Shop cardigans."
- 4. Add one trend hook, like "the sweater everyone's layering this week," so it feels like H&M got there first.
- 5. Move the fine print into a collapsed link. A tiny "details" link is fine.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Oversized collars, soft cardigans, all $49.99 and under.`

# Technical Audit: H&M, "In focus: The Knitwear Edit"

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML is structurally conventional, with media-query responsive CSS and a JSON-LD block. The automated link probe returned 403 on all 12 material links, including Unsubscribe. This is probably bot-blocking on the ESP tracker `t19.email.hm.com` and not a real outage, but it can't be confirmed from this data. Alt text is missing on 14 images, and the header-based compliance checks were inconclusive because of the relay.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **403 on all 12 probed links.** This covers "Shop now" (×4), "SHOP NOW" (×4), the `Shop now` links with zero-width characters (×2), the "H&M" logo link, and "Unsubscribe". Every URL is a redirect wrapper of the form `https://t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=h8d559ea2-…,5491d32c,<linkid>&did=1418842924&rid=3326930539&erid=…&p1=DM6140555&p2=…&p3=20261015`.
  - Every link shares one host and one `id` prefix, and only the link ID suffix differs (`f8792e21`, `f8792e22`, `f8792e2f` and so on). A uniform 403 across all of them points to the tracker rejecting the non-browser probe (UA or IP reputation), not 12 independent broken links.
  - **Status: unverified.** Re-probe with a browser-like UA, or follow the redirect chain from a real client, before calling these broken.
- **Unverified links.** 61 links were skipped by the probe, so their state is unknown.
- **Zero-width characters in link text.** Two CTAs have link text `‌  ﻿Shop now`, which starts with ZWNJ and BOM characters (links `…f8792d56` and `…f8792d58`). This breaks exact-text matching and may be announced oddly by screen readers.
- **Opaque tracking.** The `p2` hash and `erid` (base64 of `3326930539`) are per-recipient identifiers carried in every URL. That is normal for ESP tracking, but it means the links can't be inspected for destination without following the redirect.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
