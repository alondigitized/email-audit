# Game on: SANTOS BRAVOS kicks us off
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- Okay so H&M is back in my inbox with a soccer collab and I genuinely had to pause and ask — who is Santos Bravos? Because the email just dropped the name like I already know the lore, gave me "match the moment" copy that sounds like a Nike reject, and then showed me a product grid of moisture-wicking jerseys with DRYMOVE™ branding. Like, yes, the oversized soccer jersey is absolutely a Gen Z fashion trend right now — I've seen it all over my FYP — but H&M executed this as a sportswear drop, not a trend moment. There's a difference between "here's a jersey you can style" and "here is athletic apparel for the game."
- What makes this worse is the cadence context. H&M has literally been in my inbox every single day this week — Father's Day menswear on June 5th, home decor on June 6th, the same Father's Day email again on June 7th, soft summer silhouettes on June 8th, ANOTHER Father's Day send on June 9th, and now this. Six emails in six days. Even if the June 8th "Soft summer silhouettes" send was actually great (and it was), by now I'm conditioned to swipe-archive anything with the H&M sender name before I even process the subject line.
- The email itself renders cleanly and the product photography is fine, but there's no offer, no discount, no urgency, and no clear reason a 24-year-old woman should care about this specific collab over any other jersey drop. I'd mark this as read instantly. Would not click, would not forward.

## What's working

- **Santos Bravos is at least a real collab hook** — the idea of a footballer-adjacent capsule is trend-adjacent even if the execution misses
- **The oversized jersey product itself is on-trend** — I can see the oversized silhouette in the hero product shot and that's genuinely what people are styling right now
- **Email renders cleanly** — no broken images, no layout chaos, product grid is legible
- **Prices are visible in the product grid** — I can see the dollar amounts without having to click through, which is at least honest

## What's weak

- **No offer, no discount, nothing** — after six emails this week from H&M, they still couldn't come through with a reason to buy *today*; ASOS's Topshop editorial at least had clear pricing anchors and implied exclusivity
- **DRYMOVE™ is the wrong brand language for a trend moment** — framing this as athletic performance tech kills the vibe; Zara would never put "moisture-wicking" in a fashion collab email
- **KIDS tab is a buzzkill for me specifically** — having WOMEN | MEN | KIDS as the three hero CTAs signals this email was spray-blasted to the entire list, not aimed at me
- **Santos Bravos has zero name recognition established in the email** — one sentence of context on who they are would do a lot of work; right now it's just a name I'm supposed to already care about
- **The June 8th "Soft summer silhouettes" email was actually built for me** — and then six days later H&M is right back to male-coded athletic drops with no connective thread for a female subscriber

## Recommendations

- 1. **Reframe the hero as a fashion moment, not a sports drop** — swap "match the moment" for styling copy that positions the oversized jersey as streetwear, not activewear; show a female model wearing it oversized with low-rise jeans or a miniskirt, not against a green field
- 2. **Add a concrete offer** — even "free shipping on the collab" or a member price would give me a reason to click today versus never
- 3. **Segment this send** — the KIDS tab and the gender-neutral shotgun approach tells me H&M sent this to their entire list; the women's segment deserved a women's-forward version
- 4. **Give Santos Bravos context in one line** — "Santos Bravos × H&M: the Buenos Aires football club that broke the internet" or whatever is actually true; let the collab mean something
- 5. **Cool down the send cadence** — literally anything would help at this point; six emails in six days has burned my goodwill

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading will silently fail in Gmail (web + mobile)**
```html
<style>
  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
</style>
```
Gmail strips `<style>` block `@import` directives entirely. H&M's custom typefaces (`Northstar`, OCES) will not render for Gmail recipients — the largest email client segment. Fallback font behavior is uncontrolled.

**`img.image + div { display: none !important; }` is a broad selector**
This hides any `<div>` immediately following an `.image`-classed `<img>`. If a client reflows or wraps images differently, legitimate content blocks could be suppressed. Low risk but worth auditing against the full DOM tree.

**Yahoo-specific media query uses non-standard syntax**
```css
@media screen yahoo { .show-webkit-table { display: none; } }
```
`@media screen yahoo` is a legacy H&M/ESP workaround. Confirm this is intentional and still needed — some modern Yahoo Mail versions now support standard media queries.

**No `alt` attributes visible in the truncated source.** Full-source check required; missing `alt` text on product images breaks screen reader access and renders blank in image-off clients.

### 5. Compliance

**Stale/empty JSON-LD DiscountOffer block**
```json
{
  "@type": "DiscountOffer",
  "availabilityStarts": "2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00",
  "availabilityEnds": "",
  "discountCode": "",
  "description": ""

```
`availabilityStarts` is hardcoded to `2018-12-31` — over 7 years stale. This is an unfilled template default.
