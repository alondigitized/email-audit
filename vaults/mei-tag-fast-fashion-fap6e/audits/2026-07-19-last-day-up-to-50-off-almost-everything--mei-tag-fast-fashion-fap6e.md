# Last day: Up to 50% off almost everything
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- H&M has officially been in my inbox every single day this week — and I mean *every* day, plus twice on the 16th — and we are now on our third "50% off sitewide" signal in seven days. The July 17 TGIF send was "Up to 50% off ALL of this," then yesterday was "Summer-to-fall favorites from $9.99" which also led with 50% off sitewide, and now today rolls around with "Last day: Up to 50% off almost everything." At some point "Last day" stops meaning anything. Like, I've lived through three last days this week already.
- What actually caught me was the fashion imagery — a brown strappy mini and a dusty rose spaghetti-strap dress, both on young models, both legitimately on-trend right now. I'd genuinely three-tab those against Zara (who just sent me a grid of children's graphic tees and called it "Discover what's new," which — yikes). But then I read the hero subtext and it says "Gear up for a winning season with transitional picks and flexible fits from H&M MOVE." That's the activewear line. The woman in the photo is in a sundress. Someone copy-pasted from the July 16 MOVE hiking email and nobody caught it.
- I would click — 50% sitewide is genuinely compelling — but I would not forward this, and I'm getting close to the cadence threshold where my thumb starts hovering over unsubscribe.

## What's working

- **Offer lands immediately.** "UP TO 50% OFF SITEWIDE" in bold, first thing on screen — no scrolling, no mystery, no "shop the collection to find out." Respect.
- **Fashion imagery is actually trend-forward.** The brown strappy mini and dusty rose mini are current pieces I'd want to click through. Zara just sent me kids' shirts; H&M sent me something I'd wear.
- **"Last day" urgency is structurally the right call.** A deadline in the subject line earns opens — even if the credibility is eroding after a week of this.
- **TikTok in the footer social row.** Small but meaningful signal that this brand is trying to be where I actually am, not where my mom is.
- **Model diversity done right.** Male model flanked by two female models — real people, not a stock catalog.

## What's weak

- **The MOVE copy under the hero is a straight-up error.** "Gear up for a winning season with transitional picks and flexible fits from H&M MOVE" while showing a woman in a strappy sundress is dissonant in a way that makes me trust the brand slightly less. That's July 16's hiking email copy. It doesn't belong here.
- **Offer fatigue is doing real damage.** Three "50% off sitewide" signals in seven days means "Last day" has lost its teeth. ASOS ran their "Extra 30% off selected sale styles" twice in a week and I still noticed the repeat — H&M is doing it harder and faster.
- **No trend anchor anywhere in the email.** ASOS gave me a code (XTRA30) that felt like mine specifically. H&M's "almost everything" is the broadest, least exciting copy possible. What's the trend? What should I be clicking on?
- **HOME and BEAUTY tiles below apparel hero.** If I opened because of a strappy dress, showing me a home decor category box two scrolls down kills the momentum.
- **Fine print wall at the bottom.** I know it's legally required but it's a mortgage doc. A single collapsed line with "Terms apply" would land better.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the MOVE hero copy right now.** It's the most urgent thing. Replace it with something that matches the dress imagery: *"Your whole wardrobe, half the price. Last day to shop."* The MOVE tagline belongs in the MOVE section, not the hero of a sitewide sale.
- 2. **Make the urgency real and specific.** "Ends tonight at midnight" > "Last day" because a clock time feels verifiable. I've seen too many H&M "last days" to believe the generic version.
- 3. **Add one trend hook to earn the open.** After seven identical emails, I need a reason this one is different: *"The quiet luxury edit is 50% off — today only."* Give me a lens on the sale.
- 4. **Add a visible promo code.** ASOS's XTRA30 made the discount feel targeted at me. A code — even a generic one — creates the psychology of exclusivity.
- 5. **Cut the HOME/BEAUTY tiles or move them below the fold.** Let the fashion imagery breathe and convert first.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `The sale ends at midnight — your cart won't fill itself.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Strappy dresses, basics, MOVE styles — all half price today.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility
