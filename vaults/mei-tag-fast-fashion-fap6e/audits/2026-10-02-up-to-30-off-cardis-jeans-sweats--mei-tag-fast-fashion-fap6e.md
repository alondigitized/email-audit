# Up to 30% off cardis, jeans, sweats...
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-02**
## Executive summary

- Okay, H&M is back on the discount banner, and honestly it's the most boring version of it. Yesterday's "flash sale" email had that "LAST DAY!" confusion. The day before was "The Outerwear Edit," with the leather coat and sunglasses and an actual point of view. This one is a flat product grid with "UP TO 30% OFF FALL ESSENTIALS" on top. It's clean and I understood it in two seconds, but nothing in it feels like a trend. It's a catalog.
- The fine-knit cardigan shows up three times (the dusty-blue one, the cream one, and the black one), and every one is basically the same cardigan. That's not a drop, that's a stock clearance. The prices are clear, with red sale and gray strikethrough. But "up to 30%" while the actual tags look like $16.99 from $24.99 doesn't feel urgent.
- Would I click? Maybe once, if I'm bored on Saturday during my three-tab compare. Forward it to a friend? No. Zara's Re{form} drop and the ASOS Lioness campaign both feel like someone decided what the vibe is. This feels like inventory management.

## What's working

- The "UP TO 30% OFF FALL ESSENTIALS" headline is huge and instantly readable, so no hunting for the offer.
- Red sale price next to gray strikethrough makes the savings obvious on every product.
- The model mix is decent: different skin tones and styling, and the denim and skirt pairings look wearable for my age.
- Product names and prices sit right under each image, so I don't have to click to find out what's what.
- The SHOP NOW and FIND A STORE buttons are clear and big enough to tap.

## What's weak

- No trend angle at all. Nothing says "TikTok's wearing this" or "new this week," so it can't tell me whether H&M is early or copying someone.
- The fine-knit cardigan repeats three times in different colors, which feels like padding.
- The sale banner and the discount-heavy cadence (flash sale 9/30, this on 10/2) train me to wait for the next markdown instead of buying.
- One hero button goes to a generic SHOP NOW. Nothing links to a specific product or category like jeans or sweats, even though the subject promised them.
- The subject says "cardis, jeans, sweats...", but I see only knitwear and one sweatshirt. The jeans are only on the models.
- The fine print is a wall of gray text: exclusions, "select kids', women's and men's items," and the 10/07 end date is buried at the bottom. The deadline is barely visible up top.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the deadline (ends 10/07) in the hero and name the actual categories. "Up to 30% off" with no urgency isn't a reason to click now.
- 2. Lead with one styled, trend-forward look (the dusty-blue cardigan with wide-leg denim) instead of a plain cardigan shot. Give me an outfit, not a rack.
- 3. Cut the repeated cardigan. Use that space for jeans and sweats to match the subject line.
- 4. Link each product tile to its own page and add a "trending now" or "most added to bag" tag so I know what's moving.
- 5. Move the sale terms up or shorten them so the offer feels honest.
