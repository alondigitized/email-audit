# In focus: Summer partywear
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- Okay so H&M slides back into editorial mode after yesterday's "15% or 20% off ends tonight" urgency play, and I notice the vibe shift immediately. Yesterday they were doing math with me, today they want to sell me a lifestyle. "Getting Hotter" is doing its job — the hero image is moody, the backless dress is genuinely cute, and partywear is exactly the kind of Saturday-night content that stops my thumb. I'll give them that.
- But the more I scroll, the more I feel like I'm watching a lookbook rather than getting a reason to buy. There's no offer. Not even a soft "new arrivals, limited sizes" to create any pull. And then halfway down the page the email pivots to a beauty section — "Glowy, Glossy & Bronzed" with a close-up eyeball photo — which is just fully a different email. The category SHOP BY grid at the bottom feels like someone appended a homepage footer and called it done.
- I would not click. I might tap the dress in the hero out of curiosity, but I'd be price-checking Zara and ASOS in the next two tabs before H&M's product page even loads. Mark as read, no forward.

## What's working

- **Hero casting is right for me.** The model is young, the dress is sleek and backless — that's Saturday night energy and it's styled without being overdone.
- **"Getting Hotter" lands.** It's one of H&M's better headline plays this week — more evocative than "Game on: SANTOS BRAVOS kicks us off" (June 11) and less random than the Father's Day detour on June 13.
- **"New Arrival" tags on products.** The Flounced High-Low Skirt and Twist-Detail Twill Dress are both labeled — that tiny signal tells me these are worth looking at now, not six weeks from now.
- **No render issues.** Images load, text is legible, layout is clean. That's table stakes but H&M has had messier sends.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer.** Yesterday was "15-20% off ends tonight." Today? Nothing. That whiplash is confusing — did the sale end and now we're back to full price with no warning? ASOS just hit me with 25% off 1000s of styles and gave me a member badge to feel special. H&M gives me vibes.
- **Three "SHOP NOW" CTAs, all generic.** One for the hero, one mid-page, one for beauty. None of them tell me what I'm actually clicking into. "Shop the Look," "Get the Dress," "Explore Partywear" — anything more specific would outperform this.
- **The beauty pivot breaks the flow.** I opened this for partywear and halfway through I'm staring at an extreme close-up of an eyelid. The "Glowy, Glossy & Bronzed" section feels glued on — it's a different email, from a different brief, dropped into the bottom half.
- **Five emails in five days.** The cadence is getting noisy. June 11 soccer collab, June 12 shorts, June 13 Father's Day + promo, June 14 promo end, June 15 editorial partywear. I'm starting to feel like H&M doesn't know what story it's telling me this week.
- **SHOP BY grid at the bottom includes KIDS and HOME.** I'm 24, shopping for Saturday night. Why am I looking at a kids' category tile in a partywear email?

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an offer or at least a tension point.** Even "New arrivals, going fast in your size" or "Drop just landed" would give me a reason to click today vs. next week. After a two-day promo blitz, an empty editorial feels like a missed conversion.
- 2. **Make the CTAs specific.** Replace every "SHOP NOW" with something tied to the section: "Shop Partywear Dresses," "Shop the Bag," "Shop Beauty Looks." One second of specificity is worth three seconds of generic.
- 3. **Cut or sequester the beauty section.** Either send it as its own email or make it a smaller module that doesn't compete with the partywear narrative. Right now it's splitting my attention at exactly the wrong moment.
- 4. **Kill the SHOP BY footer grid.** Or at minimum, remove KIDS and HOME from a partywear send. A segmented email should feel like it knows who it's talking to — this one forgets halfway down.
- 5. **Lean into the "In Focus" editorial framing harder.** If this is a content-first send, give it editorial cred — style it like the Zara summer shoots that make me feel like I'm missing something. Right now the photography is good but the copy doesn't close the loop.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**External font @import will fail broadly:**

```html

  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```

- Outlook (all versions) ignores `@import` entirely — fonts will fall back silently.
