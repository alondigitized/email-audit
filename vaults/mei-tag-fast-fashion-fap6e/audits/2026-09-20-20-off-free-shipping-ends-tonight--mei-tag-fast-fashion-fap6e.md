# 20% off + free shipping ends tonight
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-20**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is literally the exact same email H&M sent me on 2026-09-19 — same "20% off $125 + free shipping" hero, same black fuzzy turtleknit girl, just now with "ends tonight" slapped on for urgency. I already reviewed this offer four days ago at 5/10 and nothing creative changed, just the deadline got moved up. That's lazy — if you're going to reuse the hero shot, at least swap the product grid around so it doesn't feel like the same email twice.
- The WOMEN/MEN/KIDS/HOME grid dump is the same sprawling everything-store format H&M always does, and it still buries denim and basics under generic category headers instead of showing me an actual trend. Compare that to the H&M runway email from 2026-09-17 — backstage London show shots actually had texture and felt like a flex. This is just product photography on white boxes, again.
- Would I click? Maybe, because $125 + free shipping + a real "ends tonight" deadline is a legit reason to act fast, and I do need basics. Would I forward to a friend? No — there's nothing here worth sharing, it's not a fit or a trend moment, it's a catalog.

## What's working

- The 20% off $125 + free shipping offer is big, bold, and impossible to miss right at the top — no scavenger hunt needed.
- "Ends tonight" adds real urgency this time, which the 09-19 send didn't have.
- The hero model's oversized fuzzy leopard-print turtleknit is genuinely a fall trend piece, not a random flatlay.
- WOMEN/MEN/KIDS/HOME quick-nav buttons right under the hero make it easy to jump straight to my category.

## What's weak

- This is the same hero image and same offer format as the 2026-09-19 "Weekend deal" email — reused creative four days later feels like H&M ran out of ideas, not like a new drop.
- Nothing here says trend-first — no "as seen on," no styling context, no influencer tie-in, just a catalog grid of sweatpants and vases.
- The KIDS and HOME sections are irrelevant filler for me — I don't shop those categories and scrolling past them just adds friction before I hit anything I care about.
- Product names are literally truncated ("BRUSHED-INSI...") which looks sloppy and unfinished, like nobody proofed the render.
- Zero social proof, zero "trending now" badges — compare to Zara or ASOS, who at least try a styled campaign shoot instead of a plain product wall.

- 3. Cut HOME and KIDS out of my version of this email entirely, or let me segment my categories — I only care about WOMEN.

2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All 12 probed links return **403 Forbidden**, including primary CTAs ("Shop now" x3, "SHOP NOW" x2), navigation ("Women", "Men", "H&M" logo), and both product images. Example: `https://t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=h7d2fb7bb-...&p3=20260920`
