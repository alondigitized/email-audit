# Sale is here: up to 60% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- Okay so H&M is back in my inbox *again* with that same "up to 60% off" energy — literally yesterday I got "Early app access: up to 60% off" and now it's just... "Sale is here: up to 60% off." Same number, slightly more declarative. The escalation that started June 14 with "ends tonight" percentages has now just plateaued into a rerun. Cool cool cool.
- The email loads clean, no chaos — but when I actually look at what they're showing me, it's a draped romper, a linen polo (men's), some twill chinos (also basically men's), and — I'm not kidding — a **canvas cushion cover**. A home item. In a fashion sale email. In June. That's a choice.
- I'd mark it read. I might tap into the WOMEN tab out of habit but I'm not excited, and I'm definitely not forwarding this to anyone. After the Anitta email two days ago (which was actually incredible), this feels like the brand put on sweatpants.

## What's working

- The **UP TO 60% OFF** hero is immediately legible — big, bold, hierarchy is clean, I don't have to hunt for the offer
- **$19.99 on the draped romper** — that price visible in the product grid actually makes me pause, that's genuinely good for a summer piece
- **H&M Members free shipping over $50** at the very top is a nice retention nudge and it's the first thing I see
- **Category buttons** (WOMEN, MEN, KIDS, HOME) are well-organized tap targets — easy to skip straight to my section
- The **social icons in the footer include TikTok** — small signal but it tells me they know where I live

## What's weak

- A **canvas cushion cover** is getting prime product-grid real estate in a womenswear sale email — this is what Home does to a fashion email's focus
- The **linen polo shirt and twill chinos** are both men's cuts; I'm a 24-year-old woman looking for what's trending this summer, not shopping for a boyfriend
- **Zero trend language** — no "what's on TikTok right now," no editorial framing, nothing that says H&M *started* this trend vs. just clearing inventory
- This is the **second "up to 60% off" email in two days** — the June 16 "Early app access" email had the exact same headline number, so the urgency has completely evaporated for me
- "Upgrade your summer days with big markdowns on **new select essentials***" — the asterisk is doing a lot of work and "new select essentials" is weasel copy that hedges the whole offer

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with one women's trend item, not a product grid mix** — give me the romper full-bleed with a TikTok-style caption like "seen everywhere this summer" before I get to the sale numbers
- 2. **Retire the asterisk or put the exclusion language in the CTA copy** — "Shop women's sale" is more honest than "UP TO 60% OFF*" with three lines of fine print at the bottom
- 3. **Cut the Home product from the hero grid** — a canvas cushion cover in a Gen Z fashion sale email is a relevance killer; segment it out or put it in a secondary scroll section
- 4. **Differentiate from yesterday's send** — the June 16 email already said 60% off; today's needs a new angle (new drops added? last sizes? specific category?) or it reads as spam cadence, not news
- 5. **Borrow the Anitta email's energy** — that June 15 send felt like culture, this one feels like clearance

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` in `<style>` blocks (medium risk)**
Two external font sheets are loaded via `@import`:
```
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` is stripped by Outlook (all versions), AOL Mail, and some Yahoo Mail clients. Fallback system fonts will render instead — confirm fallback stack is acceptable in those clients.

**`<script>` tag present**
The JSON-LD block uses `<script type="application/ld+json">`. All email clients except Gmail strip `<script>` tags. This is expected behavior for email, but the data must be correct for Gmail's promo-tab enrichment to work (see §4).

**Alt text** — truncated source prevents full audit of `<img alt="">` coverage. Flag for full review.
