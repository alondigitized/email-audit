# New sale styles added from $7.99
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- Okay so I know exactly what this email is before I even finish reading it — H&M is dumping new markdowns into their existing sale and they want me to know there's more to shop. And honestly? In a different week I would have stopped on this. "From $7.99" in the subject is genuinely the kind of anchor price that makes me open a tab just to see what $7.99 even gets you at H&M right now. The 80% off hero lands loud and the Puff-Sleeved Dress right below the fold is actually kind of cute in that quiet coastal way that's everywhere on my For You page.
- But here's my real reaction: I've now gotten roughly eleven emails from H&M in about a week. Eleven. The June 25th flash sale, the June 26th soft-tones editorial, the bestsellers one before that — it's starting to feel like they're carpet-bombing my inbox hoping one sticks. At some point the drumbeat makes every individual send feel cheaper, not more urgent. The 80% off hero should feel exciting; instead it registers as "oh, another one."
- The product grid is also doing me dirty — three of the six featured items are menswear. A resort shirt, ribbed menswear shirt, and twill chinos. I'm a 24-year-old woman doing a Saturday price compare. Why am I looking at chinos? I would definitely not forward this. I might click the Puff-Sleeved Dress out of idle curiosity, but I'm not rushing anywhere.

## What's working

- **Price anchor in the subject does the job** — "from $7.99" is a concrete number and it made me do the math on whether that's actually possible; that's the exact kind of pull needed to earn a tap.
- **80% OFF hero is unmissable** — big, red, above the fold; I didn't have to hunt for the offer, it's the first thing my eye went to.
- **Women's items lead the grid** — the Oversized Tie-Detail Blouse and Puff-Sleeved Dress are the first two product cards, and the Puff dress is genuinely cute, trend-adjacent stuff.
- **"FURTHER MARKDOWNS" subhead adds a nudge** — there's an implicit "prices just dropped again" signal that creates mild FOMO without feeling desperate.
- **No layout breaks** — everything renders clean, nothing overlapping or broken, which puts it ahead of some Shein sends I've seen this month.

## What's weak

- **Half the product grid is menswear** — Resort shirt, rib-knit shirt, twill chinos all take up three of six slots; this email does not know I'm a 24-year-old woman.
- **Cadence is obliterating the signal** — this is at minimum the eleventh H&M email in one week; even the best offer feels like noise at this point.
- **No deadline anywhere** — Zara's June 25th send said "Today at 9 pm ET" and I felt it; this says "up to 80% off" with zero end date, which makes the urgency flat.
- **"No better time to snap up the deals price drops"** — that subhead copy reads like it lost a word mid-sentence and nobody caught it before send.
- **BOPIS preheader wastes the slot** — "NEED AN ITEM SOONER? BUY ONLINE, PICK UP IN STORE" is the first line of the email and likely what shows in my inbox preview; it doesn't connect at all to the $7.99 sale angle from the subject.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut menswear from this send entirely** — if you know I'm a woman (which H&M does), show me six women's items. Three menswear cards in a sale email to a female subscriber is a targeting failure that kills click intent on half your real estate.
- 2. **Fix the preheader** — swap "BUY ONLINE, PICK UP IN STORE" for a line that extends the sale narrative: something like "Seersucker dress, $9.99. Puff dress, $12.99. New cuts just hit sale." That makes the preview text do actual selling.
- 3. **Add a deadline** — even "sale ends Sunday" gives me a reason to act now. ASOS's Bershka/Pull&Bear send felt urgent because the framing implied limited window; this feels like it'll be here forever.
- 4. **Pull one hero item into the subject or preheader** — "Puff dress: $12.99. New sale styles from $7.99" is far more clickable than the current subject because it puts a visual in my head before I even open.
- 5. **Throttle the cadence** — this is the structural fix no single email can solve, but one great weekly send beats eleven mediocre ones for building actual brand affinity.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Seersucker dress, floral blouses, summer knits — all on sale now`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` for fonts will fail in Gmail and Outlook:**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
Both Gmail (web and Android) and Outlook strip `@import` inside `<style>` blocks. These fonts will silently fall back to system fonts. Should be replaced with `<link rel="stylesheet">` tags or inlined font stacks.

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`:**
```html
<html style="border:0;margin:0;outline:0;padding:0">
```
No `lang="en"` (or locale-appropriate equivalent). This breaks screen reader language detection and violates WCAG 3.1.1. Easy fix.

**`format-detection` meta uses deprecated syntax:**
```html
<meta content="telephone=no" name="format-detection"/>
```
Correct attribute order is `name` before `content` and the value should be `name="format-detection" content="telephone=no, date=no, address=no, email=no"`. The current form works in most clients but is incomplete.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Multiple JSON-LD fields are empty — likely unfilled template tokens:**

These are either render-time merge failures or intentionally suppressed fields. If suppressed intentionally, the keys should be omitted entirely rather than sent as empty strings — some Gmail parsers treat empty `url` as a malformed card.
