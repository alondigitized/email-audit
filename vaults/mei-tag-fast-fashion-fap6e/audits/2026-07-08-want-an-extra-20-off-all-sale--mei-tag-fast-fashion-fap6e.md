# Want an extra 20% off all Sale?
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- Okay so ASOS just ran it back. They went editorial for literally two days — the July 7th palette email was actually giving brand identity, the July 8th new arrivals had some momentum — and now we're right back to sale mode with a Premier-exclusive 20% extra off. Like, I see you ASOS, I do. The yellow-green hero hits hard immediately and the code PREM20 with a real Thursday 11pm deadline is not a fake urgency play. I actually respect the specificity.
- But this is email number eight in one week from them, and it's also the third time they've done "extra % off Sale" since July 4th. That July 4th send was a 9/10 in my mental ranking because it had higher stakes and felt like a drop. This one reads like the anxious follow-up text after the first one didn't get a reply. The Premier exclusivity framing is interesting in theory — "EXCLUSIVE" in the hero is doing work — but then there's a "SIGN UP TO PREMIER NOW" button slapped right in the middle that tells me this wasn't cleanly segmented. Either send this to Premier members and let the offer land clean, or send it to non-members as a Premier pitch. Mixing both is confusing and undermines the whole "exclusive" angle.
- I'd probably click SHOP WOMEN'S because the sage green two-piece in the grid is exactly what's on my For You Page right now and 20% off sale is a real number. But I'm not screenshotting this to send to anyone — Zara's editorial emails at least feel like they own the trend; this one feels like ASOS is sprinting to catch up on their own sale.

## What's working

- The yellow-green hero banner with "EXCLUSIVE: EXTRA 20% OFF ALL SALE" is loud, clear, and the visual hierarchy doesn't make you hunt for the offer — it hits before anything else
- Promo code PREM20 is visible in the body copy, not buried in 8pt footer text like some brands do
- "Ends Thursday, 11pm" is a specific, credible deadline — not "limited time only" nonsense
- The sage green two-piece and chocolate brown midi dress imagery sync directly with the palette edit from the July 7th email, making the visual language feel cohesive rather than disjointed
- "SAVINGS LOOK GOOD ON YOU" headline is cheeky and matches ASOS's actual brand voice — didn't feel like a template

## What's weak

- "SHOP MEN'S" gets equal button real estate in the hero alongside "SHOP WOMEN'S" — I'm a 24-year-old woman, the demographic targeting here is doing nothing; H&M's campus shop email this week at least had a clear intended audience even if the execution was flat
- The Premier paywall is buried inside an "EXCLUSIVE" promise — non-members open this expecting to shop 20% off and instead get hit with "SIGN UP TO PREMIER NOW," which Zara would never do mid-funnel
- The lookbook images are editorial with zero product context — no price, no "tap to shop," no size signal; the brown midi dress is cute but I don't know if it's £18 or £80 or whether it's even in stock in my size
- The "SHOP OUR TOP BRANDS" section (adidas, Fila, Levi's, New Balance logos) near the footer feels like a different email taped onto this one — completely breaks the sale momentum

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split the send by member status** — Premier members get the offer clean with code auto-surfaced and no "sign up" CTA; non-members get a Premier pitch that leads with the value of the membership, not just the 20% off. The current hybrid undermines both audiences.
- 2. **Kill the "SHOP MEN'S" hero button** or drop it to a secondary/smaller placement — lead with women's for female-segmented sends, full stop.
- 3. **Tag the editorial product images with price + "In Sale" badge** — the sage green set is the conversion moment; give me the price and a direct link before I lose interest and open Zara.
- 4. **Remove the "SHOP OUR TOP BRANDS" logos section** from this email type — it's a homepage module, not a sale urgency email component.
- 5. **Throttle the cadence** — eight emails in a week means even a legitimately new offer lands with diminished impact; after the July 7th palette reset they had real momentum to protect.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Duplicate charset declaration** (lines 3–4): Both `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8">` and `<meta charset="utf-8">` are present. Per the HTML spec the `charset` shortform should appear first; the `http-equiv` form is legacy. Harmless in practice but indicates copy-paste template debt.

- **`font-size: 0em` on `table, tr, td, th`**: Ghost-text suppression technique that can cause child elements to inherit 0 font-size in clients that do not reset explicitly. Any `<td>` without an explicit inline `font-size` will render invisible text in affected clients (primarily older Android WebKit).

- **Mobile breakpoint at 460px**: `@media screen and (max-width:460px)` is narrower than the de facto standard of 480px. Devices with viewport widths of 461–480px (some mid-generation Android phones) will receive the desktop layout.

- **`FuturaPT-Heavy` mapped to `font-weight: 600`**: CSS weight 600 is semibold, not heavy (typically 800+). On clients that fall back to the system Futura or Arial, the rendered weight will be lighter than intended. Should be `font-weight: 800` or `900`.

- **SVG font format included**: Both `@font-face` blocks include `.svg` format. SVG fonts were deprecated in 2013 and only relevant for iOS < 4.2. Including them increases `<head>` size without benefit.

- **`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `body`**: Using `none` (rather than `100%`) blocks user-initiated text scaling, which is an accessibility regression for low-vision users. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize text) applies to email viewable in browsers. Change to `100%`.

- **Conflicting `-ms-text-size-adjust`**: `body` sets `-ms-text-size-adjust: none` while the `*` rule sets `-ms-text-size-adjust: 100%`. The `*` rule wins due to specificity; the `body` declaration is dead code.

- **Animation without `prefers-reduced-motion` guard**: The `@keyframes ticker` animation has no `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` override. The `@supports` guard handles client capability but not user accessibility preference.

- **`mso-hide: all` missing on hidden elements**: The `.nodesktop` class uses `display: none; max-height: 0; overflow: hidden` but omits `mso-hide: all`. Outlook ignores CSS `display:none` and will render `.nodesktop` content in desktop Outlook versions.
