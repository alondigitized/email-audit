- "LAST DAY" + "20% OFF $125 + FREE SHIPPING" is a clean, real offer with an actual dollar threshold, no fine-print hunting needed at a glance.
- The "For Her" trench coat and ruffle-trim blouse are actually decent new-arrival pieces I'd tap on if I were scrolling anyway.
- Clean grid layout, easy to scan by category — no render bugs, nothing broken.

## What's weak

- This is the exact same $125/20%/free-shipping structure as 2026-08-21's "Weekend deal" email, just re-skinned with urgency — feels like the same coupon copy-pasted with a new subject.
- Kids section again. Third time this week across my inbox (Zara twice, now H&M) that a category-dump email is shoving kids' clothes at a 24-year-old with no kids.
- Zero trend-first messaging — no "as seen on TikTok," no specific viral item flagged, just generic New Arrival tags across dresses, tees, and cushions.
- The category buttons up top (Women/Men/Baby/Kids/Home) plus six product sections is a LOT before I hit anything that feels curated for me specifically.

- 2. Actually name the trend — swap generic "New Arrival" tags for something like "TikTok's favorite fall layer" on the trench coat, since that's the one piece with real cross-post potential.

- 4. Add a specific countdown time ("Ends tonight at midnight ET") instead of just "LAST DAY" — that's the difference between credible urgency and boilerplate urgency.
