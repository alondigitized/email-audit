# Go-to summer looks
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-29**
## Executive summary

- Okay so this is H&M email number... I genuinely stopped counting somewhere around nine. This is definitely eleven or twelve sends in the span of a single week and my thumb is pre-trained to swipe before I even register the subject line. But then the hero loaded — that zebra-print dress on the model — and I actually paused. That image has real energy. The styling is editorial-level clean and the model is serving. For a second I thought H&M was finally doing Zara things.
- Then I read the copy. "Long live the essentials." These are TikTok-trend pieces — a metallic knit top, a fitted mesh dress, ruffle-trimmed high-low — and H&M is calling them *essentials*? That's the brand misreading its own products. No sale, no urgency, no offer. The same week they sent a 25% off flash sale (June 25th, that was actually good), now they're asking me to pay full price for satin pants with zero reason to act today instead of next Tuesday.
- I'm marking this as read and maybe bookmarking that zebra dress in my head for a future sale cycle. I'm not clicking through, and I'm definitely not forwarding — there's nothing to share with a friend except "H&M exists, which you already knew."

## What's working

- That zebra-print hero model stops the scroll — strong composition, trend-adjacent, genuinely the most viral-energy photo H&M has put in my inbox this week
- "GO-TO SUMMER LOOKS" headline is confident without being desperate — feels curated, not clearance
- The product range is actually on-point: metallic knit tops ($19.50), mesh dress ($49.99), satin pants ($34.99) — these are real TikTok-trending silhouettes, not basic filler
- "New Arrival" tag on every item is a clean trust signal — I know exactly why I'm being contacted, it doesn't feel like a recycled sale dump
- Email renders cleanly: no broken images, no layout collapse, product grid scans fast even on a small screen

## What's weak

- No offer whatsoever — ASOS literally opened its last email with "now up to 70% off" in big red letters before I even hit the hero; H&M wants me to pay full price for everything with zero incentive
- "Go-to summer looks" as a subject line is as generic as it gets — Zara would've made this feel like an event (see: "SALE | Today at 9 pm ET online" — that had appointment-viewing energy), this just feels like content noise
- The "SALE" tab in the top nav is a tease that goes nowhere — the entire body is full-price new arrivals, which makes that nav link feel actively misleading
- Eleven emails in a week and counting — the cadence has broken trust to the point where even a decent creative executes at a disadvantage
- Zero urgency or social proof — no "selling fast," no "17 people viewing this," no trend callout that explains *why now*

## Recommendations

- 1. **Reframe the products as trends, not essentials** — "metallic knit top" and "fitted mesh dress" are TikTok-coded; call them that and the email has an identity beyond "we made clothes"
- 2. **Add a soft urgency hook** — "New arrivals go fast, especially that zebra dress" or even a "limited sizes" callout next to specific products would convert me faster than a static SHOP NOW
- 3. **Drop the cadence to one or two sends a week maximum** — I'd actually open H&M emails eagerly if they were rare; right now I'm building immunity
- 4. **Pull the SALE nav tab out or make this a hybrid send** — if the top nav teases SALE, give me one sale item somewhere in the body, otherwise that tab is actively confusing
- 5. **Give the zebra dress a TikTok moment** — "as seen on [creator name]" or "trending now" badge on the hero product would stop me cold; they're leaving viral energy completely untapped
