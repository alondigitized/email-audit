# This week’s arrivals
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-15**
## Executive summary

- Ugh, menswear again. This is the second "this week's arrivals" style Zara send where I'm not in the picture at all. Every single model is a guy, and I scrolled the whole thing hoping a woman would show up. The only one is a blurry runner at the bottom. After the 11/14 "Trending now: Party" email, which finally had women's black jumpsuits and a satin halter, this feels like a step backwards.
- The photography is clean and expensive-looking, and "ZARA ORIGINS" is a bold hero. But there's no price, no offer, no trend callout and no "new" tag, so "arrivals" is just a label. I can't tell what's actually new or what's trending. Compared to H&M, who at least gave me a clear "up to 40% off gifts for her" with a cozy shearling coat, this tells me nothing. I'd skim and mark it read. I would not forward it, because nobody in my group chat is buying a bomber jacket from this.

## What's working

- The giant ZARA wordmark over the hero looks editorial and confident. It definitely reads as the brand setting the mood and not chasing anyone.
- The black-and-white type for ORIGINS, THE PARTY EDIT and ATHLETICZ SPEED RUN is easy to read on my phone.
- The grid of full-body outfit shots is clean, so I can see fits and layering fast.
- The sports section with the colour-pop tops against the grey gives me some energy, and it feels like a real trend moment.

## What's weak

- It's almost all men. Every model in the hero, outerwear, party edit and athletic sections is male, and I'm a 24-year-old woman.
- There's no price, no offer, and no shipping or delivery info. The price-per-trend comparison I do on my Saturday three-tab check is impossible here.
- The CTAs are tiny "VIEW" links and one "DISCOVER THE COLLECTION" label floating in white space. They're hard to tap, and nothing says what I'm viewing.
- "THE PARTY EDIT" is shown with two menswear looks, so it contradicts the women's party email from yesterday.
- There's no hint of what's trending or new. ASOS's Lioness drop at least told me it was an exclusive. Nothing here makes it feel like Zara got there first.
- The long scroll of near-identical grid photos with no product names is tiring.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment by gender. Send this to men, and send me a womenswear version with the same production quality.
- 2. Make the top of the email do the work. Put one concrete "new in" callout and a price from tag at the top, like "New in: from $29.90".
- 3. Replace the tiny "VIEW" links with real buttons. They should say what they lead to, like "Shop women's party edit".
- 4. Add a trend label to each section, such as "TikTok's favorite quarter-zip", so I know why it matters.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Outerwear, knits and a new run capsule. From $29.90.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `alt` on 18 images.** `img_01.jpg` through `img_12.jpg`, `img_13A.jpg`, `img_13B.jpg`, `img_14.jpg`, `img_15.png` and `img_16.jpg` all lack `alt`. With images blocked, screen readers and image-off clients get no content. If these are the linked product tiles, the links will also have no accessible name.
- **The tracking pixel** (`/pub/as`) has no `alt`. It should carry `alt=""` and `width="1" height="1"`.
- **Zoom is disabled and the viewport meta is malformed.** The tag is `content="width=device-width; initial-scale=1.0; maximum-scale=1.0; user-scalable=no;"`. `user-scalable=no` and `maximum-scale=1.0` block pinch-zoom, which fails WCAG 1.4.4. Semicolons are also non-standard separators, since the spec uses commas.
- **Invalid CSS.** `.heightmobile { line-height: auto!important; }` is not a valid value, so the declaration is dropped.
- **External web font.** `<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Roboto+Condensed...">` is stripped by Gmail and Outlook. Rendering there relies on the fallback stack, and the only fallback visible in the truncated source is the Outlook-only `HelveticaNeue, Helvetica, Arial` rule.
