# PUKAS x ZARA
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-31**
## Executive summary

- Okay wait — I actually stopped scrolling when I saw "PUKAS x ZARA" in my inbox. After the chaos of Man emails, Zara Home patio furniture, and a Kids collab that had literally nothing to do with me, this one felt like Zara finally remembered who I am. Pukas is a Basque surf brand, and the "cold Atlantic surf girl" aesthetic has been everywhere on my TikTok FYP for weeks — the moody grey coast, the vintage van, the film-grain group shots on a breakwater. Zara didn't just hop on a trend here, they went and made a whole editorial film of it.
- That said — this email is almost entirely vibe. It's cinematic and beautiful, but I'm scrolling through nine editorial shots and there's not a single close-up of a specific piece, not a price, not a size hint, nothing that tells me whether this is a $35 linen set or a $120 outerwear collab. The "DISCOVER THE COLLECTION" button is doing all the heavy lifting for conversion and "Discover" is genuinely one of the weakest CTA verbs in fashion email.
- I would click — but I'd click out of trend-radar curiosity, not because this email sold me anything. I wouldn't forward it; I'd screenshot the van shot for my moodboard and then open the link alone.

## What's working

- **The collab framing landed immediately** — "PUKAS x ZARA" in the subject is the exact format I recognize from streetwear drops; I knew before I opened it that this was a real collab, not just a sale.
- **The cold Atlantic surf aesthetic is exactly on-trend right now** — film grain, overcast rocky coast, vintage van — this doesn't look like a Mediterranean summer ripoff, it looks like the actual TikTok aesthetic that's been running for three months.
- **Hero image hierarchy is strong** — the big "ZARA PUKAS" wordmark over the cliff diver is cinematic and stops the thumb. No render bugs, no broken images, no lorem ipsum anywhere.
- **Young, natural-looking cast** — the group shots on the harbour wall feel like a friend group's surf trip, not a polished catalogue shoot. That demographic signal hits for me specifically.
- **Timing is right** — surf season is opening, this dropped May 31, and Pukas has been on my radar. Zara read the room after yesterday's "Stripes, prints and vibrant colors" send finally got back on track.

## What's weak

- **Zero product detail visible in the entire email** — not one close-up, not a single garment name, no hint of price range. I finished the scroll knowing the vibe but not knowing what I'd actually be buying.
- **"DISCOVER THE COLLECTION" is the laziest CTA in fashion email** — the rubric literally calls this out. If H&M can write "SHOP THE RESORT EDIT" on their editorial sends, Zara can do better than a verb they've used on every collab since 2019.
- **The body copy is microscopic** — the intro paragraph and the "Made for endless summer days" line are so small they're basically decoration. On a phone, that's unreadable without a pinch-zoom.
- **No urgency signal anywhere** — no "limited collab," no "dropping now," no "available while stock lasts." If this is a limited collab (Pukas is a small-ish brand), that scarcity should be doing work here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one hero product shot with a name and price** — even a single styled flat lay with "Pukas x Zara Board Short, $49" would give me something to anchor to. The ASOS x Cotton On send from earlier this week at least had product shots. Editorial is great but it doesn't close.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA** — "SHOP THE DROP" or "GET THE COLLAB" are both more specific and more energy-matched to a collab drop. "Discover" is what you write when you're afraid to sell.
- 3. **Add a scarcity line near the CTA** — something like "Limited pieces, Pukas x Zara collab" or even a small "Limited run — ships now" banner. If it IS limited, say it. If it's not, still say when it ships.
- 4. **Increase the body copy size** — the intro copy and "Made for endless summer days" tagline should be legible without zooming. Right now they're functioning as decorative text.
- 5. **Surface one size/fit detail** — even "runs true to size" under the CTA would reduce friction for people who've been burned by Zara's sizing inconsistencies before.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed preheader structure (confirmed)**
A `<div>` element is inserted directly between the outer `<table>` and a `<tr>`, outside any `<td>`:
```html
<div style="mso-hide:all;">
  <tr>
    <td align="center" height="0" ...>
```
A `<div>` cannot be a direct child of `<table>` in valid HTML. Most clients will auto-repair this, but Outlook (Word rendering engine) is likely to misparse it and may duplicate or suppress the preheader row.

**`line-height: auto` is invalid CSS (confirmed)**
```css
.heightmobile { height: auto!important; line-height: auto!important; }
```
`auto` is not a valid value for `line-height`. Valid values: `normal`, a unitless number, a length, or a percentage. This declaration is silently discarded; affected mobile elements will inherit an unintended line-height.

**Viewport meta uses semicolon delimiters (confirmed)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width; initial-scale=1.0; maximum-scale=1.0; user-scalable=no;" />
```
The viewport spec requires comma-separated values, not semicolons. Most modern clients tolerate this, but it is non-conformant. Additionally, `maximum-scale=1.0; user-scalable=no` disables pinch-to-zoom, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is flagged by accessibility validators.

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>` (confirmed)**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">
```
No `lang` attribute. Screen readers cannot infer language for correct text-to-speech rendering. Should be `lang="en"` for this US/en send.

**External Google Fonts stylesheet (confirmed)**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Roboto+Condensed...">
```
Gmail (webmail), Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo block or ignore external `<link>` stylesheets. Roboto Condensed will not load in those clients; the stack falls back to system fonts. This is expected email behavior, but if the layout assumes a specific condensed-font width, it may reflow unexpectedly.
