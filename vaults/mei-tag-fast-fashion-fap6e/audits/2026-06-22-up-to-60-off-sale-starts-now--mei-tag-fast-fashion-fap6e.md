# Up to 60% off Sale starts now
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so ASOS drops a giant red hero with "SALE STARTS NOW UP TO 60% OFF" and honestly? The offer landed in under a second. No decode required, no scroll needed — that's exactly what a sale email should do visually and they nailed the execution. I'll give them that.
- But here's my thing: this is the same brand that sent me the Scandi edit on June 19 — white linen coords, actual editorial energy, a reason to care about the clothes. That email felt like ASOS had a point of view. This one feels like the clearance sign went up and someone hit send. "YOUR SALE EDIT" tries to editorialize the discount with three models, but I cannot tell you what the edit is *toward* — a beige jumpsuit, yellow co-ords, an athletic skirt, a flat sandal. What trend are we talking? Is linen having a moment? Is this coastal? There's no story.
- The double hero CTA (SHOP WOMEN'S / SHOP MEN'S) is also giving Zara "Man | Summer in Style" vibes, which I tanked at 4/10 for exactly this reason. ASOS at least leads women's first, so the damage is lighter — but the split still dilutes my focus at the most critical moment. I'd click to browse, definitely. Forward to a friend? No — there's nothing here I'd be like "oh you have to see this."

## What's working

- **The hero is genuinely unmissable** — bold red, massive sale text, immediate visual lock. I registered the offer before I registered the logo.
- **"YOUR SALE EDIT" with real models** is the right instinct — editorial context over a raw product dump is always better, even if the curation here is vague.
- **Category grid (TOPS / DRESSES / FOOTWEAR / SWIMWEAR / SKIRTS / SUMMER)** is actually useful. I can route myself instead of landing on a chaos browse page, which is more than H&M's sale emails give me.
- **"SHOP WOMEN'S" is the dominant CTA** — correct call for a majority-female list. It's bigger, it's first.
- **Clean render throughout** — no broken images, no layout collapse, no blank gaps. Floor cleared.

## What's weak

- **"Limited time only ↑" is the weakest urgency copy imaginable.** No end date, no countdown, nothing. Shein gives me "48 hours only" and I feel the clock. This feels like they forgot to fill in a variable.
- **The sale edit has no trend story.** I need a *why* — is linen finally affordable? Are the ballet flats back? The June 19 Scandi edit gave me a cultural anchor. This one just says "sale rack."
- **"SHOP MEN'S" splitting the hero is friction I don't need.** Every CTA I have to skip is a micro-annoyance. Drop it below the fold.
- **No specific hero product anywhere.** The category grid is anonymous. One "was £45, now £18" linen co-ord set would do more work than six category tiles combined.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a real deadline — the single biggest lift.** "Ends Sunday midnight" or "48 hours only" in the hero copy converts browsers into buyers. "Limited time only" is decorative at this point.
- 2. **Give the edit a trend anchor.** Rename "YOUR SALE EDIT" to something like "Summer's biggest trends. Now on sale." Pick linen, ballet flats, stripped denim — make me feel like I'm getting a moment, not a markdown.
- 3. **Remove "SHOP MEN'S" from the hero.** Move it to a secondary section. Don't split the first-impression CTA on a women's-skewing list.
- 4. **Feature one marquee deal above the fold.** Specific product, specific price drop, specific reason it's worth clicking *now* vs. in a week.
- 5. **Surface the ASOS Premier benefit in the hero** if Premier members get early access or free returns on sale — that's a conversion lever that's currently buried in the footer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `60% off starts NOW — shop before it's gone`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer trends just got a lot cheaper`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Linen, co-ords, sandals — the trends you actually want, finally on sale`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Ends Sunday. Women's sale edit is live.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Empty preheader.** The hidden preheader `<div>` (the `display:none` block after `<body>`) contains no text. Inbox clients will pull the first visible text as the preview snippet, which may surface boilerplate or navigation copy instead of intentional preheader messaging.

- **Duplicate charset declaration.** Both `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8">` and `<meta charset="utf-8">` are present. Redundant but harmless; parsers treat the first declaration as authoritative.

- **Mobile breakpoint at 460px.** The sole `@media` query fires at `max-width: 460px`. The de-facto safe threshold for modern devices is 480px; emails displayed on 461–479px viewports (some Android devices) will receive the desktop layout. Low risk, but worth aligning to 480px.

- **`font-size: 0em` on all table elements.** The global `table, tr, td, th { font-size: 0em }` rule suppresses phantom whitespace gaps in Outlook — standard practice — but will silently hide any text placed directly in `<td>` without an inner `<p>` or `<span>`. No issue if content follows the pattern; flag for QA if any table cells render blank unexpectedly.

No issues with VML Outlook conditional (`<!--[if gt mso 15]>`), web font fallback chain (`mso-font-alt: Arial`), or `-webkit-text-size-adjust` suppression — all correctly implemented.

**Cannot assess:** Image `alt` text coverage and `role`/`aria` attributes are in the truncated body.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**High severity — confirmed failure.**

```html
<title>Up to 60% off Sale starts now, </title>
```

The `<title>` ends with a trailing comma and space: `"...starts now, "`. The intended output was almost certainly `"Up to 60% off Sale starts now, [First Name]"` — the first-name merge token was not replaced at send time and was silently dropped rather than falling back to empty or a default value.

**Impact:** The dangling comma is visible in:
- Browser tab / webmail title bar when the email is opened in a browser
- Some assistive technology screen readers that read the document title on load
- Any downstream system (CRM, analytics) that scrapes the `<title>` for email metadata

**Root cause options:** Token syntax mismatch at render time, missing subscriber attribute with no fallback defined, or a template preview sent without a live data merge. Verify the ESP render log for this send batch.
