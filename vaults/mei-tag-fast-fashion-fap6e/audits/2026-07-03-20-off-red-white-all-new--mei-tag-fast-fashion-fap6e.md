# 20% off red, white & all new
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- I am genuinely begging H&M to check their calendar — not for the holiday, but for their own sent folder. Yesterday I got "Stars, stripes & 20% off sitewide." Today I'm getting "20% off red, white & all new." It is the same email wearing a different hat. Same number, same sitewide flag, same July 4th cosplay, just reworded in the subject line. I opened it because the "& all new" teased something fresh might actually be dropping, and what I got was a product catalog that scrolled for approximately forever. Women, Kids, Men, Home — category boxes, grid after grid, individual product tiles with prices. No trend hero. No single "this is THE thing to buy this weekend" moment.
- The email is clean and technically functional — no broken images, no weird overlaps, the 20% off messaging is right at the top so at least the offer is legible. But this is H&M's fourth email in three days and at minimum their third 20% off angle in a row. I'm not clicking this. I'm not forwarding it. I'm honestly considering the unsubscribe button, which I did NOT feel after Tuesday's "Warm-weather wardrobe heroes" send — that one had editorial energy. This feels like the brand handed the keys to the automation tool.
- Mark as read. Move on.

## What's working

- **The offer is immediately visible** — "20% OFF SITEWIDE" is above the fold, no hunting required, which is more than ASOS pulled off in their "Guess who's back?" send last week
- **Category navigation is genuinely useful** — WOMEN / KIDS / MEN / HOME boxes let me skip to my section instead of doomscrolling past toddler tees
- **No broken images or layout chaos** — technically clean render, which Zara somehow still manages to fumble with their mobile lookbook stack
- **Seasonal hook is present** — the red-toned banner at least attempts to justify why this email exists today vs. any other Thursday

## What's weak

- **This is literally yesterday's email** — "Stars, stripes & 20% off sitewide" sent on July 3rd scored a 8/10 here, and this is the same offer with synonymous subject copy. No new creative rationale.
- **No trend anchor** — there is no single "HERE is the piece" hero moment. It's a product dump. Compare to the July 2nd "Warm-weather wardrobe heroes" send that actually surfaced the botanical crop set with editorial framing — that had a reason to exist
- **The "& all new" promise is hollow** — the subject dangled new arrivals and the body is a sitewide catalog. I can't identify what's actually new vs. what's been sitting on the site for two weeks
- **Cadence is collapsing trust** — four H&M emails in three days, three of them on essentially the same 20% off angle. Zara does the "Woman | Discover what's new" repeat offense too, but at least they space it out. This is starting to feel like spam

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick ONE hero product and make the email about it** — the July 2nd send proved H&M can do editorial. Show me the red linen wide-leg pant or whatever is actually trending right now, then attach the 20% to it. The offer earns more when it's attached to desire, not a sitewide dump.
- 2. **Kill the duplicate send** — if you already sent 20% off yesterday with a July 4th angle, today's email should be differentiated creative or held until Monday. One strong send beats two diluted ones every time.
- 3. **Surface "new arrivals" as its own module** — if "all new" is in the subject it should appear as a labeled section in the email, not buried in a catalog grid I have to decode myself.
- 4. **Add a deadline** — "20% off sitewide" floating with no end date has zero urgency. "Ends tonight at midnight" or "through July 4th only" costs nothing and meaningfully changes click behavior.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The new drops just landed — 20% off today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Fresh arrivals, long weekend prices — 20% off sitewide`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New pieces added. Your 20% off code works on all of it — ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Not a repeat. New styles, same 20% — see what dropped this week.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` for web fonts — will fail in Outlook and some mobile clients**

```html
<style>
  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
</style>
```

`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is unsupported in Outlook (all versions) and stripped by several webmail clients. Affected recipients fall back to system fonts, which may misalign spacing-sensitive layouts. Mitigation: inline `@font-face` declarations directly inside the `<style>` block instead of using `@import`.

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>` tag**

```html
<html style="border:0;margin:0;outline:0;padding:0">
```

No `lang="en"` (or locale-appropriate equivalent) is declared. Screen readers default to the OS language, which causes incorrect pronunciation for non-matching locales. WCAG 2.1 §3.1.1 requires this.

**`<script>` tag present**
