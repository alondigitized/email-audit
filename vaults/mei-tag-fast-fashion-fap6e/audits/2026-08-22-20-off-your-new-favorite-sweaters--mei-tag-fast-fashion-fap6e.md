# 20% off your new favorite sweaters
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-22**
## Executive summary

- Okay so it's another H&M sweater email, and my brain immediately went "wait didn't I already get this?" — because I literally reviewed "20% off your order" from H&M yesterday (2026-08-21), same $125 threshold, same free shipping, and now today it's the identical offer just reskinned around sweaters. The cable-knit cardigan hero shot is genuinely cute, I'll give them that, it's giving cozy-girl-fall and the texture reads well even as a small jpeg. But this is H&M's fourth-ish send this week hitting basically the same percentage-off beat, and I'm starting to tune it out the way I do with a group chat that won't stop buzzing.
- The layout itself is clean — I like that the discount is the actual hero, bold and unmissable at the top, no scavenger hunt required. But "sweater weather" in a subject line on August 22nd feels like a stretch when it's still summer heat where I am, and there's zero trend language, zero "this is going viral" energy, nothing that tells me why THIS cardigan is the one everyone's about to be wearing.
- Verdict: I'm not opening this with excitement, I've seen the offer structure too many times this week. I wouldn't forward it to a friend — there's no single hero product worth texting about, it's just "sweaters exist, here's a coupon." I'd skim and probably archive.

## What's working

- The $125 threshold + free shipping combo is a real, legible offer — no math homework required, unlike Zara's menswear-only send that didn't even bother with a discount amount.
- The cardigan hero image has actual texture and mood, it doesn't look like stock catalog filler.
- Category buttons (Women/Men/Baby/Kids) are big, tappable, no pinch-zoom required.
- "Buy online, pick up in-store" up top is a nice honesty flex — no shipping-time guessing game like some brands bury.

## What's weak

- This is the same 20%-off-$125-plus-free-shipping structure as H&M's own "Weekend deal" email from yesterday — I'm getting fatigue from my own inbox at this point.
- Zero trend signaling — no "as seen on TikTok," no influencer tag, nothing that tells me this cardigan is a moment versus just... a cardigan. ASOS's Lioness drop at least had a mood and a strut to it.
- "Sweater weather" this early in the season reads a little tone-deaf, it's giving corporate-calendar energy not real-girl energy.
- The kid model in the ruffled sweater randomly interrupts the "cozy adult knitwear" vibe right under the hero — feels like a merchandising afterthought, not a styled moment.
- No urgency — no countdown, no "ends Sunday," just a vague expiration buried way down in the fine print at the bottom.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop reusing the exact same $125/free-shipping structure two days in a row — rotate the offer type (BOGO, flat $, first-timer bonus) so it doesn't read as the same coupon reskinned.
- 2. Add a trend hook to the copy — "the cardigan everyone's restocking" or a TikTok-style callout — H&M has zero trend-first energy right now and ASOS is eating that lane.
- 3. Move the kid's sweater photo out of the adult hero block entirely, or split into two clearly separate sections so it doesn't dilute the "cozy fall for me" moment.
- 4. Add a real deadline with a date, not buried fine print — something like "Ends Sunday" near the hero.
- 5. Feature one specific product with a name/price, not just a category grid — give me something to actually click toward.

## Technical Email Audit — H&M "20% off your new favorite sweaters"

**1. Technical Summary**
The email uses an Adobe Campaign-style redirect tracker (`t19.email.hm.com`) for all links and images, but every probed redirect returns HTTP 403, and the plain-text alternative is almost entirely URL noise — indicating a broken or misconfigured tracking/redirect layer rather than an isolated link issue.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- **All 12 probed material links return 403**, including the primary CTA ("Shop now" ×2), the BOPIS link, header logo, nav ("Women"), and both promo image links. Pattern: `https://t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=he095a155-...&did=1715370698&rid=3326930539&erid=...`. A uniform 403 across every link ID under the same `did`/`rid` suggests the redirect/click-tracking domain itself is rejecting requests (bot/UA filtering misconfigured, expired signed token, or the click tracker is down) rather than 12 independent broken destinations.
- The **Unsubscribe link is also part of the same broken redirect chain** and returns 403 — see Compliance below.
- Several link text values contain zero-width/invisible characters (`‌  ﻿Shop now`, U+200C/U+FEFF), which is a known ESP artifact but worth flagging since it can affect link-text-based automation/QA parsing and screen reader announcement.
- Image click-through links also route through the same tracker and 403 (`cq5dam.web.1400.1400.jpeg`, `cq5dam.web.750.750.jpeg` variants), meaning tapping any product image is currently broken.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- **12 images missing `alt` text**, including the H&M logo redirect image and all product photography (`1354431001_202604_LB_2016.jpg`, `1346499001_202604_LB_0101.jpg`, `1316649004_202603_LB_0551.jpg`, `NA1536_FNP_1365658002_202604_...jpg` at both 1400px and 750px variants). Screen reader / images-off users get no context for these.

- Responsive breakpoint CSS (`@media (max-width:699px)`) is present and structured normally — no issues found.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
- The embedded `application/ld+json` `EmailMessage`/`DiscountOffer` schema has **empty values for `subjectLine`, `discountCode`, `availabilityEnds`, `image`, and `url`** — these Gmail-annotation fields are unpopulated, meaning Gmail's promo card / "view offer" markup enhancements will not render correctly even though the visible HTML contains a real 20% offer.
- `availabilityStarts` is hardcoded to `"2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"` — a stale/placeholder date left in from a template, not updated for this send.
- No visible `{{merge_tag}}`-style unresolved tokens in the visible HTML.

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
- **Unsubscribe link is broken (403)** — the same tracker failure affecting other links also affects the unsubscribe URL (`...f7e0d246...&p5=9be06746af74de7271d9dcc8d83f5742`). This is a functional CAN-SPAM risk: the mechanism exists in markup but is not reachable.
- **`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** and **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not detected** (RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe). Without these, mailbox providers can't offer native one-click unsubscribe, which increasingly affects deliverability/spam placement under Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender rules. Note: QA flags this may be a relay-side capture limitation (AgentMail) rather than confirmed absence at origin — worth re-verifying against raw headers if available.
- **`Authentication-Results` header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status could not be confirmed via this relay.

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
- No UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are present in the tracked links — H&M's Adobe Campaign redirector uses proprietary params (`did`, `rid`, `erid`, `p1`–`p5`) instead. Whether these map to UTM-equivalents on the landing side can't be confirmed here since all redirects 403 before reaching a destination URL — continuity cannot be verified end-to-end.
- Cannot assess landing page alignment (product grid, promo code application) because every link in the chain fails before redirecting to `hm.com`.

**7. Recommendations**
1. Investigate the `t19.email.hm.com` redirect service — a blanket 403 across all 12 distinct link IDs under one send (`did=1715370698`) points to a systemic issue (expired signing key, WAF/bot rule, or outage) rather than per-link errors; prioritize as send-blocking, especially since it breaks unsubscribe.
2. Add `alt` attributes to all 12 flagged images, particularly the logo and product images, for accessibility and images-blocked rendering.
3. Populate the empty `ld+json` fields (`subjectLine`, `discountCode`, `availabilityEnds`, `image`, `url`) or remove the schema block if Gmail promo annotations aren't being used — an empty/stale schema is worse than none.
4. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are actually being sent at origin (re-check with a direct SMTP capture, not just the relay) to support RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe.
5. Reduce plain-text URL density (520/598 chars, 87%) by shortening or consolidating tracked links in the text alternative to lower spam-filter risk.
6. Strip stray zero-width characters (U+200C/U+FEFF) from link text where present.
