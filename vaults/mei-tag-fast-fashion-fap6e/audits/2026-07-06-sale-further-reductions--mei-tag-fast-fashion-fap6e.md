# Sale: further reductions
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- Okay so ASOS is back again — that's six days, five sale emails. I recognized the sender before I even read the subject line, and my first reaction was literally a sigh. "Further reductions" sounds like a markdown clearance rack at a department store closing, not a trend-first fashion destination. When I opened it the hero is clean — yellow background, "SALE: UP TO 70% OFF" in big bold type — but that's the exact same headline they ran on June 29th. Eight days ago. I've been watching this number like a stock ticker and it hasn't moved. The July 4th email teased "30% extra off" on top of that, so mathematically the clearance should be deeper now, but the email doesn't actually say that. "Further" with no new percentage is just marketing filler.
- I'm not clicking today. Not because the sale isn't real — I'm sure it is — but because nothing in this email told me *why today* is different from last Sunday or the Sunday before that. It feels like ASOS copy-pasted the shell of their June 29 email, swapped the subject line, and called it a new campaign. Zara does the same lazy rerun thing (their "Discover what's new" appeared twice in two weeks with no changes), but at least Zara has enough brand mystique that I'll open out of habit. Here, the creative is just tired.
- I'd mark this as read, not delete it — there's clearly still a sale happening and I might go back to browse on my own terms. But I'm not clicking any CTA right now and I'm definitely not forwarding it.

## What's working

- **Hero hierarchy is instant.** Yellow background + black bold type = I clocked "70% OFF" before I finished blinking. No ambiguity.
- **Model diversity is present.** I can see at least two different models in the "These Styles Suit You" grid — one is a Black woman, there's a blonde — and the styling skews young and summery. That's me-adjacent.
- **"These Styles Suit You" framing.** The personalization language here is doing actual work even if I can't verify the algorithm behind it. It feels like they're trying to talk to me specifically, not just blast a homepage grid.
- **Brand logos in "Shop Our Top Brands."** Seeing Adidas, Levi's, New Balance — recognizable names — adds legitimacy to the "up to 70%" claim. It's not just ASOS white-label, there's real product in there.

## What's weak

- **"Further reductions" with the same 70% number.** If you're going *further*, show me a new number. July 4th had "30% extra off," which implied even deeper cuts — this email should be landing on something like 75% or 80%, not repeating the June 29 hero verbatim.
- **Six identical black buttons, no hierarchy.** "Shop Women's," "New Lines Added," "Selling Fast," "Summer Sale," "Biggest Deals" — they're all the same size, same color, same weight. My eye doesn't know where to go after the hero. H&M, even with their 20%-off fatigue, at least picks one clear primary CTA.
- **"Download the App" green button at the bottom.** This is a jarring pivot. I'm mid-browse in a sale email and suddenly there's a green button asking me to switch platforms. That's the brand's retention play, not mine.
- **Cadence is exhausting.** This is email five in eight days from the same ASOS Sale sender. Even Zara, who does the same "new" subject weekly, spaces it out enough that I don't feel hunted.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Quantify "further."** If the reductions are genuinely deeper than June 29, put the new number in the hero. "Now up to 75% off" or "an extra 10% drops today" gives me a reason to click *now* instead of whenever I feel like it.
- 2. **Pick one CTA and make it primary.** Black button for women's shoppers, ghost/outline button for everything else. Right now the button grid reads as noise. Even H&M's over-emailed 20%-off sends have a clear single-tap entry point.
- 3. **Drop the "Download the App" mid-email placement.** Move it to the footer or make it contextual ("get push notifications when new sale drops"). Interrupting a sale browse moment with an app pitch kills momentum.
- 4. **Add a deadline.** "Limited time only" is the weakest urgency phrase in email marketing. "Ends Sunday" or "48 hours left" would actually make me act.
- 5. **Reference the escalation in copy.** You've been stacking discounts for a week — *say that*. "We've been dropping prices since June 29. Here's where we landed." That's a story, and it gives repeat openers (like me) a reason to believe today is different.

- **Subject Alt B:** `New price drops: check what moved`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Selected styles just got marked down further — here's what's new in your size`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh cuts on top brands. Ends Sunday.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**SVG font format in `@font-face` stack — no upside, potential extra request:**
```css
url("https://images.asos-media.com/notification/content/fonts/FuturaPT-Book.svg") format("svg")
```
SVG fonts are deprecated and unsupported in every current email client. This entry triggers a potentially wasted HTTP request on some older WebKit clients before falling through to TTF. Remove it from both font declarations.

**`font-display: swap` in email CSS:**
```css
font-display: swap;
```
Supported only in Gmail (Chromium-based webmail) and a handful of webmail clients. In native iOS Mail, Outlook, and Samsung Mail it is silently ignored. Not harmful, but it adds noise and implies a rendering behavior that won't occur in most clients.

**Mobile breakpoint at `460px` is too narrow:**
```css
@media screen and (max-width:460px) { ... }
```
Standard modern email frameworks use `600px` or at minimum `480px`. Viewports between 460–600px (common on mid-range Android devices at 1x zoom) receive the full desktop layout. All mobile-responsive class overrides (`mob100`, `nodesktop`, `nomob`, etc.) will fail to trigger on these devices.

**`-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased !important` on universal selector:**
```css
* { -webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased !important; }
```
Applying this globally with `!important` can affect button and image rendering in Apple Mail and iOS in unexpected ways. Should be scoped to text elements only.
