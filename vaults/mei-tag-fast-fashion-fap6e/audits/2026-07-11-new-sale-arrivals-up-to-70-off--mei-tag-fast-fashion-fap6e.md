# New sale arrivals up to 70% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- Okay so H&M is in my inbox again — sixth time in recent memory — and this one opens with "UP TO 70% OFF + STYLES UNDER $6.99" in big bold type. That hero stopped my thumb for a second, I won't lie. The two-part offer structure (percentage off AND a floor price) is a real combo. But then I kept scrolling and there's a full "BACK-TO-SCHOOL BUNDLE" giveaway inserted in the middle, and it's July 11. That whiplash is real. This feels like two separate campaign briefs got merged into one send, and neither one fully lands.
- The bigger issue is that "$6.99" — H&M literally used that exact price point in the July 8 email "New sale price drops from $6.99." Three days later it's back as an anchor. That's not a sale, that's the same markdown wearing a new subject line. And the whole email has zero trend language. It's just: sale, giveaway, category nav, footer. ASOS's recent extra-30%-off email had cleaner funnel direction and at least *felt* like it knew what I care about. This feels like a broadcast to everyone on the list.
- I'd click WOMEN out of muscle memory, look for two minutes, and close. Not forwarding to anyone.

## What's working

- Hero offer is the first thing my eye hits — "UP TO 70% OFF + STYLES UNDER $6.99" is loud, clean, and two-pronged which makes it feel like a real value moment
- The model is a young woman in a dark casual set — demographically she looks like she's 22-25, which is actually right for me, unlike Zara's recent Men's email where I genuinely double-checked I opened the right send
- H&M's monochrome layout keeps the sale copy winning with no visual noise competing against it
- "MEMBER GIVEAWAY" badge on the Back-to-School section reads as a loyalty perk — I notice being a member has upside, even if the timing is off
- The category nav (WOMEN / MEN / KIDS / HOME / BEAUTY) lets me route myself without guessing

## What's weak

- "$6.99" is a recycled anchor — it was the headline of the July 8 "New sale price drops from $6.99" email; three days later it's back and I clocked it immediately
- "BACK-TO-SCHOOL BUNDLE" giveaway crashing a summer sale email is jarring — this is two campaigns stitched into one and the seam is visible
- No trend hook anywhere — the word "trend" or any specific style callout is absent; this could've been sent in August 2023 and nothing would've changed
- No deadline on the 70% off offer — "UP TO 70% OFF" floating without an end date doesn't create urgency, it just makes me think I'll wait
- Email runs long — by the time I hit the SHOP BY tiles and DOWNLOAD APP section I'm gone mentally

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a deadline on the hero** — "UP TO 70% OFF — ENDS SUNDAY" creates urgency that the current hero completely lacks; this is the single highest-impact change
- 2. **Drop the Back-to-School giveaway into its own send** — that's a separate story with a separate audience hook; it's diluting the sale message and making this email feel unfocused
- 3. **Trade the $6.99 anchor for a specific trend callout** — "70% off the linen cargo sets" or "70% off dopamine-dressing basics" gives me a reason to click beyond generic discount math
- 4. **Add one prominent SHOP SALE button in the hero** — the category nav works as navigation but it's not a CTA; the first tap-target after the hero offer should go directly into the sale landing page
- 5. **Cut the DOWNLOAD APP and STORES & SERVICE blocks** — these are footer-level utility links that are eating scroll depth on a sale email

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` in `<style>` blocks (high risk):**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` is unsupported in Outlook (all versions), Yahoo Mail, and several mobile clients. Custom fonts will silently fall back to system fonts — acceptable only if the fallback stack is defined. No fallback font-family is visible in the truncated `<head>` CSS.

**Responsive breakpoint is non-standard:**
- `@media (max-width:699px)` — industry standard for mobile email is `600px`. At 601–699px (many tablet viewports) the mobile layout applies but was likely designed for 375px screens. Risk of layout overflow at mid-range widths.

**`img.image + div { display: none !important; }`** — suppresses alt-text containers in some clients; valid technique, but if images fail to load the alt text fallback is also hidden. Accessibility impact on image-blocked renders.

**Alt text** — cannot audit without full body source.
