# Go from city to coast with Cotton On
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-31**
## Executive summary

- Wait — I opened what I thought was my ASOS email and I'm staring at a full Cotton On takeover? Like, the logo, the branding, the CTA, it's all Cotton On. ASOS is my sender but the entire email is for a different brand. That's a weird energy — it's giving "ASOS is renting me out as an ad slot" and something in my brain immediately went sideways. Like, did I accidentally open a sponsored post?
- To be fair though, the visuals are doing serious work. The sunset gradient backgrounds on all three looks — that purple-golden-hour sky? That's pulled straight from a coastal TikTok aesthetic that's genuinely running right now. The white mini dress, the burgundy bandeau with light shorts — both pieces are exactly what's circulating on my FYP. The hero model with the curly natural hair is the kind of face I see on indie editorial accounts, not stock photography. Points for that.
- But there's zero offer in this whole email. Not even a "members save extra" nudge. This is the ninth ASOS email I've gotten in eleven days and this one doesn't include a deal? I'd scroll through it on a Saturday during my three-tab compare, but I'm not clicking through today. Not forwarding this to anyone.

## What's working

- **Sunset aesthetic is completely on-trend** — the warm purple-gold backgrounds read coastal TikTok summer, not generic "it's warm outside" filler
- **Product selection lands for right now** — white mini dress and bandeau-plus-shorts both have real FYP momentum this week; these aren't wishlist throwaways
- **Hero model casting feels authentic** — natural textured hair, natural look, doesn't feel like a diversity checkbox, feels like someone I'd actually follow
- **Layout is tight and clean** — three product shots, no render bugs, readable without zooming, visual hierarchy drops me on the hero immediately
- **Subject line is mobile-safe** — 37 characters, no truncation, no spam gimmicks

## What's weak

- **No offer, at all** — this is a brand awareness play dressed up as an action email; after eight prior sends with deals, landing on a no-discount editorial send breaks the pattern contract
- **Cotton On bait-and-switch on the sender line** — I opened ASOS, I got Cotton On; ASOS is functioning as a media vehicle here, not a retailer, and that chips away at trust
- **"Download the App" button hijacks the scroll** — a giant yellow app CTA appears right after the fashion content and completely kills the coastal mood; H&M's LA Escape email kept secondary CTAs tucked in the footer where they belong
- **No trend editorial angle** — zero language about *why* Cotton On *right now*; Zara's "Stripes, prints and vibrant colors" gave me a whole Italian summer story that made me feel like Zara owned the trend; this is just "brand + season" with no POV
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — nine emails in eleven days means even a solid send starts reading as noise; this one needed to earn its spot harder

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a real offer** — even "Cotton On exclusive: extra 15% for ASOS members" turns this from awareness into action; the "Going somewhere?" email from May 26 worked because it gave me a reason to shop in the moment, not just a reason to look
- 2. **Tell me why Cotton On is happening *today*** — "The coastal summer formula is all over TikTok right now — Cotton On figured it out first" gives me the trend hook I need; make ASOS feel like the curator, not the billboard
- 3. **Strip the app download CTA out of the editorial section** — move it to the footer or remove it entirely; the context switch from sunset fashion to "DOWNLOAD THE APP" is jarring
- 4. **Own the brand collab framing upfront** — something like "We pulled the best of Cotton On for your summer" at the top turns the Cotton On reveal from confusing to intentional
- 5. **Pull back on email frequency** — one strong, well-timed send beats nine that blur together; even a 9/10 email loses potency when I've already seen eight others from the same sender this week

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate charset declaration**
Both `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8">` and `<meta charset="utf-8">` are present. Redundant but harmless; one should be removed for cleanliness.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust` conflict**
```css
body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }   /* disables scaling */
*    { -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; }   /* re-enables it */
```
The `*` rule overrides the `body` rule on all child elements. The `none` on `body` is effectively dead. Intent appears to be `none` (suppress iOS font size inflation) — the `*` selector should be audited.

**`FuturaPT-Heavy` weight mismatch**
```css
@font-face { font-family: "FuturaPT-Heavy"; font-weight: 600; }
```
The face is named "Heavy" but declared at weight `600` (semi-bold). True heavy/black weight is `800`–`900`. If any selector requests `font-weight: 800` on this family, the browser may synthesize bold rather than serving the intended face.

**Alt text on images** — not assessable from truncated source. Must verify all `<img>` tags carry descriptive `alt` attributes; the base CSS sets `img { display: block; border: none; }` with no alt enforcement.

**Media query breakpoint**: 460px is non-standard (industry norm is 600px). Intentional for ASOS's design system but worth documenting if this template is shared across brands.
