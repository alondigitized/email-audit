# Up to 50% off sitewide ends tonight
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- Okay so I need to say this upfront: this is the second H&M email I've opened today. The other one this morning said "Last day: Up to 50% off almost everything." This one says "Up to 50% off sitewide ends tonight." Same sale. Same urgency. Same day. That's not a cadence, that's a panic spiral.
- The email itself is clean — the denim editorial is genuinely good, the model looks like she shops the same trends I do, and "LAST DAY" in that little badge above the hero type is doing real work. If this were the *first* time H&M had shown me this offer this week, I'd probably click through to the Women's section right now. But we are on round three (at least) of the "50% off sitewide" message in the past seven days, and the novelty is completely gone. It's starting to feel like H&M is just screaming into the void hoping someone blinks.
- I'd mark this read, maybe click to the Women's sale page if I'm bored, and I would absolutely not forward it — because my friends have already gotten this same email too. Hard pass on the share.

## What's working

- **The hero image lands.** Denim corset top + wide-leg jeans is genuinely a trend moment right now and the model looks like she's actually my age, not a 35-year-old in a catalog.
- **"LAST DAY" badge + "UP TO 50% OFF ALMOST EVERYTHING" in all-caps is impossible to miss.** Visual hierarchy is doing exactly what it should — eye goes straight there.
- **The member perk at the very top.** "Free shipping over $50 for H&M members" is small but smart placement — reminds me why being on their list is worth it.
- **"Transitional layers" copy is seasonally on.** It's July, fall is actually dropping soon, and framing the sale as a seasonal wardrobe moment vs. just "we need to move units" is a nice creative call.

## What's weak

- **Second email today. Same offer.** This is exhausting. ASOS at least gave me a new code (XTRA30) when they reran their extra 30% off play last week — H&M is just copy-pasting the same message with a different subject line.
- **BABY and HOME in the navigation.** I'm 24, I don't have kids, I rent a room. Seeing BABY as the third tab in a fashion email is a vibe kill that makes me feel like I'm on the wrong list.
- **No product specificity.** The "SHOP BY" section is just category tiles — WOMEN, MEN, KIDS, HOME, BEAUTY. Nothing says "this viral linen set is 50% off" or "the wide-leg jeans from the hero are in stock in your size." Zara's emails frustrate me for different reasons but at least "Discover what's new" gestures at something specific even when it fails.
- **Dense legal text at the bottom.** The fine-print block at the end is a wall of tiny text that feels like H&M is hedging on "almost everything" without telling me what's actually excluded. That "up to 50%" qualifier stings more when you can see the asterisks from space.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the double-send on the same day.** If the morning email said "Last day," the evening email needs to either be gone or be urgency escalation with a countdown timer visual, not a rephrased version of the same subject. Pick one send or give me new info.
- 2. **Drop BABY from the women's fashion email.** Segment this or at minimum bury the family categories below the fold. The Women's + Beauty section should own the whole email for a subscriber like me.
- 3. **Lead with one hero product, not just a person.** Put the specific wide-leg jeans or the denim top from the editorial in the email with a price and "was $X, now $Y" — that's what actually makes me tap. ASOS does this way better even on their lazy sends.
- 4. **Replace the fine-print wall with a one-liner exclusion notice.** "Excludes new arrivals, third-party brands, and selected items" — that's it. The current block looks like they're trying to hide something.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading will fail in most clients:**
```html
<style>
  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
</style>
```
`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is stripped by Gmail (web), Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo Mail. These clients will fall back to system fonts. If the design depends on brand typography, fonts must be embedded via `@font-face` declarations directly in the `<style>` block, with system-font stacks as fallbacks.

**Viewport meta missing `initial-scale`:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width"/>
```
Should be `content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1"`. Some Android WebView-based clients zoom out without `initial-scale=1`.

**Mobile breakpoint hardcoded to iPhone-specific width:**
```css
.footer-table{width:375px!important}
.header-menu-container{width:375px!important}
```
375px is the iPhone SE/8/X logical width. Samsung Galaxy S-series and most Android flagships are 360–412px. Elements will either overflow or show unexpected whitespace on non-Apple devices.

**Redundant/conflicting CSS declarations in media query:**
- `.department-title` declared twice with different `font-size`/`line-height` values (24px/30px first, then 16px/20px).
- `.set_font_heading_xlarge` declared three times in the same `@media (max-width:699px)` block with identical values — dead weight that increases parse time.
- `.cta-link-mobile-border-ns` and `.cta-link-mobile` each declared twice.

**Potential hidden-content accessibility issue:**
```css
img.image + div { display: none !important; }
```
This hides any `<div>` immediately following an `img.image` element. If that div contains alt-text fallback content or accessible descriptions, screen readers will skip it. Verify the hidden siblings are purely decorative.

**JSON-LD structured data has a 2018 hardcoded date — a confirmed bug:**
```json
{
  "@type": "DiscountOffer",
  "availabilityStarts": "2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00",
  "availabilityEnds": "",
  ...
}
```
- `availabilityStarts` is `2018-12-31` — a template placeholder that was never updated. Gmail's Promotions tab uses this to display deal timing; a 2018 date will either suppress the annotation or display incorrect information.
- `availabilityEnds` is **empty string** — for an email whose subject line is "ends tonight," this field must contain tonight's UTC cutoff timestamp. Gmail cannot surface the deal card without it.
- `"subjectLine": ""` in the `EmailMessage` schema is also empty, preventing Gmail from matching it to the actual subject for annotation display.
