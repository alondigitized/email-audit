# 40% OFF selected items
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-26**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one is just a sale announcement, and Zara didn't try to dress it up. The hero is three black-and-white models in dark coats. A woman is on the left, a man is in the middle, and a kid in a puffer is on the right. Then there's a giant red "40% OFF" and nothing else. I get the offer in two seconds, which I like. But there isn't one actual product in the whole email. Not one trend, not one price, not one thing I could tap and add to a cart.
- I'm 24 and I do my three-tab compare every Saturday. This gives me nothing to compare. H&M sent me "Up to 60% off EVERYTHING" and at least that has a bigger number and a vibe. Zara's "selected items" could mean anything. Would I click? Maybe one of the category links, out of curiosity, because it's Black Friday week. Would I forward it? No. There's nothing to send.

## What's working

- The 40% OFF is huge, red and impossible to miss. The offer reads before I even scroll.
- The 5PM ET online start and the 11/27 store date are specific. That's a real deadline, not fake urgency.
- The monochrome hero with the red ZARA logo looks expensive and on-brand. It feels more editorial than H&M's loaf of bread.
- The category row (Woman, Man, Kids, Beauty, Massimo Dutti) gets me to my section in one tap.

## What's weak

- There are zero products and no "shop now" button. The only actual button-ish thing is "Download our app" at the bottom, and I'm not downloading an app for this.
- "Selected items" tells me nothing. I don't know if the trend pieces I'm watching are included, or if it's last season's leftovers.
- The "starting time may vary a few minutes due to technical reasons" line is tiny and feels sloppy next to a hard 5PM ET deadline.
- The last nav item, "MASSIMO DUTTI", looks cut off at the right edge. It's the only visible render glitch.
- This is the fourth Zara send since 11/17 that isn't aimed at me. Two weeks ago it was menswear, then the kids sends, and now there's a kid and a man in the hero too. Only the "Woman | The weekly edit" on 11/20 felt like it was talking to me.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put 3–4 real women's pieces under the headline, with sale prices and a "Shop women's sale" button. Right now the email gives me a number but not a reason to click.
- 2. Say which categories are in the sale. "Coats, knits, party" is better than "selected items".
- 3. Lead with a woman-only hero if the subscriber's history is women's. I've opened the women's emails, and the kids and menswear sends can go to other people.
- 4. Fix the cut-off nav item and move the app download below a real CTA.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible markup is a Responsys-built XHTML 1.0 table layout with a mobile media query and an MSO fallback. QA flagged one FAIL (no plain-text part) and 4 warnings. Nine images lack alt text and there are markup and accessibility defects. The HTML was truncated, so links, footer, unsubscribe and UTM parameters could not be checked.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Not verifiable.** The source is cut off after the preheader, so I can't audit CTA hrefs, redirect wrappers or the footer links.
- **Tracking pixel has no alt.** `https://news.zara.com/pub/as?_ri_=X0Gzc2X%3D...` is the Responsys open pixel. QA lists it as image "as" with no alt. This is low severity, but it should have `alt=""` and explicit `width="1" height="1"`.
- **Tracking token in URL.** The `_ri_` parameter is a long recipient-scoped token. Forwarding the email exposes it.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Invalid table structure.** `<div style="mso-hide:all;">` wraps a `<tr>` directly inside `<table width="640" class="width90">`. A `<div>` is not a valid child of `<table>`, so clients may reflow or drop the preheader row. Use `<tr><td><div style="mso-hide:all">…` instead.
- **Zoom disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width; initial-scale=1.0; maximum-scale=1.0; user-scalable=no;">` blocks pinch-zoom, which fails WCAG 1.4.4. The semicolon separators are also non-standard; commas are the spec.
- **Missing alt text (9 images, per QA):**
  - `image_01b.jpg`
  - `image-woman-en-us-us_0.jpg`
  - `menu_v5d_01b_en.jpg` through `menu_v5d_05b_en.jpg`
  - `download_en_b.jpg`
  - the `pub/as` pixel

  The `menu_v5d_*` images look like navigation, so with images blocked or a screen reader the nav is unusable.
- **No `lang` attribute.** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang`/`xml:lang`, although the campaign is `US_en`.
- **Low link contrast.** The global rule `a { color: #999898; }` on a `#ffffff` background is about 2.9:1, below the 4.5:1 AA minimum. Inline overrides could not be checked because the source is truncated.
- **Tiny mobile text classes.** `.font5` (5px), `.font7` (7px) and `.font8` (8px) are defined in the media query. I couldn't confirm where they're applied.
- **Web font via `<link>`.** The Google Fonts `<link>` (Roboto Condensed) is stripped by Gmail and Outlook. The Outlook-only `font-family: Arial` override covers MSO, but no fallback stack is visible for other clients. The `<link>` is also not self-closed under the XHTML doctype.
- **Internal comments left in production HTML.** Examples are `CAMPAIGN_CODE:zara;2026_11_25_norte_promo_bf_prewarming;US_en` and the Spanish macro comments. This is low severity and adds payload and leaks internal naming.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens such as `{{ }}`, `%%`, `$!` or `[[ ]]` appear in the visible source. The macro comments are inert, though the truncated body could not be checked.

## 5. Compliance

- **[WARN] No physical address detected.** CAN-SPAM requires one. This is unconfirmed because the footer is truncated, so re-check against the full HTML.
