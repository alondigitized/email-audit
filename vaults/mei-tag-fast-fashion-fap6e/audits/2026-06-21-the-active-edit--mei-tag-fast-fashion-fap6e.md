# The active edit
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- Okay so H&M slid into my inbox again — this is genuinely the sixth time I've heard from them in the past week — and "the active edit" is a step up from the pure sale-hammer emails they've been running since June 16. The hero is a clean B&W shot, the athleisure angle is relevant, and then I scroll and see... a kids section. A *men's* soccer section. The Lotto x H&M collab, which is actually the most interesting thing in the entire email, buried two thirds of the way down like an afterthought.
- ASOS dropped their Scandi edit this week and it was one cohesive story — every scroll felt intentional. This feels like H&M put four different briefs in a blender. The "IN MOTION" hero copy is fine but it's the same "comfort, functionality & style" language they've been recycling. Nothing feels like a trend drop, it feels like a department store menu.
- I'd mark this as read. No forward. If the Lotto collab had been the hero I might've screenshotted it for my group chat — that's the only thing with actual trend energy here.

## What's working

- The 60% off banner hits immediately — price is the first thing I see and it's concrete with a floor price ($5.99), which actually makes it feel real and not fake-sale.
- The B&W hero shot is clean and doesn't look cheap — better than the average H&M send.
- The Lotto x H&M logo pairing caught my eye when I got there — a brand collab is exactly the "who got the trend first" content I care about.
- Subject line is short (15 chars) and doesn't feel spammy — "the active edit" is at least an aesthetic claim, not just "SALE SALE SALE."
- Email renders cleanly — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum disasters.

## What's weak

- The Lotto collab is the headline news and it's buried below a kids "TINY MOVES" section — that is a genuine editorial crime.
- The kids CTAs ("AGE 2-8Y" and "AGE 8-14Y") are literally in the first scroll for me — I am 24, I do not have children, remove this from my life.
- "BUILT TO PERFORM" with the soccer ball man feels like a completely different email that someone copy-pasted in.
- The sale banner says the same "UP TO 60% OFF IN STORES & ONLINE" language they've been running since June 17 — I've now seen this exact phrase four times in five days and it's lost all urgency.
- "MOVE WITH CONFIDENCE / Designed for comfort, versatility & freedom of movement" is the most boring active copy I've read this month — Zara at least tries to sound editorial, H&M sounds like they wrote this in 2014.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Lotto x H&M collab** — make it the hero, not a buried scroll-to-find. A collab is a trend signal, which is exactly what I open emails looking for. Everything else in this email is product inventory; the collab is *news*.
- 2. **Kill the kids section for this audience segment** — or at minimum push it to a thin footer strip, not a full scroll-height block mid-email. Mei does not have a toddler in an H&M tracksuit.
- 3. **Separate the men's section** — send it to the men's segment. Give women's subscribers one focused visual story. ASOS's Scandi edit got an 8 because it was *one thing*.
- 4. **Retire the "60% off" banner or add a hard deadline** — "Sale is here" with no end date reads as permanent markdown, not urgency. Put "ends Sunday" on it or it means nothing.
- 5. **Rewrite the active copy with trend language** — "athletic girl summer," "mob wife meets tennis court," literally anything that signals H&M is *on* the trend instead of selling function specs.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Lotto x H&M just dropped — sport your look`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Active era is here (and it's 60% off)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The collab giving sports-chic energy — collab + sale favorites from $5.99`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Lotto x H&M, women's active, up to 60% off — shop the trend first`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**External font blocking:**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is render-blocking in supporting clients and silently ignored by Outlook. No fallback font stack is declared in the visible CSS — if either CDN is slow or blocked, text rendering degrades with no system fallback.

**Risky rule:**
```css
img.image + div { display: none !important; }
```
This hides any `<div>` immediately following an `.image`-classed `<img>`. If content is ever placed in such a position (e.g., captions, ALT overlays), it will be silently suppressed with no visible error.

**Alt text:** Cannot confirm — image tags not in visible truncation.
