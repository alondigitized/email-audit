# ASOS Premier: 25% off 1000s of styles
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so I open this and my first thought is: didn't I just see this email? Because I did — June 9 was "20% off 1000s of styles," and now we're back six days later with 5% more bolted on and an "ASOS Premier" badge to dress it up. The "1000s of styles" phrasing is officially ASOS's filler template and they are not even hiding it anymore.
- That said, the Premier framing does something real for me. My name isn't in the subject but "ASOS Premier" tells me this isn't a blast to everyone — it's the member offer. The code is front and center, the deadline ("Ends Sunday 9am") is visible without digging, and the personalised picks section is actually doing work: straw mesh tote, gold coin sandals, sheer-over-black-shorts — these are all things I have pinned or saved right now. ASOS clearly ran some actual data on me here.
- But compare this to the June 12 tennis-heritage send — that email had a *point of view*. It showed up with an angle, a trend, a cultural hook. This is just ASOS saying "we like you enough to give you a coupon." I'll click because the deal is real and the picks landed, but I'm not forwarding this. It's a utility email dressed up as a relationship email.

## What's working

- **Premier exclusivity in the subject** — knowing immediately this is a member-only offer, not a mass blast, makes me take it more seriously before I even open.
- **Hero hierarchy is genuinely clean** — offer headline, code, deadline, CTA button — all visible in one glance at the top. No hunting.
- **Personalised picks are legitimately on-trend** — the straw mesh bag and gold coin sandals are my summer 2026 TikTok saves made real. Whoever built my taste profile did their homework.
- **Models fit my demo** — young women, current styling (brown tones, off-shoulder, wide-leg), not the safe catalog energy H&M sometimes defaults to.
- **Zero render drama** — clean layout, no broken images, no overlapping text. It just works.

## What's weak

- **"1000s of styles" is tired copy** — literally the same four words ASOS used on June 9. At this point it reads as "we ran out of ideas."
- **Deadline is buried in the body** — H&M put "ends tonight" right in the subject line on their discount send and it made me pause mid-scroll. ASOS left "Ends Sunday 9am" in the hero text where I could easily skim past it.
- **No trend angle whatsoever** — the picks are trend-forward but the email framing is purely discount. The June 12 tennis email felt like ASOS *owned* a trend; this feels like ASOS running a clearance with nicer models.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is filler** — every email from every brand has "SHOP NOW." "Claim My 25% Off" or even "Shop the Premier Edit" would feel like the button was written by a human.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop the deadline into the subject** — "ASOS Premier: 25% off — ends Sunday 9am" immediately creates the urgency the current subject line is missing. H&M does this better every time.
- 2. **Rename the picks section with a trend hook** — "Your personalised picks" is fine but "Your summer TikTok saves: 25% off" or "What's trending for you right now" ties the discount to the trend reason, which is what I actually care about.
- 3. **Specific CTA copy** — replace "SHOP NOW" with "Claim My 25% Off" or "Shop My Premier Picks." Verb plus specific noun, not verb plus adverb.
- 4. **Push the Premier exclusivity harder in the hero body copy** — "Your Premier offer. Not everyone gets this." positions it as a reward, not an automated script.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your member reward: 25% off this weekend only`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<meta>` tags placed inside `<body>/<p>` — confirmed defect.**

The document opens as `<html><body><div ...></div><p><meta http-equiv="Content-Type" ...><meta charset="utf-8">...`. There is no visible `<head>` block. All four `<meta>` declarations (Content-Type, charset, viewport, format-detection, x-apple-disable-message-reformatting) are nested inside a `<p>` element in `<body>`.

- HTML spec requires `<meta>` in `<head>`. Placement inside `<body>/<p>` is invalid.
- Mozilla/Chrome parsers will silently relocate or discard these; some email clients (notably older Lotus Notes, some Samsung Mail builds) will not, causing charset mis-detection or viewport scaling failures.
- The `<title>` tag is also inside this `<p>` block, which is invalid.

**Preheader text misconfiguration — confirmed.**

Two hidden `<div>` elements serve as preheader:

```html
<!-- First div — body-level, appears before the conditional: EMPTY -->
<div style="display:none !important;visibility:hidden;mso-hide:all;font-size:1px;..."></div>

<!-- Second div — inside <!--[if false]><!--> conditional: HAS TEXT -->
<div style="display: none; mso-hide: all;">Sign up to claim your discount</div>
```

The first `<div>` (the one visible to all clients unconditionally) is empty. The second `<div>` containing `"Sign up to claim your discount"` sits inside a `<!--[if false]><!-->`  MSO conditional, which renders it invisible to Outlook. Outlook's preview pane will fall through to the first visible body text rather than the intended preheader string. Non-Outlook clients receive `display:none` on both divs, so the preheader renders as empty in preview — only the spacer zero-width characters (`&nbsp;‌`) are present.

**Net effect:** preheader preview text is effectively blank or shows raw body copy across all major clients.

**`img` tags have no `alt` attributes confirmed missing** from the source structure (the `img { display: block; border: none; }` CSS rule is present but there are no `alt=""` declarations visible for any images). Images-off rendering will show broken image icons with no descriptive text.
