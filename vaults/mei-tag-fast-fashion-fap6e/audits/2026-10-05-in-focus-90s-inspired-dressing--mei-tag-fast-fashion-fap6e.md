# In focus: '90s-inspired dressing
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-05**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is actually the first H&M email in a week that has a point of view. After four days of the same "up to 30% off fall essentials" banner, I opened this expecting more of the same. Instead it's all black tailoring on a blue-grey backdrop, with a cropped belt-detail jacket and a bodycon slip dress. It reads very '90s minimalism, which is a real aesthetic that's moving on TikTok right now.
- It doesn't feel like H&M is *leading* the trend, though. It feels like they're catching up to it. Zara's "Re{form} Zara. Vol. 1" gave me a named designer and a story. This gives me one paragraph of "sleek silhouettes and sculptural tailoring." There's no discount at all, which is a relief after the Oct 3 and Oct 4 "20% off for Members" emails. But I also get no reason to click today instead of Saturday during my three-tab compare.
- Would I click? Probably the slip dress, and probably only to check it against Zara and ASOS. Would I forward it? No. It's pretty, but it's not a "omg look at this" email. Mark as read and maybe come back.

## What's working

- The hero is strong. The model in the black cropped jacket against the blue-grey backdrop looks editorial, not like a catalog.
- Prices are on the products ($69.99, $79.99, $39.99), so I don't have to tap to find out if it's in my budget.
- A single clean "'90S MINIMALISM" headline tells me the trend immediately.
- The four-up grid of the slip dress in different poses lets me see how it moves, which is what I want before buying online.
- It's a break from the discount-banner repetition of the last four sends, so it feels fresher.

## What's weak

- The "CROPPED BELT-DETAIL JACKET $69.99" text over the hero is thin white type on a light background. It's hard to read.
- Everything is black and the same model type, so there's no range in styling, size, or vibe. For a trend email I want to see how it's worn in more than one way.
- There's no urgency and no member perk. I'm a member, and nothing here acknowledges that, especially after the "Your 20% off ends soon, Member" email yesterday.
- The jacket shows up twice, once in the hero and again as the close-up. That wastes space that could have been another product.
- The "Shop By" and app-link footer is a lot of scrolling for little payoff. Leggings are the only thing for under $40, and they sit way down the page.
- Nothing says "new this week" or "trending now." The only label is "New Arrival."

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a trend-proof line, like "the look that's all over TikTok" or "the '90s minimal edit, trending now," so it feels like H&M got there first and isn't just following.
- 2. Make the product text over the hero readable, using dark text or a gradient, and link each product directly.
- 3. Bring back the member angle with something like "Members: your 20% applies to this edit." It would tie back to the last two sends and give me a reason to act now.
- 4. Drop the duplicate jacket and show a wider range of looks, with different colors and body types, plus a styled accessory.
- 5. Cut the footer clutter so the email ends on the product.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's HTML is a conventional table-based template. All 12 probed links returned 403, and the List-Unsubscribe and authentication headers could not be seen. Neither the 403s nor the missing headers can be confirmed as defects. The JSON-LD block has empty fields, and most images lack usable alt text.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **All 12 probed links returned 403** (16% pass rate). They go through the click-tracking redirector `https://t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=h88c587f8-…,548ed102,<suffix>&did=1418645762&rid=3326930539&…`. The affected links include:
  - Both "SHOP NOW" CTAs (`…f85d384f`, `…f85d3850`)
  - Both "Shop now" CTAs (`…f85d3843`, `…f85d3845`)
  - The "H&M" logo link (`…f85d3842`)
  - Image-wrapped links (`…f85d384d`, `…f85d384e`, `…f85d3851`, `…f85d3852`, `…f85d3855`, `…f85d3856`)
  - Unsubscribe (`…f85d3887`)
- A uniform 403 across every link, including the logo, points to the redirector rejecting the automated probe (bot/UA filtering) rather than to 12 broken destinations. This is unconfirmed, and a real-client click test is needed to settle it.
- **Unprobed links:** 47 links were skipped, so their status is unknown.
- **Wrapped destinations:** Every `href` is a redirect wrapper, so final landing URLs can't be checked from the source.
- **Duplicate IDs:** Several CTAs share the `548ed102` prefix and differ only in the hex suffix, so each link is individually tracked. That is fine.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** Nine campaign images and the tracked link/image at `t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=…,1` are flagged. Examples are `FNP-WA10264P07-4x5-1-90s-Fall.jpg` and `FNP-WA10264P07-2x3-2-90s-Fall.jpg`, plus `…2x3-4…`, `…3x2-5…` and `…2x3-6…` at both the 1400px and 750px variants.
- **Filename-style link text:** The 403 report shows link text as `[image: R1QaRscPaw=]`, `[image: dfHx2FXg=]` and `[image: cq5dam.web.1400.1400.jpeg]`. This means the alt text is empty or a CMS filename. Image-only links with no meaningful alt are unusable to screen readers and show nothing when images are blocked.
- **Invalid CSS:** `line-height:auto!important` in `.is-hidden-desktop` is not a valid value.
- **Web fonts:** The `@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/...)` and `@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/...)` rules are stripped by Gmail and some other clients. This is a fallback-font risk only, and the source doesn't show whether fallbacks are declared.
- **JSON-LD:** The `<script type="application/ld+json">` block is mostly empty:
  - `"subjectLine":""`
  - `"discountCode":""`, `"description":""`, `"availabilityEnds":""`
  - `PromotionCard` with `"image":""` and `"url":""`
  - `availabilityStarts` is a stale `2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00`

  Empty values can cause Gmail to reject the schema markup, and an empty `DiscountOffer` is leftover template boilerplate.
