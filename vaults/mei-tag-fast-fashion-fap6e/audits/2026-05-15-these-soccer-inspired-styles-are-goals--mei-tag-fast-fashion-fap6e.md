# These soccer-inspired styles are goals
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- Okay so "goals" — I see what you did there, H&M. The subject pun lands and honestly the sport-inspired trend is *genuinely* my thing right now. Soccer girl aesthetic is all over my For You page so when this hit my inbox I was like, okay fine, I'll open it. The STYLE GOALS hero is clean, the layout doesn't make me want to close my eyes, and the crinkled hoodie in the first product row is cute. But then I scroll and there's this big editorial photo that gives kids campaign, not womens drop, and that immediately knocked me sideways. Then I keep scrolling and find out there's a **Stella McCartney collab** just... sitting there below the fold? Like, buried after the soccer sets? That should be the entire email. That's the thing that would make me screenshot and send to my group chat.
- H&M sent me a flash sale app push yesterday and a TikTok Shop email two days before that — so this is what, email number four or five in the week? The cadence is starting to feel noisy. The trend concept is right, the execution is a 6, and the strategic priorities are backwards.
- I'd click the Stella McCartney section if I spotted it. I would not forward the email as-is. I'd mark it as read and move on.

## What's working

- **"Style Goals" wordplay lands.** It's not trying too hard and soccer puns are living rent-free on TikTok — subject got me to open.
- **SHOP MEN / SHOP WOMEN split CTAs** in the hero are clean — I don't have to guess where to go.
- **Stella McCartney collab is in here.** The "MOVE IT NOW" CTA is genuinely fun and the collab badge signals this is a limited thing, which activates my "don't miss it" instinct.
- **Prices are visible on individual products** — £18.99 for a hoodie, £8.99 for a soccer set, no click required to reality-check the price.
- **No render bugs.** Every section loads, no broken images, text is readable without zooming.

## What's weak

- **The Stella McCartney collab is buried.** That is easily a 3x more interesting subject line than a soccer aesthetic drop, and it's sitting below two product rows and a kids editorial shot. Zara would have led with the collab and made the sport stuff secondary.
- **The mid-email editorial photo reads kids campaign.** I'm 24. Zara already sent me a literal Children's drop last week and it felt alienating — this triggers the same confusion. Even if those are young adults, the vibe is off for a 20-something women's push.
- **No promotional offer or discount anywhere.** Yesterday's H&M was "20% off in the app" — today I'm being asked to pay full price with zero reason why now is the moment to buy. That's a hard pivot with no bridge.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** Fourth or fifth H&M email in five days. ASOS spaces these out better — they don't make me feel like I'm on a marketing assembly line.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Stella McCartney collab, make sport the supporting act.** The designer name in the subject line would spike opens significantly. No one is opening for "soccer-inspired styles" the same way they open for "Stella McCartney × H&M."
- 2. **Cut or reframe the kids editorial image.** If it's an age-diverse campaign, crop it to the adult models. If it's actually kids product, it has no business being in a women's fashion email.
- 3. **Add a time signal to the sport section.** Even "new arrivals this week" or a low-inventory callout would give me a reason to click now vs. later.
- 4. **Tighten the email length.** Remove the Shop By thumbnail grid — it's the most forgettable section and it adds scroll friction before the footer.
