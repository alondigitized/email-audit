# ZARA & Aaron Levine Capsule
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-13**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is Zara x Aaron Levine and it's a whole ass moodboard — vintage cabins, cigarette lighting, road trip aesthetic — and it's genuinely a cool visual world. But it's the third capsule collection with this guy and it's 100% menswear, every single model, every single look. I'm not the customer for a single frame of this email.
- The photography is doing something — moody, film-grain, "your dad's leather jacket but make it fashion" — and I get why someone would screenshot it for inspo. But there's zero product I can buy, zero CTA that isn't a single "VIEW" button buried at the very bottom after I've scrolled through what feels like twelve separate hero images. This is the same problem as August 8's "This week's selection" and August 1's "This week at Zara" — Zara just keeps sending me menswear catalogs and calling it a newsletter.
- Would I click? No. Would I forward it? Maybe to my boyfriend who's actually into this aesthetic, not because Zara earned the forward but because the photography's genuinely good. This is a straight mark-as-read.

## What's working

- The visual direction is cohesive and cinematic — vintage cabin, warm lighting, film-still vibes, way moodier than anything H&M's flash sales have sent me this week.
- The copy about "worn-in fabrics aged over the course of time" actually tells a story instead of just screaming a discount code at me.
- Brand consistency is on point — the little Zara aaronlevine lockup tag shows up repeated through the email so I always know whose world I'm in.

## What's weak

- Entirely menswear. Not one womenswear look, not one female model, in an email that landed in my inbox as a "shopper" — third time running this exact miss.
- No price, no product grid, no "shop the look" — just a wall of lifestyle photography before a single generic "VIEW" button at the bottom.
- Scroll length is brutal — this is like ten hero images deep before any actual CTA, which is way more friction than ASOS's Lioness drop email, which at least got me to a shoppable moment faster.
- Zero urgency, zero offer, zero reason to act today versus next week.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this — if I've never engaged with menswear/capsule drops, don't send me the third one in a row. That's the single biggest fix.
- 2. Add an actual shop-the-look grid with prices near the top, not just mood images — even Zara Home's Retro Selection send at least tried to look catalog-y.
- 3. Cut the scroll by 60%. Nobody's swiping through ten images before a CTA on their phone during a commute.
- 4. Give the "VIEW" button real copy — "Shop the Capsule" tells me what I'm clicking into.
