# Start the season in SculptMove™
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-14**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so this is H&M going full activewear catalog mode — "IT'S SCULPTMOVE™ SEASON" with basically their entire tank-top-and-legging lineup laid out in a grid. It's a hard pivot from the "tennis favorites" editorial moment they had on 9/11 and the quiet trench coat vibe from 9/10 — no more storytelling, just product-product-product, which reads more like a product feed than a campaign.
- The hero shot is genuinely strong — moody gym lighting, one model mid-stretch, arms up, and the price tag ($24.99) floating right on the image like a shoppable tag. That's a good hook. But then it just... repeats the same tank top four separate times down the page in slightly different colorways with "New Arrival" stamped under almost everything, which starts to feel like they didn't edit the send at all. Sephora would never let a product list run this repetitive without breaking it up with a use-case or routine angle.
- Would I click? Maybe, on the hero image alone since SculptMove leggings are a legit TikTok staple right now. But I wouldn't forward this — there's no single standout piece or price moment worth texting a friend about, it's just "here's our activewear catalog," and I've already seen H&M hit me with three straight days of tiered-discount emails this week (9/11, 9/12, 9/13) so my guard is up for another catch.

## What's working

- The hero image actually looks like a real workout moment, not a stock photo — dark gym lighting, motion, felt current.
- Price tag overlay directly on the hero image is a nice touchless CTA — I can see $24.99 without hunting for it.
- Model diversity across the grid (different builds, at least one visibly older model in the ASOS-adjacent lifestyle sense) is a plus over Zara Kids' all-child cast from my last read.
- "SHOP NOW" button is clean, high-contrast, easy tap target.
- Category grid at the bottom (Women/Men/Kids/Home/Beauty) is genuinely useful if I wanted to explore past activewear.

## What's weak

- The same shelf bra tank top shows up FOUR times in different crops/colors — that's lazy merchandising, not a curated drop.
- Zero urgency, zero offer — no %, no BOGO, nothing time-bound. After three straight tiered-discount emails this week, going offer-less feels like a tonal whiplash rather than a breather.
- No sizing/fit language at all for a "contour-hugging" product line — as someone who actually cares about fit before I click "shop," that's a real gap. ASOS's Lioness drop at least had outfit styling context.
- It's an incredibly long scroll of near-identical product tiles before I hit anything else — feels like a catalog dump, not a story.
- Cadence fatigue is real: this is H&M's 6th email to me in 5 days, and jumping from "20% off last day!!" urgency straight to zero-urgency product grid feels disjointed.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the repetition** — that shelf bra tank shows up 4x. Consolidate to one clean "shop the edit" carousel of 5-6 distinct SKUs instead of scrolling fatigue.
- 2. **Add a hook offer or member perk** — even a soft "Members get early access" line would justify sending this right after three discount emails.
- 3. **Add one line of fit/sizing info** near the hero — "true to size, extra stretch through the hip" — since fit is the #1 thing that stops me from clicking on activewear.
- 4. **Break up the grid with a styling moment** — one outfit-styled shot (like ASOS's Lioness shoot) instead of pure product-on-white repetition.

## Technical Audit: H&M "Start the season in SculptMove™"

**1. Technical Summary**
The email renders via a heavily media-query-optimized HTML/MSO template, but automated QA returned only a 16% pass rate: every probed CTA/tracking link returned HTTP 403 (including the unsubscribe link), and RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe headers are absent.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- All 12 probed links — both "SHOP NOW" CTAs, "Shop now" text links, the "H&M" header link, 6 product images, and the unsubscribe link — resolve through the `t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=...` redirector and every one returned **403 Forbidden** during probing.
- Example: `https://t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=h3193a687-fd7b-4d6f-8881-17f04ae515e4,54878479,f82605b9&did=1418167417&rid=3326930539&erid=...` → 403.
- Given that *all* links share the same redirector domain and *all* fail identically, this pattern is consistent with bot/UA-based blocking at the `t19.email.hm.com` edge (link-wrapping vendor rejecting automated probes) rather than per-link breakage — but it cannot be ruled out that the redirect service itself is down or misconfigured for this send. Recommend a manual click-test from a real client to confirm live-user impact before treating this as a false positive.
- Two CTA anchors ("‌ ﻿Shop now") contain a zero-width non-joiner/BOM (`‌`/`﻿`) in the visible link text — cosmetic only, but worth cleaning in the ESP template since it can affect screen-reader pronunciation and text-based link audits.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- 20 `<img>` tags are missing `alt` text, including the two hero product images (`cq5dam.web.1400.1400.jpeg`, `cq5dam.web.750.750.jpeg`) and all secondary product shots — fails WCAG 1.1.1; screen-reader and images-blocked-by-default users get no fallback content.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
