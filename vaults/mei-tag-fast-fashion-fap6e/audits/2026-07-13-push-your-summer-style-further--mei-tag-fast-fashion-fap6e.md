# Push your summer style further
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- Okay so ASOS comes back two days after the broken merge tag catastrophe from July 11 — the one that literally opened with a comma because the first-name field failed — and this is their reset move. And honestly? It's decent. The hero model is giving everything: tall Black woman, yellow crop top, olive cargos, tile backdrop — the styling is legit and the aesthetic is clean. The "Summer forever" headline reads as an editorial play, not a sale push, which makes the pivot to "Looking for the Sale?" halfway down feel like ASOS can't quite decide if this is a lookbook or a discount announcement.
- The core problem is that "Push your summer style further" tells me absolutely nothing I couldn't say about literally any fashion email sent in July. There's no trend angle, no "this just dropped," no "Shein already has dupes of this." It's competent but not exciting, and competent doesn't make me stop scrolling TikTok to open my inbox.
- I'd read it, I'd scroll it, I probably wouldn't click unless the sale section surfaced a real number. I would not forward this — there's nothing shareable about it. Mark as read after a skim.

## What's working

- **Model casting is right on.** The hero model looks like someone I'd actually follow on TikTok — the proportions, the confidence, the oversized yellow off-shoulder with cargo shorts is genuinely trend-forward for summer 2026.
- **Layout is clean and renders properly.** After the July 11 merge-tag disaster, just not breaking is a win. No overlapping text, no broken images, hierarchy flows naturally top to bottom.
- **Editorial-first structure.** Leading with the lookbook vibe before pivoting to sale is the right call — it's what H&M did with "New summer details" (which I scored 9/10) and it respects the subscriber more than opening straight with a discount percentage.
- **"Looking for the Sale?" section is honest.** It doesn't bury the promo in fine print — it surfaces it as its own named block, which I actually appreciate.
- **Top-nav is clean and functional** — WOMEN / NEW IN / SALE right in the header, so even if nothing in the body hooks me I can navigate fast.

## What's weak

- **"Summer forever" is a nothing headline.** It's not a trend statement, it's not a mood I recognize from TikTok, it's just... words. Compare to H&M's "New summer details" — at least that implies something specific is happening. This could have come from any brand, any year.
- **No trend specificity anywhere.** I'm a tab-compare girl. I want to know if ASOS is first on cargo shorts versus Zara, or if they're chasing Shein on the yellow-top moment. This email has zero opinion about what's actually trending right now.
- **The sale section undersells itself.** "Looking for the Sale?" is a weak header — it sounds like a FAQ link, not a promotion. If there's a real percentage off I can't see it at a glance, which means it's not doing its job.
- **"DOWNLOAD THE APP" CTA mid-scroll is a momentum killer.** I'm browsing fashion, not looking to manage my apps. Burying that in the scroll path interrupts the vibe right before the footer.
- **Zero personalization.** Given I've been on their list long enough to get ten emails in two weeks, they still treat me like a stranger. Zara's misfire (sending me the men's collection) at least signals they have segments — ASOS is just blasting.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Replace "Summer forever" with a trend-specific headline** — something like "Cargos are the moment. Grab yours before everyone else does." Give me a reason this email exists *today* and not in June.
- 2. **Surface the sale number in the hero or sub-hero.** If there's X% off, show it above the fold. The "Looking for the Sale?" section reads like an afterthought — if a sale is live, lead with it or give it a real discount call-out.
- 3. **Kill the "DOWNLOAD THE APP" mid-scroll CTA.** Put it in the footer if at all. It breaks the shopping flow right when I'm warming up.
- 4. **Add at least one trend signal to the body copy.** Even a single line — "The cargo short moment your FYP keeps serving, now in our edit" — would differentiate this from a generic editorial.
- 5. **Move the brand logos section or cut it.** "SHOP OUR TOP BRANDS" with tiny logos is clutter for a lookbook-style send — it belongs in a dedicated brand-discovery email, not buried under the editorial.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Duplicate charset declaration.** Both `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8">` and `<meta charset="utf-8">` are present. Redundant; harmless in practice but indicates template slop.

- **`font-size: 0em` on `table, tr, td, th`.** This is a standard ghost-text suppression hack, but it forces every `<td>` to explicitly reset its own `font-size`. Any cell that omits a reset will render invisible text. Risk is low for a mature template, but it's a latent trap.

- **`@keyframes ticker` + `@supports` animation.** CSS animation support in email is narrow: Apple Mail/iOS Mail render it; Gmail (all), Outlook (all), and Yahoo suppress it silently. The `@supports` guard correctly hides `.ticker` and shows `.fallback` for non-supporting clients — implementation is sound. No issue.

- **Web fonts (`FuturaPT-Book`, `FuturaPT-Heavy`) will not load in Gmail, Outlook, or Yahoo.** The fallback stack `Futura, Arial, sans-serif` is appropriate. `mso-font-alt: Arial` is set correctly for Outlook. No rendering failure, but visual parity is not achievable across clients.

- **SVG font format included in `@font-face` src stack.** SVG fonts are deprecated (removed from all modern browsers; never supported in email clients). The entry adds bytes with zero benefit:
  ```
  url("...FuturaPT-Book.svg") format("svg")
  ```
  Safe to remove.

- **`img { display: block }` is global** — correct for email to suppress descender gaps. No issue.

- **Alt text on images unverifiable** due to truncation. Must be confirmed in full source.
