# Early app access: up to 60% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- OK so H&M comes in hot with a subject line that actually makes me stop — 60% is a big number, and three days ago they were calling 20% off a limited-time event. That's a suspicious escalation and I clocked it immediately. But the "early access" hook still works on me; I opened it.
- Then the email opens and the first half is doing its job — model is giving exactly the black minimalist vibe all over my FYP right now, the headline is big and clear, I feel the FOMO. And then the email just... becomes an H&M app brochure. Feature bullet points. Scan & Find. Visual Search. DOWNLOAD ANDROID. I'm sitting here expecting to see a silk cami for $9 or a trendy cargo pant at 60% off, and instead I get app onboarding copy. No product. Not a single item. Just a sales pitch for a sales pitch.
- Compare that to ASOS's "Destination: the holiday shop" email — they opened on a model in a mustard maxi dress on a Mediterranean coast and immediately made me want to be there wearing that dress. H&M opens on a great model and then pivots to a tech product explanation. Mark as read, don't forward.

## What's working

- **60% off in the subject line** — the number does real work after a week of 15–20% emails; I opened specifically because the math jumped so much.
- **Model aesthetic is right on trend** — the all-black tank and wide-leg silhouette is exactly what's circulating on my FYP this week; H&M got the vibe right.
- **"A day early" framing** — small but effective; this is the kind of exclusive I'd actually mention to a group chat.
- **Clean hierarchy** — the hero reads instantly; no clutter, no overlapping text, no mystery about what the email is about.

## What's weak

- **No actual products shown** — an "early access sale" email that doesn't show me a single sale item is just an app ad dressed as a deal.
- **The CTA is an app install, not a shop button** — if I already have the H&M app (which I do), these "DOWNLOAD IOS / DOWNLOAD ANDROID" buttons mean nothing. There's no deep link into the actual sale.
- **The app feature block kills the momentum dead** — Member Hub, Scan & Find, Visual Search — cool, but I did not open a sale email to read an app review.
- **Discount escalation breaks trust in past urgency** — H&M sent "ends tonight" for 20% off on June 14 like it was urgent. Now they're doing 60% with zero ceremony two days later. That "ends tonight" language is retroactively theater.
- **"Shop By" grid is too small to generate desire** — the five category thumbnails at the bottom (WOMEN, MEN, KIDS, HOME, BEAUTY) are postage-stamp sized; they add zero discovery value.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Replace the app feature block with 3–4 actual hero sale items** — strikethrough prices, the original cost, and the sale price. Let me see what I'm early-accessing. Even Zara, who runs cold editorial emails, at least shows me the product.
- 2. **Swap the download CTA for a deep-link "Shop the Sale" button** — send existing app users straight into the sale. Route non-app users through the download flow after the click.
- 3. **Add a visible countdown or end date** — "Early access closes Sunday at midnight" would actually give the urgency teeth. Right now "early access" is vibes with no deadline.
- 4. **Cut the app explainer entirely for this segment** — this is an email list, which means I'm probably already a customer. The onboarding pitch is wasted real estate.
- 5. **One "we know this is a lot of emails" line** — five sends in four days with escalating discounts. A single "this is actually our biggest sale of the summer" line helps re-anchor trust.
- **Subject Alt A:** `You're in first: 60% off drops early in the app`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Summer's biggest sale — app-only, today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop before anyone else — summer's biggest drops, one day early, H&M app only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Up to 60% off. Opens to everyone Sunday. You're early.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading will silently fail in Outlook**
```css
<style>
  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
</style>
```
Outlook (all desktop versions) strips `@import` inside `<style>` tags. Both custom font declarations will be ignored, falling back to system fonts. This is a known Outlook behavior — fonts must be inlined via `font-face` declarations or accepted as a Outlook-specific fallback.

**Non-standard Yahoo Mail media query**
```css
@media screen yahoo { .show-webkit-table { display: none; } }
```
`screen yahoo` is not a valid CSS media query; it relies on Yahoo Mail's legacy parser bug to apply. This is a known workaround but fragile — Yahoo Mail rendering pipeline changes can silently break this toggle without warning.

**Mobile breakpoint at 699px is non-standard**
```css
@media (max-width:699px)
```
Most email clients and templates use 600px. At 700px viewport widths (common on some Android devices), the desktop layout will render instead of the mobile layout. Low severity but worth normalizing.
