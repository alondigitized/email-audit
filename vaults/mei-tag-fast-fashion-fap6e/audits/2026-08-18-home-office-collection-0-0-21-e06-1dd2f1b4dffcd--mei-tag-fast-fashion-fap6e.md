# Home Office Collection
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-18**
## Executive summary

- Okay, Zara sent me a home office collection email and... I don't work from home in a way that requires a whole aesthetic upgrade, so this is immediately a "not for me" wall, kind of like the Kids emails I keep hitting (August 9th, "Kids | New season updates"). That said, I'm not gonna pretend the styling isn't gorgeous — that red enamel desk, the sculptural lamp, the leather stool with books on it, it's giving very "European architect's studio" and I get why someone's saving these to a Pinterest board. But there's zero price, zero specific product I can tap into, and it's clothes-brand-me being sold furniture, which just doesn't compute.
- I'm not clicking this. It's beautifully shot filler that belongs in a design magazine, not a fast-fashion inbox that's supposed to be telling me what's trending on my FYP. Would I forward it? Maybe to my friend who's redoing her apartment, not to anyone in my group chat who cares about clothes.
- Compared to Zara's own "Woman | The New" from August 14th — which actually had florals and a bustier top that's genuinely trending right now — this is a step backward into pure lifestyle-brand territory with nothing shoppable-feeling. H&M's tennis drop with @sierrarenas at least tied a real trend to a real creator; this is just moodboard.

## What's working

- The red enamel desk shot is a genuine scroll-stopper, visually distinct from anything else in my inbox this week.
- Photography is consistently elevated and moody — no cheap product-on-white-background energy anywhere.
- Clean, uncluttered layout — no busy banners or stacked discount badges cluttering the eye.
- The closing "Home Fragrances" section at least ties back to something more giftable/impulse-buy than a desk.

## What's weak

- Nothing tells me this is Zara's clothing/trend world — it reads like a random furniture catalog dropped into my inbox.
- Zero price points, zero product names, zero "shop this" specificity anywhere in the whole scroll.
- No people, no models, no styling context — this whole email has no demographic signal at all, which is wild for a brand built on "does this look like me."
- The single CTA ("THE NEW") is buried deep in the scroll after like a dozen images, and it's vague as hell.
- This is such a long, image-heavy scroll for mobile — my thumb was going for ages before hitting any button.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this scroll by 60%. Nobody's home office needs 12 different vignettes of the same red desk from different angles — pick the 3 best shots and get to a CTA fast.
- 2. Add at least one visible price or "from $X" anchor — even lifestyle/interiors emails need one concrete number to feel shoppable instead of aspirational-only.
- 3. Put a real CTA button after the hero image, not 90% down the page — "Shop Home Office" right under that red desk shot.
- 4. If this has to exist, pair it with an actual person using the space — even Zara's own Woman/Fold-er sends know outfits need bodies; furniture needs someone living in it.
- 5. Consider whether this collection even deserves a solo send versus a small section inside a broader "Home" roundup — this feels like a stretch to fill an inbox slot.
