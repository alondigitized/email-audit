# Introducing Solar Club H&M MOVE
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- Okay so "Solar Club" as a concept? Actually kind of a vibe. The sun-soaked running collective idea, lightweight fabrics, collective movement energy — I can feel what H&M MOVE is going for and it doesn't feel like a knockoff. The hero shot of two people mid-run has that outdoor girl summer aesthetic that's everywhere on my FYP right now, and the product tech callouts (SculptMove™, CoolCool™, ByNature™) make it feel like H&M invested in the line rather than just relabeling basics and calling it a sub-brand.
- But then there is literally a "GIFTS FOR DAD. UP TO 50% OFF*" banner slapped right at the very top of the email. I opened "Introducing Solar Club H&M MOVE" and the first thing I see is a Father's Day deal. That is the email equivalent of a brand showing up to their own drop party and immediately handing you someone else's coupon. It obliterates the editorial moment before it even starts. Zara would never — when they dropped the Caramel London collab even though that email was shaky execution, at least it stayed on theme from first pixel to last.
- And I have to be honest about context here: this is H&M's fourth email to me in four days. The soirée edit on June 2nd was actually a 9 in my brain — that one earned the open. By the time Solar Club landed, my thumb muscle memory was already mid-hover over archive. The concept is strong enough that I'd click if I saw it on TikTok, but as an email fighting for inbox real estate against its own brand siblings? Mark as read, maybe revisit later.

## What's working

- Solar Club aesthetic is cohesive — sun-bleached athletes, minimal palette, the "collective movement" angle reads original rather than trend-chasing
- SculptMove™, CoolCool™, ByNature™ tech branding visible on product cards — signals that MOVE is a real sub-brand, not a clearance rack with a new label
- Prices are fully visible and genuinely accessible: $14.99 shorts, $24.99 sports bra, $29.99 long sleeve — this is the price-per-trend math I actually care about vs. ASOS where I sometimes can't find pricing without clicking into the product
- "New Arrival" tags on multiple products tell me this is a fresh drop, not repackaged old stock
- Female athletic models read young and genuinely active — demographic alignment is real, they're not just posed in a studio with a water bottle

## What's weak

- "GIFTS FOR DAD. UP TO 50% OFF*" header banner is a hard mismatch at the top of a collab intro email — it reads like two separate campaigns got stitched together at 11pm the night before send
- Every CTA is "SHOP NOW" — it appears at least twice with zero variation; compare this to ASOS's "Summer pieces everyone's wearing" email which at least used social-proof framing on the button copy, and that email scored a 7 in my notes
- The Solar Club wordmark/logo text near the hero (the stylized "SkēpDō"-looking treatment with the butterfly and sparkle icons) is unreadable at email width — if that IS the Solar Club logo, I had no idea what I was looking at until I read the body copy
- This is the fourth H&M email in four days and the cadence is actively working against the brand equity — the soirée email built real energy and this one partially burns it just by showing up

## Recommendations

- 1. Strip the Father's Day banner from this email entirely. Solar Club is a launch moment — own the full creative canvas. Put the Dad promo in its own email or a footer footnote; don't let it compete at the hero level with a brand new collection concept.
- 2. Swap every "SHOP NOW" for "Shop Solar Club" or "Join the Club" — the identity hook is the best thing about this email and the CTA copy completely ignores it.
- 3. Legibility fix on the Solar Club logo/wordmark — whatever that stylized treatment is at the top, it needs to be readable on a 375px screen without squinting.
- 4. Add one social proof signal: a "now live — already trending" tag or a single TikTok-style UGC shot of someone wearing the pieces outside, not in a clean studio context.
- 5. Soft scarcity line in the hero copy: "New Season Drop — Limited Styles" would create enough urgency to move the click decision forward without screaming fake deadline energy.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` for web fonts** — two external font sheets are loaded via `@import` inside `<style>`:
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
Gmail (web), Outlook, and several mobile clients strip or ignore `@import` in `<style>` blocks. Fonts will silently fall back on those clients. Web fonts in email must be loaded via `<link>` in `<head>` to have any chance of rendering, and even then support is limited to Apple Mail, iOS Mail, and Samsung Mail.
