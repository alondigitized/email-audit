- Every model is a woman, and the styles (slip dress, fur coat, velvet blazer, leather boots) match what I'm actually shopping for now.

- Category tiles like Accessories, Dresses and Knitwear, plus Jackets/Shirts and Coats/Blazers, let me jump to what I want.
- The Golden Edit makeup block with the glittery eye look is a nice surprise. It feels like a Pinterest board.
- There's no percentage-off screaming. It's closer to H&M's quiet Studio Holiday drop than H&M's "Flash sale: 25% off" sends.

## What's weak

- No offer, no price and no shipping info anywhere. ASOS's Lioness drop at least told me it was exclusive.
- The "VIEW" links are tiny, and the labels under the images ("SHOES", "MAKEUP") are small for a phone tap.
- The broken-word headlines ("PRO JECTS", "TRF CO LLEC TI ON") are hard to read, and I can't tell what "TRF" is.
- It's a very long scroll with nothing telling me what's new this week, even though the subject says "weekly edit". The Fifth Avenue flagship story at the bottom is irrelevant to me.
- The email doesn't say which pieces are trending, so there's no proof Zara got there first. That's the thing I'm checking for.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put one clear hero with a real CTA, like "Shop the velvet slip dress", and a price. A hero that links to a specific product beats five "VIEW" links.
- 2. Label what's actually new ("New in: 24 pieces") so I know it's an edit and not a rerun.
- 3. Add trend names or social proof ("most saved this week") so Zara looks like it started the trend.
- 4. Make the type larger, stop splitting words, and make the tap targets bigger.
- 5. Cut the flagship block, or move it into a footer. It's corporate news, not shopping.
