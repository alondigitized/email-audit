# Leopard-print lovers – this edit is for you
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- Okay so ASOS hit me for the *third time in four days* — "What's hot this summer" on the 18th, the embellished pieces edit on the 20th, and now leopard print on the 22nd. My thumb was already hovering over the archive. But "leopard-print lovers" actually stopped me because leopard is genuinely having a TikTok moment right now — this isn't ASOS chasing a trend six weeks late, it feels timed. The hero delivers: big model energy, the print reads immediately, the headline is exactly what it says on the tin.
- The problem is the email loses focus fast. You scroll past the leopard section and suddenly there's a Wrangler collab drop with striped wide-leg trousers — which, fine, Wrangler is cute, but it has nothing to do with why I opened. And there's no discount. No urgency. Just a vibe edit with a SHOP NOW button. H&M's Studio Resort email two weeks ago had the same editorial-image energy but the hero image was *stopping-mid-scroll* level — ASOS's leopard hero is solid but not screenshot-worthy.
- I'd click the leopard section, skip the Wrangler, and definitely not forward this. It's a 'mark as read, revisit later' situation.

## What's working

- **Trend timing is actually right** — leopard print is everywhere on FYP right now and ASOS called it before it peaks, not after. That earns credibility.
- **The hero shot delivers the promise** — model in a full leopard look, readable immediately, the headline "The leopard-print edit" is unambiguous. No bait-and-switch.
- **Multiple leopard options shown above the fold** — I can see the jumpsuit/coord, a dress silhouette, a top-and-trouser combo. Range is there, which reduces the fear of clicking and seeing one boring midi skirt.
- **"YOUR BEST ITEMS" mid-email** — algorithmic personalization in the middle of a trend edit is genuinely useful. If those are actually my browsed items, that's smart.
- **Clean render, no bugs** — layout held together, images loaded, CTAs are tappable-looking.

## What's weak

- **The Wrangler collab tanked the edit's focus** — I opened for leopard, not a denim brand partnership with stripes. Feels like a revenue-share insert that broke the narrative.
- **Zero offer** — no discount, no free shipping threshold, nothing. Zara runs the same full-price editorial format and at least the brand mystique justifies it; ASOS is not Zara.
- **Cadence alarm is going off** — three emails in four days from the same sender is starting to train me to ignore them. The embellished pieces send two days ago had a higher score; this one risks living in its shadow.
- **"Trending" section mid-email is too generic** — after a specific leopard edit, dropping into a generic trending grid with unrelated pieces (a green floral? a boho orange dress?) dilutes the theme and kills scroll momentum.
- **No urgency signal anywhere** — if the leopard edit sells out, I'd want to know. "While stock lasts" or even a "selling fast" badge on the hero would change my urgency calculus entirely.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the Wrangler section or give it its own email** — a collab deserves its own moment, not a footnote in a leopard print edit. The focus break loses me right at the point where I'm warming up to buy.
- 2. **Add a scarcity/urgency hook** — even "trending fast — sizes going" on the hero product would push me from browse to buy. H&M's Studio Resort email didn't have this either and it still scored high because the image was editorial enough; ASOS isn't quite at that image quality level yet.
- 3. **Sharpen the "Trending" section to leopard-adjacent prints** — snake print, tortoiseshell, abstract animal — stay in the print lane if you're doing a print edit.
- 4. **Personalization on the subject line** — "Mei, the leopard edit is here" would've felt different from a mass blast.
- 5. **Dial down cadence** — or at minimum, make sure the third email in four days has a harder offer than a vibe scroll.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `FuturaPT-DemiBold` referenced but never declared:**
```css
h1, h2, h3 {
  font-family: 'FuturaPT-DemiBold', Futura, Arial, sans-serif;
}
```
Only `FuturaPT-Book` and `FuturaPT-Heavy` have `@font-face` blocks. `FuturaPT-DemiBold` is absent, so all headings fall through to `Futura` (rarely installed) → `Arial`. This affects every client that supports web fonts and will produce a visibly different heading weight than designed.

**Issue — Mobile breakpoint at `max-width: 460px` is narrower than convention:**
Most email client viewport widths for phones range 375–414px (safe) but the gap between 461–599px (small tablets, landscape phones) receives zero responsive overrides. If the desktop layout is 600px wide, these widths render at desktop scale in a viewport that may be under 500px.

**Minor — `font-size: 0em` on `table, tr, td, th`:**
```css
table, tr, td, th { font-size: 0em; }
```
This prevents whitespace gaps in Outlook (intended) but any text node placed directly in a `<td>` without an explicit `font-size` override inherits `0em` and renders invisible. Verify all text-bearing `<td>` elements have inline `font-size` set.

**Minor — `*` selector with `-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased !important`:**
Applying this universally with `!important` can interfere with thin-weight text on low-DPI displays. No functional bug, but worth scoping to body text only.

**No issues found:** `lang="en"` is set on `<html>`, `<title>` matches subject line, VML conditional comments are correctly scoped to `gt mso 15`, preheader zero-width non-joiner padding (`&zwnj;`) is present.
