# Up to 40% off premium knits
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-15**
## Executive summary

- Ugh, H&M again. This is the ninth member-sale email in about two weeks. After "Up to 40% off gifts for her" yesterday and "Member-exclusive outerwear deals from $29.99" on 11/13, "Up to 40% off" feels like their permanent state. The hero is a guy in a taupe quarter-zip knit. The top of the email also pushes a "Give $50, get $10" e-gift card thing, so the first screen is already busy.
- The sweaters are fine, but nothing about this says trend. There's no TikTok-core, no "this is the look," just "premium knits" in the same neutrals I've seen all week. Zara would have styled this as a drop with a point of view. ASOS's Lioness send at least felt like a campaign. This feels like a clearance rack with a nice photo.
- I'd mark it read. I might forward the mohair-blend one to a friend, but I wouldn't click through for it. The whole product grid is menswear and neutral unisex-ish knits, and I can't tell where the women's picks are.

## What's working

- The 40% off headline is huge and clear, so I get the offer in one second.
- The product grid shows member price next to regular price (like $79.99 → $59.99), so I can see the savings quickly.
- The oversized mohair-blend sweater on the first model is cozy and very "cold girl aesthetic."
- The "New Arrival" tags give me a tiny hit of newness.

## What's weak

- The hero is a man, and the grid has more men than women. Zara's menswear email last week had the same problem, and I felt just as left out.
- The top banner is a totally different offer ($50 gift card) that competes with the 40% headline.
- The black "30% off in-store" block repeats the sale idea and is lower than the online offer, which is confusing. The red text on black is hard to read.
- The legal fine print is a giant wall of text at the bottom. It makes "Offer feels honest" harder to believe.
- There's no trend angle, no reason to care about these knits now. Shein and H&M's own competitors would at least give me a vibe.
- I'm seeing the same member-sale hero energy every single day.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the sends to two a week and give each one one idea. The cadence is what's killing H&M for me right now.
- 2. Lead with one clear offer (40% off knits) and drop the gift-card banner and the in-store block from this send.
- 3. Make the hero a woman in the cropped, oversized knit and style it as a trend ("the cardigan everyone's wearing").
- 4. Split the grid into women's and men's rows so I can find my side fast.
- 5. Shrink the fine print into a short expandable line and a link to terms.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The oversized knit everyone's wearing`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Members: 40% off cozy knits, ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Mohair, merino and cashmere blends from $39.99 for members`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your member price is already applied. Shop women's knits first.`

## Technical Audit

# Technical Audit: H&M "Up to 40% off premium knits"

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based HTML send from `newsletter@email.hm.com`. All links go through the `t19.email.hm.com/r/` tracking redirector. The QA run reports 12 of 12 probed links returning HTTP 403, and every probed image is missing alt text. Authentication and unsubscribe headers could not be checked because of the relay. The source is truncated, so the footer and body markup were not fully inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **All 12 probed links returned 403.** They include "Find a store" (x2), "Shop now" (x2), "Women", "Men", "Sign in", the H&M logo link, the gift-card promo link, two image links and "Unsubscribe". The redirect IDs are different, for example `...,f8c4cb2a`, `...,f8c4cade` and `...,f8c4cb4e`.
- **The 403s are probably bot blocking, not broken links.** Every response came from the same ESP redirector (`t19.email.hm.com/r/`). A uniform 403 across unrelated destinations points to the redirector rejecting the probe's user-agent or IP. Real-recipient behavior is unverified.
- **Redirect targets could not be resolved.** The final landing URLs are unknown, so the destinations could not be checked.
- **Coverage is partial.** Only 12 links were probed and 64 were skipped.
- **The link text on both "Shop now" CTAs starts with zero-width characters** (`‌  ﻿Shop now`). This is padding or preheader-style hacks inside the `<a>`, and it pollutes the accessible link name.
- **Image links expose no useful link text.** They surface as `[image: cq5dam.web.1400.1400.jpeg]`, which follows from the missing alt text covered in section 3.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text.** QA flagged 17 images, including:
  - the 1400px and 750px `cq5dam.web.*.jpeg` pairs under `image.hm.com/content/dam/hm/regional-local-activities/ame/2026-ame/wk-46/us174_s4/` (for example `1358680001_202604_LB_0952.jpg`, `1242877066_202604_LB_6334.jpg`);
  - a `wk48/ca0106_s4/CA0106_FP3_1358728002...` asset, which is a different week folder (`wk48`) and region code (`ca`) from the rest (`wk-46`, `us174`);
  - the tracking-pixel-style image at `t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=hce1a9e23-...,1`.
- **Images are duplicated in 1400px and 750px pairs.** This looks like the desktop/mobile toggle pattern driven by `.is-hidden-mobile` and `.is-hidden-desktop`. Both variants are in the DOM, and without alt text both are announced by screen readers. `display:none` normally hides the hidden variant. But Outlook-targeted rules such as `mso-hide` are only set on some classes in the visible CSS.
- **Mixed hide/show mechanisms.** The CSS uses `.hide-webkit`, `.show-webkit-table` and `img.image + div{display:none}` hacks. These depend on client-specific selectors such as `#MessageViewBody`, so rendering is not guaranteed in other clients.
- **`@import` for web fonts** (`fabric.hmgroup.com`, `s1-cdn.hm.com/.../oces.css`) is dropped by Gmail and others. Fallback font stacks were not visible in the truncated source.
- **The viewport meta tag is present** (`width=device-width`), and so are `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` and `format-detection`. No issue there.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **No unresolved merge tokens** were visible in the visible source or the QA data.
- **Per-recipient identifiers are in every link URL**: `rid=3326930539`, `erid=MzMyNjkzMDUzOQ==` (base64 of the same ID), and `p2=0982dd0a...`. The unsubscribe link additionally carries `p4=3326930539` and `p5=9be06746...`. This is expected ESP tracking, not a defect.
- **The JSON-LD block has empty fields.** The `EmailMessage` has `"subjectLine":""`, the `DiscountOffer` has `"discountCode":""`, `"description":""` and `"availabilityEnds":""`, and the `PromotionCard` has `"image":""` and `"url":""`. The `availabilityStarts` is `2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00`, which looks like template default data. Gmail may ignore or reject the schema markup, so it does nothing useful.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **The unsubscribe link is present in the body** ("Unsubscribe", `.../f8c4cb4e...&p4=3326930539&p5=9be06746...`). It returned 403 on probe, probably for the same bot-blocking reason as above. It should be verified manually.
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found.** The QA tool notes that AgentMail may strip these headers. This is unconfirmed. The headers are required for Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender compliance, so check the raw message headers.
- **`Authentication-Results` was not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown from this capture. This is a limitation of the relay, not a confirmed failure.
- **The physical postal address** could not be checked because the footer is truncated.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **No UTM parameters are visible.** All links are opaque redirector URLs (`/r/?id=...&did=1427406191&rid=...&p1=DM6220793&p2=...&p3=20261115`). The parameters `p1` (likely a campaign ID) and `p3` (date, 2026-11-15) are internal ESP fields. Whether the redirect appends `utm_*` or equivalent on the destination is unknown.
- **Landing page alignment cannot be assessed.** The 403s prevented resolution of final URLs, so it is unknown whether the "premium knits" subject maps to a matching category page.
- **Linked image assets reference different campaign weeks** (`wk-46` and `wk48`) and regions (`us174` and `ca0106`). This may be a mixed-locale asset reference. It is worth confirming as a content-sourcing issue.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Re-probe with a browser-like user-agent, or manually click-test** the redirector links, especially Unsubscribe and the primary "Shop now" CTAs, to separate bot-blocking from real breakage.
2. **Add meaningful `alt` text to all images**, or `alt=""` on decorative ones and the tracking pixel. Add `mso-hide:all` / `aria-hidden` to the duplicate desktop/mobile variant so screen readers do not read it twice.
3. **Remove zero-width characters from link text** and move them to a preheader element outside the `<a>`.
4. **Check the raw headers** for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment on `email.hm.com`.
5. **Populate or remove the JSON-LD block.** Empty strings and a 2018 `availabilityStarts` should not ship.
6. **Resolve redirect chains** to confirm the final URLs and the UTM or analytics parameters they carry.
7. **Confirm the `wk48`/`ca0106` asset** is meant to be in a US `us174` send.
8. **Reduce the URL share of the plain-text part** (522 of 600 chars). It currently looks link-heavy and can hurt spam scoring.
