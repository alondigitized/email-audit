- The runner hero is a real photo with real motion, and it doesn't look like a stiff studio shot. It feels closer to how ASOS shoots the Lioness drop than to a catalog.
- The headline "H&M moves with you" is big and easy to read on my phone.
- The two-button layout (Shop Now / Find a Store) is clean, and the tap targets are big.
- The Women / Men / Kids buttons under the pajamas make it easy to get to my section.
- The pajama collage has pretty colors. The red bow, the plaid and the pink satin pop.

## What's weak

- The subject promises NYC and the email delivers none of it. That's a bait-and-switch feeling, and the only NYC mention is the tiny footer address.
- There's no offer, no price and no deadline anywhere. The gift card line at the top is the only hook, and it's tiny.
- The pajama collage is cropped so tight I can't tell what the products are. A bow and some fabric aren't clickable products.
- The runner is a good photo, but it's activewear, which isn't my Saturday three-tab compare. Shein, Zara and ASOS aren't doing marathon recaps.
- The "Shop by" grid and the footer are long and generic. There's nothing trend-first or "new this week" about it.
- It's the second pajama email in five days, so the cadence feels repetitive.

- 1. Make the subject and the hero match. If it's NYC, show NYC-themed pieces, like an NYC graphic tee or a skyline hoodie. If it's running, say that.
- 2. Put one concrete offer above the fold, such as "Gift card bonus" or "Free shipping on holiday sleepwear." Right now the gift card line is buried.
- 3. Link individual products with prices instead of a cropped collage. I want to see what the pajamas cost.
- 4. Pick one story per send. The running and pajamas halves don't connect.
- 5. Add a "new this week" or "trending now" row. That's what would make me feel H&M is first to the trend, not following Zara.
