# Light layers for warm days
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- Okay so H&M went full editorial on me today and I'm... lukewarm. "Soft tailoring" is genuinely on my FYP right now — the linen-meets-ruffle-blouse moment is real — but this email *presents* it the way a magazine does, not the way a TikTok creator would. It's clean, it's pretty, and it does absolutely nothing to make me feel like I need these pieces *today*.
- The product grid is actually solid — the crinkled romper and the ruffle-collar blouse both caught my eye, and the prices are right there under each tile. But there's zero sale hook in the body, which feels like a deliberate miss coming off their May 14th "Today only: 20% off" push. That email had urgency. This one just... sits there being aesthetic. If ASOS had dropped this same lineup and slapped "trending: soft tailoring" somewhere in the copy, I'd have tapped already. H&M trusted the vibe to do the work and the vibe alone isn't enough.
- I'm marking it read. Maybe I come back to the romper later, but I'm not clicking today and I'm definitely not forwarding — there's no "omg look at this" energy here.

## What's working

- **The aesthetic is genuinely on-trend.** Soft blues, fluid silhouettes, tie-shoulder details — this is the "quiet luxury but soft girl" moment that's actually happening on my FYP, H&M called it correctly.
- **Prices are visible under every product tile.** $29.99, $34.99, $44.99 — I don't have to click to know what I'm looking at before I commit to a tap.
- **"New Arrival" tags add a freshness signal.** Especially on the ruffle blouse and the jumpsuit — that's all I need to know this isn't clearance repackaged as a trend story.
- **H&M Studio Resort teaser is actually intriguing.** A named collection with a "May 21" drop date is the one thing in this email that creates forward momentum. "Artisan elegance" is a phrase that does something.
- **Renders perfectly.** No broken images, no overlapping text, the product grid is readable on mobile. Clean execution.

## What's weak

- **Zero discount in the body.** The top nav has a "SALE" link but the actual email content has no price reduction, no shipping offer, nothing. Coming one day after the @nassnyc soccer editorial (also no discount), H&M has sent me two full-price editorial blasts in a row. Shein would never do this without at least "free shipping over $25" anchoring the scroll.
- **"SHOP NOW" is the laziest CTA alive.** The section is "Soft Tailoring" — the button should say "Shop Soft Tailoring" or "Shop the Drop." "SHOP NOW" is what you write when you haven't decided what the email is for.
- **The hero model is a step backward from yesterday.** The @nassnyc email had a real person, a specific face, a creator energy. Today's model is generic editorial — pretty, but she could be from any brand's lookbook. One step forward, one step back on personality.
- **Studio Resort is buried.** That teaser is the most interesting thing in this email and it lives below a six-item product grid. It should be the hook, not the footnote.
- **Cadence is becoming noise.** May 14, 15, 16, 18, 19 — five sends in six days. None of them are doing enough work to justify that frequency, and this one is the softest of the five.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Studio Resort teaser or give it its own email.** A named collection dropping May 21 is a *preview moment* — that's the kind of thing I'd screenshot and send to a friend. Bury it and the energy dies.
- 2. **Add a low-friction offer somewhere in the body.** Even "free standard shipping this week" gives me a reason to click now vs. later. Right now there's nothing perishable.
- 3. **Rename the CTA.** "Shop Soft Tailoring" takes two seconds to change and is ten times more clickable than the default.
- 4. **Bring back a creator or real person wearing something.** Yesterday's @nassnyc framing worked. One image of a real girl styled in the crinkled romper does more than three editorial flats.
- 5. **Thin the cadence.** Bundle the editorial sends — one trend story + one product spotlight per email, not five separate blasts in a week.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading (high impact)**
```html
<style>
  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);

```
Gmail strips `<style>` blocks containing `@import`; Outlook ignores them entirely. No inline `font-family` fallback stack is declared in the same rule block. Affected clients will fall back to the system default serif/sans, breaking typographic intent silently.
