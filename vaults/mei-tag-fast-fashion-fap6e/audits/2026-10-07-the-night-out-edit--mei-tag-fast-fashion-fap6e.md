# The Night Out Edit
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-07**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is the first H&M email in a week that I'd actually screenshot. After the "up to 30% off fall essentials" banner (10/2 to 10/4) and the quiet "Style & support for every move" send on 10/6, a night-out edit with real prices and no discount shouting feels like a reset. The black tiered mini dress in the hero is the exact silhouette that's been on my FYP. The sequin slouchy socks with satin slingbacks at the bottom are the best thing in the whole email.
- It's still a catalog, though. "Elevated layers, statement pieces and polished accessories" is generic, and there's no trend name or "as seen on" hook. Zara's "Re{form} Zara. Vol. 1" had a point of view and a named designer. This one says "Night Out Edit" and leaves the trend work to me. It does feel more like H&M is leading than chasing, which is rare.
- I'd click, mostly for the dress and the slingbacks. I'd forward it to my group chat only if they were planning a night out. It's a solid 6, not a screenshot-and-send.

## What's working

- The hero dress is full-length on a clean grey background, and "NIGHT OUT EDIT" sits right under it. I know the theme in one second.
- Prices are on every product ($29.99 top, $39.99 pants, $49.99 dress, $39.99 blouse, $44.99 slingbacks, $14.99 earrings). That's the price-per-trend info I check across three tabs, and it's cheap enough for a night-out outfit.
- "New Arrival" tags tell me this is fresh, which matters when I'm comparing who ships the trend first.
- The "Complete the Look" section styles an outfit instead of dumping product. The sequin socks with the slingbacks are the standout.
- The ruffle-trimmed lace blouse with the baggy jeans is the model styling I'd copy.
- Models look like my age, and the styling is lived-in (boots, jewelry, messy hair), not stiff.

## What's weak

- There's no offer, no member perk and no deadline. After last week's 20% Member email, I feel no reason to click now instead of Saturday.
- Every CTA is "SHOP NOW" or "FIND A STORE". They're generic, and the two side-by-side buttons repeat twice.
- The top banner "NEED AN ITEM SOONER? BUY ONLINE, PICK UP IN-STORE" is a delivery message. It isn't a trend message, and it leads the email.
- The two-tone hoops and the scarf-look bag are small and don't say much. "Scarf-look shoulder bag" is a trend I'd want a closer look at.
- The email is very long, and the "Shop by" and footer blocks add scroll. There's no reviews, sizing or stock info anywhere.
- It doesn't name the trend (mesh, ballet, sequins) or give me any TikTok context.

## Recommendations

- 1. Name the trend in the hero. "Night Out Edit" is a category, so something like "The going-out dress everyone's styling with socks" would give me a reason to open.
- 2. Make the CTAs product-specific: "Shop the ruffle mini" or "Get the sequin socks". Drop the duplicate "Find a store" buttons.
- 3. Add one honest hook such as free shipping, a Member perk or a delivery date for this weekend. A "Get it by Friday" line would match the night-out timing.
- 4. Move the pickup banner below the hero, or tie it to the occasion: "Need it for Saturday?"
- 5. Make the slingbacks and sequin socks the second hero. They're the best thing here and they're buried.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your Saturday night fit, sorted`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Ruffle minis + sequin socks, $14.99+`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New-arrival going-out looks from $14.99, with pickup in time for the weekend.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's tracked links all returned HTTP 403 to the QA prober, including the unsubscribe link, so the 16% pass rate is mostly this one result. It also has widespread missing `alt` attributes and an empty, stale JSON-LD block. The 403s are probably bot-blocking by the `t19.email.hm.com` redirector rather than dead links, but that is unconfirmed.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **All 12 probed links returned 403.** They are all `https://t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=hb0a05fdf-…,548f106a,<hash>&did=1418661994&rid=3326930539&…`. This covers "Shop now" (×6), "Find a store" (×4), "Need an item sooner? Buy online, pick up in-store" (`…f862b9db`) and "Unsubscribe" (`…f862ba9b`).
- **Likely cause is bot filtering, not a broken link.** Every link was blocked in the same way, including the unsubscribe link. Click-tracker redirects commonly reject non-browser user agents. The final destinations were never resolved, so I can't confirm that real recipients land on a working page.
- **The tracked URLs hide their destinations.** Only 12 of 80 links were probed (68 skipped), so the rest are unverified.
- **Several CTAs share one link text with different tracking IDs.** Examples are "Shop now" `…ba59`, `…ba6b` and `…ba6d`, and "Find a store" `…ba58`, `…ba5a`, `…ba6c` and `…ba6e`. That is normal for per-slot tracking, but it makes the link text ambiguous for screen readers (see section 3).
- **Two CTA texts contain invisible characters.** "‌  ﻿Shop now" has a zero-width non-joiner and a BOM (U+FEFF) prefix (`…f862b9dd`, `…f862b9df`). These are typically preheader or spacing hacks, but they can break link-text matching and add noise for assistive tech.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on 19 images.** Each product image appears twice, as `cq5dam.web.1400.1400.jpeg` and `cq5dam.web.750.750.jpeg`. The paired sizes suggest a desktop/mobile swap, so each product image is flagged twice. The sources are under `https://image.hm.com/content/dam/hm/regional-local-activities/ame/2026-ame/wk-41/na1561_s4/`, for example `1354801001_202604_LB_0730.jpg`, `1354934001_202604_LB_1190.jpg`, `1361953001_202604_LB_0928.jpg`, `1364542001_202604_LB_0482.jpg`, `1365014002_202604_LB_3012.jpg`, `1352482001_202604_LB_0016.jpg`, `2627015_Accroa3132_0875.jpg` and `1342432002_202604_LB_0203.jpg`.
  - If any of these are meaningful content images, they are unlabeled for screen readers and for image-blocked clients.
  - If they are decorative, they should have `alt=""` explicitly. The QA check cannot tell the two cases apart.
- **Two flagged images have a tracker URL as `src`** (`https://t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=hb0a05fdf-…,1`). These are likely the open-tracking pixel or a linked header image. A tracking pixel should carry `alt=""`.
- **Invalid CSS in the `optimize-css` block.** `.is-hidden-desktop{…line-height:auto!important…}` uses `auto`, which is not a valid `line-height` value, so it is ignored. `@media screen yahoo` is also a non-standard media query.
- **External font `@import`s.** `fabric.hmgroup.com/…/fonts.css` and `s1-cdn.hm.com/…/oces.css` are stripped by Gmail and some other clients. The email depends on fallback fonts there.
- **The HTML was truncated.** Table structure, `lang`/`dir`, `role="presentation"` and the `<title>` could not be checked.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The visible source has no unrendered merge tokens (`{{…}}`, `%%…%%`, `[FIRST_NAME]`). Only the first part of the HTML was available, so tokens further down could not be checked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Footer unsubscribe link is present.** It is `…f862ba9b…&p4=3326930539&p5=9be06746af74de7271d9dcc8d83f5742` and returned 403 to the prober. The probe's 403 doesn't show that it fails for real users, but it should be tested in a real browser session.
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found.** The QA tool notes that the AgentMail relay may strip these headers, so this is **unconfirmed**. Raw headers from the sending side are needed to settle it.
- **`Authentication-Results` was not found, so SPF/DKIM/DMARC status is unknown.** This is expected via the relay and is not evidence of a failure. The `From` is `newsletter@email.hm.com` and the tracking domain is `t19.email.hm.com`, so the domains are aligned.
- **A physical postal address could not be verified.** It would be in the truncated footer.
- **The plain-text part is more than 70% URLs (518 of 596 chars).** This is a deliverability signal, since the text part is almost entirely tracking links with little readable copy.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **No UTM parameters are visible.** Click tracking goes through the `t19.email.hm.com/r/` redirector and uses proprietary params (`did`, `rid`, `erid`, `p1=DM6114295`, `p2`, `p3=20261007`). There are no `utm_*` params in the email-side URLs.
- **Landing-page alignment could not be verified.** Every redirect returned 403, so the final URLs and any appended UTMs were never observed.
- **Slot IDs are consistent.** Each link's unique suffix (`f862ba57`, `…ba58`, and so on) is incremental, which suggests proper per-placement tracking.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Verify the 403s with a browser-like user agent.** Re-probe the `t19.email.hm.com/r/` links, follow the redirects and record the final URLs. Treat the 403 as a prober false positive unless a browser also fails. Check the unsubscribe link first.
2. **Add `alt` text to all 19 flagged images.** Use descriptive alt text for product images, and `alt=""` for decorative images and the tracking pixel.
3. **Fix the CSS.** Replace `line-height:auto` with `normal` or `initial`, and remove the invalid `@media screen yahoo` hack.
4. **Clean up the JSON-LD block.** The Gmail-annotation JSON-LD has empty `subjectLine`, `discountCode`, `image` and `url` values and a stale `availabilityStarts` of `2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00`. Populate it or remove it.
5. **Capture raw headers for the next audit.** Get `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results` from the original message, not the relay copy, so compliance and authentication can be assessed.
6. **Expand the plain-text part.** It should include readable copy, not mostly tracking URLs.
7. **Check that the zero-width characters in the CTA text are intentional.** Remove them from "Shop now" if they are not.
