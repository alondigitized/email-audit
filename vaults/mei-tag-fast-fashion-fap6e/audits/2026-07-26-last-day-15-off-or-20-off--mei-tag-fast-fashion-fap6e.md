# Last day: 15% off or 20% off?!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-26**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so this is DAY FIVE. Five days straight H&M has been in my inbox — sitewide 15% on the 22nd, a random "new arrivals" reset on the 23rd, BEDDING (still don't know why) on the 24th, a "weekend deal" on the 25th, and now today it's "Last day: 15% off or 20% off?!" which is basically yesterday's email with the 20% threshold bumped from $60 to $80. That's not a new offer, that's the same offer wearing a different hat.
- The hero shot is actually kind of fire — the oversized layered look, wide-leg trousers, that whole '90s-revival thing is very much what's trending on my FYP right now. If they'd leaned into THAT ("get the look before it's everywhere") instead of a generic spend-tier discount, I'd have been way more interested. Instead I get a math problem: 15% off $60 OR 20% off $80, figure it out yourself.
- Compared to ASOS's "Final hours" email with the ticker banner and stacked 70%+40% off (7/10, and deservedly aggressive), this feels sleepy. It's cleaner than Zara's ATHLETICZ shirtless-guy-on-a-rock disaster (4/10) and way more useful than H&M's own bare-bones welcome email (3/10), so it's not bad — it's just the fourth or fifth version of the same sale email this week, and "last day" doesn't feel true anymore when they said basically the same thing yesterday. I'd scroll through, maybe tap the trending blouse, and not forward this to anyone.

## What's working

- The hero photo is genuinely trend-accurate — oversized fit, wide-leg pants, that's what I'd screenshot for a lookbook, not just marketing filler.
- Category grid (Women/Men/Baby/Kids/Home/Beauty) is clean and scannable, no clutter.
- Product tiles have real prices and "New Arrival" tags, which at least signals freshness even if the copy doesn't lean into it.
- Layout is organized by Trending → Fresh → Adorable sections, easy to skim on mobile without zooming.

## What's weak

- "Last day" urgency in the subject doesn't show up anywhere in the actual email body — no countdown, no banner, nothing reinforcing that this actually ends today. ASOS's ticker-tape "SALE: FINAL HOURS" banner does this so much better.
- The offer itself ($60/$80 spend tiers) is just a slightly reshuffled version of yesterday's "Weekend Deal" — same 15/20% structure, barely different threshold. If I read both back to back I'd feel like they're just recycling.
- No single obvious CTA button under the big offer — just six category boxes, so my eye doesn't know where to tap first.
- Zero trend-chasing language anywhere ("as seen on," "before it sells out," "TikTok's favorite") despite the hero image literally being trend content. Feels like a missed layup.
- Fine print at the bottom excludes sale items, furniture, H&M Studio, and collabs — that's a lot of asterisks on a "simple" discount.

## Recommendations

- 1. Actually use the trend angle — swap the generic "New chapters call for new arrivals" line for something that names the trend (e.g. "The oversized layer everyone's copying is back in stock"). That hero photo is doing more work than the copy.
- 2. Add a real urgency element in the body — a countdown or a bold "ENDS TONIGHT" banner like ASOS uses — if you're going to promise "last day" in the subject, prove it in the email.
- 3. Put one clear CTA button directly under the offer text instead of making me choose between six category tiles.
- 4. Space out the sends — five promo emails in five days is why "last day" doesn't land; save the urgency language for when it's actually rare.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last chance: up to 20% off ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The trend everyone's copying — 20% off today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Oversized layers, wide-leg fits, and 20% off before it's gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Spend $80, save 20% — final hours to shop`

# Technical Email Audit: H&M "Last day: 15% off or 20% off?!"

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's tracking redirector (`t19.email.hm.com`) is returning HTTP 403 on every probed link, including the unsubscribe link, and the automated QA pass rate is 16% (12 issues, 4 warnings) — this is a broken-experience-level failure, not a minor defect.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **All 12 probed material links return 403**, including primary CTAs ("Shop now" x2, "Need an item sooner? Buy online, pick up in-store"), nav links (Women, Men, Baby, Kids, HOME), the logo link ("H&M"), both product images, and **Unsubscribe**. Example:
  `https://t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=hbe171ffd-6979-4aea-a11f-f154508d85fd,6635289f,f788dd6e&did=1714759839&rid=3326930539&erid=MzMyNjkzMDUzOQ%3D%3D&p1=DM5880305&p2=0982dd0aa7b117bf086dee6ed4af7d0c&p3=20260726`
  All links route through the same `t19.email.hm.com/r/` redirector with a shared `id`/`did`/`rid`/`erid` pattern — the 403 is consistent across every link, indicating the redirector itself (not individual destinations) is rejecting the request (likely bot/UA filtering, an expired signed token, or an IP/rate block at the ESP edge).
- Two CTA link texts contain embedded zero-width/invisible characters (U+200C ZWNJ and U+FEFF BOM: `"‌  ﻿Shop now"`), suggesting a template merge artifact leaking into anchor text.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **26 images are missing `alt` text**, spanning the hero/product images (`cq5dam.web.1400.1400.jpeg`, `cq5dam.web.750.750.jpeg`) and the full product grid (e.g. `5952e13ac130d2e4abfe49f0751977c6a4dfdcb0.jpg`, `6867d71d91f74b8d178209a5e521d4068cdd5449.jpg`, and 20+ others across `imWidth=344`/`imWidth=322` variants). Screen readers and blocked-image fallback states will render this email with no meaningful content.
- Mobile CSS (`@media (max-width:699px)`) correctly toggles `.is-hidden-mobile`/`.is-hidden-desktop`, and Outlook/Yahoo-specific hooks (`#MessageViewBody`, `@media screen yahoo`) are present — no structural issues found there.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The `application/ld+json` `EmailMessage`/`DiscountOffer`/`PromotionCard` schema block ships with **unresolved empty merge fields**: `"subjectLine":""`, `"discountCode":""`, `"availabilityEnds":""`, `"image":""`, `"url":""`. These are structured-data fields Gmail/Google use to render annotated promo cards in the inbox — as shipped, the JSON-LD is present but functionally empty, so no rich promo card will render, and any downstream consumer of `discountCode` gets nothing.
- `availabilityStarts` is populated (`"2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"`) but appears to be a stale/incorrect placeholder date relative to the campaign send window (week of 2026-07-26 per the `p3=20260726` tracking param) — the year doesn't reconcile with the campaign date.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Unsubscribe link itself returns 403** (same redirector failure as above) — this is a functional compliance failure, not just a broken CTA: `.../r/?id=hbe171ffd...&p4=3326930539&p5=9be06746af74de7271d9dcc8d83f5742`. If subscribers cannot complete unsubscribe, this is a CAN-SPAM risk regardless of header-level compliance.
- **`List-Unsubscribe` header not found** in the captured message — may be a capture artifact of the relay rather than absence in the original send, but as observed it means mail clients can't offer one-click unsubscribe from the header UI.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found** (RFC 8058) — one-click unsubscribe via POST is not confirmed present.
- **`Authentication-Results` header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is indeterminate from this capture.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- No links use standard UTM parameters (`utm_source`/`utm_medium`/`utm_campaign`). Attribution instead relies entirely on the proprietary redirector query string (`id`, `did`, `rid`, `erid`, `p1`–`p5`, e.g. `p1=DM5880305`, `p3=20260726`). This is consistent H&M ESP practice, not itself a defect, but it means landing-page/analytics alignment can't be verified independently of the redirector — which is currently down (see §2), so **no destination URLs could be validated** for this send.
- Plain-text part is **>70% URLs** (522/600 chars) — this both hurts spam-filter scoring and reflects that the plain-text alternative carries almost no readable content independent of links.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **P0:** Investigate the 403s at `t19.email.hm.com` — check for an expired/invalid signing token, WAF/bot-detection rule, or rate limiting on the redirector; this is blocking every CTA and the unsubscribe path.
2. **P0:** Confirm unsubscribe works via an alternate path (List-Unsubscribe header/RFC 8058 one-click) given the in-body link is broken.
3. **P1:** Populate the empty JSON-LD fields (`subjectLine`, `discountCode`, `availabilityEnds`, `image`, `url`) or remove the schema block if not in use — partial/empty structured data provides no benefit and may confuse Gmail's promo card renderer.
4. **P1:** Add `alt` text to all 26 flagged images for accessibility and blocked-image fallback.
5. **P2:** Strip the stray zero-width/BOM characters from the "Shop now" anchor text.
6. **P2:** Reduce plain-text URL density to improve deliverability scoring.
