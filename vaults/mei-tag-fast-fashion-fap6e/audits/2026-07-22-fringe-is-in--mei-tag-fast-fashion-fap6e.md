# Fringe is in
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- Okay so ASOS has been absolutely flooding my inbox this week — 70% off sale, XTRA30 code, the Rina Lipa drop, two emails on the same day on July 20th — and now they pivot to THIS. Pure editorial mode. No code, no discount, just "fringe is in." And honestly? The vibe is right. Fringe has been blowing up on TikTok all summer and ASOS calling it in a full send actually feels like they're *leading* something instead of chasing a Shein dupe. The hero shot is clean — the fringe halter-neck with the wide-leg pants is exactly the kind of outfit I screenshot and send to my group chat.
- But I'm not going to pretend the whiplash doesn't register. After five emails in seven days, three of which were screaming SALE in all caps, switching to "here's a vibe" with zero price anchor feels like they ran out of percent-offs and pivoted to content marketing. Zara would never — Zara just posts one cryptic image with a designer's name and lets me spiral. This is the opposite energy.
- I'd click. Fringe is genuinely my focus right now and the "COMPLETE THE LOOK" accessories tiles actually match how I shop on a Saturday. But I wouldn't forward it — there's no hook to send a friend without a discount attached. It's a solo browse, not a "OMG look at this" moment.

## What's working

- The fringe hero is perfectly timed — this trend is everywhere on TikTok right now and ASOS is calling it with confidence, not just throwing it in a sale carousel.
- The model and styling read as genuinely Gen Z: festival-meets-boho, not the H&M "here's a blouse" energy.
- "COMPLETE THE LOOK" cross-sell with bags, sunglasses, heeled sandals, and bangles is exactly how my brain works when I find a hero piece — ASOS knows I shop head-to-toe.
- The Adidas Originals x ASOS collab section is a smart bonus — collabs are the kind of thing I actually save and come back to, and it gives the email a second reason to exist beyond the editorial.
- Layout is clean top to bottom — no broken images, no janky overlaps, and the hierarchy flows from hero to products to accessories without me having to hunt.

## What's weak

- Zero price anchoring anywhere — after a week of 70% off and XTRA30, the sudden "editorial only" pivot feels like bait-and-switch in reverse. I don't know if fringe is $15 or $65.
- The product grid images are tiny — on mobile those six product shots are basically thumbnails and I can't tell what I'm looking at without zooming in.
- "Summer forever" section below the fringe edit feels like padding that breaks the momentum; it's a completely different visual vibe and I genuinely forget what email I'm in.
- This is email number five from ASOS in seven days. Cadence fatigue is real and this one doesn't earn its spot the way the 70% off send did.
- The "SHOP OUR TOP BRANDS" logos at the bottom are so small they're basically decorative — that row serves no one on mobile.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a "from $X" price point in the hero** — even just "Fringe pieces from $22" under the hero CTA converts browsers to clickers. Editorial is fun but I need a reason to tap through.
- 2. **Upgrade the product grid to 2-up on mobile** — the 3-across thumbnail layout makes it impossible to see details on a phone screen. Shein gets this right; ASOS is leaving conversions on the table.
- 3. **Cut "Summer forever" or roll it into the fringe narrative** — it reads like a separate email got stitched in and dilutes the editorial authority ASOS is trying to claim.
- 4. **Move the Adidas collab above the fold** — collabs have higher intent signal than generic edits. Lead with it or at least surface it in the hero area.
- 5. **Reduce cadence** — five emails in a week means this one is competing with itself. Save the editorial send for when there's some breathing room post-sale.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Malformed document structure — `<meta>` tags inside `<body><p>`**

The source opens as `<!DOCTYPE html><html><body>` with no `<head>` element, then immediately places all `<meta>` tags inside a `<p>` tag within `<body>`:

```html
<body>
  <div style="display:none ..."></div>
  <p>
    <meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8">
    <meta charset="utf-8">
    ...
  </p>
```

`<meta>` is a void element that belongs in `<head>`. Placing it inside `<p>` in `<body>` produces invalid HTML per spec. Gmail strips `<head>` entirely, so some senders embed meta in body as a workaround — but they should use a wrapper `<div>`, not `<p>`, and the `<head>` block should still be present for clients that preserve it. If the `<head>` was stripped by the truncation process rather than absent from the actual send, this is not an issue — verify against the raw source.

**[MEDIUM] Empty preheader**

The preheader `<div>` contains no visible text content:

```html
<div style="display:none !important;visibility:hidden;..."></div>
```

Without preheader text, email clients (Gmail, Apple Mail) fall back to pulling the first rendered text as the preview snippet. Confirm whether preheader copy is injected at send time via ESP merge or is genuinely missing.

**[LOW] Font-weight mismatch on `FuturaPT-Heavy`**

The `@font-face` rule declares `FuturaPT-Heavy` with `font-weight: 400` in the fallback spec but then uses `font-weight: 600` in the CSS rule for `h1, h2, h3`. "Heavy" conventionally maps to weight 800–900. In clients that substitute the system fallback (`Arial`), this will render as semibold rather than heavy, causing a visible weight difference between fallback and webfont rendering.

**[LOW] `@keyframes` ticker animation**

The live ticker tape uses `@keyframes` animation gated behind `@supports`. Animation support in email is limited to Apple Mail and a small subset of webmail clients. The `@supports` gate is implemented correctly (hiding the `.ticker` and showing `.fallback` in unsupported clients), so this is handled — but confirm the `.fallback` static element contains equivalent content so non-animating clients get the same information.

**[LOW] `font-size: 0em` on all table elements**

```css
table, tr, td, th { font-size: 0em; ... }
```

This is a standard email hack to eliminate whitespace gaps between inline-block table cells. Confirm content `<td>` elements all explicitly reset `font-size` to a non-zero value, otherwise text within any table cell that lacks an explicit override will be invisible.
