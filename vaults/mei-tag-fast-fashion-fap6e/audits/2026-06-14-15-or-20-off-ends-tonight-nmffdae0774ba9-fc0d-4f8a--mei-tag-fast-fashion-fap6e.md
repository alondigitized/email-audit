# 15% or 20% off ends tonight
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- The subject line earned the open — "ends tonight" with actual percentage numbers is the kind of thing I pause on mid-scroll, especially when the numbers are specific enough to do math in my head. But the very first thing I see when the email loads is a Father's Day gift banner. H&M, I literally audited your Father's Day email from June 13th. And the one from the 11th. This is at minimum the third time this banner has shown up in my inbox this month, and you put it at the top of a discount-urgency send where it has zero business being.
- Past the banner, the hero model in that white floral ruffled top is actually gorgeous and very on-trend for my summer mood right now. The "LAST DAY / 15% or 20% OFF" reads immediately. But then I scroll and the product grid goes WOMEN → BABY → KIDS → HOME → BEAUTY and suddenly I'm looking at baby clothes next to a stainless steel vase. I don't have children. I don't have a home decor budget. I have a Depop wishlist and a fast fashion tab open on my phone.
- Compare this to the ASOS tennis heritage email I saw recently — that thing knew exactly who it was talking to, one trend, one aesthetic, locked in. This H&M send is trying to be the entire store in one scroll and it ends up feeling like a blast to every subscriber segment at once. I'd probably click through to Women's anyway because the deal is real and the timing pressure is real, but I'm not forwarding this. Mark-as-read.

## What's working

- The "LAST DAY" + tiered percentage structure ($80 = 15%, $100 = 20%) is immediately legible — I know exactly what I'm dealing with before I've scrolled at all
- "ends tonight" in the subject hits clean urgency without exclamation marks or ALL CAPS; doesn't feel like spam, feels like a real deadline
- The hero model in the white ruffled floral top is exactly the aesthetic that's on my FYP right now — summer-coded, trend-forward, and the styling actually sells the item
- Specific price callouts on the product tiles (HIGH WAIST DENIM SKIRT $29.99, 2-PIECE RUFFLED COTTON SET $14.99) give me something real to evaluate, not just vibes
- No visible render bugs — layout is clean, images loaded, text is readable without zooming

## What's weak

- Father's Day in-store banner is sitting at the very top of a discount email — this was already the energy of the June 13th send and it's showing up as the inbox preheader, which actively undermines the subject line
- Baby and Kids sections in the product grid have zero relevance to me as a 24-year-old solo shopper; they kill scroll momentum right after the women's section
- The STAINLESS STEEL VASE and SUNLIT BRONZER feel like they wandered in from a completely different send — the category scatter makes this feel like a catalogue blast, not a curated trend drop
- The spending threshold ($80 minimum to save anything) is visible but easy to miss at a quick read — I didn't clock what I'd need to spend until I looked twice
- "SHOP NOW" in the nav is generic and links god-knows-where; ASOS would have put "Shop Women's Summer" on a direct collection link by now

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill or footer the Father's Day banner.** The discount is the campaign — that in-store gift message is eating the preheader slot in every inbox client and muddying the offer for anyone who's already seen it three times this month.
- 2. **Women-first grid, no exceptions above the fold.** One curated women's block with 4–6 trend-relevant items. Baby, Kids, and Home can live in the footer shop-by row — not a full product section competing for scroll attention.
- 3. **Surface the spending threshold prominently in the hero.** "Spend $80, save 15% — spend $100, save 20%" as a hero subhead so I calculate immediately rather than feeling surprised at checkout.
- 4. **Replace "SHOP NOW" with a category-specific CTA.** "Shop Women's Summer" or even "Get the Look" pointed at a curated landing page. The June 11th day-to-night email (9/10) actually did this well — the styling categories made every click feel intentional.
- 5. **Fix the preheader so it matches the campaign.** Right now the inbox preview is pulling Father's Day copy. The send earns better than that.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Tonight only: 15–20% off your summer haul`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Ends tonight — 15% or 20% off, your call`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $80 → 15% off. Spend $100 → 20% off. Tonight only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The summer pieces you've been eyeing just got cheaper. Ends midnight.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Font loading via `@import` (high risk)**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` tags is blocked or ignored by Outlook (all versions), Yahoo Mail, and older Android clients. These clients will fall back to system fonts with no branded typography.

**Responsive CSS via media queries**
The `@media (max-width:699px)` block is well-structured, but media queries are unsupported in Outlook on Windows and Gmail (web). Elements relying on `.is-hidden-mobile` / `.is-hidden-desktop` toggling will not hide/show correctly in those clients.

**Alt text** — Cannot assess from truncated source. All `<img>` tags should have non-empty `alt` attributes, particularly promotional imagery.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Multiple unfilled JSON-LD fields** — The `<script type="application/ld+json">` block contains empty-string values that appear to be unfilled template tokens:

| Field | Expected value | Actual value |
|---|---|---|
| `EmailMessage.subjectLine` | "15% or 20% off ends tonight" | `""` |
| `DiscountOffer.availabilityEnds` | Tonight's deadline timestamp | `""` |
| `DiscountOffer.discountCode` | promo code or `null` | `""` |
| `DiscountOffer.description` | offer description | `""` |
| `PromotionCard.image` | hero image URL | `""` |
| `PromotionCard.url` | landing page URL | `""` |

**Stale `availabilityStarts` date (critical)**
```json
"availabilityStarts":"2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"
```
This timestamp is 7.5 years in the past. Gmail uses this field to render the Promotions tab deal card. A past `availabilityStarts` combined with an empty `availabilityEnds` and empty `discountCode`/`image`/`url` means **the Gmail deal card will not render**, eliminating inbox preview prominence for Gmail Promotions tab subscribers.

**HTTP vs HTTPS in schema context**
```json
"@context":"http://schema.org/"
```
All four schema objects use `http://` instead of `https://`. Google's structured data documentation expects `https://schema.org`.
