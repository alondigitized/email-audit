# Up to 50% off ends tonight
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-08**
## Executive summary

- It's literally "UP TO 50% OFF SITEWIDE" again — I'm not even mad, I'm just tired. This is the FIFTH H&M email in a row hitting sitewide 50% off with a fake urgency wrapper slapped on top ("Last Day" this time, after yesterday's "ends tonight" energy and the day before's "everyday essentials" rebrand). Nothing here says trend, nothing says "this drop is ours first" — it's just a straight-up department store catalog scroll: women, desk-to-dinner, men, kids, baby, home. That's Old Navy energy, not the brand that's supposed to be racing Zara and Shein to the runway knockoff.
- Zara's kids-only puffer email was a worse miss because it was 100% off-persona, but at least it looked like a campaign. This is just inventory-clearance vibes with stock photography that could've shot in 2019. I'm not opening my phone for this — deleting, not forwarding. If a friend asked "did H&M drop anything new," the honest answer is "no, they're still doing the Labor Day sale, it's day 5."

## What's working

- The hero shot (maroon knit sweater) is at least a clean, well-lit product photo — no render bugs, text is legible.
- "LAST DAY" + "UP TO 50% OFF SITEWIDE" hierarchy is unmissable at the top — I know the offer in one glance.
- Member incentive (extra 10% off, "it's fast and free") is a real, concrete lever for someone who isn't already a loyalty member.
- Section breakdown (Women/Men/Kids/Baby/Home) at least organizes the chaos so I'm not just staring at a wall of product.

## What's weak

- Same sitewide 50% headline as Sunday, Monday, and now today — this is the least trend-forward this brand has sounded all week, and trend-first vs. trend-follow is the ENTIRE reason I have H&M in my rotation next to Zara and Shein.
- Zero mention of specific trending pieces, no "as seen on TikTok," no styling context — just generic lifestyle stock photos (guy in a jacket, kid in a hoodie) that could be any mall brand's fall catalog.
- I'm 24 and childless — the "All Day Fun" kids/baby block and "Fall Touches" home section are dead weight scrolling real estate for me specifically.
- "Clock's ticking" urgency reads hollow when yesterday's subject also said "ends tonight" and the sale clearly didn't end.
- No price-per-item, no specific product callouts, no social proof — just category buttons ("Women," "Men," "Shop Now") that dump me on a generic landing page instead of one hero item I'd actually tap for.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the sitewide-50%-every-day cadence — if I open one more of these this week I'm unsubscribing. Anchor the NEXT send around one actual trend drop (a specific silhouette, color, or TikTok-viral item) instead of the discount tier.
- 2. Cut Kids/Baby/Home from my segment entirely — as a 24-year-old with no kids, that's 40% of this email that's pure scroll fatigue.
- 3. Replace generic category photos with one hero product that's actually trending right now, plus a real price tag on it, not just "up to 50%."
- 4. Make the CTA button copy specific ("Shop the Knit Set") instead of blank "SHOP NOW" repeated four times.
- 5. If urgency is going to be the lead, make the countdown feel real — a literal timer graphic, not just repeated "last day" copy across five emails.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call: your fall staples are 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus an extra 10% when you join free`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The oversized knit everyone's restocking`

# Technical Audit — H&M "Up to 50% off ends tonight"

## 1. Technical Summary
The email fails the majority of automated QA checks (16% pass rate), driven almost entirely by systemic 403 errors on all probed tracking-redirect links, including the unsubscribe link.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All 12 probed links route through the `t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=...` click-tracking redirector and return **403 Forbidden**, including primary CTAs ("SHOP NOW" ×2, "Shop now" ×2), the membership links ("BECOME A MEMBER" ×2), the H&M logo link, both hero image links, and the header "H&M" link.
- Because every link shares the same redirector host/path pattern (`t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=h1cc8fe96-...&did=1715735799&rid=3326930539...`) and all return 403, this points to a systemic issue with the tracking domain/token validity at scan time (e.g., expired or environment-restricted redirect token) rather than isolated broken destinations — cannot confirm final landing URLs since the redirector itself blocks resolution.
- Two link texts contain zero-width/invisible characters (`‌  ﻿Shop now`) — zero-width non-joiner and BOM-like characters embedded in anchor text, which can cause inconsistent rendering/screen-reader announcement.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 12 `<img>` elements are missing `alt` text, including all product imagery (`cq5dam.web.1400.1400.jpeg`, `cq5dam.web.750.750.jpeg` variants across 5 product images) and 2 tracking pixel images. Screen readers will announce these as unlabeled or fall back to filename/URL.
- Extensive mobile-responsive CSS overrides (`@media (max-width:699px)`) are present and appear well-formed in the truncated source; no MSO/Outlook-specific breakage observed in the visible markup.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The `application/ld+json` `EmailMessage`/`DiscountOffer` schema block has multiple empty string fields (`"subjectLine":""`, `"availabilityEnds":""`, `"discountCode":""`, `"image":""`, `"url":""`), suggesting unresolved merge tokens or a templating fallback that failed to populate structured data. `availabilityStarts` is also hardcoded to a stale date (`2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00`), indicating this JSON-LD block is either legacy/unmaintained boilerplate or a merge failure.
- No other visible merge-tag artifacts (e.g., `{{first_name}}`) found in the truncated HTML.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- Unsubscribe link is present but returns **403** on probe (same redirector failure as above) — functionally, the unsubscribe mechanism could not be verified as working.
- `List-Unsubscribe` header not detected — required for RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe and increasingly enforced by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender rules; may be a relay-capture limitation rather than sender omission.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not detected — one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) cannot be confirmed as supported.
- `Authentication-Results` header not found — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status cannot be verified from available data.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Links use proprietary H&M tracking params (`did`, `rid`, `erid`, `p1`–`p5`) rather than standard UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) — this is consistent with a dedicated ESP click-tracking domain (t19.email.hm.com) rather than a UTM-based analytics setup, so no comment on UTM absence itself.
- Landing page alignment cannot be assessed: every link resolves through the 403-failing redirector, so the actual destination URL/page content is unverifiable from the data provided.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Investigate the 403 responses from `t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=...` — verify whether tracking tokens (`id`, `did`, `rid`, `erid`) are time-limited, IP/referrer-restricted, or require a specific User-Agent/session that the QA prober lacks; confirm real-world click-through still works from an actual inbox.
2. Fix or remove the stale/empty `EmailMessage` JSON-LD block — populate `subjectLine`, `discountCode`, `availabilityEnds`, `image`, and `url`, or drop the schema entirely if not actively used by Gmail promotions markup.
