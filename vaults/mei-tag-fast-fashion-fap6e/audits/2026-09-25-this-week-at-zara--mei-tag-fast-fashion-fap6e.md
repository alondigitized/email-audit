## What's working

- THE KNIT EDIT and TRF COLLECTION headers are bold, chunky, on-trend typography — actually scroll-stopping compared to H&M Studio's more restrained editorial layout
- Martin Parr collab feels like a genuine cultural moment, not just "new arrivals" — way more interesting than ASOS's Lioness drop which at least had a clear campaign hook but no bigger reference point
- Clean category breakdowns (Knitwear, Accessories, T-Shirts, Shoes, Hoodies, Trousers, Jackets) make it easy to skim to what I care about
- Model diversity is decent — different looks across the sections, not just one aesthetic repeated

## What's weak

- Zero pricing anywhere — not one % off, not a single dollar sign, in a scroll this long. Even H&M's "Studio A/W 2026" send (5/10, no discount either) at least felt premium enough to justify it; this just feels like a missed chance
- No urgency, no "shop before it's gone," nothing time-bound — TRF and Martin Parr both feel like they could sit in my inbox unopened for a week and lose nothing
- CTAs are just "VIEW" — no verb-noun specificity like "Shop the Edit" or "Get the Look"
- This is the second knit-focused Zara send this month if you count the Kids cashmere knitwear from 9/13 — cadence is getting repetitive on the knitwear beat

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price or % off somewhere — literally anywhere — even just "New TRF from $29.90" under the collection header. This is the single biggest lever; right now it's a lookbook, not a marketing email.
- 2. Swap "VIEW" CTAs for something specific like "Shop The Knit Edit" or "See the Collab" — generic CTAs kill click intent for someone who compares three tabs every Saturday.
- 3. Add a small urgency line under Martin Parr — "Limited run, while it lasts" — since collabs are the one category that actually supports real scarcity messaging.
- 4. Lead with Martin Parr instead of burying it mid-scroll — it's the most shareable, most distinct content in the whole email and it's currently competing with five other sections for attention.
