# Guess who's back?
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-10**
## Executive summary

- Okay so ASOS pulled the classic "Guess who's back?" and honestly? My first instinct was curiosity — the subject is playful, it doesn't scream SALE SALE SALE like yesterday's "20% off 1000s of styles" did. But then I opened it and it's... a back-in-stock grid. Five summery products on five different models, a "SHOP BACK IN STOCK" button, and then immediately — a feedback survey asking me to pick my "vibe." That tonal whiplash is real. The clothes themselves are cute and 100% my summer 2026 energy: the gingham mini, the ditsy floral top, the polka dot. Visually it works. Editorially it has no spine.
- What kills me is that ASOS had a genuinely good email two days ago — the World Cup "Styled for match day" send actually had a *reason* to exist. This one? The back-in-stock concept is fine, but restocking is reactive energy. It says "we ran out and now we have more" not "we saw what's popping and pulled it first." For a brand I follow specifically because they *move fast on trends*, framing the hero as leftovers is a weird choice. Zara's Pre-Owned email from last week had more editorial confidence than this.
- I would not click. I would not forward. I'd glance at the gingham dress, maybe tap it, then close the tab when it dumps me on a generic category page.

## What's working

- **Model diversity is actually good here** — five different models, different skin tones, natural hair, varying body types. Feels genuinely inclusive, not checkbox-diverse.
- **Summer product selection is on-point** — gingham mini, ditsy floral, polka dot? All three are in my TikTok feed right now. The merch team is paying attention.
- **"SHOP BACK IN STOCK" CTA is more specific than their usual "SHOP NOW"** — at least it tells me what I'm clicking into.
- **Clean render, zero breakage** — after H&M's Father's Day disaster sending me menswear three times in a row, I appreciate an email that just… shows up properly.
- **The preference survey section is actually smart** — "YOUR MOST HYPED / MOST WANTED / THE HOT LIST" is a clever way to let me self-segment. Just buried too deep.

## What's weak

- **No offer, no urgency, no reason to act now** — back-in-stock without any deadline ("selling fast," "limited sizes") or incentive is just a catalog page in my inbox.
- **"DOWNLOAD THE APP" green bar mid-email** — absolutely kills the momentum. I was looking at cute summer clothes and then suddenly it's an app install ad. Classic ASOS multi-CTA chaos.
- **"Guess who's back?" doesn't connect to the content** — it sounds like a brand comeback, an influencer collab, a celeb restock. Not "your fave polka dot top has 12 units left." The subject set up a different expectation.
- **No price signals anywhere** — I do three-tab comparisons on Saturdays. If I can't see at least a price range in the email, I'm starting my compare on Zara's tab, not ASOS's.
- **The survey section breaks the shopping loop** — put it in the footer or a dedicated email. Asking me to label my content preferences in the middle of a shopping email is confusing the intent.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make "back in stock" feel urgent and scarce** — "Only 12 left in your size" or a timestamp. Back-in-stock without scarcity is a non-event. One line of copy does this.
- 2. **Drop the app download CTA entirely from this email** — that's a separate campaign. It dilutes the back-in-stock focus and the green color clashes with the editorial vibe.
- 3. **Anchor a price** — "From £18" or "under £30" somewhere in the hero. I need a reason to stay in ASOS's tab vs. bouncing to Zara.
- 4. **Move the preference survey to the footer or a standalone send** — it's genuinely a good idea but it belongs after the shop moment, not interrupting it.
- 5. **Subject should hint at the stakes (scarcity/timing)** — the playful teaser works once, but with five emails in eight days it needs more substance.
- **Subject Alt A:** `These sold out fast. They're back.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your faves are back in stock (not for long)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `5 summer pieces that went fast — grab them before they're gone again`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The gingham dress. The polka dot top. Back, briefly.`
