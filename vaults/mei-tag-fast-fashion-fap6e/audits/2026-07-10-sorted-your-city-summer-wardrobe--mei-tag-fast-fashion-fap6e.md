# Sorted: your city summer wardrobe
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- Okay so I need to start with the context because it matters: yesterday H&M was in my inbox with "25% off flash sale starts now" — like, twenty-five percent off, actual numbers, real urgency. And today I open up "Sorted: your city summer wardrobe" and it's... a $44.99 shirt with zero discount. Clean editorial, sure, full-price energy, absolutely. That tonal whiplash after a flash sale is the most H&M thing they've ever done and I don't mean it as a compliment. They literally trained me to wait yesterday and now want me to pay full retail today.
- The actual email is fine aesthetically — the hero model in that white oversized silk-ish shirt with sunglasses reads very "quiet European summer," which is a real vibe right now. The "SUMMER IN THE CITY" headline lands. The lace-trimmed tops in the product section are cute and the "New Arrival" label is doing something useful. But "SHOP NOW" as a CTA button copy is the laziest thing in email marketing, and there is not a single word of trend language in here — no reference to what TikTok is actually obsessing over, no micro-trend framing, nothing that makes me feel like H&M clocked the moment first.
- Mark as read. Definitely not forwarding. The outfit is cute but I'm not clicking through to pay full price twenty-four hours after a flash sale that ended in my inbox.

## What's working

- **"SUMMER IN THE CITY" headline** — aspirational framing, not just "summer sale," actually evokes a lifestyle that a 24-year-old urban woman clicks into
- **Hero model casting** — young, styled, sunglasses, city backdrop: the demographic signal is right for me even if the energy is more editorial than trend-first
- **"New Arrival" badge** on the lace tops — this is the one thing that creates actual urgency without needing a discount; new arrival signals scarcity and freshness better than a countdown timer
- **Visual hierarchy holds** — hero image → headline → CTA → products in that order, nothing fights for attention, the eye moves cleanly through
- **Product prices visible** — $27.99, $37.99, $44.99 all scannable without clicking; Zara's editorial emails routinely hide prices until you tap through, which is annoying

## What's weak

- **Full-price pivot the day after a 25% flash sale** — this is a trust-and-timing misfire; they just conditioned me to wait for discounts and now expect me to pay $44.99 for a shirt
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA copy** — no noun, no category specificity, could be any email from any brand on earth; compare to ASOS's "Must-have summer dresses" which at least names the product category and gives me a reason to click
- **Zero trend language** — "monochromatic to timeless florals" reads like corporate copywriting from 2019, not a 2026 Gen Z email; no reference to what's actually viral, no micro-trend name, no "seen on" framing
- **Hero isn't shoppable / linked to a specific product** — the oversized white shirt in the hero is the whole visual story but it doesn't appear as a clickable product card below; Shein does this better by making every hero product directly buyable
- **SHOP BY grid at the bottom** — Women / Men / Kids / Home / Beauty is pure navigation boilerplate; it belongs in the footer, not as a featured content block that wastes prime scroll real estate

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the "New Arrival" products, not the hero editorial** — the lace-trimmed tops with "New Arrival" are the actual hook; make those the hero, price them upfront, and let the city editorial be the vibe wrapper, not the centerpiece
- 2. **Retire "SHOP NOW" forever** — replace with "Shop the city edit" or "Shop new arrivals" so the CTA tells me what I'm getting when I click
- 3. **Add one trend anchor line** — even something like "The quiet luxury shirt is back — and it ships in 3 days" connects this to a real cultural moment and gives me a reason to care today vs. waiting for the next sale
- 4. **Acknowledge the sale transition** — if you just ran 25% off yesterday, at minimum tee up "new drops just landed — at full price, but first to get them" to justify why I should buy now; pretending the sale never happened is an honesty gap
- 5. **Make the hero model's outfit shoppable** — the oversized white shirt is clearly the product but it's not labeled or linked; that's a click left on the table

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh arrivals, from $27.99 — the quietly viral ones`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard table-based layout with responsive CSS and JSON-LD structured data for Gmail promotional annotations. The most critical technical issue is stale/empty JSON-LD fields that will suppress Gmail's promotional card features entirely.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully audit** — the HTML source is truncated before body links appear. From what is visible:

- The JSON-LD `PromotionCard` block has `"url":""` — empty. If this is the promotional card deep-link, it will produce a broken Gmail promo annotation.

**Recommend**: provide the full HTML to audit all `<a href>` targets for UTM presence and redirect chain validity.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

| Issue | Evidence |
|---|---|
| No `lang` attribute on `<html>` | `<html style="border:0;margin:0...">` — `lang` is absent; screen readers cannot infer document language |
| External fonts loaded via `@import` in `<style>` | `@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/...)` and `@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/...)` — `@import` is blocked or ignored in Outlook (all versions), Apple Mail on some configs, and several webmail clients; fallback stack not visible in truncated source |

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unrendered `{{token}}` or `%%token%%` merge syntax visible in the visible source. However, the JSON-LD structured data contains multiple fields that appear to be unpopulated merge slots:

```json
"subjectLine": ""          // EmailMessage — empty
"availabilityEnds": ""     // DiscountOffer — empty
"discountCode": ""         // DiscountOffer — empty
"description": ""          // DiscountOffer — empty
"image": ""                // PromotionCard — empty
"url": ""                  // PromotionCard — empty
```

These look like template placeholders that were never populated by the ESP. If this campaign has no discount code, these fields should be omitted from the JSON-LD entirely rather than sent blank.

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM / unsubscribe**: Footer CSS classes are present (`.footer-unsubscribe`, `.footer-toc-wrapper`) indicating an unsubscribe block exists — but the footer HTML is outside the truncated source. Cannot confirm physical address or one-click unsubscribe presence. Flag for full-source audit.

**Authentication headers**: Not derivable from HTML source. Sending domain is `email.hm.com` (subdomain of `hm.com`). SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment should be verified at the MTA level; no issues can be confirmed or ruled out from the HTML alone.

**`<script>` tag in email body**: The JSON-LD block uses `<script type="application/ld+json">`. This is supported by Gmail specifically for promotional annotations but is stripped by virtually all other clients. Not a compliance issue, but worth noting the narrow support surface.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Critical finding**: `availabilityStarts` in the `DiscountOffer` JSON-LD is hardcoded to `"2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"` — a date over 7 years in the past:

```json
"availabilityStarts": "2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"
```

Google uses this field to determine whether to surface the promotional card in Gmail's Promotions tab. A stale 2018 start date combined with an empty `availabilityEnds` means Gmail's deal/annotation system will likely suppress or deprioritize the promotional card. This looks like a template default that was never updated.

UTM parameters on body links cannot be verified from the truncated source.
