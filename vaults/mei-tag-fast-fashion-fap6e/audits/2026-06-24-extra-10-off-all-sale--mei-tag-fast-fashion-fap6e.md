# Extra 10% off all Sale
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- Okay so ASOS comes back two days after "New arrivals" — the most forgettable email in their recent rotation — and drops an actual follow-up to the June 22 sale that started this whole thing. And honestly? This is the right move. The June 22 email was the one that got me — big red hero, "SALE STARTS NOW UP TO 60% OFF," no decoding required. This one is the extension play: extra 10% on top, code in the header, deadline visible. Clean.
- But here's where it gets annoying for me specifically: the hero literally gives equal real estate to "SHOP WOMEN'S" and "SHOP MEN'S" side by side. I don't shop the men's section. I've never clicked a men's CTA in my life. And for a sale extension email where you're trying to close the deal fast, splitting that CTA feels like ASOS forgot who's on the list. Compare this to the June 19 Scandi edit which was clearly coded women's from hero to footer — this one feels like a blast to the whole database.
- The product grid is decent — coords, sandals, some floaty maxis — but nothing is named, nothing is linked, nothing has a "trending now" or "this went viral" hook. I would click "SHOP WOMEN'S," scroll the sale, probably save a few things to my wishlist. I would not forward this to a friend. It's transactional, not exciting.

## What's working

- **Hero offer lands in under two seconds** — "EXTRA 10% OFF ALL SALE" in big bold white on black, no fluff before the ask.
- **Code + deadline visible in the header** — "Use code: EXTRA · Ends: Monday 9am" removes all friction; I don't have to hunt for the fine print.
- **"YOUR STYLE, FOR LESS" positioning** — this is the right language for a sale edit. Doesn't feel like a clearance dump; frames it as a curated value moment.
- **Product grid is visually compelling** — white linen, lime coords, sandals, denim shorts all look current enough that I'd click into the section.
- **Top brands section is a bonus** — if Levi's or New Balance is in that sale grid, that's actually a reason to click that the hero didn't give me.

## What's weak

- **"SHOP WOMEN'S" and "SHOP MEN'S" as co-equal hero CTAs** — I'm a woman. This is my inbox. Zara pulled this exact same split on me with their "Man | Summer in Style" send and I called it out then. ASOS is smarter than this, or should be.
- **No trend language anywhere** — not a single "seen on TikTok," "trending this week," or "everyone's wearing." The June 22 sale email didn't need it because 60% off sells itself. But an *extension* needs a reason to come back, and "same sale, extra 10%" alone doesn't give me that hook.
- **App download banner mid-email** — neon green "DOWNLOAD THE APP" interrupts the product flow. That belongs in the footer if it's here at all.
- **Nothing specific is linked** — the product grid is vibes-only. No SKU, no product name, no "limited sizes left" signal. H&M's "Summer basics" send at least anchored to identifiable pieces.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Ditch the split CTA in the hero** — for a women-coded list segment, lead with "SHOP THE WOMEN'S SALE →" alone. Men's can live as a text link below the fold. This is the single highest-impact change.
- 2. **Add a trend anchor to the hero subtext** — swap "We know how hard you've been waiting these prices" (a bit awkward) for something like "The pieces everyone's been screenshotting — now extra 10% off." That ties the discount to desire instead of just urgency.
- 3. **Pull one or two specific hero products into the email** — link them directly. "This linen co-ord: was £45, now £18" is a click. A grid of unidentified items is browsing.
- 4. **Move the app banner to the footer** — it breaks the shopping flow mid-scroll.
- 5. **Subject could do more work on the deadline** — "Ends Monday" in the subject itself would drive opens faster from inbox.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your extra 10% is live (code: EXTRA)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `On top of up to 60% already — the co-ords everyone saved are still in stock`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Code EXTRA · Monday 9am cutoff · shop the women's sale now`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty preheader (confirmed issue)**
The hidden preheader `<div>` contains no text:
```html
<div style="display:none !important;...max-height:0px;...overflow:hidden;"></div>
```
With no preheader content, inbox clients (Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail) will pull preview text from the first visible body content — likely a nav link or hero copy. This is a common mistake that damages open-rate signal.

**SVG font format in `@font-face` (minor)**
Both `FuturaPT-Book` and `FuturaPT-Heavy` declarations include `format("svg")`. SVG fonts are deprecated; no current email client requires them. This adds unnecessary request overhead on old WebKit.

**Duplicate charset declaration (harmless but redundant)**
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8">
<meta charset="utf-8">
```
Both declare UTF-8. Only the second is needed per HTML5; the first is legacy. No rendering impact.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on body**
```css
body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
`none` (vs. `100%`) fully disables iOS auto-scaling. On `*` the value is `100%` (correct), but the body override to `none` may suppress scaling in some iOS Mail versions at very small viewport widths.

**Mobile breakpoint at 460px**
The sole responsive breakpoint is `max-width: 460px`, which is narrower than the common 480px standard. Devices rendering at 461–479px (e.g., some Android phones in portrait) will receive the desktop layout. Functional but worth noting.

**`font-size: 0em` on all table elements**
```css
table, tr, td, th { font-size: 0em; }
```
Intentional whitespace-gap suppression — standard practice, not an issue.

**VML CTA fallback present**
`.vml-cta { display: block !important; }` inside `[if gt mso 15]` conditional — correct Outlook button rendering pattern. No issue.
