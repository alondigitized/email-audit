# Summer's must-have shorts
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- Opening this email, I land on the SHORTS SEASON hero and honestly the editorial pull is real — the denim styling is clean, the model fits the summer moment, the WANT / NEED / LOVE copy structure is something I'd actually pause on. And then my eye drifts up to the very top of the email and there it is: "FIND FATHER'S DAY GIFTS IN-STORE FROM CLASSIC STYLES TO SEASONAL ACCESSORIES." H&M. We have been through this. I got Father's Day emails on June 5th, June 7th, June 9th — the June 9th one was literally the third send of that same campaign. And now it's haunting my shorts email like a ghost that hasn't gotten the memo. That banner has no business being in this template.
- Pulling past it, the actual body is doing a lot right. The WANT / NEED / LOVE structure feels more editorial-magazine than blast-email, and that's the vibe I'm here for. But H&M buries the only real offer — 15% off with a clothing donation — past three editorial panels, and then it's conditional on physically walking into a store. ## What's working

- The SHORTS SEASON hero is genuinely strong — clean styling, summery denim, matches exactly where my FYP is right now
- WANT / NEED / LOVE copy structure is editorial and clever; feels like a magazine spread, not a mass blast
- The gender/size tabs (WOMEN, MEN, KIDS 2-8Y, KIDS 8-14Y) directly below the hero are a smart UX move — I can self-sort without clicking into a category page first
- Visual hierarchy lands correctly — eye goes hero → tabs → editorial panels, no confusion about what this email is selling
- Clean render throughout, no broken images or layout breaks

## What's weak

- The Father's Day banner at the very top of the email is a campaign management nightmare — H&M has been sending Father's Day content since June 5th and it's now contaminating a completely different sends' template; it also likely bleeds into the inbox preview text, which means the subject/preview combo is incoherent before I even open
- Zero urgency anywhere in the email — no "this week only," no "limited sizes," nothing that separates clicking now from clicking never
- The 15% off offer is buried three-quarters down and is conditional — requires an in-store clothing donation, not a simple code at checkout
- No anchor product — ASOS's tennis-core send last week had a single tight hero product that I could picture wearing; this is all category-level "here are shorts" without a standout piece
- "SHOP NOW" as the primary CTA is as generic as it gets

## Recommendations

- 1. **Strip the Father's Day banner from this template immediately** — it's a different campaign, a different audience signal, and it's actively poisoning the inbox preview text
- 2. Lead the hero with a specific editorial hook — "The 3 shorts you'll wear until September" or a named product moment beats "SHORTS SEASON" copy that could apply to any brand any summer
- 3. Move the 15% off clothing donation callout to the top third of the email — hiding your incentive mid-scroll is leaving clicks on the table
- 4. Add credible urgency — even "new this week" or a limited size callout gives me a reason to act now vs. my next Saturday scroll session
- 5. Replace "SHOP NOW" with product-specific CTA copy — "Shop Denim Shorts" or "Find My Pair" does real work; compare how ASOS's tennis-core email pulled with specific named entry points

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading (risk):**
```html
<style>
  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
</style>
```
Gmail (webmail) strips `<style>` blocks containing `@import` in many rendering paths, causing font fallback to system defaults. These imports should be converted to `<link rel="stylesheet">` tags or inlined as `@font-face` declarations inside a separate `<style>` block without `@import`.

**CSS duplication:** The `optimize-css` `<style>` block contains several duplicate rule declarations (e.g., `.cta-link-mobile-border-ns`, `.cta-link-mobile`, `.flexible-elements-secondary-text-block-headline`) repeated verbatim within the same media query. This inflates payload size and suggests a template concatenation bug.
