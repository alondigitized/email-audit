# 15% or 20% off all weekend
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-15**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so it's a tiered spend threshold — "15% off $75 or 20% off $100" — which is at least a different math trick than the flat 25% off sitewide from two days ago, but it's still H&M running the exact same weekend-deal playbook they've been running literally all week. The hero girl in the striped rugby-collar top and khaki skirt is cute, the styling's fine, but nothing here screams "trend drop" — it's just a discount tier stacked on top of a "shop by category" grid that goes on forever (Women, Men, Baby, Kids, Home, Beauty). This reads like an inventory clearance email, not a "we called the trend first" email.
- Compare this to H&M's own "Preppy Layers" send from the 13th — that one actually had a styling point of view (trench, baggy denim, oversized shirt, crossbody) that felt like it was chasing something real off my FYP. This one's just a spend-more-save-more math problem with a categories menu underneath. Zara's "Kids" emails at least commit to being genuinely gorgeous even when they're not for me — this is H&M just... filling space with product-grid tiles down to Home and Beauty.
- Would I click? Maybe the Women button out of habit, but I'm not opening this on my phone hunting for the trend. Would I forward it? No, there's nothing here worth sending a friend — no specific piece, no specific vibe, just a discount threshold. Marking as read and moving on.

## What's working

- The 15%/20% tiered threshold is at least a slightly different offer structure than yesterday's flat percentage, so it doesn't feel like a total copy-paste.
- Hero model's outfit (stripe top, khaki mini, white sock-and-sneaker combo) is a look I'd actually screenshot on its own.
- "Buy online, pick up in-store" banner up top is genuinely useful info if I'm impatient about shipping — that's an honest signal H&M rarely leads with.
- Clean white background, no overlapping text, everything renders fine — no bugs to dock them for.

## What's weak

- Zero trend language anywhere — no "back," no "new drop," no "restock," just category buttons. It's giving warehouse clearance, not "we saw this on TikTok first."
- The email is enormous — Women/Men, then Change Is Good (Women/Men again), then All The Feels (Baby/Kids), then Perfect Vibes (Home), then Shop By (five more categories) — that's a scroll marathon for someone who wants ONE hero item, not a full store directory.
- "Change Is Good," "All The Feels," "Perfect Vibes" — these section headers are trying so hard to sound fun but say literally nothing about what's actually new or trending.
- No urgency on the discount — no countdown, no "ends Sunday at midnight" like the flash sale two days ago had. Just "all weekend" floating with no anchor.
- This is the fourth-ish promo email from H&M in five days and none of them have differentiated themselves with a real trend hook — cadence fatigue is setting in hard.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email in half — Women/Men/Kids/Home/Beauty as one shop-by grid at the bottom is fine, but four separate hero sections before it is bloat. Lead with ONE trend-forward hero look and the discount, then stop.
- 2. Actually name the trend. Tell me what's new about the striped-top-and-skirt look instead of a generic section header — give me the "why this, why now."
- 3. Add a real deadline to "all weekend" — "ends Sunday 11:59pm" creates actual urgency instead of vague timing.
- 4. Vary the creative from the last three sends — three flat/tiered-percentage emails in a row with generic category grids is starting to blur together in my inbox.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Pick up today, save up to 20%`

## Technical Audit — H&M "15% or 20% off all weekend"

### 1. Technical Summary
The email renders via a standard MJML/AEM-style responsive table layout with mobile breakpoint overrides, but automated QA shows nearly all outbound links returning HTTP 403 and a 16% overall pass rate, indicating the link-tracking redirector is broken or blocking automated probes.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **12/12 probed links return 403**, including primary CTAs ("Shop now" ×2), navigation (Women/Men/Baby/Kids/HOME), the H&M logo link, both hero product images, and the "Buy online, pick up in-store" link. All routes through the ESP redirector `t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=hc4887b1d-...&did=1715225823&rid=3326930539&erid=...`.
- The consistent 403 across every link (not just one broken URL) points to the redirector itself rejecting the request — likely bot/UA filtering on the tracking domain rather than per-link destination errors. Confirm with a manual click-through from a real client before treating this as a true breakage; if it reproduces manually, this blocks 100% of email traffic to hm.com.
- Two CTA links contain zero-width/invisible characters in anchor text (`‌  ﻿Shop now` — ZWNJ + BOM-like characters), which won't affect the underlying `href` but is worth flagging as it can break some link-text-based QA/analytics tooling and screen reader pronunciation.
- Only 12 of 78 total links were probed (66 skipped per the QA tool's click-likelihood ranking); footer/social links were deprioritized, so untested link count is significant and not fully covered by this audit.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **16 images missing `alt` text**, including both the 1400px and 750px variants of every product image (`cq5dam.web.*.jpeg` assets under `image.hm.com/content/dam/hm/regional-local-activities/...`) and two tracking-pixel/redirector images. Screen readers will announce these as blank or fall back to filename.
- Head includes standard accessibility/rendering safeguards: `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, `format-detection` meta, Yahoo/Gmail-specific CSS overrides (`#MessageViewBody`, `@media screen yahoo`), and a mobile breakpoint at 699px — no issues found in this layer.
- Font loading depends on two external `@import` stylesheets (`fabric.hmgroup.com`, `s1-cdn.hm.com`) with no visible fallback font stack shown in the truncated source; if these fail to load in restrictive clients, text may render in default serif/sans with no explicit fallback confirmed from the excerpt provided.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

This will prevent Gmail/Google annotations (e.g., promo carousel, "View offer" actions) from rendering correctly, since the structured data is present but unpopulated.

- No visible merge-token artifacts (e.g., unresolved `{{first_name}}` or `%%TOKEN%%`) in the visible HTML excerpt.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)
- Unsubscribe link is present in-body (`link text: "Unsubscribe"`) but **also returns 403** via the same redirector — if this reproduces on manual test, it's a CAN-SPAM risk since the required opt-out mechanism would be non-functional.

Note the QA tool flags this may be a relay-side capture issue (AgentMail) rather than confirmed absence at the source MTA; treat as unconfirmed but worth verifying against raw headers.
- **`Authentication-Results` header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status could not be verified from available data. Same relay-capture caveat applies.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)

Since the redirector is currently returning 403 for all sampled links, **no destination/landing-page alignment could be verified** — this should be re-run once the 403s are resolved.
