# Swim is in & up to 50% off
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- Okay so H&M just slid into my inbox for the *third time in three days* with the same sitewide 50% off energy — May 20 was "up to 50% off sitewide starts now," May 22 was "kick-start summer with up to 50% off," and now it's "swim is in & up to 50% off." My thumb almost auto-archived it before I even looked. The swim hook in the subject is the most interesting thing about this email, but the body doesn't fully deliver on it — you scroll expecting a curated swim edit and you get a broad sitewide sale with a "BREEZY PICKS" section that's more resort cover-up than actual swimwear.
- That said, the layout itself is clean and does its job. Three editorial content blocks ("Breezy Picks," "Heat Mode," "Red, White & New"), each with a strong model shot and a clear SHOP NOW button. The models are diverse and the styling reads current. If this were the *first* 50% off email this week, it'd be a solid send. But as the third? The sale is starting to feel like H&M can't move product at full price, which honestly makes me second-guess the brand a little.
- I'd probably click "Breezy Picks" out of mild curiosity, but I wouldn't forward this — ASOS's leopard print edit from yesterday felt more like a trend moment and less like a markdown panic. This one I'd mark as read, maybe revisit Saturday during my compare-tab session.

## What's working

- **"Swim is in" subject hook is genuinely seasonal** — it's the right beat for late May and actually made me pause on the subject line instead of skipping.
- **Hero offer is impossible to miss** — "UP TO 50% OFF SITEWIDE" in big bold type above the fold, no guessing required.
- **Three distinct content blocks with editorial model shots** — "Breezy Picks," "Heat Mode," "Red, White & New" give the email some visual variety and don't feel like a product dump.
- **Model diversity is solid** — different body types and skin tones across the three sections; that matches how I actually see myself in this brand.
- **Sale deadline language is visible** ("Long Weekend Sale Ends") — there's a credible urgency signal, not just a vague "limited time."

## What's weak

- **Third near-identical 50% off email in three days** — the cadence is doing real damage to how I feel about H&M right now; by send three the deal feels desperate, not exciting.
- **"Swim is in" is a subject line promise the body doesn't keep** — I opened expecting a swim-specific edit; instead I get a general sitewide sale with one loosely swimwear-adjacent section.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTAs are generic across every block** — ASOS does things like "Shop the edit" or "Get the look" and it feels more curated; all three H&M blocks hit the same flat button.
- **"Red, White & New" feels like a memorial day/patriotic angle layered on top of a summer sale** — as a trend-chaser that framing reads as a dated retail calendar move, not a genuine aesthetic moment.
- **No swim-specific product visible** — if the subject says swim, I want to see actual swimwear, not a polka-dot maxi and some earrings.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the sitewide sale repetition or reframe it** — if you have to run the offer again, lead with a specific category (swim, vacay, cover-ups) and make the sale the *reason* to buy *now*, not the entire headline.
- 2. **Build a real swim section hero** — one model in actual swimwear above the fold. Lean into the subject line promise. Show me the bikini, the one-piece, the rashguard. Earn the "swim is in" claim.
- 3. **Vary the CTA copy per block** — "Shop Breezy Picks," "Get the Heat Mode Look," "Shop Red, White & New." Same clicks, feels 10× more editorial.
- 4. **Surface the sale end date more visibly** — I can see the deadline language but it's small; make it count. "Sale ends Sunday" in the hero does more work than fine-print urgency.
- 5. **Swap "Red, White & New" for a trending aesthetic label** — "coastal cowgirl," "quiet luxury beach," literally anything that sounds like you're plugged into TikTok rather than a department store circular.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Font loading via `@import` inside `<style>` blocks:**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` is blocked or stripped by Gmail, Outlook (all versions), and several other major clients. Fonts will silently fall back with no error — this is a known rendering limitation, not a crash risk, but means custom brand fonts are not rendering in the majority of email clients.

**Non-standard media query:**
```css
@media screen yahoo { ... }
```
This is a Yahoo Mail targeting hack (recognized by Yahoo's CSS parser). It is functional but not standard CSS; document for maintainability.
