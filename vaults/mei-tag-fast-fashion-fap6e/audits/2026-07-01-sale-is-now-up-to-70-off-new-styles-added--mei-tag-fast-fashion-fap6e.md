# Sale is now up to 70% off + new styles added
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- Okay so H&M really said "what if we just kept going" and... sent this. I've been getting their emails every single day for at least two weeks straight and I genuinely cannot tell if this is email twelve or fourteen at this point. The "UP TO 70% OFF + NEW STYLES ADDED" hero is doing its job — it stopped my thumb for half a second — but like, the same brand sent me "New sale styles added from $7.99" literally on June 27th, so H&M has now used the word "added" in at least two sale subjects in less than a week. I see you.
- What actually got me is the free shipping for H&M members banner at the top. That's a real benefit and it's the first time they've led with it this hard in this sale run. The female model in that pink/blush summer dress is cute, it reads current, and the 70% off number is impossible to ignore. But the email does absolutely nothing to show me *what* the new styles actually are — it's just category tiles. I'm clicking over to the site already knowing I'll spend twenty minutes scrolling the WOMEN section anyway, so fine.
- I'd mark this as read and maybe tap through on a slow afternoon, but I'm definitely not forwarding it. My group chat has already been spammed with H&M sale links this week.

## What's working

- **"UP TO 70% OFF + NEW STYLES ADDED"** in big red text is the hero and the eye goes there immediately — clean visual hierarchy, no confusion about what this email is for
- **Free shipping for members** is called out right at the top banner, and that's a real perk H&M hasn't surfaced as prominently in recent sends
- **The female model's summer dress** reads on-trend, contemporary, age-appropriate — she actually looks like someone I'd follow on TikTok, not a J.Crew catalog
- **"Peak summer staples"** is seasonal and specific enough to feel timely, not like a recycled April sale
- **No render bugs** — email loads clean, images load, text doesn't overlap, layout holds

## What's weak

- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is somewhere around send eleven or twelve this week; the "sale is growing" mechanic was clever once but H&M has now used it so many times I'm starting to filter them mentally before I even open
- **"UP TO 70% OFF" is doing the most vague work possible** — the fine print block at the bottom is long and dense; that "up to" language is doing classic bait-and-switch energy and I've been burned enough times to distrust it
- **Category tiles with no product previews** — ASOS would at least show me *the actual trending item* so I feel like I'm seeing something new; this is just grid navigation with model thumbnails
- **Zero urgency anchor** — Zara literally told me "Today at 9 pm ET online" on their June sale drop and I set a reminder; H&M gives me nothing, so there's no reason to act now versus next Tuesday
- **The MEN section is taking up half the hero** — I'm a 24-year-old woman shopping for myself; the male model getting equal billing in the hero pull isn't wrong but it dilutes the relevance immediately

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the category grid and put one hero product in the fold** — show me the *specific* new arrival that's trending this week. I don't need to see that WOMEN / MEN / BABY / KIDS / HOME all exist. I know. Give me the dress, the bag, the exact thing I'd screenshot and send in my group chat.
- 2. **Add a countdown or "ending soon" mechanism** — Zara's timed drops make me act; H&M's open-ended "sale is happening" makes me procrastinate indefinitely. Even "prices drop more Friday" would create urgency.
- 3. **Replace "UP TO 70% OFF" with the actual category discount** — lead with "Women's summer dresses from $9.99" or whatever the real steal is. The vagueness is killing trust.
- 4. **Trim the cadence** — one great email beats three "we added more" nudges. The June 27 "new styles added from $7.99" and this July 1 email are too similar to feel additive.
- 5. **Member-exclusive framing** — lean into the free shipping benefit more. If I'm a member and this is my exclusive price view, tell me that in the hero, not just in a banner strip.

- **Subject Alt B:** `New summer drops just hit the sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping on $50+ — members only`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your next scroll-stop piece just dropped to $9.99`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email contains a broken JSON-LD structured data block with multiple unpopulated fields and a hardcoded 2018 timestamp, and uses CSS `@import` for fonts in a way that fails silently in Gmail and Outlook. The HTML is truncated so compliance elements (unsubscribe, physical address) cannot be fully verified.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**`PromotionCard.url` is empty string in JSON-LD:**
```json
{"@type":"PromotionCard","url":"","image":"","@context":"http://schema.org/"}
```
The promotion card URL was never populated — Google Inbox Actions and any schema-aware client will fail to render a deep link.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS `@import` inside `<style>` block — not supported in Gmail:**
```css
<style>
  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
</style>
```
Gmail strips `<style>` content that starts with `@import`. Both font families will silently fall back to the browser/client default in Gmail, which is the dominant client for this sender's likely audience. Fonts should be declared via `<link>` or loaded via a compatible method.

**Viewport meta missing `initial-scale=1`:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width"/>
```
Standard is `content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1"`. Some mobile clients may zoom on load without it.

**Duplicate CSS rules in mobile breakpoint (`@media (max-width:699px)`):** The following selectors are declared twice inside the same block with identical values, adding byte weight without effect:
- `.department-title`
- `.department-link`
- `.cta-link-mobile-border-ns`
- `.cta-link-mobile, .set_font_interactive_standalone_link_default`
- `.set_font_heading_xlarge`

Not a rendering blocker, but inflates email size unnecessarily.

**`schema.org` context uses `http://` not `https://`:**
```json
"@context":"http://schema.org/"
```
Google currently resolves both, but `https://` is the canonical form and `http://` may generate schema validation warnings.
