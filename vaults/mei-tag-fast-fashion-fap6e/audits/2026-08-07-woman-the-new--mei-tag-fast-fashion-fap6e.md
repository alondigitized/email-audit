# Woman | The New
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- Okay, déjà vu — this is basically the same move Zara pulled on July 30 with "Woman | The New," except this time it's not even trying to hide it. ZW Collection, Mint Collection, TRF, Lingerie Edit, Selected Pieces — that's FIVE categories stacked in one endless scroll, and not a single offer, discount, or "new drop" hook anywhere. It's not an email, it's their site nav pasted into my inbox.
- The styling is genuinely pretty — that red corset-lace jacket in the Mint Collection and the red bag in Selected Pieces both stopped my thumb for a second — but stopping my scroll isn't the same as giving me a reason to tap. There's no trend story here, no "this just landed," no urgency. Compare that to H&M's WARDROBE.NYC drop I looked at, which opened with an actual named designer collab and a reason to care in the first two inches — no "up to 30% off" banner needed because the concept did the work. Zara's just... showing me clothes. All of them.
- Verdict: I'm scrolling past this one. No click, no forward. Maybe I screenshot the mint jeans for my "if I had money" folder and move on.

## What's working

- The red-lace-up jacket and matching corset dress in the Mint Collection are genuinely trend-right — that's the one section that felt like an actual editorial pick, not filler.
- Clean, no-bug render — every image loaded, category links are legible, nothing broken or overlapping.
- The models skew young and the styling (color-blocked prints, corset detailing, zebra top) reads current, not stale mom-Zara.
- Section dividers (ZW / Mint / TRF / Lingerie / Selected) at least organize the chaos so I'm not totally lost scrolling.

## What's weak

- Zero offer. No %, no free shipping, no "new markdowns" — nothing to actually act on, which tanks any reason to tap through instead of just screenshotting.
- This is a full catalog dump disguised as a newsletter, same complaint I had about the July 30 "Woman | The New" send — Zara is recycling the exact same kitchen-sink format.
- No single hero product or CTA — every link just says the category name (BERMUDAS, JACKETS, DRESSES), so it's routing me to a browse page, not a product I can actually decide on.
- "NEWSLETTER. ONLINE VERSION" as literally the first line I see is such a weird flex to lead with — nobody cares, and it reads like junk before I even get to the hero.
- No urgency, no "first to drop" energy — which is exactly the trend-first-vs-trend-follow test I run every brand through, and Zara just isn't making the case here.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick ONE hero moment (the Mint corset jacket set is the obvious winner) and build the whole email around it — ditch the five-category scroll entirely. ASOS's Lioness drop did this right: one campaign, one mood, one reason to click.
- 2. Add an actual offer or newness hook — even just "just landed" badges on 2-3 pieces would beat a silent grid.
- 3. Swap category-name links ("BERMUDAS") for actual CTAs ("Shop the Bermudas") — give me a verb.
- 4. Cut this down to 2 collections max per send and rotate the others next week — stop dumping the whole catalog every time.
- 5. Kill or shrink "NEWSLETTER. ONLINE VERSION" — bury it in the footer where it belongs.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The mint set everyone's about to copy`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New in: corset jackets, zebra tops, red everything`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh off the runway, already in your size`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `5 pieces we'd wear on repeat this week`

# Technical Audit: Zara "Woman | The New" Campaign Email

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional table-based layout (Oracle Responsys platform, per `news.zara.com` asset paths and `CAMPAIGN_CODE` comments) with acceptable MSO/Outlook conditional handling, but it fails multiple deliverability and accessibility checks — most notably a missing plain-text MIME part, no accessibility alt text on any content image, and no detectable unsubscribe/authentication headers at the transport layer.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- Tracking pixel present: `https://news.zara.com/pub/as?_ri_=X0Gzc2X%3DBQjkPkSTRQG2G34p2kqIGNokFzbAK5019RnP8I6tNbawD45EYMIC7pJloozdw4rygHT1XcppgWXM` — standard Responsys open-tracking beacon (`/pub/as`), functioning as expected.
- QA probe covered 12 of 36 detected links (24 skipped as low-priority footer/social/utility per the tool's click-likelihood ranking) — this is expected probing behavior, not a defect, but means the majority of hrefs (including the actual PDP/category CTA destinations) were not validated for 200-status/redirect health in this pass.
- The truncated HTML provided does not expose actual `href` values for CTA links, so link destinations, redirect chains, and click-tracking domains beyond the `/pub/as` pixel cannot be verified from source. No broken/malformed `href` evidence found in the visible markup.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `alt` text on all 11 content images** (`img_01b.jpg` through `img_11.jpg`, all served from `news.zara.com/assets/responsysimages/.../2026_08_07_norte_woman`) — confirmed by QA. This fails WCAG 1.1.1 and degrades the experience for screen-reader users and any client that blocks images by default.
- **Viewport meta blocks user zoom**: `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width; initial-scale=1.0; maximum-scale=1.0; user-scalable=no;" />` — `user-scalable=no` + `maximum-scale=1.0` disables pinch-zoom, a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) violation on mobile clients that respect this attribute.
- **Non-standard viewport delimiter**: the `content` attribute uses semicolons (`;`) between key-value pairs instead of the spec-required commas. Most mobile mail clients parse this leniently, but it is technically invalid per the HTML viewport spec and risks silent parsing failure in stricter renderers.
- **Google Fonts subset mismatch**: `<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Roboto+Condensed:400,700&display=swap&subset=cyrillic,cyrillic-ext" ...>` requests only `cyrillic,cyrillic-ext` glyph subsets with no `latin` subset specified, despite the campaign being `US_en` (English copy). This can cause the custom font to fail to apply to Latin-script text in clients that honor the subset restriction, silently falling back to the default sans-serif.
- Mobile responsive `@media (max-width: 640px)` block is present and comprehensive (width/font/padding overrides), with legacy `.ExternalClass`/`mso-table-lspace` resets for Outlook — no issues found here.
- **Plain-text fallback missing**: QA confirms the text/plain MIME part is 0 chars. This is both a rendering/accessibility issue (screen-reader and text-only clients get nothing) and a spam-filtering risk (see §5).

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No merge tags, personalization variables, or dynamic content blocks (e.g., `*|FNAME|*`, `${...}`, RPL tokens) are visible in the provided source excerpt. Note the source is truncated, and the header comments reference Responsys "supplemental content" macros (`Macro para traer el texto de la supplemental`) that were stripped out in this excerpt — any personalization logic likely lives in that removed section and could not be audited here.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header not found** (RFC 2369) — confirmed by QA.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found** (RFC 8058) — confirmed by QA, meaning one-click unsubscribe is not supported at the header level even if a footer link exists.
- **No physical mailing address detected** in the body — confirmed by QA. CAN-SPAM §7704(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email; this could not be located in the truncated content or footer.
- **Authentication-Results header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown via the AgentMail relay path; cannot confirm authentication posture from this data.
- Note: the QA tool states "an unsubscribe link is always included" in its footer link probing, implying a *visible* unsubscribe link likely exists in the footer (part of the 24 skipped/deprioritized links) — the compliance gap here is specifically the missing transport-layer headers (List-Unsubscribe/List-Unsubscribe-Post), not necessarily the absence of an in-body unsubscribe mechanism.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Cannot be assessed — the truncated HTML source does not include the actual outbound `href` values for CTA/product links (only the tracking pixel URL and font stylesheet link are visible). No UTM parameters or landing-page URLs are present in the visible markup to compare against a destination site. Recommend re-running this check against the full, untruncated HTML to extract CTA hrefs for UTM/landing-page alignment validation.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to all 11 content images (`img_01b.jpg`–`img_11.jpg`); use `alt=""` explicitly for the `/pub/as` tracking pixel.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1.0; user-scalable=no` from the viewport meta tag to restore pinch-zoom, and switch delimiters from `;` to `,` per spec.
3. Add a `latin` subset (or drop the `subset` param entirely) to the Google Fonts request so `Roboto Condensed` actually applies to the English body copy.
4. Generate and attach a proper `text/plain` MIME alternative (currently 0 chars) — required for spam-filter scoring and text-only/screen-reader clients.
5. Configure `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers (RFC 2369 / RFC 8058) at the ESP/relay level to enable one-click unsubscribe, which major mailbox providers (Gmail, Yahoo) now require for bulk senders.
6. Add a physical mailing address to the email footer to satisfy CAN-SPAM §7704(a)(5).
7. Verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment and confirm the AgentMail relay is preserving/exposing the `Authentication-Results` header — currently unverifiable.
8. Re-audit with full (non-truncated) HTML to validate actual CTA link destinations and UTM parameter alignment with landing pages.
