# Up to 50% off sitewide starts now
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- Okay so H&M just dropped the sitewide sale email and honestly? Not mad at it. The hero dress on that model is doing real editorial work — that floral oversized print, the long limbs, the confident stance — it stopped my thumb before I even registered the offer. And then "UP TO 50% OFF SITEWIDE" hits right below it in big bold type and yeah, the hierarchy works. My brain went hero → offer → scroll down to products in exactly the right order.
- Here's my thing though: H&M sent me two emails yesterday — the Lotto collab and then that soft tailoring editorial — and now there's a third today. I'm not unsubscribing but I am starting to feel like someone in my DMs who texts again before I've replied. The sale announcement itself is fine; the pacing is starting to strain goodwill.
- Would I click? Yes — the Tie-Detail Slip Dress at $19 is a direct tap, that silhouette is trending on my FYP right now. Would I forward it to a friend? No. This is a "shop alone with my headphones in" email, not a "bestie you need to see this" email.

## What's working

- Hero image is genuinely editorial-level — the floral print on a tall Black model is exactly the kind of saturated, full-body shot that would stop a TikTok scroll; it doesn't feel stock or filler
- Offer hierarchy is the clearest H&M has been in this recent cycle: hero → "THE DRESS CODE" → "UP TO 50% OFF SITEWIDE" reads as a single clean visual sentence
- Individual product tiles below the hero show crossed-out original prices next to new prices — that's actual evidence of savings, not just a headline claim, and it made me do the math on the Tie-Detail Slip Dress immediately
- Clean render: no broken images, no overlapping text, readable font at mobile size without pinching

## What's weak

- "Event-worthy maxi that move with you from work days to..." — I am 24, I do not have a "work days to" life, and this copy is clearly written for someone who does; ASOS would've said "your summer haul starts here" and it would've hit different
- "THE DRESS CODE" as a pun feels like it was workshopped in a meeting — clever but cold, not the kind of copy that feels native or like H&M originated the trend
- No end date on the sale: "starts now" is fine but without "ends Sunday" or "48 hours only" I'm liable to close the tab and never return; the Zara newsletter I audited last week had the same fatal flaw
- "SHOP NOW" CTA is the laziest three letters in email marketing — ASOS runs "Shop the Drop" or even "Get 50% Off" and that tiny word change actually shifts urgency

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline** — "Ends Sunday" or "48 hours only" right above the CTA; this single change would lift click rate more than anything else on this list
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA** from "SHOP NOW" to "Shop 50% Off" — specific, costs nothing to change, changes intent
- 3. **Kill the "work days to..." copy** in the subhead — replace with something trend-coded like "summer's biggest silhouettes, all on sale"
- 4. **Remove the SHOP BY category grid** (Women / Men / Kids / Home / Beauty) — during a dress-sale email this is dilution; I already know H&M sells home goods
- 5. **Lead the product tiles with the most trend-forward piece**, not the most generic; the Patterned Wrap-Front Dress is more FYP than the Lace-Trimmed Cotton Dress, put it first
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dress season just got 50% cheaper`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer dress haul is half off — now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Lace trims, wrap fronts, slip dresses — all in the sale`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The floral era is ON and it's on sale`

No blocking rendering defects were found in the structural HTML, but duplicate mobile CSS rules and non-HTTPS schema URIs are present.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS `@import` unsupported in most email clients.**
Both font loads are via `@import` inside `<style>` tags:
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` is not supported in Outlook (all versions), Samsung Mail, and some Yahoo Mail contexts. Fonts will silently fall back to system fonts in those clients with no error surfaced.

**Duplicate mobile CSS rules** (may signal copy-paste template debt, not a render-breaking issue):
- `.department-title` — declared twice in `@media (max-width:699px)`
- `.department-link` — declared twice
- `.cta-link-mobile-border-ns` — declared twice
- `.cta-link-mobile, .image-text-cta-link, .image-text-flexible-elements-cta, .set_font_interactive_standalone_link_default` — declared twice

Duplicate declarations are resolved by cascade (last wins) but add unnecessary payload weight and are a template hygiene issue.

**`format-detection` meta tag is correct** (`telephone=no`) — prevents iOS from auto-linking phone numbers. No issue.
