# Kids | Discover the latest arrivals
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-09**
## Executive summary

- I don't have kids. I'm not shopping for kids. Full stop — this is Zara's Kids catalog and I'm not the audience, so this is basically the same swing and miss as August 2nd's "Kids | New season updates," except now they've dressed it up with a slightly punchier subject line ("discover the latest arrivals" vs "new season updates") that tricked me into opening it a second time this month. Big mistake on their part, honestly, because now I know it's a pattern, not a fluke.
- Visually? Fine, it's Zara — the editorial styling is clean, the "95" jersey and the striped rugby shirt are cute in a J.Crew-kids way, but I scrolled through six full sections (Girl, Boy, toddler, baby) and there is nothing here for a 24-year-old shopping her own wardrobe. Compare that to ASOS's Lioness drop email, which was a moody, styled shoot that made me want to BE the girl in the photo — this makes me want to forward it to my sister who actually has a toddler, not screenshot it for myself.
- Not clicking, not forwarding to a friend in my world, marking as read and mentally docking Zara for the second kids-blast this month.

## What's working

- The hero shot is genuinely well-art-directed — soft, lived-in, not sterile catalog lighting.
- Clear section dividers (A New Season Story, Trendy Classic League, etc.) so at least it's organized if I were the target.
- No render bugs — images loaded, layout held together top to bottom.
- "Download our app" CTA at the bottom is at least a real, unambiguous button.

## What's weak

- Every single section is age-gated kidswear (Girl 6-14, Boy 6-14, Girl 1½-6, Baby 0-18 months) — zero relevance to my actual closet.
- No offer anywhere. No %, no BOGO, no free shipping — just "look at outfits," which isn't even a CTA I can act on.
- This is the second kids email from Zara in a week (Aug 2 was "Kids | New season updates"), and it's basically the same format recycled with new photos.
- Subject says "latest arrivals" but there's no trend framing, no "shop now," not even a single shoppable button under any product — it reads like a lookbook PDF got emailed to me by accident.
- Compare to H&M's "back to school" send — at least that one tried (badly) to funnel by category with a real offer banner up top. This one doesn't even try.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment me out of kids sends entirely — I've now gotten two in a week with zero interaction. That's a list-hygiene problem, not a creative problem.
- 2. If Zara insists on sending it, add an actual offer or shoppable CTA per section ("Shop Girl" button) instead of a wall of unlinked photography.
- 3. Lead with something that could cross-sell to a childless 24-year-old, like "gifting" framing, if they must reach this segment.
