# Meet your new-season palette
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- Okay so after six straight sale emails — four of which dropped in basically one week — ASOS just walked into my inbox dressed in chocolate brown and sage green going "palette era, let's go." And honestly? The creative is cute. Earthy neutrals, that deep-V halter situation in the hero, the olive slip dress in the grid — this is genuinely a trending palette right now. I was literally watching chocolate brown fits on TikTok last week.
- But here's the thing: I was just buying stuff at 70% off three days ago. ASOS had me in sale-brain. And now you're showing me full price no-discount editorial content and just… expecting me to switch gears that fast? The whiplash is real. There's no offer here — no percentage off, no "new in, free shipping today," nothing. It's all vibes and "SHOP THE EDIT."
- I'd scroll through, screenshot a couple of the looks to save for inspo, and close the tab. No click, no purchase. I'm not forwarding this — the visuals are pretty but there's nothing exciting enough to send to my group chat.

## What's working

- **Palette is genuinely trend-aligned.** Earthy browns and olive green are having a major moment right now — chocolate, mocha, sage — and ASOS nailed the color story. This feels current, not a season behind.
- **The hero model is giving.** Curly hair, statement jewelry, that deep-V top — the styling is aspirational without being unattainable. Feels like something I'd screenshot for inspo.
- **Product grid is cohesive.** Every piece in the grid holds the same earthy palette. It doesn't feel like a random dump — it tells a story. Zara's "Woman | Discover what's new" sends are a chaotic mess by comparison; this is tighter.
- **Clean layout, no render bugs.** Nothing broken, nothing overlapping. The waterproof jewelry callout at the bottom is a smart secondary module — gold leaf pendant, quietly on-trend.
- **WOC model representation throughout the grid.** Multiple models, different skin tones, actually visible. H&M's "Welcome Fashion Lover" email was just monochrome text — this is doing more with visual diversity.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer after six sale emails is a hard pivot.** I was in deal mode. ASOS trained me to expect a discount, and now full-price editorial with no percentage, no free shipping, nothing — I'm not in that headspace.
- **"SHOP THE EDIT" goes to a category, not the hero piece.** I want to tap the halter top directly. Sending me to a whole edit means I have to scroll and filter, and I will absolutely not do that.
- **No price transparency at all.** Not even a "from £X" anchor. Zara does the same thing and it drives me crazy — if I can't see price on scroll, I assume it's expensive and I move on.
- **Cadence fatigue is hitting.** This is the seventh email I've seen from ASOS in recent history. Even though this one breaks from sale format, my guard is already up. The creative has to work a lot harder when trust is depleted.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one anchor price or a "new in from £X" line in the hero.** Even £22 from, £34 from — anything. Full price editorial works if I know I can actually afford the stuff.
- 2. **Make the hero product directly tappable.** The halter-and-linen combo in the hero is doing the most work. Link it directly instead of routing to the full edit.
- 3. **Drop one contextual line that signals "after all those sales, here's what's actually new."** Something like "The sale noise is real — here's what just landed fresh." Acknowledges the cadence fatigue without apologizing for it.
- 4. **Include at least one styling detail that creates urgency.** Even "restocking now" or "limited sizes in the olive midi" — something to make me feel like waiting costs me.
- 5. **Add a TikTok-native signal.** A "trending on our FYP" tag or a creator style reference on one of the products would land instantly for my demographic. H&M did this much better in their "Summer styles to wear on repeat" email even when their offer was weak.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The brown era is here — shop the palette`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Chocolate, sage, and everything in between`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New arrivals in the shades your FYP has been serving all week`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh drops in earthy tones — from £XX`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Global link style reset** — `a, span { text-decoration: none; color: inherit; }` removes all visual link differentiation. If CTAs rely solely on background color to signal interactivity, they fail WCAG 1.4.1 (use of color) in high-contrast or monochrome contexts.

- **SVG font format included unnecessarily** — `@font-face` declarations load `FuturaPT-Book.svg` and `FuturaPT-Heavy.svg`. SVG fonts are deprecated and unsupported in all modern email clients. These are dead HTTP requests on every open.
  ```
  url("https://images.asos-media.com/notification/content/fonts/FuturaPT-Book.svg") format("svg")
  ```
  Remove both SVG entries.

- **`font-size: 0em` on `table, tr, td, th`** — Intentional ghost-text suppression, correct practice. No issue.

- **CSS animation fallback** — `@keyframes ticker` with `@supports` guard and `.fallback`/`.ticker` toggle is correctly implemented. Clients without animation support will show the fallback static block.

- **`-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased` on `*`** — Overly broad; harmless in email clients but unnecessary specificity.
