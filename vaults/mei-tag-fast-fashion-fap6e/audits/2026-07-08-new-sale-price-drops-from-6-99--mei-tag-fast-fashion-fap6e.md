# New sale price drops from $6.99
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- OK so after the Campus Shop drop yesterday and what felt like a week-long 20% off tunnel before that, H&M is back in my inbox with a markdown announcement. My gut reaction is: cool, sale, let me see if "$6.99" is real or a floor-price trap. The number does something to my brain — I immediately want to know *what* is $6.99 — but it also sets off a tiny alarm because in my experience, floor anchors like that usually mean "this is the one item no one wanted in June and we are getting rid of it."
- The layout is clean, the women's section models actually look like someone I'd follow on TikTok — blue floral sundress, striped oversized dress, current silhouettes — and the photography is doing H&M's usual competent thing. But then the email immediately pivots to men, baby/kids, and terracotta plant pots, and I've lost the thread. Three of four main sections are not for me. This is not a segmented send, it's a department store catalog with a sale sticker on it.
- Would I click? Yeah, probably on the WOMEN section just to see what's actually at that price. Would I forward to a friend? No — ASOS's extra 20% off sale email that landed today is a tighter, more direct deal and that's what I'd be texting my group chat.

## What's working

- **"$6.99" is a real hook** — a concrete number in the hero creates enough curiosity to earn the click even when nothing else is working hard.
- **Women's section models are on-point** — young, diverse, the styling is current (the blue floral sundress especially), this looks like a real campaign and not leftover assets.
- **Render is clean** — no broken images, no overlapping text, the H&M grid system is holding up, which is not a given when I think about some of the Shein emails I've suffered through.
- **"GOING FAST" on baby/kids** — I have zero use for it but that urgency label is actually smart for that audience; whoever wrote that section was thinking about conversion.
- **Subject line is under 50 chars and price-specific** — "New sale price drops from $6.99" at 32 characters hits mobile cleanly and doesn't feel like spam.

## What's weak

- **"SO GOOD" and "SMART MOVES" are copy that means nothing** — these want to sound Gen Z but they're giving corporate brainstorm whiteboard. Zara would never. ASOS's editorial block labels have an actual POV; H&M's here are filler.
- **Baby, kids, and home are eating half my email** — if H&M knows anything about me it's that I am not shopping the BABY section. No segmentation means this email is working for everyone and connecting with no one.
- **No deadline anywhere** — "further markdowns" with no end date is not urgency, it's just information. ASOS's extra 20% off email today had clearer stakes; this has none.
- **Floor anchor might backfire** — if I click through and the $6.99 items are three tank tops in size XS only, I'm bouncing and feeling baited. The offer needs some specific product proof to earn trust.
- **Sixth H&M email this week** — the July 3–5 20% off tunnel was already a lot, then the lookbook, then Campus Shop, and now this. Even if it's technically a different angle I am starting to tune these out.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment baby/kids/home into a separate send** — the women's section should take up the full first scroll. Three women's looks minimum before I see a toddler.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the offer** — "through Sunday" or "72 hours only" in the hero subtext would make the $6.99 feel real instead of open-ended.
- 3. **Replace "SO GOOD" with a trend-connected label** — "THE $6.99 EDIT" or "WHAT'S TRENDING CHEAP" ties the copy to the offer and gives the click a reason.
- 4. **Hero product proof** — show me one specific dress or top at $6.99 in the hero image, not just category models. That single product shot earns more clicks than any copy label.
- 5. **Preheader should name a specific category or product** — "Upgrade your summer style with the biggest markdowns of the season" is the blandest possible complement to a price-specific subject.
- **Subject Alt A:** `H&M just dropped to $6.99 — see what's in the summer edit`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Further markdowns: from $6.99 now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dresses, tops, basics — new lows hit the women's section.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The summer purge is live. Here's what actually dropped.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading (high risk)**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is blocked by Gmail, Outlook 2007–2019, and Yahoo Mail. Web fonts will silently fall back to system fonts, which is acceptable only if fallback stacks are defined. Confirm fallback `font-family` stacks are present in downstream selectors.

**`<script>` tag stripped by most clients**
The JSON-LD block inside `<script type="application/ld+json">` is only parsed by Gmail. All other email clients strip it entirely. This is expected behavior for Gmail annotations, not a defect — but issues within it (see §4) will affect Gmail-only features.

**Missing `lang` attribute**
```html
<html style="border:0;margin:0;...">
```
No `lang="en"` (or locale equivalent) on the `<html>` element. Screen readers cannot infer language; also affects spell-check heuristics in some clients. Minor accessibility gap.

**`alt` text on images** — cannot confirm; images appear below the truncation point.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Stale hardcoded date in JSON-LD (confirmed defect)**
```json
"availabilityStarts":"2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"
```
This date is 7+ years in the past. Either a static placeholder was never replaced by the ESP merge layer, or this field was copy-pasted from a legacy template. Gmail interprets this field for its promotional tab annotations — a stale date may cause the promotion to not surface or be deprioritized.

**Multiple empty fields in JSON-LD**
```json
{"@type":"EmailMessage","subjectLine":"","@context":"http://schema.org/"}
{"availabilityEnds":"","discountCode":"","description":""}
{"image":"","url":""}
```
Six fields are empty strings. For `DiscountOffer`, empty `discountCode` and `description` mean the Gmail promo tab annotation will not render a usable offer card. For `PromotionCard`, empty `image` and `url` prevent the visual card from appearing.

These appear to be unfilled merge tokens, not intentional omissions. The `subjectLine` field being empty when the actual subject is "New sale price drops from $6.99" confirms the ESP did not populate this schema block.
