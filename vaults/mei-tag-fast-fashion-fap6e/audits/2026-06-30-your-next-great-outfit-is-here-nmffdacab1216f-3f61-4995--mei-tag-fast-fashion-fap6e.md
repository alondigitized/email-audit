# Your next great outfit is here
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- Okay so I opened this one on autopilot — H&M is basically permanent real estate in my inbox at this point, and my thumb knows the logo before my brain does. "Dressed for the Occasion" is a decent angle, the hero model has great curly natural hair and she looks like she could be in my For You page, and the sale banner does its job (60% off, new arrivals from $7.99 — I see you). The subheadline "for every event on your social menu" is actually kind of cute? Like someone at H&M has been on TikTok. But then I scroll down and hit the category buttons and there it is: GIRLS 2-8Y and BOYS 2-8Y, sitting right next to WOMEN and MEN like I'm shopping for a niece's birthday. I am not. I am 24 and childless and I do not need a reminder that this email was built for literally everybody.
- The products are fine — Dress with Godet Pleats and a Cotton Dress with Embroidery, both $29.99, both tagged New Arrival, which I respect — but then there's a Slim-Fit Jacket for $79.99 in a clearly men's cut dropped right in the middle of what I thought was a women's occasion edit. Mixed signals. This email doesn't know who it's talking to, and that matters when ASOS just hit me with a 70% off banner that felt laser-focused.
- I would not forward this. I might click the dress if I was already in a shopping mood, but I would absolutely not open a thirteenth email from this brand this week on good faith.

## What's working

- The sale anchor in the top banner is immediate — 60% off + from $7.99 tells me the range before I even process the hero.
- Hero model has curly natural hair, relaxed styling, looks like a real person not a catalog robot — that's the vibe I actually want.
- "For every event on your social menu" — that copy is doing genuine work, it's light and current without being try-hard.
- Godet pleat dress and embroidered cotton dress as New Arrivals at $29.99 — specific SKUs, specific prices, New Arrival tags visible. That's the right product info hierarchy.
- Email renders clean — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds.

## What's weak

- GIRLS 2-8Y and BOYS 2-8Y category buttons in an occasion-dressing email aimed at me: completely irrelevant and dilutes the targeting.
- Men's Slim-Fit Jacket ($79.99) dropped in the women's product row — broke the occasion-dress flow mid-scroll.
- No urgency anywhere in the body — no "today only," no low-stock signal, no end date on the sale. Zara's "Today at 9 pm ET" drop email created actual scarcity; this creates none.
- This is send thirteen-ish in a week. The creative reset is cosmetic — "Dressed for the Occasion" is a costume over the same promotional skeleton as yesterday's "Go-to summer looks."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the children's categories.** For a segmented send to 20-something women, those buttons should not exist. Segment properly or hide them behind a "more" link.
- 2. **Lead with a time-bound signal.** Zara's 9 PM drop beat this email on urgency alone. Even "Sale ends Sunday" would do real work here.
- 3. **Women-only product grid.** The men's Slim-Fit Jacket in the middle row kills the editorial flow. Either make this a women's-only email or commit to a couples/occasion angle and design for it intentionally.
- 4. **More specific subject line.** "Your next great outfit" is the single most generic sentence in fashion email. ASOS's "Sale: now up to 70% off" made me do math; this one made me shrug.
- 5. **H&M+ member signal needs to land harder.** "H&M+ Premium Selection" buried on a linen shirt label is doing nothing. If you have a loyalty program, make it feel like a benefit, not a product tag.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New arrivals hit the sale — dresses from $29.99`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The godet dress era is here (and 60% off)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Embroidered cotton, godet pleats, linen blends — new and on sale`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh occasion drops added — shop before your size goes`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard table-based layout with responsive CSS and schema.org JSON-LD annotations. Several JSON-LD fields are empty placeholders, web fonts are loaded via `@import` (which most email clients strip), and one structured-data date is eight years stale. The HTML is truncated so link-level and footer compliance checks are partially incomplete.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**JSON-LD PromotionCard `url` is empty**
```json
{"@type":"PromotionCard","url":""}
```
Gmail renders PromotionCard entries in the Promotions tab carousel. An empty `url` disables the card's click-through entirely.

**JSON-LD `subjectLine` is empty**
```json
{"@type":"EmailMessage","subjectLine":""}
```
Should match the actual subject line (`"Your next great outfit is here"`). Gmail may deprioritize the Promotions tab annotation when this field is blank.

**Cannot verify UTM parameters** — the HTML is truncated before `<body>` link content. A full-source audit is required to confirm all `<a href>` values carry `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign`.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` inside `<style>` — fonts will not render in most clients**
```html
<style>
  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
</style>
```
Gmail (web and Android), Outlook (all desktop versions), and Yahoo strip or ignore `@import` inside `<style>` blocks. Custom fonts will fall back to system defaults in these clients, which represent the majority of H&M's likely audience. Use `<link rel="stylesheet">` in `<head>` instead, and always include a CSS font-stack fallback (`font-family: 'HM Font', Arial, sans-serif`).

**`x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` present** — correct, no issue.

**`format-detection` for telephone set** — correct, no issue.

**`alt` attribute coverage** — cannot confirm from truncated source. The selector `img.image + div { display: none !important; }` implies product images exist; each must carry a descriptive `alt`.

**`role`/ARIA** — none visible in `<head>` section; main content is truncated, so cannot assess body-level accessibility.
