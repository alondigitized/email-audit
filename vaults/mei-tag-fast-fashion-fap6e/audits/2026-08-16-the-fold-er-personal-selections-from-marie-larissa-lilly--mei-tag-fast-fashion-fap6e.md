# The Fold-er: Personal selections from Marie, Larissa & Lilly
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-16**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so this is "The Fold-er" and it's Zara doing an editorial-influencer-collab thing with three women — Larissa, Lilly, Marie — giving their "personal selections." And like... the photography is genuinely elevated, moody, editorial, I get it. But there's zero offer, zero price, zero percent-off anywhere in this entire scroll. This isn't a shopping email, it's a mood board dressed up as a newsletter, and one of these women — Marie — is literally saying she likes "timeless rather than overly trend-driven" pieces in a Zara email, which is such a weird thing to plaster in an inbox to someone like me who's here for the trend-first hustle.
- It's giving the same energy as the August 13th Aaron Levine capsule — cool visual world, no urgency, no reason to act today. At least that one had a named collab hook. This one's just... three strangers' Zara picks. I don't know Larissa Mills, I don't know Lilly Sisto, I don't have any context on why their opinion matters to me specifically.
- I'm not clicking this. I'd scroll past it in about two seconds, no forward, straight to "seen it, next."

## What's working

- The hero photography is genuinely gorgeous — that golden-hour sweater shot has a real film-grain, editorial feel, not stock-photo energy.
- "SHOP WITH [NAME]" as a CTA is at least a specific, personalized-feeling label instead of a generic "Shop Now."
- Three distinct looks (utility jacket, striped top + polka skirt, polka maxi dress) means there's some visual variety if you're just browsing for inspo.
- Clean typography, no clutter — feels premium and easy to read without zooming.

## What's weak

- There's no offer. Not a %, not a $, not free shipping — nothing. For a promo email this reads like it's not even trying to sell me anything today.
- Marie's whole quote about wanting pieces that are "timeless rather than overly trend-driven" is directly working against what I actually open Zara emails for — I want the trend that just hit my FYP, not an anti-trend philosophy lesson.
- I have no idea who these three women are or why I should trust their picks — no follower count, no "as seen on," no bio, nothing establishing credibility.
- Zero urgency, zero deadline, zero reason to act right now versus next week.
- All three models are the same demographic — thin, white, adult, minimalist aesthetic — no range at all, which feels stale next to how ASOS shot that Lioness drop with a girl actually strutting down cobblestone like she had somewhere to be.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add an actual offer or at least a "shop the edit before it sells out" urgency line — right now there's zero reason to click today vs. whenever.
- 2. Cut or reframe Marie's "timeless not trend-driven" quote — it's actively repelling the trend-chasing audience this brand usually courts. If you're going to run influencer voices, pick ones whose POV matches "I found this before everyone else," not "I don't care about trends."
- 3. Add a one-line credibility hook under each name — follower count, "TikTok's [x]," something that tells me why I should care what Larissa thinks versus a random stranger.
- 4. Diversify the cast — three visually similar women in similar poses reads as one look repeated three times, not three distinct voices.
- 5. Tighten this into a "first to shop it" framing instead of "personal selections" — compare it to H&M's own trend emails; even their generic tiered-discount ones at least lead with a number.
