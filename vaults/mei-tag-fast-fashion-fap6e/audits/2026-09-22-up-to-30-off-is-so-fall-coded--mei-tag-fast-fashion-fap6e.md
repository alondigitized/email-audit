# Up to 30% off is so fall-coded
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-22**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is basically the same "Up to 30% off" markdown email H&M ran on 2026-09-20 and 09-19, just retitled "fall-coded" so it sounds trendy without actually being trend content. The hero girl in the maroon wide-legs and white tank is cute, I'll give it that, but "wear-forever basics and knits" is the opposite of what I open H&M for — that's Zara Kids energy, not TikTok-drop energy. This is the third markdown-cadence send from H&M in a week and my thumb is already twitchy to archive it.
- I'm not clicking this one. It's not bad, it's just... nothing new. I'd mark it read and move to my Shein tab.

## What's working

- Hero image is clean and on-model for my age bracket — one girl, one trend-adjacent maroon trouser moment, no clutter.
- "Up to 30% off select styles" is a real, legible number sitting right under the hero, no scavenger hunt.
- Category buttons (Women/Men/Baby/Kids) are big, tappable, thumb-friendly.
- Layout hierarchy is clean top-to-bottom — no overlapping text, no broken images this time (unlike the 09-18 "Fall Edit" disaster where every image was broken).

## What's weak

- "Fall-coded" in the subject promises Gen Z trend language but the body copy is "wear-forever basics and knits" — that's a mismatch, feels like a copywriter bolted trend slang onto a basics dump.
- Zero urgency or deadline visible anywhere in the rendered email — no "ends tonight," no countdown, just a vague markdown.
- This is the same 20-30%-off-basics beat H&M has run three sends running (09-19 denim, 09-20 "ends tonight," now this) — the cadence is genuinely fatiguing me.
- Baby and Kids sections eat real estate that does nothing for me — I'm not shopping toddler hoodies.
- No trend-specific product callouts (no "as seen on TikTok," no drop language) — just generic "Time for New" / "Easy Upgrades" / "Soft & Sweet" section headers that could be any department store circular.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut Baby/Kids from a persona-targeted send like this entirely, or push them below a hard fold — give me Women hero, then straight to a trend callout.
- 2. Actually make the subject match the body: either put real trend copy in the hero ("the wide-leg trouser everyone's wearing right now") or drop "fall-coded" from the subject since the copy doesn't deliver it.
- 3. Add a deadline. Compare to ASOS's Lioness drop email — even though I scored that a 5, at least it felt like a *moment*. This just feels like inventory clearance.
- 4. Space out the markdown cadence — three near-identical % off sends in a week trains me to stop opening.

- **Subject Alt B:** `30% off — this drop won't last the week`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The trouser everyone's already wearing, 30% off through Sunday`
