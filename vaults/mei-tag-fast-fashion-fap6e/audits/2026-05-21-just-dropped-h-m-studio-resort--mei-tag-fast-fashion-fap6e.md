# Just dropped: H&M Studio Resort
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- Okay so H&M dropped their Studio Resort collection email and the hero image made me stop mid-scroll — that white, voluminous, feathered-hem dress is genuinely doing editorial work. Like, that's the kind of image you screenshot and send to the group chat, not just swipe past. The rest of the collection photography commits too: leopard print coat, ivory wide-legs, the whole Amalfi-coast-if-you-had-the-budget aesthetic. I respect the vision.
- But here's my actual problem with this email: H&M was literally in my inbox yesterday with a "50% off sitewide" blast, and now today it's a premium Studio launch with zero price anchoring on any of the pieces? I went from "half the site is on sale" to "here's a gorgeous collection, figure out the prices yourself" in less than 24 hours. That whiplash is real. Also I can see they've packed the Lotto x H&M collab into the bottom half of the same email — two completely different aesthetics (resort editorial vs. soccer streetwear) fighting for space in one scroll. Pick a lane.
- I'd save the hero image and then mark it as read. I wouldn't forward it. The photography is beautiful but it's not doing the trust-building work of "I found this first" — this feels like H&M chasing resort content that Zara has already owned on TikTok for two seasons running.

## What's working

- The hero dress is genuinely arresting — all-white, dramatic volume, feathered or ruffled hem — it communicates "collection moment" without needing a headline to sell it
- "Just dropped" in the subject line is the right energy; it reads like a TikTok notification, not a corporate newsletter
- The leopard print coat shot is trend-accurate — that exact silhouette has been all over my FYP this cycle and H&M owns it cleanly here
- Layout is clean from top to bottom — no render bugs, good section spacing, the eye knows where to go
- "SHOP HER" / "SHOP HIM" CTAs on the Lotto x H&M section are more specific than the rest of the email and cut friction fast for that section specifically

## What's weak

- Zero price anchoring in the entire Studio Resort section — I'm a three-tab comparison shopper, "how much?" is always question one and this email makes me go find it myself
- The Lotto x H&M collab section embedded at the bottom feels like a separate campaign got copy-pasted in — two audiences, two aesthetics, one confused email
- After yesterday's 50% off sitewide blast, switching to full-price premium editorial without any acknowledgment creates a weird dissonance — am I shopping a sale brand or a fashion brand today?
- "SHOP NOW" is the most generic CTA in existence — ASOS would give me "Shop the Trend," Zara would give me a mood, H&M is just pointing at the store

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price anchor on the hero** — even "From $24.99" or a starting-price callout right under the headline. Studio doesn't have to mean mystery pricing; transparency is what earns the click.
- 2. **Break this into two sends** — Studio Resort is a distinct editorial event; Lotto x H&M is a street-sport collab that deserves its own send with its own energy. Both get diluted sharing one email.
- 3. **Upgrade the CTA copy everywhere** — "Shop the Drop" or "Shop Studio Resort" beats "SHOP NOW" in every A/B test, every time.
- 4. **Add a TikTok-style social proof signal** — a "trending now" badge, a creator tag in-email (like the "@nassnyc" move they made on 5/18, which worked), anything that tells me this already has traction beyond H&M telling me so.
- 5. **Address the cadence contradiction** — yesterday was sitewide sale, today is a new full-price launch. Either bridge the two ("new arrivals outside the sale") or wait one more day; sending both back-to-back undermines both.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading will fail in most clients (confirmed).**

```css
<style>
  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
</style>
```

`@import` inside `<style>` is not supported by Gmail (web or Android), Outlook (all desktop versions), Yahoo Mail, or AOL. Both custom font families will silently fall back to the client's system font stack on the majority of the subscriber base. This is a rendering regression, not a crash — but it means brand typography is not rendering as intended for most recipients.

**Alt-text fallback suppression:**

```css
img.image + div { display: none !important; }
```

This rule hides any `<div>` that directly follows an `.image` img element. If that pattern is used to wrap alt-text fallback content or VML-based Outlook image substitutes, those fallbacks are suppressed. Cannot confirm impact without full source.

**No other rendering issues confirmed** from the visible markup. `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` and `format-detection=telephone=no` are correctly set.
