# The best denim of summer
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- Okay so H&M has been in my inbox basically every single day this week — 70% off flash sales, $6.99 styles, the whole discount spiral — and now they pivot to a clean editorial denim moment. And honestly? The denim brief is so right. Barrel jeans, wide-leg silhouettes, denim shorts — this is exactly what's all over my FYP right now. The hero copy "WEAR. WASH. REPEAT." is simple and it lands. The model in the blue printed top over denim is giving summer-editorial and I'm here for it.
- But then the email keeps going. And going. ELEVATED. CLASSIC. CUTE. Three separate denim categories with MEN and KIDS tucked in mid-scroll — and then, a hard pivot to a back-to-school bundle giveaway. It's July 14th. I'm not thinking about back-to-school. I just got back from the beach. The two storylines running through this email — summer denim and BTS prep — don't belong in the same send.
- I'd mark this as a good idea, mediocre execution. I'd probably click one of the women's denim CTAs because I'm genuinely looking for new fits right now, but I would not forward this — it's not "you need to see this" energy. It's more "yeah okay H&M has jeans."

## What's working

- **Denim is the right topic right now.** "Longer lengths, looser fits, modern washes" — this is TikTok-accurate language for the barrel jean / wide-leg moment and H&M is actually naming the trend instead of just saying "shop denim."
- **Hero visual is clean and demographic-matched.** The opening model (blue patterned top, denim shorts) feels like she's 22-26, which is me, not my mom.
- **Three-category framework (ELEVATED / CLASSIC / CUTE) is smart.** It lets me self-sort immediately — I know I'm ELEVATED, and I can jump there. ASOS does personalized category funnels better but this is a decent shorthand.
- **No render bugs.** Clean layout, images load, buttons are tappable. After ASOS's July 11 merge-tag disaster that opened with a comma, just rendering correctly feels like a win.
- **Member giveaway is visible.** If I'm already a member, the $250 gift card mention at least got a glance.

## What's weak

- **Zero discount after a week of 70% off.** Yesterday was "The art of keeping it simple" (no price), the day before was also editorial. H&M has now strung together multiple no-offer emails after training me to expect deep discounts. The whiplash is real — I'm waiting for the other shoe to drop.
- **Back-to-school bundle mid-summer denim email makes no sense.** These are two different customer jobs. I came in for trend denim; the BTS pivot broke the mood.
- **MEN and KIDS dilutes the send.** I have to scroll past men's denim styling and a kids' shorts button to get to more women's content. Zara just got dinged in my last review for the exact same multi-audience problem — don't copy that mistake.
- **No trend specificity.** "Longer lengths, looser fits" is right but vague. Naming it — barrel leg, wide leg, Y2K wash — would've made this feel like H&M was *leading* the trend, not just acknowledging it.
- **CTAs are nouns, not actions.** "JEANS" and "SHORTS" don't tell me what to do with them. Even "SHOP JEANS" or "SEE BARREL FITS" would land harder.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut MEN, KIDS, and Back-to-School entirely from this send.** This is a women's denim trend email — protect that story from noise. BTS gets its own campaign, its own email.
- 2. **Name the specific trends.** Replace "longer lengths, looser fits" with "barrel legs, Y2K washes, the wide-leg shorts" — that's the language my FYP uses and it signals H&M knows the trend.
- 3. **Add one price anchor.** Even "from $24.99" resets the no-offer hangover from recent editorial sends. Doesn't need to be a flash sale — just a hook.
- 4. **Change CTA copy.** "SHOP BARREL LEGS" and "SHOP DENIM SHORTS" over "JEANS" and "SHORTS" — verb + trend label.
- 5. **Move member giveaway to a separate email.** Or at minimum put it after the full denim story, not mid-scroll where it breaks the editorial flow.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Barrel legs are having a summer`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The denim fits your FYP is obsessed with`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Wide leg, longer lengths, Y2K washes — all on sale from $24.99`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Loose fits, clean washes, the denim moment is now`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**External font @imports will fail in most clients**

```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```

`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is stripped by Gmail, Yahoo Mail, and most webmail. Fonts will silently fall back to system fonts. H&M's own fallback stack is not visible in the truncated source, so whether the fallback degrades gracefully is unverifiable here.

**Duplicate mobile media query rules**

Inside `@media (max-width:699px)`, the following selectors are declared twice with identical properties:

- `.cta-link-mobile-border-ns { border: 1px solid #000 !important; }`
- `.cta-link-mobile, .set_font_interactive_standalone_link_default { line-height: 17px !important; font-size: 14px !important; }`

Redundant rules increase CSS weight and can trigger parser bugs in older clients (Outlook 2016, Samsung Mail).

**Alt text on images — cannot verify** (HTML truncated before `<img>` tags).

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Stale hardcoded date in JSON-LD `DiscountOffer`**

```json
"availabilityStarts": "2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"
```

This date is ~7.5 years in the past. Gmail Promotions tab will either reject or misrender the offer annotation. This is either an un-replaced merge token or a copy-paste artifact from a 2018 campaign template.

**Empty required fields across all JSON-LD nodes**

```json
{"@type":"EmailMessage","subjectLine":"", ...}
{"@type":"DiscountOffer","availabilityEnds":"","discountCode":"","description":"", ...}
{"@type":"PromotionCard","image":"","url":"", ...}
```

`subjectLine`, `discountCode`, `description`, `image`, `url`, and `availabilityEnds` are all empty strings. These are unfilled merge tokens or deliberately blanked-out fields. For the Gmail Promotions tab annotation to function, `PromotionCard.url` and `PromotionCard.image` must be populated; blank values will suppress the annotation entirely.
