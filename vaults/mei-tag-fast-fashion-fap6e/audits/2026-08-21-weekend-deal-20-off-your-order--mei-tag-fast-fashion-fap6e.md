# Weekend deal: 20% off your order
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-21**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "20% off $125 + Free Shipping" is a real offer with a real dollar threshold, so at least I'm not squinting for the fine print like I was in H&M's "20% off in the app" email from yesterday (2026-08-19) that had zero visible discount on an actual product. But $125 minimum is steep for me — I'm not dropping $125 in one H&M cart unless I'm doing a full closet reset, and the "grid of nine random products with no styling story" layout does nothing to convince me to get there.
- The bigger issue is this feels like generic clearance-bin H&M, not trend-first H&M. Cotton polo shirt, denim pull-on pants, a desk organizer, tufted wall art — this reads like a "everything must move" catalog dump, not "here's the piece everyone's about to be wearing." Compare that to H&M's own "Ace Your Tennis Look à la @sierrarenas" send from 2026-08-18 — that one had an actual creator and a cohesive aesthetic. This one has none of that; it's just SKUs.
- Would I click? Maybe, only because the discount is concrete and I do sometimes buy H&M basics. Would I forward it to a friend? No — there's nothing trend-worthy or screenshot-worthy here, it's just a coupon email with product tiles.

## What's working

- The hero offer is impossible to miss — big bold "20% OFF $125 + FREE SHIPPING" right up top, no scrolling needed.
- BOPIS banner ("Buy Online, Pick Up In-Store") at the very top is genuinely useful info I care about for delivery-time honesty.
- Category nav buttons (Women/Men/Baby/Kids/Home) are big, tappable, thumb-friendly — no tiny tap targets here.
- "Back to Campus" section at least attempts a lifestyle angle instead of just being more product tiles, and the model skews right for my age bracket.
- Prices are visible on every product without me having to tap through — I like knowing $19.99 vs $44.99 before I commit to opening the app.

## What's weak

- The main product grid (polo, pants, cargo pants, tee, skirt, sweatshirt) is completely disconnected from "Back to Campus" — it's random home decor and basics mashed together with zero trend narrative, unlike Zara's "Explore our newest collections" mega-scroll (4/10) which at least organized by named collections.
- "Ready to check the last things off your list? P.S. Don't forget a jacket." — off your list for WHAT? There's no campaign context up top; "Back to Campus" theming only shows up two-thirds down the email, so the opening feels like leftover copy from a different send.
- A desk organizer and tufted wall art sitting next to a cargo pant and a printed tee is such a random home+apparel mashup, it kills any sense this was curated for someone tracking trends.
- No influencer, no styled outfit shot, no "as seen on" — just isolated product-on-white-background shots, which is the opposite of what worked in the Sierra Renas tennis email.
- "*20% OFF $125 + FREE SHIPPING" footnote excludes gift cards, select collabs, furniture — buried at the very bottom in tiny gray text, so the "honest offer" points get partially clawed back once you actually read it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with "Back to Campus" as the hero, not buried mid-email — that's the one section with an actual point of view and a model who could pass for my age. Right now the campaign identity is backwards.
- 2. Cut the desk organizer / wall art from the top grid or move Home into its own clearly-labeled section — mixing categories with zero transition reads sloppy.
- 3. Drop the $125 minimum lower or add a second tier (e.g. "15% off $75") — $125 is a big ask for a Saturday scroll-and-compare shopper like me who's cross-checking Shein and Zara in the same sitting.
- 4. Add one outfit-styled flat lay or model shot up top instead of nine isolated product photos — give me a "get the look" moment like the tennis email did.
- 5. Move the exclusions/fine print higher or at least flag "some exclusions apply" near the hero so it doesn't feel like a bait-and-switch when I scroll to checkout.

## Technical Audit

1. Technical Summary
The email is delivery-functional but nearly every trackable link resolves through H&M's ESP redirector (t19.email.hm.com), and automated probing returned HTTP 403 on all 12 tested links, including the unsubscribe link; no unsubscribe or authentication headers were captured.

2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All sampled links use the same redirect domain pattern `https://t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=...&did=1715378650&rid=3326930539&erid=...&p1=DM5971090&p2=...&p3=20260821` — consistent with a standard ESP click-tracking wrapper (Responsys-style params: `did`, `rid`, `erid`, `p1`-`p5`).
- 12/12 probed links returned `[FAIL] Broken link (403)`, including primary CTAs ("Shop now" ×2), nav links (Women, Men, Baby, Kids, HOME), the logo link ("H&M"), the "Buy online, pick up in-store" link, and the footer "Unsubscribe" link.
- A 403 on an automated prober against ESP redirect domains is frequently caused by bot/User-Agent filtering or IP-based blocking at the redirector rather than a genuinely dead destination — this should be verified with a manual click-through in a real client before treating it as confirmed breakage, but as tested, 100% of sampled links failed.
- Link text contains injected zero-width/formatting characters (`‌  ﻿Shop now`), indicating a ZWNJ/BOM or invisible personalization artifact embedded in anchor text — likely leftover from template tokenization, not a functional link error but worth flagging for markup cleanup.
- 85 of 97 total links were not probed (deprioritized footer/social/utility links per the QA tool's click-likelihood ranking) — link health for those is unverified, not confirmed passing.

3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 22 `<img>` elements flagged with missing `alt` text, spanning both product imagery (`image.hm.com/.../cq5dam.web.*.jpeg`, in 1400px and 750px variants) and tracking pixels (`t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=...`) — none carry `alt` attributes, which fails WCAG 4.1.2/1.1.1 and degrades screen-reader and images-off rendering.
- Client-targeting CSS present for Outlook/Yahoo/Gmail (`#MessageViewBody`, `@media screen yahoo`, `mso-hide`), and a mobile breakpoint at 699px (`.is-hidden-mobile` / `.is-hidden-desktop`) — standard defensive email-client scoping, no issues found in the visible markup.
- `@import url(...)` used for custom web fonts (`fabric.hmgroup.com`, `s1-cdn.hm.com`) inside a `<style>` block — many mail clients (Outlook desktop, Gmail app) strip `@import`, meaning font fallback stack should be verified as functional; not confirmed broken from source alone.

4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tags (e.g. `{{first_name}}`, `%%FIELD%%`) found in the visible HTML.
- The embedded JSON-LD (`application/ld+json`) `EmailMessage`/`DiscountOffer` schema has multiple empty string fields: `subjectLine`, `discountCode`, `availabilityEnds`, `image`, `url`, `description` all render as `""`. These are structured-data merge tokens that failed to populate — `discountCode` and `availabilityEnds` being empty is notable since the subject line promotes "20% off," suggesting the Gmail promotional markup/discount schema will not render correctly.
- `availabilityStarts` is hardcoded to `"2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"` — a stale/unresolved date token from years prior, confirming the JSON-LD block is not being populated correctly for this send.

5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- Unsubscribe link is present in the footer, but the automated prober returned a 403 on it (`p5=9be06746af74de7271d9dcc8d83f5742` suggests a valid per-recipient unsubscribe token, so this is likely the same redirector-blocking issue affecting all links rather than a missing mechanism) — functional status unconfirmed, flag for manual verification.
- `List-Unsubscribe` header: not found in captured headers. Could not confirm RFC 2369 one-click unsubscribe support.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header: not found. Could not confirm RFC 8058 one-click (POST) unsubscribe support.
- `Authentication-Results` header: not found — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status for this message is unknown from available data.
- Both header omissions are flagged by the QA tool as possibly an artifact of the AgentMail relay not capturing/forwarding these headers, rather than confirmed absence at origin — cannot be treated as a confirmed compliance failure without checking raw headers at the receiving MTA.

6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- No standard `utm_source`/`utm_medium`/`utm_campaign` query parameters are present on any sampled link; H&M uses proprietary ESP tracking params (`did`, `rid`, `erid`, `p1`-`p5`) instead of UTM, so GA/analytics attribution continuity cannot be assessed from this markup alone.
- Landing page alignment cannot be verified: all destination URLs are ESP redirect wrappers (t19.email.hm.com), and 100% of probed redirects returned 403, so the actual final landing page (e.g. hm.com product/category page matching "20% off" promo) could not be confirmed to load or match the offer.

7. Recommendations
- Manually click-test (real browser, non-automated UA) the "Shop now" CTAs and Unsubscribe link to distinguish genuine 403 breakage from prober-side bot blocking at t19.email.hm.com.
- Populate the empty JSON-LD `DiscountOffer` fields (`discountCode`, `availabilityEnds`, `image`, `url`) and correct the stale `availabilityStarts` (2018) so Gmail promotional/annotated markup renders the offer correctly.
- Add `alt` text to all 22 flagged `<img>` tags, particularly the two product images per row (1400w/750w variants) and tracking pixels (use `alt=""` for purely decorative/tracking pixels).
- Strip the stray zero-width/BOM characters (`‌`, `﻿`) from CTA anchor text ("Shop now").
- Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers and `Authentication-Results` are present at the raw MTA level (outside the AgentMail relay) to close out RFC 8058/CAN-SPAM/authentication verification, since the current warnings may be relay artifacts rather than true gaps.
