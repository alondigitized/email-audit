# Explore our newest collections
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-05**
## Executive summary

- Okay wait — this is literally an entire men's collection. Puffer jackets, street fits, cologne, blazers, jeans, all on male models. I'm a woman on this list to see what's new for ME, and Zara just sent me their guy's lookbook. This isn't even a targeting miss, it's a full segmentation fail — like they sent me the wrong list entirely.
- Visually it's very Zara — moody black-and-white puffer hero shot, editorial street-style photography, that stark serif logo — so the aesthetic is on-brand and honestly kind of striking on the puffer shot. But aesthetic doesn't save an email that has literally nothing for me to buy. No price, no offer, no percentage off, nothing — just section headers ("THE GLOSSY PUFFER JACKET," "STREET FITS," "FRAGRANCE COLLECTION") stacked into a men's catalog scroll.
- I'm not clicking, I'm not forwarding — unless it's to a guy friend as a joke about Zara emailing me his wardrobe. This is an immediate mark-as-read, maybe an eyebrow raise at the puffer photography, then gone.

## What's working

- The glossy puffer hero shot is genuinely a strong, campaign-quality image — moody lighting, good scale, feels expensive.
- Layout is clean and easy to scroll — clear section dividers, no render bugs, no overlapping text.
- The street-style shots (guy eating pizza on a stoop, guy walking with sunglasses) have that off-duty editorial energy Zara does well.

## What's weak

- This is a men's-only email — jackets, shirts, trousers, fragrance, knitwear, blazers, jeans, all shown on male models. Nothing here is for me.
- Zero offer, zero price, zero urgency — same complaint as their 2026-09-04 "Woman | Discover what's new" send, which was also just a category directory with no reason to click now.
- No trend hook, no "this is THE jacket everyone's wearing" framing — just flat category labels like "JACKETS," "TROUSERS," "PERFUMES."
- Compared to H&M's "Denim for the whole fam from $12.99" (7/10 in my queue), at least H&M puts a number in front of me even when it's a recycled sitewide %. Zara gives me nothing to anchor to.
- Compared to ASOS's Lioness drop, which had a specific styled moment I could picture wearing, this just reads like a product database dump with nice lighting.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the segmentation first** — if I'm flagged as a woman on this list, do not send me the men's lookbook. This is the single biggest miss; nothing else matters until this is fixed.
- 2. Put an actual offer or at least a "new drop" framing at the top — even "first to drop this season" trend-language would give me a reason to open beyond curiosity.
- 3. Replace flat section labels ("STREET FITS," "SELECTED PIECES") with trend-coded copy — tell me why this puffer is the one, not just that it exists.
- 4. Add at least one social-proof or styling cue (worn-by, styled-with) since Gen Z buys off "how do I wear this," not off a hanging product shot.
