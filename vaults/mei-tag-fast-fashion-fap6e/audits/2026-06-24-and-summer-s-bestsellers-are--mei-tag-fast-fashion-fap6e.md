# And summer's bestsellers are...
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- Okay so H&M is back and this is genuinely their eighth-or-ninth send in like six days — I've lost count at this point. But I'll be real: the subject actually stopped my scroll for half a second. "And summer's bestsellers are..." with the ellipsis is the most interesting thing H&M's put in a subject line all month. There's a cliffhanger there, a reveal format — it's the kind of hook that works on TikTok and it *almost* works in email.
- Then I open it and it's organized into tiers — ICONIC, ELITE, MOST-LOVED, PRESTIGE — which is structurally interesting but also kind of... pretentious? Like H&M calling their graphic white tees "Prestige" is a choice. The models are fine, the layout is clean, the editorial black-and-white palette feels more grown-up than their recent sale-blast emails. But there's no actual offer in the hero — just vibes and category labels. After seeing the 60% off hammer they've been running since June 16, suddenly pulling back to pure editorial feels like a bait-and-switch from the brand that trained me to expect a discount.
- I would not click. I'd maybe save it for my "trends to check Saturday" mental list, but I wouldn't reach for my card today. Definitely not forwarding — I forwarded the ASOS 10% off one to my group chat last week; this one doesn't have that "you need to see this" energy.

## What's working

- The subject line ellipsis is genuinely clever — "And summer's bestsellers are..." reads like a TikTok reveal video and it's the first H&M subject in this run that made me curious instead of annoyed
- The tier naming (ICONIC, ELITE, MOST-LOVED, PRESTIGE) gives the email a clear skeleton — your eye moves down naturally from one section to the next
- Editorial black-and-white palette is a visual step up from the pure sale-blast emails they've been sending all week; it actually feels like a brand moment
- Models are clearly in the right demographic range for me — young, styled casually but intentionally
- The colorful block near the bottom ("SEEN HERE CELEBRATED" area) breaks the monotony and adds a bit of that mood-board energy I associate with TikTok trend collages

## What's weak

- Zero concrete offer in the hero — after conditioning me with "Up to 60% off" for four consecutive days, going cold-turkey editorial without a single price point feels like a trap
- "Prestige" and "Elite" as tier names for H&M basics is aspirational in a way that doesn't land — Zara can get away with that framing; H&M doesn't have the same positioning to pull it off
- This is at minimum the seventh email from H&M in six days — my thumb is genuinely on autopilot to scroll past before I register the sender, which undercuts even a good subject line
- The top banner (appears to be promo text) is too small to read at mobile scale — if there's an offer buried up there, it's invisible to a fast-scroll inbox session
- No social proof, no "trending on TikTok," no urgency — for a bestsellers reveal, the payoff inside the email is quieter than the subject promised

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a real number in the hero** — "bestsellers" as a concept lands better when it's anchored with social proof or a price. Even "starting at $12" or "#1 bestseller this week" would give me a reason to act, not just browse.
- 2. **Cut the cadence to 3x/week max** — the single biggest drag on this email's performance isn't the creative, it's the fact that I've already seen seven sends from H&M this week. ASOS ran their sale follow-up two days after the initial send and it hit harder because it felt earned.
- 3. **Make the tier names actually mean something** — if ICONIC means "most-reordered," say that. If MOST-LOVED means "highest rated," show the stars. The names are just decorative right now.
- 4. **Lean into the TikTok angle** — "summer's bestsellers" is trending-content language; add a "seen on TikTok" callout or a view count-style social signal to justify the editorial reveal format.
- 5. **Surface the colorful collage block higher** — that "SEEN HERE CELEBRATED" color moment near the bottom is the most Gen Z thing in the whole email and it's buried after three scroll-lengths of monochrome.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `@import` for fonts (high risk):**
```css
<style>
  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
</style>
```
`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is stripped by Gmail (both webmail and Android app). Web fonts should be loaded via `<link rel="stylesheet">` in `<head>`, or the `@import` should be inside a dedicated style block that the email client won't touch. As-is, all recipients on Gmail fall back to system fonts silently.

**Issue — Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`:**
```html
<html style="border:0;margin:0;outline:0;padding:0">
```
No `lang="en"` (or locale equivalent). Screen readers cannot infer language; WCAG 2.1 § 3.1.1 requires it. Add `lang="en-US"` (or market-appropriate locale).

**No issues found:** Charset UTF-8 declared, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` present, `format-detection` for telephone suppression present, responsive breakpoint at 699px with `is-hidden-mobile`/`is-hidden-desktop` toggle pattern is correct.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Issue — Multiple empty JSON-LD fields (likely unresolved merge tokens):**

```json
{"@type":"DiscountOffer","availabilityEnds":"","discountCode":"","description":""}

```
Six fields are empty strings. `subjectLine` should match the email subject. `discountCode` and `description` are empty, which is acceptable if no promo applies — but `image` and `url` on `PromotionCard` being empty will break Gmail Promotions tab rendering entirely.

This is over 7 years in the past. This appears to be a default/placeholder value that was never updated. For Gmail's structured data to validate correctly, this field should either reflect the actual campaign start date or be omitted.
