# RSVP required: Up to 25% off + free shipping
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-08**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "RSVP required" is a weird subject line for an H&M sale email. There's no RSVP anywhere in the body. I opened it expecting some kind of event or invite and got the same tiered "spend more, save more" hero I've now seen three times. It's the 4th H&M promo in five days: 11/04 flash sale, 11/06 "Up to 25% off your order starts now," 11/07 "15%, 20% or 25% off all weekend", and now this. I'm numb to it.
- The hero is a sparkly black jacket on a model in moody black and white, and that part is pretty. But it's party-season glam, not "this is the trend." The "LAST DAY" label above the 15/20/25% tiers is the only thing that made me look twice. Below that it's a grab-bag grid: women's, men's, baby/kids, home. Nothing says "this is what's blowing up on TikTok," and that's what I care about.
- Would I click? Maybe "Shop Now" if I was already on my Saturday three-tab compare. Would I forward it to a friend? No. Mark as read.

## What's working

- The "15% OFF $75 / 20% OFF $100 / 25% OFF $125 + FREE SHIPPING" stack is huge and easy to scan, so I get the offer in about two seconds.
- "LAST DAY" gives real urgency, and it's believable since the sale has been running all week.
- The black-and-white hero with the sequin jacket looks good, like an editorial shoot, and the model is around my age.
- The section headers (WOMEN, MEN, BABY/KIDS, HOME) each have their own button, so I can jump straight to what I want.

## What's weak

- The subject says "RSVP required" but there's no RSVP anywhere. It's misleading and feels like a bait hook.
- It's the same sale for the fourth time in five days, and the 11/07 weekend email already had the identical tiers.
- The red banner at the top says "30% off select styles in-store," which competes with the 25% hero. Which deal is the real one?
- The legal fine print at the bottom is a giant wall of text in a tiny font. It sits right next to a "free shipping" claim, so it reads as buried conditions.
- Nothing here says "trending now." "'Tis the Season," "Polished Pieces," and "Ready to Shine" are generic holiday headings. Men's and kids' blocks take up half the email, and I'm not shopping for either.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the subject match the email. Drop "RSVP" or actually build an RSVP/early-access moment.
- 2. Collapse the 3-4 promo sends into one per week, or vary the creative. Right now the cadence is training me to ignore H&M.
- 3. Lead with one trend-forward product. Zara and ASOS (the Lioness drop) at least feel like they're showing me something new, while H&M just shows me a discount.
- 4. Cut the kids/men's rows for subscribers who only click women's.
- 5. Shrink the fine print and link out to the full terms.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Sequins are 25% off today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $125, save 25%. Ends tonight at midnight ET.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The party-season pieces everyone's saving, on sale until midnight.`

# Technical Audit: H&M "RSVP required: Up to 25% off + free shipping"

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML uses a conventional table-based layout with responsive CSS. However, all 12 probed tracked links returned HTTP 403 (16% pass rate), including the unsubscribe link. Most image `alt` attributes are missing, and the JSON-LD block is empty. The 403s are probably bot-blocking of the click-tracker, so they need confirming with a real client before being treated as outages (see section 2).

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **All 12 probed links returned 403.** The affected links go through the click-tracker `https://t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=h67159ba0-…&did=1419248132&rid=3326930539&erid=…`. They are:
  - "Shop now" (`…f8b46abe`, `…f8b46ac0`, `…f8b46a9c`, `…f8b46a9e`)
  - "Find a store" (`…f8b46abf`, `…f8b46ac1`)
  - the headline link "EARLY BLACK FRIDAY DEALS: 30% OFF SELECT STYLES IN-STORE*" (`…f8b46a9a`)
  - the "H&M" logo link (`…f8b46a9b`)
  - image links (`…f8b46abc`, `…f8b46abd`, `…f8b46ac2`)
  - Unsubscribe (`…f8b46afe`)
- **Likely cause:** the identical 403 on every link, unsubscribe included, points to the tracker rejecting non-browser or probe traffic rather than 12 separate broken destinations. This is inference, not confirmed. The final landing URLs could not be resolved, so destination health is unverified.
- **Hidden characters in link text:** two "Shop now" links contain zero-width or invisible characters (`"‌  ﻿Shop now"`). These can degrade screen-reader output and any text-based link matching.
- **Coverage gap:** only 12 of 76 links were probed (64 skipped, deprioritized as footer or social links), so those 64 are untested.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on 21 images.**
  - The product images are `cq5dam.web.1400.1400.jpeg` and `cq5dam.web.750.750.jpeg`, served from `image.hm.com/content/dam/hm/regional-local-activities/ame/2026-ame/wk-45/…`.
  - The two `t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=…` images also lack alt text.
  - Each product appears twice, as a 1400px and a 750px asset, which suggests a desktop/mobile image swap.
  - Because these images sit inside links, the link's accessible name falls back to the filename, as in the QA output `[image: cq5dam.web.1400.1400.jpeg]`.
- **Web fonts via `@import`:** `<style>@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/…/fonts.css); @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);</style>` is unsupported in Gmail and several other clients. Check that fallback font stacks are defined.
- **Display toggling:** the `.is-hidden-mobile` and `.is-hidden-desktop` classes and the `#MessageViewBody .hide-webkit / .show-webkit-table` rules are standard hacks. I found no defect in the visible source, but the source was truncated, so I cannot verify the fallback paths.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No issues found. The truncated source shows no unrendered merge tokens (no `{{ }}`, `%%…%%`, or `[FIRST_NAME]`).
- The JSON-LD block is unpopulated: `"subjectLine":""`, `"discountCode":""`, `"description":""`, `"image":""`, `"url":""`, and `"availabilityStarts":"2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"`. This looks like an unfilled template. Gmail annotations will not render, but nothing visible to subscribers breaks.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Unsubscribe link:** present in the body (`…f8b46afe&…&p4=3326930539&p5=9be06746…`). It returned 403 on probe, so it needs a manual test. If it fails for real users, that is a CAN-SPAM problem.
- **Missing headers:** `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found. The QA tool notes the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is unconfirmed. Verify against the raw message at the original receiving MTA.
- **Authentication:** the `Authentication-Results` header was not captured, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The sender is `newsletter@email.hm.com`.
- **Postal address:** not visible in the truncated source, so it could not be checked.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)

- **Landing pages:** the 403s prevented resolution, so landing page URLs and any UTM parameters appended after redirect could not be verified. Continuity is unverified, not confirmed broken.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Re-test the `t19.email.hm.com` links with a browser-like client or a real inbox. The unsubscribe link is the highest priority. Treat the 403s as an outage only if they reproduce there.
2. Add meaningful `alt` text to all 21 flagged images. For linked images, describe the destination or action. Use `alt=""` only for purely decorative ones.
3. Remove the zero-width characters from the "Shop now" link text.
4. Populate the JSON-LD (`subjectLine`, `discountCode`, `url`, `image`) or remove the block.
5. Confirm that `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` are present, and that SPF, DKIM and DMARC pass, using the raw original headers rather than the relay copy.
