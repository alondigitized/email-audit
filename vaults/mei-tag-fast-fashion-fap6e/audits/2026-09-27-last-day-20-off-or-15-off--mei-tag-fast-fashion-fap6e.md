# Last day: 20% off or 15% off
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-27**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "last day" urgency and then it's the exact same 15%/20% tiered-discount math H&M ran on 09-25 ("15% off or 20% off all weekend"), just with a countdown slapped on it now that the weekend's over. There's no hero image, no model, no trend story — just white space, a discount tier, and a giant "SELECT" category grid (Women/Men/Baby/Kids/Home) that reads more like a department store flyer than anything that's supposed to feel like it was made for me on a Saturday scroll.
- The product grid underneath is fine — new arrivals, clean grid, prices visible — but nothing in the top third tells me WHY any of this is trending right now. Compare that to ASOS's Lioness drop (5/10) which at least had a moody campaign shoot doing storytelling, or Zara's Alice in Wonderland collab (4/10) which was visually the most interesting thing any of these brands sent this week. This is H&M's fourth-ish variation on "percent off, act now" in a week and I'm numb to it.
- Would I click? Maybe scroll to Women out of habit since I'm already in the inbox, but I'm not opening this with any urgency, and I'm definitely not forwarding it — there's nothing "omg look at this" here to send a friend.

## What's working

- The "LAST DAY" + specific tiers (15% off $75 / 20% off $100) is at least concrete math, not vague "up to X% off" vagueness.
- Clean nav bar (Women/Men/Baby/Kids/Home) makes it easy to jump straight to my category without scrolling.
- Product grid has real prices and "New Arrival" tags visible, no broken images or lorem ipsum — this one at least renders clean.
- Sale banner at the very top duplicates the CTA early, so I don't have to hunt for it.

## What's weak

- Zero hero visual — no model, no styled shot, nothing that says "this is the look." Just text on white. Compare to the 09-24 H&M Studio send, which at least had that striking lace-veil close-up as a scroll-stopper.
- This is the same discount-tier format as 09-25's "15% or 20% all weekend" email, just recycled with new urgency copy. I already did this math four days ago.
- No trend language anywhere — no "TikTok's obsessed with," no styling angle, nothing that tells me this is a moment vs. inventory clearance.
- Cadence fatigue is real: this brand keeps hitting "percent off + deadline" (09-23 "freshly picked," 09-25 tiers, now this) with basically no creative variation.
- No social proof, no reviews, nothing showing real people wearing this — just product-on-white shots.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with an actual styled hero shot — one outfit, one model, one vibe — before the discount tiers. Sephora and even ASOS know the image sells the click, not the math.
- 2. Cut the tiered discount math ($75/$100 thresholds) down to one clear number or make it visually simpler — I shouldn't have to calculate which tier I land in from my phone.
- 3. Add ONE line of trend context — "the layered-knit look everyone's copying this fall" — something that makes this feel like H&M called it first, not chasing.
- 4. Space out the promo cadence — three tiered-discount emails in one week trains me to skim past the subject line entirely.

- **Subject Alt B:** `20% off $100, last call before it's gone`

- Redirect wrapper domain: `t19.email.hm.com` — 403 suggests either expired/invalid `erid`/`did`/`rid` tokens at send time, or QA-tool lacking session/referrer context the ESP requires. Given 12/12 probed links fail identically, this points to a systemic wrapper/token issue rather than isolated dead links.
