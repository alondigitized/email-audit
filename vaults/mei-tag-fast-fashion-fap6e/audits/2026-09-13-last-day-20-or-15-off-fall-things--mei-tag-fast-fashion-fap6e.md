# Last day: 20% or 15% off fall things
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-13**
## Executive summary

- Okay so this is the same tiered discount math I got yesterday ("15% off $75 or 20% off $100"), just now with a "LAST DAY" stamp slapped on top for urgency. It's literally the exact same offer structure, same threshold math, just recycled with a countdown vibe — which honestly makes it feel less like a real deadline and more like H&M panic-reposting the same email because the last one didn't convert. The hero guy in the suede jacket is fine, autumn-coded, but there's zero trend language here — no "serving looks" tennis energy like Wednesday's email, no "new classics" editorial mood, just a menswear model and a math problem.
- I'm not opening this expecting anything new, and I'm right not to — it's a straight rerun. Compared to ASOS's moody Lioness campaign shoot or even H&M's own tennis-core send from two days ago, this is the laziest possible creative: product grid, discount banner, category nav buttons. I'm not clicking, I'm definitely not forwarding this to anyone — there's nothing here worth telling a friend about, it's just "oh they're doing the discount thing again."

## What's working

- The hero jacket photo is clean and shot well — no render bugs, no weird cropping.
- Category nav buttons (Women/Men/Baby/Kids) are big, tappable, thumb-friendly.
- Product grid below is legible — price, name, "New Arrival" tag all visible without squinting.
- "LAST DAY" at least gives a reason to act now instead of just floating discount language forever.

## What's weak

- This is the identical tiered-discount offer from yesterday's email (2026-09-12, "15% off or 20% off all weekend") — same $75/$100 thresholds, just re-skinned with urgency copy. Feels like they're recycling, not campaigning.
- Zero trend framing. After the tennis-core and "new classics" trench editorials this week, going back to generic "fall lineup: bomber jackets, leather looks, timeless trenches" is a step backward in voice.
- The hero model is a guy in a basic jacket — nothing here signals "this was made with a 24-year-old girl in mind." My eye has nothing to latch onto emotionally.
- Math-before-shopping friction: I have to do "do I have $75 or $100 in my cart" arithmetic before I even see a product I want.
- "Last day" urgency feels manufactured since it's literally the same promo mechanics from the day before — doesn't read as credible scarcity.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the tiered-math framing entirely and lead with one clean number — "20% off everything" reads faster and converts better than a two-tier threshold puzzle.
- 2. Swap the hero to something with actual trend hooks tied to what's been working this week (the tennis-core or trench editorial energy) instead of a generic guy-in-a-jacket shot — consistency builds a "this feels like their thing" impression.
- 3. If it's genuinely the last day of the same promo, say so explicitly ("last chance on the deal from this weekend") instead of presenting it like a fresh drop — the disguise reads as filler, not urgency.
- 4. Add a countdown timer or explicit end-time (e.g., "ends tonight at midnight") to make "LAST DAY" feel real instead of decorative.
