# Sale: Bershka, Pull&Bear + more just landed
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- Okay so the second I saw "Bershka, Pull&Bear" in the subject I literally stopped mid-scroll — those are Inditex brands, same parent as Zara, and they have a whole different customer than ASOS's usual roster. That's actually a signal? Like ASOS is pulling in brands that know how to do trend-first drops, not just trend-adjacent basics. The hero hits clean: black background, "SALE: NEW BRANDS ADDED" in white, two buttons, done. No guessing required.
- But here's my problem: I just got "Extra 10% off all Sale" from ASOS yesterday, and "New arrivals" the day before that. I'm now on email five in three days from this sender. The open felt earned purely because Bershka and Pull&Bear are names I actually have opinions about — not because ASOS gave me a reason to care on top of the cadence fatigue. The specific sale percentage is buried in unreadable fine print under the hero and I still don't know what I'd actually be saving. Compare that to the June 22 "Up to 60% off Sale starts now" email that screamed the number at me — this one made me work for it.
- I'd click through to Bershka, probably not Pull&Bear. I wouldn't forward this — it's not "omg look at this" energy, it's "oh okay cool, brands landed." Mark as read with a tap into Bershka.

## What's working

- **Bershka and Pull&Bear brand names in the subject** — that's specific enough to stop a Gen Z trend-chaser mid-inbox. Most sale emails say "UP TO 50% OFF" and say nothing. This one named the thing.
- **Black hero with two-button split** — "SHOP WOMEN'S" vs "SHOP MEN'S" is clean and I knew immediately which lane I was in. Zara's June 22 sale email did a similar no-nonsense split and it worked there too.
- **Model casting is actually good** — diverse, the styling reads current (mini ruffled dresses, wide-leg pants in neutral tones, white bandeau top), feels like it was shot this season not recycled from 2024.
- **Three Inditex brands under one roof** — Bershka, Pull&Bear, Oysho in one email is almost a flex. Someone who shops Zara and has been curious about Bershka will immediately see the appeal.

## What's weak

- **Zero percentage callout in the hero** — the "SALE" means nothing if I don't know what discount I'm looking at. The fine print under the hero is genuinely unreadable; I'm not squinting at 8px text to find out what I'm saving.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTAs on each brand section are lazy** — H&M does this too and it's always a miss. "Shop Bershka Sale" or "See Bershka drops" would give me a reason to click that specific button instead of just defaulting to the women's hero.
- **Cadence is cooked** — five emails in three days from ASOS. This one landed a day after they already sent an "Extra 10% off" email. Bershka alone can't carry the goodwill needed to justify that frequency.
- **Oysho feels like a mismatch** — Bershka and Pull&Bear are Gen Z trend brands. Oysho is more lifestyle/lingerie. Burying it mid-email next to its own "SHOP NOW" makes the brand story muddy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the % in the hero** — "Bershka & Pull&Bear: up to X% off" or even "now in Sale" with the number. Literally one line. The June 22 send did this and it's why that email scored higher than this one.
- 2. **Replace "SHOP NOW" on brand sections with brand-specific action copy** — "Shop Bershka" / "Shop Pull&Bear" at minimum. If you can do "See Bershka's new drops," even better.
- 3. **Drop Oysho or give it its own dedicated send** — it doesn't fit the trend-chaser brief and it dilutes the Bershka/Pull&Bear story. One Inditex brand per email, or at least lead harder with the two headliners.
- 4. **Pull back one email per week** — not a creative fix, but this send would have landed harder if it wasn't the fifth touch in 72 hours.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — Malformed HTML structure**
As delivered, the document opens `<html><body>` with no `<head>` block. The `<meta>` tags (charset, viewport, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, `format-detection`) appear inside a `<body><p>` element:

```html
<!DOCTYPE html> <html><body> <div ...preheader...></div> <p>
  <meta http-equiv="Content-Type" ...>
  <meta charset="utf-8">
  <meta name="viewport" ...>
```

Meta tags inside `<body>` are invalid per HTML spec. While most email clients are lenient, this can cause viewport scaling to be ignored on iOS (the `width=device-width` meta may not be applied) and triggers validator failures. **Put all `<meta>` tags inside a proper `<head>` block.**

**Empty preheader slot**
```html
<div style="display:none !important;visibility:hidden;...font-size:1px;...overflow:hidden;"></div>
```
The preheader div has no text content. Inbox clients (Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail) will pull the first visible body text as the preview snippet instead of a crafted message.

**Ticker tape animation — client support gap**
The `@keyframes ticker` animation is gated with `@supports (animation: ticker 5s linear infinite)` which correctly hides the animated version in unsupporting clients via `.fallback`/`.ticker` class swap. However, `@supports` itself is not honored in Outlook (all versions) or older Samsung Mail. Verify the `.fallback` element renders acceptably as a static replacement in those clients.

**Mobile breakpoint at 460px**
```css
@media screen and (max-width:460px) { ... }
```
Standard email client breakpoints are typically 480px (Gmail app) or 600px. At 460–479px viewport width (common on some Android devices), the desktop layout will render without the responsive overrides. Consider bumping to `max-width:480px`.

**`font-size: 0em` on table elements**
```css
table, tr, td, th { font-size: 0em; ... }
```
This is a known whitespace-collapse hack for email. If any `<td>` renders inline text without an explicit `font-size` override on a child element, text will be invisible. Confirm all text-bearing cells have explicit `font-size` set.
