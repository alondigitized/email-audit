# What's hot this summer
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- Okay so ASOS landed in my inbox with "What's hot this summer" and my muscle memory said tap — same way it did with Zara's "Man | Explore our newest collections" email, which opened to literally just guys in linen. ASOS didn't gender-swap me like Zara did, but they pulled something almost as bad: I'm scrolling through a cute fringe dress, some summer whites, a Polo Ralph Lauren collab I can't even read — and then boom, New in Menswear. Hoodies and Adidas sneakers for men. In my women's email. Why.
- The hero is actually doing something. "You look hot" is cheeky and the teal fringe dress is genuinely trending right now. That image made me slow down. But then the email just… keeps going, section after section, all labeled "SHOP NOW," and by the time I hit the brand logo bar at the bottom I've completely forgotten what I was even feeling at the top. No offer, no deadline, no reason to tap today instead of next week.
- I'd click the hero maybe — purely because of the fringe dress. I wouldn't forward this. H&M's occasion dress email from this week had better editorial hierarchy even though it was aimed at a totally different person than me. This one hits the basics but feels like ASOS set it and forgot it.

## What's working

- **Teal fringe dress in the hero** — that's a real trend moment right now and it legitimately stopped my scroll; good creative choice
- **"You look hot" headline** — short, a little flirty, more personality than anything in the H&M "Welcome, Fashion Lover" welcome email which was just corporate monochrome nothing
- **Accessories quick-links bar** — "SANDALS | BAGS | SPF ESSENTIALS | SUNGLASSES" as tappable chips is exactly how I actually shop in summer; that section earned a tap from me
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum disasters; layout holds up visually throughout

## What's weak

- **Menswear section mid-scroll** — this is the Zara "Man" problem but slower; instead of opening to the wrong gender I just hit a wall of guys' hoodies two-thirds of the way through my women's email; ASOS should know better from behavioral data
- **Zero offer anywhere** — no percentage off, no free shipping, no member pricing; Shein would have a 30%-off banner above the fold and ASOS is asking me to browse at full price with no reason to click today vs. never
- **The Polo Ralph Lauren copy is illegible** — the body text under that section is tiny on mobile; I have no idea what the collab hook actually is without zooming in, which I'm not doing
- **"SHOP NOW" four times** — every single section has identical CTA copy; it tells me nothing about where I'm going and makes the email feel templated
- **Subject is invisible in a crowded inbox** — "What's hot this summer" is the generic summer promo subject line that every brand from Forever 21 to H&M sends in May; nothing here screams ASOS specifically

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment out the menswear block** — remove it from the women's send entirely; this single fix improves scroll depth and stops the "wait, is this email for me?" mid-read dropout
- 2. **Add an offer above the fold** — free next-day delivery this week, 20% off new arrivals, anything; right now there's no hook to buy today instead of saving for later
- 3. **Make CTAs section-specific** — "SHOP FRINGE NOW" / "SHOP THE POLO COLLAB" / "SHOP SUMMER BAGS" instead of four identical SHOP NOWs; specificity tells me where the link goes and drives the click
- 4. **Increase font size on the collab block** — the Polo Ralph Lauren copy is unreadable at mobile scale; if the collab deserves a section, give it a readable headline
- 5. **Anchor the subject to something ownable** — ASOS has trend data and a huge catalog; use it; the subject should feel like insider access, not a generic blast

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — `<meta>` tags inside `<p>` body tag:**
```html
<p>
  <meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8">
  <meta charset="utf-8">
  <meta name="viewport" ...>
  ...
</p>
```
Meta elements placed inside `<body>` within a `<p>` wrapper is invalid HTML. Most clients will silently handle it, but strict parsers (some webmail clients, Lotus Notes) may drop or misinterpret these directives. `<meta>` belongs in `<head>`.

**CSS selector bug — `*[class=nomob]` exact-match attribute selector:**
```css
*[class=nomob]  { display: block; }
*[class=nodesktop] { display: none; }
```
The attribute selector `[class=nomob]` only matches elements where the class attribute is exactly and solely `"nomob"`. Any element with multiple classes (e.g. `class="nomob container"`) will not match. This is a known ESP anti-pattern that silently breaks responsive hiding on multi-classed elements.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on body:**
```css
body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Using `none` disables font scaling entirely on iOS Safari. The correct value is `100%`, which prevents automatic resizing while still allowing user-controlled zoom. `none` will suppress both.

**`font-size: 0em` on all table elements:**
```css
table, tr, td, th { font-size: 0em; }
```
Standard gap-collapse technique, but any text content placed directly inside `<td>` without a child `<p>` or `<span>` will be invisible. Requires that all leaf-level content uses explicit font-size overrides.

**Empty preheader div:**
```html
<div style="display:none !important;visibility:hidden;..."></div>
```
The preheader container has no text content. Inbox clients (Gmail, Apple Mail) will fall through to the first visible body text as preview copy. This is either intentional (unusual) or a missing merge token.

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`:**
No `lang="en"` or locale equivalent. Screen readers default to the OS language; assistive tech cannot confirm document language for correct pronunciation rules.

**Duplicate charset declarations:**
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8">
<meta charset="utf-8">
```
Redundant but harmless. Minor bloat.

**SVG font format included in `@font-face`:**
```css
url("FuturaPT-Book.svg") format("svg")
```
SVG fonts are deprecated and unsupported in all modern browsers and email clients. Dead weight in the CSS payload (3 `@font-face` declarations × 5 formats each = 15 format entries, 3 of which are no-ops).
