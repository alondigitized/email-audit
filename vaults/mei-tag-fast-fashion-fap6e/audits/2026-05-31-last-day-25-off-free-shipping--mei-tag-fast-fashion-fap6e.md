# Last day: 25% off + free shipping
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-31**
## Executive summary

- OK so here we are — I've lost count, but this is somewhere around the eighth H&M email in five days. The May 30 send was literally also "25% off your entire order," so when I saw "Last day: 25% off + free shipping" I had a brief moment of genuine deja vu before I realized H&M is basically speedrunning my unsubscribe button. That said, "Last day" still triggers something in me. I opened it.
- Inside, the offer is real and the layout is clean: 25% off $125+ stacked with free shipping, with actual new arrivals in the women's section — that $9.99 Bandeau Dress and the $19.99 Flared Denim Dress are the kind of prices that make you stop scrolling. But then I kept scrolling. MEN section. KIDS section. Another SHOP BY grid. By the time I got back to anything relevant to me, I'd mentally checked out.
- Verdict: I'd screenshot the Bandeau Dress price, close the email, and not click through. Definitely not forwarding — everyone on my contacts list has already received the same seven emails from H&M this week.

## What's working

- "DON'T WAIT" + "Last day" stacked on the hero is peak deadline psychology — even exhausted, I responded to it
- Both benefits (25% off AND free shipping) are visible in the hero without scrolling — that double-stack reads as a real deal, not a fake-out
- The "ENDLESS SUN" women's section leads with "New Arrival" labels in red, which signals trend-freshness rather than clearance-dump
- Visual hierarchy is tight — offer sits at the top, products cascade below with no visual noise in between
- The sale pricing on the women's items ($9.99, $19.99 for on-trend summer pieces) is genuinely competitive — that Bandeau Dress rivals what Shein charges for the same silhouette

## What's weak

- The $125+ threshold is doing quiet damage — the hero reads "25% OFF" big, then "$125+" small, and I didn't fully absorb the cart minimum until I'd already started calculating items
- MEN and KIDS sections take up more than half the scroll length in what should be MY email; I have no kids and I'm not shopping for my boyfriend's closet
- This is the second "25% off" email in two days from H&M — the May 30 send was also "25% off your entire order," so "last day" feels like a last day of an offer they literally just sent me yesterday
- The SHOP BY grid (WOMEN / MEN / KIDS / HOME / BEAUTY tiles) at the bottom adds a full scroll of pure navigation — this is email real estate wasted on a site menu
- No trend narrative anywhere — compare to the PUKAS x ZARA send I opened last week, which gave me a reason to care before showing me price; this one just says "June days and getaways" and calls it editorial

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the list and kill MEN + KIDS from this send.** ASOS does this — when they run the Cotton On co-branded emails (chaotic as those are), at least the product is curated toward a persona. H&M sending me a full family catalog in 2026 feels like they're still using 2015 batch-and-blast logic.
- 2. **Lead with a trend concept, then reveal the discount as the reward.** Flip the current hierarchy: open with "ENDLESS SUN just dropped — flared denim, bandeau tops, linen sets." Then: "And today only: 25% off $125+ + free shipping." The sale becomes validation for caring about the trend, not the only reason to open.
- 3. **Surface the $125 threshold more honestly.** Either a visual progress mechanic ("Add $X to unlock 25% off") or just call it out plainly in the hero: "Spend $125, save 25% — plus free shipping on everything." Hiding the math in a "+" is how you lose trust after the click.
- 4. **Cut the SHOP BY grid entirely.** The top-nav already has WOMEN / MEN / KIDS / HOME. The tile grid is a redundant navigation element that adds scroll without adding value — replace it with "New this week →" linking to a curated landing page.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Final hours: new arrivals 25% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer haul — 25% off, ships free today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Flared denim, bandeau tops, linen sets — last chance to save`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Spend $125, keep 25% — plus free shipping right now`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS `@import` font loading will fail silently in most clients:**

```html
<style>
  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
</style>
```

Gmail strips `@import` rules entirely. Outlook ignores external CSS. Apple Mail may load them, but external font requests are blocked in privacy-relay scenarios. Both calls will silently fall back to system fonts — acceptable only if fallback stacks are defined (unverifiable from truncated source).

**`format-detection` is scoped only to telephone numbers:**

```html
<meta content="telephone=no" name="format-detection"/>
```

iOS also auto-links dates, addresses, and email addresses. Add `date=no, address=no, email=no` to suppress all auto-detection:

```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no, date=no, address=no, email=no"/>
```

**Alt text cannot be confirmed** — image tags are not visible in the provided source. Verify all `<img>` have non-empty `alt` attributes; decorative images should use `alt=""` with `role="presentation"`.
