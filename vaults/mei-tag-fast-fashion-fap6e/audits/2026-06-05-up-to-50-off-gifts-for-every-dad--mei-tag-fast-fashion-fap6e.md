# Up to 50% off gifts for every dad
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so I opened this and immediately clocked it: Father's Day gifting email from H&M. And like... I'm a 24-year-old woman who shops for herself, not for her dad's cargo shorts. The hero screams "up to 50% off all menswear" in big bold type which is a clear, concrete offer — I'll give them that — but every single model, every single product, every single CTA is directed at middle-aged men wearing linen resort shirts and cargo pants. There is genuinely nothing here that speaks to me as the person who might *buy* the gift. No "treat your dad" framing that centers my experience as the gift-giver. No women's fashion crossover. No styling inspo of how I'd put together a dad gift.
- The member giveaway section in the middle — win a $250 gift card — is the one moment I actually stopped scrolling. That pulled me back in briefly. But the rest of the email is just a product grid for a demographic I don't dress. Compare this to yesterday's Solar Club send, which had actual creative energy and spoke to *my* movement aesthetic — this feels like H&M switched departments and forgot who was reading.
- Would I click? Maybe the giveaway, honestly. I'd click "Join Now" for the $250 gift card entry, then immediately bounce. I would not forward this to a friend. I might forward it to my dad, which tells you everything about the targeting miss here.

## What's working

- **Big, clear offer up top** — "Up to 50% off ALL Menswear" is unambiguous and concrete. No mystery discount, no buried fine print visible in the hero.
- **Member giveaway hook** — "$250 Gift Card" giveaway for Father's Day is actually a smart retention play and it's the most compelling creative moment in the email.
- **Clean product grid layout** — No render bugs, no broken images, products are labeled with prices and discounts legible at a glance.
- **No spam vibes on the header** — "SHOP NOW" and "SALE" nav chips are clean and the H&M branding is immediately recognizable.

## What's weak

- **Zero effort to speak to the gifter** — I'm the buyer here but the entire email talks *at* a man about his own clothes. ASOS's recent "Summer pieces everyone's wearing" at least gestured toward a lifestyle frame. This is just a men's sale catalog.
- **Product selection is deeply un-me** — Cargo shorts, linen resort shirts, printed polo tees. None of this is trend-forward — it's department store dad-core. H&M MOVE from yesterday felt like a brand with a pulse; this feels like a different brand entirely.
- **No urgency or deadline anywhere visible** — Father's Day is presumably this weekend but there's no date, no countdown, nothing that says "order by X." That's a real miss for a time-sensitive gifting window.
- **The giveaway copy is too small** — "WIN A SURPRISE BUNDLE & $250 GIFT CARD" is buried halfway down after a wall of cargo shorts. That should be hero-level.
- **"Shop By" section at the bottom is generic filler** — Women / Men / Kids / Home / Beauty chips add nothing when this email has already committed 100% to menswear gifting.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the gift-giver angle, not the product.** Reframe the hero as "Find something Dad will actually wear" and show a diverse range of gifting price points ($20–$80), not a men's-clearance-style dump. One strong hero gift edit, not a grid of six cargo options.
- 2. **Promote the $250 giveaway to the top half.** This is the most engaging thing in the email — move it above the fold or make it a sticky hero bar. Right now it's hidden behind five product rows.
- 3. **Add a shipping deadline.** "Order by June 14 for Father's Day delivery" — visible, credible, creates urgency. Zara's recent Caramel London drop at least had a clear campaign window.
- 4. **Show one women's-perspective shot** — a woman handing a gift bag, or a styled flat-lay of the products as a gift. Re-anchor the email to me as the buyer.
- 5. **Tie back to Solar Club / MOVE** — if Dad is active, the crossover pitch from yesterday's Solar Club send writes itself. "Dad runs too." One sentence, one link.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dad's got 50% off — pick something he'll actually keep`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Father's Day gifts, starting at $[X] — up to 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Order by Sunday for guaranteed delivery — browse the full men's edit`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus: enter to win a $250 H&M gift card for the dad who has everything`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS `@import` for web fonts will fail silently in most clients.**

```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```

Gmail strips `@import` directives entirely. Outlook ignores them. iOS Mail and Apple Mail are the only major clients that honor `@import` inside `<style>`. Fallback system fonts will render instead. This is an accepted trade-off only if the design degrades gracefully — verify the fallback font stack is explicitly declared on body/td elements.

**Positive signals present:**
- `<meta name="x-apple-disable-message-reformatting"/>` — correct
- `<meta content="telephone=no" name="format-detection"/>` — correct
- `@media (max-width:699px)` responsive breakpoint with `is-hidden-mobile`/`is-hidden-desktop` classes — correct pattern

**Alt text:** Cannot audit — body content is truncated.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

Multiple fields in the JSON-LD block (`<script type="application/ld+json">`) appear to be unfilled template placeholders:

| Field | Value | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| `EmailMessage.subjectLine` | `""` | Empty — should match subject line |
| `DiscountOffer.description` | `""` | Empty |
| `DiscountOffer.discountCode` | `""` | Empty |
| `DiscountOffer.availabilityEnds` | `""` | No expiry set |
| `PromotionCard.image` | `""` | Empty |
| `PromotionCard.url` | `""` | Empty |

**Critical: stale hardcoded date.**
```json
"availabilityStarts":"2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"
```
This is a 2018 timestamp on a `DiscountOffer` object, almost certainly a template default that was never populated. Gmail uses this block to render promotional tab annotations and deal highlights. An `availabilityStarts` nearly 8 years in the past will cause Gmail to suppress or mispresent the annotation entirely.

**Minor:** All `@context` values use `http://schema.org/` (HTTP, not HTTPS). Google's structured data documentation recommends `https://schema.org/`.
