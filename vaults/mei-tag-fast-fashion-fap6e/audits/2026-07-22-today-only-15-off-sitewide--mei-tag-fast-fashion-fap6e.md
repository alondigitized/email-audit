# Today only: 15% off sitewide
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- OK so I need to be real with you: H&M has been absolutely flooding my inbox this week. The big 50% off sitewide sale ran for like four or five days straight, and now three days later they're sliding into my Wednesday with "today only: 15% off." That's not a flash sale — that's a markdown on a sale that already ended, and I feel it. The math hits different when I'm sitting here knowing I could've saved way more seventy-two hours ago.
- That said, the email itself is clean. The offer is impossible to miss — "15% OFF SITEWIDE" is the biggest thing on the page and the "TODAY ONLY" urgency reads fast on a phone. The thing that actually caught my eye was the little banner at the very top: "TAX-FREE DAYS ARE COMING." That's genuinely interesting. That's forward momentum. Everything below it though is just... basic summer basics.
- Would I click? Maybe if I already had tabs open. Would I forward to a friend? No. This is a "mark as read and move on" email unless I was already planning a cart checkout.

## What's working

- **The TAX-FREE DAYS ARE COMING banner** is the sleeper hit here — it gives me a reason to keep watching H&M's inbox, which is exactly what a brand should do after a sale window closes
- **"15% OFF SITEWIDE" as the hero** is massive, unambiguous, and doesn't make me guess — that clarity is doing the heavy lifting
- **New Arrival labels on every product** signals freshness, not clearance dumping — I'm actually less annoyed by the discount because the product feels current
- **No render bugs** — everything loaded clean, which honestly ASOS fumbles more than H&M
- **"TODAY ONLY" + "Treat yourself to a mid-week pick-me-up"** is at least a coherent narrative, even if it's generic
- **15% off after 50% off is a vibe killer** — I opened two H&M emails on July 19th that both screamed "up to 50% off sitewide ends tonight," so dropping to 15% three days later feels like they're testing my memory
- **None of these products are trend signals** — relaxed-fit printed tees, an appliquéd cotton tee, a ruffle-trimmed set: I'm not running to TikTok to show any of this; ASOS's "Fringe is in" editorial from last week felt more like a trend brief than an inbox coupon
- **No CTA button in the hero** — just nav links at the top; Zara at least commits to a single-click destination even when their editorial is confusing (looking at you, Katharine Pole cushions email)
- **Baby/Kids/Home/Beauty in the category bar** wastes my eyeballs — I'm 24 and shopping for me, not a nursery
- **"Treat yourself to a mid-week pick-me-up"** is copy a brand automation tool wrote at 2am; it connects to nothing in my life
