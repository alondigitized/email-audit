# 20% off + the long weekend starts now
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- Okay so H&M is back — this has to be email fifteen at this point, I genuinely stopped counting somewhere around send twelve when they were hammering 70% off like the building was on fire. And now we've stepped *back* to 20% off sitewide, which made me do an actual double-take. Yesterday was 70% off, today it's 20%? Is the big sale over? Is this on top of it? The math isn't adding up and I am not doing homework to figure it out.
- The actual email looks clean though. The hero shot — a tall model in this black graphic patterned maxi dress — is genuinely editorial and I immediately want it. "THIS IS THE BEST PART" as copy is doing its job. But then I scroll and there's a full men's section, then what looks like home stuff with woven textures, and suddenly the "sitewide" in 20% off sitewide starts feeling less exciting because I'm sharing my discount with throw pillows and menswear I will never buy.
- I'd click through to the women's section. The dress got me. But I wouldn't forward this — it reads like an email I've already received, and my friends are swimming in the same H&M volume I am at this point. Click, maybe buy the dress, archive immediately.

## What's working

- **The hero dress is genuinely editorial** — that black graphic print maxi is the kind of thing I'd open three tabs for to compare across Zara and ASOS right now
- **"20% OFF SITEWIDE" is clean and unambiguous** — no "up to," no visible fine print asterisk, just a flat number at the top
- **"THIS IS THE BEST PART" as copy lands** — confident and editorial, not "explore our new arrivals" corporate-speak
- **Long weekend hook is timely** — the July 4th framing gives this email a real reason to exist rather than "we have inventory and a send button"

## What's weak

- **20% off after 70% off yesterday is genuinely confusing** — the July 1st send was screaming 70% off and new styles added; today's 20% off reads like a step backward even if they're structurally different offers
- **Men's section and home content dilutes everything** — I have to mentally filter past beige linen menswear and textured baskets to get back to the dress I actually want
- **CTAs are tiny text links with no visible button shape** — on mobile, those small text links below each section are not easy tap targets; compare this to ASOS which anchors a clear bordered button under each editorial block
- **Cadence has hit a wall** — even with a good offer, this is the 12th+ send in under two weeks and the "long weekend starts now" hook doesn't feel meaningfully different from the last several H&M emails I've reviewed

## Recommendations

- 1. **Flip the hierarchy — open with the dress, then the offer** — lead with the hero product image, drop "20% off this weekend" as supporting copy underneath. ASOS editorial emails feel like content before they feel like ads; this is the opposite.
- 2. **Clarify the 70% → 20% relationship in a one-line banner** — something like "70% off sale still running + 20% off everything new this weekend." Right now it reads as a downgrade.
- 3. **Cut or segment the men's and home sections** — or send them separately. Zara gets roasted for sending me kids' looks (I have no children); H&M is making me scroll past menswear and home decor when I just want the dress.
- 4. **Make CTAs actual buttons** — bordered, tappable, legible on mobile. Text ghost links are invisible on a commute.
- 5. **Name the hero product and price it** — "Patterned Printed Maxi Dress — $49.99, now $39.99" turns the hero from inspiration to a cart item.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Long weekend = 20% off, including that dress`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New styles drop + 20% off sitewide — long weekend only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping on $X+ · Ends Monday · Shop women's new arrivals`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `70% off sale still on + 20% off everything new this weekend`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**External font `@import` — high risk of blocking**

```html
<style>
  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
</style>
```

`@import` inside `<style>` is stripped by Gmail (web), Outlook (all versions), and Apple Mail on iOS in some modes. Fallback font rendering will apply across a large portion of the audience.

**Duplicate CSS rule declarations in the mobile media query**

The following selectors are declared twice inside `@media (max-width:699px)`, which is a sign of template/partial concatenation error and adds unnecessary weight:

- `.department-title` (lines ~53 and ~58 in the mobile block)
- `.cta-link-mobile-border-ns` (declared twice consecutively)
- `.cta-link-mobile, .set_font_interactive_standalone_link_default` (declared twice consecutively)
- `.set_font_heading_xlarge` (three occurrences in the mobile block)
- `.set_font_body_text_preamble` (declared twice)
- `.set_font_heading_xsmall` (declared twice)

**Non-standard Yahoo Mail media query**

```css
@media screen yahoo { .show-webkit-table { display: none; } }
```

`@media screen yahoo` is a non-standard, undocumented hack. Its behavior is undefined and inconsistent; Yahoo's rendering engine may silently ignore or misparse it.
