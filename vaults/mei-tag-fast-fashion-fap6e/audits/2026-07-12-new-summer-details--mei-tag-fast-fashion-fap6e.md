# New summer details
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- OK so context matters here: H&M has been sending me an email basically every single day this week — 70% off, price drops from $6.99, the flash sale, the "city summer wardrobe" editorial — and "New summer details" sounded like it could be accessories, styling, maybe some cute little trend capsule drop. I opened it ready to be a fashion girlie. And I got straw coasters.
- This is the exact same energy as that Zara "Man" email — the one where I opened it expecting women's and got linen trousers for guys. Wrong segment, wrong content, total miss. H&M Home is a real thing and maybe someone loves it, but that someone is not a 24-year-old who does three-tab Saturdays comparing Shein vs. ASOS vs. Zara on who shipped the trend first. The email technically renders clean — layout is fine, prices are readable, no broken images — but if I wanted linen curtains and hurricane candle holders I'd be on Pinterest, not clicking H&M marketing at noon on a Saturday.
- The back-to-school giveaway mid-scroll is also a vibe-killer. It's July. We're looking at straw coasters and curtain panels and then BAM, jeans-and-white-shirt model, "WIN A $250 GIFT CARD," sign in or join now. Editorial whiplash. This reads like two campaigns stitched together by someone who missed a deadline. I would mark as read, not click, not forward — and honestly I'm now thinking about hitting the "manage preferences" link at the bottom.

## What's working

- Sale banner is the very first thing you see — "SALE UP TO 70% OFF + STYLES UNDER $6.99" — the price hook is immediate and doesn't make me scroll
- Product prices are clearly labeled throughout ($6.99 coasters, $69.99 curtains) — no fine print games, no "starting at" ambiguity
- "New Arrival" tags on the cushion cover and curtains give me a sense of what's actually fresh vs. clearance
- Email renders completely clean — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum — execution quality is genuinely high
- The SHOP BY grid at the bottom (Women / Men / Kids / Home / Beauty) gives me an escape hatch to the stuff I actually came for

## What's weak

- "New summer details" totally obscures that this is a home email — felt like a bait-and-switch the moment the leopard-print throw and coasters loaded
- Every primary content block — tumblers, cushion covers, coasters, linen curtains, candle holders — is H&M Home, which is not my category at all; this should have landed in a different segment's inbox
- The Back-to-School Bundle giveaway is jarring mid-email — two totally different campaign vibes with zero editorial bridge between "summer home refresh" and "win a $250 gift card for school"
- This is effectively the fifth H&M email in five days; the daily-send cadence is starting to desensitize me, and a pivot to home decor on day five makes the noise worse, not better

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment home buyers from fashion buyers.** I have never bought a H&M Home item. This email should not have landed in my inbox. ASOS manages to surface the 30% sale on categories I actually browse — H&M has the same data and isn't using it.
- 2. **Be honest in the subject line.** "New summer home drops" or even just "H&M Home: starting at $6.99" — give me the category signal before I open. "New summer details" is deceptive-by-omission when the email is entirely home goods.
- 3. **Pull the back-to-school giveaway into its own dedicated email.** It's a genuinely strong member hook (a $250 gift card is real money), but it dies buried mid-scroll in a cushion-cover email.
- 4. **If H&M wants cross-sell between home and fashion**, open on a fashion hero with a secondary "also loving for your space →" module — don't lead with linen curtains to a fashion-list subscriber.
- 5. **Ease off the daily cadence.** Five emails in five days means even when the offer is real I'm starting to tune out. Yesterday's 70% off new arrivals was a better email than this — that send deserved space to breathe.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading (high risk):**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is blocked by Outlook (all versions), Gmail (non-Googlemail), and Apple Mail on iOS in some configurations. Web fonts will silently fall back with no error — acceptable if fallback fonts are defined, but no fallback stack is visible in the truncated CSS.

**Non-standard Yahoo media query:**
```css
@media screen yahoo { ... }
```
The correct Yahoo Mail hack is `@media yahoo { ... }` (without `screen`). The `screen` qualifier causes this rule to be ignored in Yahoo Mail, defeating its intended override.

**Duplicate CSS rules** — The following selectors appear twice in the `optimize-css` block with identical declarations:
- `.cta-link-mobile-border-ns { border: 1px solid #000 !important; }`
- `.cta-link-mobile, .set_font_interactive_standalone_link_default { line-height:17px!important; font-size:14px!important; }`
- `.flexible-elements-secondary-text-block-headline, ...preamble { width:327px!important; }`
- `.flexible-elements-secondary-text-block-headline-text { font-size:24px!important; ... }`

No functional breakage, but indicates the stylesheet is generated/concatenated without dedup — inflates email size (counts toward 102KB Gmail clipping threshold).

**Missing `<title>` tag** — Not present in `<head>`. Some clients display the title in tab/window chrome; absence is a minor but confirmed gap.

**Alt text on images** — Cannot verify; image tags not visible in truncated source.
