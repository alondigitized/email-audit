# What's trending?
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so this is email number EIGHT from ASOS in roughly ten days. Eight. I had to stop and count because at some point they all blur together. The ironic thing? The content here is actually... fine? "Shop What's Trending" with a numbered grid — festival, sundresses, sequins, co-ords — those are legitimate trend drops that feel current. The SPF face + body section even signals they know where TikTok discourse lives right now. I genuinely paused on the sequins tile.
- But here's the problem: no offer. None. Three days ago ASOS was dangling "up to 30% off" at me and now it's pure editorial with a "Download the App" button as the main CTA. I already have the app, and I definitely didn't click through just to get a tutorial on knitted warm-weather styles — in late May, going into June. That section had no business being in this email.
- I would scroll the trend grid, vaguely clock the festival and co-ord tiles, and then close it. I would not click. I would not forward it to anyone. If a friend texted me "did you see the ASOS email?" I'd say "which one, they sent like three this week."

## What's working

- **Trend grid is on-brief** — Festival, sundresses, sequins, co-ords: four actual TikTok-adjacent trend buckets, numbered and scannable. This is how I think about trends, so the format clicks.
- **SPF section shows cultural awareness** — SPF discourse is massive on TikTok right now; including a face + body SPF section signals ASOS is paying attention, not just recycling last season's template.
- **Model casting reads authentic** — Looks young, diverse, not overly editorial-glossy. Feels like content I'd scroll past on Instagram and not flinch at.
- **No render bugs** — Clean layout, no broken images or overlapping sections. It just... works visually.
- **Visual hierarchy is clear** — Numbered boxes lead the eye, sections break logically. Easy to skim on mobile.

## What's weak

- **Cadence is completely out of control** — This is email eight in ten days. The previous "Going somewhere?" and "New arrivals" back-to-back on May 26 already had me side-eyeing my unsubscribe button. Today I'm hovering over it.
- **No offer after an offer-heavy stretch** — You hit me with 30% off on the 25th, and now I'm supposed to care about editorial content with no deal attached? The ask-to-give ratio is completely off.
- **"Download the App" as the primary CTA is a miss** — That's not why I opened a trends email. That yellow button at the bottom is doing nothing for me; I want to shop the grid, not re-download an app I already have.
- **"Knitted styles are warm-weather approved" section** — It's May 28. Heading into June. Why is this here? H&M just ran a full editorial on summer editorial looks and even *they* didn't try to push knitwear at me right now.
- **Subject line is totally anonymous** — "What's trending?" could have come from Shein, Zara, H&M, anyone. There's no ASOS fingerprint on that subject line. Compare to the May 26 "Going somewhere?" — that had personality.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the email frequency immediately** — 8 in 10 days is actively training subscribers to ignore you. Batch the editorial and the sale into the same send or go to twice a week max.
- 2. **Make the subject own a specific trend** — Don't ask me what's trending, TELL me. Something like "Sequins are a summer thing now" or "Festival-to-festival: the 4 looks" puts a stake in the ground and sounds like ASOS has an opinion.
- 3. **Replace "Download the App" with a direct shopping CTA** — "Shop all 4 trends" or "See what dropped this week" tied to the trend grid would convert infinitely better than an app download prompt inside an already-opened email.
- 4. **Cut the knitted styles section** — Dead weight in a summer email. Use that real estate for SPF product specifics or trend editorial with actual product cards.
- 5. **Add a "new this week" timestamp or trend origin cue** — As someone who's comparing three tabs every Saturday, I want to know: did these land TODAY or is this last month's assortment wearing a trending hat?

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `<body>`** (line: `body, #body_style { … -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }`). The `none` value suppresses all font scaling in older WebKit mobile browsers. The safer value is `100%`, which prevents auto-scaling while preserving user zoom. The `*` rule later sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%` but body-level `none` may win due to specificity.

- **`font-size: 0em` on all table elements** (`table, tr, td, th { … font-size: 0em; }`). Blanket zero font-size on `<tr>` and `<th>` kills whitespace gaps but breaks any cell that doesn't explicitly reset font-size — screen reader text and VML fallback copy inside tables will be invisible/zero-sized if not individually overridden.

- **SVG font format declared** in both `@font-face` blocks (`format("svg")`). SVG fonts are deprecated; last relevant client was iOS ≤ 4.1 (2010). Dead weight in the source — remove to reduce parse time and payload.

- **`role="presentation"` not visible** on layout tables in the `<head>` or opening `<body>` markup. Without this, screen readers may attempt to interpret table structure as data. Full source needed to confirm whether it appears on outer wrapper tables.

- **No `alt` text visible** on any `<img>` tags in the available source. Images are set to `display: block` globally but alt attributes are not confirmed. If images are missing alt text, image-blocking clients (Outlook default, corporate proxies) will render blank spaces with no context.

- **Media query breakpoint at 460px** — ASOS uses 460px rather than the more common 480px. Not a bug, but worth flagging if third-party ESPs or rendering test suites use 480px as the mobile boundary.
