# Get the look: @triszyk in LOTTO H&M
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-01**
## Executive summary

- **From:** H&M · **Subject:** Get the look: @triszyk in LOTTO H&M
- Okay so I genuinely did a double-take when I opened this. LOTTO x H&M? That's actually a collab worth a second look — Lotto is one of those retro Italian sportswear labels that feels like it belongs on a Pinterest board from two years ago that's somehow cycling back into real trend territory right now. The creator hook with @triszyk is smart too, because "get the look" framing is exactly how I consume styling inspo on TikTok. The hero shot is clean, the product grid is organized, and the whole thing has this understated sporty energy I can respect.
- BUT. And this is a massive but — this email is for men. Every single model in the product grid is male. Every product listed — Loose-Fit Soccer Jersey, Relaxed-Fit Football Shorts, Relaxed-Fit Coated Shorts — is clearly menswear. I'm a 24-year-old woman who H&M presumably knows is subscribed and buying women's fashion. Sending me a full men's drop with zero women's product in the hero or grid feels like they pulled the wrong segment. It's not even ambiguous "unisex oversized" styling — it's men's soccer cuts modeled by men.
- On top of that, this is at minimum my second H&M email today (the summer layering one already hit this morning) and probably my ninth or tenth this week. The collab is cool enough that I'll mark it as read instead of deleting it, but I wouldn't click to shop, and I definitely wouldn't forward it — unless I was sending it to my boyfriend.

## What's working

- **The collab branding is legitimately interesting.** LOTTO x H&M is the kind of retro European sportswear play that actually has cultural momentum right now — this doesn't feel chasing, it feels like H&M caught a wave.
- **Creator positioning landed.** "Get the look: @triszyk" is cleaner than the generic "Shop now" language from H&M's last six sends. Using a real creator handle in the subject is the right instinct.
- **Hero image is editorial, not catalog.** The staircase shot with the Lotto jersey has a genuine lookbook feel — it's not just a product on a white background.
- **Product grid is organized with prices and "New Arrival" tags visible.** I can see what things cost ($14.99–$59.99 range) without clicking, which is rare for H&M lately.
- **Layout renders clean.** No broken images, no lorem ipsum, no layout weirdness — everything stacked correctly.

## What's weak

- **This is a men's email sent to a women's subscriber.** Every model, every cut, every product name reads male. There's a "WOMEN'S" link buried in the "SHOP BY" nav at the bottom, but that's not a women's email — that's a nav link.
- **Zero women's product shown anywhere in the hero or grid.** Even if H&M wanted to cross-sell the collab, one oversized women's option or a female model wearing the jersey streetwear-style would've made this relevant to me.
- **No discount offer, no urgency.** After eight days of "25% off," "last day," "free shipping" — dropping back to full price with zero offer context feels jarring. I don't know if this is a limited drop or just new inventory.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is completely generic.** The email earns a creator collab frame and then sends me to... shop now. Compare this to how ASOS structures their Cotton On collab send — they at least called out the collection name as a CTA label. H&M went flat here.
- **Cadence is cooked.** This is the second email today from a brand that's sent me almost ten in the last week. Even if the content is good, I'm filtering on autopilot at this point.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this properly.** This should only land in inboxes of subscribers who've browsed or bought menswear — or at minimum, include one women's Lotto H&M item. Right now it's misfiring on half the list.
- 2. **Add a women's anchor.** Even one "shop the women's version" or a female creator wearing the jersey oversized would flip this from a men's email to a unisex collab email. Gen Z shops across gender — lean into that.
- 3. **Give "SHOP NOW" a job.** Change it to "Shop the Lotto Drop" or "Get the Look" — match the subject energy. "SHOP NOW" is what old H&M did.
- 4. **Add a scarcity or drop signal.** Is this a limited collab? How long is it available? "New Arrival" tags are fine but don't create any pull. Even "available while sizes last" would help.
- 5. **Cool the send cadence.** Two emails in one day is inbox abuse. Consolidate.
