# Warm-weather wardrobe heroes
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- Okay so H&M is back in my inbox and honestly the actual *email* here is kind of nice? The hero model has the right vibe — that botanical-print crop set is giving summer-in-the-south-of-France energy, the whites and eyelet throughout are genuinely on-trend, and the product grid is cleanly organized. "Seasonal Essentials" as a concept works. I can see the individual pieces with prices and New Arrival labels, and those eyelet bermuda shorts? I actually want them.
- BUT. Here's the thing. Yesterday H&M was literally screaming 70% OFF at me. I opened that one ready to checkout. And now, literally the next day, we're pivoting to full-price new arrivals with zero urgency and zero discount? That whiplash is real. My brain registered "H&M" in the sender field and *expected* a sale extension or something concrete. Getting a mood board instead feels like a bait-and-switch on the send cadence, not on an individual offer. This is like send fifteen or sixteen in two weeks — I genuinely stopped counting — and switching to editorial mode this deep into a sale run feels off.
- I'd probably click one of the product tiles because the eyelet pieces are legitimately cute and in my wheelhouse, but I wouldn't forward this. It's not "drop everything and look at this" energy. It's "add to cart quietly on Saturday morning" energy at best. Mark as read with occasional tab.

## What's working

- **The hero is aesthetically right for me** — botanical print crop, floaty silhouette, the model reads young and cool, not aspirational-mom energy.
- **Eyelet embroidery is an actual 2026 summer trend** — the repeated eyelet pieces throughout the grid feel intentional, not random. That's trend-first thinking, not trend-chasing, and I notice the difference.
- **Individual product tiles with prices are clean** — I can see $27.99, $44.99, $46.00 listed clearly, no mystery pricing, no "as low as" games.
- **Layout is genuinely uncluttered** — the scroll is clean, no banners fighting each other, the "SHOP NOW" CTA at the hero and bottom of grid are both visible without hunting.
- **The white/cream/linen palette is cohesive** — this looks like one capsule collection, not a clearance dump.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer in an email following a 70% off push** — after yesterday's aggressive sale email (scored 9/10 in my feed), full-price new arrivals with no hook feel like the vibe dropped overnight.
- **"SHOP NOW" is the laziest CTA that exists** — this is my fifteenth H&M email and that button has never changed. Compare to ASOS's recent "Guess Who's Back" — even that confused energy at least tried to create a moment.
- **No urgency, no scarcity, no reason to click now vs. Sunday** — "New Arrival" is not urgency. "Only 3 left" would be urgency. I'm not clicking today, I'm bookmarking in my head and forgetting it by morning.
- **Cadence is completely burnt at this point** — daily emails for two weeks is too much. The eyelet pieces deserve a better send window when my inbox isn't numbed.
- **No size diversity or plus visible in the grid** — the editorial feel is very thin/tall-model coded, and as someone who notices which brands perform inclusion vs. just post about it, this reads like 2019.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lean into the trend framing the subject promises** — "wardrobe heroes" is a teaser that goes nowhere inside the email. Lead the hero copy with *why* eyelet is the texture story of the summer, not just "seasonal essentials" which means nothing. Give me the TikTok context: this trend is everywhere right now, H&M got there, this is their take.
- 2. **Add a member-exclusive price or early-access hook** — the footer has H&M member text in tiny print. That belongs in the hero. "Members shop first" would have converted my click on the spot.
- 3. **Collapse the product grid to 4 hero pieces** — showing nine tiles on a scroll is a homepage, not an email. Pick four and make them feel intentional.
- 4. **Rethink the send timing** — dropping a full-price "here's new stuff" email the morning after a 70% off sale push sets you up to look like you're stalling. Either extend the sale or wait 48 hours to reset expectations.

### 1. Technical Summary

The send infrastructure appears standard for a large ESP, but the JSON-LD structured data block contains a stale hardcoded date from 2018 and multiple unpopulated fields, indicating a template that was never fully wired to campaign data. The HTML source is truncated, so link/UTM and unsubscribe audits below are based on what is visible.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully audit — HTML truncated before `<body>` links appear.**

What is visible:
- Logo asset at `https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/assets/1.0.170/images/logo/hm-logo-gmail.png` — versioned path (`1.0.170`), verify CDN cache is not serving a stale asset.
- Font CSS loaded from `https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css` and `https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css` — both are external `@import` calls; if either CDN is unavailable at render time, fallback fonts activate and layout can shift. `@import` in `<style>` blocks is also blocked by some email clients entirely.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**External font loading via `@import`**
Gmail strips `<link>` tags and many clients block `@import`; the two font imports may silently fail for a significant portion of recipients. System-font fallbacks should be declared in the same rule.

**`display:none` / `max-height:0` content hiding**
`.is-hidden-mobile` and `.is-hidden-desktop` rely on `@media` query support. Outlook (Windows) ignores media queries entirely, so desktop-hidden content will be visible to Outlook users and vice versa.

**`#MessageViewBody` targeting**
The rule `#MessageViewBody .hide-webkit { display: block !important; }` targets a Gmail-specific wrapper. This is intentional but fragile — any Gmail DOM change breaks it silently.

**Alt text** — Cannot audit; image tags not present in the truncated source.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Stale hardcoded date in JSON-LD — confirmed bug:**

```json
"availabilityStarts":"2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"
```

This date is ~7.5 years in the past. It is a static string in the template, not a merge token, meaning every send carries this incorrect value. Gmail uses this field to render promotional tab annotations.

**Multiple unpopulated JSON-LD fields:**

```json
"subjectLine": ""
"availabilityEnds": ""
"discountCode": ""
"description": ""
"image": ""
"url": ""   // inside PromotionCard
```

These are empty strings rather than populated values or absent keys. For Gmail's Promotions tab, `subjectLine`, `image`, and `url` are the fields that drive rich card rendering — all three being empty means the email falls back to a plain listing in the Promotions tab despite the structured data being present.

No ESP merge token syntax (`{{`, `%%`, `*|`) visible in the truncated source.
