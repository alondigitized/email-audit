# Homepage snapshot · www.microsoft.com · 2026-06-10
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-10**
## Executive summary

- This looks like Microsoft trying to be an AI-forward retail store — the search bar with "Ask a question" is front and center, which honestly aligns with how I think about buying tech. But there's a literal "Placeholder text" dropdown sitting mid-page like someone pushed to prod before QA, which undercuts the whole professional vibe immediately. The Surface + Xbox pairing in that product card is clearly aimed at someone like me, so at least they know their audience.

## What's working

- **"Consoles under $500" chip** — hits my Xbox Game Pass context instantly; I'm always watching for hardware deals that pair with my subscription
- **"Refurbished Surface" chip** — I wait for refurb stock when launch supply is tight (and it always is), so this is actually a smarter hook for me than a brand-new launch banner
- **"Trade-in offers" chip** — I've got aging Surface hardware; this is a genuine pull
- **Surface + Xbox gaming imagery on the product card** — the convergence of laptop + controller imagery is exactly how I think about my setup; they didn't have to say a word
- **AI search integration** — as a tech-native, I'd actually use this; beats digging through a nav tree

## What's weak

- **"Placeholder text" dropdown** — this is a live bug on production; as someone who works in software, this makes me trust the cart/checkout less, not just the design
- **No specific Surface model or price** — the product card teases "$500+ in added value" but doesn't tell me which Surface, which config, or what the actual price is; I can't evaluate it without tapping
- **Zero urgency or deadline** — "Microsoft Store Deals" is evergreen copy; there's nothing telling me this offer expires, which means there's no reason to act today vs. next week
- **No loyalty or rewards signal** — Microsoft Rewards exists; I accumulate points. The fact that it's invisible here is a missed shot at a returning customer
- **Hero copy is generic** — "Microsoft Store Deals" is as bland as it gets; it's a department heading, not a campaign

## Recommendations

- **Replace "Placeholder text" with the actual intended dropdown** — this is a ship-now fix; it's reading as "we launched broken code" which is the worst signal for a hardware brand
- **Add a Microsoft Rewards balance or points-to-$ callout** to the hero row — even a small "You have X points — redeem today" chip would make this feel personal to a returning customer
- **Give the product card a model name and starting price** — "Surface Laptop 7, from $999 + $500 Xbox Game Pass value" is specific and tappable; the current card asks me to do the math myself
- **Add a countdown or sale-end date** to at least one chip — "Consoles under $500 — ends June 15" turns a browsing moment into a conversion moment
