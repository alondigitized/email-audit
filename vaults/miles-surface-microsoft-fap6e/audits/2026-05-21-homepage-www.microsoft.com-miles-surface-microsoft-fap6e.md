# Homepage snapshot · www.microsoft.com · 2026-05-21
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so Microsoft is leading with an AI-powered shopping assistant angle on the Store homepage — "personalized help from AI" right below the headline. It's commerce-focused, not OS or platform storytelling, which is an interesting call. The vibe is functional and clean, but the hero area has a literal "Placeholder text" dropdown sitting in the middle of the screen, which is not a good look.

## What's working

- **"Refurbished Surface" quick-chip** — that's me. I watch launch supply closely and will absolutely consider a certified-refurb to dodge the first-batch lottery.
- **"Trade-in offers" chip** — I upgrade on a cycle and trade-in economics matter. This is a smart shortcut.
- **"Consoles under $500" chip** — I'm an Xbox Game Pass guy, so an entry-point console deal is relevant.
- **AI-powered search prompt ("Ask a question")** — fits Microsoft's current AI narrative and I'm receptive to it; asking "which Surface is right for my workflow?" is a real thing I'd do.
- **"$500+ in added value" callout** at the bottom of the visible card — that's the kind of bundled-value framing I respond to as someone evaluating the Microsoft ecosystem.

## What's weak

- **"Placeholder text" dropdown** — a live production page with placeholder copy is an immediate credibility hit. If this is how the store ships, what does that say about the hardware QA?
- **No concrete offer or price** — "Microsoft Store Deals" is the hero headline, yet I see zero dollar amounts, zero percentages, zero sale badges above the fold. It's a promise with no proof.
- **Hero is all utility, zero identity** — there's no Surface Studio lifestyle shot, no Xbox controller in a living-room setup, nothing that says "this is your brand." It feels like a generic e-commerce skeleton.
- **No loyalty or rewards signal** — I'm a repeat Microsoft customer. There's no Microsoft Rewards balance, no member-exclusive pricing, nothing that rewards my ecosystem loyalty.
- **The laptop card is only partially visible** — cropped at the bottom, so I can't tell what product or deal it's anchoring.

## Recommendations

- **Kill the "Placeholder text" accordion immediately** — even a collapsed FAQ or a "Popular categories" module would be better than shipping this.
- **Replace the AI-search hero with a split layout**: Surface Studio 2+ lifestyle shot left, a concrete deal ("Save $400 on Surface Studio 2+") right — give me a reason to tap, not just a prompt to ask.
- **Surface the Microsoft Rewards balance in the nav bar** — I'm logged in (account icon is there), show me my points. That's a free retention hook that costs zero real estate.
- **Add a time-bounded "Back to School" or "Limited refurb stock" badge** to the Refurbished Surface chip — scarcity framing works on me because I know Surface supply is historically constrained.
