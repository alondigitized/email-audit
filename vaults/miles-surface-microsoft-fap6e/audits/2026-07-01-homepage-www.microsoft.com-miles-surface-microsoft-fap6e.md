# Homepage snapshot · www.microsoft.com · 2026-07-01
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- Microsoft is leading with "Microsoft Store Deals" and an AI-assisted shopping experience — which is a genuinely interesting move. The back-to-school angle is front and center, and the education discount card for Surface is exactly the kind of hook I wait for every year. This feels like a functional, no-nonsense deals hub rather than a lifestyle brand moment, but for me personally, the content alignment is pretty close to ideal.

## What's working

- **"Refurbished Surface" pill** — this is exactly how I shop. One tap, right to my category, no digging through nav. Smart shortcut for a returning Surface customer.
- **"Trade-in offers" pill** — I always have an old Surface kicking around. This is money on the table and Microsoft put it front and center, which earns trust.
- **AI search bar ("Ask a question")** — as a native-tech Millennial, I'll actually use this. It's the right call to surface it prominently instead of burying a chatbot behind a drawer.
- **Education card copy** — "Eligible students, parents, and teachers — when signed in" tells me exactly what to do and what I get. The sign-in gating is a friction-reducer for me since I already have a Microsoft account.
- **"Consoles under $500" pill** — I'm a Game Pass subscriber, so this catches my eye even if it isn't my primary shopping mode today. Reinforces that this is a tech ecosystem play.

## What's weak

- **Hero is headline-only, no product** — "Microsoft Store Deals" with a gradient background doesn't show me a Surface, an Xbox, or any product. For a hardware brand, this is a missed visual anchoring moment.
- **No Microsoft Rewards hook** — I have Rewards points. Where are they? I expected to see a balance or at least a "member savings" badge. This is the one loyalty mechanism Microsoft actually has and it's invisible.
- **Education card targets students and teachers, not self-purchasers** — 10% off for eligible students is great, but I'm a 37-year-old buying for myself. The framing excludes me unless I read carefully enough to notice it says "parents" too.
- **No specific deal amount above the fold** — I see "deals" in the headline but no number. Is it 10% off? 20%? "$200 off Surface Pro"? Generic deal language without a number doesn't make me lean forward.
- **Floating chat bubble (bottom right)** — redundant next to the AI search bar at the top. Looks cluttered on mobile and reads as a dark-pattern-adjacent nudge.

## Recommendations

- **Put a Surface product shot behind the hero copy** — even a tight crop of the Surface Studio or Surface Pro 11 would anchor the brand and signal "this is the store where your device lives."
- **Add a Microsoft Rewards balance chip to the nav bar** — I'm already logged in (the account icon suggests that), so show me my points. "You have 4,200 Rewards points" is a conversion trigger that costs nothing to display.
- **Reframe the edu card for self-purchasers too** — the headline buries "parents." Lead with "Back-to-school pricing: 10% off Surface for students, parents & teachers" so I don't self-exclude before reading the fine print.
- **Drop one pill that reads as a specific dollar save** — replace one of the three pills with something like "Surface Pro from $899" or "Up to $200 off Surface" to give the deals section a concrete number above the fold.
