# Homepage snapshot · www.microsoft.com · 2026-05-15
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- Microsoft's mobile homepage is running full "we have deals, ask our AI" energy right now — it's less of a product storefront and more of an AI concierge pitch bolted onto a deals page. As someone who's deep in the Surface + Xbox ecosystem, I appreciate the Surface imagery and the quick-filter pills, but the student bundle positioning immediately telegraphs that I'm not the primary target here.

## What's working

- **"Consoles under $500" pill** — one tap to exactly the category I browse when my Game Pass sub reminds me to actually own hardware
- **"Refurbished Surface" pill** — speaks directly to how I shop: I wait for education discount or refurb pricing, not launch day MSRP
- **"Trade-in offers" pill** — as a loyalist cycling through Surface generations, trade-in is always on my radar
- **AI "Ask a question" bar** — native-feeling for a tech-comfortable millennial; I'll actually use this instead of hunting nav
- **Surface + Xbox controller imagery** — the product card visually locks in both of my primary interests without me having to dig

## What's weak

- **"Get the ultimate student bundle"** — I'm not a student, and this is the only product card visible above fold; immediately signals this page isn't curated for me
- **No Microsoft Rewards hook** — I have points sitting in my account and there's zero mention of them in this viewport; that's a missed retention lever
- **"Ask a question" as the primary CTA** — the dominant interactive element is an AI chat box, not a shop button; if I'm on deals intent, this creates a detour
- **No social proof** — no star ratings, no review counts, no "X people bought this" on the visible card
- **No price specifics in hero copy** — "Microsoft Store Deals" promises savings but shows no anchor price or % off

## Recommendations

- **Swap the student bundle card for a Surface + Xbox Game Pass bundle card** — "Surface + 12 months Game Pass Ultimate" speaks directly to returning ecosystem loyalists, not just new students
- **Add a Microsoft Rewards balance nudge** — even a small "You have 4,200 points — use them on your next Surface" callout would make me feel seen as a returning customer
- **Surface the trade-in value calculator inline** — put an estimated trade-in value ("Your Surface Pro 9 could be worth $350") directly on the trade-in pill or as a module, not buried post-tap
- **Replace "Ask a question" placeholder with category-specific prompts** — pre-populate "Compare Surface Studio 3 models" or "Best Xbox bundle under $600" to reduce friction for a returning buyer
