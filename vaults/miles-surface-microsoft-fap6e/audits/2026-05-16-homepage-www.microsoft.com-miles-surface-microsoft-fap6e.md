# Homepage snapshot · www.microsoft.com · 2026-05-16
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- Microsoft is leading with a "Microsoft Store Deals" positioning and leaning hard on an AI-assisted shopping angle — the hero is a big search bar that says "Ask a question," not a traditional product hero. Peeking below the fold is a Surface + gaming bundle with "$500+ in added value" copy that's squarely aimed at students. It's a store I know inside-out, but this page feels like it's trying to serve everyone and nobody at once.

## What's working

- **"Refurbished Surface" chip** — as someone who is perpetually skeptical of new Surface launch supply and waits for a sane price, this chip is the first thing I want to tap. It exists. That matters.
- **"Consoles under $500" chip** — Xbox Game Pass loyalist seeing a price-anchored console chip above the fold is a legitimate hook. Tells me Microsoft knows I'm deal-aware.
- **"Trade-in offers" chip** — three chips and two out of three apply to me directly. The chip-based navigation row is doing real work fast.
- **AI "Ask a question" bar** — as a tech-native I actually appreciate this as a power-user shortcut. I can type "Surface Studio deals" and skip four layers of nav.
- **Clean mobile layout** — no crowding, no collapsed text, no banner ads fighting for space. The hierarchy breathes.

## What's weak

- **"Ask a question" as the primary CTA** — the biggest interactive element on the page is a search input, not a buy button. Microsoft is betting I'll use the AI shopping assistant, but I came here to shop, not to chat. That's friction dressed up as a feature.
- **Student bundle positioning** — the hero card partially visible below the fold says "Get the ultimate student bundle." I'm 37 and bought my last Surface with an educator discount I barely qualified for. "Student bundle" signals this isn't for me.
- **Zero Microsoft Rewards integration** — I have a Rewards balance sitting there and this page shows me nothing about it. That's table stakes for a returning customer.
- **No concrete savings number above the fold** — "Consoles under $500" is a category filter, not a deal. There's no "Save 20%" or "$200 off" in the visible frame. The deal signal is implied, not stated.
- **Floating chat bubble** — there's already an AI bar in the hero. The second chat bubble in the bottom-right corner is redundant and visually noisy.

## Recommendations

- **Swap "Ask a question" as the lead CTA for a "Shop Deals" button with the AI bar as secondary** — the AI assist is interesting but it shouldn't be the primary conversion path for a returning loyalist. Give me a button first.
- **Surface a Microsoft Rewards balance or point estimate in the hero** — "You have ~2,400 Rewards points — use them on your next Surface" converts returning customers faster than generic deals copy.
- **Rename or reframe the student bundle for broader millennials** — "Microsoft 365 + Surface bundle" or "Work from anywhere bundle" would pull in my cohort without the college-dorm connotation.
- **Add a specific savings figure to the chips** — turn "Consoles under $500" into "Xbox from $299 — save up to $100" to close the gap between browse intent and purchase intent without adding a single extra tap.
