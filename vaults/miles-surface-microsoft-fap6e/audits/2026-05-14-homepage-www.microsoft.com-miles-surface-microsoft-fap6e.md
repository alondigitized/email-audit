# Homepage snapshot · www.microsoft.com · 2026-05-14
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- Opening microsoft.com and getting hit with "Microsoft Store Deals" as the hero headline — okay, fine, it's transactional but at least it's honest. The AI-powered search bar front-and-center is a flex for a company that's betting everything on Copilot, and the shortcut chips ("Consoles under $500," "Refurbished Surface," "Trade-in offers") feel like they were written specifically for someone like me. The student bundle card is half-visible at the bottom fold, which tells me they're in deal-stacking season, not product launch season.

## What's working

- **"Consoles under $500" pill** — That's the first thing I tap as an Xbox Game Pass loyalist. No friction, no navigation required.
- **"Refurbished Surface" pill** — This is smart targeting for someone skeptical of day-one Surface supply and pricing. A direct path to the deal tier I actually shop.
- **"Trade-in offers" pill** — I have an aging Surface Pro sitting in a drawer. This one actually makes me think.
- **AI search bar as the hero interaction** — As a native tech user, this feels right. Microsoft leading with Copilot on its own store homepage is a coherent bet. I'd actually type something there.
- **Surface + Xbox controller imagery** — One image, two ecosystems, both mine. No wasted visual real estate.

## What's weak

- **"Student bundle" framing below the fold** — I'm 37, not a freshman. The copy "Get the ultimate student bundle" doesn't make me feel seen. The deal might be relevant to me (buying for a family member, or just wanting the bundle value), but the framing excludes me.
- **No returning-customer signal** — I've bought from Microsoft Store before. There's no hook for logged-in state, no "your rewards" callout, no "you left something in your cart." This page has no memory.
- **No price specifics above the fold** — "Consoles under $500" is a price ceiling, not an actual deal. I want to see a real number — "Xbox Series X $399 for 48 hours" — before I commit my attention.
- **Headline "Microsoft Store Deals" is flat** — This is a search bar for what to write when you have nothing to say. It's descriptive but not motivating. "Spring Savings on Surface + Xbox" would've told me the same thing while actually pulling me in.

## Recommendations

- **Put a real numbered deal in the hero** — Replace "Microsoft Store Deals" with something like "Surface Laptop 7 from $849 · Xbox Series X $399" and give it a time stamp. Price specificity converts browsing to intent.
- **Add a returning-member strip** — Even a thin banner that says "Sign in to see your Microsoft Rewards balance" would significantly increase relevance for loyal customers. That's table stakes for a hardware brand.
- **Swap the student bundle framing for "Spring Upgrade Deals"** — The bundle value is real, but "student" loses me. Reframe it around the upgrade cycle (Surface + Game Pass + Office) and it hits 30-something loyalists who upgrade every 2–3 years.
- **Put a spec-comparison chip in the pill row** — "Compare Surface models" next to "Refurbished Surface" would catch someone like me who's in consideration mode, not impulse mode.
