# 20% off bedding, duvet sets & more
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- H&M pivots away from the apparel-heavy cadence into a **home/bedding segment drop** — 20% off bedding, duvet sets, and pillowcases, positioned as an "H&M Home Exclusive." The offer is narrow and credible, a marked contrast from the past week of sitewide-50%-off blasts. The email is split into two distinct zones: an H&M Home section at the top (the actual offer) and a "Just Landed" apparel section below that has nothing to do with bedding and dilutes the message significantly.
- Visually the email is clean but generic. Product photography shows green/earth-toned bedding well enough, but the hero copy block is text-heavy for a home send — you get one bolded offer headline and then a paragraph of descriptive copy before you see any products. The segmentation here is oddly ambiguous: this is a bedding email sold to a general H&M subscriber list, not a verified H&M Home buyer. For a subscriber who doesn't follow H&M Home, the "Just Landed" apparel tail section will be more relevant than the hero — and that's a structural problem.
- The email is functional but unfocused. The 20% offer is real and visible, the product grid is legible, but the dual-theme layout means H&M is trying to serve two audiences at once and ends up under-serving both.

## What's working

- **Offer is clear and specific**: "20% OFF ALL BEDDING" in large bold type is the first thing you read — no mystery about what's on sale.
- **Product grid is informative**: Striped king/queen duvet, 2-pack pillowcases, patterned twin cover, beerislucker twin/duvet — actual SKUs with prices and "New Arrival" tags give it real browsability.
- **Calm, on-brand aesthetic**: The hero imagery (green tones, clean linen, warm light) matches a home lifestyle look that's consistent with H&M Home's visual identity.
- **Two CTAs in hero**: "Shop Now" + "Find a Store" — covers both online and in-store purchase paths without friction.
- **Price anchors visible**: Crossed-out original prices on some items signal real savings, not just a vague % promise.

## What's weak

- **Apparel "Just Landed" section below is completely off-topic**: A sundress grid and "WOMEN / MEN / BABY / KIDS / HOME / BEAUTY" category nav has no relationship to the bedding offer — it reads like a second email stitched below the first.
- **Hero body copy is too wordy**: "Give your bedroom the refresh it deserves with fresh sheets in crisp cotton, any muslin, and linen, from cribs-sized to dorm-to-king." That's a sentence block below a sale headline — home emails live or die on imagery, not copy.
- **No urgency signal**: "20% off" with no deadline, no "this weekend only," no end date. Compared to the credible urgency in the July 19 "ends tonight" send, this just floats.
- **No social proof**: No ratings, no "bestseller" callouts, no UGC — these are home goods where "2,000 5-star reviews" would actually move intent.
- **Category nav duplication**: There's a full WOMEN/MEN/BABY/KIDS/HOME/BEAUTY grid *twice* — once as image buttons, once as text links immediately below. That's visual clutter that adds no value.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the apparel section or give it its own send.** A bedding email and a "Just Landed" fashion drop are two different emails. The subscriber who opened for duvet sets does not want a sundress grid, and vice versa. Either segment the list or send two sends.
- 2. **Add a deadline.** "Offer ends Sunday" or "This week only" transforms a passive 20% announcement into a reason to act today.
- 3. **Trim the hero body copy to one line.** "Fresh cotton, linen & muslin — in sizes from crib to king" does the job in half the words and lets the imagery breathe.
- 4. **Lead with a lifestyle photo, not the paragraph.** The green bedding shot at the very top is the strongest asset in this email — make it the hero above the fold, not a thumbnail beside body text.
- 5. **Add a social proof callout to at least one product.** Even "Fan Favorite" or a star rating on the top-selling SKU would provide purchase confidence for a new H&M Home buyer.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Fresh sheets, new duvet sets — 20% off today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cotton, linen & muslin bedding — sizes from crib to king. Ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New duvet covers + pillowcases, 20% off. In stores & online.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS `@import` failure (high severity)**

```html
<style>
  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
</style>
```

`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is stripped by Gmail (web and Android) and ignored by Outlook on Windows. Both font families (`northstar`, `oces`) will fail to load for the majority of recipients. The email will fall back to system fonts throughout. Fonts should be loaded via `<link>` tags or inlined — though most email clients block external font loading entirely; system-font fallbacks in inline CSS are the safe path.

**Responsive CSS strategy** — The `#optimize-css` block uses `@media (max-width:699px)` with class-based overrides (`.is-hidden-mobile`, `.is-hidden-desktop`, etc.). This approach works on modern clients but will produce desktop-only layout on Outlook 2016–2021, which ignores media queries entirely. Not a new issue for H&M, but noted.

**Alt text** — Cannot assess; image tags are not visible in the truncated source.
