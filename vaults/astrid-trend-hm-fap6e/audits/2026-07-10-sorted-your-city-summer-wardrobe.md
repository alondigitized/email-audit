# Sorted: your city summer wardrobe
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- "Sorted: your city summer wardrobe" arrives the morning after H&M closed out a brutal 25%-off flash sale. This is the brand hitting reset — pure editorial, zero discount, no offer mechanic whatsoever. The hero is a clean cream-and-white outdoor look under the headline "SUMMER IN THE CITY," and the body leans on a Lyocell shirt and a lace-trimmed top / tie-shoulder shirt pair, all priced at $44.99–$57.99, framed as "New Arrival."
- The strategy is defensible. H&M ran 25% off sitewide yesterday, 20% off for five days before that. Stacking another offer today would devalue the brand further. Instead they're doing what they did on July 6 with "Silhouettes of Summer" — a palette-cleanser editorial send. The problem is the execution is noticeably thinner: one hero image, two product tiles that share the same two products in split color-ways, and a SHOP BY department nav that looks like a footer appendix. There's no urgency, no clear benefit for opening or clicking, and the subject line is vague lifestyle copy with no concrete hook.
- Net verdict: acceptable cadence move, weak execution. If you're not actively shopping H&M this week you skip this one entirely.

## What's working

- **Cadence discipline** — pulling the offer the day after the flash sale close is correct; not hammering 25% again preserves margin and reduces fatigue.
- **Clean visual hierarchy** — the hero shot is strong: natural light, aspirational outdoor setting, clear "SUMMER IN THE CITY" headline immediately below.
- **New Arrival framing** — both product tiles carry "New Arrival" labels, which at least gives a reason to visit other than discount.
- **No render bugs** — images loaded cleanly, text is legible, no overlapping elements, layout is intact.

## What's weak

- **No offer, no CTA hook** — the sole CTA is a generic "SHOP NOW" button. There's no reason to click over any other H&M browse session.
- **Subject is vague lifestyle copy** — "Sorted: your city summer wardrobe" is pleasant but specific to nobody. Compared to yesterday's "25% off flash sale starts now," this is invisible in an inbox.
- **Product selection is thin and repetitive** — two product tiles show essentially the same top in two variants. That's a single-item scroll for a full email.
- **Price-only, no urgency** — prices are shown ($44.99, $57.99) but no sale signal, no stock scarcity, no deadline. The "New Arrival" label does light work but isn't compelling.
- **SHOP BY nav block looks like boilerplate** — five department squares (Women, Men, Kids, Home, Beauty) feel like footer filler, not curated content.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with one specific product + specific detail.** The Lyocell shirt deserves its own hero moment: "Short-Sleeved Lyocell Shirt — our most-breathable fabric, $44.99." That turns a browse email into a product email.
- 2. **Add a lightweight offer to justify the send.** Even "free shipping today" or a "member early access" hook gives a reason to click that transcends editorial mood.
- 3. **Replace the SHOP BY grid with a second editorial outfit.** City summer wardrobe should show at least two full outfit compositions, not a department nav.
- 4. **Rewrite the hero subhead** — "Dressing for warm weather has never been easier. Discover the season's must-have styles, from monochromatic looks to timeless florals" is generic filler. Cut it or replace with a single sharp benefit line.
- 5. **Tighten the footer fine-print block** — the dense legal/member-offer copy at the bottom is significantly longer than the content above it on mobile. It makes the email feel like a legal notice with a photo on top.
