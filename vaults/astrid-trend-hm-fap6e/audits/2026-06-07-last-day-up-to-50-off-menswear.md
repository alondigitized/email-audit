# Last day: Up to 50% off menswear
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- This is the urgency-close to the June 5 "Up to 50% off gifts for every dad" send — and it executes that job well. The hero lands immediately: big headline, 50% off, menswear, two clear CTAs. The male model is on-brief, the Father's Day gifting frame is still live with two weeks to go, and the member giveaway ($250 gift card) is a smart loyalty hook that adds a second reason to engage beyond the price cut.
- The biggest structural miss is the preheader. The email opens with a utility banner — "NEED AN ITEM SOONER? BUY ONLINE, PICK UP IN-STORE" — which is almost certainly leaking as the inbox preview text. That kills the preview-line opportunity when the urgency ("last day") is the single strongest asset this email has.
- Verdict: a competent urgency close with a wasted preheader slot and slightly generic CTA copy. It will work on H&M regulars but leaves performance on the table.

## What's working

- **"Last day" urgency in subject is the sharpest signal H&M has deployed in this campaign cycle** — a clear upgrade over the June 5 "gifts for every dad" framing.
- **Hero is above the fold and unambiguous**: offer, model, two CTAs — all visible without scrolling on mobile.
- **Member giveaway section is a genuine differentiator** — a $250 bundle prize gives members a second, non-price reason to open and click.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean**: white space used effectively, no busy competing modules fighting the hero.
- **"SHOP GIFTS" CTA alongside "SHOP NOW"** correctly splits intent between self-purchase and Father's Day shopping — smart two-CTA strategy.

## What's weak

- **Preheader is a utility message, not sale copy** — "NEED AN ITEM SOONER? BUY ONLINE, PICK UP IN-STORE" has nothing to do with 50% off and wastes the second line of inbox real estate.
- **"SHOP NOW" is a weak primary CTA** — verb + adverb, not verb + noun. Doesn't tell the reader what they're getting.
- **No on-body deadline reinforcement** — the subject says "last day" but the email body doesn't echo a specific end date or time. The urgency signal evaporates once you open.
- **No social proof** — a sale close is exactly where reviews, star ratings, or a "bestsellers flying out" module would push fence-sitters.
- **The June 6 H&M HOME send creates a confusing day-before experience** for subscribers who got a coastal lifestyle home-goods editorial immediately before this menswear sale closer — different category, jarring pivot.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader immediately** — this is the single highest-leverage change. Replace the pickup-utility banner with urgency copy that echoes the offer.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sale ends tonight — shop the full menswear edit before it's gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Up to 50% off ends today. Don't miss your size.`
- 2. **Add a countdown or explicit end-time in the body** — "Sale ends midnight tonight" or a visual timer reinforces the "last day" claim and drives same-session conversion.
- 3. **Rewrite "SHOP NOW" to "SHOP THE SALE"** — ties the button directly to the offer context and passes the verb + noun test.
- 4. **Insert one social-proof module before the SHOP BY section** — a "Trending now: 4.7★ / 12K reviews" strip on a hero product, or a "Selling fast" badge on the polo in the hero, would push undecided openers.
- 5. **A/B test a subject line that leads with deadline instead of offer:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Tonight only: 50% off all menswear`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your cart is about to expire — 50% off ends today`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` for web fonts (high risk):**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
Gmail, Outlook, and Yahoo strip `@import` rules. If no system-font fallback stack is declared, recipients on those clients will render the browser default (usually Times New Roman). Confirmed: the CSS block containing these imports has no explicit `font-family` fallback chained after.

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`:**
```html
<html style="border:0;margin:0;outline:0;padding:0">
```
No `lang="en"` (or locale-appropriate value). Screen readers default to the OS language; recipients using assistive technology in a non-English locale may get incorrect pronunciation.

**Mobile CSS — `display:inline` on block elements:**
```css

  display: inline !important;
}
```
Block-level elements (tables, divs) revealed on mobile with `display:inline` collapse horizontal layout. Should be `display:block` or `display:table` depending on element type. Risk: broken single-column layout on mobile.
