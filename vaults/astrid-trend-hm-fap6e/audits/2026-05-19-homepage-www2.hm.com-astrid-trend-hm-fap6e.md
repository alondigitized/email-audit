# Homepage snapshot · www2.hm.com · 2026-05-19
**Score:** 2/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- Okay so the first thing H&M hit me with was a full-screen cookie wall — I can barely see the actual homepage behind it. Whatever campaign they're running right now is completely buried under a legal disclaimer. Not a great first impression for a brand that's trying to feel current and fashion-forward.

## What's working

- **Clean modal design** — the "ACCEPT ALL" button is a clear black CTA, easy to tap on mobile; no confusing layout within the overlay itself
- **Readable typography** — the cookie text is legible at mobile size, not a squinting situation
- **Two clear options** — "Accept All" vs. "Cookies and Services Settings" gives me agency without overwhelming me

## What's weak

- **Full-screen cookie wall before any content** — I have literally zero idea what H&M is selling today, what the campaign is, or why I should care; the modal takes up 100% of the viewport
- **No brand personality in the modal** — this reads like legal boilerplate, not H&M; there's no brand color, no editorial tone, nothing that says "this is a fashion brand"
- **Background imagery is almost completely obscured** — I can see a sliver of something dark on the left edge, but it tells me nothing
- **Zero hook before the consent ask** — on mobile especially, you need me to want to be here before you ask me to consent to tracking; the ordering is backwards
- **No skip or "X" visible** — feels like I'm trapped before I've seen a single product

## Recommendations

- **Show a teaser strip above the modal** — let the hero image peek through so I have a reason to hit "Accept All"; even a 30% visible background creates desire before the ask
- **Brand the modal** — use H&M red or the wordmark somewhere in the overlay; right now it reads like a generic GDPR template, not a fashion destination
- **Compress the cookie copy** — the wall of text is four paragraphs on mobile; condense to one sentence + a "learn more" link so I'm not reading a legal brief to get to new arrivals
- **Add a seasonal hook in the modal headline** — swap "BEFORE YOU START SHOPPING" for something like "New in for Summer — accept to personalize your feed" so the value exchange feels real
