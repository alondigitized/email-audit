# Elevated leisure
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- "Elevated Leisure" is a full-price editorial send — no discount, no urgency, no offer. It lands the morning after H&M fired off two "ends tonight" sale emails on July 19 and has been running near-identical 50%-off blasts all week. That context makes this pivot to editorial feel jarring rather than aspirational; the subscriber who was conditioned by five days of sitewide discount emails isn't primed to shop full-price denim and blazers.
- The creative itself is restrained and clean — lifestyle photography, a subdued hero headline, and a "new arrivals" product grid. The "Summer in the City" midpage section adds a second editorial beat, which is nice, but it competes with the hero rather than building on it. The result is a well-shot email that doesn't close the deal: no price reduction, no specific product hook in the subject, and CTA buttons that simply say "SHOP NOW."
- This is a credible brand reset after sale fatigue, but it needs more editorial conviction or a concrete hook to justify opening.

## What's working

- **Clean visual hierarchy.** Full-width hero image with bold "ELEVATED LEISURE" headline lands first; the eye moves naturally into the product grid below.
- **Two distinct editorial beats.** "Elevated Leisure" + "Summer in the City" give the email a magazine-page feel rather than a pure dump of SKUs.
- **Product grid is tidy.** Named products (Balloon Sleeved Coated Jacket, Tie-Detail Scoop-Neck Top, etc.) with "New Arrival" tags signal fresh inventory — the right message after a week of discounting the same old stock.
- **Category nav at the bottom** (Women / Men / Kids / Home / Beauty) gives a clear fallback path if the hero doesn't convert.

## What's weak

- **No offer whatsoever.** After five consecutive discount emails, "Elevated Leisure" with zero discount and no urgency will get skipped by the same subscriber who opened for 50% off.
- **Subject line "Elevated leisure" is vague to the point of being skippable** — no hint of what's new, no offer, no category specificity. Most subscribers won't know what they're opening.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is generic.** Every button in this email says the same thing; there's no differentiated entry point (e.g. "Shop Jackets" vs "Shop Tops").
- **Product prices shown are full price** but given no context (no "new in," no MSRP comparison) — they read as expensive without a hook to justify them.
- **"Summer in the City" section is underserved** — single model shot, thin copy, one more "SHOP NOW." It needed a product link or specific look to be worth the real estate.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the subject line specific** — "Elevated leisure" tells you nothing. Lead with what's new or the category.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New in: relaxed tailoring & wide-leg denim`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Balloon Jacket is back — and it's a new arrival`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Tailored pieces, flowy silhouettes, and looks styled for the city`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Wide-leg jeans, draped tops, and the coated jacket everyone will ask about`
- 2. **Differentiate the CTAs.** Replace every "SHOP NOW" with product-specific copy: "Shop Jackets," "Shop the Look," "Shop Wide-Leg Denim."
- 3. **Add a soft anchor offer.** Even "Free shipping on orders over $40" or "New Arrivals — no code needed" gives the non-sale subscriber a reason to click without undermining the editorial tone.
- 4. **Cut "Summer in the City" or develop it.** As currently executed, it's a dead section. Either link it to a specific curated collection or remove it and tighten the email.
- 5. **Preheader is invisible in the screenshot** — verify it isn't leaking technical junk (common after a long discount-email run).

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue: Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`**
```html
<html style="border:0;margin:0;outline:0;padding:0">
```
No `lang` attribute present. Screen readers default to OS language, which breaks pronunciation for international sends. WCAG 2.1 §3.1.1.

**Issue: External font `@import` in `<style>` block**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is stripped by Outlook (all versions), Yahoo Mail, and some Android clients. No inline font-family fallback stack is visible in the truncated source — if fallbacks are absent, clients that block `@import` will render system-default fonts with no degradation path.

**Positive signals:**
- `<meta name="x-apple-disable-message-reformatting"/>` present — correct
- `format-detection` meta present — suppresses unwanted tel/address auto-linking
- `@media (max-width:699px)` responsive breakpoint present with `mso-hide:all` Outlook overrides

**Cannot assess:** `alt` text on images — source truncated before `<img>` tags.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Issue: Stale and empty JSON-LD structured data fields**

The `<script type="application/ld+json">` block contains:

```json
{
  "@type": "DiscountOffer",
  "availabilityStarts": "2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00",
  "availabilityEnds": "",
  "discountCode": "",
  "description": ""
}
```

- `availabilityStarts` is hardcoded to **December 31, 2018** — 7.5 years stale. This is an unfilled or unreset template default.
- `discountCode`, `description`, and `availabilityEnds` are all empty strings.
- `PromotionCard` block has empty `image` and `url` fields.
- `EmailMessage.subjectLine` is an empty string despite the subject line being "Elevated leisure".

Gmail's Promotions tab parser consumes this block to render deal badges and expiry countdowns. Empty/stale values result in either no annotation or a malformed one. The `DiscountOffer` type is semantically wrong for a non-promotional lifestyle send — it should either be populated correctly or removed.
