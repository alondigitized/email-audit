# 15% or 20% off ends tonight
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- This is a last-day deadline reminder for the same threshold discount H&M sent yesterday (June 13, "Up to 20% off is up to you"). The core mechanics are solid — "LAST DAY / 15% OFF $80 OR 20% OFF $100" lands immediately in the hero, the offer is specific and honest, and the short subject line gets the job done in the inbox. It earns its place in the send cadence as a closing trigger.
- But the execution has two visible drag factors. The Father's Day in-store banner sitting above the hero is a non-sequitur — it's promoting a completely different behavior (go to a physical store) in the same breath as a sitewide discount, and June 13's send already had the same disconnected banner. Second, the body copy under the hero reads "Things are finally heating up. Remove from your summer outerwear, shorts and sandals immediately" — "remove from" is almost certainly a copy error (probably meant "refresh your" or "stock up on"). A subscriber who notices it loses confidence in the brand.
- Net verdict: a functional last-day push that scores well on fundamentals but leaves points on the table from repeated mixed messaging and a copy error that should have been caught before deployment.

## What's working

- **Subject line is the strongest element** — 28 chars, specific %, clear deadline, zero spam noise. It does exactly what a last-day reminder should do.
- **"LAST DAY" call-out in hero is prominent** — eye lands on the offer immediately; no ambiguity about what day it is in the promotion cycle.
- **Product grid with prices** — six real products with visible pricing grounds the threshold offer in actual inventory rather than a pure brand editorial.
- **Offer thresholds are stated clearly in the hero** — no need to dig into fine print to understand the deal.

## What's weak

- **Father's Day in-store banner at the top is a dead end** — it promotes a physical store visit with no price incentive, directly above a sitewide discount email. This misalignment has now appeared in back-to-back sends (June 13 and June 14).
- **Body copy reads as a production error** — "Remove from your summer outerwear, shorts and sandals immediately" is grammatically broken; this is the first thing a reader sees below the offer.
- **No single dominant CTA button** — the category tabs ("WOMEN / BABY / KIDS / HOME / BEAUTY") are the only clickable action, but they're undersized and underpowered for a final-day urgency send.
- **Offer countdown specificity is missing** — "ends tonight" without a timezone or time (e.g., "midnight ET") leaves the urgency vague; the June 10 flash sale email did this better with a hard deadline.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Replace the Father's Day banner with a countdown clock or hard time** ("Sale ends at midnight ET") — this turns passive awareness into actual urgency and removes the in-store/online contradiction.
- 2. **Fix the body copy** — "Things are finally heating up. Stock up on summer outerwear, shorts and sandals before they sell out." One edit, immediately more credible.
- 3. **Add a single dominant CTA button** — something like "SHOP WOMEN'S SALE →" in a high-contrast button above the product grid. The category tabs are too quiet for a last-day send.
- 4. **Anchor the product grid to the threshold** — show a curated $80 and $100 outfit that unlocks each tier. Right now the mix (vase, bronzer, denim skirt, tank) doesn't connect to "here's what $80 looks like."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 20% off expires at midnight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last hours: 15% off $80, 20% off $100`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Summer shorts, skirts and sandals — pick anything over $80 and save automatically.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Spend $80, save 15%. Spend $100, save 20%. Cart closes tonight.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Font loading via `@import` (high risk)**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` tags is blocked or ignored by Outlook (all versions), Yahoo Mail, and older Android clients. These clients will fall back to system fonts with no branded typography.

**Responsive CSS via media queries**
The `@media (max-width:699px)` block is well-structured, but media queries are unsupported in Outlook on Windows and Gmail (web). Elements relying on `.is-hidden-mobile` / `.is-hidden-desktop` toggling will not hide/show correctly in those clients.

All four schema objects use `http://` instead of `https://`. Google's structured data documentation expects `https://schema.org`.
