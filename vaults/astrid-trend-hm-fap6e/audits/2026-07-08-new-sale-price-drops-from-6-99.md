# New sale price drops from $6.99
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- H&M breaks from two consecutive editorial sends (July 6 "Silhouettes of Summer," July 7 "Campus Shop") with its most price-forward subject since the July 5 LAST DAY close. "New sale price drops from $6.99" is a sharper inbox hook than anything the brand used mid-week — concrete, short, and priced. But the email doesn't pay that promise off. Once opened, the $6.99 anchor evaporates. There's no item with a crossed-out price, no percentage savings, no deadline — just four editorial department blocks with label-style CTAs.
- The layout is the same four-quadrant skeleton as yesterday's Campus Shop send (So Good / Smart Moves / Going Fast / Fresh Finds, each with a category button below). It renders cleanly, the section headers add some voice, but nothing in the body actually shows you what $6.99 buys. That mismatch between subject promise and body delivery is the central failure.
- This email opens better than it clicks. The subject is doing real work; the body squanders it.

## What's working

- **Hero is instantly legible**: "FURTHER MARKDOWNS FROM $6.99" in bold red — no ambiguity about what the email is for.
- **Subject line is the strongest since July 5**: 31 characters, a specific price point, no spam signals — clean inbox proposition.
- **Section headers give the grid some personality**: "Going Fast" and "Fresh Finds" earn their department labels better than plain "KIDS" or "HOME" would.
- **No render bugs**: All four image blocks load, buttons are present and readable, no layout breakage.
- **Smart tonal shift**: After five days of percentage-off hammer, a price-floor anchor ("from $6.99") is a fresh enough angle to avoid feel like repeat promo noise.

## What's weak

- **The $6.99 disappears after the hero**: No item, no original price struck through, no savings visible anywhere in the four department blocks — the subject promise goes completely unfulfilled inside the email.
- **CTAs are nouns, not actions**: "WOMEN," "MEN," "HOME" tell you the destination but not the reason to go now; "Shop Women's Sale" would outperform these consistently.
- **No urgency mechanics**: "Going Fast" is editorial copy, not a real signal — there's no deadline, no countdown, no "sale ends Sunday." The implied urgency is decoration.
- **Member pricing dropped with no replacement anchor**: The July 2–5 run trained subscribers to expect a 20% member benefit; this send offers nothing equivalent and makes the $6.99 floor feel thinner by comparison.
- **Hero models don't visually reinforce a markdown**: The women's block shows a floral dress and a striped set that read as full-price editorial fashion — not what you imagine when you hear "$6.99."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fulfill the $6.99 anchor in the body** — show at least one item with the original price crossed out and the sale price visible next to it. "Was $24.99 → $6.99" is the payoff the subject promised and the single change most likely to lift clicks.
- 2. **Add a real deadline** — "Sale ends Sunday" or a red countdown bar in the hero turns "Going Fast" from decoration into a decision driver. This is what separated the July 5 send (10/10) from everything that followed.
- 3. **Rewrite CTAs to verb-led copy** — "Shop Women's Sale," "Shop Men's Deals," "Shop Kids" removes the label-vs-button ambiguity and is a trivial copy change.
- 4. **Surface a hero product from the Women's block** — one specific item with its price removes the "I'll browse later" exit and anchors the deal in something tangible.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading (high risk)**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is blocked by Gmail, Outlook 2007–2019, and Yahoo Mail. Web fonts will silently fall back to system fonts, which is acceptable only if fallback stacks are defined. Confirm fallback `font-family` stacks are present in downstream selectors.

**`<script>` tag stripped by most clients**
The JSON-LD block inside `<script type="application/ld+json">` is only parsed by Gmail. All other email clients strip it entirely. This is expected behavior for Gmail annotations, not a defect — but issues within it (see §4) will affect Gmail-only features.

**Missing `lang` attribute**
```html
<html style="border:0;margin:0;...">
```
No `lang="en"` (or locale equivalent) on the `<html>` element. Screen readers cannot infer language; also affects spell-check heuristics in some clients. Minor accessibility gap.

**`alt` text on images** — cannot confirm; images appear below the truncation point.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Stale hardcoded date in JSON-LD (confirmed defect)**
```json
"availabilityStarts":"2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"
```
This date is 7+ years in the past. Either a static placeholder was never replaced by the ESP merge layer, or this field was copy-pasted from a legacy template. Gmail interprets this field for its promotional tab annotations — a stale date may cause the promotion to not surface or be deprioritized.

**Multiple empty fields in JSON-LD**
```json
{"@type":"EmailMessage","subjectLine":"","@context":"http://schema.org/"}
{"availabilityEnds":"","discountCode":"","description":""}
{"image":"","url":""}
```
Six fields are empty strings. For `DiscountOffer`, empty `discountCode` and `description` mean the Gmail promo tab annotation will not render a usable offer card. For `PromotionCard`, empty `image` and `url` prevent the visual card from appearing.

These appear to be unfilled merge tokens, not intentional omissions. The `subjectLine` field being empty when the actual subject is "New sale price drops from $6.99" confirms the ESP did not populate this schema block.
