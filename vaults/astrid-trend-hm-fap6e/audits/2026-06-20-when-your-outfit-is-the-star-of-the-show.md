# When your outfit is the star of the show
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- This is H&M in full editorial mode — five summer outfit stories ("The Americana Edit," "Summer Spirit," "Easy Does It," "Stars & Stripes," "The Homestead") plus a Pride-themed "Seen, Heard, Celebrated" panel. No prices, no percentages, no deadlines. Pure lookbook.
- Coming off four consecutive sale emails since June 16 — the 60% off drumbeat that peaked with June 19's "Up to 60% off = cool for the summer" — this editorial reset is the right brand-hygiene move. The creative earns it: the Americana and Stars & Stripes stories are calendar-sharp for a June 20 send, the Pride panel is visually distinct and timely, and the photography is H&M's strongest work in this sequence.
- The execution is let down almost entirely by the subject line. "When your outfit is the star of the show" is mood copy, not inbox copy. It communicates zero about the five stories inside. An editorial this well-crafted deserved a subject that previewed even one of them.

## What's working

- Five distinct stories in one send create variety — the Americana, Stars & Stripes, and Pride stories each hit a different cultural moment relevant to late June.
- The Pride section ("Seen, Heard, Celebrated") is rendered as a bold colorful typographic block — it reads as deliberate creative, not afterthought checkbox marketing.
- Visual hierarchy is clean throughout: full-bleed image → bold editorial headline → "SHOP" CTA, repeated consistently across every story block.
- The editorial break from the sale blitz is the right call — after three 60%-off emails in four days, a lookbook send protects brand equity.
- Stars & Stripes / Americana is well-timed for July 4th priming with a week of lead time.

## What's weak

- Subject line does no work: no product, no offer, no story name, no season — "star of the show" could be a talent show newsletter.
- No prices, no specific product names anywhere in the body — zero conversion hooks for a shopper with any purchase intent.
- "SHOP" alone is weak CTA copy across all five stories — no story name, no product type, nothing to differentiate one click from another.
- Five full editorial stories is too much scroll for a single send; the final "Homestead" block likely gets very low engagement relative to the top Americana story.
- The top banner text is illegible at rendered size — whatever it says (likely a delivery or member benefit) is invisible in practice.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Subject should name a story:** The Americana Edit is the strongest hook — lead with it rather than hiding five stories behind abstract mood copy.
- 2. **Sharpen CTAs to the story name:** "Shop The Americana Edit" does more than "SHOP" — it sets expectation and drives qualified clicks.
- 3. **Surface at least one price or product name:** One anchor like "$29 summer top" or "New: the denim midi" converts browsers to buyers without sacrificing the editorial tone.
- 4. **Trim to three stories per send:** Americana + Stars & Stripes + Pride is a coherent, timely triple. The remaining two stories (Summer Spirit, Homestead) should each anchor their own sends.
- 5. **Make the top banner legible:** If there's a delivery threshold or member perk up there, it should be readable — a benefit buried in 8px type is no benefit at all.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Font loading via CSS `@import`**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` in `<style>` blocks is blocked by Outlook (all versions) and some iOS Mail configurations. Font fallbacks in the stack will render instead — not a breakage, but custom typography will not appear for a significant share of the list.

**Alt text** — truncated source does not show `<img>` tags; verify all product images carry descriptive `alt` attributes. The CSS rule `img.image + div { display: none !important; }` suppresses image-replacement divs, so plain-text clients relying on that fallback will render nothing for those image blocks.

**Dark mode** — no `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` rules visible. Not a blocking issue, but worth flagging if H&M targets dark-mode Gmail/Apple Mail users.
