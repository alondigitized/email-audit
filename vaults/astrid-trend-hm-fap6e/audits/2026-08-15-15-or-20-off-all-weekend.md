# 15% or 20% off all weekend
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-15**
## Executive summary

- This is H&M's clearest sitewide-discount send since the 8/12 "25% off flash sale" — "15% OFF $75 OR 20% OFF $100" sits in oversized black type right under the hero image, unmissable and unambiguous. It's a tiered spend threshold rather than a flat percentage, which is a slightly weaker offer than 8/12's flat 25%, but it's still a real, specific number with no discount-code friction implied — this reads as automatic at checkout.
- Where it falls apart is structure: after the offer block and one clean "WOMEN / MEN / BABY / KIDS / HOME" grid, the email sprawls into three more mini-campaigns — "CHANGE IS GOOD" (women/men apparel), "ALL THE FEELS" (baby/kids), "PERFECT VIBES" (home decor) — each with its own imagery and category buttons, then a "SHOP BY" grid that repeats women/men/kids/home/beauty a fourth time. It's the same everything-to-everyone catalog dump seen in the 8/14 back-to-school send, just wearing a discount banner this time instead of an editorial one.
- Net: strong, scroll-stopping offer at the top, but the email doesn't trust that offer to carry the send and buries it under four redundant category modules. Judgement: solid open/click bait in the first screen, diminishing returns after that — 6/10 territory.

## What's working

- The offer is stated in large, high-contrast type immediately below the hero — no scrolling needed to find the deal.
- The tiered structure (15% off $75 / 20% off $100) rewards bigger baskets, which is a more sophisticated lever than a flat blanket discount.
- Category CTA buttons (Women/Men/Baby/Kids/Home) are clean, tappable-looking outlined buttons directly under the offer, giving an immediate path to shop.
- Hero model image is crisp with no render bugs, broken images, or overlapping text anywhere in the send.
- Weekend framing ("WEEKEND DEAL") gives credible, short-fuse urgency consistent with a Friday-Sunday promo window.

## What's weak

- Three additional themed modules (Change Is Good, All The Feels, Perfect Vibes) after the offer dilute focus — none of them restate or reinforce the discount, so by mid-scroll the reader has forgotten there's a deal at all.
- The "SHOP BY" grid at the bottom repeats Women/Men/Kids/Home (already covered by the top button grid) plus Beauty — pure redundancy with no new information.
- No product pricing or dollar-amount preview anywhere — a shopper can't tell if any specific item they'd click actually crosses the $75/$100 threshold.
- The top-of-email eyebrow "NEED IT SOONER? BUY ONLINE, PICK UP IN-STORE" is a stronger, more differentiated message than anything below it and gets no visual weight — it's tiny gray text above the logo.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email from four merchandising modules to two — keep the top offer block and one supporting lifestyle module (either apparel or home), and drop the redundant bottom "SHOP BY" grid entirely since it just re-lists categories already linked above.
- 2. Add a repeated offer reminder (a slim banner like "20% off $100 — ends Sunday") above the fold break into "Change Is Good," so the discount stays top-of-mind through the scroll instead of vanishing after the first screen.
- 3. Show at least one anchor price point near a hero product (e.g., "$79.99 → qualifies for 20% off") so the threshold offer feels concrete rather than abstract.
- 4. Promote "Buy online, pick up in-store" into a real visual element (icon + line) rather than tiny gray preheader-style text — it's a genuine differentiator against pure-online competitors.
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% off $100 — this weekend only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your weekend just got 15–20% cheaper`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $75, save 15%. Spend $100, save 20%. Ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Buy online, pick up today — plus 15-20% off through the weekend.`

## Technical Audit — H&M "15% or 20% off all weekend"

### 1. Technical Summary
The email renders via a standard MJML/AEM-style responsive table layout with mobile breakpoint overrides, but automated QA shows nearly all outbound links returning HTTP 403 and a 16% overall pass rate, indicating the link-tracking redirector is broken or blocking automated probes.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **12/12 probed links return 403**, including primary CTAs ("Shop now" ×2), navigation (Women/Men/Baby/Kids/HOME), the H&M logo link, both hero product images, and the "Buy online, pick up in-store" link. All routes through the ESP redirector `t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=hc4887b1d-...&did=1715225823&rid=3326930539&erid=...`.
- The consistent 403 across every link (not just one broken URL) points to the redirector itself rejecting the request — likely bot/UA filtering on the tracking domain rather than per-link destination errors. Confirm with a manual click-through from a real client before treating this as a true breakage; if it reproduces manually, this blocks 100% of email traffic to hm.com.
- Two CTA links contain zero-width/invisible characters in anchor text (`‌  ﻿Shop now` — ZWNJ + BOM-like characters), which won't affect the underlying `href` but is worth flagging as it can break some link-text-based QA/analytics tooling and screen reader pronunciation.
- Only 12 of 78 total links were probed (66 skipped per the QA tool's click-likelihood ranking); footer/social links were deprioritized, so untested link count is significant and not fully covered by this audit.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **16 images missing `alt` text**, including both the 1400px and 750px variants of every product image (`cq5dam.web.*.jpeg` assets under `image.hm.com/content/dam/hm/regional-local-activities/...`) and two tracking-pixel/redirector images. Screen readers will announce these as blank or fall back to filename.
- Head includes standard accessibility/rendering safeguards: `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, `format-detection` meta, Yahoo/Gmail-specific CSS overrides (`#MessageViewBody`, `@media screen yahoo`), and a mobile breakpoint at 699px — no issues found in this layer.
- Font loading depends on two external `@import` stylesheets (`fabric.hmgroup.com`, `s1-cdn.hm.com`) with no visible fallback font stack shown in the truncated source; if these fail to load in restrictive clients, text may render in default serif/sans with no explicit fallback confirmed from the excerpt provided.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The embedded JSON-LD (`schema.org/EmailMessage` / `DiscountOffer` / `PromotionCard`) has multiple **empty required fields**: `subjectLine`, `availabilityEnds`, `discountCode`, and `image`/`url` under `PromotionCard` are all blank strings. This will prevent Gmail/Google annotations (e.g., promo carousel, "View offer" actions) from rendering correctly, since the structured data is present but unpopulated.
- `availabilityStarts` is hardcoded to `2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00` — a stale date from what appears to be a reused template, not the current campaign. This won't break rendering but is factually wrong metadata.
- No visible merge-token artifacts (e.g., unresolved `{{first_name}}` or `%%TOKEN%%`) in the visible HTML excerpt.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)
- Unsubscribe link is present in-body (`link text: "Unsubscribe"`) but **also returns 403** via the same redirector — if this reproduces on manual test, it's a CAN-SPAM risk since the required opt-out mechanism would be non-functional.
- **`List-Unsubscribe` header not found** and **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found** — no one-click unsubscribe support detected. Note the QA tool flags this may be a relay-side capture issue (AgentMail) rather than confirmed absence at the source MTA; treat as unconfirmed but worth verifying against raw headers.
- **`Authentication-Results` header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status could not be verified from available data. Same relay-capture caveat applies.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Links use H&M's proprietary redirector scheme (`id`, `did`, `rid`, `erid`, `p1`–`p5` params) rather than standard UTM query parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) — no UTM tagging observed, so standard web-analytics attribution (GA4, etc.) would rely entirely on this redirector correctly resolving and forwarding params to the landing page. Since the redirector is currently returning 403 for all sampled links, **no destination/landing-page alignment could be verified** — this should be re-run once the 403s are resolved.
- Product image destinations point to `image.hm.com/content/dam/hm/regional-local-activities/ame/2026-ame/wk-33/...` — consistent regional/week-coded asset path (`ame`/`wk-33`), no mismatched or cross-campaign asset paths observed in the sample.

### 7. Recommendations
1. **Priority:** Manually click-test 2–3 sampled links (a nav link, a CTA, and Unsubscribe) from a real mail client/IP to confirm whether the 403s are real or a bot-detection artifact of the automated prober; if real, this is a full campaign-breaking issue.
2. Populate the empty JSON-LD fields (`subjectLine`, `discountCode`, `availabilityEnds`, `PromotionCard.image`/`url`) or remove the schema block if not maintained — partial/empty structured data provides no benefit and may cause client-side validation warnings.
3. Update the stale `availabilityStarts: "2018-12-31..."` value to the actual campaign window.
4. Add `alt` attributes to all 16 flagged images, particularly the two product hero images and the redirector-served images.
5. Strip the invisible ZWNJ/BOM characters from the two "Shop now" anchor text strings.
6. Independently verify `List-Unsubscribe`/`List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers and `Authentication-Results` (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) against raw source headers outside the AgentMail relay, since the QA tool's own findings note these may be relay-capture gaps rather than true absences.
