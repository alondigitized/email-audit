# Big deals! 20% off app orders through tomorrow
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-20**
## Executive summary

- This is an app-install push dressed as a Black Friday deal. The black hero says "EARLY BLACK FRIDAY DEALS: 20% OFF IN THE APP" in red on black. The subject promises a deadline ("through tomorrow"), but the body never shows one. The fine print gives 11/21/2026 as the end date, and you only find it by reading the legal block at the bottom.
- The execution is weak. The red-on-black hero text and the red-outlined DOWNLOAD IOS / DOWNLOAD ANDROID buttons are low-contrast and hard to read. The hero itself is a large black block with no imagery. The category tiles (Women, Men, Baby, Kids, Home, Sport) are clean but are plain links with no products or prices. The app pitch is repeated lower down with the same two buttons.
- Compared with the Nov 14–16 "up to 40% off for Members" sends, this one offers a smaller, narrower discount (20%, app only) and asks for more effort (install the app first). The best part is the clear subject line. The weakest part is a hero that hides its own offer. I'd call it a competent template with a hard-to-read hero.

## What's working

- The subject has a concrete offer (20%) and a deadline ("through tomorrow").
- The top banner restates the broader "up to 50% online & in stores" offer and mentions sweaters and denim.
- The category grid covers the whole family (women, men, baby, kids, home, sport) in clean, uncluttered tiles.
- The app benefits section (Member Hub, App Exclusives, Scan & Find, Visual Search) gives real reasons to install.
- The H&M branding is consistent with earlier sends.

## What's weak

- The hero text and buttons are red on black and low-contrast, so the offer and CTAs don't stand out.
- The hero is a big black block with no product or model image, and it pushes the product tiles well down the page.
- The "through tomorrow" deadline isn't visible in the body. It only appears in the fine print.
- The category tiles are generic and unpriced. No single product or hero item is featured.
- The legal block is very long and dense. The app-only 20% sits next to a separate "up to 50%" banner, so two offers compete.
- The CTAs are just app-store download buttons. There is no "shop now" path for people who won't install the app.

## Recommendations

- 1. Rebuild the hero with white or high-contrast type on an image, and put "20% OFF IN THE APP · ENDS TOMORROW" in it.
- 2. Add one clear action: a "Get the app & save 20%" button plus a secondary "Shop online" link.
- 3. Replace the generic category tiles with 3–4 priced products (sweaters, denim) that match the top banner.
- 4. Show the promo code or the in-app steps near the top, and move the details out of the legal block.
- 5. Pick one offer to lead with, either 20% in the app or up to 50% everywhere, and make the other secondary.
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% off in the H&M app ends tomorrow`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call: extra 20% off, app only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Download the app and save on sweaters, denim and more before it ends.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Early Black Friday: up to 50% off plus 20% more in the app.`

## Technical Audit

# Technical Audit: H&M "Big deals! 20% off app orders through tomorrow"

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML uses the ESP's standard responsive structure with per-recipient click tracking through `t19.email.hm.com`. All 4 FAIL results are 403 responses from that redirector, so they look like bot blocking, not confirmed dead links. The other findings are missing image alt text, empty JSON-LD markup, and header checks the relay may have stripped.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **4 probes returned 403, all on the `t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=hc53135b7-…` redirector.**
  - Hero link, text "EARLY BLACK FRIDAY DEALS: UP TO 50% OFF…", id `…f8d37e3a`.
  - Two "Shop now" CTAs, ids `…f8d37e3c` and `…f8d37e3e`.
  - "Unsubscribe", id `…f8d37ea8`.
- Every link in the email goes through the same host, so a uniform 403 points to click-tracker bot filtering. A genuinely broken campaign would not normally fail on all four. The QA data can't show whether real recipients get through, so treat this as unverified.
- The probe never followed the redirects, so final landing URLs are unknown.
- The probe covered only 12 of 86 links. The other 74 (footer, social, secondary) were never checked.
- The unsubscribe link also returned 403 to the probe. This should be confirmed in a real client, because a failing opt-out is a compliance risk (see section 5).

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on 14 images.**
  - 12 are the content images on `image.hm.com/content/dam/hm/regional-local-activities/ame/2026-ame/wk-47/us178_s4/…`. Each appears as a 1400px and a 750px pair (e.g. `1363316002_202604_CN_LB_1174.jpg`, `1342531002_202604_LB_2036.jpg`). The pairs suggest separate desktop and mobile variants, so both need alt text.
  - 2 are `t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=hc53135b7-…,1`, probably the tracking pixel. If so, an explicit `alt=""` is enough.
- **Empty JSON-LD block.**
  - `<script type="application/ld+json">` has empty `subjectLine`, `discountCode`, `description`, `image` and `url`.
  - `availabilityStarts` is a stale `2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00`.
  - Gmail may ignore or reject this markup.
- **Invalid CSS.** In `.is-hidden-desktop`, `line-height:auto!important` is not a valid value, so the declaration is dropped.
- **Web fonts via `@import`.** The two `@import` rules for `fabric.hmgroup.com` and `s1-cdn.hm.com` fonts are stripped by Gmail and Outlook. The font-family fallback stack is in the truncated part of the source, so I can't confirm it.
- The `<script>` and `@import` markup is not supported in several clients.
- The source was truncated, so the table structure, `lang` attribute, `role="presentation"` and preheader weren't checked.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (`{{…}}`, `%%…%%`, `[FIRST_NAME]`) appear in the visible source or QA output. The per-recipient parameters (`rid`, `erid`, `p2`, `p5`) are populated.

## 5. Compliance
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found.** The QA note says the AgentMail relay may not capture these headers, so this is inconclusive. Large senders are expected to include both, and Gmail and Yahoo require them for bulk senders.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown, again because of relay capture. The From domain `email.hm.com` (a subdomain) matches the tracking host's parent domain.
- **Body unsubscribe link present** ("Unsubscribe", `…f8d37ea8`, with `p4`/`p5` identifiers), but the probe got a 403.
- **Physical address:** not visible in the truncated source, so it can't be verified.
- **Plain-text part:** 520 of 598 characters are URLs (87%). It is very thin and nearly all tracking links, which can hurt spam scoring and screen-reader fallback.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- **UTM parameters:** none are visible. Tracking uses opaque redirect params (`p1=DM6231978`, `p3=20261120`), and the final URLs weren't resolved, so UTM presence and landing alignment can't be confirmed.
- **Redirect chain:** all three CTAs and the unsubscribe link are behind the 403-returning redirector, so no landing page was reached.
- **Offer wording:** the subject says "20% off app orders", while the hero link text says "UP TO 50% OFF ONLINE & IN STORES". Whether the landing pages carry the app offer can't be checked.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Re-probe the `t19.email.hm.com` links with a realistic browser User-Agent and follow redirects. This should confirm whether the 403s are only bot filtering and capture the final URLs and UTMs. Verify the unsubscribe link first.
2. Fetch the raw `.eml` from the sending side to check `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results` (SPF, DKIM, DMARC).
3. Add `alt` text to the 12 content images. Set `alt=""` on the tracking pixels.
4. Populate the JSON-LD fields or remove the block, and drop the stale 2018 `availabilityStarts`.
5. Replace `line-height:auto` with a valid value or remove it.
6. Add more descriptive text to the plain-text part so it isn't mostly URLs.
7. Re-run the audit on the full, untruncated HTML to check the footer address, `lang`, preheader and table structure.
