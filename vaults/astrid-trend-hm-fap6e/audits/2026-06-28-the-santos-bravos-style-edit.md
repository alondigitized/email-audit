# The SANTOS BRAVOS style edit
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-28**
## Executive summary

- H&M is back with a brand collaboration send — this time spotlighting a "SANTOS BRAVOS" x LOTTO style edit. The creative goes full editorial: a large olive-toned hero image, athletic figure, and a tagline ("Sporty, stylish and easy to wear — discover the LOTTO H&M pieces everyone wants right now") that attempts to launch a product family rather than push a discount. Compared to the previous seven days of sale drumbeating, this is the right creative instinct — give the list a reason to open that isn't just another price cut.
- The problem is execution: the collaboration brand name "SANTOS BRAVOS" will be opaque to most subscribers who haven't been primed on who or what that is. The hero doesn't explain the name, the LOTTO badge is buried in the body copy, and there's no product detail — just a generic "SHOP NOW." The email trusts name recognition it hasn't yet earned with this audience.
- The sale banner running across the very top ("SALE JUST GOT BETTER: UP TO 60% OFF + NEW STYLES ADDED FROM $7.99") is both a lifeline and a contradiction. It gives the email a commercial reason to click — but it also undermines the editorial pitch, making the whole thing feel like a sale email in costume.

## What's working

- **Subject line stands out in context.** After six sale-driven subjects in a row, "The SANTOS BRAVOS style edit" is genuinely different — editorial, curious, under 30 characters, mobile-friendly.
- **Sale banner doubles as strong preheader.** "SALE JUST GOT BETTER: UP TO 60% OFF + NEW STYLES ADDED FROM $7.99" pairs well with the editorial subject — subject teases the collab, banner anchors a commercial hook.
- **Hero visual is clean and on-brand.** The olive/yellow palette and athletic framing are cohesive; no broken images or layout breaks.
- **Clean hierarchy.** Top-to-bottom read is logical: banner → logo → hero → CTA → shop-by nav. The eye follows without confusion.

## What's weak

- **"SANTOS BRAVOS" is unexplained.** The name appears in the subject, the hero headline, and nowhere else is it explained. A subscriber who doesn't already know who Santos Bravos or LOTTO is gets no context — and the body copy ("LOTTO H&M pieces everyone wants right now") adds brand soup rather than clarity.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is dead weight.** Every send this week has a "SHOP NOW" button. This collab warranted "Shop the LOTTO Edit" or "See the Collection" — something that rewards the editorial framing.
- **No product-level specifics.** The "best sellers" send on June 24th showed actual pieces with names — this one shows a single hero and no product imagery below the fold in the visible render. It's a campaign with nothing to touch.
- **The sale banner and the editorial pitch conflict.** The subject says "style edit," but the first thing you see is a sale flash. H&M can't decide if this is content marketing or a clearance email, and it reads as both, weakly.
- **"SHOP BY" nav feels like footer filler.** WOMEN / MEN / KIDS / HOME / BEAUTY below the hero adds no editorial relevance to the Santos Bravos pitch — it signals the email ran out of things to show.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Explain the collab in the hero subtext.** Replace "Sporty, stylish and easy to wear" with something like "LOTTO × H&M — the Santos Bravos kit is here: game-ready pieces built for the stands and the street." Give the name a world.
- 2. **Swap the CTA.** "SHOP NOW" → "Shop the LOTTO × H&M Edit" — matches the editorial frame and sets expectation for what the landing page is.
- 3. **Show at least two to three products beneath the hero.** Even thumbnails with names and prices turn a vague brand story into something shoppable. The June 24 "bestsellers" send proved H&M can do this — deploy it here.
- 4. **Move or soften the sale banner.** The 60% off strip on top of an editorial send is cognitive dissonance. Either commit to editorial (remove it) or commit to sale and reframe the whole email around the discount with LOTTO as the hook, not the headline.
- 5. **Rewrite the subtext to include a deadline.** If the collab is limited or launching now, say so: "Available now — limited LOTTO × H&M styles in the summer sale."
