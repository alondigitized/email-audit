# Sale is now up to 70% off + new styles added
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- H&M has escalated the sale floor by another 10 points in 24 hours — yesterday it was "SALE JUST GOT BETTER: UP TO 60% OFF," today it's "UP TO 70% OFF + NEW STYLES ADDED." The ratchet tactic is transparent at this point: a subscriber who opened yesterday's email will immediately recognize this as the same send with a bigger number bolted on. That's not inherently wrong, but it does erode trust in the discount claim.
- The creative itself is clean. The hero text is large, high-contrast, and unmistakable. The "FREE SHIPPING OVER $52 FOR H&M MEMBERS" banner at the top is a smart preheader — it adds loyalty-layer value that the subject doesn't cover. The category tiles (WOMEN / MEN / BABY / KIDS / HOME) do routing work without cluttering the fold.
- The miss is structural: there is no single dominant hero CTA button. The email asks readers to self-select into a category tile immediately, which is fine for a browse session but kills impulse action on the sale. A subscriber who doesn't know what they want leaves without clicking. Combined with no time deadline, the urgency the subject promises dissolves the moment you open the email.

## What's working

- **Hero copy lands instantly.** "UP TO 70% OFF + NEW STYLES ADDED" in large bold type over a clean white background — the hierarchy is unambiguous.
- **Preheader is additive.** "FREE SHIPPING OVER $52 FOR H&M MEMBERS" is a different benefit from the 70%-off headline, so it genuinely extends the subject rather than repeating it.
- **Subject line is under 50 characters** (46) and fully mobile-legible without truncation.
- **Loyalty signal is above the fold.** The members free-shipping banner is the very first element — useful for H&M loyalty members who are the core of this list.
- **SHOP BY editorial section** gives the email a visual resting place after the hard-sale hero, softening the transactional tone for browsing-mode subscribers.

## What's weak

- **No time deadline anywhere in the email body.** The June 25 flash sale ("25% OFF UNTIL 12AM ET") is the reference point — that had a clock, this doesn't. "Up to 70% off" with no end date is a standing invitation, not a reason to act now.
- **The escalation from 60% → 70% in 24 hours feels manufactured.** A loyal subscriber who opened yesterday's email will clock this immediately, which risks the brand looking like it's playing games with the headline number.
- **No single hero CTA button.** The first clickable prompt is six category tiles (WOMEN / MEN / BABY / KIDS / HOME / FIND A STORE). Category routing is useful for navigation but is not a conversion mechanism.
- **No specific product or price anchor.** "From $7.99" (June 27) was more clickable than "up to 70% off" — the former gives a concrete floor, the latter is abstract.
- **Dense fine-print block at the bottom** undercuts the clean aesthetic and, more importantly, contains the exclusions that "up to" language depends on — putting that work entirely on readers who won't read it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline.** Even "Ends Thursday" or "Sale ends soon — while supplies last" changes the behavioral equation. The June 25 flash sale showed that H&M's list responds to time pressure.
- 2. **Introduce one price anchor in the hero.** Pair the 70% headline with a floor — "Up to 70% off — styles from $5.99" — so the offer has a concrete handle. This is the single highest-lift change.
- 3. **Replace the category-tile grid with a single hero CTA button** — "SHOP THE SALE" or "SHOP WOMEN'S SALE / SHOP MEN'S SALE" as two buttons, not six tiles — then let category navigation live below.
- 4. **Acknowledge the escalation.** If the sale genuinely got bigger, say so: "We just added 10 more points off — up to 70% now." That reframes the jump as news rather than creative fatigue.
- 5. **Surface one featured product.** Pick a single hero item at a specific marked-down price (e.g., a summer dress, $19.99 → $9.99) to give the browse-mode reader a first rung on the ladder.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Styles from $5.99 — sale hits 70% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New styles dropped into the sale — shop while sizes last`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Members get free shipping on everything in the sale`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email contains a broken JSON-LD structured data block with multiple unpopulated fields and a hardcoded 2018 timestamp, and uses CSS `@import` for fonts in a way that fails silently in Gmail and Outlook. ### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

The promotion card URL was never populated — Google Inbox Actions and any schema-aware client will fail to render a deep link.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS `@import` inside `<style>` block — not supported in Gmail:**
```css
<style>
  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
</style>
```
Gmail strips `<style>` content that starts with `@import`. Both font families will silently fall back to the browser/client default in Gmail, which is the dominant client for this sender's likely audience. Fonts should be declared via `<link>` or loaded via a compatible method.

**Viewport meta missing `initial-scale=1`:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width"/>
```
Standard is `content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1"`. Some mobile clients may zoom on load without it.

**Duplicate CSS rules in mobile breakpoint (`@media (max-width:699px)`):** The following selectors are declared twice inside the same block with identical values, adding byte weight without effect:
- `.department-title`
- `.department-link`
- `.cta-link-mobile-border-ns`
- `.cta-link-mobile, .set_font_interactive_standalone_link_default`
- `.set_font_heading_xlarge`

Not a rendering blocker, but inflates email size unnecessarily.

**`schema.org` context uses `http://` not `https://`:**
```json
"@context":"http://schema.org/"
```
Google currently resolves both, but `https://` is the canonical form and `http://` may generate schema validation warnings.
