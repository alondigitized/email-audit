# New sale arrivals up to 70% off
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- This is H&M doubling down on its sale-momentum play — "New sale arrivals up to 70% off" lands two days after the July 9th flash sale closed and one day after the editorial reset ("Sorted: your city summer wardrobe"). The brand is pivoting back into price mode fast, and the combination of a "SALE" stamp, "UP TO 70% OFF," and "+ STYLES UNDER $6.99" stacks two distinct value propositions in the hero. That's aggressive, and it signals that the editorial breather was tactical rather than a true cadence reset.
- The problem is structural: the email is doing three things at once — sale arrivals, a Back-to-School Member Giveaway, and a department nav grid — and none of them wins. The hero offer (70% off + $6.99 styles) is concrete, but it links to a generic sale page rather than a curated "new arrivals" collection. Then, mid-email, a completely unrelated sweepstakes (win a $200 gift card + Back-to-School bundle) hijacks the scroll path. The two don't reinforce each other.
- The net result is a send that will perform below the July 9th flash sale despite being bolder on discount depth. The audience reads "70% off" and expects curation; what follows is a category nav and a giveaway entry form. That gap between the subject promise and the body delivery is where click-through dies.

## What's working

- **Hero discount is the most aggressive number H&M has shown this month** — "UP TO 70% OFF" beats the July 9th 25% and is visually dominant above the fold.
- **"+STYLES UNDER $6.99" anchors the low end concretely** — a price floor is a useful second hook for value shoppers who dismiss "up to X% off" as marketing math.
- **The giveaway module adds a non-purchase CTA** — "BACK-TO-SCHOOL BUNDLE" giveaway is timely (July back-to-school window) and gives fence-sitters a reason to engage without buying.
- **Category nav grid (WOMEN / MEN / KIDS / HOME / BEAUTY) is clean** and gives re-engaged browsers a clear path if the hero doesn't match their category.
- **No visible render bugs** — images load, text is readable, no broken layout.

## What's weak

- **Hero image and offer are mismatched** — the model is wearing a black minimalist outfit (not a sale-arrival vibe), and nothing in the visual screams "new arrivals" or urgency.
- **"New arrivals" is promised but not delivered** — there are zero product cards below the hero; after the hero you get a sweepstakes module, then a plain department nav. No actual sale arrival items are shown.
- **The giveaway module breaks the sale funnel cold** — a sweepstakes ask mid-scroll on a sale email is an attention tax; the two CTAs compete rather than support.
- **No deadline or urgency mechanic visible** — the July 9th send had a midnight countdown; this one has nothing. The 70% number alone doesn't move you.
- **Fine-print disclaimer block at the bottom is unusually long and visible** — the dense legal text at the footer (likely tied to the sweepstakes) is a scroll-stopper in a bad way and reinforces hesitation.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add at least 6 product cards below the hero** — "New arrivals" in the subject is a promise; fulfill it with a shoppable grid of actual discounted new-arrival items. This single change likely lifts CTR 20–30% on a send like this.
- 2. **Give the offer a deadline** — "Sale arrivals refreshed today — new prices drop at midnight" or similar. The July 9th flash sale outperformed precisely because of a credible time boundary.
- 3. **Split the giveaway into its own dedicated send** — the Back-to-School Bundle sweepstakes is a solid retention play but it belongs in a standalone email, not buried under a 70%-off sale hero. Mixing the two dilutes both.
- 4. **Swap the hero image for a product flat or a styled multi-item shot** — a single model in a black dress reads editorial, not sale discovery. A grid of colorful marked-down items would be far more conversion-oriented.
- 5. **Tighten the subject** — "New sale arrivals up to 70% off" is 34 chars and workmanlike, but "New arrivals: up to 70% off — from $6.99" pulls both hooks into one scannable line.
- **Subject Alt A:** `70% off + styles from $6.99 — new arrivals just landed`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New arrivals hit the sale. Up to 70% off.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh drops, marked down. Shop new-in sale picks before they go.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New to sale: summer styles from $6.99. Plus enter our back-to-school giveaway.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` in `<style>` blocks (high risk):**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` is unsupported in Outlook (all versions), Yahoo Mail, and several mobile clients. Custom fonts will silently fall back to system fonts — acceptable only if the fallback stack is defined. No fallback font-family is visible in the truncated `<head>` CSS.

**Responsive breakpoint is non-standard:**
- `@media (max-width:699px)` — industry standard for mobile email is `600px`. At 601–699px (many tablet viewports) the mobile layout applies but was likely designed for 375px screens. Risk of layout overflow at mid-range widths.

**`img.image + div { display: none !important; }`** — suppresses alt-text containers in some clients; valid technique, but if images fail to load the alt text fallback is also hidden. Accessibility impact on image-blocked renders.

**Alt text** — cannot audit without full body source.
