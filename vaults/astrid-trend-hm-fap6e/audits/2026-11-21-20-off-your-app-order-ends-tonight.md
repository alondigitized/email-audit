# 20% off your app order ends tonight!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-21**
## Executive summary

- This is H&M's second 20%-off-in-the-app send in two days, and it has the same weakness as the 2026-11-20 "Big deals! 20% off app orders through tomorrow" email. It is an app-install ad, not a shopping email. The hero is a woman in a denim jacket and wide-leg jeans with "LAST DAY / 20% OFF IN THE APP" above it. Every button below it says DOWNLOAD IOS or DOWNLOAD ANDROID. There is no product link and no shopping path in the body.
- The urgency is credible. "Ends tonight" in the subject matches "LAST DAY" in the hero, and the legal copy gives 11/21/2026 as the end date. But the offer is gated behind an install, and the code is hidden ("Download to unlock your code"). That is a lot of friction for someone who is already a subscriber. The black banner above it advertises "UP TO 50% OFF IN STORES*" with "The best finds are even better in person," which pulls against the app message. The result is two competing offers and no clear primary one.
- Judgement: a clean template with a real deadline, but weak on click appeal. It also repeats a push H&M has already made this week.

## What's working

- The "LAST DAY" eyebrow and "20% OFF IN THE APP" headline are large, centered and easy to read.
- The subject deadline ("ends tonight") matches the hero, so the urgency is consistent.
- The denim hero image is clean and fits the season.
- The app feature list (Member Hub, Scan & Find, Visual Search) gives some reason to install.
- The Shop By grid (Women, Men, Kids, Home, Beauty) offers a way into the site.

## What's weak

- Every primary CTA is a download button, so there is no direct way to shop the sale.
- The discount code is hidden until after install, which makes the offer feel gated.
- The red-on-black banner offers a competing "up to 50% off in stores" message and is hard to read.
- The download buttons appear twice, in outlined, low-emphasis styling, and look like secondary links.
- The legal block is very long and dense, and the exclusions (sale items, gift cards, collaborations) take away from the honesty of the offer.
- The Shop By grid sits far down the email, below the second pair of download buttons. The Home and Beauty tiles are also laid out unevenly, with two tiles in the last row.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a single filled, high-contrast button above the fold that says "Shop 20% off in the app" and links to a deep link that works whether or not the app is installed.
- 2. Show the code or the savings mechanism in the email itself, or say clearly how it works ("Open the app, code applies at checkout").
- 3. Drop the in-store 50% banner from app-focused sends, or merge it into one offer.
- 4. Move the Shop By grid above the app feature list, and use specific product tiles with prices.
- 5. Collapse the legal text into a short summary with a link to full terms.
- 6. Space out the repeated 20% app sends, or vary the creative between them.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last day: 20% off in the H&M app`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 20% app code expires at midnight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Open the app, find your code, save on denim, knits and more before tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Today only: app-exclusive pricing on women's, men's and kids.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is built on the H&M/Oracle-style tracking template, with all links wrapped in `t19.email.hm.com/r/` redirects. Every probed CTA and the unsubscribe link returned 403 to the automated checker, and the source has empty structured-data fields and missing image alt text. The 403s are probably bot filtering rather than real breakage, but they could not be confirmed.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **4 of 4 probed links returned 403.** The affected links are:
  - Two "Shop now" CTAs: `...f8d5f4b4` and `...f8d5f4b6`
  - "Scan & Find": `...f8d5f4de`
  - "Unsubscribe": `...f8d5f510`
- All four are on `https://t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=hf2c33876-...&did=1427498891&rid=3326930539`.
- The uniform 403 across the whole tracking domain, including the unsubscribe link, suggests the redirector rejects non-browser or datacenter clients. It does not necessarily mean the links are dead for recipients. **Destinations were not resolved, so this is unverified.** Retry with a browser-like UA or from a residential IP before treating it as a defect.
- The two "Shop now" links use different link IDs (`f8d5f4b4` vs `f8d5f4b6`), so each CTA is tracked separately. That is fine.
- **Link text contains invisible characters.** The "Shop now" text is `"‌  ﻿Shop now"`, with a zero-width non-joiner, spaces and a BOM (U+FEFF). Some clients and screen readers may announce or trim this inconsistently. The link text is also duplicated across different CTAs.
- **Probe coverage was limited.** 12 links were probed and 58 skipped, so most of the email's links are unchecked.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Four images are missing alt text:**
  - Two are tracking-wrapped `src` values, `https://t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=hf2c33876-7e9b-4536-ae39-bab0231bdc32,5515e78b,1...`.
  - Two are `cq5dam.web.1400.1400.jpeg` and `cq5dam.web.750.750.jpeg`, from `image.hm.com/content/dam/hm/regional-local-activities/ame/2026-ame/wk-47/ca0101_s4/...`.
- Without alt text, clients with images blocked show an empty or filename-only slot. Screen readers may announce the URL or filename.
- The two `cq5dam` images are different sizes (1400 and 750), which suggests a desktop/mobile pair. Verify that only one of them is exposed at each breakpoint and that both carry alt text.
- **Web fonts.** `@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/...)` and `s1-cdn.hm.com/.../oces.css` are stripped by Gmail and by Outlook desktop. A fallback font stack should be present, but the truncated source doesn't let me confirm it.
- Not an issue: the `<meta name="viewport">`, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` and `mso-hide` handling are present.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (`{{...}}`, `%%...%%`, `[FIRSTNAME]`) are visible in the available source. The per-recipient values (`rid`, `erid`, `p2`, `p5`) are rendered in the URLs. The source is truncated, so the rest of the body is unchecked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) were not found.** The checker notes the AgentMail relay may strip them. Treat this as **unverified**. Check the raw headers of the original message, because Gmail and Yahoo require one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders.
- **Authentication-Results is missing**, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment could not be assessed. Verify via `dig` on `email.hm.com` and the original message headers. The sender `newsletter@email.hm.com` is a subdomain, so alignment should be checked on that subdomain.
- **In-body unsubscribe is present**: the "Unsubscribe" link carries `p4`/`p5` per-recipient tokens. It returned 403 to the probe (see §2), so it is unconfirmed functional.
- The physical postal address could not be confirmed in the truncated source.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- No UTM parameters are visible on the redirect URLs. The campaign identifiers are `p1=DM6231948`, `p3=20261121` (the send date) and `did=1427498891`. UTMs may be appended at the redirect destination.
- **Final landing URLs are unknown** because the redirects returned 403. Landing-page alignment with the "20% off your app order" offer and "Scan & Find" could not be verified.
- **The structured-data block has empty or stale fields.** The JSON-LD `<script type="application/ld+json">` contains:
  - `"subjectLine":""`
  - `"discountCode":""`
  - `"availabilityEnds":""`
  - `"availabilityStarts":"2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"` (a stale template default)
  - `"image":""` and `"url":""` in the `PromotionCard`
- Gmail promotion annotations will not work from this. At best the block is ignored, and at worst it is flagged as invalid markup.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Re-probe the `t19.email.hm.com` redirects with a browser-like client and record the final destination URL and status for each CTA. Check them for UTM or campaign tagging.
2. Inspect the raw original message for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC results, since the relay may have hidden them.
3. Add descriptive `alt` text to all four flagged images. Use `alt=""` for purely decorative ones.
4. Populate the JSON-LD (`subjectLine`, `discountCode`, `availabilityStarts` and `availabilityEnds`, `image`, `url`), or remove the block. Do not ship the `2018-12-31` placeholder.
5. Strip the zero-width characters and BOM from the CTA link text, and use distinct, descriptive link text for each CTA.
6. Increase the plain-text part's real copy. It is 514 of 592 characters URLs, which can hurt spam scoring and the text-only experience.
