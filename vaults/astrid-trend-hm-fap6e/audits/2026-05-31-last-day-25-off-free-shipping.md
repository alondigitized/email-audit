# Last day: 25% off + free shipping
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-31**
## Executive summary

- This is the "last day" urgency companion to yesterday's May 30 Weekend Deal send — same offer (25% off $125+, free shipping), same audience, different urgency signal. H&M runs this playbook often: launch the deal, send the deadline reminder, done. It works because the subject line is genuinely one of H&M's cleaner recent efforts: short, concrete, zero noise. The email itself is tidy — three category blocks (Endless Sun/Women, Cool Escape/Men, Sunny Mood/Kids) filled with new-arrival product grids, strong photography, and stripped layout.
- The limitation is structural: there's no hero CTA button. The offer headline floats above a cold product grid with nothing in between to nudge the click. "DON'T WAIT" as preheader is wasted real estate — it restates the urgency the subject already owns, then adds nothing. And with seven emails in six days this week (this is the seventh), list fatigue is a real risk even if this individual send is technically solid.
- On balance: this is a competent, on-brand deadline reminder. The subject line is the star. The body is a supporting cast that doesn't punch above its weight.

## What's working

- **"Last day" in the subject** is the most direct urgency signal H&M has used all week — cleaner than anything in the May 30 or earlier sends.
- **Offer reads instantly.** "25% OFF $125 + FREE SHIPPING" in large bold type occupies the first viewport with no competing elements.
- **"New Arrivals" framing** elevates the email above a clearance-shelf feel — this reads as value on fresh product, not end-of-season remnants.
- **Clean category structure** (Women / Men / Kids) makes the grid navigable for the right shopper without requiring scrolling commitment.
- **No render bugs.** Every image loads, no overlapping text, layout is intact across the visible email.

## What's weak

- **No hero CTA button.** The offer headline ends and the product grid starts immediately — there's no "Shop Now" or "Claim Offer" button anchoring the hero.
- **Preheader is squandered.** "DON'T WAIT" is 9 characters that repeat the subject's urgency without adding product, deadline, or category context.
- **$125 minimum is buried in the headline copy.** "$125 +" reads as shorthand for "min spend $125" but a skimmer can easily misread it as a flat deal — the asterisk doesn't help.
- **Seven emails in six days.** Even with good individual sends, H&M is running at a pace that erodes open rates. A subscriber who saw the May 28 Pride email, May 29 Summer Shop, May 30 LA event, May 30 Weekend Deal, and now this will register noise before deal.
- **Product grid is identical in structure to yesterday's send** — no creative differentiation to signal "this is the last-chance version."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hero CTA button.** A single "Shop New Arrivals" or "Shop the Deal" button directly under the offer headline would anchor the click for users who don't scroll to the product grid.
- 2. **Rewrite the preheader to carry new information.** "DON'T WAIT" wastes the one piece of real estate the subject doesn't already own. Use it to name a category, add a specific deadline, or reinforce the new-arrival angle.
- 3. **Clarify the $125 threshold in the hero.** Rewrite "25% OFF $125 + FREE SHIPPING" to "25% OFF ORDERS OF $125+" — the current formatting reads ambiguously.
- 4. **Surface one hero product.** Pick one editorial hero image (a strong Women's or Men's look) above the product grid to give the eye somewhere to land before the multi-column scroll.
- 5. **Consider skipping a product category.** The Women + Men + Kids grid makes the email long and dilutes focus. A persona-targeted version (Women's only for a Women's segment) would likely convert better.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends tonight: 25% off new arrivals`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your last chance: 25% off + free ship`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New summer arrivals. Offer ends at midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Women's, men's, kids' — all new, all 25% off today only`


Gmail strips `@import` rules entirely. Outlook ignores external CSS. Apple Mail may load them, but external font requests are blocked in privacy-relay scenarios.
