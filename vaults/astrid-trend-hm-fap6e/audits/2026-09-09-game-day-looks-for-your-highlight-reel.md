# Game-day looks for your highlight reel
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-09**
## Executive summary

- This is the first H&M send in over a week that isn't a Labor Day discount rerun — and it shows. After five straight days of "up to 50% off" variants (09/03 through 09/08), "Game-day looks for your highlight reel" pivots to a football-season merchandising email built around new arrivals, a themed hero ("GO TEAM"), and a member free-shipping offer instead of a sitewide percentage. The layout is clean, the product grid is well organized by category (jackets, denim), and there's no render breakage anywhere in the scroll.
- The tradeoff is that this send trades urgency for freshness. There's no discount, no deadline, no "ends tonight" — just full-price new arrivals and a $50 free-shipping threshold for members. That's a legitimate cadence reset after a promo-fatigued week, but it also means the click case rests entirely on product desire rather than a deal.
- Net: a well-executed seasonal reset that finally breaks the Labor Day loop, held back by a subject line that undersells the actual content and a CTA structure that's more "browse" than "buy."

## What's working

- Clean break from the five-day "up to 50% off" Labor Day loop — this is genuinely new creative, not a re-skin.
- Clear visual hierarchy: hero image → "GO TEAM" headline → WOMEN/MEN nav buttons → product grid, all easy to scan.
- Product tiles show price and "New Arrival" tags consistently, which sets clear expectations before a click.
- No render bugs — images, type, and spacing all render cleanly through the full scroll.
- Free shipping over $50 for members is a real, usable benefit foregrounded at the very top.

## What's weak

- No actual discount or urgency anywhere in the body — after a week of 50%-off messaging, the sudden full-price pivot may read as a letdown to price-trained subscribers.
- "GO TEAM" / "Game-day" framing is thin — outside of the sweatshirt/jacket layout, nothing in the product selection (slim jeans, baggy denim) visually reinforces a football theme.
- Primary CTAs are generic navigation (SHOP NOW, WOMEN, MEN) rather than a single specific, compelling action.
- No social proof, sizing/fit info, or reviews anywhere in the product tiles.
- "DENIM MVPS" section has no visible connective tissue to the game-day concept — feels like two unrelated modules stitched together.

## Recommendations

- 1. Sharpen the subject to name the actual hook (free shipping or new arrivals) since "game-day" doesn't fully deliver on the body content — right now the subject over-promises a themed collection that's only one section of the email.
- 2. Give "DENIM MVPS" its own thematic framing or merge it into "GO TEAM" so the email doesn't feel like two unrelated sends stapled together.
- 3. Swap generic WOMEN/MEN/SHOP NOW buttons for a single specific CTA tied to the strongest product (e.g., "SHOP THE JACKETS").
- 4. Add a light urgency or scarcity cue (limited sizes, new-arrival countdown) since there's no price incentive doing that work this time.
