# 25% off flash sale starts now
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-10**
## Executive summary

- H&M is running a true flash sale — midnight ET deadline, 25% off sitewide — and the hero communicates it clearly: big red type, offer visible immediately, no ambiguity. For a one-night urgency trigger, the email does its primary job. The "FLASH SALE / 25% OFF / UNTIL 12AM ET" block is the cleanest hero H&M has sent in this batch of emails.
- The problem is context. Three days ago H&M was closing out a **50% off** menswear sale (June 7 "Last day" send scored 9/10 here). A subscriber with any memory of that send is looking at tonight's "flash sale" and doing the math: this is half the discount. That undercuts the urgency framing badly. "Flash sale" implies an exclusive, time-limited deal better than normal — but 25% is a step down from what the inbox held last Tuesday.
- The email also suffers from the standard H&M structural weakness: no single hero CTA. You get a category grid (WOMEN / MEN / KIDS / HOME) instead of one button that says "SHOP THE FLASH SALE." For a midnight-deadline email, that diffusion of action is a real miss.

## What's working

- **Hero is immediate and legible.** "25% OFF / UNTIL 12AM ET" in large red type on white — lands without a scroll, reads clearly on mobile.
- **Deadline adds real urgency.** Midnight ET is specific and credible; this isn't "limited time" vagueness.
- **"Flash sale" framing is new.** After two editorial mood-board sends (June 8–9), this feels like a distinct event rather than another promotional nudge.
- **No render bugs.** Layout is clean, images load, category tiles are sharp.

## What's weak

- **25% off reads weak against June 5–7's 50% off.** Any subscriber who remembers the menswear sale 3–5 days ago won't feel like this is a better deal.
- **No single primary CTA button.** Four category tiles replace a hero action button; clicks scatter rather than concentrate on the flash sale.
- **Subheadline is soft for a flash sale.** "Summer is just getting started – dive in ✓" is editorial copy, not urgency copy. It deflates the midnight-deadline pressure the hero sets up.
- **No featured products.** A flash sale with no product imagery forces subscribers to imagine what they might want — more friction, fewer clicks.
- **SHOP BY section adds clutter.** A second five-category grid (WOMEN / MEN / KIDS / HOME / BEAUTY) below the primary grid dilutes the single-action message.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a single hero CTA button.** "SHOP 25% OFF — ENDS MIDNIGHT" below the headline. This is the one change that lifts click rate the most.
- 2. **Feature 2–3 specific products.** A summer dress at $X → $Y, a polo at $X → $Y. Concrete product + price = impulse.
- 3. **Sharpen the subheadline.** Drop the "Summer is just getting started" line; replace with something that reinforces the deadline: "Tonight only — wear it all summer."
- 4. **Remove the SHOP BY secondary grid.** One category grid is enough; the second one adds noise.
- 5. **Contextualize the offer if you can.** If 25% genuinely covers more SKUs than the June 7 sale, say so: "25% off *everything* — no exclusions." That turns the lower % into a breadth story.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Flash sale: 25% off ends at midnight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Tonight only: 25% off everything`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `No code needed — 25% off storewide until 12AM ET tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `All categories, no exclusions — your cart is waiting.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading will fail silently in most clients:**

```html
<style>
  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
</style>
```

`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is stripped by Gmail, Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo Mail. Fonts will fall back to system defaults. Correct approach is to load web fonts via `<link rel="stylesheet">` in `<head>` for clients that support it, with stack fallbacks.
