# Peak-summer shorts from $12.49
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- H&M is running a tight, well-produced shorts-focused sale email on the heels of yesterday's "Up to 50% off is on the rise" send — same discount depth, same sitewide claim, different subject angle. The subject line is legitimately one of the better ones in the recent run: a specific price anchor ($12.49) tied to a specific category (shorts) in 30 characters. The body delivers on it with a clean product grid showing before/after prices across five distinct shorts styles.
- The problem is the cadence. Any subscriber who opened yesterday's email is seeing essentially the same offer repackaged one day later. The "UP TO 50% OFF SITEWIDE" hero headline is doing a lot of heavy lifting for a product grid where several marked-down items appear to be 20-30% off, not 50%. "New Arrival" labels on some shorts sit awkwardly next to the sitewide sale claim — it's unclear whether new arrivals are also discounted.
- The tax-free days banner at the top is genuinely additive — it complements the shorts discount rather than competing with it — but it fragments the visual entry point for a reader scanning in three seconds. Overall: a solid B-grade execution undercut by discount repetition and a weak urgency mechanism.

## What's working

- **Subject line is the email's best asset** — "Peak-summer shorts from $12.49" is specific, short (30 chars), mobile-safe, and price-anchored without screaming discount.
- **Product grid delivers the subject's promise** — five distinct shorts styles shown with before/after pricing make the value tangible rather than abstract.
- **Tax-free days banner adds a second deal angle** without cannibalizing the main message; it functions as an effective preheader complement.
- **Category tabs (WOMEN / MEN / KIDS / HOME)** give readers a clear navigation fork immediately under the hero — no dead ends.
- **Female-led hero model** is demographically on-target and the photography is clean with no render issues visible.

## What's weak

- **Cadence fatigue** — yesterday's send was also "up to 50% off sitewide." Two consecutive identical discount-depth emails erode urgency.
- **"New Arrival" + sale headline tension** — some featured shorts are labeled New Arrival, which sits awkwardly under "UP TO 50% OFF SITEWIDE"; the reader can't tell if new arrivals are also discounted.
- **No deadline** — "PEAK-SUMMER SAVINGS" is a seasonal gesture, not a credible close date. Nothing motivates clicking now vs. later.
- **CTA copy is navigation, not action** — "WOMEN / MEN / KIDS / HOME" buttons tell you where you'd land, not why you'd click; no verb+noun phrasing anywhere in the product section.
- **No social proof** — zero review indicators or "trending" signals despite a product grid that would naturally support them.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Break the 50%-off loop** — after back-to-back sitewide sale sends, this email needs a different frame to feel fresh. Lead with the $12.49 price story in the hero copy rather than the percentage discount: "Shorts from $12" as the headline differentiates from yesterday's send and anchors value more concretely.
- 2. **Add a deadline** — even "Ends Sunday" or "While sizes last" in the hero subtext converts a browse email into an act-now email.
- 3. **Resolve the New Arrival ambiguity** — label sale items with a red "SALE" badge and new arrivals separately; don't let them share a grid under a sitewide discount headline.
- 4. **Rewrite CTA buttons** — "SHOP WOMEN'S SHORTS" beats "WOMEN" every time. One verb, one noun.
- 5. **Surface one hero product** — pick the $9.99 Bermuda short, make it the clickable centerpiece of the hero, and drive all eyeballs to one action before the grid.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Shorts under $15 — sale ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `5 styles of shorts, all on sale from $12.49`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Bermuda, denim, sweat — stocked and marked down`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Tax-free days are coming + sitewide sale up to 50% off`

This is a hardcoded 2018 timestamp that was never updated — likely a copy-paste artifact from an original template. While this does not affect CAN-SPAM compliance directly, it will cause incorrect promotional-tab rendering in Gmail and may trigger spam classifier signals.

**Multiple empty required JSON-LD fields:**

"discountCode":""      // DiscountOffer — no code (expected if no code)
"image":""             // PromotionCard — no promo image URL
"url":""               // PromotionCard — no deep link URL

These empty strings render the Gmail promotion annotation non-functional.

**Unsubscribe / CAN-SPAM:** Footer section is present (`.footer-unsubscribe` class confirmed in CSS). Full `List-Unsubscribe` header and physical address cannot be verified from HTML alone — must check raw MIME headers and the rendered footer.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Cannot confirm UTM coverage without the full `<a href>` inventory. The truncated source ends before any link tags. Required checks:

- Every product CTA should carry `utm_source=email&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=<campaign-id>`.

### 7. Recommendations

**P1 — Fix before send:**
1. Update `DiscountOffer.availabilityStarts` from `2018-12-31` to the actual campaign start date.
2. Populate `EmailMessage.subjectLine`, `PromotionCard.url`, and `PromotionCard.image` in the JSON-LD block to restore Gmail promotional annotation.

3. Pull full HTML and audit all `<a href>` values for UTM completeness and link resolution.

5. Confirm all product `<img>` tags have descriptive `alt` text.
