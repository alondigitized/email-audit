# New summer details
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- This is H&M's "Summer Home" editorial send — a pure home decor and lifestyle push showcasing tabletop, soft furnishings, and candles. There is no sitewide or category discount visible; everything is priced at retail, and the hero cop is "Refresh your home with the new summer details." After yesterday's "New sale arrivals up to 70% off" blitz, this pivot to a full-price editorial feels intentional — a breather play, like the July 10th "Sorted" send.
- The problem is the same one that hurt "Sorted": zero incentive to click now. The product grid is long and scroll-heavy with a lot of white space, and the prices — $9.99 coasters, $8.99 pillow covers, $5.99 glasses — are so low they almost undercut the aspirational mood. The "Summer Home" concept is real and the photography is clean, but the email lacks a single hook that earns an immediate click.
- The second module — a "Member Giveaway / Back-to-School Bundle" with a $200 gift card — is buried below a long product scroll and feels mismatched with the home decor theme above it. The email is doing two unrelated jobs and does neither particularly well.

## What's working

- Photography is strong and on-brand — the leopard-print throw/sofa hero and the straw coasters read instantly as summer home
- Product grid is clean and scannable; prices are visible under each item
- "New Arrival" badges on select items add a freshness signal without a full sale mechanic
- Top banner ("Sale up to 70% off + styles under $6.99") preserves sale momentum without making this another all-discount send
- The Back-to-School member giveaway is at least a distinct CTA — gives members a reason to engage beyond the product browse

## What's weak

- Subject "New summer details" is vague to the point of generic — it could belong to any brand, any category, any season
- No offer in the hero section means the eye lands on the product photos with zero incentive to click
- The Back-to-School giveaway is tonally jarring — student denim below a linen cushion cover grid is a hard pivot
- Scroll depth is punishing: 8+ products before any CTA or page break
- Prices are very low ($5.99, $8.99) but the email frames this as an editorial lifestyle moment — the mismatch undermines both the quality story and the deal story

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one clear offer to the hero.** Even "Free shipping on home orders today" or a threshold deal ("Spend $30, get free shipping on home") gives the section a reason to click. The photography is doing all the work alone right now.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to be specific.** "New summer details" describes nothing. Name the category or the hero item:
- **Subject Alt A:** `Summer Home is here — coasters, candles & more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New: linen, straw & glass for your summer home`
- 3. **Preheader should add urgency or a product call-out, not repeat the vibe.**
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New arrivals priced from $5.99 — refresh your space`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Coasters, curtains, candles — all new for summer`
- 4. **Break the Back-to-School module into its own send.** It doesn't belong here — it disrupts the home editorial mood and dilutes the click path. If the giveaway must stay, move it to the footer above social links.
- 5. **Cut the product grid by half.** 8 products without a CTA break is exhausting on mobile. Surface 4 hero items, put a "Shop Summer Home" button, then let the second grid live below the fold.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading (high risk):**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is blocked by Outlook (all versions), Gmail (non-Googlemail), and Apple Mail on iOS in some configurations. Web fonts will silently fall back with no error — acceptable if fallback fonts are defined, but no fallback stack is visible in the truncated CSS.

**Non-standard Yahoo media query:**
```css
@media screen yahoo { ... }
```
The correct Yahoo Mail hack is `@media yahoo { ... }` (without `screen`). The `screen` qualifier causes this rule to be ignored in Yahoo Mail, defeating its intended override.

**Duplicate CSS rules** — The following selectors appear twice in the `optimize-css` block with identical declarations:
- `.cta-link-mobile-border-ns { border: 1px solid #000 !important; }`
- `.cta-link-mobile, .set_font_interactive_standalone_link_default { line-height:17px!important; font-size:14px!important; }`
- `.flexible-elements-secondary-text-block-headline, ...preamble { width:327px!important; }`
- `.flexible-elements-secondary-text-block-headline-text { font-size:24px!important; ... }`

No functional breakage, but indicates the stylesheet is generated/concatenated without dedup — inflates email size (counts toward 102KB Gmail clipping threshold).

**Missing `<title>` tag** — Not present in `<head>`. Some clients display the title in tab/window chrome; absence is a minor but confirmed gap.

**Alt text on images** — Cannot verify; image tags not visible in truncated source.
