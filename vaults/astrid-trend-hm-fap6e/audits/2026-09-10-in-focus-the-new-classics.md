# In focus: The new classics
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-10**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuine break from the Labor Day discount treadmill H&M has run since 09/03 — "In focus: The new classics" pivots to editorial, prices-visible product storytelling instead of "up to 50% off" recycled hero shots. It's clean, well-photographed, and reads like a lookbook rather than a blast. The trade-off is real: there's no discount anywhere in the fold, which means the email leans entirely on product desirability to earn a click.
- The layout does a lot right — individual product tiles with names and prices, a clear "New Arrival" tag, and a genuinely new in-store event tease (A/W '26 launch event, $60 spend → gift card) buried further down. But that event section is the most compelling offer in the whole email and it's positioned after seven scrolls of product tiles, which most subscribers will never reach on mobile.
- Net: a tasteful editorial send that will underperform on clicks precisely because it has no upfront hook, but it's a welcome creative reset after a week of identical Labor Day copy.

## What's working

- Individual product cards show name, price, and "New Arrival" status — good product-decision info visible without clicking through.
- Hero image and typography are clean with no render bugs, overlapping text, or broken images visible.
- The A/W '26 launch event section (Sept 18, $60 spend → $15 gift card + beauty bundle) is a genuinely fresh, non-repeated offer for this brand.
- Store list for the in-person event adds specificity and local relevance.

## What's weak

- No discount, sale badge, or price-off signal anywhere above the fold — despite "SALE" appearing in the top nav bar, it's never followed through on in the body.
- The strongest content (the members event with a real dollar incentive) is buried near the bottom, after 6+ product tiles a mobile reader is unlikely to scroll to.
- Primary CTA "SHOP NOW" is generic and repeated with no product or category specificity — doesn't tell the reader what's actually on offer.
- Very long single-column scroll of near-identical product-on-model shots risks fatigue before reaching the actual news (the event).

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the A/W '26 launch event teaser (with its $60→$15 gift card + beauty bundle hook) up near the top, right after the hero — it's the only concrete incentive in the email and it's currently invisible to anyone who doesn't scroll far.
- 2. Cut the product grid roughly in half — 3-4 hero pieces instead of 7+ — to reduce scroll fatigue and get to the event section faster.
- 3. Replace "SHOP NOW" with category-specific CTA copy tied to what's actually shown, e.g. "Shop the tailoring edit."
- 4. Since "SALE" appears in the top nav but nothing in-body backs it up, either remove it or add one clearly tagged discounted item to avoid a bait-and-switch feel.
