# 15% off or 20% off this weekend
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a mid-weekend downgrade: after a week of "up to 40% off sitewide" (10/11–10/13), H&M is now offering a threshold deal of 15% off $100 or 20% off $125. The hero is clean: a model in a camel trench with the bold headline "15% OFF $100 OR 20% OFF $125" and one outlined SHOP NOW button. The offer is easy to read.
- The problem is the two offers competing at the top. A Halloween banner, "UP TO 50% OFF SELECT STYLES*", sits above the logo and outranks the hero deal. Below the hero, the Women, Men and Kids grids mix sale prices with full prices, and the kids section is Halloween graphics. The subscriber gets two promotions, one seasonal and one threshold, and neither is the clear winner. Compared with 10/14's "Trick-or-treat: Up to 50% off all this" (6/10) and 10/13's "ends tonight" (7/10), this reads as a weaker, muddier follow-up.
- Verdict: a competent, on-brand template send with a clear hero and a confused offer stack. A spend-more-save-more mechanic after a week of 40% sitewide also teaches subscribers to wait for the bigger discount.

## What's working

- The hero headline states both tiers of the offer in plain numbers, so the value is clear in a glance.
- The trench-coat image is strong and seasonal, and the copy ("cozy knitwear to essential outerwear") matches it.
- The Women section shows real markdowns with red sale prices and strikethroughs, such as $13.99 against $17.99.
- The layout is clean and renders properly, with no broken images or overlap, and the Women/Men/Kids split makes the grids easy to scan.
- The subject names both discount tiers and a time frame, so it is concrete.

## What's weak

- The "Halloween Edit: up to 50% off" banner above the logo competes with the hero offer, and the Kids section leans Halloween too.
- The CTA is a generic "SHOP NOW" with no category, and it appears only once, under the hero.
- The Men's products show no sale prices ($49.99, $29.99), which undercuts the 15–20% promise.
- Kids product names are truncated ("PRINTED BRUSHED-INSI… SWEATSHIRT"), and the "New Arrival" tags sit awkwardly next to sale prices.
- The fine print is a very long, dense block of exclusions, and the body has no visible "this weekend" deadline to back up the subject.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one offer. Drop the Halloween banner from this send, or move Halloween into its own email, so the 15%/20% hero is the only top message.
- 2. Add a visible deadline to the hero, such as an eyebrow reading "THIS WEEKEND ONLY · ENDS SUN 10/18".
- 3. Make the CTA specific, for example "SHOP COATS & KNITWEAR", and repeat a CTA under each section.
- 4. Show the threshold math on products ("Add $100 of these, take 15% off"), and make sure the Men's grid features eligible items.
- 5. Fix the truncated product titles and shorten the fine print by linking to terms.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Take 20% off $125 — through Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Fall coats + knits: 15–20% off this weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $100, save 15%. Spend $125, save 20%. Ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Trench coats, cardigans and cozy layers, all included.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML source shows a structurally normal ESP-built email (table layout, MSO and mobile CSS, tracked redirect links). The automated QA flags are mostly probe-limited (403s from the tracking redirector, uncaptured headers). The confirmed issues are missing image alt text and empty structured-data fields.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **All 12 probed links returned 403.** That covers the "Shop now" CTAs, the logo link, product images and Unsubscribe. Every one is on `t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=h9f797d36-0966-41e6-a0b2-4746f8cdaef2,5491eca2,<hex>`. Because every link fails the same way, including the unsubscribe link, this is most likely the redirector rejecting an automated client (bot or UA filtering). It is not evidence of 12 separately broken links. The QA data can't confirm that real recipients get a 403.
- **No final destination was resolved.** The probe never got past the redirect, so landing-page status is unverified.
- **132 links were not probed.** Only the 12 highest-ranked links were checked, so coverage is partial.
- **Tracking parameters are consistent.** `did=1418849442`, `rid=3326930539`, `erid=MzMyNjkzMDUzOQ%3D%3D` (the base64 of the `rid` value) and `p3=20261016` are the same across links. No malformed tracking URLs were found.
- **Empty link text.** Several flagged links have no usable text: the image-only links, and two "Shop now" links whose text is prefixed with zero-width characters (`‌  ﻿Shop now`). The padding is an invisible-character preheader or spacing technique, but it adds noise to the accessible name.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on about 30 images.** This is confirmed by QA. Examples:
  - Hero: `1367124001_202604_LB_0675.jpg` (`cq5dam.web.1400.1400.jpeg` and `cq5dam.web.750.750.jpeg`).
  - Product grid: `a1b08db2e44f16e4a7a9de771168aeb70354a088.jpg`, `802f44dea37dffa5f3060a0a3185936cbca02e34.jpg`, `fabedf3f372872334b0d22f6ae76018e681e8de6.jpg` and others. Each appears at `imWidth=344` and `imWidth=322`.
  - The tracking pixel-style image `t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=…,1` is also flagged.
- **Link-wrapped images with no alt are the worst case.** Screen readers and images-off clients fall back to the filename or the redirect URL as the link name. Both hero images and the product thumbnails are wrapped in links.
- **Duplicate image sets.** Each product image is served in 344 and 322 widths, which looks like separate desktop and mobile variants. The source uses `img.image + div {display:none}` and the `.is-hidden-mobile` / `.is-hidden-desktop` classes (breakpoint 699px). If both variants lack alt text, the problem is doubled.
- **Web fonts.** `@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/…/fonts.css)` and `https://s1-cdn.hm.com/…/oces.css` are loaded. `@import` is ignored by Gmail and several other clients, so fallback fonts must be defined. The visible CSS doesn't show whether they are.
- **The HTML was truncated**, so the rest of the rendering and accessibility review is incomplete.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Add descriptive `alt` text to every image,** and use `alt=""` for decorative ones. Prioritize the hero images and the link-wrapped product thumbnails.
2. **Re-test the tracked links** from a real mail client or a browser-like UA. Confirm the redirects resolve and capture the final landing URLs and UTMs. In particular, confirm the unsubscribe link returns 200 and unsubscribes.
3. **Verify `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post`** (RFC 8058) and SPF/DKIM/DMARC from raw headers captured outside the relay. Until then, treat these as unconfirmed.
