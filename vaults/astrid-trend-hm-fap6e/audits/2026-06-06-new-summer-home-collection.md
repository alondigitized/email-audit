# New summer home collection
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- H&M HOME is doing a hard pivot from apparel to home goods with an "After-Sun Siesta" coastal lifestyle concept — white bedding, natural textures, beach-adjacent decor. The creative is genuinely strong: the photography is clean, the palette is cohesive, and the "Beach Bundle" block mid-email at least gestures toward a packaged offer. But this lands the day after a sharp Father's Day 50%-off email, and the subject — "New summer home collection" — is the flattest possible announcement. There's no offer hook, no urgency, no curiosity gap. Subscribers who click on "Up to 50% off gifts for every dad" yesterday are calibrated for deals; this email gives them nothing to latch onto upfront.
- The category shift also matters. This is an H&M HOME send, not apparel. For a subscriber whose inbox association with H&M is fashion and athletic wear (LOTTO, MOVE), a home goods collection email with no explicit "why you, why now" framing reads as low-relevance. The "After-Sun Siesta" concept is charming but requires the reader to already care about home textiles to get there.
- Bottom line: good creative execution, weak positioning, and a subject line that will get buried in a crowded summer inbox.

- **Category mismatch with no bridge copy** — nothing in the visible email acknowledges the shift from apparel to home goods or gives a fashion subscriber a reason to care.
- **No discount or concrete value signal** — the Beach Bundle is visible but no price reduction, specific savings, or member benefit is apparent at a glance.
- **Post-50%-off-day hangover** — dropping an editorial home send the day after a hard-offer Father's Day email invites unfavorable comparison; subscribers will feel the offer drought.

- 4. **Add a single line of bridge copy near the top** for subscribers who think of H&M as an apparel brand — one sentence like "H&M HOME: everything your summer bedroom needs" stops the cognitive mismatch cold.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading will fail silently in most clients**

```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```

`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is stripped by Gmail (both web and app), Outlook 2007–2021 (ignores entirely), and Yahoo Mail. Custom fonts will silently fall back to system fonts. This is a known H&M pattern — if fallback stacks are specified in the CSS, the visual impact is contained, but the external CDN requests still add DNS lookup latency for clients that do render them.

**`<meta http-equiv="content-type">` is legacy**

```html
<meta http-equiv="content-type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8"/>
```

Should be `<meta charset="UTF-8">` per HTML5. The legacy form works but is unnecessary for modern clients.
