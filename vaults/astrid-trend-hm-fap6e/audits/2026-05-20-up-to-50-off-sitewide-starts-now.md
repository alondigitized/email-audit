# Up to 50% off sitewide starts now
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- This is H&M's Memorial Day / early-summer sale launch — "UP TO 50% OFF SITEWIDE" is the clearest possible sale signal after a week of near-continuous editorial sends. The timing reads smart in context: five editorial emails primed the catalog across soccer style, soft tailoring, Lotto, and occasion dresses, so a sale launcher lands with genuine contrast value. The creative is women's-dress-forward — floral hero model, four product cards with struck-through prices, single "SHOP NOW" CTA, clean white layout.
- The fundamental weakness is execution depth. "Sitewide" is the broadest possible scope and the email does nothing to segment — the same blast hits every subscriber regardless of gender preference or purchase history. More critically, there is no deadline anywhere. "Starts now" creates a mild urgency signal but zero close pressure. A subscriber who likes what they see can bookmark it and come back in three days with no FOMO penalty.
- Compared to the May 16 occasion dress send (9/10), which had tight category targeting and a confident hero concept, this is the blunter instrument. It announces the sale clearly and gets out of the way — which is functionally correct — but misses the personalization and urgency scaffolding that would make it genuinely excellent.

## What's working

- **Unmissable offer above the fold.** "UP TO 50% OFF SITEWIDE" in large bold type sits directly below the hero with a clear "SHOP NOW" button — no hunting required.
- **Struck-through pricing on all four product cards.** $67→$24 and $24→$19 are concrete, believable before/after signals that make the discount feel real rather than manufactured.
- **"THE DRESS CODE" framing.** Ties H&M's editorial voice to the promotional moment rather than dropping a bare discount banner — keeps the brand tone intact.
- **Clean, uncluttered layout.** Single-column, white background, strong photography; nothing competes with the offer.
- **Cadence contrast.** After five straight editorial emails, a pure sale email reads differently in inbox — it doesn't feel like more of the same.

## What's weak

- **No deadline anywhere.** "Starts now" with no end date removes close pressure entirely — there's nothing to act on *today* that won't still be there tomorrow.
- **Generic CTA copy.** "SHOP NOW" on a sale email is a missed layup. "Shop 50% Off" or "Shop Sale Dresses" reinforces the offer at the moment of decision.
- **"Sitewide" undermines the dress-focused creative.** The hero and product grid are 100% women's dresses, but the body copy and "SHOP BY" nav broadcast that this is actually everything — the mismatch diffuses the message.
- **No loyalty callout.** H&M has a loyalty program; a sale email is the highest-ROI moment to surface a member benefit (early access, free shipping, extra points), and there's nothing here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline — highest impact single change.** "Ends Sunday" or "48 hours only" transforms this from a passive announcement into a reason to act today.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA.** Change "SHOP NOW" → "Shop 50% Off" or "Shop Sale Dresses" — the offer should carry into the button copy.
- 3. **Segment or fork the hero.** If subscriber gender is known, serve the matching category as hero. If not, add a "Women | Men | Kids" strip below the hero CTA so every subscriber sees a relevant path within two seconds of opening.
- 4. **Surface a loyalty hook.** A single line — "Members: free standard shipping on all sale orders" — rewards loyalty and provides a credible second reason to click.
- 5. **Set an explicit preheader.** The preview text is not visible in the render; if it's defaulting to "SHOP NOW" link text or body copy, that's a wasted impression.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Sale ends Sunday — 50% off sitewide`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer wardrobe just got 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dresses from $19. Ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The biggest sale of the season just started.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS `@import` unsupported in most email clients.**
Both font loads are via `@import` inside `<style>` tags:
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` is not supported in Outlook (all versions), Samsung Mail, and some Yahoo Mail contexts. Fonts will silently fall back to system fonts in those clients with no error surfaced.

**Duplicate mobile CSS rules** (may signal copy-paste template debt, not a render-breaking issue):
- `.department-title` — declared twice in `@media (max-width:699px)`
- `.department-link` — declared twice
- `.cta-link-mobile-border-ns` — declared twice
- `.cta-link-mobile, .image-text-cta-link, .image-text-flexible-elements-cta, .set_font_interactive_standalone_link_default` — declared twice

Duplicate declarations are resolved by cascade (last wins) but add unnecessary payload weight and are a template hygiene issue.

**`format-detection` meta tag is correct** (`telephone=no`) — prevents iOS from auto-linking phone numbers. No issue.
