# Contrast dressing
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-04**
## Executive summary

- This is H&M in full-price editorial mode again, and it's a clean execution — a "contrast dressing" trend story (bold orange against muted grays) that's coherent from hero to product grid. No discount, no urgency, just a styling narrative: the barrel-leg jeans, jacquard bow-collar blouse, and denim peplum blouse all read as one considered outfit story rather than a category dump. It's the same mode as 7/30's "golden summer color palette" send — mood-led, not promo-led — and it executes just as well, maybe better, since the styling concept ("contrast") is more concrete and visually legible than "golden summer" was.
- The WARDROBE.NYC collab block bolted onto the bottom is the one structural oddity — it's a genuinely interesting hook (designer collab, launching August 6) but it's buried after three full product sections with only a "PREVIEW" CTA, no urgency, no reason to click today versus in two days.
- After five straight days of "up to 30% off," this is a welcome change of pace — no discount fatigue, no repeated ceiling copy. But precisely because there's no offer, this email lives or dies on the subject line and hero doing enough to earn a click, and "Contrast Dressing" is vague enough that it won't pull much open volume outside of a highly engaged trend-following segment.

## What's working

- The visual concept is genuinely legible in the render — orange top/gray jeans in the hero, orange bra top and jacket contrasted with gray jacquard pieces in the mid-section — the "contrast" story is visible, not just claimed in copy.
- Clean single-column layout, no overlapping text, no broken images, consistent product-card formatting (name/price/New Arrival tag).
- The WARDROBE.NYC collab is a real point of differentiation from the last several sends — new designer news instead of another 30%-off variant.
- Top banner ("FREE SHIPPING OVER $50 FOR H&M MEMBERS") gives loyalty members a concrete, visible benefit right at the open.

## What's weak

- No offer or urgency anywhere in the email — fine for brand storytelling, but it caps click likelihood for a price-motivated shopper.
- "Contrast Dressing" as a subject is abstract fashion-editorial language; it doesn't say what's being sold or why to open now.
- The WARDROBE.NYC collab — arguably the most newsworthy item in this email — gets a single small "PREVIEW" button with no launch-day urgency treatment despite having a concrete date (August 6).
- Every hero and product model in the render is female; if this send also targets menswear shoppers, there's zero representation of that here.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead the subject/preheader with the WARDROBE.NYC collab instead of burying it — a named designer collab with a countdown ("launches Aug 6") is a stronger open driver than an abstract style term.
- 2. Add a date/urgency treatment to the WARDROBE.NYC block — "Launches in 2 days" beats a flat "Launching August 6 in select stores and online."
- 3. Give the hero product a size/fit or "shop the look" multi-item CTA instead of a single SHOP NOW, since this is styled as an outfit, not one item.
- 4. Pull "New Arrival" tags into the visual hierarchy more (badge/color) — currently plain gray text easy to miss on mobile scroll.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **23 images missing `alt` text**, spanning both content/product images (e.g. `FNP-DD10263P16-2x3-top-T…`, `NA90263P04-2x3-secondary.jpg`) and tracking pixels. Missing alt text on content images degrades the experience for screen-reader users and for any client that blocks images by default.
- CSS rule `img.image + div { display: none !important }` suppresses the sibling `<div>` immediately following image elements — in many ESP templates this div carries the image's text fallback/caption; combined with missing `alt` attributes, image-blocked/screen-reader users get no fallback content at all.

- Full DOM not available (source truncated) — body content, ALT attributes on remaining images, and closing markup could not be fully verified.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Unsubscribe link returns HTTP 403** (see §2, token `f7a6ff7c`) — a functioning unsubscribe mechanism is a CAN-SPAM requirement; as observed, the one-click link in the body is currently non-functional.

- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header (RFC 8058, required for Gmail/Yahoo one-click unsubscribe compliance as of 2024 bulk-sender rules) also not detected — same relay-capture caveat applies.

- Plain-text alternative is 74% raw URLs (518/596 chars) — not a legal compliance issue, but elevates spam-filter risk and is worth flagging alongside the header gaps.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Priority — fix the redirect resolver on `t19.email.hm.com`.** All 12 probed links, including Unsubscribe, are down (403); this is a full functional and compliance-blocking outage, not a cosmetic issue.
2. Verify raw SMTP headers directly (outside the AgentMail relay) for `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` to confirm RFC 8058 one-click support is actually present and not just relay-swallowed.
3. Confirm `Authentication-Results` (SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass) via raw headers or a header-inspection tool, since the relay didn't surface it.
4. Repopulate the empty `ld+json` fields (`subjectLine`, `discountCode`, `availabilityEnds`, `image`, `url`) and correct the stale `availabilityStarts` date so Gmail promo/structured-data rendering functions correctly.
5. Add descriptive `alt` text to the 23 flagged images, particularly the two "SHOP NOW" CTA and product images, and reconsider the `img.image + div { display:none }` rule so fallback content isn't suppressed for image-blocked/screen-reader users.
6. Strip stray zero-width/BOM characters (`‌`, `﻿`) from link text ("Shop now") to ensure clean rendering and predictable accessible names.
7. Once links are restored, add UTM parameters (or confirm the proprietary `p1`–`p5`/`erid` scheme maps to on-site analytics) so email-to-site attribution can be validated end-to-end.
