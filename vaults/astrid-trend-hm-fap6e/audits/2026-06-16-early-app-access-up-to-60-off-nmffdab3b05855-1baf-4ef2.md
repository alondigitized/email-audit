# Early app access: up to 60% off
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- This is an app-acquisition campaign with a clearance-sale hook. H&M leads with a genuinely strong number — 60% off is the biggest discount they've sent in this sequence — but every button in the email drops you into the App Store, not a sale grid. A subscriber who opens this expecting to browse and buy immediately hits a wall: download the app, log in, then shop. That's three friction points before a product is even in front of them.
- Visually the email is clean. The hero image is competent, the headline is dominant, and there are no render failures. But the body is padded with an app-feature tour (SCAN & FIND, VISUAL SEARCH, MEMBER HUB) that reads like onboarding documentation dropped into a flash-sale email. It splits attention between "shop now" and "here's why the app is good" — two goals that undermine each other.
- Context makes this worse: this is the seventh H&M email in six days. Subscribers who converted on the June 13–14 threshold discount (15%/20% off) are now seeing 60% off two days later with zero acknowledgment. That erodes trust in the whole promotional calendar.

## What's working

- **Hero headline is unambiguous**: "UP TO 60% OFF IN THE APP" registers instantly at the top of the render — no need to hunt for the offer.
- **"SALE EARLY ACCESS" creates mild exclusivity**: The early-access frame gives existing app users a reason to feel rewarded and gives non-users a concrete reason to download.
- **Clean render**: No broken images, no clipped text, no layout drift — the email displays without visual noise.
- **Subject line is efficient**: 31 characters, no spam signals, leads with the exclusivity angle before the discount.

## What's weak

- **All CTAs route to the App Store, not the sale**: There is no path to purchase for someone who already has the app — every button asks them to re-download, which reads as broken.
- **Zero products shown**: The hero is a lifestyle model with no SKUs; you can't impulse-buy what you haven't seen, and a 60% off sale landing page can prove the discount is real in a way this email cannot.
- **App-feature tour is off-brief**: Three paragraphs about VISUAL SEARCH and MEMBER HUB dilute the clearance urgency. If someone is motivated to download for 60% off, they don't need a feature orientation right now.
- **No hard deadline**: "Early access" implies time sensitivity but no date or hour is visible, so the urgency is soft and ignorable.
- **Cadence damage**: Seven emails in six days; the last sale ran two days ago at a lower discount. This send retroactively punishes recent buyers and trains unsubscribes.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deep-link CTA for existing app users**: A second button — "Already have the app? Shop now" — that deep-links directly into the sale collection removes the primary friction point for the most engaged audience.
- 2. **Replace the app-feature section with 3 hero product cards**: Show three items at original and sale price under the headline. Proving the 60% is real kills doubt and drives desire before the download ask.
- 3. **Put a hard deadline on the early-access window**: "App-exclusive until [date + time]" converts "early access" from a vague brand concept into an actual deadline.
- 4. **Cut the duplicate DOWNLOAD buttons and the app-feature copy entirely**: Keep one clean CTA pair after the headline. The current email repeats the download ask three times across the body and footer.
- 5. **Suppress for recent buyers from the June 13–14 sale**: Or acknowledge the deal explicitly ("Bigger savings just unlocked") — anything is better than pretending the 20%-off send didn't happen.
- **Subject Alt A:** `App-only: the 60% off sale just opened`
- **Subject Alt B:** `You're in early — 60% off starts now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `First access to H&M's biggest summer markdown — app only, limited time.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Head start on 60% off. Open in the app before it goes wide.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading will silently fail in Outlook**
```css
<style>
  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
</style>
```
Outlook (all desktop versions) strips `@import` inside `<style>` tags. Both custom font declarations will be ignored, falling back to system fonts. This is a known Outlook behavior — fonts must be inlined via `font-face` declarations or accepted as a Outlook-specific fallback.

**Non-standard Yahoo Mail media query**
```css

```
`screen yahoo` is not a valid CSS media query; it relies on Yahoo Mail's legacy parser bug to apply. This is a known workaround but fragile — Yahoo Mail rendering pipeline changes can silently break this toggle without warning.

**Mobile breakpoint at 699px is non-standard**

Most email clients and templates use 600px. At 700px viewport widths (common on some Android devices), the desktop layout will render instead of the mobile layout. Low severity but worth normalizing.
