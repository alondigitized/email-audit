# Up to 60% off = cool for the summer
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- This is H&M's third dedicated "60% off" email in four days — following June 16 (app-exclusive early access) and June 17 (sale launch). The creative is competent: clean editorial photography, a clear FOR HER / FOR HIM / FOR THEM content structure, and readable type. But there's a critical self-inflicted wound at the very top: the email opens with a Father's Day gift-shop banner reading **"ORDER BY 06/16 TO RECEIVE BY 06/21"** — a deadline that had already passed three days before this email landed. Any subscriber who reads that first line before seeing the hero sees a brand that can't keep its own house in order. In many inbox clients, that Father's Day banner is also the preheader — so the subject line says "cool for the summer" while the preview says "FIND PERFECT PICKS FOR HIS DAD STYLE." That's a hard mismatch before the email is even opened.
- Strip away the broken banner and the structural issue remains: every CTA for all three audience segments (HER / HIM / THEM) resolves to "TEES" and "SHORTS" only. For an email headlining 60% off across the store, that's a remarkably narrow funnel. The in-store push at the bottom ("SEE WHAT'S NEW IN-STORE") is a category detour that pulls attention away from the core sale message.
- The net result is an email that looks fine in isolation but reads as a sloppy third act in a fatiguing sale sequence.

## What's working

- **Hero offer lands immediately.** "SALE / UP TO 60% OFF" in large type over a clean model shot — the core message is unambiguous above the fold.
- **FOR HER / FOR HIM / FOR THEM structure** is logical segmentation that mirrors how a sale-browsing shopper actually thinks.
- **Photography is editorial and current.** Seasonal styling (jeans, oversized tees, casual shorts) matches the summer campaign beat.
- **Subject line is clean.** "Up to 60% off = cool for the summer" at 36 characters has no spam signals and passes mobile truncation.

## What's weak

- **Expired Father's Day banner at the top.** "ORDER BY 06/16" in an email sent June 19 is dead copy — and likely the preheader text visible in the inbox before the email is opened.
- **Subject-preheader collision.** The summer sale subject is immediately contradicted by Father's Day language if that banner bleeds into preview text.
- **Every CTA collapses to TEES or SHORTS.** A 60% off sale covering the whole store deserves more than two category links per segment.
- **No urgency anywhere.** Three 60% off emails and still no deadline, no countdown, no "ends Sunday" — the sale now reads as permanent.
- **In-store section is off-mission.** The "SEE WHAT'S NEW IN-STORE" block is a benefit-sell for physical retail that dilutes an email whose job is to drive online clicks during a sale.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill or fix the Father's Day banner immediately.** Either suppress it entirely for any send after 06/16 or replace it with a live sale deadline. A stale gift-shop deadline is worse than blank space.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the offer.** "60% off — ends Sunday" would be the single highest-lift change given this is the third send with no urgency. June 17's launch email introduced nothing; this one needs to close the loop.
- 3. **Expand the CTAs beyond TEES + SHORTS.** Add DRESSES, SWIM, or DENIM for HER; POLOS, SHORTS, and SHOES for HIM. The email says "our entire summer" but links to two categories.
- 4. **Lead the preheader with the sale deadline, not category copy.** The preheader should do the work the subject doesn't: supply the urgency number or the category hook.
- 5. **Drop or move the in-store section.** It works as a footer note but not as a mid-email detour during a sale push.

- **Subject Alt B:** `The summer sale: up to 60% off, today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Tees, shorts, swim & more — sale ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Final days: up to 60% off across the store`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` for external fonts — will fail silently in most clients**

```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```

`@import` inside `<style>` is stripped by Gmail, Apple Mail (some versions), and all Outlook/Exchange clients. Fallback fonts must be specified via `font-family` stack; if they are not, system defaults will render instead. This is a known anti-pattern in email HTML.

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`**

```html
<html style="border:0;margin:0;outline:0;padding:0">
```

No `lang="en"` (or locale equivalent). Screen readers cannot infer language; this fails WCAG 2.1 SC 3.1.1 and will be flagged by accessibility audits.

**CSS media query `line-height:auto` is invalid**

```css
.is-hidden-desktop { line-height: auto!important; ... }
```

`line-height: auto` is not a valid CSS value (valid: `normal`, a number, a length). Most email clients will ignore this declaration; the intended behavior (restoring collapsed elements) may not trigger reliably on Android clients.

**`img` alt attributes** — cannot confirm from truncated source; flag for manual check.
