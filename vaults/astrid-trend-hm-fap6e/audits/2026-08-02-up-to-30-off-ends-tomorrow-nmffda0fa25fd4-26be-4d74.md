# Up to 30% off ends tomorrow
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-02**
## Executive summary

- This is H&M's fourth back-to-school/30%-off send in a row, and it shows — the layout is the same category-grid template used all week (WOMEN/MEN/BABY/KIDS/HOME/FIND A STORE nav grid, then FOR HER/FOR HIM/FOR THEM category blocks) with only the models and headline swapped. The genuine escalation here is real urgency: "UP TO 30% OFF... ends tomorrow" plus a deadline is a step up from 7/30's open-ended "checklist" framing and 7/31's "campus favorites from $6.99" price-anchor, and it's paired with a legitimate BOPIS hook ("Need an item sooner? Buy online, pick up in-store") that none of the prior sends had.
- Visually it's clean and on-brand — no render bugs, clear product photography, consistent typography — but it's dense: three near-identical "model wearing graphic sweatshirt + Shop Now" blocks stacked vertically before you even reach the tax-free section. That's a lot of scrolling for one offer restated three times with different demographics.
- Given the urgency angle is new and credible (a real deadline, not just a percentage ceiling repeated for the fourth day), this edges out 7/31 and 7/30 on click intent even though the visual template is fatigued. Solid, not exceptional — a 7.

## What's working

- Real deadline urgency ("ends tomorrow") is new for this campaign arc and creates a credible reason to act today, not just browse.
- "Buy online, pick up in-store" is a concrete, differentiated hook not seen in the 7/30–8/1 sends.
- Clear three-way segmentation (Her/Him/Them) lets a shopper skip straight to their category.
- Clean, bug-free render — no overlapping text, broken images, or placeholder content.
- Tax-free callout adds a second, distinct reason to shop beyond the discount.

## What's weak

- The hero offer ("UP TO 30% OFF") requires scrolling past a full nav grid before any product appears — no product is visible above the fold.
- Three sequential "model + Shop Now" blocks (Her/Him/Them) are visually repetitive and push the tax-free/store-locator content far down.
- No visible price or specific discount tier — "up to 30%" is vague compared to 7/31's concrete "$6.99" anchor, which tested better.
- This is the same core promo mechanic run four days straight (7/30, 7/31, 8/1, now 8/2) with escalating fatigue risk for anyone who's opened more than one.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a product shot and the "ends tomorrow" deadline above the fold — right now the top third is all nav buttons and copy, no imagery, which wastes the strongest asset (urgency) on a wall of text.
- 2. Collapse the three near-identical Her/Him/Him blocks into one scrollable carousel or a single grid so the email feels shorter and less repetitive after four straight days of this format.
- 3. Swap "up to 30% off" for a concrete price point (as 7/31 did with "$6.99") — vague ceilings underperform specific anchors.
- 4. Since this is the fourth consecutive send on the same promo, consider pairing this "last chance" framing with a genuinely different creative treatment (not just a new headline) to reset fatigue.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last call: 30% off ends tomorrow`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Tax-free + 30% off — today's the day`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Buy online, pick up in-store today — no shipping wait`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Back-to-school picks for her, him & the kids`
