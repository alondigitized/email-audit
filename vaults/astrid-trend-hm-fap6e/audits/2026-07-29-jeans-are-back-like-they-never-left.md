# Jeans are back (like they never left)
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-29**
## Executive summary

- This is H&M running its full-price editorial playbook again — no discount, no urgency, just a category reset ("A Return to Jeans") followed by a "Back to Campus" push. It's the same beat as 7/23's "New arrivals: Back to school" and 7/28's "POV: Your pre-fall office wardrobe," which makes three non-discount editorial sends in six days. The denim category grid (women/men/kids/kids) is clean and genuinely useful for cross-shopping a household, and the campus section has a strong, on-brand hero image. But there's zero offer, zero urgency, and the top banner leads with tax-free shopping info — a segment-specific, low-relevance hook for most subscribers — instead of the jeans story the subject promises.
- The subject line "Jeans are back (like they never left)" is a decent hook, but the email doesn't reward the open with anything beyond product browsing. For a subscriber who's already seen two editorial, no-discount sends this week, this reads as more of the same — pleasant, on-brand, but not a reason to click today rather than next week.

## What's working

- Clear denim category breakdown (Women / Men / Kids 2-8Y / Kids 9-14Y) makes it easy to jump straight to a relevant shopper.
- The "Back to Campus" hero image is well-composed and feels current for late-July/August back-to-school timing.
- Consistent H&M visual identity — logo, typography, black/white/cream palette all match prior sends.
- "Shop By" footer grid (Women/Men/Kids/Home/Beauty) gives a clean fallback path if the main content doesn't land.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, layout is intact top to bottom.

## What's weak

- No offer anywhere in the email — no %, no $, no BOGO, nothing to click for beyond browsing.
- Top banner ("TAX-FREE DAYS ARE COMING") is irrelevant to most subscribers (state-dependent, asterisked) and buries the actual jeans message under an unrelated hook.
- Hero hierarchy is muddled — the tax-free banner and nav sit above the jeans hero image, so the eye doesn't land on the subject's promise first.
- "A Return to Jeans" and "Back to Campus" are two disconnected campaigns stitched into one email, diluting focus (same structural issue flagged in the 7/27 "Off-duty dressing" audit).
- Third non-discount editorial send in six days (7/23, 7/28, now 7/29) — cadence is starting to feel repetitive for anyone not actively shopping.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the tax-free banner entirely or move it to the footer — it competes with the jeans hero for top-of-email attention and isn't relevant to most readers.
- 2. Add one concrete anchor offer (e.g., "New denim, from $19.99" or a loyalty-member early-access angle) — three full-price sends in a row with no discount signal risks training subscribers to skip.
- 3. Split "A Return to Jeans" and "Back to Campus" into two separate sends or clearly subordinate one as a footer teaser — right now they compete for the same real estate.
- 4. Make the category buttons more visually distinct from plain outlined boxes — add a subtle fill or arrow to signal they're clickable CTAs, not just labels.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New denim, every fit for fall`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your jeans lineup just got wider`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Barrel, wide-leg, straight — find your fit in women's, men's & kids'`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus: campus-ready fits for the new semester`

# Technical Audit — H&M "Jeans are back" Email

## 1. Technical Summary
The email uses a standard AMPscript/CDN-hosted responsive template, but automated QA shows the tracking-redirect domain returning 403 on every probed link (12/12), and several `EmailMessage` structured-data fields never populated at send time.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **All 12 probed links return 403**, including primary CTAs ("SHOP WOMEN'S JEANS", "SHOP MEN'S JEANS", "SHOP FOR KIDS 2‑8Y", "SHOP FOR Kids 9‑14Y"), the tax-free-days banner link, two "Shop now" links, the header logo/wordmark link, both hero product image links, the "Women" nav link, and the **Unsubscribe link** itself.
  - Example: `https://t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=h2f182ac0-b4ce-4796-8329-5e9869b9d14a,66360c73,f7944506&did=1714818163&rid=3326930539&erid=...&p1=DM5890448&p2=0982dd0aa7b117bf086dee6ed4af7d0c&p3=20260729`
  - All links route through the same `t19.email.hm.com/r/` click-tracking redirector before resolving to a destination — the 403 is occurring at the redirector itself, not the landing pages, which points to a server-side/ESP issue (expired redirect map, bot-protection blocking the QA fetcher, or campaign ID `id=h2f182ac0-...` not yet activated in the ESP).
- Redirect params (`p1=DM5890448`, `p2=<hash>`, `p3=20260729`) are ESP click-tracking identifiers, not human-readable UTM parameters — destination URL and true landing page cannot be verified due to the 403s.
- Two links share identical anchor text "‌ ﻿Shop now" but different destination hashes (`f7944318` vs `f794431a`), and contain zero-width/invisible Unicode characters (U+200C, U+FEFF) in the link text — cosmetic template artifacts, not functional errors, but worth flagging to the template team.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **16 images missing `alt` text**, including all product hero images (`cq5dam.web.1400.1400.jpeg`, `cq5dam.web.750.750.jpeg` variants across 6 product slots) and both tracking pixel/click images (`src=".../r/?id=h2f182ac0-...,1"`). Screen readers and images-blocked clients will render these promos as blank.
- `.hide-webkit` / `.show-webkit-table` / `#MessageViewBody` selectors indicate Yahoo/Gmail-specific display hacks are in use — standard practice, no issue found.
- `.is-hidden-mobile` / `.is-hidden-desktop` responsive toggles rely on `max-height:0` + `overflow:hidden` rather than `display:none` alone for MSO compatibility — no issue found, this is correct defensive coding for Outlook.
- No issues found with viewport meta or `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` — both correctly present.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The `ld+json` `EmailMessage`/`DiscountOffer`/`PromotionCard` structured-data block has multiple **unpopulated fields at send time**: `"subjectLine":""`, `"discountCode":""`, `"availabilityEnds":""`, `"image":""` (PromotionCard), `"url":""` (PromotionCard). These are Gmail Promotions-tab / annotation fields — empty values mean Gmail's markup-based promo card/action button will not render correctly, though this doesn't affect the visible HTML body.
- `"availabilityStarts":"2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"` is a stale placeholder date (7+ years old relative to today) — indicates this field is a hardcoded template default that was never overwritten by the campaign build, not a live merge token.
- No customer-level personalization tokens (name, loyalty tier, etc.) are visible in the truncated source to evaluate for fallback/rendering issues.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)
- **Unsubscribe link is broken (403)** — `https://t19.email.hm.com/r/?...&f7944556&p4=3326930539&p5=9be06746af74de7271d9dcc8d83f5742`. This is the most severe compliance finding: a non-functional unsubscribe mechanism is a CAN-SPAM violation risk if it fails in production and not just in the QA fetch environment.
- **`List-Unsubscribe` header not found** — one-click unsubscribe via mail client UI (Gmail/Apple Mail "Unsubscribe" button) is unavailable or not being captured by the relay.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found** — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is not confirmed supported, which is required by Gmail/Yahoo's 2024 bulk-sender rules for senders at H&M's volume.
- **`Authentication-Results` header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status cannot be verified from this capture.
- Caveat: the last three findings are flagged WARN because the AgentMail relay may simply not be capturing/forwarding these headers — they do not confirm the headers are absent from the original send. Recommend verifying directly against raw source from the originating MTA.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Cannot confirm landing-page alignment: all outbound links resolve through the `t19.email.hm.com` redirector and every one returns 403 before reaching a final H&M destination URL, so destination path, query params, and page content cannot be validated in this check.
- No standard UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_campaign`, etc.) are present on the redirector URLs — H&M's ESP tracking uses proprietary `p1`–`p5` params instead, which is consistent across all links (no inconsistency found in tracking scheme itself).

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Priority 1** — Investigate the 403 on `t19.email.hm.com/r/` immediately; this is blocking every link in the email including unsubscribe, which is a functional and compliance risk regardless of root cause (expired redirect map, WAF/bot-block false positive, or premature campaign deactivation).
2. Add `alt` text to all 16 flagged product and tracking images for accessibility and images-blocked rendering.
3. Populate the empty `ld+json` fields (`subjectLine`, `discountCode`, `availabilityEnds`, `image`, `url`) or remove the `DiscountOffer`/`PromotionCard` schema block if not in use this campaign — stale/empty structured data can suppress Gmail promo annotations.
4. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers and `Authentication-Results` (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) directly against raw MTA logs, since the relay-based capture used here is inconclusive.
5. Confirm the `availabilityStarts` value is intentional — a 2018 date on a 2026 campaign suggests an un-updated template default.
