# Kick-start summer with up to 50% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- This is H&M's second straight email anchoring the same Memorial Day sale — May 20's "Up to 50% off sitewide starts now" (8/10) was the launch; today's send is the reminder. The offer is front and center, the creative is polished, and the category navigation is well-organized. But this email makes a structural mistake the launch didn't: it splits itself between two conversion goals. The top half is an online sale email; the bottom half is an in-store recruitment email ("SEE WHAT'S NEW IN-STORE"). Neither undermines the other badly, but the dilution is real — you scroll expecting to find sale product and instead hit a store-finder pitch.
- The bigger miss is the preheader. "NEED AN ITEM SOONER? BUY ONLINE, PICK UP IN STORE" opens the email — both visually at the top of the render and likely as the inbox preview text — without a single word about the sale. That's a wasted shot at reinforcing the subject line before the click.
- Verdict: solid execution of a second-day sale reminder, knocked down a tier by a misaligned preheader and a mixed-message body structure.

## What's working

- **"UP TO 50% OFF SITEWIDE" reads instantly.** Large, bold, above the fold — no hunting required.
- **"LONG WEEKEND. BIG DEALS." anchors the timing** without needing an explicit date; Memorial Day is self-evident context.
- **Category navigation (WOMEN / MEN / BABY / KIDS / HOME)** gives immediate jump-off points so the email doubles as a landing page.
- **Product photography is on-brand** — the red wide-leg pants editorial and the men's print look both read as summer.
- **No render bugs visible** — clean layout, no overlapping text, images load.

## What's weak

- **Preheader fires about in-store pickup, not the sale.** "NEED AN ITEM SOONER? BUY ONLINE, PICK UP IN STORE" is the first thing an inbox renders next to the subject — it completely ignores the 50% off message.
- **"SEE WHAT'S NEW IN-STORE" section is a different email inserted mid-scroll.** It recruits for in-store discovery while the rest of the email is a sale conversion play; neither goal gets full attention.
- **No explicit sale deadline visible.** "Long weekend" implies Memorial Day but there's no end-date or countdown anywhere — urgency is soft.
- **No specific product hero with a direct link.** Every CTA routes to a category page; a single "Editor's pick at 50% off → [product]" link would give deal-seekers a shortcut.
- **Cadence starting to feel repetitive.** This is the same sitewide 50% off offer as May 20 with no new hook — no escalation ("extra 10% for members"), no highlighted category, nothing new to justify a second open.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the preheader to reinforce the offer.** The single highest-ROI fix — change "NEED AN ITEM SOONER? BUY ONLINE, PICK UP IN STORE" to something that amplifies the 50% off before the click. The in-store prompt could be a footer note or a late-body module.
- 2. **Add an explicit deadline.** "Ends Monday, 11:59 PM" costs nothing and converts urgency from implied to real.
- 3. **Feature one specific "most wanted" product at the hero.** A single sale item with its own CTA outperforms a category grid for decision-ready shoppers.
- 4. **Move or collapse the in-store section.** Drop it below the "SHOP BY" module or eliminate it entirely — the in-store ask belongs in a separate send or as a footer strip.
- 5. **Give deal-seekers a differentiated reason to open vs. May 20.** Angle it as a category highlight ("Women's styles up to 50% off") or a member escalation — anything that doesn't read as a forwarded copy of Monday's email.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last days: up to 50% off sitewide — ends Monday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Memorial Day sale is still on — 50% off sitewide`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Summer styles, sunlit hues, massive markdowns — shop now`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Women's, Men's, Kids', Home — all up to 50% off this weekend`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**External font imports will fail in most clients:**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` is blocked by Outlook (all versions), Yahoo Mail, and older Android clients. No fallback font stack is declared in the visible CSS — if these imports fail, the client's default serif or sans-serif is applied.

**Non-standard media query:**
```css
@media screen yahoo { ... }
```
This is a Yahoo Mail hack, not a W3C media feature. It functions as intended but will silently no-op in any client that doesn't recognize it — confirm test coverage on Yahoo.

**`alt` attributes** — not visible in truncated source; cannot confirm coverage on product images.

**`x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` meta** — present and correct.
**`format-detection` telephone** — present and correct.
