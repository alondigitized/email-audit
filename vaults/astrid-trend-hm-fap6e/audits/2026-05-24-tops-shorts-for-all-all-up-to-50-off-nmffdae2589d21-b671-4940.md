# Tops & shorts for all, all up to 50% off
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-24**
## Executive summary

- This is H&M's sixth Memorial Day sale email in five days — and the fatigue is starting to show. After the May 20 sitewide launch, the May 22 reminder, swim and denim category-pivots on May 23, today's send tries another category cut: "tops & shorts for all." Smart instinct, weak execution. The hero is a recycled-looking promotional banner; the body is a three-tile lookbook ("Perfect Combo," "Summer Uniform," "Mix & Match") that feels generic.
- The bigger problem is that the rendered creative leans on lookbook tiles when, by send #6 of the same sale, subscribers need either escalation (steeper discount, deadline) or genuine novelty (specific product, sub-$20 entry price). This send offers neither. The kids' tile is the most distinctive thing in the email, which is a poor signal for a "tops & shorts for all" promise.
- Net: a competent but tired entry. It earns an open from a recognizable sender on a holiday weekend, but it doesn't earn the click for a Walker-persona shopper who already saw four versions of this offer.

## What's working

- The sale ceiling ("up to 50% off") is preserved in the subject — recipients know the floor.
- Three category tiles cleanly segment men/women/kids, which serves the "for all" promise.
- Layout is clean — no broken images, no overlapping text, consistent typography.
- The kids' pink-tank tile is the strongest visual moment, with clear contrast and styling.
- "Shop What's New In Store" footer module gives the email a second purpose beyond the sale.

## What's weak

- Hero appears to be a static promo banner rather than product — wastes prime real estate on send #6.
- "Perfect Combo / Summer Uniform / Mix & Match" headers are generic; could apply to any season, any year.
- No specific prices visible — after five sale sends, "up to 50%" alone is no longer a hook.
- No deadline visible in the screenshot — Memorial Day urgency is implied but not stated.
- Zero loyalty / member callout, no specific hero product, no styling tip that would differentiate this from yesterday's denim send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with a price, not a percentage.** "Shorts from $9.99, tees from $7.99" beats "up to 50% off" on send six of the same campaign.
- 2. **Put a hard deadline in the hero.** "Ends Monday 11:59pm" — Memorial Day weekend demands it.
- 3. **Swap one of the three category tiles for a single hero product** with reviews/star rating — give shoppers one specific thing to click.
- 4. **Rename the tiles with category nouns** — "Men's tees," "Women's tanks," "Kids' shorts" — search-style clarity beats lookbook poetry on a sale email.
- 5. **Add a member-exclusive sweetener** (extra 10% with code, free shipping over $40) so loyalty subscribers have a reason to choose this send over the sitewide one.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Tees from $7.99, shorts from $9.99 — ends Monday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Memorial Day final hours: up to 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Members get an extra 10% — code inside.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock up the family before the sale ends Monday.`
