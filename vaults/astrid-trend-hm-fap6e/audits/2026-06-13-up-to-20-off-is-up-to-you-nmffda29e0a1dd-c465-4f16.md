# Up to 20% off is up to you
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- H&M is running a threshold-based weekend deal — 15% off when you spend $80, 20% off at $100 — framed as a broad summer styles send covering Women, Men, and Kids with a Father's Day in-store banner tacked on at the top. The creative execution is competent but the offer is structurally weaker than what H&M sent three days ago (June 10: 25% off sitewide, no threshold, midnight deadline). A subscriber who opened that flash sale email will feel the regression immediately.
- The subject line's wordplay — "Up to 20% off is up to you" — is clever in isolation but obscures the actual mechanic. "Up to you" implies optionality or personalization; what it means is "spend more, save more." That gap between the subject and the body creates mild disappointment on open. The Father's Day banner at the top competes with the weekend deal headline without reinforcing it — the email is trying to do two jobs (drive Father's Day in-store traffic and convert summer styles online) and does neither particularly well.
- The product sections (THE STANDOUTS, KEEP IT COOL, HELLO SUNSHINE) are clean and well-organized, but none of the product tiles are the primary hook. There's no hero product, no single moment the eye is meant to land on. This is a catalog email wearing deal clothes.

## What's working

- The 15%/20% off mechanic is spelled out clearly in the hero — no ambiguity about what the thresholds are.
- Three distinct sections (Women, Men, Kids) with labeled headers make it easy to navigate without reading everything.
- "WEEKEND DEAL" framing with "HELLO, SUNSHINE" kids section ties the send to a credible seasonal context.
- Subject line character count (28 chars) is well inside mobile-safe range and avoids spam signals.
- Father's Day banner is well-timed (email arrives June 13, Father's Day June 15), even if it's not integrated into the main offer.

## What's weak

- The June 10 flash sale offered 25% off sitewide with zero threshold — this "weekend deal" is objectively worse and subscribers will notice.
- Body copy beneath the hero offer reads "Bold and bright! Maybe red-minima? What will your summer style set?" — the middle phrase is awkward and unclear, weakening the moment right after the offer lands.
- No deadline anywhere in the email for the "WEEKEND DEAL" — "weekend" implies Sunday cutoff, but H&M doesn't say it. The June 10 email had "midnight ET" in the hero; this one leaves urgency to inference.
- No dominant CTA button — the only clickable elements are category navigation tiles (WOMEN / MEN / KIDS) that route to section homepages, not product pages or a dedicated sale landing page.
- Seven emails in six days from H&M; cadence fatigue is real and this send doesn't justify the frequency with an inferior offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **State the deadline explicitly in the hero** — add "Ends Sunday" or "This weekend only — ends 11:59 PM" directly beneath the offer line. The June 10 send proved a credible deadline converts; replicate it here.
- 2. **Rewrite the hero subhead** — cut "Maybe red-minima?" entirely. Replace with something that justifies the threshold: *"Spend $80, save 15%. Spend $100, save 20%. This weekend only."*
- 3. **Add a single CTA button** — below the offer block, one button labeled "SHOP THE WEEKEND DEAL" that goes to a filtered sale/summer collection page, not a category homepage.
- 4. **Acknowledge the threshold mechanic in the subject** — "Up to 20% off" implies a simple discount; the subject should signal the earn-to-unlock nature.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Spend $80, save 15% — spend $100, save 20%`
- **Subject Alt B:** `This weekend: the more you spend, the more you save`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Weekend-only tiers — unlock up to 20% off before Sunday ends`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Summer styles you want, discounts you earn — this weekend only`
- 5. **Retire the Father's Day banner or integrate it** — either lean into Father's Day with a specific menswear subsection and a "Gift him today, 15% off" message, or drop it. The current banner is a half-measure that dilutes both messages.
