# Your vacation wardrobe is here
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a vacation editorial from H&M — clean photography, two named products with prices, a single "VACATION EDIT" headline, and a tidy layout. Technically competent. But it lands the morning after H&M blasted "Sale is here: up to 60% off" (June 17), and the day after that they sent an app-exclusive teaser of the same sale (June 16). A subscriber fresh off two 60% off emails now receives a vacation edit with a vague "Save More" label and no stated discount percentage. That context makes this feel evasive rather than editorial.
- The creative logic is fine in isolation — "VACATION EDIT" echoes the confident tone of the June 15 partywear send ("GETTING HOTTER"), which got an 8. But that email was a deliberate no-discount editorial; it knew what it was. This one straddles: the hero is shot like editorial, the products have individual price tags and "Save More" labels, and neither signal is made to do real work.
- The single change that would rescue this email is tying it explicitly to the sale still running. Without that, it's a good-looking email that doesn't give a subscriber any reason to act today.

## What's working

- **Photography is genuinely strong:** the cream crepe skirt shot (daylight, airy, flowing) and the balloon-sleeve dress shot (moody, dark background) are both well-art-directed individually.
- **"VACATION EDIT" headline:** seasonal, occasion-specific, and confident — the right headline for a summer travel editorial.
- **Products are named and priced:** Gathered Crepe Skirt ($46.99) and Drawstring-Detail Balloon-Sleeved Dress ($59.99) are identified by name and price, not treated as anonymous hero images.
- **Layout is clean:** no visual noise, no over-crowding; the skim path from hero to product to shop-by grid is intuitive.
- **No render bugs:** all images load, text is legible, layout intact on desktop.

## What's weak

- **No sale connection:** after two consecutive 60% off sends, "Save More" without a specific amount or link to the active sale is a trust eroder.
- **Subject is passive and priceless:** "Your vacation wardrobe is here" is a statement, not an offer — low urgency, no hook, no discount signal.
- **Two disconnected product stories:** a neutral daytime skirt and a dark moody dress don't tell one cohesive vacation narrative; they feel like two mini-ads bolted together.
- **Generic CTAs:** "SHOP NOW" appears twice with no noun attached — no reason to click *this* CTA rather than just going directly to H&M.com.
- **Third email in three days:** cadence fatigue is real; a subscriber who received June 16 (app sale), June 17 (sale launch), and now this on June 18 is already hitting the unsubscribe threshold.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Tie to the active 60% off sale** — one line above or below the headline: "Now in the sale: up to 60% off." The sale is the live news; the vacation edit is the creative wrapper. Use both or choose one.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to lead with price or occasion specificity:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Vacation dresses from $46 — now in the sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Pack this: up to 60% off vacation picks`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Gathered skirts, balloon sleeves, sandals — everything you forgot to pack.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your summer getaway styles — now included in the H&M sale.`
- 3. **Add a was/now price pair to at least one product** — "Gathered Crepe Skirt — was $59.99, now $46.99" converts "Save More" from a vague label into a concrete reason to click.
- 4. **Sharpen both CTAs** — replace "SHOP NOW" with "Shop Vacation Dresses" for the hero and "See the Full Edit" for the second product block. Specificity reduces click hesitation.
- 5. **Space out sends** — a content/editorial email the morning after a major sale launch reads as filler. Two days' rest would let this creative breathe and avoid subscriber burn.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` for web fonts (confirmed issue)**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
Outlook (all versions) ignores `@import` entirely. Fonts will silently fall back. This is expected behavior for Outlook-targeted emails, but if brand font fidelity matters in desktop Outlook, these should be served via `<link>` in a conditional MSO comment or inlined as `font-face` declarations.

**`viewport` meta missing `initial-scale`**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width"/>
```
Should be `width=device-width, initial-scale=1` to prevent iOS Safari from auto-zooming on narrow layouts.

**Client-specific selector coupling**
```css
#MessageViewBody .hide-webkit { display: block !important; }
```
`#MessageViewBody` is a Gmail webmail DOM ID. This selector is Gmail-only and will have no effect in other clients, which is fine as long as the fallback display state is correct for non-Gmail. No bug if the hide/show logic degrades gracefully.

**Alt text**: Cannot verify — image tags are in the truncated body. Flag for full-source check.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
