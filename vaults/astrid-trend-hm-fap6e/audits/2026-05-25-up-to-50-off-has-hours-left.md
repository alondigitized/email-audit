# Up to 50% off has *hours* left
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- This is H&M's eighth Memorial Day sale email in six days — and the second send on May 25 alone, landing after "Last day: Up to 50% off your summer wardrobe" (8/10, same day). The creative instinct is right: "FINAL HOURS" positioning and the italicized *hours* in the subject are the strongest scarcity signal this campaign has used. But the email body doesn't honor the premise. Below the hero, the layout reverts to the same six-category navigation grid deployed across most of this series — no countdown, no "sale ends at midnight" specificity, no featured product, and no single primary CTA button.
- The campaign's arc was well-constructed: launch (May 20) → reminder (May 22) → swim and denim category pivots (May 23) → closing sends (May 24–25). Sending twice on closing day makes sense in principle. What doesn't hold up is reusing near-identical creative to yesterday's "Last day" send without escalating the urgency in the body. Most engaged subscribers either already converted or have learned to ignore the cadence.
- The email is clean and professionally executed — it just doesn't earn the urgency it promises in the subject line.

## What's working

- "FINAL HOURS" + "UP TO 50% OFF SITEWIDE" lands immediately at the top — the offer hierarchy is clear and the eye goes straight to it.
- The *hours* asterisk treatment in the subject adds editorial drama without triggering spam signals.
- The FREE SHIPPING member benefit is surfaced as the very first line — a useful loyalty signal for H&M members.
- No render bugs: clean layout, properly loaded hero image, no overlapping text.
- Hero model and summer styling are seasonally coherent — nothing feels recycled from a winter campaign.

## What's weak

- "FINAL HOURS" urgency has no hard deadline behind it — no "sale ends tonight at midnight ET" anywhere in the visible body.
- The category navigation grid (WOMEN / MEN / BABY / KIDS / HOME / BEAUTY) is copy-pasted from multiple prior sends; it signals routine, not final-hours emergency.
- Second send in one day after the "Last day" email — subscribers who saw that one will recognize this as filler and tune out.
- No featured product or linked hero item — the model is wearing something that isn't linked or priced, which kills the impulse-click moment.
- CTA is fragmented across six category buttons with no single "Shop the Final Hours Sale" primary button beneath the hero copy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an explicit deadline and make it prominent.** "Sale ends tonight at 11:59 PM ET" goes in the subject, the preheader, *and* directly under the hero copy in the body. "Hours" is vague — a specific cutoff is a commitment device.
- 2. **Replace the category grid hero with 2–3 featured products at sale prices.** A dress originally $59.99 now $29.99, a pair of shorts $49.99 now $24.99 — with a direct product link. That's the click driver. The category grid can live below it.
- 3. **Add one primary CTA button beneath the hero copy:** "Shop Final Hours — Up to 50% Off →". Put it before the category grid, not instead of it.
- 4. **Cut the redundant "SHOP BY" image grid** below the category buttons — it mirrors the buttons above it exactly and dilutes urgency with visual noise.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Hours left: 50% off ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Sale closes at midnight — up to 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop before it's gone — sale ends tonight at 11:59 PM ET`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Final hours: women's, men's, home & beauty all up to 50% off`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**External fonts via `@import` will fail in Gmail:**

```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```

Gmail strips `<style>` block `@import` rules entirely. Both font imports are inside `<style>` tags (not inline), so H&M's custom typeface will silently fall back to system fonts in Gmail — the largest email client by volume. This should be loaded via `<link>` in `<head>` or accepted as a known limitation with a tested fallback stack.

**Responsive breakpoint is 699px** (non-standard; industry convention is 600px). Unlikely to cause real failures but may clip on 640px–699px viewport widths depending on content widths.
