# New in: The ski collection by MOVE
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-02**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, editorial product-launch send for H&M's MOVE ski collection. "READY FOR SKI SEASON" sits under a strong snow-and-mountain hero with a model carrying skis. The imagery is good and the season fits. It is a catalog email, though, not an offer email. The only deal is the black banner at the top ("EARLY BLACK FRIDAY DEALS: 30% OFF SELECT STYLES IN-STORE*"). That banner is in-store only, has an asterisk, and doesn't apply to the new-arrival ski gear below it.
- The product grid is shoppable. Each item has a name, a price ($109 pants, $199 jacket, $64.99 top, $59.99 leggings) and a "New Arrival" tag. Every CTA is a generic category button (WOMEN / MEN / KIDS), and none link to a specific product that I can see. The hero shows a $199 price tag, which is a nice touch, but nothing says which item it belongs to.
- Compared with the 11/01 "Today only: 20% off in the app" and the 10/28 tiered-threshold send, this one is softer. It's a pure product-discovery beat after a run of promo-heavy sends. That's a fine rhythm change. As a standalone email it has weak urgency and a muddled offer, so I'd call it a pleasant 5.

## What's working

- The hero is striking and on-season: the model, skis and snowy backdrop make the ski concept clear at a glance.
- The "READY FOR SKI SEASON" headline is short and bold, and the body copy explains the collection (ThermoMove™, StormMove™).
- The product tiles show name, price and "New Arrival" tag, which makes the grid easy to scan.
- Prices start at $59.99 and are shown up front, so there is no price friction.
- The layout is clean, with generous white space and no visible broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- The only offer is the black banner. It reads in-store only, carries an asterisk, and doesn't relate to the new-arrival ski products, so it confuses the message.
- The CTAs are generic WOMEN / MEN / KIDS buttons, repeated twice. There is no verb and no product-specific link such as "Shop ski jackets."
- There is no urgency or deadline for the ski collection, so there's no reason to click today.
- The banner's red text on black is hard to read at small size, and "See it, try it, love it" is filler.
- The fine print takes up a large share of the footer, and the Black Friday terms read as exclusionary.
- There is no loyalty or member benefit in the body, and no reviews or social proof.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace the in-store banner with one clear offer tied to this collection, or drop the banner. Even "Free shipping on ski orders $75+" would give people a reason to click.
- 2. Swap the generic gender buttons for specific CTAs, such as "Shop ski jackets" and "Shop ski pants," and link each product tile to its product page.
- 3. Pin a headline price to the hero ("Ski pants from $59.99") so the offer is visible without scrolling.
- 4. Add a deadline or scarcity cue, like "limited run" or "sizes selling fast."
- 5. Move the fine print lower and shorten it.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
All 12 probed links returned 403 (16% pass rate), including the unsubscribe link. Every URL is a `t19.email.hm.com/r/` tracking redirect, so the 403s most likely come from bot filtering on the tracker rather than dead destinations. The email also has widespread missing alt text, and its JSON-LD block is empty. The HTML source was truncated, so the footer and MSO/conditional markup couldn't be checked.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **403 on all 12 probed links.** Examples are the hero CTA (`...f8a4f83a`), the "Shop now" links (`...f83c`, `...f83e`), the logo (`...f83b`), WOMEN/MEN/KIDS nav (`...f848`–`f84c`) and Unsubscribe (`...f89c`). Every probe was blocked, with no 2xx or 3xx among them. That pattern points to the tracker rejecting non-browser clients, not 12 independent broken links. This is unverified, and a real-client click test is needed to confirm.
- **Only 12 of 96 links were probed.** The other 84 are unverified.
- **Zero-width characters in link text.** The "Shop now" anchors contain invisible characters (`‌` U+200C, U+FEFF) before the visible text. This is likely a preheader-style spacing hack, but it pollutes link text for assistive tech and text extraction.
- **Image-only anchors fall back to `[image: …]` link text.** Examples are `bM=` and `WogmrXro=`, which tells us the link has no accessible name.
- **Recipient-specific identifiers in every URL.** `did`, `rid`, `erid`, and `p2` (a 32-char hex) are in the query string. This is normal for ESP tracking, but it means the URLs are per-recipient and can't be compared across sends.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **16 images flagged for missing alt text.** These include:
  - hero and campaign images: `SS50265P01-FN-3x2-1.jpg`, `-2x3.jpg`, `-3x4.jpg`, `-3x2-2.jpg`
  - product images: `203db210…jpg`, `e3db0418…jpg`, `8e723ee7…jpg`, `30a65e0d…jpg`, each in `imWidth=344` and `imWidth=322` variants
  - two images whose `src` is a `t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=…` tracking URL (likely tracking pixels or tracked images, which should carry `alt=""`)
- **Duplicate image sets.** Each product appears twice at widths 344 and 322, probably desktop and mobile variants toggled with `.is-hidden-mobile` and `.is-hidden-desktop`. Hidden duplicates still go into the accessibility tree and double the screen-reader output unless they're also `aria-hidden`/`mso-hide`. I couldn't confirm this in the truncated source.
- **Web fonts via `@import`** (`fabric.hmgroup.com`, `s1-cdn.hm.com/.../oces.css`) are unsupported in Gmail and Outlook. The fallback font stacks couldn't be checked in the truncated source.
- **Plain-text part is mostly URLs.** 518 of 596 characters are URLs, so the plain-text alternative is close to unusable.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No unresolved merge tokens (`{{…}}`, `%%…%%`) are visible in the supplied source or QA data. The JSON-LD schema block is unpopulated: `subjectLine: ""`, `discountCode: ""`, `availabilityEnds: ""`, `image: ""`, `url: ""`, and `availabilityStarts: "2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"`, which looks like a template default. The block provides no value as written and may be ignored or rejected by Gmail's markup parser.

## 5. Compliance
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found.** The QA tool says the AgentMail relay may strip these headers, so this is **not confirmed as missing at the sender**. Verify against the raw message at the origin.
- **`Authentication-Results` was not found.** SPF, DKIM, and DMARC status is unknown from this data and is not a confirmed failure.
- **The in-body unsubscribe link exists** (`...f89c`, with `p4`/`p5` recipient tokens) but returned 403 to the probe. Its function is unverified. Test it in a real browser.
- **The physical postal address** wasn't visible in the truncated source, so it couldn't be checked.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- **UTM parameters can't be assessed.** All links use the redirect pattern `?id=…&did=…&rid=…&erid=…&p1=DM6187715&p2=…&p3=20261102`. The final destination URLs, and any UTM parameters appended after the redirect, are hidden behind the 403s.
- **The campaign identifier is `p1=DM6187715`** and the send date is `p3=20261102`. These are the only trackable campaign markers visible.
- **Landing page alignment can't be checked** for the same reason. No destination was resolved.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Re-probe the links with a browser-like client (a real user agent, or a headless browser that follows redirects). That will confirm whether the 403s are bot filtering or real failures. Prioritize the unsubscribe link and the hero CTA.
2. Check the raw message at the origin for `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and for SPF, DKIM, and DMARC results. The relay may have dropped them.
3. Add `alt` text to all content images and set `alt=""` on tracking and decorative images. Add accessible names to image-only links.
4. Remove the zero-width characters from the "Shop now" link text.
5. Populate the JSON-LD (`subjectLine`, `url`, `image`, valid offer dates) or remove the block.
6. Shorten the plain-text part's URL load, and hide the duplicate mobile or desktop images from assistive tech.
7. Probe more than 12 links, and capture post-redirect destinations so the UTM and landing page checks can run.
