# Up to 20% off your order starts now
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-25**
## Executive summary

- This is H&M's ninth-ish promotional send in a two-week window, and it swaps the blunt "sitewide %" framing from July 19-22 for a tiered spend threshold: 15% off $60 or 20% off $80. That's a step up in offer sophistication — it rewards bigger baskets instead of just screaming a flat discount — but the subject line ("Up to 20% off your order") still leads with the ceiling number, which means most shoppers clicking in expecting a flat 20% will land on a $60-minimum reality first.
- The body itself is clean and on-brand: bold "WEEKEND DEAL" hero copy, then an immediate six-tile category grid (Women/Men/Baby/Kids/Home/Beauty) with zero single hero product. That's the real weakness — there's no one CTA to follow, just a wall of equally-weighted doors, so the email reads more like a homepage teleport than a curated pitch.
- Given the brand history — five-plus near-identical sale blasts in the prior week, alternating "ends tonight" urgency with full-price editorials — this send is a mild positive break in creative but a continuation of fatigue-inducing cadence. Middling execution on a middling idea: 6/10 territory, consistent with H&M's recent run.

## What's working

- Clean, bug-free render — no overlapping text, no broken images, grid tiles all populated correctly.
- The tiered offer (15% off $60 / 20% off $80) is more transparent than a flat "up to X%" banner alone, since both tiers are spelled out in the hero.
- "WEEKEND DEAL" plus a valid-through date in the fine print (7/26/2026) gives the urgency real teeth instead of vague "limited time" language.
- Strong top-of-funnel utility line ("Need it sooner? Buy online, pick up in-store") answers a real shopper question before the pitch even starts.

## What's weak

- No single primary CTA — six identically-weighted category tiles force the reader to make a choice the email should have made for them.
- Subject says "up to 20% off" but that requires an $80 spend; the more likely outcome for most baskets is 15%, which is a soft bait-and-switch on the headline number.
- Heavy, buried fine print (exclusions on sale items, "Final Sale" merchandise, gift cards, no combining with other offers) undercuts the "honest offer" read — a shopper won't know if their cart qualifies until checkout.
- Zero product-level detail (no price, no fabric, no fit) anywhere in the grid — it's category thumbnails only, not merchandising.
- This is another near-weekly discount email in a brand history dominated by discount emails (07-19, 07-22, 07-24) — cadence fatigue risk is real.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one hero category or product and lead with it — e.g., feature the Women's denim look actually shown in the grid as the hero image with its own "SHOP WOMEN'S NEW ARRIVALS" button, then drop the other categories below as secondary links. One clear CTA beats six.
- 2. Rewrite the subject to state the guaranteed discount, not the ceiling — lead with 15% (the floor everyone gets) and tease 20% as the upside, so nobody feels misled.
- 3. Move the $5 Member Bonus Reward mention out of the legal fine print and into the visible body — if a shopper is a member, that stacking detail is a real click driver and it's currently invisible.
- 4. Trim the exclusions block or at minimum flag "excludes sale items and Final Sale" near the hero offer, not six paragraphs down — reduces the "gotcha" feeling at checkout.
- 5. Space this out from the four other promo sends this week — even a strong offer erodes engagement at this frequency.
