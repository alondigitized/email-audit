# In focus: '90s-inspired dressing

- This is an editorial lookbook with no offer in it. After a week of "20% off" and "up to 30% off" sends, H&M switches to a pure "'90s Minimalism" story. The black cropped jacket, bodycon slip dress and creased leggings are shot well, and the hierarchy is clean. The tone is on-brand but a step down in urgency from the 10/3 and 10/4 member sends.
- The price tags are on the images but there is no deal anywhere. The hero's "CROPPED BELT-DETAIL JACKET $69.99" overlay is white text over a pale blue-grey background, so it's hard to read. The single "SHOP NOW" button is generic and goes to a whole category. Anyone who was primed by the discount sends gets nothing here to act on.

## What's working

- The hero is strong. One model in a sharp black blazer sits over a tidy "'90S MINIMALISM" headline.
- The copy is short and evocative: "sleek minimalism… sharp silhouettes, sculptural tailoring."
- The palette is consistent, with black on a cool blue-grey backdrop, so the email feels designed rather than templated.
- Each product block has a name, a price and a "New Arrival" tag, so basic product info is visible.
- The "Shop by" grid and app/store links give easy exits to other categories.

## What's weak

- No offer or member benefit appears anywhere, even though the last five sends led with percent-off banners.
- The hero price overlay ("$69.99") is white on light grey-blue and nearly illegible.
- The bodycon dress is shown in a 2x2 grid of near-identical poses. It reads as repetitive and wastes the space.
- There is only one CTA, a generic "SHOP NOW", and the product blocks don't show a clear button.
- There are no reviews, sizing or fit cues, and no urgency.
- The large gaps and empty space around the leggings block make the middle feel sparse.

- 1. Add a clear value hook, such as a thin "Members: 20% off new arrivals" strip above the hero. It would carry over the previous days' momentum and make the email feel like a campaign.
- 2. Fix the hero overlay. Put the price on a solid or darkened chip, or move it below the image.
- 3. Swap the 2x2 dress grid for one hero shot plus a different angle or an accessory, and give each product its own button ("Shop the dress").
- 4. Make the CTA specific, for example "Shop the '90s edit" instead of "SHOP NOW".
- 5. Tighten the vertical gaps and add a social-proof line such as "Back in stock" or a rating.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's HTML is a conventional table-based template. All 12 probed links returned 403, and the List-Unsubscribe and authentication headers could not be seen. Neither the 403s nor the missing headers can be confirmed as defects. The JSON-LD block has empty fields, and most images lack usable alt text.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **All 12 probed links returned 403** (16% pass rate). They go through the click-tracking redirector `https://t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=h88c587f8-…,548ed102,<suffix>&did=1418645762&rid=3326930539&…`. The affected links include:
  - Both "SHOP NOW" CTAs (`…f85d384f`, `…f85d3850`)
  - Both "Shop now" CTAs (`…f85d3843`, `…f85d3845`)
  - The "H&M" logo link (`…f85d3842`)
  - Image-wrapped links (`…f85d384d`, `…f85d384e`, `…f85d3851`, `…f85d3852`, `…f85d3855`, `…f85d3856`)
  - Unsubscribe (`…f85d3887`)
- A uniform 403 across every link, including the logo, points to the redirector rejecting the automated probe (bot/UA filtering) rather than to 12 broken destinations. This is unconfirmed, and a real-client click test is needed to settle it.
- **Unprobed links:** 47 links were skipped, so their status is unknown.
- **Wrapped destinations:** Every `href` is a redirect wrapper, so final landing URLs can't be checked from the source.
- **Duplicate IDs:** Several CTAs share the `548ed102` prefix and differ only in the hex suffix, so each link is individually tracked. That is fine.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** Nine campaign images and the tracked link/image at `t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=…,1` are flagged. Examples are `FNP-WA10264P07-4x5-1-90s-Fall.jpg` and `FNP-WA10264P07-2x3-2-90s-Fall.jpg`, plus `…2x3-4…`, `…3x2-5…` and `…2x3-6…` at both the 1400px and 750px variants.
- **Filename-style link text:** The 403 report shows link text as `[image: R1QaRscPaw=]`, `[image: dfHx2FXg=]` and `[image: cq5dam.web.1400.1400.jpeg]`. This means the alt text is empty or a CMS filename. Image-only links with no meaningful alt are unusable to screen readers and show nothing when images are blocked.
- **Invalid CSS:** `line-height:auto!important` in `.is-hidden-desktop` is not a valid value.
- **Web fonts:** The `@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/...)` and `@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/...)` rules are stripped by Gmail and some other clients. This is a fallback-font risk only, and the source doesn't show whether fallbacks are declared.
- **JSON-LD:** The `<script type="application/ld+json">` block is mostly empty:
  - `"subjectLine":""`
  - `"discountCode":""`, `"description":""`, `"availabilityEnds":""`
  - `PromotionCard` with `"image":""` and `"url":""`
  - `availabilityStarts` is a stale `2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00`

  Empty values can cause Gmail to reject the schema markup, and an empty `DiscountOffer` is leftover template boilerplate.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The truncated source shows no unrendered tokens such as `{{…}}`, `%%…%%` or `[FirstName]`. Per-recipient values appear only in the tracking parameters (`rid`, `erid`, `p2`, `p4`, `p5`), and they are populated.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** Both were not found. The QA tool notes the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this can't be confirmed as a sender defect. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require one-click unsubscribe, so the original message should be checked.
- **Authentication-Results:** Not found. SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unverified, again probably because of the relay.
- **In-body unsubscribe:** A link is present (`…f85d3887`, with `p4`/`p5` recipient tokens). It returned 403 to the probe, which is probably the same bot filtering. It needs a manual click test.
- **Physical address:** The footer is outside the truncated source, so it was not verified.
- **Plain-text part:** 514 of 592 characters (about 87%) are URLs. The text part is mostly long tracking URLs, which is a minor spam-filter signal.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **No `utm_*` parameters:** The tracking URLs use only the proprietary `did`, `rid`, `erid`, `p1` (`DM6110279`), `p2` and `p3` (`20261005`). If the destination URLs add UTMs, they are applied after the redirect, which this audit can't see.
- **Landing pages not verified:** The redirects can't be resolved because of the 403s. The campaign image paths (`wa10264p07 … 90s-Fall`) are consistent with the "'90s-inspired dressing" subject, but landing alignment can't be confirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Re-probe `t19.email.hm.com` links with a browser-like UA, or follow them manually, to tell scanner blocking from real failures. Test the unsubscribe link first.
2. Capture the raw message (not the relay copy) to check `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results` for SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment.
3. Add descriptive `alt` text to the `FNP-WA10264P07-*` images and to the image-wrapped links, especially the ones with `[image: …]` filename labels.
4. Populate or remove the empty JSON-LD fields (`subjectLine`, `discountCode`, `url`, `image`, and the stale 2018 `availabilityStarts`).

6. Shorten the plain-text part or add more readable copy to it.
