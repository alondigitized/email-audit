# Up to 50% off is on the rise
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- H&M is back in sale mode after a four-day editorial cool-down, and this time the headline number is bigger than anything in the recent run: 50% off vs. the 25% flash sale that scored 8/10 on July 9. The escalation strategy makes sense — each deal email in this sequence has pushed a slightly larger discount claim to re-engage whoever didn't bite earlier. The hero is clear, the render is clean, and the member giveaway adds a loyalty layer that the editorial run was missing.
- The problem is that this email is trying to tell two separate stories. The main body is a sitewide sale pitch; halfway down there's a "BACK-TO-SCHOOL BUNDLE" member giveaway for a $250 gift card. These two mechanics — shop-the-sale vs. enter-to-win — compete for attention and neither lands as hard as it would in a dedicated send. The giveaway drops in mid-scroll like it was inserted into the template slot rather than integrated into the narrative.
- The deeper issue is the double qualifier: "UP TO 50% OFF ALMOST EVERYTHING." Two hedge words on the same headline claim. A subscriber who saw "New sale arrivals up to 70% off" on July 11 will read "up to 50% off almost everything" as a step backward, not forward. The July 9 flash sale worked precisely because it said "25% off" with no asterisks. This email has a bigger number but less conviction.

## What's working

- **Hero is immediately legible:** "UP TO 50% OFF ALMOST EVERYTHING" in heavy type reads in two seconds at thumbnail size — no ambiguity about what the email is.
- **Category grid is inclusive:** Five department links (Women, Men, Baby, Kids, Home) give different household members an entry point without funneling everyone through one gendered page.
- **Member Giveaway is a smart loyalty touch:** A $250 gift card sweepstakes is a genuine reason to create or log into an H&M account — even if placement is awkward, the mechanic is sound.
- **Clean render throughout:** No broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breakage visible at this viewport.
- **"PEAK-SUMMER SAVINGS" label** anchors the offer to the calendar without straining credibility.

## What's weak

- **Double qualifier kills the headline:** "Up to 50% off almost everything" stacks "up to" and "almost" in the same sentence — two layers of hedge that erode confidence in the number right at the moment of peak attention.
- **Two competing mechanics:** A sitewide sale and a sweepstakes entry in the same send creates decision fatigue. The giveaway is orphaned mid-scroll.
- **No deadline anywhere:** The July 9 flash sale (8/10) had a midnight cutoff that created real urgency. This email has none — "on the rise" in the subject is the closest thing to a time signal and it's vague.
- **Back-to-School framing feels early:** Mid-July BTS messaging alongside "PEAK-SUMMER SAVINGS" sends a confused seasonal signal — summer or back-to-school?
- **CTA diffusion:** Category buttons, SIGN IN, JOIN NOW, app download links, Find Store — the email doesn't commit to a primary action.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split the two mechanics into two sends.** Run the 50% off sale email clean with zero giveaway. Send the Back-to-School member bundle next week with BTS product wrapped around it — it earns its own real estate.
- 2. **Cut the double qualifier.** Test "50% OFF SITEWIDE" or pick the cleanest sub-category headline you can defend without hedging. The July 9 flash sale proved this brand's subscribers respond to precision.
- 3. **Add a hard deadline.** Even "Offer ends Sunday at midnight" transforms "on the rise" intrigue into genuine urgency. No other email in this July run (except July 9) had one.
- 4. **Consolidate the CTA.** One primary button above the fold — "Shop the Sale" — then let the category grid serve as secondary navigation below.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Peak summer sale: 50% off ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The summer sale just got bigger — shop now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Warm-weather styles, seriously marked down. New drops added today.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `50% off — shop before the best styles sell out.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` for web fonts — not supported in most clients**

```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```

`@import` inside `<style>` is stripped by Gmail, Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo Mail. Fonts will silently fall back to system fonts in those clients. This is expected behavior in email HTML but worth logging — if brand typography is critical, web-safe fallback stacks should be validated.

**Duplicate CSS rules in mobile media query**

Four rule blocks are duplicated verbatim inside `@media (max-width:699px)`:

```css

.flexible-elements-secondary-text-block-headline,...{width:327px!important}
.flexible-elements-secondary-text-block-headline-text{font-size:24px!important;line-height:28px!important}
```

No rendering impact (last rule wins and they're identical), but adds dead weight to the style block.
