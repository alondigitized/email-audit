# Go-to summer looks
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-29**
## Executive summary

- H&M is back in editorial mode with "Go-to summer looks" — the third lookbook-style send in four days, following the SANTOS BRAVOS collaboration on June 28 and "Light layers in soft summer tones" on June 26. The creative is clean and the product grid is genuinely generous: nine SKUs across multiple rows, each priced and tagged "New Arrival," anchored by a bold zebra-print hero image. As a piece of design, it works.
- But the email is entirely unremarkable as a marketing send. "Go-to summer looks" is arguably the most generic subject line a fashion brand can write — it says nothing specific about price, product, or style. There's no offer, no deadline, no social proof, and both CTAs say the same thing. For a subscriber who has now received five or six H&M emails in a row, this one will scroll past without friction, which is the same as not existing.
- The email succeeds at filling the inbox. It does not succeed at earning a click.

## What's working

- Clean visual hierarchy: zebra-print hero → bold "GO-TO SUMMER LOOKS" headline → "SHOP NOW" → product grid. Eye lands exactly where it should.
- Product grid shows nine items with real prices ($9.99–$49.99) and "New Arrival" labels — more shoppable than the June 26 purely atmospheric lookbook.
- The $9.99 T-shirt and $9.99 Asymmetric Turtleneck Top act as strong low-anchor price signals in the grid.
- No render bugs, no broken images, no overlapping text — technically clean.
- Subject is 18 characters — excellent mobile length.

## What's weak

- "Go-to summer looks" is the generic-est possible subject for a fashion brand. No product, no price, no hook — nothing to separate it from the dozen other summer emails in any inbox.
- No offer at all: no discount, no free shipping, no urgency signal of any kind.
- Both CTAs are identical "SHOP NOW" with no destination specificity — they almost certainly land on a generic category page, not a curated landing matching the editorial.
- Third editorial-style email in four days — lookbook fatigue is real, and this one is more forgettable than the SANTOS BRAVOS collab two days prior.
- Zero social proof: no ratings, no "bestseller" badge, nothing that makes any of the nine products feel validated.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the price in the subject.** "New arrivals from $9.99 — summer done right" transforms this from ambient marketing into a reason to click. The grid proves the value is there; the subject should say so.
- 2. **Differentiate the two CTAs.** The mid-grid "SHOP NOW" should link specifically to a "New Arrivals" filter or the exact products shown. The hero CTA can stay broad.
- 3. **Add a price line to the hero copy.** Below "GO-TO SUMMER LOOKS," a single line like "From $9.99" would dramatically lift click intent without redesigning anything.
- 4. **Add at least one bestseller signal.** One "Top Rated" or "Back by Demand" label in the product grid creates social proof that editorial copy can't.
- 5. **Break the editorial-only cadence.** If H&M is going to send three editorial emails in four days, each needs a stronger differentiating hook than the last. This one has none.
