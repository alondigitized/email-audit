# Tennis favorites for every fan
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-11**
## Executive summary

- This is the first genuinely non-discount, non-Labor-Day send from H&M in over a week — "SERVING LOOKS" is a clean tennis-core product story with prices visible, no "up to 50% off" banner, no fake urgency. It's a welcome break from the promo treadmill flagged on 09/03 through 09/09. The problem is it's also just product photography and category buttons — there's no offer at all, which caps how hard it can pull a click compared to something like 09/09's "Game-day looks" send (8.00/10), which paired a fresh creative angle with a real reason to act now.
- The bigger miss is relevance drift: after the tennis hero and six product tiles, the email abruptly pivots to "FORM & FUNCTION" and H&M Home's Fall 2026 collection — a jarring non-sequitur that dilutes the tennis focus and reads like two unrelated emails stapled together.
- Solid seasonal creative, zero urgency or discount hook, and a tacked-on home-goods section that undercuts the theme. Middle-of-the-pack send — better than the Labor Day fatigue, but not as sharp as 09/09.

## What's working

- Hero image is a clean, on-brand tennis action shot (model mid-serve on a clay court) — strong seasonal/category signal.
- "SERVING LOOKS" headline and subcopy ("Comfortable, breathable, flexible... from the court to the stands") is specific and on-theme, not generic.
- Three clear audience segment buttons (Women/Men/Kids) right under the hero make navigation easy.
- Six product tiles show real prices ($19.99–$39.99), product names, and consistent SoftMove™/DryMove™ technology callouts — feels like real merchandising, not filler.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, no overlapping text or broken layout.

## What's weak

- No offer anywhere — no %, no $ off, no BOGO, nothing time-bound. Pure browse-only email.
- The "FORM & FUNCTION" / H&M Home section breaks the tennis narrative entirely — it has nothing to do with the subject line and feels bolted on.
- Primary CTA is diffuse — three category buttons plus a "SHOP NOW" for Home means no single unambiguous next action.
- Subject "Tennis favorites for every fan" oversells scope slightly — six items across three categories is a modest assortment for "every fan."

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut or spin off the H&M Home section — it has zero connection to tennis and actively weakens the email's focus; if cross-sell is required, use a smaller, clearly-labeled "You might also like" module instead of a full second hero.
- 2. Add one concrete hook — even a soft one like "New arrivals" badge or member-early-access framing would lift click intent since there's currently no reason to act now versus later.
- 3. Collapse to a single primary CTA above the fold (e.g., "SHOP TENNIS") and push the Women/Men/Kids split lower or into the product grid itself.
- 4. Tighten the subject to match the actual assortment size instead of overpromising:
- **Subject Alt A:** `New tennis styles, on and off the court`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Match-ready looks from $19.99`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `SoftMove™ and DryMove™ pieces for women, men and kids`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Six new tennis pieces just landed`
