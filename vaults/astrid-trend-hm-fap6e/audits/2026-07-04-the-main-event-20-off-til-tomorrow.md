# The main event: 20% off 'til tomorrow
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-04**
## Executive summary

- H&M fires its third July 4th-adjacent send in roughly 36 hours — this time hanging "the main event" framing on the same member 20% off that has been the engine of every send since June 30. The subject is the sharpest of the week: concrete percentage, specific deadline, no patriotic noise. The email itself is cleaner than July 3rd's two sends — the hero stacks "HAPPY FOURTH OF JULY" over a bold "20% OFF ALMOST EVERYTHING" and keeps the seasonal nod to a red-striped polo and a breezy white dress. Functional. Unfussy.
- The inbox read is where it stumbles. The preheader appears to pull from the top banner — "BECOME A MEMBER: GET 10% OFF YOUR FIRST PURCHASE + MY PERKS!" — which is a member-acquisition pivot that works against the urgent deadline the subject just promised. A subscriber opening to "the main event ends tomorrow" and previewing "become a member" gets a dissonant first impression before they even tap in.
- Below the hero, the "UP TO 70% OFF + NEW STYLES ADDED" sale section reappears for the third consecutive email. At this point the ratchet is visible and the "main event" positioning loses credibility — if 70% off is also in every send, 20% off is not the main event. The cadence is starting to tax trust more than it's building urgency.

## What's working

- Subject line is the brand's cleanest of the week — "'til tomorrow" is a specific, credible deadline that creates real urgency without screaming
- "20% OFF ALMOST EVERYTHING" in the hero is unambiguous; the eye lands there in under a second
- July 4th framing stays tasteful — the red-and-white striped menswear shot is a subtle, on-theme product nod
- Category grid (WOMEN / MEN / KIDS / HOME) makes navigation immediate and frictionless
- No visible render bugs; images load, layout is intact

## What's weak

- Preheader pulls the member-recruitment bar ("BECOME A MEMBER: GET 10% OFF YOUR FIRST PURCHASE + MY PERKS!") — completely off-message from the deadline urgency the subject established
- "ALMOST EVERYTHING" qualifier in the hero headline introduces exclusion doubt without resolving it anywhere above the fine print wall
- The UP TO 70% OFF sale section reappears below the fold for the third straight email, directly undermining the "main event" positioning
- No visible expiry time — "'til tomorrow" is in the subject but not reinforced anywhere in the email body itself
- No personalization, no segmentation signal — reads identically across the entire list

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader immediately.** Replace the member-recruitment bar as the leading inbox text with deadline reinforcement — "Offer ends midnight July 4th" beats a cold acquisition pitch for anyone already subscribed.
- 2. **Cut or bury the 70% off section.** Two major competing offers in one email dilute both. If this is "the main event," own it — remove the stacked sale section or push it to a small footer link.
- 3. **Surface the deadline inside the email body.** Add "Offer ends 11:59 PM July 4" under the hero CTA buttons. The subject creates urgency the body never closes.
- 4. **Sharpen the art direction.** A red/white/blue lifestyle scene would deliver on the seasonal framing; the current product-on-white feels like a standard sale email wearing a July 4th header.
- 5. **Segment by category.** Even a simple Women vs. Men split would lift click relevance on an email where four equal-weight CTAs compete for the click.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your July 4th 20% off — ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `20% off almost everything. Today only.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Offer ends midnight — no code needed. Just your H&M account.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Beach days, BBQs, 20% off. Tonight only — shop before it's gone.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading — confirmed broken in Outlook:**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
Outlook (all versions), Yahoo Mail, and some Android clients strip `@import` inside `<style>` blocks. Custom fonts will silently fall back to system defaults — but only if the stack includes a web-safe fallback. If the fallback is missing from the font stack, Outlook will render in Times New Roman.

**`alt` text on images** — not verifiable from truncated HTML. Flag for full-source check.

**`.prevent-auto-links` pattern** — correctly implemented to suppress iOS auto-detection of phone numbers/addresses. No issue.
