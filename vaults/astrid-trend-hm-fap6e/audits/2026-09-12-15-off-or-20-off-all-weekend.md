# 15% off or 20% off all weekend
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-12**
## Executive summary

- This is a tiered-discount weekend deal — "15% off $75 or 20% off $100" — built around fall transition pieces (tops, sweaters). It's a clean, well-organized send: one hero, a clear offer tier, then a "For Her / For Him / For Them" segmented shop structure that's genuinely useful for navigation. Compared to the brand's recent run of flat "up to 50% off sitewide" spam, this is a more honest, structured offer with real spend thresholds spelled out, though the two-tier math ("15% off $75 OR 20% off $100") adds a beat of mental math before the reader knows what they're actually getting.
- Against the brand history, this lands mid-pack — better organized than the generic Labor Day reruns but still a straightforward percentage-off promo with no new creative idea, following just a day after two non-discount editorial sends (09/10, 09/11) that scored the same 5/10. It's a step back toward the discount treadmill.

## What's working

- Clear tiered offer stated in large type immediately below the hero ("15% OFF $75 OR 20% OFF $100").
- "Only 10 days until fall" gives a credible, season-relevant reason for the promo timing.
- For Her / For Him / For Them structure with dedicated Tops/Sweaters buttons makes navigation genuinely easy.
- No render bugs — images, buttons, and text all display cleanly.
- Free shipping over $50 for members is called out at the very top, adding a second visible benefit.

## What's weak

- The dual-tier discount math ("15% off $75 or 20% off $100") requires the reader to do arithmetic before understanding the real value — a single clear number would land faster.
- The hero model image doesn't show the actual sweater/top product clearly enough to sell the "cozy sweater" story — it's more atmospheric than product-forward.
- This is another straight percentage-off promo, breaking the momentum of the last two days' well-received editorial, non-discount sends (09/10, 09/11).
- No urgency/deadline is visible anywhere in the rendered body (just "weekend deal" with no explicit end date/time shown).

## Recommendations

- 1. Simplify to one clean number instead of a two-tier calculation — e.g., "Spend $75, save 15%" as a single visible line, or just lead with the higher tier ("Up to 20% off") and let fine print carry the threshold.
- 2. Add a visible countdown/deadline (e.g., "Ends Sunday 11:59pm") near the offer block — currently nothing signals when this expires.
- 3. Swap the hero image for one that actually shows the sweater/top category being promoted, since the copy explicitly sells "cozy sweaters."
- 4. Keep the For Her/Him/Them segmentation — it's a strong structural pattern worth reusing on future sends.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Save 15-20% this weekend only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your fall sweater refresh: up to 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $75+ or $100+ and save — ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New tops and sweaters for the season ahead`

## Technical Audit

1. Technical Summary
This is a standard H&M Adobe Campaign transactional/promo email with valid deliverability structure, but every probed redirect link (t19.email.hm.com click-tracker) returned a 403, and both compliance-relevant email headers were absent from the capture.

2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 12/12 probed links returned HTTP 403, including both primary "Shop now" CTAs, the member-benefits banner link, the H&M logo link, and 5 product image links — all routed through `https://t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=h3a492e19-dea4-44ec-86a8-a037778035a9,...&did=1418149086&rid=3326930539&erid=...&p1=DM6038416&p2=0982dd0aa7b117bf086dee6ed4af7d0c&p3=20260912`.
- The Unsubscribe link itself is also 403'ing (`...&p4=3326930539&p5=9be06746af74de7271d9dcc8d83f5742`) — this is the most severe finding since it blocks a CAN-SPAM-required mechanism, not just marketing content.
- 403 on a redirector domain is typically a bot/User-Agent block, IP allowlist, or an expired/invalid click-tracking signature rather than a dead destination — cannot confirm from static HTML alone whether a real subscriber click would also fail; recommend a live click-through test.
- 60 additional links (footer/social/utility) were deprioritized and not probed per QA methodology.

3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 15 images missing `alt` text, including all 5 product hero images (`cq5dam.web.1400.1400.jpeg` / `cq5dam.web.750.750.jpeg` pairs at `image.hm.com/content/dam/hm/regional-local-activities/ame/2026-ame/wk-37/na1547_s4/...jpg`) and the tracking pixel at `t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=...,1`. With images blocked by default in most mail clients, this leaves the entire visual content non-communicative for screen-reader and images-off users.
- Mobile responsive CSS (`@media (max-width:699px)`) and Outlook/Yahoo/webkit conditional hacks (`#MessageViewBody`, `@media screen yahoo`) are present and structured normally — no malformed selectors observed in the truncated source.

4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The `application/ld+json` schema.org block has multiple empty fields that appear to be unresolved merge tokens: `subjectLine:""`, `discountCode:""`, `availabilityEnds:""`, `image:""`, `url:""` under the `DiscountOffer`/`PromotionCard`/`EmailMessage` objects. `availabilityStarts` is also hardcoded to `"2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"`, a stale value inconsistent with the current send date.
- These fields feed Gmail/Outlook promotional markup (annotated offers, logo display) — empty/stale values mean rich promotion markup will not render correctly, though this doesn't affect the visible HTML body.

5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- Unsubscribe link is present in the HTML but returns 403 when probed (see §2) — functional compliance cannot be confirmed.
- `List-Unsubscribe` header: not found in capture (WARN notes this may be an artifact of the AgentMail relay rather than the sender omitting it — cannot confirm sender fault from this data alone).
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header (RFC 8058 one-click): not found — same caveat.
- `Authentication-Results` header (SPF/DKIM/DMARC outcome): not found — cannot verify authentication passed; caveat as above re: relay capture.
- Recommend re-checking headers against a direct (non-relayed) mailbox capture before treating these as sender-side defects.

6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- No UTM parameters (`utm_source`/`utm_medium`/`utm_campaign`) present on any link; H&M uses proprietary Adobe Campaign params instead (`did`, `rid`, `erid`, `p1`–`p5`) for attribution. This is consistent with their platform, not a defect, but means standard GA-style UTM continuity cannot be evaluated.
- Cannot verify landing page alignment since all destination links 403 before reaching the final H&M PDP/category page.

7. Recommendations
- Priority 1: Investigate the 403s on `t19.email.hm.com` — check whether the probe's User-Agent/IP was blocked by H&M's bot protection vs. a genuinely broken/expired tracking signature. Re-test the Unsubscribe link specifically with a standard browser session, since a non-functional unsubscribe is a compliance risk.
- Priority 2: Add descriptive `alt` text to the 5 product images and hero banner; at minimum ensure alt text isn't empty for content-bearing images.
- Priority 3: Fix the empty/stale `ld+json` fields (`subjectLine`, `discountCode`, `availabilityEnds`, `image`, `url`, `availabilityStarts`) so Gmail/Outlook promotional annotations render correctly.
- Priority 4: Re-verify `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and `Authentication-Results` against a raw (non-relay) header capture before escalating as sender defects.
