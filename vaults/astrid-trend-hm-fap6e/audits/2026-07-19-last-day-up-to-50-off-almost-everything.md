# Last day: Up to 50% off almost everything
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- This is H&M's fifth consecutive sitewide-50%-off email this week — and "Last day" is the one genuinely new thing it brings. The hero lands cleanly: bold headline, three lifestyle shots, a row of category buttons. But there's a critical disconnect: the subject screams deadline urgency and the email body doesn't reinforce it. Not once does the body say "ends tonight" or "sale closes at midnight." That's a direct conversion miss.
- Meanwhile the subheadline pivots to H&M MOVE editorial voice — "Gear up for a winning season with transitional picks and flexible fits from H&M MOVE" — which is copy-pasta from a different brief. It has no business appearing in a last-day urgency close.
- The structural fatigue is real. Anyone who opened July 15's "Up to 50% off is on the rise," July 17's TGIF blast, and July 18's "Summer-to-fall favorites from $9.99" will recognize this as the same sale wearing its fifth outfit. "Last day" is the right close for a sale run — but if you've cried wolf four times already, subscribers discount the urgency sharply.

## What's working

- **"Last day" in the subject is the right close** — naming the deadline is the correct final-act move for a multi-day sale run; it's the strongest new hook H&M had available.
- **Hero headline is immediately legible** — "UP TO 50% OFF SITEWIDE" is large, bold, and unambiguous above the fold.
- **Editorial photography earns its keep** — three lifestyle shots (dark strappy bodysuit, casual menswear, slip dress) give the offer visual texture without feeling cluttered.
- **Broad category coverage** — the SHOP BY grid spans Women, Men, Kids, Home, and Beauty, ensuring the sitewide claim is structurally reinforced.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping elements, no layout issues.

## What's weak

- **Urgency is subject-only — the body is timeless** — "Last day" never appears in the email body. No countdown, no "ends tonight," no explicit deadline. The urgency evaporates the moment someone opens.
- **H&M MOVE subheadline is mismatched copy** — "Gear up for a winning season with transitional picks" is editorial brand copy, not a sale-close message; it actively dilutes the deadline energy.
- **No price anchors** — July 18's "from $9.99" approach gave shoppers a floor to evaluate; this email shows nothing. "Up to 50% off" is vague without a concrete number to pull the eye.
- **Four fragmented category CTAs** — WOMEN / MEN / KIDS 2-8Y / KIDS 9-14Y spread the click equally rather than driving one decisive action.
- **Sale fatigue** — four prior sitewide 50%-off emails this week means the "last day" claim will read skeptically to anyone paying attention.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Carry the deadline into the hero body** — a single bold line below the headline: "Sale ends tonight at midnight." This is the highest-leverage change; it closes the gap between the subject promise and the in-body experience.
- 2. **Kill the MOVE subheadline** — replace with price floors: "Dresses from $15 · Tees from $9.99 · Jeans from $24.99." Price anchors are more persuasive than editorial brand copy in a last-day email.
- 3. **Collapse four category buttons into one primary CTA** — "Shop 50% Off" as a single prominent button above the grid. Let the SHOP BY grid serve as secondary navigation.
- 4. **Add a visible scarcity signal** — even a plain-text "Offer ends tonight" banner underneath the headline would work; a countdown image is better if the send-time window supports it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Sale ends tonight: up to 50% off everything`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Final hours — half off sitewide`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dresses from $15, tees from $9.99 — offer closes tonight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Last chance to save 50% — ends at midnight`
