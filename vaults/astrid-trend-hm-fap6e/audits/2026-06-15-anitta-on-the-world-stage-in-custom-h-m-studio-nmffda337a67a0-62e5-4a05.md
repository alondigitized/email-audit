# @Anitta on the world stage in custom H&M Studio
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- This is a pure editorial/PR send — H&M dressing Brazilian singer Anitta for a major world-stage performance, shot in a sleek custom white ensemble from H&M Studio. The creative is arresting: "ANITTA TAKES CENTER FIELD" lands immediately, the hero image is strong, and the pull-quote from Anitta herself gives the moment genuine weight. The execution is clean and confident.
- But this email doesn't sell anything. There is no price, no product link, no offer, and no deadline. "LEARN MORE" as the only CTA sends you deeper into editorial content rather than to a cart. For an H&M subscriber who opened expecting the brand's usual promotional language — especially fresh off four days of shorts, threshold discounts, and summer partywear — this reads as brand advertising stuffed into an inbox send. The gap between what the email promises (a world-stage moment) and what it delivers (a landing page, not a shoppable look) is the central miss.
- Compared to the June 14 "15% or 20% off ends tonight" send — which hit nearly every conversion signal cleanly — this one is a different category of email wearing the same wrapper. It's a press release in newsletter clothing. Whether that's the intent or not, the mechanics don't land in an inbox context.

## What's working

- **Hero headline is confident and specific** — "ANITTA TAKES CENTER FIELD" under "TAILORED PRECISION" is the right editorial frame; the subhead does real work here.
- **Photography is strong** — The white custom look reads as genuinely high-fashion, elevating H&M Studio above the brand's everyday perception.
- **Celebrity quote adds real credibility** — Anitta's pull-quote is personal and specific ("representing my country," "strong, sporty, a little wild"), not a generic endorsement.
- **Clean render** — No broken images, no layout breakage, no lorem ipsum. The email displays crisply top to bottom.
- **Current campaign hook** — Tying to an actual world-stage performance gives the send a news-peg urgency that purely seasonal emails lack.

## What's weak

- **Zero shoppable offer** — No price, no product link, no way to buy the look or anything like it. "LEARN MORE" dead-ends the conversion path entirely.
- **"LEARN MORE" CTA is the weakest possible copy** — Vague verb, no noun, no destination hint. For an email about a specific look, "Shop the Studio Collection" or "See Anitta's Look" would be strictly better.
- **Second email in one day** — Brand history shows H&M already sent "In focus: Summer partywear" on June 15; this is back-to-back sends on the same date, which compounds fatigue from a week of near-daily emails.
- **No shoppable product grid** — The multi-image block below the hero is behind-the-scenes content, not a product grid. There's no path from inspiration to purchase.
- **No preheader visible** — No preheader text appears in the render; the inbox view likely leaks nav/footer junk, wasting the second line of real estate in the inbox.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a "Shop the look" product rail below the hero** — even 3–4 Studio pieces in the same white/neutral aesthetic turns this from a press release into a revenue send. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA to match the moment** — swap "LEARN MORE" for "Shop Anitta's Look" or "Explore H&M Studio."
- 3. **Add a preheader** — the subject does the event hook; the preheader should do the product pivot: "Custom Studio looks, now shoppable."
- 4. **Stagger this send** — if the partywear editorial went out earlier on June 15, hold this for June 16. Two brand sends in one day on top of a heavy week erodes open rates.
- 5. **Consider a member-exclusive angle** — H&M membership is mentioned only in the footer; tying the Anitta drop to early access for members would give loyalty subscribers a reason to act.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**External font imports (blocking concern):**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` tags is blocked by Outlook (all versions) and some corporate proxies. Fallback system fonts must be present on all `font-family` declarations — cannot confirm without full source.

**Duplicate CSS rules in `#optimize-css`:**
- `.department-title` defined twice at different font sizes (`24px/30px` vs `16px/20px`)
- `.cta-link-mobile-border-ns` declared twice identically
- `.cta-link-mobile, .set_font_interactive_standalone_link_default` declared twice identically
- `.set_font_heading_xlarge` declared three times

These duplicates are parse-harmless but bloat payload and create maintenance risk for future edits.

**`<script>` tag in `<head>`:** The JSON-LD `<script type="application/ld+json">` block is correctly typed and inert in email clients — no rendering risk.
