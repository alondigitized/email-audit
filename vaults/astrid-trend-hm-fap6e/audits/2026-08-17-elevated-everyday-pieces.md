# Elevated everyday pieces
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-17**
## Executive summary

- This is H&M in pure editorial mode again — "CITY CHIC" with peplum tops, tiered lace, and a polka-dot dress, individually priced, no discount code, no percentage-off banner anywhere in the render. It's the same full-price playbook as 8/13's "The preppy way to layer" and 8/11's "The denim update you've been waiting for" — three full-price editorial sends in the last week alone, sandwiched around the 8/15–8/16 tiered-discount arc.
- The execution itself is clean: one hero look, a clear "SHOP NOW" CTA, then a tidy product cascade (single item → two-up grid → single item) with real prices attached to every piece. No render bugs, no broken images, no lorem ipsum. But there's zero urgency, zero offer, and zero segmentation — this reads like a lookbook, not a marketing email, and it's the third time this exact beat has been hit in a week.
- Verdict: well-built, poorly timed. Cadence fatigue is the real problem here, not craft.

## What's working

- Clean visual hierarchy: hero image → "CITY CHIC" headline → one clear "SHOP NOW" button, all above the fold.
- Every product has a visible price and clear name (Tiered Lace Top $22.99, Draped Bubble-Hem Top $24.99, etc.) — no guessing what you're buying.
- "New Arrival" tags on three of the five products add a light freshness signal even without a discount.
- No layout breakage, no overlapping text, no broken images anywhere in the render.
- "SHOP BY" category grid at the footer gives an easy secondary path for browsers not sold on the editorial theme.

## What's weak

- No offer of any kind — no %, no $ off, no free shipping — the weakest possible position after two straight days (8/15, 8/16) of tiered-discount messaging trained subscribers to expect a number.
- Subject "Elevated everyday pieces" is generic editorial copy with zero specificity — same failure mode as 8/14's "New semester, new essentials" and 8/13's "The preppy way to layer."
- This is the third full-price/no-discount send in five sends (8/11, 8/13, now 8/17) — the brand is oscillating so fast between discount and full-price modes that neither read feels credible.
- Hero image model is styled for city/office wear, but nothing on the page signals occasion, season change, or why "now" — no urgency hook at all.
- Two of five products lack a "New Arrival" tag or any differentiator (Tiered Lace Top, Square-Neck Dress) — inconsistent metadata across the grid.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete offer or loyalty hook to this send — even a small member-only perk — since three full-price editorial sends in a week with no discount is starting to look like a brand that can't decide on a strategy. If full-price is deliberate, say so explicitly ("New in, no markdown yet — get it first").
- 2. Sharpen the subject line to name the actual trend or a specific hero piece instead of vague "elevated" language.
- 3. Add a one-line urgency or scarcity cue near the hero CTA (e.g., "New styles, limited runs") since nothing currently gives a reason to act today vs. next week.
- 4. Standardize product tagging — apply "New Arrival" consistently across all five items or drop it entirely so it doesn't look like an oversight.

# Technical Email Audit — H&M "Elevated everyday pieces"

**1. Technical Summary**
Email uses a standard responsive AEM/Adobe Campaign template with mobile breakpoint overrides, but every probed tracked link (CTA, header, and images) returns HTTP 403, and 12/12 image elements lack alt text.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- All 12 probed material links resolve through the `t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=...` click-tracking redirector and return **403 Forbidden**, including both "Shop now" CTAs, the "H&M" header link, all 6 product image links, and the **Unsubscribe** link (`...p4=3326930539&p5=9be06746af74de7271d9dcc8d83f5742`). This is a 100% failure rate on sampled links — likely an ESP-side redirector/bot-protection issue (WAF blocking the QA fetcher) rather than a per-link problem, since the same `id=hcbdf7939-...` base params repeat across all failing URLs with only the trailing segment (`f7cfee2c`–`f7cfee6c`) varying.
- Two CTA link texts contain invisible Unicode characters (`‌  ﻿Shop now` — ZWNJ + BOM/ZWNBSP), which won't affect rendering but pollutes accessible/link text and could trip spam filters that inspect anchor text.
- 50 additional links were deprioritized/not probed per the QA tool's click-likelihood ranking (footer/social/utility links).

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- **12/12 images missing `alt` text**, including product images (`FNP-DD10263P19-sablier-*.jpg`) and the tracking pixel. No fallback text for images-off email clients or screen readers.
- Template correctly implements Gmail-specific hiding (`#MessageViewBody`), Yahoo (`@media screen yahoo`), and Outlook mobile handling (`mso-hide`), plus `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` — standard defensive markup, no issues.
- Mobile breakpoint CSS (`@media (max-width:699px)`) is present and comprehensive.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
- The embedded `application/ld+json` schema block has multiple **empty required fields**: `subjectLine: ""`, `discountCode: ""`, `availabilityEnds: ""`, `image: ""`, `url: ""` in the `PromotionCard`/`DiscountOffer` objects. These appear to be unresolved merge tokens that failed to populate — this will break Gmail Promotions annotations (e.g., no promo image/CTA card render) and any discount-code auto-fill integrations.
- `availabilityStarts` is hardcoded to `2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00`, a stale date unrelated to the current send — indicates a static/unmaintained JSON-LD template rather than a dynamic merge failure.

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
- Unsubscribe link is present in the markup, but it returns a 403 per the broken-link scan above — if reproducible outside the QA probe, this is a functional CAN-SPAM risk (non-working unsubscribe mechanism).
- `List-Unsubscribe` header not found — cannot confirm RFC 2369 one-click support at the header level (may be a relay capture gap rather than a true absence).
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe cannot be confirmed.
- `Authentication-Results` header not found — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unverifiable from this capture.
Note: the two header-related warnings are flagged by the QA tool as possibly a relay/capture limitation, not necessarily a sender-side defect — worth confirming against raw headers from a non-relay capture before treating as a real compliance gap.

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
- Links use a proprietary tracking schema (`id`, `did`, `rid`, `erid`, `p1`–`p5`) rather than standard UTM parameters — no `utm_source`/`utm_medium`/`utm_campaign` observed in the truncated HTML, so email-to-site attribution continuity can't be assessed from this data alone.
- Because all sampled destination links 403, landing page alignment (correct PDP/category page, correct promo) **cannot be verified** at all — this is the highest-priority blocker.

**7. Recommendations**
1. **Priority 1:** Investigate the 403s on `t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=...` links — confirm whether this is a QA-fetcher/WAF block (e.g., missing user-agent, IP block) vs. an actual broken redirector in production. If real, both CTA clickthrough and unsubscribe are broken for recipients.
2. Add descriptive `alt` text to all 12 images, particularly the two product-image variants and the H&M logo.
3. Populate or remove the empty JSON-LD fields (`subjectLine`, `discountCode`, `availabilityEnds`, `image`, `url`) to enable Gmail Promotions card rendering, and refresh the stale `availabilityStarts` date.
4. Strip the invisible ZWNJ/BOM characters from the two "Shop now" anchor texts.
5. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers and `Authentication-Results` (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) directly against raw SMTP headers, since the relay capture may not be reflecting what was actually sent.
