# Up to 60% off puffer deals for everyone
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-27**
## Executive summary

- This is the best-aligned H&M send in the recent run. The subject promises "puffer deals," and the body delivers: a hero of a model in a taupe puffer, then two puffer-only banners ("WARM THOUGHTS" and "GETTING COLDER"). That is a real improvement on the 11/26 "sweater deals from $12.99" send, which never showed its price. It also avoids the muddle of the 11/25 "Gifts on gifts" send.
- The structure still weakens it. The top banner is a red-on-black gift card promo ("Give a $50+ gift card, get 30% back"), which competes with the 60% headline. After the puffer banners, the email pivots to a holiday home section that has nothing to do with the subject. The puffer banners have no price and no visible button. The offer stays at "up to 60% off everything," the same line H&M has sent all week.
- Verdict: a coherent hero and a relevant subject, but a generic offer and too many competing promos. It's a solid middle-of-the-pack send.

## What's working

- The subject and hero match. The puffer in the hero is exactly what the subject sells.
- The offer is unmistakable: "UP TO 60% OFF EVERYTHING" in bold type directly under the hero.
- The category buttons (Women, Men, Baby, Kids, Find a Store) give clear routing for different shoppers.
- The editorial banners ("Warm Thoughts," "Getting Colder") are on-season and visually clean, with no render bugs.
- The puffer banners show men, women and kids, so the email works for a broad audience.

## What's weak

- The gift card banner at the very top (red on black, small type) competes with the main offer and reads like fine print.
- The holiday home section is off-topic after two puffer banners. It dilutes the story and adds length.
- The puffer banners show no price, product name or visible CTA button. A shopper can't tell where they link or what a puffer costs.
- The "up to 60% off everything" headline is the same one used on 11/26 and 11/27. The fatigue is real, and nothing here says why this send is different.
- The legal block is very long and sits close to the footer. It adds to the "buried fine print" feel, and the gift card terms contradict the "everything" claim.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the home section and the gift card banner. Let puffers carry the whole email, with a "Shop puffers" button under each banner.
- 2. Put a concrete price in the hero, for example "Puffer jackets from $XX.99," so the "up to 60%" claim has a number behind it.
- 3. Replace the generic category buttons with puffer-specific ones: "Women's puffers," "Men's puffers," "Kids' puffers."
- 4. Add a real deadline, such as "Ends Cyber Monday." Right now there's no time pressure.
- 5. Move the gift card promo to a thin footer strip, or send it separately.

- **Subject Alt B:** `The puffer you'll wear all winter, now 60% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Women's, men's and kids' styles from $XX. Online and in stores.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Our warmest layers are marked down. Shop before sizes sell out.`

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **Zero-width characters in link text.** The "Shop now" links at `…f8e47e3a` and `…f8e47e3c` have link text of `"‌  ﻿Shop now"`, with leading ZWNJ/BOM and spaces. These can affect screen-reader labels and text-based click mapping.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
