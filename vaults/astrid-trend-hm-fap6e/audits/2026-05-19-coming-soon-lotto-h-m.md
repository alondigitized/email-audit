# Coming soon: LOTTO H&M
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- This is H&M's formal drop announcement for their Lotto collaboration — a proper heritage-sport collab with a storied Italian brand, launching May 21 (two days from send). The email is structured as an editorial/launch teaser: co-branded logo, lifestyle hero, a short brand story, two products with prices, and dual CTAs to view the collection or find a store. The execution is clean and on-brand.
- The problem is context fatigue. This is the third soccer-themed send in five days (May 15 editorial, May 18 @nassnyc influencer version, now the actual collab drop). By the time this lands, subscribers who follow fashion have already been primed twice — so the "coming soon" framing undersells the moment. This should have been the payoff send, not another tease.
- The email does its job competently but misses the energy of a true drop announcement. The ghost/outline CTA buttons are low-contrast and undersell the urgency of a two-day countdown to a real product launch.

## What's working

- **Co-branded logo placement is clean** — the "eLotto × H&M" treatment reads as a genuine collab, not a brand slap.
- **Launch date is in the body copy** — "Launching May 21 in stores and online" gives the subscriber an actual anchor, even if it's not in the subject.
- **Product detail is present** — two Loose-Fit Soccer Jersey listings with prices ($44.99 and a lower-priced variant) and "New Arrival" tags ground this as real product, not pure editorial.
- **Visual hierarchy works top-to-bottom** — hero model → logo + story → CTAs → product shots → secondary CTAs flows naturally.
- **No render bugs** — images load, layout holds, typography is clean throughout.

## What's weak

- **"Coming soon" framing wastes a launch moment** — the collection drops in 48 hours; the email should feel like a countdown, not a preview.
- **Both CTAs are ghost/outline buttons** — on a white background these are visually weak; the primary "VIEW THE COLLECTION" needs to be filled to draw the eye.
- **The subject buries the collab story** — "Coming soon: LOTTO H&M" tells you a name, not why Lotto matters; the 50-year soccer heritage angle is only in the body.
- **Third soccer send this week creates cadence fatigue** — a subscriber who opened the May 15 and May 18 sends is already familiar with the soccer moment; this lands as repetitive rather than climactic.
- **No urgency mechanism beyond the copy** — a countdown timer or explicit "2 days only" header would convert the launch date into a click driver.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a "Drops May 21" urgency banner or countdown** — the single highest-impact change. Put it immediately below the co-branded logo so the date is unmissable before the body copy.
- 2. **Switch to a filled primary CTA button** — make "VIEW THE COLLECTION" a solid black or Lotto-red fill; move "FIND A STORE" to a text link below it.
- 3. **Reframe the subject to lead with heritage** — Lotto's 50-year story is the hook that justifies the collab; lean on it.
- 4. **Add the launch date to the subject line** — closes the loop the body copy opened.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading will fail in Outlook (all versions) and some webmail clients.**

```html
<style>
  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
</style>
```

`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is not supported by Outlook (Word rendering engine) and is blocked by several webmail clients that strip or sandbox `<style>` tags. Fallback system fonts should be explicitly declared in every font-stack rule (e.g., `font-family: 'NorthStar', Arial, sans-serif`). If the CSS already does this, the visual degradation is acceptable — but it should be confirmed.

**`<script>` tag in `<head>`** — the `application/ld+json` block is schema markup, not executable JS, so it is safe. However, aggressive spam filters can flag `<script>` presence regardless of type. Low risk but worth noting.

**Alt text** — truncated source prevents full audit. Must verify all `<img>` tags carry meaningful `alt` attributes (not empty strings) per accessibility and deliverability best practices.
