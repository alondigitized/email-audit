# Just dropped: H&M Studio Resort
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- This is H&M's full-price Studio Resort collection launch — an elevated, editorial send anchored by a striking white feathered/ruffled gown hero. The photography is confident: the dress communicates "premium sub-line" immediately, and the scroll reveals a tight product grid plus a secondary Lotto x H&M module. Structurally, this is H&M executing their drop-launch template well.
- The problem is timing. Yesterday's email (May 20) primed the entire list with 50% off sitewide. Today's ask is full retail for a prestige sub-collection. That psychological whiplash is the single biggest drag on performance — subscribers who just unlocked 50% off don't pivot easily to a new-at-full-price mindset. The Lotto section also fragments the message: the collab teaser dropped on May 19 and now gets recycled here as a secondary block, diluting the Studio Resort hero story.
- Solid creative, wrong sequencing. A send like this would have landed harder before the Memorial Day sale, not after it.

## What's working

- **Hero photography is strong** — the full-length ivory gown reads instantly as a premium capsule, no ambiguity about what this collection is.
- **"H&M STUDIO RESORT" headline placement** is clean and centered; visual hierarchy leads the eye correctly top-to-bottom.
- **"Just dropped" in the subject** is the right urgency lever for a new-arrival send — concrete and honest.
- **Subject line length** (~30 chars) is mobile-optimized and won't truncate on any major inbox client.
- **No visible render bugs** — layout is clean, no broken images or overlapping text in the render.

## What's weak

- **Full-price drop the day after a 50% off sitewide sale** — the subscriber's reference price is now "half off everything"; Studio Resort at full retail will feel expensive by comparison.
- **Lotto x H&M module at the bottom** dilutes the Studio Resort story; this collab already had its own dedicated send (May 19) and doesn't need another appearance 48 hours later.
- **No concrete offer** — no free shipping threshold, no member pricing, no early-access hook. For a premium sub-line, even a small loyalty perk would close the gap.
- **Product copy and prices are rendered too small to drive urgency** — the grid feels decorative rather than transactional.
- **No preview text visible** — if the preheader is leaking boilerplate ("View in browser," etc.), that's a wasted impression-share opportunity in the inbox.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Delay this send by 48–72 hours** — put distance between the sale-end and the full-price drop. Or flip the sequencing for the next campaign cycle: launch Studio Resort *before* the sale, let the sale sweep in as an uplift.
- 2. **Add a loyalty/member hook** — "H&M Members: early access to Studio Resort" turns a generic new-drop into a segmented privilege send and justifies full-price.
- 3. **Cut the Lotto module entirely** — it had its send. Keep this email single-collection, clean.
- 4. **Make the product grid scannable** — larger type on prices, a "new this season" tag, or a featured piece with a specific detail (fabric, limited qty) gives subscribers a reason to click *this* product vs. browsing from the homepage.
- 5. **Write a real preheader** — something that complements the subject rather than restating it or leaking code.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Studio Resort just landed — shop H&M's new capsule`
- **Subject Alt B:** `H&M Studio Resort is here. Resort season, elevated.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Featherweight fabrics, resort-ready silhouettes — shop the full collection`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The new Studio Resort drop: elevated pieces for your summer wardrobe`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading will fail in most clients (confirmed).**

```css
<style>
  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
</style>
```

`@import` inside `<style>` is not supported by Gmail (web or Android), Outlook (all desktop versions), Yahoo Mail, or AOL. Both custom font families will silently fall back to the client's system font stack on the majority of the subscriber base. This is a rendering regression, not a crash — but it means brand typography is not rendering as intended for most recipients.

**Alt-text fallback suppression:**

```css
img.image + div { display: none !important; }
```

This rule hides any `<div>` that directly follows an `.image` img element. If that pattern is used to wrap alt-text fallback content or VML-based Outlook image substitutes, those fallbacks are suppressed. Cannot confirm impact without full source.

**No other rendering issues confirmed** from the visible markup. `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` and `format-detection=telephone=no` are correctly set.
