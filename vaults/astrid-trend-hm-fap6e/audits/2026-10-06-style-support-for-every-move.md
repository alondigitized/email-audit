# Style & support for every move
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-06**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean brand-awareness send for the H&M Move activewear line, with no offer in it. The hero is strong: a single model in a cobalt windbreaker and black leggings, then a short "H&M MOVE" block with two buttons. Four sport tiles (running, training, yoga & pilates, tennis & golf) follow in a zig-zag layout that scans well. It's well built and nothing is broken, but nothing in it gives a reason to act today.
- The timing is odd. For a week H&M ran "25% off flash sale" (9/30), "Up to 30% off fall essentials" (10/2) and "Your 20% off ends soon, Member" (10/4). Then came the no-offer "'90s-inspired dressing" lookbook on 10/5, and now another editorial send on 10/6. A subscriber trained to wait for a percentage-off banner has no reason to click a send that leaves it out. The member-discount hook from the last three emails is gone.
- Verdict: a competent catalog page that wastes an inbox slot. It's fine for browsing, but it won't drive urgency or revenue without a price, a deadline or a member perk.

## What's working

- The hero model in the cobalt jacket is a clear, high-contrast focal point, and the eye lands there first.
- The four sport categories (Running, Training, Yoga & Pilates, Tennis & Golf) let a reader pick their own path.
- The zig-zag image/text alternation gives the middle section visual rhythm, and no images are broken.
- The "SoftMove™" and "SculptMove™" fabric callouts with the close-up fabric shots add real product-detail value.
- The "Shop by" row and footer are standard and tidy.

## What's weak

- There is no price, percentage, member benefit or deadline anywhere in the email, which is a sharp break from the previous week's offers.
- The primary CTAs, "SHOP NOW" and "FIND A STORE", are generic and sit side by side. Two equal-weight outlined buttons split the decision.
- The category tiles have no visible buttons or arrows. The labels ("STYLES FOR MILES", "POWER THROUGH") are small grey-on-white text with lots of dead whitespace beside them.
- The body copy under the hero is small and dense for a mobile screen. "Flattering fit" is a vague claim.
- There are no named products or prices, so every link seems to go to a category page. There's also no review or social proof.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete hook to the hero, such as "20% off H&M Move for Members" or a free-shipping threshold. This would lift the email more than anything else.
- 2. Replace the paired outlined buttons with one solid, high-contrast CTA like "Shop activewear". Move "Find a store" to the footer.
- 3. Turn the four sport tiles into product tiles with a name and price, for example "Seamless leggings $29.99", and make the text a visible link or button.
- 4. Enlarge the intro copy and cut it to one line: "Soft stretch, real support."
- 5. Add social proof near the fabric section, such as a star rating or "Bestseller" tag.

## Technical Audit

# Technical Audit: H&M "Style & support for every move"

## 1. Technical Summary
The QA pass rate is 16%. 12 of the 12 probed links returned 403, but every probe hit the `t19.email.hm.com/r/` click-tracking redirector, so this is more likely bot-blocking than a real outage. The confirmed code-level issues are missing alt text on all content images, empty JSON-LD schema fields, and zero-width characters inside link text. The compliance and authentication findings can't be confirmed from this data.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **403 on all 12 probed links**, including the CTAs ("SHOP NOW", "Find a store", "Shop now"), the logo link ("H&M"), the image links and "Unsubscribe". Examples:
  - `https://t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=h512e398a-62b6-4401-9cf0-a1561f925aa7,548ec45b,f86042d1&did=1418642523&rid=3326930539&...`
  - Unsubscribe: `...,f860430b&...&p4=3326930539&p5=9be06746af74de7271d9dcc8d83f5742`
- **Caveat:** every failure is on one redirector domain, with the same parameter structure and the same status. That pattern points to the ESP's click tracker rejecting the automated probe (no cookies, a non-browser UA, or a rate limit). It does not show that real recipients get a 403. This needs a manual check from a real client before it is called broken.
- **Probe coverage:** 57 of 69 links were skipped, so the footer, social and utility links are unverified.
- **Click-tracker design:** every link is wrapped, including the logo and the unsubscribe link. That is normal for an ESP, but the unsubscribe path depends on the tracker being up.
- **Zero-width characters in link text:** the "Shop now" links have `"‌  ﻿Shop now"`, with U+200C and U+FEFF characters before the text. This is padding or preheader residue that leaked into the anchor text. It pollutes link text for screen readers, text-based click reports and the QA tooling.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on content images.** The QA run flagged 15 or more instances, all of them the `cq5dam.web.1400.1400.jpeg` and `cq5dam.web.750.750.jpeg` pairs. Sources include `https://image.hm.com/content/dam/hm/regional-local-activities/ame/2026-ame/wk-41/na1560_s4/1350380002_202604_LB_3301.jpg` and the other `na1560_s4` product images (`1368152001`, `1366472002`, `1354663005`, `1333340001`, `1243210082`, `1355691002`).
- **Filenames as link names.** The images are wrapped in links, so the filename becomes the accessible link name (`[image: cq5dam.web.1400.1400.jpeg]`). Screen readers and image-blocked clients will announce "cq5dam web 1400 1400 jpeg".
- **Likely tracking pixel without `alt=""`.** The image at `https://t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=h512e398a-...,548ec45b,1` is flagged for missing alt. It looks like the open-tracking pixel. It should carry an explicit `alt=""`.
- **Duplicate desktop and mobile image pairs.** Each product image is output at 1400px and 750px, and the `.is-hidden-mobile` and `.is-hidden-desktop` classes toggle them. Both copies are in the DOM, and the missing alt text doubles the screen-reader noise. Clients that ignore `display:none` inside media queries may show both.
- **Invalid CSS in `optimize-css`.** `.is-hidden-desktop{...line-height:auto!important...}` is invalid, because `auto` is not a valid `line-height` value. It is ignored but harmless.
- **`@import` font stylesheets.** `@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/...)` and `@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css)` are stripped by Gmail and by Outlook desktop. The fallback fonts need to be verified.
- **Truncated source.** Only the head and the start of the CSS were provided, so the body markup, `lang` attribute and table roles could not be checked.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tokens (`%%...%%`, `{{...}}`) are visible in the supplied source or the QA data.
- The tracking URLs carry per-recipient identifiers: `rid=3326930539`, `erid=MzMyNjkzMDUzOQ==` (base64 of the `rid`), `p2=0982dd0a...` and `p5=9be06746...`. This is expected tracking behavior, but the redirect URLs are recipient-unique.
- The body could not be fully checked because the source is truncated.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058): not found.** The QA tool notes that "Header may not be captured by AgentMail relay". These are probably relay artifacts, not confirmed gaps, so verify against the raw message at the original receiving mailbox. If they are truly absent, one-click unsubscribe is missing, which Gmail and Yahoo require for bulk senders.
- **`Authentication-Results`: not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown because of the relay. No finding either way. Check the raw headers at the original recipient.
- **In-body unsubscribe link:** present ("Unsubscribe", with `p4` and `p5` params), but it returned 403 to the probe. The same bot-blocking caveat applies, so confirm manually that it resolves.
- **Physical postal address:** not visible in the truncated source. Not assessable.
- **Plain-text part:** 512 of 590 characters are URLs (>70%). A text part exists but is nearly all links, which is a minor spam-filter signal.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **Not verifiable.** Because every link went through `t19.email.hm.com/r/` and returned 403, the final destination URLs and any UTM parameters could not be inspected. The only visible campaign identifiers are `p1=DM6111070` and `p3=20261006`. No `utm_*` parameters appear on any probed URL, but they could be appended at the redirect. Landing page alignment is unconfirmed.
- **Empty schema.org markup.** The JSON-LD block in the head is almost entirely empty:
  - `"subjectLine":""`
  - `"discountCode":""`
  - `"description":""`
  - `PromotionCard` with `"image":""` and `"url":""`
  - `"availabilityEnds":""`
  - `"availabilityStarts":"2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"`, a stale 2018 date
- This is template boilerplate that was never populated. It gives Gmail no promotion annotations, and the 2018 start date could cause the markup to be ignored or flagged. Either populate it or remove it.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Re-test the `t19.email.hm.com/r/` links from a real browser or an allow-listed client** to separate probe blocking from a real 403. Pay particular attention to the CTAs and the unsubscribe link.
2. **Add meaningful `alt` text** to every product image, and `alt=""` to the tracking pixel (`...,548ec45b,1`). Alt text on linked images becomes the link's accessible name.
3. **Strip the U+200C and U+FEFF characters** from the "Shop now" anchor text (the `f86042a0` and `f86042a2` links).
4. **Fix or remove the JSON-LD block**: populate `subjectLine`, `description`, `url`, `image` and the offer dates, or drop it.
5. **Verify `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results` on the original message** (before the AgentMail relay). Add RFC 8058 headers if they are missing.
6. **Remove `line-height:auto`** from `.is-hidden-desktop`, and confirm the font fallback stack, because the `@import` fonts will not load in Gmail.
7. **Add a plain-text part with readable copy**, not only URLs.
8. **Capture the redirect chain** (follow redirects with a browser-like UA) so UTM parameters and landing pages can be audited in future runs.
