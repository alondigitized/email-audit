# H&M&Pride: today & every day
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- This is H&M's Pride campaign send — cause-marketing, not commerce. After eight Memorial Day sale emails and two editorial pivots this week (the "Effortless summer staples" on May 26 and "Summer 2026: The Refined Edit" earlier today), H&M has now sent a second email on May 28 with zero offers, zero products, and a single "LEARN MORE" CTA pointing toward a Trevor Project partnership. The creative is clean, the mission is legible, and the messaging is appropriate. As a brand values statement, it works.
- As an email to a subscriber whose inbox just absorbed ten H&M sends in nine days, this is one too many. Dropping cause content on top of two editorial pivots in the same day — after a sale barrage — risks reading as a checklist move rather than a genuine brand moment. The subscriber doesn't have time to breathe.
- The bigger strategic miss is structural: the "SHOP BY" row at the bottom (Women, Men, Kids, Home, Beauty) is generic category navigation copy-pasted from every other H&M send, sitting awkwardly below a cause-marketing hero. Either lean fully into the values play and strip the category shelf, or bridge Pride to shoppable product ("Shop Pride-inspired looks"). Right now it's two emails stapled together.

## What's working

- **Hero is immediately readable.** "SEEN, HEARD, CELEBRATED." in bold with the Pride rainbow treatment lands clearly; no squinting required.
- **Trevor Project name-drop is specific.** Citing the partnership by name ("long-standing partnership with The Trevor Project") gives the cause claim credibility beyond vague allyship language.
- **Email renders cleanly.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breakage — the render is production-quality.
- **Subject is short and distinctive.** 30 characters, the "H&M&Pride" ampersand chaining is a memorable typographic touch that stands out in a list of promotional subjects.

## What's weak

- **Zero commerce hook.** There's no offer, no product, no collection linked — the "SHOP BY" category grid is placeholder-grade and links to nothing specific tied to Pride.
- **"LEARN MORE" is the weakest CTA in H&M's playbook.** It's a dead end for conversion and gives the subscriber no reason to click if they already know about Trevor Project.
- **Second email of the day, third in three days.** Cadence is the real deliverability enemy here — even loyal subscribers start ignoring brands they hear from this often.
- **No Pride-specific products visible.** A Pride collection, exclusive colorway, or co-branded product would have made this email earn its place. Instead it's pure PR.
- **The Shop By grid undercuts the moment.** Generic category navigation at the bottom sends the signal that this cause send is just a repurposed template.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Replace "LEARN MORE" with a mission-linked commerce CTA.** Something like "Shop Pride styles" or "Explore the Pride edit" converts the values moment into a revenue moment without abandoning the cause framing.
- 2. **Cut the generic "SHOP BY" grid or replace it with a curated Pride collection shelf** — three to five specific products or looks that connect the campaign to something the subscriber can actually buy.
- 3. **Don't send this on the same day as "Summer 2026: The Refined Edit."** This email deserved its own send day. Stacking two non-sale, non-offer emails in a single inbox day dilutes both.
- 4. **Add a donation mechanic or member moment.** "H&M members: we'll donate $1 per purchase to The Trevor Project through June" gives subscribers a reason to act rather than just feel good.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Pride styles are here — shop the edit`
- **Subject Alt B:** `H&M x Trevor Project: shop Pride`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `All month, every purchase supports The Trevor Project.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New Pride collection — shop now and give back.`

## Technical Audit — H&M Pride Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard HTML marketing mailer with JSON-LD structured data and MSO conditional comments. The truncated source reveals a stale JSON-LD template with several unfilled fields and a font-loading strategy that silently degrades in Gmail.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Incomplete assessment** — link `href` values are cut off in the truncated source. The following is flagged for full-source verification:
- No UTM parameters are visible in asset URLs (`s1-cdn.hm.com`, `fabric.hmgroup.com`). Confirm product and CTA links carry `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` before sending.
- Sender subdomain is `newsletter@email.hm.com` — confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment on this subdomain specifically (see §5).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` in `<style>` — confirmed failure in Gmail**

```css
<style>
  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
</style>
```

Gmail's CSS parser strips `@import` rules. Both H&M brand fonts (`Northstar`, `OCES`) will silently fall back to system defaults for all Gmail users. This is a known, documented Gmail limitation. Fonts must be inlined as `@font-face` declarations or accepted as a non-rendering environment.

**Non-standard breakpoint**

Mobile breakpoint is set at `699px`. Industry standard is `600px` or `640px`. Devices with viewport widths between 600–699px will receive desktop layout.

**`mso-hide: all` coverage**

`.is-hidden-mobile` uses `display:none` with `mso-hide:all` — correct. `.is-hidden-desktop` uses `display:none` but no `mso-hide:all`, which may cause hidden elements to render in Outlook.

**Incomplete assessment** — `alt` text on `<img>` tags cannot be verified from the truncated source.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

  "discountCode": "",
  "description": "",

  "availabilityEnds": ""

```json

```

```json
{

```

**Issues:**
- `availabilityStarts: "2018-12-31"` — hardcoded date over 7 years in the past. This is either a template default that was never overridden or a copy-paste artifact. It will cause Gmail's promotion tab to show incorrect or expired offer metadata.
- `discountCode`, `description`, `availabilityEnds`, `subjectLine`, `image`, `url` — all empty strings. If these are intentional (no promo code, no action card), the entire `DiscountOffer` and `PromotionCard` blocks should be removed rather than emitted empty — empty structured data degrades Gmail Promotions tab scoring.
