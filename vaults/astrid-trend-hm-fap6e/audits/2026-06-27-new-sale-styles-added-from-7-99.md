# New sale styles added from $7.99
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- H&M is back in sale mode — again. This is the seventh sale-adjacent email in roughly ten days, and while "New sale styles added from $7.99" at least signals fresh inventory rather than repeating the same drumbeat verbatim, the cumulative inbox effect on a real subscriber is fatigue. The email itself is clean and competent: a bold "UP TO 80% OFF + FURTHER MARKDOWNS" hero, six product cards with visible pricing, and a logical department-tile layout below the fold. It does what a mid-tier promotional email is supposed to do.
- What it doesn't do is earn attention. There is no time-bound deadline anywhere in the body. The six product cards spread visual energy too thin across two genders. "SHOP NOW" lives in the navigation bar rather than as a dedicated in-body CTA button. Most critically, the "$7.99" anchor in the subject — the single sharpest creative decision in this send — is never paid off with a featured item at that price in the body.
- Workmanlike sale refresh. Competent execution, heavily fatigued cadence.

## What's working

- **The "$7.99" subject anchor is concrete and specific** — stands out against H&M's recent run of percentage-only subjects.
- **Hero headline is dominant and legible** — "UP TO 80% OFF + FURTHER MARKDOWNS" reads instantly with strong red contrast against white.
- **Visual hierarchy holds up** — logo → offer → categories → products → department tiles flows cleanly with no confusion about what this email is.
- **No render bugs** — all six product images load, text is readable, layout is intact.
- **Seersucker dress shows explicit strikethrough pricing** — at least one product card makes the markdown tangible rather than aspirational.

## What's weak

- **The "$7.99" subject promise is never paid off in the body** — no featured item at that price point above the fold, making the subject feel like bait.
- **No deadline or urgency anywhere in the email** — the June 25 flash sale ("25% off flash sale starts now") worked because of the "until 12AM ET" hammer; this has none of that.
- **Six products across two genders dilutes the message** — blouse, puff dress, and seersucker (women's) alongside resort shirt and chinos (men's) reads like a clearance rack, not a curated edit.
- **No CTA button in the hero** — "SHOP NOW" is a navigation-bar text link, not an in-body action; it carries far less visual weight than a dedicated button.
- **Cadence fatigue is the silent killer** — this is the seventh sale-oriented email in ten days; a real subscriber has already trained themselves to defer or ignore these.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pay off the $7.99 subject line** — Feature one or two specific items at the entry price as a hero product with its own CTA. The subject writes a check the body never cashes.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline** — "Ends Sunday" or "48 hours only" recovers urgency that every other element here lacks. The June 25 flash sale earned its click because of the 12AM ET cut-off; replicate that.
- 3. **Add a hero CTA button below the offer copy** — A red or black "Shop the Sale" button directly under "UP TO 80% OFF" converts better than a nav bar link.
- 4. **Segment by gender** — Six mixed-gender product cards mean neither segment gets a message that feels like it's for them. A Women's vs. Men's split send would lift relevance and CTR for both.
- 5. **Break the cadence** — The June 22 "Summer Basics" and June 24 "Bestsellers" sends showed H&M can do editorial. Interleaving one more of those here would have made this sale email land harder when it arrived.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` for fonts will fail in Gmail and Outlook:**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
Both Gmail (web and Android) and Outlook strip `@import` inside `<style>` blocks. These fonts will silently fall back to system fonts. Should be replaced with `<link rel="stylesheet">` tags or inlined font stacks.

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`:**
```html
<html style="border:0;margin:0;outline:0;padding:0">
```
No `lang="en"` (or locale-appropriate equivalent). This breaks screen reader language detection and violates WCAG 3.1.1. Easy fix.

**`format-detection` meta uses deprecated syntax:**
```html
<meta content="telephone=no" name="format-detection"/>
```
Correct attribute order is `name` before `content` and the value should be `name="format-detection" content="telephone=no, date=no, address=no, email=no"`. The current form works in most clients but is incomplete.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Multiple JSON-LD fields are empty — likely unfilled template tokens:**
```json
{"@type":"EmailMessage","subjectLine":""}
{"@type":"DiscountOffer","availabilityEnds":"","discountCode":"","description":""}

```
These are either render-time merge failures or intentionally suppressed fields. If suppressed intentionally, the keys should be omitted entirely rather than sent as empty strings — some Gmail parsers treat empty `url` as a malformed card.

**`availabilityStarts` is hardcoded to a 2018 date:**

This is almost certainly a stale placeholder. Today is 2026-06-30. If Gmail uses this field for Promotions tab display logic, a 7.5-year-old start date may trigger anomaly filtering.
