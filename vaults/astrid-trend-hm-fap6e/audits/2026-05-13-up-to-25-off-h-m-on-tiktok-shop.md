# Up to 25% off H&M on TikTok Shop
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- H&M is using its TikTok Shop grand opening as the peg for a discount email — an interesting cross-channel moment, but one that immediately narrows its own audience. Not every H&M subscriber is a TikTok user, and "Up to 25% off...on TikTok Shop" pushes the recipient off-brand-site before they've even seen a product. The render is clean, the model styling is on-trend, and the member giveaway banner is a nice loyalty acknowledgement — but the campaign never really focuses. It tries to be a discount email, a platform launch email, and a sweepstakes email simultaneously, and does none of the three sharply.
- The offer is the weakest link. "Up to 25% off" is the most hedged discount language in retail — it signals that most things are probably 10% off, with one or two hero SKUs hitting 25. Paired with an "EXPLORE NOW" CTA that links to a TikTok Shop destination rather than a curated landing page, this email asks for a lot of trust in exchange for a vague promise. The visual hierarchy also buries the deal: the oversized model photo dominates above the fold, and the actual offer line sits below it in small, plain type.
- Final read: a competent template fill-in for a real campaign moment, but the strategy is diffuse and the execution doesn't close the sale.

## What's working

- **Render is clean.** No broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds up.
- **Member giveaway banner** is a smart top-of-email hook — $500 gift card creates an instant second reason to engage beyond the discount.
- **Model and styling match the brand.** Beige co-ord, casual chic, contemporary — fits H&M's current aesthetic direction.
- **Subject line is short (34 chars)** — mobile-friendly, no spam signals.
- **"Now through May" urgency** is present in the body copy, even if not surfaced in the subject.

## What's weak

- **"Up to 25% off" is the weakest possible offer framing** — no floor, no specific product, no minimum purchase clarity. It reads like a legal hedge, not a compelling deal.
- **"EXPLORE NOW" CTA is too vague.** It doesn't say what you're exploring, where you're going, or what you'll find.
- **Sending people to TikTok Shop, not HM.com,** introduces friction and audience mismatch — a meaningful slice of this subscriber list won't have TikTok accounts.
- **Four shop-by tiles (Women, Men, Home, Beauty) dilute focus.** For a launch announcement, one clear hero category would drive more clicks than a full nav grid.
- **Heavy legal fine print** at the bottom is visually jarring and undermines the aspirational fashion tone above.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Replace "EXPLORE NOW" with a destination-specific CTA** — "Shop Women's Deals" or "Claim 25% Off Now" tells the reader exactly what happens when they click.
- 2. **Lead with one hero product, not a model look.** A tagged, shoppable image of a specific trending item at a specific discount price converts better than a campaign editorial shot.
- 3. **Put a floor on the offer.** "25% off everything" or even "at least 15% off sitewide" is more compelling than "up to 25% off." If the deal has a real floor, say it.
- 4. **Surface the May deadline in the subject line** — urgency converts.
- 5. **Move or condense the fine print block** — the legal wall kills the visual close; link to a separate page instead.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Grand Opening: 25% off H&M on TikTok Shop — thru May`
- **Subject Alt B:** `H&M hits TikTok Shop — members get 25% off today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus, enter to win a $500 gift card. No purchase necessary.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Top-trending styles, exclusive TikTok prices — deals end in May.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**External font imports via `@import` — will silently fail in most clients:**
```css

@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
Gmail (both web and Android) strips `<style>` blocks containing `@import`. Outlook ignores them entirely. Fonts will fall back to system defaults — acceptable only if fallback stacks are defined (not visible in this excerpt).

**Viewport meta missing `initial-scale`:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width"/>
```

Should be `content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1"`. Without it, some Android clients render at incorrect zoom levels.
