# Sale update: prices just dropped — again
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- This is H&M's fourth dedicated sale email in eight days — following June 16 (app early access), June 17 (sale launch), and June 19 (60% off drumbeat). The "prices just dropped — again" subject is at least honest about the repetition, but the email itself doesn't deliver on that promise: there are zero prices visible in the body, no "before/after" callouts, and no indication of what actually dropped or by how much. The hero copy says "UP TO 60% OFF + FURTHER MARKDOWNS" but that's the same claim H&M has been running since the 17th. The creative is fine — four editorial vignettes (women, men, baby/kids, home) with clean photography — but it's structurally identical to the June 19 email, down to the category CTAs.
- The subject teases a meaningful update ("prices just dropped") but the body delivers nothing new. A subscriber who opened the June 19 send and this one would see near-identical layouts, the same 60%-off headline, and no specific product callouts with prices. This is the most glaring trust problem: the email promises new information and delivers recycled creative. The H&M member benefits banner at the very top ("FREE SHIPPING OVER $50 FOR H&M MEMBERS") is a small loyalty touch, but it's tiny and easy to miss.
- **Judgement:** Competent execution on a creatively exhausted concept. The brand has hit this exact note too many times in eight days. Without visible price drops, the "again" in the subject reads as admission of fatigue, not a reason to click.

## What's working

- **Four-vignette layout is clean** — The Bright Side (women), Summer Steals (men), Sunny Deals (baby/kids), Happy Place (home) gives clear category navigation without clutter.
- **Photography is strong** — All four hero shots are bright, well-styled, and summer-appropriate. The blue floral midi dress leads well.
- **Category CTAs are unambiguous** — Each vignette has a labeled button (WOMEN, MEN, BABY + KIDS, HOME) directly beneath the image.
- **Member banner is present** — "Free shipping over $50 for H&M Members" at the very top reinforces a loyalty benefit, even if it's easy to miss.
- **No render bugs** — Clean layout, no broken images, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **Zero prices in the body** — The subject promises "prices just dropped — again" but there is not a single dollar or percentage figure attached to a specific product anywhere in the scroll.
- **Hero headline is identical to June 19** — "Up to 60% off + further markdowns" is word-for-word the same claim from the prior sale send; this undercuts the "again" hook entirely.
- **No specific products linked** — Every CTA goes to a category page, not a featured item. The June 18 vacation email named actual products; this one names nothing.
- **"Further markdowns" is unsubstantiated** — The sub-headline promises more than 60% off but provides no evidence (no "was $49, now $19" callout, no percentage tier).
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — This is the fourth sale push from H&M in eight days; a real subscriber's inbox has been saturated with this creative since June 16.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show actual price drops in the body** — Pick two or three hero products with a "was / now" price pair under each image. The subject promises drops; the body must deliver them. This is the single change that would close the gap between subject and content.
- 2. **Replace the generic headline** — "Up to 60% off + further markdowns" has appeared in every sale email since June 16. Use specific tiers ("New 70% styles added") or a category callout ("Dresses from $9.99 today only").
- 3. **Add a deadline** — Nothing in this email says when the further markdowns expire. A visible end date ("Sale ends Sunday") makes the "again" feel urgent rather than arbitrary.
- 4. **Link at least one specific product per vignette** — The blue floral dress in the hero is compelling; name it, price it, link directly to the PDP.
- 5. **Elevate the member benefit** — Free shipping is buried in 8px text at the very top. Move it into the body ("Members: free shipping, no minimum") as a mid-email loyalty reminder.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New 70% styles just added — shop before they're gone`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Dresses from $9.99: fresh markdowns are live`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Further markdowns hit today — prices lower than sale launch`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Select styles now 70% off. Free shipping for members.`

## Technical Email Audit — H&M "Sale update: prices just dropped — again"

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard table-based layout with responsive CSS media queries and JSON-LD structured data annotations. The most significant technical finding is a hardcoded 2018 date in the JSON-LD `DiscountOffer` node and multiple completely unpopulated structured data fields, indicating a broken or abandoned template rendering step.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully audit** — HTML is truncated before the body content, so CTA links and tracking pixel `src` values are not visible.

**External font `@import` as a soft link risk:** Two `@import` directives load external CSS at render time:
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
Gmail, Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo strip `@import` from `<style>` blocks — these fail silently. No broken links, but a dependency that will never resolve in the majority of inboxes.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading (high impact):** As noted above, `@import` is stripped by every major email client. Font stack will silently fall back to system defaults. The correct pattern is `<link rel="stylesheet">` in `<head>`, which has broader (though still limited) client support.

**Duplicate CSS declarations (low impact, payload bloat):** The `optimize-css` `<style>` block contains repeated declarations for the same selectors:
- `.set_font_heading_xlarge` — defined 3 times
- `.department-title` — defined twice
- `.cta-link-mobile-border-ns` — defined twice
- `.cta-link-mobile, .set_font_interactive_standalone_link_default` — defined twice

These redundant rules add payload without effect.

**Defensive meta tags:** `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` and `format-detection=telephone=no` are correctly present. `charset=UTF-8` is declared. No issues here.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Stale hardcoded date (critical):** The `DiscountOffer` JSON-LD node contains:
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"
```
This date is 7.5 years in the past. It was almost certainly set during initial template authorship and never updated. Combined with `availabilityEnds: ""`, Gmail's Promotions Tab and any structured data parsers will receive a nonsensical or expired offer signal.

**Multiple unpopulated JSON-LD fields:** The following fields exist in the structured data but are empty strings — indicating the template render step either skipped population or the campaign data was never wired:

| Node | Field | Value |
|---|---|---|
| `EmailMessage` | `subjectLine` | `""` |
| `DiscountOffer` | `availabilityEnds` | `""` |
| `DiscountOffer` | `discountCode` | `""` |
| `DiscountOffer` | `description` | `""` |
| `PromotionCard` | `image` | `""` |
| `PromotionCard` | `url` | `""` |

An `EmailMessage` node with a blank `subjectLine` contradicts the actual subject line. A `PromotionCard` with no `url` or `image` is inert. These nodes should either be populated or removed.

### 7. Recommendations

**P0 — Fix the JSON-LD `availabilityStarts` date.** `"2018-12-31"` is stale by years. Either update it to the actual campaign start date or remove the `DiscountOffer` node entirely. A structurally present but date-expired offer may trigger spam classifier penalties.

**P0 — Populate or remove empty JSON-LD nodes.** `EmailMessage.subjectLine`, `PromotionCard.url`, `PromotionCard.image`, `DiscountOffer.description` are all empty. Structured data with empty required fields provides no value and risks being flagged by inbox providers as malformed annotation. Populate all fields from campaign metadata or strip the nodes.

**P1 — Replace `@import` font loading.** Move font CSS to `<link rel="stylesheet">` tags in `<head>`, or remove the external font dependency and rely on a declared `font-family` fallback stack inline. `@import` will never execute in Gmail or Outlook.

**P2 — Update `@context` to HTTPS.** All JSON-LD nodes use `"@context": "http://schema.org/"` — the canonical form is `https://schema.org/`. HTTP is deprecated by Schema.org.

**P2 — De-duplicate CSS declarations.** Consolidate the repeated `.set_font_heading_xlarge`, `.department-title`, and `.cta-link-mobile-border-ns` rules to reduce payload.

**P3 — Provide untruncated HTML for full audit.** Link tracking coverage, unsubscribe link functionality, UTM parameter consistency, and image alt text completeness cannot be verified from the truncated source.
