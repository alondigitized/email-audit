# Homepage snapshot · www2.hm.com · 2026-05-20
**Score:** 2/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so the first thing I see when I land on H&M is not a hero, not a sale banner, not cute styling inspo — it's a wall of GDPR cookie legalese. Like, the entire viewport is a consent modal. That's the brand experience right now: legal text and three stacked buttons before I've seen a single product. H&M is clearly a global fast-fashion player, but what they're pushing at me first is bureaucratic CYA copy, not fashion.

## What's working

- **Cookie modal renders cleanly** — the three buttons ("ACCEPT ALL," "ONLY REQUIRED COOKIES," "COOKIES AND SERVICES SETTINGS") are clearly labeled, distinct, and tappable on mobile. At least the gate itself isn't broken.
- **Plain language** — the consent copy is more readable than average GDPR walls; it explains what "Accept all" actually means in plain-ish sentences.

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage is blocked** — I cannot see a single product, hero image, banner, or CTA related to fashion. The modal takes up 100% of the visible viewport.
- **No brand warmth before the gate** — there's no H&M logo, no teaser of what's inside, no "Welcome back" — just dense legal text. A cold visitor has zero reason to feel excited to accept and continue.
- **Three-button consent UX is friction-heavy on mobile** — stacked full-width buttons in a scroll-heavy modal is not a smooth mobile experience. I have to read and decide before I've seen anything.
- **No loyalty or membership hook pre-consent** — as a member-rewards-aware shopper, there's zero incentive surface ("log in to see your rewards") to get me to accept and engage.
- **Background content is completely obscured** — even a blurred/dimmed hero behind the modal would build anticipation; instead it's just gray/black behind the text.

## Recommendations

- **Show a blurred hero behind the modal** — even a semi-transparent overlay on a seasonal campaign image gives me a reason to want to accept and get inside. Make me curious.
- **Add a one-line hook above the buttons** — something like "Accept to unlock your member rewards and this week's offers" ties consent to a concrete value exchange, which is more compelling than pure legal compliance framing.
- **Reduce the modal to two buttons max on mobile** — "Accept all" and "Manage preferences" is the industry-standard minimal friction pattern; the three-button stack feels like H&M is trying to make me work for it.
- **Pre-populate the background with a seasonal hero** — if the consent modal dismisses with a swipe or a tap, I should land immediately on the campaign. Don't make me wait for a second load state.
