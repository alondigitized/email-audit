# In focus: Summer basics
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- H&M is pivoting to pure product editorial after four consecutive sale emails (June 16–19). "Summer Basics" is a clean, photo-forward send featuring six new arrivals at regular price — no discount, no urgency, no sale tie-in. That's a coherent strategy for protecting new inventory from markdown expectations, but it creates real inbox confusion: subscribers who received the June 19 "Up to 60% off" blast just three days ago will land here and wonder why the sale suddenly disappeared. The "SALE" link sitting in the nav bar makes it worse — it hints at discounts the email never delivers on.
- The execution is technically tidy. Good photography, readable hierarchy, two prices visible, no render bugs. But the concept doesn't earn its place in the cadence. This is the seventh H&M email in seven days, and "In focus: Summer basics" is the most skimmable subject in the run — no hook, no offer, no reason to open ahead of anything else in the inbox.
- Calling a ruffle-trimmed cardigan a "summer basic" is a stretch. The product assortment feels editorially uncertain.

## What's working

- **Clean visual hierarchy.** The red-tank hero lands the eye immediately; "SUMMER BASICS" reads as the brand's editorial voice at its best.
- **Two products have prices visible** ($60.99 cardigan, $25.49 dress) — more transparency than the June 20 outfit-story email, which had zero prices.
- **No render bugs.** Layout, images, and text all load correctly with no overlapping elements or broken assets.
- **"New Arrival" labeling** clearly differentiates this send from the sale emails — subscribers who pay attention will understand these are fresh-in items.

## What's weak

- **No sale connection despite the ongoing "60% off" event.** The nav bar says "SALE" but nothing in the body bridges to it. Subscribers left wondering if these new arrivals are excluded from the sale.
- **Two of four products show no price.** The H&M Premium Selection linen shirt says "See Arrivals" with zero price signal — a friction point that kills click intent for price-sensitive shoppers.
- **Cadence fatigue.** Seventh email in seven days. "Summer basics" is the least-urgent subject in that sequence and arrives when subscriber attention is most depleted.
- **"Ruffle-trimmed cardigan" as a summer basic is a positioning misfire.** The product doesn't match the headline promise — it plants doubt about the editor's curation.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is generic.** Used three times with no variation; "Shop the Edit" or "Shop Basics" would be more on-brand and specific.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Tie one item to the sale, explicitly.** Even "These styles are not in the sale — here's why they're worth full price" would resolve the cognitive dissonance. Or just badge one product as sale-eligible and link to the sale page.
- 2. **Show prices for all products.** The linen shirt hiding behind "See Arrivals" is a click deterrent. Price transparency = conversion confidence.
- 3. **Swap the cardigan for a genuine summer basic.** A linen tee, a terry polo, a cotton cami — something that earns the "basics" framing.
- 4. **Vary the CTA copy.** "Shop Summer Basics" once, "See the Linen Edit" for the Premium Selection section — earns more clicks than three identical "SHOP NOW" buttons.
- 5. **Rest the cadence.** H&M has emailed daily since June 16. This editorial would land harder on Monday after a weekend break.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Font @import stripping (high risk)**
Both font stylesheets are loaded via `@import` inside `<style>` blocks:
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
Gmail and Outlook strip `@import` directives; custom fonts will silently fall back to system defaults in all major clients. This is expected behavior for email and likely intentional, but confirms custom typography will not render in Gmail/Outlook.

**Incomplete `format-detection` meta**
```html
<meta content="telephone=no" name="format-detection"/>
```
Only telephone auto-linking is suppressed. iOS Mail and some Android clients also auto-link dates, addresses, and email addresses. The complete suppression directive is:
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no, date=no, address=no, email=no"/>
```
