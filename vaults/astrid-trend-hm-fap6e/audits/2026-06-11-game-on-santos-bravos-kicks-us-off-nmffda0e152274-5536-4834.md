# Game on: SANTOS BRAVOS kicks us off
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- H&M is launching a soccer-themed collaboration with Santos Bravos — an illustrated-jersey drop for the summer season. The email reads as a clean editorial product announcement: bold headline, four segmentation CTAs (WOMEN / MEN / KIDS / FIND A STORE), followed by a product grid of printed soccer jerseys featuring DRYCOOL™ tech. No discount, no deadline, no urgency.
- The timing is the immediate problem. This lands the day after June 10's "25% off flash sale starts now" (8/10) — a hard-offer email that almost certainly drove strong opens and clicks. Going from a sitewide discount to an unfamiliar collaboration brand with no offer the very next morning is a jarring cadence pivot. Subscribers primed to expect a sale follow-up instead get a branded launch they have no context for.
- Santos Bravos is the bigger structural issue. It's the first mention of this brand in the subscriber's experience, and there's no scaffolding — no "meet the collab," no artist/brand story, nothing that would make the name mean anything. This email tries to sell the product without first selling the collaboration. As a pure product-grid send, it's competent; as a collab launch, it undersells the hook.

## What's working

- **Hero copy is clean and on-brief.** "KICKOFF WITH SANTOS BRAVOS" lands immediately, the soccer pun in the subject echoes the visual, and the body copy ("feels like a win") is consistent.
- **DRYCOOL™ is visible on every product card.** This is a credible functional differentiator — it gives buyers a reason beyond aesthetics.
- **No render bugs.** The email is fully rendered, image-free gaps are absent, text isn't overlapping, and the product grid is even.
- **Segmentation CTAs give the subscriber an immediate decision point.** The four buttons (WOMEN / MEN / KIDS / FIND A STORE) appear without scrolling and cut through category ambiguity quickly.

## What's weak

- **Santos Bravos is context-free.** No "who is this?" moment — the name appears as a label above the headline and on product cards with no brand story or artist callout. For a subscriber who doesn't recognize the name, it reads as another jersey.
- **No offer.** Following a sitewide 25% flash sale, a no-discount email feels like a step back with nothing to replace the incentive.
- **Four CTAs with no primary.** WOMEN / MEN / KIDS / FIND A STORE — there's no single "SHOP THE COLLECTION" button that drives the collaboration concept. The segmentation is helpful but the collab launch energy dissipates across four destinations.
- **Preheader is missing or leaking junk.** The render shows no visible preheader copy — inbox clients will likely surface nav text or "VIEW IN BROWSER" rather than anything that complements the subject.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a one-line brand origin sentence above the CTAs.** Even "Santos Bravos is a São Paulo-born label bringing football energy to your summer" would make the collaboration legible and motivate exploration. Without context, the name is dead weight.
- 2. **Add a single hero CTA before the segmentation row.** "SHOP THE DROP" or "EXPLORE SANTOS BRAVOS" as a full-width button above the WOMEN / MEN / KIDS split would establish the collab as the primary action before funneling into category.
- 3. **Write a real preheader.** The subject says "kicks us off" — the preheader should complete the thought.
- 4. **Consider an early-access or member-only framing** to replace the missing discount. "H&M members, first access to the Santos Bravos drop" would give loyalists a reason to click now without a blanket price cut.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading will silently fail in Gmail (web + mobile)**
```html
<style>
  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
</style>
```
Gmail strips `<style>` block `@import` directives entirely. H&M's custom typefaces (`Northstar`, OCES) will not render for Gmail recipients — the largest email client segment. Fallback font behavior is uncontrolled.

**`img.image + div { display: none !important; }` is a broad selector**
This hides any `<div>` immediately following an `.image`-classed `<img>`. If a client reflows or wraps images differently, legitimate content blocks could be suppressed. Low risk but worth auditing against the full DOM tree.

**Yahoo-specific media query uses non-standard syntax**
```css
@media screen yahoo { .show-webkit-table { display: none; } }
```
`@media screen yahoo` is a legacy H&M/ESP workaround. Confirm this is intentional and still needed — some modern Yahoo Mail versions now support standard media queries.

**No `alt` attributes visible in the truncated source.** Full-source check required; missing `alt` text on product images breaks screen reader access and renders blank in image-off clients.

### 5. Compliance

**Stale/empty JSON-LD DiscountOffer block**
```json
{
  "@type": "DiscountOffer",
  "availabilityStarts": "2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00",
  "availabilityEnds": "",
  "discountCode": "",
  "description": ""
}
```
`availabilityStarts` is hardcoded to `2018-12-31` — over 7 years stale. This is an unfilled template default. Gmail uses this schema to render Promotions tab deal badges; a past date will cause the annotation to not surface or display incorrectly. `availabilityEnds`, `discountCode`, and `description` are all empty strings, further disabling the feature.

**Empty PromotionCard fields**
```json
{
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  "image": "",
  "url": ""

```
Both `image` and `url` are empty. Gmail cannot render a Promotions tab hero image or deep link without these values.
