# The father of all gifts...
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- H&M returns to Father's Day territory for the third time in five days — but this time without a discount. The June 5 sale email ran hard ("Up to 50% off gifts for every dad"), the June 7 send closed it ("Last day"), and now June 9 delivers an editorial gift guide with no new offer attached. The structural problem is obvious: subscribers who wanted to buy for Dad already had every reason to act. What's left to say?
- The creative is competent. Three distinct dad archetypes (Style Influencer, #1 Fan, In Good Taste) give the guide some personality and prevent the email from feeling like a generic hero dump. The photography is clean, the layout is orderly, and "GIFTS FOR DAD" lands immediately above the fold. But the absence of any offer — after you already opened and closed a Father's Day sale — makes this feel like a stale leftover rather than a useful reminder.
- The bottom third is the real problem: a "SEEN HEARD CELEBRATED" section with bright festival/Pride-adjacent visuals appears mid-scroll, completely breaking the Father's Day tonal thread. A subscriber scanning on mobile will be confused about what they're looking at.

## What's working

- **Three-persona structure is smart.** Style Influencer / #1 Fan / In Good Taste gives shoppers a mental shortcut to find the right gift without reading product names.
- **Hero arrives above the fold.** "GIFTS FOR DAD" with a male model in a full look lands immediately — no wasted real estate at the top.
- **Clean render, no layout bugs.** Text, images, and structure all appear intact across the email.
- **Subject line lands the pun.** "The father of all gifts..." is clever and short enough to read cleanly on mobile.
- **Seasonally on-brief photography.** Male models in warm-weather outfits fit the June Father's Day moment without looking recycled.

## What's weak

- **No offer after closing a sale two days ago.** The June 7 "Last day" email set a hard endpoint. A gift guide on June 9 with full price feels like a mixed signal — did the sale re-open, or not?
- **"SEEN HEARD CELEBRATED" section breaks the narrative.** Colorful, festive, Pride-coded imagery mid-scroll is jarring inside a Father's Day edit — it reads as two separate campaigns stitched together.
- **CTA buttons are small and unreadable at mobile scale.** No button copy is legible in the render; clicking is a leap of faith.
- **No urgency mechanism.** Father's Day is June 21 — 12 days away — but nothing in the email acknowledges the deadline or creates a reason to buy today.
- **No offer differentiation from the June 5 email.** If H&M had dropped even "free shipping this week," this send would justify itself. Without it, it's a mood board.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a new offer or deadline, otherwise don't send.** "Free shipping on orders over $X through June 19" would give this email a job to do. Without it, the gift guide just tells subscribers to go shop at full price with zero urgency — at which point they'll wait for the next sale.
- 2. **Cut or quarantine the "SEEN HEARD CELEBRATED" section.** If it's a Pride send, send it separately. If it's a footer module, make it visually subdued. Right now it functions as a tonal wrecking ball at scroll depth.
- 3. **Make the CTA buttons bigger and add specific copy.** "Shop the Style Influencer Edit" and "Shop the #1 Fan Edit" are concrete and scannable. Generic "Shop now" fades into the layout.
- 4. **Add a deadline line near the hero.** A simple "Order by June 17 for on-time delivery" anchors the purchase decision without a discount.
- 5. **Lean harder into the delivery angle.** The brand has the data on shipping cutoffs. Father's Day gifting emails live or die on delivery confidence.

## Full review
## 1. Overview

H&M returns to Father's Day territory for the third time in five days — but this time without a discount. The June 5 sale email ran hard ("Up to 50% off gifts for every dad"), the June 7 send closed it ("Last day"), and now June 9 delivers an editorial gift guide with no new offer attached. The structural problem is obvious: subscribers who wanted to buy for Dad already had every reason to act. What's left to say?

- **Seasonally on-brief photography.** Male models in warm-weather outfits fit the June Father's Day moment without looking recycled.

## 3. What didn't

- **No offer after closing a sale two days ago.** The June 7 "Last day" email set a hard endpoint. A gift guide on June 9 with full price feels like a mixed signal — did the sale re-open, or not?
- **"SEEN HEARD CELEBRATED" section breaks the narrative.** Colorful, festive, Pride-coded imagery mid-scroll is jarring inside a Father's Day edit — it reads as two separate campaigns stitched together.

3. **Make the CTA buttons bigger and add specific copy.** "Shop the Style Influencer Edit" and "Shop the #1 Fan Edit" are concrete and scannable. Generic "Shop now" fades into the layout.
4. **Add a deadline line near the hero.** A simple "Order by June 17 for on-time delivery" anchors the purchase decision without a discount.
5. **Lean harder into the delivery angle.** The brand has the data on shipping cutoffs. Father's Day gifting emails live or die on delivery confidence.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` in `<style>` block (lines 8–9):**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
Gmail and Outlook both strip `@import` inside `<style>` tags in the `<head>`. Custom fonts will silently fail in these clients. Use `<link rel="stylesheet">` or inline the `@font-face` declarations for better client coverage — though note that Outlook on Windows will ignore custom fonts entirely regardless.
