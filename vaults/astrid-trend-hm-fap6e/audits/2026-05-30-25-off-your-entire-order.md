# 25% off your entire order
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- This is H&M's first post-cycle "Weekend Deal" send — landing Friday May 30, just five days after the Memorial Day 50%-off blitz ended. The offer is 25% off $125+ plus free shipping. The structure is clean: a headline offer up top, then a six-category tile grid (Women, Men, Kids 2-8, Kids 9-14, Home, Beauty), followed by lifestyle model shots for each category. There is no single prominent CTA button in the hero — the category tiles do the conversion lifting.
- The core tension here is subscriber memory. Anyone who opened H&M's May 25 "Up to 50% off has *hours* left" send (9/10, the strongest of the Memorial Day run) is now being asked to get excited about half the discount with a $125 spend minimum attached. The subject line makes it worse: "25% off your entire order" implies no minimum, but the body immediately says "$125+." That disconnect is the email's biggest liability.
- The execution is competent but generic. This could be any mid-tier retailer's weekend trigger. Nothing about it is specific to any product, season story, or subscriber segment — a sharp contrast to the May 26 and May 28 editorial sends that actually tried to say something.

## What's working

- **Offer is legible in under two seconds.** "25% OFF $125 + FREE SHIPPING" in bold at the top — no hunting required.
- **Subject line is clean and under 50 characters.** At 25 chars, "25% off your entire order" is mobile-safe and direct.
- **Preheader complements rather than repeats.** "So many new styles to save on. So many looks you'll love." is soft but editorial — it doesn't echo the subject word-for-word.
- **No render bugs.** Clean white layout, images loaded, no broken blocks or overlapping elements visible.
- **Category breadth is a strength for browsing.** Six-category tile grid with model shots makes it easy for any household member to find their lane.

## What's weak

- **Subject says "entire order," body says "$125+."** That's a mismatch — subscribers who opened expecting no minimum will feel the friction when they hit the body copy.
- **No deadline on "WEEKEND DEAL."** The label implies urgency but there's no "Ends Sunday at midnight" or any specific date. Vague urgency is weak urgency.
- **No hero CTA button.** The category tiles are the only clickable elements in the upper half. There's no single "Shop Now" or "Claim 25% Off" button to capture intent from someone who doesn't know which category they want.
- **The offer regresses vs. Memorial Day.** Subscribers just received eight emails offering up to 50% off. 25% off with a $125 minimum is objectively weaker — this email doesn't address that shift or reframe it.
- **Lifestyle shots are generic.** No specific products called out by name, no featured styles, no "the one thing you should buy this weekend" hook.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the subject-to-body mismatch first.** Either change the subject to reflect the threshold ("25% off when you spend $125+") or restructure the offer so there is no minimum. Right now the subject oversells and the body undersells.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the "WEEKEND DEAL" framing.** "Ends Sunday, 11:59 PM" added to the hero block turns a vague label into an actual conversion lever.
- 3. **Add a single hero CTA button.** Beneath the offer headline, one button — "Shop Women," "Shop the Deal," or even "Shop All" — closes the loop. Category tiles are great for browsing; a button is for intenders.
- 4. **Acknowledge the step-down from Memorial Day.** A single line like "New arrivals, now 25% off" reframes this as a fresh-inventory offer rather than a worse version of last week's sale.
- 5. **Lead with one featured product or edit.** Even a single "this week's pick" with a real product image would give the email a reason to exist beyond being a generic weekend trigger.
- **Subject Alt A:** `25% off $125+ this weekend only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New arrivals, 25% off — this weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping too. Ends Sunday at midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh drops. One weekend. No code needed.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` for custom fonts will silently fail.**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is stripped by Gmail (web and app), Apple Mail iOS (partial), and Outlook all versions. Font will fall back to client default — no declared `font-family` fallback stack is visible in the provided CSS, meaning fallback behavior is undefined.

**No `lang` attribute on `<html>` element.**
```html
<html style="border:0;margin:0;outline:0;padding:0">
```
Missing `lang="en"` (or locale-appropriate value). Screen readers cannot determine language; some spam filters also flag this absence.

**`<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">` syntax error** — correct attribute is `content="telephone=no"`, but the tag is written as `content="telephone=no" name="format-detection"` with attributes reversed. While browsers/clients are tolerant of attribute order, this deviates from spec and could cause parse issues in strict clients.

### 5. Compliance

**JSON-LD `DiscountOffer` fields are unpopulated:**
```json

  "description": "",
  "discountCode": "",
  "availabilityStarts": "2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00",
  "availabilityEnds": ""

```
- `discountCode: ""` — for an email whose subject line is a promotional offer, an empty discount code is a data integrity gap (may not be CAN-SPAM relevant but is a deliverability/Promotions tab signal issue).

**JSON-LD `EmailMessage.subjectLine` is empty:**

**Unsubscribe mechanism** — `.footer-unsubscribe` class is referenced in the responsive CSS, indicating a footer unsubscribe element exists. Cannot verify the actual link href or one-click List-Unsubscribe header from the truncated source. **Recommend confirming `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are present in the sending infrastructure** — required for bulk senders under Gmail/Yahoo 2024 requirements and CAN-SPAM.
