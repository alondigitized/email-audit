# Your next great outfit is here
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- This email is H&M attempting a hybrid move — editorial hero ("DRESSED FOR THE OCCASION") stacked on top of a hard sale banner ("SALE JUST GOT BETTER: UP TO 60% OFF"). The combination is coherent enough but slightly schizophrenic: the subject promises aspirational outfit discovery, the preheader banner pivots immediately to sale mode, and the hero image delivers a lookbook editorial. It lands somewhere between the strong June 29 "Go-to summer looks" send and the bargain-bin drumbeat of earlier in the week — and commits fully to neither lane.
- The top banner does the real work here. "UP TO 60% OFF + NEW STYLES ADDED FROM $7.99" is the only concrete, actionable signal in the email. The "DRESSED FOR THE OCCASION" section below adds brand equity, but the hero has no CTA button — subscribers who want to shop the editorial look have to hunt for the gender tabs or scroll down to individual product thumbnails. That's friction that costs conversions.
- Worth flagging: the "$7.99" price floor appeared verbatim in the June 27 subject line ("New sale styles added from $7.99"). Running the same anchor in consecutive sends makes the sale feel permanent rather than fresh. After seven sale-adjacent emails in ten days, this brand needs a new signal — not a rephrased version of the same one.

## What's working

- **Sale banner placement is immediately actionable** — "UP TO 60% OFF + NEW STYLES FROM $7.99" is above the fold, before the subscriber even processes the hero image.
- **Occasion framing is well-timed** — "DRESSED FOR THE OCCASION" lands well at end of June when summer events (weddings, vacations, outdoor parties) are in full swing.
- **Gender + age navigation tabs** right below the hero (WOMEN / MEN / GIRLS 2-14 / BOYS 2-14) make self-segmentation frictionless.
- **H&M+ Member Selection badge** on the linen-blend shirt is a subtle but real loyalty signal that rewards members.
- **Product mix covers the brief** — embroidery dress ($34.99 New Arrival), slim-fit jacket ($74.99), linen shirt spans accessible occasion looks in one product row.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is inert** — "Your next great outfit is here" is template-speak with zero specificity, urgency, or curiosity gap; the preheader is doing the job the subject should be doing.
- **No hero CTA button** — the editorial section has headline and body copy but no "Shop the Edit" or "Dress for the Occasion" button; clicks require finding the gender tabs or scrolling to product thumbnails.
- **$7.99 anchor is recycled** — the same price floor anchored the June 27 subject line; reusing it in the banner here reinforces a "this sale never ends" feeling rather than signaling fresh inventory.
- **No sale deadline anywhere** — "SALE JUST GOT BETTER" implies momentum but there's no end date visible, so the urgency is a bluff the subscriber can see through.
- **Two-track messaging dilutes both signals** — the email can't fully commit to being an inspiring lookbook *or* a crisp sale alert; the editorial hero and the sale banner compete for attention without reinforcing each other.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the subject and preheader** — the preheader is carrying concrete value the subject should own. Flip them so the inbox hook is immediately specific.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dressed for the occasion — up to 60% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Summer event looks, now up to 60% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dresses, tailored pieces & standout accessories — new styles added`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New occasion styles just dropped. Sale ends soon.`
- 2. **Add a hero CTA button** — a single "Shop the Edit" button directly under the headline converts the editorial section from brand wallpaper into a click surface.
- 3. **Add a deadline to the sale** — even "Sale ends Sunday" or "Offer ends July 4" sharpens the urgency the banner promises but doesn't deliver.
- 4. **Retire the $7.99 floor anchor** — it appeared in the June 27 subject line and now again in the banner; rotate to a new anchor ("new occasion styles from $19.99" or "just added: up to 60% off event dresses") to signal that this is actually a new event, not a rolling discount.
- 5. **Make the featured product the editorial hero** — rather than a generic model shot, center the cotton dress with embroidery ($34.99, New Arrival) as the hero with a direct product link; a specific "New Arrival" price point gives subscribers a concrete reason to click beyond "vague summer vibes."

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**JSON-LD PromotionCard `url` is empty**
```json
{"@type":"PromotionCard","url":""}
```
Gmail renders PromotionCard entries in the Promotions tab carousel. An empty `url` disables the card's click-through entirely.

**JSON-LD `subjectLine` is empty**
```json
{"@type":"EmailMessage","subjectLine":""}
```
Should match the actual subject line (`"Your next great outfit is here"`). Gmail may deprioritize the Promotions tab annotation when this field is blank.

**Cannot verify UTM parameters** — the HTML is truncated before `<body>` link content. A full-source audit is required to confirm all `<a href>` values carry `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign`.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` inside `<style>` — fonts will not render in most clients**
```html
<style>
  @import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
  @import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
</style>
```
Gmail (web and Android), Outlook (all desktop versions), and Yahoo strip or ignore `@import` inside `<style>` blocks. Custom fonts will fall back to system defaults in these clients, which represent the majority of H&M's likely audience. Use `<link rel="stylesheet">` in `<head>` instead, and always include a CSS font-stack fallback (`font-family: 'HM Font', Arial, sans-serif`).

**`x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` present** — correct, no issue.

**`format-detection` for telephone set** — correct, no issue.

**`alt` attribute coverage** — cannot confirm from truncated source. The selector `img.image + div { display: none !important; }` implies product images exist; each must carry a descriptive `alt`.

**`role`/ARIA** — none visible in `<head>` section; main content is truncated, so cannot assess body-level accessibility.
