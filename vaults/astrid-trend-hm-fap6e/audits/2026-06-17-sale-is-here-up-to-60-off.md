# Sale is here: up to 60% off
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- This is the "official" sale launch for the same 60% off H&M promoted yesterday as an app-exclusive (June 16, "Early app access: up to 60% off"). That context is damaging: subscribers who received both emails saw a supposedly exclusive perk evaporate in 24 hours, and the headline number is identical. As a standalone creative execution it's competent — "SALE / UP TO 60% OFF" lands immediately, the free-shipping-for-members callout at the very top is a smart loyalty hook, and the product grid (romper at $15, linen polo, chinos) makes the discount feel tangible rather than theoretical.
- What undercuts it is the same structural failure H&M keeps repeating: six competing category buttons (WOMEN, MEN, KIDS 2-8Y, KIDS 8-14Y, HOME, FIND A STORE) with no single dominant CTA, no deadline anywhere in the email, and a product mix so broad it reads as a blast to the full undifferentiated list. The "new select essentials" phrasing in the hero subhead is counterproductive — "new" contradicts the clearance/markdown frame — and the Canvas Cushion Cover sitting in the product grid alongside apparel dilutes the summer-wardrobe story.
- Solid execution on the fundamentals, but no urgency mechanism and no segmentation means this is a 7 masquerading as a 9.

## What's working

- "SALE / UP TO 60% OFF" lands hard and fast at the top — no scrolling required to understand the offer.
- Free-shipping-for-members strip at the very top rewards loyalty before the pitch starts, a small but meaningful sequencing win.
- Product grid anchors the offer with real prices — $15 Draped Romper as the lead item is a strong concrete hook.
- Summer seasonal framing ("Upgrade your summer deals") is appropriately on-brief for mid-June.
- Clean render — no broken images, no overlapping text, consistent H&M visual identity throughout.

## What's weak

- Six category buttons create decision paralysis with no dominant "shop the sale" CTA; the eye scatters instead of converting.
- No deadline anywhere — "Sale is here" with no end date removes any reason to click now rather than later.
- Same 60% number as the June 16 app-exclusive email makes the "exclusive" framing from yesterday look like a hollow tactic.
- "New select essentials" is confusing sale copy — "new" and "sale" are contradictory signals.
- Canvas Cushion Cover in the product grid is off-brief for a summer wardrobe sale story.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the six-button grid, add one decisive hero CTA.** "SHOP WOMEN'S SALE" as the primary button, then secondary links for Men/Kids below. Decision paralysis is the biggest click-rate leak here.
- 2. **Add a deadline.** "Through Sunday" or "Ends Monday" would lift urgency across subject, hero, and CTA simultaneously — this is the single change with the highest expected lift.
- 3. **Swap the Cushion Cover for a second apparel item.** Keep the product grid on-brief; home goods belong in a separate send.
- 4. **Rewrite the hero qualifier.** Replace "big markdowns on new select essentials" with "up to 60% off marked-down summer styles" — cleaner, no contradictions.
- 5. **Don't relaunch an "exclusive" offer the next day.** The June 16 → June 17 sequencing erodes the app-download incentive; if the sale goes broad, give it at least 48–72 hours.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Members get free shipping on every sale order over $50.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` in `<style>` blocks (medium risk)**
Two external font sheets are loaded via `@import`:
```
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` is stripped by Outlook (all versions), AOL Mail, and some Yahoo Mail clients. Fallback system fonts will render instead — confirm fallback stack is acceptable in those clients.

**`<script>` tag present**
The JSON-LD block uses `<script type="application/ld+json">`. All email clients except Gmail strip `<script>` tags. This is expected behavior for email, but the data must be correct for Gmail's promo-tab enrichment to work (see §4).

**Alt text** — truncated source prevents full audit of `<img alt="">` coverage. Flag for full review.
