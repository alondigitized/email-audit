# Swim is in & up to 50% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- H&M's third Memorial Day sale send in four days uses the swim angle as creative refresh — the right instinct after May 20's launch ("Up to 50% off sitewide starts now") and May 22's reminder ("Kick-start summer with up to 50% off"). But the email doesn't commit to the premise. The hero model wears floral vacation wear, not swimwear. The three editorial sections — BREEZY PICKS, HEAT MODE, RED WHITE & NEW — are summer-toned but category-agnostic. There is no "SHOP SWIM" CTA anywhere in the body. The subject teases swim; the body delivers another generic sale email in a summer costume.
- The sale mechanics are clean: "UP TO 50% OFF SITEWIDE" owns the hero zone, layout is tight, no visible bugs. H&M's template discipline holds. But by the third near-identical send, a subscriber who's already passed on this offer twice needs a new reason to act today — a specific product, a deadline, or an actual swim shop. This gives them none of those.
- Competent execution of the wrong email. The swim pivot is smart strategy; the creative didn't follow through.

## What's working

- "UP TO 50% OFF SITEWIDE" is unmissable in the hero zone — no hunting for the offer.
- Subject-line pivot to swim is the right strategic call after two identical-feeling sale subjects; it signals category relevance.
- "RED, WHITE & NEW" section ties the sale to Memorial Day without being heavy-handed — a specific, timely hook.
- Three editorial sections (two female-led, one male) broaden appeal within a single scroll.
- Layout is clean end-to-end — no broken images, no overlapping text, no empty fields visible.

## What's weak

- Subject promises swim; body never delivers — no dedicated swimwear section and no "SHOP SWIM" CTA anywhere.
- All three editorial CTAs say "SHOP" — identical, verb-only, zero differentiation between BREEZY PICKS / HEAT MODE / RED WHITE & NEW.
- No expiration date visible — the Memorial Day deadline is the strongest urgency lever available and it's completely absent.
- "SEE WHAT'S NEW IN STORE" section breaks the sale/swim narrative and redirects to foot traffic — feels like a different email inserted mid-scroll.
- Third sale email in four days with no new conversion hook; cadence fatigue is the ceiling on both open and click.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Build an actual swim section.** A "SHOP SWIMWEAR" hero with one or two specific products (one-piece, bikini, cover-up) and a "SHOP SWIM" button would close the subject-body gap. This is the single change that most directly honors the send's premise.
- 2. **Add a visible deadline.** "Sale ends Monday" or "Ends Memorial Day" with the date — anywhere in the email — converts fence-sitters who've already seen the offer twice.
- 3. **Differentiate the CTA copy.** Match the section name into the button: "SHOP BREEZY PICKS," "SHOP HEAT MODE," "SHOP RED WHITE & NEW." Costs nothing, signals intentionality.
- 4. **Remove or relocate the in-store section.** "SEE WHAT'S NEW IN STORE" with a "FIND A STORE" CTA kills sale momentum mid-scroll. Push it to the footer or cut it entirely for this send.
- 5. **Hero product with a visible price reduction.** A single swimsuit shown at "Was $39.99 → $19.99" beats a lifestyle model for a discount-driven email — makes the 50% off concrete, not abstract.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Memorial Day swim — 50% off, ends Monday`

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Bikinis, cover-ups & breezy picks — all in the sale.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Font loading via `@import` inside `<style>` blocks:**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` is blocked or stripped by Gmail, Outlook (all versions), and several other major clients. Fonts will silently fall back with no error — this is a known rendering limitation, not a crash risk, but means custom brand fonts are not rendering in the majority of email clients.

**Non-standard media query:**
```css
@media screen yahoo { ... }
```
This is a Yahoo Mail targeting hack (recognized by Yahoo's CSS parser). It is functional but not standard CSS; document for maintainability.
