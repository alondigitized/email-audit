# The active edit
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- This is H&M's activewear editorial — "The Active Edit" — arriving on June 21, the day after the June 20 outfit-story editorial and amid a heavy June sale drumbeat. The email leads with a white-background hero shot of a woman in a casual athletic look, headline "IN MOTION," and tagline "Discover modern sportswear designed to deliver comfort, functionality & style wherever the day takes you." That's a clean concept. The layout then delivers a three-tile editorial grid with specific sub-headlines ("Move with Confidence," "Built to Perform," "Tiny Moves") before pivoting hard into a Lotto × H&M collab feature in the lower half.
- The Lotto section is the most interesting thing in this email and arguably deserves top billing. A bold collab with a heritage football brand, striking colorblock photography, and copy that reads "Whether you're heading from the stands, the court, or the streets, find the looks that bring scoring energy to every moment." That's the strongest creative moment in the send. Burying it below the fold is a genuine miss.
- Overall: competent activewear editorial, no prices shown anywhere, soft CTAs throughout, and a collab that deserved the hero slot. It's a cut above the generic sale emails from the prior week, but it will feel thin to anyone who opened hoping for a deal after days of "up to 60% off" messaging.

## What's working

- **Lotto × H&M collab is genuinely differentiated** — an actual named brand partnership gives this email a hook that pure H&M product shots don't have.
- **Three-tile editorial grid is clean and scannable** — "Move with Confidence / Built to Perform / Tiny Moves" gives structure without clutter.
- **No render bugs** — layout renders crisply, no broken images, overlapping text, or empty fields visible.
- **Top-of-email sale banner** — the "SALE IS HERE / UP TO 80% OFF IN STORES & ONLINE" strip at the very top anchors price-conscious openers before the editorial begins.
- **Gender segmentation tiles at the bottom** (WOMEN / MEN / KIDS / HOME / BEAUTY) make navigation easy.

## What's weak

- **No prices anywhere in the editorial** — three product tiles, a collab feature, zero dollar or percent figures attached to specific items. The reader can't act on any single product.
- **Lotto collab is buried below the fold** — the most interesting creative moment is the second half of the email; it should be the hero.
- **"IN MOTION" headline is generic** — it could be any activewear brand's campaign line; it does nothing to differentiate H&M specifically.
- **CTAs are weak and repetitive** — every tile says "SHOP NOW." No specificity about what you're clicking into (Lotto gear? Womenswear? A specific style?).
- **Sale banner and editorial tone are in conflict** — the top strip screams 80% off, then the body pivots into a mood-board with no sale integration. The same dissonance H&M created on June 18 (vacation editorial the day after the sale launch) is repeating here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with Lotto × H&M** — swap the hero to the collab shot. It's a real reason to open and click that a generic "IN MOTION" spread doesn't provide.
- 2. **Add at least one anchoring price** — even "From $19.99" or "Pieces from $24.95" in the hero tile converts browsers. The sale banner says 80% off but nothing in the body confirms it applies here.
- 3. **Make CTAs specific** — replace every "SHOP NOW" with the destination: "SHOP LOTTO COLLAB," "SHOP WOMEN'S ACTIVE," "SHOP PERFORMANCE." One verb + one noun beats one vague verb.
- 4. **Reconcile the sale strip with editorial content** — either integrate sale pricing into the tiles or drop the sale banner so the editorial can breathe as a true brand story.
- 5. **Tighten the subject** — "The active edit" is bland and will be lost in an inbox after a week of sale subjects.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**External font blocking:**
```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is render-blocking in supporting clients and silently ignored by Outlook. No fallback font stack is declared in the visible CSS — if either CDN is slow or blocked, text rendering degrades with no system fallback.

**Risky rule:**
```css
img.image + div { display: none !important; }
```
This hides any `<div>` immediately following an `.image`-classed `<img>`. If content is ever placed in such a position (e.g., captions, ALT overlays), it will be silently suppressed with no visible error.
