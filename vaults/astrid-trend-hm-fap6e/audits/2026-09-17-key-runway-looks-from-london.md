# Key runway looks from London
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-17**
## Executive summary

- This is a designer-editorial send — "H&M&YOU&I," a London runway capsule with backstage-style photography, no discount banner, no urgency copy. It's a clean break from the last two weeks of "up to 50% off" / "15%/20% off" tiered-discount emails and even reads more premium than the 09/14 SculptMove launch or 09/11 tennis edit. The backstage shots (safety pins, exposed set, models getting dressed) are a genuine style signal, not stock catalog imagery.
- The problem is the same one this brand keeps running into on non-discount sends: gorgeous imagery, weak commercial mechanics. There's no price framing beyond bare tags ($99, $379, $89.99, $139 — no context on whether that's high or low for this capsule), no "shop by category" prioritization until the very bottom, and the hero CTA ("SHOP THE RUNWAY") is generic enough to apply to any collection drop. Runway/editorial sends are inherently a lower-intent, lower-conversion play than a percent-off email, and this one doesn't do anything extra to compensate — no lookbook video, no "shop the exact look," no styling notes.
- Judgement: a nicely art-directed brand email that will underperform commercially — good for brand equity, not for click-through.

## What's working

- Backstage/runway photography is distinctive and clearly differentiated from routine product-grid emails.
- Consistent product-then-price format under each hero image (name in caps, price beneath) is easy to scan.
- Two "Shop the Runway" CTAs (top and mid-email) give the reader more than one chance to convert without needing to scroll to the very bottom.
- "Shop By" category grid (Women/Men/Kids/Home/Beauty) at the footer covers the full catalog for readers not interested in the runway edit specifically.

## What's weak

- No sizing, fit, or availability information visible on any of the four featured pieces — a real barrier to clicking through on higher-priced items like the $379 ruffled maxi dress.
- Individual product images aren't linked to a clear "shop this item" CTA — only the two generic "Shop the Runway" buttons exist, so intent is diffused rather than captured per-product.
- No social proof, reviews, or "as seen on" runway context (this is literally runway product, an easy trust lever left unused).
- Boots image looks like an unstyled product photo dropped into an otherwise backstage-editorial aesthetic — visually inconsistent with the rest of the email.
- Header "SHOP NOW / SALE" nav bar promises "SALE" but nothing in this send is discounted, creating a slight bait-and-switch feel for anyone who clicks that nav link expecting this campaign.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add per-product "Shop this look" links directly under each price, not just two generic runway CTAs — this is the single highest-leverage fix since it converts diffuse interest into immediate clicks.
- 2. Add one line of context per hero product (e.g., "as seen on the London runway, now in stores") to justify price points that otherwise look arbitrary next to a $379 dress.
- 3. Swap the flat-lay boots shot for a styled/worn shot to keep visual consistency with the backstage aesthetic used everywhere else.
- 4. Move "Shop By" category grid higher for readers who bounce off the runway theme, or add a "New This Week" secondary module.
