- This is H&M's back-to-school pivot — "FIRST DAY FEVER" with a "work desk or school bench" hook, individually priced items, no discount code anywhere in the render. The one genuine offer signal — a "$9.99" badge — sits buried inside the kids' hula-hoop hero, not the adult hero, so most adult subscribers scrolling past the crop jacket ($49.99) and polo ($44.99) see zero price incentive.
- The kids/back-to-school section is the strongest, most legible block in the email — clear category buttons (GIRLS 2-8Y, BOYS 2-8Y, etc.) and a fun, on-brand hero shot. But the top two-thirds is generic lookbook grid + one hero model + product tiles, no CTA differentiation beyond a single "SHOP NOW" button. Compared to the 8/15–8/16 tiered-discount sends (6.00/10 each), this reads weaker on urgency and offer clarity, closer to the 8/17–8/18 editorial dip (5.00/10, 4.00/10).

## What's working

- Clear seasonal narrative ("FIRST DAY FEVER" / "A TIME TO GROW") that ties visuals to a recognizable back-to-school moment.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text is legible, grid alignment holds.
- Kids category buttons (GIRLS 2-8Y / BOYS 2-8Y / GIRLS 9-14Y / BOYS 9-14Y) give a concrete, low-friction next step for parent shoppers.
- Product tiles show name + price consistently, and one item is flagged "New Arrival."

## What's weak

- No sitewide or category discount anywhere in the adult section — every hero and tile item is full price.
- The only visible price cut ($9.99) is a small badge on a kids sweatshirt image, easy to miss and not called out in copy or subject.
- Single generic CTA ("SHOP NOW") repeated with no product- or category-specific link language.
- Subject line "Return to routine in style" gives no hint of the back-to-school/kids angle that's actually half the email.

## Recommendations

- 1. Surface the $9.99 kids price (or best available discount) in the subject/hero copy — it's the only real offer in this send and it's currently hidden.
- 2. Split CTAs by section instead of one repeated "SHOP NOW" — e.g., "Shop the Jacket," "Shop Kids' Back-to-School."
- 3. Add a visible price-off banner or code for the adult hero products if one exists, matching the clarity of the 8/15 "15% OR 20% OFF" send.
- 4. Move the kids back-to-school block higher — it has stronger urgency and specificity than the adult lookbook opener.

# Technical Email Audit — H&M "Return to routine in style"

**1. Technical Summary**
The email's link-tracking domain is returning HTTP 403 on every probed URL, breaking the entire click path (CTAs, header logo, all product images, and unsubscribe); authentication and unsubscribe headers are also unverifiable at the transport level.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- All 12 probed links resolve through the `t19.email.hm.com/r/?id=...` redirect/tracking domain and return **403 Forbidden**, including both "SHOP NOW" CTAs, both obfuscated "Shop now" text links (containing zero-width `‌` and BOM `﻿` characters embedded in anchor text), the "H&M" logo link, and 6 product image links (`cq5dam.web.1400.1400.jpeg`, `cq5dam.web.750.750.jpeg` variants).
- The Unsubscribe link itself also 403s: `...f7daea7c&did=1715351058&rid=3326930539...` — this is a compliance-relevant failure, not just a UX one (see §5).
- Since all failures share the same redirect host and timing pattern, this looks like a transient/environmental block (e.g., bot/UA filtering, IP throttling, or an expired preview-render session on `t19.email.hm.com`) rather than 12 independently broken destination URLs — worth re-probing before treating as a hard link-rot issue.
- Anchor text contains invisible Unicode characters (ZWNJ `\u200C` and BOM `\uFEFF`) on the "Shop now" links — not a rendering bug per se, but worth flagging as it can affect accessibility tooling and copy-paste behavior.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- 13 `<img>` tags are missing `alt` text, spanning the tracking pixel, all `cq5dam.web.*` campaign images, and the kids' product image (`kkmi263c01m`) instances (each appears twice, likely desktop/mobile duplicates). Screen readers will announce these as blank or fall back to filename/URL.
