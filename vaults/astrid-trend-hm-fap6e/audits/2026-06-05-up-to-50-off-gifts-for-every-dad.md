# Up to 50% off gifts for every dad
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- This is H&M's first hard-offer email in five days — following four straight editorial sends (June 1–4) with no discount. That cadence actually helps here: subscribers who sat through "summer soirée edit" and "must-have pieces for summer layering" without a price hook are primed for a real deal. The timing against Father's Day is well-executed, and the menswear product grid is clean and well-organized with fit descriptors and visible markdown pricing.
- The problem is the preheader. The preview text showing in-inbox is "NEED AN ITEM SOONER? BUY ONLINE, PICK UP IN-STORE" — pure operational BOPIS copy, completely disconnected from a 50% off Father's Day send. That's the most valuable real estate in the inbox, and it's wasted on a fulfillment reminder. Every other element is solid; this single miss drags the open rate.
- Honest verdict: a competent, timely promo that would perform meaningfully better with a single preheader fix and an explicit deadline. The "up to 50% off" framing and the wall of fine print at the bottom are the other strikes — they erode the trust that a clear "50% off all menswear" would build.

## What's working

- **Timing is earned.** Five days of editorial content means this discount lands without conditioning exhaustion — unlike the June 1 editorial that dropped right after the May 31 "last day" send.
- **Father's Day is specific and seasonal.** The "GIFTS FOR DAD" hook and June timing is credible urgency without artificial pressure.
- **Product grid uses fit language.** "Loose Fit," "Regular Fit," "Relaxed Fit" labels give shoppers a real signal before clicking — better than most H&M sends.
- **Markdown pricing is visible.** Crossed-out original prices alongside sale prices make the value tangible in the scroll.
- **Member giveaway adds a second action hook.** The $250 gift card + bundle draw for signed-in members gives non-buyers a reason to engage.

## What's weak

- **Preheader is dead weight.** "NEED AN ITEM SOONER? BUY ONLINE, PICK UP IN-STORE" is operational copy — it actively undermines the "50% off" subject line by signaling logistics, not deals.
- **"Up to 50% off" is the vaguest possible offer framing.** It could mean one $12 tee is half off. It trains the subscriber to expect disappointment.
- **No deadline visible anywhere.** Father's Day is presumably the implied end date, but the email never says it. That's a free urgency lever left on the table.
- **Primary CTA copy is generic.** "SHOP NOW" is the laziest possible button. "SHOP GIFTS" is better but still not pulling weight.
- **Fine print block at the bottom is enormous.** The sheer volume signals exclusions and erodes the offer's credibility before the subscriber can even check what's excluded.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the preheader** to actually support the subject line — this is the single highest-leverage fix. Something that echoes the offer and adds information the subject line doesn't already give.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline to the hero** — "Offer ends June 15" takes two seconds to insert and is worth several points of urgency.
- 3. **Replace "SHOP NOW" with something specific** — "Shop the Dad Edit" or "See All Sale Styles" turns a generic tap into a promise.
- 4. **Sharpen the offer claim.** "50% off select menswear" is more honest and less likely to generate bounce-back frustration than "up to 50%."
- 5. **Surface the giveaway higher** — or tighten the fine print block. The $250 gift card is a strong hook that's buried after the product grid.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Father's Day sale: 50% off menswear`
- **Subject Alt B:** `50% off the dad edit — ends June 15`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop the full menswear sale before Father's Day weekend`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Over 100 styles marked down — plus enter to win a $250 gift card`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS `@import` for web fonts will fail silently in most clients.**

```css
@import url(https://fabric.hmgroup.com/fabric-fonts/0/northstar/css/fonts.css);
@import url(https://s1-cdn.hm.com/global/hm-fonts/4.0.1/oces.css);
```

Gmail strips `@import` directives entirely. Outlook ignores them. iOS Mail and Apple Mail are the only major clients that honor `@import` inside `<style>`. Fallback system fonts will render instead. This is an accepted trade-off only if the design degrades gracefully — verify the fallback font stack is explicitly declared on body/td elements.

**Positive signals present:**
- `<meta name="x-apple-disable-message-reformatting"/>` — correct
- `<meta content="telephone=no" name="format-detection"/>` — correct
- `@media (max-width:699px)` responsive breakpoint with `is-hidden-mobile`/`is-hidden-desktop` classes — correct pattern

**Alt text:** Cannot audit — body content is truncated.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

Multiple fields in the JSON-LD block (`<script type="application/ld+json">`) appear to be unfilled template placeholders:

| Field | Value | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| `EmailMessage.subjectLine` | `""` | Empty — should match subject line |
| `DiscountOffer.description` | `""` | Empty |
| `DiscountOffer.discountCode` | `""` | Empty |
| `DiscountOffer.availabilityEnds` | `""` | No expiry set |
| `PromotionCard.image` | `""` | Empty |
| `PromotionCard.url` | `""` | Empty |

**Critical: stale hardcoded date.**
```json
"availabilityStarts":"2018-12-31T18:59:59-05:00"
```
This is a 2018 timestamp on a `DiscountOffer` object, almost certainly a template default that was never populated. Gmail uses this block to render promotional tab annotations and deal highlights. An `availabilityStarts` nearly 8 years in the past will cause Gmail to suppress or mispresent the annotation entirely.

**Minor:** All `@context` values use `http://schema.org/` (HTTP, not HTTPS). Google's structured data documentation recommends `https://schema.org/`.
