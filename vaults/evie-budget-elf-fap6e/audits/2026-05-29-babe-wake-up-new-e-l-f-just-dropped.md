# Babe wake up, new e.l.f. just dropped 🤩
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- This is e.l.f.'s new-product digest: at least five products in a vertical scroll format, covering Soft Glow Cream Blush, Soft Glow Cream Bronzer, Power Grip Boost Melting Spray, Main Stain Lip Marker, and Press 'N Blot Stick. The format is competent — clean product photography, prices visible, consistent layout — but the ambition stops there. "Babe wake up, new e.l.f. just dropped" gives away nothing specific, and for a subscriber who has already received six e.l.f. emails in six days (including the Main Stain Lip Marker's dedicated launch send on May 26), the novelty signal is exhausted before the email opens.
- The multi-product dump is e.l.f.'s weakest format. When everything is a hero, nothing is. Each product gets roughly the same visual weight, so the eye wanders without landing on a reason to click. Compare this to the May 27 Sheer For It shade-expansion send — a single product, named swatches, one clear action — which earned a 9. This email does the same work across five products and dilutes itself proportionally.
- Bottom line: competently rendered, fatally unfocused. The subject line is a wasted opportunity to name the one product worth waking up for.

## What's working

- Hero headline "drop e.l.f. everything, new makeup is here!" lands the brand pun cleanly without forcing it.
- Products are uniformly priced $7–$11 — the affordability cue is visible at a glance, which is core to e.l.f.'s identity.
- Multiple "SHOP NOW" buttons are unambiguous; no mystery CTAs or vague "Discover" copy.
- Model imagery is diverse and current — consistent with the brand's demographic positioning.
- No visible render bugs; layout is clean on the rendered width.

## What's weak

- Subject names zero products — six days into a promotional blitz, "new e.l.f. just dropped" blends into inbox noise.
- Main Stain Lip Marker was already the subject of a dedicated launch email three days ago (May 26, $5 price hero, 8-shade grid) — surfacing it again here reads as content recycling, not newness.
- No sale, discount, or urgency mechanism anywhere in the visible email — all products appear at full price, with no deadline, the day after a flash-sale cadence that primed subscribers to wait for deals.
- Five products competing for equal space means none of them win; there's no clear "this is the one thing" moment.
- No social proof or early reviews — for products genuinely just launched, even a "new arrival" badge or a waitlist signal would add credibility.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Crown one product.** Pick the most scroll-stopping new SKU — probably Soft Glow Cream Blush or the Melting Spray — give it a full-width hero, and relegate the others to a "also new" secondary strip. One decision at a time is easier to act on.
- 2. **Put the product name in the subject.** "Babe wake up" is a great hook; waste the next 30 characters on the name, not on "new e.l.f. just dropped."
- 3. **Hold the Main Stain.** It had its moment on May 26. Reappearing three days later in a digest undercuts both sends and signals content drought.
- 4. **Add a credibility signal.** "Just launched," "bestseller incoming," a waitlist count — anything that makes "just dropped" feel more than copywriter flair.
- 5. **Reconsider the cadence.** Six emails in six days from the same brand is the fastest path to a mass unsubscribe. If the new products all launched simultaneously, batch them. If they didn't, space the sends.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` tag is empty:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers and some preview pane renderers use the title; leaving it blank degrades accessibility.

**Logo anchor has `tabindex="-1"`:**
```html
<a href="..." tabindex="-1">
```
Explicitly removes the logo link from keyboard navigation. This is intentional in some email templates (to prevent tab-focus on decorative links) but should be confirmed as deliberate UX.

**`<style>` block embedded inside body content (block-3 `<div>`):**
```html
<div style="..."><style type="text/css"> ... </style>
```
Inline `<style>` inside a content `<div>` is invalid HTML. Gmail strips `<style>` from body in some contexts; the `.responsive-text` mobile breakpoint rule may be silently dropped, locking font size at 16px on all devices.

**Nested `nl-container` table duplication in block-1:** The outer `nl-container` wraps an inner `nl-container` with identical structure. This appears to be a template assembly artifact (two wrapper layers concatenated). It adds markup weight and could cause double-rendering of padding in some clients.
