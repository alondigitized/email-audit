# Dead tired? Take off your makeup first 👀
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-29**
## Executive summary

- This is a skincare-focused send that leads with a clear, on-theme Halloween pun. A pumpkin wearing a towel turban surrounded by Holy Hydration products anchors the hero, and "sleeping in makeup is e.l.f.ing spooky" lands the message fast. The subject ("Dead tired? Take off your makeup first 👀") matches the creative, and the Makeup Melting Cleansing Balm at $12 is the one product that gets a full spotlight.
- The structure is a repeat of the "stack of black panels" pattern, though. The balm is pitched twice, once in the hero and again in a freckled-face panel with the price. The gift-with-purchase offer ($50+, "no code needed") competes for attention as a third message. The category bars at the bottom are bare gray strips and look unfinished.
- Overall this is a decent, focused send but not as sharp as the Oct 28 "scary good lippie prices" email. The theme is strong, but the offer is split between a $12 product price and a $50 threshold gift, and the hero never shows the balm itself.

## What's working

- The pumpkin-with-towel hero is a memorable, on-season image that ties skincare to Halloween and fits the pun headline.
- The headline "sleeping in makeup is e.l.f.ing spooky." is short, readable white-on-black type with clear hierarchy.
- The balm gets an explicit price ($12) with a plain benefit line ("balm-to-oil formula that melts away makeup").
- The "Top Picks for You" row shows three products with prices ($8, $11, $13) and individual Shop Now buttons.
- The footer is complete and trust-building, with a free shipping callout, loyalty and receipt scanning, and app download links.

## What's weak

- The hero image doesn't show the featured balm, so the product being sold only appears by name in the copy.
- The balm is promoted twice back to back, with two near-identical "Shop Now" buttons, which wastes the second panel.
- The gift-with-purchase banner ("limited time! these gifts are giving") is small and its prize is not shown, so it reads as filler.
- The six category bars (New, Eyes, Lips, Face, Skincare, Hair) are blank gray rectangles with no imagery, and they look like a rendering gap.
- The "Top Picks for You" product names are tiny and the buttons are small, hard to tap on mobile.
- The fine print is dense and the $50 and November 1 terms are buried at the very bottom.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace the second balm panel with a product shot of the balm (or a melt demo), and drop the duplicate CTA. The reader should see what they're buying.
- 2. Put the $12 price in the hero so the offer is visible at the top.
- 3. Give the gift-with-purchase block a visible product image and the exact threshold in the headline.
- 4. Fill the category bars with imagery or remove them.
- 5. Make the Top Picks relevant to cleansing (balm, cleanser, toner) rather than an unrelated mix.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML is structurally sound, using table layout, `role="presentation"`, `lang="en"` and a preheader. However, the QA run could not verify the final landing page for any of the 4 failing CTA links, and there are several header and metadata gaps. The pass rate was 27% (4 failures, 4 warnings), and only 12 of 41 links were probed.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **4 probes failed with `unknown url type`.** Each tracked `links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/...` link resolved to a relative path with no scheme or host:
  - `/products/holy-hydration-eye-cream?Size=Regular+0.53+oz+%2F+15+g` ("Shop Now")
  - `/products/holy-hydration-thirst-burst-drops?Size=Regular+1+oz+%2F+30+ml` ("Shop Now")
  - `/account/login?return_to=%2Faccount` ("Receipt scanning - Earn rewards anywhere you shop e.l.f.")
  - `/pages/mobile-app?af_reengagement_window=30d&bxid=6a013826e53882007d6a1618&utm_medium=email&pid=Email&source_caller=ui&utm_source=b...` ("beauty squad - Download the e.l.f. app")
- **Likely cause:** the click tracker returned a relative `Location` header, or the probe failed to join it to the base URL. Browsers resolve relative redirects, so this may be a probe limitation rather than a real break. It is still unconfirmed that these links reach live pages.
- **Login wall:** the receipt-scanning CTA lands on `/account/login?return_to=%2Faccount`, not on rewards content. Logged-out recipients hit a login form.
- **Link coverage:** 29 links were skipped, so the footer, social and utility links are unverified.
- **Shopify URL:** the `?Size=Regular+...` variant parameter is passed as a human-readable string. This is fragile if the option label changes.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Empty title:** `<title></title>` is empty.
- **Missing alt text:** one image has no alt text. Its src is `https://links.elfcosmetics.com/q/-jYthejealVu_WWQYsAg8Q~~/AAQRxRA~/Jj98db57...`. The `/q/` path may be a tracking pixel, which would be harmless, but the QA check doesn't say.
- **Invalid font-family:** the `font-family:'',Verdana,Sans-serif` declaration has an empty quoted family. It falls through to Verdana.
- **Empty block:** the `mobile_hide` `html_block` contains only an empty `<div>`, leaving a dead nested table.
- **Nested duplicate structure:** nested `.nl-container` and `.row-1` classes are reused inside `html_block` wrappers. This is harmless but bloats the markup.
- **Web font:** the Google Fonts `<link>` is wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`, which is correct. Clients that strip `<link>` fall back to Arial.
- **Other checks:** the hero image has `alt="e.l.f."`. It is a brand name, not a description of the image content, and the hero links to a tracked URL. The preheader uses `display:none` with `mso-hide:all`.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The truncated source shows no unrendered tokens such as `{{ }}`, `%%`, or `[FIRST_NAME]`. The latter part of the HTML was truncated, so this applies only to the visible portion.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe** and **List-Unsubscribe-Post** (RFC 8058) were not found. The QA note says AgentMail may strip these, so treat this as unconfirmed. If they are really absent, that conflicts with Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements.
- **Authentication-Results** was not found, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment are unknown. The sender is `m.elfcosmetics.com` and click-tracking runs on `links.elfcosmetics.com`, which is a different subdomain. This is not necessarily a problem, but alignment couldn't be checked.
- **Footer:** the visible HTML doesn't include the footer, so the in-body unsubscribe link and physical postal address can't be confirmed. The QA note says an unsubscribe link is always probed, but no result is shown for it.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **App link:** this link carries `utm_medium=email` and `utm_source=b...` (cut off in the log), plus `af_reengagement_window`, `pid=Email` and `bxid`. This is consistent AppsFlyer and Braze attribution.
- **Product links:** the product paths show no visible UTM parameters, only `Size=`. Attribution may be appended by the tracker at redirect time. This couldn't be verified because the probes failed.
- **Landing alignment:** unverifiable for the 4 failed links, since no final URL or status code was captured.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Re-probe the 4 failing links, resolving the relative `Location` against the final host (probably `www.elfcosmetics.com`). Record the final URL, status code and UTM parameters.
2. Confirm that the `/account/login` rewards CTA is intended to be a login gate. If not, link directly to the rewards page.
3. Fill in `<title>`. Add alt text to the `/q/` image, or `alt=""` if it is decorative. Remove the empty `font-family:''` and the empty `mobile_hide` block.
4. Check the raw message headers outside the relay for List-Unsubscribe, List-Unsubscribe-Post, SPF, DKIM and DMARC.
5. Verify the footer unsubscribe link and postal address in the full, untruncated HTML.
6. Raise the probe budget, or add footer and unsubscribe links to the probed set, so the 29 skipped links are covered.
7. The plain-text part is 85% URLs (12,082 of 14,092 characters) because of long tracking links. Shorter tracking tokens or more descriptive text would improve the text-to-link ratio.
