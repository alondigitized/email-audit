# NEW! Be so blurreal ☁️💗 
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- e.l.f. is launching the "Blurreal Lip & Cheek Whip" — a new diffused-color product exclusive to elfcosmetics.com and Target — and this email earns its send by doing something the last two lip emails didn't: it introduces a genuinely dual-use product and shows you exactly how to use it. The "2 Ways to Whip It" format is smart tutorial content, not just product promotion. The hero image, wordplay, and step-by-step application shots all land together.
- The weak spot is urgency and pricing clarity. The hero body copy ends with what reads like an orphaned "10." — presumably "$10," but it's illegible enough at render size to feel like a bug. There's no deadline, no discount, and "SHOP NOW" appears three times without ever telling you what you're shopping for by name. After yesterday's "$15 lip combos" send, a subscriber who hasn't clicked on lips all week might feel like this is more of the same, even though the product is meaningfully different.
- Net judgment: a solid new-launch email. The creative concept is e.l.f.'s strongest in the current run, but it leaves a click on the table by not anchoring the price clearly in the hero and giving the CTA zero specificity.

## What's working

- **"Be Blurreal" wordplay is immediately legible** — blur + surreal maps directly to the diffused-finish product story without needing explanation.
- **"2 Ways to Whip It" is rare and useful** — showing separate lip and cheek application in the same email turns a new-product launch into a mini tutorial.
- **Target exclusivity is a credibility signal** — "only at elfcosmetics.com & Target" positions this as a genuine launch, not a clearance push.
- **"Top Picks for You" personalized rail** — the $8–$12 product row at the bottom keeps the email useful after the hero CTA has been considered.
- **No render issues** — all images load, hierarchy is clean from hero to category nav to footer.

## What's weak

- **Price is buried or broken in the hero** — the body copy ends with "10." which reads like a truncated "$10" or a rendering artifact; either way it doesn't clearly communicate the price at a glance.
- **"SHOP NOW" three times, zero specificity** — the CTA never says "Shop the Blurreal Whip" or "Get the Lip & Cheek Whip"; a subscriber who missed the headline won't know what they're clicking toward.
- **No urgency mechanism** — the exclusive launch angle is the natural urgency hook ("available now" / "limited first drop") but it's completely unused; the email reads like this product will be there forever.
- **Back-to-back lip territory** — July 7 was also a lip email; while the Blurreal Whip is a new product, the visual palette (close-up pink lip shots) looks nearly identical in the inbox and risks being skipped by anyone in lip-click fatigue.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the price in the hero** — if the product is $10, say "$10" clearly in the headline or as a price callout below the product name. This is e.l.f.'s sharpest competitive weapon; burying it or breaking the render loses the whole story.
- 2. **Make the hero CTA specific** — swap "SHOP NOW" for "Shop the Blurreal Whip" or "Get the $10 Blurreal Whip" so it functions independently of the headline for skim-readers.
- 3. **Add a launch-urgency hook** — "Just dropped. Shop before it sells out." or an "In stores now at Target" datestamp would transform this from a browse email into an action email.
- 4. **Differentiate the hero image from the July 7 send** — the cheek application shot is the creative point of difference; lead with it (or split-test it as hero) since lips-only imagery blends into yesterday's email.
- **Subject Alt A:** `NEW $10 Blurreal Lip & Cheek Whip ☁️`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Just dropped: blur your lips AND cheeks for $10`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Swipe on lips. Dot on cheeks. One product, two blurreal looks.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Exclusive launch at Target + elfcosmetics.com — shop the Blurreal Whip`

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard Braze-delivered table-based email built with a BEE-editor structure. The truncated source prevents full compliance footer audit, but several rendering and tracking issues are evident in the visible sections.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Braze click-wrapper present but UTM params unverifiable pre-redirect.**
All links route through `links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/...` (Braze encoded redirect). UTM parameters, if any, are embedded inside the encoded payload — they cannot be inspected or validated without following the redirect chain. No raw UTM params (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, etc.) are visible in the href before encoding.

**Image asset hosted on Braze CDN with timestamp token:**
```
https://braze-images.com/appboy/communication/assets/.../original.png?1697823889
```
The `?1697823889` cache-buster timestamp dates to October 2023. This is cosmetically stale but functionally fine as long as the asset remains hosted.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` element:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Several email clients (notably Outlook on Windows) render the `<title>` value as the window/tab label. Empty title degrades the experience in those clients.

**`tabindex="-1"` on primary logo link:**
```html
<a href="https://links.elfcosmetics.com/..." tabindex="-1">
```
This explicitly removes the element from keyboard tab order. Keyboard-only users cannot navigate to or activate the logo link. This fails WCAG 2.1 SC 2.1.1 (Keyboard).

**Global link style override strips all link affordances:**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This rule, targeting Gmail's message wrapper, removes underlines and color from all links in Gmail. Any inline-styled text links (not buttons) in the body will be visually indistinguishable from body text in Gmail for iOS/Android.

**Nested `nl-container` tables (double-wrap):**
The outer `nl-container` contains an `html_block` which injects a *second* `nl-container` with its own full `row-1` structure. This creates unnecessary DOM nesting depth and could cause unexpected width calculation in older Outlook versions that interpret nested table widths differently.

**Image `alt` text is minimal:**
```html
alt="e.l.f."
```
The logo image is the primary content block. Alt text of `"e.l.f."` is technically present (not empty), so it passes the hard fail case, but it does not convey the email's offer ("So Blurreal Lip & Cheek Whip") for image-off rendering.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No Liquid/Braze merge tags are visible in the truncated section. The preheader is hardcoded:
```html
So Blurreal Lip &amp; Cheek Whip. $8.
```
No subscriber-name personalization, segment-conditional blocks, or dynamic content tokens are observable. The Braze link URL does contain what appears to be a per-recipient encoded payload (the `~~/AAQRxRA~/` segment is characteristic of Braze's per-user link tracking), so click attribution is individualized even without explicit merge tags in visible markup.

**Truncation caveat:** Product recommendation blocks or dynamic content lower in the email cannot be assessed.

---

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**Cannot fully audit — HTML is truncated.** The footer (physical mailing address, unsubscribe link) is not present in the provided source. These are required under CAN-SPAM §5. A full audit requires the complete HTML.

**What can be verified from headers/domain:**
- Sending domain `m.elfcosmetics.com` is a subdomain — DKIM alignment and SPF pass/fail cannot be determined from HTML alone; must be checked against received headers.
- `lang="en"` is correctly set on `<html>` — passes basic i18n/accessibility requirement.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

UTM parameters are not inspectable pre-redirect (see §2). The risk is that if Braze's redirect appends UTMs, they are present; if not configured, all clicks will appear as direct traffic in analytics rather than email-attributed.

**To verify:** Follow the encoded link and inspect the final destination URL for `utm_source=email&utm_medium=...` or equivalent. If absent, all click attribution will be lost in GA4/site analytics.
