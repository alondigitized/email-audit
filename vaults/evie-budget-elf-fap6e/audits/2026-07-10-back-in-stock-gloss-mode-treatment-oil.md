# Back in stock! ✨💛 Gloss Mode Treatment Oil
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- This is a restock alert for e.l.f.'s Gloss Mode Treatment Oil ($10), sent from the "e.l.f. Hair" sender address — a segmented sub-list, not the main makeup blast. The trigger is legitimate and inherently high-signal: subscribers who missed the first run have been waiting, and "back in stock" subject lines outperform standard promos by a wide margin. The mustard-yellow hero with the curly-haired model holding the bottle is visually confident and personality-forward, and "$10" lands in the body copy without requiring a scroll. That's a strong above-the-fold package.
- Below the fold, the email falls apart. The "Top Picks for You" personalization module fires face products — a foundation and pore products — inside a dedicated hair email. That's a segmentation failure, not a minor nitpick; it tells the reader the brand doesn't actually know what list they're on. Worse, the body copy says "Just launched" while the subject says "Back in stock!" — these are contradictory frames that would confuse any first-time buyer trying to understand the product's history. And the GWP banner (Hottest Drops Duo) is a copy-paste carryover from the July 9 skincare send with no hair-specific angle.
- Net judgment: the above-fold is a B+, the below-fold drags it to a B-. Fix the face recommendations, reconcile the "new" vs. "back in stock" copy, and this is a strong restock send.

## What's working

- Restock trigger is inherently high-intent — back-in-stock emails hit subscribers who already showed purchase intent; the mechanic earns the send
- Mustard-yellow hero with curly-haired model holding the yellow bottle creates instant product association and strong visual personality
- "$10" price surfaces in the body hero without a scroll — no friction for the impulse decision
- "Everyone's obsessed with this glossy styling oil" is light social proof that converts without feeling forced
- "*While supplies last" scarcity disclosed honestly directly under the price

## What's weak

- "Just launched" body copy directly contradicts "Back in stock!" in the subject — the product cannot be both new and restocked, and first-time openers will notice
- "Top Picks for You" module surfaces face products (foundation, pore putty, pore grip setting spray) inside a segmented hair email — category mismatch undermines the focused hair story
- GWP banner (Hottest Drops Duo, use code BURST) appears to be a sitewide carryover from the July 9 skincare send — no hair-category relevance
- "Meet the mane crew" product lineup is decorative, not shoppable — no individual product names or prices visible on any of the bottles
- Both primary CTAs read "Shop Now" — generic verb, no product specificity

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the new vs. back-in-stock contradiction**: Hero body should read "Back in stock — and going fast." Drop "Just launched" entirely; the email was positioned as a restock alert, own that.
- 2. **Swap face Top Picks for hair recommendations**: Cross-sell within category — scalp treatment, curl cream, heat protectant. Face products in a hair email signal a broken personalization engine.
- 3. **Make "meet the mane crew" shoppable**: Add product names and prices under each bottle in the lineup. A family-shot with no prices is a conversion dead-end.
- 4. **Sharpen the hero CTA**: "Shop Gloss Mode Oil — $10" beats "Shop Now" every time; specificity reduces hesitation.
- 5. **Swap or cut the GWP banner**: If Hottest Drops Duo is sitewide, either remove it or replace with a hair-relevant bundle offer to preserve the email's single-category focus.

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is built in Braze with standard MSO/table layout. Two confirmed rendering defects: a double-nested container structure (builder artifact) and a 9px mobile heading — both are code-level bugs, not design choices.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Click-tracking domain:** All hrefs route through `links.elfcosmetics.com` (Braze click-tracking). Expected, but the redirect chain should be validated end-to-end for HTTPS-only hops and correct final destination.

**Image asset age:** The hero logo is served from:
```
https://braze-images.com/appboy/communication/assets/image_assets/images/6532bc91543698004cb67f6e/original.png?1697823889
```
The query-string timestamp `1697823889` decodes to **October 2023** — a recycled asset. Verify this is intentional for a "back in stock" email that may have product-specific imagery requirements.

**UTM parameters:** Not visible in the pre-redirect href strings in the provided source. Confirm UTMs are appended post-redirect or injected by Braze; they are not present in the raw source links.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — 9px mobile heading:**
```css
@media (max-width:660px) {
  .row-6 .column-1 .block-1.heading_block h1 { font-size: 9px !important }
}
```
9px is below the WCAG minimum (~14px) and practically unreadable on any mobile device. This appears to be an accidental override, not intentional sizing.

**Double-nested container (builder artifact):** The `html_block block-1` div contains a complete duplicate `nl-container → row-1 → row-content stack` structure, wrapping the logo inside a second full email skeleton. This redundant nesting can cause box-model issues in Outlook and Gmail:
```html
<table class="html_block block-1" ...>
  <tr><td class="pad"><div ...>
    <!-- Entire nl-container/row-1/row-content structure re-declared here -->
    <table class="nl-container" ...>  ← duplicate root
```

**`tabindex="-1"` on logo link:**
```html
<a href="..." tabindex="-1">
```
Removes the logo link from keyboard tab order entirely. Screen-reader and keyboard-only users cannot navigate to it.

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Yahoo Mail and some webmail clients display this in tab labels. Should be set to the subject line or brand name.

**Google Fonts reliability:** `Jost` and `Open Sans` are loaded via `<link>` tags (inside `<!--[if !mso]>` conditional). Gmail strips external `<link>` tags — Jost has no declared fallback in the visible source (only `Open Sans, Arial, Sans-serif` appears in the div styles). If Jost is used for body copy elsewhere in the template, those elements will fall through to Arial in Gmail without a matching stack.
