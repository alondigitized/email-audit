# Leveled-up lip combos for only $20 💋
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- This is a curation play, not a launch play — and that's exactly the right move one day after the June 30 "NEW drops make lips & cheeks pop" send. Instead of pushing more new arrivals, e.l.f. packages existing lip products into two named, priced combos ($20) and puts a creator face on them. The @elitheartist Global Makeup Artist framing gives the editorial angle a human anchor, which is smarter than another "here are products" grid.
- The $20 price point is the clearest signal in the email. It's in the headline, it's the whole strategic premise, and it lands without clutter. Two named combos — Berry Iconic and Keep It Mauve-Prs — give the subscriber a choice without overwhelming them. That structure is tight.
- Where this wobbles: the "Top Picks For You" section at the bottom is just category nav links (NEW / EYES / LIPS / FACE / SKINCARE / HAIR), not actual personalized recommendations. It signals personalization without delivering it, which is a small trust leak. And there's no urgency mechanism — no deadline, no quantity signal — so the $20 offer floats without a reason to act now.

## What's working

- **$20 hero pricing is immediate and specific** — visible in the first screenful, no ambiguity about what you're getting.
- **Two named combos create a low-friction choice** — Berry vs. Mauve lets the subscriber self-select rather than face a blank product grid.
- **Creator attribution (@elitheartist, e.l.f. Global Makeup Artist) adds legitimacy** — the combo curation feels expert, not algorithmic.
- **"CHOOSE & GET IT" CTA is action-oriented** — more specific than "Shop Now" on the primary offer.
- **Clean render, no visible layout breaks** — the email looks exactly as intended on the webview.

## What's weak

- **No urgency mechanism** — no deadline, no stock signal, no "today only." The $20 offer just exists indefinitely, which dulls the impulse.
- **"Top Picks For You" is fake personalization** — those are category nav tabs, not curated picks. Sharp subscribers notice.
- **$20 pricing needs clarification** — it's unclear at a glance whether $20 buys one combo or both; the individual SHOP NOW buttons under each combo imply separate purchases.
- **Back-to-back lip territory from June 30** — the angle is different enough (combos vs. launches) to justify it, but a subscriber who sees both may feel like e.l.f. is hammering lips this week.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline or scarcity signal** — "Offer ends July 4" or "Limited combo run" turns a browseable promo into a reason to act now. This single addition would lift click-through more than any copy tweak.
- 2. **Clarify the combo price structure** — add a "$20 each" or "$20 per combo" callout directly under each combo header so there's zero ambiguity.
- 3. **Replace the fake "Top Picks For You" nav** — surface 3–4 actual recommended products based on past browse/purchase behavior, or drop the section entirely.
- 4. **Rewrite the subheadline** — "A sheer-tint, glossy lip oil & NEW: the pixel lip pencil in shades curated by our e.l.f. pros" is cramped and trails off. Tighten to: "Two pro-built looks. One $20 combo. Choose your shade."

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**No issues with redirect domain itself** — `links.elfcosmetics.com` is a dedicated subdomain, not shared IP, which is correct practice.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Nested duplicate `nl-container` table structure (high confidence bug):**
The outer layout wraps `row-1 > html_block block-1`, and inside that block the builder emitted a *complete second* `nl-container` table with its own `row-1 > row-content > column-1 > image_block`. This double-scaffold is a builder artifact and adds redundant nesting. In Outlook (MSO Word renderer), extra `<table>` nesting can produce unexpected cell padding or column width collapse.

```html
<!-- outer scaffold -->
<table class="nl-container">...<td class="column column-1">
  <table class="html_block block-1">
    <div>
      <!-- inner scaffold — this is the problem -->
      <table class="nl-container" width="100%">...<table class="image_block">
```

**Empty content blocks (blocks 2, 3, 4):** Three `html_block` rows contain only whitespace `<div>` wrappers with no content. Each adds a full table row to the DOM. Negligible on rendering but inflates raw HTML size and is a builder hygiene issue.

**Google Fonts `<link>` in `<head>`:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" rel="stylesheet">
```
External stylesheet loading is blocked in Gmail (web/Android), Outlook (all), and Apple Mail with Mail Privacy Protection active. The fallback chain `Open Sans, Arial, Sans-serif` renders fine, but the `<link>` tag itself is wasted bytes in most clients.

**Keyboard / focus accessibility:**
```html
<a ... style="outline:none" tabindex="-1">
  <img src="..." alt="e.l.f.">
</a>
```
Both `outline:none` and `tabindex="-1"` are set on the hero logo link. `tabindex="-1"` removes the element from the tab order entirely; `outline:none` removes the focus ring. Together these make the logo unfocusable by keyboard. Acceptable for a decorative logo only if the link destination is reachable another way — but if this is the only nav path to the homepage, it fails WCAG 2.1 SC 2.1.1.

**`alt` text on logo image:** `alt="e.l.f."` — minimal but technically sufficient for a logo.

**`<title>` is empty:** `<title></title>`. Some clients (notably Apple Mail) surface the title tag. Should be set to the subject line or a meaningful value.

**`#MessageViewBody a` global override:**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This suppresses Gmail's blue auto-link styling but also strips all default link affordances email-wide. Every CTA and inline link in the body must have explicit inline `color` and `text-decoration` styles or they will be invisible as links. Confirm all downstream CTA buttons and text links have inline style declarations.
