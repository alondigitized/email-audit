# NEW! Power Grip Rosé Setting Spray 🌹
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a new-product-launch email for the Power Grip Rosé Setting Spray, and e.l.f. knows how to launch a product. The hero pun — "rosé all day." — is their sharpest creative in this batch: it ties the product name directly to a cultural shorthand that their audience lives in, and the pink spray bottle renders beautifully against a clean background. The "e.l.f.escent ingredients" section is another standout — effervescent + e.l.f. is exactly the kind of wordplay this brand earns.
- The problem is what comes after the launch. A "more lash, less cash" mascara cross-sell section appears roughly halfway down — and that is the verbatim tagline and creative beat from the May 15 email. Three days later, it reads as copy-paste filler, not curation. The Target rewards section below that adds a third agenda to an email that should be singularly focused on a product debut.
- Clear judgement: strong launch, undercut by a recycled cross-sell and an unfocused lower half. This could have been a 9; it lands at a 7.

## What's working

- "Rosé all day." is a perfect launch headline — pun, product, and cultural moment in three words.
- "e.l.f.escent ingredients" is legitimately clever brand wordplay; it makes you stop.
- "Grip it & spritz it." continues the voice consistency with a recognizable cultural riff.
- Hero product photography is clean and color-accurate — the rose-tinted spray bottle pops.
- Multiple "SHOP NOW" / "SHOP IT" CTAs are consistently styled and unambiguous throughout.

## What's weak

- "More lash, less cash." cross-sell section is lifted almost verbatim from the May 15 mascara email — it reads as recycled filler within the same week's cadence.
- The Target rewards section mid-email breaks the product-launch momentum with a different agenda entirely.
- No price is legible in the hero at a glance — the cost of the spray requires closer reading or scrolling to the body copy.
- No time pressure or launch urgency ("available for a limited time," "just dropped," etc.) makes "NEW!" feel static.
- The email is long — four distinct sections before footer — which dilutes the launch focus.

- 1. **Cut the mascara cross-sell entirely.** If you sent that email three days ago, you don't re-run the same creative block this week. Replace it with a secondary spray use-case shot or a before/after skin-finish visual.

- 3. **Add launch urgency.** "Just dropped" or "first 48 hours" framing makes the NEW! signal mean something beyond a badge.
- 4. **Collapse or remove the Target section.** If it must exist, push it to post-footer or a dedicated send. It competes with the launch.
- 5. **Preheader should complement, not mirror, the subject.** If the preheader echoes "new setting spray," rewrite it to lead with the finish or the feeling.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag.** `<title></title>` is blank. Outlook and some webmail clients surface this as the window/tab label. Low severity, but should contain the subject line or brand name.

**`tabindex="-1"` on the logo link.** The anchor wrapping the logo image sets `tabindex="-1"`, removing it from keyboard tab order:
```html
<a href="https://links.elfcosmetics.com/..." tabindex="-1">
```
This is incorrect for a navigable link. Keyboard-only users cannot reach it. Should be removed or set to `tabindex="0"`.

**9px heading on mobile.** The media query includes:
```css
.row-6 .column-1 .block-1.heading_block h1 { font-size: 9px !important }
```
9px is well below the WCAG 2.1 minimum recommended text size (~14px) and Apple's 11px floor for legibility. This will be effectively unreadable on mobile and may trigger accessibility flags.

**Nested duplicate `nl-container` structure.** Inside the outer `html_block` elements, the source contains a full inner `nl-container` table with its own `row row-1` / `row-content stack` hierarchy — this is a double-wrapping pattern that can cause fixed-width overflow or collapsed columns in Outlook and older Android Gmail clients.

**Google Fonts loaded unconditionally for non-MSO clients.** Two separate `<link>` calls load `Open Sans` and `Jost` via `fonts.googleapis.com`. This is behind the `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <!--<![endif]-->` conditional, so Outlook is excluded correctly. Fallback stack (`Arial, Sans-serif`) is present. No issue, but two separate font requests could be consolidated into one (`family=Open+Sans|Jost`) to reduce render-blocking round trips.
