# NEW drops make lips & cheeks pop 💋💥
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- This is a tight, confident new-launch email that does the basics well. e.l.f. pivots back to its core lip-and-face territory after the June 22–27 hair product blitz, and the category shift is immediately legible: glossy-lipped model, stacked shimmer tubes, "lips & cheeks pop" in big type. The dual-product structure — Pout-Clout Lip Plumping Pen ($8) and So Blurred Lip & Cheek Whip ($8) — is smart family-building, but it introduces a naming wrinkle worth flagging.
- The "So Blurred Lip & Cheek Whip" in this send is almost certainly the same product (or a shimmer variant) of the "So Blurreal Lip & Cheek Whip" from June 25. Five days after a dedicated launch email, re-featuring a product without clearly signaling it's a new colorway rather than the same SKU creates quiet subscriber confusion. If these are shimmer additions to the Blurreal line, the email doesn't say that loudly enough; the subheadline handles the shimmer angle but doesn't call it a "new shade drop" of a known product.
- Overall: clean, on-brand, appropriately energetic. The $8 price point is strong value signaling but there's no discount urgency, no time bound, and no member hook — which caps click motivation for anyone who isn't already sold on the products.

## What's working

- **Category reset is well-timed.** After six consecutive hair sends, returning to lips & cheeks feels fresh, not fatigued. The subject lands without feeling like a repeat.
- **Hero layout is effective.** Stacked product tubes plus model with glossy lips — two visual cues that reinforce each other. Eye goes product → face → headline, which is the right order.
- **"Shimmery shades" subheadline earns its keep.** It adds tactile, finish-specific detail the subject doesn't say, and "2-in-1" communicates versatility without burying the reader.
- **$8 price point is visible in the body.** Showing the price directly removes a decision barrier and signals accessibility — core to e.l.f.'s brand promise.
- **Bottom trust row is well-populated.** Free shipping threshold, 30-day returns, Afterpay, cruelty-free/vegan — all visible without scrolling far, and all friction-reducers.

## What's weak

- **"So Blurred" vs "So Blurreal" naming collision.** The June 25 send introduced "So Blurreal." This email says "So Blurred." Without a "new shimmer shades of your fave..." frame, subscribers either think it's the same product (why open?) or a different product (what is it?).
- **No urgency mechanism anywhere.** "NEW" is a signal, not a deadline. No "for a limited time," no "launching today," no countdown — click motivation relies entirely on impulse and product appeal.
- **"Top Picks For You" section is too small to read.** At mobile render width the four product thumbnails are decorative noise. If this is personalized, the render doesn't communicate it — captions are illegible at this size.
- **"SHOP NEW ARRIVALS" CTA is slightly generic.** For a dual-product launch email, "Shop New Drops" or "Shop Shimmer Shades" would sharpen the connection to what was just described.
- **No social proof.** No star ratings, no "bestseller" badge, no "X sold" — and e.l.f. typically has strong review counts they could surface here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Clarify the "So Blurred" naming in body copy.** Add one line: "NEW shimmer shades of your fave So Blurreal — now in three new finishes" (or whatever the actual distinction is). This turns a confusion point into a product story.
- 2. **Add urgency to at least one CTA.** "Shop Before They Sell Out" or "Limited Shades — Shop Now" gives hesitant clickers a reason to act today vs. saving for later and forgetting.
- 3. **Make "Top Picks For You" larger or cut it.** Either increase the tile size so the personalized products are actually legible, or replace with a single featured bestseller that earns real estate.
- 4. **Tighten the primary CTA copy.** "SHOP NEW ARRIVALS" is generic across all e.l.f. emails. "SHOP SHIMMER DROPS" or "SHOP LIP + CHEEK" makes it specific to this email.
- 5. **Add a star rating or review count** under at least the Pout-Clout Pen. Even "⭐ 4.7 (2.1k reviews)" next to the $8 price dramatically increases click intent.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag.**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some email clients (Outlook Web, Apple Mail) render the `<title>` value in tab labels or preview UX. An empty title is a minor but avoidable gap.

**Nested `nl-container` tables (likely template duplication).**
The outer email shell opens `<table class="nl-container">`, and `html_block block-1` immediately opens a second `<table class="nl-container">` inside it. This double-nesting suggests a content block was copied with its wrapper intact, producing redundant layout scaffolding. While most clients render through it, it adds ~800 bytes of unnecessary markup and can cause MSO rendering quirks.

**Three consecutive empty html_blocks (block-2, block-3, block-4).**
```html
<table class="html_block block-2">...<div>&#160;</div>...
<table class="html_block block-3">...<div>&#160;</div>...
<table class="html_block block-4">...<div>&#160;</div>...
```
These are placeholder blocks with only a non-breaking space. No content, no visible output. They add parse weight and should be removed before send.

**`alt` text is present but minimal.** Logo image has `alt="e.l.f."` — acceptable, not a defect.

**Google Fonts conditional load is correct:**
```html
<!--[if !mso]><!--><link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" ...><!--<![endif]-->
```
Standard MSO exclusion pattern — no issue.
