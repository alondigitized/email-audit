# Skingredients to help create a bouncy, plump look 💫
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, ingredient-led single-product launch for the **Glass Slick Serum Stick** — e.l.f. SKIN's newest entry. The hero does its job: product shot against an icy blue backdrop, punchy headline ("BOUNCE-BOOSTING SKINGREDIENTS."), a secondary benefit breakdown ("nourishing peptide blend" → three bullet claims), and two "SHOP NOW" buttons. The layout is competent but punches below the brand's recent peak.
- Compare this to the May 30 Allure award send — that email hung everything on a third-party credential and a specific SKU with earned authority. The May 31 lip combo send put "$12" front and center in the hero. This one just says: "new product, here are some benefits." No price. No urgency. No social proof. For a brand that's been hitting real highlights this week, "trust us, it plumps" is a step backward. Solid execution, modest ambition.
- The double-section structure (hero CTA → peptide benefit breakdown → second CTA) adds scroll length without adding new information or conversion hook. The repetition dilutes rather than reinforces.

## What's working

- **Hero is clean and front-loaded.** Product shot, headline, and "SHOP NOW" are all visible before any scrolling — no hunting required to understand what this is.
- **Ingredient storytelling is specific.** Three benefit bullets give the peptide angle concrete claims: plumping over time, smoothing texture, softening the look of fine lines.
- **No render bugs.** The layout is clean across the entire email — no overlapping text, broken images, or empty placeholder fields.
- **Model casting is on-brand.** Pink-haired Gen Z model is consistent with e.l.f.'s identity and visually cohesive with the icy blue hero palette.

## What's weak

- **No product price anywhere.** Unlike the May 31 lip combo send with "$12" anchored in the hero, this email asks the consumer to click into the unknown on a brand-new SKU — a real conversion drag.
- **"SHOP NOW" is the laziest CTA available.** When you're launching a specific SKU with differentiated ingredient claims, "Shop Serum Stick" or "Get Bouncier Skin" would carry significantly more pull.
- **Zero social proof.** The May 30 Allure send demonstrated exactly how much a single credential can do. This email has nothing equivalent — no star rating, no reviewer count, no award.
- **Second section doesn't earn its real estate.** The "Nourishing Peptide Blend" block repeats the hero benefit claims and drops another generic "SHOP NOW" — it's scroll without payoff.
- **Seven emails in eight days.** Based on brand history, subscriber fatigue is real, and a no-discount product launch is a hard ask this deep into the blast cycle.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the price in the hero.** One number kills the "is this expensive?" hesitation before the click. If it's in a competitive range, show it — it converts.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA to name the product.** "Try the Serum Stick" or "Get Bouncy Skin for $X" ties the action directly to the SKU and the price anchor.
- 3. **Add one social proof element.** Star rating, a user count ("10K+ sold"), or even a brief pull-quote near the CTA would lift credibility on a brand-new product.
- 4. **Cut the second section or make it earn its keep.** Replace the repeated peptide copy with a before/after visual, a close-up texture shot, or a comparison claim — something that advances the story rather than restating it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New: Glass Slick Serum Stick — here's what peptides do`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your skin wants this serum stick 💫 (new drop)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Bounce-boosting peptide formula. Plumper, smoother skin — shop the new drop.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `NEW Glass Slick Serum Stick: visibly plumper skin in one swipe.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Table-based layout using Braze as ESP, with standard MSO/Outlook conditionals and a 640px fixed-width container. Several structural anomalies and a verifiable accessibility gap were found; compliance assessment is partial due to truncated source.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Braze opaque redirect wrapping**
All click-tracked URLs route through `links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/wapQmtYq-23iHAqZgogTBg~~/.../` — Braze's standard hashed redirect format. The destination URL and any UTM parameters are encoded into the hash and cannot be verified from the HTML source. This is a process gap, not a rendering defect, but final-destination QA requires Braze click-report inspection or manual link expansion.

**Single visible CTA**
Only one trackable link is present in the truncated source (the hero image). If additional CTAs exist in the clipped portion, this is not a finding — but the audit cannot confirm full link coverage.

**Image asset domain**
Hero image is served from `braze-images.com` (Braze CDN):
```
https://braze-images.com/appboy/communication/assets/.../original.png?1698158310
```
The cache-busting query param (`?1698158310`) is a Unix timestamp from 2023, which is expected for immutable assets. No issue, but note the asset is not on elfcosmetics.com's own CDN.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Samsung Mail and some preview panes render the `<title>` value. An empty title shows as a blank string or falls back to the subject line inconsistently. Populate with the subject or email name.

**Three consecutive empty `html_block` divs**
`block-2`, `block-3`, and `block-4` are structurally present but contain only whitespace:
```html
<div style="font-family:Open Sans,Arial,Sans-serif;text-align:center" align="center"> </div>
```
These add dead markup weight (~300 bytes) with no rendered output. Likely leftover from template scaffolding.

**Nested `nl-container` inside `html_block`**
The first `html_block` (block-1) contains a fully re-declared `nl-container > row > row-content > column` table tree — a complete nested email skeleton inside the outer skeleton. This suggests content was copy-pasted from another template or a Braze content block was embedded without stripping its wrapper. While this renders in most clients, it's technically invalid nesting and will cause unpredictable behavior in strict renderers.

**Image alt text is non-descriptive**
```html
alt="e.l.f." title="e.l.f."
```
The hero image links to a product page. The alt text identifies the brand but not the linked destination or image content. When images are blocked, subscribers see only "e.l.f." with no call-to-action context. WCAG 2.1 §1.1.1 requires alt text to convey the image's purpose when it is a link.

**No issues found:** Viewport meta is present; MSO conditionals are correctly applied; Google Fonts is gated behind `<!--[if !mso]>` to prevent Outlook download failure; `lang="en"` is set on `<html>`; responsive breakpoint at 640px uses correct `.stack .column` approach.
