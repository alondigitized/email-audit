# Look Who's e.l.f.ing Hair 💁‍♀️
**Score:** 10/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- This is e.l.f. Hair's fourth dedicated hair email in eight days — following June 22's "Hair it is! 💁‍♀️ NEW e.l.f.ing launch" — and the creative is showing wear. The "e.l.f.ing" wordplay returns for the third iteration, the 💁‍♀️ emoji reappears for at least the second time this month, and the "Look Who's" subject hook promises a reveal that the hero copy doesn't deliver. There's no visible name drop, influencer call-out, or ambassador credit above the fold. The mystery is unanswered.
- What saves it: production quality is high, the hero model photography is doing real work (voluminous hair as the product demo), and the free-shipping kicker is cleanly isolated with its own button. The "Top Picks for You" section adds scroll-extending personalization that few brands bother with.
- But this is the fifth swing at the same hair sub-brand in ten days to the same list. The email is competent; the campaign is stretched.

## What's working

- Hero photography earns its placement — the model's voluminous, bouncy hair is the visual argument for the product.
- "LOOK WHO'S E.L.F.ING HAIR." lands fast at mobile scale; the display type is oversized and confident.
- Free shipping ("shipping's on us!") is cleanly isolated as a secondary offer moment with its own "SHOP NOW" button — a clear decision point.
- "Top Picks for You" personalized carousel extends perceived relevance and scroll depth.
- No render bugs; layout holds cleanly at the visible mobile width.

## What's weak

- "Look Who's" is a tease that never pays off — no influencer name, no press quote, no social-proof reveal visible in the hero.
- The "e.l.f.ing" wordplay and 💁‍♀️ emoji are recycled from June 18 and June 22 sends; novelty is gone.
- "Limited time only" on the shipping offer has no end date — vague urgency converts no one.
- The four-product grid renders too small to read product names or prices without zooming.
- This is send five of a hair sprint; open rate suppression is the predictable downstream cost.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pay off the "Look Who's" hook.** If there's an influencer or press angle behind this email, put their name and face in the hero above the fold. A mystery that resolves to "just our products" is a broken promise.
- 2. **Kill the "limited time only" vagueness.** "Free shipping ends Sunday" is three words that actually create urgency. The current phrasing is boilerplate that subscribers have trained themselves to ignore.
- 3. **Give the product grid room.** Four products in a tight row at mobile scale means unreadable names. A 2×2 layout with readable tiles would convert better.
- 4. **Retire the 💁‍♀️ in the subject.** It has appeared in at least two prior June subjects from this sender. It no longer signals anything new.
- 5. **Cap the hair sprint or re-segment.** Remaining sends in this campaign should route only to the purchaser/clicker cohort; the full list is saturated.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Several email clients (Apple Mail, Outlook Web) surface the title element. An empty title degrades screen reader context.

**Google Fonts loaded via `<link>` in `<head>`:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" ...>
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Jost" ...>
```
Gmail (all variants), Yahoo, and Outlook strip `<link>` tags from `<head>`. Fonts will silently fall back to `Arial, Sans-serif`. This is acceptable only if the design is validated at fallback — confirm no broken spacing or visual hierarchy at fallback weight.

**Nested `nl-container` inside `html_block`:**
The first `html_block` (block-1) contains a fully self-contained `nl-container` table tree duplicating the outer wrapper structure. This double-wrapping is a template builder artifact and creates extra DOM nesting that can misfire in Outlook's Word renderer.

**Three empty `html_block` elements (block-2, block-3, block-4):**
```html
<div style="font-family:Open Sans,Arial,Sans-serif;text-align:center" align="center"> </div>
```
These appear to be blank spacer blocks left in from template editing. They add DOM weight with no layout value; remove or replace with proper `padding` on adjacent cells.

**Global link color/decoration override:**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This resets all anchor styling in Gmail. Intended to prevent Gmail's blue auto-links, but will suppress underline and color on any text CTAs or the unsubscribe link unless those are explicitly re-styled inline. Verify unsubscribe link remains visually distinguishable.
