# Bestie, start your summer glow up ✨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- This is e.l.f.'s "Weekly Highlight Reel" — a digest format that lands after six consecutive focused, single-hero sends (highlighter launch, skin tint, blush finder, hydration routine, eye duo, pore toner). The contrast is stark. Where the June 11 Halo Glow launch or June 9 blush-finder had one product, one price, one clear narrative, this email throws a lipstick hero, two competing CTAs, six unlabeled product thumbnails, four more grid products, and a buried hair care Instagram pivot at the reader simultaneously. Nothing lands.
- The subject line — "Bestie, start your summer glow up ✨" — promises a journey or transformation moment, but the body opens with "YOUR WEEKLY HIGHLIGHT REEL. Peep our latest picks for you." That's a bait-and-switch on tone: the subject teases a personal inflection point; the body delivers a catalogue digest. The most interesting thing in this email (e.l.f.haircare debuting on Instagram, a genuine new category signal) is buried as a mid-email banner beneath two product carousels.
- The email is clean, on-brand, and renders without visual bugs — but the format is doing the work of seven emails at once and lands with the impact of none of them. Weakest send in the recent run by a clear margin.

## What's working

- **Lipstick hero image is arresting** — the red glitter bullet on a pink background is high-contrast and eye-catching; above-the-fold delivery is solid.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, no broken images, no overlapping text, no placeholder copy.
- **e.l.f.haircare banner is the right instinct** — leading with a new category via an Instagram join mechanic is interesting creative. The instinct to tease something new is right, even if the placement is wrong.
- **Brand voice is consistent** — "Hot as E.L.F. This Week" and "Bestie" carry the Gen Z casual register e.l.f. has built across this run.
- **Beauty Squad footer loyalty block** — receipt scanning + rewards nudge in the footer is a reliable retention mechanic, present here as expected.

## What's weak

- **Zero prices anywhere** — every prior send anchored on a specific number ($9 highlighter, $15 toner, "under $10"). This email shows products at unknown prices; there's no value hook.
- **Six unlabeled product thumbnails in "Your New Obsessions"** — small, unnamed, no CTA per product. You can't tell what you're looking at.
- **Dual CTA at the top splits focus** — "SHOP BEST SELLERS" and "SHOP NEW ARRIVALS" side by side means the email cannot commit to one direction. One CTA wins; two CTAs hedge.
- **Subject/body disconnect** — "start your summer glow up" promises a transformation frame; "YOUR WEEKLY HIGHLIGHT REEL" delivers a digest. The tone shift lands as disjointed.
- **Hair care pivot is buried** — the most newsworthy thing here (e.l.f.haircare IG launch, apparent new category) appears third in the scroll, under two generic product modules, with no subject or preheader tease.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the digest format or own it completely** — if this is the weekly digest, structure it as three clean cards (Best Seller of the Week / New Drop / Coming Soon) with product names and prices on each. A digest without prices is a mood board, not a shop.
- 2. **Lead with the hair care news** — "our new IG is hair" is genuinely interesting and new. Make that the hero module (or a dedicated send), not the third scroll. A subject like "Wait, e.l.f. does hair now?" would outperform anything in this send.
- 3. **Cut one CTA** — consolidate to "SHOP THE REEL" or "SEE WHAT'S HOT" as a single top CTA tied to a curated landing page, not two competing shelf categories.
- 4. **Name and price the "Hot as E.L.F." grid items** — four mystery products with generic "SHOP NOW" labels are invisible. Even the product name would double click rates here.
- 5. **Align subject to body** — the digest is a real format; own it in the subject line.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Hot this week + e.l.f. just went haircare 👀`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Best sellers, new drops, and a category you didn't see coming.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New obsessions inside — plus, e.l.f. haircare just launched on IG.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue: Nested `nl-container` table structure (high severity)**
The outer layout contains a `.html_block.block-1` cell whose inner content is a *complete second* `nl-container`/`row-1` table tree — effectively an entire email layout nested inside the first one. This is a Beefree editor artifact from embedding an HTML block that includes its own wrapper tables. In strict-mode rendering clients (Outlook, older iOS Mail), this double-nesting can cause width containment failures or broken layout. Evidence:

```
outer: <table class="nl-container">…
  └─ .html_block > <div> 
       └─ <table class="nl-container">   ← duplicate root
            └─ <table class="row row-1">
```

**Issue: Invalid CSS `font-family` declaration (low severity)**
Inside the nested HTML block:

```css
font-family:'',Verdana,Sans-serif
```

An empty string (`''`) as the first font value is invalid CSS. All clients will silently skip to `Verdana`. If Jost was the intended first choice, this declaration is broken.

**Issue: Duplicate Google Fonts loading (medium severity)**
Jost is loaded twice with conflicting API versions:

- `<head>`: `<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Jost">` — legacy `css` API, no `font-display`
- Inside `.html_block`: `@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Jost:ital,wght@0,100..900;1,100..900&display=swap')` — modern `css2` API with `display=swap`

This fires two separate HTTP requests for the same typeface on every open, and the `<link>` version (without `display=swap`) can cause invisible-text flash in webmail clients that honor it.

**Issue: Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` is present but blank. Yahoo Mail and AOL Mail surface the `<title>` value in browser tabs and some accessibility trees. Should match the subject line or be a meaningful fallback.

**Preheader pattern:** Two hidden `<div>` spacers used correctly — first with visible preheader copy ("Peep our top picks for you!"), second with zero-width filler characters to prevent body bleed. Implementation is standard.
