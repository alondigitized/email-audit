# NEW ☁️ So Blurreal Lip & Cheek Whip
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- After an eight-day sprint of hair launches that was visibly showing creative fatigue by June 24, this new product send lands as a smart reset. The So Blurreal Lip & Cheek Whip is a genuine novelty — a new product, a new category, a new wordplay hook — and the email leans into the product itself rather than repeating a tired framework. The hero is clean, the shade names are characteristically cheeky, and the "only at elfcosmetics.com & Target" exclusivity signal anchors the whole thing.
- The execution is mostly strong but not exceptional. The free shipping incentive is doing double duty as the sole urgency mechanism, but e.l.f. already ran a free-shipping-as-hero email on June 23 ("e.l.f.rything ships FREE!") — using the same lever four days later softens the urgency considerably. Subscribers who opened that email will register "limited time only — shipping's on us!" as a recurring feature, not a reward.
- The verdict: a welcome category pivot with solid product-forward creative and a clear CTA, slightly undercut by a recycled incentive and a generic button label. A genuinely new-launch email that earns its send but doesn't fully capitalize on the freshness of the product.

## What's working

- **Shade lineup as conversion content.** Eight named shades with swatches ("fudge around," "airy berry," "red velvet") give a subscriber something concrete to self-select into — this is the email's strongest scroll-stopper.
- **Product exclusivity framing.** "Only at elfcosmetics.com & Target" does real work; it signals scarcity channel without feeling fake.
- **Clean hero hierarchy.** The product shot occupies most of the above-fold real estate with "BE SO BLURREAL" clearly legible — eye path is unambiguous.
- **Cadence pivot.** After six straight hair-heavy sends, a lip/cheek launch reads as fresh even before the copy lands.
- **"Top Picks for You" tail section.** Adds a personalized-feel cross-sell without cluttering the primary offer.

## What's weak

- **Recycled incentive.** "Limited time only — shipping's on us!" is nearly identical language to June 23's free-shipping email; two identical incentives in four days turns urgency into ambient noise.
- **Generic CTA copy.** "GET IT NOW" appears twice but never names the product or action specifically — "Shop So Blurreal" or "Pick Your Shade" would do more lift.
- **Feature bullets feel like a spec sheet.** "Candy feel & hydrating benefits" reads as filler copy alongside the much stronger shade names; it doesn't add conviction.
- **No social proof visible.** For a new product where subscribers have zero prior exposure, a TikTok view count or a single pull-quote would meaningfully reduce hesitation.
- **Preheader not readable at this render size** — whatever is above the hero likely squanders the inbox real estate that the June 23 audit already demonstrated e.l.f. can use well.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Replace the recycled free-shipping incentive with a shade-specific hook.** "8 shades, zero wrong answers" or a first-48-hours exclusivity angle creates genuine novelty rather than replaying last week's mechanic.
- 2. **Rewrite the primary CTA to name the product action.** Change both "GET IT NOW" instances to "Pick Your Shade" — aligns with the shade-parade creative and is more specific.
- 3. **Add one social proof signal.** A TikTok play count badge or a brief "already trending on TikTok" callout in the hero anchors a new product launch in existing social velocity.
- 4. **Tighten feature bullets.** Drop "candy feel & hydrating benefits" or fold it into the hero headline; the shade names carry the weight here, so let them.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `So Blurreal Lip & Cheek Whip just dropped ☁️`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `8 shades of Blurreal — new drop`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Whipped color for lips & cheeks — 8 shades, only at elfcosmetics.com & Target`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Pick your shade before it sells out — new So Blurreal launch`

### 1. Technical Summary

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot verify UTM parameters.** All links are wrapped in Braze server-side redirects (`links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/...`). Final destination URLs and UTM attribution (utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign) are not visible in source. Recommend decoding the primary CTA redirect to confirm UTM tagging is applied downstream.

**`tabindex="-1"` on logo CTA anchor:**
```html
<a href="https://links.elfcosmetics.com/..." target="_blank" style="outline:none" tabindex="-1">
```
Explicitly removes this link from keyboard tab order. If the logo is a navigable CTA (it links to the site), this is incorrect. If intentionally decorative, the link itself should be `aria-hidden="true"` rather than using tabindex suppression.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Apple Mail (macOS/iOS) and some other clients surface the document title. Should be set to the subject line or brand name to avoid a blank tab/window label.

**Google Fonts via `<link>` in `<head>` — blocked by major clients:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Jost" rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail (web and Android), Outlook, and Yahoo strip external `<link>` tags from `<head>`. Fallback stack (`Open Sans, Arial, Sans-serif`) activates in those clients — functionally acceptable, but `Jost` (loaded but not in the fallback stack) will silently drop in most environments. If `Jost` is used anywhere in the body, add it to the fallback or accept silent degradation.

**Global link style override in Outlook:**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This suppresses all link underlines and inherits text color in Outlook/Windows Mail. Any linked text whose color is not explicitly set on the `<a>` element itself risks becoming invisible if it matches the background or surrounding text.

**Empty spacer tables (block-2, block-3, block-4):** Three consecutive `html_block` tables contain only whitespace and serve no layout function. Likely builder artifacts; they add byte overhead with no semantic value.

**Logo alt text minimal but present:** `alt="e.l.f."` — passes the baseline (non-empty), but could be more descriptive for screen readers (e.g., `"e.l.f. Cosmetics"`).
