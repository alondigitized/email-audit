# Toner 101 💧
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- This is a content-led education send — "Toner 101" is a walkthrough of e.l.f. SKIN's toner lineup organized by formula and skin-type fit. Three products get individual hero panels with prices visible: a **NEW** Hydrocolloid Toner ($13), the Thirst Burst Pore-fecting Toner ($13), and the Holy Hydration Luminous Toner ($6). A personalized "Top Picks For You" grid rounds out the bottom.
- The concept is solid — toner is genuinely confusing for most buyers, and the editorial framing earns the inbox slot better than a straight product blast. The execution is clean: color-coded panels per product, consistent "SHOP NOW" CTAs, and accessible price points throughout.
- Where it stumbles: there's no offer here. No discount, no urgency, no threshold — just a full-price lineup explainer. In isolation that's a defensible content play. As the **seventh e.l.f. email in eight days**, it lands thin. The "NEW" label on the Hydrocolloid Toner is the one hook that earns attention; the rest reads as a repeat of existing catalog.

## What's working

- **Price anchoring is immediate** — all three toner prices appear within the panel hero, no hunting required.
- **Color-coded panels** give each product its own visual identity without breaking the overall brand system.
- **"NEW" badge** on the Hydrocolloid Toner gives the email a reason to exist; a restock/new-arrival signal is higher-intent than a generic roundup.
- **"Top Picks For You"** algorithmic grid at the bottom adds light personalization and a secondary click path.
- **$6 entry point** on the Holy Hydration toner is a strong value signal for an e.l.f. subscriber who expects wallet-friendly pricing.

## What's weak

- **No offer whatsoever** — no percentage off, no GWP, no free shipping threshold. After the July 9th BURST code / free shipping send and the July 11th $14 bundle deal, a full-price educational send feels like a step down.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTAs are generic** — three panels, three identical button labels. "Try the $6 Toner" or "Shop the NEW Hydrocolloid" would outperform.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — seven emails in eight days from the e.l.f. / e.l.f. SKIN / e.l.f. Hair ecosystem. Even with sub-sender segmentation, this adds to inbox noise.
- **Hero is editorial, not product-first** — the model/lifestyle shot and "what's the e.l.f.ing difference" headline are charming, but push the actual products below the fold.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price-entry offer** — even a "pick your toner, free shipping on orders $30+" would transform this from a browse send into a conversion send. The $6 toner alone could anchor a threshold GWP.
- 2. **Differentiate the CTAs** — match the button copy to the panel: "Shop the NEW Hydrocolloid →", "Shop $13 Toner →", "Try the $6 Toner →".
- 3. **Lead with the new product** — put the Hydrocolloid Toner panel first (the "NEW" hook is buried as the second item under a lifestyle hero); newness is the only urgency signal in this email.
- 4. **Pull the $6 price point into the subject** — the Holy Hydration toner at $6 is a genuine standout for e.l.f.'s audience and belongs in the subject line.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your toner match: starting at $6 💧`
- **Subject Alt B:** `NEW: Hydrocolloid Toner is here + your full lineup guide`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three formulas. One for every skin type. Prices from $6.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Our NEW Hydrocolloid Toner just dropped — plus find your match.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — Nested email template (double `nl-container`):**
Inside `html_block block-1`, the editor has dropped a full `nl-container → row → row-content → column` structure. This duplicates the outer scaffold and will produce unexpected behavior in Outlook (double `margin: 0 auto` conflicts) and may break stacking on mobile.

**`<title>` is empty:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some clients (notably Outlook on Windows) display the `<title>` value in the window title or preview. Should be populated with the subject line or brand name.

**Three consecutive empty `html_block` tables** (block-2, block-3, block-4) with no content — dead markup that adds weight with zero function. Should be removed.

**Google Fonts external request** (`fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans`) is non-MSO only, but Gmail, Outlook web, Samsung Mail, and most major clients block external CSS. The fallback stack (`Arial, Sans-serif`) is present, but the font request is wasted for the majority of recipients.

**Preheader text is garbled:**
```html
Featuring Save Your e.l.f. Hypochlorous Mist.
```
"Save Your" appears to be truncated body copy or a merge artifact — not a complete sentence. Preheader text surfaces directly in inbox previews.
