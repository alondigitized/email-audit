# NEW $15 lip combos on main 💋✨ 
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- This is e.l.f.'s second lip-combo email in six days — and it earns the repeat by anchoring on genuinely new products (Nude Dewy Lip Stains + Glitter Fantasy Lip Oil) at a $15 price point, $5 lower than the July 1 "Leveled-up lip combos for only $20" send. The personalization engine is the structural win: five named combos (Feeling Rosy, Hot Good Lips, Pink Twice Up) plus a shade-quiz CTA directs subscribers into a decision path rather than a product grid. That's smart. The @GAILIEBEAUTY UGC section mid-email adds credible social proof, and the "Top Picks for You" rail personalizes the close.
- The weakness is the hero itself: two buttons at equal visual weight ("SHADE A QUIZ" and "FIND MY COMBO") split the primary action and dilute hierarchy. Pick one. The "on main" subject copy is intentionally Gen Z-coded, which fits the brand voice but will land as noise for older segments.
- Overall: a confident, well-structured send that doesn't fully justify its place so close to July 1. The $5 price drop and new product angle do the minimum to make it feel fresh, but just barely.

## What's working

- **"$15 lip combos" is the first legible headline** — price and category read instantly from the hero before any scroll.
- **"FIND MY COMBO" is specific CTA copy** — verb + possessive + noun beats "Shop Now" and signals a personalized path.
- **Three named combos give a decision scaffold** — subscriber can self-select by vibe without clicking aimlessly.
- **@GAILIEBEAUTY UGC integration** adds mid-email social proof without feeling forced; a real creator handle vs. a stock testimonial.
- **"Top Picks for You" close** signals algorithmic personalization, making the email feel less like a blast.

## What's weak

- **Two hero CTAs at equal weight** ("SHADE A QUIZ" + "FIND MY COMBO") compete with each other and dilute conversion focus.
- **Combo names carry no shade intel** — "Feeling Rosy Combo" is evocative but opaque; subscribers can't self-select without clicking first.
- **Category fatigue from July 1** — the lip-combo format is six days old; the $5 drop and new product angle are the only real differentiators, and they're not surfaced prominently enough.
- **"On main" headline skews narrow** — fits core e.l.f. audience but is age-gated slang that will read as confusing to a broad subscriber list.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill one hero CTA** — make "FIND MY COMBO" the sole primary action. The quiz IS the combo discovery; you don't need both.
- 2. **Add shade descriptors under each combo name** — e.g. "Feeling Rosy: warm nude stain + pink glitter oil" so the subscriber can match to intent without a click.
- 3. **Lead with the price-drop story** — if July 1 was $20 and this is $15, say it. "Now $5 less" or "our lowest-priced lip pair" turns repeat-category into news.
- 4. **Move @GAILIEBEAUTY earlier** — social proof is buried mid-scroll; a creator quote or "as worn by" callout in the first combo card would lift the entire section.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` element:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers (VoiceOver, NVDA) announce the document title. A blank title provides no context. Should be set to the email subject or a short description.

**Redundant nested layout shell:**
Inside `block-1`'s `html_block`, a complete second `nl-container` > `row row-1` > `row-content stack` layout is embedded — a full email shell nested within another. This is the Braze "custom HTML block inside drag-and-drop template" anti-pattern. It produces duplicate `width:640px` frame constraints and can cause double-border or misalignment artifacts in Outlook (which processes nested tables independently).

**Three empty content blocks:**
`block-2`, `block-3`, and `block-4` contain only whitespace:
```html
<div style="font-family:Open Sans,Arial,Sans-serif;text-align:center" align="center"> </div>
```
These appear to be unfilled template placeholders. They add ~450 bytes of dead markup and may create unexpected vertical spacing in some clients (particularly older Android Gmail).

**`#MessageViewBody a` override is too broad:**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This Gmail-targeting override strips underlines and inherited color from all links in the message body — including any unsubscribe or footer links that rely on visual distinction for compliance readability.

**Google Fonts via `<link>` tag:**
```html
<!--[if !mso]><!-->
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" ...>
<!--<![endif]-->
```
Correct MSO conditional guard. However, the `?family=Open+Sans` query string is missing the `display=swap` parameter, which can cause invisible text during font load in webmail clients that do render web fonts (Apple Mail, some iOS mail apps).
