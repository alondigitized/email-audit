# NEW 👄 dew more for your lips 
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- This is a new-product-launch email for what appears to be a lip-focused hydration product — the "dew more for your lips" framing leans squarely into serum/gloss territory. The hero is large, colorful, and unmistakably e.l.f.: pastel gradients, product tubes front and center, and a punny headline. The visual package is competent. The problem is context: e.l.f. already sent a lip email five days ago ("This $12 lip combo is e.l.f.ing sweet") that earned an 8/10 here, and that one had a sharper subject, a concrete price anchor, and a tighter hero. This send feels like a close relative arriving too soon.
- The email is long — a full scroll through hero, product tiles, a Target availability block, and footer — but the individual sections hold together. No render bugs. The "dew more" pun works, but it's a step below "rosé all day" (5/18) and "e.l.f.ing sweet" (5/16). The real miss is the subject: no price, no product name specificity, just a pun with a category emoji. Compared to their own best work this week, this subject punches below weight.
- Verdict: a solid mid-tier launch email that would rate higher in isolation, but the cadence and lack of a price anchor hold it back.

## What's working

- **Hero visual is strong** — colorful product tubes with a gradient background command attention immediately; the eye lands on the products without fighting the layout.
- **Pun lands** — "dew more for your lips" is clean and fits the hydration/dewy positioning.
- **Target block adds credibility** — a retail availability callout signals mainstream availability and gives a second conversion path beyond the DTC site.
- **No render bugs** — clean rendering throughout; no overlapping text, no broken image gaps, no lorem ipsum.
- **Consistent brand voice** — the pastel palette, playful copy, and emoji use are 100% on-brand for e.l.f.

## What's weak

- **No price in the subject or hero** — the two highest-scoring recent e.l.f. sends ("$7 blush," "$12 lip combo") both anchored on a number. This one doesn't, and it costs open and click motivation.
- **Cadence risk** — a lip email already ran 5 days ago; subscribers who remember it will feel déjà vu.
- **"dew more" is the feature, not the benefit** — the subject tells me what the product is named/themed but not what it does for me (plumper, fuller, glossier lips?).
- **Product tiles mid-email feel like a browse session, not a launch** — individual product cards below the hero dilute the "new launch" energy into a catalog scroll.
- **No urgency signal** — nothing in the email creates a reason to act today vs. next week.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price anchor to the subject** — their own data shows this works. "NEW: Dew Drops Lip Serum, $X" outperforms a pun-only subject for launch emails.
- 2. **Tighten the hero to one hero SKU** — the multi-product tube shot is pretty, but it diffuses what's actually new. The 5/17 Soft Glam Cream Blush email nailed the single-product hero; replicate that here.
- 3. **Add a time-bound hook** — even "launch week only" framing creates credibility without a hard discount.
- 4. **Move the Target callout higher** — retail availability is a trust signal; it shouldn't be buried below the fold.
- 5. **Trim the product tile grid** — cut to 2-3 hero products, not a full catalog scroll; it reads like a weekly digest, not a launch.

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard table-based HTML email built on what appears to be a Braze + Sailthru hybrid stack (Braze image CDN, Sailthru-style link encoding). The truncated source limits compliance and footer verification, but several rendering and tracking issues are identifiable from the header and first content block.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**ESP stack mismatch — confirm intentional**
Images are served from `braze-images.com` (`https://braze-images.com/appboy/communication/assets/...`), but click-tracking links use the Sailthru `~~` encoding pattern on `links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/`. This is consistent with an ESP migration where Braze assets were not re-hosted, or a deliberate hybrid setup. Either way, the Braze image URL contains a hardcoded timestamp (`?1698158310` = Oct 2023) — if Braze rotates or expires that asset, the image will 404.

**UTM parameters — unverifiable**
All links use opaque Sailthru-encoded redirects (e.g., `links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/KblwVySJCN0trwghZbiWdQ~~/...`). Final destination URLs and UTM parameters cannot be confirmed without resolving the redirect chain. This should be verified by decoding or clicking a test send.

**`tabindex="-1"` on the logo link**
```html
<a href="..." tabindex="-1">
```
Explicitly removes the logo link from keyboard tab order. Any keyboard or assistive-tech user cannot reach it.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` element**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers announce the email tab/window title. Empty `<title>` degrades the experience for VoiceOver/NVDA users opening the email in a browser preview or accessible mail client.

**Focus outline suppressed on links**
```html
<a href="..." style="outline:none">
```
Removes the browser's default keyboard focus indicator on the primary CTA/logo link. Fails WCAG 2.1 SC 2.4.7 (Focus Visible).

**Gmail link color override**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none; }
```
This suppresses Gmail's default link styling globally. Any text links in the email body will be visually indistinguishable from body text — a common source of invisible CTAs in Gmail.

**Google Fonts load correctly**
Fonts are gated behind `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`...`<!--<![endif]-->` conditionals, with proper Arial/Sans-serif fallbacks in inline styles. No issue.

**`role="presentation"` applied correctly**
All layout tables carry `role="presentation"`. No issue.

**Mobile breakpoint**
`@media (max-width:660px)` against a 640px container — standard buffer. No issue.
