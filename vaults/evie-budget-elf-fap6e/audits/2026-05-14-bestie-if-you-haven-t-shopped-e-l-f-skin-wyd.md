# Bestie, if you haven’t shopped e.l.f. SKIN, wyd?
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- This is a triggered/behavioral email from e.l.f. SKIN — the hero leads with "high-key results. low-key prices. Don't sleep on half-price skincare," which is a clean, on-brand promise. The email stacks a browsed-product personalization block, a Target co-branded rewards section, a "Top Picks For You" product grid, and a full category nav at the bottom. That's a lot of sections, but the Gen Z-casual brand voice holds it together reasonably well.
- The core weakness is the subject line. "Bestie, if you haven't shopped e.l.f. SKIN, wyd?" is trying to sound like a text from a friend but lands as brand-account-doing-Gen-Z energy. No urgency, no offer, no reason to open now versus later. The body is better than the subject deserves — the half-price skincare hook is strong and the personalization sections add relevance — but many recipients will never see it.
- Overall: a mid-tier promotional email with genuine personalization signals that is undermined by a subject line optimizing for brand voice over open rate.

## What's working

- **Hero promise is concrete**: "half-price skincare" + "low-key prices" gives a real reason to click, not just browse.
- **Personalization is visible**: The "browsed" widget and "Top Picks For You" section signal that this email knows something about the recipient — that raises click relevance.
- **Target co-brand rewards section**: Surfacing the Target rewards tie-in is smart; it extends perceived value beyond the DTC channel.
- **Clean render, no bugs**: No broken images, no overlapping text, no empty product tiles in the visible portion.
- **Brand voice is consistent**: "high-key / low-key," "wyd," "bestie" all read as the same character — e.l.f.'s tone doesn't fracture mid-email.

## What's weak

- **Subject has zero urgency or offer**: Nothing in the subject communicates why today is the day to act. The half-price hook is buried inside the body.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTAs are generic**: Every button says the same thing. With multiple product sections, differentiating the CTAs ("Get Half Off," "Shop Serums") would sharpen intent.
- **No deadline anywhere visible**: "Half-price skincare" with no expiration is a weak nudge — it implies the offer will still be there tomorrow.
- **Category nav at the bottom duplicates the earlier sections** without adding information — it reads as filler padding below the fold.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the offer into the subject line.** The half-price skincare hook is the email's strongest asset and it's invisible in the inbox. Rewrite the subject around it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Half-price e.l.f. SKIN — don't sleep on this`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your e.l.f. SKIN picks are 50% off rn`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Serums, cleansers, moisturizers — all low-key half off.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The e.l.f. SKIN line you browsed? It's on sale.`
- 2. **Add a deadline to the hero.** Even "Today only" or "Ends Sunday" would convert fence-sitters. Right now there's no cost to waiting.
- 3. **Differentiate the CTA copy per section.** Browsed products → "Pick Up Where You Left Off." Top Picks → "See Your Deals." Generic "SHOP NOW" everywhere wastes the personalization signal you already have.
- 4. **Trim the bottom category nav.** If the user hasn't clicked by the time they hit EYES / LIPS / FACE / SKINCARE links, another set of links isn't going to save it. Use that space for a second urgency reminder or a trust signal (reviews count, bestseller badge).

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag (confirmed bug):**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some clients (Samsung Mail, Outlook Mobile) surface `<title>` content in notification previews. An empty title degrades deliverability UX in those clients.

**Empty primary font-family string (confirmed bug):**
```html
style="font-family:'',Verdana,Sans-serif"
```
The first value is an empty string `''`. The browser/client skips it and falls through to Verdana, but this is broken CSS — the intent was almost certainly `'Open Sans'`. Affects the desktop-hidden block specifically.

**Google Fonts via `<link>` tag:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail (all variants), Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo strip `<link>` tags in `<head>`. Open Sans will not render for the majority of recipients. The fallback chain `Open Sans, Arial, Sans-serif` handles this acceptably, but the font load is wasted bandwidth for ~80%+ of opens.

**Alt text:** Hero image has `alt="e.l.f. SKIN"` — present and non-empty. No issue.

**Role attributes:** All tables carry `role="presentation"` — correct.
