#  Must-duos for your skin 👯‍♀️
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- Best Friends Day (June 8) gives this send a legitimate seasonal peg, and e.l.f. leans into it well — a warm hero image with three joyful, diverse models, "bestie" language throughout, and three skin bundles priced at a sharp $10–$14. The execution is clean and the brand voice is unmistakably e.l.f.
- The problem is split attention. Three duos with identical "SHOP NOW" CTAs and no price anchor in the subject line means there's no single conversion target — subscribers scroll through three equal-weight asks and often choose none. The other issue is cadence fatigue: the Thirst Burst Drops were the hero product in yesterday's June 6 single-product send and appear again here as the "Thirst Burst Dew-o" 24 hours later. Engaged subscribers who already clicked are now seeing the same SKU repackaged.
- This is solid foundational work — clean render, on-brand, timely — but it reads like a catalog page rather than a call to action.

## What's working

- Best Friends Day tie-in lands naturally; the hero model trio executes the concept without feeling forced.
- Bundle prices ($14, $10, $10) are prominently displayed under each duo — no hunting required.
- Brand voice is tight throughout: "e.l.f.ceptional," "Dew-o," "bestie" all feel unmistakably native.
- No render bugs, clean product photography, consistent section formatting.
- Free shipping callout in the footer adds a low-friction final nudge.

## What's weak

- The Thirst Burst Drops already starred in yesterday's dedicated send — recycling the SKU as a duo 24 hours later risks feeling redundant to the most-engaged segment.
- Subject hides the price story: "Must-duos for your skin" is punny but soft; "$10 skin duos" would convert harder.
- Three identical "SHOP NOW" buttons with no differentiation fragment click intent across all three sections.
- No urgency language: Best Friends Day is June 8 but the email never says "today" or "tomorrow" to make the peg actionable.
- No social proof (ratings, best-seller flags, "subscriber favorite") to help subscribers choose between the three duos.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Single hero duo.** Make the Brightest Besties Duo ($14) the lead — it's a unique SKU not featured yesterday. Push Thirst Burst to a secondary section or suppress it from this send entirely.
- 2. **Price in the subject.** The $10 entry point is a genuine hook; bury it in the body and you lose opens.
- 3. **Differentiate CTA copy.** "SHOP THE BRIGHTENING DUO" / "SHOP THE HYDRATION DUO" links the click to a specific intent and measurably improves attribution.
- 4. **Explicit deadline.** "Best Friends Day is tomorrow — shop by midnight" turns the seasonal peg into an action driver.
- 5. **Rest the Thirst Burst SKU for 3–5 days** post its single-product hero send before re-featuring.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Glowing besties start at $10 ✨`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three duos, one gift — brighten, hydrate, dewify. Shop now.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` is empty:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some clients (notably Apple Mail, Samsung Mail) surface the `<title>` value in tab previews or notification previews. An empty title is a missed slot; not a breakage.

**Google Fonts loaded via `<link>` in `<head>`:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" rel="stylesheet" type="text/css">
```
Wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!--> … <!--<![endif]-->` to exclude Outlook, which is correct. However, Gmail (web), Yahoo, and most mobile clients strip `<link>` tags entirely — the fallback `Arial,Sans-serif` will be used by the majority of recipients. This is acceptable if visually tested, but worth noting as Open Sans will not render in most clients.

**`#MessageViewBody a` rule strips all link styling in Gmail:**
```css
#MessageViewBody a{color:inherit;text-decoration:none}
```
This CSS selector targets Gmail's message container and resets every `<a>` to inherit body color with no underline. Unless CTAs have explicit inline `color` and `text-decoration` styles, links will be **visually indistinguishable from body text in Gmail**. This is a frequent source of click-rate suppression.

**Empty HTML blocks generate unnecessary DOM weight:**
`block-2`, `block-3`, `block-4` in row-1 contain only whitespace inside `<div>` wrappers — they add markup with no rendering output. Likely builder artifact.

**Nested `nl-container` duplication:**
The outer `nl-container` wraps an inner `nl-container` inside `html_block block-1`. This double-nesting is a builder export artifact and while it renders correctly, it adds ~200 bytes of redundant table structure per block.

**`role="presentation"` properly applied** to all layout tables. `lang="en"` set on `<html>`. MSO/VML namespaces declared. Responsive breakpoint at `max-width:660px` for a 640px layout — appropriate margin.

**Alt text:**
Logo image uses `alt="e.l.f."` — minimal but acceptable for a brand logo. `title="e.l.f."` is redundant (browsers ignore `title` on images in most contexts) but harmless.
