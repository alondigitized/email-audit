# Join the waitlist: advent cals are back! 🎁
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-06**
## Executive summary

- This is a teaser, not an offer. e.l.f. is asking you to join a waitlist for an advent calendar you can't buy yet. The purple "express your e.l.f. advent calendar" block is clear and has a good black "JOIN THE WAITLIST" button. But it sits under a huge, abstract e.l.f. SKIN line-art hero that says nothing except "NEW FOR 2026!", so the real message arrives about 800px down.
- The email then pivots to a second story, the e.l.f. POP Tromsø fragrance and body collection. That's the same campaign as the 10-05 "NEW & Limited Edition! e.l.f. POP holiday fragrance" send, so it's a repeat for anyone who opened yesterday. Below that come a thin "Top Picks for You" row and six empty grey category bars. The result is a muddled one-two punch with no price, no date and no reason to act today.
- Judgement: a decent list-building play, but weak as a revenue email. The waitlist pitch is honest and clean, but the hierarchy and the recycled POP content dilute it.

## What's working

- The waitlist CTA is unambiguous: a high-contrast black pill on the purple block, with copy that says exactly what happens.
- "24 limited edition e.l.f.ssentials in full & mini sizes" gives a concrete picture of what's in the calendar.
- The purple block's white-on-indigo headline is the strongest typographic moment in the email.
- The POP block is moody and premium, with the aurora backdrop and a clear "SHOP E.L.F. POP" button.
- The footer is tidy and honest: free shipping, TikTok Shop, receipt scanning, app badges, and clear unsubscribe and account links.

## What's weak

- The top hero is a giant line-art e.l.f. SKIN graphic with a "NEW FOR 2026!" badge. It's oversized, doesn't explain itself, and pushes the waitlist message below the first screen on mobile.
- Two competing stories (advent waitlist and POP fragrance) fight for the eye. Neither gets the full stage.
- There's no price, date or deadline anywhere. "The countdown is on!" is vague, and nothing says when the calendar drops or how many will be made.
- The "Top Picks for You" row has tiny product shots and "SHOP NOW" buttons, and it has no connection to the advent story. At $2–$11 the products are cheap enough that they could have been a strong hook.
- The six grey category bars (NEW, EYES, LIPS, FACE, SKINCARE, HAIR) look like blank, empty modules and eat a lot of vertical space.
- "Free shipping — limited time only!" is buried in the footer with no threshold or end date.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace the line-art hero with the purple waitlist block, and make the reward concrete. Show the calendar itself, or a flat-lay of the 24 items, and add an incentive ("first access + free shipping").
- 2. Cut the POP block, or move it to a separate send. It repeats the 10-05 email and splits attention.
- 3. Add a real deadline and a scarcity cue, such as "Drops [date]" or "Limited quantities. Last year's sold out." Without them the waitlist has no urgency.
- 4. Tie "Top Picks" to the advent calendar, for example "Inside the cal: full-size faves", or drop it. Make the product images larger.
- 5. Collapse the grey nav bars into a compact text row.
- 6. Move the free-shipping line up and add a threshold and end date.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML is a table-based, Braze-style template with click-tracked links. The QA run found a 27% pass rate: 4 of the 12 probed links failed on relative-URL redirects, the text/plain part is empty, and the compliance and authentication headers could not be verified.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Four probed links failed with `unknown url type`.** Each failure was a relative path, which means the redirect chain from `links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/...` ended in a `Location` that wasn't an absolute URL:
  - `/products/power-grip-primer?Size=Regular+0.8+oz+%2F+24+ml` ("Shop Now")
  - `/products/cream-glide-lip-liner?Color=Mauve+Aside+-+Warm+Rose+Mauve` ("Shop Now")
  - `/account/login?return_to=%2Faccount` ("Receipt scanning - Earn rewards anywhere you shop e.l.f.")
  - `/pages/mobile-app?af_reengagement_window=30d&pid=Email&bxid=6a013826e53882007d6a1618&utm_campaign=100626_adventcal_waitlist_0-3&utm...` ("beauty squad - Download the e.l.f. app")
- **I can't tell from the data whether this is a probe artifact or a real defect.** If a hop really returns a relative `Location`, a browser resolves it against the tracker host (`links.elfcosmetics.com`) and would likely 404. If the probe is mishandling a redirect from the destination domain, readers are unaffected. A manual `curl -I` on one of these links would settle it.
- **The "Receipt scanning" link resolves to `/account/login?return_to=%2Faccount`.** A promotional link that lands on a login wall is friction for logged-out recipients. This one is confirmed by the destination path.
- **Coverage is partial.** Only 12 of 39 links were probed. The 27 skipped links, including footer and social links, are unverified.
- **One image has no `alt`.** Its src is `links.elfcosmetics.com/q/ZWmT3Z8bdr-...`, so it looks like an open-tracking pixel. The impact is low.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)

- **Footer unsubscribe and physical address:** Neither appears in the truncated excerpt, so I can't assess them. The QA states an unsubscribe link is always probed, but no result for it is listed.

- The "Shop Now" product links go to PDP URLs with variant parameters (`Size=`, `Color=`) and no UTM parameters visible in the failure output. Tracking may be applied at the redirect hop, which couldn't be confirmed because the probe failed.
- For a "join the waitlist" email, the probed links go to generic PDPs, the login page and the app-download page. The waitlist CTA itself was not among the failed links, so I can't verify where it lands.

## 7. Recommendations

3. Confirm that a text/plain MIME part exists. If it doesn't, enable auto-generated plain text in the ESP.
4. Check the raw `.eml` for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and DKIM/SPF/DMARC results, instead of relying on the relay-processed copy.
5. Add a meaningful `<title>`, and add `alt=""` to the tracking pixel image.
6. Raise the probe budget, or manually test the 27 skipped links, particularly the unsubscribe and waitlist CTAs.
