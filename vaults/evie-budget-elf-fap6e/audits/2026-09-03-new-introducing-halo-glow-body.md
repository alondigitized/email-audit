# NEW! Introducing Halo Glow Body ✨💫 
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-03**
## Executive summary

- This is e.l.f.'s launch send for the Halo Glow Body line-extension, and it's a clean, three-product hero cascade: Soft Beam Body Cream ($12), Goddess Balm ($14), Body Brush ($10). The category extension is genuinely new news for the brand — "halo glow is serving body" is a decent hook that leans on the existing Halo Glow skin-tint equity rather than launching cold, and each product gets its own full-bleed glow-shot with a named product, price, and one-line benefit.
- The execution problem is the same one that dogs the brand's multi-product sends: every hero shot is a tight crop of a torso/collarbone with the product held up against skin, and after the third nearly-identical composition the visual variety collapses. There's no swatch comparison, no before/after, no ingredient callout (the cream mentions "vanilla and magnolia scent" but nothing about the shimmer finish or skin type), and no social proof anywhere in the launch section — unusual for e.l.f., which leaned on an Allure badge for the 8/28 Halo Glow Skin Tint send and a clinical stat for the 8/30 Hypochlorous Mist send. This reads as a routine catalog-expansion email, not a hero send with a real hook.
- Against the brand history, this sits mid-pack: better than the 9/1 weekly roundup (6.00) because it's a focused three-SKU launch, but weaker than the 8/30 Hypochlorous Mist (8.00) or 8/28 Halo Glow Skin Tint (7.00) sends, which both had a genuine proof point (clinical stat, award badge) this one lacks.

## What's working

- Clear "new!" badge on every hero image signals this is a genuine launch, not a reissue.
- Each of the three products gets a full hero treatment with name, price, and one-line benefit — no ambiguity about what's for sale.
- "Starting at $12" in the top banner sets a low price anchor before the reader even scrolls.
- Consistent CTA button ("Shop Now" / "Glow Halo to Toe") on every section — no ambiguity about what to click.
- "Top Picks for You" section adds a personalization signal and cross-sell without cluttering the main launch story.

## What's weak

- All three hero shots are near-identical torso/collarbone crops in the same pink-glow lighting — no visual variety to distinguish the products from each other.
- No proof point (clinical stat, award badge, review count, before/after) anywhere in the launch — a step back from the brand's recent 8/28 and 8/30 sends.
- No skin-tone range shown — every hero is the same fair-skin close-up, which undercuts "e.l.f. is for every eye, lip, and face" in the footer.
- Body Brush section doesn't show the brush in use or its bristle/texture detail, just the compact held near skin — a texture/tool product is exactly the case where a demo shot matters.
- "Top Picks for You" thumbnails are tiny and cropped tight against a busy white background, weakest visual moment in an otherwise clean layout.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap at least one hero shot for a genuine demo/action shot (brush bristles, balm swiped on skin) instead of three static "product held near torso" crops — the sameness is the single biggest drag on this send.
- 2. Add one proof point to the top banner or first product block — a review quote, star rating, or "sold out twice" urgency line — matching the credibility beats the brand used in 8/28 and 8/30 sends.
- 3. Include a bundle CTA ("Get the trio for $30") since three products at $12/$14/$10 naturally bundle to $36 — a bundled price point would lift AOV and give the email a stronger single offer instead of three parallel asks.
- 4. Show a second skin tone in at least one hero shot to back up the footer's "every eye, lip, and face" claim.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `3 new holy grails, starting at $12 — shimmer cream, glossy balm, blending brush.`
