# Get a Jelly Pop Glow Stick on us! 🍉
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- This is a multi-module digest email built around a genuinely strong hook: a free Jelly Pop Glow Stick ("on us") leading the hero. The timing is smart — e.l.f. launched the Jelly Pop line just four days ago with the "dew more for your lips" send, and gifting a unit to existing subscribers seeds trial and UGC while the product is still fresh. But the email immediately undermines itself: three "see what's e.l.f.ing new" product tiles, the Indy 500 giveaway carryover, and a "Top Picks For You" personalization grid all compete for attention before the reader has absorbed the hero offer. The free-product concept gets lost in a catalogue.
- The heavier structural problem is cadence. This is at least the fifth e.l.f. email in five days, and the formats are starting to blur. The May 23–24 flash-sale emails trained readers to scan for a hard price or window; a "free with purchase" offer that doesn't show its conditions in the hero reads as vague by comparison. Subscribers who open expecting a clean claim flow and land on a multi-section digest hesitate, then bail.
- Verdict: the right idea, wrong execution. Strip to one CTA with visible terms and this jumps two points.

## What's working

- **Hero concept earns attention.** "On us" signals genuine gift, not percentage off — emotionally that hits differently and stands out against the week's discount cadence.
- **Product-launch sequencing is sharp.** Gifting a Jelly Pop four days after the launch email keeps the SKU top-of-mind at exactly the right window.
- **Render is clean.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breakage — e.l.f.'s template holds up.
- **"Top Picks For You" module.** Personalization signal visible even in a digest context; shows the brand is working with purchase data.
- **Subject specificity.** Naming the exact product and the mechanic ("on us") beats vague teaser copy.

## What's weak

- **Too many modules competing with the hero offer.** Three product tiles + giveaway carryover + personalized picks = the freebie is buried before the first scroll ends.
- **Offer terms absent from the hero.** "On us" almost certainly requires a purchase threshold — that condition needs to be visible beneath the headline, not hidden in fine print or on a landing page.
- **Giveaway module is stale.** The "FUEL YOUR e.l.f." Indy 500 beat ran yesterday as its own dedicated email; repeating it with no new information a day later cheapens both sends.
- **No urgency signal.** The May 23 Halo Glow send used a 6-hour window to compelling effect; this email has no visible deadline, making "on us" feel like a standing offer rather than a reason to act now.
- **Multiple "shop now" buttons dilute the primary action.** By the time a reader reaches the hero CTA, they've already seen identical button copy on three product tiles below it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut everything below the hero.** Remove the product digest and giveaway modules. Let the Jelly Pop GWP be the sole ask. One email, one click.
- 2. **Show the mechanics in the hero copy.** Add "spend $30, we'll add it to your bag — no code needed" directly beneath the headline. Visible conditions accelerate clicks.
- 3. **Put a clock on it.** Add "today only" or a specific cutoff time. Borrow the urgency format from the May 23 flash-sale email, which worked.
- 4. **Rewrite the hero CTA.** Replace "shop now" with "claim my free Jelly Pop" — verb + specific noun outperforms the generic.
- 5. **Retire the giveaway module until there's a new story beat** (deadline approaching, winner count, etc.).
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free Jelly Pop Glow Stick — today only 🍉`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your free Jelly Pop is waiting (today only)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $30, we add a Jelly Pop Glow Stick to your bag. No code. One day.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `A $10 fave, on e.l.f. — no code, just add to cart before midnight.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Email renders with standard table-based layout targeting MSO (Outlook VML) and mobile breakpoints. Three confirmed technical issues warrant immediate attention: a stale hardcoded preheader date, a body-scoped `<style>` block that will be stripped by Gmail, and a CDN provenance mismatch between the image host and the link tracking domain.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Possible ESP/CDN mismatch (moderate risk)**
- Click tracking routes through `links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/.../~~` — the `/f/a/` path and double-tilde token pattern are characteristic of **Sailthru**.
- The logo image is served from **`braze-images.com`**: `https://braze-images.com/appboy/communication/assets/image_assets/images/6532bc91543698004cb67f6e/original.png?1697823889`
- Mixing image assets from a former ESP (Braze) with a current ESP's click-tracking (Sailthru) is a migration artifact risk. If the Braze account is deactivated or the asset is purged, the logo goes dark. The image timestamp `?1697823889` resolves to October 2023, confirming this is a legacy-hosted asset.

**Encoded destination URLs**
- All hrefs are Sailthru redirect URLs with base64-encoded payloads. Final landing page URLs and UTM parameters cannot be verified from the source alone — confirmed only at redirect resolution time.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<style>` block inside `<body>` `<div>` (high risk for Gmail)**
- `html_block block-3` contains a `<style type="text/css">` block nested inside a `<div>`, not in `<head>`. Gmail's rendering engine strips `<style>` tags that appear in the body. The `.responsive-text` class (controlling `font-size: 14px` on mobile) will silently fail in Gmail for Android/Web, falling back to the inline `font-size: 12px` declaration on the `<p>` tag — smaller than intended on all viewports.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
- `<title></title>` — Screen readers that announce the document title will read nothing. Low severity but a quick fix.

**`tabindex="-1"` on the logo anchor**
```html
<a href="https://links.elfcosmetics.com/..." tabindex="-1">
```
- Explicitly removed from the tab order. Keyboard-only users cannot navigate to the logo link. Should be removed (omitting `tabindex` entirely restores default browser behavior).

**Google Fonts `<link>` in `<head>`**
- Wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!--><link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans"...><!--<![endif]-->`. Gmail strips `<link>` tags from `<head>`, so Open Sans will not load in Gmail Web/Android. The declared fallback stack (`Arial, Sans-serif`) handles this gracefully — no breakage, but worth documenting.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Hardcoded promo code**
- `KBWZ-P3DM-R74H-GTCK` appears as a literal string. If this is intended as a unique-per-recipient welcome code it is not personalized — it is a single broadcast code. If that is intentional, no issue. If it was meant to be a Sailthru merge variable (e.g. `{promo_code}`), this is a critical send error.

**No visible unresolved merge token failures** in the truncated source. No `{{`, `{%`, or `*|` patterns found unfired.
