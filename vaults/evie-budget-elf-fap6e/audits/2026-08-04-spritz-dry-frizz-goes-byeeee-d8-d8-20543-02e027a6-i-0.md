# Spritz. Dry. Frizz goes byeeee 👋
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-04**
## Executive summary

- This is the direct follow-up to e.l.f. Hair's 7/30 launch email ("We're taking care of frizzness," 8/10) for the Humidity Hero Anti-Frizz Styling Spray — except now it's reformatted as a 5-step usage tutorial for the same $9 product. Production quality is solid: consistent chartreuse branding, a convincing before/after hero, and genuine step-by-step product education layered with repeated CTAs.
- The problem is redundancy. Five days after the launch send, this is still selling the identical product with no new offer, no discount, and a subject line so vague it doesn't even name what's being sold. It reads more like content marketing (a how-to) than a commercial push, which is fine as a supplementary send but risky as a near-back-to-back repeat in a high-frequency brand's cadence.
- Solid execution, thin hook. This will perform on click-through for anyone who already wants the product, but the subject line and lack of any deal will suppress opens.

## What's working

- Before/after hero comparison labeled "e.l.f. filter" is a relatable, credible proof format that lands immediately.
- The numbered 5-step routine gives real, actionable product education rather than just a sales pitch.
- Chartreuse/lime color consistency visually links this send back to the original Humidity Hero launch, reinforcing brand recall.
- CTAs are placed at every stage of the tutorial, minimizing friction between education and purchase.
- Pricing is shown plainly ($9, $10) with no hidden fine print.

## What's weak

- Subject line ("Spritz. Dry. Frizz goes byeeee") never names the product or states a concrete reason to open — pure vibes, low specificity.
- No preview text is visible/available to add context alongside the subject.
- This is functionally a rerun of the 7/30 launch email just 5 days later — same product, same "frizz" hook — with no new angle beyond "here's how to use it."
- Zero discount, urgency, or loyalty incentive anywhere in the email — it's full-price education, which is a weaker click driver than the brand's typical GWP/BOGO sends.
- No reviews, ratings, or social proof despite this being a trust-building, education-heavy format where it would help close the sale.

## Recommendations

- 1. Rewrite the subject to name the product or the payoff directly — vague taglines underperform against this brand's own GWP/flash-offer subjects (7/29, 7/31 both scored 8-9/10 with concrete mechanics).
- 2. Add a preview line that complements the subject with a concrete detail (e.g., the before/after claim) instead of leaving it blank.
- 3. Space this tutorial further from the 7/30 launch, or pair it with something new — a limited-time bundle, a review callout — rather than repeating the same product story within a week.
- 4. Add a star rating or short customer quote near the hero to build purchase confidence since there's no discount doing the convincing here.
- 5. Swap generic "SHOP NOW" repetition for product-specific CTA copy like "Shop the Spray" or "Get Frizz-Free Hair."
