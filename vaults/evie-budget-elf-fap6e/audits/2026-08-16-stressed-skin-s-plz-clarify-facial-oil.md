# Stressed skin 💚s PLZ Clarify Facial Oil
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-16**
## Executive summary

- This is e.l.f. SKIN's clearest hero-product send yet in this batch — a real product, a real ingredient callout (2% salicylic acid, aloe extract), and a bottle shot big enough to actually read the label. "PORE REAL" is a decent pun, and "breaking the skinternet with its soothing, pore-refining power" at least tells you what the product does, which most of this brand's sends this week haven't bothered to do.
- But the hero offer gets undercut immediately: right below the product block sits a second, unrelated banner pushing the Bronzing Drops GWP — the same GWP already sent standalone on 8/13 ("Get Bronzing Drops on us ✨," 7.00/10). Two competing CTAs in the first two scrolls means neither wins, and it dilutes what should be a focused new-product story. The bottom two-thirds is filler: a "Top Picks for You" grid with no obvious relevance and six collapsed accordion-style category bars that add scroll without adding information.
- Net: a stronger single-product story than most recent e.l.f. sends, dragged down by the bolt-on GWP block and a bloated lower half. Middle-of-the-pack for this brand.

## What's working

- The hero product shot is large, in-focus, and the label is fully legible — "PLZ CLARIFY FACIAL OIL," "CLARIFY + CALM," "2% salicylic acid acne treatment + aloe extract."
- Subject and hero headline both name the actual product, unlike several recent sends that lead with vague hype.
- One clear primary CTA ("SHOP NOW") sits directly under the hero copy with no scroll needed.
- The green backdrop and bottle photography feel premium and on-brand for a skincare-forward SKIN sub-line.

## What's weak

- The Bronzing Drops GWP banner competes with the hero offer for attention and is a repeat of the 8/13 send — no reason for a subscriber to care twice.
- No price is shown anywhere for the Plz Clarify Facial Oil itself — a skincare buyer evaluating a new active ingredient product wants to see the cost before clicking.
- "Top Picks for You" shows three unrelated products (makeup remover, thirst-drops spray, cleanser) with no visible personalization logic, which reads as generic filler rather than curated picks.
- The six gray category bars (NEW/EYES/LIPS/FACE/SKINCARE/HAIR) are visually flat and take up a huge amount of vertical space for zero content.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the Bronzing Drops GWP block from this send entirely (or move it to a footer mention) — let the Clarify Facial Oil own the email, since GWP has already had its own dedicated send.
- 2. Add the price under the hero product name/CTA — a specific price is table stakes for a purchase decision on a new SKU.
- 3. Replace "Top Picks for You" with actual skincare-adjacent cross-sells (e.g., other Clarify + Calm line items) so it reinforces rather than distracts from the hero.
- 4. Shorten or visually enliven the six category bars — icons or thumbnail images would make them worth the scroll.
