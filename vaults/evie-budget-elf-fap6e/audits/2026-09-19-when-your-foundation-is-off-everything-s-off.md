# When your foundation is off, everything’s off. ✨
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-19**
## Executive summary

- This is e.l.f.'s routine-builder send for Soft Glam Satin Foundation, Concealer, and Bronzer/Blush, and it's the most disciplined structural execution I've seen from this brand's recent sends. The step 1/Cover, step 2/Conceal, step 3/Blend cascade is genuinely well-built: each product gets its own hero shot, a clear numbered badge, one-line benefit copy, and a repeated "Shop Now" button — no ambiguity about what to buy or why. It also cross-references the "e.l.f.scape Unglammy Valley" campaign (2026-09-09, 7.00/10) as a "Watch Now" tie-in, which is smart reuse of an asset that already tested well.
- Where it drags is length and repetition — six nearly identical "Shop Now" CTAs before you even reach "Top Picks for You," plus a full flat category nav (New/Eyes/Lips/Face/Skincare/Hair) that adds scroll without adding decision-making value. The subject line itself is a clever double meaning ("when your foundation is off, everything's off") but it undersells the concrete 3-step routine payoff. Solid send, competent but not a standout — 6.5/10 territory, dragged down mostly by bloat rather than by any execution flaw.

## What's working

- Clear numbered step structure (Cover → Conceal → Blend) makes the routine easy to follow and mentally file.
- Each product has its own price point visible ($8, $5, $7) — no hidden cost, no "starting at" vagueness.
- Hero image (dark spot treatment close-up) is arresting and on-theme with "when your foundation is off."
- "Top Picks for You" section adds a personalization/cross-sell layer with visible prices.
- Reuses the Unglammy Valley creative as a mid-email tie-in rather than a bolted-on afterthought.

## What's weak

- Six repeated "Shop Now" buttons in a row create CTA fatigue — nothing distinguishes the primary action.
- No visible bundle/kit offer or discount despite three complementary products — pure product push, no incentive.
- The flat gray category nav (New/Eyes/Lips/Face/Skincare/Hair) is filler; it pads scroll length without helping this specific email's goal.
- Subject's clever wordplay doesn't map cleanly to what's inside — reader can't predict "3-step routine" from the subject alone.

## Recommendations

- 1. Consolidate the three-step CTAs into a single "Shop the Routine" bundle CTA up top, then keep individual buttons secondary/smaller — reduces redundant clicks and pushes basket size.
- 2. Cut or collapse the flat category nav block — it's dead weight in a routine-focused send.
- 3. Add a routine bundle price ($8+$5+$7 = $20, framed as "get the full routine for $20") to give a concrete purchase incentive.
- 4. Tighten subject to name the routine directly so open intent matches content.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 3-step Soft Glam routine, $20 total`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Cover. Conceal. Blend. Done. ✨`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Foundation, concealer, bronzer — one routine, three steps.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Build flawless Soft Glam base in under 5 minutes.`
