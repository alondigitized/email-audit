# NEW! Set it the ✨bright✨ way
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-15**
## Executive summary

- This is e.l.f.'s launch send for the Set It Bright powder quad + velvet puff ($12), and it's the most product-clear NEW launch email in this batch of sends — a real named product, a real price, and a genuinely legible "why" (color-correct and brighten shades in one compact). The hero close-up model shot with the "new" pill badge lands the launch moment well, and the second section actually explains how to use the product (solo or swirled) with clean annotated callouts on the quad itself.
- Where it falls apart is pacing and focus: after two full "shop now" pushes for the same $12 quad, the email pivots hard into a Bronzing Drops GWP banner (already the subject of the 8/13 send, "Get Bronzing Drops on us"), then a three-item "Top Picks" grid, then six flat category nav bars before the footer. That's four distinct offers/asks stacked in one email, and the launch product gets buried under recycled promo real estate by the time you're two-thirds down.
- Net: strong top, cluttered bottom. This would score well if it stopped after the second "Shop Now."

## What's working

- Clear NEW badge + close-up beauty shot immediately signals "new launch," not a generic promo.
- Product name, format, and price are all stated in the hero copy: "Set It Bright powder quad & velvet puff, $12."
- Annotated product diagram (color-correcting shades / brightening shades) teaches the product instead of just showing it.
- Second lifestyle shot with the velvet puff applicator reinforces the specific use-case (bake & set) rather than a generic face shot.
- Cruelty-free/vegan badges and "e.l.f. is for every eye, lip, and face" reinforce brand trust cues near the close.

## What's weak

- The Bronzing Drops GWP banner recycles the exact hero of the 8/13 send verbatim, muddying this email's identity as a launch email.
- Four separate CTAs compete (quad x2, Bronzing Drops, Top Picks x3, category nav) — no single dominant action by the time you scroll past the fold.
- The six category nav bars (New/Eyes/Lips/Face/Skincare/Hair) are flat gray text blocks with zero visual interest, dead weight before the footer.
- "Top Picks for You" claims personalization but shows generic bestsellers (lip liner, primer, setting spray) unrelated to the Set It Bright launch — a mismatch between "for you" framing and undifferentiated products.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after the second "Shop Now" (end of the velvet puff section) or replace the Bronzing Drops/Top Picks/category-nav block with a single reinforcing CTA — this email doesn't need to also carry the GWP promo, that's what 8/13's dedicated send is for.
- 2. If Bronzing Drops stays, retitle it to explicitly connect the two ("stack it with your new powder quad") so it reads as a launch bonus, not a competing offer.
- 3. Swap "Top Picks for You" for genuinely related shades/tools (other Set It Bright shade options, a brush) if personalization is claimed.
- 4. Turn the flat category nav bars into a 2-3 column icon grid — cheap visual upgrade, currently pure filler.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Meet your new bake-and-set essential — shades for every undertone.`
