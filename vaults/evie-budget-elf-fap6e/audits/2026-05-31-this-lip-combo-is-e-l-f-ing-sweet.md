# This lip combo is e.l.f.ing sweet 🫶🍭 
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-31**
## Executive summary

- e.l.f. leads with a clean, punchy hero: **"LINE & SHINE WITH THIS $12 LIP COMBO"** anchors a pink/magenta gradient render showing the Cream Slide Lip Liner Palette + Glow Bestie Melting Lip Balm Pen. The $12 price is front-and-center in the headline — no hunting, no scroll. The shade module below ("YOUR GLOW-TOS:") names all four shades with swatches, which is exactly the kind of specific detail that converts. That's the strongest section of the email.
- The momentum then stalls. A mid-email Target receipt upload rewards module breaks purchase intent at the worst possible moment — right after the first SHOP NOW. The bottom of the email has a "Top Picks For You" personalization grid that is so small the products are unreadable. By the time the email ends, the focused lip combo message has been diluted into a three-section department store.
- This is a solid 6-day-of-7 send from a brand running at maximum cadence. Yesterday's Allure viral certification email was genuinely differentiated; this one is competent but disposable. The subject leans on wordplay over information, and there's no urgency or deadline anywhere in the body.

## What's working

- **$12 price in the hero headline** — no ambiguity about the value proposition, visible before any scroll.
- **Shade naming in the GLOW-TOS module** — Cotton Candy, Fuchsia Forward, Peach Ring, Spill the Tea are evocative and specific, matching the playbook that worked in the 5/27 Sheer For It Blush Tints email.
- **Pink/magenta aesthetic is on-brand and high-energy** — consistent with e.l.f.'s visual identity and the recent product color story.
- **Dual product naming in subhead** — calling out both Cream Slide Lip Liner Palette AND Glow Bestie Melting Lip Balm Pen by name sets up a clear bundle narrative.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no layout collapse, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **Target partnership module kills purchase momentum** — it appears immediately after the first SHOP NOW and hijacks the intent loop with an off-brand task (upload a Target receipt).
- **"Top Picks For You" grid is unreadable** — three product thumbnails at bottom-of-email thumbnail size, prices barely visible, no click reason to distinguish them from the main offer.
- **No urgency anywhere** — no deadline, no "limited shades," no "while supplies last." The 5/26 Main Stain Lip Marker email had the $5 price as urgency signifier; this one has nothing equivalent.
- **Subject buries the price** — "e.l.f.ing sweet" is a fun pun but a subscriber on their sixth consecutive e.l.f. email this week would see nothing in the subject to break the scroll.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is at least the seventh send in seven days. Even a strong offer lands softer when the inbox has e.l.f. in it every single morning.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the price in the subject.** The single highest-lift change. "$12 gets you both" or "Liner + Lip Balm. $12. That's it." forces the value prop into the inbox decision window. The pun can stay as the preheader.
- 2. **Move or remove the Target module.** If it must be included, push it to post-footer or after the "Top Picks" section. Mid-email is a conversion killer. At minimum, put a hard visual divider between it and the purchase CTA.
- 3. **Replace "Top Picks For You" with a single shade-focused CTA.** At email length, the personalization grid is noise. A single "Find Your Glow-To Shade →" link to the shade quiz or combo PDP would outperform four unreadable thumbnails.
- 4. **Add a mild urgency signal.** "New this week" in the hero sub-copy or "Shades selling fast" as a micro-copy line under SHOP NOW — something to make the send feel time-sensitive without fabricating a deadline.
- 5. **Tighten "YOUR GLOW-TOS" into a direct visual comparison.** Show both products side by side for each shade, not just the liner. The Glow Bestie Balm Pen is half the combo but only implied.

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard Braze-built table-layout email with a custom redirect domain and Google Fonts loading. Several structural and accessibility issues are present; compliance section cannot be fully verified from the truncated source.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Custom redirect domain is properly configured** — all links route through `links.elfcosmetics.com` (not a generic ESP subdomain).

**UTM parameters unverifiable from source.** All destination URLs are Braze-encoded opaque blobs:
```
https://links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/5zcfUnOkhsex9c6S3KW9yA~~/AAQRxRA~/6uqyaSYn...
```
UTM params may be appended post-redirect, but cannot be confirmed without decoding or a live click-trace. This is a standard Braze pattern but worth flagging for QA validation.

**`tabindex="-1"` on every linked image** — the hero image anchor uses `tabindex="-1"`:
```html
<a href="https://links.elfcosmetics.com/..." tabindex="-1">
```
This removes image-wrapped CTAs from keyboard tab order. If this pattern repeats across all image links, keyboard-only users cannot activate any image-based CTA.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag.**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers and some preview panes use `<title>` as document label. Should be set to the subject line or a short description.

**Double-wrapped `nl-container` inside `html_block > div`.** The outer layout wraps inner `html_block` cells in a `<div>`, then re-opens a full `nl-container` table tree inside it. This nested-table-in-div pattern can cause Outlook width calculation failures.

**`<style>` blocks embedded inside `<td>` elements.** Gmail and several webmail clients strip `<style>` tags that appear outside `<head>`. The responsive `.responsive-text` `@media` query is declared inside a `<td>`-nested `<div>` — it will silently fail in Gmail.

**Google Fonts load will fail universally for email clients.**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail, Outlook (all versions), Yahoo, and Apple Mail block external `<link>` stylesheets. The fallback `Arial, Sans-serif` will render in all clients; the `Open+Sans` load is wasted overhead.

**`Futura Std` / `Jost` declared but never loaded.**
```css
font-family: 'Futura Std', Futura, 'Jost', Verdana, sans-serif;
```
Neither `Futura Std` nor `Jost` is included via any `@font-face` or external `<link>`. Rendering falls through to `Verdana` in all clients.
