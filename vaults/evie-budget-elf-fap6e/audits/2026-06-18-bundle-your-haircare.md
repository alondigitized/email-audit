# Bundle your haircare 💁‍♀️
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- This is e.l.f. Hair's bundling follow-up to the June 16 category-launch email, and the pivot is smart in theory: you've introduced the sub-brand, now move units with a value angle. The creative leans into the brand's wordplay register ("your bundles, bundled") and surfaces four distinct bundle SKUs with clear dollar prices — a value-anchoring move that makes sense for a new sub-brand converting curious first-timers into buyers.
- The structural problem is clutter. Four separate bundle modules each carry their own "SHOP NOW," so the eye doesn't know where to land and there's no editorial "buy this one" pull. The implicit savings logic — a bundle presumably costs less than buying separately — is never made explicit. Customers won't do math they don't have to, and without a "save $X" callout, the bundle offer reads as a product shelf rather than a deal.
- The cadence risk is real: this is the second e.l.f. Hair email in three days. Subscribers who opened the June 16 intro are already aware of the sub-brand; this feel like a catalog push before any relationship has been established with the new line.

## What's working

- **Dollar-anchored pricing visible in the hero** — $16, $27, and $19 price points are immediately legible; no friction hunting for costs
- **"NEW ARRIVALS" badge** — correctly signals fresh inventory and reinforces that this is a launch-phase drop, not a clearance push
- **Four bundles signal a real product line** — moisture, frizz, wonder, and mane-core naming implies genuine category thinking, not a single novelty SKU
- **On-brand creative language** — "your bundles, bundled" is textbook e.l.f. pun register; consistent with the June tone set by "WHAT THE H.E.L.F.?" on June 16
- **"Top Picks for You" footer** — curated picks section adds a personalization note even if it's editorial rather than algorithmic

## What's weak

- **No savings callout** — bundles are priced but there's no "save $X vs. buying separately," so the value proposition is implied, not felt
- **Four competing CTAs fragment attention** — every bundle has its own "SHOP NOW" button; no single offer wins and none of them carry urgency
- **Sub-brand sender-name mismatch** — "e.l.f. Hair" in the from-field is different from the "e.l.f." name subscribers recognize; that delta costs opens before anyone reads a word
- **No urgency mechanism anywhere** — no deadline, no limited-edition signal, no low-stock flag; nothing to push action now vs. next week
- **Second hair email in three days** — rapid follow-up on a brand-new sub-brand risks subscriber fatigue before the Hair line has built any inbox goodwill

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one anchor bundle and make it the hero.** The "Never Thirsty Duo: $16" is the most accessible entry price — front-load it as a single recommended buy with a lifestyle shot, then let the other three live in a secondary row below. One recommendation beats four options every time in email.
- 2. **Add an explicit savings line.** Even something like "worth $22 separately — yours for $16" converts the bundle from a price point into a deal. This is the highest-leverage change in the email.
- 3. **Collapse to one primary CTA.** Replace four module-level "SHOP NOW" buttons with a single "Shop All Bundles" at the bottom of the bundle grid.
- 4. **Move from "e.l.f. Hair" to "e.l.f." in the from-name.** Sub-brand lives inside the email; the from-field should match what subscribers whitelisted.
- 5. **Add one credible urgency lever** — "new launch, limited stock" or a "selling fast" signal on the hero bundle.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Save on e.l.f. Hair bundles — from $16`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your first e.l.f. Hair purchase should be this bundle`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Four bundles, four price points — starter-friendly from $16.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Moisture, frizz control, and hold — find your bundle match.`

## Technical Audit — e.l.f. Hair "Bundle your haircare" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Table-based layout with MSO conditional comments, sent via Braze infrastructure (`braze-images.com`, `links.elfcosmetics.com`). HTML source is truncated, so the footer/compliance block and all CTAs beyond the header logo could not be fully audited — flagged findings below reflect only what is visible.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Sender domain / local-part mismatch**
`From: "e.l.f. Hair" <makeup@m.elfcosmetics.com>` — the local-part `makeup` was likely inherited from the parent brand's sending config and does not match the Hair sub-brand. Not a deliverability blocker, but can surface in phishing-detection heuristics and confuses inbox preview.

**Click-tracking domain**
All links route through `links.elfcosmetics.com` (Braze click wrapper). Correct and expected, but the encoded URL for the logo CTA is exceptionally long (~700 chars). If any ESP relay truncates query strings at 2 KB, this is at risk. Verify the redirect resolves cleanly end-to-end.

**Final destination URLs unverifiable**
Because all hrefs are Braze-encoded redirects, the actual landing page URLs and their UTM parameters cannot be confirmed from the HTML source alone. See §6.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers and some email clients (notably VoiceOver on iOS Mail) announce the document title. An empty title produces silence or a fallback string. Should be set to a descriptive value matching the subject line, e.g. `<title>Bundle your haircare — e.l.f. Hair</title>`.

**Logo link removed from tab order**
```html
<a href="..." tabindex="-1">
```
Keyboard and assistive-technology users cannot navigate to the logo/header link. If this is intentional (decorative), the image `alt` should be `alt=""` rather than `alt="e.l.f."`. As written, it's contradictory: the image has meaningful alt text but the link is unfocusable.

**Global link decoration reset**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This strips underlines and color from all links in Samsung Mail and some Outlook WebApp versions. CTAs relying only on text (no button background) will be invisible as links in those clients.

**Nested `nl-container` tables**
The outer `nl-container` wraps an inner identical `nl-container` inside an `html_block`. This double-nesting is redundant and can cause Outlook column width miscalculations at 640 px. No functional breakage confirmed from the truncated source, but worth a targeted Outlook rendering test.

**`html_block` blocks 2–4 are empty**
Three consecutive `<div>` blocks (`block-2`, `block-3`, `block-4`) contain only whitespace. These render as phantom spacing rows and add unnecessary DOM weight. Remove or consolidate.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**HTML truncated — footer not auditable**
CAN-SPAM requires: (a) physical postal address, (b) clear unsubscribe mechanism, (c) honest subject line. The subject line ("Bundle your haircare 💁‍♀️") is non-deceptive. Physical address and unsubscribe link could not be confirmed because the source was cut off before the footer. **These must be verified against the full render.**

**Authentication headers not provided**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment cannot be assessed from HTML alone. Given the sending domain is `m.elfcosmetics.com` (a dedicated marketing subdomain), DKIM signing should be scoped to that subdomain. Request header dump from a received copy to verify `dkim=pass` and `dmarc=pass`.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM Params, Landing Page Alignment)

**UTM parameters unconfirmable from HTML**
All click URLs are Braze redirect tokens. The encoded destination and any UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content`) are not readable in the raw HTML. Confirm via Braze link audit or by resolving the redirect chain manually:

```
https://links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/8RqRqO7AMIk0uM9Yc0impw~~/...
```

Expected minimum: `utm_source=email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=<campaign_id>`.

**Logo CTA destination unknown**
The header logo is the only confirmed link in the visible HTML. Its landing page alignment (homepage vs. haircare landing page) cannot be verified. Best practice: logo → brand homepage; body CTAs → campaign-specific landing page.
