# Curl the e.l.f. up with this eye-ssential duo 👀
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- This is e.l.f.'s first eye-product pivot in the current June run — after five consecutive sends on complexion and skincare (skin tint, highlighter, hydration spray, blushes, Best Friends Day duos), the category rotation is welcome. The step-1/step-2 duo structure is the strongest thing here: it positions a $6 curler and a $9 mascara as a routine, not a random pairing, and that framing justifies opening your wallet for both. The hero eye close-up is the most visceral, high-production photo e.l.f. has used in this sprint.
- The weakness is that this reads as an informational product announcement, not a promotional send. There's no discount, no deadline, and no member pricing — you're converting on product merit and brand trust alone. At e.l.f.'s prices ($15 total for the duo) that's arguably fine, but the email doesn't lean into the value story. Compare to the June 11 Halo Glow Highlighter send, which led explicitly with the "bougie dupe for a fraction of the price" framing — that email converted the value prop into the copy. This one just shows prices and says SHOP NOW.
- Seven e.l.f. emails in one week is the ceiling on subscriber patience. This needed either an urgency hook or a value angle to justify the cadence — neither is present.

## What's working

- Step-1/step-2 format makes the duo feel like a routine, not an upsell — concrete and scan-friendly
- Hero eye close-up is arresting and aspirational; best product photography in this June run
- Individual prices are legible ($6 curler, $9 mascara) without requiring the reader to do bundle math
- Product copy ("lock in curl and lift that lasts," "extreme curl & mega volume") speaks to a specific lash goal, not marketing filler
- "Peep the ultimate curl combo" headline is short, punchy, and action-oriented

## What's weak

- No discount, deadline, or urgency mechanism — purely informational; nothing forces a decision
- "SHOP NOW" appears three times with zero variation; CTA copy does no selling
- No social proof visible: no star ratings, no "bestseller" badge, no user imagery on either product
- "Top Picks for You" personalization row is visually buried at the bottom, undersized, and easy to scroll past
- Cadence: seventh e.l.f. email in seven days means this competes with itself for inbox attention

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a value or urgency hook to the hero** — "the $15 curl duo" or a "limited run / back in stock" badge would shift this from an announcement to a promotional reason to act today. That single change does the most lift.
- 2. **Differentiate CTA copy per product** — "Get the Curler – $6" and "Get the Mascara – $9" turn the three generic SHOP NOW buttons into price-anchored CTAs that reinforce value at the point of click.
- 3. **Surface social proof** — a star rating on the Incredi-curl Mascara and a "bestseller" or "cult favorite" label on the curler would address the trust gap without adding complexity.
- 4. **Promote the personalized row** — move "Top Picks for You" higher in the email or increase its visual weight; personalized modules get click rates that a product-category grid cannot.
- 5. **Rework the subject** to carry a value signal alongside the wordplay:
- **Subject Alt A:** `Curl + mascara — both under $10 each 👀`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The $15 lash duo e.l.f. swears by`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Step 1: $6 curler. Step 2: $9 mascara. The two-step curl routine.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Pro Eyelash Curler + Incredi-curl Mascara. Lock in the lift.`

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This is e.l.f.'s first eye-product pivot in the current June run — after five consecutive sends on complexion and skincare (skin tint, highlighter, hydration spray, blushes, Best Friends Day duos), the category rotation is welcome. The step-1/step-2 duo structure is the strongest thing here: it positions a $6 curler and a $9 mascara as a routine, not a random pairing, and that framing justifies opening your wallet for both. The hero eye close-up is the most visceral, high-production photo e.l.f. has used in this sprint.

The weakness is that this reads as an informational product announcement, not a promotional send. There's no discount, no deadline, and no member pricing — you're converting on product merit and brand trust alone. At e.l.f.'s prices ($15 total for the duo) that's arguably fine, but the email doesn't lean into the value story. Compare to the June 11 Halo Glow Highlighter send, which led explicitly with the "bougie dupe for a fraction of the price" framing — that email converted the value prop into the copy. This one just shows prices and says SHOP NOW.

Seven e.l.f. emails in one week is the ceiling on subscriber patience. This needed either an urgency hook or a value angle to justify the cadence — neither is present.

## 2. What worked

- Step-1/step-2 format makes the duo feel like a routine, not an upsell — concrete and scan-friendly

- Product copy ("lock in curl and lift that lasts," "extreme curl & mega volume") speaks to a specific lash goal, not marketing filler

## 3. What didn't

- "SHOP NOW" appears three times with zero variation; CTA copy does no selling
- No social proof visible: no star ratings, no "bestseller" badge, no user imagery on either product
- "Top Picks for You" personalization row is visually buried at the bottom, undersized, and easy to scroll past
- Cadence: seventh e.l.f. email in seven days means this competes with itself for inbox attention

3. **Surface social proof** — a star rating on the Incredi-curl Mascara and a "bestseller" or "cult favorite" label on the curler would address the trust gap without adding complexity.
4. **Promote the personalized row** — move "Top Picks for You" higher in the email or increase its visual weight; personalized modules get click rates that a product-category grid cannot.
5. **Rework the subject** to carry a value signal alongside the wordplay:
   - **Subject Alt A:** `Curl + mascara — both under $10 each 👀`

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

- **UTM chain is opaque** (see L1). The Braze redirect will resolve to a final `elfcosmetics.com` URL; confirm the redirect destination carries `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content` before send. A broken UTM at the redirect layer will drop all attribution for this campaign in GA4/analytics.
- **Image CDN domain is `braze-images.com`**, not `elfcosmetics.com`. If a recipient's security gateway blocks third-party image hosts, the header image will fail to load — no fallback background color is set on the containing `<td>`. Set a brand-color `background-color` on the image cell as a defensive fallback.

### 7. Recommendations

1. **Audit full HTML** — the provided source is truncated. Confirm physical address, unsubscribe link, and all CTA links are present.
2. **Verify UTM parameters** by following at least one Braze redirect link to the final landing URL and confirming campaign parameters are present.
3. **Remove empty block-2/3/4 tables** — they add dead markup and potential spacing artifacts.
4. **Set `background-color` on the logo `<td>`** to the brand color as a fallback when `braze-images.com` images are blocked.
5. **Pull raw message headers** (From an inbox capture or Braze test send) to verify DKIM alignment and `List-Unsubscribe` header presence before evaluating deliverability risk.
6. **Consider removing `tabindex="-1"` from the logo link** if keyboard accessibility is in scope.
