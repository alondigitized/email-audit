# All hits, no skips. 🫰
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a "weekly roundup" grab-bag email — six unnamed skincare products under "YOUR NEW OBSESSIONS," four unnamed lip products under "HOT AS E.L.F. THIS WEEK," then a recycled Bronzing Drops GWP banner that's literally the same offer from the 2026-08-13 "Get Bronzing Drops on us" send. Nothing in this email has a product name, a price, or an ingredient callout visible anywhere in the two hero grids — just thumbnails and "SHOP NOW" buttons stacked in rows. Compared to recent standouts like the 08-16 PLZ Clarify send (real product, real ingredient, legible bottle) or the 08-15 Set It Bright launch (named product, $12 price), this is a step backward into generic catalog-dump territory.
- The subject "All hits, no skips. 🫰" promises curation and confidence but the body delivers the opposite — an anonymous product wall with no single hero to anchor attention. The only concrete, honest offer in the whole email is the Bronzing Drops GWP, and it's a repeat from five days ago. This reads as a low-effort "clear the queue" send, not a targeted push.

## What's working

- The hero image is a clean, well-lit lifestyle shot with a product visibly in hand (bronzer compact), giving at least one recognizable item early.
- Two clear top-of-email CTAs ("SHOP BEST SELLERS" / "SHOP NEW ARRIVALS") give an immediate choice without forcing a scroll decision.
- The Bronzing Drops GWP band is a genuinely honest offer — no code needed, clear $50 threshold, clear deadline in the footer fine print (8/31 2:59am ET).
- Category nav bar (New/Eyes/Lips/Face/Skincare/Hair) at the bottom is a nice low-friction fallback for browsers who didn't click a product.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, grids align, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- Every single product in both grids (10 items total) is unlabeled — no product name, shade, or price anywhere in the visible layout, so the "hits" in the subject are unidentifiable at a glance.
- "HOT AS E.L.F. THIS WEEK" shows four near-identical pink/red glossy sticks with zero differentiation — no way to tell what's actually different between them.
- The Bronzing Drops creative is a repeat of the 08-13 send with the same product, same offer, same copy — this is the second time this exact GWP has run in under a week.
- No social proof, star ratings, or review counts anywhere in the email despite two full product grids.
- The subject line's "no skips" promise is undercut by a body that gives the reader no information to judge whether anything is actually a "hit."

## Recommendations

- 1. Put names and prices under every product tile in both grids — this is the single biggest lift available; right now the email asks for a click on faith alone, no product info to justify it.
- 2. Swap the repeated Bronzing Drops banner for a fresh offer or at minimum a different visual treatment — running the identical GWP twice in five days reads as recycled content to anyone who opened the 08-13 email.
- 3. Cut the four-lip-gloss grid down to 1-2 items with an ingredient or finish callout ("high-shine," "hydrating") so "HOT AS E.L.F." actually differentiates the picks.
- 4. Add a star rating or "X,XXX reviews" strip under at least the top-row skincare grid to borrow social proof, matching what worked well in recent single-hero sends.
- 5. Tighten the subject to reference an actual category or product instead of a vague superlative.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New lip drops + a gift for you 🫰`
- **Subject Alt B:** `10 picks, 1 gift with $50`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Full-size Bronzing Drops free with $50+ — plus this week's new arrivals.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your skincare and lip lineup for the week, curated.`
