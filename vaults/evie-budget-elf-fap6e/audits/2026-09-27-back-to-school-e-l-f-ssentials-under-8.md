# Back-to-school e.l.f.ssentials under $8 😍
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-27**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, well-executed multi-product bundle send anchored on "back-to-school e.l.f.ssentials" — pairing Clear Brow & Lash Mascara ($4) and Sheer For It Blush Tint ($6) for "$10 together." The hero-to-body cascade is tight: bold headline, clear discount math, then two product sections each with their own hero shot and CTA, followed by a GWP banner and a music tie-in. It's dense but organized, similar in structure to 2026-09-25's "beauty slaylist" send.
- The back-to-school framing is a slight miss for persona relevance since it skews younger/student-focused rather than a general beauty consumer, but the concrete "$10 for both" offer and sub-$8 individual price points are genuinely compelling and easy to scan. Overall a solid, on-brand execution let down mainly by a cluttered lower half and a seasonal hook that doesn't fully land.

## What's working

- The "$10 for both" bundle math is immediately visible in the hero and reinforced with individual $4/$6 pricing on each product block.
- Two distinct hero images (mascara wands fanned out, lipstick/blush tubes scattered) are visually clean with no render bugs or overlapping text.
- The GWP banner ("full-size Clear Brow & Lash Mascara on us when you spend $50+, no code needed") is a strong secondary incentive placed logically after the core offer.
- "Top Picks for You" section adds a personalization signal with three additional SKUs and prices.

## What's weak

- "Back-to-school" is a dated/narrow seasonal hook for late September — it reads as leftover campaign language rather than a fresh seasonal tie-in.
- The album/music banner ("MIRROR MIX," "Dropping a New Album") is a jarring non-sequitur wedged between the GWP offer and Top Picks, diluting focus.
- The bottom third (category nav buttons, trust badges, shipping/scanning icons, social icons) is a lot of gray-box clutter before the footer — visual fatigue sets in.
- No visible model demographic cues beyond the single hero image at top, so it's unclear this is speaking to a back-to-school/student audience at all.

## Recommendations

- 1. Drop or reframe "back-to-school" — with fall in full swing, swap to a straight "get both for $10" value hook without the seasonal label, or commit harder to it with a student/dorm visual.
- 2. Cut the album/music banner from this send entirely — it competes with the actual offer and isn't personalized to a beauty-focused persona.
- 3. Consolidate the bottom category-nav block (NEW/EYES/LIPS/FACE/SKINCARE/HAIR) — six full-width gray buttons is excessive; convert to a single row of pills.
- 4. Lead with the GWP ($50+ free mascara) higher up if it's the stronger incentive, since free-with-purchase often outperforms a $10 bundle for average order value.

## 1. Technical Summary
The email uses standard Braze-generated MJML/HTML with an ESP link-tracking wrapper (`links.elfcosmetics.com`); automated QA returned only a 27% pass rate, primarily due to four broken destination links, a missing/empty plain-text MIME part, and absent unsubscribe/authentication headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 4 of 12 probed material links fail resolution with "unknown url type" errors. The tracking redirector (`links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/...`) is unwrapping to **relative paths instead of absolute URLs**, e.g.:
  - `/products/power-grip-primer?Size=Regular+0.8+oz+%2F+24+ml` ("Shop Now")
  - `/products/cream-glide-lip-liner?Color=Mauve+Aside+-+Warm+Rose+Mauve` ("Shop Now")

- These are missing the `https://www.elfcosmetics.com` (or equivalent) host prefix before the redirect target was encoded — a link-building/template bug, not a dead page per se, but it will render as a broken/unresolvable link in most click-tracking and preview tools, and may fail in-app deep-linking on the mobile CTA.
