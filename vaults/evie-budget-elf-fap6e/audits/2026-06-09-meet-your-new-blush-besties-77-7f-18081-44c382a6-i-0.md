# Meet your new blush besties 💖
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- This email is structured as a blush-finder — three product sections, each positioned for a specific coverage preference and skin goal. That comparison mechanic is genuinely useful, and it sets this apart from a straight product push. The hero model is clear, the price points ($7–$9) are e.l.f.'s sweet spot, and the three-section layout scans cleanly on mobile. If you're shopping blush, the email is actually helpful.
- The problem is context. This is the third send in three days working the exact same "besties" theme — June 7's "Must-duos for your skin" and June 8's "The ultimate beauty besties" already stretched it thin. By day three, "besties" reads like a stuck record, not a campaign, and the comparison angle — the email's actual strongest idea — gets lost inside an overused concept.
- There's also no hook to act now. No sale, no limited availability, no urgency. The email invites browsing with no reason to buy today. For a format built around "find your fit," that's a conversion left on the table.

## What's working

- **Chooser format** — each section frames a product as the right pick for a specific skin type, which is service-oriented and differentiated from a plain new-launch push
- **Specific prices visible** — $7, $9, and $8 shown inline; e.l.f.'s value pricing does real persuasion work on its own
- **Hero model is on-category** — woman holding a blush product, immediate visual lock on the category
- **Clean three-section layout** — one product per block, easy to scan without reading every word
- **No render bugs** — layout is intact, images load, type is legible throughout

## What's weak

- **Third "besties" email in three days** — the June 7 "Must-duos" and June 8 "ultimate beauty besties" already saturated the concept; this send has no room to breathe
- **All three CTAs say "SHOP NOW"** — zero differentiation across three distinct products that are supposedly competing for your attention
- **No urgency anywhere** — no deadline, no "new launch" badge, no limited-availability flag
- **The comparison angle never surfaces in the subject** — the email's actual hook is buried; the subject sounds like a third chorus, not a useful tool
- **No social proof** — no review stars, bestseller labels, or buyer counts to tip hesitant clickers

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject around the chooser angle** — the email's real value is "here's how to find your blush match," not "more besties." A specificity-first subject lifts open rates and sets the right expectation before the open.
- 2. **Differentiate the CTAs** — replace the three identical "SHOP NOW" buttons with "Shop Soft Glam — $7," "Shop Halo Glow — $9," "Shop Camo — $8." One click, right product, no detour through PDP disambiguation.
- 3. **Add one urgency signal** — a "New Drop" badge on the Soft Glam Cream Blush (which appears to be the newest SKU based on the June 5 send), a limited-shade callout, or a time-bound free shipping threshold.
- 4. **Rest the "besties" theme** — this is the third back-to-back send on the same concept. Either shift to a different creative territory or give the theme a one-week break before it loses all signal power.
- 5. **Add a social proof hook** — a single "4.8★" line or "fan-favorite" label per product section would meaningfully lift click confidence, especially for a low-AOV brand where reviews are the main conversion lever.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`tabindex="-1"` on logo link** — removes the element from keyboard navigation order:
```html
<a href="..." tabindex="-1" style="outline:none">
```

**`outline:none` on the same link** — eliminates the focus ring for keyboard/switch users. Combined with `tabindex="-1"`, this link is completely inaccessible to non-pointer users.

**Google Fonts `<link>` tag will not render in production clients:**
```html
<!--[if !mso]><!--><link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" ...><!--<![endif]-->
```
Gmail (web and Android), Apple Mail on iOS, and Outlook all strip or block external `<link>` stylesheets. Open Sans will silently fall back to Arial in the majority of recipients' inboxes. This is a known email limitation, but the fallback stack (`Open Sans, Arial, Sans-serif`) is correctly specified.

**Three consecutive empty HTML blocks** (`block-2`, `block-3`, `block-4`) contain only whitespace `<div>` wrappers — likely leftover spacing artifacts from a Braze drag-and-drop template. They add ~300 bytes of dead markup and no visual or functional output.

**Nested `nl-container` table structure inside `html_block block-1`** — the inner block embeds a full standalone email table layout (including its own `nl-container`, `row-1`, `column-1`). This is valid but indicates the block was likely copy-pasted from another template rather than built natively, which increases nesting depth and parse complexity in older Outlook versions.
