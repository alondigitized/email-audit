# Go from frizz to rizz 💁‍♀️
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- This is a new-product launch email for the Humidity Hero Anti-Friz Styling Spray at $9 — a clean, energetic send from e.l.f. Hair. The hero does exactly what it needs to: model with great hair, bold "FROM FRIZZ TO RIZZ." headline, product name, price, and CTA all visible above the fold. The lime/yellow color palette is punchy and on-brand, and the seasonal timing (late June = peak humidity) is genuinely smart.
- The cadence, however, is getting heavy. This arrives on June 27 — the sixth e.l.f. email in five days and at least the fifth dedicated e.l.f. Hair send this month. The "frizz to rizz" wordplay is fresh, but even solid creative loses lift when subscribers are conditioned to ignore the next wave. The June 23 free-shipping email and the June 26 "last chance" follow-up already created a complete arc; pivoting straight back to a hair product launch the very next day undercuts both sends.
- The "Top Picks for You" personalization grid is a real own-goal: it surfaces skincare and face-prep products (a Pink Gradated Toner Serum and Power Grip Primer) inside a hair-focused email. That cross-sell breaks the narrative momentum right when the subscriber should be clicking into hair products.

## What's working

- **"FROM FRIZZ TO RIZZ." headline** — punchy, readable, and the alliterative hook earns the 💁‍♀️ emoji; feels earned rather than tacked on.
- **$9 price in the hero** — "Only $9" as visible hero copy removes price friction before the CTA; effective for a new launch.
- **Seasonal relevance** — late-June + humidity control is precise timing; the angle is actually earned this time of year.
- **"Meet the mane crew" secondary module** — good merchandising of the full e.l.f. Hair lineup; positions this as an ecosystem, not a one-off SKU.
- **No render bugs visible** — layout is clean, images load, text is legible at thumbnail scale.

## What's weak

- **"Top Picks for You" is off-persona** — surfacing skincare and face-prep products inside a hair email breaks the funnel at the worst moment; it signals the personalization engine isn't segmenting by email context.
- **Cadence fatigue** — sixth email in five days; the June 26 "Shipping's on us! 📦 last chance!" already felt like a frequency ceiling, and this pushes past it.
- **Zero urgency signal** — the hero has no "just launched today," "limited launch," or time-bound language; $9 forever is less motivating than $9 right now.
- **Generic CTA copy throughout** — "SHOP NOW" appears twice without any verb specificity; "Shop the Humidity Hero" or "Get it for $9" would be stronger.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the "Top Picks" segment logic** — when the email campaign tag is `elfhair`, the product recommendation grid should filter to the hair category only. A skin toner and face primer underneath a frizz-control hero is actively confusing.
- 2. **Add a launch urgency signal** — even lightweight copy like "Just dropped" or "New this week" in the hero sub-copy would increase click intent; the $9 price does the heavy lifting but urgency closes it.
- 3. **Rewrite the CTA to match the product** — change "SHOP NOW" → "Shop Humidity Hero" on the primary button; reserve "Shop the crew" for the secondary module.
- 4. **Throttle cadence or merge the next hair send** — if there's a follow-up planned, fold it into a broader summer hair edit rather than a seventh dedicated send.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers and some email clients (Outlook desktop, Apple Mail) surface the `<title>` as document identity. An empty title degrades screen reader experience and may trigger spam heuristics.

**Duplicate nested `nl-container` structure**
Inside `html_block block-1`, a second full `nl-container > row > row-content > column` table hierarchy is nested. This appears to be a copy-paste artifact from the template builder — the header image is rendered inside a `div` wrapper that contains a redundant full email scaffold. Produces unnecessary DOM depth and inflated byte size.

**Google Fonts `<link>` stripped by most clients**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail (all variants), Outlook, and Samsung Mail strip external `<link>` tags. The fallback `Arial, Sans-serif` is present and will render, but Open Sans will never load for the majority of recipients. Low severity given the fallback, but the `<link>` fetch is wasted.

**`#MessageViewBody a` removes link decoration globally**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This Gmail-targeting selector (`#MessageViewBody` is Gmail's wrapper) strips underlines and color from all anchor tags in Gmail. CTAs relying on text color or underline for affordance will lose that visual cue for Gmail users.

**`blocks 2–4` are empty**
`html_block block-2`, `block-3`, `block-4` render only whitespace `<div>` elements. These are dead template slots adding ~600 bytes and extra DOM nodes. Should be removed before send.
