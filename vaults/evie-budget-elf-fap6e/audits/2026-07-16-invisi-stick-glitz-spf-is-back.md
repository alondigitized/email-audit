# Invisi-Stick Glitz SPF is back ☀️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a restock alert for the Invisi-Stick SPF 30: Glitz Edition — a limited-edition, glitter-finish SPF product being positioned as a summer festival essential. The golden hero is gorgeous and immediately communicates the product's aesthetic promise. Visually it's one of the stronger executions in e.l.f. SKIN's recent run: the "#ULTRAGLOW" badge, warm-toned model, and sparkling product render all cohere into a deliberate summer moment.
- The weakness is structural, not aesthetic: there's no price anywhere in the hero or the feature section. For a brand that has built its entire equity on democratic pricing ("$6–$14 for great skincare"), burying the product cost is a meaningful missed opportunity. The hero CTA "OUTWARDLY YES" is delightfully on-brand but genuinely ambiguous to anyone who hasn't been steeped in e.l.f.'s copy voice. Compare this to July 11's "Cream Team" send — which front-loaded "$7 each, $14 bundled" — and you can feel the conversion energy missing here.
- Bottom line: this is a pretty, well-composed restock email that will drive brand love but leaves conversion points on the table. Good, not great — and after seven emails in eight days, subscriber fatigue is real.

## What's working

- **Hero aesthetic is on-point**: gold palette, glittery product render, and a warm-toned model create instant season/category clarity for a summer SPF with a glitz finish.
- **Headline lands**: "SPF that gets glitz done." is specific, playful, and earns the real estate — no wasted words.
- **Benefit bullets are concrete**: "no white cast," "clear or sun-kiss tones," "glides smooth" address real SPF anxieties directly below the hero.
- **#ULTRAGLOW badge** contextualizes the limited-edition positioning without requiring body copy to explain it.
- **"Top Picks for You" grid** adds browsing depth for subscribers who landed on the email but weren't moved by the hero.

## What's weak

- **No price in the hero or feature block**: for a brand that routinely leads with its affordability ($8, $10, $12), omitting the price is a structural miss. The Gloss Mode restock on July 10 showed "$10" in the hero and scored 8/10 — this one doesn't.
- **"OUTWARDLY YES" is ambiguous**: witty, yes, but it doesn't tell you what you're clicking to. "OUTWARDLY YES" to what? A product page? A quiz? First-time e.l.f. subscribers won't have the cultural context.
- **No urgency mechanism**: back-in-stock carries implicit scarcity, but there's no "limited quantity," deadline, or stock counter anywhere visible.
- **Cadence pressure building**: this is the seventh e.l.f. SKIN email in eight days (July 9–16). Even the best creative starts to erode at this frequency.
- **"Find your skincare" quiz block dilutes focus**: a full mid-email acquisition module competes with the product story; it belongs in the footer or a separate send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add the price to the hero** — "$12. All the glitz, none of the white cast." One line below the headline would sharply lift click intent. e.l.f.'s price is a differentiator; hiding it is leaving the brand's main weapon holstered.
- 2. **Rewrite the primary CTA**: swap "OUTWARDLY YES" for something like "Shop the Glitz Edition" or "Get Invisi-Stick SPF" — keep the voice but add the action.
- 3. **Inject scarcity signal**: "Limited quantities — last restock sold out fast" is credible because it's true. One line of subtext earns the urgency without feeling manufactured.
- 4. **Move the quiz block to the footer** or cut it entirely from this send. It splits the conversion path at exactly the moment when the subscriber is deciding.
- 5. **Consider dropping send frequency** by one day. At seven emails in eight days, even engaged subscribers are heading toward fatigue — and the restock pattern on Gloss Mode (July 10: 8/10, July 14: 6/10) already shows a measurable decay curve.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Braze-encoded redirect URLs obscure final destinations**
All CTAs route through `links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/…~~/AAQRxRA~/…` — Braze's click-tracking format. The actual destination URL is base64-encoded inside the token. UTM parameters cannot be confirmed without decoding. If UTMs are appended post-redirect at the Braze level, they will be invisible to link validators and QA tooling that don't follow the full redirect chain.

**Recommendation:** Decode a sample link and confirm UTM params are present on the final landing page URL.

**Image asset hosted on `braze-images.com`**
```
src="https://braze-images.com/appboy/communication/assets/image_assets/images/6537d6e7f775160058956f5a/original.png?1698158310"
```

The `appboy` path prefix is Braze's legacy CDN namespace (Appboy was Braze's prior name). This is functional but indicates the asset predates a potential CDN migration. ### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
The document title is blank. Some email clients (notably Apple Mail on macOS) surface the title in thread previews or tab labels. This is a minor gap.

**Google Fonts `<link>` will fail in most clients**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" rel="stylesheet" type="text/css">
```
Web font loading via `<link>` is blocked by Gmail, Outlook (all versions), Yahoo Mail, and most corporate clients. The stack fallback (`Open Sans, Arial, Sans-serif`) means rendering degrades gracefully, but the font load is a dead HTTP request for the majority of recipients. This is inside `<!--[if !mso]><!--> … <![endif]-->` so it is at least suppressed in Outlook — correct pattern.

**Three consecutive empty `html_block` elements**
```html
<table class="html_block block-2" …><tr><td class="pad"><div … > </div></td></tr></table>
<table class="html_block block-3" …><tr><td class="pad"><div … > </div></td></tr></table>
<table class="html_block block-4" …><tr><td class="pad"><div … > </div></td></tr></table>
```
Blocks 2, 3, and 4 contain only a non-breaking space. These appear to be leftover placeholder rows from the BEE editor. They add ~300 bytes of dead markup and create invisible 0-height rows that some clients may render with default cell padding.

**Preheader present and correct**
```html
<div style="display:none !important;visibility:hidden;mso-hide:all;…">Returning by e.l.f.ing demand!</div>
```
Properly hidden with belt-and-suspenders CSS and MSO hide. No issues.

**Logo alt text is minimal but present**
```html
alt="e.l.f." title="e.l.f."
```
Technically sufficient; images-off rendering will show "e.l.f." No issues.
