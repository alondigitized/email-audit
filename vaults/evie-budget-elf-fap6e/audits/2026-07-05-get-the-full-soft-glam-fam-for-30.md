# Get the full Soft Glam fam for < $30! 🤑
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- This is a confident "build the routine" email — e.l.f. packages the entire Soft Glam face routine into four numbered steps (Cover It → Conceal It → Blur It → Bronze It) and anchors the whole thing at $27. The format is smart: it educates while it sells, and the numbered structure makes the collection feel like a curated system rather than a random grab bag.
- The comparison to the July 1 "lip combos for only $20" send is hard to avoid — same mechanism, same value-pricing frame, slightly higher ticket. That send scored a 9 partly because it came one day after a new-launch email and gave the category breathing room. This one follows the July 4 SPF email, which was seasonally precise and arguably the strongest send in the recent run. By contrast, today's email is an evergreen bundle play with no seasonal hook — competent and well-executed, but not the sharpest creative this brand has put out this week.
- The one concrete flaw: no urgency anywhere in the email. There's no "this weekend only," no countdown, nothing that creates a reason to act today vs. next Tuesday. For a price-value play, that's a missed lever.

## What's working

- **Clear, specific hero value prop:** "GO FULL SOFT GLAM FOR ONLY $27" is immediately legible — the price and the product line are both in the headline.
- **Numbered routine structure:** Framing four products as steps in a complete look is smarter than a product grid — it explains how to use everything and removes the "but what do I do with all this?" hesitation.
- **Individual SHOP NOW per product:** Each step has its own CTA, so a subscriber who only wants the bronzer isn't forced through a bundle checkout.
- **Cohesive product selection:** Cover → Conceal → Blur → Bronze is a logical makeup order — the email teaches and sells simultaneously.
- **Clean render, no visual bugs** across hero, product rows, and footer.

## What's weak

- **Zero urgency signals:** No "ends Sunday," no "while supplies last," no countdown. A bundle price with no deadline is easy to bookmark and forget.
- **Subject/hero price gap:** Subject says "< $30," body says "ONLY $27." This isn't harmful but the body should match the subject's framing or reinforce it — not quietly revise it.
- **Generic CTAs across all four steps:** Every button says "SHOP NOW." Missed a chance to mirror the step name — "Get Coverage," "Blur It," "Bronze Now" would reinforce the routine language and feel less copy-pasted.
- **No seasonal anchor:** The July 4 SPF email was strategically tied to outdoor weekend exposure. Today's send feels like it could have gone out any Tuesday in March. A "post-holiday glam reset" hook would have given it a reason to exist today.
- **"Top Picks for You" footer dilutes focus:** The category links (Skin, Eyes, Face, Skincare) at the bottom scatter attention after a tightly focused four-product email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add urgency to the hero — this is the single biggest lift.** "48 hours only" or "Weekend price" next to the $27 would meaningfully raise CTR with no creative rework.
- 2. **Match CTAs to the step names.** Replace "SHOP NOW" on each row with the step verb: "Cover It →", "Conceal It →", "Blur It →", "Bronze It →". Reinforces the routine story and makes the email feel intentional top-to-bottom.
- 3. **Show individual retail prices next to each product** so the subscriber can see the bundle math. If each product is $8–9 individually, showing "usually $36, today $27" makes the value explicit.
- 4. **Add a seasonal hook in the preheader.** The July 4 weekend is over — "post-holiday" or "fresh start to the week" framing connects this to today's send date.
- 5. **Cut or tighten the "Top Picks" footer.** Either remove it (keeps Soft Glam the hero throughout) or replace with a single "Shop All Soft Glam" row.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Build the full Soft Glam look for $27 (this weekend)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cover it. Conceal it. Blur it. Bronze it. Four products, one look, $27 total.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The complete Soft Glam routine is $27 — here's how to build it step by step.`

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The Braze redirect pattern (`~/AAQRxRA~/`) is standard and will pass through to the destination URL — but only if UTM parameters were configured in the Braze campaign settings. Without resolving the redirect URL, destination alignment (correct landing page, UTM source/medium/campaign values) cannot be confirmed. This is the highest-risk gap in this audit.

---

### 7. Recommendations

1. **Resolve the Braze redirect URL** and confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` are present on the destination — this is the most impactful item.
2. **Add a meaningful `<title>`** (e.g., the subject line) for accessibility.
3. **Remove `tabindex="-1"`** from the logo link, or document why it's intentional.
4. **Audit `#MessageViewBody a` rule** — if text CTAs exist elsewhere in the email (not visible in truncated source), this rule will make them invisible as links in Gmail webmail.
5. **Append `&display=swap`** to the Google Fonts URL.
6. **Encode `<` as `&lt;`** in the subject line to eliminate any parsing edge cases.
7. **Verify the footer** (not in truncated source) contains: physical address, unsubscribe link, and any required legal copy.
