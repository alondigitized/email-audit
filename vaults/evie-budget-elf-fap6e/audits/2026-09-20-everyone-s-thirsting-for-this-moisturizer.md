# Everyone’s thirsting for this moisturizer 🤩 
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-20**
## Executive summary

- This is e.l.f. SKIN's send for Holy Hydration! Daily Moisturizer, and it's the cleanest, most confidence-inspiring hero I've seen from this brand's recent run. Two glossy tubes suspended mid-splash in aqua-blue water is a genuinely arresting product shot — it communicates "hydration" visually without needing a headline to explain it, and it doesn't have the uncanny-valley or acne-close-up problems that dinged the 9/16 and 9/9 sends. The bold "EVERYONE'S THIRSTING FOR THIS" headline, price callout ($9), and single white "SHOP NOW" pill button are unambiguous and sit right where the eye lands.
- Where it falls down is everything after the hero: a "Top Picks for You" row is nice, but then the email dumps into six flat gray category-nav bars (New/Eyes/Lips/Face/Skincare/Hair) that add scroll depth without adding sell. That's dead weight compared to the tight, well-paced cascades in the 9/18 lip-duo send. Still, the hero-to-CTA mechanics here are the best-executed part of any send I've reviewed from this brand, and it earns a solidly above-average score.

## What's working

- The splash/water hero visually reinforces "hydration" and "thirsting" without relying on the copy to do the work.
- Single, unambiguous CTA ("SHOP NOW") in a high-contrast white pill against the blue hero band.
- Concrete price point ($9) is stated right in the hero subhead — no hunting for it.
- "Top Picks for You" cross-sell row shows three related products with prices and their own CTAs, giving a second, lower-friction path to purchase.
- No render bugs — clean gradients, product photography sharp, text crisp.

## What's weak

- Six identical gray category bars (New/Eyes/Lips/Face/Skincare/Hair) add pure scroll length with zero visual interest or product content.
- No social proof — no review stars, no "e.l.f.ies" quote, no UGC despite the headline claiming "everyone's" obsessed.
- No urgency or time-bound offer — this is a permanent price point, not a promo, so there's nothing pushing an immediate click.
- Subject emoji (🤩) plus "Everyone's thirsting" is a slightly odd, mildly cringe pairing that undersells a genuinely strong product story.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the six gray nav bars entirely or compress them into one horizontal icon row — that vertical real estate should go to a second product benefit (ingredient callout: hyaluronic acid + vitamin E) or a star-rating/review snippet instead.
- 2. Add a review count or star rating near the hero or in the Top Picks row — the headline claims universal obsession but shows zero proof.
- 3. Give the moisturizer its own ingredient/benefit bullet (e.g., "24-hr hydration, hyaluronic acid + vitamin E") directly under the hero — right now the $9 price is the only supporting detail.
- 4. Test a subject line that leads with the ingredient or benefit rather than the "everyone's thirsting" bit, which reads a little try-hard next to a genuinely good visual.
