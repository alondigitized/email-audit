# NEW! Soft Glam Cream Bronzer, $7 🤎
**Score:** 10/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a sharp product launch for the Soft Glam Cream Bronzer ($7) — a new addition to e.l.f.'s Cream Team franchise that debuted July 11. The hero is doing real work: a bronzed model holding the product above a clean headline, shade ladder, and price. The $7 is never hidden, and the Cream Team cross-sell below is smart sequencing — it gives a new subscriber context and a returning subscriber a reason to bundle. The ingredient callout ("Ingredients for Butter Skin") leans into e.l.f. SKIN's skincare-adjacent positioning, which has been consistent through the Toner 101 and Power Grip sends.
- Where this softens: there are five distinct content sections (hero, shade ladder, ingredients, Cream Team, Top Picks), which is a lot of real estate for a $7 single-SKU launch. And for any subscriber who got the July 11 "Cream Team" email — which also featured a Glow Cream Bronzer at $7 — there's a real risk of confusion between the two bronzers. That distinction is never explained.
- Honest verdict: a well-executed, on-brand product launch that earns its score. The price does the heavy lifting; the creative execution backs it up. The main opportunity is tightening the email to the hero + CTA without the full five-section treatment.

## What's working

- **$7 is front-loaded** — it's in the subject line and repeated in the hero, so the value lands before the scroll.
- **Shade ladder** (light/medium → medium/tan → tan/deep → deep) adds purchase confidence and reduces friction around fit.
- **THE CREAM TEAM cross-sell** connects this launch to the July 11 franchise send — building habit and upsell context.
- **"Ingredients for Butter Skin" section** anchors the product in skincare-adjacent positioning, consistent with recent e.l.f. SKIN sends.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping copy, no lorem ipsum.

## What's weak

- **No urgency mechanism** — "NEW!" tells you it just launched but gives no reason to act now vs. next week.
- **"SHOP NOW" is generic** — for a product with specific shades, "Shop [Shade Name]" or "Shop All Shades" would sharpen the click.
- **Cream bronzer disambiguation is missing** — a subscriber who got the July 11 Glow Cream Bronzer email may not understand how Soft Glam differs. No differentiation copy visible.
- **Five sections is too long** for a $7 impulse item — the Cream Team cross-sell and Top Picks could live in a follow-up or be collapsed.
- **No price anchor** — $7 is remarkable for a cream bronzer but the email doesn't say so. "vs. $25+ department-store dupes" or similar would make the value land harder.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a value anchor to the hero** — "A cream bronzer for $7? Yes, really." or a brief "vs. $28 at Sephora" comparison line. The price is the sell; make the contrast explicit.
- 2. **Cut the email in half** — serve hero + shade selector + one CTA. Move the Cream Team cross-sell and Top Picks to a follow-up or cart abandonment flow.
- 3. **Swap "SHOP NOW" for a shade-specific CTA** — "Find Your Shade" or "Shop Medium/Tan" — gives the click a more specific destination.
- 4. **Add a soft deadline** — "Launching now — grab it before it sells out" or a 48-hour intro window. The Invisi-Stick Glitz send (July 16) showed scarcity language works.
- 5. **Distinguish Soft Glam from Glow Cream Bronzer** with one sentence in the hero sub-copy for subscribers who have both products in their inbox history.
- **Subject Alt A:** `NEW! $7 cream bronzer that looks like $27`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Soft Glam Cream Bronzer drops today — $7`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Pick your shade: light/medium to deep — only $7 a pop`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The Cream Team just got a new member. Grab it while it's in stock.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Opaque redirect chain — UTM unverifiable**
The logo/hero link resolves through `links.elfcosmetics.com` using a Braze-encoded redirect:

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Several email clients (Samsung Mail, Apple Mail) surface the title in thread previews. An empty value degrades the experience on those clients.

**`tabindex="-1"` on the hero link**
```html
<a href="..." target="_blank" style="outline:none" tabindex="-1">
```
This explicitly removes the primary CTA link from keyboard tab order, breaking keyboard-only and switch-access navigation. `outline:none` compounds the issue by hiding focus indicators even when focus lands there via other means.

**Google Fonts `<link>` stripped by Gmail**
```html
<!--[if !mso]><!-->
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" rel="stylesheet">
<!--<![endif]-->
```
Gmail (webmail and Android app) strips `<link>` tags from `<head>`. The fallback stack `Open Sans, Arial, Sans-serif` will engage — confirm Arial renders acceptably in affected clients during QA.

**Double-nested `nl-container` tables**
Inside `html_block block-1` there is a full inner `nl-container → row → row-content → column` stack embedded inside a `<div>` inside the outer container. This structural redundancy is a sign of template copy-paste error and may cause unexpected cell width calculations in Outlook 2016/2019.

**Three empty `html_block` elements**
`block-2`, `block-3`, and `block-4` contain only whitespace:
```html
<div style="font-family:Open Sans,Arial,Sans-serif;text-align:center" align="center"> </div>
```
These add DOM weight and can introduce phantom spacing in some clients. Remove or consolidate.
