# NEW! Set It Bright 🤩
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- "Set It Bright" is a shade-range launch for what reads as a tinted setting product with SPF — and e.l.f. executes the template competently. The hero is clean: model, product, a sharp pun ("the better setter"), and a coral CTA. The shade-swatch grid below is genuinely useful information for a category where shade matching matters. But this email commits the exact sin that made the May 17 Soft Glam Cream Blush launch an 8/10: that email had `$7` baked into the subject line. This one doesn't show the price anywhere in the visible hero — you have to scroll down past a retail-partner placement to find what amounts to the best offer in the email (a jelly-pop GWP at Target).
- The cadence is now a real problem. This is the sixth e.l.f. email in seven days, following a new lip serum, a Survivor collab, a weekly digest, the Rosé Setting Spray, and the Cream Blush. Even subscribers who love the brand are in tolerance-erosion territory. The email itself is fine; the send environment is working against it.
- The Target partnership section mid-scroll is an unexpected bright spot — a concrete GWP offer buried where it'll lose 60–70% of readers. That's a placement mistake, not a creative one.

## What's working

- **Hero pun lands.** "The better setter" is crisp, on-brand, and immediately tells you the product category.
- **Shade grid is legible and informative.** Six shades with clear skin-tone groupings (Fair → Deep/Rich) address the most common objection for a shade-range product.
- **CTA copy is playful and product-linked.** "GET IT BRIGHT" is more memorable than "Shop Now."
- **Target GWP offer is a real value signal** — "get a jelly pop for every stick" is the kind of concrete incentive that drives trial.
- **No render bugs or layout breaks visible** — the email is clean end to end.

## What's weak

- **No price in the hero or subject.** The May 17 blush email proved e.l.f.'s own template: `$7` in the subject line drove opens. This email leaves that lever unpulled.
- **Best offer is buried below the fold.** The Target GWP is one of the most compelling reasons to buy — it's sitting below the shade grid and above the footer. Most recipients won't see it.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** Sixth email in seven days; the subject "NEW! Set It Bright" reads similarly to "NEW! Power Grip Rosé Setting Spray" and "NEW! Soft Glam Cream Blush" — the template is wearing thin.
- **SPF callout is not prominent.** If this is a setting product with SPF, that's a genuine differentiator and it's not visible in the hero where it would drive urgency.
- **"LILAC HAZE" editorial section is confusing.** The copy isn't legible at render size and reads as filler between the shade swatches and the retail section.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the price in the subject.** The single highest-impact change. If Set It Bright retails at $X, say so. The Cream Blush formula (`NEW! [Product], $X`) works.
- 2. **Move the Target GWP offer to the hero secondary.** Run it as a subheadline or callout banner directly beneath the hero, not buried mid-email.
- 3. **Make SPF the hero differentiator.** A one-line callout — "with SPF XX" — in the hero anchors this product in the skincare-meets-makeup space where e.l.f. is underindexed.
- 4. **Thin the send cadence.** Consider batching the weekly digest with one new-product launch rather than sending both as separate emails. The brand's own May 19 highlight reel could have teased Set It Bright.
- **Subject Alt A:** `NEW! Set It Bright — SPF setting in 6 shades`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your setter just got brighter ✨ ($X)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Get a free Jelly Pop with every Set It Bright at Target — today only`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The shade-range setter with SPF you didn't know you needed`
