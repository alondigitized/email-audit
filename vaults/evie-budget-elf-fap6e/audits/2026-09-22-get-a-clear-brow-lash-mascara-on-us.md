# Get a Clear Brow & Lash Mascara on us 😚👀
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-22**
## Executive summary

- This is e.l.f.'s GWP (gift-with-purchase) send tied to spending $50+, and it's a genuinely strong, high-intent hero: a close-up of a model applying a mascara wand to her own brow, with a "GIFT FOR YOU!" arrow callout pointing right at the applicator. It's one of the more visually confident opens in this brand's recent run — the eye/brow crop is sharp, in-focus, and the product connection is instantly legible without needing caption text to explain it.
- Where it falls apart is structural greed: after the GWP hero, the email keeps going through four more distinct product pushes (lip balm duo, lip oil stick, Halo Glow Collection, Top Picks) before finally hitting the nav grid. The GWP offer — the actual subject line promise — gets buried under a pile of unrelated new-launch content, which dilutes the one thing this email should be optimized for: getting people to hit $50 and grab the free mascara.
- Net: excellent hero execution, poor prioritization. If this were trimmed to GWP + one cross-sell, it'd be an 8+. As sent, it's a solid open but a diffused click.

## What's working

- The hero image is sharp and single-focus: a real close-up eye/brow shot with the mascara wand mid-application, not a flat product-on-white shot.
- "GIFT FOR YOU!" with a directional arrow is a clean, low-copy way to call out the free-item mechanic visually.
- The $50+ threshold and "no code needed" friction-remover are both stated clearly in the hero copy block.
- Color-blocking (black hero, red-orange for lip balm, blush-pink for Halo Glow) gives each section visual distinction as you scroll.
- Each section follows a consistent hero-image → headline → CTA button pattern, so despite the volume of sections it doesn't feel chaotic.

## What's weak

- Five separate product pushes before the nav grid is too many — the GWP promise from the subject line gets lost among lip balm, lip oil, and Halo Glow launches.
- No visible countdown, date, or "ends soon" language anywhere near the GWP offer, so there's no urgency reinforcing the "online only" tag.
- "Top Picks for You" claims personalization but shows generic Power Grip/Cream Glide items with no visible signal of why these were picked for this recipient.
- The GWP hero never restates the $50 threshold near a clear progress cue (e.g., "just add to cart" is passive — no visual sense of how close a typical cart already is).

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email down to GWP hero + one cross-sell section max, then straight to nav — right now the offer this email is named for competes with three unrelated launches for attention.
- 2. Add a real deadline under "online only" (e.g., "today only" or a date) to convert the GWP into a time-bound reason to act now, not just eventually.
- 3. Move "Top Picks for You" directly under the GWP hero, framed as "add these to hit $50" — turn it into a functional cart-builder instead of a generic afterthought at the bottom.
- 4. Repeat the GWP mascara visually right before the final CTA/nav block as a closing reminder, since by the time a reader scrolls past lip balm/lip oil/Halo Glow, the opening offer is likely forgotten.
