# 6 HOURS ONLY ‼️ $10 Halo Glow Liquid Filter!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- This is e.l.f.'s Memorial Day flash sale email — a sharp departure from the daily product-launch cadence that's dominated the last six sends (Set It Bright, Jelly Pop, Power Grip Rosé, Survivor collab, Soft Glam Cream Blush). The format change is smart: a single-product flash deal with a time wall reads as more urgent and distinct than another "NEW!" drop. The hero executes cleanly — product bottles in a warm flatlay, a punchy headline, and a friction-free offer ("no code needed"). That's the ceiling of what works here.
- The floor drops fast below the fold. The Beauty Squad welcome-code banner at the very top hijacks the inbox preheader — so the flash sale urgency in the subject is immediately undermined by an unrelated loyalty-code pitch before the email body even begins. Then, inside the email, two secondary offers (the Jelly Pop glow stick + the Target receipt-upload program) compete with the flash sale hero and scatter click intent. An email that should scream "six hours — one thing — act now" instead asks the reader to make three decisions.
- Bottom line: the top half earns attention; the bottom half bleeds it. Fix the preheader and cut the competing modules, and this would be the strongest email in this batch.

## What's working

- **Hero is immediate and clean:** The Halo Glow Liquid Filter bottles in multiple shades dominate above the fold; the offer mechanics ($5 off, no code, 5–11 PM PST) are visible without scrolling.
- **"No code needed" reduces friction at the point of decision:** Explicitly stated next to the CTA — removes the copy-paste tax that kills flash sale conversions.
- **Flash sale format breaks cadence:** After six product-launch emails this week, a time-gated price drop reads as genuinely different, not more of the same.
- **Memorial Day hook on the Jelly Pop module:** The "Memorial Day weekend long" framing is timely and gives the secondary offer its own narrative hook rather than just stacking another promo.
- **Beauty Squad loyalty banner is visible:** Members know their status is in play from the first pixel.

## What's weak

- **Preheader is completely off-message:** The top banner welcome-code offer ("YOUR WELCOME OFFER IS WAITING! USE CODE...") is what subscribers see in their inbox next to the subject line — it contradicts the flash sale urgency entirely.
- **Three competing CTAs fragment the email:** Flash sale → Jelly Pop glow stick → Target receipt upload. Each is a different action, a different brand context, and a different time horizon. The urgency of "6 hours only" is diluted by two offers that have nothing to do with that countdown.
- **"SHOP NOW" is generic:** The same copy appears on both the flash sale and the Jelly Pop modules, making the buttons visually interchangeable. The Halo Glow CTA should name the product or the deal.
- **Subject line ALL CAPS + ‼️ reads as spam-adjacent:** "6 HOURS ONLY ‼️" is the right message with the wrong execution. Spam filters and trained inboxes alike treat all-caps urgency and stacked punctuation as signals.
- **No social proof:** Halo Glow is one of e.l.f.'s most-reviewed products — a quick "4.8★" or "fan favorite" callout near the hero CTA costs nothing and reinforces the click decision.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader immediately** — the welcome-code banner is appearing as inbox preview text and killing the flash sale subject line. The first readable text in the email body should reinforce the urgency, not redirect to a loyalty code.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `No code needed. Halo Glow for $10 — today, 5–11 PM PST only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `$10 Halo Glow. Ends tonight at 11 PM PST. No code, no catch.`
- 2. **Clean up the subject line spam signals.** Drop the ALL CAPS and the ‼️ — the content is urgent enough on its own. Use mixed case and a colon structure that reads as credible, not screaming.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Today only: Halo Glow Liquid Filter for $10 — ends 11 PM PST`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Halo Glow hits $10 for 6 hours — no code needed`
- 3. **Remove the Target receipt-upload module from the flash sale email entirely.** It belongs in the weekly digest or a standalone loyalty email — here it breaks the urgency frame and asks the reader to do homework when they should be clicking.
- 4. **Rename the CTA button to match the specific offer.** Change "SHOP NOW" on the hero to `Get $10 Halo Glow` or `Score the Deal` — something that signals which SHOP NOW this is, since the same copy appears twice below.
- 5. **Add a one-line social proof callout near the hero.** "Over 10,000 reviews · 4.8★" or similar. Halo Glow has the receipts — show them.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Many spam filters penalize missing title tags. Set to a descriptive value (e.g., the subject line).

**Google Fonts `<link>` tags load in non-MSO clients but are stripped by Gmail**
```html
<!--[if !mso]><!-->
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" rel="stylesheet" type="text/css">
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Jost" rel="stylesheet" type="text/css">
<!--<![endif]-->
```
Gmail's rendering engine strips `<link>` tags from the `<head>` in all display modes. The font stacks (`Open Sans, Arial, Sans-serif` and `'Futura Std', Futura, 'Jost', Verdana, sans-serif`) will correctly fall back, but any pixel-precise typographic design relying on Jost or Open Sans will degrade in Gmail (the dominant client for this sender segment).

**Nested full email template inside `html_block`**
Inside `block-1`, a complete `nl-container` table structure is re-embedded — including its own `row`, `row-content`, `column`, and a second full `image_block`. This looks like the outer wrapper was not stripped before the block was inserted. Some email clients will double-render or apply conflicting widths.

**Conflicting responsive breakpoints**
- Main `<style>` in `<head>`: `@media (max-width:660px)`
- Inline `<style>` inside `html_block` (block-3): `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)`

These two breakpoints trigger at different viewport widths. Elements in block-3 won't collapse until 480px while the rest of the layout collapses at 660px — visible seam on mid-size screens (480–660px range, common on tablets).

**`tabindex="-1"` on logo link**
```html
<a href="…" target="_blank" style="outline:none" tabindex="-1">
```
This removes the logo link from keyboard tab order entirely, failing WCAG 2.1 Success Criterion 2.1.1 (Keyboard). Combined with `style="outline:none"`, any keyboard user cannot access the primary CTA if it is this link.

**Sparse alt text on hero image**
```html
<img … alt="e.l.f." title="e.l.f.">
```
The hero image is the primary communication vehicle for the offer. Alt text `"e.l.f."` provides no offer context for screen reader users or image-blocked clients. Should reflect the email offer (e.g., `"$10 Halo Glow Liquid Filter — App Exclusive, 6 hours only"`).

**`<style>` block injected inside `html_block` body (block-3)**
A `<style type="text/css">` block defining `.responsive-text` appears inside the email body HTML, not in the `<head>`. Gmail's inbox rendering (non-Promotions tab) strips body-level `<style>` blocks in some modes. The `.responsive-text` rule may be silently ignored.
