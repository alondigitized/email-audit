# Set It Bright for the right price. 🤑
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-26**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuine single-hero-product send with a real value hook: "$64 for a setting powder?! yeah bright" sets up "$12" as the punchline, comparing e.l.f.'s new Set It Bright powder quad against premium-priced competitors. It's a clean, honest price-anchoring play — the kind of specific, benefit-driven copy ("Blur, bake, brighten & correct") that actually differentiates from e.l.f.'s recent run of pun-heavy fragrance/scent sends (2026-08-25 Haircare, 2026-08-24 pickle lippie). The secondary "Bronzing Drops on us" GWP banner (spend $50+) adds a second concrete incentive without cluttering the top.
- Where it falls down is everything below the fold: six flat gray "NEW / EYES / LIPS / FACE / SKINCARE / HAIR" nav bars eat a huge amount of scroll with zero visual interest, and the "Top Picks for You" row is generic cross-sell (primer, lip liner, setting spray) that has nothing to do with the hero product or its use case (you'd expect a brush, a puff, or complementary face products).
- Net: strong hero, honest offer, weak back half. This lands mid-pack — better than the recent scent-fatigue sends on offer clarity, worse on layout economy.

## What's working

- The price-contrast hook ($64 vs $12) is a concrete, credible value signal, not vague "save big" language.
- Hero product shot is well-lit, single focal point, no clutter.
- Bronzing Drops GWP banner gives a clear secondary incentive with a specific threshold ($50+).
- Two full-width "SHOP NOW" CTAs above the fold are unambiguous and easy to tap.

## What's weak

- Six identical gray category bars (NEW/EYES/LIPS/FACE/SKINCARE/HAIR) create a long, visually dead stretch with no imagery or hierarchy.
- "Top Picks for You" products (primer, lip liner, setting spray) don't relate to the hero powder — a missed cross-sell opportunity for actually complementary items (brush/puff/face products).
- No price/percentage comparison of the "$64" competitor product shown — the claim is asserted, not visually substantiated.
- No social proof (ratings/reviews) anywhere near the hero product despite it being the star of the email.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the six flat category bars to a single 3-4 icon nav row or drop it entirely — it's pure scroll-tax with no conversion value.
- 2. Swap "Top Picks for You" to genuinely complementary items (blending brush, powder puff, translucent setting spray) to reinforce the hero purchase.
- 3. Add a star rating or review count under the hero product to back up the value claim.
- 4. Consider showing the "$64" comparison visually (crossed-out price or side-by-side) rather than only in the headline.
