# Welcome to e.l.f.!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-11**
## Executive summary

- This is a competent, well-structured welcome email doing exactly what a welcome email should do: confirm the relationship, deliver the signup offer immediately, and stack reasons to shop. The hero is tight — "WELCOME TO E.L.F. BESTIE!" with a promo code, dollar-off promise, and two CTAs visible above the fold on mobile. For a beauty brand with a distinct, playful voice, the creative is on-brand and the model photography is strong.
- Where it underdelivers is email 1's core job: getting the first purchase. The body sprawls across five distinct modules — four value props, an app download push, a Beauty Squad rewards pitch, and a product grid — before the reader ever has a reason to stop scrolling. That's a lot of competing asks. The promo code is static text rather than auto-applied, which adds friction precisely when motivation is highest.
- Verdict: a solid 7/10 welcome send. Better than most. Loses ground to module bloat and a subject line that wastes the one moment it has to say something compelling.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the offer in the subject line.** "Welcome to e.l.f.!" squanders the only moment the inbox sees. The $8-off hook belongs there.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$8 off your first order — welcome to e.l.f., bestie`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your $8 welcome gift is inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping too — use code KBWZ at checkout on $25+`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Meet your new beauty obsession. Claim your $8 off now.`
- 2. **Auto-apply the promo via CTA link.** Replace the static code with a URL that pre-fills the discount at cart. Remove the code field entirely from the hero — it signals friction before the click.
- 3. **Cut the app module from this send.** The app download is a second-email job (post-first-purchase). Removing it tightens the email to three modules: offer + value props → Beauty Squad → product grid. Better flow, less ask fatigue.
- 4. **Consolidate the "four reasons" copy.** Collapse "first access to new products" and "major rewards and surprises" into one point. Use the recovered space to surface a single hero product — one item the new subscriber should want, with real product detail visible.
- 5. **Make the product grid shoppable.** The NEW / EYES / LIPS / FACE / SKINCARE thumbnail row is too small to generate desire. Either enlarge two or three hero products with names and prices, or replace the grid with a curated "start here" single-product card.

## 1. Take

This is a competent, well-structured welcome email doing exactly what a welcome email should do: confirm the relationship, deliver the signup offer immediately, and stack reasons to shop. That's a lot of competing asks. The promo code is static text rather than auto-applied, which adds friction precisely when motivation is highest.

Verdict: a solid 7/10 welcome send. Better than most. Loses ground to module bloat and a subject line that wastes the one moment it has to say something compelling.

---

## 2. What stood out

The hero photography is the strongest asset here — a high-energy, close-cropped portrait of a smiling Black woman with bold, saturated makeup that mirrors what e.l.f. actually sells. The offer is legible at a glance: "$8 off + FREE Shipping on $25+" and the code is prominently displayed, not buried in fine print. The dual CTAs ("SHOP HOT BEAUTY" / "SHOP NEW ARRIVALS") give the reader a meaningful choice without being paralyzing.

The four-reason trust block works visually — icon + label pairs are scannable — though "first access to new products" and "major rewards and surprises" are the same promise dressed in different words. The Beauty Squad module is genuinely smart: it surfaces loyalty benefits to someone who hasn't shopped yet, planting a reason to return. The product grid at the bottom (NEW / EYES / LIPS / FACE / SKINCARE) is well-organized but too small to drive desire — thumbnails at this scale don't do the products justice.

The miss that sticks: the module count. Five distinct structural blocks in a welcome email is three too many. The app download push competes with the purchase funnel and the rewards pitch. A first-time recipient hasn't bought anything yet; asking them to download the app before they've checked out is premature. The promo code as copyable text — not auto-applied via click — will bleed conversions. And the subject "Welcome to e.l.f.!" is generic; it tells you nothing about the $8 offer sitting one click away.

## Technical Audit — e.l.f. Cosmetics Welcome Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound for a table-based layout but contains a confirmed nested `nl-container` duplication indicating a template-composition error. There are also mixed ESP artifact signals (Braze image CDN + Sailthru-style link tracking) that warrant investigation.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**ESP mismatch — high confidence signal.**

- Logo CTA uses Sailthru link-wrapping format: `https://links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/<token>~~/` — the `/f/a/` path prefix and `~~/` token delimiters are Sailthru's canonical redirect pattern.
- Logo image is hosted on Braze CDN: `https://braze-images.com/appboy/communication/assets/image_assets/images/6532bc91543698004cb67f6e/original.png?1697823889`

This combination — Sailthru link tracking wrapping a Braze-hosted asset — suggests either (a) an in-flight ESP migration where image assets were not re-uploaded to the new platform, or (b) a hardcoded Braze URL that survived a migration to Sailthru. If the brand is fully on one ESP, the image should be re-hosted there to eliminate the external Braze dependency.

**Stale image asset.** The `?1697823889` cache-buster is a Unix timestamp resolving to **October 20, 2023** — this asset has not been updated in ~2.5 years. Not blocking, but verify the logo hasn't been refreshed since then.

**Destination URLs not assessable** from truncated source — full link audit requires decoded redirect targets.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag.**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some email clients (notably Apple Mail and Outlook desktop) surface the `<title>` value. Empty title is a missed opportunity and may display as blank in client previews.

**Nested `nl-container` table — confirmed template composition error.**

The outer shell already establishes `class="nl-container"`, but the logo block embeds a second complete `nl-container` tree inside `block-1`'s `<div>`:
```html
<table class="nl-container" …>          <!-- outer -->
  …
  <td class="pad"><div …>
    <table class="nl-container" …>      <!-- inner duplicate -->
```
This creates redundant table nesting that can cause rendering drift in Outlook (extra cell spacing) and inflates HTML weight unnecessarily.

**Google Fonts `<link>` stripped by Gmail.**
```html
<!--[if !mso]><!-->
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" …>
<!--<![endif]-->
```
Gmail (web + app) strips `<link>` tags from `<head>`, so Open Sans will not render there. Fallback chain `Arial, Sans-serif` is functional — this is a rendering degradation, not a breakage.

**Futura (brand headline font) is not web-loaded anywhere.** The 72px headline uses:
```css
font-family: 'Futura Std ExtraBold', 'Futura Extra Bold', 'Futura', Verdana, Geneva, sans-serif;
```
Futura is not a system font on Windows or Android. Clients without it fall to Verdana — visually inconsistent with brand intent. No `@font-face` or web font `<link>` loads Futura.

**`tabindex="-1"` on logo anchor** — correct; removes the decorative link from keyboard tab order.

**`alt="e.l.f."` on logo image** — adequate and present.
