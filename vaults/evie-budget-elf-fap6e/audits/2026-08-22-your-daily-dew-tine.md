## What's working

- Three named SKUs with prices and specific benefit copy (pore-refining, hydrating gel serum, lip-plumping) — no lorem-ipsum, no placeholder gaps.
- Consistent pastel water-splash visual system ties the whole Thirst Burst line together and reads as a cohesive launch, not a mashup.
- "NEW!" badge correctly flags the Lip Treatment as the newest SKU, with scent/format detail ("3 delicious scents").
- "Top Picks for You" carousel with three additional priced items adds personalization signal without cluttering the primary offer.
- Clean category nav bar (New/Eyes/Lips/Face/Skincare/Hair) gives a fallback path if the hero products aren't a match.

## What's weak

- No percentage or dollar discount on any Thirst Burst product — it's a straight product launch, not an offer, despite the promo tone ("Thirst Things First").
- The Bronzing Drops GWP banner is the third near-identical run of this exact creative since 2026-08-13 — subscribers who opened last time will recognize the reused artwork instantly.
- Three consecutive "product + SHOP NOW" blocks in a row with no visual break creates a repetitive scroll — there's no clear single hero, just three co-equal offers.
- The GWP banner interrupts the Thirst Burst narrative with an unrelated bronzing product, muddying what this email is actually about.
- No urgency or deadline signal anywhere in the body — "for a limited time" on the GWP banner is the only nod to urgency and it's generic.

Pick one hero — lead with Thirst Burst Drops (the serum, the most differentiated SKU) as a single full-width hero with a launch discount or bundle price, then demote the toner/lip treatment to a secondary "complete the routine" row. Three equal-weight products with no pricing incentive gives the eye nowhere to land.

- 3. Add a bundle offer across the three Thirst Burst products (e.g., "get all three for $28") to justify the promotional subject/tone and give one unambiguous CTA.
