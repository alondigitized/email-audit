# Homepage snapshot · www.elfcosmetics.com · 2026-05-30
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is literally my brand, so I'm not exactly a neutral observer here — but even accounting for that, e.l.f. is hitting on the homepage right now. It's leading with new summer drops (creamy blush sticks, lip color, bronzers), the copy is written for me specifically ("e.l.f.ing heat" is very much our language), and the $14 Halo Glow Skin Tint callout in the banner is the kind of steal-alert that makes me stop mid-scroll. The vibe is: budget beauty brand that knows its girlies are paying attention.

## What's working

- **The promo banner is a product-specific steal alert.** "$4 Off! Try e.l.f.ie fave Halo Glow Skin Tint SPF 50 for only $14" names the product, names the price — that's how you stop a budget girlie mid-scroll. Halo Glow is already a cult item; $14 is a no-brainer for someone who knows it.
- **"e.l.f.ing heat" is exactly the right register.** It's punchy, it's Gen Z-coded, it doesn't try too hard. The brand voice is consistent and I immediately feel like this was written for me, not a 45-year-old copywriter's idea of me.
- **The product flat-lay is genuinely gorgeous.** Row of lip/makeup pencils on pink — the color range is visible, it looks editorial without looking expensive. That combo hits exactly the "affordable but make it cute" frequency.
- **Beauty Squad strip is immediately visible.** Even if the messaging is acquisition-first, the loyalty program is surfaced above the fold. That's more than most brands bother with.
- **Subhead does real work:** "Creamy blush and bronzer sticks, life-proof lip color & more" tells me exactly what's in the drop without making me hunt for it.

## What's weak

- **No model/person in the hero.** I see products only — no one wearing the blush sticks, no real-skin lip swatch, nothing to tell me how "creamy blush stick" actually looks on a face. I know these products but a new shopper can't visualize the payoff.
- **Beauty Squad strip is cut off on the right.** The second tile ("Scan… when you join Squad") is partially out of frame, which makes it look like a layout glitch even if it isn't. First impressions.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is extremely generic.** For a launch moment, "Shop the Drop" or "Shop New Arrivals" would be so much stronger — it reinforces the new-arrival urgency instead of just saying the most boring two words in e-commerce.
- **No shade count or variant hint.** The pencils in the flat-lay look like different colors but there's no copy that tells me how many shades are in the launch. That's a conversion signal e.l.f. is leaving on the table.
- **Zero social proof above the fold.** No star ratings, no "X,XXX reviews," no TikTok badge — the brand has enormous TikTok equity and none of it is visible at this scroll depth.

## Recommendations

- **Put a real person in the hero, even as a secondary image.** Side-by-side: product flat-lay on the left, a close-up lip/cheek swatch on a warm medium skin tone on the right. Budget girlies convert on "will this look good on me," not on "cool packaging."
- **Replace "SHOP NOW" with "Shop the Drop."** Tiny copy change, but it echoes the new-arrivals urgency built into the headline and feels intentional instead of template-default.
- **Swap the Beauty Squad strip message to member-first.** For logged-in users: show their points balance or a "You're X points from free shipping" nudge. The acquisition message is wasted on the people who already opened the app.
- **Add a TikTok/viral badge to the Halo Glow callout.** Something like "🔥 1M+ TikTok saves" next to the $14 offer. e.l.f. is famous for going viral — lean on that proof right in the banner where the deal lives.
