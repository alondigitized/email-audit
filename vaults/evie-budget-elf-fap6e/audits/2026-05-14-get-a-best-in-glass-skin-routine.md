# Get a best-in-glass skin routine 🌟
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- e.l.f. SKIN leans hard into the "glass skin" Korean beauty trend and packages it as a prescriptive three-step routine with dollar prices on every product. That's the right instinct — it gives the reader a clear path from open to cart. The email is clean, light-blue-dominant, and on-brand; no major visual breakage.
- The central weakness is that this is an editorial email masquerading as an offer email. Every CTA button says "Shop Now" and the prices shown ($11, $13, $9) are catalog prices, not promotional ones. There's no urgency, no discount, no reason to act today versus next week. The SPF education section and the Target callout fragment the narrative — by the time you hit the "Top Picks for You" grid at the bottom, the glass-skin routine story has dissolved entirely.
- Verdict: a well-produced brand-building email that will earn some clicks from skincare devotees, but leaves money on the table by confusing content marketing with a conversion send.

## What's working

- **Routine framing is persuasive.** Numbering the three steps (cleanse → serum → moisturize) creates a natural purchase justification — it's not one product, it's a system.
- **Price transparency.** Showing $11 / $13 / $9 at a glance signals e.l.f.'s core value proposition (accessible price point) without needing to claim a discount.
- **"NEW!" badge on the Glass Stick Serum Stick** draws the eye and creates mild novelty pull.
- **SPF humor block** ("not wearing SPF is comfortable…") is on-trend with Gen Z / millennial skin-care content — voice feels native rather than corporate.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds across the full-width mobile crop visible in the screenshot.

## What's weak

- **Zero urgency.** No sale end date, no limited stock, no "this week only" — nothing creates a reason to click before closing the tab.
- **"Shop Now" CTAs are generic.** Three identical gray buttons with identical copy — no differentiation by step, no verb-noun specificity.
- **SPF section disrupts the routine narrative.** It's a good piece of content, but it breaks the step-1/2/3 momentum and reads like a separate email stapled on.
- **Target partnership mention is buried and unclear.** It's unclear whether "at Target" means exclusive availability, a sale, or just general stocking — so it adds noise.
- **Bottom product grid (NEW / EYES / LIPS / SKINCARE) is unfocused.** Four category tiles at the bottom of an email positioned as a skin routine send dilutes the message.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a time-bounded offer to turn this into a conversion email.** Even "Free shipping on orders over $20 through Sunday" gives someone a reason to act now. The editorial routine framing is fine — pair it with a hook.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTAs to match the step.** Step 1: "Cleanse First →" / Step 2: "Try the New Serum →" / Step 3: "Lock It In →". Specificity lifts clicks.
- 3. **Cut or relocate the SPF block.** Move it to a dedicated send or append it as a P.S.-style footer; don't let it interrupt the routine story.
- 4. **Remove the product category grid.** It's the wrong content for this email's job. If SKU breadth is the goal, that's a separate send.
- 5. **Sharpen the subject to promise a concrete outcome or benefit.**
- **Subject Alt A:** `3 steps to glass skin — starting at $9`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your e.l.f. glass skin routine (steps 1–3 inside)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cleanser + new serum + moisturizer. Build the whole routine for under $35.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New Glass Stick Serum just dropped — here's how to use it.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`tabindex="-1"` on logo link** — the logo is the only interactive element visible in the truncated source and it is explicitly excluded from keyboard tab order:
```html
<a href="..." target="_blank" style="outline:none" tabindex="-1">
```
Keyboard-only users cannot activate this link. Remove `tabindex="-1"` or provide an equivalent accessible control.

**`#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none; }`** — this Gmail-targeting rule strips all link affordances (underline, color) for every anchor in the email body. Downstream links will have no visual indication they are clickable unless each link has inline style overrides.

**`<title></title>` is empty** — some clients (particularly webmail tabs and screen readers) display the title element. A descriptive value (e.g., `e.l.f. SKIN — Glass Slick Serum Stick`) improves accessibility and tab labeling.

**Google Fonts via `<link>`** — Open Sans is loaded from `fonts.googleapis.com` inside an `<!--[if !mso]>` conditional:
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" rel="stylesheet">
```
Web font loading is blocked in Gmail (all clients), Outlook (all versions), Yahoo Mail, and Apple Mail with remote content blocked. The fallback stack `Open Sans, Arial, Sans-serif` is correctly specified and will render safely, but this is a cosmetic note.

**No `lang` attribute on inner tables** — the outer `<html>` has `lang="en"`, which is correct. No issues within the visible scope.
