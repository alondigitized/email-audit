# Go Jumbo for 3X the makeup grip 💚 
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- This is a product launch email for the Jumbo Power Grip Primer ($34, positioned as a $53 value) — essentially a XL-size extension of e.l.f.'s bestselling Power Grip Primer franchise. The teal hero is clean, the value math is visible front-and-center, and the brand voice ("SIZE THE E.L.F. UP ON YOUR FAVE PRIMER.") is sharp and confident. The drag-queen model is a bold creative swing that reinforces e.l.f.'s inclusive positioning without clearly signaling demographic targeting.
- The mechanics are solid: two "GO JUMBO" buttons, benefit bullets that do real work ("3X the size of the OG," "$34 for a $53 value"), and a "Top Picks for You" grid that adds personalization texture at the bottom. Nothing is broken.
- The problem is cadence. July 13 was already a Power Grip email (the Rock Setting Spray). Four days later, we're back in the Power Grip franchise with a different SKU. The creative doesn't feel stale — it's a legitimately new product — but a subscriber who opened Tuesday's Power Grip send will arrive here with less energy. The other gap is urgency: there's no deadline, no limited-quantity framing, no reason to act today instead of next week.

## What's working

- **Value math is front-loaded and clear**: "$34 ($53 value)" appears in the hero — no scrolling required.
- **"3X the size of the OG"** is a concrete, scannable benefit that makes the upgrade case instantly.
- **Two CTA placements** — one above the fold in the hero, one after the benefit bullets — both without competing links nearby.
- **Benefit bullets are specific**: size comparison, price, hydration/grip function all visible without reading the fine print.
- **"Top Picks for You" grid** with prices ($9, $14, $14) adds cross-sell and a personalization feel that nudges the basket.

## What's weak

- **Cadence overlap**: Power Grip Rock Setting Spray dropped July 13 — this is the same franchise four days later. Subscriber fatigue is a real risk.
- **"GO JUMBO" CTA copy is cute but soft**: it doesn't tell you what you're getting or where you're going; "Shop Jumbo Primer" or "Get the XL Size" would convert better.
- **Zero urgency signals**: no deadline, no "limited quantities," no early-access framing. Nothing to act on *today* rather than *eventually*.
- **"23 different nicknames (all from you)"** is playful but does no heavy lifting as social proof — no star rating, no review count, no quote.
- **No above-the-fold loyalty hook**: Beauty Squad appears only in the tiny footer; a returning member has no incentive differential.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add urgency to the hero**: "Introducing" alone isn't enough — add "Available now — early access for Beauty Squad members" or "Limited first-run quantities" to convert browsers into immediate buyers.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA**: Change "GO JUMBO" to "Shop Jumbo Primer" — preserves the personality while removing ambiguity.
- 3. **Add social proof to the "Why You'll Love It" section**: Pull the star rating and review count from the original Power Grip Primer ("Based on 18,000+ reviews of the OG") to anchor trust.
- 4. **Give Beauty Squad members a reason to be here**: A member-exclusive preview window or +1 reward point per dollar would differentiate this from a public product page.
- 5. **Space this further from the July 13 Power Grip send**, or position it explicitly as the sequel ("You saw the Rock Setting Spray — now meet its XL partner.").

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Nested template artifact — duplicate `nl-container`**
Inside `html_block block-1`, there is a full second `<table class="nl-container">` wrapping a second `<table class="row row-1">`. This is a block-within-block nesting artifact, likely from embedding a full layout template inside a Beefree/Braze drag-and-drop block. The outer container already provides the row structure; the inner container is redundant and can break column stacking on mobile.

```html
<!-- Outer row -->
<table class="row row-1" ...>
  ...
    <div ...>
      <!-- Inner duplicate layout -->
      <table class="nl-container" ...>
        <table class="row row-1" ...>   ← duplicate
```

**Click-tracking links wrap destination URLs opaquely**
All hrefs route through `links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/...` with base64-encoded payloads. This is expected for Braze, but means UTM parameters on destination URLs are not inspectable from the email source alone. See Section 6.

**`tabindex="-1"` on the logo CTA link**
```html
<a href="https://links.elfcosmetics.com/..." tabindex="-1">
```
This removes the logo link from keyboard navigation order. If this link is intentional (e.g., branded homepage CTA), removing it from tab order is an accessibility regression. Only use `tabindex="-1"` for purely decorative/redundant links.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some email clients (Apple Mail tabs, Samsung Mail) display the `<title>` value as a fallback label. An empty title is a missed slot; it should mirror the subject line or preheader.

**Google Fonts loaded via `<link>` — blocked in most clients**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" ...>
```
Gmail (web and Android), Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo strip external `<link>` tags. Open Sans will silently fall back to Arial/Sans-serif in the majority of opens. This is a known limitation; the fallback stack is correctly defined, but the Google Fonts call has no practical effect at scale.

**Empty HTML blocks (block-2, block-3, block-4)**
Three consecutive `html_block` elements contain only whitespace divs:
```html
<div style="font-family:Open Sans,Arial,Sans-serif;text-align:center" align="center"> </div>
```
These render as invisible zero-content cells but add DOM weight and can produce unexpected spacing in Outlook due to `mso-table-lspace/rspace` interactions. Should be removed if they serve no structural purpose.

**Image `alt` text is minimal**
```html
<img ... alt="e.l.f." title="e.l.f.">
```
Acceptable for a logo, but when images are blocked (common in B2B/Outlook environments), the above-fold experience degrades to just "e.l.f." with no product or offer context. Consider `alt="e.l.f. Cosmetics — Go Jumbo Power Grip Primer"` to preserve message intent in image-off rendering.
