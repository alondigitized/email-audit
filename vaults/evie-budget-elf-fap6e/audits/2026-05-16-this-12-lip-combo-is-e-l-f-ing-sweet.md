# This $12 lip combo is e.l.f.ing sweet 👄😋 
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a lip-focused promotional email anchored on a "punch up your pout" hero and a $12 liner-plus-gloss concept. The subject is the strongest asset — concrete price, clear category, light pun — and the hero section delivers on it with a prominent lip pencil image and a clean "line & shine" product grid. On paper, this is a solid mid-week lip promo.
- The problem is what happens after the hero. The email doesn't stop. It pivots to a mascara reminder ("more lash. less cash.") — which is nearly verbatim from *yesterday's* standalone mascara send — then appends an SPF panel, a Target loyalty section, and a "Top Picks for You" personalized block. That's five distinct editorial bets in a single email. The $12 lip combo concept, which is genuinely compelling, drowns in the content pile. The email needed an editor, not a homepage.
- On cadence: this is e.l.f.'s second email today after the birthday-month trigger, the third in 36 hours, and the seventh in four days. The marginal open on this one is paying a real frequency tax even if the individual send is clean.

## What's working

- "Punch up your pout" / "line & shine" hero copy is punchy, on-brand, and lands the category message immediately.
- The lip product grid gives subscribers a genuine range to browse — liner and gloss in multiple shades is the right editorial unit for a combo pitch.
- SHOP NOW CTA is unambiguous and placed directly under the hero.
- The email renders cleanly — no broken images, no layout collapse, no lorem ipsum visible.
- Subject line does the heavy lifting: `$12`, `lip combo`, and the e.l.f.ing pun in under 40 characters.

## What's weak

- The mascara "more lash. less cash." section is a near-verbatim copy of yesterday's dedicated mascara email — subscribers who opened both get identical messaging 24 hours apart.
- The $12 price from the subject isn't visibly confirmed as a hero callout in the body — a price badge or headline would close that loop.
- Five distinct content blocks (lip, mascara, SPF, Target rewards, personalized picks) mean the email has no single call to action — it's a digest, not a promo.
- SPF and Target loyalty sections are completely disconnected from the lip combo hook; they feel like append-ons from a different brief.
- Second email today compounds an already heavy send week; the birthday email earlier today was the warmer send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the email in half.** Hero lip section + one SHOP NOW + footer. Drop mascara, SPF, and Target. Let the $12 combo be the whole story.
- 2. **Surface the $12 in the body.** A price badge on the hero image ("The $12 duo") or a subhead directly under "line & shine" pays off the subject promise visually.
- 3. **Add a "shown together" combo shot** — liner + gloss side by side with a lip swatch. The grid of individual products doesn't communicate "combo."
- 4. **Time-bound the offer** — "Through Sunday" or "This week only" gives the subject line real urgency hooks next time.
- 5. **Retire the mascara block.** It ran yesterday. Use that slot for a second lip shade story or a UGC-style lip swatch.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — mobile h1 at 9px:**
```css
@media (max-width:660px) {
  .row-6 .column-1 .block-1.heading_block h1 { font-size:9px!important }
}
```
9px is below the 11px minimum legible threshold and will fail WCAG 1.4.4. This selector is almost certainly a residual override from template copying — verify intended value.

**`tabindex="-1"` on logo anchor:**
```html
<a href="https://links.elfcosmetics.com/..." tabindex="-1">
```
Removes the logo CTA from the keyboard tab order. Keyboard-only users cannot activate it. Remove the attribute or set `tabindex="0"`.

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers announce the tab/window title on open. Empty title provides no context; use a descriptive string matching the subject line.

**Google Fonts via `<link>`:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" ...>
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Jost" ...>
```
Wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <![endif]-->` so Outlook skips it, but Gmail (webmail), Apple Mail, and most mobile clients will block external stylesheet requests. Fallback stack `Open Sans, Arial, Sans-serif` is present and will engage — not a breakage, but custom fonts will not render in most inboxes.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

UTM parameters are not visible in the raw tracking URLs (e.g., the logo CTA: `links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/fo7NJJ661_...`). They may be appended by Braze at redirect time, but this cannot be confirmed without following the redirect chain.

**Recommended check:** Curl or manually follow one tracked link and inspect the final destination URL for:

If UTMs are absent on the landing page, attribution in Google Analytics / GA4 will be misattributed to direct traffic.
