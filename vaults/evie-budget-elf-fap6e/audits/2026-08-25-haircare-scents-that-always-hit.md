## What's working

- The scent-pairing macro photography (peach/blackberries for "bright, fruity," peonies for "vanilla and peony," citrus for "effervescent citrus") is a strong, differentiated creative device.
- Each product card is complete and self-contained: name, price, benefit callout, and its own CTA — no ambiguity about what's being sold.
- "Top Picks for You" section adds a personalization signal and cross-sell without cluttering the primary hero flow.
- Clean color-blocked sections make the email easy to scan even though it's long.
- No render bugs — images, gradients, and buttons all display correctly.

## What's weak

- No offer or discount anywhere in the email — every other e.l.f. send this week (bronzing drops, toner, lip balm) had a price or promo hook; this one is price-as-is.
- Subject/hero theme is scent, but the "E.L.F.ECTIVE FORMULAS" headline undersells that hook — it reads generic rather than leading with fragrance, which is the actual differentiator.
- This is the second haircare-focused send in five days (after 2026-08-21's "great summer 'do" email) — cadence risk for anyone not deep into e.l.f. Hair specifically.
- The category nav bars (NEW/EYES/LIPS/FACE/SKINCARE/HAIR) are plain gray blocks with no icons or visual interest, a flat stretch in an otherwise lively email.

- 2. Add a concrete incentive (bundle discount on shampoo+conditioner+oil, or a "buy 2 get $5 off" haircare bundle) — a scent story alone won't move someone off the fence without a price nudge.
