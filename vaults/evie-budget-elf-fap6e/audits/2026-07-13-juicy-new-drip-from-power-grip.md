# Juicy NEW drip from Power Grip 🌹
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- This is a Power Grip franchise expansion email — e.l.f. is launching the Power Grip Rock Setting Spray ($9) and using the moment to parade the full Power Grip lineup (Dewy Setting Spray, Primer, Primer+, Matte Primer) in a scrollable product-card format. The teal/aqua palette is on-brand for the line, the hero is clean, and the product images are sharp. It reads like a catalog drop rather than a campaign.
- The core problem is structure: five separate "SHOP IT" calls-to-action with no anchoring offer creates decision fatigue before the user gets to the first scroll. e.l.f.'s recent sends — the $14 Cream Team bundle, the $27 Soft Glam routine, the GWP code BURST — all gave subscribers *one reason to act now*. This email gives five reasons to browse later. The new product is the hook, but it gets equal billing with four SKUs the subscriber already knows.
- The subject line is doing e.l.f.'s worst habit: trendy slang ("drip," "juicy") that feels good in a brainstorm but loses precision in the inbox. Power Grip is a *grip* product — priming, setting — not a juicy, drippy texture. The rose emoji compounds the misread.

## What's working

- **Hero visual is strong** — the stacked Power Grip tubes shot against a teal-washed model is cohesive and immediately recognizable as the Power Grip line.
- **"A grip for every look" tagline** is clean and does real positioning work in two seconds.
- **Price transparency throughout** — every product card shows the dollar amount upfront, no "starting at" hedging.
- **NEW!! callout on the Rock Setting Spray** clearly flags the hero SKU within the catalog.
- **Layout renders cleanly** — no broken images, no overlapping text, consistent card spacing top to bottom.

## What's weak

- **Five parallel CTAs with no primary** — "SHOP IT" repeated five times trains the eye to skip every one of them.
- **"Juicy" and the rose emoji are texture-wrong** — Power Grip is a matte-grip primer line; "juicy drip" implies a gloss or treatment, which is a misdirect that erodes trust if subscribers open expecting a lip or glow product.
- **No offer** — no discount, no bundle price, no free shipping threshold, nothing to activate urgency. The July 9 GWP email (code BURST) showed exactly how to do this; this send abandons that playbook entirely.
- **Hottest Drops Duo GWP at the bottom is a recycled module** — that offer ran July 9; surfacing it again with no updated framing makes this feel like a template swap, not a fresh send.
- **The new product doesn't own the email** — Power Grip Rock Setting Spray is the news, but it shares equal visual weight with four existing SKUs.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the Rock Setting Spray the entire email** — cut the four existing SKUs to a footnote row ("Complete the grip routine →"). Let the new launch breathe. Every recent high-performer (Blurreal Lip & Cheek Whip, Gloss Mode restock) was a single-product spotlight.
- 2. **Add one offer mechanic** — bundle the Rock Setting Spray + any Primer at $17 (vs. $18 alone), or offer free shipping on any Power Grip order. Give people a reason to click today, not someday.
- 3. **Fix the subject line creative direction** — "drip" and "juicy" belong on a lip or gloss product. Power Grip's equity is hold, grip, and finish — lean into that vocabulary.
- 4. **Replace the recycled Hottest Drops Duo module** — if cross-sell is needed, make it Power Grip Primer + Rock Spray as a native pairing, not a bolted-on GWP from four days ago.
- **Subject Alt A:** `NEW: Power Grip just added a setting spray ($9)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your grip routine just got a new step 🌿`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Power Grip Rock Setting Spray is here — shop the full grip lineup`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Meet the newest drop from the Power Grip fam — $9`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Mixed ESP asset hosting**
- Click tracking: Sailthru (`links.elfcosmetics.com`, `~~/AAQRxRA~/` subscriber hash pattern)
- Image CDN: `braze-images.com/appboy/…` (Braze)

**Opaque redirect — UTM not visible in source**

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some clients (notably older Android Gmail) surface `<title>` in the tab/window label. An empty value is benign but adds no fallback context.

**`tabindex="-1"` on hero link**
```html
<a href="…" target="_blank" style="outline:none" tabindex="-1">
```
Removing the hero image link from keyboard tab order fails WCAG 2.1 SC 2.1.1 (Keyboard). Screen-reader users and keyboard-only users cannot activate the primary CTA. `outline:none` compounds this by removing focus visibility.

**Google Fonts load path**
Correctly gated behind `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`; Outlook falls back to Open Sans → Arial → Sans-serif stack as intended. No issue.

**Fixed 640 px column with no fluid override at root**
`row-content` is hard-capped at `width:640px`. The `@media (max-width:660px)` block sets `width:100%!important` on `.row-content`, which is correct. No issue in practice, but the 660 px breakpoint (not 640 px) means a 641–660 px viewport gets the desktop layout at partial clip — minor.

**`row-6 .column-1 .block-1.heading_block h1` font size forced to 9 px on mobile**
```css
.row-6 .column-1 .block-1.heading_block h1 { font-size:9px!important }
```
9 px is below the WCAG 2.1 recommended minimum (~12 px / 16 px body) and will likely fail legibility checks. Verify whether this is intentional (e.g., a legal-copy row) or a build artifact from the email editor.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM / Landing Page)
