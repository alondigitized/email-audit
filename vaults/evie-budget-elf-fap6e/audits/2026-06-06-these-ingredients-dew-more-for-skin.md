# These ingredients dew more for skin 👩‍🔬 
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- e.l.f. SKIN is running the Thirst Burst Drops through the same single-product template they used for the Glass Slick Serum Stick on June 4 — ingredient callouts, clean product shot, punchy hero headline, two "SHOP NOW" buttons. The subject-line pun ("dew more" / "do more") is cute and category-appropriate, and the model's luminous skin does real selling work by literally demonstrating the product promise. The ingredient specificity (5% Hydrating Hibiscus Complex, 3% niacinamide) is the clearest differentiator in the body copy and targets the shopper who reads the back of the bottle before buying.
- The problem is this is the fourth e.l.f. email in five days, and it follows an almost identical structural playbook to the June 4 send — SKIN sub-brand, ingredient-forward, no price anchor, no urgency. The cadence is eroding the novelty that makes each of these single-product sends effective. There's also no price shown anywhere in the body, which is a conspicuous miss given that e.l.f.'s lowest prices are one of its strongest conversion levers (compare the May 31 "$12 lip combo" send, which put the price in the hero and scored 9/10).
- The email is competent and on-brand, but it's a B+ execution of an increasingly familiar template. The hero copy "DRIP DRIP, SKIN SO LIT." brings personality, but without a price or deadline, there's nothing to close the deal.

## What's working

- **Hero model proves the product.** The model's visibly dewy, luminous skin is direct evidence of what Thirst Burst Drops claims to do — no abstract lifestyle shot needed.
- **Subject-line pun lands.** "Dew more for skin" reads cleanly and earns the emoji without feeling forced — fits e.l.f.'s playful voice.
- **Ingredient specificity builds trust.** Naming 5% Hydrating Hibiscus Complex and 3% niacinamide with percentages speaks directly to the ingredient-aware shopper.
- **Multi-use positioning is a clear differentiator.** "Anti-blur serum, primer and foundation booster" in one product — that's a real value story, and it's visible in the render.
- **Single-product focus is disciplined.** No menu overload; the email is about one thing.

## What's weak

- **No price anywhere.** E.l.f.'s strongest converting emails front-load a price (see May 31's "$12 lip combo" front-and-center hero). This email offers no anchor — a shopper has to click just to find out what Thirst Burst Drops costs.
- **Generic CTA.** "SHOP NOW" appears twice but neither instance names the product. "Get Thirst Burst Drops" or "Shop the Dew Drop" would reinforce the campaign without adding friction.
- **No urgency or scarcity.** The June 4 Glass Slick Serum Stick email had the same gap. Two back-to-back SKIN emails with zero deadline signal make both feel like evergreen catalog pages, not sends you open before they expire.
- **Navigation tiles undercut the focus.** The generic NEW / EYES / LIPS / FACE / SKINCARE grid at the bottom adds visual noise without reinforcing Thirst Burst Drops — it turns a disciplined single-product send into a mini-catalog at the bottom half.
- **Aggressive cadence is compressing impact.** This is the fifth send in six days. For a subscriber who also got the June 4 SKIN single-product email, this risks feeling like a repeat rather than a fresh drop.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the price in the hero.** Even a simple "Thirst Burst Drops — $XX" under the hero headline would close the gap between interest and click. E.l.f.'s price point is a selling point, not something to hide behind a click.
- 2. **Make the CTA copy product-specific.** Replace both "SHOP NOW" instances with "Shop Thirst Burst Drops" or a voice-forward line like "Get the glow, $XX."
- 3. **Add one urgency mechanism.** A lightweight "New drop — limited first-batch stock" or a brief intro window ("This week only: free sample with purchase") gives the reader a reason to act now rather than later.
- 4. **Remove the navigation tile grid or replace it with product pairings.** Show Thirst Burst Drops next to a complementary SKU (Glass Slick Serum Stick is a natural pair from the June 4 email) with both prices visible. This deepens the basket instead of diluting focus.
- 5. **Add one social-proof signal to the body.** A TikTok view count, an Allure mention, or a single five-star pull quote — anything that positions Thirst Burst Drops as validated, not just introduced.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Thirst Burst Drops = your dew-it-all serum 👩‍🔬`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New drop: dewy skin in one step`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `5% Hibiscus Complex + 3% niacinamide for post-shower glow — only $XX`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Serum, primer, and foundation booster in one bottle. Meet Thirst Burst Drops.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some clients (notably Gmail web) surface the title in tab labels. Should contain the subject or brand name.

**Dead content blocks:** Three consecutive empty `html_block` divs (block-2, block-3, block-4) contain only whitespace:
```html
<div style="font-family:Open Sans,Arial,Sans-serif;text-align:center" align="center"> </div>
```
These are builder artifacts from Braze's drag-and-drop editor. They add ~300 bytes of no-op markup and can occasionally trigger spam filters that flag tag-to-content ratios.

**Nested `nl-container` tables:** The hero image block wraps a full `nl-container`/`row`/`row-content` table structure inside an outer `html_block` — effectively two complete layout layers. This is a Braze template composition issue (embedding a full template inside an HTML block) and may cause unexpected width behavior in edge-case clients.

**Alt text:** `alt="e.l.f."` on the hero image is present but generic — if images are blocked, the hero renders as a single letter-period string with no product context. Acceptable for a logo lockup, but the hero appears to be a full-bleed creative, not just a logo.

**Responsive breakpoint:** Media query fires at `max-width:660px` against a 640px container — correct and consistent.

**Google Fonts loading:** Conditional comment correctly excludes Outlook from the `<link>` tag:
```html
<!--[if !mso]><!--><link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" ...><!--<![endif]-->
```
Fallback stack `Open Sans, Arial, Sans-serif` is well-formed.
