# Here’s a bright idea 🍊
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-17**
## Executive summary

- This is e.l.f. SKIN's best comparison send of the batch — it takes the "$185 alternative" price-anchor gimmick that's floated around skincare marketing forever and actually executes it cleanly: named product (Vitamin C+E+Ferulic Serum), split-bottle visual showing $185 vs. $17, and a "twinfluencer" before/after bit with two real faces. It's more confident and more legible than the 8/16 Clarify Facial Oil send and miles ahead of the e.l.f. x Bubble repeats — this doesn't lean on urgency language it can't back up, it leans on a genuinely sharp price comparison.
- Where it loses points: the GWP module for Bronzing Drops right below the hero is a second, unrelated offer competing for attention seconds after the eye lands on the serum, and the split-price treatment ("$185" / "$17") is legible but visually busy against the orange-on-orange background. Still, the hero itself is one of the more scroll-stopping, honest value props in this brand history — a rare case where the "twin test" bit isn't just wordplay, it's the actual selling mechanism.

## What's working

- Clear price-anchor mechanic: $185 alternative vs. $17 e.l.f. serum, both bottles shown side-by-side with matching benefit call-outs (visibly brightens, boosts radiance, helps even skin tone).
- Real named product with a real price — continues the trend from 8/15 and 8/16 of getting more product-specific instead of vague roundups.
- Two authentic-feeling model photos with first names (Veronica, Vanessa) reinforce the "twin test" concept visually.
- Clean CTA structure: "BRIGHT THIS WAY" pill leads into the comparison, "SHOP NOW" black button is unambiguous and appears twice.
- No render bugs — text is legible, images loaded, layout holds together despite the busy orange background.

## What's weak

- The Bronzing Drops GWP block sits immediately under the primary CTA and introduces a second, unrelated offer before the eye has settled — it dilutes an otherwise single-minded hero.
- Orange-on-orange background makes the "$185" vs "$17" price text harder to scan than it should be; the numbers are the entire point of the email and they're not the visual peak.
- "Top Picks for You" section shows three unrelated products (makeup remover, thirst drops, cleanser) with no obvious personalization logic — feels like filler after a focused hero.
- Subject emoji (🍊) doesn't connect to anything in the body — no orange-scented or citrus ingredient story, so it reads as decorative rather than informative.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut or move the Bronzing Drops GWP module below the "Top Picks" section — let the serum comparison be the only offer above the fold-equivalent scroll depth.
- 2. Increase contrast on the $185/$17 price callouts (white plate or bold outline) so the actual value proof — the number — is the loudest thing on the graphic, not just another orange element.
- 3. Swap "Top Picks for You" for cross-sells actually related to the vitamin C serum (e.g., SPF, brightening moisturizer) to keep the narrative coherent.
- 4. Tie the subject emoji to the product story — reference the price gap or the vitamin C angle instead of a generic orange.

- **Subject Alt B:** `We put our C+E+Ferulic Serum to the test`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Two friends, one blind test — see who paid $185`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Dermatologist-grade brightening for $17, not $185`
