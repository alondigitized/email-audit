# Get double the dewy makeup grip 💚
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- This is a cross-sell bundling email pairing the Jumbo Power Grip Primer — introduced just three days ago in the July 17 "Go Jumbo" send (8/10) — with the Power Grip Dewy Setting Spray. The "power couple" concept is clever: linking the two grip products into a franchise story gives the send a reason to exist beyond a repost. The numbered product callouts (1./2.) are clean, the teal visual identity carries through from the launch email, and the $10 setting spray surfaces an accessible entry price early.
- The problem is structural: you're being asked to buy two products at full price ($34 + $10 = $44) with no bundle incentive. The "power couple" framing is marketing copy, not a reason to act. A subscriber who just received the primer pitch on July 17 and didn't buy hasn't been given new information — just a second product to ignore alongside the first.
- Four consecutive e.l.f. emails in four days is also a cadence risk. This send would hit harder as a week-two nudge when the launch noise has settled and a bundle discount could justify the return trip.

## What's working

- "The ultimate power couple." headline is sticky — it frames two full-price products as a desirable set without feeling hard-sell.
- Numbered product structure (1./2.) gives the reader an easy reading path: promise → product 1 → product 2.
- Teal visual identity is consistent with the July 17 launch, building real brand coherence across sends.
- The $10 setting spray price is visible and low-barrier — smart to put the easier buy alongside the $34 ask.
- "Top Picks For You" personalized grid at the bottom adds cross-sell depth beyond the hero two.

## What's weak

- No bundle mechanic — "buy both at full price" undercuts the entire "couple" concept; without a discount or gift-with-purchase, there's no action-now trigger.
- "SHOP NOW" CTA copy is generic — "Shop the Duo" or "Get Both" would actually reinforce the pairing story.
- Three days after the Jumbo Power Grip Primer launch email, this reads as a quick follow-up, not a fresh campaign beat; subscribers who skipped July 17 have no new reason to buy.
- Zero urgency signal anywhere — no deadline, no stock limit, nothing to prompt action today over next week.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a bundle incentive**: Even a modest "save $5 when you buy the duo" or "bundle & get free shipping" transforms the creative from "here are two products" into a genuine value unlock. Without it, the power couple story is flat.
- 2. **Sharpen the hero CTA**: Replace "SHOP NOW" in the hero with "Shop the Power Couple" — makes the bundling concept actionable, not just decorative.
- 3. **Add urgency to the primer**: Something like "Jumbo is selling fast" or a limited-time bundle window gives subscribers a deadline that the July 17 email lacked.
- 4. **Push this send to July 24+**: A week-two follow-up with a bundle discount would be a much stronger reason to return. Right now it feels like a same-week revisit of the same product.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`tabindex="-1"` on the logo anchor — keyboard navigation broken**
```html
<a href="..." target="_blank" style="outline:none" tabindex="-1">
```
`tabindex="-1"` removes the element from the tab order entirely. Combined with `outline:none`, keyboard-only users cannot focus or activate this link. This is a WCAG 2.1 Level A failure (2.1.1 Keyboard).

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers announce the page/email title. An empty `<title>` degrades SR experience; should be `<title>Get double the dewy makeup grip</title>` or equivalent.

**Google Fonts load blocked in most email clients**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail (webmail), Outlook, and Apple Mail all block external CSS. The font is wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <!--<![endif]-->` to hide from Outlook, but the `Arial, Sans-serif` fallback in inline styles will be used universally. This is not a breakage — just confirm the fallback renders acceptably.

**Multiple empty spacer blocks**
`block-2`, `block-3`, `block-4` are full `<table>` elements containing only whitespace `<div>`s. Zero-height spacers should use `<tr><td height="N"></td></tr>` or a single pixel GIF pattern, not full HTML block structures — these add parse weight and can render inconsistently in Outlook.
