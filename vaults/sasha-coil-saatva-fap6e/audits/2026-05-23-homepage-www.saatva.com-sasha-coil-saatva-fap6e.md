# Homepage snapshot · www.saatva.com · 2026-05-23
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- Saatva knows exactly who it is — a luxury-adjacent mattress brand trying to convince me I don't have to choose between quality and price — and the Memorial Day sale banner lands at exactly the right moment for someone like me who saves her big-ticket splurges for sale season. That said, the first thing eating up half my screen is a cookie consent wall, and the hero image is a moody ocean photograph with no mattress, no bedroom, no product in sight. Premium positioning with a frustrating first impression.

## What's working

- **Memorial Day banner urgency** — "Last Chance / Ends 5/24 / Up to $625 off" is exactly the signal I wait for. Specific dollar off (not %) feels credible rather than inflated.
- **"Luxury Mattresses Made Affordable" headline** — speaks directly to the tension I carry: I want quality coil construction but I'm not paying Duxiana prices. This tagline is the right promise.
- **"Visit a viewing room" CTA** — I appreciate that Saatva has showrooms. Even as someone who'd prefer not to trek in, knowing that option exists adds legitimacy. It signals this isn't a pure bed-in-a-box operation.
- **Search bar visible** — medium-tech-comfort shopper like me appreciates being able to type "organic cotton" or "classic coil" without hunting through a nav.

## What's weak

- **Cookie consent modal dominates the first screen** — I'm 47, I am not going to read four paragraphs of cookie policy as my first brand interaction. This covers the bottom 40% of the hero and immediately puts me on the defensive.
- **Hero image is an ocean scene** — I'm here to buy a mattress. Show me the mattress. Show me a bedroom. Show me someone in their 40s actually sleeping well. A seascape is a choice, but it's not doing conversion work.
- **No white-glove delivery mention above the fold** — this is a baseline expectation for me, not a bonus. If it's buried, I start to worry it doesn't exist.
- **No financing teaser above the fold** — at $625+ off an already premium price, I still want to know "can I split this into 12 months?" A quick "0% APR available" line would make me much more likely to keep scrolling.
- **No reviews or star ratings in view** — I'm skeptical of brands I can't try in person. Social proof in the first scroll is table stakes.

## Recommendations

- **Dismiss or minimize the cookie modal by default** — show a slim one-line banner at the bottom instead of a full-screen takeover. The current implementation kills the first impression before it starts.
- **Swap the ocean hero for an editorial bedroom lifestyle shot** — a beautifully made bed with organic cotton bedding, soft light, maybe a 40-something woman reading in it. That's the showroom-without-the-showroom visual I'm looking for.
- **Add a trust strip below the hero** — one line: "Free white-glove delivery · 0% APR financing · 365-night trial." These three signals together would address my three biggest hesitations before I even scroll.
- **Make the sale banner more specific** — "Up to $625 off" is good, but "Up to $625 off the Classic — our most popular innerspring" tells me exactly which mattress is on sale and whether it's the one I care about.
