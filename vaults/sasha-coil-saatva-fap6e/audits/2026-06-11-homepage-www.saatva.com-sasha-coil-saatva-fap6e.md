# Homepage snapshot · www.saatva.com · 2026-06-11
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- This looks like a brand that knows exactly what it is — premium, handcrafted, not apologizing for the price point. The "Luxury Mattresses Made Affordable" headline hits my sweet spot immediately, and the pre-summer sale banner up top tells me I timed this right. That said, a full-screen cookie consent wall eating the bottom third of my hero is not the first impression a brand calling itself luxury should be giving me.

## What's working

- **The sale banner strip** — "Our BIGGEST Pre-Summer Sale Starts NOW! | 15% off Orders $1K+" lands immediately. I know there's a deal, I know the minimum, I know when it expires. That's exactly what I need to decide whether to browse now or come back later.
- **"Luxury Mattresses Made Affordable" headline** — That's my exact tension stated back to me. I don't want to compromise quality, but I'm not paying department store markup. This copy earns a second of my attention.
- **"Handcrafted mattresses, bedding & furniture"** — The word "handcrafted" does real work for me. It draws a line between Saatva and the foam-in-a-box companies I've been avoiding.
- **"Visit a viewing room" CTA** — Counterintuitive but actually compelling for my skepticism. Saatva has physical showrooms? Now I'm interested. This differentiates them from every roll-up-in-a-tube competitor.
- **Search bar front and center** — On mobile, having "What can we help you find?" right under the nav is smart. I'm 47, not scrolling carousels to find the Classic Hybrid specs.

## What's weak

- **Cookie consent banner** — It's covering roughly the bottom third of the hero at the moment the page loads. For a brand positioning itself as luxury, having a wall of GDPR legalese be my first visual experience is a real mood killer. An "I Understand" button in the same visual weight as my primary CTA is confusing.
- **Hero background image is an ocean scene, not a bed** — I came here to think about a mattress. A coastal horizon is pretty but it's not helping me picture the coil layers I care about. I'm skeptical of bed-in-a-box brands that use lifestyle photography to avoid showing their product.
- **No trust signals above the fold** — No review count, no award badge, no "as seen in" strip, no mention of white-glove delivery. Nothing that says "47,000 five-star reviews" or "free in-home setup." For my first visit, I need social proof before I'll take the sale seriously.
- **No financing teaser** — A $1K+ threshold on a 15% sale means I'm looking at $2,000+ mattresses. I always read financing terms twice, and there's zero hint here that options exist.
- **"More Info" link in banner** — Low-contrast, underlined, small. On a phone screen I'd likely miss it or mispress it.

## Recommendations

- **Swap the ocean background for a close-up product lifestyle shot** — A made bed with visible premium linens, photographed at a slight angle showing mattress depth. Keep it aspirational but anchor it to the product category. I need to picture *my bedroom*, not a beach house.
- **Add a one-line trust proof to the hero** — Something like "50,000+ 5-star reviews · Free white-glove delivery" sitting right under the subhead. This is the single thing that would move me from "interesting" to "I'm scrolling."
- **Shrink or reposition the cookie banner** — Make it a thin bottom bar with a single dismiss tap instead of a modal block. Luxury brands do not slap consent walls over their hero image.
- **Add a "financing available" micro-line near the sale banner** — Even just "0% APR financing available" in the same strip. Buyers in my category need to see that before we commit to exploring further.
