# Homepage snapshot · www.saatva.com · 2026-07-23
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- Saatva is doing several things right for someone like me — the "Luxury Mattresses Made Affordable" headline and "handcrafted" language are exactly the signals I look for to separate this from a Casper or Purple. There's a live sale event with a real deadline, which gets my attention. But that cookie consent wall eating up nearly half the screen is a rough first impression, and below that I'm getting almost no product detail — nothing about coil systems, organic materials, white-glove delivery, or financing. For a brand that sells $2,000+ mattresses to skeptical buyers, this above-the-fold loads too thin.

## What's working

- **"Luxury Mattresses Made Affordable" headline** — this is exactly the positioning I need to hear. Not "sleep better" fluff, not a tech pitch. Premium aspiration + accessible price point = the frame I'm shopping from.
- **"Handcrafted mattresses, bedding & furniture"** — the word "handcrafted" signals craft over commodity. I immediately don't feel like I'm looking at a warehouse brand.
- **15% off Latex Hybrid Bundle with a hard end date** — the announcement bar is doing real work. A bundle deal with a named product and a "Ends 7/23" creates legitimate urgency without feeling manufactured.
- **"Visit a viewing room" CTA** — I appreciate that this brand has physical showrooms. Even if I'm shopping from my couch, knowing I *could* go touch the mattress matters. That button builds trust before I've scrolled an inch.
- **Search bar front and center** — medium-tech-comfort me is not going to dig through a hamburger menu if I don't have to. A prominent search field lowers friction.

## What's weak

- **Cookie consent modal blocking the hero** — It covers roughly the bottom third of what should be a premium bedroom lifestyle image. My very first impression of a luxury brand is a legal wall. Not great. I understand it's required but it's eating into the brand moment.
- **Hero background image is almost invisible** — Behind the cookie modal I can barely see what's back there. Is it a room? A bed? A lifestyle scene that might speak to me? Can't tell. The visual case for the brand is lost.
- **Zero mention of white-glove delivery above the fold** — For a $2,000 mattress, setup and removal is table stakes to me. It's not a perk. I want to see it called out before I decide if this brand takes the buying experience seriously.
- **No financing preview** — I will absolutely read the financing terms twice before I buy. There's no "as low as $X/month" signal to tell me this is even offered. That's a missed hook for a purchase in this price bracket.
- **No coil or organic cotton callouts** — The promo is about the Latex Hybrid. I'm a coil + organic cotton buyer. I need to see my material on the first screen or I'm already wondering if this is the right brand.
- **Hamburger-only navigation on mobile** — I cannot see any category tiles or nav links. One extra tap between me and "Mattresses → Classic Saatva" is one too many.

## Recommendations

- **Swap the blocked hero moment for a split approach:** move the cookie acknowledgment to a slim bottom bar so the lifestyle bedroom image is fully visible. The product deserves the full screen.
- **Add a sub-headline callout below the hero copy** — something like "American-made innerspring & organic cotton options | Free white-glove delivery & removal" — this costs zero design real estate and answers my two biggest pre-click questions.
- **Surface financing in the announcement bar or immediately below the CTA** — even "Financing available from $X/month" next to the "Visit a viewing room" button would stop me from bouncing to go Google "Saatva financing terms."
- **Add a persistent "Shop by material" quick-filter strip** (coil / latex / organic / hybrid) visible below the hero so I can route to my category without the hamburger menu.
