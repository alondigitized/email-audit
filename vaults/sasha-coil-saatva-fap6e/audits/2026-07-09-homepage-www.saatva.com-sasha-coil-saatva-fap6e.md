# Homepage snapshot · www.saatva.com · 2026-07-09
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- This is exactly the kind of brand I want to be shopping. Saatva is making a clear pitch for quality-minded buyers who don't want to gamble on a rolled-up foam tube. The "Luxury Mattresses Made Affordable" positioning lands right, and the Summer Sale banner is the first thing I see, which tells me they're actively trying to close deals. The "Visit a viewing room" CTA is genuinely surprising — in the best way.

## What's working

- **"Visit a viewing room" CTA** — I almost fell out of my chair. I've been told by every online brand to "shop now" and here's a button that acknowledges I might actually want to see this in person. That's the showroom feel I've been hunting for, delivered digitally.
- **Summer Sale banner with hard numbers** — "15% off $1K+" and "Ends 7/13" is exactly how I want to be talked to. Not "huge savings," not "up to X%." A floor, a rate, a date.
- **"Luxury Mattresses Made Affordable" headline** — positions them directly against cheap foam alternatives without having to say it. I feel like the target audience.
- **"Handcrafted" subheadline copy** — for a premium coil buyer, that word means something. It's a signal that this isn't a mattress built by a robot in an Amazon warehouse.

## What's weak

- **Cookie modal is massive and blocks half the page** — it's not just a subtle banner; it's a full white card eating up the bottom third of the viewport. Before I can even read the hero, I'm navigating legal disclosures.
- **Hero background image is abstract (ocean/sky)** — I can't tell if I'm buying a mattress or booking a vacation. There's no product visible whatsoever above the fold. I need to see coils, stitching, something tactile.
- **No financing mention visible** — at $1K+ thresholds, I want to know what the payment options look like before I click anything. That information is buried.
- **Hamburger-only nav on mobile** — I can't see a single category without tapping a menu. The brand sells mattresses, bedding, and furniture — none of that is scannable.

## Recommendations

- **Replace the abstract ocean hero with a lifestyle shot of an actual mattress** — the bed made with organic cotton layers visible, perhaps a mid-40s woman reading in it. Let me feel the product.
- **Add a thin financing callout below the hero CTA** — something like "0% APR for 24 months" right under the "Visit a viewing room" button closes the trust gap before I even ask.
- **Move the cookie consent to a bottom bar, not a blocking modal** — this is a UX pattern that doesn't lose compliance but doesn't wall off the product experience on first load.
- **Add one category strip below the hero** — even 3 tap targets (Mattresses / Bedding / Furniture) would help me orient without opening the hamburger menu.
