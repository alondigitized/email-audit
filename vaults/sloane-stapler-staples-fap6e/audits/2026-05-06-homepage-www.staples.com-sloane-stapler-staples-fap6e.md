# Homepage snapshot · www.staples.com · 2026-05-06
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-06**
## Executive summary

- Staples presents itself as a reliable office supply brand pushing custom print services and email capture right now — but I barely got to see any of it. The first thing this homepage did was throw an email signup modal over literally everything, which is about as welcoming as a cold call during lunch. I can see a promo banner for custom print and the bones of what looks like a product shot at the bottom, but that modal is the whole story above the fold.

## What's working

- **Custom print promo banner** — "Save $30 or more on custom print" at the very top is exactly the kind of offer I'd act on. We do client mailers and welcome packets constantly.
- **Search bar** — visible above the modal, which at least tells me I can escape this trap by searching directly.
- **Partial product imagery** — the peek of office supplies at the bottom of the screen (Staples-branded items, what looks like fine-line pens) at least signals I'm in the right store.

## What's weak

- **Full-viewport email modal** — this is the biggest UX sin I've seen this week. I opened the homepage and immediately got a door slam in the face. I haven't seen a single category, a hero, a sale — nothing. Just "give us your email."
- **No Staples Rewards hook** — I have a Rewards account. There's no login nudge, no balance tease, no "member price" badge anywhere. That's table stakes for a loyalty program.
- **No navigation exposed** — the hamburger menu means zero category browsing without an extra tap. For a store this wide, hiding the nav entirely on mobile is a miss.
- **Modal fine print** — "By clicking 'sign up now' you agree to sign up to receive emails from Staples about special offers, products and services" feels like boilerplate legal cover, not a value exchange. No mention of what I actually get.

## Recommendations

- **Kill or delay the email modal** — at minimum, show it after 30 seconds or on exit intent, not the millisecond I land. Let me see one product before you ask for my inbox.
- **Replace the modal with a Rewards sign-in strip** — a slim "Sign in to see your Rewards balance and member prices" bar at the top converts returning customers without blocking the homepage.
- **Surface free-shipping threshold above the fold** — as a weekly orderer, I buy based on whether I'll hit free shipping. Put "$X away from free shipping" in the header bar next to the cart.
- **Expose 2–3 top categories in the header** — Print & Marketing, Ink & Toner, Office Supplies — don't make me tap a hamburger to get there.
