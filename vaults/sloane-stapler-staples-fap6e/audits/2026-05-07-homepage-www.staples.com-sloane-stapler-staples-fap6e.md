# Homepage snapshot · www.staples.com · 2026-05-07
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-07**
## Executive summary

- I landed on Staples.com and the first thing I get is a newsletter sign-up modal eating up 80% of my screen. I can barely see the actual homepage behind it — just a thin promo strip at the top ("Save $30 or more on custom print") and the ghost of some product imagery below. This reads like a brand that's more interested in capturing my email than helping me get in and out with what I need.

## What's working

- **Top promo strip** — "Save $30 or more on custom print" is a concrete, dollar-denominated offer. As someone who orders branded materials for client thank-yous, that's actually relevant. Short and scannable.
- **Persistent search bar** — It's right there at the top, always accessible even behind the modal mess. For a repeat buyer who knows exactly what she wants (HP 910 toner, Hammermill 8.5x11, Ricola for the snack drawer), search-first is how I shop.
- **Cart + Orders nav** — The top nav has Orders with a dropdown and a cart counter. Quick order-status access is something I use constantly.

## What's weak

- **The modal itself is the main event** — A forced email-capture overlay is the biggest possible first impression for a returning buyer. I did not come here to subscribe; I came here to reorder.
- **"New subscribers only" fine print** — I'm a Staples Rewards member. This offer is dead on arrival for me, and reading that small gray disclaimer text is not how I want to start a shopping session.
- **Zero merchandise visible above the fold** — Behind the modal I can see a blurry lifestyle shot with some pens and markers, but I cannot tell what it's selling, what it costs, or where to tap.
- **No Rewards balance or account personalization** — There's no "Hi, Sloane — you have 3,200 points" moment. For a loyalty-program-heavy retailer, that's a wasted opportunity on a returning user.
- **Feedback tab jutting in from the right** — Minor, but on mobile it competes for tap space with actual content.

## Recommendations

- **Suppress the email modal for recognized/returning users** — If I'm logged in or have a cookie, kill the modal. Show me my Rewards points and my top reorder items instead. Email capture is for strangers.
- **Swap the modal default for a bottom-sheet banner** — If you must capture emails, a slim dismissible strip at the bottom is far less disruptive than a full-screen overlay. At minimum add a visible X close button above the fold.
- **Promote the custom print offer to a hero with a product visual** — "Save $30 on custom print" is good copy. Put it in the hero with an image of branded materials and a shop-now button. Right now it's buried in a 12px top strip.
- **Surface Rewards status in the nav or hero** — Show the member's point balance next to the cart icon. One number, no extra tap. That's the hook that keeps me from going to Amazon.
