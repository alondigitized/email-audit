# You heard right! You've earned some welcome deals!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-11**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy is already in my rotation — it's where I go when I need ten tees for two kids before school starts and I don't want to think about it. So getting a "Welcome to the Fam" email feels a little sideways, like being handed a "first visit" lanyard at a restaurant I've been going to for five years. I have to assume I just finally signed up for the rewards account, which tracks — but the subject hyping "You heard right!" as if I uncovered buried treasure just makes the whole thing feel a little desperate before I've even scrolled.
- Once I'm inside, the email is long. Genuinely long. By the time I get past the jeans hero and the "Deals In Our DNA" section and into the product grid, I've already mentally moved on to my grocery list. The product grid does show kids' categories — I can see colorful kids' pieces in there — and that's exactly what I'd actually shop. But I have to work to find it.
- I would not click from this email today. I'd acknowledge it, note the rewards perks section for later, and wait for the real sale email with a clear X% off. Mark as read.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with an actual offer.** The hero should be anchored to a specific value — "20% off your first order" or "free shipping on $50+" — not just a warm welcome. Welcome emails with a concrete offer convert dramatically better than ones that just say hello.
- 2. **Surface the kids' clothing earlier.** If Old Navy knows I'm a family shopper (or even if they don't), a "Gear Up the Whole Crew" module should be the second thing I see, not something I find while scrolling past the jeans section.
- 3. **Rewrite "Deals In Our DNA."** This is trying to sound clever but lands as empty. Replace with the actual deal mechanic: "First week perks: X% off, every day."
- 4. **Move Perks to the top third.** If I just joined rewards, the perks module is the entire point of this email. It's earning real estate it's not getting.
- 5. **Tighten the subject.** "You heard right!" is hype with no substance attached. Swap for something that lands the deal immediately.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Welcome! Here's 20% off your first order`
- **Subject Alt B:** `You're in — take 20% off this week only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your first perk is ready. Shop the whole fam now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Jeans, kids' sets, and your first member deal — all inside.`

## 1. Take

Old Navy is already in my rotation — it's where I go when I need ten tees for two kids before school starts and I don't want to think about it. So getting a "Welcome to the Fam" email feels a little sideways, like being handed a "first visit" lanyard at a restaurant I've been going to for five years. I have to assume I just finally signed up for the rewards account, which tracks — but the subject hyping "You heard right!" as if I uncovered buried treasure just makes the whole thing feel a little desperate before I've even scrolled.

Once I'm inside, the email is long. Genuinely long. By the time I get past the jeans hero and the "Deals In Our DNA" section and into the product grid, I've already mentally moved on to my grocery list. The product grid does show kids' categories — I can see colorful kids' pieces in there — and that's exactly what I'd actually shop. But I have to work to find it.

I would not click from this email today. I'd acknowledge it, note the rewards perks section for later, and wait for the real sale email with a clear X% off. Mark as read.

---

## 2. What stood out

The hero copy — "Welcome To The Old Navy Fam!" — is warm and the "Fam" framing is genuinely on-brand for how I use Old Navy. It's the family-volume store. That word does real work. But then the visual hierarchy goes soft. The "Jeans With A Reputation" section is gorgeous — the model looks like someone I'd recognize at school pickup — and it made me stop, but it's a category pitch without a sharp deal attached to it. The "Deals In Our DNA" headline sounds like an internal memo someone accidentally published. That's not how I think about deals. I think about them in dollar signs and percent signs.

The "Perks Worth Raving About" section is the most useful thing in here for a new rewards member, and it's buried maybe two-thirds of the way down. Flip that up. The product grid is genuinely mixed — I spotted what looks like kids' sets and some women's basics — so the assortment signals are there, just not surfaced loudly enough. No specific discount in the hero means I don't have a reason to click *now* versus *later*, and for me, later means never.

---

## 3. What I'd change

1. **Lead with an actual offer.** The hero should be anchored to a specific value — "20% off your first order" or "free shipping on $50+" — not just a warm welcome. Welcome emails with a concrete offer convert dramatically better than ones that just say hello.
2. **Surface the kids' clothing earlier.** If Old Navy knows I'm a family shopper (or even if they don't), a "Gear Up the Whole Crew" module should be the second thing I see, not something I find while scrolling past the jeans section.
3. **Rewrite "Deals In Our DNA."** This is trying to sound clever but lands as empty. Replace with the actual deal mechanic: "First week perks: X% off, every day."
4. **Move Perks to the top third.** If I just joined rewards, the perks module is the entire point of this email. It's earning real estate it's not getting.
5. **Tighten the subject.** "You heard right!" is hype with no substance attached. Swap for something that lands the deal immediately.

   - **Subject Alt A:** `Welcome! Here's 20% off your first order`
   - **Subject Alt B:** `You're in — take 20% off this week only`
   - **Preheader Alt A:** `Your first perk is ready. Shop the whole fam now.`
