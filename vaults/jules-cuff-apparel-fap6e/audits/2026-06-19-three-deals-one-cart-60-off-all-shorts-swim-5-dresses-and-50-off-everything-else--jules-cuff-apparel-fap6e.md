# Three deals, one cart: 60% off all shorts & swim*, $5 dresses and 50% off everything* else
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so Old Navy came in hot with THREE deals stacked into one email, and honestly my first reaction was: *which one am I supposed to care about?* The 60% off shorts and swim is familiar territory — we've been here every single day this week. The $5 dresses section is the only thing that genuinely made me stop. Five dollars. That is a lunchbox-staining, playground-wrecking, who-cares-if-it-fades price point I actually act on. But that anchor offer is buried under a 50% off everything hero that takes forever to give way to the $5 section.
- The email is doing too many jobs at once. Three separate percentage-or-dollar deals competing inside one scroll means I'm doing math when I should be clicking. Old Navy's June 17 "Steals for the fam" subject — with actual dollar amounts — got more of my attention than almost anything they've sent this month, and this email could have leaned into that formula even harder. Instead it's back to stacking headlines.
- I'd mark it as read and maybe come back to the $5 dresses link specifically. I would not forward this — it reads like a store circular, not a tip to a friend.

## What's working

- **$5 dresses hero is genuinely disruptive** — that specific dollar figure cuts through the "60% off everything" noise that's been running all week, and the model shot in that section looks age-appropriate and relatable.
- **Summer-relevant product selection** — shorts, swim, dresses, athletic gear; this is exactly what I'm restocking for both kids and myself right now, so the categories land.
- **The "HOT NEW GEAR" section at the bottom** reads like a different email — cleaner, brighter teal photography, feels more intentional and slightly more premium than the promo clutter above it.
- **No broken images or render bugs** — everything loaded, text is legible, layout held. After some of the Hanna Andersson rendering issues I've seen, a clean mobile render is worth noting.

## What's weak

- **Three competing lead offers kill the hierarchy** — 50% off, then 60% off, then $5 — my eye doesn't know where to land. Hanna Andersson's welcome email had one clear hero with one clear CTA and it felt like a gift; this feels like a flyer stuck to a telephone pole.
- **60% off is calendar wallpaper at this point** — June 15, 16, 18, and now 19 all led with 60% off something. The number stopped signaling "act now" and started signaling "this is just always how it is."
- **The asterisks** — I can see them dotting the entire email. I know what they mean: there's a long exclusion list. That's the fine print that makes "50% off everything*" feel like a mild lie. Old Navy has been burned by this before.
- **"And So, Just Your Style." section feels generic** — that's a personalization *gesture* without actual personalization. It's the kind of copy Banana Republic would use ironically; here it just feels like filler between deal blocks.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero deal and own it.** The $5 dresses is the genuinely novel offer this week — build the whole email around that number. Let 60% off shorts be the secondary CTA, not a co-equal hero.
- 2. **Drop the asterisk-heavy "everything" language** — after four straight sends of asterisked 50-60% off claims, "50% off everything*" reads as noise. Be specific: "50% off swimwear and activewear" with no asterisk lands more honest.
- 3. **Add a kids' callout** — I'm the family uniform buyer. A "stock up for both of them" line or a kids' section callout would tell me this email was sent to *me*, not just any woman on the list.
- 4. **The "HOT NEW GEAR" section should be closer to the top** — the teal athletic photography is the most visually distinct section in the email. It's doing real differentiation work buried below three deal blocks.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$5 dresses. Yes, actually $5.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$5 dresses + 60% off swim — stock up weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 50% off shorts and activewear — all in one cart`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Kids' swim from $X, dresses from $5 — the family haul is on`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Tracking pixel missing `alt` attribute**
The Coherent Path pixel (`src: https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/64db9620-...`) has no `alt=""` attribute. While functionally inconsequential for a 1×1 pixel, it triggers accessibility linting tools and some strict ESPs flag it.

**[INFO] Duplicate CSS class selectors**
In the `@media (max-width: 639px)` block, `.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice (bound to both `space-top5`/`space-top10` and `space-bot5`/`space-bot10`). The second rule silently overrides the first — whichever `.MspaceTop` element appears will always get `padding-top: 10px`, never `5px`. Low severity but worth cleaning.

**[INFO] Outlook line-height override is global**
The MSO conditional style sets `td { line-height: 0px }` globally for Outlook 2013+. This is a common legacy pattern but can clip single-line text cells if any `td` lacks an explicit `line-height` override.
