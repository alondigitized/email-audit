# Find his perfect pair: Men's denim for every fit
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- "Find his perfect pair" — and right there, before I've even scrolled an inch, Old Navy has already told me this email isn't for me. The hero is a man posed next to a numbered denim grid, and I'm a 36-year-old woman who opened this inbox to figure out what the kids are wearing to school in three weeks. I didn't close it immediately because I've learned Old Navy packs more than one message into a send — and sure enough, buried a few swipes down is a Back-to-School Deal section with those tiered price points that make my brain go into bulk-buy mode. There's even a "1 Year Warranty on All Kids Clothing" callout that genuinely made me pause. That's Hanna Andersson energy out of an Old Navy send — smart.
- But here's the thing: I had to earn that information. The email led with the wrong person. This is also the fifth Old Navy email in five days — the July 21 activewear send, the July 20 $6 tees, two July 19 sends, and now this. I'm starting to feel like a blast-list casualty, not a subscriber. The July 21 activewear email felt like it knew me. This one feels like it got forwarded to me by mistake.
- I would not click. I would not forward. I'd skim the BTS section, maybe screenshot the price tiers for later, and move on.

## What's working

- The men's denim grid is actually well-conceived — showing multiple fits side by side in a numbered visual is genuinely useful if I'm buying for a partner, and Old Navy doesn't always communicate fit variety this clearly
- The Back-to-School Deal tiered pricing is the kind of concrete anchor that stops the scroll — price ladders work, and this one is immediately legible
- "1 Year Warranty on All Kids Clothing" is a real premium signal; that's the language Hanna Andersson uses to justify its price, and Old Navy including it here earns trust points I wasn't expecting
- Clean render top to bottom — no broken images, no text overlap, layout holds; at Old Navy's send volume that's not a given
- Old Navy logo is instantly recognizable; I'm not spending a second wondering who this is

## What's weak

- "His" in the headline is a one-word demographic mismatch — I'm the person opening this email, and the word immediately signals I'm shopping proxy for someone else, not for myself or my kids
- The BTS deal and the kids warranty section are buried several modules down — everything I actually came to Old Navy for is below the fold, after a full men's denim catalog
- Five emails in five days is starting to feel like saturation; the July 19 kids send already covered the BTS territory, so this email feels additive in the worst sense — one more thing to clear out
- "The Comfy" section in the middle reads like a third email shoved inside two others — the tonal shift from structured denim fits to cozy loungewear to kids uniforms has whiplash energy
- No deadline on the BTS deal — "Back-to-School Deal" tells me nothing about whether this expires Sunday or in September

## Recommendations

- 1. **Flip the email structure**: lead with the BTS kids deal and the warranty callout — that's the hook for the person most likely opening this inbox — then offer men's denim as the secondary module for household top-up
- 2. **Add a deadline to the BTS section**: even "through Sunday" or "this week only" creates urgency that the current send completely lacks; the July 21 activewear email used "TODAY ONLY!" above the fold — this should borrow that energy
- 3. **Kill "his" in the subject** or frame it as a household shop: the word is a self-selecting filter that excludes the primary opener before she even clicks
- 4. **Surface the warranty earlier** — possibly in the subject or preheader; that's a differentiating claim that Hanna Andersson wins on, and Old Navy could chip away at that gap if it actually led with it
- 5. **Trim the send cadence**: the fifth email in five days is eroding the goodwill the July 21 send built
- **Subject Alt A:** `BTS deals + men's denim: the full family haul`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Back-to-school prices are live — kids, him, everyone`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `1-year warranty on all kids styles + denim fits for every body. Shop now through Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Tees from $5, jeans for every fit — and yes, kids gear has a 1-year warranty now.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Tracking pixel missing `alt` attribute**

```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/30c9e989-9e1a-42a2-882e-3261cb941441?..." width="1" height="1">
```

The open-tracking pixel from `click.email.oldnavy.com` correctly uses `alt=""`. The `cp.oldnavy.com` pixel omits the `alt` attribute entirely. Screen readers will read the full src URL. Fix: add `alt=""`.

**[WARN] Duplicate CSS selector collision — `.MspaceTop` / `.MspaceBot`**

In the `@media (max-width: 639px)` block:

```css
.space-top5, .MspaceTop { padding-top: 5px; }
.space-top10, .MspaceTop { padding-top: 10px; } /* duplicate */
.space-bot5, .MspaceBot  { padding-bottom: 5px; }
.space-bot10, .MspaceBot { padding-bottom: 10px; } /* duplicate */
```

Last-declaration wins: any element using `.MspaceTop` or `.MspaceBot` will always render at `10px`, ignoring the `5px` intent. This is a latent layout defect that will silently misfire on any element where `5px` spacing was intended.

**[INFO] Outlook conditional block sets `td { line-height: 0px }`**

```html

```

This is a known Outlook fix pattern, but a blanket `line-height: 0px` on all `td` elements can collapse cells without explicit per-cell overrides. Not confirmed broken, but worth verifying in Outlook 2016/2019 renders.

May be a relay capture gap, but cannot be confirmed present.
