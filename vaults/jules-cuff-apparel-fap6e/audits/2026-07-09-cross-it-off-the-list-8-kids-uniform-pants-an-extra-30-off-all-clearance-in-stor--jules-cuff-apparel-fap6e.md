# Cross it off the list: $8 kids uniform pants + an extra 30% off all Clearance in-store
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one actually earned the open. "Cross it off the list" plus $8 uniform pants landed in my inbox the same week I've been mentally building the back-to-school cart, and the hero delivers instantly — big "2 DAYS ONLY!" countdown, the pants right up front, price visible without a single scroll. After twelve-ish days of Summer Grind emails, this is the first subject line since July 6's $6 uniform shorts that felt like Old Navy was talking to *me* specifically instead of broadcasting to the entire subscriber list.
- But then the email does what Old Navy always does: it can't commit. I'm here for uniform pants and the clearance discount, and instead I get "How To Go Blue" and "The Low Blue Basics" — both adult denim sections, both priced at $40 and $36 respectively, dropped right into the middle of a back-to-school email. It's the same content-drift problem the July 7th and July 8th "$15 & under" sends had, except here the subject was *so specifically* kids-uniform-focused that the pivot to mom jeans feels like a bait-and-switch.
- The back-to-school deals grid at the bottom saves it slightly — $5, $8, $12, $14 price callouts confirm the value story. I'd click the uniform pants CTA. I would not forward this; I'd find the $6 shorts email from July 6 first and stack them.

## What's working

- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" urgency is front and center** — it's the first thing I see, it's big, and it's credible given the back-to-school window.
- **$8 price point on the uniform pants is the hero** and it doesn't hide — I didn't have to scroll or squint to confirm the offer from the subject line matched reality in the body.
- **"1 Year Guarantee on All Kids Clothing"** visible near the top is the kind of trust signal Hanna Andersson leans on hard in their welcome email, and Old Navy smartly borrows it here — for uniform pants that survive gym class, that matters to me.
- **The Back-to-School Deals grid at the bottom** reinforces the volume-buy case with clean price ladder ($5 → $8 → $12 → $14), which is exactly how I think about stocking up.
- **Subject line specificity** is genuinely high — two offers, two prices, no vague "savings await" language.

## What's weak

- **The adult denim detour kills the momentum.** "How To Go Blue" and "The Low Blue Basics" are not what I came for, and at $40/$36 they're also not clearance-level impulse buys. Old Navy broke the one job this email had.
- **Subject line is 87 characters — completely truncated on mobile.** The critical "extra 30% off Clearance" part gets cut. Only the $8 pants half of the offer survives in most inboxes, which makes the clearance section inside feel like a surprise tax when I scroll.
- **No sizing detail or availability signal for the uniform pants.** The $6 shorts email from July 6th had the same issue — I'm a mom buying for two kids in different sizes, and "uniform pants" with no fit/sizing callout means I still have to do homework before I add to cart.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is day twelve of the Summer Grind. Even when the content is relevant, the daily drumbeat erodes urgency. "2 DAYS ONLY" means less when yesterday's email also felt urgent.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the adult denim sections entirely from this version** — or relegate them to a secondary email for the same day. A dedicated back-to-school email that drifts into mom-jeans upsell tells me Old Navy doesn't have enough confidence in the kids-uniform story to let it breathe. The July 6th shorts email made the same mistake but recovered because $6 is so cheap I clicked anyway. At $8 for pants, there's more friction to cut through.
- 2. **Shorten the subject line to survive mobile truncation.** The clearance offer is buried past character 50. Pick one lead offer per email; the other can live in the preheader.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$8 uniform pants — 2 days only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Back-to-school list: $8 uniform pants + 30% off Clearance`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `+ extra 30% off all Clearance in-store. Stocking up season starts now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `1-year guarantee included. Grab both kids' sizes before they sell out.`
- 3. **Add a sizing/availability nudge in the hero copy.** Something like "Boy, Girl, and Gender-Neutral fits" or "Sizes 2–18" underneath the $8 callout makes this feel more like a targeted send and less like a broadcast. Hanna Andersson's welcome email earns trust by calling out fit specifics — Old Navy could steal that beat here.
- 4. **Lean into the checklist metaphor harder.** "Cross it off the list" is a genuinely good hook for a back-to-school email, but the body doesn't follow through. A simple "What's on your list?" mini-grid — uniform pants ✓, uniform shorts ✓, graphic tees ✓ — would make this feel like a companion to the July 6th send instead of a standalone blast.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional table-based layout with VML/MSO conditional comments — structurally sound. One broken tracking pixel URL was confirmed by QA, and several compliance headers are absent or unverifiable through the relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken tracking pixel URL**
The Contentsquare/Movable Ink pixel embedded in the hidden preheader div returns an unknown URL type error:
```
https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/c418ffa6-b050-4cad-9837-49f9e1a64bd7?...
Error: unknown url type: '/g/c418ffa6-b050-4cad-9837-49f9e1a64bd7?txn=ca098e80-7b9a-11f1-85ae-9295eb1db778'
```
This suggests the pixel vendor's redirect endpoint is resolving to a malformed internal path (`/g/...`) rather than a valid destination. Open tracking or engagement data from this pixel may be lost for this send.

**[WARN] 23 click-tracking links skipped**
All body links route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. These were not probed, so broken destinations cannot be confirmed or ruled out. Standard pre-send link validation against the click-redirect endpoint is recommended.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Tracking pixel missing `alt` text**
```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/c418ffa6-..." width="1" height="1">
```
No `alt=""` attribute present. While a 1×1 pixel has no meaningful content, the absence of an explicit empty `alt` can trigger accessibility linters and screen reader verbosity in some clients. The open-pixel `<img>` in the same div does carry `alt=""` — this one does not.

**No other rendering issues found.** The MSO conditional blocks (`<!--[if gte mso 9]>`), VML namespaces, `.ExternalClass` resets, and the single `@media (max-width: 639px)` breakpoint are all correctly structured. The `td { line-height:0px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly }` Outlook fix is present.

**Minor — duplicate CSS selector targets:**
`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each declared twice in the responsive block with different values (10px overrides 5px silently). Not a rendering break but creates maintainability risk.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens detected in the truncated source. No issues found.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] CAN-SPAM physical address not detected in source**
QA did not find a physical mailing address in the parsed content. If it exists below the truncation point, no action needed — but it must be present in the final rendered footer.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent (or not captured)**
The AgentMail relay did not surface this header. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to render the native unsubscribe UI. Its absence (or relay-level suppression) degrades inbox placement and user trust signals. Verify the header is present at the MTA level:
```
List-Unsubscribe: <mailto:...>, <https://...>
List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click
```
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is also absent — required for high-volume senders under Gmail/Yahoo 2024 sender requirements.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not found**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status is unknown through the relay. Cannot confirm authentication chain for `email.oldnavy.com`. This should be verified at the MTA or in a seed-list inbox test.
