# It's go-time: 50% off ALL activewear
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-28**
## Executive summary

- Twelve days. I know because I went back and counted in my brand history notes, and this is now the third time in two days I've seen "50% off ALL activewear" from Old Navy — June 27 had two sends on the same beat, and now here we are on June 28 with the same headline wearing a slightly different hat. "It's go-time" is doing a lot of lifting as a differentiator, and honestly it's not enough. My muscle memory almost swiped past it before I registered the "2 DAYS ONLY" counter in the hero.
- That said, when I actually stopped and looked, the email isn't bad on its own merits. The hero is clean, the countdown creates urgency, and the product grid is more browseable than most of their sends this week. The "HOT NEW GEAR" section at the bottom even caught my eye — that's a real novelty hook, similar to what the TrekTech mention did on June 27. But I'm buying kids' clothes at scale, not rebuilding my own fitness wardrobe, so this lands as "useful if I have five free minutes" and not "I need to click this right now."
- Mark as read, not delete. No forward to a friend — my mom friends are in the same email fatigue spiral I'm in.

## What's working

- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" hero reads instantly.** The countdown urgency is the single strongest thing in this send; my eye went there before the logo.
- **The product grid is genuinely shoppable.** They showed actual items — tops, shorts, pants — not just lifestyle vibes, which is more than Hanna Andersson's welcome email did for product discovery.
- **"HOT NEW GEAR" section creates a second scroll-stop.** It has energy; the models feel athletic without being intimidating, and it breaks the visual monotony of the tile grid.
- **Subject line is clean at 37 characters.** No all-caps, no triple exclamation points — a lesson Kohl's still hasn't learned after a decade.
- **Color palette is summer-appropriate.** Mint, coral, bright athletic colors — this email knows what month it is.

## What's weak

- **Third activewear send in roughly 36 hours.** I scored the June 27 evening send an 8, and another June 27 morning send also an 8 — this is the same creative concept with a new subject line. The creative team is spinning, not rotating.
- **No kids callout, anywhere.** I shop Old Navy primarily for my kids. There's nothing in this email that earns my attention as a mom — not a single matching-set mention, not "activewear for the whole family." Hanna Andersson would have led with the kids-and-adults coordination angle.
- **"40%-70% OFF DEEP DIVES BIGDEALS!" runs together visually.** Whatever that section header is doing, it reads like the designer ran out of kerning budget. It took me a beat to parse it.
- **No loyalty hook.** My NavyRewards balance is real money to me — Kohl's always shows me my Cash, Old Navy keeps leaving that conversion lever on the table.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface the kids/family angle in the hero.** Even a single line — "activewear for the whole crew" — would make me stop for my kids' PE days instead of scrolling past because I'm not personally rebuilding an athleisure drawer.
- 2. **Show a NavyRewards balance or member-exclusive callout.** "Members save an extra 10%" turns a mediocre open into a real click. Kohl's does this by default; Old Navy keeps forgetting it exists.
- 3. **Rest the activewear beat for one send.** Put a jeans or back-to-school teaser in between. The pattern-interrupt is what earned the TrekTech mention its extra points on June 27.
- 4. **Fix the "BIGDEALS!" section header.** Give it breathing room or restructure it — right now it reads like a render glitch even if it isn't one.
- 5. **Tighten the subject to add a family or kid signal** — e.g., "2 days: 50% off activewear for the whole crew."
- **Subject Alt A:** `2 days only: 50% off activewear — kids & adults`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Go-time: 50% off ALL activewear ends soon`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop the whole family's summer workout looks before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your NavyRewards stretch further on activewear — 2 days only.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**WARN — Missing `alt` text on tracking pixel**
```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/076c1a63..." width="1" height="1">
```
The pixel has no `alt=""` attribute. While a 1×1 pixel is decorative, an explicit empty `alt=""` is required for strict accessibility conformance (WCAG 1.1.1) and suppresses screen-reader noise.

The open-beacon pixel at `click.email.oldnavy.com/open.aspx?...` also has `alt=""` — that one is correctly handled.

**INFO — CSS duplicate selectors**
`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each assigned twice in the mobile media query (`space-top5`/`space-top10` share `.MspaceTop`; `space-bot5`/`space-bot10` share `.MspaceBot`). The second rule silently overrides the first. Low severity, but unexpected spacing behavior can occur at mobile breakpoints.

**INFO — Outlook `td { line-height: 0px }` override**
The MSO conditional block sets `td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }` globally, relying on inline overrides to restore correct spacing. Any `<td>` that lacks an explicit `line-height` inline style will collapse in Outlook 2013+.

### 5. Compliance

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe` header not captured**
The QA relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to render the one-click unsubscribe UI; its absence (or relay-drop) degrades user unsubscribe UX and risks inbox placement.

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found**
One-click machine unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy as of Feb 2024) depends on this header. If it's genuinely absent rather than relay-stripped, this is a compliance gap with major ISP requirements.

**WARN — No physical mailing address detected in HTML source**
CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email. The truncated source does not include one. If it is present further down in the footer (outside the provided truncation), this warning is a false positive — but it should be confirmed.

**INFO — SPF/DKIM authentication status unknown**
`Authentication-Results` header not forwarded by the AgentMail relay. Sending domain is `email.oldnavy.com`; authentication posture cannot be confirmed from available data.
