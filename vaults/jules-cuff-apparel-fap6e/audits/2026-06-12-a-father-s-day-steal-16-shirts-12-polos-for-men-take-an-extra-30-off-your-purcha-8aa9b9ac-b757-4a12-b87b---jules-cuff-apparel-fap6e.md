# A Father's Day steal: $16 shirts & $12 polos for men + take an EXTRA 30% off your purchase
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-six. I actually whispered it before I typed it. This is the twenty-sixth Old Navy email in roughly two weeks, and by all rights it should have hit my trash folder on autopilot — except this one caught me mid-coffee because Father's Day weekend is two days out and I genuinely haven't bought anything for my husband yet. That's the only reason this landed. Old Navy accidentally timed a campaign to my procrastination cycle, and I'm almost annoyed at how well it worked.
- The offer is concrete and the hero doesn't bury it: $16 shirts, $12 polos, extra 30% off on top. That's a real, stackable deal I can do math on in my head while standing in the kitchen. For volume gifting — my dad, my father-in-law, the teacher appreciation add-on — this is exactly the kind of email I'd screenshot and act on in the next twenty minutes. The "TODAY ONLY" urgency is visible early, which is smart, because the subject line is so long it reads like a run-on sentence on a Post-It note.
- That said, the email doesn't treat me like a known customer. No "Hey Jules, dad stuff is 30% off today" — just a broadcast blast dressed up in holiday language. Hanna Andersson's welcome email last month felt like a brand that knew my name even on the first contact. This feels like Old Navy yelling into a stadium. I'd click. I wouldn't forward it to a friend — I'd text them the offer separately, because the email itself doesn't feel shareable.

## What's working

- **Price anchors in the hero are impossible to miss.** "$12" and "$16" render big and bold before any scrolling — that's the right call for a deal-first customer like me.
- **Father's Day timing is a genuine save.** I have a live gift need this weekend; the seasonal peg made me pay attention when I otherwise wouldn't have.
- **"Extra 30% off your purchase" stacks with the already-low prices**, which is deal math I can feel. That layered-discount structure is what separates a good Old Navy send from a mediocre one.
- **TODAY ONLY urgency appears early in the email body**, giving me a real reason to act now rather than tab-hop to look at shorts.
- **Clearance callout at the bottom** is a bonus for bargain-hunters — I noticed it and it's a legitimate secondary click driver for me.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is 89 characters long.** "A Father's Day steal: $16 shirts & $12 polos for men + take an EXTRA 30% off your purchase" — by the time I get to "take an EXTRA 30% off" on my phone screen, the whole thing has been clipped. The all-caps EXTRA is doing the most and it tips into mild spam-flag territory.
- **The hero pivots to women's clothing mid-scroll**, which is a jarring tone shift. If you hooked me on a Father's Day gift, stay in that lane for a few more panels before pivoting to yellow sundresses and activewear.
- **No personalization signal anywhere.** Not my name, not "last chance for Dad," not even a "ships by Sunday" logistics note that would tell me this email knows I'm buying for a specific date.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** Even when the offer is good, I've been marinating in Old Navy emails for two weeks. The brand has trained me to skim-and-defer, which is bad muscle memory when a genuine deadline exists.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Gut the subject line to its core.** The price anchors are the hook — lead with them and stop. "Father's Day: $12 polos, $16 shirts + extra 30% off — today only" fits on a phone screen and still delivers all three value props.
- 2. **Add a "ships by Sunday?" logistics line** above or near the primary CTA. Father's Day is a hard date. A single line — "Order by [date] for Father's Day delivery" — turns a good deal email into an urgent gift-buyer tool.
- 3. **Pin the men's section for the full scroll.** Don't pivot to women's activewear halfway down. Father's Day is a coherent mission; fragmenting into All Women Everything mid-email loses the thread.
- 4. **Show a model that looks like a dad-gift recipient**, not just a catalog man. A family photo, a polo-and-shorts combo on someone age 45-60, something that says "this is for him" rather than "here is a man in a shirt."
- 5. **Clearance section deserves a better label.** "Clearance Clearance" reads redundant and rushed — it's a signal that the bottom of the email was assembled fast.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Father's Day: $12 polos + $16 shirts, extra 30% off today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Ships before Sunday: men's polos $12, shirts $16 + extra 30% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stack the savings — already-low prices just got lower for one day.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Order today, unwrap Sunday. Extra 30% applies at checkout.`

One hard-broken redirect (broken experience) and three compliance gaps (List-Unsubscribe headers + missing physical address) are the critical issues. The plain-text part is also nearly unreadable to spam filters at 82% URL content by character count.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect — cp.oldnavy.com Movable Ink pixel**

The QA probe followed `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/61bea2e7-1163-4cce-ba7b-d1423e9df113?mi_mid=019eb9c5-ab80-7000-a724-5346e628b4b6&cp_tp=v3.eJyN...` and received a redirect to a relative path `/g/61bea2e7-...` — not a valid absolute URL. This breaks the Movable Ink personalization render pipeline; the dynamic image or content block backed by this token will fail to load for affected recipients.

- **Root cause:** The Movable Ink `o/` (open) endpoint is redirecting to a `/g/` (generate) path without a host, which is invalid as a redirect target. Likely a misconfigured Movable Ink campaign or an expired/malformed `cp_tp` payload.
- **Blast radius:** Every subscriber who opens this email will get a broken dynamic image/content block, not just edge cases.

**[INFO] 23 tracking links skipped**

All click-redirect links route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. HTTP probing was skipped for these (expected behavior for click-tracking domains). No evidence of broken destinations, but cannot confirm landing page UTMs without manual spot-check.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate CSS selector definitions (minor)**

In the `@media (max-width: 639px)` block, both `.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are defined twice on different rules:

```css
.space-top5, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 5px;}
...
.space-top10, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 10px;}   /* overwrites previous */
```

The second definition silently overrides the first. `MspaceTop` will always resolve to `padding-top: 10px` on mobile; any elements relying on `5px` via that class will render incorrectly. Same pattern for `MspaceBot`.

**Outlook `td { line-height: 0px }` override (moderate)**

Inside the `<!--[if gte mso 9]-->` conditional block:
```css
td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }
```
This is a global zero-line-height reset applied to all `<td>` elements in Outlook 2013+. If any `<td>` contains live text that isn't individually overridden, text will collapse. Safe only if every text-bearing cell has explicit `line-height` inline styles.

**Missing alt text on dynamic image**

The Movable Ink dynamic image (`cp.oldnavy.com/o/61bea2e7...`) is flagged with no `alt` attribute. For the open/tracking pixel at `click.email.oldnavy.com/open.aspx`, `alt=""` is present and acceptable. The dynamic content image should carry descriptive alt text for screen readers and image-off render environments.

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post headers absent**

Neither `List-Unsubscribe` (RFC 2369) nor `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click) headers were captured by the AgentMail relay. Note these may be present on the actual SMTP envelope and not captured — however:

- Google and Yahoo's bulk sender requirements (enforced since February 2024) mandate `List-Unsubscribe-Post` for senders above 5k/day. Old Navy almost certainly exceeds this threshold.
- If these headers are genuinely missing, this creates inbox placement risk with Gmail and Yahoo Mail, which use the absence of one-click unsubscribe as a negative signal.

**Action:** Verify headers are present on the raw SMTP message (not just as forwarded by relay). If missing, ESP must inject them at send time.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**

CAN-SPAM §7 requires a valid physical postal address in the email body. The HTML source is truncated so the footer may contain it — but if the QA check did not find it in the full HTML parse, the address is either absent or rendered as an image (which fails the requirement). Must be live text.

**[INFO] Authentication-Results header not captured**

SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown due to AgentMail relay not forwarding the `Authentication-Results` header. Old Navy's sending domain (`email.oldnavy.com`) should be fully authenticated — cannot confirm from this sample.
