# Save on summer 'fits! Grab $12 women's dresses & adult shorts
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-three. I genuinely counted before I started typing. This is email number twenty-three from Old Navy in roughly two weeks, and I know that because I have been clocking them for this project. The fatigue is real and it sits in my chest the moment I see that blue Old Navy header, but — and this is where it gets complicated — the email itself isn't bad. "2 DAYS ONLY" punched above the fold in bold red, $9 and $12 price tags sitting right underneath, adult women models in actual summer clothes. If this were email number three, I'd probably be reaching for my phone.
- The layout stacks four or five distinct content sections: the hero urgency block, a tiered pricing grid ($5 / $8 / $12), a "Dresses Everyone's Obsession" feature, some campaign-y florals/stripes imagery, and then a clearance pile at the bottom screaming "Clearance Clearance Clearance" three times like it's trying to convince itself. Hanna Andersson's welcome email — which I audited back in May — opened with a single, confident offer and trusted me to scroll if I wanted more. Old Navy is doing the opposite: throw everything at the wall and hope the $12 hooks me before I swipe left.
- Would I click? Grudgingly, maybe, on the $12 dresses — that price point is genuinely useful for volume shopping. Would I forward to a friend? Absolutely not. I'd feel embarrassed to pass along email number twenty-three.

## What's working

- **"2 DAYS ONLY" hero lands first** — red text, high contrast, no ambiguity. My eye went there immediately before anything else competed for it.
- **$12 is a real number** — not "up to 50% off" math I have to decode; a specific dollar price I can hold in my head while I'm chasing kids around the house.
- **Models are adult women, not teens** — demographic match for me at 36; the styling read as people my age actually wearing these clothes.
- **Seasonal alignment is on point** — every product, every color, every image says "summer." Old Navy isn't accidentally serving me sweaters in June.
- **Clearance section adds genuine value** — 30–40% off summer styles at the bottom is the kind of thing I scan for when I'm stocking up before a family trip.

## What's weak

- **Four competing hero sections with no clear winner** — after the urgency block, the email fractures into $5/$8/$12 grid, then a branded dress feature, then a lifestyle campaign, then clearance. Hanna Andersson would have committed to one story; Old Navy is running four simultaneously.
- **The "Clearance Clearance Clearance" repeat is desperate** — typing the word three times in a row doesn't make the offer more compelling; it makes the brand look like it's having a moment of panic.
- **No loyalty or rewards callout** — I have an Old Navy account and a Card; this email treats me like a complete stranger walking in off the street.
- **The campaign-y "Tu Ra, Ra" lifestyle block is wasted space** — editorial vibes in a promotional email with five other offers just slows me down on the path to the $12 dress.
- **Cadence fatigue poisons the well** — this is roughly the same $12 shorts/dresses offer I saw yesterday ("Go ahead, take a peek 👀") and the day before. The urgency of "2 DAYS ONLY" means nothing when I've been seeing this price point for two weeks.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Consolidate to one hero offer per email** — pick either the $12 dresses OR the clearance section, not both plus two lifestyle modules. Scatter-shot layout trains me to stop looking.
- 2. **Put the 2-day deadline in the subject line** — the in-body urgency is working harder than it has to because the subject doesn't carry any time signal. Make me open because I know I'll miss something, not just because I see a price.
- 3. **Add a loyalty hook** — one line: "Your rewards balance: $XX — use it today." Hanna Andersson's welcome email personalized the 20% code; Old Navy has my account data and isn't using it.
- 4. **Cut the "Clearance Clearance Clearance" triple repeat** — one clear "Summer Clearance · 30–40% off" line with a button is cleaner and doesn't read as frantic.
- 5. **Slow down the send cadence** — structurally, this email would score two points higher if it weren't the twenty-third in two weeks. The brand is trading long-term credibility for short-term volume.
