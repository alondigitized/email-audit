# Your sign to stock up: 50% off everything* ends soon!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- Three Old Navy emails in roughly 24 hours. This one landed after yesterday's linen-focused 60%-off send and the morning-of "$12 & under" send, and I am genuinely fatigued. The subject says "ends soon" — but ends soon as in tonight, tomorrow, end of the week? That urgency is hollow without a date, and I've seen "ends soon" from Old Navy so many times this month it's become wallpaper.
- The email itself is almost entirely a women's dress parade — a 3x4 grid of adult dresses, a "Top 10 Dresses" editorial block, and a clearance section. If I'm being straight: I'm not dress shopping right now. I'm stocking up on kids' basics for summer. I'm the person who empties the kids' clearance rack at Old Navy semi-annual, not the one browsing a curated top-10 dress list. This whole send is aimed at a different version of me — the childless, weekend-brunch version — and I'm not her.
- Would I click? No. Would I forward to a friend? Maybe to my sister who doesn't have kids yet and would actually care about a black midi dress. But for me, personally, I'm marking this read and moving on.

## What's working

- **50% off everything** is legible and dominant — big white text on the navy bar communicates the offer at a glance, even on a small screen.
- **The "Shop Clearance in Your Size" section** at the bottom is the one place that felt like it was talking to me — quick-tap size buttons (XS through 4X) is a genuinely useful, friction-reducing UI element I wish more emails did.
- **No render bugs visible** — images load cleanly, layout holds, text doesn't overlap.
- **"Dresses Everyone's Obsessing Over"** editorial framing is at least a slightly warmer tone than a raw product dump; the language has some personality.

## What's weak

- **Zero kids content.** This is Old Navy, a brand I use primarily for volume kids' basics. Not a single item aimed at my actual shopping behavior.
- **"Ends soon" without a date** is noise at this point — I've seen it four times this week from this sender and it has stopped meaning anything to me.
- **The dress grid is undifferentiated.** Nine dress thumbnails in a row with no price callouts, no size info, no "only 3 left" scarcity signal — it's just a catalog page, not an email.
- **Mass, not premium, but also not useful.** Hanna Andersson's welcome email gave me a code, clear brand tone, and product categories I care about — every section felt intentional. This feels like a batch broadcast with my name swapped in nowhere.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** The 19th had TWO Old Navy emails. This is the third in roughly 36 hours. Even an offer I'd normally love is getting filtered out because the frequency has trained me to deprioritize.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Inject at least one kids' section** — I shop Old Navy for kids at a higher basket size than women's. A single "Kids' summer must-haves — 50% off" row with three or four product tiles would have me clicking immediately. The absence of kids entirely signals this is an unsegmented blast.
- 2. **Replace "ends soon" with a hard deadline** — "Ends tonight at midnight" or "48 hours left" is copyable and testable. "Ends soon" is a credibility drain at this send frequency.
- 3. **Give the dress grid some triage signals** — even a badge ("Fan Fave," "Low Stock," a star rating) would give me a reason to click a specific tile rather than bouncing.
- 4. **Throttle the cadence or at minimum vary the angle** — if you're going to send a third email in 36 hours, it has to feel different. This reads as a copy-paste of the last two.
- 5. **Surface the clearance size-selector higher** — that "Shop Clearance in Your Size" module is the most useful thing in the email and it's buried below the fold.
- **Subject Alt A:** `50% off ends tonight — stock up before it's gone`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last chance: kids + family styles, 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dresses, kids' basics, clearance — shop by size before the deal closes.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your size, your price — clearance + 50% off, ending tonight.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Duplicate CSS class selectors in mobile media query**
- `.MspaceTop` is assigned twice: once to `.space-top5` and again to `.space-top10`. Same for `.MspaceBot` on `.space-bot5` and `.space-bot10`. The second rule silently overwrites the first. Any element using `.space-top5` or `.space-bot5` on mobile will receive the 10px value instead of 5px.
- Evidence: `@media only screen and (max-width: 639px)` block, lines defining `.space-top5, .MspaceTop` and `.space-top10, .MspaceTop`.

**[WARN] Outlook MSO `td` line-height reset**
- `td { line-height:0px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly }` in the MSO conditional block resets all `td` line-heights to zero for Outlook 2013+. Without element-level overrides on every `td`, this can clip text content in Outlook desktop clients.

**[OK] Tracking pixel alt attribute**
- QA flagged `alt=""` on the `cp.oldnavy.com` 1×1 pixel. Empty alt is correct per WCAG for decorative/invisible images — no action needed.

**[OK] Responsive structure**
- Single 639px breakpoint, VML namespace for Outlook background support, and MSO conditional comments are all present and structurally appropriate.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

- One-click unsubscribe has been required by Google/Yahoo since February 2024 for bulk senders. Absence means Gmail may suppress the one-click UI and flag the sender negatively in spam classification.

- CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid postal address in the message body. HTML source is truncated; the footer was not included in the provided extract. Must confirm presence in the full rendered footer before clearing this item.

**[WARN] SPF/DKIM authentication status unknown**
- `Authentication-Results` header not captured by relay. Sending domain is `email.oldnavy.com`. Cannot confirm DKIM signature validity or SPF pass from available data.
