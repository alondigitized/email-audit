# GASP 😮 50% off everything* AND $6 tees & $5 tanks
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy is stacking. After two separate emails yesterday about the $6 tees and $5 tanks, today they've layered a 50%-off-everything event on top of the same deal like they're building a sale sandwich. The "GASP 😮" in the subject line made me pull a face — I'm 36, not on BeReal, and performative shock copy from a brand I've already heard from twice this week reads as desperation, not excitement. That said, I can't pretend the offer stack doesn't land. 50% off plus flat-dollar prices on the exact items I stock up on before summer? That's the math I actually wait for. The "2 DAYS ONLY" hero gave me enough countdown to stop scrolling past it.
- But this email is long. Very long. By the time I hit the "Clearance Clearance Clearance" banner at the bottom, I felt like I was cleaning out a warehouse, not browsing a sale. The asterisk on "50% off everything*" put me on defense before I'd even gotten to the first product module — I've been burned by that asterisk before and I will be burned again.
- I'd click. I would not forward this. Hanna Andersson sent me one clean welcome email with a single 20% off code and I trusted every word of it. This is the fourth Old Navy ping since Thursday, and the energy is starting to feel less "semi-annual event" and more "we're panicking."

## What's working

- The hero delivers the full offer stack — 50% off, $6 tees, $5 tanks — without making me scroll. That trifecta is exactly why I'm subscribed.
- "2 DAYS ONLY" is a real urgency signal. Not "prices end soon" mush — an actual timeframe I can act against.
- "Dream Dresses Under $15?" is the right women's lifestyle hook for a May email. I'm already thinking about weekend dresses I can wash on hot.
- The clearance section at the bottom is a legitimate reason to scroll — serves a second-pass shopper well.
- The brand voice is consistent: direct, casual, no pretense. Old Navy sounds like Old Navy throughout.

## What's weak

- "GASP 😮" is performative and tries too hard. It doesn't match the practical, flat-dollar brand that earned my inbox spot — it sounds like a brand imitating a TikTok sound.
- The asterisk on "50% off everything*" is an instant trust hit. Hanna Andersson doesn't asterisk their sale copy. When Old Navy does it, I immediately start inventorying what's excluded.
- The hero model skews male — blue top, relaxed fit jeans. That's a genuine miss when the headline offer includes women's tanks and the email contains a whole dresses module.
- Four emails in four days. The cadence has crossed into fatigue territory. The two tees-and-tanks emails yesterday mean this "new" offer doesn't feel new — it feels like the same sale with a new subject line.
- No kids callout anywhere visible. I'm shopping for two kids and myself. This email forgot half my cart.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill "GASP" and lead with the offer** — the offer IS the hook. "50% off everything + $6 tees" lands harder than a performative exclamation from a brand I already trust.
- 2. **Swap the hero model** — put a woman in a dream dress in the hero. The demographic signal has to match the purchase intent, and tanks + dresses skew heavily female.
- 3. **Address the asterisk inline** — "50% off almost everything (a few exclusions apply)" is more honest than the buried fine print. It won't kill conversion; hiding it will.
- 4. **Add one kids module** — even a single "kids' tees $6 too" strip would turn this from a women's email into a family email and close the gap for shoppers like me.
- 5. **Cut the email in half** — remove the accessories flat-lay grid and compress the clearance section to a single CTA row. One message, one scroll depth.
- **Subject Alt A:** `50% off everything + $6 tees. Two days only.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your semi-annual moment: 50% off + $6 tees & $5 tanks`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dream dresses under $15 — and the $6 kids' tees are back too.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack the 50% off with flat-dollar prices. Two days to use it.`

## Technical Audit — Old Navy "50% off everything" (2026-05-17)

### 1. Technical Summary

One tracking pixel endpoint returns a broken redirect (relative path, not an absolute URL), causing a hard QA failure. Compliance headers and a physical mailing address are absent from the captured message, which poses CAN-SPAM and RFC 8058 risk at scale.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken Salesforce Personalization pixel redirect**
`https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/b61f6570-a81c-4a8e-b578-f874e3ccffc0?...`
The endpoint resolves with a redirect to a relative path: `/g/b61f6570-a81c-4a8e-b578-f874e3ccffc0?txn=781e3b00-...`
Relative redirects are invalid for HTTP 301/302 responses in many email-client webview and proxy environments. The pixel will silently fail to fire for any client that enforces absolute redirect targets. This is likely a Salesforce Interaction Studio / Personalization beacon; the issue is on the `cp.oldnavy.com` origin server.

**[WARN] 23 click-redirect links not probed**
All CTA links route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. Destination URLs and UTM passthrough were not verified. See §6.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Tracking pixel missing `alt` attribute**
The `cp.oldnavy.com/o/b61f6570...` `<img>` has no `alt` attribute at all (distinct from `alt=""`). Screen readers will announce the full src URL. Fix: add `alt=""` to suppress announcement.

```html
<!-- current -->
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/b61f6570..." width="1" height="1">
<!-- fix -->
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/b61f6570..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
```

**[INFO] Duplicate CSS selector definitions**
`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice in the `@media` block (once for `.space-top5`/`.space-bot5` and again for `.space-top10`/`.space-bot10`). The second declaration silently wins; the 5px variants are dead. Not rendering-breaking in practice, but a maintenance hazard.

**[INFO] Outlook `line-height: 0px` override**
The MSO conditional style sets `td { line-height: 0px }` globally. This is a known Outlook rendering hack but can cause zero-height cells if a `<td>` has no explicit height and contains only an image with a broken src.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (e.g., `%%FIRST_NAME%%`, `{{email}}`) are present in the visible HTML source.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent from captured message**
QA did not detect a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to surface the native unsubscribe UI; its absence increases the likelihood of spam complaints. Note: header may have been stripped by the AgentMail relay — confirm against raw SMTP delivery logs before treating as a definitive send-side gap.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent**
One-click unsubscribe is required by Google and Yahoo bulk-sender policies (≥5k/day). If the header is genuinely absent at send time (not just stripped in relay capture), this is a deliverability and policy compliance failure.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected in HTML**
CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) mandates a valid physical postal address in every commercial email. The truncated source does not include one. If it is present in the footer section not captured, this warning is a false positive — verify against full HTML.
