# SCORE—$5 tees
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- Nine days in, and Old Navy's Summer Grind is still banging the $5 tee drum. I've opened nearly identical emails on July 4th — twice — and July 3rd, so the subject "SCORE—$5 tees" lands with the emotional weight of a recurring alarm I keep snoozing. What keeps me from immediately marking this read is the one thing this send does that the previous ones didn't: "3 DAYS ONLY!" at the top is an actual hard deadline, not just the "going fast!" vibes the last few sends leaned on. And a $4 price circle has appeared alongside the familiar $5 and $6 — that's new money, and I notice it.
- Then I scroll, and Old Navy completely loses the plot. Sandwiched between the tee hero and the back-to-school section — the exact section where I would actually spend money — is a full editorial titled "Why You Need Low Rise." I am 36. I have two kids headed back to school. Low-rise jeans are not a lifestyle aspiration I hold right now. Hanna Andersson's welcome email made me feel like a known customer from the first pixel. This email makes me feel like I landed in a teen flow by accident. The back-to-school section buried below the low-rise editorial is the part that should have been the hero for anyone who looks like me.
- I'd click — but only to the back-to-school section, after scrolling past content that wasn't meant for me. I would not forward this to a friend.

## What's working

- **"3 DAYS ONLY!" is the first credible deadline this campaign has given me** — compared to July 3rd's "going fast!" hedge, this is the urgency language I actually respond to.
- **$4 enters the price lineup** — slipping a fourth price tier below $5 is genuinely new information after nine days of identical framing; it earns a second look from Summer Grind fatigue subscribers like me.
- **Back-to-School Basics section exists** — this is the content that closes the loop for a family uniform shopper buying volume tees in July; glad it's here, even buried.
- **Clean render, no bugs** — images load, text is legible, nothing is overlapping or broken; it looks professional even when the strategy is scattered.

## What's weak

- **"Why You Need Low Rise" editorial is the wrong segment entirely** — that headline and its models are targeting someone ten years younger than me; planted between tees and back-to-school, it feels like a CRM misfire.
- **The tee fatigue is structural by now** — July 2nd's biker shorts email ($5, but with the friend-text-a-tip energy of "*absolute* steal") scored the best of this run precisely because it felt different; this subject just doesn't.
- **"Discover More" CTA is a copout** — after a hero showing me multiple price tiers, clearance, and back-to-school basics, "Discover More" tells me nothing about where I'm landing; compare this to how Hanna Andersson always pairs a CTA with a specific product category.
- **No kids or family signal in the tee hero** — the hero models appear to be adults; one kid model, even small, would have immediately told me this email was for my cart.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the back-to-school section up to the hero for family shoppers** — "$4–$5 tees for the whole crew, 3 days only" with a kid model in the hero would have closed me immediately.
- 2. **Cut or route "Why You Need Low Rise" to a teen/young adult flow** — remove it from the mom/family segment; it's not wrong content, it's wrong placement.
- 3. **Rewrite the CTA to be destination-specific** — "Shop Back-to-School Basics" or "Grab $4 Tees" over "Discover More"; verbs that tell me where I'm going.
- 4. **Give the $4 price point a headline line in the hero copy** — it's the only genuinely new offer in nine days; hiding it in a badge circle undersells it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$4 tees just dropped · 3 days only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Back-to-school from $4 — clock's ticking`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New lower price joins the lineup — back-to-school basics inside for the whole family`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `$4, $5, and $6 tees. Real deadline. Grab what you need before Thursday.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken tracking pixel / redirect URL**
The Coherent Path pixel at `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/84ddc3bc-d681-4110-981f-49f247c25a50?...` resolves to an unknown URL type: `/g/84ddc3bc-d681-4110-981f-49f247c25a50?txn=509f3280-7860-11f1-a476-d2520cded5b8`. ### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing alt text on tracking pixel**
The Coherent Path `<img>` element at `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/84ddc3bc-d681-4110-981f-49f247c25a50?...` has no `alt` attribute. Screen readers will announce the full URL. Should be `alt=""` (empty string, not absent) for decorative/tracking images.

**Duplicate CSS selector definitions**
`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice in the responsive block — once for `.space-top5` and again for `.space-top10` / `.space-bot10`. The second definition silently wins. This is dead code risk; consolidate or rename.

**Outlook conditional comments**
VML namespace and `o:OfficeDocumentSettings` blocks are present and correctly structured. The `td { line-height: 0px }` Outlook override in the MSO conditional block is aggressive and may collapse spacing in Outlook 2013+ for any `<td>` without an explicit override. Acceptable for this layout pattern but worth regression-testing in Litmus/Email on Acid against Outlook 2016/2019.

**`min-height: 500px !important` on body**
This precedes the body reset and is an unusual defensive rule. It prevents Gmail's "clipping" behavior (emails >102KB get clipped with a "View entire message" link). If the HTML payload approaches that threshold, this rule alone won't save it — the actual HTML size needs monitoring.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `[FNAME]`, `%%first_name%%`) are visible in the truncated source. The Coherent Path `cp_tp` parameter contains what appears to be a base64/encoded personalization payload — its resolution failure (see §2) means any dynamically injected product recommendations may have silently failed to render for this recipient.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM / RFC 8058)

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not detected**
The AgentMail relay did not surface this header. If absent from the actual SMTP envelope, major mailbox providers (Gmail, Yahoo) will surface their own unsubscribe UI, which increases spam complaint risk. Verify the header is set at the ESP level, not just in the HTML body.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected**
One-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders (>5k/day) as of February 2024. If this header is missing from the envelope, Old Navy is out of compliance with those provider policies.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected in truncated source**
CAN-SPAM §7 requires a valid physical postal address. The HTML was truncated — the footer likely contains it, but the QA checker did not confirm it. Manual verification of the footer required.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters: unverifiable**
All destination URLs pass through `click.email.oldnavy.com` redirects. UTM attribution cannot be confirmed from the source alone. Standard Old Navy email UTM structure (`utm_source=email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=...`) should be verified by resolving at least two CTA redirects manually.

**Coherent Path personalization failure**
If the `cp.oldnavy.com/o/...` endpoint is broken (see §2), any product recommendation slots driven by CP will either be empty or fallback to static content — creating a continuity gap between what the recipient sees in-email vs. what they land on. Confirm whether fallback static products are wired in the template.
