# SCORE—$8 tees
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-02**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is a lot of email in one scroll — jeans, tees, low-rise everything, then a whole kids section, then a red clearance wall at the bottom. My eye actually landed on "2 DAYS ONLY!" and the $6/$8 tee grid first, which is the same red urgency block I called out in the "Ooooh! 👀$8 tees" send from yesterday (8/10 for me) — so either this is a resend or Old Navy is running that creative for a second day, which starts to dull the urgency a little.
- The kids section is genuinely the strongest real estate in this email for me — "KIDS MUST-HAVES ON SALE" with the $5-$8 price ladder and "1 YEAR GUARANTEE ON ALL KIDS CLOTHING" is exactly the kind of line that gets me to stock up during a semi-annual-style haul. But I have to scroll past a whole jeans/low-rise campaign to get there, and low-rise anything is not my uniform-shopping brain at 36 with two kids in tow.
- Would I click? Maybe, straight to the kids section if I'm patient enough to scroll. Would I forward it? No — nothing here is surprising enough to text a friend about, it's the same purple-adjacent Old Navy grab-bag I've seen all week, just with a red banner slapped on top this time.

## What's working

- The $6/$8 tee red block with "2 DAYS ONLY!" still pops against all the purple/lifestyle noise — it's the one spot with real urgency.
- Kids section has actual price anchors ($5, $6, $7, $8) laid out like a menu — easy to scan on a phone.
- "1 YEAR GUARANTEE ON ALL KIDS CLOTHING" is a real trust cue that speaks directly to me as the parent buying volume basics — Hanna Andersson leans on durability messaging too, but Old Navy rarely says it out loud like this.
- Kids models are diverse in age and look like they're actually playing, not just posing — feels more lived-in than the jeans section above it.

## What's weak

- Way too much jeans/low-rise real estate before I get to anything relevant to me — three separate low-rise callouts (regular, loose, baggy wide leg) that read like the brand's priority, not mine.
- This is the second day running with the same $6/$8 tee red urgency creative — yesterday's "Ooooh! 👀" send already used this exact beat, so the "2 DAYS ONLY" clock feels recycled rather than credible.
- No matching-set or family-coordinated callout anywhere, even though the kids section is right there — a "shop the family" moment would've been an easy win and Hanna Andersson's whole identity is built on that exact idea.
- Bottom "Clearance Clearance Clearance" wall in giant red block letters feels like a different, cheaper email got stapled onto this one — that's the "mass vs. premium" tell I always flag, and it undercuts the tidier kids section right above it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the kids section, not jeans — move "KIDS MUST-HAVES ON SALE" and the $5-$8 grid to the top of the fold. That's the section actually built for someone like me, don't bury it under three low-rise variants.
- 2. Add one "shop the whole family" tile pairing an adult tee with a kid tee in the same stripe/print — Hanna Andersson would never skip that beat, and Old Navy has all the assets already sitting in this same email.
- 3. If the tee sale really is still running today, say so explicitly ("last day!") instead of repeating yesterday's "2 DAYS ONLY" banner verbatim — repeated deadlines read as fake urgency.
- 4. Tone down the clearance wall at the bottom — one clean banner, not a three-stack of the word "Clearance" in red block caps.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Kids tees $5, adult tees $8 — 2 days left`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call: $6-$8 tees for the whole family`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 1-year guarantee on every kid's piece`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock up before the $6 tees are gone`
