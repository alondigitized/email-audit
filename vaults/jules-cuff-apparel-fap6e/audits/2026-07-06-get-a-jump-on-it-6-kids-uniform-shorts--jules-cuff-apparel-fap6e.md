# Get a jump on it: $6 kids uniform shorts
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- Okay, fine — I'll admit it. After ten days of the Old Navy Summer Grind pummeling me with $5 tees, this one actually stopped me. "Get a jump on it: $6 kids uniform shorts" hit different in the inbox because it spoke directly to the one thing I'm already mentally doing in July — pre-loading the uniform drawer before the back-to-school stampede starts. The "TODAY ONLY!" hero is right there at the top, price is giant, the relevance is real.
- But then I keep scrolling and it turns into a greatest-hits sampler: $3.99 fleece, adult pant clearance "in your size," then a whole "HOT NEW GEAR" activewear panel with what looks like teen/adult models in neon. The pivot from "let me help you crush your uniform list" to "here's our fall adult fashion" happens fast and it's jarring. Old Navy does this — throws multiple stories in one email and hopes something sticks. Hanna Andersson would never. Their welcome email (which I still have bookmarked mentally) stayed in one lane from header to footer.
- I'd click the uniform shorts. But I'd do it despite the email, not because of it. I would not forward this to a friend — it's too scattered.

## What's working

- **"TODAY ONLY!" + $6 in the hero** — The flash-sale mechanic is immediately legible. Price is huge. I don't have to hunt for the offer.
- **Subject line pivots off tees at exactly the right moment** — After nine days of $5-tee fatigue, uniform shorts is a genuine gear shift. The "get a jump on it" framing is smart for a mom who likes to stock before the August rush.
- **Back-to-school timing lands** — Early July is when I actually start building my uniform spreadsheet. This email is early enough to feel useful, not desperate.
- **Clean render, no broken images** — Everything displayed. No white boxes, no lorem ipsum, no layout blowout.
- **Hero section has clear visual hierarchy** — Eye goes: logo → TODAY ONLY → $6 → product. Exactly right.

## What's weak

- **Email sprawls into adult fashion mid-scroll** — The "HOT NEW GEAR" activewear block with bright-palette adult/teen models pulls me completely out of the uniform mission. I'm here for my kids' clothes, not my own.
- **$3.99 section reads like an afterthought** — Hard to parse what it is from the image. Feels shoved in between the uniform hero and the clearance section.
- **"PANT CLEARANCE IN YOUR SIZE" is a non-sequitur** — Great offer, wrong email. This belongs in a dedicated adult clearance send, not sandwiched in a back-to-school kids uniforms push.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — Ten days of daily emails means my trust battery is low. Even a relevant offer gets a raised eyebrow at this point.
- **No sizing or bundle signal in the hero** — "Uniform shorts" for what ages? What fits? Hanna Andersson's welcome email called out sizing ranges upfront. Old Navy leaves me to click and find out.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Strip the email to one story: kids uniforms.** Cut the adult pant clearance block and the HOT NEW GEAR activewear entirely. Let the uniform shorts breathe. One product, one click, one mission.
- 2. **Add an age/size range line under the hero price** — Even "Sizes 4–16" under the $6 would answer the first question I always have before clicking.
- 3. **Lead the $3.99 item more clearly** — If it's fleece or another uniform piece, call it by name and stack it as a bundle with the shorts: "Top the look: $3.99 [item]."
- 4. **Give the preview text a job** — Right now the cadence has conditioned me to skim. Preview that earns the open would say something like "Enough for both kids, delivered before August."
- 5. **Acknowledge the cadence honestly** — A light "We know your inbox is full — this one's for the uniform list" would actually work on me. Self-awareness converts.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Today only: $6 kids uniform shorts (sizes 4–16)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Enough pairs for the whole school year. Seriously.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock both kids before the rush hits.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing `alt` text on tracking pixel**
```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/a800780e-..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
```
Empty `alt=""` is technically valid for decorative/tracking images, but screen readers in verbose mode will announce an unlabeled image. Low severity.

**CSS duplicate selectors**
`MspaceTop` and `MspaceBot` are each defined twice in the `@media` block (for `.space-top5`/`.space-top10` and `.space-bot5`/`.space-bot10` respectively). The comment in the source acknowledges this. Second definition silently overrides the first — verify intended padding values are preserved.

**MSO line-height reset**
The Outlook conditional sets `td { line-height: 0px }` globally, then relies on individual elements to restore spacing. This is a known but fragile pattern — any `<td>` without an explicit `line-height` or `mso-line-height-rule` override will collapse to zero height in Outlook 2013+.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header absent (relay artifact)**
The AgentMail relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. If this header is absent from the actual SMTP envelope (not just a relay capture issue), Gmail and Yahoo will not render the native unsubscribe button — a direct violation of their 2024 bulk sender requirements for lists >5k/day.

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click machine unsubscribe is required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy. Absence means users must click through to a preference center, increasing spam complaints.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**
CAN-SPAM §7704(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email. None is visible in the truncated HTML. Confirm it appears below the fold in the full source; if omitted, this is a statutory violation.

**Authentication-Results header not found**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status cannot be confirmed. `oldnavy.com` has published DMARC, so a DKIM failure here would cause inbox filtering. Needs direct header inspection from the received message.
