# $6 tees & tanks inside ($4 for kids) + take an EXTRA 30% off your purchase
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-eight, twenty-nine — honestly, I've stopped counting. This landed the same day as "$6 TEES & TANKS (Yes, $6!)" which I already opened, stared at, and partially processed. Now here's the sequel: same hero pricing, same tees-and-tanks angle, except this time the kids' $4 price point finally made it into the subject line. That part is actually a win — it's the first time in this entire blast cycle Old Navy flagged the kids price upfront, and as someone buying volume for two kids plus myself, I noticed. But then I open the email and it's doing three jobs at once: the tees/tanks lead, then "HOT NEW GEAR," then a triple-stacked CLEARANCE section that screams end-of-season desperation. Pick a lane.
- I'd click the tees/tanks section — I always do — but I'm doing it on autopilot, not because this email earned it. Hanna Andersson's welcome email last month treated me like a customer they wanted to keep; this feels like Old Navy treating me like a clickthrough rate. The "2 DAYS ONLY" banner in the body adds genuine urgency they forgot to put in the subject line, which is the backwards version of how that's supposed to work.
- Mark as read? No. Click? Probably, but straight to kids tees and out. Forward to a friend? Not a chance — this is a personal Old Navy account thing, and nobody needs a forwarded coupon for something they could find by just… opening their own Old Navy emails, all twenty-nine of them.

## What's working

- **Kids price finally in the subject.** "$4 for kids" is the first time this blast cycle explicitly led with the price point that matters most to how I shop. That's a meaningful segmentation signal even if the execution around it is noisy.
- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" visual urgency.** The coral/red banner landing above the hero is hard to miss — it does what the subject line failed to do and creates a real deadline.
- **$4/$6 pricing is the first thing my eye finds.** The visual hierarchy on the hero section earns its placement. Numbers big, model contextual — this is the Old Navy formula working as designed.
- **No render bugs.** Clean load, no broken images, no lorem ipsum leaking. At this frequency of send, a broken email would be unforgivable.
- **Subject delivers on the offer clearly.** Whatever else I think about this email, I know exactly what I'm walking into before I open it.

## What's weak

- **Same email, same day, twice.** I already got this promo earlier on 06-14 — the freshness clock expired before I even opened this one.
- **Subject line is 73 characters.** On my phone this gets sliced in half; the kids price and the "EXTRA 30% off" fight for the truncation window and one of them loses.
- **The creative doesn't show a kid.** I'm being pitched $4 kids tees but the hero model is an adult in a crop top. If you're going to put "$4 for kids" in the subject, show me a kid in the email. Hanna Andersson never forgets who's actually wearing the clothes.
- **HOT NEW GEAR and triple CLEARANCE dilute the lead.** A focused tees/tanks email becomes a department-store catalog scroll. By the time I hit "Go, Go, Go! Clearance Clearance Clearance" I've already mentally closed the tab.
- **Urgency is body-only.** "2 DAYS ONLY" is a banner I only see after I open — it belongs in the subject line where it might actually influence the open decision.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Consolidate the sends.** Two emails on the same day about the same promo is the single biggest lift available — halving send frequency immediately restores the urgency that repetition has burned down.
- 2. **Put the deadline in the subject.** "2 DAYS ONLY" in the body is wasted urgency. Move it forward into the subject line where it changes behavior.
- 3. **Show a kid.** If the kids deal is in the subject, the creative should reinforce it — a mom + kid, a kiddo in a graphic tee, anything. Right now the visual and verbal promises are disconnected.
- 4. **Cut the email to one section.** Tees/tanks lead, kids callout, one CTA. Drop HOT NEW GEAR and CLEARANCE to a separate email or a footer link. Don't make me parse three campaigns in one scroll.
- 5. **Add the extra-30% math.** "$6 tee, extra 30% off = $4.20" spelled out would make this irresistible in a way raw percentages don't.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken cp.oldnavy.com redirect**

The pixel URL at `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/c8caeb5b-...` resolves with an internal redirect to a relative path:

```
unknown url type: '/g/c8caeb5b-3faf-40f3-a39f-2d9e6a2fce47?txn=9ade9080-6ab8-11f1-95a0-ca2f212c8bca'
```

This is a relative-path redirect bug on the `cp.oldnavy.com` tracking/preference server — it returns a `Location` header with `/g/...` instead of an absolute URL. The HTTP client cannot follow it. If this is a tracking pixel, open-rate data for this send is likely incomplete or corrupted.

**[WARN] 23 click-redirect links skipped by QA probe**

All outbound links pass through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. The QA runner skipped HTTP probing on these, so broken destination URLs cannot be confirmed or ruled out from this audit alone.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Tracking pixel missing `alt` text**

The `cp.oldnavy.com` open-tracking `<img>` has no `alt` attribute:

```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/c8caeb5b-..." width="1" heigh…
```

Screen readers will attempt to read the raw URL. The `alt=""` attribute should be explicitly set on all tracking pixels (the `click.email.oldnavy.com` open pixel in the same `display:none` div has `alt=""` correctly set — this one does not).

**[WARN] Truncated `height` attribute**

The same pixel has `heigh…` — the HTML source provided is truncated, but if the production source is also truncated, the `height="1"` attribute is malformed and some clients may render the pixel at default height.

**CSS duplicate selector collision**

In the responsive `@media` block, `.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each declared twice with different values:

```css
.space-top5, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 5px;}
.space-top10, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 10px;}  /* overwrites above */

.space-bot5, .MspaceBot {padding-bottom: 5px;}
.space-bot10, .MspaceBot {padding-bottom: 10px;}  /* overwrites above */
```

The latter declaration wins in all cases, so `.MspaceTop` elements that were intended to get 5px padding will receive 10px on mobile. The comment in the source acknowledges this (`/* MspaceTop was duplicated, kept one */`) but the fix was not applied — both declarations remain.

**Outlook line-height zero override**

The MSO conditional block sets `td { line-height: 0px }` globally for Outlook 2013+. This is a known workaround for ghost spacing but will collapse any `<td>` that relies on inherited line-height for vertical rhythm. Only safe if every `<td>` with text content has an explicit `line-height` inline style or class — worth verifying in Outlook rendering.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header absent**

`List-Unsubscribe` was not captured by the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header for their native unsubscribe UI. Its absence means:
- Gmail may show a "Report spam" prompt instead of a managed unsubscribe link.
- Without `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058), one-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender rules for >5k/day senders) is not supported.

This is likely a relay capture issue rather than a send-side omission, but should be verified against raw headers in the ESP's send log.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected in parsed body**

CAN-SPAM §7(a)(3) requires a valid physical postal address. The HTML source is truncated — the address may exist in the footer below the truncation point. Confirm it is present in the full source. If absent, this is a legal violation.

**[WARN] SPF/DKIM authentication status unknown**

`Authentication-Results` header was not captured. Sending domain is `email.oldnavy.com`; the `From` domain matches. Cannot confirm DKIM signature validity or SPF alignment from this audit. Verify via ESP send log or a direct header capture.
