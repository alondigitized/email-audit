# For their whole closet — 50% off all kids & baby styles*
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- Okay, I'll be honest — when I saw this land in my inbox I did a double-take because I'm pretty sure I opened the exact same email yesterday. "School days are here — 50% off all kids & baby looks" on July 18, and now today it's "For their whole closet — 50% off all kids & baby styles." Same offer depth, same brand, same target, different subject wrapper. I'm not offended, I'm just... aware. Old Navy does this.
- That said, the email itself earns its keep. The "whole closet" framing is smarter than yesterday's "school days" hook — it talks to my actual shopping behavior, which is stocking an entire drawer, not just grabbing one backpack item. The layout is energetic and clean. I can see specific price points, there's a countdown, and the VIP Lounge callout caught my eye because I do have a card. The "Hot New Gear" section actually surprised me — it's more fashion-forward than Old Navy usually plays in these BTS emails.
- But I've already been to this rodeo. Would I click? Probably yes, because the offer is real and I was already watching for BTS deals. Would I forward it to a friend? No — I'd mention it in a text before I'd share the email. Mark as read and shop from the link if I need to top off sizes.

## What's working

- **"Whole closet" framing is exactly how I think** — I'm not buying one piece, I'm restocking both kids top to bottom, and this copy knows it.
- **$3.99 price point is visible in the grid** — that specificity does more heavy lifting than any percentage-off headline.
- **"Hot New Gear" section breaks the formula** — it's not just polo shirts and leggings again; there's something with personality in that block.
- **"3 Days" urgency is visible in the hero area** — a real deadline, not vague "limited time" filler.
- **VIP Lounge section acknowledges me as a cardholder** — Hanna Andersson's welcome email (8/10) led with a code for everyone, but this segmented callout for existing loyalists actually respects the relationship more.

## What's weak

- **This is yesterday's offer in a new subject line.** July 18 was "50% off all kids & baby looks." Today is "50% off all kids & baby styles." I noticed. And if I noticed, the algorithm noticed. Cadence fatigue is real.
- **The email is long and section-heavy.** By the time I hit VIP Lounge I'm already mentally checked out — it scans like a catalog, not a tight editorial.
- **No sizing or fit signal visible anywhere** — this is back to school season and I need to know if the fleece comes in a 6-7. Not a single mention. Hanna Andersson would have called out a specific fit in the hero.
- **The asterisk on the subject is doing a lot of work** — the exclusions likely kill some items I'd actually want, and I won't know until I'm three clicks in.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rotate the offer creative, not just the subject.** Two consecutive days of 50% off kids & baby is a dead giveaway that the calendar is being padded. Tomorrow, either pivot to a specific category (denim, packs, shoes) or hold fire until something meaningfully different is ready.
- 2. **Put a fit or sizing callout in the hero.** "Fleeces in every size, 6M–XXL" earns a click from me. "50% off all kids styles" tells me nothing I don't know.
- 3. **Tighten the email to two or three sections max.** Hero offer, Hot New Gear (this is good, keep it), and one CTA. The VIP Lounge belongs in a separate loyalty email, not buried at the bottom of a BTS blast.
- 4. **Show a real deadline in the hero headline**, not just a "3 Days" badge — "Ends Sunday" is more credible than a countdown I have to do math on.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Whole-closet BTS: 50% off every kids & baby style`
- **Subject Alt B:** `3 days: stock up the whole school wardrobe at 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Tees, fleeces, shoes — everything they'll wear from drop-off to practice`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sizes 6M–XXL, all 50% off. Ends Sunday.`

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — broken CDP redirect**
The Contentsquare pixel URL resolves to an internal redirect path `/g/a7631f75-b5c6-4ff4-aa39-00c9a1ce14db?txn=857faf00-...` which the ESP reports as `unknown url type`. The outer URL is:
```
https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/a7631f75-b5c6-4ff4-aa39-00c9a1ce14db?mi_mid=019f792c-b180-7000-b7c0-765230b67ffd&cp_tp=v3.eJyFkc0r...
```
The `/g/` internal path is not a valid HTTP redirect target — this pixel will silently fail to fire on send. CDP open/session data for this campaign will be incomplete.

**WARN — 23 click-redirect links not probed**
All CTA links route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. QA skipped HTTP probes on these, so dead destination URLs cannot be confirmed clean. Manual spot-check of at least the primary hero CTA is recommended before send.

**Open pixel** uses `click.email.oldnavy.com/open.aspx?OJYM5V2PDY7UZNTQTSH7JUH7WU.60265` — standard ESP open tracker, no issues.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**WARN — tracking pixel missing `alt` text**
```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/a7631f75-b5c6-4ff4-aa39-00c9a1ce14db?..." width="1" height="1">
```
No `alt=""` attribute. Screen readers may announce the raw URL. Fix: add `alt=""`.

**INFO — duplicate CSS class targets**
`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice in the responsive stylesheet (lines targeting both `.space-top5` and `.space-top10`, and `.space-bot5` and `.space-bot10`). Last-rule wins in most clients, but this is a latent maintenance hazard — the earlier rules are dead.

**INFO — Outlook line-height override is aggressive**
The MSO conditional block sets `td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }` globally. This is a known pattern for Outlook height fixes but can collapse single-line `<td>` cells that rely on inherited line-height. No confirmed breakage from source alone, but warrants Outlook 2016/2019 render test.

**INFO — XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype**
Using the legacy XHTML Transitional DTD (`//W3C//DTD XHTML 1.0 Transitional//EN`). Functionally harmless in 2026 email clients but inconsistent with the HTML5 `<meta>` patterns used elsewhere in the head.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

Neither `List-Unsubscribe` nor `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click) headers were detected by the AgentMail relay. These may be stripped by the relay rather than absent from the actual send — but if genuinely missing, Gmail and Apple Mail will suppress the native unsubscribe UI, and Google/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (>5k/day) will flag the campaign.

**Action required:** Confirm headers are present in the raw SMTP envelope, not just the relay-captured payload.

**WARN — No physical mailing address detected in source**
CAN-SPAM §7 requires a valid physical postal address in the email body. It was not found in the truncated HTML. If it exists below the truncation point (e.g., in the footer table), this is a non-issue — but must be verified before send.
