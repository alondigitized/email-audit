## Executive summary

- The "$4 / 2 DAYS ONLY" hero stopped me cold — and I mean that as a compliment. After an entire week of Old Navy $8 uniform pants and "$15 & under" roundups that all started blurring together, finally dropping to $4 on the exact item I'm mentally stacking in my cart is the right move at the right moment. - But then the email goes somewhere weird on me. By the second scroll I'm suddenly in an "ALL-DAY STRETCH" denim editorial with $40 and $36 adult fashion pieces and some kind of styling section that has absolutely nothing to do with why I came here. - Visual hierarchy is clean at the top: the price is the biggest element, the eye lands exactly where it should
- The clearance "$3.99" section underneath is a smart value layer — doesn't cannibalize the hero, adds another win for me

## What's weak

- The adult fashion pivot (ALL-DAY STRETCH, $40/$36 adult items) kills the uniform-shopper momentum completely — I came for polos and suddenly I'm in a jeans editorial
- No pack-quantity callout on the polo hero — at $4, the entire purchase logic is "buy a stack," but the email doesn't acknowledge that and it should

- The "LIMITED TIME" prefix in the subject is showing cadence wear — this is at least the fourth urgency send in five days; the phrase is losing its punch even if the $4 price point is genuinely new

- 1. **Cut or quarantine the adult sections.** If they can't be removed entirely, push everything adult below a hard separator or a second email. This hero deserves a clean runway all the way to the CTA.
- 2. **Add a pack callout to the polo hero.** "Buy 3 for $12" or "Grab a 5-pack for $20" — that's the actual transaction Hanna Andersson designs for in their stripe sets and it converts because it answers the parent's real question.
- 3. **Show size range.** One line: "Sizes XS–3XL available." It prevents the click-through-to-disappointment cycle that kills repeat open rates.
- 4. **Tighten the clearance CTA.** "$3.99" is a great secondary beat but it needs a single clear button, not a browsable grid that splits my attention.
