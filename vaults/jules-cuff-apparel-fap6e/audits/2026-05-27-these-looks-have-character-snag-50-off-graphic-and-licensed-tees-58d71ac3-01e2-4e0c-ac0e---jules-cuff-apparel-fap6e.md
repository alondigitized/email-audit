# These looks have character 😉 Snag 50% off graphic and licensed tees
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- Okay, I'll be completely honest: I almost didn't open this. This is Old Navy's *eleventh* email in roughly a week — I counted, because at this point it's become a reflex to notice — and the subject phrasing "these looks have character" sounds like a junior copywriter trying to be cheeky about graphic tees rather than just telling me what's on sale. The winking emoji doesn't help; it reads like it's trying too hard to be playful when I just want the math.
- That said — *licensed tees at 50% off* is actually relevant to me. My kids are obsessed with character prints right now, and Old Navy's licensed section is legitimately where I stock up before the school year. The "TODAY ONLY" banner at the top of the email is the first thing I see, and the triple "Clearance Clearance Clearance" block below it confirms this is inventory-clearing season. The TEAM SPIRIT section with the graphic tees is right in my wheelhouse.
- But here's the problem: by the time I actually landed on the licensed tee offers, I'd already scrolled through at least three other sections — clearance tops, some kind of "new arrivals" drop, accessory/bag shots — and I lost the thread. This email is trying to do too many jobs at once, and the subject line promised me one thing (character tees) that doesn't show up as a clear hero. I'd probably click on the tee section if I bothered to scroll, but I'm not going to forward this to anyone. I'm one more email away from hitting "unsubscribe" and just browsing direct.

## What's working

- **TODAY ONLY at the very top** — hard to miss, immediately signals urgency and anchors the 50% figure before I do anything else.
- **"Clearance Clearance Clearance"** — blunt and honest. I respect that they didn't dress it up. End-of-season clearance is exactly why I open Old Navy emails in the first place.
- **Licensed/graphic tee positioning** — subject alignment with TEAM SPIRIT section is relevant for my household; character tees for kids is genuinely a category I'll act on.
- **Vibrant, high-energy color blocking** — the red, yellow, and green sections give clear visual breaks; the layout doesn't feel like a gray blob of sameness.

## What's weak

- **Eleventh email in seven days.** Hanna Andersson's welcome email (May 8) sent *one* email and gave me a clear 20% offer I still think about. Old Navy's cadence has completely eroded whatever urgency "TODAY ONLY" was supposed to generate — I don't believe them anymore.
- **Subject oversold one thing, body delivers everything.** "Graphic and licensed tees" is in the subject, but by the time the email ends there's clearance, new arrivals, bags, and at least two other unrelated categories. Feels bait-y.
- **TEAM SPIRIT section isn't the hero** — it's buried mid-scroll. If that's what you led with in the subject, it should be the first thing I see, not the fourth.
- **No visible sizing or family-set callout** — I shop for two kids and myself simultaneously. Nothing in this email says "grab the matching set" or calls out kids' vs. adult sizing, which is a miss when licensed tees are almost always a kids-first purchase for someone like me.
- **The "NEW TECK TECK" (or whatever that section is)** — I genuinely cannot tell what it's selling from the visual. Unclear copy + unclear product = scroll past.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the licensed tees the *actual* hero, not the third scroll.** If the subject promises graphic and licensed tees, that image — kids/teens in character prints, price callout — needs to be above the fold. The clearance banner is fine as a secondary strip; don't let it eat the hero.
- 2. **Cut two sections.** One email, one job. The clearance send and the graphic-tee send should be separate emails on different days — not stacked. Hanna Andersson doesn't shotgun five categories at once; they know what they're selling that day.
- 3. **Add a family or kids explicit callout** — "Grab one for every kid on the list" or a visible "Kids | Adults | Matching Sets" nav strip would do it. I need to see that this isn't just for teens.
- 4. **Dial back to 3 emails a week maximum.** This is a creative/strategy note, not a single-send fix, but the volume is actively destroying trust in the urgency signals.
