# 60-70% OFF dresses (Cue happy deal dance) + an extra 30% off all Clearance in-store
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- I already did this dance yesterday. Old Navy sent me essentially the same promotion on July 16 — dresses at 60-70% off, extra 30% off clearance — and here it is again with a new subject line wearing a party hat. The only thing that actually changes my read on this is the "2 DAYS ONLY!" banner. That tells me yesterday was the launch and today is the final push, which is useful. That's real deadline energy, not manufactured scarcity. Fine, I'll take it.
- The VIP Lounge callout stopped my scroll for a second because I do have an Old Navy account and I'm mildly curious whether there's a member-specific angle in there. The $3.99 price point visible in the clearance section is the kind of number that makes me open a new tab. But then "It's Shorts Season" appears mid-email and I'm suddenly lost — are we doing dresses, clearance, or shorts? That's three different shopping trips in one scroll.
- I'd probably click through to the dress section because I've been sitting on this deal since yesterday. I would not forward it. My friends have inboxes. Mark as read and act or don't.

## What's working

- The 2 DAYS ONLY counter gives yesterday's repeat send a purpose — it reframes the cadence as a launch-plus-close sequence instead of just noise.
- VIP Lounge appearing mid-email is a nice loyalty signal; Old Navy knows I have an account and is nudging me toward it.
- $3.99 clearance price is the kind of anchor number that makes me stop scrolling — it's a real price, not vague "savings."
- Visual hierarchy is clean: the offer lands first, the percentage is big, the eye goes right to the deal before anything else competes.
- Summer dresses in July is the right product at the right time — no disconnect between offer and season.

## What's weak

- This is yesterday's email with a different punchline. "Cue happy deal dance" is cute, but it doesn't earn a second send on its own. Hanna Andersson sends maybe twice a week and every email feels like a distinct event — Old Navy is averaging one a day right now.
- "It's Shorts Season" mid-email is a focus killer. The hero says dresses; a section break pivots to shorts. Pick the hero story and stick with it.
- Subject line is 84 characters — no mobile inbox is showing that in full. The best part ("60-70% OFF dresses") is up front but the "Cue happy deal dance" add-on is dead weight eating into preview real estate.
- No kids content visible anywhere in the scroll, which is half of why I shop Old Navy. A single row of uniform pieces or matching family sets would've made this feel relevant instead of purely women's fashion.
- Unclear whether the VIP Lounge has an exclusive offer or if it's just a branded name for the same sale. If there's a member-only price, say it plainly.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the deadline explicitly** — "Last day: 60-70% off dresses" instead of a playful tagline. The urgency is the whole reason to send twice.
- 2. **Kill the shorts pivot mid-email** — if you need to include shorts, make it a secondary block with its own clear header, not a competing hero. Or save it for tomorrow's send.
- 3. **Add one uniform/kids row** — even a single "While you're in the clearance section, back-to-school starts at $X" callout would make me feel seen as a family shopper, not just a women's fashion prospect.
- 4. **VIP Lounge needs a payoff** — if the section has an extra member discount or early access, put the number right in the callout. "VIP members get an extra 10%" is a click. "VIP Lounge" alone is furniture.
- 5. **Tighten the subject** — cut the parenthetical, lead with the deadline.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last day: 60-70% off dresses + extra 30% off Clearance`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Dresses 60-70% off — ends tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `VIP members: check your exclusive price before it's gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `$3.99 clearance + the dress sale ends today`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect — `cp.oldnavy.com/o/` resolves to unknown path type `/g/`**

The QA probe followed the redirect chain for:
```
https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/05e9715d-f13b-4677-b554-80f2515ef9bc?mi_mid=019f7060...
```
The final resolved URL landed on a `/g/` path, which the checker flagged as `unknown url type`. **[WARN] 23 click-tracking links skipped**

All click links route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. HTTP probing was skipped for these, so their final destinations are unverified in this audit. No specific link failures were reported, but destination health is unconfirmed.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate CSS selectors**

Two media-query classes are defined twice in the responsive block, which is harmless but sloppy and can cause specificity confusion if the sheet is ever modified:
- `.MspaceTop` appears under both `.space-top5` and `.space-top10`
- `.MspaceBot` appears under both `.space-bot5` and `.space-bot10`

**Outlook `line-height: 0px` override**

The MSO conditional comment sets `td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }` globally. This is a known hack for Outlook height bugs, but it clobbers any td with text that doesn't have an explicit inline `line-height`. Confirm all text-bearing tds have inline overrides.

**Missing alt text on tracked image**

```
src: https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/05e9715d-...
```
This image element lacks an `alt` attribute entirely (the open-pixel has `alt=""` correctly; this one does not). If it renders visible in any client, it will show a broken-image icon with no fallback text.

**No issues** with charset declaration (`UTF-8`), viewport meta, or IE compatibility meta.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No merge tokens visible in the provided HTML excerpt. The email appears to be a flat broadcast with no recipient-level personalization (no `%%FIRST_NAME%%`, `{{name}}`, or equivalent). The `mi_mid` and `mi_cid` parameters in tracking URLs are message/contact IDs injected by the ESP, not content personalization — no render-time token risk.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not captured**

The relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Gmail and Apple Mail use this for the native unsubscribe button; its absence degrades deliverability reputation with both ISPs. This may be a relay capture gap rather than a true omission — verify against raw SMTP headers from the ESP send log.

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not captured (RFC 8058)**

One-click machine unsubscribe (required by Gmail's February 2024 bulk sender policy for lists >5k/day) is not confirmed present. Same caveat as above — check the raw headers.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**

CAN-SPAM §7704(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in the body. The HTML is truncated, so this may exist in the footer below the cutoff. If absent, it is a legal compliance gap, not just a best practice.

**Authentication** — SPF/DKIM status unknown; `Authentication-Results` header was not captured by the AgentMail relay. Cannot confirm signing domain alignment with `oldnavy.com`.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The tracking URLs use ESP-native parameters (`mi_mid`, `mi_cid`, `cp_cid`, `cp_tp`) rather than standard UTM parameters. If Old Navy's analytics stack relies on GA4 or a similar tool expecting `utm_source` / `utm_medium` / `utm_campaign`, click traffic from this send will land as direct/none in site analytics — no campaign attribution.

The encoded `cp_tp` blob appears to be a base64+zlib payload (Salesforce MC `personalization` token), so landing page personalization may still function on the server side even without UTM params. However, cross-channel attribution in any non-Salesforce analytics tool will be blind to this send.
