# You've earned it — 50% off everything* including NEW arrivals
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-27**
## Executive summary

- Five. This is the fifth "50% off everything" email I've gotten from Old Navy in under four days — 7/24, twice on 7/25, twice on 7/26, and now this one on 7/27 with a shiny new "You've earned it" wrapper slapped on the same math. My gut reaction isn't excitement, it's mild fatigue with a little bit of "okay, fine, show me the jeans." The "you've earned it" framing is trying to make this feel like a loyalty reward, but nothing in the email backs that up — no points balance, no "as a member since 2019" line, just the same 50%-off-everything banner I've now memorized down to the pixel.
- What actually got my eye moving was the best-of-sale grid — Women's jeans, Men's tee, Kids fleece, Baby onesie, all with real prices stacked right there ($25/$13/$15/$8). That's the one thing this send does that some of the others haven't: it hits every bucket I actually shop for my family in a single scroll, kids and baby included, which is exactly my Old Navy use case. Hanna Andersson's welcome email earned an 8 from me by feeling like it knew who I was; this one still reads as "blast everyone," just a well-organized blast.
- I wouldn't click from the inbox at this point — I've already acted on this exact offer or scrolled past it four times this week, so the marginal pull is gone. I'd skim the best-of picks section if I happened to open it, maybe screenshot the kids fleece for later, but I'm not forwarding this to a friend. This is background noise now, not a moment.

## What's working

- The best-of-sale grid covers my actual shopping list in one place — women's, men's, kids, and baby, each with a clear price, which matches how I actually buy for the whole family at Old Navy.
- Hero banner is legible at a glance: pink block, "50% OFF EVERYTHING," bold price call-out — no squinting required.
- The "Looking For A T-Shirt Refresh?" fit guide (Snug/EveryWear/Huz/Vintage) is a nice touch — fit is the one thing I actually hesitate over before buying tees online, and it's front and center instead of buried in a size chart link.
- No render bugs — everything loaded, nothing overlapping, clean grid layout throughout.

## What's weak

- Five nearly identical "50% off everything" sends in four days is just fatigue at this point — the 7/26 "This is HUGE" email already burned this exact goodwill for me and scored a 5.
- "You've earned it" promises a loyalty angle the email never delivers — no member tier, no points, no "because you're a regular" signal, just generic sale copy wearing a personalization costume.
- The top banner ("Become an Encore Member for $1...ends 8/16") is crammed and hard to parse at a glance — it's competing with the hero for my first two seconds of attention and loses.
- Clearance grid at the bottom ("FROM $3.99") is visually loud (red banner, dense product wall) but feels bolted on after an already-long email — by that point I've scrolled past three separate CTAs already.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the subject-line "50% off everything" repetition — I've earned it, this is HUGE, not a drill, don't wait — pick ONE hook per week and stick with it, or lead with the best-of picks (kids fleece $15, baby onesie $8) instead of the sale math I've already memorized.
- 2. Make "you've earned it" actually mean something — surface an Encore/loyalty stat ("You've saved $X with us this year") or drop the loyalty framing entirely and just lead with the family category grid, which is the strongest asset in this email.
- 3. Move the best-of-sale grid (Women/Men/Kids/Baby) above the giant hero banner — that's the part that made me stop scrolling, not the fifth "50% off" claim this week.
- 4. Simplify the top banner — the Encore Member call-out is unrelated to the main offer and just adds clutter before I've even seen the sale.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Jeans, tees, fleece & baby basics — all half off, all in one place`
