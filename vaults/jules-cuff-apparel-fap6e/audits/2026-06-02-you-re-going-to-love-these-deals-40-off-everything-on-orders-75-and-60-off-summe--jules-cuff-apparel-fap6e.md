# You're going to LOVE these deals: 40% off everything* on orders $75+ and 60% off summer faves
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- Eighteen. I actually stopped and counted again because I couldn't believe it. This is the eighteenth Old Navy email in roughly two weeks, and the subject line is functionally the same as yesterday's — "Big savings inside! It's 60% off summer steals and 40% off everything on orders $75+" — just reworded. They didn't even bother flipping which offer leads. The hero delivers "2 DAYS ONLY! 60% OFF Summer Steals" in big, loud type, and the grid below dumps what feels like every product category at once. Kids & Baby Clearance does show up near the bottom, which is the one thing that actually speaks to me, but by the time I scroll there I've already mentally checked out.
- The visual is loud and colorful in a way that reads Old Navy perfectly — this brand has never whispered — but loud doesn't close the deal when I already know the sale. I processed this exact offer yesterday. Hanna Andersson's welcome email felt like a letter from someone who knew me; this feels like a flyer shoved under my windshield wiper a second time.
- I'd mark this read without clicking. I would not forward it. If I were buying, I already bookmarked the deal yesterday.

## What's working

- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" urgency in the hero** — visible immediately, no scrolling required, and it's the one thing that could flip a hesitant buyer today.
- **Kids & Baby Clearance section near the bottom** — 50–60% off clearance callout is exactly the signal that gets my thumb moving; that's the Old Navy value prop I actually shop.
- **Layout is clean, no obvious render bugs** — colorful grid of product tiles loads correctly, text doesn't overlap, no broken images I can see.
- **Seasonal relevance is strong** — summer colors, swim-adjacent lifestyle shots, bright palette. It's June and it reads June.

## What's weak

- **Exact repeat of yesterday's offer** — same two deals, same creative energy, one day later. This isn't a reminder; it's a copy. Even volume shoppers notice that.
- **Subject line is 93 characters with an asterisk** — it truncates on mobile before you even reach "60% off," which is the more compelling number. The asterisk is a small-print signal that creates hesitation before I've even opened.
- **40% off everything requires a $75+ order** — that conditional is buried in the subject. My brain hears "40% off everything" and then has to mentally walk it back when I see the threshold. Feels slightly bait-and-switch, even if it isn't.
- **No kids-first framing in the subject or hero** — I'm here for the kids' section. Nothing in the above-the-fold area speaks to me as a mom shopping for two kids. The Kids & Baby block could be anywhere in my scroll queue.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Kids & Baby offer in the subject** — that's where my money actually goes. A subject like "Kids & Baby: 50–60% off clearance + 40% off your $75 order" would stop my thumb cold.
- 2. **Rest this creative for 48 hours** — yesterday's send already seeded the offer. If you need a follow-up, send to non-openers only. Flooding the full list with the same deal is why unsubscribe rates creep up.
- 3. **Add a spend-to-unlock progress nudge** — "You're $X away from unlocking 40% off everything" would do real work for a brand whose strength is cart-building. Hanna Andersson doesn't do this (she doesn't need to, her price points are set), but Old Navy's volume model is perfect for it.
- 4. **Make the Kids CTA its own hero block, above the fold** — even just swapping the hero image to a kids/family lifestyle shot signals that this isn't just an adult fast-fashion blast.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Kids & Baby 50-60% off clearance — 2 days only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$75+ order? You just unlocked 40% off everything`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 60% off summer steals while they last.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Kids clearance, swim, and more — sale ends soon.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Old Navy "40% off everything" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

One broken redirect link will silently drop users before reaching a landing page. Compliance headers are unverified due to relay capture gaps, and the plain-text part is pathologically URL-heavy, raising spam-filter risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken Sailthru/Marigold redirect**
The `cp.oldnavy.com` click proxy returned `unknown url type: '/g/...'` for the following URL:
```
https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/8f4e4dbf-3867-40fe-8a95-c4fd641db914?mi_mid=019e88d9-4180-7000-839f-8136fc804d79&...
```
The error indicates the stored destination URL in the ESP begins with a relative path (`/g/...`) instead of an absolute `https://` URL. The redirect engine cannot resolve it, so affected recipients hit a dead end. This link appears in both the open-pixel position and at least one content slot — verify all instances in the Marigold campaign editor.

**[WARN] 23 tracking links skipped by QA probe**
All click links route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. HTTP probing was skipped, so destination URLs and UTM chains for those 23 links are unverified this run.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Duplicate CSS class selectors (mobile stylesheet)**
`.MspaceTop` is declared twice — once for `.space-top5` and once for `.space-top10`. Same for `.MspaceBot` / `.MspaceBot` on `.space-bot5` and `.space-bot10`. The second declaration silently overrides the first; spacing behavior in affected modules is unpredictable on mobile.

```css
/* Both of these target .MspaceTop — second wins */
.space-top5, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 5px;}
.space-top10, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 10px;}
```

**[WARN] Aggressive Outlook line-height reset**
Inside the MSO conditional comment:
```css
td { line-height:0px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly }
```
A blanket `line-height:0px` on all `<td>` elements in Outlook 2013+ will collapse text to zero height unless each cell explicitly overrides it. Any `<td>` without an inline `line-height` style will render as invisible in Outlook.

**[WARN] Missing alt text on tracking beacon**
```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/8f4e4dbf-3867-40fe-8a95-c4fd641db914?..." alt="">
```
Alt is present but empty. Acceptable for a 1×1 pixel, but the image is not hidden via `display:none` — it occupies layout space if images are blocked and the broken-link error above may cause a visible broken-image icon.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens visible in the truncated source. The `mi_mid` value (`019e88d9-4180-7000-839f-8136fc804d79`) and `mi_cid` are populated, indicating subscriber context was injected at send time. No issues found in the visible markup.

---

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header — unverified**
The QA relay did not capture a `List-Unsubscribe` header. If this header is absent at delivery, Gmail and Yahoo will not render their native one-click unsubscribe button, and the send may fail Yahoo's 2024 bulk-sender requirement.

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click machine unsubscribe (POST method) appears unsupported. Required by Gmail/Yahoo for bulk senders (>5k/day). Needs verification against raw delivery headers — the relay may have stripped it.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**
CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in the body. The truncated source does not show one. Confirm it exists in the footer of the full HTML — if omitted, this is a legal violation.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**[FAIL] Broken redirect breaks continuity for affected link**
The `cp.oldnavy.com` redirect failure (§2) means that link slot has zero UTM pass-through — traffic never reaches oldnavy.com at all.

**[WARN] UTM chain unverified for 23 click links**
Because all `click.email.oldnavy.com` links were skipped by the HTTP probe, UTM parameters on final landing pages are unconfirmed. A manual spot-check of 2–3 primary CTAs (hero, top offer) is recommended to confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` are appended post-redirect.
