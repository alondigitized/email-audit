# 50% off EVERYTHING?* (Yes, please!) + $5 dresses & 60% off all shorts & swim*
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- Day five of the same sale, and they tried to dress it up with a tennis theme. I'll give them that — "SERVING LOOKS" is a cute angle, and the yellow-green athletic section at least gave my eyes something different to land on. But the moment I hit that hero? Same 50% off everything, same 60% off shorts and swim, same $5 dresses. My thumb knows this layout. My brain has already filed it under "I'll get to it before the sale ends, which apparently is never."
- The "(Yes, please!)" in the subject line is doing a lot of emotional labor for a sale I've been staring at since Tuesday. Hanna Andersson's welcome email last month opened with a 20% off code and two sentences that actually told me what the brand stood for — this is the opposite of that energy. Old Navy is swinging for breezy-fun and landing somewhere between familiar and invisible.
- I wouldn't click today. I might scroll through tonight when the kids are in bed, but only because I'm already mentally building a back-to-school cart. If a friend texted asking about the sale, I'd say "yeah they've had it going all week" — I wouldn't forward this specific email.

## What's working

- **"SERVING LOOKS" tennis section** is the first creative hook this campaign has shown all week — the color palette pops (yellow, green, white) and it at least breaks the monotony of the hero
- **"Shop Clearance in Your Size"** near the bottom is the one personalization signal that actually earns its place; size-specific clearance is how you get a practical mom to scroll past the fold
- **Offer is unambiguous** — 50% off everything, 60% off shorts/swim, $5 dresses, stacked and readable from a glance
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds together on mobile

## What's weak

- **Identical offer for the fifth day straight** — no escalation, no "final hours," no reason for me to act today vs. when I already didn't act Monday through Thursday
- **"(Yes, please!)" subject line gimmick** — it reads like the email is doing a little dance to get my attention, which is exactly what you do when the actual content isn't doing the work
- **Heavily adult/young-aesthetic** — the tennis serves looks, the colorful athletic models; I'm here for the kids' basics pipeline and there's no signal this was sent to a parent of a 6-year-old
- **No urgency mechanism** — no countdown, no "ends Sunday," nothing to make me put down the coffee and act now vs. Tuesday

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a sale-end date in the hero** — "Sale ends Sunday" is worth more than "EVERYTHING* 50% off" for the fifth time. Without a deadline, I'm training myself to treat this brand like wallpaper.
- 2. **Lead with the most-changed offer, not the most-familiar one** — if $5 dresses is the most aggressive price, make that the hero. Bury the "50% off everything" because I already know it; surprise me with the floor.
- 3. **One parent/family signal** — a single kids' section callout, a "stock up the whole crew" line, anything. She's sending to a list segment that shops for families; give me a hook.
- 4. **Retire "(Yes, please!)"** — it's doing ironic enthusiasm and coming off as desperate. Let the price do the exclaiming.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Stock up the crew: 50% off sitewide, ends soon`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The lowest prices of the summer — clearance in your size too`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email's HTML is structurally sound for a legacy table-based layout, but the QA pipeline surfaced one broken tracking pixel URL, missing authentication headers, incomplete CAN-SPAM compliance signals, and multiple images without alt text. No merge token failures detected.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken tracking pixel URL**
The Cheetah Digital (cp.oldnavy.com) open-tracking pixel resolves to a redirect that produces an unknown URL type error:

```
https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/5cd02092-0a86-4b52-bd4c-be5483edd754?mi_mid=019eec32...
→ /g/5cd02092-0a86-4b52-bd4c-be5483edd754?txn=6e86dc00-716a-11f1-a2ac-aea38baf8e0c
```

The `/g/` redirect target is unresolvable. This means open-rate data for this send is unreliable. The pixel is placed in the hidden preheader div, so no user-facing breakage occurs, but open attribution is lost.

**[WARN] 23 click-redirect links skipped**
All CTAs route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. These were not probed — destination URL validity is unverified by automated checks.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**[WARN] Physical mailing address not detected** by QA scanner. CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email. Either the address is present further down in the footer (outside the truncated source window) or it is absent — must be verified against full HTML before clearance.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not found.** QA notes this may be an AgentMail relay artifact, but if the header is genuinely absent, Gmail and Apple Mail will not render their native unsubscribe UI. For a sender of this volume, this is a deliverability risk under Gmail's 2024 bulk sender requirements.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found.** RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail for bulk senders (>5k/day). If missing from the actual SMTP envelope, this is a compliance gap.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not found** via AgentMail relay — SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unverified. Cannot confirm DMARC alignment.
