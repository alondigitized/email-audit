# $6 tees & $5 tanks + 50% off everything* (yes, really!)
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- I opened this one already knowing the deal. Old Navy sent me the "GASP 😮" email earlier this same morning with the same stacked offer — so when a second send landed with "(yes, really!)" appended to the subject, my honest reaction was half-charmed, half-tired. It's a self-aware wink that only works once, and they already used up their goodwill credits. The hero is fast and clean — "2 DAYS ONLY!" and "50% Off Everything*" hit before I even have to scroll — and the $6/$5 price anchors are right where I need them. That part is Old Navy doing what Old Navy does well.
- But I'm staring at four emails in two days about tees and tanks. At this point the discount isn't creating urgency, it's creating fatigue. The "yes, really!" reads less like confidence and more like Old Navy knows they've overdone it and are trying to charm their way past my archive finger. And this email is doing a lot — tees, tanks, 50% off, clearance, and a dress section — when one clear lane would've landed harder.
- I'd scan this, not click. If I hadn't already handled it from the morning send, I might have acted. As the follow-up? Mark as read and circle back when I'm actually ready to checkout.

## What's working

- **Price anchors are immediate** — "$6" and "$5" are in large type before the first scroll; that's the Old Navy promise and it hits fast.
- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" badge in the hero** — urgency is visible above the fold, which is the right place for it.
- **"SHOP CLEARANCE IN YOUR SIZE"** — that one line does real work. It tells me I won't click through and land on a picked-over size grid. Smart.
- **Female adult model in casual summer wear** — demographic fit is clean. No confusion about who this is for.
- **"Dream Dresses Under $35?" section** adds a discovery beat that breaks up the tee/tank monotony; useful for a different shopping occasion without derailing the core message.

## What's weak

- **Second email today, fourth in two days** — the urgency they built on May 16 has been spent down to zero; this send arrives as noise, not news.
- **Offer stack is too wide** — tees, tanks, 50% off everything, clearance, AND dresses is a catalog page, not a focused pitch; Hanna Andersson's welcome email proved that one clean offer converts better than five competing ones.
- **No loyalty or rewards callout visible** — I'm an Old Navy rewards member; this email treated me like a stranger who just walked in off the street.
- **"(yes, really!)" is a tell** — it signals Old Navy knows the offer is starting to feel unbelievable, which is a credibility problem they created with their own send cadence.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the double-send on the same day** — the GASP email and this email cover identical ground. One send per day max; if the goal is a reminder, let it replace, not stack.
- 2. **Add a family/kids lane** — I buy tees for two kids, not just myself. A single line like "Stock up for the whole crew" with a kids tee link would've made this click-worthy for me specifically.
- 3. **Surface the rewards tie-in** — "Use your Super Cash on top of 50% off" would've created a reason to act today vs. "I'll do it from my laptop later."
- 4. **Cut to one hero offer per email** — $6 tees OR 50% off everything; not both plus dresses plus clearance. Clarity beats volume every time.
- **Subject Alt A:** `50% off + $6 tees — 2 days left to stock up`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Whole store, half off. $6 tees too. 2 days only.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Including kids. Including clearance. Use your Super Cash on top.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `$6 tees, $5 tanks, and half off everything else — ends Sunday.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing alt text on Movable Ink pixel**
`<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/0bdb3552...">` — no `alt` attribute present. The ESP open pixel correctly has `alt=""`, but this one does not. Screen readers will announce the full URL.

**[WARN] Duplicate CSS class selectors in mobile media query**
`.MspaceTop` is assigned to both `.space-top5` and `.space-top10`; `.MspaceBot` is similarly duplicated. The second definition wins, silently breaking the 5px spacing variant on mobile.

```css
.space-top5, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 5px;}   /* overridden below */
.space-top10, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 10px;} /* wins — 5px variant is dead */
```

**[WARN] Aggressive Outlook line-height reset**
The Outlook conditional block sets `td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }` globally. This collapses line height on all `<td>` elements in Outlook 2013+ unless explicitly overridden per cell — a common source of collapsed/missing text.

**[INFO] No dark mode media query**
No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block. White (`#ffffff`) backgrounds and black (`#000000`) text will invert unpredictably in Apple Mail dark mode.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header — unconfirmed**
Header not captured by the AgentMail relay. RFC 2369 compliance cannot be verified. If absent from actual delivery, this violates Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender requirements (enforced since Feb 2024).

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header — unconfirmed**
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe header not captured. Google and Yahoo require this for senders above 5,000/day. Absence triggers suppression of the native unsubscribe UI in Gmail.

**[WARN] Physical mailing address — not detected in captured HTML**
CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid postal address. The HTML source is truncated at the footer; address may exist but could not be confirmed. Must verify in full render.

**[WARN] SPF/DKIM/DMARC — unknown**
`Authentication-Results` header not captured via relay. Sending domain is `email.oldnavy.com` — DKIM should be present. Relay stripping prevents confirmation; recommend checking at MTA level.
