# ALL women's Crinkle Gauze looks are 50% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy knows I open their emails — and fifty percent off crinkle gauze is exactly the kind of offer that catches me in May. I'm already thinking about weekend dresses I can throw in the wash, and gauze is that sweet spot of "looks intentional but requires zero effort." The hero is clean, the offer is specific, and the TODAY ONLY deadline has enough weight to feel real rather than lazy urgency theater.
- But then the email keeps going. By the time I scroll through dream dresses, sandals, a HELLO YELLOW section, and a clearance pile-on at the bottom, the crinkle gauze message has been thoroughly buried under its own siblings. Old Navy's going for the kitchen-sink approach — I've seen it from them a hundred times — and it reads exactly like what it is: a list-wide blast with one solid lead offer and four extras nobody asked for.
- I'd click the hero CTA fast. I would not forward this to anyone. It's a get-in-and-buy, not a tip worth sharing.

## What's working

- The hero offer is surgical: one category, one percentage, one hard deadline. I knew what I was being sold before I finished reading.
- "TODAY ONLY!" has teeth here — it's not the vague "limited time" non-urgency I usually ignore.
- The stacking offer (extra 40% off when you spend $75) is genuinely clever. If I'm buying a gauze dress anyway, I'm already mentally adding a second item to hit the threshold.
- May timing is exactly right — this is when I'm actively thinking about gauze, linen, and anything that doesn't trap heat.
- The models look like women I might see at school pickup, not a catalog shoot that makes me feel like the email's meant for someone else.

## What's weak

- The email sprawls badly after the hero — crinkle gauze into dream dresses into sandals into HELLO YELLOW into clearance is five detours too many, and the lead offer loses momentum with each one.
- The "Go! Go! Go! Clearance Clearance" section at the bottom reads frantic in a way that actively undercuts the cleaner gauze messaging up top.
- Nothing signals they know who I am — no nod to past purchases, no category preference, no name. It's a blast and it feels like one.
- The HELLO YELLOW section reads like filler padding, not a conversion driver. If it can't stand on its own, it shouldn't be sharing screen time with the actual offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut everything below the stacking offer.** Crinkle gauze hero + 40%-when-you-spend-$75 nudge + one CTA. That's a complete, focused email. Dream dresses and clearance are different sends.
- 2. **Add a style count.** "47 styles, all 50% off today" signals real selection, not a closeout on two SKUs.
- 3. **Kill the clearance section entirely.** "Go! Go! Go!" makes the brand feel desperate. If clearance needs its own push, it earns its own email.
- 4. **Surface the stacking offer in the subject** — it's the stronger hook and it never makes it to the inbox preview.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Today only: 50% off every crinkle gauze style`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Crinkle Gauze 50% off — stack to 40% more at $75`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `40+ breezy styles, one day to grab them.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Spend $75, save even more — but only until midnight.`

### 5. Compliance

CAN-SPAM §7704(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in the email body. Not present in the truncated source — likely in the footer (outside the truncation window). Confirm the full render includes a street address or P.O. box.
