# Stars, stripes & stitched-in magic 🪄 The Old Navy x Disney summer collection has arrived
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- Oh, this caught me immediately — Old Navy plus Disney with a patriotic color palette right before Memorial Day weekend? That's basically engineered for me. The "THAT SUMMER FEELING!" hero lands with energy and the matching-family-character angle scrolled into view just fast enough that I didn't lose interest. This is the kind of email I actually open on my phone while waiting in the school pickup line.
- That said, once the initial sugar rush wore off, I realized there's no actual deal in here. This is a collection launch, not a sale. Old Navy trained me to expect a number — 40% off, $5 tees, something. When I get a full-length scroll with beautiful photography and no price anchor, I feel a little played. I opened expecting to shop and ended up just... looking.
- I'd mark it read rather than click through. I might screenshot the matching family sets and forward it to my sister-in-law whose kids are obsessed with Donald Duck, but I'm not pulling out my card today.

## What's working

- **Hero image is immediately joyful.** The family group shot with adults and kids in coordinating red/white/blue outfits reads "this is for me" in under two seconds.
- **Old Navy x Disney co-branding is front and center.** Dual logos at the top, no ambiguity about what this is — Disney collab signals instant kid excitement.
- **"MATCHING FAMILY CHARACTER" callout is the right hook.** As a mom buying for two kids and looking for easy coordinating sets, that copy does real work.
- **Color palette is seasonally locked in.** The patriotic red, white, and blue visuals feel like they belong on my phone screen right now, not six weeks ago.
- **Donald Duck visible mid-scroll.** My kids would lose their minds — this is exactly the character that sells the click for me even if I hesitate.

## What's weak

- **Zero price signal anywhere.** A collection launch email without a single dollar sign or percentage feels like a tease. I want to buy, not admire.
- **Subject line is doing too much.** Stars + stripes + magic + Disney + summer = I read half of it and filled in the rest. The emoji in the middle of a long subject is chaotic on mobile.
- **"Cool, comfy, and sticker-adorned" copy is a miss.** Sticker-adorned? On my white Old Navy tee that goes through the wash three times a week? That's a dry-clean-only mindset I don't have.
- **No CTA button I can land on cleanly.** Scrolling through the whole email and never finding a clear "Shop Now" button with a product category is friction I don't need at pickup time.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the strongest family-set image as a clickable hero straight to the matching-sets shop page** — not a collection overview. Moms buying for multiple kids need one tap to the right shelf.
- 2. **Add one price anchor in the hero.** Even "Starting at $14.99" earns the scroll. Without it, the email reads like a lookbook, not an offer.
- 3. **Cut the subject line in half.** The Disney x Old Navy collab name alone is enough — the rest is decoration.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Old Navy x Disney is here — matching sets for the whole crew`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Disney dropped into your summer wardrobe`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Patriotic. Magical. And yes, the whole family can match.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Mickey, Donald, and denim — shop the summer collab now`
- 4. **Replace "sticker-adorned" with a washability or durability claim.** If these actually hold up in the laundry, say that. That's the thing that makes me choose Old Navy over the Hanna pieces.
- 5. **Add a visible Shop by Character or Shop by Size shortcut row** mid-email. When I have 90 seconds I want to go straight to the 5T section, not browse the whole collection.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility
