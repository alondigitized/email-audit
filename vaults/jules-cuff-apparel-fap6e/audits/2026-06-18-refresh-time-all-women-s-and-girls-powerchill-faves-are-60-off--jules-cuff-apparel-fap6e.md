# Refresh time! ALL women's and girls PowerChill faves* are 60% OFF
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- This one actually made me pause mid-scroll — "PowerChill" is that soft-stretch line I've grabbed before for the gym-to-errands scramble, and the lavender athletic set on the model looked like exactly my summer uniform. The "TODAY ONLY!" red banner lands clean, and they remembered to drag in "girls," which means I've got a reason to shop for both me and my daughter in one order. That two-for-one hook is the most relevant thing Old Navy has put in my inbox this week.
- But I kept scrolling and hit the Flag Tees section — fine, July 4th is two weeks out, I'll allow it — and then I hit "Clearance" stacked three times in giant block letters like the email was shouting at me because it forgot to say something useful. This is three campaigns stitched into one email and none of them land fully because of it. Compare this to Hanna Andersson's welcome email (scored an 8 from me in May): one message, one voice, completely earned. This Old Navy send is the opposite — it's a merch meeting that nobody resolved.
- I'd click the PowerChill link because I was already primed. I would not forward it. And "Today Only!" is now empirically false — I've had 60%-off "today only" urgency from Old Navy on June 14, 15, 16, and 17. By June 18, that phrase is just wallpaper.

## What's working

- The PowerChill hero is tight — "TODAY ONLY!" over a model in a stretch athletic dress, right age range, right vibe for what I'd actually wear
- Women's **and** girls in the same offer is a real hook for me — one click serves my closet and my daughter's drawer
- "Soft cotton with stretch, for all-day comfort" is exactly the right product copy — it tells me how it feels, not just what it looks like
- Flag Tees section earns its spot — mid-June, Fourth of July approaching, the seasonal timing is correct for once
- Email renders cleanly — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds

## What's weak

- Three campaigns, one email: PowerChill hero → Flag Tees → triple Clearance stack. Pick one and do it well.
- "Today Only!" urgency is completely hollow after four consecutive days of the exact same framing — this is the fifth 60%-off send in a row
- "Clearance Clearance Clearance" stacked in giant block type is chaotic; even Target's clearance emails land with more composure than this
- The subject still has "ALL" in caps — Old Navy's familiar shout habit I flagged on the June 16 review; one step forward, one back
- No loyalty or NavyCash signal anywhere visible — no points balance, no member pricing, no reason to feel like a known customer instead of a cold list address

## Recommendations

- 1. **One campaign per email.** PowerChill is a specific, strong offer with genuine demographic pull — let it breathe. Push Flag Tees and Clearance to separate sends or to a condensed footer only.
- 2. **Retire "Today Only!" until it's actually rare.** After five days of the same structure, earn the urgency back: "Last call," "Back in stock + 60% off," "Ends at midnight" — anything that costs something to say.
- 3. **Add a loyalty hook in the hero.** A single line — "NavyCash members, check your cart for member pricing" — turns a mass email into something that feels personalized. Hanna Andersson put the discount code front and center in their welcome email; Old Navy won't even remind me I have points.
- 4. **Isolate the CTA button under the PowerChill hero.** From what I can see, there's no standalone button beneath the product shot — I'm expected to click into the image, which is fine on desktop and friction on mobile.
- 5. **Give Flag Tees their own send.** The July 4th angle is actually a defensible email on its own — patriotic palette, family staple since '94, seasonal window — don't bury it as the second act after a completely different offer.

## Technical Audit — Old Navy PowerChill 60% OFF

### 1. Technical Summary
One confirmed broken redirect in the tracking/pixel infrastructure causes a hard link error. Compliance headers are absent or uncapturable at the relay level, which is significant given Old Navy's send volume and current Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender requirements.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect — cp.oldnavy.com tracking URL**
The campaign platform pixel URL resolves to an invalid relative path:
```
Resolved destination: /g/12937b4b-1b19-47c5-b440-aeba5bbde0a2?txn=f53baa80-70d1-11f1-a677-aea38baf8e0c
Source: https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/12937b4b-1b19-47c5-b440-aeba5bbde0a2?mi_mid=019eda63-...
```
The `cp.oldnavy.com` redirect chain returns a relative `/g/` path instead of an absolute URL. This pixel appears in two places: the hidden preheader div and the open-tracker block. If this URL doubles as a click destination (not just a pixel), affected recipients hit a dead end. The `o/` → `/g/` redirect handler on cp.oldnavy.com appears misconfigured for this campaign ID.

**23 click-tracking links skipped** — all route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. Cannot confirm final destination integrity without probing the redirect chain. No broken final URLs were detected where probing was possible.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate CSS class definitions (minor)**
In the `@media (max-width: 639px)` block, `.MspaceTop` is assigned to both `.space-top5` and `.space-top10`, and `.MspaceBot` to both `.space-bot5` and `.space-bot10`. Last-write wins, silently overriding the first definition. This is a CSS authoring bug; mobile spacing for those classes will be inconsistent.

**Aggressive Outlook line-height reset**
The MSO conditional stylesheet sets:
```css
td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }
```
This is a known Outlook height-fix pattern but must be overridden on every content `<td>`. If any content cells lack an explicit `line-height`, Outlook 2013+ will collapse them to zero height.

**Missing alt text — tracking pixel**
```
src: https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/12937b4b-...
```
This `<img>` has no `alt` attribute at all (distinct from `alt=""`). Screenreaders may announce the raw URL. For tracking pixels, `alt=""` is the correct pattern.

**Doctype** — XHTML 1.0 Transitional is intentional for email and not an issue.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header absent at relay**
Gmail and Yahoo have required `List-Unsubscribe` (and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` for one-click, per RFC 8058) for bulk senders (>5k/day) since February 2024. These headers are not present in what the AgentMail relay captured. This may be a relay capture gap (headers stripped upstream), but if they are genuinely absent from delivered mail, Old Navy is out of compliance with current Gmail/Yahoo requirements and risks inbox demotion.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**
CAN-SPAM §7(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address. The HTML is truncated — the address is likely in the footer. Confirm it is present in the full render before flagging as a true violation.

**Authentication-Results header not found**
SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unknown at the relay level. Cannot confirm DMARC alignment. For a sender of this volume, unverified authentication is a deliverability and compliance risk.
