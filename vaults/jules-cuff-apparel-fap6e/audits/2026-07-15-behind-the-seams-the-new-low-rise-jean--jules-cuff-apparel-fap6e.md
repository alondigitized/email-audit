# Behind the seams: the new Low Rise jean
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy just did a full 180 on me. I spent the last four days in a loop of $4 kids' polos and $5 tanks — I reviewed yesterday's send ("$5 TANKS INSIDE + an extra 20% off everything*") and the day before that, and honestly I was starting to feel like the brand only knew two notes. So opening "Behind the seams: the new Low Rise jean" and landing on a styled editorial hero was a genuine surprise. Appreciated. But here's the problem: I'm 36, I've got two kids to dress, and low-rise jeans are doing their absolute best to belong to someone ten years younger than me. Every model in this email is styled like she found this outfit on TikTok. That's fine — but Old Navy spent a week conditioning me to think in school uniforms and clearance tanks, and then threw me into a runway editorial without a transition.
- The VIP LOUNGE section caught me — I'm a frequent enough Old Navy buyer that I likely have some loyalty status, and a members-only callout is the right instinct. But it's vague. I can see it exists; I cannot quickly tell what it's worth to me right now. The email does find its footing deeper in the scroll, where $10 items and $3.99 clearance anchors give me a transactional reason to be here. But that's a long way down from a hero that's asking me to care about denim construction philosophy.
- I would not click the hero. I'd scroll to clearance, maybe tap something there. I would not forward this to a friend — Hanna Andersson's welcome email gave me a 20% code in the first screen and I forwarded that one. This one I'd mark as read and move on.

## What's working

- **VIP LOUNGE callout stands out** — it's visually distinct from the rest of the email and signals the brand is trying to reward frequent buyers; that's the right instinct for someone like me who stockpiles during semi-annuals.
- **The editorial pivot breaks the fatigue** — after four consecutive sends that led with the same $4 polo/tank promo, this actually reads as intentional brand communication rather than clearance desperation.
- **Price anchors appear deeper in the email** — $10 items and $3.99 clearance are both visible in the scroll and give me a concrete reason to stay engaged once the editorial hero doesn't land.
- **Subject line is clean on mobile** — 39 characters, no exclamation points, reads like a brand that has something to say.

## What's weak

- **Low-rise is the wrong product for my demo** — I'm a 36-year-old mom of two; low-rise jeans are a Gen Z category play and the models in the hero all but confirm it. Gap has been running the same low-rise push but at least their editorial emails show women in their 30s alongside the younger styling.
- **The editorial frame buries the offer** — "Behind the seams" sounds like a content marketing blog post. Hanna Andersson earns storytelling because they open with the offer first and the warmth second; this one asks me to trust the story before I know what I'm shopping for.
- **VIP LOUNGE is a tease without a punchline** — I can see the label but I cannot quickly parse what my perk is. H&M's loyalty emails call out the specific member discount in the banner; this just gestures at exclusivity.
- **No urgency, no deadline** — the prior week of sends trained me to expect "today only" hooks. Removing them without explanation makes the whole email feel unmoored.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put one member offer line in the hero** — even "VIP: shop it first + 15% off" turns a brand story into a shopping reason within the first scroll. Without it, the editorial is doing all the lifting and the offer is invisible until you work for it.
- 2. **Swap the hero model or add a second one** — show me the jean on a woman who looks like she buys uniforms AND wants to wear something good on the weekend. That model exists; use her.
- 3. **Make the VIP LOUNGE perk explicit** — "Members get early access + extra 10%" is specific enough to click. "VIP LOUNGE" alone is furniture.
- 4. **Carry one thread from the polo week** — a small footer banner saying "Uniform polos: still $4" respects the customer journey instead of abandoning it cold.

- **Subject Alt B:** `The jean everyone's wearing (and it runs true to size)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Members: early access + extra savings inside`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New denim, $10 finds, and the $3.99 clearance you came for`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**INFO — Two open-tracking pixels in hidden div**

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate media-query class bindings — silent override**
In the `@media (max-width: 639px)` block, `.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each assigned twice to different selectors:
```css
.space-top5, .MspaceTop { padding-top: 5px; }   /* overridden */
.space-top10, .MspaceTop { padding-top: 10px; }  /* wins */

.space-bot5, .MspaceBot { padding-bottom: 5px; }  /* overridden */
.space-bot10, .MspaceBot { padding-bottom: 10px; } /* wins */
```
Any element using `.MspaceTop` or `.MspaceBot` alone will always receive the 10px value on mobile, ignoring the 5px variant. This is a silent layout bug.

**Missing alt text on Contentsquare pixel**
`<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/a8decda3-..." width="1" height="1">` has no `alt` attribute (not even `alt=""`). The ESP open-pixel (`open.aspx`) correctly has `alt=""`. Tracking pixels should carry explicit `alt=""` to avoid screen-reader announcement of the raw URL in some clients.

**Outlook line-height override is aggressive**
The MSO conditional style sets `td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }` globally. This is a known pattern but must be overridden on every text-bearing `td`; any `td` without an explicit `line-height` inline style will collapse to zero height in Outlook 2013+.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**List-Unsubscribe header absent**
The `List-Unsubscribe` header was not captured. This is required for Gmail and Yahoo bulk senders (>5k/day threshold, enforced since Feb 2024). If the header is present at SMTP level but stripped by the AgentMail relay, it needs to be confirmed at the MTA; if genuinely absent, this is a deliverability and policy risk.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post header absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) is absent. Gmail requires this for bulk senders. Without it, Gmail renders its own unsubscribe UI which may not honor the sender's suppression list.

**No physical mailing address detected**
The truncated HTML does not include a postal address in the visible body. CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email. This may be present below the truncation point — confirm in the full footer.

**Authentication-Results header not found**
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from the captured message. The sending domain is `email.oldnavy.com`; its authentication posture is unverified in this capture.
