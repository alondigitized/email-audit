# Summer deals from just $2
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- Ten days. Ten consecutive promotional emails from Old Navy. I know because I kept count after that June 22 "FYI: 60% off dresses" send, and at this point my brain auto-sorts the orange envelope straight to "probably 50% off everything again." Except this time they threw me a curveball: "$2." That's enough of a pattern-break to make me look twice — and I did. When I opened, the hero delivered "40–70% off deep deals," which is legitimately the sharpest language they've used in this whole run. Product grid is busy but moves fast: sandals, shorts, kids' gear, a hot-pink backpack section with some "HOT NEW GEAR!" energy that feels like a back-to-school whisper.
- There's a "TODAY ONLY!" banner at the bottom with what looks like a $6 price point. That's buried — I almost scrolled past it — but for a family-volume shopper like me, "$6 anything" is the most actionable thing in this email and it's in the footnotes.
- Honestly? I'd click the kids' section. I wouldn't forward this. It's still mass-blast vibes, just with a punchier number in the subject.

## What's working

- **"$2" subject line breaks the streak.** After nine sends of "50% off everything," a price anchor this low is a genuine pattern interrupt — it made me actually open, which is a win at this point in the cadence.
- **"40–70% off DEEP DEALS" hero copy is the strongest language of the whole promotional run.** The word "deep" implies something more aggressive than the standard 50%.
- **Kids + adult product mix is visible** — sandals, kids' clothing, accessories — which is exactly my shopping basket. Old Navy earns points for covering both in one scroll.
- **"TODAY ONLY!" creates real urgency.** It's not the fabricated countdown-ticker urgency; it's a simple claim. For a TODAY ONLY offer, that's credible.
- **No render bugs.** Layout holds together, images loaded, text is readable. Given the density of product blocks, that's a low bar they clear.

## What's weak

- **The "$6 TODAY ONLY" offer is buried.** If that's the anchor they built the subject line around, it belongs in the first scroll, not below the fold after the HOT NEW GEAR section.
- **"HOT NEW GEAR!" section mid-email reads like a gear-shift that lost me.** Backpacks and accessories feel back-to-school adjacent — which isn't where my brain is on June 26. It interrupts the summer-deals flow.
- **No size-inclusive or family-set callout I could see.** Hanna Andersson's welcome email explicitly showed sibling stripe sets; Old Navy's showing individual items on individual models. For a family-volume shopper, "outfit the whole kid lineup" messaging would land better than a grid of singles.
- **Still feels mass.** No personalization signal, no acknowledgment that I've bought kids' uniforms through this brand for three years. Hanna Andersson treats me like a known customer in their very first email; ten sends in and Old Navy still doesn't know my name.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the $6 TODAY ONLY offer above the fold.** If "$2" in the subject got me to open, the payoff needs to be in the first visible block — not discovered by the third scroll. That's the single biggest lift available here.
- 2. **Create a "kids' summer pack" hero.** Bundle the sandals + shorts + tee into a "set your kid up for $X" callout. That's how I actually shop for back-to-school/summer — in batches, not individual items.
- 3. **Cut the HOT NEW GEAR section or re-sequence it.** If it's back-to-school, say so and build a separate email. Mid-summer gear interrupts the summer sale story.
- 4. **Add a personalization token.** Even a "Shop Jules' favorites" or "Kids & women's picks for you" would stop the mass-blast feeling cold.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$2 summer picks + a $6 TODAY-ONLY surprise`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Sandals from $2. (Plus something extra today only.)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Deep deals on kids' and women's summer — 40–70% off. Today's $6 deal inside.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your $6 TODAY ONLY deal + summer styles from $2.`
