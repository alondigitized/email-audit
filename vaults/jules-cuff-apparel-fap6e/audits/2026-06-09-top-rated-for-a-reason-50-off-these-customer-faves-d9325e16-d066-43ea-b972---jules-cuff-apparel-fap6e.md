# Top-rated for a reason: 50% off these customer faves
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-five. That's roughly where I've landed in my two-week running tally of Old Navy emails, and this one arrived the morning after "Yes, really: 50% off top-rated styles." Same offer, different synonym. "Customer faves" in June 9's subject is just "top-rated styles" from June 8th wearing a hat. At some point the marketing team stopped trying to convince me there's a sale and started hoping I'd forget yesterday's email.
- That said — I'm a volume buyer and this is my volume brand. "2 DAYS ONLY" is front and center, the 50% figure is big and readable, and the product grid shows women's and kids' items stacked together, which is exactly how my cart looks. The email isn't broken. It's just exhausted.
- I'd click, because I always click Old Navy sales and I need to stock the fall uniform drawer before July. But I'd be clicking with muscle memory, not enthusiasm. I would not forward this to anyone. My friends already know Old Navy is on sale — it's always on sale.

## What's working

- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" leads the visual hierarchy** — first thing the eye lands on, gives me a real reason to act today rather than tab away
- **50% is legible and prominent** — no hunting, no asterisks in the hero, the number does its job
- **Summer palette (coral, yellow, bright pink)** matches the season I'm actively buying for right now
- **Women's + kids' sections both appear** — relevant to how I shop; this isn't a single-SKU blast
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds together across the sections I can see

## What's weak

- **"Top-rated" and "customer faves" are unearned claims** — I see no star ratings, no review counts, no social proof anywhere in the render; the subject line sets up a promise the email body doesn't pay off
- **This is June 8's email with a thesaurus** — the June 8th send was literally "50% off top-rated styles"; today is "50% off customer faves"; this isn't a new campaign, it's copy drift
- **Email is extremely long with a clearance dump at the bottom** — bolting markdown clearance onto a 50%-off hero story dilutes both; Hanna Andersson's welcome email knew what it was and stayed there
- **No family-set or matching-set callout** — Hanna Andersson surfaces coordinated family looks effortlessly; Old Navy has the SKUs to do it and keeps leaving that opportunity on the table
- **Kids' section feels buried or labeled generically** — I spotted what looks like an activewear section for kids, but it wasn't merchandised as a destination; a family uniform shopper like me wants that front and loud

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse to one offer, one product story.** This email tries to be a department store catalog across five sections. Women's + kids' 50% off with one tight hero grid, one CTA, done. The clearance module should be its own email or a footer whisper.
- 2. **Prove "top-rated" — put a rating on something.** ★★★★☆ (4.8k reviews) next to the hero item earns the subject line. Right now "top-rated" is just a claim. Hanna Andersson uses product credibility signals even in their welcome series; Old Navy shouldn't be behind on this.
- 3. **Add a family-set row.** "The stripe that fits everyone" — one mom top + one kid top, same print. Hanna does this and it's the reason I spend more per item there. Old Navy has the inventory to own it.
- 4. **Hard deadline, not soft urgency.** "2 days only" is vague. "Ends Wednesday at midnight" with a date converts better because it forces a decision.
- 5. **Let the cadence breathe.** This is the 25th email in roughly two weeks. A single well-timed email after 3 days of silence would outperform five daily sends — but I know that's a strategy call, not a copy call.
- **Subject Alt A:** `2 days: 50% off the styles your cart keeps revisiting`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The top-rated list, finally half off — ends Wednesday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Kids activewear, women's dresses, the classics you actually wear.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `4.5 stars or better. 50% off. Ends Wednesday at midnight.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

`https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/ea4997d3-337e-4cb2-aef9-c8a56a4ecb95?mi_mid=019ead53-...`
The redirect chain resolves to `'/g/ea4997d3-337e-4cb2-aef9-c8a56a4ecb95?txn=560b8780-...'` — a relative path with no scheme or host. The link checker correctly rejects this as `unknown url type`. ### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing `alt` attribute (not empty string — fully absent)**
```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/ea4997d3-...&cp_cid=1bc829e71098f268"
     width="1" height="1">
```
Screen readers may announce this as an unlabeled image. Fix: add `alt=""`.

**Duplicate responsive CSS class definitions**
`.MspaceTop` is defined twice (`space-top5` and `space-top10` both map to it); same for `.MspaceBot`. A comment in the source acknowledges the duplication (`/* MspaceTop was duplicated, kept one */`), but both definitions remain. The later rule wins — verify the intended spacing value is the one left in place.

**Outlook `line-height: 0px` blanket override**
The MSO conditional block sets `td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }` globally for Outlook 2013+. This is an aggressive reset that can collapse cell height on any `<td>` not explicitly re-set. Inspect rendered output in Outlook 2016/2019 specifically.

**No issues** with charset declaration (`UTF-8`), viewport meta, or VML namespace inclusions.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent** from relay capture. CAN-SPAM requires a functional unsubscribe mechanism; Gmail and Yahoo also enforce `List-Unsubscribe` + `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) for bulk senders or face inbox demotion. The AgentMail relay may be stripping these headers before capture — confirm against raw SMTP headers at the MTA, not the relay.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent** — one-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail/Yahoo for senders above 5k/day threshold.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected** in the truncated HTML. CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) mandates a valid postal address in every commercial email. The HTML is truncated; this may exist in the footer. Verify the full footer render includes Gap Inc.'s San Francisco address.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header absent** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from this capture. `oldnavy.com` is a high-volume sender domain; any DKIM misalignment would be significant.
