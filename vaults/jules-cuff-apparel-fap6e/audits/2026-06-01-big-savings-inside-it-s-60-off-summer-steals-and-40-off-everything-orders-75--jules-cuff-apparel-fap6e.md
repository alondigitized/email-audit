# Big savings inside! It's 60% off summer steals and 40% off everything* (orders $75+)
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-01**
## Executive summary

- Seventeen. That's the number I said to myself when I saw this hit my inbox — seventeen Old Navy emails in roughly two weeks, and this one is somehow trying to carry two distinct offers at once. "Big savings inside!" is the most tired subject line construction in retail email, and I say that as someone who has now read it in four variations from Old Navy alone this month. The hero does its job — "2 DAYS ONLY!" lands first, 60% off summer steals is a real number I've acted on before. But the 40% threshold condition ($75+) lives in the fine print of the subject line before I've even opened it, which tells me the offer isn't as clean as they want it to seem.
- Once I'm inside: the email is full. Product grids, clearance stacks, a "Hot New Gear" section with what looks like patriotic imagery staging for the Fourth of July stretch, a loyalty callout. It's competent volume retail. The clearance section at the very bottom — "Go, Go, Go! Clearance" in stacked bold type — is actually the part that would make me stop scrolling. That's where the per-unit math gets good enough for school-year stocking.
- What's missing is me with kids. The hero and the first two product rows are women's-first. I'm buying for two children, not just myself. Compare that to Hanna Andersson, whose welcome email put a family-oriented visual language front and center from line one — I knew immediately that email understood my household. Old Navy buried that signal. I'll go back when I need to actually place an order. I won't forward this to anyone who isn't already on the list.

## What's working

- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" opens the hero** — before I read a word of product copy, I see the time limit. That's the right visual hierarchy call.
- **60% off summer steals is a real number** — I've bought into this exact semi-annual promotion and it delivers; the credibility is earned.
- **Multiple category sections give the cart-builder a path** — shorts, new gear, clearance — the layered structure matches how I actually shop Old Navy: load the cart, hit the threshold, justify the trip.
- **Old Navy Rewards is surfaced** — not elegantly, but it's there. Better than ignoring loyalty program holders entirely.
- **Clearance stacking at the bottom** — "Go, Go, Go! Clearance" in bold stacked type actually stopped my thumb. That section does the most practical work for a volume shopper.

## What's weak

- **"Big savings inside!" is the laziest possible opener** — this subject construction could belong to any brand, any week, any category. It signals nothing about me or my shopping context.
- **No kids signal in the hero or first scroll** — I'm buying for a family; the dominant visual language is adult women's. Hanna Andersson never makes me hunt for the kids section.
- **The 40% threshold is a hurdle, not a reward** — "$75+" in the subject fine print reads as a catch, not a perk. The email doesn't help me feel like hitting that threshold is easy.
- **Urgency fatigue is real at email seventeen** — "2 DAYS ONLY!" carries a fraction of the weight it should when a "limited time" version of this email ran four days ago.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the urgency into the subject line where it belongs** — "2 Days Only: 60% Off Summer Steals" does more decision-making work before I even open than "Big savings inside!" does inside.
- 2. **Add a family/kids callout in the first scroll** — even one row of kids' summer basics or a "matching family styles" lane above the fold signals this is a household send, not a solo women's blast.
- 3. **Reframe the $75 threshold as a challenge, not a condition** — show two-item math. "Grab two summer sets, hit $75, unlock 40% off everything." Make it feel achievable rather than lawyerly.
- 4. **Pull back cadence one send this week** — this email can't fix the fatigue it's fighting. One fewer near-identical send would make this one's urgency land harder.
- **Subject Alt A:** `2 Days Only: 60% Off Summer Steals`
- **Subject Alt B:** `60% off summer + unlock 40% off everything — 2 days left`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Hit $75, unlock 40% off everything — easy to do when summer basics are already 60% off.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Kids, women's, clearance — two days to stock up before summer prices reset.`

### 1. Technical Summary

One confirmed broken redirect terminates at a relative path rather than a valid URL, creating a dead click destination. Several compliance headers are absent or unverifiable through the AgentMail relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Broken redirect chain**
The cp.oldnavy.com redirect resolves to an unknown URL type:
```
https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/5d86a524-6694-47e0-8042-bbbf69b147db?mi_mid=...
  → /g/5d86a524-6694-47e0-8042-bbbf69b147db?txn=4bfbe000-5dc3-11f1-9425-8e0ec7ba23ea
```
The destination `/g/...` is a bare relative path, not an absolute URL. This is a broken experience — the redirect target either was never set or the redirect service returned a malformed response. This same URL is also the open-tracking pixel host, so it may be doubling as both a beacon and a content link, which compounds the failure.

**WARN — 23 click-redirect links unverified**
All CTA and product links route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. HTTP probing was skipped for this domain, so destination URL correctness, UTM presence, and landing page alignment cannot be confirmed for any of them.

**Open pixel** (`click.email.oldnavy.com/open.aspx?...`) — present, standard implementation, no issues.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text**
The cp.oldnavy.com tracking/content image has no `alt` attribute:
```
src: https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/5d86a524-6694-47e0-8042-bbbf69b147db?mi_mid=...
```
With images blocked (default in many clients), this renders as a blank space with no fallback text.

**Aggressive Outlook line-height reset**
The MSO conditional injects `td { line-height: 0px; }` globally for Outlook 2013+:
```css
<!--[if gte mso 9]>
  td { line-height:0px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly }
```
This can collapse text-only cells in Outlook if a matching `mso-line-height-rule` override is not applied to every `td` that contains live text.

**Duplicate CSS rule selectors**
The stylesheet comment itself flags this: `.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice in the `@media (max-width: 639px)` block. Duplicate rules are dead weight and create specificity ambiguity; the last declaration wins silently.

**No issues with:** charset declaration (UTF-8), viewport meta, VML namespace declarations for Outlook, or `display:block` on images.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

`mi_mid` and `mi_cid` parameters in tracking URLs carry populated values (`019e84c5-8e00-7000-b82a-4973d69dbe9c`, `67859151839b71d0`), indicating subscriber-level merge data fired correctly.

No unfired token placeholders (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `*|FNAME|*`) are visible in the truncated source. No issues found from available source.

---

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header — not found**
The relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Gmail and Yahoo require this for bulk senders; its absence risks inbox placement and may trigger "Report spam" instead of "Unsubscribe" in supported clients.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post header — not found (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe (the machine-readable `POST` mechanism) is not confirmed present. Gmail has enforced this for senders >5k/day since 2024.

**Physical mailing address — not detected**
The QA scanner did not find a physical address in the scanned portion of the HTML. CAN-SPAM §7 requires a valid postal address in every commercial email. This may be present in the truncated footer — confirm before flagging as a definitive violation.

**SPF/DKIM/DMARC — unverifiable**
`Authentication-Results` header was not captured by the AgentMail relay. Sender domain is `email.oldnavy.com`; authentication posture (SPF pass/fail, DKIM signature validity) cannot be confirmed from available data.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 23 CTAs pass through the `click.email.oldnavy.com` redirect layer, which should append UTM parameters before forwarding to `oldnavy.com`. Because HTTP probes were skipped for this domain, UTM presence on final landing URLs is unconfirmed.

The one probed link (cp.oldnavy.com) returned a broken destination, so continuity for that specific path is broken end-to-end.
