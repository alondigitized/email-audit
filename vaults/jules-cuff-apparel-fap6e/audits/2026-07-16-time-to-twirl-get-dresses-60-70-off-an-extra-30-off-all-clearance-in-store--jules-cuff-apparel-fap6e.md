# 👗Time to twirl! Get dresses 60-70% OFF + an extra 30% off all Clearance in-store
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy gave me actual fashion content yesterday — the low-rise jean email had a story, a voice, something almost editorial — and today we're back to the megaphone. Dresses at 60-70% off is a real number, the kind that makes me pause mid-coffee. But I looked at this and then kept scrolling past dresses into $3.99 basics, then "VIP Lounge," then "Hot Swim Gear," then tiny kids' thumbnails at the bottom. By the time I got to the swim section I had completely forgotten why I opened this.
- The core offer is strong and I do care about a summer dress deal — I'm a person who wears clothes, after all, not just a uniform-buying machine. But this email can't decide what it is. It opens like a fashion send and dissolves into a clearance dump halfway through. Hanna Andersson's welcome email knew exactly what it was from hero to footer. This one loses the thread by module three.
- I'd mark it read, maybe screenshot the top offer to come back to, and definitely not forward it. There's nothing targeted enough to feel like a find.

## What's working

- **60-70% off lands immediately** — it's the first real content I see, before I have to scroll or hunt, and the math is clear
- **"3 DAYS ONLY!" creates actual urgency** — this week's Old Navy emails have felt like rolling promos with no end date; a defined window is more believable
- **Models are adult women in normal summer context** — age-appropriate styling, nothing aspirational in a way that makes me feel like I'm shopping the wrong catalog
- **VIP LOUNGE module signals they know I might be a member** — even if I can't read the fine print, the presence of a loyalty section says "we see you"
- **Clean render throughout** — loads fast, no broken images, no gaps, which matters when I'm opening this in 30 seconds on my phone

## What's weak

- **The email is having four conversations at once** — dresses, then basics, then loungewear, then swim gear; Hanna Andersson leads a single lane start to finish, and Old Navy could learn from that discipline
- **"in-store" on the extra 30% clearance is buried at the tail of the subject line** — if I'm an online shopper I will feel deceived at checkout when that discount doesn't apply, and that's a trust problem
- **Five emails in five days, five different hero products** — polos, tanks, tanks again, jeans, dresses; the cadence makes this feel like a brand panicking through its clearance inventory, not a brand with a plan
- **No kids' angle anywhere in the hero** — a huge chunk of what I actually buy from Old Navy is for my kids, and this email treats me like I only exist from the waist up in a sundress
- **"EXPERIENCE $3.99 AND UP" is confusing naming** — that word "experience" reads like brand-campaign language, not a product section; I had to stop and re-read it

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the email by half.** Lead with dresses, add one supporting clearance module, close. The swim and kids add-ons dilute the message and make this feel like a kitchen-sink blast rather than a curated send.
- 2. **Make the in-store restriction a design element, not a footnote.** Either a clear "Clearance deal valid in-store only" callout in the hero or a two-column split (online / in-store offers) so online shoppers know what applies to their cart.
- 3. **Name the end date, not just the window.** "3 Days Only" is urgency; "Ends Sunday" is actionable. One tells me to care; the other tells me when to move.
- 4. **Add one girls' dresses callout** — a "Shop Girls' Dresses" module directly below the hero turns this from a me-purchase into a two-for-one add to cart, which is how I actually shop Old Navy.
- 5. **Rename "$3.99 and Up"** to something that front-loads the value: "Basics from $3.99" tells me exactly what I'm clicking into without making me pause.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dresses: 60-70% off, 3 days only — extra 30% off clearance in-store`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The dress window: 60-70% off ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Summer dresses under $20 — plus your clearance bonus at the register`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Three days to grab dresses at 60-70% off. Don't wait on this one.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

The Contentsquare/personalization pixel resolves to an internal redirect (`/g/9491c59d-31aa-40ca-916f-e4c498cd7880?txn=93de8880-...`) that returns an unknown URL type. The full URL:

This pixel also appears inline in `<div style="display:none">` — the redirect failure means open-tracking and personalization attribution for this send are broken for any recipient where this fires.

**[WARN] 23 click links skipped** — all route through `click.email.oldnavy.com` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud redirect). Not a defect, but upstream click-tracking health can't be confirmed from this audit.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing `alt` text on tracking pixel**

```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/9491c59d-..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
```

Empty `alt=""` is acceptable for decorative/tracking images per WCAG, but the QA checker flagged it — confirm this is intentional and not a templating gap that should carry descriptive text on content images.

**No issues** with responsive breakpoint logic (`@media max-width: 639px`), Outlook VML conditionals, or the `mso-line-height-rule: exactly` fix. Desktop/mobile content toggling via `.desktop-content`/`.mobile-content` classes is correctly structured.

**Minor:** `MspaceTop` and `MspaceBot` class names are defined twice in the responsive block (with different `padding-top`/`padding-bottom` values). The second definition wins, silently overriding the first. Low risk but worth cleaning up to avoid confusion.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `%%first_name%%`) visible in the truncated source. The `mi_mid` parameter in the pixel URL (`019f69f0-8c00-7000-b7c2-d1e3313df5a7`) appears to be a rendered subscriber ID, not an unfired token — consistent with correct send-time personalization.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] Physical mailing address not detected**

CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email. The QA scan found none in the captured source. Either:
- It's present in the truncated portion not provided, or
- It's genuinely missing — a CAN-SPAM violation risk.

**Verify** the footer of the full HTML contains an address block before clearing this.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers not captured**

RFC 2369 (`List-Unsubscribe`) and RFC 8058 (`List-Unsubscribe-Post`) headers are absent from what the AgentMail relay surfaced. These are almost certainly present on the actual MTA-level delivery (SFMC adds them by default), but the relay isn't forwarding them. This is a **relay configuration gap**, not necessarily a sender defect — but it means one-click unsubscribe compliance can't be verified through this pipeline.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not captured**

SPF/DKIM pass/fail status unknown. Same root cause as above — relay is not forwarding authentication headers. `oldnavy@email.oldnavy.com` sending via `email.oldnavy.com` should be covered by Gap Inc.'s SPF record and DKIM signing, but this pipeline cannot confirm it.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**No UTM parameters detected** on the 23 click links — all route through `click.email.oldnavy.com` redirects. SFMC click tracking typically appends UTMs at redirect time based on campaign metadata, so this may be fine. However, if UTM passthrough isn't configured in the SFMC send definition, GA4/analytics will attribute resulting sessions to `(direct)` rather than the email campaign.

**Risk:** Cannot confirm UTM values without probing a live redirect — the 23 click links were skipped by the QA checker. Spot-check one resolved URL to confirm `utm_source=email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=...` survives the redirect chain.
