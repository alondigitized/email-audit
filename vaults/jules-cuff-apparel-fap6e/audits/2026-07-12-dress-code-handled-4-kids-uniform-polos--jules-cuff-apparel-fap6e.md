# Dress code, handled: $4 kids uniform polos
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- Third rodeo with "$4 kids uniform polos" in under 24 hours — and I say that not to be dismissive, because today's subject hook is genuinely the strongest iteration of this promo. "Dress code, handled:" is punchy in a way yesterday's two sends weren't. It names the problem before it gives me the answer, which is how you should write a subject line. But I am, undeniably, on my third lap around the same track, and my thumb knows it.
- The email opens well — $4 polo, 2 DAYS ONLY, classic uniform colors visible — and if I had never seen this brand before, I'd be clicking immediately. The VIP Lounge rewards section tells me Old Navy remembers I'm a member, which I always appreciate. But the scroll is long. Clearance from $3.99, then back-to-school essentials, then another product module. What should be a laser-focused "polo email" becomes a general promotional blast by the time I reach the bottom. Hanna Andersson's welcome email — a solid 8 — stayed disciplined about one message the entire length. This one loses its nerve halfway down.
- Would I click? Yes, actually — because I was already mentally in the cart and the hero delivers the goods fast. Would I forward it? No. Would I remember it as distinct from yesterday's send? Barely.

## What's working

- **"Dress code, handled:" is the best subject framing this promo has run** — verb-forward, problem-first, and it earns the price reveal rather than just leading with it
- **$4 hero with 2-DAYS-ONLY urgency is above the fold and immediate** — I don't have to scroll or guess what I'm being offered
- **Polo colorways read "uniform" at a glance** — navy, white, red visible in the hero; no ambiguity about whether this is a school-appropriate item
- **VIP Lounge section acknowledges I'm a member** — small thing, but Carter's almost never does this and it shows
- **Back-to-school framing at the bottom confirms category intent** — Old Navy knows why I'm opening in July

## What's weak

- **Third send on the same price point in under 24 hours** — even a strong subject hook can't fully offset the fatigue of seeing the same number again
- **The email does too much** — polo hero, clearance pricing, VIP module, back-to-school essentials all in one scroll is diluting the urgency of the actual offer
- **The $3.99 clearance section right below the $4 polo hero undercuts the whole thing** — it makes the polo feel like just another item in a bin sale rather than a deliberate, curated recommendation
- **No sizing or availability signal anywhere visible** — buying for two kids means I need to know if XS through XL is still in stock before I bother clicking; Target's Cat & Jack always surfaces this and it removes a friction point I genuinely feel

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the deadline the hero if this is genuinely the last send** — "2 days left on $4 polos" forces a decision in a way that "Dress code, handled:" doesn't. Save the clever framing for a full-price brand moment; close the promo with urgency.
- 2. **Cut the clearance section from this email entirely** — let the polo breathe. If clearance is a separate campaign, send a separate email.
- 3. **Add one line of sizing confidence** — "XS–XXL available" or "Most sizes still in stock" removes hesitation before the click, especially for families buying across size ranges.
- 4. **If you send a fourth time, change something real** — a bundle angle, family-set framing ("outfit all three kids for under $15"), or a free-shipping sweetener. Don't re-skin the same offer; earn the send.
- 5. **Trim the email by 30–40% in scroll depth** — Hanna Andersson keeps their single-message sends focused; borrow that editorial discipline for promotional closes.

- **Subject Alt B:** `$4 polo. 2 kids. Done.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Navy, white, red — all $4 through Sunday while sizes last`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Close the back-to-school list tonight`

## Technical Audit — Old Navy "Dress Code, Handled" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

One confirmed broken click-redirect and a missing physical address are the highest-priority issues. Compliance headers (List-Unsubscribe, RFC 8058) were not captured by the relay, which is a deliverability risk given Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender requirements active since Feb 2024.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken click redirect**
The cp.oldnavy.com click-tracking wrapper resolved its destination to a relative path instead of an absolute URL:
```
Error: unknown url type: '/g/b3e75ac0-efda-4969-86e2-1151254ddd19?txn=7f795380-7de0-11f1-aeb5-9295eb1db778'
Source URL: https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/b3e75ac0-...[truncated]
```
This is likely the CDP tracking pixel doubling as a redirect, with a misconfigured `Location` header returning `/g/...` (relative) instead of `https://cp.oldnavy.com/g/...`. Any CTA routed through this redirect will 404 for subscribers.

**[WARN] 23 tracking links skipped**
All click-redirect links on `click.email.oldnavy.com` were not HTTP-probed. Destination reachability and UTM integrity for these links are unverified.

**Tracking pixel structure**
Two pixels fire on open from separate systems (click.email.oldnavy.com and cp.oldnavy.com). This is normal for dual ESP+CDP setups but means open counts may be deduplicated differently across platforms.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing alt text on tracking pixel**
```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/b3e75ac0-efda-4969-86e2-1151254ddd19?..." width="1" height="1">
```
No `alt=""` attribute. Screen readers may announce the raw URL. Add `alt=""` to suppress.

**Duplicate CSS class selectors**
`.MspaceTop` is bound to both `.space-top5` and `.space-top10` in the responsive block; same for `.MspaceBot`. The HTML source comments acknowledge this ("MspaceTop was duplicated, kept one") but the duplicate rules remain, creating specificity ambiguity. Low severity but should be cleaned.

**`min-height: 500px !important` on body**
```css
body {min-height: 500px !important;}
```
This forces a minimum body height in all clients including preview panes, which can expose blank whitespace below short content in Apple Mail and some webmail clients.

**No issues found:** Doctype (XHTML 1.0 Transitional), VML namespace for Outlook, single 639px responsive breakpoint, `-webkit-text-size-adjust`, and `.ExternalClass` resets are all correctly implemented.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post headers not captured**
The QA relay did not see these headers. Gmail and Yahoo have required `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click) for bulk senders (>5k/day) since February 2024. If Old Navy's ESP injects these headers upstream of the relay, the gap is an instrumentation issue only. If they are genuinely absent, this will suppress deliverability and Gmail will show the unsubscribe UI in a degraded state.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected in captured HTML**
CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in the message body. The HTML source is truncated — the address may exist in the footer below the cut. This finding should be verified against the full render; if absent, it is a legal compliance violation.

**[WARN] Authentication-Results header not captured**
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown. Not actionable without full header capture, but should be verified against Old Navy's DMARC record (`_dmarc.oldnavy.com`).

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Broken redirect breaks at least one CTA** (see §2). The affected URL is the cp.oldnavy.com CDP pixel/redirect — if any hero or product CTA routes through this domain, subscribers will land on an error page.

**UTM coverage unverifiable** — all 23 click-redirect links run through `click.email.oldnavy.com` and were not probed. Whether UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content`) survive the redirect chain to oldnavy.com cannot be confirmed from this audit. Manual spot-check of one resolved redirect URL is needed.

This is well above the 70% flag threshold and is a spam-filter risk. Plain-text versions of commercial emails should summarize content in prose with a few key links, not mirror the full HTML link list.
