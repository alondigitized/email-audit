# The cozy math checks out: $16 sweatshirts + take an EXTRA 30% off your purchase*
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-03**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "cozy math" is a cute subject line but the email itself doesn't lead with cozy math — it opens with "All Jeans On Sale" and "Extra 30% Off," and I have to scroll past a whole kids' sweatshirt block before I even see the sweatshirt price the subject promised. That's a disconnect I've flagged before with this brand. This is basically the same structural sprawl I called out on 8/2 and 8/1 — jeans up top, then a kids' callout, then five different low-rise denim silhouettes, then a utility pants micro-launch, then the red clearance wall at the very bottom. It's a lot of email for one scroll.
- What actually caught my eye was the $12/$16 sweatshirt block with the kid model in pink — that's the one section that's genuinely "me," since I'm buying Old Navy sweats for volume for my kids. Everything after that — five near-identical low-rise jean crops, a "Made for You-tility" pants launch — reads like Old Navy stapled three separate campaigns together and mailed it as one. I wouldn't forward this to a friend; I'd maybe screenshot just the sweatshirt price if I were stocking up, then close the email.

## What's working

- The $12/$16 kids' sweatshirt block up top is genuinely on-brand for how I shop Old Navy — volume basics, clear price, kid model actually wearing it.
- The red "2 DAYS ONLY!" banner still cuts through — Old Navy's using urgency again instead of the purple 50%-off wallpaper they were stuck on for weeks.
- Prices are big and unmissable throughout ($10 tees, $22 jeans, $12/$16 sweatshirts) — no hunting for the number.
- The extra 30% off code (EXTRA) is visible at the very top, so at least the top-of-funnel offer is legible before I scroll.

## What's weak

- The subject promises "$16 sweatshirts" but the hero of the email is jeans and 30% off — the thing I opened for is buried two scrolls down.
- This email tries to do sweatshirts + tees + five jean fits + a whole new pants sub-brand + clearance in one send — no hierarchy, just stacked campaigns.
- Zero kids'-vs-adult sizing distinction on the sweatshirt block — as the family uniform shopper, I need to know at a glance if this is the kids' semi-annual restock or an adult crossover, and it's not called out.
- The "Made for You-tility" pants section is a totally different product story (adult women's utility pants) wedged in with no transition — feels like three brands' worth of email glued together.
- Compared to Hanna Andersson's welcome email, which led with one offer and stayed disciplined, this is the opposite: no editing, just volume.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the sweatshirt block to the very top** so subject and hero match — right now the $16 sweatshirt promise is buried under jeans, which is the same subject/hero mismatch problem, just on a different product this time.
- 2. **Cut this into two sends** — one for the sweatshirt/kids restock, one for the low-rise denim relaunch. Trying to cover both in one email is why nothing gets the visual weight it needs.
- 3. **Add a kids vs. adult label directly on the sweatshirt tile** — "Kids' Fleece" or similar — so a parent scanning fast doesn't have to guess.
- 4. **Drop or relocate the "Made for You-tility" pants section** — it reads like a separate campaign and dilutes the sweatshirt/jean focus the subject and top banner set up.
- 5. **Tighten the low-rise jean section** from five near-identical crops to two — Old Navy's suffering from the same "one more scroll" bloat I flagged on 8/2.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Kids' sweatshirts $12–$16 + extra 30% off everything`

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stock up on fleece before the extra 30% code expires`
