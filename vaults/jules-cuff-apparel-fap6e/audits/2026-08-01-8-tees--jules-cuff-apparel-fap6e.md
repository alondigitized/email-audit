# 👕 $8 TEES
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-01**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "2 DAYS ONLY!" in red with $6/$8 tees actually competes for my attention for a second — Old Navy hasn't done urgency-with-a-deadline in a while, it's been wall-to-wall purple 50%-off since forever (looking at you, 7/30 and 7/31 sends). But then I scroll and it's the same PowerChill leggings/joggers parade I've seen a dozen times, just with a green background instead of purple this time. The "VIP Lounge" banner is trying so hard to feel exclusive and it just... doesn't, there's no member perk attached to it, it's just a section header over the same $10 PowerChill grid.
- This is a mid-tier Old Navy send for me — better than the straight 50%-off-everything wallpaper spam, worse than yesterday's genuinely fresh $8 tee sale. I'm not clicking on the leggings carousel, but the tee price point and the size-inclusive grid at the bottom (XS through 4X, visible right there) might actually get a tap if I need summer tees for the kids' camp rotation.
- Would I forward this to a friend? No. Would I click? Maybe, for the tee sale specifically, and only because I already know from wearing their stuff that Old Navy tees don't survive two kids and stripes the way Hanna Andersson does — so this is a volume buy, not a keeper buy, and that's exactly the math I run every time this brand hits my inbox.

## What's working

- "2 DAYS ONLY!" in red actually breaks the purple monotony I called out in the last three Old Navy emails — genuine urgency signal for once.
- $6/$8 tee price point is dirt cheap and legible at a glance, no digging required.
- The clearance size grid (XS–4X, 1X–4X plus curve sizes) is right there in the email, not hidden behind a click — that's a real size-inclusive signal Hanna Andersson doesn't even bother with in their welcome series.
- "All Jeans On Sale From $22" at the bottom gives me a second concrete price anchor if tees don't land.

## What's weak

- "VIP Lounge" is a label with nothing behind it — no points balance, no member-only code, no "you've earned" copy. It's borrowing loyalty language without paying the loyalty tax, which is exactly the mass-vs-premium gap I flag constantly on this brand.
- Four near-identical PowerChill product shots (leggings, joggers, tank, tee) in a row is repetitive scrolling for one $10 price point I've already seen this week in a different color background.
- "Is he headed for the coffee shop or the couch? (Yes.)" — cute copy, wrong persona, this is styled at a solo twentysomething, not a mom buying uniforms for two kids. Nothing here screams "family sizes together" or "matching set."
- Two competing offers stacked at the very top (Extra 30% off jeans + $6-8 tees) muddy the hierarchy before I even scroll — my eye doesn't know which one is the actual headline.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the "VIP Lounge" label or actually back it with a perk (early access hours, bonus points) — right now it's a premium word slapped on a mass email, and it reads exactly like the "no offer feels honest" trap I keep dinging Old Navy for.
- 2. Cut the PowerChill block from four product shots to two — the repetition is the same complaint I had about last week's identical purple sends, just in a different color.
- 3. Lead with ONE offer above the fold, not two — pick the tee sale (it's the fresher, more specific hook) and demote the jeans+30% to a secondary strip.
- 4. Swap the "coffee shop or the couch" line for something that nods to back-to-school/family buying since that's clearly a chunk of their list right now.
- **Subject Alt A:** `2 days only: $6 tees for the whole crew`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Stock up: $6-$8 tees, sizes for everyone`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Grab extras before back-to-school — tees from $6, ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus $10 PowerChill and jeans from $22`
