# Go ahead, take a peek 👀 New arrivals from $10 + $12 adult shorts & women's dresses
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-three. I said it out loud before I even opened this one. Yesterday Old Navy landed two separate emails in my inbox — both pitching the same $12 shorts — and now here's a fresh one with an eyeball emoji asking me to "take a peek." At this volume, opening an Old Navy email feels less like shopping and more like a chore I keep putting off.
- That said, the hero actually earns its space: "2 DAYS ONLY!" in big letters, real dollar prices ($9, $12), and a cute model in an orange dress. That's the format that works. The problem is everything south of the fold — the activewear-on-sale section showing up for the third time in four days, a "Hot New Gear!" callout that competes for attention, and then "Clearance Clearance Clearance" stacked three times at the bottom like a marquee sign mid-glitch. This email is trying to be five emails at once.
- Would I click on the hero dress? Maybe, if I had a specific need today. Would I scroll to the clearance section? No. Would I forward this to anyone? Absolutely not. Most likely I'd tap the hero out of mild curiosity and close the tab when I realize there's no kids section and no reason to linger.

## What's working

- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" with dollar prices in the hero** — $9 and $12 visible at a glance; no math required, no percentage conversion, just a number I can act on immediately
- **The orange dress in the hero is genuinely appealing** — well-styled, seasonal, and the model reads as my age range; it's the rare Old Navy shot that doesn't feel like clip art
- **Warm summer palette throughout** — not selling fleece in June, which sounds like a low bar but compared to some of the stale promo sends this week it counts
- **Multiple concrete price anchors ($6, $8, $12 in the women's section)** — budget math done for me, good for a volume shopper like me who needs to know what I'm walking into

## What's weak

- **Activewear on sale. Again.** — June 4 was "HALF OFF all activewear," June 5 was "50% off all active," and now there's an activewear module buried in today's email; at this point that section is invisible to me, I've trained myself to skip it
- **Five competing sections with no narrative** — new arrivals, women's dresses, activewear, Hot New Gear, clearance all in one scroll; this is an everything-drawer, not an email, and Hanna Andersson's welcome series showed what a single focused story looks like
- **"Clearance Clearance Clearance" stacked at the bottom** — reads like a broken design asset, not intentional branding; it feels desperate rather than urgent
- **Zero family or kids signal** — I am one of Old Navy's highest-value shoppers specifically because I buy school uniforms in bulk every fall, and this email treats me like a childless adult-only shopper the entire scroll

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero offer and commit** — new arrivals OR clearance OR activewear; not all three in the same send; the 2-days-only dress is the hook, let it breathe
- 2. **Pull activewear from the rotation entirely this week** — three activewear pushes in four days has burned the section for me; rest it until next week so it reads as news again
- 3. **Add a single family/kids module** — even one row of "Uniforms from $8" or "Kids shorts $8" with a CTA would signal that Old Navy knows who I am; right now this email could be addressed to anyone
- 4. **Replace "Clearance Clearance Clearance" with a single clear CTA line** — "Shop clearance — up to 70% off" does the same job without looking like a font test
- 5. **Cut send frequency in half** — Hanna Andersson sent me one welcome email and I still remember the 20% code; Old Navy has sent 23 and I remember none of the individual offers

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has one confirmed broken redirect in the tracking infrastructure and three compliance header gaps; all other core rendering logic is structurally sound. The plain-text part is a spam-signal liability due to near-total URL saturation.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect chain — `cp.oldnavy.com` open-pixel**

QA probe of the Contentsquare/Movable Ink pixel returned a relative path instead of an absolute URL:

```
Resolved path: /g/afbaf220-5177-4133-8667-2fbb9d338953?txn=bea40a00-6260-11f1-b68a-4e8366552ae6
Expected:      https://cp.oldnavy.com/g/...
```

The pixel at `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/afbaf220...` issues a redirect to a bare `/g/...` path. Any client that follows the redirect without re-attaching the origin will receive a 404/invalid resource. This breaks open-tracking and any engagement metric derived from this pixel.

**[INFO] 23 click-tracking links skipped** — all route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. Not probeable without live recipient session cookies; treat as untested.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate CSS class definitions (mobile stylesheet):**

```css
.space-top5, .MspaceTop  { padding-top: 5px; }   /* line ~93 */
.space-top10, .MspaceTop { padding-top: 10px; }  /* line ~96 — overrides above */
.space-bot5,  .MspaceBot { padding-bottom: 5px; }
.space-bot10, .MspaceBot { padding-bottom: 10px; }
```

The source even contains an inline comment acknowledging the duplication (`/* MspaceTop was duplicated, kept one */`) but the duplicate was not removed. On mobile, any element using `.MspaceTop` will always receive `padding-top: 10px` regardless of intent — the 5px variant is effectively dead.

**Missing alt text on tracking pixel:**

```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/afbaf220-..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
```

QA flagged this pixel for missing alt. An empty `alt=""` is acceptable for decorative/tracking pixels per WCAG — confirm the attribute is present in the untruncated source (the HTML cuts off before closing the tag). If the attribute is absent entirely, screen readers will read the full `src` URL aloud.

**No issues** with Outlook VML conditional blocks, mobile breakpoint logic (639px), or `ExternalClass` Outlook resets — these are correctly implemented.
