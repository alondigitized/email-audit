# Warm-weather deals: $12 shorts & women's dresses
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-one. Said it out loud again, right there in my kitchen. Twenty-one Old Navy emails in roughly two weeks, and today's subject is honestly one of their better ones — "$12 shorts" is a real number, not another percentage stacked on top of a percentage. That's the kind of thing that makes me pause mid-swipe. Points for specificity.
- I opened it. The hero is bright red, "2 DAYS ONLY!" badge slapped on, and a woman in a coral dress with $9 and $12 price tags floating beside her. That part works. Concrete prices, seasonal product, clear urgency badge. But then I keep scrolling and there it is — "ALL ACTIVEWEAR ON SALE" — for the third time this week. I audited an activewear send on June 5th and another on June 4th. Having it as module two in today's email, occupying the same amount of real estate as the warm-weather hero, is pure filler. And then there's a new "TekTech" section competing for my attention right next to it. Here I've got five competing modules, none of them knowing what job to do.
- The kids and baby sale clearance is at the very bottom, which is the backwards priority for me. That's the section I'm actually here for — volume buying for two kids — and it's an afterthought. I might click through on the $12 shorts for myself, but I'm not forwarding this, and the "2 DAYS ONLY!" urgency badge doesn't land the same way when Old Navy has been in perpetual sale mode since before Memorial Day.

## What's working

- **"$12 shorts" in the subject** is the sharpest thing in this email — a specific number beats a percentage every time, and this is how you write a deal subject line.
- **The hero price tags ($9 and $12) are immediately visible** without scrolling; I know the floor price before I've even read a word of copy.
- **Red hero with a clear "2 DAYS ONLY!" badge** catches the eye — whatever I think about the manufactured urgency, the visual execution of the hero module is clean and fast-loading.
- **Kids & Baby sale clearance exists** — it's buried at the bottom but it's there, and it signals Old Navy at least knows their subscriber base shops across all ages.
- **No render bugs** — images load, text isn't scrambled, nothing is overlapping. The basics are cleared.

## What's weak

- **Activewear module is back. Again.** This is the third consecutive day Old Navy has pushed active wear at me; it's taking up the second-most prominent slot in today's email behind the hero, and I do not buy activewear here.
- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" credibility is shot.** When every email for three weeks has had some kind of urgent deadline, the badge stops working. It reads like a car dealership "this weekend only" sign that's been in the window since February.
- **Kids section is buried at the very bottom.** I'm a family-volume buyer. The clearance rack for kids should be one scroll down, not the last thing before the footer.
- **Five competing modules with no editorial hierarchy** — warm weather hero, activewear, TekTech, 50–60% off select styles, and kids clearance. There's no main character. Hanna Andersson would never let five sections compete this evenly.
- **TekTech launch buried next to an activewear deal** — if "TekTech" is a new product line, it deserves its own send, not a cameo sandwiched between two other modules.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the kids sale, not third-party women's fashion.** I am here for family volume. Put kids clearance in module two so it's one scroll below the hero, not five.
- 2. **Kill the activewear module.** It has had its own dedicated sends two days running. Give that real estate back to warm-weather or kids.
- 3. **Add a deadline that feels real.** "2 DAYS ONLY! Ends Sunday 11:59 PM" is more credible than a floating badge. A countdown or explicit calendar date would make me take it seriously.
- 4. **Give TekTech its own send if it's actually new.** Don't launch product innovation inside a clearance email — it deserves focused attention, not a sidebar cameo.
- 5. **Pull one hero product into the subject line and link it directly.** Instead of the homepage link, take me straight to the $12 shorts PDP.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

The Conversant pixel at `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/8fb52c33-1cc5-4976-a9bd-867271755870?...` resolves to a relative URL:

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate mobile CSS selectors (silent specificity bug)**
In the `@media (max-width: 639px)` block, `.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice with different values:
```css
.space-top5, .MspaceTop { padding-top: 5px; }
...
.space-top10, .MspaceTop { padding-top: 10px; }  /* overwrites */

.space-bot5, .MspaceBot { padding-bottom: 5px; }
...
.space-bot10, .MspaceBot { padding-bottom: 10px; } /* overwrites */
```
Any element using `.MspaceTop` on mobile gets `10px` regardless of intent. The comment in source acknowledges this: `/* MspaceTop was duplicated, kept one */` — but both declarations remain. Elements that should render with 5px top/bottom padding on mobile will silently render at 10px.

**Outlook global line-height zero**
The MSO conditional sets `td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }` globally. This is a known Outlook normalization pattern but is aggressive — any `<td>` without an explicit override will collapse line-height to zero in Outlook 2013+.

**Missing alt text — tracking pixel**
```
Image missing alt text: src https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/8fb52c33-...
```
The pixel has `alt=""` (empty string) rather than a null/decorative `role="presentation"`. Minor for a 1×1 pixel, but will flag in accessibility audits.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header absent from relay capture**
The AgentMail relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to render the native unsubscribe button. Its absence — whether missing at send or stripped by the relay — means one-click unsubscribe at the MUA level is non-functional.

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post absent (RFC 8058)**
No `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` header present. Google's February 2024 bulk-sender requirements mandate this for senders over 5k/day. Old Navy almost certainly qualifies. If the header is present on the wire but the relay is dropping it, that needs to be confirmed with a raw header capture.
