# Kids uniform polos are $4 (and have a 1-year guarantee!)
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so Old Navy is doing something interesting here — and I noticed it immediately. The subject dangles a 1-year guarantee like it's a new piece of information, and that *is* new. I've been watching this $4 polo promo roll out across at least three sends in the last 24 hours (I'm not exaggerating — I checked my folder), and every time I thought they'd wrung out every angle, they found another one. The guarantee is genuinely the freshest hook in this whole cycle.
- But then I opened the email and the polo hero is... there, sure. Visible. "2 DAYS ONLY," $4, kids in polos. Fine. What I didn't expect was how fast the email pivots away from me. I scroll six inches and I'm in "THE NEW TREFOIL" territory — trendy teen looks in hot pink and orange with zero relevance to a mom who just needed seven polos by August. Then it drops into linen looks and adult fashion. This is not a uniform email anymore. It's a uniform tease stitched to a general-catalog send.
- Would I click? Yes — but only on the polo section, and only because I was already mentally in the cart. I would not forward this. The guarantee headline is the best thing here; the email body doesn't cash that check.

## What's working

- **The guarantee is a real differentiator.** Hanna Andersson charges $28 for a polo and sells you on longevity. Old Navy coming in at $4 with a 1-year guarantee is a direct shot across the bow — that's the argument I've been waiting to make to justify stocking up.
- **Hero delivers the promise fast.** "2 DAYS ONLY" + $4 + kids in polos is visible without scrolling — the subject line's core offer is right there in the hero. No bait-and-switch on what's above the fold.
- **Sender identity is trusted.** "Old Navy Back to School" as the display name signals exactly what I need it to — this is the annual stock-up moment, not a random promo.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, nothing janky. The email loads and reads without friction.
- **Season-right context.** "Back to School" framing lands. I'm in that mode; this isn't March.

## What's weak

- **The guarantee headline from the subject is not reinforced in the body.** That 1-year guarantee is the most compelling thing in this send — and from what I can see, it doesn't get a hero callout, a badge, or a dedicated section. It's doing heavy lifting in the subject and then disappearing.
- **The email becomes a general catalog by section three.** "THE NEW TREFOIL" in neon pinks has nothing to do with uniform polos. Hanna Andersson's welcome email stayed in its lane the entire scroll — this email loses mine before I get to the bottom.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is the fourth near-identical promo in under 24 hours. The guarantee angle is new; the creative is not. I'm starting to scroll-skip by muscle memory.
- **Adult fashion sections feel misrouted.** Linen looks, $40 price points, adult models — these are fine for a different send but feel algorithmically stuffed in rather than curated for a parent shopping uniform week.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the 1-year guarantee a visible hero badge, not just subject-line copy.** That's the entire news hook. A small lockup — "Guaranteed for 1 full year" — on or under the polo image turns the opener into a reason to *actually change brands*, not just top up.
- 2. **Cut the email at the polo section.** Strip "THE NEW TREFOIL" and the adult fashion content. Send those to a different segment. A dedicated uniform email for parents is more powerful than a combined send trying to serve every customer at once.
- 3. **Add a quantity/sizing signal.** Something like "Sizes XS–XXL, stock limited" would create urgency without manufacturing it. Hanna Andersson shows size availability inline; Old Navy could do this on their hero image or just below the CTA.
- 4. **Reorder the $3.99 all-day stretch section** — if that's kids' bottoms, pair it immediately with the polo. A "build the uniform" two-item hero does more work than presenting them three sections apart.

- **Subject Alt B:** `The only $4 polo that comes with a guarantee (2 days left)`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text on tracking pixel**
The `cp.oldnavy.com` pixel image has no `alt` attribute:
```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/a23fde8d-..." width="1" height="1">
```
Screen readers will announce the full src URL. Should be `alt=""`.

**Duplicate CSS class selectors**
`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice in the `@media` block. The second declaration silently overrides the first. No rendering breakage observed, but the CSS is inconsistent and the comment `/* MspaceTop was duplicated, kept one */` in the source suggests this was noticed but not fully resolved.

**Outlook MSO line-height override**
```css
td { line-height:0px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly }
```
This blanket reset inside the `[if gte mso 9]` conditional collapses all `td` line heights to 0 in Outlook 2013+. Individual cells must explicitly set `line-height` or content will stack. Standard pattern but fragile — any `td` without an explicit override will be zero-height.

**No issues found:** XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype, VML namespace declarations, and responsive breakpoint structure are all standard and correct.
