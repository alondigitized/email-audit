# Stay breezy 😎 Get 60% off Linen-Blend styles + 50% off everything* else
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- This is the second Old Navy email I've gotten TODAY. Earlier this morning it was the "$12 & under summer deals + 50% off everything" send, and now here comes a linen-focused riff with 60% off a specific category stacked on top of the same 50%-off-everything offer. My thumb was already hovering over archive before I finished reading the subject.
- That said — I'm not going to pretend the content is bad. The hero is clean, the TODAY ONLY urgency is prominent, and the "Shop Clearance in Your Size" section is genuinely useful. Old Navy's size breakdown grid is something Hanna Andersson would never do, and I actually appreciate seeing it here. But the linen-blend focus skews fully adult women, no kids pull whatsoever, which means Old Navy is treating me as a fashion shopper today rather than the family uniform buyer I actually am for them.
- I'd probably click — but not because this email earned it. I'd click out of habit and because 60% off linen pants is real money. I would not forward this. A friend who's more into Old Navy's adult women's line might find it useful, but this doesn't feel like a "text your group chat" email. Mark as read energy, maybe a grudging click.

## What's working

- **"TODAY ONLY!" lands immediately** — the urgency is in the hero, big and centered, before you have to scroll.
- **The linen hero is on-season** — wide-leg pants, breezy silhouette, summer vibes. It reads like an actual editorial moment, not a clearance fire sale.
- **"Shop Clearance in Your Size" is a genuinely useful section** — the size grid (XS through 4X+) with individual prices is more useful than anything Hanna Andersson puts in their promotional sends.
- **50% off everything* as the anchor offer is visible without scrolling** — dual-offer structure (60% on linen + 50% everything) gives the eye two places to land, and both are clear.
- **Models read adult women, approximately my age range** — not styling college students at me.

## What's weak

- **This is the second email today and I'm losing count** — after "GASP 😮" Saturday, "(yes, really!)" Saturday, the $6 tees sends, "Major news" Sunday, and a $12 & under email earlier this morning, each new send chips away at the urgency credibility. "TODAY ONLY!" means less every time they say it.
- **Zero kids content** — Old Navy is where I stock up on school-year basics and sale-season hauls for both kids. An email with no nod to boys' or girls' is a miss for how I actually use this brand.
- **"Dresses Everyone's Obsessing Over" is marketing speak, not social proof** — no star ratings, no review counts, nothing. Hanna Andersson at least earns their warmth; this feels like a headline that ran out of substantiation.
- **The asterisk on "everything\*" does real work here** — when you've sent five emails this week and every one has an asterisk, I start mentally discounting the headline number before I even read the fine print.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Consolidate the double-send into one email** — the $12 & under email and this linen email could have been one cohesive "Summer Haul" send. Two emails in one day for the same sale event is the fastest way to train subscribers to ignore both.
- 2. **Add one kids callout** — even a single "kids from $X" banner row would signal that Old Navy knows why I actually open their emails.
- 3. **Make "TODAY ONLY" appear in the subject line** — it's the strongest hook in the email and it's buried inside after you open. Put the clock on the subject where it competes with every other inbox message.
- 4. **Replace "Dresses Everyone's Obsessing Over" with actual social proof** — a star rating, a sold-count badge, anything observable. "Obsessing Over" is a claim; 4.7 stars from 3,200 reviews is evidence.
- 5. **Trim the subject line** — it's running long for mobile.
- **Subject Alt A:** `TODAY ONLY: 60% off linen + 50% off everything`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Linen-blend styles, 60% off — one day only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Wide-legs, sets & more — plus kids from $8`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The breezy summer pieces actually worth buying, all on sale today`

### 1. Technical Summary

One broken redirect link will silently fail for affected recipients, and three CAN-SPAM/RFC 8058 compliance gaps are present. Authentication header visibility is blocked by the AgentMail relay, leaving SPF/DKIM status unverifiable from captured headers.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken consent/preference link**

The Movable Ink personalization pixel URL resolves to an unknown URL type error:

```
https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/8a556507-40c1-4973-97e0-338324a2f717
  ?mi_mid=019e4209-d080-7000-9fb0-11e007f0a19f&cp_tp=v3.eJx9...
```

QA reports: `unknown url type: '/g/8a556507-40c1-4973-97e0-338324a2f717?txn=40142500-53a4-11f1-bd2a-4e61e507eb6e'`. This indicates the Movable Ink redirect is resolving to a malformed relative path (`/g/...`) instead of an absolute URL. Likely a mis-configured redirect rule in the MI content block, or a stale `txn` token that no longer maps to a valid destination.

**[WARN] 23 tracking links unverified**

All click-tracking links via `click.email.oldnavy.com` were skipped during HTTP probing. Final destination URLs and their health are unknown.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Tracking pixel missing `alt` text**

```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/8a556507-..." width="1" height="1">
```

No `alt=""` attribute present. Screen readers may announce the raw URL. Should be `alt=""` (empty string, not omitted).

**CSS duplicate selector conflict**

In the `@media (max-width: 639px)` block, `.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice with different `padding-top`/`padding-bottom` values (5px and 10px). The second declaration wins, silently discarding the first. Source comment acknowledges this: `/* MspaceTop was duplicated, kept one */`. Unclear if this is intentional or a template merge artifact — worth confirming against design spec.

**Outlook line-height override**

```css
/* Outlook 2013+ Height Fix */
td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }
```

Setting `line-height: 0px` on all `<td>` elements inside the MSO conditional block is a broad reset that can collapse text rows in Outlook if any `<td>` contains inline text without an explicit override. Standard practice is to apply this only to spacer rows.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `[FIRST_NAME]`) detected in the truncated source. The personalized content appears to be rendered server-side via Movable Ink before send — consistent with the `cp.oldnavy.com` pixel pattern. No issues found beyond the broken redirect documented in §2.

---

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**

CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email. Not present in the captured source. May exist below the HTML truncation point — must verify against full source.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**

Not captured by the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Yahoo Mail use this header to surface the native unsubscribe button. Its absence (even if present upstream) means recipients on those clients may not see the one-click unsubscribe UI, increasing spam complaints.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent**

RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policies (>5k/day). If Old Navy sends at that volume, this is a deliverability risk, not just a courtesy gap.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**No UTM parameters detected on destination URLs**

The 23 tracked links all route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`, which presumably appends UTM params on redirect. However, since those redirects were not probed, it cannot be confirmed that `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content` are being passed through to `oldnavy.com`. If the Movable Ink redirect chain that is already broken (§2) is part of the UTM passthrough path, attribution for that content block will be lost entirely.
