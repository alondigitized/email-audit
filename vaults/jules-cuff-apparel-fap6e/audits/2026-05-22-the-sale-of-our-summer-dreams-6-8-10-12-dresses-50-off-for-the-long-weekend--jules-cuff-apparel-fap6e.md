# The sale of our summer dreams! $6, $8, $10 & $12 dresses + 50% off for the long weekend
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- Okay, fifth Old Navy email in four days, and I actually *almost* didn't open it — then I saw the price ladder in the subject and my finger moved on its own. That's muscle memory, not desire. The hero delivers: MAJOR DRESS SALE with $6/$8/$10 anchoring is a real offer, and for someone like me who treats Old Navy dresses like a renewable resource, that lands. I grabbed my phone, I tapped.
- But here's where it falls apart: this email doesn't know when to stop. By the time I've scrolled past the MAJOR DRESS SALE hero, the 50% off tees-and-tanks block, the "HELLO, YELLOW!" hoodie moment, and the four-stacked-Clearances at the bottom, I've lost the thread. I came for dresses and got a department store circular. The HELLO, YELLOW! section is genuinely fun — it has a brand-voice beat that reminds me why Hanna Andersson's welcome email felt *curated* while this feels *dumped*. Hanna said one thing and said it well. Old Navy said six things and hoped one would stick.
- I'd click the hero dress CTA — honestly, I probably already did — but I wouldn't forward this. I'd feel embarrassed forwarding something this heavy. Mark it read the second I've clicked.

## What's working

- **The price ladder in the hero is genuinely arresting.** "$6, $8, $10" stacked visually does what the subject line promises — it earns the scroll.
- **MAJOR DRESS SALE hero placement is correct.** The eye lands there first, the woman in the white dress is clean and aspirational without being alienating, and the offer is legible at arm's length.
- **The 50% off mid-email section includes kids.** Active-lifestyle shots with children alongside adults signals this is a family send, which is exactly my buy mode at Old Navy.
- **"HELLO, YELLOW!" has actual personality.** It's the one section that feels like a creative decision rather than a discount alert. Yellow as a summer hero color, with that phrasing — I noticed it.
- **Current timing is solid.** Long weekend hook tied to summer dressing is appropriate and not manufactured.

## What's weak

- **This is the same email as yesterday.** The May 21 send led with "$6 DRESSES + 50% off for the long weekend" — today's is a subject-line remix of the same offer. That's not a campaign, that's a rebroadcast. The deal's the same, the urgency is the same, and if anything it tells me the long weekend discount was never going anywhere.
- **Four competing sale zones destroy focus.** Dresses → tees/tanks → yellow → clearance. I'm a family shopper with limited scroll budget. Pick one hero, let me buy.
- **The clearance section at the bottom is buried.** "Go, Go, Get Clearance" stacked four times in rainbow type should probably not be where I discover you have a clearance event. Clearance is often where I actually *go* at Old Navy — this deserved a dedicated email or a higher position.
- **No NavyRewards/member angle visible.** Hanna Andersson's welcome email surfaced my discount code in the first paragraph. Old Navy — where I *am* a rewards member — treats me like a stranger. No points balance, no member-only access, nothing.
- **The subject is 88 characters.** On my phone, it truncates well before "50% off for the long weekend." I'm reading "The sale of our summer dreams! $6, $8, $10 &..." and guessing the rest.

## Recommendations

- 1. **One hero. One offer. One CTA.** The dress sale deserved its own email. The clearance event deserved its own email. Stack them in one send and both get devalued. Old Navy could learn from how Hanna Andersson runs their promotions — single-focus sends, one clear thread.
- 2. **Lead with the specific price in the subject, not the aspiration.** "Summer dreams" is filler. The $6/$8/$10/$12 is the hook — swap them.
- 3. **Surface the clearance section above the fold** or cut it entirely from this email. Clearance shoppers (me, half the time) will abandon a long scroll if they don't know it's coming.
- 4. **Add a NavyRewards callout near the hero.** Even "Earn points on every dress" with my tier name would make this feel like they know me. Ulta does this on every promotional email — member tier visible top-right, points balance a tap away. Old Navy has the data; use it.
- 5. **HELLO, YELLOW! deserves its own dedicated send** — or at minimum the section needs a CTA that's more specific than a generic shop button. That editorial voice is an asset; don't bury it in block four.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$6, $8, $10 & $12 dresses — long weekend sale starts now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Dress sale: $6 to $12 + 50% off everything else`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stock up before Monday — prices go back up after the holiday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Grab your summer dresses before the long weekend ends.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS class duplication — `.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot`**
Both selectors are defined twice in the responsive block:
```css
.space-top5, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 5px;}
.space-top10, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 10px;}   /* duplicate */
.space-bot5, .MspaceBot  {padding-bottom: 5px;}
.space-bot10, .MspaceBot {padding-bottom: 10px;} /* duplicate */
```
The second definition wins in cascade; any element using `.MspaceTop` on mobile gets 10px top padding regardless of intent. This is a latent bug — low impact unless elements rely on the 5px variant.

**Outlook line-height override:**
The MSO conditional sets `td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }` globally. This is an intentional Outlook fix but aggressively zeroes all `td` line-heights in Outlook 2013+, requiring every content `td` to redeclare line-height inline. If any `td` is missing an inline override, text will collapse in Outlook desktop.

**Missing `alt` text on Movable Ink image:**
The `cp.oldnavy.com` content image has an empty/missing `alt` attribute per QA findings. Images-off clients (and screen readers) get no fallback description for that content block.

**No issues found** with the responsive breakpoint logic, VML fallbacks, or overall table structure.
