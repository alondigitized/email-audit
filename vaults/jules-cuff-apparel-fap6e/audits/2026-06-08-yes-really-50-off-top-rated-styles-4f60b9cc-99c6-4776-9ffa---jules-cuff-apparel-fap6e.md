# Yes, really: 50% off top-rated styles
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-08**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-four. I'm not even going to pretend I miscounted. This is the twenty-fourth Old Navy email in roughly two weeks, and the subject line — "Yes, really" — is doing something smart and a little desperate at the same time. It's Old Navy acknowledging that I've been trained to scroll past their big discount claims, so they're preemptively arguing with my skepticism before I've even opened. For a moment that actually worked on me, because 50% isn't nothing and the "2 DAYS ONLY!" banner hits you the second the email loads.
- But here's the thing: the creative is busy. Really busy. It's section after section of women's bright summer looks, a kids' section buried lower, some beach party theme I half-read, and then sandals. I don't hate any of it — Old Navy knows how to do loud summer palettes — but there's no single moment where I think "that's the thing I need to click on." It's a catalog disguised as a flash sale.
- I'd mark this read and maybe click through if I was already planning a summer wardrobe order. I would not forward it to a friend — not because it's bad, but because I've already sent three Old Navy sale links this week and everyone in my mom group is muted on that thread.

## What's working

- **"Yes, really" is a rare moment of brand self-awareness.** It's a disarming little line that actually cuts through because it doesn't oversell — it winks. After 23 previous emails, I appreciated the honesty.
- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" lands above the fold and means business.** The urgency is immediately credible and visible — no hunting for a deadline buried in fine print.
- **50% off is a real number, front and center.** Hanna Andersson's welcome email gave me a 20% code with a lot of brand love; this gives me an actual halved price on things I'd buy anyway. Different strokes.
- **Summer palette is punchy and cohesive.** The warm tones, the lifestyle imagery, the movement in the photos — it reads like summer and it renders clean.
- **Kids section does appear** further down, which matters to me since I'm buying for a whole household, not just myself.

## What's weak

- **"Top-rated styles" is marketing filler.** Which styles? Ranked by whom? This is the same vague language Hanna Andersson avoids by showing you exactly which stripe survives sixty washes. Give me a product name or a category I can act on.
- **No kids callout in the hero.** I'm a family uniform-shopper, not just a women's fashion buyer. The kids content is below the scroll and I almost missed it — that's a miss for someone like me.
- **Too many sections, no single hero product.** I counted at least four distinct content blocks before I got bored scrolling. There's nothing that made me stop and say "I need that specific item."
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** The twenty-fourth email in two weeks means my trust in "limited time" is at a low. The "yes, really" helps, but the cumulative effect is still wear.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a family or kids callout in the hero.** If 50% is storewide, say "the whole family" — that's the unlock for a shopper like me who's doing back-to-school math in her head even in June.
- 2. **Cut to three content blocks maximum.** Women's hero, kids, shoes. The beach party section, the layering section, the separate sandals block — pick two and cut the rest. Clarity over catalog.
- 3. **"Top-rated styles" needs receipts.** Show me a star rating or a number sold. Or just say "fan favorites" and lose the claim. Either way, give it some texture.
- 4. **The subject "Yes, really" is actually the strongest creative choice here — lean into it in the preheader too** rather than reverting to generic urgency copy.
- **Subject Alt A:** `"Yes, really: 50% off. 2 days, whole family."`
- **Subject Alt B:** `"Our most-loved styles, half off — 2 days only"`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `"Bestsellers in women's, kids, and shoes — all on sale through Sunday"`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `"Stock up before it's gone — the whole family qualifies"`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — tracking pixel**
`<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/ccab53c6...">` has no `alt` attribute at all (not even `alt=""`). While this is a 1×1 pixel, absent `alt` causes screen reader noise and fails strict accessibility validators. Should be `alt=""`.

**Duplicate CSS selector conflict**
In the `@media (max-width: 639px)` block, `.MspaceTop` is defined twice (bound to both `.space-top5` and `.space-top10`) and `.MspaceBot` likewise. The second declaration wins, silently discarding the 5px variant on mobile. Low visual impact but indicates a CSS maintenance issue.

**Outlook line-height override**
The MSO conditional block sets `td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }` globally for Outlook 2013+. This is a common Outlook hack but can collapse text cells if any `<td>` lacks an explicit `line-height` override. Should be scoped to layout-only cells, not applied globally.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (`{{`, `%%`, `*|`, `[%`) visible in the truncated HTML source. No issues found in the visible portion; recommend a full-source scan if the send tool doesn't enforce pre-flight token validation.

---

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
Not captured by the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header for native unsubscribe UI. If missing at the SMTP envelope level (not just relay capture), this violates Google/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (Feb 2024 mandate) and degrades inbox placement.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent**
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is required by Google/Yahoo for senders above 5,000/day. Absence means one-click unsubscribe via mail client UI will not function.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**
CAN-SPAM §7(a)(3) requires a valid physical postal address in the message body. The HTML source is truncated — verify the footer contains a street address before treating this as confirmed. If it is in a `display:none` element or image-only block, it does not satisfy the requirement.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters — unverifiable**
All 23 CTAs route through `click.email.oldnavy.com` redirects which were not probed. Cannot confirm UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) survive the redirect chain to the final Old Navy destination URLs. This is a measurement gap.

**Preference-center redirect is broken** (see §2 above) — users clicking any unsubscribe or preference link routed through `cp.oldnavy.com/o/ccab53c6...` will hit a dead end, creating a compliance exposure and a support burden.
