# $5 TANKS INSIDE + an extra 20% off everything*
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- Day two. Same tanks. Same yellow model. Same "Extra 20% Off Everything" banner. I opened yesterday's version — the one that led with "ATTN: $5 tanks + snag an extra 20% off everything else" — and I'm looking at the same body creative with a reshuffled subject line. Old Navy is running a legitimate 2-day window and I get why they send a reminder, but if you're going to show up in my inbox two days running with the same hero and the same section order, you need to at least rotate the lead image or add a "last chance tonight" module. Hanna Andersson's welcome email from May gave me a single focused offer on a single freshly styled page and I remembered it weeks later. This one is already blurring with yesterday's.
- The underlying math is genuinely good — $5 tanks, extra 20% off, clearance already marked to $3.99 before the stack — and I won't pretend that doesn't make me pause. I have two kids heading into back-to-school and I'm also a human who needs summer basics for myself. The tanks section and the Short Story shorts grid hit real categories for me personally. I probably would have clicked through this on day one.
- Today? Mark as read. I'm not forwarding it. If a friend asked me "is Old Navy running anything good right now?" I'd tell them verbally — I wouldn't share this email.

## What's working

- **Clearance inclusion is called out explicitly up top** — "Extra 20% Off Everything, Including Clearance" is a meaningful distinction and Old Navy puts it where I can read it without squinting
- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" in the hero does its job** — orange on white, bold, above-the-fold; the visual urgency treatment is cleaner than the all-caps subject approach from day one
- **Female model in the hero reads my demographic** — relaxed, summer styling, not a 22-year-old influencer or a 50-year-old in a white linen set; she looks like someone I'd walk past at Target
- **Price floors cascade clearly**: tanks $5, clearance items $3.99 — this email commits to the value positioning and doesn't bury the numbers
- **The Short Story shorts section adds legitimate variety** — denim, linen, tailored, with distinct model shots; it's not filler in the way some padding modules feel

## What's weak

- **Repeat creative on a 2-day promo is a trust erode** — if I'm paying attention to my inbox (and I am, I've been watching the polo-to-tanks pivot for four days), I notice; it signals autopilot, not curation
- **Kids/family content essentially disappears** — I came into this Old Navy promo cycle on the back of four days of "$4 uniform polo" emails and now there's almost no handoff to the family section; the audience who cared about that promo just got orphaned
- **The asterisk on "everything\*" never resolves in the visible copy** — I can see it in the header text and I can't see the exclusions; that asterisk is a small thing that creates disproportionate skepticism, especially for a brand that just ran a week of extremely credible low-price claims
- **The shorts section reads tacked on** — "Short Story" feels like a second email bolted to the bottom; neither section gives the other air

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a "Last Day" or "Ends Tonight" banner or countdown** — day-2 reminder emails live and die on explicit finality; "2 DAYS ONLY" is not the same as "today is day 2, here's your last window"
- 2. **Rotate the hero image** — a different tank color, a styled flat-lay, or a mother/daughter set shot would signal fresh send without rebuilding the whole email
- 3. **Drop one or two family/uniform callout tiles between tanks and shorts** — I'm in back-to-school planning mode and Hanna Andersson would never let me forget why I showed up; Old Navy is walking away from its own recent polos-for-kids story
- 4. **Resolve the exclusions in a single visible line of body copy** — "Excludes [category] — everything else is fair game" takes four words and kills the asterisk anxiety
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Last day: $5 tanks + 20% off (clearance too)`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Ends tonight — tanks $5, everything else 20% off`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Clearance drops to $3.99 after your extra 20%. Today only.`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Day 2 of 2 — stacks on clearance, no code needed.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken tracking pixel URL — `/g/` redirect returns unknown URL type**

The Content Passport pixel in the preheader div resolves to a `/g/` redirect that the checker cannot follow:

```
https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/9e64fab6-e70f-4427-96d8-865e90dd8a9f

  &cp_tp=v3.eJyFks...
  → Error: unknown url type: '/g/9e64fab6-...'
```

This is likely a Movable Ink / Content Passport pixel with a malformed or expired redirect chain. While it won't break visible links, it means impression/open data from this pixel may be silently dropped.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing `alt` text on tracking pixel**

```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/9e64fab6-..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
```

Empty `alt=""` is present, which is acceptable for decorative/tracking pixels per WCAG. However, the open-tracking pixel also has `alt=""`:

```html
<img src="https://click.email.oldnavy.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
```

Both are technically compliant but noted for completeness.

**CSS duplicate selectors** — `.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice in the responsive block (conflicting `padding-top: 5px` and `padding-top: 10px` for `.MspaceTop`). Last-rule wins in CSS, so mobile spacing will apply the 10px value regardless of intent for the 5px variant.

**MSO Outlook line-height conflict** — The Outlook conditional sets `td { line-height: 0px; }` globally, then tries to restore with `mso-line-height-rule: exactly` per-element. This is a known pattern but aggressive; any `td` without an explicit line-height override will collapse to 0px in Outlook 2013+.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not detected**

The QA relay did not capture a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Gmail and Apple Mail use this to surface one-click unsubscribe UI. Its absence (or relay-layer stripping) degrades inbox placement and risks the email being flagged under Gmail's bulk sender policies (required for senders >5k/day since Feb 2024).

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected**

Required for Gmail's native one-click unsubscribe button. Without it, users must click through to a landing page — Google treats this as non-compliant for high-volume senders.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected in truncated source**

CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) mandates a valid physical postal address. The source is truncated so this may appear in the footer — but the QA check flagged it as absent. Needs manual footer verification.

**[WARN] SPF/DKIM/DMARC authentication status unknown**

`Authentication-Results` header was not captured by the AgentMail relay. Cannot confirm SPF pass for `email.oldnavy.com` or DKIM signature validity. This is likely a relay capture issue, not an actual auth failure, but should be verified via a direct header inspection.
