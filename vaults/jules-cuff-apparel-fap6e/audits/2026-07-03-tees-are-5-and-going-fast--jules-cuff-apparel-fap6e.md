# Tees are $5 (and going fast!)
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- Day seven of what I am now fully calling the Old Navy Summer Grind, and here we are with a $5 tee email twenty-four hours after the $5 biker shorts email. I see what they're doing — running a "$5 thing of the day" series through the semi-annual window — and honestly, the first one landed because it felt fresh. This one feels like the formula showing its seams.
- That said: the hero delivers. A color-coded pricing ladder ($3 / $4 / $5 / $6) over a rainbow of tees in the first visible frame is exactly the kind of clear, no-nonsense sale communication I want from Old Navy during a stock-up window. I came here to fill a cart with basics for two kids and myself, and that top section earns the click. Then there's a TRENTECH module in the middle — an unexplained fashion thing that I scrolled past — and then stripes, and then a product grid that feels like the site map threw up. I got what I needed from the top quarter of this email. Everything below was friction.
- I'd click the tees. I would not forward this — yesterday's biker shorts email I might have texted to my sister, but this one is too muddled to recommend. Mark as read after clicking.

## What's working

- **The $3 / $4 / $5 / $6 pricing ladder in the hero is the right call.** Setting expectations across a range means I won't feel misled when something rings up at $6. Clean, honest, fast to read.
- **"3 DAYS ONLY!" lands because Old Navy actually enforces these windows.** I've been trained to believe the deadline. Hanna Andersson never runs this kind of urgency play, so when Old Navy does it, it still feels credible.
- **The visual rainbow of tees communicates variety without copy.** I can see there are options — I don't have to hunt for my kids' colors. That scan-ability is real value.
- **The stripes section is seasonally right.** July stripes make sense. And I notice Old Navy is leaning into stripes the same month Hanna Andersson basically invented their brand identity around them — the comparison doesn't hurt Old Navy at $5, but it does underscore why I pay $35 for the Hanna version when I want it to survive three hundred washes.

## What's weak

- **TRENTECH is a non-starter for me mid-scroll.** I have no idea what it is, it wasn't introduced, and dropping a tech-fabric story into a bargain-hunting email breaks my momentum completely. This is a new-arrivals email inside a sale email, and they don't belong together.
- **No explicit kids' callout in the hero.** I'm not buying one tee — I'm buying eight. If the $5 deal covers kids', say it plainly above the fold. If it doesn't, I'm going to feel burned when I click through and find out.
- **This is the second "$5 item" play in two days.** Yesterday was biker shorts; today is tees. The formula is showing. The first time is a tip from a friend; the second time is a content calendar.
- **The mid-email product grid reads like inventory overflow.** Unrelated items stacked below the stripes section — no editorial voice, no reason to browse. Hanna Andersson's welcome email had less SKU and more intention; this is the opposite.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Explicitly name kids' tees in the hero copy.** Something like "Adults from $5 · Kids from $3" would make this feel targeted at family shoppers instead of generic.
- 2. **Cut TRENTECH from this email entirely.** It belongs in a dedicated new-arrivals send to people who opted into fashion content. Don't bury the semi-annual with editorial.
- 3. **Trim the bottom product grid or give it an editorial frame.** "Summer essentials under $15" or "what to grab before the sale ends" — anything that explains why these items belong together.
- 4. **Break the "$5 thing of the day" pattern.** Give yesterday's win room to breathe, or vary the format so today's email doesn't feel like a sequel no one requested.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Kids' tees from $3 — 3 days left in the sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$3–$6 tees: stack 'em before they're gone`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Every color, kids' and adult sizes in the mix. 3 days only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock-up pricing starts at $3. See what's left.`
