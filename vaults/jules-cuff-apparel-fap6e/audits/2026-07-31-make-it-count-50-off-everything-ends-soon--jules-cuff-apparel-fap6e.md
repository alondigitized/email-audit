# Make it count! 50% OFF everything* ENDS SOON
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-31**
## Executive summary

- Another Tuesday, another purple starburst — this is the eighth-ish "50% off everything" send from Old Navy this week and I'm now able to recite the layout from memory: purple flat-lay hero, "Haul Into Fall," category grid, new arrivals, clearance strip. At this point my brain has started auto-skimming Old Navy subject lines the way it skims terms-and-conditions text.
- That said, this one does something the last few didn't — it breaks up the wall of purple with an actual "New In. Made For Fall." section showing real product on real models, and a clearance-from-$3.99 strip at the bottom that's genuinely eye-catching in that traffic-cone orange. So it's not a total copy-paste of Thursday's or Wednesday's email, it's just... 70% the same email with a fresh coat of new-arrivals paint.
- Would I click? Maybe, out of habit more than desire — I'd go straight to the "All Kids/Baby" category rows since that's the only thing I'm actually shopping for right now. Would I forward it to a friend? No. This is a "mark as read while waiting for my coffee order" email, not a "look what I found" email.

## What's working

- The category grid (Tees, Jeans, Activewear, Pants, Dresses, Sweatshirts & Hoodies) is scannable fast, which matters when I'm doing this one-thumbed at pickup line.
- Kids/Baby/Toddler nav row right under the hero — Old Navy at least remembers I shop multiple sizes in one house, unlike a lot of brands that only nav to Women's/Men's.
- The "New In. Made For Fall." section actually shows styled outfits on models, not just flat-lays — that's the first bit of "real person wearing this" signal I've seen from them all week.
- Free shipping banner up top on $50+ orders is at least a concrete, honest number, no fine-print games.
- Clearance-from-$3.99 block breaks the purple monotony and reads like a genuine deal, not a repackaged 50%-off claim.

## What's weak

- I have seen "50% OFF EVERYTHING" in this exact yellow-on-purple starburst so many times this week that it's lost all urgency — Hanna Andersson's welcome email (8/10 in my own book) earned its 20%-off code by pairing it with a real "why you're here" story; this is just volume.
- "ENDS SOON" in the subject with zero date or countdown timer anywhere in the body — I don't believe it, and neither does my inbox history, since it's "ended soon" about six times already this month.
- No kids-specific hero imagery until you scroll past two full purple screens — for a family email list, leading with adult flat-lays for that long is a miss when Hanna Andersson leads with kid-first, family-set framing almost immediately.
- The bottom clearance section is genuinely useful but it's buried under three separate "50% off everything" reinforcements — by the time I get there I'm fatigued.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop repeating "50% off everything" as the entire creative strategy — I've now seen this from Old Navy more times this week than I've seen my own kids' school emails. Rotate in a different lead (new arrivals, kids-specific bundle, clearance) as the hero at least every other send.
- 2. Move a kids/family visual into the hero block itself, not three scrolls down — I'm the parent buying uniforms in bulk, show me that early.
- 3. Add a real countdown or specific end date/time next to "ENDS SOON" — right now it reads like the boy who cried sale.
- 4. Lead clearance-from-$3.99 higher in the email — that's the actual scroll-stopper here, not the fifth "50% OFF" banner.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Kids' fall styles: 50% off, ends Sunday 11:59pm`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Clearance from $3.99 + 50% off fall arrivals`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New sweaters, boots & matching sets for the whole family — all half off`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock up before school starts: kids' 50% off + $3.99 clearance`
