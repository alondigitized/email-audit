# Summer closet refresh, unlocked: Check out Summer Steals from $5 + 50% off all licensed tees & hoodies*
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- Nineteen. I said that out loud when I saw this land in my inbox — nineteen Old Navy emails in roughly two weeks. I know my count because at this point it's a reflex. Before I even opened it I could sketch the layout: bright kid hero, large percentage, a few category blocks, Clearance at the bottom in triplicate. And I was right.
- That said, the email itself isn't bad. The hero is a young girl in summer brights, the "TODAY ONLY" badge is doing real urgency work, and the Summer Steals from $5 anchor is genuinely the kind of price that clears a dresser drawer when camp season is coming. The Kids & Baby Sale and activewear sections both touch live needs in our house. I'm the exact buyer Old Navy drew this for.
- But "unlocked" in the subject line is doing nothing. The 101-character subject is getting chopped in half on my phone, which means I'm seeing "Summer closet refresh, unlocked: Check out Summer Steals from…" and nothing else. Compare that to Hanna Andersson's "❤️ Welcome To Hanna!" — one clear signal, no run-ons. And honestly, after nineteen sends, a one-day gap in my inbox would do more for my click-through than any copywrite change. I'd mark this read without clicking today.

## What's working

- **"TODAY ONLY" badge is visible and legible in the hero** — it's the only hard deadline I've seen from Old Navy in several sends, and it makes the 50% off licensed tees feel like an actual window, not ambient noise.
- **Hero model is a young girl in summer brights** — immediately reads as a family email, not just general adult Old Navy; that demographic signal is the fastest filter for someone like me.
- **"From $5" price anchor is real** — school-bag-change territory. It says "entry point" rather than just "percent off" which is fresher than yesterday's "40% off everything on orders $75+."
- **Activewear + Kids & Baby Sale dual-section** covers two distinct household use cases in one scroll; efficient for a busy family buyer.
- **Clean render, no broken images or layout disasters** — more reliable on mobile than several competitor sends I've seen recently.

## What's weak

- **The subject is three emails in one line** — "unlocked" + "from $5" + "50% off licensed tees & hoodies" is competing with itself, and at 101 characters it truncates before the second offer on any standard mobile inbox.
- **Nineteen emails in two weeks** — the TODAY ONLY badge would carry weight if I hadn't gotten a functionally identical offer yesterday and the day before; the urgency is technically present but emotionally spent.
- **Triple Clearance blocks at the bottom** undercut the "Summer closet refresh, unlocked" aspirational frame from the subject — it reads less like a curated summer launch and more like end-of-aisle bin liquidation.
- **No kid sizing range visible** — a single "sizes 2–14" or "Baby through Big Kid" callout anywhere in the hero would tell me immediately whether it's worth clicking into for our specific age spread.

## Recommendations

- 1. **One offer per email, full stop.** The 50% off licensed tees OR the Steals from $5 — not both. Hanna Andersson's welcome email landed an 8 with a single 20% code and one CTA. Old Navy is splitting its own attention every send.
- 2. **Add a sizing range or age callout to the hero.** "Sizes 2–14 in stock" or "Baby through Big Kid, from $5" gives the family buyer the filter she needs before clicking.
- 3. **Rename the Clearance triple-block.** "Last chance summer picks" or "End-of-season finds" preserves the inventory-clearing intent without making the bottom third of the email look like a garage sale.
- 4. **Rest the inbox for one day.** The single highest-ROI move Old Navy could make this week is skipping tomorrow's send entirely. The TODAY ONLY badge would actually land.
- 5. **Tighten the subject to one punchy line under 50 characters.**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Today only: 50% off licensed tees + picks from $5`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Summer tee run — $5 and up, today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Camp shirts, graphic tees, play sets — stocked for under $10.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sizes 2–14, limited time. Don't wait on this one.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect — opens pixel / cp.oldnavy.com**

The Movable Ink personalization pixel at `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/333b7969...` is returning an internal redirect to a URL of type `unknown url type: '/g/333b7969...'`. This indicates the Movable Ink content block is resolving to a relative path instead of an absolute URL — the redirect chain breaks before reaching the destination page.

- Evidence: QA probe returned `unknown url type: '/g/333b7969-0f8f-46c9-bb21-ff722543d82c?txn=d4aa8580-5f55-11f1-9ec9-0690aac23d16'`
- Impact: Any click routed through this block will likely 404 or loop.
- Root cause: Movable Ink block is probably misconfigured — the `cp_tp` payload encodes a relative `/g/` path rather than an absolute `https://` destination.

**23 tracking links skipped** — all route through `click.email.oldnavy.com` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud). Not an error, but none were probed; destination health is unverified by this audit.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility


The `alt=""` is present but empty. If this block renders a product or promotional offer, a screen reader will silently skip it — the offer is invisible to assistive technology users.

**Open pixel** rendered in the visible DOM with no `alt`:


Empty `alt=""` is technically correct for a tracking pixel (decorative), so no issue here.

**Duplicate CSS class selectors** in the mobile media query block — `.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice with different values. The second definition wins; the first is dead code. Low severity but indicates a copy-paste error in the template.

**Outlook line-height override** in the MSO conditional comment sets `td { line-height: 0px }` globally. This is a standard Outlook fix, but it can collapse single-line cells that rely on inherited line-height rather than explicit values. Acceptable if tested; flag if any Outlook rendering regressions appear.
