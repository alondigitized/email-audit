# Deals you don't want to miss: $15 & under styles
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- We are on day twelve-ish of the Old Navy Summer Grind and I genuinely could not tell you whether this is a new email or the one from yesterday. "Deals you don't want to miss: $15 & under styles" versus July 7th's "Fill that cart with $15 & under styles" — same price point, same adult-women fashion grid, same energy. The "2 DAYS ONLY!" banner in the hero is doing the heaviest lifting here, and I respect that it creates real urgency, but the goodwill burned by sending me nearly the same creative two days in a row is significant.
- What I notice is that the email is stacked — four or five content blocks deep — moving from the $15 hero down through a "$3.99" clearance pitch, a "CLEARANCE IN YOUR SIZE" callout, a "HOT NEW GEAR" athletic section, and eventually something that looks like "TIME TO CHILL" further down where I might finally see kids. For me, a Millennial mom who shops Old Navy specifically to keep four-drawer dressers stocked on a budget, the kids content should not be buried below three adult fashion blocks. July 6th's $6 kids uniform shorts email actually stopped me mid-scroll. This one reads like Old Navy forgot who texted them back.
- I'd mark this as read. I wouldn't forward it. If the "CLEARANCE IN YOUR SIZE" section links to something real, maybe — but only if I'm already in the mood.

## What's working

- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" is prominent and credible.** The urgency banner sits right in the hero, doesn't feel manufactured, and gives me an actual reason to act today rather than bookmark-and-forget.
- **$15 & under is the right price anchor for Old Navy.** This is the number I use to justify Old Navy over Hanna Andersson — seeing it big in the hero confirms the brand knows its lane.
- **"CLEARANCE IN YOUR SIZE" is a smart hook.** That copy is doing something Hanna's welcome email didn't bother with — speaking to practical scarcity, not aspirational styling. A busy mom hears "your size" and leans in.
- **Layout is clean.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem-ipsum surprises. The email renders correctly and the visual hierarchy flows hero → deal → secondary deals. Nothing embarrassing.
- **Summer palette feels current.** Bright yellows, blues, greens — this doesn't feel like stale inventory from March.

## What's weak

- **This is basically yesterday's email with a new subject line.** The July 7th send was already a $15-and-under adult women's grid. Sending the same creative concept two days later at the same price point trains me to wait — if today is the same as yesterday, I'll just open it tomorrow.
- **Kids content is buried.** For a mom who buys Old Navy for kids' volume, pushing a wall of adult women's fashion first and surfacing kids items somewhere around block four is a targeting miss. The July 6th email knew why I'm here; this one forgot.
- **No specific product hero.** There are many items shown but no single one they're betting on. Hanna Andersson built their welcome email around one hero product image that felt curated. This reads like a category dump.
- **The subject is 49 characters of mild desperation.** "Deals you don't want to miss" is the email equivalent of a guilt trip. It says nothing about what the deals are beyond what I already know from yesterday.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with one hero product — a uniform or kids essential — not a fashion grid.** The July 6th email proved I'll open for $6 uniform shorts. Make the $15-under umbrella work harder by anchoring it to a specific item that speaks to back-to-school parents. That single swap would separate this from yesterday.
- 2. **Put kids items in block one or two, not block four+.** Segment the send — parents who click kids should get the kids-first version. This isn't complicated and would lift CTR for a sizable portion of the list.
- 3. **Retire "Deals you don't want to miss" as subject-line copy.** It's filler. The urgency is "2 DAYS ONLY" — lean into that.
- **Subject Alt A:** `2 days left: $15 & under (including kids)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$15 & under ends Thursday — here's what to grab`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Clearance in your size + $3.99 picks for the whole family`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock up before this drops — $15 & under for 2 more days`
- 4. **Add a "family set" callout block.** One row showing a matching adult + kid outfit at combined-under-$30 would make me feel seen as a family shopper, not just a women's apparel target.
- 5. **Explicitly surface the 2-day countdown in the subject.** The urgency is in the body but invisible from the inbox. Move it forward.
