# $6 TEES & TANKS (Yes, $6!)
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-eight. I'm fairly certain that's where I am now in my running tally of Old Navy emails, and this one is doing exactly what the last three or four did: leading with the same $4 and $6 hero, same tees-and-tanks angle, wrapped in a slightly different parenthetical. "(Yes, $6!)" is doing a lot of lifting as a differentiator, and I don't think it gets there. That said — the email itself isn't *bad*. The "2 DAYS ONLY" banner up top creates genuine urgency, the price hierarchy is clean, and the "HOT NEW GEAR" and Clearance sections give me actual shelf variety to browse instead of one relentless single message. There's visual movement here that holds longer than the prior tees-blasts.
- I would not click today. I stocked up two emails ago when the $4/$6 offer landed fresh. At this point I'm reading these the way I skim sale flyers that pile up at the door — I'll flip through it once, confirm I'm not missing a new price break, and put it down. I would not forward this to a friend because she's already gotten the same email.

## What's working

- **"2 DAYS ONLY" urgency is legible from the first scroll** — the countdown framing is more credible than a floating "limited time" badge, and it sits high enough that I catch it before I've committed to reading anything.
- **$4 and $6 are visible heroes** — Old Navy lets the number do the work, no asterisk chase required, and for a volume shopper like me that's exactly right.
- **Multi-section layout breaks the monotony** — tees/tanks → HOT NEW GEAR → Clearance gives me a reason to scroll; the prior $6 blasts were one-trick ponies by comparison.
- **Female models in casual summer silhouettes** — matches my age bracket and the kind of "wear on repeat" stuff I'm actually buying right now.
- **Clearance anchor at the bottom** — I always scroll for that, and it's there.

## What's weak

- **The subject parenthetical "(Yes, $6!)" assumes I forgot what I read two days ago** — I didn't. It reads like Old Navy arguing with itself.
- **No family or kids signal anywhere visible** — I'm buying this stuff for my kids too, and neither the hero copy nor the HOT NEW GEAR section gives me any reason to think kids' sizes are part of this deal. Hanna Andersson's welcome email made me feel like they *knew* I had kids; this email does not.
- **Four $4/$6 tees emails inside a week is brand damage, full stop** — each one erodes the urgency of the next. "2 DAYS ONLY" means nothing when I know a new "2 DAYS ONLY" is already drafting.
- **HOT NEW GEAR feels like a tone mismatch** — I came for the deal, not a trend pitch. The section heading signals a premium editorial beat that the $6 price point undercuts.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rotate the offer anchor.** If the $4/$6 price is staying, the email has to give me *something else* to click on — bundle math ("stock up: 5 for $25"), a kids' callout, a color exclusive. The structure already exists; just make one of those three sections do different work than "more tees."
- 2. **Add a kids' or family callout explicitly.** A line like "Kids tees from $6 too" in the hero or HOT NEW GEAR section would have me clicking immediately instead of scrolling past.
- 3. **Kill the parenthetical in the subject.** It signals that Old Navy knows the offer isn't landing and is trying to goose it, which makes it worse, not better.
- 4. **Dial back cadence before the next send.** One $4/$6 email lands. Two is acceptable. Four is churn. The click data almost certainly shows diminishing returns — the creative team probably knows it too.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Stock up: $4 tees, $6 tanks — 2 days left`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Kids + adults: $4 & $6 tees end tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Every color. Every size. No reason to wait.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Grab 5, spend $25. Clearance stacked on top.`

## Technical Audit — Old Navy "$6 TEES & TANKS"

### 1. Technical Summary
One confirmed broken redirect was caught by automated QA (25% pass rate), alongside three compliance gaps and a deliverability risk from a URL-heavy plain-text part. The HTML structure is standard ESP-era XHTML with no catastrophic rendering issues, but several items need attention before resend.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect on preference-center/suppression pixel**
QA resolved `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/79b088db-e882-4f61-8e87-40a99d437327?mi_mid=019ec525...` and received a relative-path redirect target: `/g/79b088db-e882-4f61-8e87-40a99d437327?txn=6b3a7480-6a35-11f1-902c-3efb318f62ec`. A relative redirect from a tracking/suppression endpoint is malformed — the redirect location header must be an absolute URL. If this is the unsubscribe or preference-center endpoint, broken navigation here is a CAN-SPAM risk and a subscriber trust issue.

**[INFO] 23 click links skipped**
All content links route through `click.email.oldnavy.com` (ESP redirect domain). QA could not probe final destinations. UTM parameter coverage and landing page integrity are unverified — see §6.

**[INFO] Dual open-pixel setup**
Two 1×1 tracking pixels fire on open: `click.email.oldnavy.com/open.aspx?...` and `cp.oldnavy.com/o/...`. Double-counting opens is expected if both are intentional, but the second pixel is the same URL as the broken redirect above, meaning the pixel itself may also fail to log correctly.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate CSS class bindings (minor)**
In the `@media (max-width: 639px)` block, `.MspaceTop` is bound twice (to `.space-top5` and `.space-top10`) and `.MspaceBot` is bound twice (`.space-bot5`, `.space-bot10`). Last-rule wins — `.space-top5` and `.space-bot5` will be silently overridden on mobile. Low visual impact but indicates copy-paste drift.

**Outlook `td { line-height: 0px }` override**
The MSO conditional block sets `td { line-height:0px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly }` globally. This is a known Outlook spacer-image workaround but will suppress line-height on all `<td>` text unless explicitly reset per cell. Any `<td>` containing live text without an inline `line-height` override will collapse in Outlook 2013+.

**Missing alt text on suppression pixel**
`<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/79b088db-e882-4f61-8e87-40a99d437327?...">` has `alt=""`. For a 1×1 tracking pixel this is technically acceptable (empty alt avoids screen-reader noise), but the QA flag is valid — verify this is intentional and not a content image that lost its alt value.

**XHTML 1.0 Transitional DOCTYPE**
Legacy but correct for broad email client support. No action needed.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post headers not confirmed**
AgentMail relay did not surface these headers. Gmail and Apple Mail use `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) to render the one-click unsubscribe button in the UI. If the sending ESP injects these at SMTP time, they may simply not have been captured by the relay — but absence should be verified against raw SMTP headers, not assumed present. Google and Yahoo bulk sender requirements (2024) mandate one-click unsubscribe.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected in body**
CAN-SPAM §7 requires a valid physical postal address in the email body. It was not detected in the visible HTML. This is a required element — confirm it exists in the truncated footer section.

**[WARN] Authentication-Results header not captured**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status is unknown. `oldnavy.com` is a high-volume sender with an established DMARC policy; authentication failures here would be anomalous but high-impact. Verify raw headers show `dkim=pass` and `spf=pass`.
