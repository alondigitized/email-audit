# SO MANY DEALS⚡60% off ALL swim* and 50% off all men's styles*
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-31**
## Executive summary

- Fifteen. I counted. This is Old Navy's fifteenth email in roughly two weeks, and I say that not as a complaint but as a statement of fact — at this point counting is a reflex. The subject leads with "SO MANY DEALS" in all caps and a lightning bolt, which is exactly the energy of someone announcing a yard sale on a highway sign. It does not make me feel like a known customer. It makes me feel like a recipient.
- What changed my mind once I was inside: "2 DAYS ONLY!" is an actual deadline, and there's a Kids & Baby Sale section in the lower half that is the reason I have Old Navy in my life at all. That section — "Practically Priced For You" — is the message. But I have to scroll past a men's tie shorts feature to get to it, which is backwards. My husband's inbox is right next to mine and he can manage his own promotions.
- I wouldn't forward this. I'd scroll to kids, click if the asterisks don't eat the savings at checkout, and move on. Mark as read, not as a keeper.

## What's working

- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" is real urgency** — unlike yesterday's "Splash into 60% off swim," which ran a deal with no hard deadline, this one actually draws a line.
- **The Kids & Baby Sale section shows up** — "Practically Priced For You" is exactly the right framing for a family volume shopper; it's just in the wrong position.
- **60% off swim is a genuine escalation** — after two weeks of 50% off everything, bumping to 60% on a category signals that this is actually a better deal, not just more noise.
- **Female model in the hero is seasonally clean** — bright summer palette, looks like real Old Navy styling rather than stock photography.
- **Visual hierarchy is readable at a glance** — 60% and the deadline banner are legible before I even focus in, which Hanna Andersson doesn't even try to do in their promotional sends (they trust the brand to do the heavy lifting instead).

## What's weak

- **Men's styles are half the subject line and irrelevant to me** — this email is running two campaigns in one and serving neither audience completely. Hanna Andersson would never dilute a kids-sale hero with an adult crossover lead; Old Navy buried the family offer under a couples photo.
- **"SO MANY DEALS" is clearance-bin shouting** — Hanna Andersson opened their welcome email with warmth and one clean offer; this opens with all-caps hype that reads spam-adjacent in the inbox before I even click.
- **"ALL swim\*" with an asterisk is not all** — I've been trained by fourteen previous emails to assume the exclusions are significant, and that erodes whatever goodwill the 60% number earned.
- **Kids & Baby Sale is below the fold** — that is the section a family shopper opens Old Navy for; tie shorts for a man should not appear between me and that content.
- **Cadence has broken trust** — fifteen emails in two weeks means the urgency on any individual send feels manufactured. I genuinely cannot tell if "2 DAYS ONLY" is real or if the same deal shows up Monday with different subject copy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Promote Kids & Baby Sale to the hero** — put 60% off swim as a secondary module; family pricing is why I stay subscribed.
- 2. **Drop "SO MANY DEALS"** — the 60% number does the work; the all-caps preamble just costs credibility. Start with the number.
- 3. **Explain the asterisk in one inline line above the fold** — "excludes [X]" reads as honest; discovering the exclusion at checkout reads as bait-and-switch.
- 4. **Batch men's and swim into a single secondary send** — if kids/baby is the hero, consolidate the adult deals so there's a reason to have two separate emails this week.
- 5. **Slow the cadence** — one fewer email this week would make the "2 DAYS ONLY" feel credible again.
- **Subject Alt A:** `2 days only: 60% off swim for the whole family`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Kids & baby sale + 60% off swim — ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stock up before summer break. Plus: 50% off men's styles.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Kids' swim, tees, and bottoms — priced for the whole season.`

## Technical Audit — Old Navy "SO MANY DEALS" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

XHTML 1.0 Transitional table-based layout with responsive CSS at a 639px breakpoint and 23 click-redirect tracking links through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. One confirmed broken redirect and several compliance/deliverability gaps detected.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken personalization redirect**
`https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/4d9fbd21-ff82-4e5d-a924-1c5a2127acb5?mi_mid=...`
The redirect resolves to a relative path: `/g/4d9fbd21-ff82-4e5d-a924-1c5a2127acb5?txn=90627f80-5ce0-11f1-8c84-8e0ec7ba23ea`
A relative redirect target is invalid at the HTTP level — this is almost certainly a broken personalized product recommendation link. Recipients clicking it will hit an error or incorrect destination.

**[WARN] 23 tracking links unverifiable**
All CTAs route through `click.email.oldnavy.com` redirect chain. Destination URL integrity and UTM parameter presence cannot be confirmed without resolving the redirect chain.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Duplicate CSS class assignments — mobile padding conflict**
In the `@media (max-width: 639px)` block, `.MspaceTop` is bound to two rules:
```css
.space-top5, .MspaceTop { padding-top: 5px; }
.space-top10, .MspaceTop { padding-top: 10px; }
```
Same for `.MspaceBot`. Last-declaration wins — any element using `.MspaceTop` alone will always render at 10px, ignoring the 5px intent. The 5px variants are effectively dead.

**[WARN] Outlook MSO global line-height zeroing**
The MSO conditional block sets:
```css
td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }
```
This is applied globally to all `<td>` elements in Outlook 2013+. Overly broad — risks collapsing any table cell that doesn't have an explicit inline `line-height` override.

**[WARN] Missing alt text on content image**
The `cp.oldnavy.com` personalized content image has no `alt` attribute. In image-blocking email clients, this renders as a blank space with no fallback text.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens detected in the visible HTML. Personalization parameters (`mi_mid`, `mi_cid`, `cp_cid`) in the `cp.oldnavy.com` URL appear to contain resolved UUIDs, not placeholder syntax. The issue is the broken redirect target, not unresolved tokens.

---

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post headers absent**
Both headers are required for bulk senders under Gmail and Yahoo Mail's 2024 sender requirements (RFC 2369 / RFC 8058). Missing `List-Unsubscribe-Post` specifically means one-click unsubscribe is not supported. Headers may be stripped by the AgentMail relay — confirm at the envelope level.

**[WARN] Physical mailing address unverifiable**
QA found no physical address in the analyzed content. CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid postal address in every commercial message. The HTML is truncated — verify the footer is present and contains a street address before clearing this finding.

**[WARN] Authentication-Results header absent**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed through the AgentMail relay. Sending domain is `email.oldnavy.com` — authentication should be verified at the MTA before treating as clear.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**[FAIL] Broken personalization CTA** — the `cp.oldnavy.com` redirect failure (see §2) means at least one product recommendation block will not deliver users to any valid page.

**[WARN] UTM coverage unconfirmed** — all 23 CTAs use `click.email.oldnavy.com` redirect chains that were not probed. It is unknown whether the final destination URLs carry `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` parameters consistent with the campaign.

**[INFO] Plain-text body is 90% URLs** — 18,633 of 20,790 plain-text characters are URLs, which is a likely result of auto-generated plain text from tracking-link-heavy HTML. This degrades spam filter scoring and makes the plain-text version unreadable.
