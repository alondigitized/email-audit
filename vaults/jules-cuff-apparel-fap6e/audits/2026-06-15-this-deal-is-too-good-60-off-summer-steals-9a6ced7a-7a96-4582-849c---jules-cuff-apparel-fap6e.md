# This deal is TOO good — 60% OFF Summer Steals
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-nine. Or thirty. I've genuinely stopped counting, and that is still Old Navy's biggest problem — but I'll give them this: "60% off" broke through the $6-tee fog in a way "going FAST" never could. When I saw that number in the subject I didn't delete immediately, which is more than I can say for the last three sends. The email itself is busy — a long scroll of summer dresses, active wear, and a clearance kicker at the bottom — and it leans hard into adult women's fashion. Which, sure, that's me too, but I opened this thing as the family uniform-buyer, and the kids section is nowhere near the hero.
- Would I click? Maybe — if I can see that 60% applies to the actual items in my cart (kids basics, a few women's pieces). But the fine-print anxiety is real. Old Navy's "60% off Summer Steals" historically means "60% off this curated selection that includes exactly two things you wanted." That makes me hesitate. The urgency (2 days only) is credible, the offer is loud, the aesthetic is summery and warm. But this is the brand's fourth-plus blast in three days and I'm running on fumes.
- Mark as read with a side-eye, not delete. I'll probably click if I'm shopping anyway — but I'm not forwarding it.

## What's working

- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" + "60% OFF" combo lands immediately** — the hero hierarchy actually works here; eye goes straight to the discount number before anything else.
- **Summer color palette is on-brief** — warm corals, pinks, and yellows read as a cohesive campaign rather than a clipart dump.
- **Multi-category stacking** — I can see women's dresses, active/tech wear, and clearance all in one scroll, which matches how I actually shop Old Navy (grab-and-go across categories).
- **Clearance call-out at the bottom** — I always scroll for clearance. Labeling it clearly is the right call.
- **Credible urgency** — "2 days only" is a real deadline I believe, unlike the vague "limited time" language that's been all over their recent $6-tee run.

## What's weak

- **Kids is buried or missing** — I'm Old Navy's most reliable customer specifically because I buy kids basics in bulk, and this email is almost entirely adult women. Where's the "kids 60% off too" callout? That's the thing that would've made me click immediately.
- **Cadence exhaustion is real** — coming off four $6-tees-and-tanks blasts in 48 hours, this feels like the brand is just cycling to the next promo rather than giving me a reason to care.
- **"Summer Steals" feels like generic campaign language** — Hanna Andersson's welcome email called out specific product details; this is just a category label. It doesn't tell me if the linen pants I've been eyeing are in the sale.
- **The email is very long** — by the time I reach clearance I've scrolled past six or seven distinct sections, and each one has its own CTA. Too many asks dilutes the one I should be clicking.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with kids in the hero or add a dedicated kids callout in the first scroll** — this is the single biggest lift. "60% off kids + adults" in the subhead would've made this a click-not-maybe. Old Navy's strongest hold on me is kids volume pricing; don't make me hunt for it.
- 2. **Cut two body sections** — women's dresses → active → clearance is plenty. The "New Tech Tech" (or whatever that section is) and any additional mid-funnel break can go. One less decision, cleaner close.
- 3. **Tighten the CTA copy** — generic "Shop Now" buttons after every section train me to ignore them. "Shop 60% Off Dresses" or "Shop Kids Clearance" — verbs plus nouns, like Hanna does.
- 4. **Acknowledge the cadence in the email itself** — a single line like "We've been in your inbox a lot this week — this one's worth it" would disarm the eye-roll and feel human.
- **Subject Alt A:** `60% off sitewide — 2 days, then it's gone`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Kids + adults: 60% off Summer Steals ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dresses, basics, and kids styles — all included`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The sale you've been waiting for actually landed`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Duplicate CSS class selectors in mobile media query**

`.MspaceTop` is defined twice — once under `.space-top5` and again under `.space-top10`. Same for `.MspaceBot`:

```css
.space-top5, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 5px;}   /* overridden */
.space-top10, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 10px;}  /* wins */
.space-bot5, .MspaceBot {padding-bottom: 5px;} /* overridden */
.space-bot10, .MspaceBot {padding-bottom: 10px;} /* wins */
```

Any element using `MspaceTop` or `MspaceBot` alone will always resolve to `10px` regardless of intent. Elements that need 5px mobile spacing are broken.

The `cp.oldnavy.com` pixel has no `alt` attribute at all (distinct from the `click.email.oldnavy.com` open pixel which correctly carries `alt=""`). Screen readers will attempt to announce the URL string.
