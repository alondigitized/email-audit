# Workout drawer: refreshed ✨ Snag 50% OFF all active*
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- Twenty. Out loud. That's the number I said when this landed — twenty Old Navy emails in roughly two weeks, and this one is selling me activewear. Again. Yesterday's email was literally "HALF OFF all activewear." Today I've got "50% OFF all active" with a sparkle emoji and a different model. Same discount, different crop top.
- To be fair, I did open it, because the "2 DAYS ONLY" urgency tugged at my reflexes and I genuinely can't tell if Old Navy is actually running a different promotion or just reskinning the same one. The hero looks sharp — big bold red type, clean photography of adult women in athletic gear, and a rewards callout that at least acknowledges I'm a returning customer. But this email isn't talking to the version of me who uses Old Navy. I'm here for 14-packs of socks and school uniform khakis, not sports bras. The whole email is adult activewear, top to bottom, with a clearance dump at the bottom that feels tacked on.
- I'd mark it read. I wouldn't click through, and I absolutely wouldn't forward it — Hanna Andersson's welcome email last month earned a screenshot to a friend because it felt like something I *discovered*. This feels like the sixth time Old Navy has tried to get me to refresh my workout drawer this month. Pass.

## What's working

- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" in the hero is doing real work** — big red text, impossible to miss above the fold, and it creates a believable deadline instead of the fake "today only" language other brands abuse.
- **50% off is concrete** — no math required, no "up to" weaseling in the hero itself, which I appreciate after years of Old Navy burying the real terms.
- **The $70 Rewards Bonus callout** is a genuine loyalty signal; Hanna Andersson would never do that for me (different price tier), but it at least makes me feel like a known customer rather than a cold lead.
- **Photography is clean and on-brand** — the pink/coral palette, fit women in motion, no awkward stock imagery. The "HOT NEW GEAR" section with the teal background breaks up the scroll nicely.
- **No broken images or layout issues** — this rendered cleanly, which puts it ahead of some of the midweek sends where images have loaded out of order.

## What's weak

- **Yesterday's email was the same promotion** — "HALF OFF all activewear" on June 4, "50% OFF all active" today. Even if these are technically different discounts or different assortments, it reads as one long ad with a daily refresh, and my trust in the urgency evaporates.
- **Zero kids content, zero family signal** — the entire email is adult activewear. Old Navy knows I'm a mom who bulk-buys school staples, but nothing here speaks to me in that role. Hanna's welcome email put family front and center; this could be from any athletic brand.
- **The clearance pile at the bottom feels like an afterthought** — "Go, Go, Go! Clearance Clearance Clearance" repeated three times in giant stacked type is visually aggressive and tonally inconsistent with the polished activewear creative above it.
- **No size or fit language anywhere I can see** — for activewear, I need to know if these leggings run small, whether they come in tall, whether this sports bra actually has support. The images show one body type. Old Navy's size range is genuinely a selling point and this email doesn't use it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment me differently** — I should not be getting the activewear send on the same cadence as someone who actually shops the active category. Even a minor kids-uniform callout in the footer would make this feel relevant. Gap does a slightly better job of acknowledging that adults and families co-exist in their customer base.
- 2. **Kill the clearance stack** — Either give clearance its own dedicated email (and actually make me feel like I'm getting access to something early) or cut it entirely from the activewear send. The tonal whiplash from athletic lifestyle photography to all-caps clearance typography is jarring.
- 3. **Add one fit or sizing signal in the hero** — "Built for every rep — sizes XS–4X" or a callout like "High-waist, high-support" costs nothing and reassures the activewear shopper who's on the fence.
- 4. **Merge or rotate these sends** — Running near-identical activewear promos on back-to-back days burns subscriber attention and trains me to wait out the cycle. Even a 48-hour gap with a different category in between would reset my urgency response.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your workout drawer is calling. 50% off, 48 hrs.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Leggings, sports bras, shorts — all half off through Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `High-waist, high-support, half the price. Shop before it's gone.`
