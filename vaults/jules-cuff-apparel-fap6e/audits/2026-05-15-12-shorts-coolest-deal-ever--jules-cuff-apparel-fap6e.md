# $12 shorts? Coolest deal EVER
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- Okay, $12 shorts dropping in my inbox the day before a long weekend? That's a good swing. "TODAY ONLY!" in the hero is doing exactly what it's supposed to — it made me stop and actually do the mental math on whether I need another pair of drawstring shorts. (I always do. It's Old Navy. They're $12.) The hero is earning its subject line.
- But then I kept scrolling. And scrolling. There's the $12 shorts hero, then a "40% off when you spend $50" badge-burst, then a product grid, then a 50% Off Deals block, then "Dresses Everyone's Obsessing Over," then a full clearance wall that literally says "Clearance Clearance Clearance" three times. That's five shopping moments competing in one email, and I'm already thinking about what to make for dinner. Yesterday's crinkle gauze send was tight — one offer, one reason to click. This one feels like somebody opened the warehouse door and said *go.*
- I'd click the hero shorts deal. I would not forward this to anyone. Everything below the fold got a fast scroll and a "maybe later" that means never.

## What's working

- "TODAY ONLY!" is a real commitment — not "limited time," not "while supplies last." A clock-on-today urgency makes me weigh it right now instead of deferring.
- $12 is a specific, believable Old Navy price. It doesn't smell like bait-and-switch — this is exactly what their shorts cost on a good day.
- The bright, warm summer palette is dead-on for mid-May. It reads as current, not recycled.
- The repeated "Clearance Clearance Clearance" block at the bottom is almost charming in its bluntness — zero pretense, just come get the stuff.
- The hero model energy is right for the audience: casual, warm-weather, doesn't feel like it's trying too hard.

## What's weak

- Five competing offers destroy the hierarchy. The "Extra 40% Off When You Spend $50+" section directly under the hero immediately makes me recalculate — am I shopping $12 shorts or am I building toward a $50 cart? That's two incompatible mindsets in the same scroll.
- "Dresses Everyone's Obsessing Over" is a completely separate email that hitchhiked onto this one. It has nothing to do with the subject line and lands with zero connection to why I opened.
- Four Old Navy emails in five days. I loved the crinkle gauze send on the 14th, I was charmed by the Disney collab on the 13th, but we're entering "hover over unsubscribe" territory now.
- No product detail in the hero — are these pull-on linen? Denim? What's the inseam? $12 is the hook but I'm clicking in completely blind, which means I'm doing extra work on the product page.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut to one offer.** The $12 shorts deal owns the subject line — let it own the email. Remove the 40% spend-threshold section entirely; it undermines the "TODAY ONLY" urgency by introducing a second transaction logic. Push clearance to its own send.
- 2. **Put one product detail in the hero.** "Pull-on, 4-inch inseam, 8 colors" is enough. Right now I'm clicking through blind, and Hanna Andersson's welcome email — which told me exactly what I was getting — set a bar Old Navy is not clearing here.
- 3. **Collapse or cut "Dresses Everyone's Obsessing Over."** If it must exist, make it two images and a single CTA, not a full scroll-stopping block competing with the shorts.
- 4. **Breathe between sends.** Four emails in five days means each successive send earns less goodwill. Batch the promos, not the inbox hits.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$12 shorts — today only. Stock up.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Summer shorts from $12. Ends tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Pull-ons, bermudas, and denim — pick your pair before they're gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock up before this price disappears — $12 only runs today.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect — cp.oldnavy.com preference center**

The QA probe followed `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/1cb606d4-8874-457f-a9aa-309b4b7abdf3?mi_mid=...` and received a redirect to a relative path:

```

```

A relative redirect from an origin-level redirect handler is unresolvable without a base URL in context — the HTTP client reported `unknown url type`. Any recipient clicking the link this pixel backs (likely the preference center or unsubscribe flow) will hit a broken destination.

**[WARN] 23 tracking links unprobed**

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing `alt` attribute — cp.oldnavy.com tracking pixel**


No `alt` attribute present. The sibling open pixel correctly uses `alt=""`. Screen readers will attempt to read the full src URL. Fix: add `alt=""`.

**[WARN] Duplicate responsive class selectors**

The comment in the source acknowledges this (`/* MspaceTop was duplicated, kept one */`), but both rules remain. The second declaration wins in all tested clients, but this is dead CSS that should be removed.

```css

td { line-height:0px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly }
```

Setting `line-height: 0px` globally on all `td` elements in Outlook 2013+ will collapse any cell that relies on line-height for vertical spacing. This is only safe if every affected `td` has an explicit height or padding set. Verify in Outlook 2016/2019 rendering.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header absent**

RFC 8058 one-click machine unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) was not detected. Gmail has required this for bulk senders (>5K/day) since February 2024.
