# Get 50% off ALL polos while you can
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-29**
## Executive summary

- Day five of the semi-annual sale season. I've been counting — six emails in three days from Old Navy — and I will say the subject line actually stopped my thumb for a second. Polos. Not activewear. Polos. In July, when you have two kids and back-to-school is already circling in the back of your head like a low-grade headache, "50% off ALL polos" is the kind of hook I actually respond to.
- Then I opened it and the polo disappeared.
- The email opens on a broad "TODAY ONLY!" banner, pivots immediately to TrekTech activewear (which, to their credit, first appeared in the June 27th send and I found it interesting then), then rolls into a dresses section, then a "40%–70% Deep Deals" stack at the bottom. There's no polo content front and center. The subject made a specific promise and the body forgot it existed. For someone who buys polos by the six-pack in August, that bait-and-switch — even an accidental one — burns trust.
- I'd probably click the deep deals section out of semi-annual-sale reflex, but I wouldn't forward this. I'd archive it faintly annoyed.

## What's working

- **The category pivot is genuinely smart.** Moving off "ALL activewear" for the first time in five sends is the right call. Polos in July = back-to-school prep = my purchasing brain, immediately engaged.
- **"TODAY ONLY!" in the hero is credible hard urgency.** After the softer "while you can" in the subject, having a concrete day-of deadline in the visual helps. It's more honest than the "2 DAYS ONLY" rhetoric from the June 28th kangaroo email, which had already started feeling performative.
- **The dresses section reads seasonally.** "Dresses Everyone's Obsessing Over" is summer-forward, the styling looked clean, and as a person who occasionally wears things that aren't mom-uniform, I noticed it.
- **40%–70% deep deals at the bottom is where the real value lives.** For a bulk buyer running an Old Navy semi-annual strategy, that range signals actual clearance depth, not a surface discount.
- **Clean render, no visual bugs.** Email lays out legibly at what appears to be a mobile-first width. No broken images, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **The body doesn't deliver on the subject's promise.** Polos are the hook; TrekTech activewear and dresses are what I actually see first. By the time I find polo content — if it's even featured — I've already recalibrated my expectations downward.
- **Same 50% off, fifth time in three days.** Hanna Andersson's welcome email offered one clean 20% off code, stated once, no gymnastics — and that single offer felt more trustworthy than six consecutive "50% off ALL [rotating category]" blasts. The offer is becoming noise.
- **No kids, no family imagery.** If you're selling me on polos and it's July, show me a kid in a polo. This is prime school-uniform stocking territory and there's no family signal visible anywhere — just adult activewear models and adult dresses.
- **"While you can" is limp urgency copy.** It undercuts the concrete "TODAY ONLY!" in the body. Those two voices should say the same thing.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the body match the subject.** If "50% off ALL polos" is the hook, the hero image should be polos — kids AND adults — not TrekTech activewear. The promise you make in the subject is the contract you have to fulfill above the fold.
- 2. **Show a family.** Polo + July + kid model = instant signal to every mom in the list that back-to-school restocking starts now. That's the purchase motivation that would actually move me.
- 3. **Pick one urgency register.** "While you can" in the subject and "TODAY ONLY!" in the hero are two different pitches. Unify them — if it's today only, say so in the subject too.
- 4. **Let TrekTech breathe.** It earned an 8/10 when it debuted June 27th because it was new. Burying it as module two under a polo-promise subject wastes the story. Give it a dedicated send when the activewear cadence resets.
- 5. **Rest the cadence.** Six emails in three days is where unsubscribes happen. The deep deals section is strong enough to anchor its own standalone send.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Back-to-school polos: 50% off today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Stock up now — 50% off kids + adult polos`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Grab them before they're gone — deal ends tonight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `School year prep starts here. All polos, all sizes, 50% off.`
