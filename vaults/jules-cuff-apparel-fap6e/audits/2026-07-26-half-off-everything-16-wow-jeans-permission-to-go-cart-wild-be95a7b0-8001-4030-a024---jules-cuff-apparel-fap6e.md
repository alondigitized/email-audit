# HALF OFF EVERYTHING* + $16 Wow jeans (🤪 permission to go cart-wild!)
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-26**
## Executive summary

- Okay, I've now gotten some version of "50% off everything + $16 Wow jeans" from Old Navy four times in three days — 7/24 sweatshirts, 7/25 twice, and now this. My eye did catch the flat-lay grid lower in the email with the red jacket, plaid, and toddler overalls all together — that's the actual "buy the whole crew's fall wardrobe at once" energy I show up for. But the top of this email is nearly identical to the one I already reviewed from the same date, right down to the "$9 Kids & Toddler / $16 Adults" jean math and the "2 DAYS ONLY" banner.
- The "Houl into Fall" headline — twice, in the two biggest hero blocks — reads like a typo for "Haul," and once I see it I can't unsee it. On a brand that's asking me to trust "50% OFF EVERYTHING*" with an asterisk I can't read from here, a sloppy hero headline undercuts the whole pitch.
- I'd scroll past this one. I already clicked through on the Wow jeans deal from Monday's email, so this is just Old Navy re-serving me the same casserole. Not forwarding — my sister already texted me the same $16 jeans link from her own inbox two days ago.

## What's working

- The Kids & Toddler ($9) + Adults ($16) denim block in one hero is exactly the side-by-side math I do for my kids and me — Old Navy keeps nailing that specific combo.
- The flat-lay product grid (jackets, stripes, plaid, overalls) actually shows a mixed kid/adult wardrobe, which reads more "family closet" than most of their hero shots.
- The category grid at the bottom (Tees, Jeans, Activewear, Pants, Dresses, Sweatshirts, all 50%) is genuinely useful — I can scan straight to "Sweatshirts & Hoodies" without hunting.
- "Become an Encore Member to Get [offer], Join for Free" up top is a real loyalty hook, and I am an Encore member.

## What's weak

- "Houl into Fall" — twice — looks like a straight-up typo in the two largest headlines. If I'm supposed to believe "50% off everything," a broken headline makes the whole thing feel rushed.
- This is the same 50%-off-everything-plus-$16-jeans combo I already got wrapped in different subject lines this week (per my 7/25 and 7/24 opens) — the cadence is exhausting, not exciting.
- "50% OFF EVERYTHING*" with the asterisk and "Even New Arrivals" is doing a lot of work to sound bigger than it probably is — I know from experience that asterisk means a chunk of the catalog is excluded, and it's never spelled out near the offer.
- Two nearly identical "50% OFF EVERYTHING" banner blocks in one email (top and again mid-scroll) is redundant — I got it the first time.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix "Houl into Fall" to "Haul into Fall" — this is the single most damaging thing in the email because it's a visible error in the two biggest headlines.
- 2. Cut the send frequency — I've had four near-identical Wow-jeans/50%-off emails in three days. Hanna Andersson's welcome series (which I scored 8/10) earns trust by not over-sending; Old Navy is doing the opposite and burning goodwill.
- 3. Drop one of the two duplicate "50% OFF EVERYTHING" hero banners — keep the jeans hero, cut the repeat further down, and use that space for something new (like the flat-lay family grid moved up).
- 4. Put the actual exclusions from the asterisk in plain text near the offer, not buried in footer fine print — "everything*" with no visible caveat reads like bait to anyone who's shopped a sale before.
- 5. Lean harder into the family/mixed-size flat-lay — that's the strongest "shop once for everyone" visual in the email and it's currently buried two-thirds down.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Wow Jeans: $9 Kids, $16 Adults — 2 Days Only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `50% off your whole fall haul (kids + adults)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stock the whole crew: $9 kids jeans, $16 adult jeans, 50% off everything else.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Two days left on Wow jeans — plus 50% off tees, dresses, and hoodies.`
