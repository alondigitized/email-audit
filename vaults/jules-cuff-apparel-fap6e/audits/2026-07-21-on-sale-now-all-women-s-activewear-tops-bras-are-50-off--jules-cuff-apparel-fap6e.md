# On sale now: All women's activewear tops & bras* are 50% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- I'll be honest — I almost clicked before I finished loading. "TODAY ONLY!" above the fold from Old Navy, activewear at 50% off — as someone who burns through sports bras and tanks like they're paper towels, that's a stop-the-scroll moment. I opened fast.
- Then the email kept going. And going. The activewear sale gives way to a "BOLD RED!" editorial section that reads like someone's mood board got stapled to a promotional email, followed by earth-tone product shots, then a $3.99 clearance drop I nearly missed, and finally a Back-to-School tack-on at the very bottom that felt like guilt from last week's two dedicated school-prep emails. This is a four-campaign-in-one situation and it makes me tired just thinking about scrolling it.
- I'd click for the activewear — the TODAY ONLY urgency is real enough that I wouldn't sit on it. I would not forward this. I'd tap through, find the tank I need, and close before the BOLD RED section convinces me I'm shopping at a different store.

## What's working

- "TODAY ONLY!" above the fold is doing exactly what it should — urgency that lands before I even read the offer
- 50% off activewear tops and bras is a legitimate deal from a brand I already buy from; there's nothing to decode
- The early tank grid with multiple colors is practical — I can see what's actually available before committing to a click
- Models read like actual women in workout clothes at this price point, not fashion-forward editorial casting; that matches my expectation of Old Navy
- The hero offer is honest — asterisk is there but the main message isn't hiding anything

## What's weak

- The "BOLD RED!" section mid-email is genuinely confusing — it breaks the activewear-sale momentum and reads like an art-director tangent rather than a shopping prompt
- The $3.99 clearance is buried so deep I almost scrolled past it; that's the most compelling price point in the email and they hid it
- The Back-to-School kicker at the bottom is the worst kind of half-measure — after sending me two dedicated school-prep emails this week, a three-line afterthought feels like they forgot what month it is
- The subject says "on sale now" but the email screams "TODAY ONLY" — those two urgency signals don't match and the weaker one is the one I see in my inbox first
- No fit or support language for bras anywhere visible — for me, 50% off a bra I can't assess by fit description is a gamble, not a deal

## Recommendations

- 1. Match subject-line urgency to the body — "TODAY ONLY" should be in the subject; "on sale now" undersells what's inside
- 2. Cut the BOLD RED editorial section or spin it into its own dedicated send; shoehorning a fashion-magazine spread into a sale email dilutes both
- 3. Surface the $3.99 clearance price in or just below the hero — that stopping price deserves to be seen, not buried
- 4. Either give Back-to-School real estate with specific items and prices, or drop it entirely; after two full school-prep emails, a limp footnote is worse than silence (Hanna Andersson's welcome email, which I audited earlier this year, never does this — one email, one message, nothing bolted on)
- 5. Add a single line of fit language under the bra category — "light-support to high-impact" lets me self-select before tapping through

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Tracking pixel missing alt text**
`<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/38c4a2db-6a68-46d6-86ec-6689e0a6370a?...">` — no `alt=""` attribute. Per WCAG 2.1, decorative/tracking images must include `alt=""` explicitly. Several email clients will render a broken image icon if the pixel fails to load.

**CSS class collision — `.MspaceTop` / `.MspaceBot`**
In the `@media (max-width: 639px)` block, `.MspaceTop` is declared twice (for `padding-top: 5px` and `padding-top: 10px`) and `.MspaceBot` is declared twice. The second declaration silently wins, making `.space-top5` and `.space-bot5` functionally inert on mobile. This is a low-severity layout bug but can cause unexpected spacing.

**Outlook line-height conflict**
The MSO conditional style sets `td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }` globally for Outlook 2013+. This is an aggressive reset that can collapse text in any `<td>` that doesn't explicitly set its own `line-height`, causing text to render invisible or overlapping in Outlook desktop.
