# Not a drill: $16 Wow jeans
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-25**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is the SAME "$16 Wow jeans" email I got two days ago on 7/25 — "Not a drill" is apparently a phrase Old Navy is now recycling wholesale, because the hero block, the $9 Kids/Toddler + $16 Adult denim math, even the "Wow Jeans" name are identical to what scored a 7 from me last time. I get that "2 Days Only!" urgency is doing some work here, but if I already opened and clicked (or ignored) this exact creative once, sending it again with a new subject line doesn't feel like a new email — it feels like Old Navy assuming I have the memory of a goldfish.
- The PowerChill leggings/bra/hoodie block that follows is honestly cuter and more relevant to me than another round of denim — soft, breathable, "goes with everything," styled on someone who could be running errands or picking up kids — but it's buried under five(!) near-identical outfit shots of the same model in the same green backdrop before you even reach clearance. That's a LOT of scroll for one $10 PowerChill message repeated five times.
- Would I click? Maybe, for the jeans, only because $16 for adult denim is genuinely a good price and I do buy in bulk for two kids. Would I forward it to a friend? No — this is a "add to cart and move on" email, not a "look what I found" email.

## What's working

- The hero delivers the exact math I need in one glance: Kids & Toddler $9, Adults $16, on a clean pale-blue background — no hunting for the price.
- "2 Days Only!" gives real urgency without being ALL-CAPS screamy about it.
- The PowerChill "$10" repeated in each panel keeps the price anchor visible even as I scroll past outfit after outfit.
- Encore Rewards banner up top ("Join up to get...") at least nods at loyalty members before the outfit blast starts.
- Clearance from $3.99 near the bottom is a real, honest floor price, not a vague "up to X% off" tease.

## What's weak

- This is a near-repeat of the 7/25 "$16 Wow jeans" send — same hero, same price points, same "entry-level denim, priced to wow" copy. Hanna Andersson would never resend the same welcome offer twice in a week; this reads mass, not curated.
- Five almost-identical PowerChill outfit shots (VIP Lounge, sports bra, leggings, bike shorts, dance pose) in a row is exhausting scroll — one strong hero + a "shop the collection" link would've done it.
- Zero kids' PowerChill or family-matching callout in that whole middle section — it's all solo-adult styling, which breaks my "uniform-shopper for the whole family" radar completely.
- "10 out of 10 dirls" (typo for "girls," I assume) under the dance-pose shot is the kind of sloppy copy that makes an email feel rushed, not premium.
- The size-selector row (XS/S/M/L/XL, Baby Girls/Boys, Toddler Girls/Boys, Big Girls/Boys) only shows up once near the very top denim block — nowhere near the PowerChill section, where I'd actually want to jump straight to my size.

## Recommendations

- 1. Don't resend the same hero creative within a week — if the jeans deal is still live, say so explicitly ("Still $16 — last call") instead of a fresh "Not a drill" framing that implies this is new news.
- 2. Cut the PowerChill block from five outfit panels to two, and add one panel with a kid in matching PowerChill — that's the actual hook for someone shopping the whole family, and right now it's a 100% solo-adult section.
- 3. Fix "dirls" — small typo, but it's the kind of thing that makes a mass retailer feel even more mass.
- 4. Move a size-quick-shop row down into the PowerChill section too, not just under the denim hero.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Still $16: Wow Jeans for the whole family`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `2 days left — $9 kids, $16 adult denim`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `PowerChill styles from $10, plus clearance starting at $3.99`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Denim math: $9 for them, $16 for you. Ends Sunday.`
