# Fill that cart with $15 & under styles
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- Day eleven — or whatever number we're on — of the Old Navy Summer Grind, and today's pivot is away from the $6 kids uniform shorts that actually stopped me yesterday and into a wall-to-wall adult women's fashion grid. The "2 DAYS ONLY!" urgency header is credible in an Old Navy way — their clocks are real — but "Fill that cart with $15 & under styles" followed by fifteen product thumbnails is the email equivalent of walking into a store and having a floor associate gesture at the whole room.
- There's a $3.99 & under section that briefly got my attention because I couldn't tell if those were kids items. That mystery is doing more work than the actual hero, which tells you something about the hierarchy problem here. There's also a VIP LOUNGE block and something called "NEW TO TECH" that breaks the grid monotony with a pop of color — the one moment the email has a personality.
- I'd mark it read. Yesterday's email about uniform shorts at $6 was the one I forwarded to my sister-in-law. This one has nothing that makes me think it was sent to me specifically versus anyone with an Old Navy account and a pulse. Hanna Andersson's welcome email in May knew exactly who it was talking to within the first scroll. This doesn't know, and it shows.

## What's working

- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" actually means something at Old Navy** — their sale windows close; I've been trained to believe the deadline, unlike the permanent "sale" banners at H&M.
- **$3.99 & under sub-section** — even buried, a price that low catches the eye of a parent who buys graphic tees by the six-pack.
- **"NEW TO TECH" section breaks the grid** — the bright color pop is the only moment I felt like a person was making a creative choice rather than dropping SKUs into a template.
- **Email renders clean** — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds on what looks like a mobile-optimized send.
- **Concrete dollar figures throughout** — $15, $3.99, $10 visible; not a percentage game, not "up to X% off," which I've been burned by at Carter's before.

## What's weak

- **No kids signal after yesterday's uniform email** — that subject line about $6 shorts was Old Navy at its best for me. Today's pivot to adult casual feels like a different brand forgot I have two kids starting school in August.
- **The grid kills intent** — fifteen-plus thumbnails in two sections means I'm not buying anything; I'm overwhelmed. Compare this to how Hanna Andersson structures a send around one or two product stories — I finish reading Hanna emails with something specific in my cart.
- **$3.99 section is unlabeled** — is that kids? accessories? clearance socks? I shouldn't have to click to find out. Label it and my click rate doubles.
- **"Everyday Favorites" is copy that quit** — it's placeholder language after ten-plus identical summer emails. Even "Everyday Basics" would feel more intentional.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment family shoppers from adult-fashion shoppers** — I use Old Navy for volume kids buying. One send for me should lean into that, especially with back-to-school six weeks out. This email works fine for a 24-year-old shopping for herself; it's wrong for me.
- 2. **Label the $3.99 section explicitly** — "$3.99 kids tees" or "$3.99 accessories" is three words that instantly route me to or away from a click. The ambiguity is friction.
- 3. **Collapse the hero to three products with names** — the "NEW TO TECH" panel works because it's opinionated. Apply that to the $15 section. Pick one top, one bottom, one versatile piece. Let me want something specific.
- 4. **Tie the headline to a real deadline I care about** — "Back-to-school is 5 weeks out. Fill the drawer now." does more work than "Everyday Favorites."

### 5. Compliance

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe` header absent from relay capture**
RFC 2369 header not present. The QA note attributes this to AgentMail relay not forwarding it, but the risk is real: if the header is genuinely missing in delivery, Gmail and Apple Mail one-click unsubscribe buttons will not render.
