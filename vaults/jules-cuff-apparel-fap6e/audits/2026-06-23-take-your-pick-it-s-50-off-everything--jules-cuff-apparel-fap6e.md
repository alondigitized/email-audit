# Take your pick! It's 50% off everything*
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- Day seven. The June 22 review said "Day six, I've been counting" — so here we are. And to Old Navy's modest credit, "Take your pick" as the subject opener actually made me pause for a half-second before my brain caught up with the content and went: oh, it's the same email. TIME TO CHILL over a model, a product row, HOT NEW GEAR in block caps, then a "Go, Go, Go! Clearance Clearance Clearance Clearance" block stacked down the bottom third like a ticker tape. Nothing has changed. The offer hasn't moved. The hero hasn't moved.
- What frustrates me specifically: I have two kids and I open Old Navy emails because they're my go-to for the volume stuff — graphic tees, leggings, the kind of things that get destroyed in a week. This email is entirely adult women's and athletic crossover. I scrolled the whole thing looking for a kids' grid and got a fire-sale clearance block instead. Hanna Andersson's welcome email from May treated me like a person who was choosing them deliberately — Old Navy right now is treating me like a warm body in a database. The contrast is hard to miss when you're in both inboxes.
- I didn't click. Not even close. Marked as read and moved on.

## What's working

- "TIME TO CHILL" lands as a genuine summer headline — it's breezy without being forced, and it fits the June moment better than last week's tennis angle
- The 50% off banner is the first visual hit, right at the top — no hunting, no hierarchy confusion
- The product row below the hero has concrete price callouts ($8 pants visible) — that feels like actual information, not just a vague percentage promise
- The HOT NEW GEAR section gives the email structural variety, which is smart when you've been running the same hero for seven days
- Clean render — no broken images, no overlapping text, nothing crashing on the layout

## What's weak

- Zero kids content in the hero, the product grid, or the clearance block — for the shopper this email is supposedly reaching, that's the whole reason I'm here
- "Go, Go, Go! Clearance Clearance Clearance Clearance" stacked in teal blocks looks frantic, not exciting — Hanna Andersson would never let a clearance section this visually desperate into the main send
- No deadline anywhere — without "ends tonight" or "through Sunday," the 50% off offer just reads as ambient background noise at this point in the campaign
- The asterisk on "everything\*" has been repeated across seven consecutive emails; I've trained myself to assume it excludes exactly what I want to buy
- HOT NEW GEAR skews toward influencer-athletic styling; I'm buying for a first-grader and a toddler, not a boutique gym

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a kids section above the fold** — even one grid block of "uniform basics for back-to-school" would turn this from a women's email into a family email, which is what Jules actually is
- 2. **Stamp a real deadline on the offer** — "Ends Sunday, 11:59 PM" in the hero does more heavy lifting than any headline variant; urgency is the one signal this campaign has been missing for a week straight
- 3. **Retire or rename the clearance block** — "Final Call" with a clean button lands better than four stacked "Clearance" lines that look like a warehouse auction
- 4. **Replace HOT NEW GEAR with a family-lens section** — matching sibling sets, new arrivals for kids, or even just a "whole family" callout would speak to how I actually use Old Navy

## Technical Audit — Old Navy "50% off everything" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

One confirmed broken redirect in a ContentPilot personalization link will silently drop traffic for affected subscribers. Compliance header gaps and a degenerate plain-text body are secondary risks that affect deliverability.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken ContentPilot redirect**
The QA checker resolved `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/681f6c16-3ee4-487b-8654-7448ec2c3c6f?...` and got back an unknown URL scheme `/g/681f6c16-...`. This indicates the ContentPilot redirect chain is returning an unresolvable internal path rather than a valid `https://` destination. Any subscriber whose personalization resolves through this token will land on a dead URL.

**[WARN] 23 tracking links unverified**
All click links route through `click.email.oldnavy.com/open.aspx` (standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud redirect). These were skipped by the probe — no broken links confirmed, but no clean bill of health either.

**Open/impression pixel**
`click.email.oldnavy.com/open.aspx?3HSGXT5CMBIETEQ4HTNNB2BFEM.60263` — structurally valid, hidden in a `display:none` wrapper as expected.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate CSS class mappings**
`.MspaceTop` is bound to both `.space-top5` and `.space-top10`; `.MspaceBot` is bound to both `.space-bot5` and `.space-bot10`. The second declaration wins via cascade, silently overriding the first. Mobile spacing for 5px variants will render as 10px.

**Aggressive Outlook line-height reset**
The MSO conditional sets `td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }` globally. This is a known pattern for fixing Outlook spacing gaps but can collapse cells with dynamic or unknown-height content. Worth validating in Outlook 2016/2019.

**Missing alt text on ContentPilot pixel**
`<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/681f6c16-...">` has no `alt` attribute (QA confirmed). For a tracking pixel this is low severity but technically violates WCAG 2.1 SC 1.1.1 — the fix is `alt=""` (explicitly empty, not absent).

**Legacy doctype and meta**
XHTML 1.0 Transitional and `@-ms-viewport` are obsolete but non-breaking in current clients.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens are visible in the available HTML. The `cp_tp=v3.eJx9k...` parameter in the ContentPilot URL is a base64-encoded personalization payload — its content cannot be audited from the source alone, but the broken redirect above suggests it may be malformed or referencing a deleted template ID.

---

### 5. Compliance

| Check | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| List-Unsubscribe header | Not detected | May exist upstream of AgentMail relay — unconfirmed |
| List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) | Not detected | One-click unsubscribe not present as observed |
| Physical mailing address | Not detected | HTML is truncated — footer may exist but was not captured |
| CAN-SPAM subject asterisk disclosure | Deferred | `*` in subject implies terms; footer not visible to audit |

The unsubscribe header gap is the most actionable: Gmail and Yahoo now enforce RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders (>5k/day). If `List-Unsubscribe-Post` is absent from the actual delivered headers, this email is out of compliance with current Gmail bulk sender requirements.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM coverage**: Cannot fully audit — HTML is truncated and all destination URLs are behind `click.email.oldnavy.com` redirects that were not probed. The ContentPilot layer (`cp.oldnavy.com`) is expected to append UTM params post-redirect; the broken redirect in §2 means any subscriber hitting that link also loses attribution.

**Plain-text body**: 87% of characters are raw URLs (19,134 / 22,020 chars per QA). This indicates the plain-text alternative was auto-generated from HTML without cleanup. Spam filters score this pattern negatively, and it provides no readable fallback for plain-text clients.
