# New moves, BIG deals! Snag 50% off ALL activewear
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- Day eleven. That's where we are, and I say that not with frustration — more like the way you'd acknowledge a neighbor who waves at you every single morning. You see it coming. You nod. This one landed with a genuine pivot though: the subject line broke from the "everything" drum we've been on and named a specific category. "50% off ALL activewear" made my thumb slow down for a half-second. That's more than I gave the June 24 "IT'S TRUE! HALF OFF everything* is inside" send, which I archived before it finished rendering.
- What's inside is genuinely mixed. The "2 DAYS ONLY" hero is the strongest piece — clear urgency, specific category, a woman in a light blue set that looks like actual workout clothes rather than fashion-editorial fitness. But then the email just keeps going: deep deals, new arrivals, and buried somewhere near the bottom, a uniforms section that is exactly what I need right now with school lists already landing in my inbox. School uniforms should have been above the fold for any subscriber who has bought kids clothing in the last eighteen months. That's a segmentation miss.
- Would I click? The activewear hero might get a tap if my leggings situation needs addressing — and it usually does. Would I forward it? No — my friends are already on the Old Navy list. Mark as read? I'd grab the activewear link and go straight to uniforms if I could find it, but I'd probably bounce before I scrolled that far.

## What's working

- The "2 DAYS ONLY" urgency callout at the top of the hero is specific and actionable — I can make a decision around a two-day window
- Switching from "50% off everything" to a named category (activewear) signals targeting; it feels less like a mass blast and more like Old Navy knows I exist
- The uniforms section landing in a late-June send is exactly right on the calendar — school supply lists are already showing up, and I would click that section if I reached it
- The hero model is wearing a real-world activewear set I'd actually consider, not a fashion concept; it reads as a woman who works out and runs errands, which is my life
- The Uniquely You loyalty card reminder at the bottom is functional — I definitely have points sitting there I'm not redeeming

## What's weak

- This is Day 11 of consecutive promotional emails; the open reflex has been trained down, and even a legitimately specific offer has to fight through accumulated promotional scar tissue
- The email runs too long — by the time I reached the uniforms section, I'd mentally moved on; Hanna Andersson's welcome email showed you can land a single focused message cleanly without scrolling fatigue
- "BIG deals!" in the subject with the capitalization reads like the brand knows I'm desensitized and is trying volume instead of precision — that's a trust signal in the wrong direction
- Activewear and back-to-school uniforms are two different audiences with two different purchase motivations; packing both into one scroll weakens both pitches
- The 40%-70% deep deals section competes with the activewear hero before I've had a chance to engage with the first offer

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the parent cohort and send them a uniforms hero, not an activewear hero** — anyone who has purchased kids' clothing in the last two years gets a version of this email with the uniform section moved to the top; the activewear is there below, not the other way around
- 2. **Move the "2 DAYS ONLY" urgency into the subject line** — it's the strongest behavioral lever in the email and it's sitting in the body where people who didn't open never see it
- 3. **Collapse the email to two sections max** — activewear deal with one hero product shown, and uniforms deal with two or three specific pieces; Hanna Andersson doesn't just say "kids stuff on sale," they show the stripe set, and it sells itself because you can picture it on your kid
- 4. **Give the deep deals section its own dedicated send** — 40%-70% off is its own strong offer that's getting diluted by sharing space with two other content blocks
- 5. **Cut the cadence to every other day** — the promotional frequency is depreciating every individual email's credibility; a three-day gap would make this exact offer feel like news instead of weather
- **Subject Alt A:** `2 days only: 50% off all activewear`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Activewear + school uniforms — both 50% off, 2 days`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Leggings, shorts, sets. All half off. Clock's ticking.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock the closet before school lists hit — uniforms included.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**WARN — Missing alt text on CDP pixel**
`<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/f3f22718...">` has no `alt` attribute. For a tracking pixel this is low severity, but it triggers accessibility linters and some corporate mail proxies flag untagged images.

**CSS duplicate selectors**
`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are defined twice in the responsive block (for `space-top5`/`space-top10` and `space-bot5`/`space-bot10` respectively). The second declaration silently overwrites the first at the `<639px` breakpoint. This likely has no visible effect given the values are the same, but it's dead code that creates maintenance confusion.

**VML namespace declared, no VML usage detected**
`xmlns:v` and `xmlns:o` are present on `<html>`. No VML shapes appear in the truncated source. If the full email has no VML, these declarations are dead weight.

**No issues found** with baseline responsive structure — single 639px breakpoint, `max-width: 640px` wrapper, and Outlook conditional comments are all present and correct.

### 5. Compliance

**WARN — List-Unsubscribe header not captured**
The AgentMail relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Major inbox providers (Gmail, Apple Mail, Yahoo) use this header to show the native unsubscribe button. Its absence increases spam complaint rates. If it exists upstream but is stripped by the relay, it needs relay configuration review; if it genuinely isn't set, this is a deliverability defect.

**WARN — List-Unsubscribe-Post not found (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender guidelines for >5k/day senders since Feb 2024) is not confirmed present. Old Navy almost certainly sends above that threshold.

**WARN — No physical mailing address detected in parsed content**
CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address. The HTML is truncated — this may appear in the footer — but the QA scanner did not find it in the extracted text. Needs manual footer verification against the full source.

**Authentication (SPF/DKIM/DMARC)**
`Authentication-Results` header not captured by relay. Sending domain is `email.oldnavy.com`. SPF/DKIM pass status is unknown from available data — infrastructure-level verification required.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

UTM parameter coverage cannot be confirmed without probing the 23 click-tracked URLs through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. The redirect chain (ESP click tracker → destination) is opaque. If UTM parameters are appended at the ESP redirect layer rather than hardcoded in the HTML, they will not appear in source inspection but should be present on final destination URLs. **Recommend spot-checking 2–3 CTAs end-to-end to confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` survive the redirect.**
