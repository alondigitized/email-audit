# EVERYTHING* is 50% off (and its all yours) + 60% off all shorts & swim* and $5 dresses
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- Day four. I have to say — and I say this as someone who genuinely stocks up at Old Navy's semi-annual and has a whole folder in her inbox just for their sale emails — I'm hitting a wall. This is the same 60% off swim and shorts deal I've been staring at since June 17th, with the same $5 dress callout, now repackaged with "EVERYTHING*" headlining in all caps like it's a new development. The asterisk is doing a *lot* of work on that word "everything," and anyone who has clicked through an Old Navy sale email in the last five years knows exactly how many exclusions that asterisk is hiding.
- The email itself renders cleanly — I'll give it that. The summer palette pops, the product sections are organized, and the price points are real. But six or seven stacked promotional blocks all screaming at the same volume is not a sale. It's a fire sale. It's what happens when a brand sends the same offer four days in a row and adds more deals to it each time hoping the noise level compensates for the diminishing surprise.
- I'd probably click through — I have a half-finished cart with kids' shorts in it and 60% off is 60% off. But forward to a friend? She's already seen three versions of this email herself this week. We've been texting about it.

## What's working

- The **$5 dress callout** is a genuine standout — that price point is surprising enough to stop a scroll, and the product imagery around it earns its placement
- Email **renders clean** front to back — no broken images, no layout weirdness, the production quality is consistent and professional
- The **summer color palette** (corals, bright blues, warm pinks) is doing exactly what it should for a swim-and-shorts push; it reads seasonal and fun
- The **"50% Off Everything*" section** is large-format and legible mid-scroll — even if I'm skimming, the offer registers
- **Multiple product sections** give me actual things to look at rather than one static hero, which helps me mentally shop across categories (even if there are too many)

## What's weak

- This is the **fourth consecutive send with the same core offer structure** — the 60% off swim/shorts deal is no longer an event, it's the assumed floor price; Hanna Andersson runs their semi-annual *once* and I act on it; Old Navy has run this one four times and I'm yawning
- **"EVERYTHING*"** in all caps is textbook bait-and-switch energy — I know before I click that "everything" means "everything except the stuff you probably came for," and that asterisk has eroded more trust than any single deal can rebuild
- The **subject line is a run-on sentence trying to announce three deals at once** — it reads like a brand that couldn't pick a hero and instead threw all three on the table hoping one would land
- **No deadline anywhere visible** — "50% off" without "through Sunday" or "today only" removes all urgency; I have no reason to click now versus later, and later almost always becomes never
- The email is **long and dense** — by the time I reach the active gear section near the bottom, I've mentally already checked out

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with $5 dresses as the sole hero** — that's the price point with genuine surprise value; the 60% off swim/shorts has been running for days and has lost its punch
- 2. **Add a hard deadline** — "Today only" or "Through Sunday" converts this from passive scrolling to actual cart behavior; without it, urgency is purely aspirational
- 3. **Lose the ALL CAPS on "EVERYTHING"** — sentence case reads more confident and less panicked; compare Hanna Andersson's clean typography to this, and it's obvious which brand feels like it trusts its customer
- 4. **Rotate one deal per send** — stacking three deals in one email didn't work on the 19th either; a single sharp offer would perform better than three diluted ones
- 5. **Surface two or three specific exclusions in the email body** — or at least acknowledge the asterisk game upfront so I don't feel set up when I get to the landing page
- **Subject Alt A:** `$5 dresses + 60% off swim — today only, Jules`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Honestly the best summer deal we've run: $5 dresses`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stock up before the weekend — kids' shorts and swim are in the deal`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `$5 dresses move fast. The swim deal ends Sunday. Don't sit on this.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Outlook line-height conflict:** The MSO conditional block sets `td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }` globally. **Duplicate CSS class declarations:** `.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are defined twice in the mobile media query (for both 5px and 10px variants). Last-write wins in CSS; the 5px variants are silently overridden. Actual rendered spacing at mobile breakpoint will always use 10px for these classes regardless of intent.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post headers not captured**
Neither `List-Unsubscribe` nor `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click) headers were detected by the QA relay. This is flagged as a relay-capture gap rather than confirmed absence — Sailthru injects these at SMTP layer and they may be present in the actual delivered message. However, if missing from delivered mail, Gmail and Yahoo (which enforce one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders as of Feb 2024) will suppress the native unsubscribe button, increasing spam complaints.

**[WARN] Physical mailing address not detected in scanned body**
CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid postal address. The truncated HTML source provided to QA did not include a footer section where this would normally appear. Cannot confirm absence — this is likely a truncation artifact. Verify the rendered footer contains `Old Navy, 2 Folsom Street, San Francisco, CA 94105` or equivalent.

**[WARN] Authentication-Results header absent from relay capture**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status is unknown. `email.oldnavy.com` is a Gap Inc. Salesforce Marketing Cloud (legacy)/Sailthru sending domain — DKIM signing is expected. Absence here is likely an AgentMail relay stripping issue, not a genuine authentication failure, but should be confirmed against raw headers.
