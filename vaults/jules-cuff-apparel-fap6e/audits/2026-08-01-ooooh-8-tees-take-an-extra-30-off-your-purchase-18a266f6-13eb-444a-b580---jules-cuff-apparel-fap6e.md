# Ooooh! 👀$8 tees + take an EXTRA 30% off your purchase*
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-01**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one actually made me look up from my coffee for a second — after a solid week of Old Navy's purple "50% off everything" starburst on repeat, they finally swapped the wallpaper. Red 2-day tee sale, $8 tees, kids stuff stacked below — it's different enough that I didn't immediately think "didn't I already delete this?"
- The kids section is where this email earns its keep for someone like me: "Build Their Winning Outfits," matching sibling sets in the sporty section, the $5-$8 leggings/polos/activewear tier — that's the actual uniform-shopper bait. Hanna Andersson's welcome email (the one that scored an 8 with me back in May) still does the "treat me like a known customer" thing better, but this is closer to that lane than the last five Old Navy sends that just yelled the same discount at me.
- Would I click? Probably scroll to the kids grid and maybe tap through on the "Sporty Top Scorers" matching sets — that's real inventory for my life. Forward to a friend? No. Mark as read and move on, but not delete-on-sight like the fourth "50% off everything" clone did.

## What's working

- The red "2 DAYS ONLY" tee hero actually breaks the purple-starburst rut this brand's been stuck in all week — first thing that's felt like a new creative in days.
- Kids section has real signal for me: matching sibling outfits, sporty sets, and a "1 YEAR GUARANTEE ON ALL KIDS CLOTHING" callout — that's the kind of durability promise that actually moves a uniform shopper, not just a discount.
- Price points on the kids grid ($5-$8) are dead simple and scannable — no math required, which matters when I'm speed-scrolling on my phone between school pickup and dinner.
- Clean layout, no broken images, no overlapping text — a low bar, but this brand has occasionally tripped on it before.

## What's weak

- The hero is women's/men's tees, not kids — for a "family uniform run" subject line moment, the thing I actually care about is buried three scrolls down.
- Asterisks everywhere ("Extra 30%*," "All Jeans On Sale*," the kids guarantee*) — feels like there's fine print I'm not being shown, and that's the exact "mass vs. premium" tell I always flag. Hanna Andersson doesn't make me hunt for the catch.
- "Become an Old Navy Member for $$" banner up top reads like a cold acquisition ask, not a reward for someone who's already bought from them a dozen times — doesn't feel like they know me.
- "Styles Picked Just For You" is generic filler copy sitting over what looks like the same product grid everyone gets — not actually personalized, just labeled like it is.
- Ending on a giant red "Clearance Clearance Clearance" banner after a tee sale and a kids sale is a third offer competing for my attention in one email — pick a lane.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead the hero with the kids matching-set content, not adult tees — that's the section with real conversion potential for a parent doing a uniform run, and it's currently buried.
- 2. Cut the fine-print asterisks or put the terms right under the offer in plain text — "extra 30% off, excludes clearance" reads more honest than a lonely asterisk.
- 3. Replace the generic membership-acquisition banner with an actual loyalty callout if I'm already a member — reward math, not a join pitch.
- 4. Drop the third offer (Clearance banner) or fold it into the kids section instead of tacking on a whole new campaign at the bottom.
- 5. Swap "Shop Now" for something specific to what's actually in the module — "Shop Matching Sets" for the kids grid, "Shop $8 Tees" for the hero.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Matching sibling sets from $5 + $8 tees for everyone`
- **Subject Alt B:** `2 days: $8 tees, kids gear from $5, extra 30% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Uniform-run essentials, stocked and priced right.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Kids' must-haves from $5 — plus extra 30% off your cart.`
