# $4 kids uniform polos that pass the dress code AND last the whole school year
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- Okay, $4. That's the number that stopped my thumb. After days of $8 pants and wall-to-wall "$15 & under" noise, Old Navy actually dropped the price point — and landed it on the exact item I was mentally adding to my cart. The subject does something smart: it doesn't just say "$4 polos," it says they'll pass the dress code AND survive the year. That's the parent brain checklist in one line. I felt seen.
- The hero delivers. The polo colors, the 2-DAYS ONLY urgency, and that 1-Year Guarantee badge are all in frame without scrolling. That guarantee is doing more persuasive work than Old Navy seems to realize — that's a Hanna Andersson move, leading with durability in a volume-brand email. It's unexpected and earns trust.
- Then I kept scrolling, and there it is: a full Low Rise jeans section. Adult fashion. Completely divorced from why I opened. This is the same bait-and-switch pattern from July 7 and 8 — hook with the kids utility item, then flood with adult content hoping for impulse buys. I would click the polo CTA because $4 with a year-long guarantee is real. I would not forward this. I'd screenshot the price and text it to a friend.

## What's working

- **$4 is genuinely impulse territory.** At that price I'm buying three without thinking. It's a step down from the $8 pants that ran for two days straight, and the drop feels like an actual escalation, not recycled creative.
- **The 1-Year Guarantee badge in the hero is a durability signal that earns the click.** I save Hanna Andersson for the stripes that need to survive two kids; Old Navy just made a case for itself in the same breath.
- **2-DAYS ONLY creates real urgency.** It's paired with a low price, so the deadline reads as scarcity, not manufactured pressure.
- **The subject line packs three parent-brain checkboxes in one sentence:** price, compliance ("passes dress code"), longevity ("last the whole school year"). That's legitimately tight copy.
- **Seasonally precise.** Back-to-school framing, uniform-specific language — this email knows what month it is and who it's talking to.

## What's weak

- **The Low Rise jeans section is a full stop.** I opened for kids polos, not adult denim. Hanna Andersson would never pivot mid-email from kids uniforms to fashion — it reads like a CRM miscalculation, not strategy.
- **Cadence fatigue is compounding.** Four or five uniform-adjacent emails in as many days; I double-checked this wasn't the July 10 $8 pants email re-sent. When the brand fires this frequently, even a price drop can feel like noise.
- **No sizing callout in the polo hero.** Uniform polos live or die on fit — slim, regular, husky, extended sizing. The subject promises the polo will "pass the dress code" but I can't confirm that without knowing if my kid's size is in stock. Hanna's welcome email led with this immediately.
- **The email runs very long.** The clearance and Low Rise modules erode the urgency that the hero built — by the time I reach the bottom, the 2-day clock feels far away.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Terminate the email after the uniform module.** If the subject is about $4 polos, the email is about $4 polos. Move the Low Rise and clearance sections to a separate send — they're not wrong products, they're wrong placement.
- 2. **Add a sizing callout to the polo hero.** Even a single line — "XS–4X, slim and regular fits" — closes the sale for parents who need fit confidence before clicking.
- 3. **Surface the 1-Year Guarantee into the subject or preheader.** It's doing the most persuasive work in the email and it's buried. Promote it forward.
- 4. **If clearance must stay, push it to a footer tile.** Not a full visual module competing with the hero for attention.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$4 polos + 1-year guarantee: stock up before Tuesday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `2 days: $4 uniform polos in every color they'll need`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dress-code approved, machine-washable, and built to outlast the school year.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `All sizes, every color — grab a few before the price goes back up.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound (XHTML 1.0 Transitional, VML/MSO conditionals present, responsive breakpoint at 639px), but has one confirmed broken click-redirect, two missing compliance headers, and a plain-text body dominated by URL strings that creates a spam-filter risk.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken click-redirect — cp.oldnavy.com tracking pixel**

The QA probe on the open/DV tracking pixel returned `unknown url type: '/g/88ef7f99-21ca-4223-bc83-2f334250a2c4?txn=7e7bef80-7d16-11f1-8963-4ec26c495bcf'`. The URL:

```
https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/88ef7f99-21ca-4223-bc83-2f334250a2c4?mi_mid=019f5331-cb00-7000-9ec8-1554b1fad221&cp_tp=v3.eJ...
```

returned a redirect to a relative path (`/g/...`) — not an absolute URL — which is an invalid HTTP redirect response. This will silently fail in email clients that prefetch images. The `mi_mid` and `cp_cid` parameters embedded in the URL suggest this is a Movable Ink or similar dynamic content pixel; the redirect failure means dynamic content or engagement tracking for this send may be broken.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Outlook 2013+ line-height collapse risk**

The MSO conditional block sets:

```css
<!--[if gte mso 9]>
td { line-height:0px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly }
```

A global `line-height: 0px` on all `td` elements in Outlook 2013+ will collapse any text-bearing cell that doesn't have an inline override. This is a known Outlook rendering hazard.

**[WARN] Duplicate CSS class selectors**

`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice in the mobile media query with different values (`padding-top: 5px` and `padding-top: 10px` for `.MspaceTop`; same pattern for `.MspaceBot`). The second definition wins per cascade, silently discarding the first. The source comment acknowledges this (`/* MspaceTop was duplicated, kept one */`) but the duplicate remains in the live HTML.

**[WARN] Missing alt text on tracking pixel**

```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/88ef7f99-21ca-4223-bc83-2f334250a2c4?..." width="1" height="1">
```

No `alt=""` attribute. The open-tracking pixel (`click.email.oldnavy.com/open.aspx`) correctly uses `alt=""`. This pixel should match.
