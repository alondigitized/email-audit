# Low Rise denim is back (don't panic — it's wearable this time)
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- Okay, the subject line got me. "Don't panic — it's wearable this time" is genuinely funny because it acknowledges exactly what I was thinking: I lived through the first wave of low rise when I was 19 and I have never forgiven it. Old Navy calling out that cultural scar tissue in the subject line earns a real laugh and a real open — that's a harder thing to pull off than it looks.
- Once I'm inside, the email does the right thing immediately: two women in the hero, styled in a way that reads modern rather than Y2K-costume. The "Low Rise Lorna" and "Low Rise Mid Lay" callouts give me enough product language to know these are specific styles, not just a vague trend push. And the VIP LOUNGE section tells me there's a member beat in here, which makes me feel like I'm not just a stranger getting a mass blast.
- Where it loses me a little: by the time I'm scrolling through kids pricing at the bottom, the email has shifted gears so completely I've mentally left. I would click through to look at the Lorna — but I'm not forwarding this to a friend. This is a "bookmark for my next lunch break" send.

## What's working

- **The subject line is actually doing cultural work.** Addressing the collective millennial low-rise PTSD directly is smart positioning — it tells me Old Navy knows who they're writing to.
- **Female models front and center, full hero.** After yesterday's men's denim miss, this corrects course immediately. The eye goes to women, in women's jeans, no pivot required.
- **Product naming is concrete.** Lorna, Mid Lay — I have something to search for when I get to the site. That's better than "Shop Denim" with a photo.
- **VIP LOUNGE section signals loyalty recognition.** I notice when a brand treats me like a member vs. a stranger. This says member.
- **The self-aware tone carries through the hero copy.** The voice matches the subject line, which makes it feel like one consistent thought rather than a subject line the copy team never told the design team about.

## What's weak

- **The bottom section breaks the spell.** Kids pricing showing up at the tail end of a women's trend denim email is a gear shift. I went from "oh I might try the Lorna" to "wait, did I wander into a different email?" Hanna Andersson would never do that — their welcome email had one lane and stayed in it.
- **No clear sale signal in the hero.** Unlike the activewear 50%-off send two days ago, I can't see a specific discount callout above the fold. The VIP section implies value, but I have to scroll to find it, which means a lot of people won't.
- **Subject line is long.** Charming, yes — but "Low Rise denim is back (don't panic — it's wearable this time)" is going to get clipped on mobile before the parenthetical lands, which is exactly the part doing all the emotional work.
- **No size or fit signal visible.** I'm 36 and I have postpartum reality to contend with. "Wearable this time" is the promise — but I can't see anything that tells me this cut accounts for real bodies, not just a trend lookbook.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the discount (or at least the VIP offer) into the hero.** The activewear send converted better because the deal was screaming above the fold. Even "Members save extra" in small text below the headline would help.
- 2. **Cut or move the kids section entirely.** Put it in a separate email, or at least put a hard dividing line and reframe it as "while you're here." Mixing trend women's denim with kids pricing reads as editorial chaos.
- 3. **Shorten the subject so the punchline survives mobile clipping.** The parenthetical is the best part — restructure so it lands before the 45-character cut.
- 4. **Add a fit or body callout in the Lorna/Mid Lay descriptions.** "Sits just below the waist" or "designed with more room through the hip" — something that earns the "it's wearable this time" promise visually, not just in copy.

- **Subject Alt B:** `We fixed low rise. (We promise.)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Meet the Lorna — the cut that actually sits right`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `VIP members get early access + extra savings inside`

## Technical Audit — Old Navy "Low Rise Denim" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional + VML/MSO structure with a single-breakpoint responsive layout. QA flagged one broken redirect, missing one-click unsubscribe headers (now required by Gmail/Yahoo), an absent physical address, and a deliverability-threatening plain-text URL ratio.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect destination**
`https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/08ccb279-9991-411c-b2d6-5c0e48ffc9e5?mi_mid=...`
QA error: `unknown url type: '/g/08ccb279-...'` — the redirect server is returning a relative path (`/g/...`) as the destination instead of an absolute URL. Any click on a link routed through this redirect will land on a broken page.

**[WARN] 23 click-tracking links skipped**
All CTA/product links route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. QA could not probe destinations, so broken or mis-targeted links in product recommendations cannot be confirmed or ruled out.

**[WARN] Plain-text body is 77.7% URLs**
17,187 of 22,111 plain-text characters are URLs. This is a known spam filter trigger; many ESPs and inbox providers score on plain-text URL density.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Tracking pixel missing `alt` attribute**
```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/08ccb279-...?" width="1" height="1">
```
No `alt` attribute at all (not even `alt=""`). Screen readers will read the full raw URL. The open-tracking pixel at `click.email.oldnavy.com` correctly uses `alt=""`.

**[INFO] Duplicate CSS selectors in `@media` block**
`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice in the `@media (max-width: 639px)` block. The comments in the source acknowledge this. No functional breakage expected, but dead code.

**[INFO] Outlook MSO override sets `line-height:0px` globally on `td`**
```css
<!--[if gte mso 9]> td { line-height:0px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly } <![endif]-->
```
This is a known Outlook compatibility pattern, but it requires every text-bearing `td` to set its own `line-height` explicitly or text will collapse in Outlook 2013+.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (`%%`, `{{`, `*|`) visible in the captured HTML. Cannot fully confirm across truncated body content, but no issues detected in the available source.

---

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
Gmail and Yahoo have required this header for bulk senders since February 2024. Its absence means inbox providers cannot surface the native unsubscribe UI, and the email is more likely to be filtered or routed to spam.

**[FAIL] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe (`POST` method) is required alongside `List-Unsubscribe` under the same February 2024 Gmail/Yahoo mandate. Missing.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected in captured HTML**
CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address. QA did not detect one in the available source. If it exists only in the truncated footer, this is not a bug — but it should be confirmed.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not found at relay**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status is unknown at the AgentMail relay layer. This does not mean authentication is failing, but it cannot be confirmed from available data.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**[FAIL] Broken redirect blocks UTM attribution**
The `cp.oldnavy.com/o/08ccb279...` redirect resolves to a relative path, meaning any UTM parameters appended to the final destination URL are unreachable. Attribution for clicks through this redirect is broken end-to-end.

**[UNVERIFIED] CTA and product link UTM coverage**
All 23 click-tracking links were skipped by QA. UTM parameter presence on denim product pages, category links, and promotional CTAs cannot be confirmed.
