# You've officially got 50% off everything* (new arrivals TOO!)
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-28**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "Haul Into Fall" on a purple flat-lay background, "50% OFF EVERYTHING! Even New Arrivals" — I've now seen some variant of this exact promise five times in five days from Old Navy, including this same "even new arrivals TOO" hook yesterday on 7/27. At this point my brain just files it under "Old Navy is always 50% off" and stops treating the number as news, which honestly undercuts their own math.
- What actually got me to pause scrolling was the plain white section further down — "New In. Made For Fall. The styles you've been waiting for are here" — with real lifestyle shots of a woman in an olive sweater and striped popover. That's the first moment in the email that feels like an actual outfit and not a clearance bin. Compare that to how Hanna Andersson opens their welcome email: clean hero, one clear offer, no fatigue because they don't email me five times a week. Old Navy's volume model is the opposite of Hanna's "whisper" — and I stock up at Old Navy for exactly that reason (cheap basics in bulk), but this cadence is making we want to mute rather than shop.
- I would not click. I've already used my Old Navy 50%-off run this month on tees and the $16 Wow jeans from the 7/25 email — I don't need a sixth prompt. I'd scroll past this one, maybe screenshot the clearance-from-$3.99 block for a friend doing a baby registry, but I wouldn't forward the email itself.

## What's working

- The purple "Haul Into Fall" hero with kids' flat-lay (flannel, corduroy, cardigans) is a fast, legible signal that this is family/kids inventory, which is my exact shopping lane.
- The 6-tile grid (All Tees, All Jeans, All Activewear, All Pants, All Dresses, All Sweatshirts & Hoodies — all 50% off) breaks the single vague offer into concrete categories I can actually jump to, which is more useful than one giant banner.
- The "New In. Made For Fall." section with real model photography (olive sweater, striped top, black boots) is the one spot that reads premium instead of blowout-bin — a nice change of pace from pure discount messaging.
- Category nav row (Girls, Boys, Baby Girls, Baby Boys, Women, Men, Maternity, Toddler Boys) up top is genuinely useful for a uniform-shopper like me trying to jump straight to kids' sizes.
- Free shipping banner at the very top (orders $50+) is visible without hunting for it.

## What's weak

- This is the fifth "50% off everything" send in five days (7/24, 7/25 x2, 7/26, and 7/27's near-identical "You've earned it" version) — the repetition is actively training me to ignore the subject line entirely.
- The clearance section ("Lots of Summer Items at Great Deals," starting from $3.99) is a mess of six random products — a bra, tank tops, tees — with no sizing or fit context, just prices stacked in a grid. Feels like an afterthought bolted onto the bottom.
- Nothing in this email acknowledges I'm a repeat shopper — no "welcome back," no loyalty-points callout, no Encore Member status shown even though there's an Encore Member banner at the very top pushing we to join.
- Zero kids'-specific product photography past the hero flat-lay — the "New In" spotlight section is 100% adult womenswear, which is a miss for a brand whose subject line leans hard on volume/family shopping.
- The purple-on-purple pricing tiles (50% OFF text on maroon background) are low contrast and start to blur together after the third tile — my eye glazes over instead of scanning them.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the daily "50% off everything" repeat send.** Five nearly identical emails in five days is the single biggest problem here — no copy tweak fixes fatigue. Space these out or rotate in a genuinely different hook (new arrivals only, one category spotlight) so the offer still feels like news when it lands.
- 2. **Move the "New In. Made For Fall." section up, above the pricing grid.** It's the best creative in the email and it's buried two full scrolls down — lead with product, not another discount tile.
- 3. **Add kids' product shots to the New In section**, not just adult womenswear — the subject line promises "everything," but the actual lifestyle photography ignores the audience most likely to open a "haul" email with kids in the hero image.
- 4. **Fix the clearance grid** — add basic size/fit labels or at least category labels (not just "Sleep Cami Tank Top" with a price) so it doesn't read like a random liquidation dump.
- 5. **Surface Encore Member status/points if I'm a member** — right now the top banner is a generic join-now ad, not a "you have $X in rewards" callout, so it treats me like a stranger even if I'm not one.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your kids' fall closet, half off (new arrivals included)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New sweaters, cords, and flannels just landed — all 50% off through Sunday.`
