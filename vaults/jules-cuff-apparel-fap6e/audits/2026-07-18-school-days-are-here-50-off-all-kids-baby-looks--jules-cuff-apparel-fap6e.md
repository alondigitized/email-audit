# School days are here 🚌 50% OFF ALL kids & baby looks*
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- Finally. After four days of dresses and tanks and dresses again, Old Navy figured out what July actually means for me: school runs. A 50% off kids and baby subject line is basically a Bat-Signal for anyone stocking a backpack right now. I opened this one fast.
- But then I scroll and the email is doing too many things at once. There's a back-to-school kids section up top — fine, great — and then the Low Rise Wide Leg section reappears from the July 15 "Behind the Seams" send. That editorial jean story was a nice moment; here it just feels stapled on. The clearance $3.99 pricing is a genuine find if I can see what's actually available, but the layout at thumbnail scale makes it hard to parse which items are kids vs. adult.
- I'd click on the kids section — 50% off everything is the Old Navy semi-annual energy I show up for — but I'm doing triage in my head while I scroll, separating the stuff my kids can wear from the Low Rise content that has nothing to do with school shopping. ## What's working

- **50% OFF ALL kids & baby** in the subject is exactly the semi-annual language I respond to — no math required, the value is obvious
- **2 DAYS ONLY** urgency framing at the top is visible and credible; it changes my behavior (I'll act this weekend, not next weekend)
- **$3.99 clearance block** is a standout anchor price — that number makes me stop scrolling regardless of what's in the tray
- **Backpacks and accessories imagery** is seasonally sharp; the colorful product spread reads back-to-school without being cliché about it
- **Sender name is "Old Navy Back to School"** — segmented display name signals this email is *for me as a parent*, not just a mass blast

## What's weak

- **Low Rise Wide Leg section is a copy-paste from July 15** — I already read that editorial; re-inserting it here mid-back-to-school email is lazy and breaks the narrative
- **Kids vs. adult items aren't visually separated** — at a glance I can't tell if the clearance grid is kids sizing or women's, which means I have to do work the email should do for me
- **No baby callout in the body** — the subject says "kids & baby" but I don't see a visible baby section; that's a promise the email doesn't deliver on scroll
- **Email is long** — by the time I hit the Low Rise section, I've mentally checked out of the back-to-school mission; the blend dilutes both messages
- **No loyalty / Navyist point balance shown** — at 50% off, knowing I'm also earning points would seal the deal; Hanna Andersson's welcome email surfaced the 20% code upfront, Old Navy buries any membership perks

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the Low Rise section entirely from this send.** It had its moment on July 15. Keeping this email 100% kids & baby makes the value proposition airtight and respects the subject line promise.
- 2. **Add a visible baby section with a hero image** — the subject line says it, the body should show it. A single carousel row of bodysuits and leggings would land.
- 3. **Label clearance thumbnails with sizing range** (e.g., "sizes 2T–12") so I can qualify items in two seconds without clicking.
- 4. **Surface Navyist perks or a bonus point event** — "earn 3x points this weekend" turns a 50%-off into a no-brainer stock-up.
- 5. **Trim the email to two sections** (hero offer + clearance) so it reads in under 30 seconds on a phone.
- **Subject Alt A:** `50% off every kids & baby look — stock up now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Back to school haul: 50% off ALL kids + $3.99 clearance`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `2 days only — grab the whole school-year lineup before it's gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Baby to big-kid: everything half off, plus clearance from $3.99`

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken unsubscribe/tracking redirect — confirmed broken**

The Cordial pixel URL resolves to a `/g/` path that returns an unknown URL type error at click time:

```
https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/a6ac94d3-ffb0-4c29-930e-5c36289759b3?mi_mid=...
  → /g/a6ac94d3-ffb0-4c29-930e-5c36289759b3?txn=83511e00-8297-11f1-a418-22bb2a244d04
  → [FAIL] unknown url type
```

This is the same URL used as the open-tracking pixel (`<img width="1" height="1">`). A broken redirect at the tracking layer means open events may not be recorded, and any downstream Cordial journey triggered by this signal would not fire.

**[WARN] 23 tracking links skipped** — all click links route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. These were not probed, so destination URL validity is unconfirmed.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] 1x1 tracking pixel missing `alt` text**


Empty string `alt=""` is present (correct for decorative/tracking images), so this is not a hard failure. However, the QA flag is technically correct — screen readers in strict modes may surface this. Acceptable as-is for a tracking pixel.

**No other rendering issues found.** Responsive breakpoint at `max-width: 639px` is correctly structured. Outlook VML conditionals and `mso-line-height-rule` overrides are present. `.ExternalClass` Hotmail/Outlook.com resets are in place.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent** — not captured by AgentMail relay. Gmail and Apple Mail require this header for one-click unsubscribe; its absence or relay-stripping risks the email being flagged or suppressed by Gmail's bulk sender enforcement (effective Feb 2024, mandatory for >5k/day senders).

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent** — one-click unsubscribe (POST method) not confirmed. Required by Gmail bulk sender policy.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected in visible HTML** — CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address. This may be in the untruncated footer; cannot confirm from source provided. If absent, this is a hard CAN-SPAM violation.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header absent** — SPF/DKIM pass status unknown. Sending domain `email.oldnavy.com` should have both aligned; inability to confirm through the relay is a monitoring gap, not necessarily a live failure.
