# This is HUGE: Here's FIFTY PERCENT OFF EVERYTHING* (even new arrivals) + $16 Wow jeans
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-26**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "This is HUGE" and "50% OFF EVERYTHING* (even new arrivals)" got my thumb to stop scrolling for a second — that's a real number, and the asterisk-caveat is at least visible in the subject instead of buried three screens deep. But once I'm actually in the email, this is the fourth Old Navy send in three days doing the exact same $9 Kids/$16 Adults denim math I already saw on 7/25 twice. "Hout Into Fall / 50% OFF EVERYTHING" as the giant hero banner is honestly a step down from the 7/25 "DON'T WAIT ⏰" send that at least led with the jeans math up top — here the 50%-off starburst eats the first two screens before I even get to a product I can picture on my kids.
- The category picks are fine — low-rise bootys, heavyweight tee, kids fleece, baby one-piece — that's a real family assortment, not a random grab. But "OUR BEST-OF-SALE PICKS" with a $25 women's jean, a $13 men's tee, and an $8 baby onesie all crammed into one scroll reads like Old Navy dumped their whole warehouse into one email rather than picking a lane. Hanna Andersson's welcome email (8/10 in my book) knew exactly what it was selling and to whom in the first fold; this one makes me scroll past a stranger in a leather jacket before I get back to anything kid-sized.
- Verdict: I'd skim it, not click. I already bought the $16 jeans mentally on Friday. This isn't going to a friend — it's the same trick with a bigger discount slapped on top.

## What's working

- The $9 Kids/Toddler and $16 Adult jeans callout is still the clearest, most concrete offer in the whole email — same winning formula from 7/25's "DON'T WAIT" send.
- Family casting is present across the email — kids in fleece, a toddler in stripes, adult models in denim — so at least I can picture this on my actual household, unlike a single-demographic brand blast.
- "Kids Favorite Fleece... thousands of reviews" is a real trust cue, something Old Navy doesn't always bother with.
- Clearance from $3.99 at the bottom is genuinely a strong closer for stock-up mode, if anyone scrolls that far.

## What's weak

- I've now gotten near-identical "$16 Wow jeans" hero blocks four times this week — 7/24's sweatshirt duo, then 7/25's "Not a drill" and "DON'T WAIT," and now this one just wraps the same jeans deal in a bigger 50%-off badge. The repetition is the story here, not the discount.
- The 50%-off starburst graphic dominates the top two screens with zero specific product attached to it — that's exactly the "offer feels honest" trap; Sephora would never lead with a blob-shaped badge and nothing to click.
- "OUR BEST-OF-SALE PICKS" jumbles women's, men's, kids, and baby into one undifferentiated scroll with no section headers — I have to hunt for the kids stuff I actually came for.
- No loyalty/rewards-member math anywhere, even though this is clearly a big-swing promo email where member pricing would've been an easy add — Hanna Andersson's welcome series at least gave me a code tied to my inbox.
- The asterisk on "50% OFF EVERYTHING*" is never resolved anywhere visible in the render — I'd have to click through just to find out what's excluded, which reads as bait-and-switch by the time I've scrolled this far.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop reusing the exact $9/$16 jeans hero for a fourth send this week — if the denim deal is the anchor, fine, but change the creative around it so it doesn't read as a copy-paste from Friday.
- 2. Move one concrete, kid-specific product (the fleece or the onesie) into the hero instead of the generic 50%-off starburst — give me something to picture in the first screen, not just a badge.
- 3. Add section dividers to "Best-of-Sale Picks" (Women's / Men's / Kids / Baby) so I can skip straight to my category instead of scrolling past a stranger's leather jacket.
- 4. Resolve the asterisk near the top — even a one-line "excludes clearance & Premium" builds more trust than making me guess.
- 5. Add a rewards-member callout or bonus stacking on top of the 50% — right now this is indistinguishable from a stranger's inbox blast.
