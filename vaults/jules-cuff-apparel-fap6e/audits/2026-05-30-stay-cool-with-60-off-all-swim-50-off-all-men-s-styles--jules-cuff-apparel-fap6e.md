# Stay cool with 60% off all swim* + 50% off all men's styles*
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- Thirteen. I counted. This is the thirteenth Old Navy email in roughly two weeks, and I will say this: at least this one has the decency to show me something new. The 60% off swim is a real escalation from the 50% everything they've been running on repeat — that caught my eye because we are genuinely heading into summer and my kids destroy a swimsuit by August. But then the email pivots hard to men's styles in the middle, which is... fine? My husband shops for himself. I don't manage his Old Navy wardrobe.
- What saves this from a quick archive is the kids and baby clearance section buried at the bottom. That is *actually* my lane. I stock up on Old Navy staples at semi-annual events — this feels adjacent to that. The "2 DAYS ONLY" banner at the top at least gives me a reason to scroll now rather than later.
- Would I click? Yes — specifically to the swim section, and then to clearance. Would I forward to a friend? No. This is functional, not exciting. It's the grocery store circular of emails.

## What's working

- **60% off swim is a concrete, escalated number** — after two weeks of 50% messaging, a 60% signal actually registers as a step up and feels worth opening
- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" at the top** creates real pressure without emoji-stacking; it's visually loud but not cheesy
- **Kids & Baby Clearance section exists** — even buried in the lower half, this is the content I actually came for; as an Old Navy volume shopper, clearance is where I live
- **Summer swim visual** is seasonally dead-on for late May; the category matches what I'm actually thinking about right now
- **Layout renders cleanly** — no broken images, no overlapping text, the hierarchy flows even if it's busy

## What's weak

- **Men's is half the subject line but I don't shop his clothes from my inbox** — framing the email as a split swim-plus-men's story dilutes the relevance immediately; feel like I'm holding a circular addressed to two different households
- **The kids section is hidden below the fold** — for a family shopper, burying the most relevant category in third position is a hierarchy fail; Hanna Andersson would never deprioritize kids content on their own site
- **Asterisks on both headline offers** (*swim** and *men's**) signal exclusions before I've even clicked; yesterday's 50% off everything felt more generous on its face
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — even if the offer is good, I am fighting the instinct to assume this is the same email reskinned because it has arrived *thirteen times*

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the family angle, not the gender split.** If swim + kids clearance are both live, the hero should say so in one breath — "Beat the heat: 60% off swim for the whole family." That is the version that stops me mid-scroll.
- 2. **Move kids & baby clearance above men's.** For the segment of subscribers who bought kids items in the last 90 days, that section should be second, not third. A simple re-sort by purchase behavior would lift family CTR noticeably.
- 3. **Drop one of the offers from the subject.** "60% off swim + 50% off men's" is two asks in one line. Pick the stronger hook — swim for summer urgency — and let the rest live in body copy.
- 4. **Name the deadline explicitly** — "2 DAYS ONLY" is fine in the hero, but surface the end date (June 1? June 2?) in the subject or preheader so I can decide to act now vs. lunch tomorrow.
- **Subject Alt A:** `60% off swim — 2 days only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Summer swim deals: 60% off, ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus kids & baby clearance is live — stock up before it's gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Kids clearance + 60% off swim. Two days to grab it.`
