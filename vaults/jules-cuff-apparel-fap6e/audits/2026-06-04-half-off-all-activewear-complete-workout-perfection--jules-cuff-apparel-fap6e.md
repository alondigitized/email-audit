# HALF OFF all activewear* — complete workout perfection
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- Twenty. That's the number in my head before I even tapped this open — twenty Old Navy emails in roughly two weeks, and this one is pitching me activewear. I'm a size-run buyer who uses Old Navy for school uniforms and the semi-annual haul, not because I'm rebuilding my gym bag. The "2 DAYS ONLY!" badge and the 50% off are hard to miss — the hierarchy is genuinely clean at the top. But then the email keeps going. And going. Dresses, tech-fit, clearance, more clearance. By the time I hit the bottom section I've forgotten what we were even talking about.
- Would I click? No — not for activewear. Maybe if I was personally in a "refresh my leggings" moment, but I'm not, and nothing in this email knows that. It's a category I could shop at any of a dozen places and Old Navy hasn't given me a reason to pick them for it specifically. Hanna Andersson's welcome email last month — clean, one hero, one offer, felt like it was written for me. This feels like it was written for a spreadsheet.
- Mark as read. Not forward.

## What's working

- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" urgency badge with 50% off is immediately legible** — no hunting, no fine print hiding the number up top.
- **Hero visual hierarchy actually lands** — eye goes to the offer before it goes to the product, which is the right order.
- **Colorful activewear photography feels energetic and seasonal** — bright pinks and oranges read as summer, not a stale inventory flush.
- **"NEW TECH FIT" section gives the activewear story a specific product angle** — that's more than the usual "shop now" void.
- **Clearance callout at the bottom is a clear secondary offer** — it's sectioned off well enough that it doesn't compete with the hero.

## What's weak

- **This is email twenty in two weeks** — the urgency language has been crying wolf since mid-May and "2 DAYS ONLY" no longer reads as a real deadline.
- **Activewear is not my reason for opening Old Navy email** — I'm here for kids basics and the semi-annual, and this email has zero signal for either.
- **The email is extremely long with too many distinct sections** — activewear → dresses → new tech → clearance → more clearance. Pick one story and tell it.
- **No loyalty or reward-points callout visible** — Old Navy has a rewards program and this email behaves like it doesn't exist; Hanna Andersson led with a 20% code in their welcome email and that's the bar now.
- **The asterisk on "all activewear\*" with no visible clarification** — I know there are exclusions, but making me hunt for them on the landing page is a small friction that adds up across twenty sends.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the email in half** — activewear gets one hero, one CTA, done. The clearance section is a different email on a different day or a different audience segment. Old Navy sends so often that splitting offers across sends would actually feel like less noise, not more.
- 2. **Surface the rewards math** — "Earn X NavyCash on this purchase" is the one line that makes me feel like a known customer rather than an anonymous inbox hit. Hanna Andersson opens cleaner than this, but Old Navy has the loyalty currency advantage and never uses it.
- 3. **Give me a family activewear or kids-workout angle** — matching kids' leggings, graphic tees for outdoor play, anything that hooks into the family-shopping mindset. The current models are adult women only and that's a missed swing when your customer base skews heavily toward parents.
- 4. **Slow the cadence or segment the list** — someone who buys exclusively kids basics shouldn't be getting adult activewear sends at the same volume as someone who browses women's workwear. Target, for all its problems, does this better.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your gym bag is calling — 50% off activewear, 2 days only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Half off activewear: leggings, sports bras, the works`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Brightest colors of the season, half the price. Ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New Tech Fit + 50% off — this offer moves fast.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Tracking pixel missing `alt` attribute**

```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/b5c3e8e3-..." width="1" height="1">
```

The pixel has no `alt=""`. While invisible by design, screen readers may announce the src URL. Add `alt=""` (empty string) to suppress it explicitly.

**CSS class duplication — low risk, note for maintainability**

`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice in the responsive block with different property values (5px and 10px respectively). The second declaration wins, silently overriding the first. This is a CSS authoring bug; any template component relying on the 5px variant will silently render at 10px on mobile.

**Outlook line-height override is aggressive**

The MSO conditional sets `td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }` globally, then relies on per-component overrides to restore correct spacing. This is a known pattern but increases fragility — any new content block added without an explicit `line-height` will collapse in Outlook 2013+.

**XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype**

The document declares `XHTML 1.0 Transitional`, which is the correct historical choice for broad email client compatibility and not an issue per se — but `<meta>` elements inside `<div>` (the Schema.org block) are invalid in this doctype and may be dropped by strict parsers.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**

The QA relay did not capture a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Gmail and Yahoo Mail (since February 2024 bulk sender requirements) require this header for senders at volume. Its absence — even if present on the originating MTA but stripped by the AgentMail relay — means the one-click unsubscribe button in Gmail will not render, increasing spam complaint rate.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058)**

Required for Gmail's native one-click unsubscribe UI. Without it, Gmail degrades to a redirect-based flow, which has significantly lower completion rates and does not satisfy the 2024 bulk sender mandate for >5k/day senders.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**

CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email. The truncated source does not show a footer address block. If absent from the full HTML, this is a CAN-SPAM violation. If present but not captured in the truncated source, flag as unverified.

**Note on header absence:** All three compliance warnings may be artifacts of the AgentMail relay stripping headers before QA inspection rather than the originating send lacking them. Verify against raw SMTP headers from the MTA, not the relay-processed copy.
