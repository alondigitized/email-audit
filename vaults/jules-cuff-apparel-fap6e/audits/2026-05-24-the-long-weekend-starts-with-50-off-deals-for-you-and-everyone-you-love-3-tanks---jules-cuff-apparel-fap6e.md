# The long weekend starts with 50% OFF deals (for you and everyone you love) + $3 tanks & $2.50 flag tees in-store
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-24**
## Executive summary

- Ninth Old Navy email in less than a week, and I genuinely laughed when I saw the subject — "for you and everyone you love" — like they're trying to wring one more tap out of me before the long weekend ends. I opened it because the $3 tanks and $2.50 flag tees pulled me again. Same muscle-memory open as yesterday's send, same as the day before.
- The creative is fine. 50% OFF DEALS hero with the family lineup, the red "2 DAYS ONLY" sidebar, the $3 tank pop-out, the $2.50 flag tee block. Nothing new. This is literally the same campaign as the 5/24 evening send I just rated 8/10 — I think they may have actually sent me the same email twice today with a tiny copy tweak.
- Would I click? No. I already mentally shopped this sale yesterday. Forward? No. Mark as read and move on — I'm at sale fatigue, and the only reason I'm not unsubscribing is because I know I'll want the back-to-school uniform restock in August.

## What's working

- Price ladder right in the hero — $3 tanks, $2.50 flag tees — that's the only thing that earns the open at this cadence.
- "2 DAYS ONLY" red callout creates a credible deadline tied to Memorial Day weekend.
- Family lineup imagery — kids, adults, matching summer vibe — actually signals "for everyone you love" without being cloying.
- Clean visual hierarchy: hero, sidebar deal, price ladder, then category grid. Eye lands where it should.

## What's weak

- This is functionally a re-send of the earlier 5/24 email. Hanna Andersson would never burn me twice in a day with the same creative.
- The "for you and everyone you love" wink is trying too hard — it's mass tone pretending to be personal, and I clocked it instantly.
- Nothing new below the hero. No fresh story, no styling angle, no "here's what's new for the weekend" — just the same grid I've seen four times this week.
- No loyalty/Navyist callout. After nine emails, throw me a member-only bone — Sephora would have layered Beauty Insider points on top of every offer by now.
- Subject line is 100+ characters. My phone truncates it well before the "$2.50 flag tees" payoff.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Stop sending the same hero creative twice in 12 hours.** If you must double-send, change the angle — lead with kids' tanks in the AM, women's dresses in the PM. Don't carbon-copy.
- 2. **Trim the subject.** Lead with the cheapest, most specific number. Everything after the first 45 characters is wasted on mobile.
- 3. **Add a Navyist line.** "Stack member rewards on every $3 tank" earns one more open from regulars like me.
- 4. **Drop the "for you and everyone you love" wink.** It reads like a Hallmark card and undercuts the price-driven urgency.
- 5. **Tease something new.** A "first look at July 4th tees" block below the fold would give the next email a reason to exist.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Memorial Day final call: 50% off + $3 tanks`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 50% off sitewide. Navyists earn 2x points on every order.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `In-store doorbusters end Monday. Stack with member rewards.`
