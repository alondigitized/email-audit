## Executive summary

- Okay, this one actually landed for me. Kids AND adults denim in one hero, both price points visible in the same block ($9 for Kids & Toddler, $16 for Adults) — that's the exact math I'm doing every August before school starts, and Old Navy did it for me above the fold. This is a real step up from "Find his perfect pair" last week, which told me in the first three seconds it wasn't my email.
- The "2 DAYS ONLY" urgency plus the actual product grid underneath (all those wash/cut thumbnails with real prices) is doing the job — I can see boot cut, the styles, the price ladder from $22 up. Then it nosedives into TrekTech athleisure with a guy in a crop top and pleather pants and matching pink "PowerSoft sports bra and layered run shorts" — cute for someone, not remotely what I opened this email for. That whole middle third feels bolted on from a totally different campaign.
- Would I click? Yes, on the jeans hero specifically — probably straight to the Kids & Toddler grid since that's where the $9 doorbuster is. Would I forward it? Not to a friend, but I might screenshot just the top block for my sister who also double-dresses two kids for fall. The TrekTech section I'd scroll straight past.

## What's working

- Kids AND adults denim pricing side-by-side in the hero — that's my actual shopping list, not a guess.
- "2 DAYS ONLY" with a real deadline reads as credible urgency, not the vague "hurry" language.
- The denim grid below the hero shows real product thumbnails, washes, and specific prices ($22+), so I'm not clicking into a mystery landing page.
- Category nav pills (Womens, Girls, Toddler Girls, Boys, Toddler Boys) right under the grid make it easy to jump to my kids' sizes fast.
- "Jeans From $22" as a second denim block reinforces the category instead of one-and-done.

## What's weak

- The TrekTech section hijacks the middle of the email — matching pink athleisure sets with a model in latex-look leggings has nothing to do with the "Wow jeans" premise I opened for.
- Clearance from $3.99 at the bottom is a third unrelated offer crammed into an email that already has two denim CTAs — it dilutes rather than adds.
- Visual tone whiplash: clean blue "2 Days Only" hero → neon gradient streetwear shoot → red clearance banner. Nothing about the design says this is one cohesive send.
- No loyalty/Navyist member callout anywhere, even though the top strip mentions "Become an Encore Member" — a missed chance to remind existing members what they already get.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the TrekTech block entirely from this send — it's a different shopping mission (athleisure vs. back-to-school denim) and it buries the actual offer under scroll fatigue. Give it its own email.
- 2. Move the category-specific denim grids (Girls/Boys/Toddler) higher, right under the hero, since that's the actual decision point for someone like me — right now I have to scroll past the intro before I even see my kid's size.
- 3. Add a member-price callout next to the $9/$16 price if Encore members get anything extra — right now "Join for Free shipping" up top and the jeans deal feel disconnected.
- 4. Drop the clearance section or fold it into a single "more ways to save" strip instead of a full standalone red banner — three competing CTAs is two too many.
