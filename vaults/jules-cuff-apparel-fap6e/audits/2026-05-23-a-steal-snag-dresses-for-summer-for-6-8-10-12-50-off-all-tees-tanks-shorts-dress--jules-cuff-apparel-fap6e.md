# A steal! Snag dresses for summer for $6, $8, $10 & $12 + 50% off all tees, tanks, shorts, dresses & swim
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- This is Old Navy's seventh email in roughly four days, and my thumb is honestly starting to drift toward the unsubscribe button on muscle memory alone. The creative is clean — MAJOR DRESS SALE hero, price ladder front and center, kids tees buried but present — but I've seen this exact frame three times already this week. Yesterday's "ON SALE NOW" sent the same $6/$8/$10/$12 price ladder with a slightly different intro line. The day before that, same ladder. I'm not discovering a sale; I'm watching Old Navy hit snooze on the same alarm clock.
- That said, the email itself is doing the job. The price points are my trigger — I genuinely stock Old Navy dresses like they're a consumable, and $10 on a summer dress is hard to scroll past even at peak fatigue. The clearance strip at the bottom is a nice secondary hook for someone who always checks the sale rack first. The wide-leg pants "Summer Blast" block in the middle is where I checked out; that's not why I'm here.
- I'd mark this as read, but honestly I already clicked through on Thursday's version. I'm not clicking again on what is functionally the same email. I would not forward this — a friend would find it repetitive if she's also on the Old Navy list.

## What's working

- **Price ladder is above the fold and instant.** $6, $8, $10, $12 as a visual staircase — I can parse the offer in under two seconds without reading copy.
- **Kids tees callout is present.** It's mid-email but visible; "KID TEES 50% OFF" matters to me in a way that a purely adult email doesn't.
- **No render bugs.** Layout is clean, images loaded, text didn't overlap — Hanna Andersson's welcome email had better white space but this is perfectly functional.
- **Clearance section at the bottom.** Smart add-on; I always scroll to clearance. It's the one section that feels like something new relative to the previous sends.
- **Lifestyle photography is summer-appropriate.** The model in the white sundress and the denim shots feel like the right season at the right time.

## What's weak

- **Seventh email in four days with the same creative spine.** The hero, the price ladder, the 50% offer — I've internalized this. Hanna Andersson sends me one well-crafted email and I trust them. Old Navy is sending me seven and I'm starting to trust them less.
- **Subject line is 104 characters.** On my phone, this truncates to noise after "for $6, $8" — the 50% off tees and tanks, which is genuinely useful, is invisible until I open it. The "$6 DRESSES" subject from May 21st was tighter and worked harder.
- **No visible deadline in the email body.** "Long weekend" was in the subject last time; this one drops that. I don't know if this ends Sunday or next Thursday, which softens the urgency that was the whole point.
- **"A steal!" opener is throwaway.** It's a hollow energy-word that doesn't earn the premium end of the price range. It signals mass blast, not a send that knows me.
- **Wide-leg pants "Summer Blast" section dilutes the message.** I came here for dresses and kids basics. The lifestyle denim block makes this feel like a catalog page, not a focused offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut email frequency immediately.** Even dropping to every other day would reset my tolerance. The offer doesn't get stronger by showing up again — it gets cheaper.
- 2. **Subject line needs surgery.** Under 50 characters, lead with the hook that moves me:
- **Subject Alt A:** `$6 dresses + 50% off summer — this weekend only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Kids & dresses: $6, $8, $10, $12 — ends Sunday`
- 3. **Add a hard deadline in the hero.** "Ends Monday at midnight" — three words that would convert the casually browsing me who already clicked Thursday's version.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dresses from $6, kids tees half off — sale ends Sunday night`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your Memorial Day haul: $6 dresses, $10 dresses, 50% off kids`
- 4. **Elevate the kids section.** Move "KID TEES 50% OFF" up to the second block. That's why a family shopper like me opens Old Navy emails at all — and it's currently buried behind the adult wide-leg moment.
- 5. **Cut the "Summer Blast" lifestyle block or replace it with a clearance preview.** The clearance strip at the bottom is more compelling to me than the jeans shoot. Swap the order.
