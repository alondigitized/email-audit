# ATTN: $5 tees + new activewear for summer
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-04**
## Executive summary

- Nine days into what I have officially renamed the Old Navy Summer Grind, and I open my inbox on the Fourth of July to find... another $5 tee email. The "ATTN:" in the subject is doing the heavy lifting of admitting they know I've been skimming past them. The thing is, it still works on me a little — I genuinely need to stock up before back-to-school hits in six weeks, and $5 is still $5. The "3 DAYS ONLY" tee grid at the top is fast, colorful, and legible at a glance. That part? Fine.
- But then I scroll and it falls apart. We go from tees to activewear ("Hot New Gear," which I'll allow for July), then to "VIP Lounge," and then — I promise this is real — to tall brown boots. Knee-high boots. On July 4th. Someone stuck a fall campaign module in a summer email and nobody caught it, and now I'm thinking about snow before I've even eaten a hot dog. The email reads like four separate briefs got stitched together in the last 20 minutes before send.
- Would I click? Probably on the tees section, but only because I'd already budgeted for this. Would I forward it? Absolutely not. This is a "mark as read and loop back on my laptop" email. Compared to the July 2nd $5 biker shorts send — which had one message, one model, one tight subject line — this one is sprawling and unfocused.

## What's working

- The tee grid hero with $5/$6/$8 price anchors communicates the entire offer in under two seconds — no reading required
- Color variety across the tee lineup does the work of saying "we have your color" without a single word of copy
- "Hot New Gear" activewear section is seasonally honest and the model reads as millennial female, which is me
- VIP Lounge module quietly reminds me I have rewards I haven't used — that's a smart loyalty nudge buried in a decent spot
- Email renders cleanly with no broken images or layout issues

## What's weak

- Knee-high boots in a July 4th email is the single most disorienting thing I've seen in this audit run — it signals the segmentation is off and makes me trust the "summer" framing less
- "ATTN:" as a subject opener is a panic move — it reads exactly like a brand that knows its open rates are tanking after the fifth identical send
- No kids or family signal anywhere visible; I'm buying tees for two kids and the entire hero skews adult — Hanna Andersson's welcome email knew exactly who I was by the second fold, Old Navy doesn't know at all here
- Four competing sections mean no clear hero — tees, activewear, VIP Lounge, and boots are all asking for equal attention, and none gets it
- "3 DAYS ONLY" urgency has shown up so many times this week that it no longer registers as urgent

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the boots module entirely** — off-season content in a July email tanks trust faster than any discount can rebuild it; if fall product needs visibility, build a separate segmented send
- 2. **Add a kids tee callout under the adult hero** — even a single "shop kids" text link would tell me this email knows I have a family; Old Navy's biggest advantage over Hanna Andersson is volume basics for kids, and this email wastes it
- 3. **Retire "ATTN:" forever** — there are a hundred ways to open a subject line that don't telegraph desperation; the July 2nd biker shorts send proved they can do better
- 4. **Give the activewear section a price anchor** — "from $X" keeps the value message consistent across the whole email instead of making it feel like brand filler
- 5. **Cut to two sections max**: tees as hero, activewear as the one secondary beat, loyalty in the footer as a badge
- **Subject Alt A:** `$5 tees for the whole family — 3 days only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Stock up now: $5 kids' + adults' tees before school`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New activewear drops too — but the tees go fast`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Kids' and adults' tees from $5, through Sunday only`

## Technical Audit — Old Navy "$5 tees + new activewear for summer"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email renders on a standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional + VML stack with responsive CSS breakpoints; core layout is functional. The primary technical concerns are a broken tracking/personalization pixel, missing compliance infrastructure, and a plain-text body that is nearly entirely URLs.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Broken pixel URL (cp.oldnavy.com)**
The Contentsquare/personalization pixel resolves to an internal redirect path `/g/97dd76b6-...` that the HTTP client cannot handle (`unknown url type: '/g/...'`). The pixel fires on open but the redirect chain terminates with an unresolvable relative-path response.

- Evidence: `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/97dd76b6-b4c1-4685-a7cd-9305ccc8e740?mi_mid=...`
- Impact: open-event personalization data (likely Movable Ink or similar) is not being recorded correctly for this send.

**INFO — 23 click-redirect links skipped**
All CTA links route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. Not a defect, but final destination URLs could not be probed; UTM parameter validation below is therefore limited to what is visible in the HTML source.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**WARN — Missing `alt` text on tracking pixel**
The cp.oldnavy.com `<img>` pixel has no `alt` attribute:
```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/97dd76b6-..." width="1" height="1">
```
Screen readers will announce the full URL. Should be `alt=""` (empty string, not absent).

**INFO — Outlook conditional CSS sets `td { line-height: 0px }`**
The MSO block applies `line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly` globally to all `td` elements in Outlook 2013+. This is a known Outlook spacing fix but will suppress any `td`-level line-height overrides set elsewhere unless they use `mso-line-height-rule: at-least`.

**INFO — Duplicate media query class selectors**
`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice in the responsive block (comment in source acknowledges this). No functional breakage, but dead CSS weight.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unfired merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `[FNAME]`) are visible in the truncated source. The schema.org `Organization` block is statically populated — no dynamic fields. No issues found from what is visible.

---

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM / RFC 8058)

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
Not detected by the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to surface the native unsubscribe button; its absence increases spam classification risk and violates Google/Yahoo 2024 bulk sender requirements for senders >5k/day.

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe (POST-based) is not supported. Required by Google/Yahoo bulk sender policy since February 2024.

**WARN — No physical mailing address detected**
CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in the message body. The HTML source truncation may exclude the footer, but the QA check found no address. Needs verification against full source.

**WARN — `Authentication-Results` header not captured**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown through the AgentMail relay. Cannot confirm `oldnavy@email.oldnavy.com` is DMARC-aligned with `oldnavy.com`.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All CTAs pass through `click.email.oldnavy.com` redirects, so UTM parameters cannot be inspected directly from the source. The redirect chain for the broken cp.oldnavy.com pixel terminates before resolving — any personalization or A/B test assignment tied to that open event is lost. No misfired destination URLs are confirmed, but 23 links were untestable.
