# 📣 COZY DEAL ALERT: $16 sweatshirts + new denim on sale from $22
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy just emailed me twice today about $16 sweatshirts. I opened the first one this morning — "The *comfiest* layers" — and now this one lands with a megaphone emoji and "COZY DEAL ALERT" like I forgot what happened six hours ago. The brand history is not working in its favor here.
- That said, once I'm inside, the email is doing more than the morning send did. The hero leads with denim at $22, there's a TODAY ONLY urgency banner with $12 and $16 price points clearly stacked, VIP LOUNGE gets called out as a loyalty beat, and the layout is clean enough that I can parse the offer fast. The $3.99 callout at the bottom made me scroll — I want to know what's that cheap and whether it's worth grabbing for the kids.
- The problem is the subject-to-hero disconnect: the subject yells sweatshirts, the email opens on jeans. And the repeat-same-day cadence on the sweatshirt angle makes the "ALERT" feel like carpet-bombing, not a heads-up. I'd mark this read and maybe click the denim section, but I'd also be mentally docking Old Navy a little goodwill. I would not forward this.

## What's working

- **TODAY ONLY! stacks $12 and $16 clearly above the fold** — I don't have to hunt for the offer, it's the first thing I clock after the hero
- **VIP LOUNGE callout is a small but real loyalty signal** — it makes me feel like there's a membership reason to pay attention, not just a blast to the whole list
- **The denim hero at $22 is new context** — yesterday's denim email ("Low Rise is back") was a vibe send; this one puts a dollar sign on it, which is the signal I actually need to add to cart
- **$3.99 at the bottom created genuine scroll curiosity** — I don't know what it is yet but I want to find out, which is rare for a fourth Old Navy email in four days
- **Clean render, no broken images** — the layout holds, the colors are bright and summer-appropriate

## What's weak

- **Second email today on the same $16 sweatshirt beat** — the morning send already burned that price point; serving it again with a megaphone emoji reads as panic, not deal-dropping
- **Subject leads with sweatshirts, hero leads with denim** — misaligned expectation from inbox to open; the denim hero is actually stronger, so why bury it?
- **"📣 COZY DEAL ALERT" is mass-brand energy, not premium-or-known energy** — Hanna Andersson's welcome email last May opened with a real human voice and 20% off; this feels like a flyer on a car windshield
- **Models read mid-20s and solo** — I'm 36 and buying for two kids; a sweatshirt-for-the-whole-family visual or even a stacked mom-and-kid callout would have made me lean in harder
- **No family or kids angle visible** — Old Navy is my volume brand for exactly this reason, but the email never acknowledges that I'm buying in multiples

## Recommendations

- 1. **Consolidate the same-day sends** — one email with both the sweatshirt and denim story, not two. The cadence erosion is the single biggest fix available; it's not a creative problem, it's a send-cadence problem.
- 2. **Lead the subject with denim and put sweatshirts as the backup beat** — denim is the fresh hook (yesterday's email built intrigue, this one can close it); sweatshirts are the $16 price-anchor
- 3. **Add a family or kids quantity signal** — "Stock up: $16 sweatshirts for the whole crew" would have me clicking faster than any model shot
- 4. **Make the $3.99 item findable above the fold** — that price point is a scroll-stopper; don't bury it at the bottom
- 5. **Drop the megaphone emoji from the subject** — "COZY DEAL ALERT" already signals this is promotional; the emoji tips it into spam-folder-adjacent
- **Subject Alt A:** `Today only: $22 jeans + $16 sweatshirts for everyone`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New denim from $22 · sweatshirts for $16 · today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stock up for the whole crew — kids' styles included, while they last`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `VIP Lounge members: your prices are already applied`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing alt text on tracking pixel**
```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/c52e8bbd-..."> <!-- no alt attribute -->
```
The Cordial pixel has no `alt=""`. While invisible pixels don't need descriptive alt text, the attribute must be present (even empty) to pass WCAG 2.1 and avoid screen-reader noise.

**[INFO] Outlook line-height conflict**
The MSO conditional style block sets `td { line-height: 0px; }` globally for Outlook 2013+, then overrides per-element. This is a known pattern but aggressive — any `td` without an explicit `mso-line-height-rule` override will collapse to zero height in Outlook. Confirm in Litmus/Email on Acid under Outlook 2016/2019.

**[INFO] `MspaceTop` / `MspaceBot` class collision**
In the responsive stylesheet, `.space-top5` and `.space-top10` both map to `.MspaceTop`, and `.space-bot5` and `.space-bot10` both map to `.MspaceBot`. The second declaration silently overrides the first — elements using the earlier class get the wrong spacing on mobile.

**[INFO] XHTML doctype with emoji subject**
The HTML is declared as `XHTML 1.0 Transitional`. The subject line contains `📣` — this is fine at the SMTP/header layer but note the HTML `<title>` also contains the emoji, which is valid in UTF-8/XHTML.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
QA did not capture a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Gmail and Yahoo require this header for bulk senders (≥5k/day) as of 2024. If the header exists at the ESP layer but was stripped by the AgentMail relay, it needs to be confirmed at the raw SMTP envelope level.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent**
One-click unsubscribe per RFC 8058 is required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy. The `POST` variant must accompany `List-Unsubscribe` with a `mailto:` or HTTPS endpoint. Absence here is a deliverability risk.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**
CAN-SPAM §7 requires a valid physical postal address in the email body. The truncated source does not show one; it may appear in the unrendered footer. This must be verified in the full HTML — failure is an FTC violation.

**[WARN] SPF/DKIM status unknown**
`Authentication-Results` header was not captured by the relay. SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status for `email.oldnavy.com` cannot be confirmed from this capture. Gap is in the relay pipeline, not necessarily a sending-side failure — but the audit trail is incomplete.
