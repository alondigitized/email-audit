# ON SALE NOW: $6, $8, $10 & $12 dresses + 50% off summer hits for the long weekend
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- Six Old Navy emails in four days — and this one landed yesterday's subject line with a different wrapper. "The sale of our summer dreams" from May 22nd was literally the same price ladder, the same long-weekend peg, just with a dreamier opener. Today I get "ON SALE NOW" in caps, and my brain parses that as Old Navy clearing its throat for the second time. I still looked. I always look when the price ladder is in the subject, because $6-$12 dresses are a household staple for me — they're consumables, not fashion moments. The hero delivers fast: big "MAJOR DRESS SALE!" headline, a clean white-dress model shot, the $10 anchor price, a 50%-off callout, and a button that's easy to find. That works. But then the email just... keeps going. Sandals, accessories, some active-wear section, an "ACE THE INVITE" resort moment, and a bottom clearance section that screams in a completely different typographic register. It's Old Navy doing what Old Navy does: yelling at full volume for the entire scroll depth.
- I'd mark it read and tab over to shop the dresses. I would not forward it to a friend — because my friend got the same email.

## What's working

- **Price ladder in the hero is the whole offer, immediately.** $6/$8/$10/$12 isn't hidden or vague — it's the reason to click, and it's front and center.
- **Clean hero layout.** White background, model in a white dress, single price callout, visible button — the eye lands in the right place without fighting the design.
- **50% off stacked on top of ladder pricing is genuinely compelling.** Two deal mechanics in one email feels additive, not confusing.
- **The clearance section at the bottom actually matters to me.** Other brands bury clearance like it's embarrassing; Old Navy stamps it in huge bold type. I respect the honesty.
- **Memorial Day/long weekend peg is credible.** Unlike last Monday's "ends soon" subject with no deadline, "long weekend" gives me an actual mental calendar anchor.

## What's weak

- **Near-duplicate of yesterday's email.** If I wanted to call out exact messaging overlap: yesterday's subject said the same price ladder + same long weekend. I notice. My spam-sense pings even when the offer is real.
- **"ACE THE INVITE" section is talking to a different shopper.** I'm buying dress inventory for summer, not planning a resort reveal. That section eats scroll real estate meant for me.
- **"ON SALE NOW" prefix is a yell that dates this email.** Hanna Andersson would never. Even Old Navy's own May 19th linen send opened with more personality ("Stay breezy 😎"). This one feels auto-generated.
- **The mid-scroll product grid loses the thread.** Sandals, accessories, bags — I'm here for dresses. The pivot from "MAJOR DRESS SALE" to a full summer lifestyle catalog fragments the message.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the freshest signal — the long weekend is now.** Instead of repeating yesterday's creative, show me something new: a specific "only 48 hours left" mechanic, a style the kids can wear too, or a clearance door-buster callout in the hero itself. Give me a reason this email exists today vs. yesterday.
- 2. **Cut the "ACE THE INVITE" section entirely or move it to a second email.** It dilutes the dress-sale message and feels like a different shopper's email grafted on.
- 3. **Kids crossover signal somewhere above the fold.** I buy for two kids and myself. One shot of a matching mom-and-kid dress moment earns me for an extra scroll.
- 4. **Trim the subject prefix.** "ON SALE NOW:" burns 12 chars and signals mass-blast. Drop it and let the price ladder open cold.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$6–$12 dresses + 50% off: long weekend stock-up starts now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Memorial Day: dress up the whole family from $6`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `48 hours. Your summer dress rotation doesn't fill itself.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Dresses from $6, sandals, the whole summer haul — tap before Tuesday.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email renders correctly on standard clients but has one broken redirect chain in a tracking pixel/preference-center URL, and several compliance headers are unverifiable through the AgentMail relay. Plain-text MIME part is URL-dominated, which is a deliverability risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken preference-center redirect**
The Liveclicker/Coherent Path pixel at `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/968babc2-5bbb-400e-86f5-558934a4e79f` resolves to an unknown URL type: `/g/968babc2-...` — the `cp.oldnavy.com` redirect handler is returning a relative `/g/` path instead of an absolute URL, causing the redirect chain to break.

- This URL appears twice: once as a 1×1 tracking pixel (hidden `<div>`) and once as a visible element. If the visible instance is a preference/loyalty link, recipients clicking it will land on a broken page.
- Evidence: QA `[FAIL] Link error: unknown url type: '/g/968babc2-...'`

**[WARN] 23 click-tracking links not probed**
All outbound links route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. HTTP resolution was skipped for these. If any campaign redirect rules are misconfigured (wrong offer codes, wrong seasonal landing page), they won't be caught here — recommend a manual spot-check of 2–3 primary CTAs against the live `oldnavy.com` sale landing pages.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing alt text on tracking image**
`<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/968babc2-...">` has no `alt` attribute at all (not even `alt=""`). Screen readers will attempt to announce the filename/URL. Should be `alt=""` to mark it as decorative/presentational.

**Outlook line-height conflict**
The MSO conditional block sets `td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }` globally. This is a known pattern but aggressive — any `<td>` containing text that doesn't override `line-height` inline will collapse to zero height in Outlook 2013+. No hard failures observed in the truncated source, but this is a latent rendering risk if new modules are added without explicit `mso-line-height-rule` overrides.

**Duplicate CSS class selectors**
`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice in the responsive block (the comment in source acknowledges this). While browsers handle duplicate rules gracefully (last wins), it's dead code that should be cleaned up.

**No dark-mode media query**
No `prefers-color-scheme: dark` handling. Not a hard failure, but images with white backgrounds will appear with harsh edges in dark-mode clients (Apple Mail, iOS Mail).

### 5. Compliance

Cannot confirm one-click unsubscribe compliance from this audit artifact alone.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected in truncated HTML**
CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a physical postal address. The HTML source provided is truncated — the address is typically in the footer table. This finding is likely a truncation artifact, not an actual absence. **Verify the full footer renders:** Old Navy's standard footer includes "Two Folsom St, San Francisco, CA 94105."
