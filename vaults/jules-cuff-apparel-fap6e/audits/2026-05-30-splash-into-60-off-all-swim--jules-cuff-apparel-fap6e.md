# Splash into 60% off all swim
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- Fourteen. That's where I am with Old Navy right now. Fourteen emails in roughly two weeks, and this is the *second* swim-focused one today — I already had "Stay cool with 60% off all swim" sitting in my inbox from this morning. "Splash into 60% off all swim" is not a new campaign. It is a renamed copy of the same deal with what looks like a different hero image. I notice, and I'm a little insulted that they think I won't.
- That said: the email itself is not a disaster. The "2 DAYS ONLY!" banner is loud and clear, the 60% hero is at the top, there's a colorful swimwear grid that actually shows the range, and the shorts section underneath is a genuine value-add for June family shopping. If I hadn't already seen this offer this morning, I might have clicked the swimwear grid because I do still need to grab the kids' swim before school's out.
- But I did see it this morning. So I'm marking this read and moving on. Wouldn't forward it — there's nothing here my friend doesn't already have in her inbox from the first send.

## What's working

- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" lands immediately** — it's the first thing I read after the logo, which is exactly where urgency should live.
- **60% is front and center** — no hunting, no scroll required, the deal is clear at a glance.
- **Swimwear color grid is smart merchandising** — showing eight-plus colorway swatches in a tight row communicates range and makes the section feel curated rather than generic.
- **Shorts season section below the hero** — adding a second category keeps the email useful even if swim isn't relevant; it reads like they thought about the full summer wardrobe.
- **Clearance section at the bottom** — I always check clearance. Dropping it in as a footer is a quiet win for value shoppers like me.

## What's weak

- **This is the second identical offer today.** Old Navy already sent "Stay cool with 60% off all swim" on the same date. One swim email per day is a choice; two is noise.
- **The 50% and 60% compete at the top of the scroll.** Two large discount percentages fighting for attention in the hero zone dilutes both — I had to look twice to understand what was 60% and what was 50%.
- **No kids swim anywhere I can see.** I shop Old Navy specifically for kids volume. Adult swimwear models with no visible children's category makes this feel like it was built for a different customer segment.
- **No Navyist rewards callout.** I'm a member. Hanna Andersson's welcome email was explicit about the 20% welcome code and what membership means — Old Navy treats me like a stranger every single send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the second same-day send, or differentiate it meaningfully.** If the morning email is adult swim and the afternoon email is kids swim, I'll open both. If they're the same offer with a different pun in the subject, I'll start filtering.
- 2. **Lead with kids swim if you know I'm a family account.** Even a single "Kids styles from $X" callout below the hero would have kept me reading longer.
- 3. **Unify the discount percentages.** Pick one story per hero — either "60% off swim" or "50% off women's" — and let the other live further down the email. Two big numbers at the top split my attention and make the offer feel less trustworthy.
- 4. **Surface the Navyist tier or cash reward.** Even a one-liner — "You've got $X in Navyist Cash to spend" — would make this feel personalized instead of batch-blasted.
- 5. **Add one kids product to the swimwear grid.** Three adult suits, one kids suit. That's enough to signal "this is for your whole family."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Kids + adult swim: 60% off, 2 days only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Stock up: 60% off all swim this weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Kids swimsuits, one-pieces, trunks — all 60% off through Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Grab sizes before they go — 60% off the full swim lineup.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken tracking pixel redirect**
The second hidden-div pixel resolves to an unrecognized path:

The `/g/` path is not a recognized scheme — likely a malformed redirect in the Sailthru/Marigold pixel chain. This pixel fires on open but silently fails, causing open-rate undercounting.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Tracking pixel missing alt text**
```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/d2014ab1-...&mi_mid=..." width="1" height="1">
```
No `alt` attribute present. While 1×1 pixels should carry `alt=""`, screen readers may announce the `src` URL fragment. Low severity but flaggable under WCAG 2.1.

**[WARN] Duplicate CSS class selectors in media query**
`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice at `@media only screen and (max-width: 639px)`. The source comment even notes this: `/* MspaceTop was duplicated, kept one */`. The second declaration silently wins; the first is dead code. No rendering breakage observed, but it's a maintenance hazard.

**[INFO] Outlook conditional `td { line-height: 0px; }`**
```css
<!--[if gte mso 9]>
td { line-height:0px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly }
```
This resets all `td` line-height to zero for Outlook 2013+. Correct usage requires every content `td` to explicitly set its own `line-height`. If any `td` lacks an inline override, text will collapse. Not confirmed broken from the truncated source, but high-risk pattern.

**[INFO]** XHTML 1.0 Transitional DOCTYPE + VML namespace — legacy but intentional and correct for broad Outlook support.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post headers absent**
QA reports neither header was captured via the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Apple Mail require `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) for one-click unsubscribe. Without it, Gmail may suppress the unsubscribe UI or route to spam. Likely a relay capture gap, but must be verified against raw SMTP headers.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**
CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email. Not present in the visible HTML fragment. If absent from the full footer, this is a statutory violation. Needs full-source confirmation.

**[WARN] Authentication-Results header not captured**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown. `oldnavy@email.oldnavy.com` sending via `email.oldnavy.com` — alignment should be verifiable but was not surfaced by the relay.
