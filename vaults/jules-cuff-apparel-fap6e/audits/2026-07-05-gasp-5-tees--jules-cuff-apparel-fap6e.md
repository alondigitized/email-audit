# GASP 😮 $5 tees
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- Here we go again. Day ten — I've lost count — of the Old Navy Summer Grind, and the subject line is literally "GASP 😮 $5 tees." The emoji is doing the emotional labor of surprise that I simply cannot provide anymore. I've opened variations of this exact email on July 4th (twice) and July 3rd and every day before that. My inbox is not gasping. I am not gasping.
- That said, I opened it anyway, because $5 tees are genuinely useful to me. I'm the mom who walks out of Old Navy with eight tees in two sizes because my kids destroy clothes faster than I can fold them — this is a real sale I actually use. The "3 DAYS ONLY!" urgency banner at the top does the necessary work; without that deadline up front, I'd have marked-as-read immediately. But what I found inside was four emails welded together: $5 tees, then $3.99 clearance, then a new activewear thing, then a "Why You Need Low Rise" denim explainer. That last section might as well have come from a stranger's inbox.
- Hanna Andersson's welcome email I audited earlier this year had exactly one job and did it with warmth and a 20% code I could apply at checkout. Clean. Focused. Earned. This send feels like someone said "throw everything in before the sale ends." I'd click the tee CTA if I had thirty seconds, but I'd forward nothing, and I'm one send away from auto-archiving Old Navy entirely.

## What's working

- **"3 DAYS ONLY!" lands first** — the urgency is front-and-center before I have to hunt for it, and that's exactly where it belongs
- **Flat-lay of colorful tees** communicates the product category instantly; no copy needed, I know what I'm shopping
- **Stacked price points** — $5 tees and $3.99 clearance are both visible early, and concrete math beats vague "up to X% off" every time for a volume shopper like me
- **Sender is trusted** — Old Navy's name and email domain pattern open on muscle memory

## What's weak

- **"GASP 😮" is the brand begging for a reaction it hasn't earned** — after the fourth near-identical subject line this week, the fake-surprise emoji reads as desperate; Gap Kids just runs "Extra 50% off sale" and doesn't apologize for it
- **Too many sections, too little focus** — tees, clearance, activewear, and a low-rise jeans editorial are four different briefs crammed into one scroll; Primary sends one product, one offer, done
- **"Why You Need Low Rise" jeans section** is a completely different email dropped in mid-scroll; I'm stocking up on kids' tees and suddenly I'm reading a denim how-to targeting a 24-year-old
- **Zero family or kids signal** — nothing in what I can see tells me this is for a household, not just me; for how I actually shop this sale, that's a miss

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill "GASP" and own the streak honestly** — ten days of $5 tees is a fact; lean into it with "Still $5. Still going." That's disarming and differentiated from fake-surprise subject lines
- 2. **Drop or separate the jeans section** — editorial denim content kills deal-mode purchase intent; it belongs in its own send or its own day
- 3. **Add a kids + adult split row** — show one kids' tee and one adult tee both at $5 so family shoppers know they can load a single cart and be done with it
- 4. **Surface a stack mechanic** — "5 for $25 across kids + adult styles" is exactly how I shop this sale and nobody has said it plainly yet
- **Subject Alt A:** `Stock up: $5 tees, 3 days left`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$5 tees + $3.99 clearance — ends soon`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Kids and adult styles. Free shipping. No code needed.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Grab 5, spend $25. Ends Monday.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing alt text on tracking pixel**

The Conversant open-tracking pixel (`cp.oldnavy.com/o/ddedf7b9...`) has `alt=""` — which is technically correct for a decorative/tracking image. However, the AgentMail-injected open pixel at `click.email.oldnavy.com/open.aspx` also has `alt=""`. Both are acceptable for 1×1 pixels; no change needed.

**[INFO] Duplicate CSS class selectors**

`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice in the responsive `<style>` block (once for 5px, once for 10px variants). The second declaration silently overrides the first. This is a minor authoring bug — whichever spacing is actually applied depends on source order, not intent. Low risk but worth cleaning in the template.

**[INFO] Outlook line-height override is aggressive**

The MSO conditional sets `td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }` globally. This is a known Outlook hack but can collapse spacing on text-only `<td>` elements that don't carry explicit height. Should be scoped to layout-only cells rather than applied globally.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post headers not confirmed**

Both headers are absent from what the relay captured. These are required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policies (Feb 2024 mandate) for senders at this volume. The QA note flags this as potentially a relay capture gap — but if the headers are genuinely absent, this is a deliverability and compliance failure. **Verify against raw SMTP headers, not just the relay-captured version.**

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**

CAN-SPAM §5(a)(6) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email. The truncated HTML source does not show one. This may exist below the fold in the footer (not included in the truncated source), but the automated checker did not find it. **Confirm the footer contains a street address before treating this as a pass.**

**[WARN] SPF/DKIM authentication status unknown**

`Authentication-Results` header not captured by relay. Sending domain is `email.oldnavy.com`; DMARC alignment requires SPF or DKIM to pass against that domain. Cannot confirm without raw headers.
