# Come see what's on sale! 60-70% OFF mystery deals for the fam
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- Day six — or maybe seven, I've lost count — of the Old Navy semi-annual email marathon, and they've switched tactics on me. The subject finally upped the discount (60-70% versus the 50% I've been staring at all week), which is the first thing that would actually stop my thumb from swiping. But when I open it, the "mystery" mechanic immediately deflates that momentum. I need to stock up on shorts and tees for two kids before back-to-school. I do not have time to click a question-mark button to find out if what's on sale is even useful to my family — I need to see "kids' tanks 60% off" or "uniform bottoms included" right away.
- The ToeKTech section embedded halfway down is genuinely baffling. This is a product feature launch dropped into the middle of a semi-annual sale email. Those are two completely different jobs. Hanna Andersson's welcome email knew exactly what one job to do and did it cleanly — this email is trying to do three jobs at once and accomplishes none of them fully.
- I'd open this from the inbox — the jump to 60-70% is a real escalation I'd notice. But I would not click the mystery reveal CTA. I'd scroll the product grid, not find a clear kids/family callout, feel vaguely annoyed by ToeKTech, and close. Not forwarding to anyone.

## What's working

- **The discount escalation is real.** Going from 50% to 60-70% is the first credible staircase this whole semi-annual run — it signals there's something new worth looking at.
- **"TODAY ONLY!" urgency is legible and placed right.** Big, above-the-fold, no ambiguity. That part of the visual hierarchy works.
- **"For the fam" in the subject is the right language for me.** It signals this isn't just adult activewear again — there's supposed to be something for the whole household.
- **Product thumbnail grid gives you options.** At least visually I can scan multiple categories quickly without hitting a single-hero dead end.
- **Brand is recognizable instantly.** Old Navy logo up top, consistent color palette — I know where I am in two seconds.

## What's weak

- **"Mystery" is exactly the wrong mechanic for practical family shopping.** I need to know if kids' bottoms are in the deal before I click, full stop. This is a friction machine dressed up as fun.
- **ToeKTech mid-email is a hard stop.** A new product feature launch does not belong inside a 60-70% off sale email. It splits the job and dilutes both messages.
- **No family or kids signal in the hero itself.** "For the fam" promised me family coverage, but the hero imagery and CTA don't confirm that the deal covers kids. That's bait-without-follow-through.
- **The product grid thumbnails are too small to evaluate on mobile.** Can't see sizes, categories, or whether school basics are included — I'm just looking at tiny squares.
- **Seventh email in the semi-annual run, and it still doesn't know what I've already purchased.** Zero behavioral segmentation. I bought activewear last week — I don't need more activewear mystery deals.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the mystery mechanic — or at least give me a taste.** Show three specific confirmed categories (e.g., "Kids' tees, denim shorts, tanks — see all deals") so I know it's worth clicking. Mystery works for flash-sale emails from DTC brands with a strong trust bank. Old Navy has sent me seven emails in a week; I need proof, not a tease.
- 2. **Cut ToeKTech to a single small banner below the fold, or its own dedicated send.** Product launches and clearance events should never share hero real estate. Every brand that does this well — including Hanna Andersson — keeps new-product emails completely separate.
- 3. **Add a kids' or family-specific callout above the fold.** The subject promised the fam. The email needs to deliver that above the scroll line. Something like "60-70% off kids, baby, and adult styles" with a kid model in the hero earns the click.
- 4. **Reduce cadence or segment me out of repeat beats.** I've seen 50% off activewear at least twice. Either suppress me from already-purchased categories or explicitly say "new inventory added." Hanna Andersson's welcome email told me exactly what the 20% off applied to — Old Navy should do that too.
- **Subject Alt A:** `60-70% off — kids, baby + adult styles. Today only.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Mystery sale: 60-70% off for the whole family. Ends tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Grab shorts, tanks, and tees before the clock runs out.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your family's semi-annual haul moment is here — see all deals.`

### 1. Technical Summary

One confirmed broken redirect from the cp.oldnavy.com tracking endpoint produces an unresolvable relative URL, which will cause open-tracking to fail for the affected cohort. Three compliance headers are absent or unverifiable, and the plain-text body is overwhelmingly URL content — a deliverability risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken open-tracker redirect**
The DV tracking pixel at:
```
https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/55dee75a-5193-4487-8582-7b0004001782?mi_mid=019f1dfa-...
```
resolves to an unresolvable relative path:
```
/g/55dee75a-5193-4487-8582-7b0004001782?txn=514a7700-7589-11f1-ad30-764a3bc6abf2
```
A relative redirect is not a valid HTTP response for a standalone domain; the client has no base URL to resolve `/g/...` against. Open-event tracking from this pixel will fail or be silently dropped.

**[WARN] 23 click-tracking links unverified**
All links routed through `click.email.oldnavy.com` were skipped by the HTTP probe. Destination URL integrity (404s, expired landing pages) cannot be confirmed from this audit.

**[INFO] Primary open pixel appears intact**
```
https://click.email.oldnavy.com/open.aspx?U36BNVCPB3KERCDL547K4XVB3U.60268&d=60268&bmt=0
```
Not flagged by QA; standard 1×1 pixel with `alt=""` (acceptable for tracking pixels per accessibility convention).

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing alt text on tracking image**
The `cp.oldnavy.com` DV pixel has no `alt` attribute at all (not even `alt=""`):
```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/55dee75a-...&cp_cid=1bc829e71098f268" width="1" height="1">
```
Should be `alt=""` to suppress screen-reader announcement of the URL string.

**[WARN] Duplicate CSS class selectors in media query**
`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice under `@media only screen and (max-width: 639px)` — later declaration wins, but the duplication indicates a template merge conflict and increases parse overhead.

**[INFO] Outlook conditional: `line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly`**
Applied globally to all `td` elements in the MSO 9+ conditional block. This is a known fix for Outlook ghost-spacing, but `line-height: 0px` on `td` without explicit per-cell overrides can collapse text-bearing cells in Outlook 2013–2019. Acceptable if all content cells have explicit `line-height` overrides, but cannot be confirmed from the truncated source.

**[INFO] XHTML 1.0 Transitional + VML namespaces**
Legacy but correct for broad Outlook compatibility. No issue.

No issues found in visible source.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post headers absent**
Both `List-Unsubscribe` (RFC 2369) and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click) are missing or were not captured by the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Apple Mail use these headers to surface the native unsubscribe UI; their absence increases spam-folder placement risk under Google's 2024 bulk sender requirements. Confirm whether the ESP injects these at send time — if not, add them.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address in body**
CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email body. The provided HTML does not include one in the visible/truncated section. If it appears below the fold in the footer (common), this is a false positive from the QA probe — verify the full source. If genuinely absent, this is a legal compliance failure.

**[WARN] Authentication-Results header not captured**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be verified. The `oldnavy.com` domain uses `email.oldnavy.com` as the sending subdomain (`From: oldnavy@email.oldnavy.com`) — confirm DKIM is signed for `email.oldnavy.com` and that DMARC alignment is met (either relaxed subdomain match or explicit `email.oldnavy.com` inclusion in the policy).
