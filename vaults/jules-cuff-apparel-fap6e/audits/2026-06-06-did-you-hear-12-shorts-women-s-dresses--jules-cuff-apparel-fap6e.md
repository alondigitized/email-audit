# 📢 Did you hear? $12 shorts & women's dresses
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-two. I didn't even need to check the brand history — I already knew. This is the twenty-second Old Navy email in roughly two weeks, and I opened it before I remembered to be annoyed because "$12 shorts" is a real number and summer is actually happening in my house right now. The "2 DAYS ONLY!" banner at the top did its job. I saw the price, I felt the clock, and for about four seconds I was a buyer.
- Then I scrolled. The whole email is adult women's — shorts, dresses, a "New Trend Pick" block, a clearance dump at the bottom with bags and accessories. Not a single kid in sight. When I shop Old Navy seriously, it's almost always because my son needs five pairs of uniform pants or my daughter ran out of leggings mid-semester. This email has zero of that. It's selling me a sundress when I came in for a backpack haul.
- I'd mark it read. Maybe glance at the clearance section if I needed a tote. I would not forward it, because my sister who actually wants sundresses got the same twenty-two emails I did.

## What's working

- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" + the dollar amount up front** — $9 and $12 price points are in the hero immediately. No click needed to know if the deal is real.
- **Warm summer palette** — the orange, coral, and yellow photography actually looks like June. Hanna Andersson's welcome email had better art direction, but Old Navy earns its season here.
- **Visual rhythm is clean** — price callouts, product blocks, and CTAs stack in a predictable order. Nothing overlaps, nothing is broken.
- **"New Trend Pick" callout** — a small differentiator amid the price pile-on. It signals the email isn't just a clearance dump.
- **"Na, Na, Na Clearance" at the bottom** — the playfulness lands. It's the one moment the brand's voice comes through instead of just a discount grid.

## What's weak

- **Zero kids content** — Old Navy is where I stock up on kids' basics, and nothing in this email speaks to that. If Hanna Andersson sent me a pure adult-fashion email, I'd unsubscribe on the spot; Old Navy gets grace because I expect the split, but I still feel unseen.
- **"Did you hear?" in the subject is dead copy** — it sounds like every mass-retailer blast I ignore. The $12 part is doing all the work; the "Did you hear?" just burns characters and reads as a stranger, not a brand that knows me.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — twenty-two emails in two weeks means I'm pattern-matching before I'm reading. The three activewear sends in a row last week (June 3–5) trained me to skim. This one benefited from a price that cut through, but the next one may not.
- **No family or multi-item bundle angle** — a "stock up the whole family for summer" frame would have made this relevant to how I actually check out: five items for the kids, maybe two for me.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment kids vs. adult fashion** — or at least thread one kids section into the email. If Old Navy knows I buy kids' items (they do, that's my whole purchase history), a single grid block of kids' shorts at $12 would triple my click likelihood.
- 2. **Kill "Did you hear?" and let the price lead the subject** — the concrete offer is strong enough to carry the line alone.
- 3. **Add a deadline timestamp** — "2 DAYS ONLY" is fine but "Ends Sunday at midnight" gives me a real anchor to decide against.
- 4. **Thin the cadence or earn the frequency** — if I got this email once a week instead of daily, I'd open it the same way I open Hanna's. Daily volume degrades every individual send. Hanna sends less, signals more premium, and I remember each one.
- 5. **Swap the clearance section CTA copy** — "Na, Na, Na Clearance" is charming, but the button should say something specific like "Shop Clearance from $5" not just "Shop Now."

- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock up the whole family: $12 shorts, $9 styles, and clearance from $5.`

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken click-redirect URL**
- `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/7da1d8ac-7927-4454-a9b9-f1a1bab68002?mi_mid=...&cp_tp=v3.eJy...`
- Error: `unknown url type: '/g/7da1d8ac-7927-4454-a9b9-f1a1bab68002?txn=10fe1000-61fa-11f1-af56-4e8366552ae6'`
- The `cp_tp` payload decodes to a redirect target that resolves to a relative-path URL (`/g/...`), which is not a valid absolute URL. This will result in a broken destination for any recipient who clicks this link.
- This URL also appears as the tracking pixel in the hidden preheader `<div>`, so open tracking for affected sends may be silently failing as well.

**[WARN] 23 tracking links skipped**
- All 23 click-redirect URLs route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. Not probed — destination validity unconfirmed.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
- Not captured by the AgentMail relay. If missing at SMTP level, this violates Google/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (enforced Feb 2024) and RFC 2369. Major ISPs use this header for the "Unsubscribe" button in the inbox UI.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent**
- Required for one-click unsubscribe per RFC 8058. Without it, Gmail and Yahoo cannot honor automated unsubscribe requests, increasing spam complaint exposure.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**
- CAN-SPAM §7(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in the email body. Not found in the truncated source — may be present in the untruncated footer, but QA did not detect it. Needs manual verification of the full footer.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**[FAIL] Broken redirect breaks UTM chain**
- The broken `cp.oldnavy.com/o/...` link means any UTMs encoded in the `cp_tp` payload are never delivered to the landing page. Conversions from this link will be unattributed.

**[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found**
- SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status is unknown. If authentication is actually failing, deliverability to Gmail/Outlook/Yahoo inboxes is at risk, which would undercut all landing page traffic expectations regardless of link health.
