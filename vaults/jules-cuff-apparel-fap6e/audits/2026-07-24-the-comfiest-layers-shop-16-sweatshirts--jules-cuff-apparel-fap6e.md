# The *comfiest* layers. Shop $16 sweatshirts
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so this is the "comfiest layers" email and I'll give Old Navy credit for one thing: the price point in the subject line got me to open it. $16 sweatshirts is real money when I'm buying in volume for two kids who will outgrow or destroy anything I give them by March. But then I open it and — there's so much happening. The hero is "TODAY ONLY!" with what looks like $12 and $16 price points against a lavender sweatshirt on a young woman, which fine, that's relevant to me. But then the email immediately pivots into "HOT NEW GEAR" activewear territory, and then a mosaic of eight different product tiles, and then a "Now In. Made For You." section with neutral-toned basics, and then clearance starting at $3.99. It's four separate emails crammed into one scroll.
- The clearance $3.99 block is actually what caught my eye most, which is a problem — clearance should be a bonus, not the most visually punchy section in the lower half. The lavender sweatshirt at the top is genuinely cute and I could see wearing it, but by the time I've scrolled past two category pivots I've lost the thread of why I opened. This email suffers from the same problem as the July 20 $6 tees send — the subject hooks me on one thing and the body tries to sell me everything.
- I would not click through directly, but I might come back to it later for the clearance $3.99 section because I am always hunting for filler tees before school starts. I would not forward this to anyone.

## What's working

- The "TODAY ONLY!" urgency block with $12/$16 price anchors is above the fold and immediately legible — I clocked the offer in under two seconds.
- The lavender colorway on the hero model is very much my aesthetic; it's soft, wearable, not trying too hard, and signals this is for grown women rather than teens.
- The clearance "$3.99" block is visually punchy and the price point is genuinely compelling for a mom stocking basics.
- The model variety in the lower mosaic — different body types, multiple skin tones — is better than the July 22 men's denim email, which felt like it wasn't even aware I existed.

## What's weak

- "HOT NEW GEAR" activewear section directly below the sweatshirt hero is a jarring pivot — two completely different shopping contexts stacked without a beat.
- The product mosaic is too dense; eight-plus images in rapid succession on mobile-sized rendering flattens everything into visual noise and I can't tell what's on sale vs. what's full price.
- No kids section, no matching family sets — I'm Old Navy's best customer for volume kid-basics and this email treats me like I'm shopping for myself only.
- The "Now In. Made For You." copy is doing zero work — it sounds like filler that a marketer wrote at 4pm on a Friday.
- Clearance starting at $3.99 is buried below three other sections; if that's your hook for deal hunters like me, lead with it or at least give it a dedicated CTA earlier.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one story and tell it.** The sweatshirt offer is genuinely strong — $16 with a "TODAY ONLY" timer is enough. Cut the activewear and product mosaic sections entirely and let the layering story breathe. The July 21 activewear email worked because it was one thing: 50% off activewear, full stop.
- 2. **Add a kids layer to the hero or a second block.** I open Old Navy emails for the adults-plus-kids overlap. Hanna Andersson's welcome email earns premium perception because it leads with the family set identity immediately. Even one line — "Sweatshirts for them too — 2T–14" — would make me twice as likely to click.
- 3. **Move clearance up or give it a dedicated section header** with a "Shop Under $10" CTA that I can tap without hunting for it.
- 4. **Kill "Now In. Made For You."** Replace with the actual category name — "Women's Tops," "Everyday Layers," anything specific.
- 5. **Tighten the subject line** — the asterisk-comfiest formatting reads slightly gimmicky in an inbox.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$16 sweatshirts, today only — shop the comfiest layers`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Today only: cozy layers starting at $12`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Soft, slouchy, and on sale — but only until midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The sweatshirt you'll live in this fall. $16, today only.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing `alt` text on tracking pixel**
The broken Cordial pixel image (`src: https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/8026446f-...`) has no `alt` attribute. Screen readers will announce the full URL. Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""`.

**CSS duplicate rule conflict**
In the mobile media query block, `.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice with different `padding-top`/`padding-bottom` values (5px and 10px). The second declaration wins in all parsers, but the duplication is a maintenance hazard and was called out in a comment in the source itself:
```css
.space-top10, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 10px;} /* MspaceTop was duplicated, kept one */
```

**Outlook line-height override**
The MSO conditional sets `td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }` globally. This is a known Outlook fix but will suppress line-height inheritance on any `<td>` that doesn't explicitly set its own value — including dynamic or personalized content blocks inserted at send time.

**`<meta>` inside `<div>` (schema.org block)**
```html
<div itemscope itemtype="http://schema.org/Organization">
  <meta content="Old Navy" itemprop="name">
  <meta content="https://..." itemprop="logo">
</div>
```
`<meta>` elements are not valid children of `<div>` in HTML. Some email clients will strip or misparse these, breaking the schema.org annotation. These should be `<span>` with `itemprop`, or placed in `<head>`.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not detected**
The AgentMail relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. If absent at the MTA level (not just relay-invisible), this is a violation of Gmail/Yahoo 2024 bulk sender requirements for lists >5k/day.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected**
One-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo for bulk senders) depends on this header alongside `List-Unsubscribe`. Absence is a deliverability risk independent of CAN-SPAM compliance.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected in source**
The truncated HTML does not include a street address in the footer. CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address. This may be present in the non-truncated footer — if so, disregard. If absent, this is a legal violation.
