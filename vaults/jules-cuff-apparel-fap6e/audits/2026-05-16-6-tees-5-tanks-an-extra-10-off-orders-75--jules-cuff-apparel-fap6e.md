# 👕 $6 TEES & $5 TANKS + an extra $10 off orders $75+
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- I saw this pop up and my first thought was: didn't you just tell me about the $6 tees? Because you did — earlier this same morning. The first send already scored high in my head, so getting a second ping before noon with the same hero felt like inbox déjà vu before I even tapped. But then I actually read the subject — "an extra $10 off orders $75+" — and okay, that changed the math for me.
- Here's the thing: I don't buy one Old Navy tee. I buy twelve. Two kids, different sizes, different colors because someone always grabs the wrong one in the morning rush. So an extra $10 off once I clear $75 is basically guaranteed savings given how I actually shop. The escalation from this morning's send is smart in theory — first you get me excited about the flat price, then you give me a threshold bonus to push me over the add-to-cart edge.
- Inside the email, though, it's doing too much. Tees and tanks, then a denim 50% off block, then the Disney stars-and-stripes collection I actually liked from the May 13 send — three story beats in one scroll is where Old Navy always loses me. I'd click, but this is a "tab I open and forget about" situation, not a "I'm buying in the next ten minutes" one. Marked important, comes back to me tonight.

## What's working

- **The $10 off $75+ sweetener is the whole email** — for a volume buyer like me, I'm hitting $75 on tees and tanks alone without even thinking about it.
- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" in the hero** is visible and credible — a short real window, not a 30-day rolling "limited time" lie that Crocs has been running on repeat.
- **The tee and tank pricing is front-loaded in the hero** — "$6" is right where my eye lands first, which is the correct call.
- **Female model in casual everyday wear** — she reads relatable, not aspirational-in-a-way-that-alienates; this isn't the Hanna Andersson welcome email's premium polish, but it matches the Old Navy promise.
- **The Disney anchor at the bottom** is a nice callback — I've been watching that collection since May 13 and seeing it here reminds me it's still live.

## What's weak

- **Second send same day, same hero.** The morning email was already doing the tees/tanks job. Sending again before noon is inbox noise — I almost swiped without opening.
- **Three competing story beats.** Tees/tanks, then denim 50% off, then Disney. Each of those is a full campaign on its own; stacking them makes none of them land as hard as they should.
- **The male model sharing the hero splits the message.** I shop for my whole family, but a split hero reads as a mass send that doesn't know who I am — Hanna Andersson never makes me feel like they're hedging on who opened the email.
- **No fit or sizing signal in the hero.** For tees and tanks I need to know if my usual L runs slim. Old Navy never includes this; Hanna always does, even in promotional emails.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the $10 off $75+ the actual headline** — that's the news, not the tee price (which you already told me this morning). Reframe as: "Spend $75, Save $10 — Plus $6 Tees & $5 Tanks." The threshold is the closer, not the footnote.
- 2. **Split the Disney section into its own send** — the May 13 email proved it can carry an entire message on its own. It's getting buried below a denim grid and losing all its energy.
- 3. **Add a one-line fit note to the tee/tank hero** — even "fits true to size" in small print under the price point would handle my hesitation at the cart.
- 4. **Suppress morning openers from the afternoon send** — anyone who opened the tees/tanks email already knows. Show them this only if they didn't click, as a re-engagement with the new sweetener.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra $10 off when you spend $75 — plus $6 tees`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$6 tees + a bonus $10 off — 2 days only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stock up on tees and tanks, then hit $75 for the bonus savings.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Spend $75, save an extra $10. $6 tees and $5 tanks make it easy.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken ConvertPipe redirect**
The QA probe on the ConvertPipe tracking pixel resolved to a relative URL rather than an absolute destination:
```
Probed:   https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/91049900-241e-4740-9012-5fec7a6bb6ac?mi_mid=...
Resolved: /g/91049900-241e-4740-9012-5fec7a6bb6ac?txn=39569980-517b-11f1-88f4-4e61e507eb6e
Error:    unknown url type
```
The `/o/` → `/g/` path rewrite is producing a relative URL, which means the redirect chain is broken. This pixel sits in a `display:none` preheader div, so it is invisible to recipients but the failed request will register as an error in ConvertPipe logs and silently drop engagement attribution for any client that follows the redirect.

**[WARN] 23 click links not probed**
All outbound CTAs route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. These were skipped by the HTTP probe. UTM integrity and destination validity cannot be confirmed without a manual spot-check of at least the primary hero CTA.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing alt text on tracking pixel**
The ConvertPipe pixel image has no `alt` attribute:
```
src: https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/91049900-241e-4740-9012-5fec7a6bb6ac?mi_mid=...
```
While invisible by design, screen readers will announce this as an unlabeled image. Add `alt=""` explicitly (not missing the attribute — an empty string signals decorative intent to assistive tech).

**[WARN] Duplicate responsive utility class definitions**
In the `@media (max-width: 639px)` block, `.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice with conflicting values:
```css
.space-top5, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 5px;}   /* line ~70 */
.space-top10, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 10px;}  /* line ~76 — overwrites above */
.space-bot5,  .MspaceBot {padding-bottom: 5px;}
.space-bot10, .MspaceBot {padding-bottom: 10px;} /* overwrites above */
```
Any element using `.MspaceTop` alone will always get 10px, never 5px. Elements intended to have 5px spacing will silently render at 10px on mobile.

**[WARN] Outlook global `line-height:0px` override**
Inside the `[if gte mso 9]` conditional:
```css
td { line-height:0px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly }
```
This is a global reset applied to every `td` in Outlook 2013+. Any `td` that relies on inherited or default line-height for text readability (rather than an explicit inline style) will collapse line height to zero. This is a known pattern but must be compensated with explicit `line-height` on every text-bearing `td`.

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL-RISK] Physical mailing address unconfirmed**
CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address. The QA checker found none in the captured content. The full HTML is truncated and the address typically appears in the footer — this must be verified against the full source. If omitted, it is a CAN-SPAM violation.

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header absent from relay capture**
The `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) headers were not found. These may be present in the original SMTP envelope but were not forwarded by the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Apple Mail use `List-Unsubscribe` to surface native unsubscribe UI — absence increases user-reported spam rates. Confirm these headers exist on the sending MTA; if not, add them.

**[WARN] One-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) not confirmed**
Without `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, Gmail's bulk sender requirements (enforced since Feb 2024 for >5k/day senders) are not met. Old Navy volume virtually guarantees they exceed this threshold.

**[WARN] Authentication-Results header missing**
SPF and DKIM status are unknown from the relay-captured version. Cannot confirm DMARC alignment. This should be verified via the original message headers.
