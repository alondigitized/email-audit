# You deserve these! SIXTY PERCENT OFF Summer Steals + get an EXTRA 20% OFF your purchase
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- Thirty. Or thereabouts. I've honestly stopped counting, but I can say this: the June 15 send already played the exact same 60%-off Summer Steals card. Now here we are two days later, same hero percentage, same seasonal hook — except now they've stacked an extra 20% on top and gone full screaming caps in the subject. The escalation is visible. It also reads a little panicked.
- That said: the "2 DAYS ONLY!" at the very top is doing real work. It creates actual pressure in a way "going FAST" from June 13 never did. And the mid-scroll loyalty callout — Old Navy Rewards — registers for me as a volume buyer. But by the time I hit the "Clearance Clearance Clearance" section stacked at the bottom, I feel like I wandered into three emails stitched together. This brand is shouting from every aisle simultaneously.
- Would I click? Maybe — fast, to the kids' tees section, if I can find it without hunting. Would I forward it? No. Hanna Andersson's welcome email made me feel like a known customer with one clean offer. This is the opposite of that.

## What's working

- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" hero is the clearest upgrade over the June 15 send** — real time pressure above the fold, not vague scarcity language.
- **60% off is unmissable at the top** — the discount doesn't make me scroll to find the number; it's the first thing my eye lands on.
- **Old Navy Rewards callout mid-email** — I'm a member, so seeing earn-on-this language in a dedicated band matters; it makes the deal feel stacked in my favor.
- **Dense product grid** — suits how I actually shop Old Navy: fast scan, high volume, grab what fits the budget window.
- **"HOT NEW GEAR!" section** adds at least a hint of editorial freshness beyond the Summer Steals repeat.

## What's weak

- **Same 60% Summer Steals hero, again** — the June 15 send already spent this coin. Returning with the identical percentage two days later without a visible new angle reads like a re-blast, not a new story.
- **Subject is doing too much** — "SIXTY PERCENT" in all caps *plus* "EXTRA 20% OFF" in the same line is a lot of screaming. The June 14 "$6 tees + EXTRA 30% off" did this better because the anchor was a dollar amount, not a stacked percentage I have to do math on.
- **The Clearance repeat-text section at the bottom** — "Clearance Clearance Clearance" tiled across the screen looks like a design placeholder, not a feature. It adds noise right when I'm about to close the tab.
- **No kids-specific callout in the hero or first scroll** — I'm here for family uniform restocking and I have to hunt for it. Hanna Andersson would never make me hunt.
- **Too many offers competing** (60% off + extra 20% + swimwear + new gear + clearance) — the math adds cognitive load and paradoxically makes me trust the headline number less.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show the stacked math as a final number** — "60% off + extra 20% = up to 68% off" is the actual story. Say it once, clearly, instead of making me calculate.
- 2. **Add one kids/family image block in the hero** — even a split-panel with a kid's graphic tee next to an adult tank tells me this email is mine, not generic womenswear.
- 3. **Cut or isolate the Clearance section** — give it its own send. Stacking it here dilutes the "Summer Steals" urgency and makes the email feel like two campaigns at war.
- 4. **Trim the subject line by half** — the same information lands harder without the caps.
- **Subject Alt A:** `2 Days Only: 60% Off Summer Steals + Extra 20% Off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 60% off is expiring — plus an extra 20% stacked`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Final hours on Summer Steals — kids, adults, all the tees you need`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack the deals: up to 68% off before it ends tonight`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

The pixel/tracking URL at `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/2ae5fef1-81a2-4a91-a4af-c67a1d16c85e` returns an error resolving to an unknown URL type:

This is embedded as a 1×1 tracking pixel in the `<body>` preheader block. ### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing `alt` text on tracking pixel**

The ConvertPipe pixel at `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/2ae5fef1...` has an empty `alt=""`. This is technically acceptable for a spacer/pixel, but the open-tracking pixel at `click.email.oldnavy.com/open.aspx?...` also uses `alt=""`. Both are correct practice for invisible pixels, so no action required — flagging for completeness.

**[INFO] Duplicate CSS class bindings**

`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each bound to two selectors in the mobile media query:

```css
.space-top5, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 5px;}
...
.space-top10, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 10px;}  /* overwrites previous */
```

The second declaration silently wins. If `space-top5` elements are expected to render at 5px on mobile, they will not. This is a CSS authoring bug, not a rendering crash, but warrants audit against the intended mobile layout.

**[INFO] Outlook line-height override**

The MSO conditional sets `td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }` globally for Outlook 2013+. This is a known workaround but can collapse text cells if any `<td>` relies on inherited line-height rather than an explicit value.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**

CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email. The QA scan found none in the parsed text. This is either in the non-truncated footer (possible, given truncation) or genuinely absent — the sending team must confirm the full HTML footer includes a street address before treating this as a false negative.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**

Neither `List-Unsubscribe` nor `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click) were detected. The QA note attributes this to possible AgentMail relay header stripping, so the headers may exist on the wire but not be visible to the checker. However:

- Gmail and Apple Mail both surface one-click unsubscribe from `List-Unsubscribe-Post`; missing it degrades the unsubscribe UX and can increase spam reports.
- Senders above 5,000 msgs/day to Gmail must support one-click unsubscribe per Google's 2024 sender requirements. This should be verified at the ESP level, not inferred from the HTML.

**[WARN] SPF/DKIM authentication result not captured**

`Authentication-Results` header was not available via the relay. Deliverability posture (SPF pass/fail, DKIM signature validity, DMARC alignment) cannot be confirmed from this audit. Must be verified via ESP delivery logs or a header-complete sample.
