# Great news! Up to 50% off this week's deals is one click away
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-20**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S falling back to its bread-and-butter "UP TO 50% OFF" hero, but the twilight-field photo behind the box is a stylistic departure from the orange-lightning FLASH SALE creative and the red-gradient clearance creative it's been alternating between all month (9/17 "Lucky you," 9/18 "60% off," 9/19 "FLASH"). Below the fold it's an exhaustive product-grid dump — golf, sauna, gloves, credit card, apparel, socks — with no real narrative thread connecting the hero offer to what's actually being sold.
- The render itself is clean: no broken images, no overlapping text, nothing obviously botched. But this is the fourth "up to X% off" subject/hero variant in about a week from the same brand, and the email reads as a template refresh rather than a genuinely new campaign. The "Order Today, Sport It Tomorrow" same-day delivery banner up top is a nice, differentiated touch that none of the recent sends in brand history called out — that's the most interesting thing here.
- Judgement: functional, on-brand, zero risk — but fatigue is setting in for anyone who's seen the 9/17–9/19 sends, and this one doesn't do anything to break that pattern.

## What's working

- Hero renders cleanly with a legible "UP TO 50% OFF" callout and a clear green "Shop Now" CTA directly beneath it.
- The twilight/field photography is a visual change-up from the orange lightning and red-gradient clearance treatments used in the four prior sends.
- Same-day delivery / order-today messaging at the very top is a genuinely new hook not seen in the last several audited emails.
- Category nav bar (Men's/Women's/Kids'/Shoes/Fan Shop/Team Sports) gives an easy secondary path if the hero offer isn't relevant.
- ScoreCard points teaser in the header nods to loyalty members without cluttering the hero.

## What's weak

- The hero offer is generic "up to 50% off" with no named category or product — same vague framing DICK'S has used repeatedly, just without specifying what's actually on sale.
- The email is a sprawling, undifferentiated product wall (golf apparel, red-light sauna, baseball gloves, credit card pitch, socks) with no hierarchy after the hero — nothing signals a "best deal here."
- This is essentially the same "up to 50%" beat as the 9/17 "Lucky you" and 9/19 "FLASH" sends, just re-skinned — cadence fatigue is real for a subscriber who's opened three of the last five.
- No urgency device visible (no countdown timer like the 9/19 "Final Hours" send, no explicit end date) despite "This Week's Deals" implying a deadline.
- $500 EZLIFE sauna and BOGO golf clothing sit oddly next to $100 sneakers and $30-under socks — the mixed price tiers make the email feel like a catalog dump rather than a curated deal.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead the hero with a specific, high-value category tie to the "50% off" number (e.g., footwear or fall apparel) instead of a generic percentage — specificity is what's been missing across every recent send in this brand's history.
- 2. Add a visible countdown or explicit end-date to the hero, mirroring the 9/19 "Final Hours 07:59:48" treatment that scored higher — vague "This Week's Deals" without a clock reads as low urgency.
- 3. Cut the body down to 3-4 curated categories instead of nine unrelated product blocks; move the credit card pitch, sauna, and socks to a secondary "more picks" carousel.
- 4. Differentiate the subject line from the last three near-identical "up to 50%" sends by naming a category or hero item.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Fall footwear drop: up to 50% off is one click away`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your weekend deal: up to 50% off + same-day pickup`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New styles from Nike, adidas & New Balance — plus same-day in-store pickup.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop now, wear it tomorrow. Deals end soon.`
