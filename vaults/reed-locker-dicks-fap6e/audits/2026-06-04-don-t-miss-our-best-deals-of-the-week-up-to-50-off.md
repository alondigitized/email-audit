# Don't miss our best deals of the week — up to 50% off 🙌
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, competent "Best Deals of the Week" compilation email with genuine structural strengths — the 50% OFF hero graphic is immediate, the per-category layout (Men's/Women's/Kids/Golf/Outdoor) gives recipients clear navigation, and the product grid in the lower half gives browse-mode shoppers real items to click. On its own merits, it's a solid weekly roundup.
- The problem is it's not on its own. DICK'S sent a near-identical "Best Deals of the Week" email yesterday (June 3, "Best DEALS of the week = Up to 50% OFF 🙌"), which scored 9/10 precisely because it was a fresh pivot away from the exhausted extra-25%-clearance blitz cycle. Sending the same creative framework back-to-back the very next day cancels that freshness entirely — what felt like a well-timed reset on June 3 now reads as another loop in the same blitz pattern.
- The hero also quietly underdelivers: the circular graphic promises 50% off, but Men's, Women's, and Kids' — the three sections most subscribers came for — are capped at 40% off. Golf and Outdoor earn the 50% headline, but they're buried below the fold. That gap is low-grade bait-and-switch perception risk even if the "up to" qualifier is technically present.

## What's working

- The "50% OFF" circular graphic dominates the hero — visual hierarchy is clear, offer lands before any scrolling
- Per-category structure with individual CTAs is genuinely useful; the email isn't just a homepage redirect
- "Must-have gear for an epic summer?" framing in the lower section nails the seasonal moment
- Layout is clean and render-bug free across all visible sections
- Product thumbnail grid in "This Week's Deals" gives browsers something specific to click

## What's weak

- Sent the day after a structurally identical send — cadence is the biggest open-rate drag here
- Hero promises 50% off; Men's/Women's/Kids' sections actually top out at 40% — the most-trafficked categories don't match the hero claim
- All five category CTAs read "SHOP NOW" — same generic label, no differentiation, no click incentive per category
- No ScoreCard/loyalty messaging visible anywhere despite DICK'S having an active points program that could have added a reason to buy today vs. next week
- "Don't miss" as a subject opener is soft urgency with no credible deadline anchoring it

## Recommendations

- 1. **Don't send this as a day-after carbon copy.** If a Tuesday resend is mandatory, change the hero to one category with a stronger lead (e.g., golf ahead of Father's Day), not a general "best deals" replay.
- 2. **Fix the hero-to-body offer gap.** If the hero says 50% off, lead with the Golf or Outdoor block — or re-write the hero to "Up to 50% off select gear" to match the category reality.
- 3. **Differentiate the CTAs.** "Shop Men's" / "Shop Women's" / "Shop Outdoor" takes 5 seconds to rewrite and meaningfully separates click attribution.
- 4. **Add a loyalty/urgency anchor.** Even a single line — "Earn 2X ScoreCard points through Sunday" — creates a concrete reason to act now.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends Sunday: up to 50% off golf, outdoor & more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Father's Day picks — up to 50% off select gear`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Golf equipment, outdoor essentials and shoes — plus 2X ScoreCard points through Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Browse this week's top markdowns by category — men's, women's, kids, golf and outdoor`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

`separate` + `border-spacing:0` approximates `collapse` behavior in most clients, but Outlook (Word rendering engine) may add unintended cell spacing. Low risk but non-standard.

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link** — source is truncated before the footer; cannot confirm presence. These must be verified in the full HTML.

**Authentication headers (SPF/DKIM/DMARC)** — sending domain is `e.dcsg.com` (ESP subdomain). Header-level authentication cannot be evaluated from HTML source alone; must be checked via raw message headers or a mail-tester tool against the delivered message.

**Critical: Invalid JSON in `<script type="application/ld+json">`** — the JSON-LD block contains JavaScript-style `//` line comments, which are **not valid JSON syntax**:

```json
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
  // Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
  "@context": "http://schema.org/",
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": ""
}
```

This will cause Google's structured data parser to reject the entire JSON-LD block, silently failing all three Schema.org objects. This is a deployment defect, not a template design choice — comments must be removed from production JSON-LD.

**Non-standard Schema.org types** — `DiscountOffer` and `PromotionCard` are not defined in the official Schema.org vocabulary. Gmail's promotional annotations rely on `schema.org/DiscountOffer` (valid) but `PromotionCard` has no schema.org definition. Verify these types are supported by the target mail client's structured data spec.

**Stale hardcoded date in `DiscountOffer`:**
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```
A 6-year-old hardcoded date — this was not dynamically populated and will cause any date-aware structured data validator to flag the offer as expired.
