# Savings to fall for 🍂 Up to 50% off our BEST deals of the week
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-30**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S weekly-deals roundup, and it repeats the 10/28 "Hauntingly good DEALS" creative. The hero is the same collage: a YETI tumbler, pickleball paddles, a mountain biker and a treadmill, with a red "UP TO 50% OFF" tile. A subscriber saw this layout two days ago. It was also the third deals-flavored send in 48 hours, after the 10/29 "ONE DAY" flash sale and this morning's "No tricks, just DEALS!" email.
- The structure is clean. There's a single hero, one green Shop Now button, category pills, a second "Up to 40% Off Select Clothing & Shoes" block, and a product grid. The eye lands on the red 50% tile first, which is correct. But the offer is generic ("select styles"), the headline is flat, and nothing says why this send differs from the others. The "best deals of the week" claim sounds a little hollow after the "best" deals were already announced today.
- Verdict: competent template, weak differentiation. It would work as the only send of the week, but it is send number three of the same message. The sensible fix is cadence, not the design.

## What's working

- The red "UP TO 50% OFF" tile is the clear focal point and contrasts strongly with the photo collage.
- The category pills (Men's, Women's, Kids', Shoes, Fan Shop, Team Sports) let a shopper jump straight to their interest.
- The green Shop Now buttons are large, consistent and easy to find.
- The 2x3 product grid has real products and clean white space, so it's scannable.
- The ScoreCard strip and the "Order Today. Sport It Tomorrow." line at the top carry loyalty and fulfillment value without crowding the hero.

## What's weak

- The creative is nearly identical to the 10/28 weekly-deals send, which is stale for anyone who opened that one.
- The offer is vague: "select styles" and "up to" with no hero product, price or deadline.
- The product grid shows no prices, discounts or ratings, so it reads as a catalog, not deals.
- Two competing "up to" numbers (50% and 40%) dilute the message.
- The "fall" and Halloween framing is gone but nothing seasonal replaces it, so the pitch feels generic.
- The footer is long, with fine-print blocks for in-stock items and price match.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the cadence. Send one weekly-deals email and fold the flash and clearance messages into it, so the "best deals" claim stays credible.
- 2. Put sale prices on the product grid ("$59.99 was $90") and add a star rating or "Top rated" tag to a few items.
- 3. Pick one hero product or category (for example the Samba or the Penn State tee) and give it a specific price headline.
- 4. Add a real deadline: "Ends Sunday".
- 5. Drop the second "up to 40%" block, or make it the same offer as the hero.
- 6. Make the ScoreCard row louder, for example "Earn 2x points on deals this week".

- **Subject Alt B:** `This week's top 6 deals (ends Sunday)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `YETI, treadmills, golf and fan gear, plus earn double ScoreCard points.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `One-hour pickup on every deal. Prices drop Sunday night.`
