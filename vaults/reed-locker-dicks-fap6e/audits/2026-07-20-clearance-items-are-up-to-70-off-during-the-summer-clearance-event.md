# Clearance items are up to 70% off during the summer clearance event.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S fourth Summer Clearance "up to 70% off" email since July 18, and it shows. The creative is structurally identical to the July 18 "SUMMER CLEARANCE EVENT is here: Up to 70% off!" send that scored 7/10 here — same dark hero, same "UP TO 70% OFF" badge, same green SHOP NOW button, same category rail. The only material difference is the sender tag ("DICK'S | Clearance" vs. the main list) and a new "Must-have gear for an epic summer!" product grid that names individual SKUs. That product grid is the one genuine upgrade.
- The subject is doing this email no favors. At 68 characters and written like a press release sentence, it reads as automated output rather than marketing copy. The preheader doubles down by leading with shipping logistics ("Order Today. Sport it Tomorrow.") rather than amplifying the clearance hook.
- At this point in the cadence, a real subscriber who already got the July 18 clearance email, the July 19 deals blast, and two Nike Deal Days emails in the last 24 hours is highly likely to skip this. The creative fatigue is the primary risk — not the email itself.

## What's working

- **Hero is unmistakable.** "UP TO 70% OFF" in massive white type on a dark hero lands instantly — visual hierarchy is solid.
- **"Limited Time" badge + green CTA pair well.** The green SHOP NOW button stands out against the white body; the badge just above it creates a low-friction visual chain.
- **Named product grid is a lift.** The "Must-have gear for an epic summer!" section with Owala, Kijaro, New Balance 740, Jordan 1 Kids', and CALIA SKUs is more actionable than a pure category browse.
- **ScoreCard loyalty block at top.** Loyalty members see the program surface immediately — good placement.

## What's weak

- **Subject reads like a sentence, not a hook.** "Clearance items are up to 70% off during the summer clearance event." is 68 characters of flat declarative copy with no urgency, no persona hook, and no differentiator from the last four sends.
- **Preheader wastes the slot.** "Order Today. Sport it Tomorrow. Discover more advice, same-day delivery and free shipping." promotes shipping logistics when it should be deepening the clearance pitch.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is the same hero creative as the July 18 clearance email, landing one day after two Nike Deal Days emails. A subscriber who sees this sender in their inbox has already been here three times this week.
- **No deadline specificity.** "Limited Time" appears on a badge but nowhere is a concrete end date or countdown visible — weakens urgency against a crowded inbox.
- **Category rail and "more top deals" icons are filler.** Golf, Outdoor, Team Sports, Footwear icons add no incremental reason to click vs. the existing clearance landing page.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the subject to under 50 chars and lead with scarcity.** The current sentence tells, it doesn't pull. A sharper hook referencing what's actually ending, not that a sale exists, would break the pattern.
- **Subject Alt A:** `70% off — clearance ends soon`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer clearance window is closing`
- 2. **Rewrite the preheader to amplify the offer, not the shipping policy.** The shipping message belongs in the footer, not the most-read inbox line.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop shoes, clothes & gear before sizes run out`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Hundreds of items marked down — shop the best before they're gone`
- 3. **Add a real deadline.** Put a specific end date or "ends Sunday" label on the hero badge and "Limited Time" text. "Limited Time" alone is wallpaper at this point in the cadence.
- 4. **Promote the product grid into the hero.** The Owala, New Balance 740, and CALIA pieces are the only genuinely new content vs. prior sends. Featuring one of them in the hero ("New Balance 740 — now 40% off in clearance") would give repeat openers a reason to re-engage.
- 5. **Retire the category icon rail for this send.** Golf/Outdoor/Fan Shop icons are placeholder content that adds scroll weight without click motivation. Replacing them with the top-3 clearance categories by inventory depth would be more useful.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods Clearance Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard ESP-wrapped tracking architecture with solid mobile CSS. However, the JSON-LD structured data block contains invalid JSON (JS-style comments) and multiple unfilled template placeholders, rendering the Gmail Promotions tab card and DiscountOffer schema non-functional. One MSO conditional CSS block also contains a syntax error affecting Outlook rendering.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**ESP redirect wrapper confirmed** — all CTA links route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/{batch_id}/...` before forwarding to final destinations. Standard pattern; no bare untracked URLs observed in the visible portion.

**Open-tracking pixel** at `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019f812f8bc2990591369ff0b384/f9a2743c263d4fd100004c5a42963aa1` — implemented correctly (`display:none; max-height:0px; mso-hide:all`), but uses single-quoted `src` attribute (`src='...'`) inconsistent with the rest of the document's double-quote convention. Not a rendering bug but worth normalizing.

**UTM parameters** — not visible in the ESP wrapper URLs in the truncated source. Confirm that final destination URLs (post-redirect) carry `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` parameters; if the redirect strips them, site analytics will misattribute this traffic.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**MSO conditional CSS syntax error** — in the `<!--[if (gte mso 9)|(IE)]>` block, the `div` rule has a stray double-quote before the closing brace:

```css
div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
```

The trailing `"` makes this an invalid CSS declaration. Outlook's parser will likely drop the entire `div` rule, which can cause layout issues (unwanted gaps, incorrect font sizes) in Outlook 2007–2019.

**Logo `alt` attribute** — the `<img id="logo" src="https://s7d6.scene7.com/...` tag is truncated before the `alt` attribute is visible. Must be confirmed present; missing `alt` on the header logo fails WCAG 2.1 §1.1.1 and will display a broken icon in image-off environments.

**Web font versioning** — Google Fonts Archivo loaded as `/archivo/v7/` (a 2019 version). Not a rendering failure in supported clients, but newer versions of Archivo exist. Risk is low; flag for font cache hygiene.

**Favicon `<link>` in `<head>`** — `<link rel="icon" type="image/x-icon" href="...favicon.ico"/>` is ignored by all email clients and adds ~100 bytes of unnecessary payload. Remove.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`) are present in the visible source, appropriate for a broadcast clearance send.

**Unfilled template fields in JSON-LD** (`<script type="application/ld+json">`):

| Field | Value | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| `DiscountOffer.description` | `""` | Empty |
| `DiscountOffer.discountCode` | `""` | Empty |
| `DiscountOffer.availabilityEnds` | `""` | Empty |
| `PromotionCard.image` | `""` | Empty — Gmail promo card won't render |
| `DiscountOffer.availabilityStarts` | `"2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"` | Stale — 6-year-old hardcoded date |

---

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe** — not visible in the truncated source. Must be confirmed present in the footer; absence would be a CAN-SPAM violation.

**Authentication** — sending domain is `e.dcsg.com` (subdomain). SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment cannot be verified from HTML alone. These should be confirmed via DMARC report or header inspection on a received copy; misalignment on `e.dcsg.com` vs. `dcsg.com` would affect deliverability.

**`from` display name** — `"DICK'S | Clearance"` uses a `'` (right single quotation mark / U+2019) in the display name. Not a compliance issue, but confirm ESP encodes this correctly in the `From` header to avoid rendering artifacts in older clients.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**JSON-LD invalid — Gmail Promotions tab card will not render.** The `<script type="application/ld+json">` block contains JavaScript single-line comments (`//`), which are not valid JSON:

```json
}, { // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
     // Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
  "@context": "http://schema.org/",
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": ""
}]
```

`//` comments cause a JSON parse error, invalidating the **entire** structured data block. Gmail's parser will reject all three schemas (`Organization`, `DiscountOffer`, `PromotionCard`), suppressing the promotional tab annotation and deal card entirely.

Additionally, `PromotionCard.image` is empty even after stripping comments, so the card would not display anyway.

**Landing page alignment** — cannot confirm without inspecting post-redirect destinations. UTM parameters (see §2) should carry `utm_campaign` values that match campaign naming in GA4/analytics dashboards.

---

### 7. Recommendations

**P0 — fix before any future send:**

1. **Strip all `//` comments from the JSON-LD block.** JSON does not support comments; this breaks every structured data schema in the email.
2. **Populate or remove empty JSON-LD fields** (`description`, `discountCode`, `availabilityEnds`, `PromotionCard.image`). Empty strings degrade schema quality signals.
3. **Fix the stale `availabilityStarts` date** (`2020-03-06`). Update to the actual campaign start date.
4. **Fix MSO conditional CSS syntax error** — remove the stray `"` from the `div` rule closing: `padding:0;}` (not `padding:0;"}`).

**P1 — confirm before deployment:**

5. Verify `alt` text is present on the `#logo` image.
6. Confirm CAN-SPAM footer (physical address + unsubscribe link) is present in the non-truncated HTML.
7. Confirm UTM parameters survive the ESP redirect and appear on landing pages.
8. Validate SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment for `e.dcsg.com`.

**P2 — low-priority cleanup:**

9. Remove the unused `<link rel="icon">` from `<head>`.
10. Normalize `src='...'` (single-quoted) on the tracking pixel to double-quoted for consistency.
