# Delivered 📩 Our best deals of the week
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-08**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S third "Best Deals of the Week" compilation in four days — June 5 scored 9/10, the earlier June 8 Father's Day-wrapped version scored 8/10, and now here's a third. The underlying creative is the same as those two: 50% OFF hero badge, multi-category product rows (clothing, drinkware, golf, outdoor, fitness), and a grid of SHOP NOW buttons. The email itself renders cleanly and the visual hierarchy holds up.
- The serious problem is the subject line. "Delivered 📩 Our best deals of the week" uses the word "Delivered" and an inbox emoji to mimic a shipping notification — a deliberate dark pattern designed to trick subscribers into opening by impersonating a transactional alert. That's inbox manipulation. It may bump the open rate short-term; it erodes brand trust and risks spam filter classification over time.
- Beyond the subject, the content is a near-carbon-copy of the June 5 and June 8 AM sends. A subscriber who opened either of those has no new reason to click here. This email is coasting on creative that already did its job twice this week.

## What's working

- **50% OFF badge dominates the hero** — the eye lands on the discount in under a second; visual hierarchy is tight.
- **Multi-category layout is clean** — no render bugs, no broken images, product rows are evenly spaced and legible even at reduced size.
- **Category breadth is strong** — clothing, drinkware, golf, outdoor essentials, fitness, fan shop — something for nearly every sporting goods buyer.
- **SHOP NOW buttons are clear and consistent** throughout every section; no ambiguity about where to click.

## What's weak

- **"Delivered 📩" subject prefix is a dark pattern** — impersonating a package-tracking alert to manufacture opens is deceptive and the kind of tactic subscribers notice and resent.
- **Second "Best Deals" send today, third this week** — cadence has crossed from helpful to intrusive; the June 5 and June 8 AM versions already owned this slot.
- **No Father's Day specificity** — the June 8 AM send already made the Father's Day angle; this one drops that seasonal hook and replaces it with nothing, making the email feel generic.
- **No specific offer in the subject** — "Our best deals of the week" is vague; the June 5 subject ("Up to 50% off!") was sharper and earned a 9/10 on the back of it.
- **CTA copy is generic** — every button says "SHOP NOW"; one category-specific CTA ("Shop Golf Deals" or "Shop Fitness") would anchor the most motivated buyers faster.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the "Delivered" fake-notification prefix immediately.** It's the single highest-risk element in this email. Swap to a subject that leads with the actual offer percentage, as June 5 did.
- 2. **Either skip this send or make it meaningfully different** — if it goes out at all today, it needs a distinct angle (e.g., Father's Day gift guide, one featured category hero) rather than a fourth repetition of the same grid.
- 3. **Put the discount percentage in the subject line** — "Up to 50% off" drove opens on June 5; this subject buries the lead.
- 4. **Swap one "SHOP NOW" to a category-specific CTA** — the golf or fitness row would benefit most.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 50% off — this week's best deals`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Dad's weekend deals: up to 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Golf, fitness, outdoor and more — shop before the deals reset`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Father's Day gifts up to 50% off — deals end soon`

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods "Best Deals of the Week"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is built on Epsilon/Harmony infrastructure with click-tracking redirects and a beacon pixel. Three confirmed technical defects: invalid JSON in the structured-data block, a stale 2020 date in that same block, and a `_dev`-path asset URL that appears to be a staging reference rather than a production CDN path.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Click-tracking:** All CTA links route through `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610000019ea8f1b12abf02def4bbcfb648/…` (Epsilon redirect). Functional, but destination URLs are not inspectable from the truncated source — UTM parameter presence cannot be confirmed (see §6).

**Beacon pixel:** `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019ea8f1b12abf02def4bbcfb648/35e69aa08245454200004c5a42963aa1` is loaded as a hidden `<img>` inside `#ssl`. The `<img>` is missing an `alt=""` attribute, which is required even for tracking pixels.

**Staging asset URL (confirmed defect):** The logo `src` attribute resolves to:
```
https://dsg.images.harmony.epsilon.com/_phoenix/_dev/AEM/img/headers/brand-logo--dk…
```
The `/_dev/` path segment is a staging/development path. If this is not a known production alias, it risks returning a 404 or wrong asset in production. Verify against the expected production CDN path (typically `/_prod/` or no environment prefix).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Invalid JSON-LD (confirmed defect):** The `<script type="application/ld+json">` block contains JavaScript-style single-line comments (`//`), which are not valid JSON:
```json
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported...
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": ""
}
```
JSON does not support comments. Any email client or Gmail preview parser that attempts to consume this structured data will throw a parse error. The entire `<script>` block will be silently ignored.

**Outlook `overflow:hidden` + `border-radius` incompatibility:** The header table applies both `border-radius:8px 8px 0px 0px` and `overflow:hidden` inline. Outlook (MSO) ignores both properties; the rounded corner will not render and any content clipping dependent on `overflow:hidden` will bleed. The MSO conditional styles at the top use `border-collapse:collapse` but the outer wrapper uses `border-collapse:separate` — inconsistency may cause spacing differences in Outlook.

**Missing `alt` on tracking pixel:** `<img src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/…'>` has no `alt` attribute. Screen readers and strict accessibility validators will flag this.

**Logo alt text:** Truncated in the provided source — cannot confirm it is present. Should be `alt="DICK'S Sporting Goods"`.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unfilled `{{token}}` or `%%token%%` style placeholders are visible in the provided source.

**Empty JSON-LD fields (confirmed defect):** The structured data block contains unpopulated fields that were not removed before send:
```json
{ "@type": "DiscountOffer",
  "description": "",
  "discountCode": "",
  "availabilityEnds": "" }
{ "@type": "PromotionCard",
  "image": "" }
```
These empty strings indicate the template was sent without populating or removing the offer schema. Not a user-visible issue but will produce malformed structured data for Gmail's promotional tab rendering.

**Stale template date (confirmed defect):**
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```
This is a March 2020 date — clearly a placeholder from the original template that was never updated. Gmail uses `availabilityStarts`/`availabilityEnds` to display promotion cards in the Promotions tab; a 6-year-old start date may cause the card to be deprioritized or suppressed.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe:** The provided HTML is truncated before the footer. Cannot confirm presence of physical mailing address or one-click unsubscribe link. These are required — audit the full source.

**Sending domain alignment:** From header shows `DSG@e.dcsg.com`. The `e.dcsg.com` subdomain must have:
- SPF record covering Epsilon's sending IPs
- DKIM signature with `d=e.dcsg.com` or a domain alignment match
- DMARC policy on `dcsg.com` that covers the subdomain

Not verifiable from HTML alone — requires header inspection on a received copy (check `Authentication-Results` header in a received message).

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM Parameters)

All links are wrapped in Epsilon click-tracking redirects. The raw destination URLs are not visible in the source. Cannot confirm whether UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are appended to destination URLs.

**Risk:** If the Epsilon redirect does not append UTMs to destination URLs, all traffic from this send will arrive at dickssportinggoods.com attributed as `(direct)` in GA4/analytics, not as email traffic.

**Recommended check:** Extract the decoded destination URL from any CTA link (e.g., by clicking in a test send or decoding the redirect) and confirm it contains at minimum:
```
?utm_source=email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=<campaign_id>
```
