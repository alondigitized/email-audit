# Your update — 20% off select Nike clothing and shoes
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-26**
## Executive summary

- A day after the "Will you shop this week's deals to get ready for summer?" soft-reset email (May 26, 9/10), DICK'S sent this one same-day — and the subject is the best thing about it. Naming Nike at a concrete 20% in the subject line is the sharpest, most specific hook I've seen across the entire Memorial Day window of sends I've reviewed. The hero delivers on it: high-contrast red banner, large "UP TO 20% OFF" typography, readable subhead, and a visible green CTA button above the fold. Clean render, no bugs, good visual hierarchy.
- Then the scroll begins, and the Nike email evaporates. Everything below the hero is the identical DICK'S multi-category deals hub I've audited in half a dozen sends this week — Golf, Outdoor Essentials, Fan Shop, Team Sports, Fitness Equipment, and a Stanley-adjacent lifestyle callout. At one point Golf shows "Up to 10% Off" while Fan Shop and Outdoor Essentials show "Up to 60% Off," which quietly makes the hero's 20% feel mid-pack rather than the lead deal. The Nike message never resurfaces.
- Net judgment: a subject line doing heroic work to rescue a template email that wasn't built for it. The opener earns attention; the body doesn't spend it.

## What's working

- Subject names a specific brand (Nike) at a specific percentage — sharpest DICK'S subject I've seen in this entire Memorial Day run
- Hero banner renders cleanly: high-contrast red, large "UP TO 20% OFF," readable subhead, and a green SHOP NOW button all land above the fold
- "Select" and "up to" qualifiers are upfront in the hero, not buried — honest scoping before the click
- The multi-category hub is organized and labeled, making it easy to scan for a relevant deal even if it dilutes the Nike focus
- No render bugs: no broken images, no overlapping text, no empty image slots

## What's weak

- The Nike subject sets an expectation of a Nike email; the body is the standard six-category deals dump with Nike only in the hero — structural bait-and-switch
- No deadline anywhere visible — "20% off" with no end date gives the subscriber zero reason to click today vs. next week
- "Your update" as a subject opener reads like a transactional alert (account change, order status) and wastes 11 characters delivering no value
- Golf's "Up to 10% Off" sits alongside Outdoor/Fan Shop "Up to 60% Off," which undercuts the hero's 20% as the lead deal rather than reinforcing it
- No individual Nike products, colorways, or styles surfaced — the click is a leap of faith with nothing to anchor it

## Recommendations

- 1. **Strip the deals hub from this send or move it to a second scroll.** A Nike-specific email should be all Nike — key shoes, featured apparel, maybe an "also shop" tertiary block. The six-category template actively contradicts the subject.
- 2. **Add a deadline.** "Ends Sunday" or "Sale ends midnight" costs one line and dramatically changes the urgency calculus.
- 3. **Replace "Your update" with offer language.** The subject's first two words are wasted on a transactional-sounding hook; every character should earn its place.
- 4. **Surface 2-3 specific Nike products in the hero block.** A shoe image with name and sale price anchors the offer and raises click confidence before the scroll.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Nike sale: 20% off select styles, this week only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Top runners, training tees, and hoodies — pick yours before they're gone.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS syntax error in MSO conditional block:**
```css
div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
```

A stray `"` appears before the closing `}`. This is inside `<!--[if (gte mso 9)|(IE)]>` so it only affects Outlook/IE rendering; may cause the entire conditional style block to be dropped in those clients.

**Google Fonts external dependency**: `@font-face` loads woff2 from `fonts.gstatic.com`. Correctly wrapped in `@media screen` to block Outlook from attempting web font fetch. Falls back to Arial — acceptable.

**`role="presentation"` on all layout tables**: correctly applied.

**`lang="en"` on `<html>`**: present — correct.

**`alt` text on logo image**: truncated in provided source — verify it is not empty (`alt=""` is acceptable for decorative logos only if a visible text equivalent exists nearby).

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Critical: JSON-LD block contains multiple unfilled template fields:**

```json
{
  "@type": "DiscountOffer",
  "description": "",          ← empty
  "discountCode": "",         ← empty
  "availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00",  ← stale date (6 years old)
  "availabilityEnds": ""      ← empty
},
{
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  "image": ""                 ← empty, designer note left in production
}
```

**Issues:**
- `availabilityStarts` is `2020-03-06` — a template default that was never updated. Gmail uses this structured data to render deal annotations in the inbox; an expired/wrong date can suppress the annotation entirely.
- `availabilityEnds` empty — Gmail deal annotations require a valid end date to display.
- `"image": ""` — PromotionCard annotation will not render without an image URL.
- Designer comment `// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!` shipped in production JSON-LD.
- JavaScript-style `//` comments inside the `<script type="application/ld+json">` block are **invalid JSON**. Spec-compliant parsers will reject the entire block; lenient parsers (like Google's) may tolerate it, but it is undefined behavior.
- `@type: "DiscountOffer"` and `@type: "PromotionCard"` are **not standard Schema.org types**. The correct type for promotions is `schema.org/Offer`. Gmail's promotion annotation parser may still accept proprietary types, but this is non-standard and fragile.
