# Best deals of the week are HERE! 📍 Up to 50% OFF.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- This is Dick's doing what Dick's does on a "normal" week — stacking five category swim lanes (Men, Women, Kids, Outdoor, Golf), each with its own "Up to X% Off" headline and a SHOP NOW button, all riding a 50% OFF hero badge. The template is clean, the visual hierarchy works, and the render is bug-free. But "Best Deals of the Week" is functionally the same promise as the May 26 "Will you shop this week's deals" send (9/10 that day), which arrived less than 24 hours ago. For a subscriber who opened that one, this reads as a same-week repeat with a louder subject line, not a new send worth opening.
- The internal clearance callout — a "HUNDREDS OF NEW CLEARANCE STYLES" banner dropped mid-email — is a direct echo of the clearance-sub-brand email that also went out on 2026-05-27. Two emails in one day, with one literally borrowing the other's headline, signals operational chaos more than strategic planning.
- The email is competent but exhausted. Dick's has now sent this same weekly-deals frame twice in two days, and this version doesn't add anything new — no sharper offer, no single hero product, no member benefit to differentiate. It will perform adequately for the unengaged segment but is a missed opportunity to actually close the post-Memorial Day window with something fresh.

## What's working

- The 50% OFF badge is visually dominant in the hero — the eye goes there first before reading a word of copy.
- Five-category swim-lane structure (Men/Women/Kids/Outdoor/Golf) covers the full household in one scroll, maximizing relevance surface area.
- "Up to 60% Off Kids'" actually outguns the other categories — a small detail that could drive clicks from parents.
- Render is clean: no broken images, no layout breaks, no overlapping text visible anywhere.
- The brand logos row ("Score the latest trends") adds credibility without requiring extra copy.

## What's weak

- Subject "Best deals of the week are HERE!" is identical in promise to May 26's "Will you shop this week's deals" — subscribers who opened that send are seeing the same pitch 24 hours later.
- "Up to 50% Off" appears five times with zero differentiation between sections — there's no single compelling lead offer to anchor the email.
- The mid-email "HUNDREDS OF NEW CLEARANCE STYLES" banner borrows today's clearance sub-brand email headline verbatim, fragmenting the message rather than reinforcing it.
- No specific product or individual item is featured — every CTA points to a category page, which adds friction for anyone wanting a fast impulse purchase.
- No loyalty or ScoreCard signal anywhere visible; for members, there's no reason this feels better than opening the website directly.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with one hero offer, not five equal ones.** Pick the single most compelling deal — a specific shoe, a specific brand — and feature it in the hero with a direct product link. The category swim-lanes can stay below the fold.
- 2. **Stop repeating the weekly-deals frame the day after sending it.** This should either be a uniquely angled send (e.g., "Nike at 50% — today only") or held 24 hours.
- 3. **Remove the clearance crosslink banner or convert it into an actual featured product.** As-is it reads like internal cross-promotion, not subscriber value.
- 4. **Add one time-bound element with a real deadline.** "This week" is soft. "Ends Sunday" would create actual urgency.
- 5. **Surface a ScoreCard benefit** — even a one-liner — for members. It's free differentiation.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Nike, adidas, Under Armour — up to 50% off this week`
- **Subject Alt B:** `50% off Men's, Women's & Kids' — ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Top brands, real discounts. Shop before the week's out.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New markdowns just dropped — all categories, all week.`

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Click-tracking redirect chain** — All links route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610000019e6b26ceb5955d6ff4bbe5be68/…`. This is expected for Epsilon, but the destination URL is opaque in the source; UTM parameter carrythrough cannot be confirmed without resolving the redirect (see §6).

**Open-tracking pixel** — `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019e6b26ceb5955d6ff4bbe5be68/…` is rendered with `display:none` and suppression styles. No `alt=""` attribute is present on the `<img>` tag, which is required per HTML spec even for hidden elements.

**Logo link** — `href` resolves to the same Epsilon click-tracking domain. Destination is correct (`dickssportinggoods.com` home), but truncated source prevents full verification.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate CSS declaration** — `font-family` is declared twice consecutively in `<body>` style, second time with `!important`. This is redundant noise; the first declaration is overridden without adding specificity benefit.

```css
body { font-family:Archivo,Arial,sans-serif; font-family:Archivo,Arial,sans-serif!important }
```

**Stale Google Fonts CDN path** — Archivo is loaded at `fonts.gstatic.com/s/archivo/v7/…`. The current font version on Google Fonts CDN is v19+. The v7 path still resolves, but it is several major versions behind and may render with older metrics.

**Tracking pixel missing `alt` attribute** — `<img src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/…'>` has no `alt=""`. While invisible, this fails HTML validation and can trigger accessibility scanner warnings.

**MSO conditional CSS** — The `div { background-color:inherit; font-size:0; … }` rule in the Outlook conditional block has an unclosed brace (trailing `"` instead of `}`). Likely a copy-paste artifact; Outlook will silently ignore the malformed rule.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens



These are not merge tokens that failed substitution — they are literal empty strings, meaning the PromotionCard Gmail annotation will have no image and the DiscountOffer will surface no discount details to Gmail's promotion tab renderer.

**`id="header-non-sc-member"`** — Suggests a segmented send (member vs. non-member). If there is a corresponding `header-sc-member` block, confirm it is suppressed via conditional content or a separate template variant, not just CSS `display:none` (which would still be in the DOM).

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**JSON-LD contains JavaScript-style comments** — `//` line comments are invalid in JSON. The entire `<script type="application/ld+json">` block will fail to parse in any spec-compliant JSON parser, including Gmail's structured data extractor. This is a silent failure — no error surfaced to the recipient, but all Gmail promotion annotations are dropped.

```json
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
  // Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
```

**Stale `availabilityStarts` date** — The DiscountOffer schema contains:

```json

```

This is a 6-year-old date, clearly a template default that was never updated. If Gmail were parsing the block (it isn't, due to the comment issue above), this would mis-represent offer availability.

**HTML comment year mismatch** — `<!--DKS Email Marketing 2025-->` appears in a 2026 send. Minor, but confirms the template header was not refreshed.

**Unsubscribe / physical address / authentication** — HTML is truncated before the footer; cannot confirm presence of CAN-SPAM physical address, unsubscribe link, or authentication headers (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) from the provided source. These must be verified against the full email and sending domain DNS records.
