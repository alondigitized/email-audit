# 📣 Starting NOW! Score $20 off $100+ in-app (today only!)
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S Deal Days' follow-through send on the summer sale event introduced June 21 — and it earns its place as a distinct email rather than another clearance clone. The hero is a clean, green-branded app-exclusive mechanic: $20 off $100+ in-app, today only. That's a real offer with a real hook that the June 21 teaser email was building toward.
- The friction problem is baked into the premise. The entire lead offer requires either downloading or opening the app, which is a genuine barrier to impulse click-through. Subscribers who receive this on desktop or who haven't yet installed the app face a detour before they can redeem anything — that cost is real and it's not addressed anywhere in the visible copy.
- The clearance sections (Up to 60% Off, specific product callouts for Crocs, golf putters, cycling shoes) dilute the message. A cleaner execution would either be a pure app-exclusive send or a pure Deal Days clearance roundup — doing both in the same scroll drains urgency from the lead offer.

## What's working

- **Hero offer is specific and scannable** — "$20 Off $100+" renders cleanly as the opening value proposition, above the fold.
- **Green Deal Days branding is consistent** with the June 21 event launch; this feels like a coherent campaign series rather than a one-off.
- **"Today only" urgency is stated twice** (subject + visible banner area), which gives the time-bound claim credibility.
- **Product sections add depth below the fold** — Crocs, golf putters, and cycling shoe deals give browsers something to click even if the app offer doesn't land.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, images load, text doesn't overlap.

## What's weak

- **App-gate friction** — the headline offer requires the app, but nothing in the visible email smooths that transition (no "already have it? open app" link, no download count/social proof).
- **The clearance 60%-off section undermines urgency** — layering a permanent-feeling clearance banner below a "today only" app offer confuses the email's identity.
- **CTA copy is generic** — "SHOP APP" tells you the mechanism but not the payoff; "Claim $20 Off in the App" would convert better.
- **Product mix is scattershot** — Crocs, golf putters, and indoor cycling shoes in the same scroll doesn't feel curated for any single buyer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the CTA from "SHOP APP" to "Claim $20 Off in the App"** — this is the single highest-leverage change. Naming the reward in the button copy closes the loop and removes ambiguity.
- 2. **Add an "Already have the app? Tap here"** deep-link alternative for existing app users — this cuts one friction step for loyal subscribers.
- 3. **Kill or separate the 60% off clearance block** — send that as its own email tomorrow. Today's send should be 100% app-exclusive Deal Days.
- 4. **Add a countdown timer or hard deadline callout** ("Offer expires midnight ET") — "today only" in the subject doesn't repeat visibly in the body with enough visual weight.
- **Subject Alt A:** `App exclusive: $20 off your next $100+ order — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Unlock $20 off in the DICK'S app — Deal Days starts now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Open the app, add $100+ to your cart, save $20. Offer ends tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your Deal Days exclusive is live — grab it before midnight.`

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods "Deal Days" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email infrastructure is standard Epsilon/Harmony ESP with MSO conditional comments and responsive breakpoints. Three confirmed bugs exist in the JSON-LD block; the rest of the audit is limited by source truncation (footer/unsubscribe section not included).

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Redirect domain confirmed:** All CTA hrefs route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610000019eeee...` (Epsilon click tracking).

**UTM parameters — cannot confirm.** The redirect URLs are Epsilon-encoded path slugs, not query-string UTMs. Whether UTMs are injected post-redirect is not determinable from the provided source. This should be verified against an actual click-through to the landing page (see §6).

**SSL/open pixel:** `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019eeee...` fires on open — standard; no issue.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- `lang="en"` on `<html>`, `charset="UTF-8"`, `viewport` meta, and `role="presentation"` on all layout tables — correct.
- Web font (`Archivo`) loaded as `woff2`-only via Google Fonts. Clients without font support fall back to `Arial` per the stack — acceptable.
- **Logo `alt` attribute:** The `<img id="logo">` tag is cut off in the provided source. Confirm `alt="DICK'S Sporting Goods"` is present; a missing `alt` breaks screen readers and renders broken text in image-blocked clients.
- `border-collapse:separate` on the root wrapper table is non-standard (most ESP templates use `collapse`). It is intentional here to support `border-radius` on child cells; no functional issue, but note it diverges from Outlook safe defaults.
- MSO conditional comments and `<o:OfficeDocumentSettings>` block are present and correctly structured.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Three unfilled merge tokens in the JSON-LD block are confirmed bugs:**

```json
"description": "",        // DiscountOffer — empty
"discountCode": "",       // DiscountOffer — empty
"availabilityEnds": "",   // DiscountOffer — empty
"image": ""               // PromotionCard — empty
```

Additionally, `availabilityStarts` contains a **hardcoded stale date from 2020:**

```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```

This means the Gmail Promotions Tab annotation (discount badge, expiry countdown, promo card image) will either fail to render or display incorrect data. The promo card image being empty is especially impactful — Gmail silently suppresses the card if the image URL is absent.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM / Landing Page Alignment)

**Cannot confirm UTM injection** from the provided source. The Epsilon redirect system may append UTMs server-side after the click resolves. Recommended verification:

1. Click a CTA in a test send.
2. Inspect the final landing URL for `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content`.
3. Confirm the landing page reflects the in-app offer ($20 off $100+ via app) — if links resolve to the desktop site without app-specific context, there is a continuity gap.
