# Up to 50% off select Nike, adidas, VRST and more is yours! 💰
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S fifth-plus email in roughly 72 hours, and it shows. The format is a carbon copy of the June 13 and June 14 Flash Sale multi-module blasts — same department-by-department grid structure, same "up to 50% off" headline, same sea of SHOP NOW buttons. The only real creative move here is naming Nike, adidas, and VRST in the subject, which at least gives the inbox preview a brand anchor.
- The Father's Day angle is criminally buried. This email was sent June 15 — exactly one week before Father's Day 2026 — and DICK'S tucked a small "Get It Today for Dad's Day" module near the bottom instead of leading with the most urgent, calendar-driven reason to buy right now. That's backwards. The deadline was staring them in the face.
- Eight competing modules with nearly identical visual weight means there's no real offer hierarchy. The Nike/VRST hero reads cleanly, then the email immediately fragments into golf, team sports, outdoor, pet gear, and fitness — losing the thread entirely. Unfocused, familiar to the point of fatigue, but not broken.

## What's working

- **Brand-anchored hero**: The oversized Nike swoosh + VRST logo pairing in the hero is clean and immediately communicates premium activewear value.
- **Concrete discount in subject**: Naming specific brands + "up to 50% off" gives the inbox preview a real reason to open.
- **Father's Day module present**: At least the seasonal hook exists, even if it's underweighted.
- **No render bugs**: Layout is clean — images load, no overlapping text, no broken modules.

## What's weak

- **Father's Day urgency wasted**: The one-week-out Father's Day deadline is the strongest sales argument in June and it's buried in the bottom third.
- **Module overload dilutes attention**: Eight department callouts at roughly equal visual weight means nothing feels urgent — subscribers have learned to scroll past this format.
- **VRST in the subject pulls focus**: Pairing an in-house private label alongside Nike and adidas in hero positioning reads as padding; subscribers who don't recognize VRST may feel the subject over-promised.
- **No offer deadline visible**: Nothing in the email body signals when this sale ends, removing purchase urgency.
- **Cadence fatigue**: Fifth DSG email in ~72 hours — the same "Flash Sale" promise from June 13 and 14 is now trading under "up to 50% off" branding with identical structure. Subscribers are pattern-matching this as a chronic discount, not a live event.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with Father's Day urgency, not a flat brand list.** Reframe the entire email around "one week left for Dad" — front-load one strong Father's Day gift hero (Nike gear, adidas shoes, VRST shorts as a bundle suggestion) and cut four of the eight modules. The gift angle justifies the discount better than another generic sale.
- 2. **Add a deadline.** "Sale ends Sunday" or "Shop by June 21" visible in or below the hero converts browsers to buyers. The ScoreCard email on June 15 (scored 8/10) nailed this with "Shop by Sunday" — replicate that urgency here.
- 3. **Collapse the module grid to three focused categories.** Golf, outdoor, and pet gear in the same email as Nike activewear for Father's Day shopping is incoherent. Pick the top three gift categories for dad and kill the rest.
- 4. **Retire VRST from hero positioning until it has name recognition.** Let Nike and adidas carry the subject; mention VRST as a value-add inside the body.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dad's Day deal: Up to 50% off Nike, adidas & more 🎁`
- **Subject Alt B:** `One week left — 50% off Father's Day picks from Nike & adidas`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Grab his gift now — sale ends June 21.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Nike, adidas & VRST up to half off. Shop before Father's Day.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS syntax error in MSO conditional block (head `<style>`):**
```css
div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
```
A stray `"` before `}` makes this declaration invalid. Outlook's CSS parser may skip the entire rule or behave unpredictably on `<div>` elements.

**MSO conditional version inconsistency:**
- `<head>` uses `<!--[if (gte mso 9)|(IE)]>` (versioned)
- `<body>` table block uses `<!--[if (gte mso)|(IE)]>` (unversioned)

Inconsistent targeting; should be unified to `(gte mso 9)|(IE)` throughout.

**`role="presentation"` on all layout tables** — correct usage.

**`lang="en"` on `<html>`** — present, correct.

**Web font:** Archivo loaded from `fonts.gstatic.com` at a pinned version (`/archivo/v7/`). Version 7 is outdated (current is v19+); cached WOFF2 files are unlikely to change, but the pin means no upstream improvements. Low risk for email.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM / Landing Page)

UTM parameters are not present in the HTML link `href` values — they are presumably appended by the Epsilon redirect layer. This is a common ESP pattern but creates a risk:

- If the redirect strips or misformats UTMs, GA4/analytics will attribute sessions to `(direct)` instead of the campaign.

**Action required:** Pull a sample redirect destination URL (e.g., by following a CTA link) and confirm UTM parameters are correctly passed through: `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content` at minimum.
