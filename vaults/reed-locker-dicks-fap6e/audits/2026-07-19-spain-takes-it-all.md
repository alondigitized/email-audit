# ⚽️ Spain takes it all! ⚽️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- This is the best email DICK'S has sent all week — and it's not close. After eight consecutive blast-and-burn "50% OFF this week's deals" sends, they finally found a reason to show up in the inbox that isn't manufactured urgency. Spain winning the World Cup (presumably the 2026 final) is a genuine cultural moment, and DICK'S tied the send to it with clean execution: co-branded DICK'S + adidas header, a red Spain jersey hero, and "SPAIN TAKES IT ALL" in commanding white type over a warm dark background. That's reactive marketing done right.
- The fatal gap is the absence of any discount. This is a moment-marketing email with no offer attached, which means it wins on relevance but loses on conversion. A subscriber who's excited about Spain's win clicks "SHOP NOW," lands on a product page, sees full retail price, and may walk. That's a missed layup — a 20% Spain victory flash offer or a bundle (jersey + match ball) with a visible price reduction would turn this into a legitimate sales driver.
- The adidas 2026 World Cup Official Match Ball product placement mid-email is the smartest piece of merchandising here. It anchors the narrative to a specific, ownable product at exactly the right cultural moment. Everything else — the bags section, the same-day delivery reminder — dilutes the focus.
- Compared to the July 19 Nike Deal Days email (scored 8/10 here), this one matches that email's brand-partnership format but substitutes deal-depth for event-relevance. The tradeoff is real: this email has stronger narrative hook, weaker close.

## What's working

- **Hero is immediately legible** — Spain jersey on dark background, bold type, one clear CTA. No noise competing with the message.
- **Timeliness is unimpeachable** — This dropped the day Spain won. Cultural moment emails live or die by timing, and this landed correctly.
- **adidas co-branding adds credibility** — DICK'S + adidas logos together signal this is official gear, not knockoff inventory.
- **Official Match Ball product is a standout placement** — Specific, ownable, collectible item that's directly tied to the win. This is the one product that practically sells itself today.
- **Thematic break from cadence fatigue** — After seven deal-blast emails in five days, even existing subscribers who tuned out will notice a Spain-jerseys email looks and feels different.

## What's weak

- **No offer anywhere in the email** — "Celebrate the victory" is a mood, not a reason to buy today over tomorrow. The prior Nike Deal Days send at least had a discount hook.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTAs throughout are generic** — When every section says "SHOP NOW," none of them say anything. "Shop Spain Gear" or "Get the Match Ball" would each pull harder.
- **Bags section is a non-sequitur** — "Scores for Soccer's Biggest Bags" feels stapled in from a different campaign. It breaks the Spain win narrative cold.
- **No size, availability, or urgency signal** — Spain jerseys will sell out. That's the real scarcity here. The email doesn't mention it.
- **Same-day delivery closer is unfocused** — It belongs in a logistics email, not a World Cup celebration send. Weakens the thematic coherence of the close.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a victory offer** — "For the next 48 hours, 20% off all Spain gear" turns this from brand moment into a converter. The event gives you permission to run a flash sale and have it feel celebratory rather than desperate.
- 2. **Rewrite every CTA to the specific product** — "Shop Spain Jerseys," "Get the Official Match Ball," "Find Your Victory Bag." One CTA per section, none of them "SHOP NOW."
- 3. **Cut the bags and same-day delivery sections** — This email should be three blocks: Spain jersey hero → official match ball → one delivery callout, full stop. Shorter and more powerful.
- 4. **Add inventory urgency** — A line like "Sizes going fast after today's final" costs nothing and activates the scarcity that genuinely exists.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Spain just won. Here's how to wear it.`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `⚽ Spain wins — official gear is here`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Jerseys, the official match ball, and same-day delivery — shop the moment.`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Celebrate with the official 2026 World Cup gear before it's gone.`

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods "Spain takes it all" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) with standard MSO/Outlook conditional comments and Google Fonts web font loading. The primary technical defect is **invalid JSON-LD syntax** that silently breaks Gmail Promotional tab card rendering; several template fields were also left unpopulated.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Tracking pixel (open tracking)**
```
https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019f7cb5b9bbc3277f369fb45280/0158569325e449eb00004c5a42963aa1
```
Standard SFMC open pixel — structurally correct. The `<img>` tag uses single quotes while the rest of the document uses double quotes (minor inconsistency, not functional).

**Click tracking**
Logo click uses SFMC redirect URL with `__dU__` subscriber lookup parameter. This is expected for SFMC personalized URLs. The final destination is not visible due to truncation — **UTM parameter presence cannot be confirmed** (see §6).

**No issues with tracking infrastructure itself**, but the tracking pixel `<img>` is missing an `alt` attribute (see §3).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

| Issue | Evidence | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| Tracking pixel missing `alt=""` | `<img src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/...' style="display:none..."/>` — no `alt` attribute | Low |
| `border-radius` on `<table>` element | `border-radius:8px 8px 0px 0px` on `#header-dicks-sporting-goods` table | Medium — Outlook ignores `border-radius` on tables; no VML fallback present, so header corners will be square in Outlook |
| MSO `a:link` global override sets `color:#141414` | `a:link { color:#141414; text-decoration:none; }` inside MSO conditional block | Low-Medium — all hyperlinks in Outlook render near-black, which may reduce CTA button contrast depending on button background color |

Google Fonts `@font-face` is correctly scoped to `@media screen` — fallback to Arial is intact. `role="presentation"` on layout tables is correct. `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` override is present and correct.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Critical — stale default template date in JSON-LD:**
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```
This is a 6-year-old hardcoded date that was never updated. It is the `DiscountOffer` start date surfaced to Gmail's promotional tab — if Gmail reads it, the offer appears expired before it starts.

**Empty merge fields in JSON-LD block:**
```json
"discountCode": "",
"availabilityEnds": "",
"description": ""
"image": ""   // PromotionCard
```
All four fields were left blank. These control Gmail Promotional tab display (discount badge, promo card image). Empty strings here mean no badge and no product image render in Gmail's promotions grid view.

No subscriber-level personalization tokens (e.g., `%%firstname%%`) are visible in the truncated source — cannot confirm presence or absence of merge tag validation.

---

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM / unsubscribe:** The HTML is truncated before the footer — unsubscribe link and physical mailing address cannot be verified from the provided source. These must be present.

**Authentication headers:** Cannot be verified from HTML source alone. Sending domain is `e.dcsg.com`. DKIM, SPF, and DMARC alignment should be confirmed against received headers in a live send.

**Preheader text** ("Celebrate with TOP Spain gear & MORE") is rendered as visible DOM text inside `#ssl` `<td>` but hidden via `font-size:0; line-height:0; height:0` — functionally correct as a preheader, though screen readers may still encounter it. An `aria-hidden="true"` on that cell would be cleaner.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters:** The HTML is truncated at the logo `<img>` tag, so destination URLs after SFMC redirect unwrap cannot be inspected. UTM coverage (source, medium, campaign, content) on all CTAs should be verified against the full source or a link-click report. The `__dU__` SFMC parameter is not a substitute for UTM attribution.

**Landing page alignment:** Cannot assess without full link list, but the email subject and preheader both reference Spain/soccer — CTA destinations should be confirmed to land on Spain-specific or soccer/football category pages, not the DSG homepage.

---

### 7. Recommendations

**P0 — Fix before any resend:**
1. **Remove JavaScript comments from JSON-LD block.** Lines beginning with `//` are invalid JSON syntax. The entire `<script type="application/ld+json">` block will fail to parse, silently dropping all Gmail Promotional tab enrichment. Strip all comment lines from the array.
2. **Populate or remove empty JSON-LD fields.** Set `availabilityEnds` to the actual campaign end date, populate `PromotionCard.image` with a valid HTTPS image URL (538×138px, JPEG/PNG only — per the comment in the source, GIF/WebP are filtered by Gmail), and provide a `description`. If no promo code exists, remove the `DiscountOffer` object entirely rather than leaving empty strings.
3. **Update `availabilityStarts` from 2020.** Replace `"2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"` with the actual send/campaign start datetime.

**P1 — Fix in template:**
4. Add `alt=""` to the tracking pixel `<img>` tag.
5. Add `aria-hidden="true"` to the preheader `<td id="ssl">`.
6. Add a VML `<v:roundrect>` fallback if rounded header corners matter in Outlook, or accept square corners and document the decision.

**P2 — Verify before each send:**
7. Confirm UTM parameters (`utm_source=email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=...`) are appended to all destination URLs after SFMC redirect resolution.
8. Verify footer contains physical mailing address and functioning one-click unsubscribe link.
9. Confirm DKIM/SPF/DMARC pass on received headers from a seed send before deployment.
