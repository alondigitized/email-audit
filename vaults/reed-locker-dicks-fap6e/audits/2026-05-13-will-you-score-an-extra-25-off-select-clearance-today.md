# Will you score an extra 25% off select clearance today?
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- This is a well-executed clearance push from DICK'S Sporting Goods. The hero lands hard — a bold red-and-black "EXTRA 25% OFF" occupies the top of the email with no ambiguity, and the SHOP NOW CTA follows immediately. That's the email doing its one job cleanly.
- The problem is everything below the fold. The email sprawls into five or six disconnected clearance categories — running shoes, men's pants, women's pants, life vests, outdoor gear, dumbbells — with no unifying logic or editorial priority. By the time you reach the "Check out these picks" shoe grid at the bottom, the email has lost any sense of curation. It reads like a warehouse dump, not a recommendation.
- The offer itself is solid: specific discount percentage up top, specific dollar prices ($19.98) on category headers, multiple SHOP NOW buttons that are clearly labeled. This is a promotional email that knows what it is. It just tries to appeal to everyone simultaneously and ends up compelling nobody in particular.

## What's working

- **Hero is immediate and scannable** — "EXTRA 25% OFF" in massive type, on a red field, reads in under a second.
- **Specific price anchoring** — "$19.98" on the pants headers gives a concrete number to latch onto, which is better than percentage-only pricing.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no layout collapse, no lorem ipsum visible anywhere.
- **Top-level category nav** (Women's / Footwear / Men's / Kids) gives skimmers a fast exit ramp to their section.
- **"Today" urgency in the subject** — the word carries real weight without resorting to countdown clocks or ALL CAPS.

## What's weak

- **Category sprawl kills focus** — running shoes, life vests, and dumbbells in the same email signals a data-blind batch blast, not a segmented send.
- **"SHOP NOW" on every button** — with five-plus sections, every CTA says the same generic thing; none of them feel earned or specific.
- **No social proof anywhere** — not a single review count, star rating, or "bestseller" badge despite multiple product tiles.
- **Life vests in a clearance email** feel off-season for May and jarring next to running footwear — the category mix undermines perceived relevance.
- **Subject line uses a question** — interrogative subjects ("Will you score…?") consistently underperform declarative statements in sporting goods verticals; the hedging works against the confident hero.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment by category** — send the running/footwear version to footwear buyers, the weights/gear version to gym shoppers. The 25% off hook is strong enough to carry a focused send.
- 2. **Replace every "SHOP NOW" with a specific verb** — "Shop Running Shoes," "Shop Weights," etc. One click of specificity lifts CTR measurably.
- 3. **Cut life vests** from this email entirely — they're jarring alongside athletic apparel and will drag down click rates on the whole send.
- 4. **Add a star-rating or review count to at least the hero product** — even one trust signal changes the conversion equation.
- 5. **Flip the subject from question to declaration:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 25% off clearance — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Clearance just got cheaper: extra 25% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Running shoes, joggers, weights — all discounted further.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Prices already cut. Now take another 25% off select styles.`
