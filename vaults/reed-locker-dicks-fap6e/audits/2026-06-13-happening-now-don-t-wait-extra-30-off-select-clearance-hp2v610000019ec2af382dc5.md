# Happening now — don't wait! Extra 30% off select CLEARANCE
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- This is the DICK'S | Clearance sub-sender's fourth email in the inbox in roughly 72 hours — arriving on the same day as a separate Flash Sale email from a third DICK'S sub-account (scored 6/10 on June 13). The hero is structurally competent: dark retail-backdrop image, bold "EXTRA 30% OFF" headline, green Clearance badge, and a SHOP NOW button that sits above the fold. On its own, it's a usable promotional email.
- The problem is what comes below the hero. A Flash Sale module ("Flash Sale: Up to 50% Off") appears inside a Clearance email — which is brand-identity confusion borrowed from the same inbox that already received a dedicated Flash Sale email today. The remaining product tiles (shoes, denim shorts, Nike running shorts, baseball bats, golf clothing) are fine in isolation, but the email runs long, mixes a Father's Day cross-sell module, and ends with a "Must-have gear for auto summer" block that reads like a completely different campaign got stapled on.
- The offer is real and readable, but subscriber fatigue is at a peak. The cadence alone would suppress opens; the internal identity confusion suppresses clicks. This lands below the June 12 clearance send, which at least had a cleaner single-focus.

## What's working

- Hero offer is instantly legible — "EXTRA 30% OFF" in large white type on a dark background, backed by a green Clearance badge, clears the hierarchy bar immediately.
- Concrete percentage (30%) is stated in both the hero and the subject line — no ambiguity about the depth of the discount.
- SHOP NOW CTA is visible and unambiguous at the hero level.
- Category breadth (footwear, apparel, golf, baseball) means there's a logical match for multiple sports shoppers, even if it reads as a grab-bag.
- Father's Day module is timely and gives the email a seasonal hook beyond pure clearance.

## What's weak

- A Flash Sale module ("Flash Sale: Up to 50% Off") lives inside a Clearance email — confusing to a subscriber who already received a separate Flash Sale email from the same brand today.
- "Select" in the hero and subject hedges the offer; after enough clearance emails with this qualifier, it starts to feel like a bait-and-switch disclaimer.
- The email is extremely long — shoes, shorts, baseball bats, golf, Father's Day, and a summer gear block compete for attention with no unifying thread.
- The subject exceeds 50 characters ("Happening now — don't wait! Extra 30% off select CLEARANCE" is 58 chars) and will truncate on most mobile clients at the exact point that makes the offer ambiguous.
- No loyalty or ScoreCard member pricing is visible anywhere — a missed lever given this is a clearance send where member exclusives could drive urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the Flash Sale module.** A clearance email should live and die on clearance. Injecting a different sub-brand's Flash Sale module — on a day when a dedicated Flash Sale email already went out — makes both sends weaker. If there's an editorial hand-off between sub-accounts, it needs a rule: no cross-contaminating creative.
- 2. **Cut the email to three modules max.** Hero → one hero category tile → one supporting category tile → footer. The current length requires a reader to scroll through six distinct campaign blocks. Most of them will never be seen.
- 3. **Bring the offer deadline front and center.** "Happening now — don't wait!" in the subject is generic urgency. Add a concrete end time ("Ends tonight at midnight" or "3 days left") to the hero block — the email already has time-bounded language without time-bounded data.
- 4. **Surface ScoreCard member pricing if applicable.** Even a "Members get an extra X% on top" line in the hero would differentiate this send from the last three.
- 5. **Drop the "Must-have gear for auto summer" section.** It reads like a wholesale copy-paste from a different campaign and undercuts the clearance framing.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 30% off Clearance — ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your clearance picks: 30% off, today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop shoes, apparel & gear before these deals expire.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New markdowns just hit clearance — grab them before they're gone.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods Clearance Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary
The email is structurally sound and follows standard ESP (Epsilon/Harmony) patterns, but contains invalid JSON-LD that will silently fail Gmail schema rendering, stale/empty structured-data fields, and no visible UTM parameters on click-through links.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open-tracking pixel** (`id="ssl"` cell):
```html
<img src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019ec2af382dc5fd9cf4bbc782e8/e0b1898f...'
  style="display:none; max-height: 0px; font-size: 0px; overflow: hidden; mso-hide: all"/>
```
Missing `width="0" height="0"` HTML attributes. CSS-only hiding is unreliable in Outlook and some Apple Mail configurations — the pixel may render as a broken-image icon.

**Click-tracking redirect chain**: All CTAs route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610000019ec2...` with no UTM parameters visible in the raw HTML. If the ESP doesn't append UTMs on the redirect, GA4/analytics receives direct traffic instead of attributed email traffic. Verify ESP redirect config appends `utm_source=email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=...`.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Invalid JSON-LD** — JavaScript-style single-line comments (`//`) inside `<script type="application/ld+json">` are not valid JSON:
```json
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": ""
}
```
JSON does not support `//` comments. Gmail's schema parser will reject the entire block, disabling any Gmail Promotions tab annotations or offer cards. These appear to be copy-paste template comments that were never stripped.

**Stale schema date**: `"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"` is a 6-year-old hardcoded placeholder. Gmail may deprioritize or reject the offer card due to an expired start date.

**Duplicate `font-family` declaration** in `<style>`:
```css
body { font-family: Archivo, Arial, sans-serif; font-family: Archivo, Arial, sans-serif !important }
```
Redundant; the second declaration overrides the first anyway. No functional impact but indicates template hygiene issue.

**Archivo font**: WOFF2 sources reference `v7` of the font file on `fonts.gstatic.com`. This is an older version; Google Fonts now serves a newer cut. No rendering breakage, but the subset unicode-range may be outdated.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`__dU__` token in logo href**: The resolved value is a long encoded string — this appears correctly populated by the ESP for per-subscriber tracking. No unfilled token issue here.

**DiscountOffer schema fields are empty strings**:
```json
"description": "",
"discountCode": "",
"availabilityEnds": ""
```
If this promo carries a trackable code or end date, these fields were not populated before send. Empty `discountCode` means the Gmail offer card (if it renders at all) will show no code.

**No recipient name personalization** visible in the truncated header section. Not necessarily an issue for a broadcast clearance email, but worth noting if the template has an optional first-name merge tag that was not activated.

---

### 5. Compliance

**Unsubscribe / CAN-SPAM**: Not visible in the truncated source. A footer with physical postal address and one-click unsubscribe is required. Assumed present below the truncation point — must be verified against the full HTML.

**Sender domain**: `DSG@e.dcsg.com` — `e.dcsg.com` is the ESP sending subdomain. SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment cannot be assessed from HTML alone, but should be verified against received headers. The `From` display name "DICK'S | Clearance" is descriptive and not deceptive — no CAN-SPAM issue there.

**Schema comment disclosure**: The `// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!` comment in the live send JSON-LD suggests the template was not fully completed before deployment.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**No UTM parameters in raw href values.** All links pass through the ESP redirect (`/T/...`). If UTM injection happens at redirect time, this is acceptable — but it is not verifiable from the HTML source alone. Recommend spot-checking a resolved redirect URL to confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` are appended.

**Landing page alignment**: The email promotes "Extra 30% off select CLEARANCE." If the destination clearance page does not surface the same 30%-off filter/badge on arrival, there is a continuity gap (a content-review concern, but worth flagging as a QA check item).
