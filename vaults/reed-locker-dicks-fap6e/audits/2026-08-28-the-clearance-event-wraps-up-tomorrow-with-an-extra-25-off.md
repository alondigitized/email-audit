# The clearance event wraps up tomorrow with an extra 25% off
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-28**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S doing the exact same move it made yesterday: layer a "final day" urgency claim on top of an offer it has already run at this exact percentage. The lake/dock hero for "EXTRA 25% OFF" is the one visual reset from 8/16-8/27's endless green-locker recycling, and it's genuinely a nicer piece of art — but everything below the fold is the identical "UP TO 50% OFF" locker imagery, TaylorMade balls, and outdoor-gear collage this brand has been running on loop all month. The subject line ("wraps up tomorrow") is the most credible urgency claim in this whole brand history — it's specific and time-bound rather than the usual vague "ends soon" — which actually helps.
- The email is long and unfocused: after the hero, it sprawls into eight more offers (back-to-school, golf balls, football gear, outdoor essentials, cardio equipment, this week's deals, six product picks) before any social proof or coherent narrative. It reads like a catalog dump stapled behind one real headline offer. For a subscriber who's seen five near-identical "up to 50%" locker sends this month, there's nothing here that breaks the fatigue except the subject line's specific deadline.
- Verdict: functional, on-brand, and the deadline framing is a real improvement over recent sends, but the body is bloated and visually repetitive with prior emails — this stays in the same 4-5 band.

## What's working

- The hero swaps the tired green-locker background for a lake/dock scene — a genuine, if small, visual departure from the last two weeks of creative.
- "Extra 25% Off Select Clearance" with a bold red price-tag box gives clear, unambiguous visual hierarchy at the top.
- The subject line's "wraps up tomorrow" is a concrete, credible deadline rather than generic urgency language.
- Fine print ("Valid online only through 8/29/26 11:59PM PT") is visible and specific, not buried.
- Clear green "Shop Now" CTA button directly under the hero offer.

## What's weak

- The back-to-school "Up to 50% Off" locker banner and the "This Week's Deals" locker banner are the same green-locker creative DICK'S has run repeatedly since 8/16 — zero refresh there.
- Email is extremely long with 8+ separate offers stacked vertically (back-to-school, golf, football, outdoor, cardio, weekly deals, product picks) with no visual pacing or section breaks — it reads as a catalog dump, not a curated send.
- No reviews, ratings, or social proof anywhere in the render.
- Product picks grid at the bottom (backpack, shoes, socks, shorts) has no pricing shown on any item, undercutting a clearance-themed email.
- This is at least the seventh "up to X% off" or "extra X% off" clearance/flash-sale send this month with the same core offer structure — cadence fatigue is real.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the body from 9 sections to 3-4 — lead with the 25% off clearance hero, follow with one complementary category (e.g., back-to-school), and drop golf balls/cardio equipment/outdoor essentials into a "more deals" link instead of full banners. Length is the single biggest fixable problem here.
- 2. Retire the green-locker imagery for at least one cycle — it's appeared in nearly every send since 8/16 and readers who've opened more than one of these are seeing the same photo for the third or fourth time.
- 3. Add prices or "Now $X" callouts to the six product picks at the bottom — a clearance email with unpriced product tiles wastes the strongest conversion real estate in the message.
- 4. Keep leaning into concrete deadlines like "wraps up tomorrow" in the subject — it's more credible than "flash sale" and should be the template going forward.
