# $20 Off $100+ starts NOW in the app!📱
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S eighth email in roughly two days — and they're swinging a different bat. The $20 off $100+ app-exclusive offer is a genuine channel-shift play, not another clearance regurgitation. The hero is clean, the discount math is simple, and the green-on-dark art direction gives the email real presence in an inbox. That's the good news.
- The bad news is structural: the offer is app-gated, which immediately cuts out every subscriber who doesn't have the app installed or isn't willing to open it mid-shop. There's no end date anywhere in the visible email body, which deflates the "starts NOW" urgency because it implies it will still be valid whenever you get around to it. And after seven consecutive sends hammering clearance and deals, the cadence fatigue is real — even a refreshed creative concept gets devalued when the brand has been screaming in your inbox since July 7.
- This is a better-than-average send for DICK'S recent output — it breaks the pattern, it's specific, and it's visually sharp — but the app friction and missing expiry date leave points on the table.

## What's working

- **Hero is instantly legible.** "$20 OFF / YOUR ORDER OF $100+" reads in under two seconds. Dark background, large white numerals, clean layout.
- **Channel differentiation is explicit.** "App Exclusive" badge in the hero communicates scarcity/access without burying the qualifier in fine print.
- **Secondary deal ladder is well-merchandised.** 60% off clearance, 30% New Balance, 40% DSG family clothing, 40% bikes, 50% football gear — multiple entry points for different shopper types, each with its own SHOP NOW CTA.
- **Subject line is the strongest in the recent run.** Dollar amount + threshold + channel + urgency word ("NOW") in 37 characters is tight, specific, and scannable on mobile.
- **App download badges in the footer.** Correct call — anyone who doesn't have the app can grab it on the way to the redemption. One-stop conversion path.

## What's weak

- **No expiry date.** "Starts NOW" is undermined by the absence of an end date. Without a deadline, "starts NOW" just means "eventually."
- **App gate is real friction.** For a web-first email audience, requiring an app install/open to redeem is a meaningful conversion barrier. No fallback offer for non-app users is visible.
- **Body CTAs are all "SHOP NOW" generics.** Every section uses identical button copy — the New Balance shoes could have "Shop Shoes," the family clothing could have "Shop Clothing." Specificity lifts clicks.
- **Cadence burn is showing.** This is email #8 in ~48 hours. Even with a fresh creative angle, the cumulative weight of this inbox-hammering makes it easy for subscribers to ignore or unsubscribe.
- **No urgency signal in the body.** The subject says "NOW" but nothing in the email body reinforces when this ends. No countdown timer, no "offer ends Sunday," nothing.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline, visibly, in the hero.** "Offer valid through July 13" next to the $20 OFF badge. That single change converts "starts NOW" from flavor copy into actual urgency.
- 2. **Give app non-users an out.** A secondary CTA — "Shop clearance without the app →" — below the hero retains the audience who won't convert on the primary. Even a low-commitment path beats a dead end.
- 3. **Variabilize the SHOP NOW buttons.** One change from a developer: pass section context into the button template. "Shop Shoes," "Shop Bikes," "Shop Football" all outperform generic SHOP NOW on click-through.
- 4. **Pull back the send cadence.** This email would land harder as send #3, not send #8. Consider suppressing subscribers who've already opened/clicked in the last 48 hours.
- **Subject Alt A:** `App exclusive: $20 off your next $100 order — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Open the app, save $20 on your next order`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `No code needed — offer auto-applies in the app at checkout. Expires Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `60% off clearance + $20 off $100 — grab both before it's gone.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound (valid DOCTYPE, charset, viewport, Outlook conditional comments, `role="presentation"` on tables), but the Gmail Promotions structured data block contains invalid JSON that will prevent schema parsing entirely, and several template fields were shipped unfilled.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Encoded destination URLs** — all clicks route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...?__dU__=<base64>`. The destination URL is opaque in the source; UTM parameters, if any, live inside the encoded value and cannot be verified externally without decoding.

**Open/SSL pixel** — `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019f4ba08cc9c15006369fb45280/...` is present and correctly hidden (`display:none; max-height:0px; mso-hide:all`). No issues.

**Logo link** — passes through the same `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/` redirect chain. The destination image URL (`s7d6.scene7.com/is/image/DSGAEMSites/brand-logo--dks-2`) uses Scene7 dynamic imaging, which is fine.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- `lang="en"` on `<html>` — present. ✓  
- `role="presentation"` on all layout tables — present. ✓  
- `charset="UTF-8"` and `viewport` meta — present. ✓  
- Outlook conditional comment block correctly sets `border-collapse:collapse` and `a:link` color. ✓

- The tracking pixel `<img>` has no `alt=""` — acceptable for a hidden pixel but should be explicit: `alt=""`.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Critical — JSON-LD block is invalid JSON.** The `<script type="application/ld+json">` block contains JavaScript-style line comments (`// GIF & WEBP images...`, `// Sample image...`, `// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!`). JSON does not permit comments. This causes a parse error; Gmail will silently discard the entire structured data block, disabling any Promotions tab annotation.

**Stale template date:**
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```
Date is six years in the past — a copy-paste relic from a 2020 template. Should reflect this send's actual offer start.

**Empty required fields shipped to production:**
```json
"description": "",
"discountCode": "",
"availabilityEnds": "",
"image": ""
```
The subject line states "$20 Off $100+" but `discountCode` is blank. `PromotionCard.image` has a designer comment still in place, meaning no promotional image will render in Gmail's Promotions tab card.

**Non-standard Schema.org type:** `"@type": "DiscountOffer"` is not a recognized schema.org type (the standard is `Offer`). `PromotionCard` is a Gmail-specific extension, not schema.org. These are likely intentional Gmail annotations, but the comments bug overrides all of this.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All destination URLs are encoded inside the `__dU__` query parameter on the ESP redirect. UTM attribution cannot be confirmed without decoding those values. The pattern `?__dU__=<base64>` suggests UTMs are appended to the decoded destination, which is standard for this ESP, but it should be spot-checked by decoding one link to confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` are present and correctly set for the campaign.
