# Will you shop this week's deals to get ready for summer?
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-26**
## Executive summary

- After ten-plus Memorial Day sends anchored to "Epic Memorial Day Sale" creative, this email attempts a quiet reset with "This Week's Deals" framing and a passive question-format subject. The strategic pivot away from the exhausted Memorial Day drum is sensible. The body actually delivers: clean hero with "UP TO 50% OFF," followed by specific named-brand category tiles — adidas at 40%, CALIA at 50%, Walter Hagen BOGO, JOOLA at 20%. The render is tight and images load throughout.
- The entry point is where it collapses. "Will you shop this week's deals to get ready for summer?" is 57 characters of soft, rhetorical copy that signals nothing urgent to a subscriber already conditioned by a week of hard-sell Memorial Day emails. There's no offer, no product, no discount in the subject at all — just an invitation phrased as a question. Post-fatigue, this is the format most likely to get scroll-past treatment in the inbox.
- This is a competent browse-and-discover email trapped behind a passive door. The body earns a click. The subject earns a skip.

## What's working

- Hero "UP TO 50% OFF" over a bright summer outdoor image lands the offer immediately at the top of the scroll.
- Named-brand category tiles (adidas, CALIA, Walter Hagen, JOOLA) give subscribers real reasons to click in their lane rather than one generic CTA.
- Category nav row (MEN'S / WOMEN'S / KIDS / SHOES) appears early, enabling quick self-selection.
- ScoreCard loyalty branding is present in the header without consuming a dedicated content block.
- No visible render bugs — product imagery and layout hold cleanly across all tiles.

## What's weak

- Subject uses a question format with zero offer specificity — weakest possible hook after a week of aggressive sale sends.
- At 57 chars, the subject clips on most mobile clients and earns none of those characters with useful copy.
- "UP TO 50% OFF" hero has no floor — the qualifier without a minimum creates a soft promise that experienced shoppers discount mentally.
- Fire pits ("Up to 15% Off Select Water Store Fire Pits") dilutes the athletic identity; it reads as a clearance afterthought.
- No deadline or urgency mechanism visible — "This Week's Deals" implies finite, but no close date is shown anywhere in the render.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the question format and lead the subject with the offer** — the single highest-leverage change. After 7+ hard-sell sends, the subject needs to earn the open immediately.
- **Subject Alt A:** `This week: up to 50% off summer gear`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New week, new deals — 50% off adidas, CALIA & more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shoes, clothing, golf, pickleball — deals change weekly.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `CALIA 50% off. adidas 40% off. Walter Hagen BOGO. All in the app.`
- 2. **Add a visible deadline to the hero** — even one line ("Offers valid through June 1") under the 50% off lockup converts "This Week's Deals" from a vague label into actual urgency. Every SHOP NOW button benefits.
- 3. **Pull one marquee product into the hero** — instead of abstract "UP TO 50%," show the CALIA or adidas hero product at the discount. A specific item is more credible and more clickable than a range.
- 4. **Move fire pits to the bottom or cut** — if it must stay, it belongs after all athletic content. As a mid-email module it breaks the sporting-goods narrative.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Google Fonts via `fonts.gstatic.com`** — Archivo is loaded at font version `v7`. Google Fonts URLs are versioned and this may be an outdated snapshot; if Google deprecates `v7`, the font silently falls back to Arial. No functional breakage, but worth pinning to a versioned import that's confirmed active.

**`border-collapse:separate` on all tables** — All `<table>` elements use `border-collapse:separate; border-spacing:0`. This is functionally equivalent to `collapse` at `0` spacing but is an unusual pattern and can produce sub-pixel rendering differences in older Outlook versions.

**Logo alt text** — The logo `<img id="logo">` tag is truncated in the provided source. The pattern `src="https://dsg.images.harmony.epsilon.com/..."` ends mid-attribute — alt text cannot be confirmed present. This should be verified against the full source.

**`role="presentation"` on layout tables** — Present and correct throughout the visible markup.

**`lang="en"` on `<html>`** — Present. No issues.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Critical — Invalid JSON in `<script type="application/ld+json">`:**

The block contains JavaScript-style `//` comments, which are not valid JSON:
```json
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
  // Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
  "@context": "http://schema.org/",
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": ""
}
```
Strict JSON parsers will reject this. Gmail's promotional annotation pipeline uses this block; malformed JSON silently disables the promotional tab card feature.

**Critical — Stale hardcoded date:**
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```
This is March 6, 2020 — over six years in the past. This is an unswapped template variable or a copy-paste artifact. Gmail's promotional annotations will treat the offer as expired before delivery.

**Empty merge fields not cleared:**
```json
"description": "",
"discountCode": "",
"availabilityEnds": "",
"image": ""
```
Empty strings should either be populated or the keys removed. Empty `image` on `PromotionCard` means no promotional thumbnail is rendered in Gmail's Promotions tab.

**Non-standard `@type`:**
`"@type": "PromotionCard"` is not a Schema.org type. Gmail's annotation spec uses `DiscountOffer` and `SaleEvent`. `PromotionCard` may be a legacy or internal type that is no longer processed.

**Sending domain** — `From: DSG@e.dcsg.com`. The subdomain `e.dcsg.com` is a standard ESP envelope domain. SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment for `e.dcsg.com` against `dickssportinggoods.com` cannot be verified from HTML source alone — requires header inspection of a delivered message.

**No issues confirmed** from the available source, but footer and authentication headers are outside the truncated scope.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM Params, Landing Page Alignment)
