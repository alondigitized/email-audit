# HUNDREDS of new clearance styles just added! Take a look 👉
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-30**
## Executive summary

- This is the first genuinely different email from DICK'S in weeks — a dark red/maroon "HUNDREDS OF NEW CLEARANCE STYLES" hero replaces the green-locker "FLASH SALE" creative the brand has run on repeat since 8/16, and the structure below it is a real category browse (cleats, drinkware, golf clubs, fan shop, cardio equipment, picks) rather than another stacked-discount countdown email. It reads more like a catalog refresh than a promo blast, which is a welcome break in cadence.
- The tradeoff is that it's vague where the last several sends were at least numerically loud. "HUNDREDS OF NEW CLEARANCE STYLES" and "Fresh Deals You Don't Want to Miss" carry no percentage, no dollar figure, and no urgency in the hero — you have to scroll to individual modules ("20% Off HOKA," "Up to 30% Off Cleats," "Up to $200 Off Golf Clubs") to find any real numbers. For a subscriber who's seen six near-identical "up to 50% off" sends this month, that's a fair reset, but it also makes the hero itself forgettable and the subject line's "take a look 👉" close feels like filler.
- Net: a solid creative palette-cleanser that trades urgency and specificity for breadth. Fine as a once-in-a-while send, not a template to repeat.

## What's working

- Fresh hero creative — dark red/maroon abstract stadium-seating background breaks the green-locker pattern run since 8/16.
- Clean category pill navigation ("Men's," "Women's," "Kids'," "$25 and Under," "Fan Shop," "All Sale") gives the eye a clear next step.
- Multiple category modules each pair a product photo with a specific offer (20% Off HOKA, Up to 30% Off Cleats, Up to 40% Off Drinkware, Up to $200 Off Golf Clubs, Up to 50% Off Fan Shop Gear), so scrolling readers do find real numbers.
- No render bugs — images, buttons, and text all display cleanly with consistent spacing.
- "Check out these picks" product grid at the bottom (Nike backpack, adidas Samba, socks, New Balance 9060) adds a personalized-feeling browse layer.

## What's weak

- Hero has zero concrete offer — no %, no $, no BOGO — just "HUNDREDS OF NEW CLEARANCE STYLES," so the first screen gives no reason to act now.
- No urgency anywhere in the hero or subhead ("these styles won't last long" is vague, not time-bound).
- Subject line "Take a look 👉" is a weak, low-specificity CTA phrase — it under-promises relative to what's actually a fairly rich email.
- Very long scroll (7+ product modules) before reaching anything resembling a strongest offer — no clear single hero product/deal to anchor the click.
- "Fresh Deals" subhead is generic boilerplate that could appear in any DICK'S email, clearance or not.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real number in the hero. "Hundreds of new styles" is a count of inventory, not a deal — swap in the best headline offer (e.g., "Up to 50% Off" or "20-40% Off New Clearance") so the top of the email does the work the subject line implies.
- 2. Add urgency copy under the hero — "New markdowns added today" or a dated window — replacing the flat "won't last long."
- 3. Reorder modules so the best percentage-off deal (Fan Shop at 50%, or golf at $200 off) sits directly under the hero, not six scrolls down.
- 4. Tighten the subject to match the browse-friendly tone but add a number, since this email actually has several.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New clearance markdowns: up to 50% off, just added`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Hundreds of new clearance styles — today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh markdowns across cleats, golf, drinkware and fan gear`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New sizes and colors just dropped — shop before they're gone`
- 5. Give the "Check out these picks" section a one-line rationale (e.g., "Based on your recent shopping") if any personalization is actually driving it — otherwise it reads as generic filler at the very bottom.
