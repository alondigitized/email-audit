# Best deals of the week are HERE! See what's up to 50% off
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S seventh send in roughly two days, and the creative confirms the pattern: they've recycled the "Best Deals of the Week" digest frame from the July 7 doubles and downgraded the hero offer from 70% to 50%. At a certain point, frequency erases differentiation, and that point was probably July 7. A subscriber who opened both of Tuesday's "50% off deals of the week" emails already has this exact visual in their memory — the red "UP TO 50% OFF" badge, the lifestyle hero, the green SHOP NOW button.
- The email itself is structurally clean. The CALIA-heavy product grid and the HydroJug Checkers "App Launch" feature add something the last two sends didn't have — a named product with a reason to care. That's the one editorial bright spot. But it's buried below the fold, and the hero message is strictly a worse version of what went out 24 hours ago.
- Judgement: a competently assembled promo email suffering from calendar fatigue and offer regression. The cadence is the bigger problem than the creative.

## What's working

- **Clear visual hierarchy** — the red "UP TO 50% OFF" badge lands first, the headline reinforces it, SHOP NOW button follows immediately.
- **Category navigation strip** (Men's / Women's / Foot / Golf / Summer Shop / Clearance) surfaces options above the fold without clutter.
- **HydroJug Checkers "App Launch" feature** breaks the pure-discount pattern with a product story — the most distinct element in the email.
- **Product grid with visible prices** in the lower section gives CALIA fans something concrete to click into.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, images load, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **Offer regression is visible** — "up to 50% off" here versus "up to 70% off" in July 8's clearance email. Subscribers who noticed will feel like the bar moved down.
- **Hero lifestyle image is generic** — beach/bike family shot could be from any sporting goods brand; no product specificity at all above the fold.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is inert** — same copy used three times in the visible portion of the email; no verb-noun specificity.
- **CALIA dominates the product section** — skews hard female in a send addressed broadly; male subscribers see nothing for them until they scroll past four CALIA SKUs.
- **Seventh email in two days** — cadence alone will punish open rate regardless of creative quality; the inbox is saturated.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the HydroJug Checkers launch as the hero** — it's the only new news in this email. "New: HydroJug Checkers are here" above a product image beats a repeated 50%-off badge.
- 2. **Differentiate the CTA by section** — replace every "SHOP NOW" with the category: "Shop Clearance," "Shop CALIA," "Get HydroJug."
- 3. **Add at least one men's or unisex product** in the featured grid — the CALIA row alienates half the list without anything to pull them in.
- 4. **Raise or drop the discount claim** — if 70% clearance is real inventory, keep hammering it. If not, retire the comparison and frame this as "new arrivals this week" rather than a weaker repeat.
- 5. **Suppress to recent openers** — blast frequency this high should at minimum exclude subscribers who didn't open the last 3 sends.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New this week: HydroJug Checkers + up to 50% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `This week's best: HydroJug launch + summer deals`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Score new drops and clearance picks before they're gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `HydroJug Checkers just launched — plus deals on CALIA, Nike, and more.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**MSO conditional style block — stray quote character:**
```html
<!--[if (gte mso 9)|(IE)]><style>
  div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
```
The `"` before `}` on the `div` rule is invalid CSS. Outlook's CSS parser may discard the entire rule block.

**Duplicate `font-family` declaration on `body`:**
```css
body{font-family:Archivo,Arial,sans-serif;font-family:Archivo,Arial,sans-serif!important}
```
Redundant but harmless — second declaration wins. No functional issue.

**Logo `<img>` alt text** — the `<img id="logo">` tag is truncated in the provided source; cannot confirm `alt` attribute presence. If absent, screen readers and image-blocked clients will render a blank.

### 5. Compliance

**JSON-LD contains JavaScript-style comments — invalid JSON:**
```javascript
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported...
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": ""
}
```
`//` comments are not valid JSON. The entire `<script type="application/ld+json">` block will fail to parse in any compliant JSON parser (Gmail promotions tab, Google Search, etc.). This silently kills all structured data annotations.

**`@type: "DiscountOffer"` and `@type: "PromotionCard"` are not valid Schema.org types.** `DiscountOffer` should be `Offer`; `PromotionCard` has no Schema.org equivalent — this is either a proprietary Gmail extension type or a copy/paste error. Neither will validate.

**CAN-SPAM / unsubscribe** — HTML is truncated; cannot confirm physical mailing address and one-click unsubscribe are present in footer. Assumed present given sender reputation, but not verifiable from provided source.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Click URLs route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com` redirects. UTM attribution cannot be confirmed from the provided source — the final destination URL is behind the redirect. If UTMs are appended by the ESP at redirect time rather than baked into the HTML, there is no issue; if they are expected to be in the HTML `href` values, they are absent from what is visible.
