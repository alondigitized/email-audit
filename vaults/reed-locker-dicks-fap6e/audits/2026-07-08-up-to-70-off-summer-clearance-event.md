# Up to 70% OFF 🤝 Summer Clearance Event
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- This is a return to the "up to 70% off" clearance frame that DICK'S ran relentlessly through July 5–6 — the same headline the brand apparently retired when it pivoted to the "This Week's Deals" weekly digest on July 7. Two days into that reset, the clearance ghost is back. The brand history makes this a notable regression: subscribers who opened the July 7 reset emails now get the old message recycled with a "Summer Clearance Event" badge slapped on top.
- The layout is clean and the multi-category grid (clothing, golf, outdoor, fan shop, fitness) gives the email genuine browsing value. The YETI Pace Purple hero product is a smart pull — a recognizable premium brand signals real deals exist, not just DSG private-label markdown. The visual hierarchy works; 70% OFF dominates the fold exactly as intended.
- The single most damaging element: the hero screams 70% off, but every section in the body caps at 40% off. That gap is visible without scrolling past the first category block. It doesn't read as "up to" nuance — it reads as the classic bait-and-switch, and it undercuts the trust the YETI callout just built.

## What's working

- **YETI hero anchor** — featuring a named premium brand (YETI Pace Purple) above the grid immediately signals "real" deals, not just DSG-label closeouts.
- **Dark hero with massive discount typography** — 70% OFF in heavy white text on black reads instantly; no ambiguity about the lead offer.
- **Multi-category grid layout** — six distinct categories with consistent SHOP NOW buttons let any sporting-goods buyer find their lane without scrolling forever.
- **Subject line brevity** — at ~38 characters the subject fits cleanly on mobile without truncation.
- **"Summer Clearance Event" framing** — gives the send a named campaign identity, which is an upgrade over the bare "select clearance styles" language used in earlier sends this week.

## What's weak

- **70% hero vs. 40% body — visible promise gap** — every category section says "Up to 40% Off"; the hero promised 70%. A subscriber can see both in the same scroll, and it reads as misleading.
- **Cadence regression** — July 7 already sent two "This Week's Deals" resets; this revives the clearance frame they were replacing. Subscribers who noticed the format shift will recognize the relapse.
- **Generic CTA copy throughout** — every button says "SHOP NOW." After six sections that's repetitive and does no selling work. A single specific button ("Shop YETI Deals") would lift the hero section meaningfully.
- **No deadline or urgency signal** — unlike the July 6 "FINAL HOURS" send (which used a live countdown timer), this email offers no time boundary. The clearance message without urgency loses its most natural conversion driver.
- **No loyalty or membership angle visible** — DICK'S ScoreCard is a real differentiator; no points or member-exclusive callout appears in the render.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the 70% vs. 40% gap or lead with the honest number.** If the best available deal is 40% off, say 40% in the hero. The credibility loss from the gap costs more than the impression loss from a smaller number.
- 2. **Add a countdown or explicit end date.** The July 6 countdown timer provably raised urgency; reintroduce it. Even "Ends Sunday" in the hero subhead would do it.
- 3. **Differentiate this from the July 7 "Deals" sends** — either retire the clearance frame entirely and fold this into the weekly digest, or make the clearance event feel genuinely distinct (named landing page, exclusive SKUs, countdown).
- 4. **Make one CTA specific** — swap the YETI section's "SHOP NOW" for "Shop YETI Clearance" to drive a higher-intent click from the hero product.
- 5. **Surface a ScoreCard / points hook** — even a single line ("ScoreCard members earn 2x points on clearance") converts loyalty members at higher rates and is invisible in this send.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Clearance ends soon — YETI, outdoor & fan shop deals inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop YETI, golf gear, fitness equipment and outdoor essentials at clearance prices.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Real brands, real markdowns — YETI, Callaway, and more while supplies last.`

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods "Summer Clearance Event"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard ESP redirect-tracking architecture (via `app.dickssportinggoods.com`) and is structurally sound for Outlook/mobile rendering, but contains **invalid JSON-LD with stale/empty structured data fields** and a **malformed MSO conditional CSS rule** that could cause Outlook rendering defects.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**No broken links detected** in the visible source. All CTAs and the logo route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...` redirect URLs, which is standard for ESP click-tracking.

**Tracking pixel observation:** The open-pixel at `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019f435e974bc871ae6e96c660f0/21048613a47c40ee00004c5a42963aa1` is missing explicit `width="1" height="1"` HTML attributes. It relies solely on inline CSS (`max-height:0; display:none`) for suppression. Some Outlook versions ignore CSS on `<img>` and may render a broken-image placeholder. Add `width="1" height="1"` as HTML attributes.

**Single-quote attribute syntax on tracking pixel:**
```html
<img src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/...'
```
HTML attributes should use double quotes for maximum parser compatibility across ESPs and legacy clients.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed MSO conditional CSS** — the `<![if (gte mso 9)|(IE)]>` style block contains a stray `"` character inside the `div` rule:

```css
div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
```

The `"` before `}` is invalid CSS. Outlook's rendering engine may reject or partially apply this rule, causing zero-height `div` elements to render incorrectly in Outlook 2016/2019/365.

**Logo `alt` attribute** — the `<img id="logo">` tag is truncated in the provided source; confirm it carries a non-empty `alt` (e.g., `alt="DICK'S Sporting Goods"`). Images-off rendering in corporate clients (common with Outlook) will show a blank header if `alt` is missing.

**Tracking pixel `alt` attribute** — the open-pixel `<img>` has no visible `alt=""`. Screen readers may announce it as an unlabeled image. Should be `alt=""` explicitly.

**`role="presentation"` usage** — correctly applied to layout tables. No issues.

**`lang="en"` on `<html>`** — present and correct.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Critical: stale hardcoded date in JSON-LD:**
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```
This is a 6-year-old date. Gmail uses this structured data to render promotion cards in the Promotions tab. A past `availabilityStarts` with an empty `availabilityEnds` may suppress the promotion card display entirely or render it incorrectly.

**Empty merge fields left unpopulated in JSON-LD:**
```json
"description": "",
"discountCode": "",
"availabilityEnds": "",
"image": ""   // PromotionCard
```
The inline designer comment `// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!` confirms the `PromotionCard.image` field was never filled. An empty `image` disqualifies the email from Gmail Promotions tab visual annotation.

---

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM / Unsubscribe / Authentication)

**Unsubscribe / CAN-SPAM footer** — not visible in the truncated source. Cannot confirm presence of physical mailing address or unsubscribe link. These are legal requirements under CAN-SPAM; confirm they appear in the full HTML below the truncation point.

**Authentication headers** — not inspectable from HTML source alone. Sending domain `e.dcsg.com` is an ESP subdomain; verify SPF, DKIM (`d=dcsg.com` or `d=e.dcsg.com`), and DMARC alignment are passing via a header trace or mail-tester.com result. No issues inferable from HTML.

**From address** — `DSG@e.dcsg.com` uses an ESP subdomain, which is standard. No issues.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM / Landing Page Alignment)

UTM parameters are not visible in the raw redirect URLs (`app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...`). These may be appended by the ESP at redirect resolution time, which is acceptable — but **confirm via a live click-through** that destination URLs carry `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` before the campaign sends. If UTMs are injected server-side at redirect, a misconfiguration will silently drop attribution for the entire campaign.

The `__dU__` query parameter on the logo link is an internal ESP user-identifier token; this is expected behavior.
