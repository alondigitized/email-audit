# This week's deals are waiting ➡️ see what's up to 50% off
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- DICK'S has downshifted from the high-urgency flash sale drum it's been beating since July 12 to a softer "This Week's Deals" weekly browse frame — and that gear-change costs it. The email opens with a sky-blue 50%-off hero, then pivots mid-scroll to the 70% Summer Clearance event that was already the headline of this morning's dedicated clearance send. Below that: a featured On Cloud X 4 shoe section, DSG family clothing, a dense product grid, and a summer-category browser. Five distinct content areas compete for attention inside a single send, and none of them fully wins.
- The individual pieces hold up: the large percentage numbers are eye-catching, the On Cloud X 4 feature gives subscribers a specific product to latch onto, and the DICK'S brand carries enough trust to justify a browse. But the subject undersells the email's own content — "up to 50% off" buries the 70% clearance that's actually the stronger hook inside. And after four days of flash-sale conditioning, "This Week's Deals" lands as a demotion in urgency rather than a meaningful pivot.
- The deeper problem is same-day cannibalization: the 70% Summer Clearance creative block inside this email is functionally identical to this morning's dedicated clearance send. Subscribers who open both see the same section twice in one day. That erodes the clearance urgency and signals to engaged subscribers that DICK'S is running out of creative options.

## What's working

- Hero 50%-off block is number-forward, large, and clean — the offer reads in under a second.
- On Cloud X 4 gets a dedicated feature placement with its own section header and image — a specific product anchor in an otherwise category-level email.
- Navigation bar (MEN'S, WOMEN'S, KIDS, SHOES, GOLF, THE SUMMER SHOP) is functional and adds browse value above the fold.
- The 70%-off Summer Clearance secondary section uses a bold red background that creates a strong visual step-down — it signals "harder deal below."
- No render bugs visible — layout is clean, no broken images or overlapping elements.

## What's weak

- Subject says "up to 50% off" but the email features 70% off clearance prominently — the subject undersells its own best offer.
- The 70% Summer Clearance block is a same-day creative repeat of this morning's dedicated clearance email — double-exposure in under 24 hours.
- Five distinct content areas (50% hero, 70% clearance, On Cloud X 4, DSG clothing, product grid) dilute focus; no single offer owns the send.
- "SHOP NOW" CTAs are generic and undifferentiated across every section, missing the chance to move subscribers from browse to intent.
- "This Week's Deals" framing is the softest, lowest-urgency creative DICK'S has used all week — a step backward after priming subscribers on flash-sale language.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the subject and hero with the 70%, not the 50%** — the stronger number is buried inside; surfacing it in the subject line alone would lift open-to-click.
- 2. **Collapse to two sections max** — clearance + one featured product (On Cloud X 4). Cut the DSG clothing section and the generic product grid into a footer browse link instead.
- 3. **Retire the 70% clearance block for at least one send** — it headlined this morning; running it again as a secondary same-day block turns "urgency" into wallpaper.
- 4. **Swap "SHOP NOW" for offer-specific CTA copy** — "Shop Clearance," "Get the On Cloud X 4," "Shop DSG Picks" — different CTAs for different sections signal different intent and lift click segmentation.
- 5. **Add a soft scarcity signal to the clearance section** — "while styles last" or a low-stock callout gives the 70% block the credibility it's missing now that it's been live for a day.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Clearance just jumped to 70% — this week's best deals inside`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 70% off + the On Cloud X 4 is here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Summer Clearance at its deepest, plus On Cloud shoes and this week's top picks.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `70% off clearance, DSG family picks, and more — all in one place.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS syntax error in MSO conditional block** — the `div` rule ends with a double-quote instead of a closing brace:
```css
div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
                                                                                  ^
```
This is `"` instead of `}`. Outlook's rendering engine will silently ignore the malformed rule, but it can cause unpredictable layout in MSO clients.

**Duplicate `font-family` declaration** in the base `body` rule:
```css
body{font-family:Archivo,Arial,sans-serif;font-family:Archivo,Arial,sans-serif!important}
```
Harmless but redundant — one declaration is sufficient.

**Logo `<img>` alt text not verifiable** from the truncated source. Must confirm `alt="DICK'S Sporting Goods"` or equivalent is present on the `#logo` element. All CTA images should also carry descriptive alt text for clients that block images.

**Mobile responsive structure looks correct** — `@media screen and (max-width:480px)` breakpoint is present, utility classes are defined, MSO conditionals use table-based layout. No structural issues found in the visible markup.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM / unsubscribe** cannot be fully verified from the truncated source — the footer (physical address, unsubscribe link) is cut off. These must be confirmed present in the full HTML before send.

**JSON-LD contains JavaScript-style comments**, which are invalid JSON syntax:
```json
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported...
  // Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": ""
}
```
`//` comments are not legal in JSON. Gmail's schema.org parser will reject this entire `<script type="application/ld+json">` block, meaning the DiscountOffer and PromotionCard rich annotations will not render. Additionally, `"image": ""` is an unfilled designer placeholder — the PromotionCard schema will be a no-op even if the JSON were valid.

**Sender domain:** `e.dcsg.com` is a dedicated sending subdomain. SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment for this subdomain against the parent `dcsg.com` / `dickssportinggoods.com` cannot be verified from HTML source — confirm via `dig TXT e.dcsg.com` and header inspection on a received copy.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All tracked links route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...` before landing on dickssportinggoods.com. UTM parameter presence on the final destination URL cannot be confirmed from source alone. If the ESP is not appending UTM params at redirect resolution, analytics attribution will be lost entirely.

The DiscountOffer `availabilityStarts` date (2020) has no continuity with any landing page offer date — this is a schema-only issue but confirms the block is stale boilerplate, not live data.
