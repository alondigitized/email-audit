# 💰 Father's Day Savings Event = Up to 50% OFF 💰
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S seasonal pivot — the same multi-module compilation format they've been running all week, now dressed in a Father's Day wrapper. The hero lands cleanly: bold event header, two golfers in the background, "Up to 50% Off" front and center. The timing is sharp — June 11 with Father's Day four days out — and the "Get It for Father's Day" shipping-deadline block at the bottom does real work.
- The format is familiar to the point of being predictable. The June 8 send scored 8/10 with an identical structure and the same 50% ceiling. Regulars will recognize this as Template A with seasonal copy swapped in. That's not a failure — it's a known-good format — but it's the third time this week the inbox has seen this skeleton, and the double money-bag emoji bookends in the subject push the send closer to promotional noise than it needs to be.
- Golf clubs at 60% off is the buried lead. That's the single most Father's Day-relevant, high-intent module in this send and it's sitting below the fold in the middle of the email. Lead with that and this email punches harder.

## What's working

- **Father's Day hero is immediate and legible** — golfer imagery + event title + "Up to 50% Off" communicates the whole pitch in one glance.
- **Golf clubs at 60% off is perfectly Dad-coded** — most relevant module for the occasion; shows an actual product image (driver head).
- **"Get It for Father's Day" shipping block creates real, date-anchored urgency** — deadline framing is the strongest closing hook in the email.
- **Visual hierarchy holds through the first three modules** — the eye flows naturally from hero → Summer Essentials → Explore More without layout breakage.
- **App module breaks the scroll rhythm productively** — dark background creates a distinct visual pause before the golf section.

## What's weak

- **Golf is buried below four other modules** — the highest-intent Father's Day category is not the hero; shoes and women's USA gear lead instead.
- **Double money-bag emoji in the subject** (💰...💰) reads as slightly desperate and nudges this into promotions-tab territory.
- **"Up to 50% off" applied to four different category headers dilutes the claim** — by the third module the ceiling feels like marketing noise rather than a real offer.
- **No personalization anywhere** — same compilation goes to every subscriber regardless of their activity in golf, fitness, or outdoor gear.
- **Email cadence fatigue** — this is at minimum the fifth DICK'S email in three days; the June 8 send had the same format, same percentage, and scored 8/10 on a cleaner execution.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Promote the golf section to the hero module** — "Up to 60% Off Golf Clubs for Father's Day" is the single most focused, occasion-specific offer in this send. Lead with it; move Summer Essentials down.
- 2. **Add a hard order deadline in the hero** — "Order by June 13 for on-time delivery" tied to the Father's Day urgency converts browsers at the top of the email, not just at the bottom.
- 3. **Drop one money-bag emoji from the subject** — one is personality; two is spam-signal.
- 4. **Collapse from 6+ modules to 3–4** — pick Golf, Summer Essentials, and one apparel story. Fewer categories = a clearer reason to click.
- 5. **Add a shipping-threshold callout above the fold** — "Free 2-day shipping on $49+" visible in the hero line anchors the value proposition without a separate module.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Golf clubs 60% off + more Father's Day deals inside`
- **Subject Alt B:** `4 days left: Up to 60% off Father's Day gifts`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Order by June 13 for guaranteed Father's Day delivery.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Golf clubs, shoes, apparel — free shipping on $49+.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Table-based email built on Epsilon/Harmony infrastructure with MSO conditional comments and Google Fonts. Two confirmed bugs: invalid JSON-LD (breaks Gmail promotional annotations) and a malformed MSO CSS rule.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**No blocking issues.** All CTA links route through the Epsilon redirect domain `app.dickssportinggoods.com` with `__dU__` subscriber tokens — standard for this ESP. Open-tracking pixel confirmed at:
```
https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019eb6491ebeb29be26e96c566a0/8ead6cc3f2404a7a00004c5a42963aa1
```
Pixel is correctly hidden (`display:none; max-height:0px; mso-hide:all`).

UTM parameters are applied at the redirect layer and are not visible in raw source — this is expected for Epsilon. Final destination UTMs cannot be verified without following redirects.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**One confirmed bug:**

The MSO conditional `<style>` block contains a stray `"` before the closing brace of the `div` rule:

```css
/* as written */
div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
```

The `"` before `}` is invalid CSS. In Outlook's MSO parser this may terminate the style attribute string early, causing the entire conditional block to be ignored. Affected clients: Outlook 2007–2021, Outlook on Windows.

**No issues found:**
- `<html lang="en">` is set
- `role="presentation"` on all layout tables
- `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` override present
- Mobile breakpoints defined at `max-width: 480px`

**Cannot confirm:** `alt` attributes on images — logo `<img>` tag is truncated in the provided source snippet.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

Table carries `id="header-non-sc-member"` — this is the non-ScoreCard-member variant, consistent with intentional segmentation. No unresolved `{{token}}` or `%%token%%` merge tags visible in the provided source. No issues found in the visible portion.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

- CAN-SPAM footer (physical address + unsubscribe link) is not present in the truncated source. Presence cannot be confirmed or denied — must verify in full HTML.
- Sending domain is `e.dcsg.com` (dedicated ESP subdomain). SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment cannot be assessed from HTML source alone.
- Internal comment `<!--DKS Email Marketing 2025-->` in the `<html>` tag exposes a stale year marker. Minor, but leaks internal tooling metadata.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

UTM tagging is handled at the Epsilon redirect layer — cannot inspect final parameters without following the redirect chain. If campaign/source/medium values are not configured in the Epsilon send job, clicks will land untagged. This should be verified in the ESP platform, not the HTML.

The `__dU__` token in redirect URLs confirms subscriber-level click attribution is active.

---

### 7. Recommendations

**P0 — Fix before send (or confirm already sent):**

1. **Invalid JSON-LD — will suppress Gmail promotional annotations.** The `<script type="application/ld+json">` block contains JavaScript-style comments, which are not valid JSON:
   ```json
   // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
   // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
   ```
   `JSON.parse()` will throw on these lines. Gmail's schema processor will silently discard the entire block, preventing deal badges or price chips from appearing in the Promotions tab. Remove all `//` comments from the JSON-LD.

2. **Stale/empty `DiscountOffer` fields in JSON-LD:**
   - `availabilityStarts: "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"` — a 6-year-old placeholder date
   - `availabilityEnds: ""` — empty string is schema-invalid
   - `description: ""` and `discountCode: ""` — both empty
   - `PromotionCard.image: ""` — designer placeholder never filled

   Even if the comment issue is fixed, these empty/stale values mean Gmail cannot render a valid promotional annotation.

3. **MSO `div` CSS rule — fix the trailing `"` before `}` to ensure Outlook conditional styles apply correctly.**

**P1 — Verify externally:**
- Confirm CAN-SPAM footer (physical address + one-click unsubscribe) exists in the full HTML below the truncation point.
- Confirm UTM parameters are populated in the Epsilon send job configuration.
- Verify `alt` text is present on logo and hero images.
