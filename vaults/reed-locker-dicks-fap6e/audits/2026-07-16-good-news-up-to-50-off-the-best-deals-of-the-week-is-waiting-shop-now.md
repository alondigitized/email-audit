# Good news: up to 50% off the best deals of the week is waiting — shop now
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S fourth consecutive "deals of the week / up to 50% off" send in three days — and it shows. The creative is structurally identical to the July 14 (7/10) and July 15 (5/10) sends: a hero badge, a "Best Deals of the Week" headline, a category navigation row, a wall of "Up to 40% off" department sections, and the now-ubiquitous dark 70%-off Summer Clearance module at the bottom. DICK'S did briefly escape this rut yesterday with the editorial "Want it? DICK'S got it" send — which scored a 10 precisely because it broke the pattern — and immediately retreated back to the discount drum.
- The most damaging visual problem is the offer discrepancy: the hero banner screams **50% OFF**, but every individual section below it caps at **up to 40% off**. That is visible bait-and-switch, and a subscriber who has been burned by that gap before will notice it immediately. The subject line compounds the problem — "the best deals of the week is waiting" is grammatically broken, 74 characters long, and feels auto-generated rather than written.
- This is a mechanically acceptable promotional email that will drive some clicks on the strength of the brand. But the cadence is burning list trust fast.

## What's working

- The **50% OFF hero badge** is large and unambiguous — it lands immediately above the fold on mobile.
- **SHOP NOW buttons** are present in every section; there is no ambiguity about how to act.
- The **"Explore more deals" category nav** (Men's / Women's / Boys / Girls / Golf / etc.) gives a fast visual path to the right department without scrolling.
- The **Summer Clearance 70% OFF** module at the bottom adds a second, harder price anchor for deal-seekers who scroll.
- Email renders **cleanly** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum visible.

## What's weak

- **50% hero vs. 40% sections**: Every department module says "Up to 40% off" — the hero overpromises by 10 percentage points. Subscribers will notice.
- **Subject line is broken English**: "…the best deals of the week is waiting" — subject-verb mismatch, 74 characters, well past mobile truncation at ~50.
- **Fourth near-identical send in three days**: The July 15 replay already dragged the score to 5/10; a third appearance of the same frame further erodes open rates.
- **No urgency anchor**: Unlike the Flash Sale series ("ends TONIGHT"), nothing in the email creates a credible deadline — "shop now" without a date is not urgency.
- **The 70% clearance module has appeared in every recent send**: it is no longer a hook — it is furniture.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the hero/section offer mismatch** — if the actual max discount is 40%, the hero should say 40%. If 50% is legitimately achievable, surface one specific item at 50% in the hero. The gap erodes trust faster than any copy fix can restore it.
- 2. **Add a deadline** — "Ends Sunday" or "Through July 18" in the hero converts "deals email" into "act-now email."
- 3. **Retire the 70% clearance module for at least 48 hours** — it has appeared in every send since July 14. Frequency kills urgency. Save it for a standalone clearance push.
- 4. **Write the subject as a human sentence** — cut it to under 50 characters, fix the grammar, lead with the number.
- 5. **Collapse the category wall** — five "Up to 40% off [Category]" sections with identical structure look like a feed dump, not curation. Pick two hero categories and link the rest through the nav.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 40% off the week's best deals — ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `This week: 40–70% off shoes, apparel & more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Men's, women's, kids, golf & outdoor all on sale. Shop before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Summer Clearance hits 70% off — plus 40% off this week's picks.`

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard responsive table-based email with MSO/Outlook conditionals and a Salesforce Marketing Cloud (or similar) click-tracking layer. Two high-severity issues: invalid JSON in the `<script type="application/ld+json">` block and a stale 2020 date placeholder in the schema data.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **All click links route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...`** — click-tracking redirects are present. Raw destination URLs are not inspectable in the provided source.
- **Open-tracking pixel** at `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019f6c8fc3ae8a7d266e96c660c0/...` is hidden via CSS (`display:none; max-height:0px`) only — no explicit `width="1" height="1"` attribute-level constraints. Some clients may suppress CSS before firing the request; a 1×1 dimension attribute is the safer pattern.
- **UTM parameters** are not visible in the provided fragment (links are wrapped in the redirect layer). Cannot confirm presence or consistency. Should be verified against the resolved destination URLs.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Outlook MSO CSS syntax error** — the `div` rule inside the MSO conditional block contains a stray `"` before the closing brace:

```css
div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
```

The `"` after `padding:0;` is invalid CSS. Outlook's rendering engine may fail to parse this rule entirely, potentially collapsing `div` spacing in Outlook desktop clients.

- **Logo `<img>` tag is truncated** in the provided source — cannot confirm `alt` text is present. This should be verified.
- **`lang="en"`** on `<html>`, `role="presentation"` on layout tables, `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` override — all correct.
- **Archivo font URLs reference `v7`** (`fonts.gstatic.com/s/archivo/v7/...`). The path still resolves, but it is an older version slug. Low severity.

### 7. Recommendations

**P0 — Fix before sending:**

1. **Invalid JSON-LD block.** The `<script type="application/ld+json">` contains JavaScript-style `//` comments, which are not valid JSON. This causes a hard parse error — all Gmail Promotions Tab annotations are silently suppressed. Remove all `//` comment lines.

   Affected lines:
   ```js
   // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
   // Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
   // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
   ```

2. **Stale 2020 template date.** `"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"` is an unfilled placeholder from a 2020 template. This will cause schema validation failures. Update to the actual campaign start date or remove the field.

3. **Empty schema fields are unfilled template placeholders.** `"description": ""`, `"discountCode": ""`, `"availabilityEnds": ""`, and `"image": ""` are all empty strings. Either populate or remove them — empty strings in required-ish fields may trigger validation warnings.

**P1 — Fix before next campaign:**

4. **Outlook CSS syntax error.** Remove the stray `"` in the MSO conditional `div` rule: `padding:0;"}` → `padding:0;}`

5. **Non-standard Schema.org types.** `"@type": "DiscountOffer"` and `"@type": "PromotionCard"` are not recognized Schema.org vocabulary entries. For Gmail Promotions Tab annotations, consult the Gmail Promotions tab schema documentation for the correct type identifiers.

6. **Verify `alt` text on logo and all product images** — confirm in the full HTML.

7. **Resolve click-tracking redirect chains** and verify UTM parameters are consistently applied to all destination URLs.
