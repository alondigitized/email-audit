# Ends. TONIGHT! ⚡ Don't miss up to 50% OFF during our Flash Sale...
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S ninth-ish promotional blast since July 18 — and it shows. The hero lands "FLASH SALE / Up to 50% Off" cleanly with an orange-on-dark treatment that reads fast, and the "Ends TONIGHT" deadline is the first genuine one-night hard stop in this entire run. That urgency hook is real and it's the single strongest thing this email has going for it.
- But underneath the hero, the email collapses into the same long scroll of stacked sections that every prior send has used: clearance, Nike, soccer balls, golf balls, cycling jersey, a "must-have summer gear" grid at the bottom. The July 22 Flash Sale email (9/10) already used this creative frame and this offer level. Sending it again 24 hours later with only a deadline swap doesn't create a new reason to act — it trains the subscriber that every "last chance" has a sequel.
- The cumulative effect is subscriber numbness. At this point the "50% off" ceiling is wallpaper, and the only live variable is whether the recipient trusts that TONIGHT actually means tonight.

## What's working

- **Hero is instant and legible.** "FLASH SALE / Up to 50% Off" in bold white on dark background reads in under a second — no ambiguity about the offer.
- **"Ends TONIGHT" is the first credible hard deadline in this run.** After multiple "2 days," "this week," and "don't miss" hedges, a tonight cutoff has real urgency teeth.
- **Specific sub-offers break up the umbrella claim.** The Adidas soccer balls section and the Titleist "Buy 3 Get 1 Free" golf balls section give category-specific shoppers something concrete to latch onto beyond the generic 50% banner.
- **The 30% Off clearance section adds a secondary offer tier** — gives fence-sitters who missed the top-line deal a different angle to engage.

## What's weak

- **The Flash Sale creative is a literal replay of July 22.** Same concept, same offer ceiling, same layout structure — this is a deadline bump, not a new email.
- **Too many sections dilute the urgency.** A "tonight" deadline should drive single-minded action; this email still has cycling jerseys, a summer gear grid, soccer balls, and a golf promo all competing for attention.
- **"Shop Now" CTAs are generic throughout.** None of the section CTAs name the product category being linked — every button says the same thing.
- **Cadence fatigue is visible in the design choices.** The same orange clearance banner and the same category tiles have appeared in at least four of the last seven sends — no creative variation signals to the subscriber that this email is different from the last one.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Strip the email to the single urgency message.** A "tonight only" email should have one hero, one deadline countdown or prominent clock, and two or three focused category CTAs — not eight modules. Cut the cycling jersey, the summer gear grid, and the soccer balls section. Let the deadline breathe.
- 2. **Make the "tonight" deadline literal and specific.** Add a time: "Ends at midnight ET" or "Ends at 11:59 PM tonight." Right now "TONIGHT" is vague enough that a subscriber who sees it at 9 PM can't tell if they have 3 hours or 15.
- 3. **Differentiate the clearance tier visually.** The Extra 50% Off clearance section and the main 50% Off Flash Sale section read as the same offer at a glance. If these are genuinely different pools of product, make the visual treatment distinct.
- 4. **Vary the CTA copy per section.** "Shop Men's Clearance" / "Shop Soccer" / "Shop Golf" over the relevant modules converts better than six identical "Shop Now" buttons.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Tonight only: up to 50% off ends at midnight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last hours — Flash Sale closes tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Extra 50% off clearance included. Final hours — shop before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Clearance, soccer, golf and more — all at Flash Sale prices until midnight.`

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods Flash Sale Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Email uses Oracle Responsys for link tracking and open pixel tracking; standard MSO conditional comments are in place for Outlook. The most significant issues are **invalid JSON-LD** (JavaScript comments inside `<script type="application/ld+json">`) and **stale/empty schema placeholders** that will silently break Gmail Promotions tab annotations.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open pixel** — `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019f8e7510cec07a4e369ff04b84/b1999284b1b047a400004c5a42963aa1` — correctly suppressed via `display:none; max-height:0px; mso-hide:all`. No issue.

**Click tracking** — All CTA links route through `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610000019f8e7510cec07a4e369ff04b84/...` with a `__dU__` subscriber token appended. This is the expected Responsys redirect chain.

**UTM parameters** — Cannot confirm from the truncated source whether UTM params are passed to the final destination URLs. This should be verified on the decoded redirect target (see §6).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Web font fallback** — `Archivo` is loaded inside `@media screen { @font-face {...} }`, which is correct — clients that don't support web fonts fall back to `Arial, sans-serif`. No issue.

**Outlook MSO conditionals** — Present and structurally correct (`border-collapse`, `font-size:0` resets). No issue.

**`<link rel="icon">` in `<head>`** — `<link rel="icon" type="image/x-icon" href="https://www.dickssportinggoods.com/home/assets/icons/dsg/favicon.ico"/>` is dead markup in email; all email clients strip or ignore it. Low severity but adds unnecessary bytes.

**Logo image alt text** — HTML is truncated at `<img id="logo" src="https://s7d6.scene7.com/is/image/DSGAEMSites/brand-logo--dks-2?wid=248&hei=…` — `alt` attribute is not visible in the provided excerpt. Must confirm `alt="DICK'S Sporting Goods"` or equivalent is present; missing alt on the logo is a WCAG 2.1 Level A failure and breaks image-off rendering.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Stale date in DiscountOffer schema** — `"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"` is a 6-year-old hardcoded placeholder. This field should be dynamically populated per send.

**Empty merge fields left unpopulated:**
```json
"description": "",
"discountCode": "",
"availabilityEnds": "",
"image": ""
```
These are template blanks that were not filled before deployment. Empty `discountCode` and `availabilityEnds` cause Gmail's Promotions tab deal annotation to render incomplete or not at all.

No subscriber-level merge tokens (first name, loyalty tier, etc.) are visible in the provided HTML excerpt — consistent with a broadcast promotional send.

---

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM / footer** — The HTML source is truncated and does not include the footer. Physical mailing address and unsubscribe link must be present; cannot confirm from provided source. **Requires manual verification on full source.**

**Sender domain** — `DSG@e.dcsg.com` uses a subdomain envelope sender (`e.dcsg.com`). SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment for `e.dcsg.com` against `dickssportinggoods.com` should be verified via `dig TXT e.dcsg.com` and header inspection — not available from HTML source alone.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM / Landing Page)

**Cannot confirm UTM passthrough** — The visible CTA link encodes the destination inside the Responsys redirect token. The decoded destination and whether `utm_source=email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=flash-sale` (or equivalent) survive the redirect chain must be tested by following the link and inspecting the final URL in a browser.

**Schema `PromotionCard` @type** — `"@type": "PromotionCard"` is not a registered Schema.org type. Gmail's Promotions tab uses its own undocumented extension types, but this non-standard type combined with an empty `"image": ""` means no promotional image will be injected into the Promotions tab card.
