# ScoreCard member exclusive! 🙌 Earn a $10 Bonus Reward on your purchase of $150+
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S Sporting Goods' loyalty-first send from June 15 — a ScoreCard member exclusive offering $10 back on purchases of $150 or more with a Father's Day-anchored "Shop by Sunday" deadline. Among the five-plus DICK'S emails that landed in the inbox this week, this one actually earns attention: the hero is clean, the offer is concrete, and ScoreCard framing gives it a segmentation signal the Flash Sale and Clearance sub-accounts never had.
- The problem is what happens below the fold. After a strong hero, the email stacks an adidas/VRAI apparel block, a 40%-off drinkware module, a Summer Shop lifestyle banner, an extra 25% off clearance block, an app promo, and finally a Father's Day gift guide — all with identical "SHOP NOW" CTAs and no connective tissue. Six competing offers is not a loyalty email; it's a newsletter that happened to start with a loyalty hook.
- The Father's Day timing (six days out, Sunday deadline) is the biggest thing working in this email's favor. That urgency is real, the deadline is credible, and the reward creates a plausible reason to pull purchases forward. DICK'S should have led every module with that logic instead of burying it halfway down.

## What's working

- **ScoreCard hero is the right creative decision** — green color block + oversized "$10 BONUS REWARD" type lands the offer fast, and "member exclusive" framing makes the email feel earned rather than broadcast.
- **Father's Day deadline visible in the hero** — "Shop by: Sunday" creates genuine time-bound urgency without manufactured scarcity language.
- **Brand-name product modules (adidas, UA/VRAI)** — name-brand logos in the secondary grid add credibility and give browsers something recognizable to click toward.
- **Clean render throughout** — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds at this viewport.
- **Sends from the main DSG account**, not a sub-account — avoids the Flash Sale / Clearance sender fatigue problem while still being clearly DICK'S.

## What's weak

- **Six competing offer modules** below the hero create a "no clear winner" problem — drinkware, clearance, app, Father's Day all compete for the click that the ScoreCard bonus should have owned.
- **"SHOP NOW" is repeated identically across every module** — no destination specificity, no reason to click one over another.
- **Subject line is ~80 characters** — well over mobile truncation (~50 chars); the "$150+" threshold likely gets clipped in most inboxes.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is approximately the seventh DICK'S inbox hit in roughly 72 hours across three sender identities; even a strong ScoreCard email lands in a depleted inbox.
- **The app promo mid-email** ("ALL IN THE APP") is a conversion path distraction — members who already have the app don't need it, and members who don't shouldn't be recruited in the middle of a purchase-driving email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the email to two modules.** Hero (ScoreCard $10 Bonus) + Father's Day gift guide. Drop drinkware, clearance, and the app promo — they belong in separate sends and dilute the loyalty message every time they appear here.
- 2. **Shorten and lead with the dollar amount** in the subject — the $10 hook is buried after "ScoreCard member exclusive! 🙌 Earn a."
- **Subject Alt A:** `$10 Bonus Reward — ScoreCard members only 🙌`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Members: Earn $10 back on $150+ (ends Sunday)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop Father's Day gifts now and your $10 reward applies automatically.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock up before Sunday — the bonus lands on almost everything.`
- 3. **Fuse the ScoreCard offer with Father's Day in a single hero.** "Get Dad sorted. Get your $10 back." — it's one sentence that ties the deadline urgency to the reward without needing two separate modules.
- 4. **Replace every "SHOP NOW" with destination-specific copy.** "Shop Father's Day Gifts," "Shop adidas," "Shop Clearance" all outperform the generic button across every module and reduce bounce-to-homepage pogo-sticking.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods ScoreCard Bonus Reward Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Table-based layout with MSO conditional blocks and a responsive mobile breakpoint at 480px — fundamentals are solid. Three confirmed technical defects: invalid JSON-LD that will silently break Gmail annotation rendering, a stale 2020 schema date, and a segmentation ID that contradicts the send audience.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Truncated image URL (likely artifact, verify in production)**
The logo `src` is cut off in the provided source:
```
https://dsg.images.harmony.epsilon.com/_phoenix/_dev/AEM/img/headers/brand-logo--dk…
```
If this truncation exists in the sent HTML, the logo renders broken in all clients.

**Epsilon redirect tracking confirmed**
All CTAs route through `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...` with a `__dU__` proprietary parameter. This is intentional for Epsilon/Harmony click tracking.

**No UTM parameters visible on outbound links**
The `/T/` redirect endpoint may append UTMs server-side, but they are not present in the HTML source. If the redirect does not inject `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign`, GA4 will attribute resulting sessions as Direct.

**Open-tracking pixel present and correctly hidden**
```html
<img src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019ecadeeba0be169d6e96c65848/...'
     style="display:none; max-height: 0px; ..."/>
```
No issues with this implementation.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Invalid JSON in `<script type="application/ld+json">` — high severity**
The structured data block contains JavaScript-style single-line comments, which are not valid JSON. This will cause a hard parse failure; Gmail's annotation engine will silently discard the entire block:

```json
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
  // Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
  "@context": "http://schema.org/",
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": ""
}
```
Comments must be removed. JSON does not support `//` or `/* */` syntax.

**`border-collapse` inconsistency**
The wrapper table uses `border-collapse:separate` (inline style) while the MSO `<![if]>` stylesheet overrides to `border-collapse:collapse`. This is intentional for Outlook but worth confirming the separate-spacing values don't bleed into Outlook renders unexpectedly.

**`role="presentation"` applied correctly** throughout all layout tables. No issues.

**Web fonts scoped to `@media screen`** — fallback to Arial is correctly specified. No rendering gap in Outlook.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Segmentation ID contradicts send audience**
The primary header table carries:
```html
id="header-non-sc-member"
```
The subject line and offer are explicitly ScoreCard-member-exclusive. If this ID drives conditional display logic in the ESP template, non-member content may be rendering for a member send. Confirm which conditional block is active at deployment time.

**Unfilled DiscountOffer schema fields**
These fields in the `DiscountOffer` structured data object are empty strings — they appear to be unpopulated template placeholders:
```json
"description": "",
"discountCode": "",
"availabilityEnds": ""
```
Even if comments were fixed, Gmail would not render a deal chip without `description` and `availabilityEnds`.

**Stale `availabilityStarts` date**
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```
This is a 2020 date — clearly a template default that was never updated. Gmail may reject the annotation as expired or ignore it entirely.

---

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM footer** — not present in the provided (truncated) source. Cannot confirm physical mailing address or unsubscribe link from what was supplied. Auditor should verify the footer table exists in the full production HTML.

**Sender domain** — `DSG@e.dcsg.com`. The `e.dcsg.com` subdomain is a dedicated sending subdomain (correct practice). SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment cannot be assessed from HTML source — must be verified via email header inspection or DMARC reporting tools.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM attribution gap (see §2)**
If `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/` does not append UTMs before the final redirect, all traffic from this email lands in GA4 as Direct, making conversion attribution impossible.

**Offer specificity**
Subject promises `$10 Bonus Reward on $150+ purchase`. If the landing page does not immediately surface this threshold and reward mechanism above the fold, or requires the user to re-authenticate to ScoreCard before the offer is visible, drop-off risk is elevated. This is a continuity concern, not a markup defect — flag for QA against the destination URL.
