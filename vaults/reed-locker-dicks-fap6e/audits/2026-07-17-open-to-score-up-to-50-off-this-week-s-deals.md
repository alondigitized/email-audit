# Open to score up to 50% off this week's deals! ✅
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth consecutive "This Week's Deals / up to 50% off" email in three days — and the subject line's "Open to score" sports pun, while cute, isn't enough to rescue a creative that's gone fully stale. The green turf hero is at least a color change from the blue 50%-off badge used July 14-15, and the field/scoreboard motif does tie thematically into the subject. But the structure underneath — hero badge, "Explore More Deals" CTA, then a six-section category dump — is structurally identical to the July 14, 15, and 16 sends.
- There's a small inconsistency that undercuts trust: the hero promises "up to 50% off" but individual category sections tout "up to 60% off Select Running Shoes" and "up to 60% Off Select DSG Clothing." Leading with the lower number in the hero makes the body feel like a bait-and-upgrade rather than a pleasant surprise. The catalog dump format — six product grids, six SHOP NOW buttons — is catalog-browsing dressed up as email, and it dilutes click intent across every section.
- The email executes the format competently. The render is clean, the hierarchy is readable, the offer is real. But at this send cadence, competent execution is not enough. The marginal subscriber who opened the July 15 replay and didn't click has already voted. Sending this again is a list-fatigue accelerant.

## What's working

- **Green turf hero is a genuine creative upgrade** — the scoreboard/field motif integrates with the "Open to score" subject in a way the prior blue hero didn't.
- **50% off badge dominates without scrolling** — mobile-first hierarchy is solid; offer is impossible to miss.
- **Category sections are correctly scoped** — running shoes, DSG clothing, fan shop, accessories each get their own lane with a dedicated CTA, giving a subscriber a reason to self-select.
- **No render bugs** — all images appear intact, text doesn't overlap, layout holds on the visible viewport.

## What's weak

- **Hero says 50% off; sections say 60% off** — inconsistent discount language in a single email reads as sloppy, not generous.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTAs are interchangeable** — six identical button labels across six different categories collapse intent into noise rather than directing it.
- **"This Week's Deals" framing is exhausted** — this is the exact same positioning sent July 14, 15, and 16; it reads as filler, not a campaign beat.
- **Zero loyalty/ScoreCard mention visible** — for a brand with an active rewards program, dropping member pricing here would separate active loyalists from deal-browsers and give the offer more credibility.
- **No time pressure with teeth** — "this week's" is a vague deadline; the July 16 send used "limited time" and it still scored better on urgency than this one does.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface the 60% off number in the hero** — if sections are already offering 60%, lead with 60% everywhere and retire the 50%-off badge. The downgrade reads as inconsistent.
- 2. **Collapse to one anchor category CTA** — pick one hero product (e.g., the running shoe on sale) and build the hero around it; cascade the other categories beneath. One specific click beats six generic ones.
- 3. **Inject ScoreCard / member value** — even a one-liner ("ScoreCard members earn 2× points this week") breaks the pure-discount pattern and gives loyalists a reason to click that non-members don't have.
- 4. **Rest the "This Week's Deals" frame** — after four sends in three days, cycle to either a product-hero format or an editorial pick list. The July 15 "Want it? DICK'S got it" send (10/10) proved the audience responds to that pivot.
- 5. **Rewrite subject and preheader to differentiate from July 16's send:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 60% off running, apparel + more — shop now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New week, new drops: 60% off inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your week's best deals are live — ScoreCard members earn double.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Running shoes, DSG apparel, soccer gear — all marked down.`

### 7. Recommendations

**P0 — Fix before send**

**Invalid JSON-LD block — Gmail promotional annotations are broken**

The `<script type="application/ld+json">` block contains JavaScript-style single-line comments (`//`), which are not valid JSON. The entire block will fail to parse silently, and no Gmail Promotions tab annotation (image carousel, discount badge, expiry countdown) will render.

Offending lines:
```json
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
  // Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
  "@context": "http://schema.org/",
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": ""
}
```
Strip all `//` comments from the JSON block. Validate with [validator.schema.org](https://validator.schema.org) or `JSON.parse()` before deploy.

**Stale `availabilityStarts` date — 6 years in the past**
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```
This is a template artifact. Set to the actual campaign start datetime. Gmail uses this field to display offer timing in the Promotions tab.

**Empty required JSON-LD fields**
```json
"description": "",
"discountCode": "",
"availabilityEnds": "",
"image": ""
```
`PromotionCard.image` and `DiscountOffer.description` are required for Gmail Promotions tab rendering. Populate or remove the schema block entirely — empty required fields suppress the annotation without error.

**P1 — Verify before send**

- Confirm physical address and unsubscribe link are present in the email footer (full source required)
- Confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass on `e.dcsg.com` sending domain
- Spot-check 3–5 ESP redirect links resolve to correct, live sale pages with UTM parameters intact
