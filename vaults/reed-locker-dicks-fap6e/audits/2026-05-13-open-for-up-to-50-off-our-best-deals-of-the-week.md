# Open for up to 50% off our best DEALS of the week >>>
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S Sporting Goods' weekly deals digest — a classic department-store scatter-shot. The 50% OFF hero badge lands cleanly and the green-on-white brand execution is professional, but the email immediately loses focus by trying to serve every customer simultaneously: Men's, Women's, Kids', Golf, Outdoor, and Clearance all share the same send. For a male athletic persona (Walker), the relevant sections are buried under Women's and Golf modules before you get to anything specifically on-point.
- The clearance callout — "EXTRA 25% OFF Select Clearance" in red — is actually the most eye-catching block below the fold, which is a hierarchy problem. It signals value, but pushes the more premium Men's deals off the screen before the reader gets there.
- Net judgement: competent, on-brand execution of a low-ambition brief. The email does the job of reminding you DSG has sales this week; it doesn't do the job of making you feel like this send was meant for you.

## What's working

- **50% OFF hero badge is impossible to miss** — the red circular badge on the hero image stops the scroll and anchors the value prop immediately.
- **"Best Deals of the Week" headline is honest and clear** — sets expectation without overpromising.
- **Green "SHOP NOW" buttons are consistent and legible** — no ambiguity about where to click, and they're spaced well across sections.
- **Category quick-links (BIKES / RUNNERS / CLOTHING)** near the top let a motivated reader skip straight to their department.
- **Extra 25% Off Clearance block** is a high-contrast, strong secondary hook for deal-hunters who scrolled past the full-price modules.

## What's weak

- **Kitchen-sink structure dilutes relevance** — Men's, Women's, Kids', Golf, Outdoor, and Clearance in one scroll means no section gets enough real estate to feel targeted.
- **"Up to 50% off Select"** is present across nearly every section — the sameness of the offer language makes each module feel identical, reducing urgency to act on any specific one.
- **No deadline or end-date anywhere visible** — "week" implies Sunday cutoff but nothing confirms it; the urgency evaporates.
- **No social proof or product specifics** — no ratings, no "bestseller" callouts, no sizing callouts — just category-level CTAs that lead to a landing page the reader has to navigate themselves.
- **Women's and Kids' sections appear before Golf in a send that likely skews male** — for Walker, this is irrelevant scroll to burn through before hitting anything relevant.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the send by gender/category** — a Men's-only version of this email (Men's + Golf + Outdoor) would triple the relevance for the Walker persona without requiring any new creative. This is the single biggest lift available.
- 2. **Add a hard end-date to the hero** — "Ends Sunday, May 18" next to the 50% badge costs nothing and converts browsers into buyers.
- 3. **Lead with one hero product, not a category** — pick the top Men's shoe or jacket at the deepest discount and feature it with a price, not just a percentage.
- 4. **Rename the CTAs** — "SHOP MEN'S DEALS" beats "SHOP NOW" every time. Specificity reduces decision friction.
- 5. **Move clearance module earlier** — if 25% Off Clearance is the strongest secondary hook, don't bury it below four department modules.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 50% off men's shoes & apparel — this week only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Best deals in men's: 50% off ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Men's running shoes, training gear, and outdoor essentials — all on sale now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your weekly DSG deals: up to 50% off + extra 25% off clearance.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is served via Epsilon Harmony ESP with standard Outlook MSO conditional comments and mobile media queries. Two meaningful technical defects are present: invalid JSON-LD that will silently fail Gmail Promotions tab annotation, and a stale 2020 timestamp hardcoded in structured data.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Redirect domain confirmed:** All CTAs route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610...` with opaque hash parameters. UTM pass-through cannot be confirmed from the truncated source — see §6.

**Open-tracking pixel:** `app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610...` placed inside `id="ssl"` TD alongside preheader text. Mixed placement is atypical but functional; pixel `src` uses single quotes (`src='https://...'`), which is valid HTML5 but inconsistent with every other attribute in the document (all double-quoted). Minor hygiene issue, not a deliverability risk.

**Favicon reference in `<head>`:** `<link rel="icon" ... href="https://www.dickssportinggoods.com/home/assets/icons/dsg/favicon.ico"/>` — ignored by all email clients; harmless dead weight.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Outlook MSO conditionals:** Present and structurally correct (`gte mso 9|IE` wrappers, `OfficeDocumentSettings`, `border-collapse:collapse` override).

**Mobile breakpoint:** Fires at `max-width:480px`; `max-width:600px` on outer table. Standard DSG pattern.

**Image alt text:** Logo `<img>` tag is truncated in the source provided; cannot confirm `alt=""` is set. All email images must carry `alt` attributes (empty string acceptable for decorative images) to avoid broken-image placeholder text in image-blocking clients.

**`role="presentation"` on layout tables:** Present — correct.

**`lang="en"` on `<html>`:** Present — correct.

**Font loading:** Archivo loaded via `fonts.gstatic.com` at path version `v7`. Google Fonts silently redirects outdated version slugs, so this resolves correctly today, but version-pinned URLs can break if Google retires the path. The `@media screen` guard correctly restricts web-font loading to screen clients.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**Unsubscribe / physical address:** Not present in the truncated source. These are required by CAN-SPAM and must appear in the rendered email footer. Cannot confirm presence or absence from the provided excerpt alone.

**JSON-LD structured data block — multiple defects (high severity for Gmail Promotions tab):**

```json
// GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
```

JavaScript-style `//` comments appear inside `<script type="application/ld+json">`. **JSON does not support comments.** Any JSON parser (including Gmail's) will reject the entire block, disabling Gmail Promotions tab annotation entirely. Comments must be removed.

**Stale `availabilityStarts` date:**
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```
Date is six years old — a placeholder never overwritten for this send. Gmail may suppress the annotation or display incorrect offer timing.

**Empty required fields in `DiscountOffer`:**
```json
"description": "",
"discountCode": "",
"availabilityEnds": ""
```
All three are empty strings. `DiscountOffer` and `PromotionCard` are Gmail-extension Schema.org types; empty `description` and missing `image` in `PromotionCard` means the Promotions tab card will render with no offer copy and no hero image.

**`PromotionCard.image` is empty:**
```json
"image": ""
```
Designer placeholder comment left in production source: `// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!`

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM Params, Landing Page Alignment)

All CTA links use opaque Epsilon redirect URLs (e.g., `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610000019e2302bcd3b56f356e96c660f0/...`). Whether UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are appended by the ESP at redirect time or encoded in the destination URL is not determinable from the HTML alone.

**Action required:** Manually follow at least two CTA links and inspect the final landing page URL in the browser address bar to confirm UTM parameters are present. If they are absent, site analytics will attribute clicks to direct/none rather than email.
