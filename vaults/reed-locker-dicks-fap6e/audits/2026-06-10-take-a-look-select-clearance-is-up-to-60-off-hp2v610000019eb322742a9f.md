# Take a look 👀 Select clearance is up to 60% off!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-10**
## Executive summary

- This is the second "DICK'S | Clearance" sub-account email hitting the inbox on June 10 — yesterday's clearance send escalated to 60%, and this one keeps the same ceiling. The creative formula is identical: red/green hero banner, bold "60% OFF" number, product grid underneath. The brand is clearly in a clearance-liquidation sprint, and this email is squarely on that track.
- The structure works but the execution feels rushed. There are eight separate product callouts crammed into a single scroll — shoes, clothing, golf, outdoor, team sports, basketball — which dilutes everything. The Father's Day hook visible at the top-right ("Get it by Father's Day!") is the only time-bound urgency in the email, but it's buried in a small corner badge rather than integrated into the headline offer.
- Solid on headline clarity, weak on focus. Good enough to convert a clearance hunter, but unlikely to pull anyone who isn't already in deal-browsing mode.

## What's working

- **60% OFF hero is immediately legible** — large, red, high contrast. The number lands before anything else.
- **"Select Clearance Scores" framing** is a small copy win — "Scores" has athletic resonance that fits DICK'S brand voice.
- **Category breadth is real** — shoes, clothing, golf, outdoor, basketball all visible, genuinely covering multiple buyer types.
- **Father's Day badge** in the top corner provides credible time-bound urgency tied to an actual calendar event (Father's Day is days away).
- **No broken images or layout bugs visible** — clean render throughout.

## What's weak

- **Eight product sections with no priority** — the eye has nowhere to land after the hero; it becomes a catalog browse, not a conversion prompt.
- **Father's Day urgency is wasted** — it's a tiny corner badge, not the headline. This is one of the biggest hooks available right now and it's treated as an afterthought.
- **"Explore more deals" CTA copy is generic** — appears under several tiles; "Explore" signals browsing, not buying.
- **No specific price anchors on products** — the grid shows discounts (30%, 40%, 60%) but no "was $89, now $39" reference pricing to make the savings feel concrete.
- **Clearance sub-sender is now sending its third email in two days** — cadence fatigue is a real risk; subscribers are seeing the same 60% ceiling repeated with minimal creative variation.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make Father's Day the headline urgency, not the fine print.** Rewrite the hero to "60% off clearance — get it before Father's Day" and put a day/date countdown there. This single change turns a browsing email into a deadline email.
- 2. **Pick one hero product per section, show the real price.** "Was $89, now $35" is more persuasive than "Up to 60% off select items." One visible anchor price lifts the whole section.
- 3. **Collapse eight sections to four max.** Force editorial curation. If everything is featured, nothing is. Lead with the two or three categories with deepest inventory or strongest margins.
- 4. **Replace "Explore more deals" CTA copy.** "Shop [category] Clearance" is specific and action-oriented. "Explore" is passive.
- 5. **Consider resting the clearance sub-sender for one day.** The main DICK'S account and the clearance sub-account are both running near-identical 60% messaging simultaneously — they are cannibalizing each other's perceived value.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last chance: Up to 60% off clearance before Father's Day`
- **Subject Alt B:** `60% off clearance — grab dad's gift before it's gone`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Golf clubs, shoes, clothing — real prices slashed while stock lasts`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Father's Day is Sunday. These deals won't make it.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`lang="en"` on `<html>`** — present. Good.

**`role="presentation"` on all layout tables** — present. Good.

**MSO conditional comments** — present and structurally correct. Includes `border-collapse:collapse` override and anchor color normalization for Outlook.

**`a[x-apple-data-detectors]`** — present in `<style>`, prevents iOS from auto-linking phone numbers/addresses inside the email body.

**Web font loading** — Archivo is loaded only inside `@media screen { @font-face { … } }`, so Outlook (which ignores `@media`) falls back to Arial without a missing-font render failure. However, the `src:` URL uses a hardcoded version path:
```
https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/archivo/v7/k3kQo8UDI-1M0wlSfdnoLmvDIaI.woff2
```
`v7` is stale — current Archivo on Google Fonts is at a higher version. File may still serve (Google preserves old versioned paths), but worth pinning to the current URL.

**Alt text on images** — the HTML is truncated before most `<img>` tags; the logo `<img id="logo">` tag is cut off mid-attribute. Cannot confirm `alt` attribute presence across all images from the provided source.
