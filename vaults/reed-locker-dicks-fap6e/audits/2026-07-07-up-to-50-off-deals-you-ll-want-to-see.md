# Up to 50% off DEALS you'll want to see >>>
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- This is the second email DICK'S has sent today — the earlier send ("They're here! Up to 50% off this week's deals," 7/10) already spent the "50% off deals of the week" headline. This one tries to differentiate with a multi-section digest format: separate lanes for men's, women's, kids', outdoor, and a buried "Up to 70% Off" clearance event section that actually contains the strongest offer in the email. The 50% hero at the top is clean and hierarchy is sound, but the lead offer is *weaker* than what's buried mid-scroll — a structural misfire.
- The format is almost identical to the earlier July 7 send, which compounds the fatigue problem. Subscribers who already opened the morning email will register this as noise before the subject even renders. The ">>>" punctuation in the subject doesn't help — it reads like a forwarded chain email, not a major retailer.
- Where the July 6 emails earned 8/10 by adding a live countdown timer and leading with the strongest possible specific offer, this send steps back: no timer, no product-level specificity, and the most compelling number (70% off) is tucked below the fold.

## What's working

- **Visual hierarchy holds up.** The 50% OFF hero banner lands first, "Best Deals of the Week" is a clean navigating header, and each section has its own discount callout and SHOP NOW button.
- **Multi-category coverage is comprehensive.** Men's, women's, kids', outdoor — all visible without confusion. A shopper who browses across categories has an obvious entry point per lane.
- **The 70% off clearance block is visually distinct.** The dark/red background section stands out from the rest of the email and creates a genuine visual escalation for scrollers.
- **Product imagery is category-appropriate.** Athletic shoes, outdoor gear, and kid's clothing all feel on-brand and seasonally relevant for mid-summer.
- **Footer is clean.** App download badges and social links without clutter.

## What's weak

- **50% leads while 70% hides.** The clearance event offering up to 70% off is the best deal in the email — but it's buried in a mid-scroll section. The subject and hero both undersell the actual offer range.
- **No time pressure anywhere.** Yesterday's send had a live countdown timer. This email has none — "Deals of the Week" is too soft to drive urgency when subscribers have seen multiple "ends tonight" messages this week.
- **Second email today, near-identical premise.** The earlier July 7 send already ran "50% off / deals of the week." This email feels like a duplicate to anyone who opened the first one.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA copy is generic across all six sections.** After the July 6 send that used category-specific CTAs, this is a step backward — every button says the same thing regardless of what's being sold.
- **">>>" in subject line reads as spam.** A brand with DICK'S volume doesn't need ASCII arrow tricks to drive opens — it undercuts the retailer's credibility at inbox level.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with 70%, not 50%.** The clearance event is the strongest offer. Move it to the hero — "Up to 70% Off Summer Clearance" — and let the category lanes (40-50%) serve as supporting content below. Never bury your best offer.
- 2. **Add a deadline.** Even "Deals end Sunday" restores the urgency the countdown timer provided on July 6. Without a deadline, "deals of the week" reads as permanent.
- 3. **Kill the second email or delay it.** Two near-identical 50%-off sends in the same morning is the fastest way to train subscribers to ignore the sender. If the cadence can't be reduced, the second send must lead with a different offer (clearance-first, product-specific, or a member-only angle).
- 4. **Make CTAs category-specific.** "Shop Men's Deals," "Shop Women's," "Shop Clearance" — one extra word per button pays for itself in click-through.
- 5. **Drop the ">>>" from the subject.** Replace with a clean offer-first format.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 70% off summer clearance — plus deals by category`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Men's, women's, kids': deals up to 70% off this week`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shoes, clothing, and outdoor gear — ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Clearance from 40–70% off. Shop by category before sizes go.`

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods "Up to 50% off DEALS" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses standard ESP redirect tracking (likely Salesforce Marketing Cloud) with proper Outlook conditional comments and responsive CSS. Three issues require immediate attention: invalid JSON in the structured data block, a CSS syntax error in the MSO conditional, and empty/stale schema fields that will silently break Gmail's native promotion rendering.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**No UTM parameters visible in outbound links.**
All links route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...?__dU__=<encoded>`. Whether UTM params are appended post-redirect cannot be confirmed from the HTML alone — verify the redirect chain resolves with `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` intact.

**Recipient identifier exposed in query string.**
`__dU__=v0G4RBKTXg2Gs8NEX0-...` is base64-encoded and appears to contain subscriber PII. Confirm it is signed/encrypted and not reversible to raw email address or subscriber ID, as this value is visible in forwarded emails and server logs.

**Tracking pixel missing `alt` attribute.**
```html
<img src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019f3e53d040a0b9abf4bbc782e8/...'
     style="display:none; max-height: 0px; font-size: 0px; overflow: hidden; mso-hide: all"/>
```
No `alt=""`. Some corporate spam filters flag open-tracking pixels without an explicit empty alt. Add `alt=""`.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS syntax error in MSO conditional block** — stray `"` before closing `}`:
```css
div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
```
The `"` makes this invalid CSS. Outlook's parser may ignore the entire rule or cascade unpredictably. Remove the errant quote.

**Web font @font-face uses versioned path `v7`** — `fonts.gstatic.com/s/archivo/v7/...`. If Google rotates the font CDN path (as it has historically), the fallback silently kicks in. Not a blocking issue, but worth pinning via a self-hosted copy or using the `@import` URL form which Google keeps stable.

**`role="presentation"` correctly applied** to all layout tables. `lang="en"` present on `<html>`. No issues with the responsive breakpoint structure at 480px.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unrendered merge tokens detected in the visible HTML. The `__dU__` parameter in links indicates recipient-level personalization is handled at redirect time rather than inline. No issues from what's visible — footer content (where `%%email%%`-style tokens typically appear) is truncated.

### 5. Compliance

**Cannot fully audit** — HTML is truncated before the footer. The following must be confirmed in the full source:

- Functioning unsubscribe link with ≤10-day processing (CAN-SPAM §5(a)(3))
- "ADV" label if required by state law (not required federally here, but California/Colorado have specific rules for promotional mail)

**Sending domain:** `e.dcsg.com` (subdomain of `dcsg.com`). SPF/DKIM/DMARC cannot be verified from HTML — check headers of a live received copy with `Authentication-Results` header.

**From name/address alignment:** "DICK'S Sporting Goods" `<DSG@e.dcsg.com>` — the from-name is clearly identifiable per CAN-SPAM §5(a)(1). No issue.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM coverage unconfirmed** (see §2). Subject line promises "Up to 50% off DEALS" — verify the redirect destination lands on a page matching that offer, not the homepage.

**No inline UTM fallback.** If the redirect chain breaks (DNS failure, ESP outage), the user lands on a bare URL with no attribution. Consider appending UTMs directly to destination URLs before ESP wrapping as a redundancy measure.

---

### 7. Recommendations

**P0 — Fix immediately:**

1. **Remove JavaScript-style comments from JSON-LD block.** The `<script type="application/ld+json">` block contains `//` line comments, which are **invalid JSON** and will cause Gmail's structured data parser to reject the entire block silently:
   ```
   // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
   // Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
   // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
   ```
   Strip all `//` comments before send.

2. **Populate or remove empty JSON-LD fields.** The following fields are blank, which makes the schema useless and may cause parser warnings:
   - `DiscountOffer.description`: `""`
   - `DiscountOffer.discountCode`: `""`
   - `DiscountOffer.availabilityEnds`: `""`
   - `PromotionCard.image`: `""`
   
   Also: `DiscountOffer.availabilityStarts` is set to `"2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"` — a 6-year-old placeholder date. Update or remove.

3. **Fix MSO CSS syntax error:** Remove the stray `"` in `padding:0;"}`.

**P1 — Fix before next campaign:**

4. Add `alt=""` to the open-tracking pixel `<img>`.
5. Verify UTM parameters survive the `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...` redirect chain by testing with a link checker or curl.
6. Confirm footer CAN-SPAM elements (address, unsubscribe) in the full HTML.
7. Audit `__dU__` reversibility — if the encoded value decodes to raw PII, it should be an opaque token only.
