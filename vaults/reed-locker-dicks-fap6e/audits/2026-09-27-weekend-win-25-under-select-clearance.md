# Weekend win 🙌 $25 & under select clearance
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-27**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S doing something they haven't done in the last five audited sends: a genuinely new offer. "$25 AND UNDER" is a specific price ceiling, not another "EXTRA 25% OFF select clearance" repaint of the same red hero that ran three times in three days (9/25–9/26, all 6.00/10). The dark maroon/red hero with the dollar-sign motif is visually distinct enough to register as fresh, and the copy stacks a real value prop underneath: "Score low prices on select styles before they disappear for good."
- Past that hero, it's the standard DICK'S kitchen-sink scroll — category pills, a 40%-off clothing/shoes block, six more category tiles, six "check out these picks" products, then footer boilerplate. Nothing breaks, nothing overlaps, but nothing after the hero earns a second look either.
- Judgement: better than the last five sends on offer novelty, but the subject line undersells it and the body dilutes the hero with too many competing secondary offers by mid-scroll.

## What's working

- The hero offer is a genuine break from the repeated "EXTRA 25% OFF" creative — a specific price point ($25 and under) is easier to grasp than a percentage.
- Big, legible white type on the dark red background — no readability issues.
- Clean CTA button ("Shop Now") directly under the offer, no scroll needed to find it.
- ScorecardCard/rewards module up top reinforces loyalty membership for enrolled shoppers.
- Product thumbnails in the "Check out these picks" section are sharp, no broken images.

## What's weak

- Subject line buries the actual news — "Weekend win 🙌" is filler, and "$25 & under" doesn't land with the same punch as the all-caps hero treatment.
- The email pivots almost immediately from "$25 and under" to "Up to 40% Off Select Clothing and Shoes," which is a different, competing offer — dilutes the single clean message.
- No deadline or urgency signal anywhere on the $25 offer ("before they disappear for good" is vague, not a real countdown) despite DICK'S usual playbook of hard "tonight"/"starts now" deadlines.
- Six more product-picks and six category tiles push total scroll length long for what should be a simple clearance callout.

## Recommendations

- 1. Rewrite the subject to foreground the number, which is the strongest asset in this email: lead with "$25 & Under" instead of burying it after "Weekend win."
- 2. Add a real deadline to the hero or subhead (e.g., "through Sunday") — DICK'S clearance sends convert on urgency, and this one has none.
- 3. Cut the "40% Off Clothing and Shoes" block or move it well below the fold — it competes with the $25 hero instead of supporting it.
- 4. Trim the category-pill and top-deals sections; a $25-and-under clearance email should stay tightly scoped, not sprawl into golf/fitness/drinkware.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Everything's $25 or less this weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sneakers, gear & apparel — all $25 and under, while it lasts.`

## Technical Audit

1. Technical Summary
Email uses tracked redirect links via `data.em.dcsg.com/ee/v1/mclick`, but ships no meaningful plain-text alternative, no verifiable unsubscribe headers, and multiple content images lacking alt text.

2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All tracked CTAs route through `https://data.em.dcsg.com/ee/v1/mclick?params=...&pid=...` (ExactTarget/SFMC click-tracking pattern) — functioning as expected for redirect-based tracking.
- Open-tracking pixel present: `https://data.em.dcsg.com/ee/v1/open?params=...` — flagged by QA as an "image missing alt text," which is a false-positive category match; tracking pixels are expected to have no alt text and this is not an accessibility defect.
- QA notes 12 of 57 total links were probed (45 skipped by design, deprioritizing footer/social); no broken/dead links identified among probed links.

3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 11 content images missing `alt` attributes (confirmed in QA output), including all seasonal category modules: `clothes-shoes-2026-0927-d`, `0903-stw-sept-wks-module-team-sports`, `-golf`, `-fan`, `-drinkware`, `-outdoor`, `-cardio`, plus icon images (`icon-vc-sms-1`, `icon-vc-sportsmatter`, `icon-vc-bpg-1`, `icon-vc-favorite-1`, `icon-location-2025-1`). Screen readers will announce these as unlabeled or skip them, losing product/offer context conveyed only visually.
- Outlook conditional comments (`<!--[if (gte mso 9)|(IE)]-->`) and MSO-specific CSS present, indicating standard multi-client fallback handling — no issues found there.
- Web font (`Archivo`) loaded via `@font-face` inside `@media screen` block with `local()`/`woff2` fallback and Arial/sans-serif stack in `body` — acceptable fallback structure, no issues found.

4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No merge tokens (e.g., `%%FirstName%%`, AMPscript `%%=...=%%`) visible in the truncated source. No issues found based on available markup.

5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- `List-Unsubscribe` header: not detected (QA WARN). Note the finding itself flags this may be a relay artifact (AgentMail may not surface the header) rather than confirmed absence — cannot confirm CAN-SPAM one-click compliance from this data alone.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) header: not detected (QA WARN) — same caveat applies; one-click unsubscribe support unconfirmed.
- `Authentication-Results` header (SPF/DKIM/DMARC): not detected (QA WARN) — sender authentication status unknown, again possibly a relay-capture limitation rather than a true failure.
- An in-body unsubscribe link is confirmed present per QA methodology notes ("an unsubscribe link is always included" in probing), so a visible unsubscribe mechanism does exist even though the header-level checks are inconclusive.
- Plain-text fallback: **FAIL** — text version is only 9 characters, effectively empty. This is a functional deliverability/accessibility gap (multipart/alternative body is not meaningfully populated) and a CAN-SPAM/accessibility best-practice miss regardless of header ambiguity.

6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Links use SFMC's `params=b.v1...` and `pid=v1...` opaque tokens rather than standard UTM query parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, etc.). No UTM parameters are present in the visible URL structure, so UTM-based attribution/continuity cannot be verified from the HTML alone — this is a tracking-methodology observation, not necessarily a defect, since SFMC resolves these server-side before final redirect.
- Final landing destination is not visible in the truncated source (link is a redirect wrapper), so landing-page alignment cannot be confirmed either way.

7. Recommendations
- Populate the plain-text MIME part with real content (subject-equivalent summary + primary CTA URL) — current 9-character fallback will hurt deliverability scoring and fails accessibility for text-only clients.
- Add descriptive `alt` text to the 11 content/icon images listed above (open-tracking pixel excluded — that one is correctly alt-less).
- Confirm via raw SMTP capture (outside AgentMail relay) whether `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and `Authentication-Results` headers are actually present at the source, since the relay may be stripping them before QA sees them — do not treat this as a confirmed CAN-SPAM gap without that verification.
