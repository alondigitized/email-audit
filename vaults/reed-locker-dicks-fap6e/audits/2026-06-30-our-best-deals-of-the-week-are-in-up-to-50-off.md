# 📥 Our BEST deals of the week are in! Up to 50% OFF. 💰
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S ninth-plus send since June 26 — and it is essentially a carbon copy of yesterday's "Say hello to our best DEALS of the week!" (scored 7/10). The "Best Deals of the Week" headline, the "UP TO 50% OFF" badge, and the three-column lifestyle hero are all structurally identical to what landed 24 hours ago. The template is clean and the offer is real, but the creative is running on fumes.
- The email works mechanically: the 50% badge dominates the hero, the green SHOP NOW CTA is unambiguous, and the New Balance 880 callout (25% off) is the only genuinely specific product hook in the entire send. But there is zero urgency, zero personalization, and zero differentiation from the email this subscriber already deleted yesterday. The season is right; the execution is on autopilot.
- The strongest argument for sending this at all is the specific shoe callout in the secondary module. Everything else — "everyone's favorite brands," soft "of the week" urgency, generic CTA copy — is filler. At this send frequency, the bar for differentiation should be higher, not lower.

## What's working

- **50% badge lands immediately** — the red callout is the dominant visual in the hero; eye-tracking would land here first on any device.
- **New Balance 880 callout is specific** — a named model with a named discount (25%) is the only concrete product hook in the email and the single strongest reason to click.
- **Summer lifestyle imagery is on-season** — beach and cycling shots read current; no stale holiday creative leaking through.
- **Category tab row is functional** — MEN'S / WOMEN'S / KIDS / GOLF / SUMMER SHOP / CLEARANCE gives quick-scan shoppers a jump point directly into their section.
- **No render bugs visible** — layout is clean throughout, images load, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **Near-identical to yesterday's send** — "Best Deals of the Week" was the frame, the headline copy, and the structural template in the June 29 audit (7/10). Subscribers will recognize this immediately.
- **"Of the week" urgency is meaningless** — no deadline date, no countdown, no specific end — the phrase signals nothing and creates no pull.
- **No ScoreCard / loyalty visibility** — this brand runs a rewards program; not surfacing member pricing or point multipliers in a deal-focused send is a missed retention lever.
- **Subject line is over-length with three spam signals** — two emojis + CAPS "BEST" + exclamation point; the line runs 53 chars and will clip on most mobile clients before the "50% OFF" claim.
- **Below-fold product grid is unnavigable at a glance** — six-plus tiny product tiles with no standout hero product; the specificity that makes the New Balance module clickable disappears entirely.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the entire email on one hero product** — the New Balance 880 25% callout is doing more work than the hero. Flip the hierarchy: lead with the shoe (or one equally specific item), make the 50% headline the context, not the other way around.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline** — "Ends Sunday" or a specific date turns "of the week" into real urgency and differentiates this send from the six before it.
- 3. **Surface ScoreCard pricing** — even one line ("Members save an extra 10%") gives the loyalty base a reason to click that non-members don't have, improving segmentation payoff.
- 4. **Trim subject to under 45 chars** — drop both emojis and the trailing period; lead with the number.
- 5. **Build one distinct visual element that wasn't in the June 29 send** — if the product, the offer, and the template are the same, subscribers will filter-delete on autopilot.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 50% off — best deals end Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `25% off New Balance 880s + up to 50% off this week`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ends Sunday: your top picks from Nike, adidas, and more — all marked down.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New Balance 880s, clearance clothing, and summer gear — up to half off.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods Promotional Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Email sends via Epsilon/Harmony ESP (`app.dickssportinggoods.com`, `dsg.images.harmony.epsilon.com`) using standard table-based layout. Three blocking issues found: invalid JSON-LD (comments in JSON), an unfilled template date from 2020, and a missing Gmail promo card image — all in the structured data block.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Tracking redirect pattern:** All CTAs route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610000019f1a439568a926986e965fc958/...` — standard Epsilon click-tracking. No bare untracked links visible in the truncated source.

**`__dU__` parameter in logo href:**
```
https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...?__dU__=v0G4RBKTXg2Gs8NEX0-...
```
This appears to be a per-recipient dynamic token. If the ESP failed to merge it for any segment, the raw token string would be passed to the landing page. Confirm this resolves correctly in seed/test sends rather than appearing as a literal string.

**No UTM params visible** on click-tracking URLs before the redirect. Confirm the redirect destination appends `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` — or that the Epsilon platform injects them server-side on redirect. If neither, analytics attribution will be lost.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**MSO conditional CSS — stray quote character (bug):**
```css
div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
```
The trailing `"` before `}` is a stray character that will cause this rule to be malformed in Outlook's CSS parser. Affected Outlook versions may fail to apply the zero-font-size collapse on the div block, potentially surfacing whitespace gaps.

**Fix:** Remove the stray `"` → `padding:0;}`

**Web font versioning:** `fonts.gstatic.com/s/archivo/v7/` loads Archivo at version 7. The current Google Fonts version is higher; v7 URLs are still served but are stale. Low risk given the `Arial` fallback, but worth updating if the font renders differently than intended in Apple Mail.

**Attribute quoting inconsistency (minor):** The open-tracking `<img>` uses single-quoted `src`:
```html
<img src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/...' .../>
```
All other attributes use double quotes. This is valid HTML5 but inconsistent; some legacy parsers may mishandle it.

**Alt text:** Logo `<img>` tag is truncated in the provided source — cannot confirm `alt` attribute presence. All product images must have non-empty `alt` for accessibility and for rendering in image-blocked clients.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`id="header-non-sc-member"`** on the header table indicates a conditional block for non-ScoreCard members. No unfilled `{{token}}` or `[TOKEN]` placeholders visible in the truncated source — assume the ESP handles the conditional server-side.

**JSON-LD `DiscountOffer` block has three unfilled fields** (see Section 6 below for full detail, since this also affects inbox rendering).

### 5. Compliance

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity / Gmail Inbox Annotations

**Critical — invalid JSON in `<script type="application/ld+json">`:**

The block contains JavaScript-style single-line comments, which are **not valid JSON**:
```json
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
  // Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": ""
}
```
JSON does not support `//` comments. Google's structured data parser will reject the **entire `<script>` block**, suppressing all three schema entries (Organization, DiscountOffer, PromotionCard). None of the Gmail inbox annotations will render.

**Stale `availabilityStarts` date — template not updated:**
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```
This is 6 years in the past. Even if the JSON were valid, Google would likely ignore or deprioritize the DiscountOffer annotation.

**Empty required fields:**
```json
"discountCode": "",
"availabilityEnds": "",
"image": ""   // PromotionCard
```
All three are empty strings. The Gmail promotional card image (`PromotionCard.image`) is blank — no inbox preview card image will display even after fixing the JSON syntax.
