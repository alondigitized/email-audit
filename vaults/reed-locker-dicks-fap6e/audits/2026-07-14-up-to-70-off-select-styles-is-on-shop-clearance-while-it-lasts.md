# Up to 70% off select styles is ON! Shop clearance while it lasts
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- After burning three consecutive sends on the same "up to 50% off" Flash Sale creative — same locker room hero, same SHOP NOW CTA — DICK'S has actually reset with a new frame: a clean dark-navy hero, a harder number (70% vs. 50%), and a "Summer Clearance Event" label. The creative does its job quickly. Problem is, this is still the seventh-plus DICK'S email in roughly four days, and the inbox fatigue is real regardless of how sharp the hero looks.
- The email suffers from category scatter. Brooks running shoes, women's/kids' DSG apparel, golf electronics, and swimming life vests are all in the same scroll — which means no single subscriber feels directly spoken to. Then the $20 off $100+ app promo resurfaces at the bottom, recycled verbatim from the July 10–11 app-exclusive campaign. It undercuts the clearance urgency: if the $20 app deal is still live, why was there a countdown on it four days ago?
- Honest verdict: the hero is the best creative DICK'S has sent this week, but the body loses the thread with too many categories and a recycled promo tacked on at the bottom.

## What's working

- **70% hero lands immediately** — dark navy background with oversized white/yellow type. The eye goes there before anything else.
- **Specific price anchor** — "Starting at $60.98" for Brooks running shoes is the kind of detail that actually drives a click; it makes the offer feel real, not just a percentage.
- **Creative refresh** — switching away from the July 12–13 locker room Flash Sale image was the right call; the new dark hero reads as a different event.
- **Summer Clearance label** is seasonally credible — it doesn't feel manufactured the way a "Flash Sale" after a "Flash Sale" does.

## What's weak

- **Category scatter kills relevance** — running shoes, golf electronics, swimming life vests, women's DSG, and kids' apparel in one email means no subscriber feels targeted.
- **"While it lasts" is meaningless urgency** — after "ends TONIGHT," "ends soon," and "last chance" in the last 72 hours, a vague open-ended deadline reads as false scarcity.
- **Recycled $20 off $100+ app promo at bottom** — this is the same offer that allegedly "ended" on July 11. Its reappearance makes the prior urgency look manufactured.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is too generic** — with five distinct category sections, each deserves a more specific CTA ("Shop Brooks," "Shop Golf," "Shop Women's Sale").
- **Inbox cadence is broken** — this is the seventh-ish DICK'S email in four days, making "open likelihood" a hard ceiling regardless of subject quality.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero category and own it** — segment the list and send runners a running-focused clearance email, golfers a golf version. A single focused CTA beats five scattered ones every time.
- 2. **Kill the recycled $20 off app block** — it undermines clearance urgency and makes the countdown on the July 10–11 sends look like a lie. If the app offer is evergreen, put it in the footer in 8pt type, not a full-width hero section.
- 3. **Add a hard deadline** — "Ends Sunday July 20" beats "while it lasts" for both urgency and trust.
- 4. **Make CTAs category-specific** — "Shop Brooks Clearance," "Shop Golf Deals," not five instances of "SHOP NOW."
- 5. **Compress cadence** — this brand needs a send-frequency governor. Seven emails in four days is a subscriber churn accelerator.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Brooks running shoes from $60 — summer clearance starts now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `70% off clearance: running, golf, apparel — this weekend only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Hundreds of styles marked down — Brooks, Nike, Adidas, and more`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Summer styles starting at $60.98 — shop before sizes are gone`
