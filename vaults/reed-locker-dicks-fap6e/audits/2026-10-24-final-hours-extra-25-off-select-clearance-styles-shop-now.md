# ⏳ Final. Hours. ⏳ Extra 25% off select clearance styles — shop now!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-24**
## Executive summary

- This is a loud, clean last-chance clearance email. The giant white "EXTRA 25% OFF" on a red maple-leaf hero is impossible to miss. The "Final Hours 07:59:48" countdown bar above it gives the urgency real credibility. The 11:59PM PT deadline in the fine print backs it up.
- Below the hero, the email loses focus. It adds a second offer ("Up to 40% Select Clothing & Shoes"), a category grid, and six unrelated products with no prices. This is also the fourth clearance or deals push in about three days. Compared with the 10/22 "Don't miss an extra 25% off select clearance!" send, nothing is new except the countdown.
- Judgement: a strong hero and a credible deadline, but a generic CTA and a long, price-free catalog after the fold. It will get opens from people who want the deal and little else.

## What's working

- The "EXTRA 25% OFF" hero is huge and high-contrast, so the offer is clear in under a second.
- The live-style countdown ("Final Hours 07:59:48") matches the "Final. Hours." subject and makes the urgency believable.
- The red "Shop Now" button sits directly under the headline "Extra 25% Off Select Clearance Styles" and is easy to find.
- The deadline is stated explicitly ("through 10/24/26 11:59PM PT"), which makes the urgency honest.
- The ScoreCard strip ("300 Points = $10 Reward") puts loyalty in view without competing with the hero.

## What's weak

- The CTA says only "Shop Now". It names no category and doesn't say it leads to clearance.
- The email stacks a second offer ("Up to 40% Select Clothing & Shoes") and a second "Shop Now" button, which dilutes the single message.
- The "Check out these picks" products show no prices, discount badges, or ratings. They are not tied to the clearance offer, so nothing shows what the 25% off applies to.
- The fine print ("Exclusions apply", "Interim markdowns may have been taken") hedges the offer. It also sits in small gray text under the CTA.
- The subject is 67 characters, so it will likely truncate on mobile. The "Final. Hours." fragments and emoji add noise.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace the generic catalog with 3–4 clearance products showing was/now prices and the extra 25% applied. This is the biggest lift, because the hero promises a deal and the body never shows one.
- 2. Change the CTA to something specific, such as "Shop Clearance Styles".
- 3. Drop the second offer block and the second "Shop Now" button so the email has one job.
- 4. Shorten the subject to under 50 characters and lead with the offer.
- 5. Make the fine print more transparent. A one-line "what's excluded" link is better than hedged copy.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (confirmed, 13 images):**
  - Tracking pixel (`data.em.dcsg.com/ee/v1/open`).
  - Category tiles on scene7: `clothes-shoes2026-1024-dk`, `team-sports`, `drinkware-1`, `fan-shop`, `golf-3`, `outdoor-1`, `fitness`.

  - For the category tiles, the image is likely the only label for the link. With images blocked or a screen reader, those links have no accessible name unless adjacent text carries it.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Plain-text fallback: FAIL.** The text part is 9 characters. A multipart/alternative message with an effectively empty text part adds a spam-filter signal and gives no fallback for text-only clients or some assistive tools. This is the only hard failure in the QA run.
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058): not found.** QA notes AgentMail may strip these headers, so this is unverified and not a confirmed defect. For a bulk sender, Gmail and Yahoo's bulk-sender rules require both headers.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Fix the plain-text part (priority).** Generate a full text alternative with the offer, CTAs and the unsubscribe URL.
2. **Add alt attributes.** Use descriptive alt text on the category tiles (for example "Team Sports"). Use `alt=""` on the decorative icons and the tracking pixel.
