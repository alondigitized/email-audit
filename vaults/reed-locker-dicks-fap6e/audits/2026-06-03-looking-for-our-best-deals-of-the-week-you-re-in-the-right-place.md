# Looking for our BEST deals of the week? You're in the right place 👉
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- This is the DICK'S Clearance sub-brand's second send on June 3 — and at minimum the fifth execution of the "Extra 25% off select clearance" creative since May 30. The subject line tries to disguise that: "Looking for our BEST deals of the week? You're in the right place 👉" frames it as a curated "best of" compilation. Open the email and you're greeted with the exact same red hero block, same headline, same multi-category grid that has appeared in every send this week. The expectation gap between subject and body is this email's biggest problem.
- The bones are fine. Visual hierarchy is sharp, the offer is unambiguous, and the category grid (clothing, drinkware, golf, team sports, outdoor, fan shop, fitness equipment) gives most sporting goods shoppers somewhere to land. The standout oddity: "Fan Shop Gear" is only up to 10% off — buried in a grid alongside 40–50% off sections — which will read as a disappointment to anyone who opened for jersey or licensed gear.
- The subject is doing cosmetic work on a brief that hasn't changed in five days. A subscriber who opened the earlier June 3 "Best DEALS of the week" send (which earned 9/10 for its genuine compilation structure) will clock this as a lesser repeat immediately.

## What's working

- Red hero dominates instantly — "EXTRA 25% OFF" reads at a glance, no hunting for the offer
- Multi-category grid is legitimately useful: seven categories with distinct CTAs gives most shoppers a path forward
- Discount depth at upper end is credible: 40–50% off fitness equipment and outdoor essentials is a real reason to click
- No visible render bugs — layout is clean, images load, no overlapping elements
- DICK'S brand palette (red/black) is consistent; the email is immediately recognizable without reading the sender line

## What's weak

- Subject creates an expectation gap — "best deals of the week" implies curation; the body is the identical clearance creative subscribers have seen since May 30
- Subject is ~67 characters — truncates on mobile, squandering even the weak curiosity the question format generates
- No expiration date anywhere visible — this clearance series has run five-plus sends with no deadline, removing the main lever for urgency-driven click behavior
- Fan Shop at "up to 10% off" sits in the same grid as 40–50% categories — conspicuously weak and likely to disappoint the segment that opened for team gear
- ScoreCard/loyalty context is entirely absent — the June 1 double-points send showed that loyalty framing lifts engagement; dropping it here is a missed stacking opportunity

## Recommendations

- 1. **Deliver on the subject or stop using "best deals" framing.** If this is a "best of" send, show hero products with actual prices, not category tiles. If it's another clearance blast, write an honest subject. The gap between promise and delivery erodes sender trust across the series.
- 2. **Add an expiration date — anywhere.** "Ends Sunday" or "Through June 8" in the hero or under the primary CTA would transform this from a passive discount into an actual reason to act now. This is the single highest-leverage change left in the clearance series.
- 3. **Shorten and specify the subject line.**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 25% off clearance — ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Best of the week: 40–50% off fitness, outdoor & more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Clothing, golf, outdoor gear + more on clearance`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack savings before the deadline hits`
- 4. **Pull the Fan Shop tile or update the copy.** 10% off in a 40–50%-off compilation is a trust tax. Either deepen the discount or swap in a stronger category.
- 5. **Refresh the hero visual.** The red-block-with-white-number creative has run five times without variation. Even a product photograph swap reduces the "I've seen this" reflex.

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods Clearance Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is built on Epsilon/Harmony ESP infrastructure with standard MSO conditional comment scaffolding. Several issues found in the JSON-LD structured data block (invalid syntax, stale dates, empty required fields) and a font dependency on an external CDN that is not email-safe.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open pixel:** `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019e8f2cd43eb9eec5369ff04b6c/63f8945f961446b900004c5a42963aa1`
- Pixel is properly hidden via inline style (`display:none; max-height:0px; overflow:hidden; mso-hide:all`). No issue.

**Click-tracking redirect chain:** All links route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610000019e8f2cd43eb9eec5369ff04b6c/...`
- The redirects are Epsilon-tracked URLs containing a subscriber token (`__dU__` param). No bare untracked links visible in provided source.
- The logo `href` also goes through the tracking domain — acceptable but worth confirming it resolves to the correct homepage destination.

**Logo `src` is truncated in the provided source** (`https://dsg.images.harmony.epsilon.com/_phoenix/_dev/AEM/img/headers/brand-logo--dk…`). Cannot confirm the full URL resolves. The presence of `/_dev/` in the path is a risk flag — verify this is not a development asset path that was accidentally promoted to production.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`role="presentation"` applied to layout tables** — correct pattern throughout visible source.

**`lang="en"` on `<html>`** — present. Good.

**External web font loaded via Google Fonts CDN (`fonts.gstatic.com`):**
```css
url(https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/archivo/v7/k3kQo8UDI-1M0wlSfdnoLmvDIaI.woff2)
```
Web fonts are blocked in most email clients (Gmail, Outlook). The font stack falls back correctly to `Arial, sans-serif`, but the `@font-face` block is inside `@media screen` so it won't cause rendering failures — low severity, but the external request is a potential privacy/CSP flag for security-conscious clients.

**Font version pinned to `v7`** — Archivo v7 is several versions behind the current Google Fonts release. Not a breaking issue, but could drift out of sync with the site's font rendering.

**Alt text:** The logo `<img>` tag is truncated in the provided source; alt attribute presence cannot be confirmed. Flag for full-source review.

**MSO conditional comments** present for Outlook table collapse and PNG support — correct implementation.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**JSON-LD `DiscountOffer` block has multiple empty and stale fields:**

```json
"description": "",
"discountCode": "",
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00",
"availabilityEnds": ""
```

- `availabilityStarts` is hardcoded to **2020-03-06** — over 6 years stale. This is a template default that was never replaced with a campaign-specific value or a merge token. Gmail's promotional annotations will parse this and may surface incorrect offer dates to subscribers.
- `availabilityEnds` and `discountCode` are empty strings. These fields should either be populated or omitted entirely from the structured data.

**JSON-LD `PromotionCard` block:**
```json
"image": ""
```
Empty — the promo card image was not populated. Gmail Promotions tab will not render a visual card without this value.

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM / unsubscribe:** HTML is truncated — footer unsubscribe link and physical mailing address cannot be confirmed from the provided source. Must verify in full source.

**Authentication headers** (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) are not visible in HTML — they exist at the SMTP/DNS layer. Sending domain is `e.dcsg.com`; alignment should be verified via a live send header inspection (e.g., MXToolbox header analyzer) to confirm DKIM `d=` matches the `From:` domain.

**No issues found** in the visible HTML for CAN-SPAM body requirements, but footer section is outside the provided excerpt.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Tracked redirect URLs (`/T/...`) encode subscriber identity but **no UTM parameters are visible in the href values** in the provided source. Epsilon redirectors can append UTMs server-side at click time, so this is not necessarily missing — but it should be confirmed in a test click that the destination URLs include `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` parameters before the email deploys. Without UTMs, clearance-driven traffic will be attributed to direct/none in analytics.
