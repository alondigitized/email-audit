# Tick, tock! ⏰ Up to 50% off will be gone is a flash... shop now. ⚡
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S tenth-or-so promotional blast since July 18, and the accumulated creative debt is impossible to ignore. The structural bones are solid — countdown timer at the top, Flash Sale hero, stacked sub-offers, product grid — but the subject line ships with a genuine typo ("gone **is** a flash" instead of "gone **in** a flash"), clocks in at ~71 characters, and buries its urgency punchline past mobile truncation. The countdown timer showing about eight hours remaining is the one element that earns its keep here; every other ingredient — the locker-room imagery, the orange-on-dark Flash Sale treatment, the "up to 50% off" copy — has run at least three times in the last five days.
- The internal offer architecture also works against itself. Three distinct value props are stacked at equal visual weight: Flash Sale at 50%, Nike Deal Days at 30%, Extra 30% Off Clearance. Each gets its own section, its own badge, and its own identical "SHOP NOW" button. The result isn't a tiered offer — it's a firehose. A subscriber who got the July 22 "Extra 30% off select clearance" email (9/10 here, the cleanest send of the week) has now seen the same clearance hook recycled as a tertiary panel.
- The verdict: structurally functional, creatively exhausted, and shipping with a proofreading miss that undermines the brand's authority. The countdown is real urgency; nothing else is.

## What's working

- The countdown timer ("Final Hours: 07 | 59 | 48") is the most concrete urgency signal DICK'S has sent this week — a subscriber can see exactly how much time is left, which is meaningfully different from "Ends TONIGHT."
- The email renders cleanly — no broken images, no overlapping text, no empty placeholders, reading flow from timer → hero → sub-offers → product grid is uninterrupted.
- Adding the "Back-to-School Shop" category link is the one seasonally fresh hook that justifies this send existing alongside the June/July promo cadence.
- The stacked offers (50% flash, 30% Nike, extra 30% clearance) cover three distinct purchase intents — new arrivals, brand-loyal, and value — giving different subscriber segments a reason to click.
- The product grid at the bottom ("Must-have gear for an epic summer!") grounds the abstract percentage claims in actual merchandise with real imagery.

## What's weak

- **Subject line typo**: "Up to 50% off will be gone is a flash" is a proofreading failure that ships in the most visible real estate of the send.
- Subject is ~71 characters — guaranteed to truncate on most mobile clients, meaning "shop now. ⚡" disappears for the majority of openers.
- Three stacked "SHOP NOW" buttons at equal visual weight create decision paralysis; no single offer is elevated as the clear priority.
- "SHOP NOW" as CTA copy is entirely generic — no noun, no destination hint, no reason why *this* click over any previous one.
- The July 22 "Extra 30% Off Select Clearance" email already served this clearance offer as the *hero*; re-running it as a tertiary panel here feels like a demotion, not a reminder.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the typo before anything else.** "Gone is a flash" is an error that erodes brand credibility — a second set of eyes on the subject line is the cheapest lift available.
- 2. **Cut the subject to under 50 characters.** Sacrifice the "Tick, tock" wordplay if needed; the percentage and deadline are what drive opens, not the clock metaphor.
- 3. **Pick one hero offer and subordinate the rest.** The countdown owns the Flash Sale; make Nike Deal Days a secondary row and clearance a single footer link. Don't give three offers identical visual authority.
- 4. **Make every CTA specific.** "Shop Flash Sale" > "SHOP NOW". "Shop Nike Deals" > "SHOP NOW". One verb, one noun, every button.
- 5. **Anchor the Flash Sale to one product.** A single "today's best deal" item with a real before/after price and a direct product link would cut through the pattern fatigue that's accumulated since July 18.
- **Subject Alt A:** `⏰ Final hours: up to 50% off ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 50% off flash sale — 8 hours left`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Nike Deal Days + extra 30% off clearance also live — shop before midnight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Hundreds of flash deals on clothing, shoes & gear — clock's running`

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods Flash Sale Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email infrastructure is well-structured (Salesforce Marketing Cloud / ExactTarget stack, proper MSO conditionals, mobile breakpoints), but the JSON-LD structured data block contains hard syntax errors and multiple unpopulated template fields that will cause Gmail's promotional card parser to reject the annotation entirely.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**ESP redirect tracking** — All links correctly route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610000019f90a4e7ae909fae6e96c660c0/…` with `__dU__` payload tokens. Format is standard for SFMC.

**UTM visibility** — Pre-redirect URLs contain no visible UTM parameters. This is acceptable if UTMs are appended at redirect resolution, but cannot be confirmed from the HTML source alone. Final destination URLs must be spot-checked to confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` are present.

**Tracking pixel** — Present and hidden correctly:
```html
<img src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019f90a4e7ae909fae6e96c660c0/…'
     style="display:none; max-height: 0px; font-size: 0px; overflow: hidden; mso-hide: all"/>
```
No issues with implementation.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate `font-family` declaration** — `body` rule declares the property twice, the second with `!important`:
```css
body { font-family:Archivo,Arial,sans-serif; font-family:Archivo,Arial,sans-serif!important }
```
Redundant; the second rule wins in all clients. No visual impact but signals copy-paste debt.

**`border-collapse:separate` + `border-spacing:0`** — All layout tables use this combination. It is functionally equivalent to `border-collapse:collapse` but is technically redundant and may produce a 1px rendering gap in older Outlook versions. Low risk given the separate/spacing:0 pair.

**Truncated logo `src`** — The logo `<img>` URL ends with `…` in the provided source (`?wid=248&hei=…`). If this truncation reflects the actual sent HTML, the image will 404. Verify the full Scene7 URL is intact in the delivered message.

**`role="presentation"`** — Correctly applied to all layout tables. `lang="en"` on `<html>` is correct.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**JSON-LD block contains multiple unresolved / empty fields:**

```json
{
  "@type": "DiscountOffer",
  "description": "",
  "discountCode": "",
  "availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00",
  "availabilityEnds": ""
}
```
```json
{
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  "image": ""
}
```

- `discountCode`, `description`, `availabilityEnds`, and `image` are all empty strings — unfilled merge placeholders.
- `availabilityStarts` is hardcoded to **2020-03-06**, over six years stale. This date predates the send by 2,330+ days and is clearly a copy-paste artifact from an old template.

These fields are non-fatal for email rendering but will cause Gmail's Promotions tab annotation to fail or display incorrect metadata.

---

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**HTML source truncated before footer** — CAN-SPAM-required elements (physical mailing address, unsubscribe link) are not visible in the provided excerpt. These must be confirmed present in the full rendered email; their absence would be a legal violation.

**Template year comment** — `<!--DKS Email Marketing 2025-->` is embedded in `<head>`. Current send year is 2026. Not a compliance issue, but flags that the base template has not been updated this year.

**Authentication headers** — SPF, DKIM, and DMARC cannot be assessed from HTML source. Sending domain `e.dcsg.com` (ESP subdomain) should have its own DKIM alignment with the `From:` header domain. Verify via raw message headers.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM Params, Landing Page Alignment)

**Cannot confirm UTM chain from source alone.** SFMC redirect links (`/T/…`) absorb click attribution server-side. The destination URLs — resolvable only by following the redirects — must carry:
- `utm_source=email`
- `utm_medium=email` (or `cpc`/`direct` as per their taxonomy)
- `utm_campaign` matching this send's campaign ID

**No campaign ID is visible in the JSON-LD** (`discountCode` is empty), which may indicate GA/analytics campaign tagging is also incomplete.
