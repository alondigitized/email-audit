# Score up to 50% off during our Flash Sale! (ends TONIGHT)
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- This is Day 2 of the same Flash Sale creative that hit inboxes on July 12 — same locker room hero, same "Two-Day Flash Sale: Up to 50% Off" headline, same SHOP NOW CTA. The only material change is the subject line appending "(ends TONIGHT)." DICK'S is effectively treating a recycled send as a fresh piece of content. That said, the email's bones are solid: clear value prop above the fold, a recognizable brand, and a dense grid of specific deals (30% clearance, 60% running shoes, 75% CALIA, a $80 canopy savings, etc.) that give a sports shopper real reasons to click.
- The problem isn't the offer — 50% off is genuinely compelling. The problem is creative fatigue compounded by extreme send frequency. This brand has pushed 7+ emails in the past two days. A subscriber who opened the July 12 Flash Sale send and didn't act will recognize this immediately as a follow-up nudge, not a new reason to shop. The urgency in "(ends TONIGHT)" is the only genuine differentiation from yesterday's drop.
- The below-the-fold product grid also works against it: canopies, golf polos, cycling shoes, and women's CALIA in the same scroll undermines any sense of "this was picked for you." It reads as a clearance liquidation dump more than a curated sale.

## What's working

- **Hero urgency lands cleanly.** "FLASH SALE" in large type over the locker image, combined with "(ends TONIGHT)" in the subject, creates a real decision moment for fence-sitters from day 1.
- **Above-the-fold value prop is tight.** "Two-Day Flash Sale: Up to 50% Off" + red SHOP NOW button requires zero effort to understand.
- **Category nav row** (SHOES | APPAREL | FOOTWEAR) lets shoppers self-select quickly without scrolling through the full grid.
- **The green "Extra 30% OFF Select Clearance" section** visually pops and adds a stacking-discount angle that feels like genuine value on top of the Flash Sale umbrella.
- **No visible render bugs** — clean layout, images load, no overlapping elements.

## What's weak

- **Identical creative to July 12.** The locker room hero and "Two-Day Flash Sale" headline are unchanged — a subscriber who opened yesterday sees nothing new.
- **Product grid is an untargeted spray.** Running shoes, golf fleece, a canopy, women's CALIA, and cycling shoes share the same scroll — no coherent persona is being served.
- **"Up to 50% off" is soft.** The range qualifier means the lead discount could apply to one obscure item; specific hero products (like the $34.99 Callaway polo or the 60% running shoe) do more persuasive work but are buried below fold.
- **No loyalty or member angle.** A ScoreCard member-exclusive hook would differentiate this from the previous day's identical send and reward engagement.
- **The "Must-have apps for an epic summer!" section** at the bottom feels disconnected from the sale urgency and dilutes the closing CTA.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the hero image.** The locker room visual has now appeared across multiple sends. One product hero — the most aggressively discounted item in the sale, shot big — would signal "new" to yesterday's non-clickers and earn the "(ends TONIGHT)" urgency.
- 2. **Collapse the grid to 3 curated picks.** Choose the highest-discount or highest-inventory items and feature them prominently instead of 7+ disconnected categories. Fewer choices, more clicks.
- 3. **Surface the stacking clearance deal higher.** "Extra 30% OFF Select Clearance" is the strongest value angle — it should be the second thing a reader sees, not the third block.
- 4. **Add a ScoreCard/member callout** — even a one-liner like "ScoreCard members: your rewards apply at checkout" would reward loyalty and differentiate from the prior send.
- 5. **Cut the "Must-have apps" section** on a closing-day urgency email — it's a distraction from the conversion goal.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Flash Sale ends tonight — shoes, apparel up to 50% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last hours: up to 60% off running shoes ends tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Extra 30% off clearance stacks on top — shop before midnight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Running shoes, golf, cycling — deepest discounts of the sale`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard ESP redirect-tracking architecture with inline responsive CSS and Outlook conditional blocks. Two categories of confirmed defects: invalid JSON-LD (will fail Gmail Promotions tab annotation) and a malformed CSS rule in the Outlook conditional that may degrade rendering in Outlook desktop clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open pixel — no `alt=""` attribute**
```html
<img src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019f5a4a3a35857e506e96c66058/...'
     style="display:none; max-height: 0px; ..."/>
```
Missing `alt=""` on the tracking pixel. Some accessibility scanners flag this; add `alt=""` to suppress.

**Single quotes on pixel `src`** — inconsistent with the rest of the document (double-quoted attributes). No functional impact but indicates copy-paste from a different template.

**Click-tracking redirect domain** — `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...` is used consistently. No bare/untracked URLs detected in the visible source.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed CSS in Outlook conditional block**
```css
<!--[if (gte mso 9)|(IE)]>
div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
```
There is a stray `"` after `padding:0;` before the closing `}`. This makes the entire `div` rule invalid for Outlook/IE, meaning `background-color:inherit` and other resets won't apply — any `div` in the Outlook render path may inherit unexpected background colors or spacing.

**Google Fonts loaded via external URL**
```
url(https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/archivo/v7/...)
```
Blocked by default in Outlook desktop, Gmail app on Android, and many corporate environments. Fallback (`Arial`) is properly defined, so rendering degrades gracefully — but worth noting if brand font fidelity matters in Outlook.

**Logo `alt` text** — truncated source cuts off before the `alt` attribute on the logo `<img>`. Confirm it's not empty or missing; a blank logo alt breaks screen readers and plain-text fallback rendering.
