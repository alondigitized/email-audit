# What's Your Passion? The Game. The Style. The Outdoors.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-03**
## Executive summary

- A broad brand-equity send from DICK'S Sporting Goods that tries to be everything to everyone. The hero positions the store as a category destination, the deals module delivers a credible 50%-off hook, and three product picks round out the body — but the segmentation is loose. For Walker (men's footwear focus), the featured shoe is women's HOKA, the subject has no footwear signal, and the primary CTA is department-agnostic. The email is clean and functional; it just isn't aimed.

## What's working

- **Deals module is the backbone.** The teal "UP TO 50% OFF / THIS WEEK'S DEALS / SHOP DEALS" block is visually anchored and stops the scroll. Clear discount, clear button, short copy — the right formula.
- **Department nav is a quiet win.** The six-category nav row (Apparel, Footwear, Team Sports, Golf, Outdoor Rec, Camp & Hiking) lets any subscriber self-route to their category without wading through irrelevant product picks.
- **Sports Matter Foundation banner earns goodwill.** A brief, non-pushy CSR module adds brand warmth without eating much space.
- **Layout is clean.** No broken images, no misaligned text, no render artifacts. The email renders well at this width.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is brand philosophy, not an offer.** "What's Your Passion? The Game. The Style. The Outdoors." is an awareness-level tagline. It gives the inbox no reason to open — no price, no product, no deadline.
- **Hero is maximally generic.** "Explore the Store for Every Athlete" over a mountain backdrop is brand wallpaper. It doesn't introduce the 50% off deal; it delays it.
- **Product picks are mis-segmented for Walker.** The only footwear recommendation is HOKA Women's Mach 10 Running Shoes. A Walker-persona subscriber cares about men's footwear, not women's trail runners.
- **Sports Matter banner dilutes the purchase intent flow.** Placed between the deal module and the product picks, it interrupts the conversion path with a charitable ask.
- **No pricing is visible in the product tiles.** Three specific products are surfaced with names and images but no price, no discount badge, no availability signal — the 50% off headline doesn't connect to anything specific below it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Connect the deal to a product.** Show one or two shoes (men's) with a struck-through original price and the deal price under the 50% off banner. Right now the discount floats with no anchor.
- 2. **Swap the women's HOKA for a men's running or lifestyle shoe.** HOKA, New Balance, On — any men's pick works. The women's shoe in a Walker-segmented email is a segmentation miss.
- 3. **Move Sports Matter below the product picks.** CSR modules belong in the footer region, not between the deal hook and the product recommendations.
- 4. **Rewrite the subject with an offer or product hook.** Something like "Up to 50% off this week — men's picks inside" would earn opens that the current tagline won't.
- 5. **Add urgency to the deal module.** "Ends Sunday" or a visible date tightens the "this week's deals" framing into a real deadline.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CONFIRMED — CSS parsing error (P1):**

In the injected `brand.min.css` block:

```css
a{color:inherit;display:block;max-width:100%;text-decoration:none text-align: center}
```

Missing semicolon between `text-decoration:none` and `text-align: center`. Every compliant CSS parser will treat `text-align: center` as a continuation of the `text-decoration` value, reject the entire sub-expression, and silently drop `text-align`. Any `<a>` element relying on this rule for centering will render left-aligned.

**CONFIRMED — `<title>` / subject mismatch:**

- `<title>`: `EVERY SEASON STARTS HERE`
- Subject line: `What's Your Passion? The Game. The Style. The Outdoors.`

Some webmail clients (notably Gmail web, Yahoo) surface the `<title>` value in thread views or tab labels. The mismatch indicates stale template reuse and can expose internal campaign naming.

**NOTED — Google Fonts via external `@font-face`:**

Fonts are loaded from `fonts.gstatic.com` inside an `@media screen` block. This is intentional and correct as a progressive enhancement — Outlook/MSO and most webmail clients will ignore it and fall back to `Arial, sans-serif`, which is declared. No action required, but external font requests are logged by corporate email proxies and may trigger security warnings in some enterprise clients.

**NOTED — Vestigial IE meta tag:**

```html
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge">
```

IE is EOL. Harmless but should be removed in the next template cleanup.
