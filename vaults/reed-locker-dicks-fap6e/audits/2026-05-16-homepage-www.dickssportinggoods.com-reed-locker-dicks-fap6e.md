# Homepage snapshot · www.dickssportinggoods.com · 2026-05-16
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- This isn't a homepage — it's a hard error page. Dick's served up a full site failure with a blue "Oops, Something Went Wrong." message, a 12-hour wait notice, and a raw error code exposed to the public. As a dad who needs to resupply cleats and track ScoreCard points before the spring season wraps up, I'm just bouncing immediately. Nothing here tells me what Dick's sells, what it costs, or why I should bother coming back.

## What's working

- The Dick's Sporting Goods logo renders correctly and is recognizable — at least I know what site I landed on.
- The error message is in plain English ("We are working on the problem") and doesn't throw jargon at me.

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage is missing** — a hard error page is the worst possible experience for any shopper.
- **Raw error token exposed:** `Error: 0.2ca7cb17.1778961629.13d75954 IP: 75.140.9.0` is shown publicly — sloppy and slightly unsettling.
- **"Try again after 12 hours"** is a brutal ask; that's a same-day loss for impulse or need-based purchases like cleat replacement.
- **No fallback content** — no cached hero, no "shop our top categories" static links, nothing to salvage the visit.
- **No ScoreCard / loyalty mention** — even in a degraded state, a "Check your rewards at the app" line would retain me.

## Recommendations

- **Serve a static fallback page** with top category links (Team Sports, Footwear, Clearance) so a CDN-level outage doesn't kill the entire shopping session.
- **Remove the raw error token and IP address** from the public-facing error page — that's internal infrastructure data that means nothing to a customer and looks unprofessional.
- **Replace the "12 hours" copy** with something like "We'll be back shortly — check the app in the meantime" and link to the mobile app or a status page.
- **Add a single ScoreCard CTA** ("Log in to the app to check your rewards") so loyal customers have somewhere to go and don't feel abandoned.
