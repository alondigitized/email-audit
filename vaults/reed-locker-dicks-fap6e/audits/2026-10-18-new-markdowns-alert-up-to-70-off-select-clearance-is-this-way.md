# New markdowns alert! 🚨 Up to 70% off select clearance is this way 👉
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-18**
## Executive summary

- DICK'S leads with a big "Up to 70% Off" over fall leaves. It's the strongest headline in this week's run, up from the "Extra 25% off" clearance sends on 10/16 and 10/17. The eye lands on the number first. The "New Markdowns Added" headline and the green "Shop Now" button sit directly under it.
- Below the hero it turns into a catalog. The email stacks eight or nine modules with eight different offers: 70%, 40% shoes, a $14.98 hoodie, 40% fan gear, 40% cleats, $200 off TaylorMade irons, Peloton shoes, and 50% flash deals. The "70% off" promise never gets a product or a price behind it. "Up to" is carrying the whole claim.
- It's a competent send with a strong top and a diffuse middle. The subject shouts "alert" and "this way 👉" but doesn't say what's being marked down or when it ends. It also lands one day after "ENDS TODAY" and two days after the 25% sends, so the urgency is stale.

## What's working

- The "UP TO 70% OFF" hero is huge and high-contrast, and it's the clear first read.
- The headline, one-line body copy and green "Shop Now" button sit directly under the hero. The CTA is unambiguous.
- Category pill links (Men's, Women's, Kids', $25 and Under, Fan Shop, All Sale) give a fast path into clearance.
- The $14.98 DSG fleece hoodie is a specific price point, and it's the most concrete offer in the email.
- Fan gear has a real deadline in the fine print ("Valid through 10/18/26 11:59PM PT"), and the module is tagged "One Day Only."
- The layout is clean, with no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- Eight-plus modules dilute the 70% story. The 40% shoes, cleats, fan gear, TaylorMade and Peloton blocks compete with the hero.
- "Up to 70%" has no product, price or example behind it. The only concrete deal is a hoodie that isn't clearance.
- Nearly every button reads "Shop Now," which is generic and gives no category-specific verb or noun.
- The urgency is weak. Only "Limited Time" tags and fine print appear, and nothing in the hero says when it ends.
- The "40% OFF" shoes graphic looks washed out, and the dark sneaker module repeats yesterday's 10/17 shoe send.
- There are no reviews or social proof, and the fine print is long and sits far below the offers.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the body to three modules: the 70% hero, one proof module with 3–4 actual clearance products and prices ("Was $90, now $27"), and one category row. Move fan gear, cleats, TaylorMade and Peloton to a different send.
- 2. Put a deadline in the hero ("Ends Tuesday" or similar) so the urgency matches the subject. The last credible deadline was the 10/17 "ENDS TODAY" send.
- 3. Replace the generic "Shop Now" buttons with specific CTAs like "Shop Clearance Shoes" or "Shop 70% Off Deals."
- 4. Keep the subject under 50 characters and say what's marked down. Drop the 🚨 and 👉 stack.
- 5. Add star ratings under the product picks at the bottom.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fleece from $14.98, shoes up to 40% off. New clearance added today.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML is structurally sound, with table layout, `role="presentation"`, a viewport meta tag and MSO conditionals. The automated QA flagged a missing plain-text part (9 chars) and widespread missing `alt` attributes. Header-based compliance and authentication checks couldn't be verified because of the relay, and the source was truncated.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **No broken links confirmed.** 12 of 64 links were probed and none were reported as failing. 52 were skipped (footer, utility and social links are deprioritized), so those are unverified.
- **Open-tracking pixel:** `https://data.em.dcsg.com/ee/v1/open?params=b.v1...&pid=v1...&tuid=6ad26d15364aac4e38256861&configId=512ffb51-...` is a 1x1 `<img>` with no `alt`. It has no `style="display:block"` or `border:0`. It also sits directly in `<body>` before the wrapper table, which is harmless but unconventional.
- The tracking URL is very long (about 700 chars). This isn't a defect, but it's a clipping risk in some clients.
- Link wrapper/redirect domains and UTM params can't be assessed from the truncated source (see section 6).

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `alt` text on 11 images** (QA WARN):
  - Hero images: `2026-1018-dks-stw-6am-fleece`, `-fan`, `-cleats`, `-cycle`
  - Banner: `banner-2026-1013-ggxy-taylormade-irons-promo`
  - Icons: `icon-vc-sms-1`, `icon-vc-sportsmatter`, `icon-vc-bpg-1`, `icon-vc-favorite-1`, `icon-location-2025-1`
  - The tracking pixel is the eleventh.
  - With images blocked, or for screen readers, the clearance offers (and any text baked into the images) are not conveyed.
- **Leftover editor and template artifacts in production markup:**
  - `<body class="is-html-mode" data-contenteditable="true">`
  - The comment `<!--Replace/Paste SSL Here-->`
  - `<link rel="icon" ...favicon.ico>`, which email clients ignore
- **Preheader:** the visible text "Get $20 off $100+ when you sign up for Text Alerts**" sits in `td#ssl` (`font-size:0; line-height:0; height:0`), followed by the `#pht-spacer` filler of `&zwnj;&nbsp;` repeats. The hiding technique is standard, but the preheader is a promo disclaimer rather than anything tied to the subject line.
- **Images:** Scene7 images use `fmt=jpeg&qlt=100&wid=1200&hei=1600`, so quality 100 at 1200px wide is heavy for a 600px container. I couldn't confirm this against the full source.
- **Fonts:** Archivo is loaded via `@font-face` from `fonts.gstatic.com`, with an `Arial, sans-serif` fallback. This is fine.
- **Outlook conditionals:** `<!--[if (gte mso)|(IE)]><table width="584"...>` is opened in the source shown. The matching close is outside the truncated portion, so I can't verify it.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (`{{ }}`, `%%`, `[FIRST_NAME]`) appear in the visible source. The tracking `tuid` and `params` are populated.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Plain-text fallback missing (QA FAIL):** the text part is 9 chars. This is a real deliverability and accessibility gap if it reflects the sent message. It could partly be a relay capture artifact.
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) not found (QA WARN):** the QA notes say AgentMail may not capture these headers. Treat them as **unverified**, not confirmed missing. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require one-click unsubscribe for senders at this scale, so check the raw headers.
- **Authentication-Results not found (QA WARN):** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown here. The From domain `em.dcsg.com` and the tracking domain `data.em.dcsg.com` are aligned, which is a good sign, but I can't verify authentication.
- **Body unsubscribe link and physical address:** the footer was truncated. The QA probe says an unsubscribe link is always included, but no result was reported, so I can't confirm that either is present.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. The truncated source exposes no CTA `href`s and no probe results for landing pages. I can't confirm UTM parameters, redirect chains or landing-page alignment with the "Up to 70% off select clearance" subject.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add a real text/plain MIME part, mirroring the offers and including the unsubscribe URL.
2. Add descriptive `alt` text to the 5 hero and banner images and the 5 icons. Use `alt=""` for decorative icons, and add `alt="" style="display:block;border:0"` to the open pixel.
3. Check the raw headers for `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and for SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass. Don't rely on the relay-captured copy.
4. Strip the `is-html-mode`, `data-contenteditable` and `Replace/Paste SSL Here` artifacts and the favicon `<link>` from the production template.
5. Reduce the Scene7 image quality (`qlt=100` to about 80) and request widths closer to 2x the rendered size.
6. Re-run the audit on the full HTML, with all links probed, to verify UTMs, the footer address, the unsubscribe link and the Outlook conditional closures.
