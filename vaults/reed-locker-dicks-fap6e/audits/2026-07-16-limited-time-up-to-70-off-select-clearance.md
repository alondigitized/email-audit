# Limited time! 💸 Up to 70% off select clearance.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- This is the 70%-off Summer Clearance email — structurally identical to the one sent July 14 (which scored 8/10 here) and thematically identical to the July 15 "This week's deals" replay. The dark hero is clean and the depth number lands hard, but DICK'S is now on its fifth or sixth clearance/discount send in four days, and that cadence tax is real. The inbox story has gone: Flash Sale → Flash Sale → Last Chance → 70% Clearance → 50% Deals → Fresh Picks → and now, back to 70% Clearance. Subscriber attention is a depletable resource, and this send is spending it.
- The most surprising thing in the body is the hero-to-product disconnect: the 70% OFF splash leads straight into a "20% Off Select On Cloud 3.4 Training Shoes" callout as the first visible product. That gap — from 70% down to 20% on the marquee item — is the fastest way to train subscribers to stop clicking. The specific price callouts ($59.98 Nike, $29.98 another item) are actually a positive development versus prior sends, but they're buried below the fold. The app promo section near the bottom is a total non-sequitur in a clearance sale context and kills momentum.
- Bottom line: a well-designed clearance email undermined by cadence fatigue, a hero-versus-body offer mismatch, and a focus-breaking app pitch dropped into the middle of a discount journey.

## What's working

- **Hero is visually strong** — dark background, bold "UP TO 70% OFF" in high contrast, Summer Clearance Event framing lands immediately.
- **Specific dollar prices appear in the body** — "$59.98 Nike" and "$29.98" callouts are more concrete than prior sends and give the subscriber a real anchor.
- **Multiple product category callouts** — Running shoes, apparel brands, and accessories all appear, giving different buyers a landing strip.
- **Clean render** — No broken images, no overlapping text, no layout artifacts visible.

## What's weak

- **Hero promises 70%, first product callout delivers 20%** — The On Cloud 3.4 shoes section immediately below the fold undercuts the hero discount; a subscriber scanning fast will feel misled.
- **Cadence is broken** — This is effectively the same 70%-off clearance email DICK'S sent July 14; two of the same send in 72 hours signals list fatigue and content drought.
- **App promo section breaks the discount funnel** — "ALL IN THE APP" with colorful balls imagery appears mid-scroll in what should be a single-minded clearance sale; it splits attention and exits the deal narrative.
- **"Limited time!" is hollow** — No end date, no countdown, no specific deadline. The urgency claim has no teeth when paired with "select clearance" (i.e., leftover inventory that will sit there regardless).
- **No ScoreCard or loyalty hook** — Given DICK'S heavy loyalty membership program, the total absence of member pricing or ScoreCard mention leaves an obvious personalization lever untouched.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the body with the hero-matching products** — If 70% is the headline number, the first product card must show a 70%-off item, not 20%. Reorder the product grid to lead with the deepest discounts; push On Cloud (20% off) to the bottom or drop it from this creative entirely.
- 2. **Give "Limited time" a real date** — "Ends Sunday at midnight" or "Clearance ends July 20" makes the urgency credible and measurable. Without it, "limited time" is furniture.
- 3. **Cut the app promo section** — It belongs in a different send with a different objective. A clearance email that wants one action (SHOP CLEARANCE) should not hand the subscriber a side-door exit.
- 4. **Add a ScoreCard callout** — Even one line — "ScoreCard members earn points on clearance" — gives loyalty members a reason to click even if the discount isn't at the hero level.
- 5. **Tighten the product grid** — Four to five categories is too many for a discount email; prioritize the two or three with the deepest cuts and biggest brand names, and let those breathe.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Clearance hits 70% off — ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your size is still here: up to 70% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Nike, On Cloud, and more — real prices, real time.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `ScoreCard members earn points on every clearance pick.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods Clearance Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email infrastructure (Acoustic Campaign, table-based layout, MSO conditionals) is structurally sound, but the JSON-LD structured data block is fatally malformed — it will silently fail in all parsers. One MSO conditional CSS block also contains a stray character that invalidates a rule.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open tracking pixel** — `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019f6a73963eb96a2d6e966a3578/...`
- ESP: Acoustic Campaign (Silverpop lineage), confirmed by `/O/` (open) and `/T/` (click) path conventions.
- The `<img>` tracking pixel is missing `alt=""`. While hidden via `display:none; max-height:0px; overflow:hidden`, the absence of an explicit empty `alt` attribute can trigger accessibility warnings in some clients.

**Click-through URLs** — wrapped in Acoustic redirect at `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...?__dU__=<encoded>`
- Destination URLs are Base64/encoded inside `__dU__`. Cannot verify UTM params or final landing page alignment without decoding — see §6.
- Tokens appear recipient-rendered (no unresolved placeholders visible), but the encoding makes QA verification opaque.

No broken or raw placeholder links found in the visible source.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Stray `"` in MSO conditional CSS** (confirmed in `<head>`):
```css
/* inside <!--[if (gte mso 9)|(IE)]><style> ... */
div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
```
The `"` before `}` is not valid CSS. The Outlook CSS parser will reject the entire `div` rule, which suppresses zero-font-size spacing resets — a known source of unwanted gaps in Outlook.

**`<link rel="icon">` in `<head>`** — email clients do not render favicons; this is dead overhead, no rendering impact.

**`@font-face` scoped under `@media screen`** — correct Outlook workaround; no issue.

**MSO conditional tables and `border-collapse:separate`** — structurally correct for border-radius support.

Logo `<img>` is truncated in the provided source; `alt` text on the logo cannot be confirmed.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**JSON-LD `DiscountOffer` has unfilled template fields:**
```json
"description": "",
"discountCode": "",
"availabilityEnds": ""
```
These are empty string literals — they appear to be template slots that were not populated at send time. Not visually harmful (Gmail ignores them), but they confirm the structured data pipeline is not being driven by campaign data.

**`availabilityStarts` is hardcoded to 2020:**
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```
This is copy-pasted template boilerplate from over five years ago. No merge token was substituted.

**`PromotionCard.image` is empty:**
```json
"image": ""
```
Designer instruction comment was left in the JSON-LD block (see §3 below). The promo image URL was never set.

No broken `{{merge_token}}` or `%%token%%` syntax detected in the visible body markup.

---

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM / footer** — the HTML source is truncated before the footer; physical mailing address and unsubscribe link cannot be confirmed from the provided excerpt. These must be verified against the full source.

**`availabilityStarts: 2020`** in JSON-LD is not a compliance issue per se, but a date six years in the past could be flagged by automated inbox validators (e.g., Validity Everest) as stale structured data.

**Authentication headers** (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) — not derivable from HTML source; must be verified via raw message headers. Sending domain `e.dcsg.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain, which is standard.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM Params, Landing Page Alignment)

All click-through URLs pass through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...?__dU__=<encoded>`. The encoded value is Acoustic's obfuscated destination URL. UTM parameters are not visible in the HTML source without decoding `__dU__`.

**Risk:** If UTM parameters are not embedded in the destination URL before encoding, all clearance traffic will arrive at dcsg.com with no campaign attribution. This cannot be confirmed from the HTML alone — requires either decoding a sample `__dU__` value or checking GA4/Adobe Analytics reports for `utm_source` presence on landing sessions.
