# EPIC Memorial Day SALE is live — shop up to 50% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S thirteenth-plus promotional send in roughly six days — and it arrives on the Saturday of Memorial Day weekend, two days before the actual holiday. The email body is well-executed: the patriotic hero is big, bold, and clear, "Up to 50% OFF" lands at a glance, and the loyalty rewards module adds genuine membership utility. Multiple category blocks (clearance overlay, HOKA coupons, Nike Golf, MLB) give different shoppers multiple entry points.
- The problem is not the email — it's the campaign. A subscriber who's been getting this same "up to 50% off" message since May 18 has either already bought or tuned out. The subject line is nearly identical to yesterday's two sends ("EPIC deals" on May 22), and the creative mirrors the May 20 and May 22 heroes. Cadence has reached the point where the offer no longer feels like an event — it feels like a permanent state, which quietly undermines trust in the discount.
- Taken alone, this is a solid promo email with clear hierarchy and no render bugs. As the thirteenth send of the week, it is competing against its own inbox presence more than against other brands.

## What's working

- **Hero is unambiguous.** The patriotic red/white/blue flag background with oversized "Up to 50% OFF" text reads instantly — no digging required.
- **Multiple category entry points.** Clearance overlay (Extra 25%), HOKA coupons (40%), Nike Golf (50%), and MLB gear give the email reach across different shopper intents.
- **Loyalty module present.** The "Earn 20% Back in Rewards on Qualifying Purchases" block late in the email is a genuine member benefit that competes with competitors who don't surface it.
- **Sender display name hack.** Packing "DICK'S | Epic Memorial Day Deals" into the From field is a clever inbox-list tactic — it adds a second line of copy before the subject even registers.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breakage visible in the screenshot.

## What's weak

- **Cadence has poisoned the offer.** The "up to 50% off" line has now appeared in at least six sends since May 18. It no longer reads as a sale; it reads as the floor price.
- **Seven competing offer blocks with identical CTAs.** Every module says "SHOP NOW." With no visual differentiation in button hierarchy, the eye flattens — nothing feels urgent or primary below the hero.
- **No explicit deadline visible.** "Memorial Day" implies Monday, but there is no "offer ends Monday" copy or countdown anywhere in the render. On the last push weekend, that's a missed urgency lever.
- **Subject line déjà vu.** "EPIC Memorial Day SALE is live" is too close to yesterday's "The SALE starts now" and "Up to 50% off EPIC deals" subjects. A subscriber scanning their inbox sees sameness, not escalation.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an explicit deadline to the hero.** "Ends Monday" or "Last day" in the hero section would convert hesitators who are waiting to see if the deal extends. This single copy change is the highest-leverage move available.
- 2. **Differentiate CTAs by category.** Replace every "SHOP NOW" with something specific: "Shop HOKA," "Shop Nike Golf," "See MLB Deals." Specificity dramatically improves click-through on the category blocks.
- 3. **Consolidate to one or two offer blocks.** Seven offer modules at 40-50% off creates decision paralysis. Cut to the two or three highest-margin categories and give each more room.
- 4. **Rotate the subject away from "EPIC" and "SALE."** Both words have appeared in every send this week. Use concrete specificity instead.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 50% off — offer ends Monday night`
- **Subject Alt B:** `HOKA, Nike Golf, MLB — last Memorial Day prices`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop clearance, brand deals, and member rewards before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Extra 25% off clearance + member rewards. Ends Monday.`

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods Memorial Day Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email's structured data (JSON-LD) block contains invalid JSON that will silently fail in Gmail's promo tab renderer, and a stale 2020 campaign date. A subject/preheader discount figure mismatch is also present at the code level.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open pixel** — `<img src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019e53b156bb956ddcf4bbe5c898/...'>`
- `alt` attribute is absent. Some ESPs and accessibility scanners flag this; add `alt=""`.
- Hidden via inline `style` only, no `width="1" height="1"` fallback for clients that strip CSS. Low risk but non-standard.

**Click tracking** — all CTA links route through `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/…` with encoded payloads. Structure is consistent with a standards-compliant redirect pattern. No raw destination URLs are exposed in the truncated source, so UTM verification is deferred to Section 6.

**No issues** with the tracking domain itself relative to the sending domain (`e.dcsg.com` / `dcsg.com` family).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**JSON-LD comment syntax (high severity):**
```json
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported...
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": ""
}
```
JavaScript-style `//` comments are **not valid JSON**. The Gmail structured data parser will reject the entire `<script type="application/ld+json">` block, suppressing promo-tab card rendering for all recipients.

**Web font path** — Archivo loaded from `https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/archivo/v7/…`. Current Google Fonts API serves `v19+`; `v7` URLs still resolve but this is a stale build artifact. Arial fallback is correctly specified.

**`border-collapse:separate` on wrapper table** — paired with `border-spacing:0`, functionally equivalent to `collapse`, but deviates from the de facto email table norm. No rendering defect observed, but worth flagging as a latent inconsistency.

**`border-radius` on inner card tables** — `border-radius:8px 8px 0px 0px` on `#header-non-sc-member` will be silently ignored by all Outlook/MSO versions (the MSO conditional block does not supply a VML fallback). Cosmetic only; corners will be square in Outlook.

**`role="presentation"` on layout tables** — correctly applied where visible.

**`a[x-apple-data-detectors]` override** — present; iOS data-detector suppression is in place.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No personalization tokens or dynamic merge fields are visible in the truncated source. The email appears to be a broadcast blast with no subscriber-level personalization. No broken or unreplaced `{{token}}` / `%%token%%` syntax detected.

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM / unsubscribe** — HTML is truncated before the footer. Physical mailing address and unsubscribe link cannot be confirmed from available source. Must be verified against full render.

**Sending domain alignment** — envelope from is `e.dcsg.com` (subdomain of `dcsg.com`). DKIM and SPF alignment depend on DNS records not visible in HTML; cannot confirm from source alone. Flag for header-level review via MX Toolbox or the ESP's authentication report.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Subject vs. preheader mismatch (code-level):**
- Subject: `…shop up to 50% off`
- Preheader text (in `<td id="ssl">`): `Up to 40% off select Nike, adidas, DSG clothing & shoes ➡️`

These are two different discount figures rendered from the same send. Recipients see "50%" in the inbox subject and "40%" in the preview snippet — inconsistent at the HTML level regardless of which figure is accurate.

**UTM parameters** — destination URLs are wrapped inside the `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/` redirect and not inspectable from encoded source. Confirm UTM `utm_campaign`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_source` are appended to final landing URLs after redirect resolution. Cannot confirm or deny from available HTML.
