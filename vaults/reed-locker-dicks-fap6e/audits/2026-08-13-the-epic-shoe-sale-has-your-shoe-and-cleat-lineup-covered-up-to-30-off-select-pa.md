# The Epic Shoe Sale has your shoe and cleat lineup covered — up to 30% off select pairs
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-13**
## Executive summary

- This is the "Epic Shoe Sale" hero again — same red locker background, same Nike/adidas/HOKA shoe-and-cleat pile — but this send finally gives it a legible date-bounded deal instead of a vague "Up to 30% off" that trails off nowhere. The clean hero card, deal deadline (8/15/26 11:59PM PT), and single green SHOP NOW button are the strongest execution of this creative DICK'S has run this week. It's a real improvement over 2026-08-10's version and closer to the well-liked 8/9 send.
- Below the hero it's the standard DICK'S digest sprawl: category chips, VRST/CALIA clothing cards, a six-tile category grid, then a six-product "Check out these picks" wall. None of that is broken, but it dilutes the shoe/cleat focus the subject promised — by the time you hit kayaks and treadmills, this doesn't read like a shoe sale anymore.
- Solid, on-brand, no render bugs — but it's the fourth near-identical "shoe sale" beat this run, and the digest tail undercuts the hero's specificity.

## What's working

- Clean hero card with a real headline ("Up to 30% Off Select Shoes and Cleats"), subcopy, an explicit expiration date/time, and one unambiguous green CTA.
- Locker-room shoe pile with visible Nike, adidas, HOKA logos ties directly to the subject's brand promise.
- ScoreCard loyalty banner up top (300 points / $10 reward, "Shop to Earn Points") is visible and relevant to a member.
- Fulfillment line ("Order Today. Sport it Tomorrow" / pickup / delivery) sets expectations early with no clutter.
- No overlapping text, no broken images, no lorem ipsum — everything renders cleanly.

## What's weak

- The shoe focus dies fast: right after the hero it pivots to VRST shorts, CALIA clothing, golf clubs, a kayak, a pickleball paddle, drinkware, a Penn State tee, and a treadmill — none of that is "shoe and cleat lineup."
- Six-item product wall at the bottom (backpack, Samba, socks, HOKA Clifton, kids' shorts, New Balance 530) has no prices, no discount badges — pure browsing filler, not offer reinforcement.
- This is the brand's fourth "shoe sale" wordmark send in a short span (8/10, plus this run) — genuine promo fatigue risk for a repeat subscriber.
- "Save on hundreds of styles from Nike, adidas, HOKA and more" is generic filler under the headline; it doesn't add anything the hero image hasn't already shown.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut or drastically shrink the digest tail — a "shoe sale" email should end after 1-2 shoe-specific upsell modules, not roll into golf/kayak/treadmill categories that contradict the subject line.
- 2. Add prices or "as low as $X" to at least the top hero product tiles in "Check out these picks" so the shoe wall actually reinforces the discount, not just browsing.
- 3. Swap the generic subcopy for something concrete, e.g. "Nike, adidas & HOKA running, training and cleats — now up to 30% off."
- 4. Vary the hero creative — three shoe-sale sends with the same red locker background in one week is going to blur together in this subscriber's inbox.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 30% off shoes & cleats — Nike, adidas, HOKA`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your shoe & cleat lineup, up to 30% off (ends 8/15)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Hundreds of styles marked down — sale ends 8/15 at midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New running shoes, cleats, and trainers from your favorite brands.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **13 images missing `alt` text**, including the open-tracking pixel and content images (`vrst.jpg`, `calia.jpg`, `golf.jpg`, `outdoor.jpg`, `team-sports.jpg`, `drinkware.jpg`, `lic.jpg`, `fitness.jpg`) and footer icons (`icon-vc-sms.png`, `icon-value-sportsmatter--light.png`, `icon-vc-bpg.png`, `icon-vc-favorite.png`, `icon-location-2025.png`). - Web font (`Archivo`) is loaded via `@media screen { @font-face }` scoped to screen rendering only, with `Arial` fallback in the base `font-family` stack — correct pattern to avoid Outlook font-loading issues.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Unsubscribe link is broken** (see Section 2) — this is a CAN-SPAM functional risk since the mechanism must actually work.
- **List-Unsubscribe header not found**: no RFC 2369 header captured, meaning mailbox providers (Gmail/Outlook) may not surface a native one-click unsubscribe UI.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found**: RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe (POST) is not confirmed present, further limiting native unsubscribe support in Gmail/Yahoo, which increasingly weight this for inbox placement.
- **Authentication-Results header not found**: SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status could not be verified via the relay; this may be a limitation of header capture through the AgentMail relay rather than a sender-side failure, but it should be confirmed against the original SMTP headers before concluding authentication is misconfigured.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Links route through the Epsilon/Cheetah click-tracking domain (`app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...`) rather than direct UTM-tagged URLs to `dickssportinggoods.com`; UTM parameters, if present, are applied server-side after redirect and are not visible in the raw HTML source, so continuity cannot be confirmed from source alone.
- The confirmed-broken unsubscribe redirect (`aspxerrorpath=/pc/subscription`) indicates the destination `/pc/subscription` page path likely changed or was removed on the site side without the email template's redirect mapping being updated — a continuity break between the ESP redirect config and the current site route structure.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Fix the unsubscribe redirect mapping so `/U/...` resolves to a working `/pc/subscription` (or current equivalent) page — highest priority given CAN-SPAM implications.
2. Add `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers (RFC 8058) at the sending-platform level to enable native one-click unsubscribe in Gmail/Outlook.
3. Add descriptive `alt` text to all 13 flagged images, or explicit `alt=""` for the tracking pixel and purely decorative footer icons.
4. Remove the invalid `//` comments from the `application/ld+json` block and populate or remove the empty `DiscountOffer`/`PromotionCard` placeholder fields so the block parses as valid JSON.
5. Verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC authentication results directly from original SMTP headers, since the relay-based capture returned no data.
