# 🔍 Up to 50% off makes this week's deals worth a closer look 🔍
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-26**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S weekly-deals roundup, and it's the same template as the last several sends. The hero is a giant white "UP TO 50% OFF" over autumn leaves, with "This Week's Deals" and a red Shop Now button directly below. The eye lands on the number first. The offer is easy to read, but the email is very long, and it repeats the 50% hero with a second "UP TO 75% OFF" banner on the same leaf background halfway down.
- Below the hero it becomes a catalog. It has category pills, adidas/shoes tiles, CALIA, cleats, YETI, L.A.B. Golf, the 75% clearance banner, a MOVE challenge, a picks grid, and a footer with the app and loyalty blocks. Each module is clean, but together they split attention. A recipient who doesn't care about golf putters or the MOVE challenge has to scroll past them to reach anything relevant. The "Order Today. Sport It Tomorrow." strip at the top is a nice touch, but it's tiny.
- Judgement: competent, on-brand and on-season, but undifferentiated. A subscriber who got the 2026-10-25 "We've conjured up some DEALS!" and the 75% clearance email yesterday gets near-identical creative today, so the cadence feels heavy.

## What's working

- The giant "UP TO 50% OFF" hero is legible at a glance, and the red Shop Now button is right under it.
- The fall leaf imagery fits the late-October season.
- The category pills (Men's, Women's, Kids', Shoes, Fan Shop, Team Sports) give a quick way to self-segment.
- Product tiles carry concrete offers: "$100 & Under Select Shoes", "40% Off Men's adidas D4T", "Up to 40% Off Select Cleats", "20% Off YETI Ridgeline".
- The shipping/pickup strip and the ScoreCard points reference add a loyalty hook and a convenience message.

## What's weak

- The email is very long and has about eight modules. Sections like L.A.B. Golf putters and the MOVE challenge dilute the deals message.
- The hero offer and the "Up to 75% Off Select Clearance" banner use nearly identical creative, so the hierarchy competes and a reader can't tell which is the main offer.
- The hero says "up to 50% off" without naming a product, so it's vague about what is actually discounted.
- The fine print under the hero and the clearance banner is small and gray, and it includes "limited time" and "online only" caveats.
- The picks grid at the bottom is generic and unpriced, with no sale prices or discount cues.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to the hero plus three or four category tiles. Drop the golf and MOVE modules, or send them in their own campaigns.
- 2. Merge the 50% and 75% banners into one offer block, or make one of them visibly secondary. Two giant percent-off banners on one leaf background read as repetition.
- 3. Make the hero specific. Pair the percentage with a hero product or category, for example "Up to 50% off shoes & fleece".
- 4. Add a real deadline, for example "Ends Sunday 11:59 PM PT". "While supplies last" isn't credible urgency.
- 5. Show prices on the picks grid (was/now) so the products carry the deal.
- 6. Rotate the hero creative. This week's leaves match the last six sends.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 50% off shoes, fleece & more — ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `This week's deals: adidas 40% off, shoes from $100`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 20% off YETI and up to 40% off cleats. Free pickup in an hour.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Order today, sport it tomorrow. Deals on shoes, fleece and gear.`

- **[FAIL] Plain-text fallback:** the text part is 9 characters. Clients and filters that use the text part will show or score an effectively empty message. This is a QA-confirmed multipart defect, though it could also be an AgentMail capture artifact.

  - The QA check cannot tell a missing `alt` from an intentional `alt=""`, so the icons may be deliberately decorative. The hero images are likely the offer content. With images blocked or read by a screen reader, those offers would not be conveyed.
