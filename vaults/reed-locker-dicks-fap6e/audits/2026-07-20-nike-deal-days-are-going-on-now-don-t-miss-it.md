# 🚨 NIKE Deal Days are going on now (Don't miss it!)
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S second consecutive Nike Deal Days email in under 24 hours. Yesterday's "Don't miss Nike Deal Days! Up to 30% OFF select styles is calling your name..." scored 8/10 here — and this one is structurally identical in everything that matters: same hero concept, same offer level, same campaign. What changed is the subject line, and the change is a downgrade. Swapping "Up to 30% OFF" visible in the subject for a 🚨 alarm emoji and "Don't miss it!" is a step toward panic-mode marketing and away from the clean, offer-led lead the July 19 send nailed.
- The email body itself is fine — Nike Deal Days hero is dominant, 30% off is visible in copy, the SHOP NOW button is high-contrast and clear. But DICK'S once again bundles the Nike story with Callaway polos, bikes, cardio equipment, and the Summer Shop, so the focused Nike message dissolves into a general circular by the time you hit the second scroll. If you came for Nike, you're gone before you reach the Callaway section; if you're a Callaway buyer, the Nike hero was noise.
- The real ceiling here is cadence. Between the Summer Clearance run (July 14–18) and two Nike Deal Days blasts in a row, this subscriber is now at 9+ DICK'S promos in six days. The second Nike Deal Days send needed to feel like a finale — "ends at midnight," a countdown, a last-chance hook. Without that, it's indistinguishable from the prior send and unlikely to produce incremental lift.

## What's working

- Nike Deal Days hero is the dominant visual; the 30% off number is legible above the fold
- SHOP NOW button is clear and high-contrast — no hunting for the CTA
- Email renders cleanly with no visible broken images or layout issues
- Multiple price signals across the scroll (30%, 40%, 50%, $100-under) that can catch different intent levels
- DICK'S brand voice is consistent throughout — no mismatched tone or off-brand copy

## What's weak

- 🚨 alarm emoji is a known spam-adjacent signal and a tone mismatch for a brand doing serious athletic retail — the July 19 subject was stronger without it
- No discount percentage in the subject line; yesterday's "Up to 30% OFF" in the subject did the job this subject punts on
- Six competing content blocks (Nike, walking apparel, Callaway, bikes, cardio, Summer Shop) dilute the stated Nike Deal Days purpose
- No expiration date or countdown anywhere — "going on now" is the weakest possible urgency signal
- Repeat send on an identical campaign with no differentiated hook (no "ends tonight," no new product angle, no exclusive member layer)

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the percentage in the subject** — "Up to 30% OFF" as visible copy was the single biggest advantage the July 19 subject had over this one. Restore it.
- 2. **Kill the 🚨, add a deadline** — The alarm emoji signals desperation. Replace it with a real urgency hook: a specific end date/time in the subject. "Nike Deal Days end tonight" beats a siren emoji every time.
- 3. **Make the re-send a closer, not a carbon copy** — If you're hitting the same list twice in 24 hours, the second send must feel like a last call. Add visible countdown language in the hero, a "Deal Days close at midnight" headline, or an exclusive final-day offer.
- 4. **Feature one specific Nike hero product** — "Select Clothing, Shoes and More!" is placeholder-level copy. Swap it for one named product (Air Zoom Pegasus, Dri-FIT, etc.) and the click-through reason becomes concrete.
- 5. **Move non-Nike sections below a hard divider or cut them** — Bikes and Callaway belong in a separate touchpoint. If they must stay, push them below a clear "More to Explore" divider so the Nike narrative completes before the pivot.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Nike Deal Days end tonight — up to 30% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last chance: 30% off Nike shoes, clothes + more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Kicks, hoodies, and training gear at Deal Days pricing. Ends at midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Nike running, training, and lifestyle gear — final hours at 30% off.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods: Nike Deal Days

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound (table-based layout, Outlook VML conditionals, responsive breakpoints) but ships with **invalid JSON-LD and multiple unfilled template placeholders** in its structured data block. The remaining HTML is truncated, so compliance and UTM sections are partial.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Tracking domain — single point of failure.**
All links and the open-pixel route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com` (Acoustic/Campaign ESP). A domain-level DNS or SSL failure kills every trackable link simultaneously. No redundancy observed.

**No visible UTM params in the tracked URLs.**
Example link:
```
https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610000019f7f12809f9f4d88f4bbc782e8/04fb9c3a...?__dU__=v0G4RBKTXg...
```
The `__dU__` token is a recipient identifier, not a UTM parameter. If the redirect target doesn't append `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, GA4 will attribute these sessions as direct/none. Verify server-side redirect appends UTMs.

**`favicon.ico` link is dead weight.**
`<link rel="icon" href="https://www.dickssportinggoods.com/home/assets/icons/dsg/favicon.ico"/>` — no email client renders this. Minor payload bloat, no functional harm.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Outer wrapper `<table>` missing `role="presentation"`.**
```html
<table class="wrapper" width="600" align="center" style="...">
```
Child tables correctly have `role="presentation"`, but the outermost table does not. Screen readers (NVDA + Thunderbird, VoiceOver + Apple Mail) may announce it as a data table with row/column semantics.

**Redundant CSS declaration on `body`.**
```css
body{font-family:Archivo,Arial,sans-serif;font-family:Archivo,Arial,sans-serif!important}
```
The non-`!important` declaration is immediately overridden by the `!important` one in the same rule. One line should be removed.

**Web font served from `fonts.gstatic.com` (Archivo v7).**
`v7` is a pinned version path — Google Fonts updates these; the pin may drift from the canonical file. More critically, Archivo will only render in Apple Mail, iOS Mail, and Android Gmail. Outlook, Gmail web, and Yahoo fall back to Arial — acceptable, but worth confirming the fallback renders as intended.

**`img` alt attribute not verifiable.**
The logo `<img id="logo" src="...">` is truncated in the provided source. Cannot confirm presence of `alt` text. This must be verified in the full source; missing `alt` on the logo fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.1.1 and breaks screen-reader identification of the sender.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Invalid JSON in the `<script type="application/ld+json">` block — this is the most critical technical defect.**

JSON does not support comments. The block contains two JavaScript-style comments:
```json
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
  // Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
  "@context": "http://schema.org/",
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": ""
}
```
`JSON.parse()` will throw on any `//` token. Gmail's structured data parser will silently reject the **entire block**, meaning no Deal/Promotion annotations appear in the Gmail Promotions tab. These are developer instructions that were never stripped before send.

**Unfilled template fields in JSON-LD.**
Within the same block:

| Field | Value | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| `DiscountOffer.description` | `""` | Empty |
| `DiscountOffer.discountCode` | `""` | Empty |
| `DiscountOffer.availabilityEnds` | `""` | Empty |
| `PromotionCard.image` | `""` | Empty |
| `DiscountOffer.availabilityStarts` | `"2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"` | Hardcoded 2020 placeholder — 6 years stale |

Even if the comment syntax were fixed, these empty/stale values would prevent Gmail from rendering a valid promotion card.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

The footer (physical mailing address, unsubscribe link) is cut off. Cannot confirm CAN-SPAM compliance from the provided source. These elements must be verified in the full HTML.

`DSG@e.dcsg.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain — standard practice. DKIM, SPF, and DMARC alignment on `e.dcsg.com` should be validated with `dig TXT e.dcsg.com` and a header inspection of a received copy. Not verifiable from HTML source alone.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM attribution risk (see §2).**
If `app.dickssportinggoods.com` redirects do not append UTM parameters to destination URLs, Nike Deal Days traffic will be misattributed in GA4. Check a live link through the redirect chain:
```
curl -I "https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/..."
```
The `Location:` header should include `utm_source=email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=...`.

**Landing page alignment cannot be confirmed** — destination URLs are behind opaque redirects in the truncated source.
