# This ends tomorrow! Extra 30% off select clearance is going fast
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- DICK'S lands a structurally cleaner email than most of what they've sent this week. The orange hero communicates "Extra 30% Off" instantly, the "ends tomorrow" subject line adds a genuine deadline hook, and the category navigation (Mens / Womens / Kids / Footwear) gives browsers a clear next step. On its own merits, this would be a solid mid-week promotional send.
- The problem is context. This is the eighth or ninth promotional blast in roughly four days from the same sender. The "Extra 30%" framing is the strongest creative pivot the brand has made since the July 19 soccer email — layering a new offer on top of existing clearance is actually meaningful — but that nuance is almost certainly lost on a fatigued subscriber. The phrase "going fast" in the subject joins "ends tomorrow" as urgency language that has been deployed so many times this week it no longer registers as credible.
- The body compounds this by stacking three separate discount levels — Extra 30% clearance, 50% Flash Sale, and 30% Nike Deal Days — into a single email. The recycled Nike section, which scored identically here to the July 20 send, is a tell: this email was assembled from parts. The result is a clear hero offer diluted by two competing campaigns that needed their own sends.

## What's working

- **Orange hero is hard to miss.** "EXTRA 30% OFF" in oversized type against the brand's signature orange delivers the offer at a glance with zero ambiguity.
- **"Ends tomorrow" is the sharpest subject line DICK'S has written all week.** Adding "going fast" is redundant, but the core deadline framing is the most credible urgency signal since July 19.
- **Category tabs (Mens / Womens / Kids / Footwear) are well-placed.** They convert a browse intent into a click path directly below the hero, which is smart UX for a clearance email.
- **"Extra 30% off" is an additive framing.** Positioning this as an additional layer on top of existing clearance is a genuinely better offer than "up to X% off" — it implies the subscriber is already getting a deal, and now more.

## What's weak

- **Three discount levels, three CTAs, one email.** Extra 30% clearance + 50% Flash Sale + 30% Nike Deal Days all compete in the same scroll — the offer hierarchy collapses after the hero.
- **Nike Deal Days is visibly recycled.** Subscribers who opened the July 20 and July 21 Nike emails will recognize this section immediately, undermining any urgency the subject line built.
- **"Going fast" adds no information.** After "ends tomorrow," it reads as desperation copy rather than scarcity signal.
- **No specific product anchor.** The hero gives a category (select clearance) with no item to covet — a single featured product at a stated price would have converted browsers into buyers more effectively.
- **Cadence has poisoned the well.** "Ends tomorrow" in a subject line from a brand that has emailed daily all week reads as the fifth boy who cried wolf this month.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Strip the email to one offer.** The Extra 30% clearance deadline is the lead — ship a single-focus email with the hero, one category grid, and one CTA. Drop the Flash Sale and Nike carryover entirely; those can live in tomorrow's "last chance" send.
- 2. **Name a product category or hero item in the subject.** "Extra 30% off Nike Running ends tonight" is more clickable than a generic urgency line.
- 3. **Replace "SHOP NOW" with offer-specific CTA copy.** "Shop Extra 30% Off" or "Grab My 30% Off" creates a clearer commitment than a generic verb.
- 4. **Add one aspirational product image in the hero.** A single shoe or apparel item at original vs. clearance+extra price turns an abstract percentage into a concrete savings story.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 30% off clearance ends tomorrow — shop now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your extra 30% off expires at midnight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stacked savings on Nike, Adidas, and more — but only until midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Final hours: shoes, apparel, and gear already on clearance, now 30% more off.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**MSO conditional comment version mismatch**
`<head>` uses `<!--[if (gte mso 9)|(IE)]>` but the body table conditional uses `<!--[if (gte mso)|(IE)]>` (no version floor). Inconsistent guards; Outlook 2003 could pick up the body conditional unexpectedly.

**`border-collapse:separate` on all tables**
Every `<table>` uses `border-collapse:separate; border-spacing:0`. This is non-standard for email — `collapse` is conventional and `separate+0` achieves the same visually but can produce 1px ghost gaps in Outlook 2016+ under specific DPI scaling. Not currently breaking but is technical debt.

**Logo `<img>` `src` URL is truncated in the provided source**
```
src="https://s7d6.scene7.com/is/image/DSGAEMSites/brand-logo--dks-2?wid=248&hei=…"
```
The `hei` parameter is cut mid-value. If this reflects the actual sent HTML (not just audit truncation), the logo renders via Scene7's default height, which may differ from the intended dimension. Requires confirmation against the live send.

**Web font version is old**
`Archivo v7` is loaded from `fonts.gstatic.com`. The current version is v19+. Older versions may have different character coverage but this is low risk for Latin-only content.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**JSON-LD block contains JavaScript-style comments — invalid JSON**

```javascript
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
  // Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
  "@context": "http://schema.org/",
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": ""
}
```
`//` comments are not valid JSON. Any client or deliverability tool that parses `<script type="application/ld+json">` will throw a SyntaxError and discard the entire structured data block. This also affects Gmail's Promotions Tab deal card rendering.

**CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe**: Source is truncated — cannot confirm presence. Must be verified in full source; their absence would be a CAN-SPAM violation.

**Authentication headers (SPF/DKIM/DMARC)**: Not evaluable from HTML source alone. Sending domain `e.dcsg.com` should be checked via MX Toolbox or header inspection of a received copy.
