# Find hot deals in up to 70% off select clearance ☀️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S running their third "up to 70% off" clearance frame in two days — July 8 already had the "Up to 70% OFF Summer Clearance Event" (8/10) and this is landing on July 9, the same day the ScoreCard+ membership pitch went out. Subscribers have now seen some variation of "70% off clearance" on July 5, 6, 7, 8, and now twice on July 9. The creative is the same hero, the same headline structure, the same SHOP NOW grid — just with a softer opener ("Find hot deals in...") that does nothing to differentiate from the prior sends.
- The email itself is competently executed. The 70% hero dominates immediately, the product grid offers genuine category variety (running, golf, MLB gear, basketball hoops), and the On Cloud 8 feature is a real product with a real deal. But the design is working against a cadence problem that no layout can fix: by the time this lands, most subscribers have already decided whether they care about the clearance sale.
- The soft subject ("Find hot deals in up to 70% off") is the wrong call for a repeat send. When fatigue is the problem, you need to earn re-engagement with a new angle — a new hero product, a tighter deadline, or a category-specific lead. This email does none of that.

## What's working

- **70% hero is immediate and dominant** — the offer reads in under a second, no ambiguity about what the email is selling.
- **Category breadth in the grid** — running, golf, MLB, basketball hoops gives the scroll something to find for multiple interest profiles.
- **On Cloud 8 callout is specific** — naming a real product with a specific discount (60% off) is the strongest click signal in the email.
- **Clean render, no visual bugs** — layout holds, no broken images or overlapping text visible.
- **Summer seasonality is accurate** — "Summer Clearance" landing in July is contextually coherent.

## What's weak

- **Cadence is broken** — this is at least the fifth "70% off clearance" send since July 5, and the second email on July 9 alone. Subscriber trust and open rates absorb real damage from this.
- **Subject opener is weak for a repeat send** — "Find hot deals in" is filler; after this many clearance sends, you need urgency or novelty, not a softer invitation.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTAs throughout** — none of the buttons say anything specific. "Shop Men's Running" or "Grab Cloud 8s" would lift click intent; "SHOP NOW" is invisible.
- **No deadline visible** — the July 6 countdown timer was the highest-engagement signal in the recent run (8/10 for a reason); dropping it here removes the main reason to act now vs. later.
- **$20 Off $100 / Text Alerts block disrupts the flow** — the bright-blue app/credit card interstitial and the SMS sign-up feel like in-email ads from a different campaign, not a coherent continuation of the clearance story.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Restore a deadline or add real scarcity** — the July 6 countdown timer drove 8/10 on a nearly identical email. Even "Ends Sunday" in the hero would justify the re-send. Without it, this is just noise.
- 2. **Lead with a hero product, not the blanket headline** — "60% Off On Cloud 8" is a more compelling email than "up to 70% off clearance" for anyone who knows Cloud 8s. Make the specific deal the subject.
- 3. **Fix the CTAs** — every "SHOP NOW" should name the category: "Shop Running," "Shop Golf Balls," "Shop NBA Gear." One extra word per button meaningfully increases click confidence.
- 4. **Cut the $20 Off / Text Alerts block** — bury it in the footer or save it for a standalone send. It kills the clearance momentum and reads as a money-grab mid-scroll.
- 5. **Rest the 70% frame** — if the team needs to send again, force a different angle (new arrivals, buy-more-save-more lead, or a persona-specific "your gear is on clearance" frame).

- **Preheader Alt B:** `Running shoes, golf balls, basketball hoops — clearance prices drop now.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods Clearance Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email's JSON-LD structured data block contains invalid syntax that will silently fail across all parsers, and the Gmail Promotions metadata fields are entirely unpopulated. No standard UTM parameters are present on visible tracking links.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**No UTM parameters on tracked links.** The logo href (and likely all CTAs) routes through the ESP redirect at `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610000019f478ac6fbbeec9ff4bbcfb648/...` with only an ESP-internal `__dU__` subscriber ID parameter. Standard analytics parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content`) are absent from the redirect destination, meaning GA4/Adobe Analytics will attribute this traffic as direct, not email.

**Open pixel placement.** The 1×1 tracking pixel at `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019f478ac6fbbeec9ff4bbcfb648/...` is embedded in the same `<td id="ssl">` as the preheader text. Functional, but the pixel and preheader sharing a cell is non-standard and could affect pixel load sequencing in clipped emails.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed MSO conditional CSS.** The Outlook/IE conditional `<style>` block closes the `div` rule with a stray double-quote inside the declaration:
```css
div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
```
The `"` before `}` is invalid CSS. IE-mode parsers may silently discard the entire rule or exhibit unpredictable cascade behavior in Outlook desktop.

**Outer table fixed-width attribute.** `<table class="wrapper" width="600" ...>` sets a hard pixel width via HTML attribute. The inline style's `max-width:600px; width:100%` overrides this in modern clients, but the attribute wins in Outlook 2016 and older, preventing fluid reflow and potentially causing horizontal scroll on small-screen Windows Mail.

**Logo `<img>` truncated in source** — alt text could not be verified. If alt is empty or absent on the primary brand logo, it will render as a broken-image box in image-blocked clients with no fallback.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**No broken merge tokens visible** in the provided source truncation. However, the preheader renders static promotional copy ("Get $20 off $100+") with no recipient personalization — not an error, just noted as confirmed.

**JSON-LD template fields never populated:**
- `"description": ""` — empty string
- `"discountCode": ""` — empty string  
- `"availabilityEnds": ""` — empty string
- `"image": ""` — empty string

These were clearly intended to be filled at send time and were not.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**No UTM parameters** (confirmed above in §2). Campaign performance will be unattributable in web analytics beyond the ESP's own click reporting.

**Stale `availabilityStarts` date in JSON-LD:** `"2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"` — a six-year-old placeholder from the template's original creation. This date will be rendered by Gmail's Promotions tab if the block were otherwise valid, but it signals this field has never been updated.

**Subject line / offer mismatch with JSON-LD:** Subject advertises "up to 70% off select clearance" but `DiscountOffer.description` and `DiscountOffer.discountCode` are both empty strings — no structured discount data is surfaced to Gmail Promotions tab or search indexing.
