# Shop up to 50% off today 🙌
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-10**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S back on the "up to 50% off" hero, but this time paired with a genuine weekly-digest sprawl rather than a single flash-sale focus — beach ball pool imagery, "This Week's Deals," then a full stack of nine-plus sub-offers (Epic Shoe Sale, DSG apparel, adidas World Cup sets, Top Flite golf, MLB gear, life vests, back-to-school, and a product picks grid). It reads as summer-clearance-meets-back-to-school, which is seasonally on-point for August 10, but the hero itself is generic — "up to 50% off" with no category attached is the same beat the brand has run all week (8/7, 8/8 twice), just without this send's countdown clock or "ends tonight" urgency that scored the 8/8 send an 8.00.
- The pool/beach-ball hero art is a mismatch for a mid-August email that's actually selling shoes, golf sets, MLB gear and back-to-school — it reads as generic summer stock art rather than tied to any specific offer, which drags visual relevance down versus the 8/9 "Epic Shoe Sale" single-focus send.
- Overall this is a mid-pack digest send: broad reach, no render bugs, honest-looking offers, but no urgency mechanic and a hero that undersells the specificity in the deals below it.

## What's working

- No render bugs — every product image, badge, and button renders cleanly with clear text.
- Clear top-of-fold value prop: "Up to 50% Off" in a bold, legible banner with a single green "SHOP NOW" CTA right below it.
- Good category navigation strip (Men's/Women's/Kids'/Shoes/Team Sports/Back-to-School) gives multiple entry points immediately after the hero.
- Loyalty/member context visible up top — ScoreCard rewards line and "Order Today, Same-Day Pickup" messaging.
- Bottom product grid (Nike backpack, adidas Samba, socks, HOKA Clifton, kids' Air Force 1) gives concrete, browsable picks with visible pricing categories/names.

## What's weak

- The hero's "Up to 50% Off" has no category or product tied to it — no urgency, no expiration date, no specific department, so it's the weakest hero of the week versus 8/8's countdown-clock version.
- Beach ball / pool imagery in the hero doesn't match anything actually being sold (shoes, golf, MLB gear, back-to-school) — feels like recycled seasonal stock art bolted onto this week's deals.
- This is now the sixth "up to X% off" digest-format send from DICK'S this week with near-identical structure (hero + long stacked offer list) — cadence fatigue is real for a daily subscriber.
- No urgency or deadline visible anywhere in the hero or "This Week's Deals" block, unlike the higher-scoring 8/7 and 8/8 sends that led with "ends tonight" / countdown clocks.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace the pool/beach-ball hero art with the Epic Shoe Sale or back-to-school imagery already used lower in the email — tie the "50% Off" headline to an actual visible product category instead of generic stock art.
- 2. Add a deadline or countdown to the hero (this brand has proven countdown clocks lift scores — 8/8 "Time's running out" hit 8.00 versus this week's plain sends at 6-7).
- 3. Cut the digest down or reorder it — lead with back-to-school given the calendar date (8/10), since that's the most timely category and it's currently buried two-thirds down the email.
- 4. Vary the subject/hero formula from tomorrow's send — six near-identical "up to X% off" emails in a row risks list fatigue; consider a single-category focus send next to break the pattern.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Back-to-school essentials: up to 50% off starts now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your shoe & gear refresh — up to 50% off ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns on shoes, DSG apparel, and back-to-school picks — while supplies last.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Same-day pickup available. Shop this week's biggest markdowns before they're gone.`

Email uses standard Epsilon Harmony/AEM ESP tooling with Salesforce Marketing Cloud-style tracked links (`app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...`, `/O/...`, `/U/...`). Automated QA passed 43% of checks, surfacing one broken link, missing unsubscribe headers, and 10 images without alt text.

- 10 images missing `alt` text, including all major promotional/content images (`dsgclothing3.jpg`, `worldcupcollection.jpg`, `topflite3.png`, `mlb.jpg`, `vests2.jpg`) and 5 footer utility icons (SMS, ScoreCard/SportsMatter, favorite, location icons). - MSO/Outlook conditional comments and `@media screen` font-face/mobile blocks are present and well-formed; no malformed conditional tags detected in the visible source.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No merge tags (e.g., `%%FirstName%%`, `{{...}}`) or unresolved personalization placeholders detected in the visible HTML.
- The embedded `application/ld+json` schema block contains empty string values for `"description"`, `"discountCode"`, `"availabilityEnds"`, and `"image"` (PromotionCard) — these appear to be unfilled ESP template placeholders left blank rather than broken merge fields, but they render an incomplete structured-data block to any consumer (e.g., Gmail annotations) parsing it.
- Note: the JSON-LD block also contains a JS-style `//` comment inside a `<script type="application/ld+json">` tag ("GIF & WEBP images are not supported...", "DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!") — this is invalid JSON and will cause the structured-data block to fail parsing entirely in any client that attempts to consume it.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Unsubscribe link is broken** (see §2) — a CAN-SPAM violation risk if the unsubscribe mechanism does not actually function for recipients.
- **List-Unsubscribe header not found** — no mailto:/https: header-based unsubscribe present or captured.

- **Authentication-Results header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status could not be verified via the relay; cannot confirm sender authentication compliance from available data.
- These header findings are flagged WARN because they may be an artifact of the AgentMail relay not capturing/forwarding headers rather than the sender omitting them — recommend re-verifying against raw SMTP headers at the source before treating as a confirmed compliance gap.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Links use the ESP's own tracked-redirect domain (`app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/.../__dU__=...`) rather than direct UTM-tagged URLs to `dickssportinggoods.com`, so UTM parameter alignment cannot be verified from the HTML alone — final landing URLs are only resolved after redirect.
- Given the unsubscribe redirect (§2/§5) terminates in a generic `/pc/Error/Error` page instead of the expected subscription-management page, there is at least one confirmed instance of tracked-redirect → landing-page misalignment; other CTA redirects were not independently verified beyond the QA probe pass.

### 7. Recommendations
1. **Fix the unsubscribe redirect immediately** — `/pc/Error/Error?aspxerrorpath=/pc/subscription` indicates the target subscription-center page is missing or misconfigured; this is both a UX and CAN-SPAM compliance risk.
2. Add `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers (or confirm they exist at the source and are simply being dropped by the relay) to meet Gmail/Yahoo one-click unsubscribe requirements.
3. Add descriptive `alt` text to all 9 non-pixel content/footer images for accessibility and text-only rendering fallback.
4. Remove the `//` comments from the `application/ld+json` block or move them outside the script tag — as written, the structured data will fail to parse.
5. Populate or remove the empty JSON-LD fields (`discountCode`, `description`, `availabilityEnds`, `image`) so the DiscountOffer/PromotionCard schema isn't shipped incomplete.
6. Verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC authentication results directly against raw sending headers, independent of the AgentMail relay capture.
