# ⚽ Get ready for the next match ⚽
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a topical, event-driven email anchored to the USA vs. Australia soccer matchup on Friday, June 18 at 3PM ET. The hook is legitimate: a real match, a real date, and a hero image (Pulisic in the USA #10 kit alongside a women's national team player) that creates immediate category recognition. "THE GAME STARTS HERE" is one of the better headlines DICK'S has written in this recent run — it earns its hero real estate without defaulting to a discount banner.
- The structural problem is that it's all atmosphere, no incentive. Six of the last seven sends from this sender led with a specific price hook — 50% off, extra 25% off clearance, countdown clocks. Subscribers have been conditioned. This send asks for a full-price "SHOP NOW" with nothing. For anyone who already owns USA gear (a reasonable assumption for the soccer segment), there's no reason to buy today instead of next week. DICK'S doesn't own the match; they just sell the merch.
- A second problem bleeds in mid-email: a "Get it by Father's Day!" module sitting inside a soccer send. It reads as a copy-paste from the June 16 logistics email and fractures the theme. The FIFA World Cup 2026 product grid buried at the bottom is actually compelling depth — adidas official balls, USA training jerseys — but it's sitting below the fold fighting for attention with a holiday that's already been milked heavily this cycle.

## What's working

- **Event hook is timely and specific:** USA vs. Australia, June 18, 3PM ET is a concrete anchor — the best kind of urgency because DICK'S didn't manufacture it.
- **Hero creative is the strongest in recent memory:** Pulisic + USA Women's kit is recognizable in a half-second. "THE GAME STARTS HERE" is punchy and brand-appropriate without screaming a discount.
- **Product catalog depth is visible:** The lower product grid shows genuine FIFA World Cup 2026 stock (official match balls, training balls, USA jerseys) — convincing to a real soccer shopper, not just a generic "sports stuff" grid.
- **Clean render:** No broken images, no layout collapse, no overlapping text — the email renders exactly as intended.

## What's weak

- **No price hook after a week of conditioning subscribers to expect one:** The brand history going back to June 15 is a parade of discounts. A no-offer send immediately after reads as low-stakes browse fodder.
- **Subject line names neither the sport nor the teams:** "⚽ Get ready for the next match ⚽" could be from any sports brand on any matchday. The USA vs. Australia angle — the actual news peg — is buried in the body.
- **Father's Day module bleeds into a soccer-themed send:** The "Get it by Father's Day!" section mid-email creates a split-theme problem; it was already handled more cleanly in the June 16 deadline send.
- **Every CTA says "SHOP NOW":** Four buttons, identical copy. Even one specific label ("Shop USA Jerseys," "Get the Match Ball") would have outperformed this across the board.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price incentive:** 15% off USA gear, free shipping on orders over $50, or a member exclusive. Without it, this send has no action trigger for a subscriber who isn't already mid-purchase intent.
- 2. **Put the match in the subject:** USA vs. Australia is the actual hook — use it. The current subject throws away the most specific, timely information available.
- 3. **Kill the Father's Day module or spin it into a separate send:** This is a soccer email. The Father's Day section reads like an accidental include from a template that wasn't cleaned out.
- 4. **Rewrite CTAs to be category-specific:** "Shop USA Jerseys," "Get the Official Ball," "Shop Red, White & Blue Shoes" — anything more specific than "SHOP NOW."
- 5. **Promote the FIFA World Cup 2026 product line to a sub-hero slot:** The adidas and Nike FIFA 2026 items in the lower grid are the most compelling reason to click; they're wasted below the fold.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Missing `alt` on tracking pixel:**
```html
<img src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/...' style="display:none; ..."/>
```
The hidden tracking pixel has no `alt=""` attribute. Screen readers may surface the raw URL on some clients. Add `alt=""`.

### 7. Recommendations

**P0 — Fix invalid JSON-LD immediately.**

The `<script type="application/ld+json">` block contains JavaScript single-line comments, which are not valid JSON:

```js
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
  // Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
  "@context": "http://schema.org/",
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": ""
}
```

JSON parsers (including Gmail's schema.org extractor) will reject the entire array on parse failure. This means the Gmail Promotions tab rich preview card **will not render for any recipient.** Strip all `//` comment lines before send.

**P0 — Stale `availabilityStarts` date in schema:**
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```
This is a 6-year-old date, almost certainly a forgotten template default. Update to the actual campaign start datetime or remove the field.

**P1 — Empty `PromotionCard.image` and `DiscountOffer` fields:**
```json
"description": "",
"discountCode": "",
"availabilityEnds": "",
"image": ""
```
Empty strings on required schema fields cause Gmail annotation suppression. Either populate all fields or remove the blocks entirely if no promotion/image applies to this send.

**P2 — Add `alt=""` to tracking pixel** (accessibility hygiene).

**P2 — Switch `border-collapse` to `collapse`** on layout tables to prevent Outlook ghost-gap rendering.

**Verify before send:** Confirm unsubscribe link, physical address, and CAN-SPAM footer are present in the full HTML. Spot-check one Epsilon redirect to confirm UTM params (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are present on the final destination URL.
