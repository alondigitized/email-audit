# One. Day. Only! $20 off $100+ in-app exclusive is HERE 📍
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- This is the second DICK'S email pushing the identical $20 off $100+ in-app offer on June 22 — the first was "Starting NOW! Score $20 off $100+ in-app (today only!)" that audited at 7/10 earlier today. On its own, the hero is clean and the urgency framing ("One. Day. Only!") is among the sharpest copy DICK'S has deployed this week. The problem is everything below the fold: the email tries to be five different campaigns simultaneously — app exclusive, Deal Days event, 60% off clearance, product picks, cycling shoes, and a Summer Shop. That's not a send, that's a dump.
- The core app-exclusive hook is legitimate and differentiated from the monotonous clearance blasts that dominated the June 14–20 stretch. But stacking it on top of repeat cadence (same day, same offer, same sender) and then padding it with half a dozen unrelated modules destroys focus. A subscriber who opened the earlier "Starting NOW!" send has already seen the offer and already made their decision. This send asks them to reconsider the same deal they just passed on, which rarely converts.
- Verdict: Structurally sound hero, execution undermined by a same-day repeat, content overload, and zero loyalty touchpoint despite ScoreCard being this brand's strongest creative performer over the past week.

## What's working

- "One. Day. Only!" fragmented urgency framing in the subject is punchy and distinct from the generic "don't miss out" language in the clearance blasts.
- App-exclusive positioning is genuinely differentiated — the phone-hero render makes the channel friction legible.
- Deal Days green visual identity is consistent with the June 21–22 brand thread, signaling a real campaign rather than another one-off clearance push.
- The hero offer ($20 off $100+) is above the fold and the dollar threshold is unambiguous.
- Render is clean — no broken images, no overlapping copy, no placeholder text visible.
- This is a same-day repeat of the "Starting NOW!" send: identical offer, identical channel, second email in hours — cadence abuse that conditions subscribers to ignore.
- Five distinct modules compete below the fold (Deal Days, 60% clearance, product picks, cycling shoes, Summer Shop) — none gets enough real estate to close.
- Every CTA reads "SHOP NOW" — no module tells you what you're shopping for, which kills click intent.
- No ScoreCard callout anywhere visible, despite the June 20–21 ScoreCard sends being the highest-performing emails in recent brand history (8/10 each).
- No social proof, reviews, or ratings visible in any of the product modules.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the second same-day send or differentiate it hard.** If you're sending twice on launch day, the second email needs a new angle — deadline countdown, new product reveal, or a ScoreCard-exclusive stacking offer. Repeating the identical offer within hours is inbox training to ignore you.
- 2. **Cut to three modules max.** Hero app exclusive → one featured product category → one secondary deal. Every extra module dilutes conversion on the primary offer.
- 3. **Swap all "SHOP NOW" CTAs for specific labels.** "Shop Men's Athletic Shorts," "Shop Cycling Shoes," "Shop Clearance" — specificity is the click driver.
- 4. **Add a ScoreCard stacking hook.** "ScoreCard members: stack your Rewards on top of $20 off" converts the segment that's already bought-in to the loyalty program.
- 5. **Surface a credible deadline beyond "one day only."** Show the clock — "Offer ends 11:59 PM tonight" — so the urgency is anchored to something real.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Deal Days: your one-day app exclusive is live. No code needed.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 60% off select clearance and new picks from the summer shop`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `ScoreCard members: stack your Rewards on top — today only`

### 1. Technical Summary

Table-based layout is structurally sound with correct Outlook conditional comments and responsive breakpoints. Three confirmed issues require fixing before send: invalid JSON-LD syntax (will silently fail structured data parsing), a stale 2020 date placeholder, and incomplete structured data fields.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**All CTAs route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/` redirect wrapper** — this is the expected Epsilon/Harmony ESP pattern. No bare untracked links observed in the visible source.

**Cannot confirm UTM passthrough** — the redirect chain (`/T/v610000019ef...`) ends at a destination URL that is not visible in the truncated source. If the destination URLs lack `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` parameters after redirect, attribution will be lost. Verify the resolved landing URL manually.

**Tracking pixel uses `display:none`** at `app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019ef...`. This is standard but will be blocked by privacy-oriented clients (Apple Mail Privacy Protection, Hey). Expected behavior; no action needed.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate `font-family` declaration in `<style>`:**
```css
body{font-family:Archivo,Arial,sans-serif;font-family:Archivo,Arial,sans-serif!important}
```
Redundant. The second declaration with `!important` is authoritative; the first can be removed. Low severity.

**Outlook link color override — potential invisibility:**
```css
<!--[if (gte mso 9)|(IE)]><style>a:link { color:#141414; text-decoration:none; }</style>
```
`#141414` matches the body text color. In Outlook 2016/2019/365 on Windows, all linked text will render identically to body copy — no underline, no color distinction. Linked CTAs will be visually indistinguishable from plain text for Outlook desktop users unless those links are inside fully-styled `<td>` button elements with background color.

**`overflow:hidden` + `border-radius` on header table (`#header-non-sc-member`):**
```html
style="border-radius:8px 8px 0px 0px; overflow:hidden;"
```
Outlook ignores both properties. The rounded corners and clipped overflow will silently degrade to square corners with potentially visible overflow in Outlook — verify against Litmus/Email on Acid Outlook renders.

**Logo `<img>` src is truncated in the provided source** — cannot confirm `alt` attribute presence. All images must carry `alt` text for screen readers and image-off environments.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Table `id="header-non-sc-member"`** signals conditional block logic (ScoreCard member vs. non-member variant). No unfired merge tokens (`{{`, `%%`, `[%`, etc.) are visible in the provided source. Full template audit required to confirm no tokens leak into the non-member path.

No subscriber-level dynamic fields (`first_name`, reward balance, etc.) are visible in the truncated source, which is consistent with the generic "Deal Days" offer type.

---

### 5. Compliance

**JSON-LD structured data block contains JavaScript-style comments — invalid JSON:**
```json
<script type="application/ld+json">
[{
  ...
  // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
  // Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": ""
}]
```
`//` comments are not valid JSON syntax. Google's structured data parser and Gmail's promotional tab rendering will silently reject this entire block. These are designer instructions that must be stripped before production send.

**Stale `availabilityStarts` date — unfilled template placeholder:**
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```
This is a 6-year-old date, almost certainly a template default that was never updated. Gmail uses this field to display offer cards; a past date may suppress the card or display stale information.

**Empty required structured data fields:**
```json
"description": "",
"discountCode": "",
"availabilityEnds": "",
"image": ""
```
All four fields are empty strings. Gmail's promotional offer annotation requires `description`, `discountCode`, and `availabilityEnds` to be populated to render an offer card. The `PromotionCard` `image` field is also empty, suppressing the visual card.

**CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link** — not visible in the truncated source. These are required by law and must be present in the footer. Flag for confirmation that they exist below the fold.
