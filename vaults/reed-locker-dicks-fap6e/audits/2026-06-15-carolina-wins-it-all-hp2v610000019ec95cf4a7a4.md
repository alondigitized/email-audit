# Carolina wins it all 🏒🏆✨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S running the exact same reactive-championship template it used on June 14 for the Knicks ("New York wins it all 🏀🏆✨", scored 8/10). Carolina wins the Stanley Cup, DSG hits the inbox within roughly 24 hours with official gear — that's the right playbook. The formula works because the emotional hook is real, timing is tight, and the product (officially licensed championship merch) has genuine day-one demand.
- The wrinkle here is the dual-message execution: the email tries to be both a championship celebration AND a Father's Day push simultaneously. The banner up top ("Get it by Father's Day! Free Same-Day Delivery...") and a dedicated Father's Day module mid-scroll pull focus away from the pure "Congrats, Carolina!" moment. The Knicks send was cleaner — one message, one emotion. Here the Father's Day add-on muddies what should be an urgent, celebratory single-minded email.
- No targeting segmentation is visible — this appears to be going to the full DSG subscriber file, not just Carolina-region subscribers or confirmed hockey buyers. That's a real missed opportunity given how location-specific Stanley Cup fandom is.

## What's working

- **Reactive timing is right.** Championship email arriving while confetti is still falling; the hero image with trophy and "CONGRATS, CAROLINA!" text captures the moment well.
- **Hero visual hierarchy is clean.** Dark celebratory background + large "CONGRATS, CAROLINA!" text makes the occasion unmistakable above the fold.
- **Father's Day delivery urgency is additive.** The same-day delivery / store pickup module adds a real purchase motivation: buy this as a gift today. That's a net positive signal even if it competes with the hero.
- **Two product lanes are clear.** "NHL® Champs Clothing" and "More NHL® Essentials" tiles give the reader a natural fork without confusion.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no layout failures visible.

## What's weak

- **Dual-message dilution.** The Father's Day module and top banner split the emotional focus — championship celebration and gift-shopping don't resolve into a single action.
- **Generic CTAs throughout.** Every button reads "SHOP NOW" or "DETAILS" — no specificity (no "Shop Hurricanes Gear," no "Get the Champion Tee").
- **No price or offer anchor on the championship product.** Free shipping on $75+ is the only incentive; no discount, no limited availability signal, nothing to create urgency on the gear itself.
- **No geo/segmentation signal.** Sending a Carolina-specific championship email to the national file without any nod to "shop your local store" or "ship to NC" leaves non-Hurricanes fans cold.
- **App download module still here.** At this point in the email, after the Father's Day push, the app download row burns attention that should be a second CTA to the championship shop.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill or compress the Father's Day module to a banner strip only.** Either lead with the championship email clean (like the Knicks send) or make Father's Day the primary frame with a "champ gift" angle. Half-and-half hurts both.
- 2. **Make CTAs championship-specific.** Replace every "SHOP NOW" under the Hurricanes modules with "Shop Hurricanes Gear" — one extra word, significant lift on intent clarity.
- 3. **Add a limited-availability or edition signal.** "Official Championship Edition — ships while supplies last" costs nothing and adds real urgency to an authenticated fan purchase.
- 4. **Geo-segment or at minimum personalize the hero.** A Carolina ZIP / purchase history split would let the national file get a softer "celebrate the champs" message while Carolinas fans get the full push.
- 5. **Remove the app download module** from this send entirely. It's noise in a time-sensitive reactive email.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Congrats, Carolina — shop official Champs gear now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Stanley Cup® Champions: get the gear before it sells out`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Official Hurricanes Championship merch — ships same day, perfect Father's Day gift`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Celebrate the Cup — free same-day delivery on $75+ through Sunday`

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Critical: Malformed and unpopulated JSON-LD block.**

The `<script type="application/ld+json">` block has three compounding issues that will cause Gmail's structured data parser to reject the entire block:

**Issue A — JavaScript-style comments inside JSON (invalid JSON syntax):**
```json
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported...
  // Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": ""
}
```
JSON does not support `//` comments. This is a syntax error; the entire array will fail to parse.

**Issue B — Stale `availabilityStarts` date (6+ years old):**
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```
Should reflect the current promotion window (send date: 2026-06-17).

**Issue C — Empty required fields across all three objects:**
```json
"description": "",
"discountCode": "",
"availabilityEnds": "",
"image": ""
```
These were never populated from the template. The `PromotionCard.image` field still has a designer instruction comment confirming it was intentionally left for manual fill and was missed.

**Net effect:** Gmail will not render any inbox annotation (promotional badge, deal highlight) for this email. No user-facing harm, but the structured data feature provides no value as sent.
