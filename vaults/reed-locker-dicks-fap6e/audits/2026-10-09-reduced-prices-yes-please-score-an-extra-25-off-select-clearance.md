# Reduced prices? Yes, please! Score an EXTRA 25% off select clearance >>>
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-09**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, loud clearance send. The hero is a giant "EXTRA 25% OFF" in white on deep red, and a green Shop Now button sits right below it. The offer is easy to find and easy to understand. It's also the cleanest offer DICK'S has sent this week. Last week's sends were all "up to 50% off" or "up to 70% off", and this is a flat extra 25% on top of clearance.
- The body is a long scroll of category tiles: running shoes, a DSG hoodie, baseball gloves, FootJoy golf shoes, fan gear, drinkware, and a "Best Deals of the Week" 50% tile. Each tile has its own "Up to 40% off" or "$30 off" message. That muddies the "extra 25%" story. After the hero, the email becomes a generic catalog, and the specific sale hoodie ($14.98) is the only concrete product price.
- The email looks consistent with DICK'S voice and is easy to scan. It's another day of discount stacking in a week of near-daily promo sends (10/6 and 10/7 clearance, 10/7 and 10/8 "up to 50%"). The "extra 25%" angle is a real change, but the follow-through is generic.

## What's working

- The hero "EXTRA 25% OFF" is big, high-contrast and reads instantly.
- The green Shop Now button is directly under the hero copy, so the CTA is unambiguous.
- "Limited Time" and the "through 10/10/26" fine print add credible urgency.
- The $14.98 DSG hoodie is a concrete, priced product.
- The category pill row (Men's, Women's, Kids', $25 and Under, Fan Shop, All Sale) gives quick navigation.

## What's weak

- The "extra 25%" message is diluted by about seven tiles with different "up to 40%" or "$30 off" offers.
- The deadline and exclusions are in small grey fine print below the hero. A reader has to hunt for "through 10/10".
- Most tiles carry a generic grey "Shop Now" button, which makes every CTA look the same.
- The 50% off "Best Deals of the Week" tile at the bottom conflicts with the clearance-focused hero.
- The email is very long, and the product picks at the end have no prices or ratings.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the deadline ("Ends 10/10") in the hero or on a countdown, not only in the fine print.
- 2. Cut the body to three tiles that all support the "extra 25% off clearance" story, each with a real before/after price.
- 3. Show prices, and ideally a "was/now" figure, on the product picks at the bottom.
- 4. Make the hero CTA specific, such as "Shop Clearance," and differentiate it from the grey tile buttons.
- 5. Drop the "up to 50% off" tile so the offer stays single-minded.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Clearance just got cheaper: extra 25% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Running shoes, hoodies, golf and more. Today's markdowns, then 25% off on top.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Ends 10/10. Same-day pickup on thousands of clearance styles.`

## Technical Audit

1. **Technical Summary**
The email is a well-formed table-based HTML build with a preheader, MSO conditionals, a `lang="en"` attribute and click tracking through `data.em.dcsg.com`. Its real defects are the 9-character plain-text part and 10 images with no alt text. The missing header findings come from the relay and can't be confirmed from the source.

2. **Link & Tracking Issues**
- All links I could see go through the ESP redirector `https://data.em.dcsg.com/ee/v1/mclick?params=b.v1...`. That is the expected tracking pattern, and the destination can't be read from the source.
- The QA probe covered 12 material links and skipped 51. The skipped links were mostly footer, utility and social links, and they are unverified.
- The tracking pixel `https://data.em.dcsg.com/ee/v1/open?params=...` has no alt attribute. This is minor, since the pixel is invisible.
- The HTML source was truncated, so I can't confirm the destinations or UTM parameters.

3. **Rendering & Accessibility**
- **Missing alt text on 10 content and icon images (QA WARN).** These are the four product or category images `runningshoes1009stwd2`, `classicfleece1009stwd2`, `softballbaseball1009stwd2` and `jersey2014stwd4pm`. The others are `banner4` and the icons `icon-vc-sms-1`, `icon-vc-sportsmatter`, `icon-vc-bpg-1`, `icon-vc-favorite-1` and `icon-location-2025-1`. With images blocked, the email loses its offer and category labels. Screen readers will announce the filenames or nothing. I can't tell from the truncated source whether the surrounding links carry text.
- **Plain-text fallback (QA FAIL).** The text part is 9 characters, so it is effectively empty. Clients that can't render HTML, and text-only readers, get no content.
- The `.wrapper` table has `width="600"` and `style="width:100%; max-width:600px"`. It uses `role="presentation"` and has a viewport meta tag, so it should scale on mobile.
- The preheader div uses `display:none; max-height:0; mso-hide:all`, which is standard. The visible `id="ssl"` text "Get $20 off $100+ when you sign up for Text Alerts**" sits before the hidden spacer. Its td has `font-size:0; line-height:0; height:0`, so it may not display, but some clients will still use it as preview text. - The Archivo web font falls back to `Arial,sans-serif`, which is fine. The `<link rel="icon">` in the head has no effect in email clients.
- The body carries the editor artifacts `class="is-html-mode" data-contenteditable="true"`. They are harmless but leftover.

4. **Personalization & Merge Tokens**
No issues found. The visible source contains no unresolved merge tokens such as `{{...}}`, `%%...%%` or `[FIRST_NAME]`. The truncated portion is unchecked.

The QA probe didn't report the final landing URLs or any redirect-chain failures, so the offer ("EXTRA 25% off select clearance") can't be matched to a landing page.
