# ☀️ Up to 50% off DEALS for summer ☀️ Shop our EPIC Father's Day Sale! 
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S Father's Day grand-finale blast — a kitchen-sink compilation that stacks at least four distinct value props (50% off summer essentials, Nike Golf, mountain bikes, the now-familiar Extra 25% Off clearance module, and the ScoreCard $10 back) into a single long scroll. The hero lands correctly: "Father's Day Savings Event / Up to 50% Off Summer Essentials" with a clear green CTA is above the fold and reads fast. But almost everything below the fold is recycled creative — the dark-red "EXTRA 25% OFF" clearance block is a straight lift from the June 16 and 17 clearance sends, and the $10 ScoreCard module is the same asset from June 15. A subscriber who has been opening DICK'S this week has already seen both of those units.
- The structural problem is dilution. Four competing offers and six-plus department modules fight for one click. The June 16 Father's Day deadline email was tighter and sharper — it had a countdown timer and a single logical throughline. This one abandons the urgency mechanism entirely, which is a meaningful step backward one day before the holiday.
- **Judgement:** Serviceable volume send, but a loyal subscriber feels the diminishing returns of a week that produced 6+ near-identical blasts. The hero is the only truly new creative in the email.

## What's working

- Hero module is clean and on-brand: golfer imagery + "Father's Day Savings Event" headline + "Up to 50% Off Summer Essentials" reads instantly without decoding.
- Green primary CTA button is high-contrast and visible directly under the hero — no hunting for the action.
- Nike Golf and mountain bike modules are genuinely Father's Day-adjacent and department-specific, which is better targeting than a generic grid.
- ScoreCard $10 bonus is carried over from the June 15 loyalty email and correctly re-surfaces it for last-push buyers — good for members who missed the first send.
- App download module at the bottom is low-friction and appropriate placement (doesn't interrupt the main conversion flow).

## What's weak

- The June 16/17 clearance "EXTRA 25% OFF" block is copy-pasted wholesale — a subscriber who opened those emails is seeing it for the third time in three days. Repetition kills urgency.
- No countdown clock. The June 16 Father's Day email had a live timer ("Final Hours: 07|59|48") and this send — sent closer to the actual holiday — drops it. That's the single most impactful urgency lever and it's gone.
- Subject line is over-long (~70 chars), uses "EPIC" and "DEALS" in caps, and runs two ☀️ emojis on either side. This hits multiple soft-spam-signal thresholds and will be clipped on most mobile inboxes.
- Too many competing offers with no forced hierarchy: 50% off, 25% off clearance, $10 ScoreCard, and app download are all present — the recipient doesn't know what the email "is about."
- No explicit deadline visible in the body — no "ends Sunday" or date-stamp. Father's Day was the credible anchor and it's never named as a hard cutoff.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Restore the countdown clock.** The June 16 send proved the format. A "Father's Day ends in X hours" timer on the hero converts better than any copy change. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Cut the clearance module.** It's been in two emails already this week. Swap it for a gift-guide angle (top-5 picks under $75) to add editorial value instead of repeating the same promo.
- 3. **Tighten the subject.** Two emojis + "EPIC" + "DEALS" in caps reads as spray-and-pray. A cleaner line beats it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Father's Day ends Sunday — up to 50% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last chance: 50% off Summer Essentials for Dad`
- 4. **Add an explicit deadline to the hero copy.** "Sale ends midnight Sunday" beneath the 50% off headline gives the email a reason to act now.
- 5. **Write a preheader that isn't competing with the subject.** The current preheader likely leaks the clearance or ScoreCard line — it should reinforce the hero's Father's Day urgency.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sale ends Sunday — grab it before it's gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Gifts for Dad + ScoreCard members earn $10 back`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard Epsilon/Harmony ESP platform with table-based layout and MSO conditionals correctly applied. Several high-severity issues exist in the JSON-LD structured data block (invalid syntax, stale dates, unfilled placeholders) and a likely dev-path asset URL is live in production.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Tracking domain:** All links route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/` with a subscriber token embedded as `__dU__=`. This is ESP-native click tracking (Epsilon/Responsys pattern) — functioning as expected.

**Dev-path image asset (HIGH):** The logo `src` begins:
```
https://dsg.images.harmony.epsilon.com/_phoenix/_dev/AEM/img/headers/brand-logo--dk…
```
The `/_dev/` path segment in a production send is a red flag. If this resolves to a staging CDN origin rather than production, the asset could be blocked, rate-limited, or removed without notice.

**Open pixel:** The SSL/open-tracking beacon at `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019eda4847b09f546b6e966f4758/…` is rendered via a hidden `<img>` with `display:none` — standard pattern, no issues beyond the dev-path concern above.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing `alt` on logo (MEDIUM):** The `<img id="logo">` element is truncated in the source but shows no `alt` attribute in the visible markup. If absent, image-off rendering (common in Outlook) shows a broken image with no fallback text.

**JSON-LD contains inline JS comments — invalid JSON (HIGH):**
```json
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported...
  "@context": "http://schema.org/",
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": ""
}
```
`//` comments are not valid JSON. Gmail's structured data parser will reject the entire `<script type="application/ld+json">` block, silently dropping any Gmail Promotions tab annotation.

**MSO/IE conditional comments:** Present and correctly structured. `border-collapse`, `a:link` overrides, and `o:PixelsPerInch` all applied. No issues.

**Responsive breakpoint:** `@media screen and (max-width:480px)` — slightly narrow; 600px is the more common email breakpoint. This may clip layout on devices 480–600px wide before the mobile overrides kick in.

**`role="presentation"` on layout tables:** Correctly applied throughout visible markup.

**`<html lang="en">`:** Present. `<meta charset="UTF-8">`: present.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**No visible personalization tokens** in the rendered subject line or visible body. The subject is fully static: `☀️ Up to 50% off DEALS for summer ☀️ Shop our EPIC Father's Day Sale!`

**Segmentation ID present:** `id="header-non-sc-member"` on the header table indicates this is a conditional block for non-ScoreCard members. If the ESP is suppressing the ScoreCard variant, confirm the member variant renders the correct alternate block and neither variant is double-rendered.

**Subscriber token:** `__dU__` in click URLs is the encoded subscriber identifier — functioning as expected, not an unfilled merge token.

### 5. Compliance

**Stale JSON-LD date (MEDIUM):**
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```
This is a 6-year-old template default. `availabilityEnds` is empty string `""`. Neither value is valid for the current promotion.

**Unfilled JSON-LD placeholders (MEDIUM):**
```json
"description": "",
"discountCode": "",
"image": ""
```
These are unpopulated template fields. The `DiscountOffer` and `PromotionCard` objects are effectively empty and will not qualify for Gmail Promotions tab rich annotations.

**Non-standard schema.org types:** `"@type": "PromotionCard"` and `"@type": "DiscountOffer"` are not in the official schema.org vocabulary. Gmail supports `EmailMessage`, `DiscountOffer` (via Google extension), and `PromotionCard` (Google proprietary) — but the invalid JSON means none of these are being parsed regardless.

**CAN-SPAM / unsubscribe footer:** Not visible in the truncated source. Cannot confirm presence of physical mailing address or one-click unsubscribe. These are required — verify they exist in the footer section.

**Sending domain:** `e.dcsg.com` (subdomain of `dcsg.com`). SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment should be confirmed for this subdomain. Not verifiable from HTML source alone — check with `dig TXT e.dcsg.com` and MX toolbox header analysis on a received copy.

**Stale HTML comment:** `<!--DKS Email Marketing 2025-->` — minor; year is stale (now 2026).

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**No UTM parameters in visible tracking URLs.** All click links pass through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/` redirects, which presumably append UTM params on redirect resolution. If the redirect does not inject `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, email traffic will surface in GA4 as `(direct) / (none)`.

Verify on a resolved click URL that it appends at minimum:
```
utm_source=email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=fathers_day_sale
```

**Landing page alignment:** Cannot assess without resolved destination URLs (all are behind redirect tokens). Confirm the `☀️ Up to 50% off` claim in the subject maps to the actual discount range on the linked landing pages.
