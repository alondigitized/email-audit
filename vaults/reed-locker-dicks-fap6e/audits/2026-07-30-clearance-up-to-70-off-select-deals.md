# < CLEARANCE: up to 70% OFF select DEALS >
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S at its most kitchen-sink — a red "UP TO 70% OFF" hero followed by roughly a dozen stacked category blocks (clothing/shoes, water bottles, gloves, golf bags, outdoor gear, weighted vests, MLB gear, text-alert signup, and a six-item "must-have gear" grid). It's the same sprawling-catalog approach as the 2026-07-28 "BEST deals of the week" send (9.00/10), just with a clearance headline instead of a weekly-deals one — and it's a genuinely different hero than the paddleboard-at-sunset creative that's been on repeat since 7/24.
- The subject promises "up to 70% OFF," the single biggest number DICK'S has floated in this stretch (previous sends topped out at 40-50%), which is a real differentiator if it's honest. The hero delivers on that promise immediately and cleanly with no scroll needed. Where it falls apart is everything after the fold: the offer dilutes fast into a wall of unrelated categories (golf bags, weighted vests, tumblers, sports bra alerts) with no visual hierarchy telling you which one is actually part of the "70% off clearance," making the top-line number feel more like a subject-line hook than a real cross-cart promise.
- Verdict: strong hero, weak follow-through — the 70% claim isn't substantiated anywhere below the fold, so it reads as bait for a "shop everything" catalog blast rather than a genuine clearance event.

## What's working

- The hero is unambiguous: big red background, bold "UP TO 70% OFF" in white/yellow, no competing elements — the eye can't miss it.
- "Limited Time" badge plus a clear H2 ("Up to 70% Off Summer Clearance Event") reinforces urgency right under the hero.
- Category quick-links (MEN'S / WOMEN'S / KIDS' / $25 AND UNDER / FAN SHOP / ALL SALE) give impatient shoppers a fast path without scrolling the whole email.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, spacing is consistent block to block.
- Green "SHOP NOW" CTA buttons are repeated consistently and are easy to spot at each section.

## What's weak

- The 70% figure never reappears after the hero — every subsequent block (gloves, golf bags, vests, MLB gear) uses its own smaller discount or none at all, so the headline offer is effectively a one-screen tease.
- Way too many category blocks (10+) with no prioritization — golf bags, weighted vests, and cornhole sets sit at the same visual weight as the clearance hero, burying the actual promo.
- "$20 OFF $100+ INSTANTLY" text-alert block competes for attention with the sale itself and reads as a separate ask crammed into an already-long email.
- No loyalty/ScoreCard callout tied to this specific clearance offer, despite ScoreCard being the brand's strongest recent creative beat (7/27 "2X Points" send scored 9.00/10).
- Fine print ("**" and "††" disclaimers, minimum quantities note under "Details") is present but the actual clearance terms (what's excluded, end date) are never shown in the visible content — just implied "Limited Time."

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email roughly in half — keep the 70% hero, the "Up to 50% Off select clothing and shoes" block (the closest match to the headline claim), and 2-3 more directly-discounted blocks; drop the golf bags/vests/outdoor-gear/tumbler filler or move it to a second send.
- 2. Put an end date or countdown directly under "Limited Time" — right now nothing tells the reader when clearance ends, which kills the urgency the badge is trying to create.
- 3. Tie at least one block explicitly to ScoreCard members, since that's the strongest-performing lever this brand has used this month (7/27 send, 9.00/10).
- 4. Move the "$20 off $100+ text alerts" block to the footer — it's a list-growth ask, not a clearance offer, and it dilutes the hero's momentum mid-email.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Summer clearance markdowns on shoes, gear and fan favorites — while sizes last.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New markdowns just dropped across clothing, shoes and outdoor gear.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
Email is built on Epsilon Harmony (app.dickssportinggoods.com / dsg.images.harmony.epsilon.com), renders via table-based HTML with MSO conditionals. Automated QA shows a 43% pass rate driven by a broken unsubscribe link, missing authentication/unsubscribe headers, and widespread missing alt text.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Broken unsubscribe link (confirmed via QA):** The "Unsubscribe" link resolves to `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/U/v610000019fb294593f93fff9369ff0b384/c2b6a0f7bc3c42b30000021ef3a0bd01`, which redirects to `/pc/Error/Error?aspxerrorpath=/pc/subscription` — an error page rather than the preference/unsubscribe form.
- All sampled links route through Epsilon Harmony's tracked-redirect domain (`app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...` and `/O/...`), consistent with standard click/open tracking; no malformed tracking URLs found among the 12 probed links other than the unsubscribe link above.
- QA notes 55 links were not probed (footer/utility/social deprioritized) — those are unverified.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **12 images missing `alt` text**, confirmed by QA, including the SSL/preheader tracking pixel (`c2b6a0f7...`), hero/promo images (`clothes-shoes.png`, `banner3.gif`, `gloves3.jpg`, `golfbags3.jpg`, `outdoorgear3.jpg`, `ethos2.jpg`, `mlb.jpg`), and four footer icons (`icon-vc-sms.png`, `icon-value-sportsmatter--light.png`, `icon-vc-bpg.png`, `icon-vc-favorite.png`, `icon-location-2025.png` — 5 total, QA lists 5 footer icons plus the items above). This affects screen-reader users and clients with images disabled.
- `banner3.gif` is a GIF; the email's own embedded schema.org script explicitly notes "GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out" — this is a self-flagged risk in the source that the deployed creative may be violating.
- MSO/Outlook conditional blocks and mobile `@media` overrides are present and structured correctly (no malformed conditional comments observed in the truncated source).

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, AMPscript, or Handlebars-style placeholders) are visible in the truncated HTML/QA output. No issues found — but note the source was truncated, so personalized sections later in the email were not reviewed.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **CAN-SPAM risk:** The unsubscribe mechanism is broken (see §2) — clicking "Unsubscribe" leads to an error page instead of completing opt-out, which is a functional failure of the required unsubscribe mechanism.
- **List-Unsubscribe header missing** (QA WARN) — may be an artifact of the AgentMail relay rather than the sender's actual header, but as observed, one-click unsubscribe support cannot be confirmed.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header missing** (QA WARN) — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe cannot be confirmed as supported.
- **Authentication-Results header missing** (QA WARN) — SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unknown from this capture; cannot confirm authentication is properly configured.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Cannot be verified from available evidence: all outbound links are wrapped in Epsilon Harmony redirect URLs (`app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...`) with opaque tokens (`__dU__=...`) rather than direct-to-site URLs with visible UTM parameters. Following the redirect chain would be required to confirm landing page alignment and UTM tagging — not performed in this audit.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Fix the unsubscribe link routing — `/U/.../c2b6a0f7bc3c42b30000021ef3a0bd01` currently 404s to an error page instead of the subscription preference center; this is a CAN-SPAM compliance risk and should be treated as high priority.
2. Add descriptive (or explicitly empty `alt=""` for decorative) alt attributes to the 12 flagged images, particularly the promotional banners which carry the offer content.
3. Replace or re-verify `banner3.gif` — the email's own schema.org comment states GIFs are filtered out by the rendering pipeline.
4. Confirm List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post headers and SPF/DKIM (Authentication-Results) are present on the actual sending path — the missing headers here may be a relay-capture artifact rather than a true absence, so validate directly against the sending MTA.
