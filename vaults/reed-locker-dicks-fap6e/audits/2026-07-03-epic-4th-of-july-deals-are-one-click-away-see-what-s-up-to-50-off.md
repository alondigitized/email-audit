# 🎆 EPIC 4th of July deals are one click away! See what's up to 50% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- DICK'S is running the twelfth-plus promotional email since June 26, and this one lands the morning of July 3 — the single best send window for a 4th of July sale. The timing is right. The creative is not new: this is a near-copy of yesterday's "EPIC 4th of July Flash Sale" (July 2, scored 8/10), swapping "Flash Sale" for "deals are one click away!" and reshuffling the same multi-offer stack. The hero UP TO 50% OFF reads cleanly in patriotic red-and-green, and the sub-category offers (30% off clearance footwear, 50% off running shoes, 40% off Coleman coolers, $100 off golf clubs) give the scroll something to do. ScoreCard loyalty points are surfaced mid-email, which is a genuine plus.
- The problem is cadence fatigue, and this send makes it worse. The subject is bloated — 70+ characters, "one click away!" adds nothing — and the "EPIC" tag is now appearing for at least the third time in a week. A subscriber who opened the July 2 EPIC Flash Sale will pattern-match this as a duplicate and delete. The multi-offer stacking also continues to dilute: there is no single product hero, no urgency deadline stated inside the body, and the CTAs default to the weak "Explore More Deals" / "Shop Now" phrasing that does no persuasive work.
- Verdict: the timing is the best thing about this send. The creative is coasting on a template that's been burned down by overuse.

## What's working

- **Hero is legible and on-brand** — large white "UP TO 50% OFF" on a 4th of July background reads instantly; no render bugs visible.
- **Multi-category depth helps repeat browsers** — footwear, apparel, coolers, golf clubs give different buyer types an entry point.
- **ScoreCard loyalty surfaced mid-email** — points offer is visible without burying it in the footer, a genuine win for members.
- **Timing is optimal** — July 3 is the highest-intent window for a 4th of July sale; the send time is correct even if the creative isn't.

## What's weak

- **Near-identical to yesterday's send** — "EPIC 4th of July Flash Sale" (July 2, 8/10) and this email share the same hero, same percentage claims, same category lineup; a subscriber who opened yesterday has no reason to open again.
- **Subject is bloated** — "are one click away! See what's" adds 20+ characters of filler; subject is ~70 chars and will truncate hard on mobile.
- **No body deadline** — "4th of July" implies urgency, but no explicit "Ends July 4 at midnight" or countdown is visible inside the email.
- **CTA copy is generic** — "Explore More Deals" and "Shop Now" appear throughout; no section CTA is specific ("Shop Running Shoes," "Grab a Cooler").
- **Cadence is broken** — 12+ sends in 8 days, four of which include "EPIC" or "50% off" in the subject; this is unsubscribe territory for marginally engaged subscribers.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill "EPIC" and write a fresh hook** — the July 2 send already used it; repeating it signals nothing new. Lead with the deadline or a specific product.
- **Subject Alt A:** `🎆 Last chance: 50% off before the 4th`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Coleman coolers, running shoes, golf clubs — all on sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Deals end July 4 — grab yours before the cookout`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Extra 30% off clearance + 40% off coolers, today only`
- 2. **Add an explicit deadline inside the body** — a single line "Sale ends July 4 at 11:59 PM ET" above the hero CTA would convert fence-sitters without requiring a redesign.
- 3. **Make CTAs category-specific** — swap "Explore More Deals" for "Shop Running Shoes" / "Shop Coolers" etc. Verb + category noun outperforms generic copy every time.
- 4. **Reduce send frequency or differentiate** — if the July 3 send must go out, it should either rotate to a new category (e.g., outdoor/camping-exclusive) or suppress the previous 24-hour openers to reduce unsubscribe pressure.

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods 4th of July Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email's `<script type="application/ld+json">` block contains invalid JSON that will silently break Gmail's inbox promotions card and structured data features. A stale 2020 template date is also live in the structured data. All other rendering and tracking infrastructure appears functional.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**No issues found** in the visible link infrastructure. All CTA links correctly route through the `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/` click-tracking redirect with per-subscriber `__dU__` tokens. The open/SSL tracking pixel at `app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610...` is present and hidden correctly.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**MSO conditional CSS — stray closing quote:**

```css
div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
```

The `"` after the final `}` is extraneous. In some Outlook/IE parsers this can prematurely terminate the `<style>` attribute value, stripping subsequent rules. The correct form is `padding:0;}` with no trailing quote.

**Logo `alt` attribute:** The `<img id="logo">` tag is truncated in the source provided — unable to confirm `alt` text presence. If absent, screen readers and image-blocked clients receive no fallback for the primary brand identifier.

**Tracking pixel `alt` attribute:** `<img src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/...' style="display:none; ...">` has no `alt=""`. While functionally hidden, validators will flag this; best practice is `alt=""` on decorative/tracking images.

**Template comment says 2025:** `<!--DKS Email Marketing 2025-->` — stale metadata, not a rendering bug but a hygiene flag.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`DiscountOffer` structured data fields are unfilled:**

```json
"description": "",
"discountCode": "",
"availabilityEnds": ""
```

Empty strings for `description` and `availabilityEnds` mean Gmail's promotions tab annotations (discount badge, expiry countdown) will not render, leaving value on the table for inbox differentiation.

**Stale `availabilityStarts` date — 2020:**

```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```

This is a ~6-year-old placeholder from the template. Gmail treats this as the offer start date. It does not cause a send failure, but it signals that this block has never been properly maintained.

**`PromotionCard.image` is empty:**

```json
"@type": "PromotionCard",
"image": ""
```

Gmail uses this to render a preview image in the Promotions tab card. An empty value means no image renders, degrading inbox visibility for subscribers in Gmail's Promotions tab.

---

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**JSON-LD contains JavaScript-style comments — invalid JSON:**

```javascript
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
  // Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
  "@context": "http://schema.org/",
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": "" }
```

JSON does not support `//` comments. The entire `<script type="application/ld+json">` block will fail to parse in any conformant JSON parser (Gmail, validators, inbox preview tools). This is the highest-severity technical defect in this email — all three structured data objects silently fail. These developer notes must be removed before the JSON is valid.

**CAN-SPAM footer / unsubscribe:** Not visible in the truncated source. Unable to audit. Physical mailing address and one-click unsubscribe are required; confirm they appear in the full template below this truncation point.

**Sending domain:** From address is `DSG@e.dcsg.com` (`e.dcsg.com` is the ESP subdomain). Unable to confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment from HTML source alone — requires header inspection from a delivered copy.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters:** All destination URLs are behind the `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/` redirect layer. The actual destination URLs and any UTM parameters are not resolvable from the HTML source. Recommend spot-checking 2–3 CTA redirects in a browser to confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` are appended at the destination (or that the redirect layer handles attribution server-side).

**No issues found** with domain consistency — click-tracking domain (`app.dickssportinggoods.com`), asset domain (`s7d6.scene7.com`, `dks.scene7.com`), and brand domain (`dickssportinggoods.com`) are consistent and expected.
