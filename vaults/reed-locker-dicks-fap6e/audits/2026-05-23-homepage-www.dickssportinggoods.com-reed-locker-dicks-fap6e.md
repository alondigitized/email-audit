# Homepage snapshot · www.dickssportinggoods.com · 2026-05-23
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- I pulled up Dick's on my phone expecting to grab some cleats or check on ScoreCard points and got hit with a wall of gray and an error card. Whatever's going on with their servers, I'm completely locked out — no homepage, no nav, no nothing. As a brand, Dick's is a go-to for me, but this visit tells me exactly nothing about what they're pushing right now.

## What's working

- **Dick's logo renders correctly** — at least I know I hit the right domain and didn't end up somewhere sketchy.
- **Error message is plain English** — "We are working on the problem. Please try accessing the site again after 12 hours" is at least legible and not a cryptic 502 code wall.

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage failed to load** — I see a white error card on a featureless gray background. No hero, no nav, no offers, no categories.
- **"12 hours" is brutal timeline language** — telling a customer to come back in half a day is a conversion killer. That's not a recovery message, that's a goodbye.
- **Raw error ID exposed to the user** — `Error: 0.2ca7cb17.1779540602.1595993d IP: 75.140.9.0` is meaningless to me and looks like they left the debug output in. Not a good look.
- **No fallback content or redirect** — not even a "shop our app" link or a store-finder CTA while the site is down. I'm just stuck.
- **Massive empty gray scroll space below** — the page renders with what appears to be thousands of pixels of blank background. Looks broken on mobile.

## Recommendations

- **Add a fallback page with app store links** — if the site is down, at minimum send me to the Dick's app so I can still browse and buy. Dead end = lost sale.
- **Replace the 12-hour message with something actionable** — "Check back soon" or "Text us at X to check store inventory" keeps the relationship alive.
- **Strip the raw error ID** — `0.2ca7cb17...` means nothing to a customer. Log it server-side, don't surface it. It erodes trust.
- **Set up a status-page redirect** — even a simple "status.dickssportinggoods.com" link gives me somewhere to go and signals they're on it.
