# The SALE starts now 🎆 Up to 50% off EPIC deals & more
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S eleventh-plus promotional email in roughly five days — and notably the first send of May 22, arriving the morning of Memorial Day weekend's peak shopping window. The email leads with a credible 50% hero that mirrors the May 18 anchor, then immediately piles on an "EXTRA 25% OFF Select Clearance" banner, a "UP TO 20% OFF Select Nike" banner, and yet another 20%-off block — four stacked discount claims before the scroll even hits mid-page. The creative is competent; DICK'S house style is clean and renders without bugs. But the strategy is discount theater: every percentage point added below the hero dilutes the one above it, and subscribers who've been hit with near-identical Memorial Day sale emails on May 18, 19, 20, 21 (twice), and now May 22 have had every signal of scarcity trained out of them.
- The "starts now" urgency in the subject is the most interesting decision here. DICK'S finally gave the sale a defined start, which is sharper than the meandering "up to 50% off" loop from the prior week. But without a hard end date in the body, that urgency collapses — and a subscriber who saw the May 18 email (scored 8/10) knows this "start" is the sixth announcement of the same event.
- Judgment: a solidly built email dragged down by an unsustainable send cadence and a cluttered body that cannibalizes its own hero.

## What's working

- **Hero lands cleanly.** "UP TO 50% OFF" in large type on a patriotic background is visible above the fold and correctly front-loads the lead offer.
- **Seasonal alignment is exact.** Deploying on the morning of Memorial Day weekend's final push is the right timing; the creative matches the moment.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no layout drift — the email displays correctly.
- **Multiple categories represented.** Baseball gear, Nike footwear, and athletic apparel give a range of entry points across the DICK'S assortment, which is appropriate for a sale broadcast.

## What's weak

- **Four competing discount banners destroy the hierarchy.** 50% + extra 25% on clearance + 20% Nike + another 20% block stacks four separate discount messages in the first scroll, making none of them feel definitive.
- **"SHOP NOW" on every CTA is meaningless.** Not one button says what you're shopping — no "Shop Shoes," "Shop Nike," or "Shop Clearance." Generic copy on five different banners is five wasted clicks.
- **No hard deadline visible.** "Starts now" implies urgency but the email shows no end date; subscribers have seen this sale "start" six times already and know it doesn't close.
- **Cadence has hit critical fatigue.** This is the second email on May 21 and the anchor of a multi-day Memorial Day blitz. The 11th+ send in five days means the "sale starts now" subject line is actively ironic to any paying-attention subscriber.
- **The clearance overlay is a trust tax.** Sandwiching an "EXTRA 25% OFF Clearance" banner immediately under the 50% hero makes the hero feel like it was lowballing — the classic discount-stacking tell that makes subscribers feel gamed.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill three of the four discount banners and commit to one.** Choose the 50% hero OR the clearance overlay — not both. Every stacked claim reduces belief in the first. This single change would do more than any copy tweak.
- 2. **Add a hard expiration.** "Ends tonight at midnight" or "Ends Monday 11:59 PM" makes "starts now" actually mean something. Without it the urgency is theater, which is exactly what the prior five sends trained subscribers to expect.
- 3. **Rewrite every CTA to name the category.** "Shop Memorial Day Shoes," "Shop Nike Clearance," "Shop Baseball Gear" — verb + noun. "SHOP NOW" on five banners gives subscribers nothing to act on.
- 4. **Add a member pricing call-out.** DICK'S ScoreCard members get early access; this email doesn't surface it. That's a free loyalty hook.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 50% off Memorial Day window closes soon`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Nike, adidas, baseball, and more — final day to save`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop before the sale ends: shoes, gear, apparel up to half off`

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods Memorial Day Sale Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on Epsilon/Harmony infrastructure with standard MSO conditional markup. Two confirmed bugs: a malformed MSO conditional stylesheet and invalid JSON-LD with stale/unfilled template placeholders.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open pixel hidden correctly** — `#ssl` img uses `display:none; max-height:0px; overflow:hidden; mso-hide:all`. No issue with the pixel itself.

**Preheader text not hidden** — The `<td id="ssl">` hides only the `<img>`, but the plain text `Just launched: Owala Soccer` immediately after it is unscoped and renders as visible preheader in most clients. This likely produces "Just launched: Owala Soccer" as the inbox preview string — inconsistent with the Memorial Day subject line.

```html
<img src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/...' style="display:none; ..."/>
<!--Replace/Paste SSL Here-->
Just launched: Owala Soccer   ← this text is NOT hidden
```

**Click-tracking redirect chain confirmed** — All CTA links route through `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610000019e4f40da41960200369ff04b84/...`. Final destination URLs and UTM params are not inspectable from source alone (see §6).

**Logo image URL truncated in source** — `https://dsg.images.harmony.epsilon.com/_phoenix/_dev/AE…` appears cut off. If the ESP is not injecting the remainder at send time, this will produce a broken image for the header logo.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Stray `"` in MSO conditional stylesheet** — The `div` rule inside the Outlook conditional block ends with `padding:0;"}`— a rogue double-quote before the closing brace. This is invalid CSS; Outlook's parser may drop all rules that follow it.

```html
<!--[if (gte mso 9)|(IE)]><style type="text/css">
  ...
  div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
                                                                              ↑ invalid
</style><![endif]-->
```

**Duplicate `font-family` declaration** — `body` rule declares `font-family` twice in succession (second adds `!important`). Harmless but indicates copy-paste artifact.

```css
body{font-family:Archivo,Arial,sans-serif;font-family:Archivo,Arial,sans-serif!important}
```

**`lang="en"` present** — No issue.

**MSO `border-collapse` + `PixelsPerInch` conditionals present** — Standard and correct.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Table ID signals conditional branching** — `id="header-non-sc-member"` implies a separate `header-sc-member` block exists. Cannot confirm both variants are populated without full source, but the naming convention is consistent.

**No visible broken merge tokens** in the provided truncated source (no `{{`, `%%`, or `[%` patterns exposed unresolved).

---

### 5. Compliance

**JSON-LD has three compliance-relevant defects:**

1. **JavaScript `//` comments inside `<script type="application/ld+json">`** — JSON does not support comments. This makes the entire block invalid JSON; schema processors (Gmail, Google Search) will silently discard it.

2. **Unfilled `DiscountOffer` template fields** — `description`, `discountCode`, and `availabilityEnds` are empty strings. `availabilityStarts` is hardcoded to `"2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"` — a 6-year-old stale default never updated at send time.

3. **`PromotionCard` image field is empty with a designer directive left in production:**
```json
// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
"image": ""
```
This is a production send with an unfinished template artifact.

**CAN-SPAM footer** — Not visible in truncated source; cannot confirm physical address and unsubscribe link presence. If footer is in the unrendered portion, no issue. Must be verified against full source.

**Sending domain `e.dcsg.com`** — Legitimate subdomain of `dcsg.com`. SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment cannot be verified from HTML alone; requires header inspection.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All links are wrapped in Epsilon redirect URLs — final destination and UTM parameters are not readable from source. The redirect format is:

```
https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610000019e4f40da41960200369ff04b84/<hash>?__dU__=<token>
```

UTM attribution cannot be confirmed without resolving redirects. To verify: follow one CTA redirect and inspect the landing URL for `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content` parameters.
