# Want up to 50% off today? 🙌
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-06**
## Executive summary

- This is the same blue-pool, beach-ball "UP TO 50% OFF" hero DICK'S has now leaned on repeatedly — it's the identical creative from the 8/2 and 8/4 sends, and per the brand history it went out under a near-identical subject earlier *today* (8/6, "Up to 50% off is happening now 🙌," 9.00/10). This send, "Want up to 50% off today? 🙌," looks like a second wave of the same day's promo rather than a new idea — likely an internal subject-line test rather than a deliberate second touch, but from the inbox it reads as a duplicate.
- Below the fold, the execution is genuinely strong: clean hero, unambiguous "SHOP NOW" CTA, a well-organized category grid (Men's/Women's/Kids'/Shoes/Team Sports/Back-to-School), and a long, well-photographed scroll through golf, outdoor, team sports, drinkware, fan shop, and fitness — each with its own offer and CTA. The ScoreCard loyalty banner up top ("300 Points = $10 Reward") is a nice touch for a member.
- The creative quality is not the problem — the cadence is. Sending the same pool-ball 50%-off hero twice in one day, after it already ran twice this week, is going to train subscribers to skim past DICK'S mail. Judged purely on render quality and offer clarity this scores well, but a real subscriber checking their inbox today would recognize this instantly and likely not open a second time.

## What's working

- Hero offer ("UP TO 50%") is legible, high-contrast, and impossible to miss at the top of the email.
- "SHOP NOW" CTA is a clear, visible green button with no ambiguity about the next step.
- ScoreCard rewards banner ("300 Points = $10 Reward, Shop to Earn Points") gives loyalty members a reason to click beyond the discount.
- Category navigation bar (Men's/Women's/Kids'/Shoes/Team Sports/Back-to-School) gives shoppers a fast path past the generic hero into something specific.
- No render bugs — every product photo, icon, and button renders cleanly with no overlapping text or broken images.

## What's weak

- This is the same blue-pool/beach-ball "50% off" creative running for the third-plus time in five days, and per the brand history it appears to be a same-day repeat of the 8/6 "Up to 50% off is happening now 🙌" send — creative fatigue risk is high.
- The hero offer is generic ("UP TO 50% OFF") with no category attached, so it doesn't tell a shopper what's actually on sale until they scroll.
- "Valid online only for a limited time. While quantities last. Other exclusions may apply." sits right under the hero — honest disclosure, but it undercuts the urgency the subject line is trying to create.
- No specific deadline anywhere in the visible copy ("limited time" isn't a date), so the "today" urgency in the subject isn't backed up by the body.
- It's a long, undifferentiated scroll of near-identical section blocks (photo strip → heading → offer buttons, repeated 7+ times) with no single standout product to anchor the click.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop reusing the pool/beach-ball hero — it's now appeared enough times that it's actively working against open and click rates; retire it for at least two weeks and rotate in the golf or outdoor imagery that's currently buried mid-email.
- 2. Anchor the hero to one category instead of a sitewide "up to 50%" — pick the strongest current margin category (e.g., shoes) and make the hero and subject both say it.
- 3. Add a real deadline ("through Sunday," "ends 8/9") to the fine print so the "today" urgency in the subject has something to point to.
- 4. Trim the email — 7+ nearly identical category blocks dilutes the primary offer; cut to 3-4 and let ScoreCard/loyalty content close it out.
- 5. If this is genuinely a second send today, either suppress it for openers of the earlier one or differentiate the subject and hero enough that it doesn't read as a duplicate.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Shoes are up to 50% off — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `🙌 Your ScoreCard points + 50% off, together`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns just landed across shoes, golf, and outdoor gear.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Earn $10 in rewards while everything's on sale.`

## Technical Audit

# Technical Audit: DICK'S Sporting Goods — "Want up to 50% off today?"

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is built on a standard ESP-tracked template (Cordial/Cheetah-style redirect domain `app.dickssportinggoods.com`) with mostly valid responsive markup, but ships with a broken unsubscribe destination, malformed JSON-LD structured data, and a CSS syntax error in the Outlook conditional block.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Broken unsubscribe link (confirmed by QA):** `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/U/v610000019fd8b36d9c96b80b969125cd18/99e7a0adb1444feb0000021ef3a0bd07` resolves to `/pc/Error/Error?aspxerrorpath=/pc/subscription` — an ASP.NET error page rather than a preference/unsubscribe form. This is a functional failure, not just a UX issue.
- All CTA/header links route through the ESP tracking redirector `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...?__dU__=...` (e.g. logo link `.../T/v610000019fd8b36d9c96b80b969125cd18/99e7a0adb1444feb0000021ef3a0bcc6/...`). Destination URLs are wrapped and not resolvable from source, so final landing targets can't be verified in this pass.
- Open/SSL tracking pixel present at top of body: `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019fd8b36d9c96b80b969125cd18/99e7a0adb1444feb00004c5a42963aa1` — correctly hidden (`display:none; max-height:0px`).
- QA notes only 12 of 71 total links were probed (deprioritized footer/utility/social links skipped by design) — link coverage in this audit is partial.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **12 images missing `alt` text** (confirmed by QA), including the SSL/tracking pixel, four promo images (`clothing.jpg`, `golf.jpg`, `outdoor.jpg`, `sports.jpg`, `jersey.jpg`, `fitness.jpg`), one GIF banner, and five footer icons (`icon-vc-sms.png`, `icon-value-sportsmatter--light.png`, `icon-vc-bpg.png`, `icon-vc-favorite.png`, `icon-location-2025.png`). This degrades screen-reader accessibility and image-blocked rendering (Outlook default).
- **CSS syntax error in MSO conditional block:** `div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}</style>` — a stray `"` before the closing brace makes this an invalid CSS rule for Outlook/mso clients, likely causing the whole rule (or worse, the block) to be dropped or mis-parsed.
- **GIF asset used despite explicit unsupported-format warning in template comments:** the JSON-LD block's own developer comment states `// GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out`, yet the body includes `banner4.gif` (`.../2026-0623-dks-outdoor-40-off-drinkware-...`). If this format restriction applies broadly (Gmail annotations/AMP or client-side filtering), this image is at risk of being stripped.
- Responsive `@media` rules for mobile (`.mobile-off`, `.header-xl`, etc.) are well-formed and standard.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No merge tags (e.g. `%%FIRSTNAME%%`, Handlebars `{{ }}`) are present in the visible/truncated source — the email appears to be non-personalized batch content.
- No issues found in this category based on available source.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Unsubscribe mechanism is broken** — see §2. A non-functional unsubscribe link is a CAN-SPAM compliance risk (opt-out must be honored/functional).
- **`List-Unsubscribe` header not found** (QA WARN) — may be an artifact of the AgentMail relay not capturing it rather than the sender omitting it; cannot confirm sender-side absence from this data alone.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found** (QA WARN) — one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) support is unconfirmed.
- **`Authentication-Results` header not found** (QA WARN) — SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unknown via this relay; not conclusive evidence of an authentication failure, just unverified.
- JSON-LD `DiscountOffer` object has empty required-looking fields (`"description":"", "discountCode":"", "availabilityEnds":""`) — not a compliance violation per se, but indicates unfilled template placeholders shipped to production (see §7).

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- All observed links are wrapped in ESP redirect URLs (`/T/...`, `/O/...`, `/U/...`) rather than direct site URLs with visible UTM parameters, so UTM presence/consistency and final landing-page alignment cannot be verified from the HTML source alone.
- The one resolvable failure is the unsubscribe redirect landing on a site error page (`/pc/Error/Error?aspxerrorpath=/pc/subscription`), confirming at least one broken email→site handoff.
- No other broken email-to-site links were identified in the 12 probed links per QA.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Fix the unsubscribe redirect so `/U/...` lands on a working `/pc/subscription` preference page instead of the ASP.NET error page — highest priority, compliance-relevant.
2. Remove the stray `"` in the MSO conditional `<style>` block (`padding:0;"}`) to restore valid CSS for Outlook rendering.
3. Add descriptive `alt` text to all 12 flagged images, particularly the four hero/category images and jersey/fitness images that carry offer content.
4. Replace or reformat the JSON-LD block: strip the `//` comments (invalid in JSON and will break structured-data parsing) and populate or remove the empty `description`/`discountCode`/`availabilityEnds`/`image` fields before send.
5. Confirm whether `banner4.gif` is subject to the same GIF-filtering behavior called out in the template's own comments; convert to PNG/JPEG if so.
6. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers and SPF/DKIM authentication status directly against DSG's outbound MTA (independent of the AgentMail relay) to rule out a real gap versus a relay-capture limitation.
