# Want it? DICK'S got it — the hottest picks you don't want to miss 🔥
**Score:** 10/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- After burning through six consecutive discount-heavy sends in three days — Flash Sale, Extra 30% Off, 70% Clearance — DICK'S has pivoted hard to an editorial "Fresh Picks" discovery frame. No deal. No price. No deadline. The creative itself is clean: a sneaker collage hero introduces five individually spotlighted products (Ray-Ban Meta Glasses, adidas CNY jacket, Nike Ja 1 shoes, Standard Issue shorts, a hydrating drink), capped by an app-exclusive section and an app download push. Each module is tidy, branded, and navigable.
- The problem is positioning. Subscribers who've been conditioned by "70% off — while it lasts" for four straight days will register this send as a browse email — low urgency, safe to skip. The subject does nothing to correct that: "the hottest picks you don't want to miss" is inert social proof at 67 characters, arriving with nothing concrete attached. The adidas Chinese New Year jacket appearing as a "Fresh Pick" in mid-July is a visible credibility crack — it reads as inventory liquidation dressed up as curation.
- This is a decent editorial template in the wrong slot. Without at least one price anchor, it can't compete with the discount expectations DICK'S itself has spent a week building. Compare to the July 14 clearance send, which reset with a hard 70% number and earned its 8/10 — today's send trades that momentum for a shrug.

## What's working

- **Module layout earns the "picks" premise** — each product gets dedicated image, name, and SHOP NOW CTA without visual crowding.
- **Product variety signals genuine breadth** — eyewear, apparel, footwear, and hydration in one send positions DICK'S as a full lifestyle brand, not just cleats and jerseys.
- **APP EXCLUSIVE badge does real work** — the hydrating checkers module gives app users a reason to tap that general inbox subscribers don't have.
- **Summer-native content** — Standard Issue shorts + "Must-have gear for an app summer!" copy lands seasonally on July 15.
- **No render bugs** — image-to-text ratio is healthy, layout is clean, no broken modules or missing alt text gaps visible.

## What's weak

- **Zero price signal after a week of heavy discounting** — subscribers expect a number; editorial copy with no offer lands as a downgrade, not a change of pace.
- **adidas Chinese New Year jacket in July** — EXCLUSIVE badge on an out-of-season product undermines the "hottest picks" framing and reads as dead inventory clearance.
- **Subject is vague and too long** — "the hottest picks you don't want to miss" could describe any retail email from any brand; at ~67 characters, it truncates on most mobile clients.
- **No single hero product** — the sneaker collage at top is five products competing for attention; there's no editorial spine to hang the rest of the email on.
- **Cadence fatigue** — seventh send in four days; even a well-executed editorial gets skipped when inbox share is this saturated.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor on one hero product** — lead with the Nike Ja 1 'Animal Pack' as the sole hero, full-width, with "from $XX" or a member price. Earn the grid modules below it rather than opening with a collage.
- 2. **Add one price signal anywhere above the fold** — even "members get early access" or "from $49" changes the calculus from browse to intent.
- 3. **Pull the CNY jacket** — swap in something that feels July (trail runners, gym shorts, a water bottle with a collab) to maintain the "hottest picks" credibility.
- 4. **Cut and sharpen the subject line** — get under 50 characters and name the product:
- **Subject Alt A:** `New drop: Nike Ja 1 'Animal Pack' just landed 🔥`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Fresh picks worth opening — Ray-Bans, Nike + more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Curated weekly finds — Ray-Bans, app exclusives inside.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `5 things worth adding to cart before they're gone.`
- 5. **Rest the send cadence** — skip tomorrow's promotional email or replace it with a triggered post-browse email. Seven sends in four days will erode the list faster than any individual creative decision.

## Full review
## 1. Overview

After burning through six consecutive discount-heavy sends in three days — Flash Sale, Extra 30% Off, 70% Clearance — DICK'S has pivoted hard to an editorial "Fresh Picks" discovery frame. No deal. No price. No deadline. The creative itself is clean: a sneaker collage hero introduces five individually spotlighted products (Ray-Ban Meta Glasses, adidas CNY jacket, Nike Ja 1 shoes, Standard Issue shorts, a hydrating drink), capped by an app-exclusive section and an app download push. Each module is tidy, branded, and navigable.

The problem is positioning. Subscribers who've been conditioned by "70% off — while it lasts" for four straight days will register this send as a browse email — low urgency, safe to skip. The subject does nothing to correct that: "the hottest picks you don't want to miss" is inert social proof at 67 characters, arriving with nothing concrete attached. The adidas Chinese New Year jacket appearing as a "Fresh Pick" in mid-July is a visible credibility crack — it reads as inventory liquidation dressed up as curation.

This is a decent editorial template in the wrong slot. Without at least one price anchor, it can't compete with the discount expectations DICK'S itself has spent a week building. Compare to the July 14 clearance send, which reset with a hard 70% number and earned its 8/10 — today's send trades that momentum for a shrug.

## 2. What Worked

- **Module layout earns the "picks" premise** — each product gets dedicated image, name, and SHOP NOW CTA without visual crowding.
- **Product variety signals genuine breadth** — eyewear, apparel, footwear, and hydration in one send positions DICK'S as a full lifestyle brand, not just cleats and jerseys.
- **APP EXCLUSIVE badge does real work** — the hydrating checkers module gives app users a reason to tap that general inbox subscribers don't have.
- **Summer-native content** — Standard Issue shorts + "Must-have gear for an app summer!" copy lands seasonally on July 15.
- **No render bugs** — image-to-text ratio is healthy, layout is clean, no broken modules or missing alt text gaps visible.

## 3. What Didn't

- **Zero price signal after a week of heavy discounting** — subscribers expect a number; editorial copy with no offer lands as a downgrade, not a change of pace.
- **adidas Chinese New Year jacket in July** — EXCLUSIVE badge on an out-of-season product undermines the "hottest picks" framing and reads as dead inventory clearance.
- **Subject is vague and too long** — "the hottest picks you don't want to miss" could describe any retail email from any brand; at ~67 characters, it truncates on most mobile clients.
- **No single hero product** — the sneaker collage at top is five products competing for attention; there's no editorial spine to hang the rest of the email on.
- **Cadence fatigue** — seventh send in four days; even a well-executed editorial gets skipped when inbox share is this saturated.

## 4. What I'd Change

1. **Anchor on one hero product** — lead with the Nike Ja 1 'Animal Pack' as the sole hero, full-width, with "from $XX" or a member price. Earn the grid modules below it rather than opening with a collage.
2. **Add one price signal anywhere above the fold** — even "members get early access" or "from $49" changes the calculus from browse to intent.
3. **Pull the CNY jacket** — swap in something that feels July (trail runners, gym shorts, a water bottle with a collab) to maintain the "hottest picks" credibility.
4. **Cut and sharpen the subject line** — get under 50 characters and name the product:
   - **Subject Alt A:** `New drop: Nike Ja 1 'Animal Pack' just landed 🔥`
   - **Subject Alt B:** `Fresh picks worth opening — Ray-Bans, Nike + more`
   - **Preheader Alt A:** `Curated weekly finds — Ray-Bans, app exclusives inside.`
   - **Preheader Alt B:** `5 things worth adding to cart before they're gone.`
5. **Rest the send cadence** — skip tomorrow's promotional email or replace it with a triggered post-browse email. Seven sends in four days will erode the list faster than any individual creative decision.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email's HTML structure and Outlook/mobile fallbacks are competently built, but the JSON-LD structured data block contains critical syntax errors (JavaScript comments in JSON) and multiple unfilled template tokens that will cause Gmail Promotions tab annotations to fail entirely.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open pixel** — `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019f654f0ccba3a201f4bbc786c8/...` is correctly hidden via `display:none; mso-hide:all`. No issue.

**Click redirects** — All links route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610000019f654f0ccba3a201f4bbc786c8/...` with opaque encoded payloads. Standard ESP pattern, but destination URLs and UTM parameters cannot be confirmed without following the redirect chain (see §6).

**Logo `src` truncated** — The logo `<img src="https://s7d6.scene7.com/is/image/DSGAEMSites/brand-logo--dks-2?wid=248&hei=…"` has a cut-off `hei=` parameter in the provided source. If the actual deployed HTML is also truncated, the Scene7 image URL will 400 and the logo will not render.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Web font fallback** — Archivo is loaded from `fonts.gstatic.com` inside `@media screen`. Blocked in Outlook, Gmail Android app, and corporate proxies. The declared fallback stack `Arial, sans-serif` is correct and will engage. No action needed.

**MSO conditional comments** — `<!--[if (gte mso 9)|(IE)]>` table wrappers and style overrides are present. Correct.

**`role="presentation"`** — Applied to layout tables. Correct.

**Alt text** — The HTML is truncated; alt text on product images cannot be verified. The logo `<img>` in the visible excerpt has no `alt` attribute — confirm the deployed version includes `alt="DICK'S Sporting Goods"`.

**`a[x-apple-data-detectors]`** CSS — Present and correct for iOS link suppression.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**CRITICAL — Invalid JSON-LD (JavaScript comments in JSON block):**

```json
// GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
// Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
```

JSON does not allow `//` comments. The entire `<script type="application/ld+json">` block will fail to parse. Gmail's structured data parser will silently discard all three schema objects (`Organization`, `DiscountOffer`, `PromotionCard`). Remove all `//` comment lines before sending.

**Unfilled template tokens in JSON-LD:**

| Field | Value | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| `DiscountOffer.discountCode` | `""` | Empty — unfilled |
| `DiscountOffer.description` | `""` | Empty — unfilled |
| `DiscountOffer.availabilityEnds` | `""` | Empty — unfilled |
| `PromotionCard.image` | `""` | Empty — no promo card image; Gmail Promotions tab will not render image annotation |
| `DiscountOffer.availabilityStarts` | `"2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"` | 6-year-stale default date; was not updated for this send |

**Stale template comment:** `<!--DKS Email Marketing 2025-->` on `<html>` — template year not updated to 2026. Minor maintenance debt.
