# Thank us later...$25 & UNDER select clearance is here! 💰
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-29**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S "$25 AND UNDER" offer from the 9/27 "Weekend win 🙌 $25 & under select clearance" send, now repackaged as a Tuesday clearance email. The hero is a big white "$25 AND UNDER" on deep red. It's legible and it's the first thing the eye lands on. The subject's "Thank us later" is a friendly, slightly cheeky hook, and the price-ceiling offer is easy to grasp.
- After the hero the email turns into a catalog. Below it are seven or eight category blocks with different discount ceilings: up to 40% off running shoes, 70% off apparel, 40% off drinkware, 50% off fan gear, 50% off bats, a $299.98 golf bag set, and a second "UP TO 50% OFF" banner. None of these blocks connects to the $25 promise. A shopper who opens for "$25 and under" ends up in a generic sitewide sale.
- Overall this is a competent but scattered send. It has a clear hero, but it's diluted by the pile of unrelated offers. It's also the third or fourth reuse of the same clearance formula in a few days. I'd call it average.

## What's working

- The "$25 AND UNDER" hero is bold and high-contrast. The offer reads in about one second.
- The single "Shop Now" button under the hero is clear and easy to find.
- The category pills (Men's, Women's, Kids', Shoes, All Clearance, All Sale) give a quick way to browse by interest.
- The ScoreCard and points strip at the top adds a loyalty cue. The "Order Today. Sport It Tomorrow." banner adds a fulfillment cue.
- The layout is clean, with no broken images or overlapping text. The "Only at DICK'S" tags on the apparel and golf blocks add some exclusivity.

## What's weak

- The hero promises "$25 and under," but the blocks below use unrelated "up to X% off" claims. That dilutes the message.
- There are about eight competing "Shop Now" buttons with no clear second priority. A shopper has nothing to anchor on after the hero.
- The hero has no product imagery, only red type. Nothing shows what you can actually buy for $25.
- The hero fine print is dense and small and reads like a legal block. Phrases like "while quantities last" and "interim markdowns may have been taken" undercut the "honest offer" feel.
- The second "UP TO 50% OFF / This Week's Deals" banner near the bottom repeats the hero idea in a weaker way. The $299.98 golf set doesn't fit a clearance-under-$25 story.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the body match the hero. Fill it with actual products priced at $25 or less, with the price on each tile. Cut the unrelated percent-off tiles.
- 2. Put product photos in or directly under the hero, such as socks, tees, and water bottles at $25 or under. This shows the value right away.
- 3. Cut the CTAs to one primary "Shop $25 & Under" button and one or two category links. Give the button specific copy instead of a generic "Shop Now."
- 4. Move the fine print to the footer and shorten it. Keep only a short "while supplies last" line near the hero.
- 5. Drop the "Thank us later" tease if the send is meant to be the deal reminder. Lead with the price.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Clearance finds under $25 — going fast`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Tees, socks, shoes & more. Prices this low don't last.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Order today, sport it tomorrow. Shop $25 & under picks.`
