# [ Clearance deals have landed — shop up to 60% off select styles ] 💲
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-23**
## Executive summary

- This is the clearance-specific angle DICK'S has been threading through its "Up to 60% off" hero, and it's actually one of the cleaner opens: bold red-on-white "UP TO 60% OFF SELECT CLEARANCE SCORES" hits immediately, with a legible black "Shop Now" button right underneath. But it collapses fast into the same sprawling digest that's defined every DICK'S send this month — clearance gives way to Back-to-School (again the green locker room), cleats, golf sets, outdoor gear, Peloton shoes, squishy toys, and a second "Up to 50% off This Week's Deals" banner that directly competes with the clearance hero for attention.
- The core problem is repetition and diffusion, not execution: the hero itself is one of the better clearance treatments seen from this brand, but by asset #7 the reader has forgotten clearance was ever the subject, and the same green-locker "Up to 50%" creative reappears as its own line item — cannibalizing its own headline offer.
- Verdict: decent top-of-funnel hero, undercut by an overstuffed, self-competing digest and a subject line dressed up with an unnecessary "💲" that doesn't earn its spot.

## What's working

- Hero clearance tag ("UP TO 60% OFF SELECT CLEARANCE SCORES") is bold, legible, and matches the subject line's offer exactly.
- Clear single "Shop Now" button under the hero with no scroll needed on mobile.
- Category quick-links (Men's, Women's, Kids', $25 and Under) give an immediate secondary path for browsers not sold on clearance specifically.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, layout holds together end to end.

## What's weak

- The "Back-to-School Deals: Up to 50% Off" locker-room banner is the same green-locker asset used repeatedly this month (8/16, 8/20, 8/22) — it dilutes the clearance framing this email was supposed to be about.
- A second "This Week's Deals — Up to 50% off" green banner appears further down, essentially restating the back-to-school banner and confusing which offer is actually "the" deal.
- Nine distinct offers (clearance, back-to-school, cleats, golf, outdoor, Peloton, toys, weekly deals, product grid) turn a clearance email into an undifferentiated catalog dump.
- Subject's "💲" emoji reads as filler/spam-adjacent rather than adding clarity or urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to clearance only, or clearly demote everything else below a hard visual break — right now clearance loses the fight to two separate "50% off" banners by the middle of the email.
- 2. Remove the duplicate green-locker banner; a subscriber who's seen it three times this month treats it as wallpaper, not a signal.
- 3. Move the category quick-links (Men's/Women's/Kids'/$25 and Under) higher, directly under the Shop Now button, since that's the natural next click for a clearance-first shopper.
- 4. Drop the emoji from the subject — it doesn't add information and slightly undercuts a legitimate flat-percentage offer.

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This is the clearance-specific angle DICK'S has been threading through its "Up to 60% off" hero, and it's actually one of the cleaner opens: bold red-on-white "UP TO 60% OFF SELECT CLEARANCE SCORES" hits immediately, with a legible black "Shop Now" button right underneath. But it collapses fast into the same sprawling digest that's defined every DICK'S send this month — clearance gives way to Back-to-School (again the green locker room), cleats, golf sets, outdoor gear, Peloton shoes, squishy toys, and a second "Up to 50% off This Week's Deals" banner that directly competes with the clearance hero for attention.

The core problem is repetition and diffusion, not execution: the hero itself is one of the better clearance treatments seen from this brand, but by asset #7 the reader has forgotten clearance was ever the subject, and the same green-locker "Up to 50%" creative reappears as its own line item — cannibalizing its own headline offer.

Verdict: decent top-of-funnel hero, undercut by an overstuffed, self-competing digest and a subject line dressed up with an unnecessary "💲" that doesn't earn its spot.

- Hero clearance tag ("UP TO 60% OFF SELECT CLEARANCE SCORES") is bold, legible, and matches the subject line's offer exactly.

- Category quick-links (Men's, Women's, Kids', $25 and Under) give an immediate secondary path for browsers not sold on clearance specifically.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, layout holds together end to end.

## 3. What didn't
- The "Back-to-School Deals: Up to 50% Off" locker-room banner is the same green-locker asset used repeatedly this month (8/16, 8/20, 8/22) — it dilutes the clearance framing this email was supposed to be about.
- A second "This Week's Deals — Up to 50% off" green banner appears further down, essentially restating the back-to-school banner and confusing which offer is actually "the" deal.
- Nine distinct offers (clearance, back-to-school, cleats, golf, outdoor, Peloton, toys, weekly deals, product grid) turn a clearance email into an undifferentiated catalog dump.
