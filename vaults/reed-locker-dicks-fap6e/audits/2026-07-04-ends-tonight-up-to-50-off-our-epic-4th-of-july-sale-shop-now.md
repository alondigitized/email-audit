# Ends. Tonight. ⏰ Up to 50% OFF our Epic 4th of July Sale (shop now!)
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-04**
## Executive summary

- This is the closing-bell send — "Ends Tonight" on the actual evening of July 4th, with the urgency mathematically correct. The creative delivers two clean offers in sequence: 50% off the main sale, then Extra 30% off clearance, both with "Ends Tonight" badges, both rendering without bugs. If this landed in a cold inbox, it would be a solid 7-8 send.
- The problem is cadence. DICK'S has now sent at minimum 15 promotional emails since June 26 — roughly two per day — and this is the **second July 4th email today**. The morning send ("EPIC 4th of July SALE: Shop deals today!") already covered the 50% off hero. This email is structurally nearly identical to that one with an "ends tonight" wrapper. By the time this lands, subscribers who haven't bought in after 14 emails are not going to buy because the countdown timer showed up.
- The product grid is the real missed opportunity here. Six specific items (Stanley tumbler, YETI can cooler, DSG armchair, Igloo cooler) are featured with no prices shown, no "was $X, now $Y" anchoring. On the night of a sale, you want to show the deal, not just name the product.

## What's working

- **"Ends Tonight" timing is credible.** Unlike manufactured FOMO, this deadline is real — it's July 4th, and the sale does end tonight. The green "Ends Tonight" badge appears twice, reinforcing urgency without feeling manipulative.
- **Dual-offer stacking is clean.** 50% off the full sale, then Extra 30% off clearance as a secondary section — they don't compete visually, they stack as escalating value.
- **Hero render is bug-free and dominant.** The red 50% OFF banner is large, legible, and the eye lands there immediately. No broken images, no layout drift.
- **"SUMMER DEALS" CTA button is unambiguous.** Clear verb-adjacent label, high-contrast green, above the fold.

## What's weak

- **Second send today with near-identical creative.** The morning email already had "UP TO 50% OFF" as the hero. Without new product callouts, expanded deals, or meaningfully different framing, this reads as a blast, not a closing argument.
- **Product grid shows no prices.** Six featured items — Stanley tumbler, YETI colster, Igloo cooler, DSG armchair — none have price tags, original price, or "now $X" anchoring. On a sale night, this is the most important missing element.
- **Broad, unfocused product selection.** Kids' shoes, a patio armchair, a can cooler, women's activewear — there's no through-line. The selection signals mass spray, not persona targeting.
- **Subject line is over 50 characters.** At ~68 chars, it truncates on mobile before "4th of July Sale" even appears. The most credible part of the message gets clipped.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show the discount math in the product grid.** "Stanley 40oz — was $45, now $22" is what drives clicks on a sale night. Names without prices are wasted real estate.
- 2. **Cut one CTA.** "SUMMER DEALS" and "SUMMER SHOP" side by side dilute each other. Pick one and make it the only button above the fold.
- 3. **Personalize the product grid by category.** If this is the outdoor/active subscriber segment, lead with the YETI, Igloo, and athletic gear — drop the patio armchair.
- 4. **Tighten the subject to under 50 chars.** The "Ends Tonight" signal and the "50% OFF" can coexist in a shorter line.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends tonight: 50% off 4th of July sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last hours. Up to 50% off — tonight only.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New deals added + extra 30% off clearance → see what's in stock`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop online or in-store — same-day delivery still available`

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Stale `availabilityStarts` date in JSON-LD:**

```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```

This value is from **March 6, 2020** — over 6 years before this email's send date. This is a hardcoded template remnant. Gmail uses this field to populate Promotions tab deal cards; the stale date will cause incorrect annotation display or suppression of the deal card entirely.

**Empty JSON-LD fields disable Gmail Promotions enhancements:**

| Field | Value | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| `DiscountOffer.description` | `""` | No deal description in Promotions tab |
| `DiscountOffer.discountCode` | `""` | No promo code shown |
| `DiscountOffer.availabilityEnds` | `""` | No expiry shown (especially damaging for a "Ends Tonight" urgency email) |
| `PromotionCard.image` | `""` | No image carousel in Gmail Promotions |

The `availabilityEnds` being empty is particularly damaging: the subject line drives urgency with "Ends. Tonight." but the structured data provides no end timestamp for Gmail to surface.
