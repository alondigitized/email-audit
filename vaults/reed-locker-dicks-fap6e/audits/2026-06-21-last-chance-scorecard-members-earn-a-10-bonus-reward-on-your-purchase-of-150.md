# 📣 Last chance, ScoreCard members! Earn a $10 Bonus Reward on your purchase of $150+
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- This is the "last chance" reminder for the ScoreCard $10 Bonus Reward offer first pushed on June 20. The structure is correct CRM playbook — if the initial send converts well, a deadline reminder with "last chance" framing earns its place. The hero is clean: ScoreCard branding prominent, $10 figure large, couple in summer athletic wear that reads friendly rather than aspirational. The email then cascades through seven category sections (clothing, drinkware, golf, team sports, outdoor, fan shop, fitness) which is a dense catalog structure but at least every section has a discount headline to anchor the scroll.
- The problem is creative fatigue compounded by occasion mismatch. This is landing on Father's Day — the same day DICK'S sent a dedicated Father's Day email. A ScoreCard member who opened both emails this morning sees two different messages from the same brand on the same day with overlapping category coverage. The "last chance" urgency is real, but the send slot is crowded and the creative is pixel-for-pixel the same as yesterday's June 20 ScoreCard send.
- The offer itself is genuinely strong and honestly presented — $10 on $150+ is a loyalty perk worth knowing about. The email would score higher with a tighter subject line and by not competing with its own Father's Day send on the exact same calendar date.

## What's working

- ScoreCard logo appears in the header alongside the DICK'S brand mark — dual-brand treatment makes membership context clear at a glance.
- Hero "$10 BONUS REWARD" figure is large enough to read at thumbnail size; offer mechanics ($150+ threshold) are immediately below.
- "Last chance" urgency in the subject is earned — this is day 2 of a 2-day window, not manufactured scarcity.
- Seven category sections below the fold give a subscriber multiple reasons to click even if the hero category doesn't match their need.
- No visible render bugs — images load, layout holds, text isn't overlapping.

## What's weak

- Sending a loyalty email on Father's Day, the same day as the dedicated Father's Day send, splits the subscriber's attention and dilutes both messages.
- Subject line is 84 characters — mobile inboxes will truncate after "ScoreCard members!" and the actual offer ($10, $150+) gets swallowed.
- Creative is essentially identical to the June 20 send — a subscriber who opened yesterday sees no reason to open again.
- Catalog-style body (seven categories, equal visual weight) dilutes click energy — there's no single hero product or featured gift idea that commands attention.
- The "SHOP NOW" CTA below the hero is generic; given the Father's Day timing, "Shop Dad's Gifts" would have been easy and on-brief.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Tighten the subject line to fit mobile.** Everything valuable is buried past the truncation point. Lead with the expiration, not the program name.
- **Subject Alt A:** `ScoreCard: Your $10 Reward expires tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last chance — $10 Reward on $150+ ends today`
- 2. **Write a preheader that adds context**, not a repeat of the subject.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop summer gear, fan shop, and more — offer ends tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Pick up Father's Day gifts and earn your $10 Bonus Reward.`
- 3. **Lean into the Father's Day collision rather than ignoring it.** If DICK'S is going to send two emails on June 21, make the ScoreCard email acknowledge the occasion — "Earn your reward on Dad's gift" turns a cadence problem into a reinforcement loop.
- 4. **Collapse seven category sections into three hero categories** with larger imagery. The current layout looks like a weekly circular, not a member-exclusive send. Fewer, larger product features would lift click rates.
- 5. **Add an explicit expiration time.** "Last chance" is vague — "Offer ends tonight at midnight" gives a subscriber a reason to act now rather than bookmark-and-forget.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**HTML is truncated** — physical mailing address and unsubscribe link are not visible in the provided source. CAN-SPAM requires both; cannot confirm or deny from this excerpt.

**JSON-LD contains JavaScript-style comments — this is invalid JSON:**
```json
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
  // Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": ""
}
```
`//` comments are not valid in JSON. Gmail's parser will reject the entire `<script type="application/ld+json">` block, suppressing Promotions tab annotations. These are developer notes that were never stripped before send.

**Stale `availabilityStarts` date:**
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```
Year 2020 — this is a template placeholder that was never updated. The end date is also blank: `"availabilityEnds": ""`.

**`discountCode` and `description` fields empty:**
```json
"description": "",
"discountCode": ""
```
Both left blank in the `DiscountOffer` object. Structured data will not surface a meaningful offer in Gmail even if the JSON were valid.

**`PromotionCard` is not a valid Schema.org type** for Gmail Promotions annotations. The correct Gmail annotation schema uses `DiscountOffer`, `SaleEvent`, or `LimitedAvailability` types, not `PromotionCard`.
