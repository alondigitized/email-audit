# Starts NOW! Up to 50% off DEALS from brands you love 🙌 The Fall Deal Drop is here!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-05**
## Executive summary

- This is a strong, well-organised promo for the "Fall Deal Drop." A big green-and-red "UP TO 50% OFF" hero sits over a moody sky photo. Below it, "3 Days of Savings" and a Shop Now button give a clear deadline of 10/7/26 at 11:59 PM PT. The render has no visible bugs. The ScoreCard $10/$20 bonus reward block adds a real loyalty angle.
- The body then turns into a long catalog scroll. It has eight or more modules: shoes, clothing, drinkware, baseball, golf, a 70% clearance banner, and product picks. Each gets its own "Shop Now" button. Dick's headline is "up to 50%," but the body shows 55%, 60%, $400 off and 70% off. That muddies the one-offer story. Nothing in the body tells a subscriber why to click one module over another.
- Against the recent history, this lands as another big-number deals send. Dick's put out "Up to 50% OFF this week's deals" on 10/3 and 10/4, plus two "$25 & under" sends. This one adds the "Fall Deal Drop" branding, and I'd expect it to be skimmed as the fifth deals email in four days. It's competent but unremarkable.

## What's working

- The hero is loud and legible: "UP TO 50% OFF" in red, "FALL DEAL DROP" in bold green and white. The eye lands there first.
- "Starts Now" and "3 Days of Savings" give a credible end date (10/7, 11:59 PM PT) in the copy under the hero.
- The ScoreCard $10 / ScoreCard+ $20 bonus reward block is a clear, differentiated member perk, with "Get Coupon" and "Join Now" buttons.
- The category chips (Men's, Women's, Kids', Shoes, Team Sports, Fan Shop) give quick navigation near the top.
- The product picks near the bottom (adidas Samba, New Balance 530, Nike Fleece) link to specific items, so they carry real purchase intent.

## What's weak

- The email is very long, with about ten "Shop Now" buttons. The Fall Deal Drop story gets diluted as you scroll.
- The discount tiers are inconsistent: 50%, 55%, 60%, 70% and $400 off. The "up to 50%" hero doesn't match the 70% banner further down.
- The "Shop Now" button under the hero is small and plain, compared with the size of the hero art.
- The fine print is dense, and the "select styles / quantities limited" caveats are easy to miss.
- It continues a daily deals cadence (10/2 through 10/5) with the same "up to 50% off" language, so it risks reading as a repeat.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the body to three or four modules, each with a distinct offer. Lead with the ScoreCard bonus, since it is the only truly differentiated hook. Drop the duplicate category modules.
- 2. Make the primary CTA bigger and more specific, such as "Shop Fall Deals" instead of "Shop Now." Use the same copy at the top and bottom of the email.
- 3. Unify the headline offer. Either lead with "up to 70% off" or move the 70% clearance block up to match the hero.
- 4. Move the product picks up, right after the hero, because those are the most clickable items.
- 5. Space out sends so a major event like the Fall Deal Drop doesn't follow the same "up to 50% off" message by a day.

## Technical Audit

# Technical Audit: DICK'S Sporting Goods, "Fall Deal Drop"

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML is structurally sound: `lang="en"`, `role="presentation"` layout tables, MSO conditionals, a hidden preheader and a viewport meta tag. The one QA FAIL is a 9-character plain-text part. The remaining findings are missing `alt` attributes on 10 images plus the open pixel, and several header checks that couldn't be evaluated because of the relay. The HTML was truncated, so the footer, unsubscribe link and later links were not available for review.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All observed links go through a click-tracking redirect, for example `https://data.em.dcsg.com/ee/v1/mclick?params=b.v1...&pid=...`. The redirect domain `data.em.dcsg.com` matches the sending domain `em.dcsg.com`, which is good for alignment.
- The open pixel is `https://data.em.dcsg.com/ee/v1/open?params=...`. It has no `alt`, which is minor. See section 3.
- The QA probe covered 12 material links and skipped 54. No probe failures were reported. The 54 skipped links (footer, utility and social) were not verified, so I can't confirm they resolve.
- The link count is high, at about 66 links. That is not a defect on its own, but it increases the unverified surface.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (QA WARN), 10 images plus the open pixel:**
  - Content images: `shoescleats21005`, `clothing1005`, `hydration1005`, `bballgear1005`, `golfclubs1005`, all from `s7d6.scene7.com/is/image/DSGAEMSites/...`.
  - Icons: `icon-vc-sms-1`, `icon-vc-sportsmatter`, `icon-vc-bpg-1`, `icon-vc-favorite-1`, `icon-location-2025-1`.
  - If the content images are wrapped in `<a>` tags without adjacent text, those links have no accessible name. I could not confirm this from the truncated source.
  - If the icons are decorative, they should carry `alt=""`. A missing attribute makes screen readers announce the filename.
- **Image weight:** the category images request `wid=1200&hei=1600&qlt=100` for a 584–600px layout. That is roughly 2x the needed width at maximum JPEG quality, which risks slow loads on mobile and Gmail clipping. I did not measure the actual file sizes.
- **Plain-text part:** see section 5.
- **Positives confirmed in source:** `<title>`, the viewport meta, `font-display:swap` on the web fonts with an Arial fallback, and `mso-hide:all` on the preheader spacer.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The visible source shows no unresolved tokens such as `{{...}}`, `%%...%%` or `[FIRST_NAME]`. The truncated portion could not be checked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **[FAIL] Plain-text fallback is 9 characters.** This is a real deliverability and accessibility defect if it reflects the sent message. A near-empty text/plain part against a rich HTML part is a spam-score signal. It also leaves text-only clients and some assistive tools with nothing. It could also be an AgentMail capture artifact, so verify against the raw MIME.
- **[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found.** The QA note says the relay may strip these headers. Treat this as unverified, not confirmed missing. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require one-click unsubscribe, so check the raw headers on the original delivery.
- **[WARN] `Authentication-Results` not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown because of the relay. Nothing can be concluded either way.
- **Unsubscribe link and physical address:** not visible in the truncated HTML, so not assessed. The QA tool says an unsubscribe link is always probed, but no probe result was reported.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Destination URLs and UTM parameters are hidden inside the opaque `mclick?params=b.v1...` redirect payloads. I could not evaluate UTM naming or landing-page alignment from the source.
- No probe failures were reported for the 12 probed links, but final destinations and status codes were not provided.
- No issues can be confirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Check the raw MIME for the text/plain part. If it really is 9 characters, generate a proper plain-text alternative with the key links and offer copy.
2. Add `alt` text to the 5 category images, with descriptive text such as "Shoes & cleats". Add `alt=""` to the 5 decorative icons and to the open pixel.
3. Reduce the Scene7 requests to about `wid=1200` for 2x retina at 600px, or `wid=600` if appropriate, and lower `qlt` to around 80.
4. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, plus SPF, DKIM and DMARC, from a direct (non-relay) delivery or the original headers before treating these as defects.
5. Re-run the audit on the full, untruncated HTML. Probe the unsubscribe link and the footer address. Resolve the redirect chains to confirm UTM parameters and landing pages.
