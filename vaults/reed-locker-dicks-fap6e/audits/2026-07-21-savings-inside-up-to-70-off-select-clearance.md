# Savings inside ➡️ Up to 70% OFF select clearance
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S fifth clearance email since July 18 and it shows in every editorial decision. The creative is stitched from two already-used campaigns — Summer Clearance (70% off) and Nike Deal Days (30% off) — crammed together under a subject line that leads with "Savings inside," one of the most recycled phrases in promotional email. The hero lands clean: a bold red badge, "UP TO 70% OFF," dominant visual hierarchy. But the email rapidly falls apart into a category dump that runs drinkware → soccer balls → golf bags → cycling gear → a Sports Mom lifestyle section, with no through-line or urgency signal tying any of it together.
- The Nike Deal Days module is the most damaging thing in this send. That campaign debuted July 19, was strong enough to score 8/10 as a standalone, and has now been recycled for a third consecutive day — buried mid-scroll inside a clearance email. Inserting 30% off Nike into a 70% off clearance email doesn't amplify either; it undermines both.
- The email checks the production boxes — renders cleanly, CTA buttons are present, seasonal relevance exists — but it reads like an automated slot-filler rather than a campaign. A subscriber who opened all four prior sends this week will experience near-zero lift from this one.

## What's working

- **Hero visual hierarchy is correct:** the red 70% OFF badge is unambiguous and eye-catching; no guessing what the email is about.
- **Category rail (Men / Women / Kids / See All) appears immediately** below the hero — easy browse shortcut for shoppers who already know they want clearance.
- **Multiple price-specific modules** (40% off drinkware, 40% off soccer balls, $200-and-under golf bags) give category-focused shoppers a concrete reason to click into their area.
- **Sports Mom section with lifestyle imagery** is seasonally appropriate for July and breaks the monotony of product-on-white-background stacks.

## What's weak

- **Subject line leads with "Savings inside ➡️"** — generic spam-bait language that wastes the most valuable real estate before the discount claim.
- **Nike Deal Days recycled for day three:** the 30% off Nike module appeared in standalone emails July 19 and 20 (both scored 8/10); embedding it mid-scroll in a clearance blast strips it of focus and makes the cadence exhaustion visible.
- **No deadline anywhere in the subject, preheader, or hero copy** — "up to 70% off" with no urgency anchor is just a standing menu, not a campaign.
- **Email length is excessive:** drinkware, soccer balls, golf bags, cycling, sports lifestyle, and a summer product grid all in one send with no editorial restraint — the "Sports Mom" section in particular feels like an unrelated audience insert.
- **Preheader appears to be leaking view-in-browser junk** rather than real copy, squandering the secondary inbox line.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill "Savings inside" from the subject entirely.** It adds zero information and reads as spam. Lead with the actual offer. The arrow emoji is also doing nothing — drop it.
- 2. **Give the clearance a deadline.** "Ends Sunday" or "48 Hours Left" turns a passive browse into a reason to act now.
- 3. **Separate Nike Deal Days back into its own send or drop it from this one.** A 30% off Nike module inside a 70% off clearance email is confusing and dilutes the Nike brand hook that made the July 19 standalone work.
- 4. **Tighten the module count to three max.** Pick the highest-performing categories by click rate; cut drinkware, soccer balls, golf, AND cycling from the same email. That's four separate campaigns in one scroll.
- 5. **Add a ScoreCard / loyalty callout** — "Members earn double points on clearance through Sunday" would tick the loyalty signal box and reward subscribers who haven't churned despite the cadence.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 70% Off Clearance — Ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Summer Clearance: 70% off shoes, apparel & more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Hundreds of styles slashed — shop before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Grab it now: Nike, adidas, and more at clearance prices.`

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**LD+JSON DiscountOffer block — empty/unfilled fields (confirmed):**
```json
"@type": "DiscountOffer",
"description": "",
"discountCode": "",
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00",
"availabilityEnds": ""
```
- `description` and `discountCode` are empty strings — appear to be unfilled template placeholders.
- `availabilityEnds` is empty — invalid for the Gmail Promotions annotation spec, which requires this field for deal expiry display.
- `availabilityStarts` is hardcoded to **2020-03-06** — a date six years in the past. Almost certainly a stale copy-paste from an old template.

**LD+JSON PromotionCard — empty image field (confirmed):**
```json
"@type": "PromotionCard",
"image": ""
```
Gmail uses this field to display a thumbnail in the Promotions tab. An empty value means no card image renders; the email loses the visual slot in Gmail's native deal carousel.

### 5. Compliance

**Invalid JSON with JavaScript-style comments (confirmed spec violation):**

The `<script type="application/ld+json">` block contains JS single-line comments, which are **not valid JSON** per RFC 8259:
```
// GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
// Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
```
Strict JSON parsers (including Google's schema.org ingestion pipeline) will reject this block entirely, silently dropping all Gmail Promotions annotations. The comments appear to be designer instructions that were never stripped before send.

**CAN-SPAM / unsubscribe** — source is truncated before the footer; physical address and unsubscribe link presence cannot be confirmed from the provided extract. Must be verified on full source.

**Sender domain** — `From: DSG@e.dcsg.com`. The `e.dcsg.com` subdomain is the ESP sending domain. SPF/DKIM/DMARC authentication for this domain cannot be assessed from HTML source alone — requires header inspection.
