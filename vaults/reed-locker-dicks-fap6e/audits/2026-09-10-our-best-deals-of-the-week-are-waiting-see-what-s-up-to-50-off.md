# Our best deals of the week are waiting... See what's up to 50% off!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-10**
## Executive summary

- This is the same green-water-bottles/treadmill hero DICK'S ran on 9/8 ("📫 Delivered: Up to 50% off this week's DEALS"), now recombined with the green-and-white t-shirt "UP TO 50% OFF" red card that's shown up repeatedly in the maroon-stadium rotation. Nothing about this send is new — it's a recycled creative shuffle with a vague "Best Deals of the Week" subject that doesn't name a single product, category, or dollar figure beyond the generic 50%.
- Below the fold it's the usual kitchen-sink catalog crawl: shoes, clothing, HydroJug, FootJoy golf, soccer gear, cardio equipment, then a second "UP TO 60% OFF SELECT CLEARANCE SCORES" banner that's been the closer in nearly every recent send. The email is competently built with no render bugs, but there is zero differentiation from the last five DICK'S sends in this history — it reads as filler between the real urgency-driven sends (9/7's "ends tonight" email scored highest for a reason).
- Judgement: functional but forgettable — a placeholder send in a fatigued cadence, not one that earns an open on its own merits.

## What's working

- Clean layout, no broken images or overlapping text anywhere in the render.
- Clear top-level CTA hierarchy: hero "Best Deals of the Week" + green "Shop Now" button is the first thing the eye lands on.
- Category picker row (Men's/Women's/Kids'/Shoes/Fan Shop/Team Sports) gives multiple easy entry points.
- Specific product-level offers are called out further down (Up to 30% Off Shoes, $50 and Under Clothing, $30 Off FootJoy Golf Shoes) which is more concrete than the vague hero.
- Loyalty program visible up top (ScoreCard, "300 Points = $10 Reward") for member relevance.

## What's weak

- Subject line is generic filler — "Our best deals of the week are waiting" says nothing concrete, unlike 9/7's "ends tonight" urgency framing that actually worked.
- Hero creative (green water bottles/treadmill + red 50% OFF card) is a straight repeat of assets already used 9/8, adding to the sense this brand cycles the same 4-5 images endlessly.
- No real urgency or deadline anywhere in the body — just "while quantities last" boilerplate.
- Extremely long, undifferentiated scroll (10+ banners) dilutes the "best deals" promise — everything can't be the best deal.
- Second 60% OFF clearance banner mid-email competes with and confuses the primary 50% OFF hero — two "best" offers in one email.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace the generic subject with a specific anchor offer or category — vague "best deals" subjects consistently underperform the urgency-framed ones in this brand's own history (9/7 scored best with "ends tonight").
- 2. Cut the email in half — 10+ product banners past a 60% clearance banner is fatigue-inducing; lead with 2-3 tightly curated offers instead.
- 3. Retire the green-bottles/treadmill hero for a few sends — it was just used 9/8, reuse this fast reads as low effort to a daily subscriber.
- 4. Pick one hero offer (either the 50% or the 60% clearance) and make the rest of the email support it, rather than running two "top" banners.
- 5. Add a real countdown or end-date if one exists — copy this brand already knows works (9/7 send).

3. Investigate the 9-character plain-text MIME part — this is effectively a missing text/plain fallback and should be regenerated from the HTML content for spam-filter and accessibility-tool compatibility.
4. Verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment directly at the sending MTA (e.g. via a raw-header capture or mail-tester.com) since `Authentication-Results` wasn't observable through this relay.
