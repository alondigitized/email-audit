# HUNDREDS of new clearance styles just dropped!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- This is the second "DICK'S | Clearance" sub-brand send in three days — May 27's "100s of NEW clearance styles await!" scored 7/10, and this one is structurally nearly identical: same red hero, same multi-lane category grid, same appended "Up to 50% Off / This Week's Deals" module bolted to the bottom. The specific items have rotated (running shoes at 40% off, Columbia swim at 45%, Movement Tees at 25%, a sunscreen BOGO, folding chairs, a Nike section), but the creative strategy is a copy of the prior send.
- The biggest structural problem is dual-message confusion. The email opens as a clearance-specific drop from the clearance sub-brand, then ends with a "This Week's Deals — Up to 50% Off" module that reads like a recycled block from the main DICK'S weekly send. A subscriber who got yesterday's main DICK'S "Up to 50% off" email will recognize that footer as the same template. It undermines the clearance sub-brand's "fresh drop" positioning.
- The specific lane discounts — 40%, 25%, 45% — are better than the generic "Up to 50% off" messaging the main brand has been hammering all week, and the "just dropped" subject creates a beat of genuine newness. But until DICK'S decouples the clearance brand from the main sale module, this send will always feel like two different emails stitched together.

## What's working

- The red hero is bold and immediate — the eye lands on "HUNDREDS OF NEW CLEARANCE STYLES" with zero ambiguity about what this email is for.
- Specific lane discounts (40% off running shoes, 45% off Columbia swim, BOGO sunscreen) are far more credible than the vague "up to 50% off everything" framing the main brand uses.
- The category nav chips (Men / Women / Kids / All Styles) below the hero give quick exit ramps without cluttering the hero section.
- "DICK'S | Clearance" sender name is clean sub-brand differentiation from the main DSG list — recognizable but distinct.

## What's weak

- The "Up to 50% Off / This Week's Deals" module appended at the bottom contradicts the clearance email's identity — feels like a copy-pasted block from the main DICK'S sends.
- "Just dropped" urgency in the subject has no supporting deadline or end-date anywhere in the body; the tension evaporates.
- The BOGO sunscreen and folding chair lanes feel off-brand for a clearance apparel/footwear drop — dilutes focus.
- This is the second near-identical clearance email in 72 hours; the "fresh drop" positioning loses credibility on repeat.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the "This Week's Deals" 50% module entirely.** The clearance email should be self-contained. If the main sale deserves mention, link to it — don't duplicate a full email module.
- 2. **Add a deadline.** "Just dropped" is meaningless without "ends Sunday" or "while supplies last, sizes going fast." Even a soft deadline lifts click urgency.
- 3. **Lead with the strongest specific offer** (45% off Columbia swim or 40% off running shoes) in the subject — not the vague "hundreds of styles" count.
- 4. **Consolidate the two-column lanes.** Sunscreen and folding chairs can go — tighten to three high-conversion lanes: footwear, apparel, outdoor gear.
- 5. **Preheader needs to work harder.** "Fresh Deals You Don't Want to Miss" is filler; use the preheader to name a category or call out the best deal.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Clearance drop: 40–45% off shoes, swim & more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Running shoes, swim, tees — all marked down. Sizes going fast.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Columbia swim 45% off · Running shoes 40% off · Today only`

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods Clearance Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses Epsilon/Harmony infrastructure with standard redirect-based click tracking and an open pixel. The most significant technical defect is a malformed `application/ld+json` block that will fail JSON parsing entirely, breaking Gmail Promotions tab card rendering.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **Open pixel present** — `<img src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019e7337c5e2c52d2f6e96c66058/...'` in `#ssl` td. Standard; no issue.
- **Click tracking** — all hrefs route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...` with a `__dU__` parameter. Redirect chain is present and functional based on URL structure.
- **UTM parameters** — not visible on any tracked URL in the available source. Epsilon's redirect wrapper captures internal click data, but downstream analytics (GA4, etc.) will receive no campaign attribution unless UTMs are appended inside the redirect destination. Confirm UTMs are injected server-side at redirect resolution time.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **MSO/VML conditionals** present — `border-collapse`, font fallbacks, and `OfficeDocumentSettings` block are correctly structured for Outlook.
- **`role="presentation"` on layout tables** — correctly applied.
- **`lang="en"` on `<html>`** — present; good.
- **Tracking pixel `alt` attribute** — the hidden open pixel has no `alt=""`. Not a real-world issue since it's `display:none`, but some linters will flag it.
- **Logo `<img>` `alt` attribute** — source is truncated before the `alt` value can be confirmed. Verify it is not empty for screen reader and image-blocked clients.
- **Google Fonts loaded via `@media screen` guard** — correctly scoped; webmail clients that ignore `@media` won't attempt the external font fetch.
- **`box-sizing:border-box` on `<table>` elements** — technically valid but non-standard on table elements; Outlook ignores it. No rendering defect expected, but it's noise.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

- **`id="header-non-sc-member"`** — indicates conditional content segmentation by loyalty status. No unreplaced tokens (e.g. `{{firstName}}`, `[FIRST_NAME]`) are visible in the available source.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

- **UTM coverage unconfirmed** — see §2. If UTMs are absent from redirect destinations, clearance campaign traffic will be misattributed as direct/none in site analytics.

---

### 7. Recommendations

**P0 — Fix before send:**

1. **Invalid JSON-LD block** — the `<script type="application/ld+json">` block contains JavaScript-style single-line comments (`// GIF & WEBP images...`, `// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!`). Comments are not valid JSON. This causes the entire schema block to fail parsing, which means Gmail Promotions tab card features (logo badge, deal highlight) will not render. Strip all `//` comment lines.

2. **Empty `PromotionCard.image`** — `"image": ""` means even after fixing the JSON parse error, Gmail will not display a card thumbnail. Populate with the actual promo image URL (the comment says 538×138 px, 3.9 aspect ratio).

3. **Stale `availabilityStarts` date** — `"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"` is a 6-year-old placeholder from the template. Update to the actual campaign start date/time.

**P1 — Fix before next send cycle:**

4. **Empty `DiscountOffer` fields** — `description`, `discountCode`, and `availabilityEnds` are all `""`. If no discount code applies, the entire `DiscountOffer` object should be removed rather than sent with blank fields, which can trigger schema validation warnings in Gmail.

5. **Confirm UTM injection** at redirect resolution; add `utm_source=email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=clearance-drop-YYYYMMDD` if not already server-side appended.
