# Holiday Deal Dash ends tomorrow! 🚨 Score up to 50% off before you miss it...
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-02**
## Executive summary

- This is the third DICK'S "Holiday Deal Dash" send in about 48 hours, and it does the job without surprising anyone. The red argyle hero with a giant gold "UP TO 50% OFF" lands first. Below it sit "Holiday Deal Dash: Up to 50% Off" and a red Shop Now button. A "Two Days Only" tag and the header line "Order Today. Sport It Tomorrow." add urgency. The sale ends 11/3 at 11:59 PM PT, so "ends tomorrow" is credible.
- The body is long and repetitive. A second hero, "UP TO 70% OFF" clearance, repeats the 11/2 clearance email. Then come running shoes, fleece, drinkware, golf, racquet gear, and a third "50% OFF" green banner near the bottom. That is three big percentage blocks in one email. The product grid at the end (Samba OG, Nike fleece, socks) is the only place with real merchandise, and it sits far down the scroll.
- My judgement is solid but undifferentiated. The offer is clear and the CTA is unambiguous. The email gives no reason to choose it over the "Flash Sale" and "Deal Dash" sends already sitting in the inbox. It fits the brand's 7/10 pattern.

## What's working

- The gold-on-red "UP TO 50% OFF" hero is the unmistakable first read, with a red Shop Now button directly beneath it.
- "Two Days Only" and the 11/3 deadline in the fine print give real, credible time pressure.
- The category pill row (Men's, Women's, Kids', Shoes, Fan Shop, Holiday Shop) lets shoppers skip to their own category.
- Specific sub-offers like "$50 Off Select FootJoy HyperFlex Golf Shoes" and "Up to 40% Off Select Running Shoes" give concrete reasons to click.
- The header strip shows the ScoreCard points and reward balance, which surfaces loyalty status.

## What's weak

- The "Up to 50% Off" message appears in the hero, the sweatshirts and fleece tile, and the bottom banner, which is repetitive and dilutes the hero.
- The 70% clearance block repeats the 11/2 "up to 70% OFF" send, so this email feels like a recycled mashup.
- The email is very long, with seven or more content modules. The real products in the "Check out these picks" grid come too late.
- Several CTAs are generic "Shop Now" buttons, and the secondary banners have no specific verb and noun.
- The hero has no product imagery, just a number on a pattern, so there's nothing to make you want a specific item.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the body in half. Keep one hero, one "Up to 70% clearance" strip, and one product grid. Drop the duplicate 50% banner at the bottom.
- 2. Move the product grid up, directly under the hero, so the first scroll shows real shoes and apparel.
- 3. Make the deadline literal in the hero: "Ends tonight 11:59 PM PT" instead of "Two Days Only", which is stale when the send says "ends tomorrow".
- 4. Replace the generic "Shop Now" buttons with specific copy, such as "Shop Running Shoes" or "Shop Golf Deals".
- 5. Give each send in the series a distinct angle. Three Deal Dash emails in two days should escalate (last chance, final hours) rather than reuse the same creative.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last call: up to 50% off ends tomorrow ⏰`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Deal Dash ends tomorrow: shoes, fleece & more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Up to 40% off running shoes, $50 off FootJoy golf shoes, and drinkware deals. Ends 11/3 at 11:59 PM PT.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus up to 70% off clearance. Order today, pick up tomorrow.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Confirmed (QA and source):** The open pixel is `<img ... width="1" height="1">` with no `alt` attribute. Add `alt=""` and `style="display:block;border:0"`.
- **Missing alt (QA-flagged, not verifiable in the truncated source):**
  - `2026-1102-dks-storewid-4pm-1`, `-2` and `-3` (scene7, `wid=1200&hei=1600`)
  - `banner4.gif`
  - `icon-vc-sms-1`, `icon-vc-sportsmatter`, `icon-vc-bpg-1`, `icon-vc-favorite-1`, `icon-location-2025-1`
- **Why it matters:** The `storewid` images are 1200×1600 and appear to be content-bearing. With images blocked, or with a screen reader, their offer text is lost. Decorative icons should use `alt=""` and meaningful ones should have descriptive text.
- **Minor:** `<link rel="icon" ...>` in the head has no function in email clients and can be removed.
- **Fine:** The viewport meta tag, the mobile media query (max-width:480px), `font-display:swap` with an Arial fallback, and the hidden preheader (`#pht-spacer` with `mso-hide:all`) all look correct.
