# 💸 EXTRA 25% off select clearance starts now! 💸
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S ninth-plus promotional send in roughly four days — and it leads with a clearance overlay that we've seen at least three times this week. The hero is serviceable: a bold "EXTRA 25% OFF" on a red/dark background communicates the offer immediately, and the clearance framing is at least a legitimate price signal. But the email loses discipline fast. Below the fold it becomes a cascade of competing modules — Nike clothing, "Get One Free" golf balls, Coleman snap-'n-go containers, Nishiki bikes, MLB® clothing, and a Memorial Day Event callout — with no coherent narrative threading them together.
- The Memorial Day Event block buried midway down is the most interesting thing here. DICK'S has spent five days running the Memorial Day campaign as the hero (including the strong May 18 anchor email), but today they've demoted it to a supporting module inside a clearance send. That's a brand inconsistency: the campaign that earned credibility gets used as filler.
- Bottom line: competent render, real offer, zero audience focus. A subscriber who opened the May 18 Memorial Day email expecting a coherent sale event gets a clearance grab-bag with a holiday callout as an afterthought.

## What's working

- Hero delivers the offer in under two seconds — "EXTRA 25% OFF Select Clearance" is impossible to miss.
- No render bugs: images load, text is legible, layout holds without overlap.
- "Shop Now" CTA buttons appear consistently beneath each module, reducing friction for scanners.
- The Explore More Deals category pills (Nike, Women's, Kids) give a fast filtering signal for a subscriber who already knows their category.
- The Memorial Day Event block, even buried, gives subscribers a second reason to click if the clearance framing doesn't land.

## What's weak

- Six-plus competing modules destroy hierarchy — the eye has no place to rest after the hero.
- Memorial Day Event is demoted to filler, undermining the campaign anchor that DICK'S spent five days building.
- "Select clearance" fine-print caveat on the hero offer softens what should be a crisp deal signal.
- The category pills (Nike / Women's / Kids) under "Explore More Deals" are too small and generic to drive real segmentation value.
- Cadence fatigue is palpable — this is at least the fourth clearance-framed send since May 17; a loyal subscriber has seen this frame repeatedly this week.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill four of the six lower modules.** Pick one story — clearance OR Memorial Day Event — and run it clean. The multi-module cascade dilutes every offer.
- 2. **Restore Memorial Day as the tent-pole, not the clearance grab.** The May 18 anchor email earned trust; a sub-send should reinforce it, not bury it under a clearance stack.
- 3. **Make "Select" do real work.** Name the specific categories that qualify (footwear, apparel, equipment) so the hero offer feels concrete instead of fine-print-hedged.
- 4. **Replace generic "Shop Now" buttons with category-specific copy** ("Shop Clearance Footwear", "Shop Bikes on Sale") to lift click intent.
- 5. **Rest the clearance frame for at least 48 hours.** Four sends in five days with identical framing is where unsubscribes accelerate.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Footwear, apparel & gear — extra savings on already-marked-down prices`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods Clearance Email

### 1. Technical Summary
The email uses a standard Epsilon/Harmony delivery stack with MSO conditional comments and a responsive mobile breakpoint. Two high-severity issues exist in the Gmail structured data block: invalid JSON syntax and a stale 2020 timestamp that will silently suppress Gmail inbox annotations.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Click tracking:** All CTA links route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610000019e4a2c676c9b4e6d6e9666b528/...` with an encoded `__dU__` payload. The final destination URLs are opaque from the source — cannot confirm UTM parameters survive the redirect without a live click trace.

**Open pixel:** Tracking pixel at `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019e4a2c676c9b4e6d6e9666b528/804e9a415b564bec00004c5a42963aa1` is correctly hidden (`display:none; max-height:0px; mso-hide:all`). No issue.

**Logo anchor:** `href="https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/..."` — logo click tracked correctly.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Preheader suppression (low):** The preheader text `+ Up to 20% off select Nike clothing & shoes...` lives inside a `<td>` with `font-size:0; height:0` applied to the parent. This works in most clients but is less reliable than a dedicated `overflow:hidden; max-height:0; max-width:0` wrapper span. Risk: visible bleed in some older Android Gmail app versions.

**Image alt attributes:** The logo `<img id="logo" src="https://dsg.images.harmony.epsilon…"` tag is truncated before its `alt` attribute is visible. If `alt` is empty or missing, images-off rendering (Outlook, corporate clients) will show a blank logo area with no fallback label.

**MSO conditional tables:** Present and correctly scoped with `<!--[if (gte mso)|(IE)]>` guards. `border-collapse:collapse` reset included. No issue.

**`role="presentation"`:** Applied to layout tables. Correct.

**`lang="en"`:** Set on `<html>`. Correct.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Critical — Stale structured data timestamp:** The `DiscountOffer` block contains a hardcoded `availabilityStarts` of `"2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"` — a date from six years ago. Gmail uses this to render inbox promotion tab annotations; a past start date with no `availabilityEnds` will cause the offer card to be suppressed or display incorrectly.

```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00",
"availabilityEnds": ""
```

**Critical — Empty merge fields in structured data:** Three fields that should be populated at send time are blank:
- `"description": ""` — offer description for Gmail annotation
- `"discountCode": ""` — promo code (blank is acceptable if none, but should be omitted, not empty string)
- `"image": ""` — `PromotionCard` image URL; empty means no card image renders

These should be populated by the ESP template engine at send time or removed from the payload entirely.

---

### 5. Compliance

**Invalid JSON in `<script type="application/ld+json">`:** The structured data block contains JavaScript-style single-line comments (`//`), which are **not valid JSON**:

```js
// GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
// Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
```

JSON parsers — including Gmail's — will reject this block entirely, disabling all inbox annotations. These comments must be removed before send.

**CAN-SPAM / unsubscribe:** Footer is not present in the truncated source. Verification required: physical mailing address, unsubscribe link, and list-unsubscribe header (`List-Unsubscribe: <mailto:...>, <https://...>`) must all be present.

**Authentication headers:** DKIM, SPF, and DMARC records cannot be verified from HTML source alone. Epsilon-managed sends (`e.dcsg.com`) should have these configured at the DNS level — confirm with delivery team.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All outbound links pass through Epsilon's redirect layer (`app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/`). Without decoding the `__dU__` payload or tracing a live click, it is not possible to confirm:
- Whether UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content`) are appended to the final destination URL
- Whether clearance landing pages match the "EXTRA 25% off" offer context

**Action required:** Click at least one CTA link in a test render, copy the final redirect URL, and confirm UTM params are present and correctly attributed (e.g., `utm_campaign=clearance-extra25`).
