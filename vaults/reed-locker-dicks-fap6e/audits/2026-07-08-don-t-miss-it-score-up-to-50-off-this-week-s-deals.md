# Don't miss it! Score up to 50% OFF this week's DEALS. 💰
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S third consecutive "This Week's Deals" format send in two days — July 7 already had two of them — and this one arrives with a worse offer. After spending July 5–6 hammering "up to 70% off," the brand has now de-escalated to "up to 50% off," which is objectively a step backward in perceived value. A subscriber who opened the July 6 "FINAL HOURS — 70% off" send and is now seeing "50% off" will clock the regression immediately. The subject line even tries to inject urgency ("Don't miss it!") but there's no countdown, no deadline, and no new hook to justify re-engagement twenty-four hours later.
- The creative execution inside the email is competent. The layout is clean, the offers are specific and varied — running shoes, CALIA swim, VRST resort, golf clubs, electronic grills, pickleball paddles, summer gear — and the Summer Shop tie-in is seasonally appropriate. The visual hierarchy works: 50% OFF hero banner at top, tabbed category navigation below, individual deal cards beneath that. No broken images, no layout collapse.
- The problem is entirely above the fold — in the inbox. This email is asking subscribers to open a nearly identical send to what they received yesterday, from a brand that already sent seven or more promotional emails this week, with a lower headline offer than the previous cycle. The content is fine; the premise is burned.

## What's working

- Hero banner leads with a concrete, legible "UP TO 50% OFF" — offer is unmissable
- Tabbed category nav (SHOES / WOMEN'S / MEN'S / BRANDS / THE SUMMER SHOP) is a smart way to help different shoppers self-select without cluttering the main scroll
- Each deal card carries a specific discount (40% off shoes, 75% off CALIA swim, 25% off VRST, $100 off golf clubs, $50 off grills, $25.89 pickleball paddles) — not vague "big savings" language
- Summer Shop section anchors the email to the season and gives a coherent theme beyond just discount depth
- Clean render with no visible bugs, broken images, or layout issues

## What's weak

- Offer de-escalates from last week's 70% off cycle to 50% off — subscribers who noticed the prior sends will feel the step backward, not forward
- Subject line tops 56 characters and splits its energy between "Don't miss it!" filler and the actual offer; the opening four words are wasted real estate
- "DEALS" in all-caps in the subject line reads as a mild spam signal and adds to the inbox noise problem
- No urgency mechanism inside the body — no countdown timer (which the July 6 send used effectively), no "ends [specific day]" callout, no stock scarcity — so the "Don't miss it!" in the subject is a promise the email body doesn't keep
- App download block and social media footer occupy meaningful vertical space that could carry another deal card or a clear deadline callout

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline and restore countdown timer.** The July 6 send proved the timer works. "Deals end Sunday at midnight" with a visible timer would turn "Don't miss it!" from noise into a credible call to action. Without it, there's no reason to click today versus tomorrow.
- 2. **Collapse the subject to a single, punchy beat.** Cut the filler intro entirely; lead with the sharpest offer in the email.
- **Subject Alt A:** `This week's deals: 75% off CALIA, 40% off shoes & more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Score up to 50% off — deals end Sunday`
- 3. **Preheader should deliver what the subject teases.** If the subject promises deals, the preheader should name the best single offer or category pair — not repeat "this week's deals."
- **Preheader Alt A:** `CALIA swim, running shoes, golf clubs, pickleball — all marked down`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your weekend deals: 75% off swim, $100 off clubs, $25 paddles`
- 4. **Lead with the sharpest offer, not the most generic one.** "UP TO 50% OFF" is the weakest headline in the email — 75% off CALIA swim is the actual best deal visible. Promote that to the hero or at minimum the first deal card.
- 5. **Cut the app store block and social footer.** Swap the screen real estate for an expiration callout or a featured product with a direct link.

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This is DICK'S third consecutive "This Week's Deals" format send in two days — July 7 already had two of them — and this one arrives with a worse offer. After spending July 5–6 hammering "up to 70% off," the brand has now de-escalated to "up to 50% off," which is objectively a step backward in perceived value. A subscriber who opened the July 6 "FINAL HOURS — 70% off" send and is now seeing "50% off" will clock the regression immediately. The subject line even tries to inject urgency ("Don't miss it!") but there's no countdown, no deadline, and no new hook to justify re-engagement twenty-four hours later.

The creative execution inside the email is competent. The layout is clean, the offers are specific and varied — running shoes, CALIA swim, VRST resort, golf clubs, electronic grills, pickleball paddles, summer gear — and the Summer Shop tie-in is seasonally appropriate. The visual hierarchy works: 50% OFF hero banner at top, tabbed category navigation below, individual deal cards beneath that. No broken images, no layout collapse.

The problem is entirely above the fold — in the inbox. This email is asking subscribers to open a nearly identical send to what they received yesterday, from a brand that already sent seven or more promotional emails this week, with a lower headline offer than the previous cycle. The content is fine; the premise is burned.

## 2. What worked

- Hero banner leads with a concrete, legible "UP TO 50% OFF" — offer is unmissable
- Tabbed category nav (SHOES / WOMEN'S / MEN'S / BRANDS / THE SUMMER SHOP) is a smart way to help different shoppers self-select without cluttering the main scroll
- Each deal card carries a specific discount (40% off shoes, 75% off CALIA swim, 25% off VRST, $100 off golf clubs, $50 off grills, $25.89 pickleball paddles) — not vague "big savings" language
- Summer Shop section anchors the email to the season and gives a coherent theme beyond just discount depth
- Clean render with no visible bugs, broken images, or layout issues

## 3. What didn't

- Offer de-escalates from last week's 70% off cycle to 50% off — subscribers who noticed the prior sends will feel the step backward, not forward
- Subject line tops 56 characters and splits its energy between "Don't miss it!" filler and the actual offer; the opening four words are wasted real estate
- "DEALS" in all-caps in the subject line reads as a mild spam signal and adds to the inbox noise problem
- No urgency mechanism inside the body — no countdown timer (which the July 6 send used effectively), no "ends [specific day]" callout, no stock scarcity — so the "Don't miss it!" in the subject is a promise the email body doesn't keep
- App download block and social media footer occupy meaningful vertical space that could carry another deal card or a clear deadline callout

## 4. What I'd change

1. **Add a hard deadline and restore countdown timer.** The July 6 send proved the timer works. "Deals end Sunday at midnight" with a visible timer would turn "Don't miss it!" from noise into a credible call to action. Without it, there's no reason to click today versus tomorrow.

2. **Collapse the subject to a single, punchy beat.** Cut the filler intro entirely; lead with the sharpest offer in the email.
   - **Subject Alt A:** `This week's deals: 75% off CALIA, 40% off shoes & more`
   - **Subject Alt B:** `Score up to 50% off — deals end Sunday`

3. **Preheader should deliver what the subject teases.** If the subject promises deals, the preheader should name the best single offer or category pair — not repeat "this week's deals."
   - **Preheader Alt A:** `CALIA swim, running shoes, golf clubs, pickleball — all marked down`
   - **Preheader Alt B:** `Your weekend deals: 75% off swim, $100 off clubs, $25 paddles`

4. **Lead with the sharpest offer, not the most generic one.** "UP TO 50% OFF" is the weakest headline in the email — 75% off CALIA swim is the actual best deal visible. Promote that to the hero or at minimum the first deal card.

5. **Cut the app store block and social footer.** Swap the screen real estate for an expiration callout or a featured product with a direct link.

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard table-based ESP email (Salesforce Marketing Cloud) with MSO conditional comments and responsive breakpoints. The primary technical concerns are invalid JSON-LD structured data (breaking Gmail Promotions tab card rendering) and unfilled template fields shipped to production.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**ESP redirect domain confirmed:** All links route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...` with a subscriber token appended as `__dU__=<base64-encoded-value>`. This is expected.

**UTM parameters: unverifiable from source.** Destination UTMs (if any) are appended server-side at redirect time. The raw HTML contains no `utm_*` parameters. Cannot confirm campaign tagging without following the redirect chain — flag for QA to verify.

**Open pixel present and correctly suppressed:**
```
src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019f4161b5ddac39b96e96c65848/...'
style="display:none; max-height: 0px; ..."
```
Pixel placement and suppression styles are correct.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`lang="en"` present** on `<html>` — correct.

**`role="presentation"` applied** to layout tables — correct.

**MSO conditional comments present** for Outlook table collapse and font handling — correct.

**`a[x-apple-data-detectors]` reset rule present** — prevents Apple Mail from auto-linking phone/address strings — correct.

**Custom font (Archivo) gated behind `@media screen`** — fallback to `Arial, sans-serif` will engage in Outlook and clients that strip `<style>`. Acceptable.

**External font CDN:** `fonts.gstatic.com` (Google Fonts). Some privacy-focused clients (e.g., Apple Mail with Mail Privacy Protection, HEY) will block this request. Fallback is in place but worth noting for render fidelity metrics.

**`<img>` alt text for logo: unverifiable** — the `<img>` tag is truncated in the source provided. Confirm `alt` is non-empty for the DSG logo link.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Critical: Empty merge fields shipped in JSON-LD block.** The `<script type="application/ld+json">` block contains three unfilled fields that were clearly intended to be populated before send:

| Field | Value in source |
|---|---|
| `DiscountOffer.description` | `""` (empty) |
| `DiscountOffer.discountCode` | `""` (empty) |
| `DiscountOffer.availabilityEnds` | `""` (empty) |
| `PromotionCard.image` | `""` (empty) |

**Critical: Stale template date.** `availabilityStarts` is hardcoded as `"2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"` — a date over 6 years in the past. This is a default placeholder that was never replaced.

**No other visible merge tokens** (e.g., `%%FIRST_NAME%%`, `[subscriber_name]`) were present in the truncated source — either personalization is absent by design or occurs later in the body.

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM / unsubscribe: unverifiable from truncated source.** The footer — which must contain physical mailing address and a working unsubscribe mechanism — is not included in the provided HTML. Confirm the footer is present and unsubscribe link routes through the ESP's suppression mechanism.

**Sender domain:** `DSG@e.dcsg.com` — `e.dcsg.com` is a legitimate ESP subdomain. SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment should be confirmed at the DNS level for this subdomain (out of scope for HTML audit but noted).

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM coverage: unconfirmed.** As noted in §2, all destination URLs are wrapped by the ESP tracker and no `utm_*` parameters appear in the raw HTML source. QA should follow at least one CTA redirect and confirm parameters such as `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content` are present on the landing page URL.

**No landing page URLs are directly inspectable** from this source, so cross-referencing offer content with landing page copy is not possible here.
