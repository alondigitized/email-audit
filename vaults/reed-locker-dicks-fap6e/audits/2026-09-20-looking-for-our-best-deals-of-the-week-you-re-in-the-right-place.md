# Looking for our BEST deals of the week? You're in the right place!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-20**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S running its default "kitchen sink" catalog send — every category rail (Men's, Women's, Kids', Drinkware, Team Sports, Clearance) stacked in one long scroll, topped by the same red "UP TO 50% OFF" box the brand has used all month (9/12–9/20 in brand history). The subject, "Looking for our BEST deals of the week? You're in the right place!", is generic filler copy with zero specificity — no percentage, no category, no urgency — which is a step down even from the brand's own weak recent subjects like "Lucky you! Up to 50% off..." at least those front-load the number.
- The render itself is clean — no broken images, no overlapping text — and the category-rail structure (photo + shoe pairing per department) is a reasonably clear merchandising pattern. But this is pure mass-catalog blast with no targeting signal for a specific persona; a shopper has to scroll past six full sections before reaching "Check out these picks" personalization at the bottom.
- Overall this is a low-differentiation send: the subject undersells the actual offer visible in the body, and the creative is a rerun of the same red 50%-off hero and multi-category rail this brand has sent repeatedly this month.

## What's working

- Clean render — no layout bugs, overlapping text, or broken images anywhere in the long scroll.
- Category chips (Men's/Women's/Kids'/Shoes/Fan Shop/Team Sports) give quick-scan navigation right under the hero.
- Each product rail pairs an outfit photo with a matching shoe, which is a clear, scannable merchandising format.
- "Check out these picks" grid at the bottom adds a personalization touch with specific named products (Samba OG, New Balance 530, etc).
- Loyalty/member cues present (ScoreBoard points, Best Price Guarantee, Favorites List) for a repeat DICK'S shopper.

## What's weak

- Subject line carries no offer, no number, no category — pure curiosity filler that undersells the 50%/40% off actually shown in the hero.
- Hero creative (red box, "UP TO 50% OFF") is the same one flagged repeatedly in brand history (9/17, 9/18) — no visual freshness.
- Email is extremely long — six full category sections plus a clearance banner plus a picks grid before reaching footer links, which is fatiguing on mobile.
- No credible urgency or deadline anywhere in the body — "Fresh Deals... won't last long" is vague, not time-bound.
- Cadence risk: this is at least the 7th similar 40–50%-off-styled send from this brand within brand history's window, all with overlapping creative.

## Recommendations

- 1. Rewrite the subject to lead with the actual number and a category, since the current subject wastes the strongest asset (the 50% off hero) — this is the single biggest lever here.
- 2. Trim the email to 2-3 category sections max, or use a "jump to your category" module up top instead of forcing a full scroll through six departments.
- 3. Add a real countdown/deadline to the hero or clearance banner — the brand has used working countdown timers before (9/19 "Final Hours 07:59:48") and this send has none.
- 4. Swap the hero creative — reusing the same red box for a fourth+ time this month risks blur/banner-blindness for repeat openers.

## Technical Audit

# Technical Audit: DICK'S Sporting Goods — "BEST deals of the week" Email

**1. Technical Summary**
Email uses a standard ESP tracking/redirect architecture (Cheetah/Marigold-style `data.em.dcsg.com/ee/v1/mclick`) with MSO conditional comments for Outlook, but fails automated QA on plain-text fallback and is missing several compliance/authentication signals; 11 of 12 images lack alt text.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- All sampled links route through `https://data.em.dcsg.com/ee/v1/mclick?params=b.v1...&pid=v1...` — standard click-tracking wrapper, structurally valid.
- Open-tracking pixel present: `https://data.em.dcsg.com/ee/v1/open?params=b.v1...`. No issues with pixel placement found.
- QA notes 12 of 64 total links were probed (52 skipped as low-priority footer/utility/social); no broken destinations reported among probed links.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- **11 images missing `alt` text** (QA `[WARN]`), including primary merchandising images (`0920-stw-4pm-clothing-shoes`, `0920-stw-4pm-womens-clothing-shoes`, `0920-stw-4pm-kids-clothing-shoes`, `0920-stw-4pm-40-off-drinkware`, `0920-stw-4pm-equipment-cleats`) and 5 footer icons (`icon-vc-sms-1`, `icon-vc-sportsmatter`, `icon-vc-bpg-1`, `icon-vc-favorite-1`, `icon-location-2025-1`). Screen-reader users get no content for any product image in this send.
- The tracking-pixel `<img>` at `/ee/v1/open` also flagged for missing alt — expected/acceptable for a 0×0 tracking pixel, not a real defect.
- MSO conditional blocks (`<!--[if (gte mso 9)|(IE)]-->`) and mobile media queries (`.mobile-off`, `.mobile-w100`, etc.) are present and structured correctly for Outlook/mobile-client rendering.
- Web font (`Archivo`) is loaded via `@font-face` inside `@media screen` only, with `Arial, sans-serif` fallback in the `body` style — correct progressive-enhancement pattern for email clients that strip `<style>`.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
- No merge tokens (`{{...}}`, `%%...%%`, `[[...]]`) present in the truncated source. No issues found.

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
- **`List-Unsubscribe` header not found** (`[WARN]`) — may be a capture artifact of the AgentMail relay rather than a true absence, but as observed this is a CAN-SPAM/RFC 2369 gap.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found** (`[WARN]`) — one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) cannot be confirmed as supported.
- **`Authentication-Results` header not found** (`[WARN]`) — SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unverifiable from this capture.
- An in-body unsubscribe link is confirmed present per QA notes ("an unsubscribe link is always included"), satisfying the baseline CAN-SPAM requirement even though header-level one-click support is unconfirmed.

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
- Links use the ESP's opaque `mclick` redirect/param-token scheme rather than plain UTM query parameters, so campaign attribution isn't inspectable from the raw href — this is by design for this ESP and not a defect, but it means UTM/landing-page alignment can't be verified from source alone (would require following redirects to final `dickssportinggoods.com` URLs).
- No issues found in the truncated source regarding domain mismatches (image CDN `s7d6.scene7.com`, favicon and tracking domain `em.dcsg.com` are all consistent with DICK'S known infrastructure).

**7. Recommendations**
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to the 5 product/promo images and 5 footer icon images flagged by QA — highest-priority fix for accessibility and for clients that block images.
2. Investigate plain-text MIME part: QA reports it at 9 characters, effectively empty. Regenerate a full plain-text fallback from the HTML content for spam-filter scoring and text-only client support.
3. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are actually being sent by the ESP (Cheetah/Marigold) and are just being dropped by the AgentMail relay capture, vs. genuinely absent — verify with a raw header capture outside this relay.
4. Similarly verify SPF/DKIM authentication independently (e.g., via mail-tester or direct header inspection) since `Authentication-Results` wasn't captured here.
5. If UTM-based attribution is needed for the site-analytics side, follow one or two `mclick` redirects to confirm resolved landing-page URLs carry expected UTM parameters.
