# 🎆 $25 & under deals 🎆 Save on select CLEARANCE
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- This is a clearance-priced $25-and-under promotional email from DICK'S — a format they've run at least once before (the June 28 send already audited at 8/10). The hero is clean and punchy: big white "$25 AND UNDER" typography on a dark denim background, with a "Clearance Deals" CTA block directly below it. The email then stacks a series of product modules — apparel, shoes, swim, golf — each with a small green "CLEARANCE" badge, a short headline, and a "SHOP NOW" button. It's efficiently assembled.
- The creative problem is brand fatigue at this point in the cadence. This is at minimum the tenth DICK'S email since June 26, and the $25-and-under clearance format is a near-repeat of the June 28 send. The product modules are tiny at mobile render scale — most lifestyle shots are thumbnail-sized and the specific products are difficult to read without zooming. There's no stated deadline on the clearance offer, which costs it urgency points.
- Competent execution on a formula that is being run too hot. The offer is real and the hierarchy is correct, but the signal-to-noise ratio for a subscriber already bathed in DICK'S promos this week is low.

## What's working

- **Hero is immediately clear** — "$25 AND UNDER" in large bold type communicates the offer at a glance with no ambiguity.
- **Green "CLEARANCE" badges** on every product module create consistent visual anchoring and signal genuine markdown (not just a promo headline).
- **Vertical stacking of distinct categories** (colors, shoes, resort collection, swim, golf, golf shoes) gives category-specific shoppers a fast scan path.
- **Primary CTA "Shop Now" appears early** — directly under the hero before any scrolling is needed.
- **No render bugs visible** — images load, text is legible, buttons are intact.

## What's weak

- **Cadence fatigue is real** — tenth-plus send in six days; this clearance format was already run June 28 and risks total desensitization.
- **Product modules are tiny at mobile scale** — lifestyle thumbnails dominate and specific products are barely identifiable without zooming.
- **No deadline on the clearance offer** — "while supplies last" logic is implied but no urgency signal (expires tonight, ends Sunday) is visible anywhere.
- **"Explore more deals" category nav bar** (Men / Women / Kids / Shoes) feels redundant next to the product modules below and adds visual clutter without driving action.
- **Bottom "Must-have gear for an epic summer" row** is extremely small — products are postage-stamp sized and the copy is generic; this section adds scroll length without payoff.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline.** A single line — "Clearance pricing valid through July 4 or while supplies last" — converts a passive browse email into time-bounded action. This is the highest-leverage single change.
- 2. **Reduce send frequency or consolidate.** This offer could have been included in yesterday's email. At 10+ sends in six days, each incremental send now cannibalizes open rate from the prior send rather than adding net revenue.
- 3. **Make at least one product module specific.** Pick one hero item (e.g., "Franklin Pickleball Set — was $49, now $24.99") and feature it large with a visible was/now price. Specific items drive clicks; category-level CTAs drive browsing.
- 4. **Cut the bottom "must-have gear" postage-stamp row.** The products are unreadable at mobile size. Replace with a single stronger product callout or remove entirely.
- 5. **Rewrite the subject to call out a specific category** — the $25-and-under frame is good but generic; pairing it with one category (shoes, golf, swim) would sharpen targeting.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS syntax error in MSO conditional block**

```html
<!--[if (gte mso 9)|(IE)]>
<style type="text/css">
  div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
</style>
```

There is a rogue `"` inside the `div` rule immediately before the closing `}`. This produces an invalid CSS declaration in the Outlook/IE-targeted stylesheet. Outlook's CSS parser may drop the entire rule or behave unpredictably for `<div>` layout.

**Tracking pixel missing `alt`**
```html
<img src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/...' style="display:none; ..."/>
```
No `alt=""` attribute. Screen readers and strict clients may flag this; best practice is `alt=""` on suppressed tracking images.

**`border-collapse:separate; border-spacing:0`** on the root wrapper table — non-standard pairing. `border-collapse:collapse` is conventional and avoids potential 1px gap artifacts in older clients. Low-severity but worth noting.

**Web font version:** `Archivo v7` loaded from `fonts.gstatic.com`. Google Fonts has newer versions available; a stale version URL may 404 if Google deprecates old paths, degrading to Arial fallback.
