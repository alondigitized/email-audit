# CLEARANCE deals: up to 70% off!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S eleventh-or-so clearance blast since July 18, and it's structurally indistinguishable from the July 21 "Up to 70% OFF select clearance" send — same 70% hero, same Nike Deal Days sub-block, same product grid footer. If you received that July 21 email and opened this one, you would feel a distinct sense of déjà vu before you finished reading the hero. The creative team appears to be rotating the same three components (Summer Clearance hero, Nike Deal Days module, This Week's Deals grid) and treating "up to 70% off" as perpetually fresh when it is now wallpaper.
- The bones are solid: the dark "UP TO 70% OFF" hero is readable at a glance, the sub-category offers stack legibly, and there are no obvious render bugs. The product grid has genuine merchandise in it. But buried near the footer is a "Earn 2X Points or Rewards on Qualifying Purchases at Gas Stations" callout that is baffling in the context of a summer clearance email — it reads like a wrong-module insertion and actively breaks the scent trail right when the email should be closing the sale.
- The verdict: functionally competent, creatively bankrupt, and marred by one module that should not exist in this template. This is a send that works by volume, not by craft.

## What's working

- The "UP TO 70% OFF" hero is large, high-contrast, and lands instantly — no ambiguity about the offer.
- Nike Deal Days as a sub-block is a smart brand anchor; Nike is a draw for sporting goods shoppers and breaks the sea of generic clearance copy.
- "DICK'S | Clearance" as the sender display name is clean inbox hygiene — the Clearance tag tells subscribers exactly what they're getting before they open.
- The "This Week's Deals" product grid puts real merchandise in front of the reader with specific items and pricing.
- Subject line at 31 characters is mobile-friendly and concrete.

## What's weak

- The creative is a near-exact repeat of the July 21 70%-off clearance send; a subscriber who opened that email sees nothing new here.
- The "Earn 2X Points on Gas Station Purchases" module is completely off-brief for a clearance email — it kills purchase momentum at the worst moment.
- "Up to 70% off" on "select styles" with no visible product count, end date, or size/category scoping feels vague after the tenth identical send.
- "SHOP NOW" on every CTA is generic; with five or six categories visible, every CTA says the same thing.
- No urgency signal anywhere in the email body — no countdown, no "ends Sunday," nothing to motivate same-session purchase.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline.** Even "Ends Sunday" in the hero subhead would lift urgency meaningfully. Without a date, 70% off feels permanent, which trains subscribers to wait.
- 2. **Kill the Gas Stations rewards module.** It does not belong in a clearance email. Replace it with a "Low stock on these deals" or a second product row.
- 3. **Differentiate this send from July 21.** Lead with a specific category ("Men's Nike Shoes hit 70% off today") rather than the same all-categories hero. The July 21 send did the broad-stroke already — this one needs a sharper lens.
- 4. **Vary the CTA copy by section.** "Shop Nike Deals," "Shop Clearance Footwear," "Shop Outdoor" — each sub-block should have a specific CTA, not a universal "SHOP NOW."
- 5. **Rotate the hero visual.** The current Summer Clearance graphic has appeared in at least three sends this week. Even a color swap would reduce fatigue.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Stray quote in Outlook conditional CSS** — the `div` rule inside `<!--[if (gte mso 9)|(IE)]>` contains a malformed closing:
```css
div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
```
The trailing `"` before `}` is invalid and will cause the entire `<style>` block inside that conditional to be dropped in Outlook. Any layout relying on that reset may misrender in Outlook 2016+.

**`@font-face` scoped to `@media screen`:** Correct for email; non-screen clients fall back to Arial. No issue.

**Logo `<img>` src truncated in provided source** — cannot fully audit alt text. The visible portion does not show an `alt` attribute before truncation; confirm `alt="DICK'S Sporting Goods"` is present.

**Tracking pixel missing `alt=""`:**
```html
<img src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/...' style="display:none; ..."/>
```
Add `alt=""` to suppress validator warnings and avoid spam filter flags.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**JSON-LD block contains JavaScript-style comments — invalid JSON:**
```json
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
  // Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": ""
}
```
`//` comments are not valid JSON. Gmail and any client that parses `<script type="application/ld+json">` will silently discard the entire structured data payload. The Gmail Promotions tab annotation (logo, discount badge, expiry) will not render.

**Designer placeholder comment shipped to production:**
`// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!` — this is a pre-send template artifact that made it into the live send.

**`PromotionCard.image` is empty string `""`** — even if the JSON were valid, no promo card image would display in Gmail's Promotions tab.

**`DiscountOffer` fields unpopulated:**
- `"description": ""`
- `"discountCode": ""`
- `"availabilityEnds": ""`

**`availabilityStarts` hardcoded to a 2020 date:**
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```
This is over 6 years stale. If Gmail parses the block at all, it may treat the offer as expired and suppress the annotation.
