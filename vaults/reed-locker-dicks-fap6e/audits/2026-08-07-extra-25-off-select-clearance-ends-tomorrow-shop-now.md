# 💸 EXTRA 25% off select clearance ends tomorrow... shop now! 💸
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S running the exact same "Extra 25% Off Select Clearance" hero — red banner over the same outdoor dock/lake lifestyle shot — that anchored the 7.00/10 "Go, go, go!" send earlier today, just re-cut with "ends tomorrow" urgency and a longer stacked-offer digest underneath (Epic Shoe Sale, Calia/VRST/Alpine, Owala bottles, Nike Golf, cleats, MLB gear, Peloton shoes, then a 6-item product grid). The hero itself is clean and scroll-stopping, but sending the same creative twice in one day with a bolted-on countdown reads as a last-call nudge rather than a fresh idea, and the sheer length of the digest buries the "25% off" promise under seven competing offers by the time a reader scrolls halfway down.
- The render is technically flawless — no broken images, no overlapping text, consistent DICK'S green/white branding throughout — but that's the baseline for this brand at this point, not a differentiator. The real weakness is structural: a single strong offer at top gets diluted into a kitchen-sink weekly digest, which is a pattern this brand keeps repeating (see 8/5 and 8/4 Epic Shoe Sale sends, 8/2–8/6 "up to 50% off" pool creative run three times).
- Net: solid execution, tired strategy. This is a "shop the 25% off before it's gone" email that immediately undercuts its own urgency by giving you seven other things to look at instead.

## What's working

- The hero is visually strong — bold white "EXTRA 25% OFF" stamped on a red card over a bright outdoor photo, impossible to miss at the top of the email.
- "Ends Tomorrow" tag + green "SHOP NOW" button give the primary offer a clear, unambiguous next action.
- Category nav pills (Men's, Women's, Kids', $25 and Under, Fan Shop, All Sale) right under the hero let a reader route themselves fast if the top offer isn't their category.
- ScoreCard rewards banner and "Order Today, Sport it Tomorrow" same-day pickup line are visible near the top, reinforcing loyalty/membership value early.
- Zero render bugs — clean grid, consistent spacing, footer with app download and social links all intact.

## What's weak

- The 25% off hero is immediately followed by seven more distinct offers (30% off shoes, 70% off Calia/VRST, Owala bottles, 25% off Nike Golf, 40% off cleats, 50% off MLB gear, 20% off Peloton shoes) — this isn't a promo email anymore, it's a catalog, and it drowns the "ends tomorrow" urgency.
- This is the second "Extra 25% Off Select Clearance" hero from DICK'S sent today (per the 7.00/10 "Go, go, go!" send with the identical red-banner-over-lake creative) — same-day repetition with no new visual idea, just added urgency copy.
- "SHOP NOW" is used as the CTA on nearly every single block (at least 8 times) — no CTA differentiation to signal which offer is actually worth clicking first.
- The fine print under the hero ("Select clearance styles only... Some markdowns may have been taken... Offer may not be valid on all items with this price") is dense enough to feel like a hedge on the headline offer.
- No specific product, price point, or size/availability detail is shown anywhere in the top-of-email hero — it's a category-level offer, not a hook a shopper can act on immediately.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the digest length by half — lead with the 25% off clearance hero and one supporting offer max, and move Golf/MLB/Peloton/Owala into a separate send. A focused single-offer email would restore the urgency this creative is trying to sell.
- 2. Vary the CTA copy per section ("Shop Clearance," "Shop Shoes," "Shop Golf") instead of repeating "SHOP NOW" eight times — gives the eye something to differentiate.
- 3. Swap the generic lake/dock lifestyle photo for a product-forward hero (stacked clearance apparel/shoes) so the offer feels tied to something purchasable, not just a mood shot reused from the earlier send.
- 4. Add a specific dollar or percentage callout in the subject/hero (e.g., "starting at $X") to make the offer feel more concrete than "select clearance."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 25% off clearance — gone after tomorrow`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last day: Extra 25% off clearance styles`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stack the extra 25% on already-marked-down gear before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Clearance shoes, apparel & gear — extra 25% off ends 8/8.`

# Technical Audit — DICK'S "Extra 25% Off Clearance" Email

## 1. Technical Summary
The email uses a standard Epsilon/Harmony ESP redirect architecture (`app.dickssportinggoods.com`) with MSO/Outlook conditionals and responsive CSS, but ships with a broken unsubscribe link, malformed JSON-LD, a CSS syntax error in the Outlook conditional block, and 11 images missing alt text — 43% automated pass rate.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Broken unsubscribe link (confirmed):** `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/U/v610000019fdde952e8a7e2c8f4bbe5be68/50014781614248260000021ef3a0bcff` resolves to `/pc/Error/Error?aspxerrorpath=/pc/subscription` — an unhandled application error, not the preference/unsubscribe page. This is a functional failure of a legally required mechanism, not a cosmetic bug.
- Header and logo CTAs route through `/T/...` click-tracking redirects (e.g. `.../T/v610000019fdde952e8a7e2c8f4bbe5be68/...?__dU__=v0G4RBKTXg2Gs8NEX0-RpwlL2_Q96sIof5wvgkGa1VsPIAdb-6PwUuXUbOES4DYwVKlEBH4wAjEqvhvBlpUfxLCgwho1v8uViS`), consistent with normal ESP redirect wrapping — no defects observed in these beyond the unsubscribe link.
- QA probed 12 of 62 total links (material/high-click-likelihood links); 50 lower-priority (footer/social/utility) links were deprioritized and not verified — those remain unconfirmed.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **11 images missing `alt` text**, confirmed by QA: `vert.png`, `owala.png`, `mapbreak.gif`, `cleats.jpg`, `mlb.jpg`, `peloton.jpg`, and 5 footer icons (`icon-vc-sms.png`, `icon-value-sportsmatter--light.png`, `icon-vc-bpg.png`, `icon-vc-favorite.png`, `icon-location-2025.png`). These are content/functional images, so missing alt text is a genuine screen-reader accessibility gap. (The 12th flagged image is the hidden 1×1 SSL/open-tracking pixel — `display:none; mso-hide:all` — alt text is not meaningful there and can be disregarded.)
- **CSS syntax error in Outlook/IE conditional block:** `div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}</style>` contains a stray `"` before the closing brace, malforming the rule. Scoped to `<!--[if (gte mso 9)|(IE)]>`, so it only affects Outlook desktop rendering of that selector.
- **Invalid JSON-LD structured data:** the `application/ld+json` block contains `//` line comments (`// GIF & WEBP images are not supported...`, `// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!`), which are not valid JSON syntax. This will cause `JSON.parse` failures in any consumer (Gmail annotations/schema.org parsers), silently dropping the structured data rather than rendering it.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tags (e.g. `%%...%%`, `{{...}}`) are present in the visible source.
- The JSON-LD template has unpopulated placeholder fields — `"description": ""`, `"discountCode": ""`, `"image": ""` — indicating the promo-card/discount-offer template was not filled in before send. Since these feed Gmail's promotional annotation surface, the offer card will not render.
- Redirect URLs carry a `__dU__` opaque personalization token; cannot verify correctness from static HTML alone.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication)
- **CAN-SPAM risk:** the unsubscribe mechanism must remain functional per 15 U.S.C. §7704; the confirmed broken unsubscribe link (Section 2) is a compliance-relevant defect, not just UX friction.
- `List-Unsubscribe` header not detected — flagged WARN because the AgentMail relay may not surface it; cannot confirm absence from the origin send.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058, one-click unsubscribe) not detected — same relay caveat applies. If genuinely absent at origin, this is a Gmail/Yahoo 2024 bulk-sender requirement gap.
- `Authentication-Results` header not present via the relay — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status cannot be assessed from available data.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
No issues found — cannot be assessed. All observed links are wrapped in ESP click-tracking redirects (`app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...`) rather than exposing final destination URLs, so UTM parameters and landing-page alignment can't be verified from the truncated HTML source. Resolving the redirect chain would be required to confirm continuity.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Fix the unsubscribe link target — route to a working preference/unsubscribe page, not `/pc/Error/Error`. Treat as high priority given CAN-SPAM exposure.
2. Confirm at the origin (not just via relay) whether `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are being sent; add RFC 8058 one-click support if missing.
3. Add descriptive `alt` text to the 11 flagged content/footer images; leave the tracking pixel as-is.
4. Remove the `//` comments from the `application/ld+json` block (or move them outside the JSON) and populate `description`, `discountCode`, and `image`, or remove the `PromotionCard` object entirely if unused.
5. Remove the stray `"` in the Outlook-conditional `div` CSS rule.
