# Hurry! Your $20 off $100+ app-exclusive can't wait
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- This is — by my count — DICK'S third distinct "app-exclusive $20 off $100+" email I've audited in this Memorial Day window, following the May 24 "*** App exclusive: spend $100+…***" (5/10) and the May 24 "$20 off $100+ starts NOW!" (6/10). The core offer is identical. What's different: a live countdown timer ("Final Hours: 07:19:55") in the header rail, which is the single most effective execution choice in this send — it makes the urgency feel earned rather than manufactured. The hero section delivers cleanly: app mockup imagery, bold $20 OFF, explicit "Sale items included! No code needed" reassurance, and a green "GET OFFER" CTA all above the fold.
- The email collapses hard below the hero. "The Summer Shop" section contains what can only be described as escaped gibberish copy — visible in the render as something close to "Get it quick with new hour, Fridays, sometimes every so fast those days!" — which signals a CMS placeholder or copy-paste error that shipped to live. The product grid beneath it is competent but unremarkable, and the secondary 50% off banner creates offer confusion: is this a $20-off email or a 50%-off email?
- Judgment: the best execution of the app-exclusive offer DICK'S has shipped this cycle, dragged down by a content error that should have been caught in QA.

## What's working

- Countdown timer ("Final Hours: 07:19:55") makes the urgency concrete and verifiable — the best trust signal in any of the three $20-off variants.
- "Sale items included! No code needed" is explicit and friction-reducing; prior versions buried or omitted this.
- App collage in the hero reinforces the channel-exclusive framing visually, not just in copy.
- Green "GET OFFER" button is prominent and unambiguous — verb + noun, high contrast.
- Subject line lands the offer specifics ($20/$100+/app-exclusive) in under 50 characters.

## What's weak

- Garbled copy in "The Summer Shop" section shipped to live — a hard quality failure that undermines brand trust.
- Two competing offer frames ($20 off vs. up to 50% off) split attention and dilute both messages.
- Product picks (HOKA, New Balance, Nike) feel stitched in from a generic algorithmic feed with no connection to the $20-off offer or the countdown urgency.
- The "Memorial Day App Exclusive" label restates the subject; no new information is added.
- Cadence fatigue is real: this is the third near-identical $20-off app email in two days — even a subscriber who wants this offer has already seen it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the garbled Summer Shop copy immediately** — it's a QA failure shipping to thousands of inboxes. Either replace it with the 50%-off sale CTA copy from the May 23 send or cut the section entirely.
- 2. **Collapse to one offer hierarchy**: commit to "$20 off $100+ via app" as the lead and either remove the 50%-off banner entirely or reframe it as the qualifying inventory pool ("50% off items count toward your $100").
- 3. **Tie the countdown to the product grid** — show products with prices + the $20 applied, so the click feels like "I'm saving $20 on this specific shoe right now," not "go browse."
- 4. Retire this campaign after today; one more app-exclusive send in this identical template will read as spam regardless of creative quality.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Final hours: $20 off your $100+ app order`
- **Subject Alt B:** `7 hrs left: app exclusive saves you $20 today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sale items count. No code. Tap, spend $100, save $20.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your $20 off expires at midnight — sale items included.`

## 1. Overview

No `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, or `utm_campaign` parameters are visible in the raw redirect URL. Whether these are appended post-redirect cannot be confirmed from the source alone; verify the final destination URL contains UTM parameters.

**Open-tracking pixel**
```html
<img src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019e60e1586f8acb296e965fc958/12ac8524793245f800004c5a42963aa1'
     style="display:none; max-height: 0px; ..."/>
```
Pixel is correctly hidden. The tracking ID (`12ac8524793245f800004c5a42963aa1`) appears to be a static campaign ID rather than a per-recipient token — confirm this is dynamically substituted at send time, not hardcoded.

**Truncated logo `src`**
