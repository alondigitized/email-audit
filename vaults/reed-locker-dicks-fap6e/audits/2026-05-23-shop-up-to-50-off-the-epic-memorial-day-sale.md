# Shop up to 50% off the Epic Memorial Day Sale! 🙌
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S fourteenth-plus send in six days — and notably the second email to hit the inbox on May 23 alone, after "EPIC Memorial Day SALE is live — shop up to 50% off" arrived earlier today. The render itself is clean: the 50% OFF hero is big, patriotic, and lands immediately. The EXTRA 25% clearance block in the middle is a recycled insert we've seen at least four times this week, including the dedicated May 21 clearance send — it's becoming wallpaper. The Summer Shop section and the HOKA/New Balance product picks add genuine browse value, but they're buried below two back-to-back offer blocks.
- The strongest thing going for this email is timing: Saturday of the actual holiday weekend, with Memorial Day two days out, is exactly when a motivated buyer acts. The weakest thing is the subject line — nearly identical to the earlier same-day send, erasing any differentiation between the two.
- Solid execution, serious cadence fatigue, zero effort to flag the closing window with a real hard deadline.

## What's working

- **50% OFF hero dominates the fold** — bold, clean, patriotic red/white, instantly readable on mobile.
- **Two stacked offers** (50% site-wide + extra 25% clearance) communicate genuine value depth without contradicting each other.
- **"Limited Time" badge** on the hero adds visible urgency, even if weak.
- **Product picks section** (HOKA Clifton 9 & 10, New Balance 530) gives specific browse hooks for an active-lifestyle subscriber.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no layout breaks, footer and social links intact.

## What's weak

- **Same-day duplicate subject** — "Shop up to 50% off the Epic Memorial Day Sale! 🙌" arrives hours after "EPIC Memorial Day SALE is live — shop up to 50% off." A subscriber with both in their inbox has no reason to open the second.
- **No hard deadline** — Memorial Day is Monday; "Limited Time" is the weakest possible urgency language when you know the exact end date. "Ends Monday" costs zero pixels.
- **EXTRA 25% clearance block is a repeat insert** — same creative, same copy, same CTA from May 21. Subscribers who opened that email see no reason to click this one.
- **Product picks are gender-fragmented** — women's HOKA, men's HOKA, kids' Nike, VRST men's, DSG women's. No demographic targeting; this reads like an unfiltered catalog slice.
- **Preview text is almost certainly leaking utility nav** — the first readable text inside the email after the logo appears to be header navigation links, not a curated preheader.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the second same-day send or meaningfully differentiate it.** If you're sending twice on Saturday, the second email needs a completely different angle — "hours left," a new category, a featured product, something. Two near-identical subjects in one inbox is the fastest path to mass unsubscribes.
- 2. **Replace "Limited Time" with "Ends Monday."** The deadline is known. Use it. It's the most powerful single-word change in this email.
- 3. **Retire the EXTRA 25% clearance block or refresh the creative.** It has run in at least four sends this week. Either give it a new headline ("Last chance to clear the rack") or drop it for a Summer Shop callout that hasn't been seen yet.
- 4. **Segment the product picks by recipient gender.** HOKA women's running shoes and kids' Nike apparel in the same scroll is unfocused. A single-gender product row converts better than a mixed one.
- 5. **Set a real preheader.** The first line of body copy is leaking into the inbox preview. A preheader like "Ends Monday — HOKA, Nike, adidas up to 50% off" would double the open signal.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends Monday: up to 50% off Memorial Day Sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `HOKA, Nike, adidas — 50% off ends Monday 🙌`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Last full weekend — grab HOKA, Nike & adidas before the sale closes`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Extra 25% off clearance + 50% off summer faves — ends Monday`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods "Epic Memorial Day Sale"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on Epsilon/Harmony infrastructure with a standard responsive table layout. Two issues require immediate attention before future sends: invalid JSON-LD (will silently fail Gmail Promotions tab annotations) and stale/empty structured-data fields that were clearly never populated from the template defaults.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Truncated image src** — The logo `<img src="https://dsg.images.harmony.epsilon.c…"` is cut off in the provided source. If this truncation reflects the actual rendered HTML (not just the audit excerpt), the image will 404. Verify against raw source.

**Epsilon redirect wrapping** — All CTAs route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v6100000…?__dU__=…`. This is expected for Epsilon click tracking, but the final destination UTM carrythrough cannot be confirmed from the truncated source (see §6).

**Open-pixel domain** — Tracking pixel fires against `app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v6100000…`. Consistent with ESP subdomain; no anomaly, but confirm SPF/DKIM covers `e.dcsg.com` and `app.dickssportinggoods.com` separately (see §5).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`width="600"` + `width:100%; max-width:600px` conflict** — The wrapper `<table>` carries both the HTML attribute `width="600"` and inline `style="width:100%; max-width:600px"`. In older Outlook/IE, the attribute wins and the email renders fixed at 600px on mobile, defeating the responsive CSS. Strip the `width="600"` attribute and rely on the CSS rule.

**Preheader structure** — The preheader text "Don't miss an EXTRA 25% off select clearance ⏳" sits in `<td id="ssl">` alongside the tracking pixel, with no explicit `aria-hidden="true"` or `role` to suppress it from screen readers. This text is intentionally hidden visually but audible to AT users; add `aria-hidden="true"` or move it to a visually-hidden span.

**Missing `alt` on logo** — The `<img id="logo" src="…">` in the truncated source has no visible `alt` attribute. If omitted, Outlook renders a broken-image box when images are blocked.

**`@font-face` only inside `@media screen`** — Archivo is loaded only in `@media screen`, so it never applies in print or certain webmail clients that strip media queries. Acceptable tradeoff but intentional.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Conditional block ID** — `<table id="header-non-sc-member">` signals this is the non-ScoreCard variant. Confirm the corresponding `header-sc-member` block exists in the ESP template and that the conditional logic is toggled correctly; if both blocks rendered simultaneously, the header would duplicate.

No unrendered `%%TOKEN%%` placeholders visible in the provided source. Full assessment requires untruncated HTML.

---

### 5. Compliance

**JSON-LD contains JavaScript comments — invalid JSON** — The `<script type="application/ld+json">` block includes `//`-style comments:

```
// GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
// Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
```

JSON does not permit comments. This will throw a parse error in any JSON consumer, silently killing Gmail Promotions tab annotations for every recipient. Strip all `//` comments before deployment.

**`availabilityStarts` is hardcoded to 2020** — Inside the `DiscountOffer` schema:

```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00",
"availabilityEnds": ""
```

This is a six-year-old template artifact. Gmail/schema.org parsers may reject or deprioritize an offer with a start date in the past and a blank end date. Both fields must be populated with current campaign dates.

**Empty `DiscountOffer` and `PromotionCard` fields** — `description`, `discountCode`, and `PromotionCard.image` are all empty strings. Gmail Promotions tab deal annotations require these to render the visual card. As-is, the schema block does nothing.

**`<!--DKS Email Marketing 2025-->`** — Year comment suggests the template base wasn't refreshed for 2026. Low-severity, but indicates stale template hygiene.

**Unsubscribe / CAN-SPAM footer** — Not visible in the truncated source. Must confirm physical address and one-click unsubscribe link are present in the full HTML. RFC 8058 `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header should be set at the ESP level for Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender compliance.

**Authentication** — Sending domain is `e.dcsg.com` (subdomain). Verify SPF, DKIM (selector on `e.dcsg.com`), and DMARC policy on the root `dcsg.com` align. Check `app.dickssportinggoods.com` is also covered if it handles pixel/redirect responses that could be flagged as mixed-origin.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All CTAs pass through Epsilon redirect URLs (`/T/v6100000…`). From the truncated source it is not possible to confirm whether UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are appended to the final destination URLs post-redirect. Pull a decoded redirect sample and verify:

- `utm_source=email`
- `utm_medium=email`
- `utm_campaign=memorial-day-sale` (or equivalent)

Without UTM passthrough, Memorial Day revenue attribution in GA4/Adobe will be suppressed or misattributed to direct traffic.
