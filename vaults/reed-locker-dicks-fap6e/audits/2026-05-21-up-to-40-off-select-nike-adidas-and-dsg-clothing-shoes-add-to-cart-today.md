# *** Up to 40% off select Nike, adidas, and DSG clothing & shoes… add to cart today *** 🙌
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S tenth-plus send in roughly four days, and it arrives as the *second* email on May 21 alone — the earlier clearance overlay (scored 5/10) already hit the inbox this morning. The lead offer is legitimately differentiated: naming Nike, adidas, and DSG at 40% off is more precise than the generic "Memorial Day Event" drums the brand has been banging all week, and the hero executes cleanly with a bold red-on-dark "UP TO 40% OFF" block and a visible SHOP NOW button.
- The problem is what comes after the fold. The content stack mixes athletic performance gear with platform sandals, golf apparel, and sunglasses — diluting the brand-specific hook from the subject line. Worse, the bottom third of the email literally re-runs the EXTRA 25% OFF red clearance banner from this morning's send. The recipient already dismissed that creative once today; seeing it again at 40% scroll depth is a trust signal in reverse.
- Net verdict: a serviceable brand-name offer undermined by cadence fatigue, a bloated subject line, and lazy clearance recycling in the same scroll session.

## What's working

- **Brand-name hero is the strongest subject line in the series** — "Nike, adidas, and DSG" gives the recipient a concrete reason to click that "Memorial Day Event" never provided.
- **Hero visual hierarchy is clean** — large bold "40% OFF" on a red field is legible and loads above the fold.
- **SHOP NOW CTA is unambiguous** — visible button, clear placement below the headline.
- **Category quick-links bar** (SALE / RUNNING / SHOES / BRANDS / ALL PLUS / CLEARANCE) lets a motivated browser navigate without relying on the curated deal sections.
- **Basketball and running sections** are relevant for the athletic persona and provide product-level visual anchoring.

## What's weak

- **Second email of the day, same brand** — the EXTRA 25% OFF clearance module at the bottom is identical to this morning's send. Recipients notice; it reads as filler padding to hit a content length target.
- **Platform sandals are the first featured section** — leading deal content with casual heeled sandals signals to an athletic subscriber that the curation is generic, not for them.
- **Subject line asterisks (`***…***`) are a spam aesthetic** — no inbox provider is blocking it, but it visually cheapens a subject that had real specificity going for it.
- **"Add to cart today" is non-urgency** — there's no deadline anywhere in the visible email body, so the call-to-action language has nothing to land on.
- **No social proof or inventory signals** anywhere in the scroll — no reviews, no "selling fast," nothing to create pressure beyond the offer itself.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop the asterisks and trim the subject to under 55 characters.** The Nike/adidas hook is strong enough on its own; the `***` decoration signals desperation.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Nike & adidas up to 40% off — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `40% off Nike, adidas & DSG starts now`
- 2. **Remove the clearance module.** The EXTRA 25% OFF block is a copy-paste from this morning's email. Delete it; the email is already long enough and recycling it erodes trust.
- 3. **Move the athletic-use sections above the sandals.** Basketball and running gear should be the first curated sections for a subscriber tagged as athletic. Sandals can appear later or be suppressed by segment.
- 4. **Add a concrete deadline to the hero.** Even "through Sunday" converts "add to cart today" from noise into urgency. Right now there's nothing anchoring the "today" claim.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ends Sunday — Nike, adidas & DSG 40% off, no code needed`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your brands, your discount — ends this weekend`
- 5. **Consolidate the two May 21 sends.** This email and the morning clearance send could have been one — clearance module at bottom, brand promo as the lead. Two separate sends on the same day from the same brand is the fastest path to unsubscribes.

### 5. Compliance

**JSON-LD block contains JavaScript-style comments — invalid JSON:**
```js
// GIF & WEBP images are not supported...
// Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
```
JSON does not permit `//` comments. The entire `<script type="application/ld+json">` block will be rejected by Gmail's structured-data parser, suppressing Google Inbox promotional offers and deal highlights. This is a hard parse failure, not a degraded experience.

**Stale `availabilityStarts` date:**
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```
Year 2020 — six years stale. Almost certainly a copy-paste artifact from a template origin send. Google may suppress the offer card if the availability window is in the past.

**Empty required schema fields:**
```json
"availabilityEnds": "",
"description": "",
"discountCode": "",
"image": ""  // PromotionCard
```
All four are empty strings. `availabilityEnds` is not a valid ISO 8601 date; an empty string will fail validation. The `PromotionCard.image` field has a designer annotation comment confirming it was never populated.

**Non-standard Schema.org `@type` values:**
- `DiscountOffer` — not a valid Schema.org type (should be `Offer`)
- `PromotionCard` — Google proprietary extension, not in Schema.org core; only valid inside Gmail's email markup spec

**CAN-SPAM / unsubscribe footer** — The HTML source is truncated before the footer. Physical mailing address and one-click unsubscribe link cannot be verified from the provided excerpt. Audit is incomplete for these items.
