# This week's deals ➡️ Up to 50% off!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- DICK'S returns to the exact same "Best Deals of the Week" compilation format that scored 9/10 on June 4. The UP TO 50% OFF hero over a sunflower field is punchy, the category navigation (Men's/Women's/Kids/Shoes/Golf/The Summer Shop) is sensible, and the sandals sub-section adds a timely seasonal hook that the June 4 send lacked. Structurally, this is a clean, competent email.
- The problem is familiarity. A subscriber who opened "Don't miss our best deals of the week — up to 50% off" on June 4 is now looking at what feels like a second printing. "This week's deals" is not meaningfully different from "best deals of the week," and the visual treatment maps closely to that prior send. The sandals angle rescues this from being a direct copy, but only barely.
- Verdict: A solid mid-effort compilation that loses a point or two precisely because DICK'S already landed this format cleanly 24 hours ago.

## What's working

- **UP TO 50% OFF hero dominates the fold** — discount claim is the first thing the eye lands on; hierarchy is exactly right.
- **Category navigation is well-structured** — MEN'S, WOMEN'S, KIDS, SHOES, GOLF, THE SUMMER SHOP gives segmented entry points without overwhelming the layout.
- **Sandals sub-section is a genuine seasonal moment** — "25% Off Select Sandals and Casual Shoes" with specific product imagery (slides, Crocs-style sandals) differentiates this from a pure replay of June 4.
- **Email renders cleanly** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout bugs visible.
- **DSG Clothing family section broadens reach** — "Up to 50% Off Select DSG Clothing for the Family" hits the value-seeker and the family shopper simultaneously.

## What's weak

- **Subject is weaker than June 4's** — "This week's deals ➡️ Up to 50% off!" is blander than "Don't miss our best deals of the week — up to 50% off 🙌"; a subscriber who got both may feel like they're opening a re-send.
- **Every CTA says "SHOP NOW"** — not one button specifies what you're shopping; the category tabs do the work the CTAs should be doing.
- **No end date anchors the urgency** — "this week's" is vague; no deadline visible anywhere in the body.
- **The "Plus, shop more top deals" grid is noise** — tumbler, t-shirt, bike with no connective offer or theme; looks like padding, not curation.
- **No loyalty/ScoreCard callout** — the June 4 send also skipped this; for a brand with a rewards program, invisible member benefits on a deals email is a recurring miss.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pin a hard end date** — "Deals end Sunday, June 8" converts the soft "this week's" into real urgency. Single highest-impact change.
- 2. **Promote sandals to the hero** — summer footwear is the freshest and most specific angle in this email; let it lead instead of burying it below a generic 50% off claim.
- 3. **Make CTA buttons category-specific** — "SHOP SANDALS," "SHOP DSG CLOTHING," "SHOP GOLF DEALS" — each sub-section CTA should match its header, not repeat a generic verb.
- 4. **Add one ScoreCard member line** — e.g., "ScoreCard members earn extra points on every deal." One line, turns the loyalty base from indifferent to motivated.
- 5. **Cut or unify the "shop more top deals" grid** — give it a hook ("Summer Essentials Under $50") or cut it; a random assortment dilutes the email's offer clarity.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Sandals, shorts & summer gear — up to 50% off this week`
- **Subject Alt B:** `This week only: up to 50% off shoes, clothing & more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Deals end Sunday — plus 25% off select sandals and slides`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your summer gear checklist, discounted — ends this week`

## Technical Audit: DICK'S Sporting Goods — "This week's deals" (2026-06-05)

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses Epsilon/Harmony for link tracking and delivery infrastructure. Two confirmed issues require immediate fix before next send: invalid JSON-LD (structured data block contains JS-style comments, making it unparseable) and stale template defaults left in the Gmail Promotions annotation schema.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open/SSL tracking pixel** — standard hidden 1×1 pixel at `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019e997f964ea0fdb5f4bbe5c898/78cd686e...`. No issue; expected Epsilon open-tracking pattern.

**Click tracking** — all links route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/…?__dU__=<encoded>`. This is Epsilon's proprietary redirect. **UTM parameters are not visible** at the redirect entry point in the source. If Epsilon is not appending `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` at redirect time, clicks will arrive untagged in GA4/Adobe Analytics. Verify in Epsilon platform settings or inspect a live redirect response.

**Truncated image URL** — logo `src` ends with `brand-logo--dk…` in the provided source. Likely a copy-paste truncation artifact, but confirm the full URL resolves (i.e., no broken image in production).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Invalid JSON-LD — JS comments inside `<script type="application/ld+json">`:**
```json
// GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
// Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
```
JSON does not support `//` comments. Every JSON parser (Gmail's Promotions annotation engine, Google's rich results validator) will reject this block entirely. The Gmail carousel card will not render.

**Missing `alt` attribute** — the logo `<img id="logo">` has no visible `alt` text in the source. Screen readers and image-blocked clients will get no label.

**Outlook conditional CSS** — `<!--[if (gte mso 9)|(IE)]>` blocks are present and correctly scoped. No issue.

**Responsive breakpoint** — `@media screen and (max-width:480px)` is set. Standard for email; no issue.

**Web font loading** — Archivo loaded from `fonts.gstatic.com` via `@font-face` inside `@media screen`. Fallback to Arial is in place. No issue.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Segment flag in DOM** — the header table carries `id="header-non-sc-member"`, confirming conditional content is in use. The corresponding ScoreCard-member variant is not shown; confirm suppression logic is correct and the wrong variant is not delivered to loyalty members.

**Empty merge fields in structured data** — the `DiscountOffer` schema block has three unfilled fields:
```json
"description": "",
"discountCode": "",
"availabilityEnds": ""
```
If these are supposed to be dynamic merge tokens, they were never substituted. Empty strings are sent to Gmail's annotation parser.

No `{{firstName}}` or equivalent merge tokens are visible in the truncated body copy — cannot confirm whether personalization is missing or simply outside the provided excerpt.

### 5. Compliance

**From domain** — `DSG@e.dcsg.com`. `e.dcsg.com` is a subdomain used for Epsilon sending. SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records are not included in the provided source (header values not supplied). Cannot verify authentication posture from HTML alone — pull the raw message headers to confirm `dkim=pass`, `spf=pass`, `dmarc=pass`.

**Physical address / unsubscribe** — not present in the provided excerpt, but the source is truncated. These are required by CAN-SPAM §5. Confirm they exist in the footer outside the provided window.

**Emoji in subject line** (`➡️`) — not a compliance issue, but some spam filters penalize high-symbol subjects. Flag for deliverability review if open rates are below baseline.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM coverage** — as noted in §2, `__dU__` is Epsilon's opaque tracking token. If Epsilon is configured to pass through UTM parameters on redirect, they should appear in the destination URL after the redirect resolves. Spot-check by clicking a link from a test send and inspecting the final URL in the browser address bar. If UTMs are absent, analytics will attribute this traffic to `(direct) / (none)`.

**`availabilityStarts` is 2020** — the `DiscountOffer` schema contains:
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```
This is a 6-year-old template default that was never updated. Even if Gmail ignores it due to the JSON parse failure, this should be corrected — it would cause the promotion to appear expired if the JSON were ever valid.
