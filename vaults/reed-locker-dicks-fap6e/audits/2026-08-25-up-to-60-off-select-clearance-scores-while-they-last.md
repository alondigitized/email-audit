# Up to 60% OFF 🤝 Select clearance scores (while they last!)
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-25**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S at its most "brand-consistent," which is both the good news and the problem. The bold red-and-white "UP TO 60% OFF SELECT CLEARANCE SCORES" hero on a dark locker-room backdrop reads instantly and is a clean, honest step up from this week's endless "up to 50%" sends — the clearance framing at least signals different inventory. But scroll past the hero and it's the exact same green-locker "UP TO 50% OFF" module this brand has run on 8/16, 8/20, 8/22, and 8/24, appearing twice more further down the same email, which undercuts the "clearance" premise the subject and hero promised.
- The email is long and well-organized with clear category chips, a good product mix (soccer gear, golf balls, bikes, cycling shoes, running shoes), and functional-looking CTAs throughout. But there's no product-level pricing shown anywhere except the golf balls, no scarcity/countdown mechanic despite "while they last" in the subject, and the repeated 50%-off locker modules make this feel like three emails stitched into one rather than a focused clearance send.
- Judgment: solid top-of-funnel creative, diluted by reused mid-funnel modules. This is a 5-6, not because it's broken, but because it's the same brand playbook with a new headline pasted on top.

## What's working

- The hero is legible and hits fast: "UP TO 60% OFF" in oversized red type against a locker backdrop, readable at a glance.
- Subhead "Check Out Reduced Prices on Select Items While They Last" reinforces urgency without shouting.
- Category chips (Men's, Women's, Kids', $25 and Under, Fan Shop, All Sale) give an immediate self-segmentation path right below the fold.
- Product grid at the bottom (Nike, adidas, HOKA) shows real merchandise with names and thumbnails — no broken images or placeholder text visible.
- ScoreCard loyalty banner and delivery/pickup promise are up top, addressing member value early.

## What's weak

- The clearance email contains two separate "UP TO 50% OFF" green-locker banners that are near-identical to creative already sent 8/16–8/24 — it dilutes the clearance angle the subject line promised.
- No visible price points anywhere except the $39.98 TaylorMade golf balls — for a "clearance" email, actual marked-down prices would drive far more clicks than another "up to X% off" banner.
- "While they last" urgency in the subject has zero visual reinforcement in the body — no countdown, no low-stock flag, no date.
- Fine print under the hero ("Exclusions apply... Prices as marked") is visible immediately below the CTA, which slightly undercuts the bold headline before the reader even scrolls.
- With back-to-school, cozy collection, soccer, golf, and bikes all crammed in, the email loses its clearance identity by the middle third.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut both "UP TO 50% OFF" locker modules — they're reused creative from four prior sends this week and actively compete with the clearance narrative this email is supposed to be selling. Replace with clearance-specific product tiles showing actual struck-through prices.
- 2. Add real markdown pricing (e.g., "$34.99, was $70") to at least the top 4-6 clearance items — this is the single highest-leverage fix, since price-anchored clearance emails convert better than generic percentage banners.
- 3. Give "while they last" a visual anchor — inventory count, a countdown timer, or "ends tonight" — so the urgency in the subject isn't abandoned in the body.
- 4. Move the exclusions/fine print below the category chips instead of directly under the primary CTA.
- 5. Trim the email length — six distinct promo modules before the loyalty/app footer is too much; a clearance send should stay tightly scoped to clearance.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Real deals, real prices: up to 60% off clearance`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns on shoes, gear and more — while sizes last`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Marked-down styles across Nike, adidas, HOKA and more`

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This is DICK'S at its most "brand-consistent," which is both the good news and the problem. The bold red-and-white "UP TO 60% OFF SELECT CLEARANCE SCORES" hero on a dark locker-room backdrop reads instantly and is a clean, honest step up from this week's endless "up to 50%" sends — the clearance framing at least signals different inventory. But scroll past the hero and it's the exact same green-locker "UP TO 50% OFF" module this brand has run on 8/16, 8/20, 8/22, and 8/24, appearing twice more further down the same email, which undercuts the "clearance" premise the subject and hero promised.

But there's no product-level pricing shown anywhere except the golf balls, no scarcity/countdown mechanic despite "while they last" in the subject, and the repeated 50%-off locker modules make this feel like three emails stitched into one rather than a focused clearance send.

Judgment: solid top-of-funnel creative, diluted by reused mid-funnel modules. This is a 5-6, not because it's broken, but because it's the same brand playbook with a new headline pasted on top.

- Open-tracking pixel present: `https://data.em.dcsg.com/ee/v1/open?params=b.v1.UVYCfyn2V33WAKKVPrmxHkVbUuIqui-xXXFO_FVTECaNhx7_tUai8gkWrd4BcC_mW8i_j_kv` — flagged by QA only because it lacks alt text (expected/inert for a tracking pixel, not a defect).

Screen readers will announce filenames or nothing for these content/nav images — a real accessibility gap distinct from the tracking pixel.

- Web font (`Archivo`) is loaded via `@font-face` inside a `@media screen` block with `font-display:swap` — correctly scoped so Outlook (which ignores `@media screen`) falls back to `Arial,sans-serif` per the `body` style.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)

- **[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found (RFC 8058)** — same caveat; if genuinely absent, Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender requirements (enforced since Feb 2024) would not be met.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Links use DCSG's proprietary encrypted redirect params (`b.v1...`), not standard UTM query strings — cannot verify landing-page campaign attribution or destination URL from the truncated source.
- Destination URLs are not resolvable from the visible HTML (they route through the `mclick`/`click` redirector); no landing-page alignment can be confirmed without following redirects.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to the 11 flagged content images (product/category shots and nav icons); leave the open-tracking pixel's `alt=""` explicit rather than omitted, to signal intentional decorative status to screen readers.
2. Fix plain-text MIME part — current fallback is 9 characters, effectively non-functional for text-only clients and a spam-filter risk signal.
3. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are actually being sent by the origin ESP (Epsilon) — verify via a raw header capture outside the AgentMail relay, since Gmail/Yahoo require RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders.
4. Verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC authentication independently (e.g., via mail-tester.com or a direct SMTP header dump) since `Authentication-Results` wasn't captured here.
5. Spot-check a sample of the 50 unprobed links (especially primary CTAs beyond the top 12) to confirm redirect targets resolve without error.
