# Score Memorial Day deals: up to 50% off
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-24**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S sixteenth-plus promotional send in roughly a week, and the fourth distinct email branded around the "Epic Memorial Day Sale" creative I've reviewed since Friday. The render itself is competent: a bold red 50% OFF hero, a tidy category grid, and a "Summer Shop" anchor at the bottom. It's polished but it is unmistakably more-of-the-same — the brand is hitting the same drum repeatedly and the diminishing returns are obvious.
- What's different here is the layered offer structure — there's a clear stacking attempt: 50% off headline, plus an exclusive "$20 off $100+" lockup, plus category-specific "up to X% off" tiles for running shoes, DSG clothing, Quest baseball gear, and kayaks. That's actually a smarter pivot than the homogenous "shop the sale" sends earlier this week — it shows the merchandiser trying to give the eye somewhere specific to land.
- Net: a well-built but fatiguing email. As a walker the running-shoes tile is on-target; everything else is noise. A reasonable 6.

## What's working

- Bold red 50% OFF hero is unmissable and clearly conveys the headline offer.
- Category tiles give multiple landing options (running shoes, DSG apparel, Quest baseball, kayaks) instead of one generic CTA.
- "Memorial Day App Exclusive: $20 OFF Your Order of $100+" stacks a concrete dollar incentive on top of the headline %.
- "Summer Shop" callout at the bottom transitions readers from holiday → seasonal evergreen.
- Visual hierarchy is clean — hero, then category grid, then evergreen — easy to scan top-to-bottom.

## What's weak

- This is the 16th-ish send in a week with near-identical "Epic Memorial Day" creative — fatigue is high and unsubscribe risk is real.
- The hero is generic — no specific product, no "starts now / ends Monday" countdown.
- Subject line ("up to 50% off") is now indistinguishable from at least four prior subjects this week.
- Category tiles each say "Up to X% off" with no specific brand or product hook — just genre buckets.
- The $20-off-$100 deal is app-exclusive, which adds friction for inbox-only shoppers.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Differentiate from the prior 15 sends** — lead with a *new* angle (last-chance, doorbusters ending, specific brand-feature like "Brooks under $100") instead of the same 50% banner.
- 2. **Put the $20/$100 offer in the subject line** — it's the most concrete incentive in the email and it's buried mid-body.
- 3. **Replace one category tile with a hero product** — pick one running shoe with a real price drop and link to PDP, not category.
- 4. **Add a real deadline** — "ends Monday 11:59 PM" creates urgency the current copy lacks.
- 5. **Trim the email** — five category tiles + Summer Shop + footer is a long scroll; cut to three best categories.
