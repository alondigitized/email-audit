# [ Select CLEARANCE: extra 25% OFF — shop now! ]
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-31**
## Executive summary

- This is the "Final Hours" resend of the extra 25% off clearance offer that ran as "Extra 25% off select clearance is happening NOW!" on May 30 — same "DICK'S | Clearance" sub-brand, same red hero block, same offer mechanics. The countdown timer ("Final Hours 07:59:55") is the only meaningful creative differentiation from yesterday's 8/10-scoring send. That countdown is actually the email's strongest asset: it reframes a stale offer as an expiring one and gives a hesitant buyer a nudge.
- The structural problem is message fragmentation. A subscriber opens an email about "Extra 25% OFF" clearance, scrolls two modules, and immediately sees a "Flash Sale: Up to 50% Off" secondary banner. That cannibilizes the primary offer — if 50% exists, why click the 25% link? The SMS acquisition module ("WANT $20 OFF $100+ INSTANTLY?") in the middle of the purchase journey compounds the confusion. Three competing calls-to-action with three different value propositions make it harder to convert on any of them.
- At ten DICK'S sends in roughly four days, the brand is deep in subscriber fatigue territory. The "Final Hours" framing on an offer that was "happening NOW!" yesterday will read as deadline theater to anyone paying attention. Solid bones, self-sabotaged by internal competition and cadence debt.

## What's working

- **Countdown timer in the header** is the email's best differentiator — "Final Hours 07:59:55" makes a recycled offer feel time-pressured and is visually prominent.
- **Clean red hero with high-contrast type** — "EXTRA 25% OFF" lands immediately; no confusion about what the offer is.
- **Fine print disclosed upfront** — "Limited time savings on select styles" appears directly under the hero, not buried in the footer.
- **Category nav buttons** (Women, Kids, Fan Shop, All Sale) let different buyer types self-select quickly.
- **"Must-have gear for an epic summer!" product grid** anchors the email in the current season without being heavy-handed.

## What's weak

- **The Flash Sale "Up to 50% Off" module directly undercuts the 25% off hero** — the higher discount appearing below the primary offer breaks purchase intent and raises the obvious question: why click 25% off when 50% is also available?
- **SMS acquisition mid-funnel is disruptive** — "WANT $20 OFF $100+ INSTANTLY?" drops a non-clearance conversion goal into the middle of a clearance email journey.
- **"Final Hours" on an offer that was "happening NOW!" yesterday** will read as manufactured urgency to any engaged subscriber.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTAs are undifferentiated** — multiple orange buttons with the same copy compete with each other; none points to a specific product or category win.
- **No loyalty or member price signal** — DICK'S ScoreCard members get no differentiated reason to click over a casual visitor.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the Flash Sale module or reconcile the offer hierarchy.** Either this email is about 25% off clearance or it's about 50% off flash sale items — pick one and remove the other. The current structure invites the subscriber to skip the lower offer entirely.
- 2. **Make at least one CTA a category-specific verb+noun.** Swap "SHOP NOW" on the hero for "Shop Women's Clearance" or "Shop Sale Shoes" — gives the algorithm a clear scent trail and reduces bounce.
- 3. **Move the SMS module to below the product grid or the footer.** Mid-funnel acquisition interrupts the click path; let the purchase intent reach a natural pause before pivoting to list-building.
- 4. **Lean harder into the countdown as the hook, not just a header ornament.** Pull the deadline into the subject and preheader explicitly: "hours left, not days."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Final hours: extra 25% off clearance ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your clearance extra 25% off expires at midnight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Hundreds of styles — prices drop further at checkout`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Clock's running. Grab it before it's gone.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods Clearance Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard ESP redirect/beacon infrastructure (`app.dickssportinggoods.com`) and is structurally sound for most clients, but contains three significant issues: invalid JSON-LD that will silently break Gmail Promotions tab enrichment, an empty PromotionCard schema preventing the promo tile from rendering, and no UTM parameters reaching the destination after ESP click-redirect.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open-tracking pixel** is correctly hidden (`display:none; max-height:0px; overflow:hidden; mso-hide:all`) via the `#ssl` td:
```
https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019e7fba5171b67335f4bbcfb648/e8f9f9557b85...
```

**Click-tracking redirects** follow the pattern:
```
https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610000019e7fba5171b67335f4bbcfb648/<hash>/<uuid>?__dU__=<token>
```
These are ESP-managed and appear structurally valid.

**Truncated image URL** — the logo `src` is visibly cut off in the provided source:
```html
src="https://dsg.images.harmony.eps…
```
If this truncation reflects the actual sent HTML (not just the audit input), this is a broken asset.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**JSON-LD contains JavaScript-style comments**, which are invalid JSON and will cause the entire `<script type="application/ld+json">` block to fail silently in all parsers:
```json
// GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
// Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
```
These must be removed before parsing can succeed.

**`DiscountOffer` schema has stale/empty fields:**
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00",  // 6-year-old date
"availabilityEnds": "",                               // empty
"description": "",                                    // empty
"discountCode": ""                                    // empty
```
Gmail will reject or ignore a promotion with an expired `availabilityStarts`.

**`PromotionCard` image is empty:**
```json
"image": ""
```
This means no visual promo tile will render in the Gmail Promotions tab carousel.

**Table layout** uses `role="presentation"` correctly throughout. `lang="en"` is present on `<html>`. Mobile breakpoints at `max-width:480px` are defined. No missing `alt` attributes are detectable in the visible source, though the logo `<img>` alt is not visible in the truncated portion.

**Preheader text** ("Get $20 off $100+ when you sign up for Text Alerts\*\*") is placed inside a `height:0; font-size:0; line-height:0` td — this is the standard hidden preheader pattern and will render in inbox preview only. Intentional and correct.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Subject line bracket anomaly:**
```
[ Select CLEARANCE: extra 25% OFF — shop now! ]
```
The leading `[` and trailing `]` are either a CMS segment-label convention (e.g., `[Select]` targeting tier) or an unfilled template token wrapper. If the latter, the subject deployed with literal brackets — a deliverability and first-impression issue. Confirm against the ESP send log.

No unfilled `{{merge_tags}}`, `%%tokens%%`, or `[FIRSTNAME]`-style tokens are visible in the provided HTML body.

---

### 5. Compliance

**From domain:** `DSG@e.dcsg.com` — `e.dcsg.com` is Dick's ESP subdomain. DKIM/SPF/DMARC alignment cannot be assessed from HTML alone; must be confirmed in message headers.

**Physical mailing address and unsubscribe link** are not present in the provided HTML excerpt. These are CAN-SPAM mandatory. The HTML is noted as truncated, so these are likely in the footer — but must be verified in the full source before deployment sign-off.

**`availabilityStarts: "2020-03-06"` in DiscountOffer schema** is not a compliance issue per se, but suggests the structured data block is a stale template copy rather than a live-populated field.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

No UTM parameters are visible on any destination URL. All clicks pass through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...` redirects, and there is no evidence of UTM params being appended post-redirect.

Without `utm_source=email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=clearance-extra25` (or equivalent) on landing URLs, GA4 / Adobe Analytics will likely attribute this traffic to direct or referral rather than email. Confirm whether the ESP appends UTMs transparently at redirect time; if not, this is an analytics attribution gap for the clearance campaign.
