# Up to 60% off select clearance 👀 You won't want to miss this!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S eighth-plus send in four days — arriving the morning after yesterday's Flash Sale "Final Hours" email scored a 9/10 with a countdown timer. The creative pivot from Flash Sale back to evergreen Clearance is structurally sound, but the execution reads as a multi-category dump rather than a focused event. The hero lands well: "CLEARANCE SCORES!" in bold red against a dark nature backdrop is punchy and the Memorial Day badge gives it a timely peg. That is the email's ceiling moment — everything below is a scroll-and-forget parade of competing offers (sandals, hoops, TaylorMade 3X points, summer essentials) with no single story to follow.
- The 60% off headline is a genuine upgrade over the 50% ceiling that defined every send since May 13, but "select clearance" is doing a lot of cover work. The subject line drags past 60 characters and ends with "You won't want to miss this!" — hollow FOMO that undercuts an otherwise credible offer. The ScoreCard 3X-points block is the most interesting creative element on the page and it's buried mid-scroll.
- Competent but fatiguing. The brand has trained this list to expect discounts daily; the bar to earn an open is now higher than this send clears.

## What's working

- **Hero creative is the strongest in this week's run** — "CLEARANCE SCORES!" in bold red with Memorial Day event badge gives the email a clear seasonal hook that's been missing since the Flash Sale launched.
- **60% off is a concrete ceiling upgrade** — every send this week topped at 50%; the jump to 60% is visible and meaningful in both subject and body.
- **ScoreCard 3X points on TaylorMade is a smart loyalty insert** — specific brand + specific multiplier is more actionable than a generic "earn points" message.
- **"$100 Off Select Basketball Hoops"** — a dollar-amount offer is more tangible than a percentage, and hoops with Memorial Day framing feels timely and occasion-driven.
- **No visible render bugs** — images load, text hierarchy is intact, buttons are clean throughout.

## What's weak

- **Offer fragmentation kills click intent** — sandals (25% off), past season collections (20% off), life hacks (20% off), TaylorMade (3X points), basketball hoops ($100 off), summer essentials (40% off), and a product grid are all competing. There is no single thing to click on.
- **"SHOP NOW" is the only CTA verb and it's repeated identically across six+ sections** — no variation in button copy means no signal about what the click will deliver.
- **Cadence is visibly exhausting** — eighth send in four days. The subscriber who opened yesterday's Flash Sale "Final Hours" at 9 PM is opening this clearance dump at roughly the same time next morning. Even good creative gets discounted.
- **"While They Last" urgency is inert** — compared to yesterday's countdown timer (the single feature that pushed that email to 9/10), this deadline is invisible and unbelievable.
- **Subject length and tail copy undermine the offer** — "👀 You won't want to miss this!" adds 29 characters of clickbait that truncates the 60%-off hook on small screens.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut to one story: "Memorial Day Clearance Event, 60% off."** Drop four of the six offer blocks and let the hero + two supporting sections carry the whole email. The multi-category dump format has been the pattern for five days; a tighter edit would feel like a reset.
- 2. **Bring the countdown timer back.** Yesterday's 9/10 email earned that score almost entirely because it had a ticking clock. Memorial Day is a real, credible hard deadline — use it. A "Sale Ends Monday" banner above the hero would do it.
- 3. **Lead with ScoreCard loyalty earlier.** Move the 3X TaylorMade points block to the second section. A logged-in member seeing a specific multiplier on a specific brand earlier in the scroll is more likely to click than a member who encounters it after fatiguing through four offer blocks.
- 4. **Differentiate CTA copy by section** — "Shop Sandals," "Shop Hoops," "Shop TaylorMade" instead of six identical "SHOP NOW" buttons.
- 5. **Fix the subject line tail.** Strip "👀 You won't want to miss this!" entirely — the 60% clearance claim is the hook. Spend those characters on specificity or leave them on the table.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Memorial Day Clearance — up to 60% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `60% off ends Monday: clearance scores`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sandals, hoops, TaylorMade — reduced while stock lasts`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `ScoreCard members: 3X points stacked on 60% off clearance`

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods Clearance Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) send stack with standard conditional MSO rendering guards. Four confirmed issues require fixes before send: invalid JSON-LD (breaks Gmail Promotions Tab), a subject/preheader content mismatch, a stale schema date, and a malformed MSO CSS rule.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open pixel** — `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019e3578334cb86de6369ff0aab4/700e392b3d02485a00004c5a42963aa1` is correctly placed and hidden. No issue.

**Click-tracking** — All visible links route through the SFMC tracker (`app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610000019e3578334cb86de6369ff0aab4/…`). This is expected. Final destination URLs are not visible in the truncated source; UTM coverage cannot be confirmed (see §6).

**Logo image src truncated in source** — `https://dsg.images.harmony.epsi…` is cut off in the provided HTML. If this reflects the actual `src` attribute value (not just the audit excerpt), the logo will be a broken image in all clients. Verify the full URL resolves to a valid asset.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**MSO conditional CSS — stray double-quote (Outlook breakage)**
```css
div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
```
The trailing `"` before `}` is invalid and will cause Outlook's parser (which interprets this block literally) to misapply `div` reset styles. Remove the stray quote.

**Duplicate `font-family` declaration on `body`**
```css
body { font-family:Archivo,Arial,sans-serif; font-family:Archivo,Arial,sans-serif!important }
```
Redundant; harmless but indicates template sloppiness.

**Tracking pixel missing `alt` attribute** — The `<img>` in `#ssl` has no `alt=""`. Accessibility validators will flag this; add `alt=""` explicitly.

**Unfilled HTML comment left in preheader cell**
```html
<!--Replace/Paste SSL Here-->
```
This placeholder comment shipped inside the live `<td>`. Not rendered, but confirms the preheader content was not managed through the intended workflow.

**`role="presentation"` on layout tables** — Correctly applied. No issue.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**JSON-LD `DiscountOffer` — multiple empty fields (unfilled template tokens)**
```json
"description": "",
"discountCode": "",
"availabilityEnds": ""
```
These are empty strings, not merge syntax — they will serialize as empty and produce a malformed/incomplete schema record. If these were meant to be dynamic, the merge token substitution did not fire.

**`availabilityStarts` is 6 years stale**
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```
Almost certainly a carry-over from a prior template. This will mismatch any current offer window.

**`PromotionCard.image` is empty**
```json
"image": ""
```
The designer instruction `// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!` was not fulfilled. Gmail will not render a Promotions Tab card without a valid image.

---

### 5. Compliance

**Critical — JSON-LD contains JavaScript-style comments, making it invalid JSON**
```js
// GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
// Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
"@type": "PromotionCard",
// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
```
JSON does not support `//` comments. The entire `<script type="application/ld+json">` block will fail to parse in any strict JSON parser, including Gmail's Promotions Tab annotation engine. This means all three schema objects (Organization, DiscountOffer, PromotionCard) are silently dropped.

**Unsubscribe footer / CAN-SPAM** — Not visible in the truncated source. Cannot confirm presence of physical mailing address, unsubscribe mechanism, or `List-Unsubscribe` header. These must be verified against the full render.

**Authentication headers (SPF/DKIM/DMARC)** — Not evaluable from HTML alone. The sending domain `e.dcsg.com` should be confirmed as aligned with DMARC policy on `dcsg.com`.
