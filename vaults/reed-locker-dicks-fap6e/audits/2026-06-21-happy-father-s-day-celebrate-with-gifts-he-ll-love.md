# Happy Father's Day! 🎉 Celebrate with gifts he'll love...
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S Father's Day send, landing on June 21 — the occasion itself — which gives it the only genuine urgency signal they've had in a week of clearance spam. The creative breaks cleanly from the dark-red "EXTRA 25% OFF" banner that has dominated every prior send since at least June 16: the hero is bright, celebratory, and features a real couple rather than product-only photography. That alone lifts it above the recent cadence.
- The email is a kitchen-sink blast — running shoes, golf balls, swim, mountain bikes, summer gear grid — which fragments attention and makes every "SHOP NOW" feel like it goes to a random department. A subscriber with a specific Dad in mind has to do the work of finding their category rather than being handed a hero offer. The $10 ScoreCard Bonus Reward (same loyalty hook as the June 20 standalone loyalty email) is buried below the hero and never given top billing, even though it's arguably the most compelling call-to-action in the send.
- This is the best DICK'S email of the week, but that bar is low. The occasion justifies the send; the execution is adequate but not sharp.

## What's working

- **Occasion lock.** Father's Day hero copy and imagery are on-point — this doesn't feel stale or mismatched to the calendar.
- **Creative refresh.** No dark-red clearance banner. The bright, lifestyle-forward hero is a genuine departure from the prior five or six identical sends.
- **Loyalty integration.** The $10 ScoreCard Bonus Reward appears in-email alongside the sale content — it cross-sells the loyalty program to non-members while rewarding existing members.
- **Specific deal copy.** "2 for $100 TaylorMade TPs and Callaway Corona Series Golf Balls" is the most specific offer in the email and the kind of line a golfer notices immediately.
- **Category nav "Explore more deals" strip.** Golf and Fishing tabs let a motivated buyer self-select rather than hunt.

## What's weak

- **Generic "SHOP NOW" CTAs wall-to-wall.** With six or more distinct product sections, every button says the same thing. A golfer, a runner, and a cyclist all see identical CTAs — no section feels like it's speaking to them specifically.
- **$10 Bonus Reward is buried.** The most conversion-friendly offer (loyalty reward that lowers effective price) sits below the fold after the hero. It should be the hero's subhead or a sticky ribbon.
- **Kitchen-sink dilution.** Running shoes, swim, golf balls, bikes, and a catch-all summer gear grid in one email means no single category gets a real push. The whole feels like a clearinghouse, not a curated gift guide.
- **No explicit deadline copy on any offer.** "Father's Day is today" is implied, not stated. "Order by 2PM for same-day pickup" or "offer ends tonight" would have converted intent to action for in-store or curbside shoppers.
- **Cadence fatigue context.** This is the eighth or ninth DICK'S email in under a week — even a good send lands into a tired inbox. The prior June 20 loyalty email ($10 Bonus Reward at $150+) made this one feel slightly redundant on the ScoreCard hook.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the ScoreCard $10 Bonus as a Father's Day hook.** Make it the hero subhead: "Spend $100 on Dad, earn $10 back — today only." The loyalty reward + occasion urgency is the strongest combo in the email and it's currently on slide 3.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline.** Even "Shop by midnight" or "Same-day in-store pickup available" creates urgency that "gifts he'll love" doesn't.
- 3. **Collapse sections into 2-3 gift lanes.** Instead of six independent sections, build three: "The Golfer Dad," "The Active Dad," "The Outdoor Dad." Fewer, sharper, easier to navigate.
- 4. **Make CTAs section-specific.** "Shop Golf Gifts," "Shop Running Shoes," "Shop Bikes" — each one takes the buyer somewhere specific, not the homepage.
- 5. **Subject line carries no offer.** "Happy Father's Day" is nice but misses the chance to lead with the deal. Test against a value-forward line.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$10 Bonus Reward + Father's Day Deals — Today Only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Dad's Day gifts up to 50% off — shop before it's over`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Running shoes, golf gear, bikes & more — earn $10 back on $100+`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Father's Day deals end tonight. Earn $10 ScoreCard reward on $100+`

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM / footer — cannot audit.** The HTML is truncated before the footer. Physical mailing address and one-click unsubscribe link are required; these must be verified in the full source. The Epsilon platform typically injects these, but confirmation is necessary.

**JSON-LD structured data — critical defect:**

```json
<script type="application/ld+json">
[{
  ...
  // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  "image": ""
}]
</script>
```

Standard JSON **does not permit `//` comments**. This makes the entire JSON-LD block syntactically invalid. Gmail's structured data parser will reject it, disabling any Gmail Promotions tab annotation (deal badge, expiry display). Additionally:

| Field | Value | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| `availabilityStarts` | `"2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"` | Stale 2020 template date — not updated for this send |
| `availabilityEnds` | `""` | Empty — required for Gmail deal badge |
| `discountCode` | `""` | Empty — template placeholder not populated |
| `description` | `""` | Empty — template placeholder not populated |
| `image` (PromotionCard) | `""` | Designer placeholder comment not removed; field empty |

These are all pre-send template hygiene failures.
