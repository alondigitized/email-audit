# 👋 Our BEST deals of the week are waiting for you!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-28**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S going kitchen-sink: instead of the single-hero "up to 50% off paddleboard" creative they've run three times this week (7/24, 7/26, 7/28's own earlier variants), this send stacks nine-plus category blocks — Nike Deal Days, drinkware, team sports, golf, outdoor, fan shop, fitness, credit card, and a "must-have gear" grid — into one very long email. It's a legitimate format change, and the top-of-fold hero grid (beach kids, an On sneaker, a hiking high-five, and a bold "UP TO 50%" red block) is more varied and better composed than the recycled paddleboard-at-sunset creative from earlier this week.
- The problem is what it's compensating for: there's no single offer here, just "select styles" and "up to X%" repeated across every section, with a wall of exclusions/APR fine print at the bottom. It reads like a catalog dump rather than a targeted send, and the subject line does nothing to differentiate it from the four nearly-identical "deals" emails DICK'S has already sent this week.
- Cadence is the real issue. A subscriber who opened Monday's 2X Points email, Sunday's clearance email, and Friday's "Nike Deal Days" email is now looking at a fifth deals-flavored send in five days. Execution is clean — no render bugs, good brand consistency — but this is a volume play, not a targeted one.

## What's working

- Hero grid (beach kids / On sneaker / hikers / 50% off block) is genuinely more visually varied than the single recycled paddleboard hero DICK'S has leaned on all week.
- Clean, bug-free render throughout a very long email — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum, even seven-plus sections deep.
- ScoreCard points ("300 Points = $10 Reward") and the DICK'S Credit Card 10% back offer are both surfaced, giving loyalty members a visible reason to click.
- Section CTAs are specific to their category (EQUIPMENT DEALS, CLEAT DEALS, CLUB DEALS, LAUNCH CALENDAR) rather than one generic button repeated everywhere.
- Seasonally on-point: beach/kids imagery, "epic summer" gear grid, and camping/fishing/outdoor content all line up with a late-July send date.

## What's weak

- No single, memorable offer — every block says "up to X% off select styles/items," which reads as vague across nine repetitions and undercuts trust in the number.
- This is the fifth deals-themed send from DICK'S this week (7/24, 7/26 ×2, 7/27, 7/28 prior sends) — the subject gives a subscriber zero reason to believe this one is different.
- No prices, sizing, or availability shown on the "Must-have gear" product grid at the bottom — just product names under thumbnails, so there's nothing to evaluate before clicking.
- Zero social proof (no ratings, review counts, or "best seller" badges) anywhere in an email this long.
- Extremely dense fine print at the bottom (multiple "exclusions apply," 30.49% APR credit disclosure, same-day delivery caveats) undercuts the "up to 50% off" headline honesty.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this to one offer and one hero. Nine stacked category blocks dilute the 50%-off headline into catalog noise — pick the single best deal (Nike Deal Days is the strongest sub-creative here) and lead the whole email with it, moving the rest behind a "Shop more deals" link.
- 2. Put a real number in the subject line. "Up to 50% off" already exists in the creative — use it in the subject instead of the vague "BEST deals" framing, so this send doesn't read as identical to the last four.
- 3. Add prices to the "Must-have gear" grid — right now those four products (tumbler, cleats, Crocs) have names but no reason to click over any other product in the store.
- 4. Space this out from the rest of the week's cadence — five deals-emails in five days is going to train subscribers to skim past the subject line entirely.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 50% off — this week's biggest deals in one place`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Nike Deal Days + up to 50% off golf, outdoor & more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `ScoreCard members: earn points on every deal below.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New markdowns across Nike, golf, outdoor, and fan shop.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email renders on a standard Epsilon/Harmony-hosted template with MSO/Outlook conditional markup and mobile breakpoints, but ships with a broken unsubscribe link, malformed JSON-LD, and no visible authentication/list-management headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Broken unsubscribe destination (critical):** The "Unsubscribe" link (`https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/U/v610000019faa68dea9bf9feb369fe94b70/2543e3e14e404fed0000021ef3a0bd0b`) resolves to `/pc/Error/Error?aspxerrorpath=/pc/subscription` — an application error page, not a working preference/unsubscribe form. Confirmed by QA: `[FAIL] Link error: unknown url type`.
- All probed links route through the Epsilon Harmony click-tracking redirector (`app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...` and `/O/...`) with opaque `__dU__` signed tokens — standard for this ESP, no anomalies in the tracked links themselves.
- QA notes 12 of 76 links were probed (CTA/priority-ranked); 64 footer/social/utility links were not individually verified, so link rot elsewhere in the footer can't be ruled out.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **11 images missing `alt` text**, confirmed by QA: the SSL/tracking pixel, `banner4.gif`, `team.jpg`, `golf.jpg`, `outdoor.jpg`, `fan.jpg`, `fit.jpg`, and four footer icons (`icon-vc-sms.png`, `icon-value-sportsmatter--light.png`, `icon-vc-bpg.png`, `icon-vc-favorite.png`, `icon-location-2025.png`). This is a screen-reader accessibility gap on every content image in the template.
- MSO conditional CSS block contains a syntax error: `div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}` — a stray `"` before the closing brace will cause Outlook's conditional stylesheet to fail parsing past that rule.
- `.gif` asset (`banner4.gif`) is present despite the inline JSON-LD comment explicitly stating "GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out" — indicates this template placeholder guidance wasn't followed and the image may be stripped by some ESPs/clients.
- Mobile responsive rules (`@media screen and (max-width:480px)`) and Archivo web font with system fallback (`Archivo, Arial, sans-serif`) are correctly implemented with `font-display:swap`.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No merge-tag placeholders (e.g. `%%FIRSTNAME%%`, `{{first_name}}`) are present in the visible source — no personalization tokens to validate.
- The embedded `schema.org/DiscountOffer` and `PromotionCard` JSON-LD blocks ship with empty required fields: `"description": ""`, `"discountCode": ""`, `"image": ""`, `"availabilityEnds": ""`. These read as unpopulated template placeholders left in production markup rather than active personalization.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **CAN-SPAM risk:** The unsubscribe link is non-functional (see §2), which is a direct compliance issue — CAN-SPAM requires a working opt-out mechanism.
- `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe` header not detected — cannot confirm mailbox-level one-click unsubscribe is offered.
- `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not detected — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender rules) cannot be confirmed present.
- `[WARN] Authentication-Results` header not detected — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown from available data (may be a relay-capture limitation rather than a true absence).

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- All CTA/logo links route through the Harmony tracking redirector rather than exposing direct UTM-tagged destination URLs in the visible source, so UTM parameter presence/consistency cannot be verified from this HTML alone.
- Given the unsubscribe redirect itself terminates in a site error page (`aspxerrorpath=/pc/subscription`), there is at least one confirmed instance of email→site continuity failure.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Fix the unsubscribe link/preference-center route so it no longer 404s to `/pc/Error/Error` — this is the highest-priority item (compliance + broken UX).
2. Add descriptive (or empty decorative `alt=""`) attributes to the 11 flagged images.
3. Remove the stray `"` in the MSO conditional `<style>` block to prevent Outlook CSS parse failure.
4. Replace or remove `banner4.gif`, or confirm with the ESP why an explicitly unsupported format is still shipping.
5. Strip the unpopulated JSON-LD fields (`description`, `discountCode`, `image`, `availabilityEnds`) or populate them — empty required schema.org fields provide no SEO/rich-snippet value and may fail structured-data validation.
6. Verify List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post headers are actually being sent at the MTA level (may just be an AgentMail relay capture gap) and confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment via a direct header dump.
