# Your order could be up to 50% OFF — check this week's DEALS
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-08**
## Executive summary

- DICK'S returns to the weekly-deals compilation format — the same structure that scored 9/10 on June 5 — but this time dressed in a Father's Day wrapper, which is the right seasonal instinct for June 8. The hero is clean: a father/child sports moment with "Father's Day Savings Event" over it, and the Summer Essentials 50% off offer is the anchor. Compared to the nine-consecutive-clearance-drip that dominated late May through June 6, this is a genuine creative reset with new energy.
- The execution stumbles on the subject line. "Your order **could be** up to 50% OFF" is a conditional hedge — "could be" invites doubt where there should be confidence. The actual deals in the body are multiple and real: 50% off Summer Essentials, 40% adidas clothing, 20% Fan Shop Polos, 40% carry luggage, and a 60%-off section mid-scroll. That's a strong deal stack; the subject just undersells it.
- The email is long — visibly so — with roughly a dozen distinct deal blocks. That length works as a weekly digest but fights itself on mobile where any given reader will bounce before reaching the 60% hero buried below the fold.

## What's working

- **Father's Day hero is seasonally precise** — June 8 is one week out from Father's Day; the athletic father/child imagery is on-brand and on-time.
- **Multiple, genuine price points** — 50%, 40%, 20%, 60% off are all visible across different categories; this is not a single re-skinned clearance offer.
- **Format reset after clearance fatigue** — after nine "Extra 25% off clearance" sends, the compilation-deals format feels fresh.
- **60% off visual block mid-email** — the large red "60%" badge is the boldest offer in the email and will stop scrollers.
- **Category navigation grid** ("Explore more deals") helps subscribers self-sort quickly, reducing friction for non-Father's-Day shoppers.

## What's weak

- **"Could be" in the subject hedges the strongest hook** — "up to 50% OFF" is compelling; "could be up to 50% OFF" is not.
- **Email length is excessive** — a dozen deal blocks dilute each other; no single offer gets enough visual air to become a conversion moment.
- **60% off is buried mid-scroll** — the biggest discount in the email is the last thing the eye finds; it should be anchoring the hero.
- **No visible time deadline** — Father's Day is June 15 and the email doesn't say "Sale ends Sunday" or "Shop before June 15" anywhere visible.
- **"Check this week's DEALS" in the subject is vague** — it reads like a placeholder, not a hook.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the 60% deal, not 50%** — if your best number is 60%, that's the hero. Move it up.
- 2. **Cut the subject hedge** — rewrite "could be" out entirely.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Father's Day Deals — Up to 60% off this week`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 60% off for Father's Day — shop this week's best deals`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Summer Essentials, adidas, Fan Shop, luggage — all marked down before June 15.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop before Father's Day: 60% off sale items + 50% off Summer Essentials.`
- 3. **Add the June 15 deadline visually** — a single "Offer ends June 15" line under the hero would create urgency the body currently lacks.
- 4. **Reduce to 5–6 deal blocks maximum** — pick the highest-converting categories (shoes, apparel, equipment) and cut the rest; a tighter email scrolls faster and converts better.
- 5. **Put the Father's Day gift angle on the CTA** — "Shop Gifts for Dad" outperforms "Shop Now" in Father's Day windows.

## Full review
## 1. Overview

DICK'S returns to the weekly-deals compilation format — the same structure that scored 9/10 on June 5 — but this time dressed in a Father's Day wrapper, which is the right seasonal instinct for June 8. The hero is clean: a father/child sports moment with "Father's Day Savings Event" over it, and the Summer Essentials 50% off offer is the anchor. ## 3. What didn't

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)

**UTM parameters:** All CTAs resolve through the ESP redirect chain (`/T/{token}`). Whether UTM parameters are appended at redirect time is not determinable from the HTML source. Send a test, follow the redirect chain, and confirm that destination URLs carry `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` at minimum.

**JSON-LD structured data — critical issue:**

The `<script type="application/ld+json">` block contains **JavaScript-style `//` comments**, which are **invalid JSON syntax**. Gmail's structured data parser will reject the entire block, meaning the Promotions tab annotation (deal badge, discount code, expiry date) will not render.

Specific violations:

```js
// GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
// Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
```

These are designer working notes that were never stripped from the production template.

Additionally, `"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"` is a **stale date from 2020** — a template default that was not updated for this send. Even if the JSON were valid, Gmail would parse an already-expired promotion.
