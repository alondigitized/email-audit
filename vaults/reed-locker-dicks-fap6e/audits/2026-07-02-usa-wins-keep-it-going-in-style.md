# USA wins! Keep it going in style
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- DICK'S nails the reactive brief here. "USA wins!" is exactly the kind of moment-triggered email that earns its place in the inbox — it's not a manufactured urgency play, it's a genuine news hook, and the "CONGRATS, USA!" hero copy lands cleanly on top of a collage of USA jerseys and gear. This is the best creative execution DICK'S has shown in this ten-day stretch, which has otherwise been a monotonous discount drumbeat.
- The structural problem is that the emotional hook isn't paired with a financial hook. There's no price-off offer anywhere in this email — no %, no $, no BOGO — just a fulfillment pitch ("ORDER TODAY. SPORT IT TOMORROW.") that's compelling but doesn't move a fence-sitter to buy. The June 30 "Up to 50% OFF" sends had the opposite issue: a strong offer with weak creative. This one has strong creative with no offer.
- Compared to the June 28 "Next round starts TODAY ⚽" send (7/10), this is a clear step up in emotional resonance. But it would have been a 9/10 send with even a limited "24-hour victory offer" layered in.

## What's working

- **Reactive timing is genuine.** "USA wins!" tied to an actual match result is earned urgency — not a calendar coupon.
- **Hero composition is tight.** "CONGRATS, USA!" in DICK'S green over a product collage reads instantly, and the visual hierarchy flows cleanly from hero → category splits → fulfillment → product grid.
- **"ORDER TODAY. SPORT IT TOMORROW."** is the best line in the email — the fulfillment pitch is specific, benefit-led, and addresses the real purchase motivator (wearing it while the win is fresh).
- **Product grid is specific.** Six named adidas/Nike USA products with individual links beats a vague category page. The "Gear Up for Soccer's Biggest Stage" framing keeps the tournament narrative alive.
- **No render bugs.** Clean render, no broken images, no layout breaks.

## What's weak

- **No price offer.** This is a purchase email with no purchase incentive. After nine days of up-to-50%-off messaging, sending a full-price email with zero discount creates whiplash.
- **All CTAs say "SHOP NOW."** Every button — hero, USA Clothing, USA Gear, fulfillment, product grid — uses the same generic copy. A "Shop USA Jerseys" or "Get Your Victory Gear" variant would convert better at the top.
- **"USA Clothing" and "USA Gear" are inert category labels.** With "victory" as the narrative, these split-panel headers are a missed copywriting opportunity.
- **High send cadence is a risk.** This is at minimum the eleventh DICK'S email since June 26. Even a good reactive email gets discounted when subscribers are fatigued from daily sends.
- **Soccer-specific framing buried.** "Gear Up for Soccer's Biggest Stage" only appears mid-scroll; the hero says generic "Top USA Gear." For fans of the specific sport, leading with soccer earlier would sharpen relevance.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a time-limited victory offer.** Even a modest "25% off USA gear — 24 hours only" layered onto this reactive hook would give subscribers a reason to act now, not bookmark and forget. The creative is already there; it just needs a number.
- 2. **Differentiate the CTAs.** Change the hero button to "Shop Victory Gear," the USA Clothing button to "Shop USA Jerseys," and the USA Gear button to "Shop Soccer Gear." Each should match the section.
- 3. **Move the soccer call-out higher.** "Gear Up for Soccer's Biggest Stage" is the most specific framing in the email — surface it in the hero subhead rather than mid-scroll.
- 4. **Rest the cadence tomorrow.** Eleven sends in six days is a churn accelerator. Let this reactive win breathe for 48 hours before the next discount blast.
- **Subject Alt A:** `USA advance! 25% off victory gear — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The USA won. Here's how to celebrate in style`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop USA jerseys and gear while the win is still hot`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Order now, wear it tomorrow — one-hour pickup available`

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Critical — Invalid JSON in `<script type="application/ld+json">`:**

The structured data block contains JavaScript-style single-line comments (`//`), which are **illegal in JSON**. Gmail's parser will reject the entire block silently:

```json
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
  // Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
  "@context": "http://schema.org/",
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": ""
}
```

These appear to be designer instructions that were never stripped before send. This will break Gmail Promotions tab card rendering entirely.

**Empty required fields in JSON-LD:**

| Field | Value | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| `DiscountOffer.description` | `""` | Gmail offer badge won't populate |
| `DiscountOffer.discountCode` | `""` | No promo code shown in Promotions tab |
| `DiscountOffer.availabilityEnds` | `""` | No expiry signal to Gmail |
| `PromotionCard.image` | `""` | No preview image in Promotions card |

**Stale date — `availabilityStarts: "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"`:** This is a 6-year-old placeholder date, almost certainly a default template value that was never updated. Gmail may deprioritize offers with past availability windows.
