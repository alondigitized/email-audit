# Don't miss Nike Deal Days! Up to 30% OFF select styles is calling your name...
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- This is the most differentiated DICK'S send in the past week. Leading with Nike — a named brand rather than a generic badge — gives subscribers an actual reason to open instead of another "UP TO X% OFF THIS WEEK'S DEALS" repeat. The Nike hero is clean, the green CTA is visible, and the category rail below gives the email real browse utility. Structurally it's the best execution in this series.
- The problem is context. DICK'S has sent seven-plus promotional emails in five days, and subscribers who've already been pummeled with 50–70% offers are now staring at 30% off Nike as the headline number. That's a step down in offer size, even if it's brand-specific and more credible. The Summer Clearance 70% badge tacked at the bottom doesn't help — it undermines the Nike campaign by loudly advertising a bigger discount two scrolls away.
- Verdict: good email in isolation, badly timed in a bloated send cadence. The Nike hook earns opens; the context erodes conversion.

## What's working

- Nike logo and co-branded hero give this email an identity the recent generic blasts lacked — a brand name is a promise, not just a number.
- Visual hierarchy is clean: Nike hero → green CTA → category rail, the eye flows correctly without fighting competing banners.
- Category rail is genuinely useful — Golf, Outdoor, Team Sports, Footwear, Fitness each carry their own discount callout and "SHOP" CTA, so any athlete can find their lane.
- 30% OFF is stated clearly and unambiguously in the hero with "select styles" qualifier visible — no buried-fine-print surprise.

## What's weak

- 30% feels like a demotion when 50–70% Summer Clearance has been the drumbeat all week — the 70% badge reappearing at the bottom of this very email makes the Nike 30% look stingy by comparison.
- Subject line is 78 characters — way over mobile clip (~45–50 chars). "...is calling your name..." trails into the ellipsis territory where most subscribers never read it.
- Send fatigue is real: this is at minimum the eighth promotional email from DICK'S in five days. Even a well-crafted Nike send lands in a poisoned inbox environment.
- No urgency anchor with a credible end date — "Deal Days" implies limited time but doesn't prove it, so the push to act now is weak.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the subject to under 50 chars** — everything after "Up to 30% OFF" is noise that clips on mobile anyway.
- 2. **Add an end date to the offer** — "Nike Deal Days ends Sunday" creates real urgency where "Deal Days" implies vague urgency.
- 3. **Remove the 70% clearance section from the bottom** — it cannibalizes the Nike campaign and invites the subscriber to skip the branded offer for a bigger generic discount.
- 4. **Lead a category with one specific Nike product** (e.g., a best-selling shoe or kit) — one concrete anchor product improves click rates meaningfully over pure browse-rail emails.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Nike Deal Days: 30% OFF ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Nike's biggest sale of the summer — shop now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Running, training, golf — 30% off the Nike styles you've been watching.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Nike Deal Days are here. Free shipping on orders $49+.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods Nike Deal Days Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard table-based responsive email with MSO conditionals and an ESP redirect-tracking layer. Four confirmed technical defects: invalid JSON-LD (will break Gmail promotional annotations), a stale promotion date, a malformed MSO CSS rule, and a missing `alt` attribute on the tracking pixel.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**ESP redirect domain**: All links route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...` with opaque token paths. No UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are present in the pre-redirect `href` values. If the redirect layer does not append UTMs, clicks will land as direct traffic in analytics.

**Recipient identity token exposed in link text**: The logo CTA contains `?__dU__=v0G4RBKTXg2Gs8NEX0-...` as a visible query parameter appended after the redirect token. This is normal ESP behavior but the token is long enough to fingerprint individual recipients if forwarded.

**Tracking pixel**: `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019f7c007dbaa884fbf4bbe5be68/...` is the open-tracking pixel. No issues with the pixel mechanism itself.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**MSO conditional CSS — stray quote character** (confirmed bug):
```css
div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
```
The `"` before `}` is a syntax error. In Outlook's MSO renderer this may corrupt the rule block and cause `div` elements to render with unexpected spacing or font sizes.

**Tracking pixel missing `alt` attribute**:
```html
<img src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/...' style="display:none; max-height: 0px; ..."/>
```
No `alt=""` is present. Screen readers encountering a broken or slow-loading image may announce the raw URL. Add `alt=""` and `role="presentation"`.

**Logo `<img>` tag**: Truncated in source — `alt` text presence cannot be confirmed. Must verify it includes descriptive alt text (e.g., `alt="DICK'S Sporting Goods"`).

**Google Fonts dependency**: `@font-face` loads `Archivo` from `fonts.gstatic.com`. This is scoped inside `@media screen` so it only fires in webmail clients, but it does transmit the subscriber's IP to Google on render.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**JSON-LD `DiscountOffer` has three unfilled template slots**:
```json
"description": "",
"discountCode": "",
"availabilityEnds": ""
```
These are empty strings, not populated values. The promotion card will render with no description or end date in Gmail's deal annotations.

**`availabilityStarts` is six years stale**:
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```
This is a hardcoded value from a prior campaign template that was never updated. Gmail's promotional tab parser may discard the entire promotion card as expired.

**`PromotionCard` image is empty**:
```json
"image": ""
```
The designer placeholder comment (`// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!`) was never resolved. No promotion card image will render.

---

### 5. Compliance

**JSON-LD contains JavaScript-style comments — invalid JSON** (critical):
```json
// GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
// Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
```
JSON (RFC 8259) does not permit `//` comments. Any strict JSON parser — including Gmail's structured data extractor — will reject the entire `<script type="application/ld+json">` block. This means zero Gmail promotional annotations will render despite the template being present.

**CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe footer**: Not present in the truncated source. Cannot confirm. Must verify the footer contains: (1) physical mailing address, (2) clear unsubscribe mechanism, and (3) sender identification. These are required.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

UTM parameters are not visible in any `href` in the provided source. All links are opaque ESP redirects (`/T/...`). If the redirect service does not inject `utm_source=email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=nike-deal-days` (or equivalent) on the outbound redirect, all click traffic will be attributed as direct in site analytics — making campaign ROI unmeasurable.

Recommend: confirm via redirect inspection (e.g., `curl -I <redirect_url>`) whether UTMs are appended at redirect time.

---
