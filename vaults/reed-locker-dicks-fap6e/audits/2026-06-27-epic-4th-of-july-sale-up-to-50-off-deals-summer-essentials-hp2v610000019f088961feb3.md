# EPIC 4th of July Sale 🤝 Up to 50% off DEALS + summer essentials...
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- DICK'S has executed a clean campaign pivot: Deal Days ended June 26 with multiple "last chance" emails, and June 27 opens a fresh chapter under the 4th of July banner. The creative reset is smart — the hero is clearly distinct from the green Deal Days look — but the underlying problem is that the subscriber's inbox has been saturated for three-plus days. "EPIC 4th of July Sale" reads like the next episode of a show that already overstayed its welcome this week.
- The email is operationally solid. The hero lands the headline, the offer stack is generous (30% off On Cloud, 60% off VRST, 75% off Calia, $400 off golf clubs, $60 off life vests, 50% off basketball shoes), and the category navigation below the fold is clean. What it lacks is a reason to act *today* — there's no explicit deadline visible in the hero. "4th of July" implies one, but a subscriber who just survived five days of Deal Days urgency messaging needs more than an implied cutoff.
- Bottom line: a well-assembled evergreen-sale email with a timely seasonal hook, hamstrung by cadence fatigue and a hero CTA that's too generic for the offer density below it.

## What's working

- **Clean campaign pivot.** The hero visually breaks from Deal Days; the 4th of July frame resets the urgency clock credibly.
- **Offer density is excellent.** Six discrete percentage-off blocks (On Cloud, VRST, Calia, golf clubs, life vests, basketball shoes) give almost any sports consumer something relevant.
- **Category nav bar** (Men / Women / Kids / Shoes / More) right below the hero reduces friction for browsers.
- **App referral block** ("Want $20 Off $100 Instantly?") is a clear, green-contrast secondary CTA that works without competing with the hero.
- **No render bugs.** Image, layout, text blocks all render cleanly; no broken images or overlapping copy visible.

## What's weak

- **Hero CTAs are too generic.** "SUMMER SALE" and "SUMMER SHOP" don't reflect the 4th of July angle or a specific category — a subscriber who arrived from the subject line expecting a tied-to-4th-of-July offer gets two interchangeable buttons.
- **No visible deadline in the hero.** The holiday implied cutoff (July 4th) is never stated; after five days of countdown urgency from Deal Days, the absence is noticeable.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is roughly the 12th DICK'S email in four days by brand history count. Even a good email is blunted at that frequency.
- **"Up to 50% off" is the lead copy but the hero image doesn't show any priced product** — the discount claim floats without an anchor product to make it concrete.
- **Subject line is too long.** At ~66 characters it truncates on mobile, cutting off "summer essentials..." before it lands.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add "Ends July 4th" to the hero** — one line of deadline copy above or below the CTA buttons. This is the single highest-lift change. Deal Days worked because "ends TODAY" was visible. This sale should say "Ends July 4th — 4 days left" or similar.
- 2. **Rewrite the hero CTAs to category-specific buttons.** Replace generic "SUMMER SALE" / "SUMMER SHOP" with split CTAs tied to the top two offers: "Shop Shoes Deals" and "Shop Golf Deals." Or, if keeping one button, make it "Shop 4th of July Sale."
- 3. **Anchor the hero to one hero product.** Feature the On Cloud or basketball shoe with a starting price ("from $89") to make the "up to 50% off" feel real.
- 4. **Reduce send frequency.** Consider a 24-hour gap before the next email; the subscriber's openability is degraded by saturation.

- **Subject Alt B:** `4 days to score big: 50% off shoes, golf, apparel`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `On Cloud, VRST, Calia, golf clubs — your summer gear, deeply discounted.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `6 deals worth clicking — shop before the holiday.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods "4th of July Sale" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Table-based layout with SFMC (Salesforce Marketing Cloud) click/open tracking, MSO conditional comment blocks for Outlook, and a JSON-LD structured data block. Three confirmed code-level defects: invalid JSON in the `<script>` block, a CSS syntax error in the MSO conditional, and stale/empty schema fields that will silently fail Gmail annotation parsing.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**SFMC tracking URLs confirmed** — all links route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/` (click) and `app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/` (open pixel), standard SFMC behavior.

**No UTM parameters visible** in any href shown. The `__dU__` query param is SFMC-internal. If destination pages don't receive `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` from the redirect, analytics attribution will be lost. Cannot confirm from truncated source whether they're appended post-redirect.

**Tracking pixel missing explicit `alt=""`:**
```html
<img src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/...'
     style="display:none; max-height: 0px; font-size: 0px; overflow: hidden; mso-hide: all"/>
```
No `alt` attribute. While hidden, some accessibility scanners flag this as a violation.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS syntax error in MSO conditional comment block** — the `div` rule has a stray double-quote before the closing brace:
```css
div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
```
The `"` before `}` is invalid. Outlook will drop the entire rule.

**Logo image `src` is truncated** in the provided source (`https://dsg.images.harmony.eps…`). Cannot confirm `alt` attribute presence. If missing, screen readers will announce the URL or filename.

**Duplicate `font-family` declaration** on `body` (minor, no functional impact):
```css
body{font-family:Archivo,Arial,sans-serif;font-family:Archivo,Arial,sans-serif!important}
```

**Preheader/`#ssl` cell text exposure risk:**
```html
<td id="ssl" ... height="0" style="padding:0; font-size:0; line-height:0; height:0;">
  ...
  Get $20 off $100+ when you sign up for Text Alerts**
</td>
```
Raw text node relies on `font-size:0` and `height:0` for suppression. This is unreliable across Outlook versions and some webmail clients — the text may render visibly in the body. Standard practice is a `display:none; max-height:0; overflow:hidden;` wrapper `<div>` around the preheader string.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Empty merge fields left in JSON-LD** (will silently fail Gmail promotion annotation):
```json
"description": "",
"discountCode": "",
"availabilityEnds": "",
"image": ""
```

**Stale `availabilityStarts` date** — hardcoded as `"2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"` in a 2026 email. This is a 6-year-old template date that was never updated.

**Unfilled designer placeholder left in production JSON-LD:**
```javascript
// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
"image": ""
```
This comment (and others) should never reach production — see next section.

---

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM elements not visible** in the truncated source (physical mailing address, unsubscribe link). These must appear in the footer — cannot confirm from provided HTML. Flag for full-source verification.

**Sending domain:** `DSG@e.dcsg.com` — `e.dcsg.com` is the SFMC bounce/sending subdomain. SPF/DKIM/DMARC authentication should be verified at the DNS level for this subdomain; not verifiable from HTML alone.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Cannot fully verify without seeing redirect destinations for the SFMC `/T/` tracking links. Key risk: if UTM parameters (`utm_source=email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=july4sale`) are not present on landing pages, all session attribution in GA4/Adobe will fall to direct.

Subject line references "summer essentials" — landing page alignment cannot be confirmed from truncated source.
