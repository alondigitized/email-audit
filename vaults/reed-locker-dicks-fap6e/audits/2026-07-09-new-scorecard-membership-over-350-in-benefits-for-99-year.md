# NEW! ScoreCard+ membership 🌟 Over $350 in benefits for $99/year! 
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S breaking a week-long discount-hammering cycle with a paid membership pitch — and the contrast is their biggest asset here. After six consecutive sends riding "up to 70% off clearance" and "this week's deals at 50% off," a $99/year membership email with a clear ROI promise ($350+ in benefits) reads as fresh and substantive. The value math is laid out plainly: join for $99, get $100 annual bonus rewards + free standard shipping every day + a limited-time $30 kickback. That stacks visibly above cost, and "It Pays for Itself!" is the right framing.
- The execution is competent but leaves points on the table. The hero is a golf-only action shot — fine for the sport, narrow for a cross-sport membership sell. The CTA "LEARN MORE" is the weakest possible label for a paid conversion ask; it hedges when the product itself doesn't need to. And the $30 limited-time offer, the most urgent element in the email, is buried in the third icon column with no visible deadline date.
- The strategic read: this email is right to exist right now and will outperform another "50% off deals" send. But the creative execution underdelivers on the conversion moment.

## What's working

- **The value math is explicit up front.** "$350+ in Member Benefits for $99/Year" is the headline and it earns the "It Pays for Itself!" call — subscribers can do the math without scrolling.
- **Format pivot after a heavy discount cadence.** After the July 5–8 clearance blitz, this membership pitch reads as genuinely different content and should lift opens by novelty alone.
- **Benefits breakdown is clear and scannable.** Three icons ($100 rewards, free shipping, $30 off) map directly to the promise and give subscribers something concrete to hold onto.
- **"New!" badge on ScoreCard+ is correctly placed** — signals this is an announcement, not a routine email.

## What's weak

- **Golf-only hero image narrows the audience.** ScoreCard+ is a cross-sport, cross-category membership; one sport's action shot implicitly signals this is for golfers.
- **"LEARN MORE" CTA is too soft for a $99 conversion ask.** This button needs to do conversion work, not discovery work.
- **Limited-time $30 offer has no visible deadline.** "Limited Time Offer" without a date is discarded urgency — and it's the most compelling accelerant in the email.
- **Dense fine print undercuts the "pays for itself" promise.** Multiple asterisks on $350+ and $99/year with a dense legal block at the bottom creates a trust drag precisely at the moment the email is trying to close.
- **No social proof.** A membership pitch with zero testimonials, reviews, or member counts asks you to trust math alone.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap "LEARN MORE" for "Join ScoreCard+ — $99/Year"** or "Start Saving Today" — anything with the price and a verb. A paid membership CTA must include the cost; hiding it creates friction at the click, not before.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the limited-time $30 offer** — even "through July 31" makes the urgency real and gives a second reason to act now rather than later.
- 3. **Replace the golf-only hero with a multi-sport grid** (golfer, runner, soccer player) to signal this is for every DICK'S shopper, not one category.
- 4. **Add a one-line member count or testimonial** near the CTA — "Join 2M+ ScoreCard+ members" or a three-word pull quote would close the trust gap.
- 5. **Condense the fine print** or move it behind a "See full terms" link — the wall of caveats directly below the benefits section undermines the ROI pitch visually.
- **Subject Alt A:** `ScoreCard+ is here — $350+ in benefits for $99/year`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Pay $99. Get $350+ back. ScoreCard+ launches today.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `$100 in bonus rewards, free shipping every day, and a limited-time $30 bonus — all for $99/year.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your $99 membership starts paying off on your very first order.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Table-based layout with proper MSO conditional comments and `role="presentation"` on layout tables. Critical issue: the JSON-LD structured data block contains invalid syntax (JavaScript comments inside JSON) and multiple unfilled template fields, which will cause parsing failures in Gmail's structured data processor.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Click-tracking domain:** `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...`
**Open-pixel domain:** `app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/...`

Both use an opaque hash-encoded redirect system with a `__dU__` parameter. UTM passthrough cannot be confirmed from the encoded URL alone — destination UTM params are not human-readable in the source.

**Tracking pixel placement:** The open-pixel `<img>` is inside `<td id="ssl">` alongside preheader text. This is unconventional; the pixel and the preheader copy share the same cell, relying on `display:none; max-height:0` on the pixel only. If `max-height` is stripped by a client, the pixel image could render visibly.

**Template artifact comment:** `<!--Replace/Paste SSL Here-->` appears directly below the open-pixel `<img>`. This suggests the SSL block was partially templated and may not have been fully replaced — flag with the ESP team to confirm the pixel URL is production-valid.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **Tracking pixel** (`<img src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/...'>`): missing `alt=""`. Screen readers may announce the URL. Add `alt=""`.
- **Logo `<img>` tag** is truncated in the provided source (`src…`) — cannot verify `alt` text. Confirm alt is set (e.g., `alt="DICK'S Sporting Goods"`).
- **Web font loading:** Archivo loaded inside `@media screen { @font-face {...} }` — correct pattern; prevents Outlook from attempting to download the font. Fallback chain `Arial, sans-serif` is valid.
- **MSO conditionals:** Present and structurally correct. `border-collapse:collapse` and `background-color:inherit` overrides in the MSO block are standard.
- **`role="presentation"`** applied to all layout tables — correct.
- **`lang="en"`** on `<html>` — present, correct.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**JSON-LD block contains multiple unfilled template fields and invalid syntax:**

```json
{ "@type": "DiscountOffer",
  "description": "",          // ← empty
  "discountCode": "",         // ← empty
  "availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00",  // ← stale date (6 years ago)
  "availabilityEnds": ""      // ← empty
},
{ "@type": "PromotionCard",
  "image": ""                 // ← empty — no promo image set
}
```

**Invalid JSON — JavaScript comments present inside the `<script type="application/ld+json">` block:**

```
// GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
// Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
```

JSON does not support `//` comments. This makes the entire JSON-LD block unparseable. Gmail will silently discard the structured data, and any Google Promotions tab annotations will fail to render. The comments appear to be designer instructions that were never removed before send.

No standard merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`) are visible in the truncated source.

### 5. Compliance

HTML is truncated before the footer — cannot audit unsubscribe link, physical mailing address, or "how you received this" copy directly. Items to verify manually:

- **CAN-SPAM:** Confirm footer contains: (a) physical postal address, (b) clear unsubscribe mechanism, (c) non-deceptive subject line. Subject `NEW! ScoreCard+ membership 🌟 Over $350 in benefits for $99/year!` is factual and not deceptive on its face.
- **Sending domain:** `e.dcsg.com` (subdomain of `dcsg.com`). Verify SPF, DKIM, and DMARC are configured on this subdomain — ESP-managed domains sometimes have looser DMARC policy (`p=none`) on sending subdomains.
- **`availabilityStarts: "2020-03-06"`** in the JSON-LD DiscountOffer schema is a stale timestamp and could be flagged by Gmail's structured data validator as a past-dated offer.
