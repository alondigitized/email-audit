# Final day tomorrow: up to 70% off select clearance picks
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-31**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S running the same red "UP TO 70% OFF" kitchen-sink playbook they've now sent at least four times since 7/30 — same hero color, same category-block cascade (Nike, golf, camp chairs, racquet gear, Peloton shoes, app promo, "must-have gear" grid). The one new lever here is genuine deadline urgency: "Ends Tomorrow" tags appear twice (hero and Flash Sale block), and the subject explicitly says "Final day tomorrow," which is a real behavioral trigger this list hasn't leaned on as hard in prior sends.
- The execution itself is clean — no render bugs, clear button hierarchy, decent product photography — but it's the fourth-plus variant of an identical red-hero-plus-stacked-deals template this brand has run in six days. A subscriber who opened the 7/29 or 7/30 sends will recognize this instantly, and the "up to 70% off" headline number has been reused enough that it's starting to read like wallpaper rather than a real offer.
- Net: solid urgency mechanic buried in a fatigued, over-familiar layout. Worth opening for the deadline; not worth much visual attention once opened.

## What's working

- Clear "Ends Tomorrow" urgency tags on both the hero and the Flash Sale sub-block, reinforcing the subject line's deadline.
- Primary CTA ("SHOP NOW") is repeated consistently and is unambiguous at every scroll stop.
- Clean category navigation pills (Men's, Women's, Kids', $25 and Under, Fan Shop, All Sale) right under the hero for fast wayfinding.
- No layout breakage, broken images, or overlapping text anywhere in the render.
- Fine print about exclusions is visible but small — not hidden or misleading, just present.

## What's weak

- This is the same red "UP TO 70%" hero treatment used on 7/30 twice and echoed by the "up to 50%" hero variants running most of the week — zero visual differentiation from recent sends.
- Kitchen-sink structure returns: golf, camp chairs, racquet sports, Peloton shoes, app download, and a 6-item product grid all stacked below the fold — no prioritization of what's actually on the "Final day tomorrow" clock.
- The subject's specific "final day tomorrow" urgency doesn't carry through the body — most category blocks below the hero don't repeat any deadline, so it reads like urgency applies only to the top two blocks, not the full sale.
- "Up to 70%" in the hero vs. "Up to 50%" in the very next block right below it creates immediate confusion about which number is real.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make "final day" urgency structural, not decorative — add a countdown or "Ends [date] 11:59pm" stamp to every major block, not just the top two, so the whole email feels like one deadline instead of two urgent sections bolted onto a generic sale email.
- 2. Cut the stacked category list by at least half — golf, racquet gear, and Peloton shoes competing for attention on the actual last day is the wrong moment for a kitchen-sink layout; last-chance emails convert better narrow and urgent.
- 3. Resolve the 70%/50% number clash — lead with one headline discount or clearly segment "up to 70%" (clearance) from "up to 50%" (flash sale) with distinct visual zones, not adjacent stacked blocks.
- 4. Move the countdown/urgency treatment higher and bolder — a literal countdown timer graphic would outperform a static "Ends Tomorrow" tag for a last-day send.

## Technical Audit: DICK'S Sporting Goods — "Final day tomorrow" (Clearance)

### 1. Technical Summary
Email renders via a standard Epsilon/Harmony-hosted template with MSO conditional fallbacks and mobile breakpoints, but ships with a broken unsubscribe redirect, invalid JSON-LD markup, and no captured authentication/unsubscribe headers.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All hyperlinks route through the Epsilon Harmony click-tracking domain `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...` (redirector) rather than direct dickssportinggoods.com URLs — standard ESP behavior, not a defect.
- **Broken redirect target**: the Unsubscribe link (`https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/U/v610000019fb92df9128bc72c6e96c566a0/256a9edea32340b90000021ef3a0bcff`) resolves to an error page — QA logged: `unknown url type: '/pc/Error/Error?aspxerrorpath=/pc/subscription'`. This is a functional failure, not just a compliance gap (see §5).
- 12 of 62 total links were probed by automated QA (material/CTA links); footer/social links were deprioritized by design, not skipped due to error.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Invalid JSON-LD**: the `<script type="application/ld+json">` block contains `//`-style JavaScript comments (e.g. `// GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.`, `// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!`). JSON does not support comments — this will fail to parse in strict JSON parsers and any Gmail/schema.org structured-data consumer, silently breaking rich preview annotations.
- **Missing `alt` attributes** on 10 images per QA, including content images (`three-images-1.png`, `banner.gif`, `secondary-base-03.jpg`, `tertiary-base-01.jpg`/`02.jpg`) and footer utility icons (`icon-vc-sms.png`, `icon-value-sportsmatter--light.png`, `icon-vc-bpg.png`, `icon-vc-favorite.png`, `icon-location-2025.png`). This degrades screen-reader accessibility and fallback text when images are blocked.
- Fonts load via `@font-face` (Archivo, Google Fonts CDN) inside `@media screen` — correctly scoped to avoid Outlook/desktop rendering issues; MSO conditional comments present for `<xml>` and legacy `<style>` fallback.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No visible merge/personalization tags (e.g. `%%FIRSTNAME%%`) in the rendered source — subject/body appear static.
- The `DiscountOffer` and `PromotionCard` JSON-LD objects contain unpopulated template fields (`"description": ""`, `"discountCode": ""`, `"availabilityEnds": ""`, `"image": ""`), indicating dynamic content placeholders that were left unfilled at send time rather than merge failures per se.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Unsubscribe link is broken** (see §2) — it lands on an aspx error page instead of a subscription-management page. This is a functional CAN-SPAM risk beyond a header/warning issue.
- `List-Unsubscribe` header: not found — one-click unsubscribe via mail client UI unavailable (or not captured by the AgentMail relay).
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header (RFC 8058): not found — one-click unsubscribe (POST) not confirmed supported.
- `Authentication-Results` header: not found — SPF/DKIM pass/fail status cannot be verified from this capture.
- Note: the two header findings carry a relay caveat from QA (may not be captured by AgentMail relay rather than genuinely absent) — flagged as WARN, not FAIL, for that reason.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Source is truncated before any link resolves past the `app.dickssportinggoods.com` tracking redirector, so destination UTM parameters and landing-page alignment cannot be confirmed from the available HTML.
- Given the confirmed broken Unsubscribe redirect, recommend verifying the primary CTA/promo links also resolve correctly end-to-end rather than assuming parity.

### 7. Recommendations
1. Fix the Unsubscribe link mapping in the ESP (Epsilon Harmony) — it currently 404s to `/pc/Error/Error?aspxerrorpath=/pc/subscription` instead of the subscription-preference page.
2. Remove `//` comments from the `application/ld+json` block or replace with valid JSON (no comments) so structured data parses correctly.
3. Add `alt` text to all 10 flagged images, prioritizing content images (banner, three-images, secondary/tertiary base) over decorative footer icons.
4. Populate or remove the empty `DiscountOffer`/`PromotionCard` JSON-LD fields rather than shipping empty-string placeholders.
5. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are actually being sent (check ESP config directly, independent of relay capture) to support RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe.
