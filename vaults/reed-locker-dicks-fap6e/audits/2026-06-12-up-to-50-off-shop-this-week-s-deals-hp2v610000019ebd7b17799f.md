# *** Up to 50% off: shop this week's deals ***
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- This is the Father's Day Savings Event in its second consecutive day — same multi-module compilation format, same 50% ceiling, same category tiles (Men's / Women's / Kids' / Golf / Outdoor), but with a noticeably worse subject line. Yesterday's Father's Day send ("💰 Father's Day Savings Event = Up to 50% OFF 💰") earned a 9/10 partly because the subject matched the body's seasonal hook. Today's version leads with `*** Up to 50% off: shop this week's deals ***` — generic deal language wrapped in asterisks that reads as lazy at best and spam-adjacent at worst. The body is still Father's Day branded and the creative holds up, but the inbox-first impression is a step backward.
- The email is competently executed. The hero banner lands clearly on "FATHER'S DAY SAVINGS EVENT" with golfer imagery, the category tiles are scannable, and the same-day delivery reminder adds practical value. But the Summer Essentials secondary module near the hero undercuts the headline — if the featured sub-offer is weaker than the category tiles below it, you're burying the lead.
- The cadence damage is real. Three "up to 50% off" emails in two days from the main DICK'S account, two of them functionally identical to yesterday's Father's Day send. Subscribers who opened yesterday have seen this creative. Opens and clicks will be measurably lower than the June 11 send.

## What's working

- Father's Day hero is still clean — golfing imagery + event header lands on context immediately.
- Category tiles (Men's, Women's, Kids', Golf, Outdoor) give the subscriber a self-sorting path to what's relevant to them.
- Same-day delivery callout is practical and low-effort for a last-minute Father's Day angle.
- Product grid at the bottom gives something concrete to engage with if the category modules don't hook.
- Layout is render-clean — no broken images, no overlapping text visible.

## What's weak

- Subject line uses `***` asterisk wrapping — spam-signal aesthetics that erode sender trust and inbox-render quality on some clients.
- "Shop this week's deals" in the subject is generic and disconnects from the Father's Day body — the June 11 send was better-aligned.
- The Summer Essentials sub-module near the hero appears to be a softer offer than the 40% off category tiles beneath it — weaker offer in the prime real estate slot.
- No urgency visible — Father's Day is Sunday June 15 (two days out), and the email never says so.
- This is the same email the subscriber opened yesterday; no creative differentiation to justify the repeat.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the deadline in the subject.** Two days out from Father's Day is genuine urgency — use it. "Father's Day is Sunday — up to 50% off ends tonight" would outperform the current generic subject significantly.
- 2. **Drop the asterisks.** `***` around a subject line is visual noise and a mild spam signal. If you need emphasis, use an emoji like yesterday's `💰` (which tested well at 9/10).
- 3. **Swap the Summer Essentials module for the strongest single-category offer** (Golf given Father's Day targeting) — the hero category should match the occasion, not dilute it with a seasonal sidebar.
- 4. **Add a visible deadline to the body.** "Offer ends June 15" anywhere above the fold would turn this compilation into a closer.
- 5. **If this email had to go out**, differentiate it from yesterday with a new hook — "Last chance" framing, a new product spotlight, or a single hero deal not in yesterday's send.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Father's Day is Sunday — up to 50% off ends soon`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last chance Father's Day deals: up to 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Golf, men's, outdoor & more — shop before Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Same-day pickup available — don't miss the Father's Day sale`

The email uses Epsilon Harmony for link tracking and is structurally sound for multi-client rendering, but the JSON-LD structured data block contains **invalid JSON** (JavaScript-style comments) and multiple unfilled template fields that will cause schema parsing failures in Gmail.

**No broken redirects detected in the visible source**, but two concerns:

- All CTAs route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610000019ebd7b1…` (Epsilon Harmony). Destination UTM parameters are not visible in the truncated source — see §6.
- The open-tracking pixel (`id="ssl"`, `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019ebd7b1…`) uses `display:none` + `max-height:0` suppression, which is standard. **However**, the HTML comment directly below it reads `<!--Replace/Paste SSL Here-->` — a production template remnant that indicates the pixel was injected by automation but the comment was not cleaned up. ### 5. Compliance

**JSON-LD structured data is malformed — this is the most significant technical defect:**

```json
// GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
```

JavaScript `//` line comments are **not valid JSON**. The `<script type="application/ld+json">` block contains at least three `//` comment lines and two `//` inline comments. Every compliant JSON-LD parser (Gmail schema renderer, Google Rich Results) will reject the entire block. Consequences:
- Gmail promotional-tab promotion card will not render
- `DiscountOffer` schema not surfaced

Additional JSON-LD field failures:

| Field | Value | Issue |
|-------|-------|-------|
| `DiscountOffer.availabilityStarts` | `"2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"` | Hardcoded 2020 date — stale template |
| `DiscountOffer.availabilityEnds` | `""` | Empty |
| `DiscountOffer.description` | `""` | Empty |
| `DiscountOffer.discountCode` | `""` | Empty |
| `PromotionCard.image` | `""` | Empty — Gmail promo card cannot render |

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The destination URLs are wrapped in Epsilon redirect tokens and cannot be decoded from this source to confirm UTM parameters. **Must verify** that unwrapped destinations include `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content` before send, as Epsilon sometimes strips or omits UTM passthrough depending on configuration.

3. **Populate or remove `PromotionCard.image`** — an empty string will suppress the Gmail promo card entirely.
