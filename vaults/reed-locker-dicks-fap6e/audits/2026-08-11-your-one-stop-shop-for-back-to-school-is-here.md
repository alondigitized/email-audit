# Your one-stop shop for back to school is here 📍
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-11**
## Executive summary

- This is the first genuinely different creative in DICK'S recent run — instead of another flash-sale digest, it's a proper Back-to-School themed send: "YOUR YEAR. YOUR STYLE." hero collage, category chip navigation (Men's/Women's/Kids'/Backpacks/Drinkware/Accessories), then a long deal stack. It reads as more intentional than the 8/8–8/10 flash-sale sprawl, but it's still the same fundamental problem this brand keeps having: one clear hero, then eight-plus additional offers crammed below it, diluting focus.
- The Back-to-School hero itself is strong — teens in varsity/letterman-style outfits, a stadium/locker-adjacent photo collage, and a clean "SHOP NOW" CTA above the fold. But by the third or fourth module (Epic Shoe Sale, 50% clothing, golf, HydroJug, cleats, Topps cards, another 50% off banner, Back to School Essentials grid) the back-to-school throughline gets buried under generic recurring promos — this is the same "Epic Shoe Sale" and "up to 50% off" banners from the 8/8-8/10 sends, just re-inserted into a BTS wrapper.
- Verdict: good subject/hero pairing, weak execution below the fold — this is a BTS email that turns into the usual everything-digest by mid-scroll.

## What's working

- Hero headline "YOUR YEAR. YOUR STYLE." with a lifestyle photo collage (teens in varsity jackets, cleats, backpack) is a real seasonal, on-persona visual — a clear break from the red-locker flash-sale template.
- Category chip row (Men's/Women's/Kids'/Backpacks & More/Drinkware/Accessories) gives quick self-navigation right under the hero — good for a BTS shopper who knows what they need.
- "Back to School Essentials" product grid near the bottom (backpack, lunch box, water bottle, socks, t-shirt, clogs) is genuinely on-theme and visually clean.
- Scorecard rewards banner and order-today shipping line are visible up top, reinforcing loyalty/logistics info without extra clutter.

## What's weak

- The back-to-school theme evaporates almost immediately — Epic Shoe Sale, 50% off clothing, Nike Golf, HydroJug, cleats, Topps baseball cards, and a second "UP TO 50% OFF" beach-ball banner are all generic, non-BTS modules reused from prior sends.
- Two different "up to 50%" offers appear in the same email (clothing module and the blue beach-ball banner) with no differentiation — this reads confusing/redundant, not urgent.
- No visible deadline or urgency signal near the hero itself — the "Valid through 8/15" fine print only shows up deep in individual modules, not the primary CTA.
- By the time the reader reaches "Back to School Essentials" (the most relevant seasonal product grid), it's after 8+ unrelated offers — most scrollers won't get there.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email in half: keep the BTS hero, category chips, and the Back to School Essentials grid, and move Epic Shoe Sale/Golf/HydroJug/Topps cards to a separate, differently-themed send. A focused BTS email would outperform this sprawl.
- 2. Move the "Back to School Essentials" product grid up directly under the hero — it's the most relevant content in the whole email and currently sits at the very bottom.
- 3. Add a real deadline to the hero itself (e.g., "Ends 8/15") so the primary CTA carries urgency instead of only the sub-modules.
- 4. Resolve the duplicate "50% off" messaging — pick one framing (either the clothing module or the beach-ball banner) so it doesn't read as sloppy reuse.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Back to school, sorted: backpacks, cleats & more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New year, new gear — shop Back-to-School picks`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Backpacks, cleats, and first-day fits — all in one shop.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Everything on the list, ready before the bell rings.`

## 1. Technical Summary

- **Broken unsubscribe link (FAIL):** The unsubscribe link (`https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/U/v610000019ff053cad588d6c1f4bbcfb648/830be1c171db4dde0000021ef3a0bcff`) resolves to an error page — `unknown url type: '/pc/Error/Error?aspxerrorpath=/pc/subscription'`. This is a hard compliance/functional defect, not a design issue.
- All sampled links route through the same tracking redirector domain (`app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...` and `/O/...`) with opaque tokens (e.g., `__dU__=v0G4RBKTXg2Gs8NEX0-RpwlL2_Q96sIof5wvgkGa1VsPIAdb-6PwUuXUbOES4DYwVKlEBH4wAjEqvhvBlpUfxLCgwho1v8uViS`), consistent with standard click-tracking wrapping — no anomalies observed in the sampled set beyond the unsubscribe failure.
- 12 of 62 total links were probed (50 skipped per click-likelihood prioritization); no other broken links were flagged in the probed set.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `alt` text on 10 images**, including content images (not just spacers/icons): `dsg.jpg`, `mapbreak.gif`, `hydrojug.png`, `cleats.jpg`, `cards.jpg`, plus footer icons (`icon-vc-sms.png`, `icon-value-sportsmatter--light.png`, `icon-vc-bpg.png`, `icon-vc-favorite.png`, `icon-location-2025.png`) and the open-tracking pixel `830be1c...cad588d6c1f4bbcfb648` (this last one is expected to be alt-less as a tracking pixel, but the content images are not).
- MSO conditional comments and `@media screen` mobile overrides are present and structured correctly (`.mobile-off`, `.header-xl`, etc.); no malformed conditional tags observed in the truncated source.
- Custom web font (`Archivo`) is loaded via `@font-face` scoped to `@media screen` with a `local()` fallback and `font-display:swap` — correct pattern for Gmail/Outlook fallback safety.
- `<script type="application/ld+json">` block contains a **stray JS-style comment** (`// GIF & WEBP images are not supported...`) inside JSON — this is invalid JSON and will fail to parse as structured data if any client attempts to read it (harmless to rendering, but the schema.org markup is broken as shipped).

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No merge tokens (e.g., `%%FirstName%%`, `{{...}}`) visible in the truncated source. The `DiscountOffer` and `PromotionCard` JSON-LD blocks have empty `description`, `discountCode`, and `image` fields — these look like unfilled template placeholders left in production output, though they don't affect rendering.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Unsubscribe link is broken** (see §2) — this is a direct CAN-SPAM functional violation (unsubscribe mechanism must work).
- **List-Unsubscribe header not found** — cannot confirm mailbox-level one-click unsubscribe is offered.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found** — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe unsupported/unconfirmed; combined with the broken link, this is the highest-severity finding.
- **Authentication-Results header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status cannot be verified from this capture.
- Note: header absence is flagged as a relay-capture warning (AgentMail), so it's possible these headers exist on the original message but weren't captured — this should be verified against raw SMTP headers before treating as a confirmed sender-side gap.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
No UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are visible on any sampled link — all tracked links use the opaque `__dU__` token via the `app.dickssportinggoods.com` redirector instead of query-string UTMs. This is consistent with SFMC/Epsilon platform tracking (attribution likely resolved server-side post-redirect), but it means landing-page UTM alignment **cannot be verified from the email HTML alone**; would need to follow a redirect chain to confirm final landing URL parameters.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Fix the unsubscribe link immediately** — it currently 404s to `/pc/Error/Error?aspxerrorpath=/pc/subscription` instead of the subscription-management page. This is the top-priority fix.
2. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are actually set on the raw outbound message (verify at the ESP/MTA level, independent of this capture).
3. Confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment via raw headers (`Authentication-Results`) rather than relying on this relay capture.
4. Add descriptive `alt` text to the five content images (`dsg.jpg`, `mapbreak.gif`, `hydrojug.png`, `cleats.jpg`, `cards.jpg`) and the footer benefit icons.
5. Remove the stray `//` comment from the `application/ld+json` block so it parses as valid JSON.
6. Populate or remove the empty `DiscountOffer`/`PromotionCard` JSON-LD placeholder fields.
7. If UTM-based attribution is needed for site-side analytics, confirm the redirector appends UTMs downstream — otherwise consider adding them to support independent landing-page analytics validation.
