# $25 & under select clearance deals is this way 👉
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-13**
## Executive summary

- This is the most legible hero DICK'S has sent in the recent stretch — "$25 AND UNDER" in huge white text over a dark red gradient reads instantly, even on a phone-sized crop. It's also a genuinely different hook than the "up to 50/60% off" clearance and Flash Sale loop the brand has run daily since 8/30, so it breaks the cadence fatigue somewhat. But the price-anchor promise falls apart the moment you scroll: every single item below the hero — running shoes up to 60% off, DSG clothing up to 60% off, Stanley tumblers, racquet gear, golf electronics, fan shop gear, kayak gear, Halloween costumes, a credit card pitch — is a percentage-off category tile, and not one of them shows a product actually priced at or under $25. The subject and hero make a specific, closeable promise and the body never delivers proof.
- The email is also enormous — at least 15 separate merchandising blocks stacked with no clear priority after the hero, which dilutes the "$25 and under" urgency into an undifferentiated catalog dump. Net: a strong subject/hero pairing wasted by a body that doesn't back up the claim and buries it in scroll.

## What's working

- The hero graphic is clean and legible — bold white "$25 AND UNDER" on a saturated red background with a visible green "Shop Now" button, no overlapping text or broken art.
- The offer angle ($25 and under) is a fresh hook distinct from the repetitive "up to 50/60% off" clearance sends this brand has run since 8/30.
- Category nav pills (Men's, Women's, Kids, Shoes, All Clearance) right under the hero give a fast path to browse without forcing a full scroll.
- Product photography throughout (shoes, Stanley cups, tennis, golf) is sharp and on-brand, no lorem ipsum or image-load failures visible.

## What's weak

- Zero products anywhere in the body are shown priced at or under $25 — the headline promise is never substantiated, which reads as bait-and-switch.
- The email is excessively long — 15+ stacked category blocks (shoes, DSG apparel, Stanley, racquet, golf, fan shop, kayak gear, Halloween, credit card, "check out these picks") with no visual hierarchy differentiating them from each other.
- The offers below the hero are all different percentages (60% off, 50% off, 40% off) unrelated to the "$25 and under" theme, so the email feels like two unrelated campaigns glued together.
- The credit card pitch ("$30 Bonus Reward") and Halloween shop section are buried deep in an already-long scroll, diluting focus further.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put at least one row of actual $25-and-under products (with visible prices) directly under the hero — right now the single strongest lever is proving the subject line's claim before the reader loses trust.
- 2. Cut the body from 15+ category blocks down to 4-5 — lead with clearance-adjacent categories only, drop unrelated full-price pushes like Stanley "New" tumblers and the credit card block from this send.
- 3. Group same-theme content — either make this entirely a "$25 and under" email or clearly separate it into two labeled sections ("$25 & Under Picks" / "This Week's Clearance") so the percentage-off tiles don't undercut the headline offer.
- 4. Move the Halloween Shop and credit card CTA to a separate send — they compete with, rather than support, the clearance narrative.

- **Subject Alt B:** `$25 and under clearance — 50+ styles inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sneakers, apparel and gear, all $25 or less — while supplies last`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New markdowns just hit $25 and under`
