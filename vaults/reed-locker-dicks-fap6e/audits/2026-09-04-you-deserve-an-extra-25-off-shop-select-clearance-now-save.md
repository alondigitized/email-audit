# You deserve an extra 25% off! Shop select clearance now & SAVE ➡️
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-04**
## Executive summary

- At least this one isn't the stadium-seating "UP TO 50% OFF" hero for once — the top block leads with a clean "EXTRA 25% OFF Select Clearance" on the same red/maroon stadium-seat background DICK'S reuses everywhere, but the offer itself is new and specific enough to register. Then it immediately undercuts itself: two sections down is another red hero for "Epic End-of-Summer Sale: Up to 50% Off," which is the literal same-week, same-color creative I've flagged four times since 8/30 (most recently 9/2 and 9/3). Running a 25%-off clearance stack directly above a 50%-off sitewide stack in the same email just teaches the reader to skip to the bigger number and ignore the top offer entirely.
- Below the fold this is a kitchen-sink send — running shoes, ScoreCard bonus reward, apparel, Stanley tumblers, fan shop, soccer, golf shoes, a "check out these picks" grid — nine-plus distinct offers stacked vertically with no real hierarchy after the hero. It's comprehensive but exhausting, and it dilutes the one offer the subject line actually promised.
- Net: decent subject/hero pairing, poor execution once you scroll, and it steps on its own message by burying the promoted 25% deal under a bigger, more repetitive 50% deal one screen down.

## What's working

- The hero clearly states the specific offer ("EXTRA 25% OFF," "Extra 25% Off Select Clearance") matching the subject line — no bait-and-switch on the top fold.
- Green CTA buttons ("Shop Now") are consistent, high-contrast, and easy to spot throughout.
- Member-exclusive ScoreCard $10 bonus reward section adds a loyalty hook mid-email, which is a real differentiator from the generic clearance push.
- Product photography (running shoes, apparel, Stanley collab) renders cleanly with no broken images or layout breakage.

## What's weak

- The "Up to 50% Off" Epic End-of-Summer hero two sections down directly competes with and outweighs the 25%-off clearance offer promised in the subject — it's the exact recycled creative flagged on 9/1, 9/2, 9/3.
- Way too many offers stacked with equal visual weight (running shoes, ScoreCard, apparel, Stanley, fan shop, soccer, golf, "picks" grid) — nothing after the hero commands attention.
- Clearance-specific product imagery is absent — the top hero is text-on-red with no actual product shown, so there's nothing concrete to click toward.
- Fine print ("Online only. Exclusions apply, valid through 8/728 12:59PM PT") is visible but easy to miss against the busy layout, and the "8/728" typo-looking date reads sloppy.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the second "Up to 50% Off" hero from this send entirely, or reorder so the 25%-off clearance offer is the only red stadium-seat hero — right now the two compete and the bigger number wins by default, defeating the subject line's promise.
- 2. Add an actual clearance product image (shoes, apparel) to the top hero instead of text-only red background — gives the eye something concrete to land on besides a percentage.
- 3. Trim the body to 3-4 offers max, or split into a dedicated clearance-only email and a separate "everything else" digest — this send is trying to be both and does neither well.
- 4. Fix/clarify the expiration date copy ("8/728 12:59PM PT" reads as a typo) — a real deadline builds urgency, a garbled one erodes trust.

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods "Extra 25% Off Clearance" Email

### 1. Technical Summary
Email is built on a Cheetah Digital/Marigold-style ESP (`data.em.dcsg.com/ee/v1/...`) with standard MSO/Outlook conditional markup; automated QA shows a broken CTA link, missing unsubscribe/authentication headers, and a near-empty plain-text part (38% overall pass rate).

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Broken link (403):** The "Get Coupon" CTA resolves to a 403 error — `https://data.em.dcsg.com/ee/v1/click?params=b.v1.wRs6VA0j...&pid=...&tuid=6a9acfbce1d8c820c459f30c&configId=512ffb51-7b68-4261-8e10-c8efcf41ba61`. This is a primary conversion CTA, so this is a high-severity broken-experience bug.
- All sampled links route through the ESP's click-tracking redirector (`data.em.dcsg.com/ee/v1/click` and `/mclick`), each carrying a unique `pid`/`tuid`/`configId` — consistent with standard click-tracking wrap, no malformed params observed in the two examples inspected.
- Coverage caveat: QA probed only 12 of 70 total links (material/high-click-likelihood links); footer, social, and utility links were deprioritized and not verified — cannot confirm those are non-broken.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **10 images missing `alt` text**, including all primary merchandising creative (`running-2026-0904-d`, `clothes-shoes-2026-0904-d`, `banner.jpg`, `fan-shop-2026-0904-d`, `soccer-2026-0904-d`) and utility icons (`icon-vc-sms-1`, `icon-vc-sportsmatter`, `icon-vc-bpg-1`, `icon-vc-favorite-1`, `icon-location-2025-1`). This is an accessibility/screen-reader gap and also degrades the experience when images are blocked by default in most mail clients.
- The open-tracking pixel (`.../ee/v1/open?params=...`) is also flagged as missing `alt`, which is standard/expected for a tracking pixel and not an actual issue.
- Outlook conditional comments (`<!--[if (gte mso 9)|(IE)]-->`) and a `@media screen and (max-width:480px)` mobile block are present, indicating standard Litmus-style responsive/MSO handling — no structural rendering issues found in the visible source.
- Web font (`Archivo`) is loaded via `@font-face` inside a `@media screen` query (correctly scoped to exclude Outlook/desktop clients that mishandle it) with a system-font fallback stack (`Arial,sans-serif`) — no issue found.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No merge tokens or personalization placeholders (e.g., `{{firstName}}`, `%%FIELD%%`) are visible in the truncated HTML provided. No issues found in the visible source — insufficient content to confirm the full template is free of unresolved tokens.

- CAN-SPAM physical address requirement cannot be verified — footer content was truncated out of the provided HTML source.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
No UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are visible on the sampled links — both examined URLs use the ESP's opaque `params=b.v1...` token scheme instead of plain UTM query params, so campaign attribution is presumably handled server-side via the `tuid`/`configId`/`pid` tokens rather than URL-based UTMs. Cannot confirm landing-page alignment since the redirect targets are opaque tracking tokens, not resolvable destination URLs, in this dataset.

4.
