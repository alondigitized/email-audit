#  Our Biggest Sale of Summer is ON! 💸 🎉 New daily DEALS & limited-time savings...
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- This is the "Deal Days is live" announcement send from DICK'S, launching the named summer sale event that was teased the day before (June 21's "Start TOMORROW!" email). The structure is ambitious — a hero, rotating daily deals with countdowns, limited-time savings, a clearance banner, and a "must-haves" product grid — but it collapses under its own weight at mobile width. The email is long, dense, and tries to be four different promotions in one scroll. There is no single, clean offer that dominates.
- The daily deals mechanic (3 products flagged "TODAY ONLY") is the strongest angle here — it gives genuine time-bounded urgency and specific product callouts. But the deals are rendered so small on mobile that the product names are unreadable in the screenshot, and the "Extra 30% Off Select Clearance" red banner buried two-thirds down the screen undercuts the "Biggest Sale of Summer" premium positioning established at the top.
- Net judgement: competent execution of a complex event email, but the creative team tried to do too much and ended up doing none of it clearly.

## What's working

- **Deal Days branding holds** — the green athletic hero and "DEAL DAYS" event identity carry over cleanly from the June 21 teaser, giving the event continuity.
- **"TODAY ONLY" badges on 3 deal tiles** create credible urgency that the pure clearance emails from the prior week lacked.
- **Specific product callouts visible** — a DayDip backpack, a pair of shoes (Nike or similar), swim shorts are individually featured with "SHOP NOW" CTAs per product.
- **Extra 30% Off banner is visually bold** — the red contrast band breaks the green monotony and catches the eye even mid-scroll.
- **App download section present** — carries forward the app-exclusive deal messaging from the June 22 sends, maintaining that push.

## What's weak

- **No single dominant offer** — five different promotional mechanics (daily deals, limited-time savings, clearance, must-haves, app offers) compete for attention and none wins.
- **Product detail unreadable at mobile width** — the deal tile text is too small to parse in the render; a subscriber on mobile cannot read the product names or prices without zooming.
- **Subject line is over 60 characters** — "Our Biggest Sale of Summer is ON! 💸 🎉 New daily DEALS & limited-time savings..." will be truncated on most mobile clients before the "daily DEALS" payoff lands.
- **"Extra 30% Off Select Clearance" buried below the fold** — after spending June 21-22 building Deal Days as a premium event, dropping a clearance banner mid-email dilutes the positioning.
- **No price anchor visible in the hero** — the top section names the event but gives no specific dollar or percent savings; subscribers have to scroll to find any offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with one number in the hero** — "Up to 40% Off" or "Daily Deals from $X" directly under the DEAL DAYS logo so the subscriber knows what they're getting before scrolling.
- 2. **Make deal tiles bigger on mobile** — each product tile should show product name, original price, deal price, and a clear savings call-out at legible size. Right now they read as decorative thumbnails.
- 3. **Move the clearance 30% banner to a separate send** — it conflicts with the premium "biggest sale of summer" framing; clearance subscribers can get their own email or it can live at the bottom with a clear visual separator.
- 4. **Trim the subject line** — everything after "is ON!" is truncated on mobile. Cut it to the punch.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Deal Days is ON — new drops every day 💸`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your daily DICK'S deals start now 🎉`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `3 TODAY ONLY deals + up to 40% off sitewide — here's what's live`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Daily deals, limited-time savings, and Extra 30% off clearance — all live now`
- 5. **Consolidate CTAs** — the email has at least 8 "SHOP NOW" buttons. Pick a primary (today's deals) and a secondary (all Deal Days), and let the hierarchy breathe.

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This is the "Deal Days is live" announcement send from DICK'S, launching the named summer sale event that was teased the day before (June 21's "Start TOMORROW!" email). The structure is ambitious — a hero, rotating daily deals with countdowns, limited-time savings, a clearance banner, and a "must-haves" product grid — but it collapses under its own weight at mobile width. The email is long, dense, and tries to be four different promotions in one scroll. There is no single, clean offer that dominates.

The daily deals mechanic (3 products flagged "TODAY ONLY") is the strongest angle here — it gives genuine time-bounded urgency and specific product callouts. But the deals are rendered so small on mobile that the product names are unreadable in the screenshot, and the "Extra 30% Off Select Clearance" red banner buried two-thirds down the screen undercuts the "Biggest Sale of Summer" premium positioning established at the top.

Net judgement: competent execution of a complex event email, but the creative team tried to do too much and ended up doing none of it clearly.

## 2. What worked

- **Deal Days branding holds** — the green athletic hero and "DEAL DAYS" event identity carry over cleanly from the June 21 teaser, giving the event continuity.
- **"TODAY ONLY" badges on 3 deal tiles** create credible urgency that the pure clearance emails from the prior week lacked.
- **Specific product callouts visible** — a DayDip backpack, a pair of shoes (Nike or similar), swim shorts are individually featured with "SHOP NOW" CTAs per product.
- **Extra 30% Off banner is visually bold** — the red contrast band breaks the green monotony and catches the eye even mid-scroll.
- **App download section present** — carries forward the app-exclusive deal messaging from the June 22 sends, maintaining that push.

## 3. What didn't

- **No single dominant offer** — five different promotional mechanics (daily deals, limited-time savings, clearance, must-haves, app offers) compete for attention and none wins.
- **Product detail unreadable at mobile width** — the deal tile text is too small to parse in the render; a subscriber on mobile cannot read the product names or prices without zooming.
- **Subject line is over 60 characters** — "Our Biggest Sale of Summer is ON! 💸 🎉 New daily DEALS & limited-time savings..." will be truncated on most mobile clients before the "daily DEALS" payoff lands.
- **"Extra 30% Off Select Clearance" buried below the fold** — after spending June 21-22 building Deal Days as a premium event, dropping a clearance banner mid-email dilutes the positioning.
- **No price anchor visible in the hero** — the top section names the event but gives no specific dollar or percent savings; subscribers have to scroll to find any offer.

## 4. What I'd change

1. **Lead with one number in the hero** — "Up to 40% Off" or "Daily Deals from $X" directly under the DEAL DAYS logo so the subscriber knows what they're getting before scrolling.
2. **Make deal tiles bigger on mobile** — each product tile should show product name, original price, deal price, and a clear savings call-out at legible size. Right now they read as decorative thumbnails.
3. **Move the clearance 30% banner to a separate send** — it conflicts with the premium "biggest sale of summer" framing; clearance subscribers can get their own email or it can live at the bottom with a clear visual separator.
4. **Trim the subject line** — everything after "is ON!" is truncated on mobile. Cut it to the punch.
   - **Subject Alt A:** `Deal Days is ON — new drops every day 💸`
   - **Subject Alt B:** `Your daily DICK'S deals start now 🎉`
   - **Preheader Alt A:** `3 TODAY ONLY deals + up to 40% off sitewide — here's what's live`
   - **Preheader Alt B:** `Daily deals, limited-time savings, and Extra 30% off clearance — all live now`
5. **Consolidate CTAs** — the email has at least 8 "SHOP NOW" buttons. Pick a primary (today's deals) and a secondary (all Deal Days), and let the hierarchy breathe.
