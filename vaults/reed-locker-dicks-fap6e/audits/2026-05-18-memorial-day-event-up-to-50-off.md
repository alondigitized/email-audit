# Memorial Day Event = Up to 50% OFF 🙌
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- DICK'S finally has a real campaign anchor. After nine-plus sends in five days cycling through generic "DEALS" and clearance framing, the Memorial Day Event label gives this email a legitimate reason to exist — a known holiday with an implied hard deadline. The hero is clean: big lifestyle image, bold event name, a concrete percentage, and two visible CTAs above the fold. That's meaningfully better than yesterday's "Stop scrolling! Up to 60% off select clearance" send, which had no hard deadline hook.
- Where this stumbles is the familiar DICK'S over-send problem. The 10th+ email in five days from this sender erodes every signal the creative tries to build. The subscriber already opened a near-identical "up to 50% off" email yesterday, and the day before, and the day before that. The Memorial Day label helps differentiate on the surface, but the body structure — hero banner, category tiles, product tiles, SMS opt-in, footer — is a photocopy of the last several sends.
- The Graduation Shop cross-sell buried in the lower half is the most interesting creative decision in the email. Memorial Day weekend is peak graduation overlap and that inclusion is smart segmentation thinking that the rest of the email doesn't match.

## What's working

- **Holiday anchor is real.** "Memorial Day Event" is a credible, date-bound campaign name — a genuine step up from the vague "DEALS" framing that scored 7/10 on May 15.
- **Hero offer is clear and fast.** "Up to 50% Off Deals" reads immediately in the hero without digging.
- **Category diversity gives multiple entry points.** Golf shoes, bikes, apparel, and general shoes all get their own tiles — something for most sporting goods shoppers.
- **Graduation Shop cross-sell is timely.** Pairing Memorial Day with graduation gifting shows actual calendar awareness and expands the email's conversion surface.
- **SMS opt-in "$20 OFF $100 OR MORE" is a specific, compelling hook.** A dollar-amount incentive (not just "join our list") is the right framing for a text opt-in.

## What's weak

- **Two hero CTAs ("SUMMER SALE" / "SUMMER SHOP") split attention.** One clear primary CTA would force a hierarchy; two equally-sized buttons create a choice-paralysis moment.
- **CTA copy is generic.** "SUMMER SALE" and "SUMMER SHOP" are nouns, not verbs-plus-nouns. "Shop Memorial Day Deals" or "See All 50% Off Picks" would be more actionable.
- **No visible urgency in body copy.** The subject implies a deadline but nowhere in the body does "ends Monday" or "Memorial Day Weekend only" appear visibly — the event name does no work below the fold.
- **Cadence has numbed the offer.** This is the same subscriber who got "Up to 50% off" yesterday, and the day before. The incremental trust cost is real, even when the creative nominally upgrades.
- **No social proof or product detail.** No star ratings, review counts, or sizing callouts anywhere in the visible scroll — the product tiles are pure image + percentage + button.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill one hero CTA.** Make "Shop Memorial Day Deals" the single primary button. Remove or demote "SUMMER SHOP" to a text link. One button, one decision, higher click rate.
- 2. **Add a deadline line.** Even plain text — "Deals end Monday, May 26" — directly under the hero offer would activate the urgency the subject promises. Right now the event name does all the urgency work and the body free-rides on it.
- 3. **Move Graduation Shop up.** It's the most differentiated section and it's buried below three standard deal tiles. Swap it to the second content block — it's the part of this email that isn't a copy of yesterday's send.
- 4. **Reduce send cadence or segment harder.** 10 emails in 5 days means even a perfectly executed Memorial Day send lands in a fatigued inbox. A 3-day send cap per subscriber would have protected the Memorial Day email's open rate.
- 5. **Sharpen the category CTAs.** "SHOP NOW" 8× is noise. Use "Shop Golf Sale," "Shop Bikes," etc. — it's two words and it doubles as segmentation data.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 50% off ends Monday — Memorial Day picks`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Memorial Day Sale: golf, bikes, shoes, all 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ends Monday: Nike, Adidas, Trek + hundreds more on sale now`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus score $20 off $100 instantly — text COACH to 41689`

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods Memorial Day Event Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard ESP-tracked table layout with Outlook conditionals and is structurally sound at the container level. However, the JSON-LD structured data block contains critical syntax errors and stale placeholder values that will cause Gmail schema parsing to fail silently, and a CSS syntax error exists in the MSO conditional block.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**No critical issues.** All visible links are wrapped through the ESP redirect domain `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610000019e3a982708926f706e96639540/...`. The open-tracking pixel at `app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019e3a982708926f706e96639540/b216b8a61dcd4afb00004c5a42963aa1` is present and loaded via HTTPS.

**Cannot confirm UTM parameters** — the redirect chain is opaque in the provided source. If UTMs are injected by the ESP post-redirect, this is fine; if not, analytics attribution will be missing on landing pages.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**MSO conditional CSS syntax error** — the `<style>` block inside the MSO conditional comment contains a stray double-quote before the closing brace:

```css
div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
                                                                               ^
```

This is invalid CSS. Outlook's parser may tolerate it, but it is technically broken and can produce unexpected inherited styles on `div` elements in Outlook.

**Preheader implementation is non-standard** — the preheader text `"Get $20 off $100+ when you sign up for Text Alerts**"` is placed in a TD with `height:0; font-size:0; line-height:0` inline styles. In Outlook (MSO), `font-size:0` is not reliably honored, which can cause this text to render as visible content at the top of the email body. Standard practice is a `display:none; max-height:0; overflow:hidden` span.

**Archivo font loading uses v7** — `fonts.gstatic.com/s/archivo/v7/...` is a stale version (current is v19+). Functionally benign since web fonts are fallback-safe, but worth noting for long-lived templates.

**Logo `src` is truncated in the provided source** (`https://dsg.images.harmony.eps…`) — cannot verify the full URL is valid. No `alt` text is visible on the logo `<img>` due to truncation.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**No dynamic merge tokens are visible** in the provided truncated source (no `{{first_name}}`, `%%first_name%%`, etc.). This appears to be a broadcast promotional email with no personalization, which is expected for a mass sale event.

---

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**Unsubscribe link not confirmed** — the source is truncated before the footer. CAN-SPAM requires a physical postal address and functioning unsubscribe mechanism. Cannot verify from the provided excerpt.

**Authentication headers not visible** — DKIM/SPF/DMARC pass/fail status requires raw MIME headers which are not included. The sending domain `e.dcsg.com` is a dedicated sending subdomain, which is correct practice. No issues can be confirmed or ruled out without header inspection.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**JSON-LD `DiscountOffer` schema has a hardcoded stale date** — `availabilityStarts` is set to `"2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"`, six years in the past, with `availabilityEnds` left as an empty string `""`. This is clearly a template placeholder that was never updated for this send. Gmail will parse but not surface this offer card correctly.

**JSON-LD block contains JavaScript-style comments** which are invalid JSON syntax:

```json
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "@context": "http://schema.org/",
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  "image": ""   ← also empty
}
```

Comments inside `<script type="application/ld+json">` are a JSON parse error. Google's structured data processor will reject this entire block. The `PromotionCard` image is also empty, meaning no promotional image card will render in the Gmail Promotions tab.

`DiscountOffer.description` and `DiscountOffer.discountCode` are both empty strings — no offer details will surface in Gmail's native discount presentation.
