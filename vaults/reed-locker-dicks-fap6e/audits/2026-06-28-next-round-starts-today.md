# Next round starts TODAY ⚽
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-28**
## Executive summary

- This is a tournament-timed soccer jersey push timed to "the next round" of what's clearly a live men's international tournament (Copa América or similar). The creative is a genuine pivot from the discount-heavy cadence DICK'S has run since June 24 — no percentage-off banners, no clearance urgency, just a clean national-team jersey push with country-specific sections for Mexico, USA, and Argentina. The "Next round starts TODAY" subject is the sharpest line DICK'S has written all week: concrete, timely, and zero noise.
- The problem is what's missing. This is a full-price collection email with no price anchor anywhere visible. After burning through Deal Days (June 24–26), a 4th of July launch, a $25-and-under clearance send, and a "favorites deals" email — all in the same 72-hour window — the audience has been conditioned to expect a discount, and this send offers none. The emotional appeal is genuine, but there's no trigger to actually purchase.
- This is DICK'S seventh distinct email in roughly three days. Even strong creative buckles under that cadence. A soccer fan who loves this jersey send has already mentally filed DICK'S as "promotion noise" and may skip it.

## What's working

- **Subject line is excellent** — "Next round starts TODAY ⚽" is under 30 characters, uses a single contextually relevant emoji, and creates real urgency because a sporting event actually is starting today.
- **Creative pivot is smart** — Moving from discount-driven to event-driven creative avoids fatigue with the sale narrative and gives the subscriber a reason to engage that isn't "here's another percentage off."
- **Country-specific sections add relevance** — Separate "Gear Up to Cheer on Mexico / USA / Argentina" blocks are scroll-worthy for fans of specific teams; this is light segmentation that works without actual personalization.
- **Visual hierarchy holds** — Hero image lands first, headline is clear, blue "SHOP NOW" button is visible and unambiguous.
- **"ORDER TODAY. SPORT IT TOMORROW."** delivery promise banner is well-placed and practically useful for someone who realizes the match is today.

## What's weak

- **No price signal anywhere visible** — After days of deal-conditioning, a pure collection send with no discount, no member price, and no free-shipping threshold is a conversion ask with no hook.
- **"SHOP NOW" is a generic CTA** — The subject promises a specific moment ("Next round starts TODAY") but the button copy doesn't follow through; "Get Your Jersey" or "Gear Up for Today" would convert better.
- **Cadence fatigue is a real problem** — This is the seventh DICK'S email in three days, following two emails on June 26 alone (including two "last chance" Deal Days messages audited at 8-9/10). The goodwill from good creative is depleted by volume.
- **Argentina/Mexico sections may miss** — Depending on list segmentation, non-soccer subscribers or fans of teams not shown get a cold email about somebody else's national team.
- **No visible loyalty hook** — No ScoreCard points mention, no member-exclusive pricing — a missed opportunity when competing against Amazon same-day for a time-sensitive purchase.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a friction-reducing price hook** — Even a "free same-day delivery on jerseys today only" or a ScoreCard bonus-points tie-in gives a reason to click now vs. closing the tab and forgetting. The delivery banner exists but it's a logistics claim, not a value hook.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA to match the subject energy** — "SHOP NOW" wastes the urgency the subject built. "Get Your Jersey Before Kickoff" or "Grab Your Jersey Now" carries the moment.
- 3. **Country-gate the send or surface fan choice above the fold** — If list data includes sport/team preference, lead with that country's section. If not, a "Pick Your Team" CTA block above the fold converts the hero into a choosing experience, not a browsing one.
- 4. **Pull back cadence** — One email on June 28 — either this soccer send or the clearance/"favorites deals" send — not three.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**MSO/IE conditional CSS syntax error** — the `<style>` block inside `<!--[if (gte mso 9)|(IE)]>` contains a stray `"` before the closing `}` on the `div` rule:

```css
div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
                                                                                ^
```

This invalidates the entire `div` rule in Outlook/Word-rendering clients, which could produce unexpected spacing or background-color bleed.

**Preheader cell artifact** — the `<td id="ssl">` contains a literal `+` character prepended to the preheader copy:

```html
+ Score gear made for your starting lineup
```

The `font-size:0` on the `<td>` suppresses it in most clients, but it surfaces as a rendering artifact in some older Android clients and plain-text fallbacks.

**Unfilled template comment left in sent email:**

```html

```

This is a production artifact. No functional impact, but it indicates the preheader injection step was skipped or left as a placeholder.

`role="presentation"` is correctly applied to layout tables. `lang="en"` is set on `<html>`. Alt text cannot be confirmed from the truncated source — verify all `<img>` tags carry descriptive `alt` values.
