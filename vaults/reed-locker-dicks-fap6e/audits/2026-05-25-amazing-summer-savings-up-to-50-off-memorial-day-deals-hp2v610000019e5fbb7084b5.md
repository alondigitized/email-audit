# Amazing summer savings! ☀️ Up to 50% off Memorial Day DEALS
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- This is, by my count, the seventh-plus distinct Memorial Day creative from DICK'S I've reviewed since Friday — and the most creatively confused of the batch. The subject pivots to "Amazing summer savings!" while the email body lands on the same "Epic Memorial Day Sale" hero used in every send since May 23. The result is a split identity: breezy summer subject, urgent sale body. Neither commits fully.
- The execution inside is competent. The 50% OFF hero is large and legible, multiple category-specific sub-offers (30% off sandals, BOGO golf balls, 40% off life vests) give the email genuine range, and the app-exclusive $20/$100 deal from yesterday reappears with its own dedicated section. There's even a Father's Day teaser at the bottom — the only genuinely fresh angle in this send — though it's buried after a long scroll.
- Bottom line: a well-assembled but exhausted send. The subject line wasted its shot, and the email can't escape the fact that it's running the same Memorial Day template for the fourth day in a row.

## What's working

- **Hero dominates the fold.** 50% OFF in large, high-contrast text is immediately legible — the strongest single element in the email.
- **Category-specific offers give the scroll real value.** Sandals at 30% off, BOGO golf balls, life vests at 40% off — these are specific enough to stop a relevant shopper mid-scroll.
- **App exclusive section is well-separated.** The dark-background $20/$100 block breaks up the visual monotony and draws the eye to the loyalty hook.
- **Father's Day teaser at the bottom is smart calendar play.** Plants the next occasion before Memorial Day closes out.

## What's weak

- **Subject opener "Amazing summer savings!" is inert.** It adds zero information and buries the stronger "Up to 50% off Memorial Day DEALS" clause that follows it.
- **Every CTA reads "SHOP NOW."** Same copy across sandals, golf balls, life vests, and the hero — none of the section specificity carries into the button labels.
- **No end date or urgency close.** "Memorial Day" implies a deadline tonight, but nothing in the email reinforces it — no "ends midnight," no countdown, no "last day" language.
- **Creative fatigue is real.** Compared to the clean, punchy sends on May 23 ("EPIC Memorial Day SALE is live"), this one feels like a copy of a copy. The same hero image, same section layout, same offer stack.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill "Amazing summer savings!" and lead with the offer.** It's the seventh send — subscribers don't need a warm-up. The subject should be the offer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `50% off ends tonight — DICK'S Memorial Day Sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last day: 50% off Nike, adidas + golf & more`
- 2. **Make CTAs match the section.** "Shop Sandals," "Shop Golf," "Shop Life Vests" — one line of work that makes every click feel intentional instead of generic.
- 3. **Add an explicit end date.** "Ends Monday night" or "Tonight only" in the hero or hero subhead closes the urgency loop the subject line opens.
- 4. **Promote Father's Day earlier.** Moving it to the second scroll position makes this send feel like new news rather than just another Memorial Day reprise.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ends tonight — up to 50% off Nike, adidas, golf & more`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Memorial Day final hours + Father's Day picks inside`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods Memorial Day Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard ESP redirect-tracking architecture (`app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/`) and is structurally sound for major clients, but ships with invalid JSON-LD (JavaScript comments in a JSON block, stale 2020 dates, and empty template fields) and lacks visible UTM parameters on click-through URLs.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open pixel present** — `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019e5fbb7084b5f5d36e96c660a0/...` fires on render. Standard practice; no issue.

**Click tracking via redirect** — All `href` values route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...`. Confirmed on logo link and preheader CTA. Redirect chain works but makes UTM inspection opaque (see §6).

**`__dU__` user token in redirect URLs** — e.g., `__dU__=v0G4RBKTXg2Gs8NEX0-RpwlL2_Q96sIof5wvgkGa1VsPIAdb-6PwUuXUbOES4DYwVKlEBH4wAjEqvhvBlpUfxLCgwho1v8uViS` — appears to be a subscriber-level identifier. Confirm this token is not PII-exposing in server logs at the redirect destination.

**Truncated image src** — `https://dsg.images.harmony.epsilo…` (logo `<img>`) is cut off in the provided source. If this reflects the actual rendered HTML, the logo `src` is broken. Needs source verification.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<link rel="icon">` in `<head>`** — Favicon declarations have no effect in email and may trigger unexpected behavior in some security filters or parsers. Remove.

**Preheader text not suppressed with CSS** — The tracking pixel `<img>` above the preheader text carries `display:none; max-height:0px; overflow:hidden; mso-hide:all`, but the preheader text node itself (`📣 $20 off $100+ is waiting for you in the app***`) does not. This text sits inside a `height:0` `<td>` which is sufficient for most clients but not universally reliable for suppression. It appears intentional as preview text — but if it's not meant to render in-body, add `display:none` to the text container or wrap in a dedicated `<div class="preheader">` with full suppression styles.

**MSO/Outlook conditional styles** — VML/conditional comment blocks are present and correctly scoped with `<!--[if (gte mso 9)|(IE)]>`. No issues detected.

**`role="presentation"` on layout tables** — Applied consistently on visible tables. Good.

**`lang="en"` on `<html>`** — Present. Good.

**Font fallback chain** — `Archivo` loaded via `@media screen` block only (Google Fonts CDN); Outlook falls back to `Arial, sans-serif`. Correct.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Table ID `header-non-sc-member`** — Indicates conditional content segmentation (ScoreCard member vs. non-member). No template logic errors are visible in the truncated source, but confirm the conditional block complement (`header-sc-member`) is suppressed for this send segment and vice versa.

**No unresolved merge tokens visible** — No `{{`, `%%`, or `[PLACEHOLDER]` style tokens appear in the visible source.

### 5. Compliance

**Unsubscribe / physical address** — Not visible in the truncated source (footer is cut off). Cannot confirm CAN-SPAM compliance from this excerpt alone. Footer must contain: (1) valid postal address, (2) one-click unsubscribe mechanism. Flag for manual footer review.

**From address** — `DSG@e.dcsg.com` — subdomain `e.dcsg.com` is the ESP sending domain. Confirm SPF/DKIM alignment for this subdomain is in place (not verifiable from HTML source; check email headers on a received copy).

**`<script type="application/ld+json">` contains JavaScript comments** — This is a hard spec violation. JSON does not support `//` comments:
```json
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
```
```json
{ // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
```
These will cause JSON-LD parsing failures in Gmail's structured data parser, meaning the Gmail Action Card / Deal annotation will not render. They are developer/designer notes that were never stripped before send.

**`"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"`** — Stale date from 2020 in the `DiscountOffer` schema block. A Memorial Day 2026 email with a 2020 availability start is semantically incorrect and will cause structured data validation warnings.

**Non-standard Schema.org types** — `"@type": "DiscountOffer"` and `"@type": "PromotionCard"` are not valid Schema.org types. Gmail's structured data requires specific supported types (e.g., `SpecialAnnouncement`, `SaleEvent`). These blocks will be silently ignored.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters not present in raw hrefs** — All click hrefs terminate at the ESP redirect (`/T/...`). If UTM params are appended at redirect resolution, this is fine — but it cannot be confirmed from the HTML alone. Manually follow a redirect to verify `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` are present on the final destination URL.

**`app.dickssportinggoods.com` preheader CTA vs. main site** — The preheader app-download CTA links to the same `app.dickssportinggoods.com` redirect domain as web links. Verify the app deep-link path resolves correctly on both iOS/Android and falls back gracefully on desktop.
