# Up to 70% off TODAY ONLY!🚨 Save on select exclusive brands: CALIA, VRST, DSG & more
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- This is July 6 — the fourth consecutive day DICK'S has led with "up to 70% off," and at least the seventh promotional send this week. The brand recognized the fatigue problem and tried to solve it correctly: swap the clearance banner for a 24-HOUR SALE frame, spotlight exclusive owned brands (DSG, CALIA, VRST), and inject "TODAY ONLY!" urgency. That framing reset is the right call, and the colorful brand-logo hero executes it better than yesterday's generic clearance creative.
- But the email overstays its welcome. It runs six distinct offer blocks plus a product grid, every one of them anchored to the same "SHOP NOW" CTA. After the hero, the eye has nowhere specific to go. The "up to 70%" ceiling feels lower with each repeat — subscribers who saw that headline three days straight have already anchored to it. The 24-hour window is the one fresh lever here and the email doesn't fully exploit it.
- A cleaner email — hero + two categories + one hard deadline — would have outperformed this six-block sprawl.

## What's working

- **24-HOUR SALE creative reset is smart.** The colorful gradient with floating brand logos is a genuine visual departure from the clearance banner that's appeared all week.
- **Multi-brand specificity (CALIA, VRST, DSG, Walter Hagen) gives different segments a hook** — the golf offer and the smart-casual VRST block read as distinct deals, not just clearance.
- **Section-level discounts are concrete:** "60% Off VRST Smart Casual" and "BOGO 50% Off Walter Hagen Golf" are specific enough to create real intent.
- **Clean render** — images load, layout holds, no visual bugs.

## What's weak

- **Every CTA is "SHOP NOW"** — six identical buttons across six different categories signal that no section is actually linked anywhere meaningful.
- **"Up to 70% off" is now three days old.** The ceiling is anchored; subscribers know "up to" means most items are 30–40% off, and repeating the same headline erodes trust with each send.
- **Subject line is 83 characters.** Everything after "TODAY ONLY!🚨" gets cut on mobile — the brand names that differentiate this email from yesterday's never appear in the inbox preview.
- **"Must-have gear for an epic summer" product grid** at the bottom is visually disconnected from the exclusive-brands story — it reads like a second email was appended.
- **No social proof, no sizing/availability detail** anywhere — nothing to pull a hesitating shopper over the line.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make CTAs section-specific:** "Shop CALIA Deals," "Shop VRST Smart Casual," "Shop Golf Savings" — each tells the subscriber where the click lands and which offer they're claiming.
- 2. **Put a hard clock on the 24-hour window in the hero:** "Sale ends at midnight tonight" or "Deals expire at 11:59 PM ET" earns the "TODAY ONLY" urgency in a way that "24-HOUR SALE" banner text alone does not.
- 3. **Cut the subject to the first clause for mobile:** Let the preheader carry the brand names so the subject lands clean in every inbox.
- 4. **Drop the summer gear grid or spin it into its own send** — it kills the exclusive-brands momentum and pads the email past the point most subscribers scroll.
- 5. **Add one credibility signal per section:** Even a bare "4.5★ (2,300 reviews)" under a CALIA hero shot would meaningfully lift click intent.
- **Subject Alt A:** `24-Hour Sale: Up to 70% Off CALIA, VRST & DSG`
- **Subject Alt B:** `TODAY ONLY — Exclusive brand deals up to 70% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `DSG, CALIA, VRST clearance — sale ends at midnight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Final hours: exclusive brand deals up to 70% off`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**MSO conditional CSS syntax error (confirmed):**
In the `<!--[if (gte mso 9)|(IE)]>` style block, the `div` rule contains a stray embedded double-quote:
```css
div { ... padding:0;" }
```
The `"` before `}` is invalid CSS and will break Outlook's rendering of `div` elements — likely causing unwanted spacing or layout collapse in Outlook 2016+.

**Tracking pixel missing `alt` attribute:**
```html
<img src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/...' style="display:none; ..."/>
```
Should be `alt=""` per accessibility standards. Low rendering impact (hidden element) but fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.1.1 and some accessibility scanners will flag it.

**Logo `<img>` tag is truncated in provided source** — cannot confirm presence of `alt` text. Verify the compiled send has `alt="DICK'S Sporting Goods"` or equivalent on the logo.

**Template comment in 2025:** `<!--DKS Email Marketing 2025-->` — minor template staleness indicator; not a rendering issue but signals this is a copied template from a prior year.
