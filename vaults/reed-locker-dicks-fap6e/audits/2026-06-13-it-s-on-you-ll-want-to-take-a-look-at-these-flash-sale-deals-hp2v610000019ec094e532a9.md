# It's ON! You'll want to take a look at these Flash Sale DEALS
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- This is a "DICK'S | Flash Sale" sub-account send — the third distinct DICK'S sender identity in the inbox this week, alongside the regular DSG account and the Clearance sub-account. The format is identical to every other DICK'S send this week: a multi-module compilation with category tiles, percentage-off badges, and SHOP NOW buttons. The hero promise of "Up to 50% off" is the exact same ceiling the Father's Day Savings Event hit on June 11 (9/10), which makes the "flash" label feel like a cosmetic rename rather than a genuine, time-bounded event.
- The module lineup is real enough: Extra 30% Off clearance, 40% off running shoes, 40% off CALIA/VRST/DSG/Alpine clothing, 2-for-$30 Callaway golf balls, 30% off chairs, and a Father's Day call-out near the bottom. Breadth is not the problem. The problem is that this exact combination of offers has appeared in some form across every send this week, and the "Flash Sale" framing adds nothing — no countdown, no deadline, no exclusive product — to justify the urgency implied in the subject line.
- The bottom line: competently assembled, offers are legitimate, but calling this a flash sale when 50% off has been the standing ceiling all week tests subscriber patience. A real flash event has a clock; this one has "many exclusions to apply, see website for details" surfaced in the preheader instead.

## What's working

- The 2-for-$30 Callaway Chrome Series / Tour Made golf ball deal is the most specific, product-level offer in the email and a genuine hook for the golf segment
- Category navigation pills (Men's, Women's, Kids', Golf, Team) appear immediately below the hero, giving subscribers a fast path to their lane without scrolling
- Multiple SHOP NOW CTAs are clearly labeled and distributed throughout — no ambiguity about where to click
- The Father's Day module near the bottom ties the send to a live seasonal moment, giving it at least one topical anchor
- The Extra 30% Off clearance badge is visually distinct (orange) and clearly separated from the main sale messaging

## What's weak

- "Flash Sale" is hollow without a deadline — the June 11 Father's Day Savings Event already ran 50% off, so nothing here reads as limited or urgent
- Subject line is 61 characters and leads with click-bait framing ("It's ON! You'll want to take a look") with "DEALS" in all caps — crossing into spam-signal territory without delivering specificity
- The hero image appears cropped at the top edge: the large display type reads "FLASH SA..." with the remainder cut off, which looks like a render bug regardless of design intent
- "Many exclusions to apply, see website for details" is surfaced in the preheader position — the first thing a subscriber reads after the subject — which plants doubt before they even open
- No ScoreCard / loyalty member callout despite DICK'S having an active rewards program that could add genuine differentiation from a straight discount email

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the flash with a real deadline.** Without "Ends midnight tonight" or "36 hours only," this is just another sale with a flashier label. Add a countdown or a hard close date to the hero and the subject — that alone separates it from the Father's Day and Clearance sends.
- 2. **Move the Callaway golf ball deal to the hero module or at minimum position two.** It's the most specific, lowest-friction offer in the email and is currently buried mid-scroll. Golf subscribers who saw the June 11 soccer and golf content are primed for it.
- 3. **Rewrite the subject around the most differentiated offer.** "Flash Sale DEALS" competes with every other promo in the inbox — name the product or the number.
- 4. **Replace the exclusions disclaimer in the preheader with a supporting benefit.** The preheader should sell, not hedge. Move legal copy to the footer where it belongs.
- 5. **Fix the hero crop.** The display-size "FLASH SALE" text running off the top edge of the container reads as a broken layout.

- **Subject Alt B:** `48 hours: running shoes, golf deals, and clearance up to 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Callaway golf balls 2 for $30 · CALIA & VRST up to 40% off · Sale ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Extra 30% off clearance, 40% off shoes and clothing — real markdowns with a real deadline`

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods Flash Sale Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on Epsilon/Harmony infrastructure with standard click/open tracking. Two concrete defects ship in production: invalid JSON-LD (which will silently kill Gmail Promotions tab annotations) and a stray quote character in the MSO conditional CSS that breaks Outlook's `div` reset rule.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**No broken redirect domains detected.** All tracked links route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/…` and the open-pixel fires via `app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/…` — both consistent with the Epsilon send infrastructure.

**Tracking pixel missing `alt` attribute:**
```html
<img src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019ec094e...' 
     style="display:none; max-height: 0px; ..."/>
```
No `alt=""` present. Some strict validators flag this; low severity but should be `alt=""`.

**Logo `src` is truncated in the source excerpt** (`brand-logo--dk…`) — cannot confirm the full URL resolves. Confirm the asset URL is complete and returns HTTP 200 before send.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Stray double-quote in MSO conditional CSS** — will cause the `div` reset rule to fail in Outlook and IE-engine clients:
```html
<!--[if (gte mso 9)|(IE)]>
<style type="text/css">
  ...
  div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
</style>
```
The `"` before `}` is invalid CSS. Outlook's parser will silently discard the entire `div` rule, leaving `font-size` and `line-height` unset. Fix: remove the stray quote → `padding:0;}`.

**`border-collapse` mismatch:** Outer tables use `border-collapse:separate; border-spacing:0` (required for `border-radius`), but the MSO conditional forces `border-collapse:collapse`. This is a known Outlook/border-radius workaround and is intentional — no action needed unless border-radius on containers matters in Outlook.

**`<link rel="icon">` in `<head>`:** Ignored by all email clients; adds dead markup. Remove.

**Web font version pinned to `v7`:** Both `@font-face` rules reference `fonts.gstatic.com/s/archivo/v7/…`. Google does not guarantee indefinite CDN availability of pinned version paths. Lower priority risk, but current Archivo is well past v7.

---

The `availabilityStarts` date (`2020-03-06`) is a stale placeholder from a prior template — it was not updated for this send.

---

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**JSON-LD contains JavaScript-style comments — invalid JSON:**
```js
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
  // Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
  "@context": "http://schema.org/",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
```
`//` comments are not valid JSON per RFC 8259. The `<script type="application/ld+json">` block will fail to parse in any strict JSON parser, including Google's Gmail annotation engine. **This silently disables all Gmail Promotions tab rich annotations (deal badge, expiry countdown, discount display).** Remove all `//` comment lines.

**Unsubscribe link and physical address:** Not visible in the truncated source. Must confirm footer contains:
- One-click unsubscribe link (CAN-SPAM §5)
- Physical mailing address of advertiser

**Sender authentication:** Cannot inspect DKIM/SPF/DMARC headers from HTML source alone. Confirm `e.dcsg.com` has valid SPF `include:`, DKIM `d=e.dcsg.com` signature, and DMARC policy alignment before evaluating deliverability risk.
