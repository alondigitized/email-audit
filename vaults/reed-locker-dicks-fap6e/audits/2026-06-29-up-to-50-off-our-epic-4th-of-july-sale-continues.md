# 👏 Up to 50% off 👏 Our EPIC 4th of July Sale continues!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-29**
## Executive summary

- This is the second distinctly-branded 4th of July email from DICK'S in three days (following the June 27 "EPIC 4th of July Sale" launch that scored 8/10) and at minimum the seventh DICK'S send since June 26. The word "continues" in the subject is the tell — the brand knows this campaign is long in the tooth and is essentially announcing it. The email itself is well-assembled: a clean EPIC 4th of July hero, a sensible category nav, and a genuine spread of specific product deals (On Cloud shoes at 20% off, adidas women's outdoor at 50% off, Stanley drinkware, a Yeti bike deal, a BOGO pickleball paddle). No broken images, no layout chaos.
- The fundamental problem is cadence. A subscriber who opened every DICK'S email this week has already seen the "up to 50% off + summer essentials" frame three or four times. "Continues" is honest but it's also a creativity tombstone. The deals are real and varied — there's genuine value in the body — but the email is running on fumes of goodwill from the subscriber's patience.
- Verdict: solid production on a fatigued concept. The send would have been better skipped in favor of a single stronger 4th of July hero send on July 1 or 2.

## What's working

- **Hero instant-reads**: "EPIC 4th of July SALE" lands in under a second — big, clear, seasonally correct.
- **Deal variety is real**: On Cloud 20% off, adidas women's 50% off, Stanley drinkware colorways, Yeti bikes, BOGO pickleball paddle — product breadth gives most shoppers at least one hook.
- **No render bugs**: Layout is clean, images load, no overlapping text or lorem ipsum visible.
- **Category nav below hero**: Golf, Outdoor, Run, Shoes shortcuts let scanners jump immediately rather than scroll through everything.
- **Franklin pickleball paddle deal** at bottom is a smart inclusion — pickleball is high-purchase-intent right now and most competitors ignore it.

## What's weak

- **"Continues" kills urgency**: The word openly signals this campaign is already old news, undercutting the "must act now" energy a sale email needs.
- **Double 👏 emoji framing is weak**: Bookending a discount claim with applause emojis reads as filler, not voice — prior DICK'S sends used more contextually-relevant emoji (⚽, 💸, 🏆).
- **CTA copy is generic**: "Summer Deals" and "Summer Shop" are noun phrases, not calls to action. A verb drives clicks; a category label just describes.
- **No deadline anchored anywhere visible**: The 4th of July is the implicit deadline, but the email body never states it — "Sale ends July 4" or a countdown-style line would sharpen the close.
- **Cadence tax is real**: Brand history shows 6+ sends in the same 72-hour window; a 7th with near-identical framing is a credible unsubscribe trigger.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the "continues" framing entirely.** Either escalate urgency ("Ends July 4 — final days") or pivot the angle ("The deals everyone's grabbing this week"). "Continues" is the worst of both worlds.
- 2. **Anchor a hard deadline in the body.** One line above the first CTA: "Sale ends July 4 at midnight." That alone will lift click-through.
- 3. **Replace the CTA buttons** with verb-led copy: "Shop Summer Deals" → "Grab the Deals" or "Shop by Sport."
- 4. **Lead with the sharpest deal, not the broadest.** The 50% off adidas women's outdoor is the best single-offer hook in this email; it should be the hero sub-headline, not the third product module.
- 5. **Drop one emoji, earn the other.** Single emoji subjects tend to outperform bookended emoji. Keep the right 👏 or swap for something seasonal (🇺🇸, 🎆).
- **Subject Alt A:** `50% Off Women's Outdoor + More 4th of July Deals`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Ends July 4: Up to 50% Off Shoes, Gear & More 🎆`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `On Cloud, adidas, Stanley, Yeti — all on sale through the holiday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Final days: grab the summer gear before July 4th.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is built on a standard Epsilon/Harmony ESP template with Outlook MSO conditionals and responsive mobile CSS. Two confirmed defects: invalid JSON-LD that will silently fail Gmail structured-data parsing, and a stale 2020 promo date left in the schema block.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**No blocking issues.** All CTAs route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/…` redirect chain (Epsilon tracking). Subscriber and message IDs are encoded in the path (`v610000019f12fc8041c28567369ff0aab4 / 8708e7198df44fb8…`), which is standard for this ESP.

**Minor:** The `<!--Replace/Paste SSL Here-->` comment immediately after the tracking pixel `<img>` in `#ssl` is a template sentinel that was not removed before send. It is an HTML comment only — no functional impact — but signals a gap in pre-send template cleanup.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **AEM asset served from a `_dev/` path.** The logo `src` begins `https://dsg.images.harmony.epsilon.com/_phoenix/_dev/AEM/img/headers/brand-logo--dk…`. If `_dev` is a staging CDN origin (not just a URL path segment), this asset may not be cached or highly available under production load. Verify the origin resolves to production CDN, not a staging host.

- **Duplicate `font-family` declaration on `body`.** The stylesheet contains:
  ```css
  body { font-family: Archivo, Arial, sans-serif; font-family: Archivo, Arial, sans-serif !important }
  ```
  The first declaration is dead code overridden by the `!important` duplicate. Not a rendering defect, but indicates template slop.

**No issues found:** `lang="en"` present on `<html>`; `role="presentation"` on all layout tables; `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` override correctly applied; MSO conditional comments present and syntactically valid; web font loaded inside `@media screen` (Outlook-safe).

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**Sender domain:** `DSG@e.dcsg.com` — `e.dcsg.com` is the Epsilon ESP sending subdomain. SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment must be configured on `e.dcsg.com`, not the root `dcsg.com`. Authentication records are not verifiable from HTML alone; confirm via a received-headers check on a delivered copy.
