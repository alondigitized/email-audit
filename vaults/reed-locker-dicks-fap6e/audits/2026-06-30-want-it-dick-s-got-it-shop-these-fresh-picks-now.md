# Want it? DICK'S got it — shop these fresh picks now 👉
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S "Picks" editorial format — a curated new-arrivals roundup, not a promotional email. After at least eight consecutive sale/discount sends since June 26, the creative pivot to editorial makes intuitive sense as a palate cleanser. But the execution exposes a deeper problem: the content modules have almost no through-line. Women's Merrell hiking shoes sit next to LEGO Soccer Sets, which sit next to Lids hats, which sit next to Collectors Clubhouse trading cards, then Baseball Lifestyle + Big League Chew gum. There's no single coherent shopper being served here.
- The hero is visually intriguing — a brand logo mosaic (SKIMS visible, plus adidas and others) with a large shoe image — but the logos are too small to read at email scale, and the "Want it? DICK'S Got It." headline doesn't deliver a reason-to-click beyond brand swagger. If SKIMS × DICK'S is a real new partnership, that's an actual story. Buried in a mosaic in a picks roundup is the worst way to tell it.
- The bigger issue is cadence. This is the 9th+ DICK'S email since June 26. The prior audit on June 29 already flagged the eighth send and noted the brand had quietly dropped the 4th of July framing. Now we get a fresh-picks send with zero discount, no urgency, and no offer. Most subscribers at this saturation point are not opening.

## What's working

- **Editorial pivot is the right call.** After a week of "50% off" hammering, a curation-based send breaks the pattern and provides genuine value.
- **Hero visual hierarchy is clean.** Logo mosaic → shoe image → headline → CTA follows a logical eye path with no layout breakage.
- **"ALL FROM PICKS" CTA label is branded and consistent.** It reinforces the editorial concept across every module and creates a sense of an ongoing property.
- **LEGO Soccer Sets is a genuinely surprising and timely inclusion.** Soccer tournament is live; LEGO appeal is broad. It earns a click from a different audience segment.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer anywhere in the email.** After training subscribers on 50% off for a week straight, a no-discount editorial send will underperform significantly on click-through — the dopamine loop is broken.
- **Product mix is incoherent.** Women's hiking shoes → LEGO → Lids hats → Collectors Clubhouse → baseball gum. No persona can claim all of this as their shopping basket.
- **Brand logo mosaic in the hero is illegible.** The logos (SKIMS, adidas, others) are thumbnail-sized — a missed opportunity if SKIMS is a new launch partnership, which would be genuinely newsworthy.
- **Subject line is too long.** At ~53 characters including the emoji, it truncates on most mobile clients before landing the "fresh picks" payoff.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** Nine emails in four days — subscribers have been conditioned to expect discounts and this send delivers nothing to convert on.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a single anchor offer or member hook.** Even "ScoreCard members get early access to these picks" or "arriving this week — grab yours before it sells out" converts the browse intent into urgency without a discount.
- 2. **Cut the product mix to a single theme.** Women's athletic lifestyle or Men's baseball lifestyle — not both plus LEGO plus trading cards in one email. Pick a lane and go deep.
- 3. **Give SKIMS its own send.** If this is a new brand launch at DICK'S, it should headline a dedicated email with context, not be a 12px logo in a mosaic.
- 4. **Shorten the subject and drop the em dash clause.** "Want it? DICK'S got it" alone is punchy and under 25 characters. The clause "— shop these fresh picks now 👉" adds length without adding meaning.
- 5. **Thin the editorial cadence.** Nine emails in four days trains subscribers to ignore. Reserve the Picks format for once a week maximum, so it lands as a signal rather than noise.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Fresh drops: new gear just landed at DICK'S`
- **Subject Alt B:** `SKIMS is now at DICK'S — plus more new picks`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Merrell, LEGO Soccer, Lids, Collectors Clubhouse, and summer must-haves — curated weekly`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New arrivals across women's, soccer, and baseball — gear up before it's gone`

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods Promotional Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is sent via Epsilon/Harmony platform (`e.dcsg.com`). The most significant technical defect is an invalid JSON-LD block that will silently fail Gmail structured data rendering; secondary issues include stale schema dates and empty required fields left from a template.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Tracking domain:** `app.dickssportinggoods.com` handles both open pixels (`/O/...`) and click redirects (`/T/...`).

- **No visible UTM parameters** in the raw click URLs before the redirect chain resolves. UTM attribution may be injected server-side during redirect, but this cannot be confirmed from source. If the redirect strips or fails, downstream analytics will lose campaign attribution.
- **`__dU__` parameter** on tracked links is Epsilon's encrypted subscriber token — expected, no issue.
- **Open-tracking pixel** (`/O/v610000019f...`) is hidden via inline `display:none` — standard, no issue. Missing explicit `alt=""` attribute (minor; most clients suppress it anyway).
- **Logo image src is truncated** in the provided source (`brand-logo--dk…`). Cannot confirm the full CDN URL resolves correctly; should be verified against the actual send.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — Invalid JSON in `<script type="application/ld+json">`:**

```json
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
  "@context": "http://schema.org/",
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": ""
}
```

`//` comments are illegal JSON (RFC 8259). Gmail's structured data parser will reject the entire block, meaning:
- No Promotions tab annotation
- No offer carousel eligibility
- No annotated "Deal" badge

This appears to be developer/designer guidance left in a production template. The entire `ld+json` block will silently no-op in every client.

**`DiscountOffer` schema — empty required fields:**

```json
"description": "",
"discountCode": "",
"availabilityEnds": ""
```

Even if the JSON were valid, empty `description` and `availabilityEnds` on a `DiscountOffer` node fails schema validation.

**`availabilityStarts` is 6 years stale:**

```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```

This is a template default that was never updated. Date is March 2020; email sent July 2026.

**`PromotionCard` `"image"` is empty string** — designer comment confirms this was never filled in: `// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!`

**Responsive/rendering:** MSO conditional comments are present and correct. `@media screen` wraps web font loading so Outlook falls back to Arial — no issue. `role="presentation"` on layout tables — correct.

**`lang="en"` on `<html>`** — present, correct.
