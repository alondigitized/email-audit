# Up to 50% off this week's deals is yours for the win! 🏆 💰
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- This is a summer transition send from DICK'S — the brand just closed out Deal Days on June 26 with multiple "last chance" emails (including two in a single day), and this is now at least the second email landing on June 27 under a "This Week's Deals" umbrella alongside the already-logged "EPIC 4th of July Sale" send. The creative is competent: a clean beach-backdrop hero with a bold "UP TO 50% OFF" overlay, followed by two genuinely useful product blocks naming specific brands (running shoes: Saucony, New Balance, Brooks, PUMA; clothing: CALIA, VRST, DSG, Alpine Design). The product-level specificity is this email's strongest asset.
- The subject line is where this email gives back the most ground. "Up to 50% off this week's deals is yours for the win! 🏆 💰" clocks in over 57 characters, the "is yours for the win!" suffix is pure filler that adds no information, and the two-emoji tail feels like a transparency bid rather than creative confidence. The urgency anchor is "this week's" — a weakly bounded phrase compared to the campaign-close sends that named actual deadlines. For a subscriber who has received upwards of ten DICK'S emails in three days, this one will read as more of the same before they even open it.
- The body delivers more than the subject promises. If a subscriber opens, the named-brand specificity in the running shoes and clothing blocks gives a real reason to click. That's the honest version of this email's value, and it's buried under a subject line that undersells it.

## What's working

- **Named-brand specificity in product blocks**: Calling out Saucony, New Balance, Brooks, PUMA (running) and CALIA, VRST, DSG, Alpine Design (clothing) gives subscribers real intel rather than generic category language.
- **Summer-relevant hero**: Beach imagery with a clean "UP TO 50% OFF" lockup is visually coherent and seasonally appropriate for the campaign transition.
- **Stacked percentage offers**: 50% off in the hero + 40% off in both product blocks creates a clear discount ladder that reads as a genuine sale, not a marketing headline.
- **Clean render**: No broken images, overlapping text, or layout failures visible — the email renders fully and predictably across the visible sections.
- **"Must-have gear for an epic summer!" product grid**: Adds browsing surface area for subscribers who aren't running or apparel shoppers.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is too long and padded**: "is yours for the win! 🏆 💰" adds 24+ characters that communicate nothing — the offer disappears on mobile before the subscriber reaches the value prop.
- **"This week's" is weaker urgency than what this campaign trained subscribers on**: After multiple "ends TODAY" and "last chance" sends, vague weekly framing is a step backward in urgency credibility.
- **All CTAs say "SHOP NOW"**: Three identical generic buttons across different product categories is a missed differentiation opportunity — nothing in the button copy tells you *what* you're clicking toward.
- **Cadence fatigue is real**: This is the 12th-plus DICK'S email in a four-day window. Even a well-executed send loses open lift when the subscriber has already tuned out the brand for the week.
- **Category nav appears to repeat "GOLF"**: At the "Explore more deals" section, the nav pills appear to list GOLF twice — if accurate, this is a content bug that undermines the category-browsing UX.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to lead with the strongest specific hook, not the generic discount**: The named-brand running shoes angle is far more thumb-stopping than "up to 50% off this week's deals." Subscribers who run already know the brands — name them in the subject.
- 2. **Add a real deadline**: "This week's" is not a deadline. "Through Sunday" or "ends July 4" would give the same "this week" framing while adding the specificity that Deal Days trained subscribers to expect.
- 3. **Differentiate the CTA buttons**: Replace the three identical "SHOP NOW" buttons with "Shop Running Shoes," "Shop Women's Apparel," and "Shop Summer Gear" — minor copy lift, major click attribution improvement.
- 4. **Fix the GOLF category duplication** if confirmed — that's a sloppy signal in an otherwise clean email.
- 5. **Pull back cadence**: Two emails on June 27 after ten in three days is overfire. The second June 27 send should be a late-day urgency play with new creative, not a broad repeat.

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard table-based layout with Outlook MSO conditionals and responsive mobile overrides. Two confirmed code defects were found: invalid JSON-LD (breaks Gmail structured data rendering) and a CSS syntax error in the MSO conditional block (risks Outlook mis-rendering).

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Click tracking:** All CTA links route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610000019f0ac3aeacca36c2369ff1d668/…` — redirect-based tracking is functional.

**Open tracking pixel:** Located in `<td id="ssl">` at:
```
https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019f0ac3aeacca36c2369ff1d668/2764b51e…
```
The pixel carries no `alt=""` attribute. While `display:none` hides it visually, the missing attribute is an accessibility/validator flag.

**Brittle pixel injection:** The comment `<!--Replace/Paste SSL Here-->` directly below the pixel `<img>` indicates manual find-replace insertion. If the SSL token was forgotten, the open-rate pixel would be absent with no automated fallback.

**UTM parameters:** Cannot be verified directly — they would need to be confirmed post-redirect. Not flagged as confirmed issue.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**MSO conditional CSS syntax error (confirmed):**

Inside the `<!--[if (gte mso 9)|(IE)]>` block:
```css
div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
```
There is a stray `"` character before the closing `}`. This is a CSS parse error that Outlook's rendering engine will encounter, potentially causing `div` elements to inherit no layout resets in Outlook 2007–2019.

**Positive checks:**
- `<html lang="en">` present
- `role="presentation"` on all layout tables
- `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` override present
- `meta charset` and `viewport` tags correct
- MSO `OfficeDocumentSettings` and `AllowPNG` present
- Mobile breakpoint CSS is well-structured
- Table math is consistent: outer `600px` → `padding:8px` td → inner `584px`

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Segmentation marker visible in source:**

<table id="header-non-sc-member" …>

The `non-sc-member` ID implies a member/non-member conditional variant. If the alternate member variant block was accidentally omitted or duplicated during assembly, both segments may receive the same creative.

---

### 5. Compliance

**JSON-LD structured data — multiple violations:**

The `<script type="application/ld+json">` block contains three issues:

1. **JavaScript-style comments inside JSON** (invalid JSON per RFC 8259):
   ```
   // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
   // Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
   // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
   ```
   JSON parsers — including Gmail's structured data processor — will reject this block entirely, meaning Gmail Promotions tab annotations will not render.

2. **Stale template date:**
   ```json
   "availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
   ```
   This date is from 2020. `availabilityEnds` is also empty (`""`). Gmail's DiscountOffer parsing requires valid date ranges.

3. **Non-standard Schema.org types:**
   - `"@type": "DiscountOffer"` — not a valid Schema.org type (should be `Offer`)
   - `"@type": "PromotionCard"` — not a valid Schema.org type (this is a Gmail-proprietary extension but must be declared under the Gmail namespace, not `schema.org`)

**CAN-SPAM:** Physical mailing address and unsubscribe mechanism not visible in truncated source — cannot confirm or deny. Standard review of the full footer is required.

**Sender domain:** `DSG@e.dcsg.com` (`e.dcsg.com` is the ESP subdomain). DKIM/SPF/DMARC alignment must be verified against `dcsg.com` at the DNS level — not assessable from HTML alone.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All click links pass through the `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/` redirect chain. UTM parameter application (or absence) is only determinable by following the redirect — not visible in source. Flag for QA click-through test before send.

Logo image URL is sourced from `dsg.images.harmony.epsilon.com` (ESP-hosted CDN) — this is expected and correct for deliverability isolation.
