# 💵 $20 off $100+ starts NOW! Claim this app-exclusive deal...
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-24**
## Executive summary

- DICK'S keeps hammering the Memorial Day drum, and this is now the fifteenth-plus send in roughly a week — the third email branded around the same "Epic Memorial Day Sale" creative I've reviewed in the last 72 hours. What's different here is the lead: a genuinely useful $20-off-$100 app-exclusive coupon, stacked above the now-familiar 50% / 40% / 40% sale tiles. That's a smart layering — the coupon gives loyal app users a reason to re-engage even if they've ignored the sale all weekend.
- The render itself is clean and on-brand. The red/blue/star-spangled hero, the coupon-card visual treatment, and the category grid all read instantly. The hierarchy works: eye lands on the $20-off ticket, then the 50% off Memorial Day Sale tile, then the Nike/adidas 40% block. Nothing is broken, nothing is overlapping.
- But fatigue is the dominant signal. Memorial Day weekend has been a creative monoculture from this sender, and "$20 off $100" — while concrete — is a thinner offer than the headline 50% the same email is already promoting two screens down. The app-exclusive framing is the only thing keeping this from being noise.

## What's working

- App-exclusive coupon is concrete, specific, and stacked above the sale grid where it gets first read.
- Coupon visual (the perforated ticket card) is instantly recognizable as a redemption asset.
- Memorial Day patriotic palette is consistent with the prior week's creative — brand cohesion is strong.
- Multiple category tiles (Nike/adidas, golf clubs, Yeti, dresses) cover broad household demand.
- Footer category grid ("Check out these picks") gives a soft re-entry path for the uncommitted.

## What's weak

- Subject leads with $20 off $100, but the body's biggest visual is 50% off — the lead feels undersold versus what's actually in the email.
- Fifteenth-plus send in a week from this brand; the offer needs to escalate to break through and it doesn't.
- "App-exclusive" is the differentiator but it's buried in the subject after the dollar amount and emoji.
- No persona-specific hook for a Walker — no walking shoes, no fitness category called out above the fold.
- Hero coupon and 50% sale tile compete for attention; the eye bounces.

## Recommendations

- 1. Reframe the subject around the app-exclusive angle, not the dollar amount — that's the actual scarcity lever.
- 2. Add a single hero product (a walking shoe, a Yeti tumbler) under the coupon so the click path is one-tap, not "browse the sale."
- 3. Cut one of the redundant 40%-off tiles — pick Nike/adidas OR Select Athletic Shoes, not both stacked.
- 4. Surface the coupon code visibly on the ticket graphic so users know what to enter even if the deep link breaks.
- 5. Add an expiration timestamp on the coupon card itself ("Ends 5/26 11:59 PM") — credible urgency, not implied.
- **Subject Alt A:** `App-only: $20 off $100 — your Memorial Day code is live`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$20 off $100 in the app — Memorial Day weekend only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Open the app, claim the code, stack it on the 50%-off sale.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your app-exclusive coupon expires Monday — sale prices already loaded.`
