# $20 OFF $100+ expires TONIGHT only in the app! ⏳
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- This is the third DICK'S email on June 22 pushing the identical $20 off $100+ app-exclusive deal — following "📣 Starting NOW!" (7/10) and "One. Day. Only!" (8/10) earlier today. As a standalone send it is competently assembled: a live countdown timer anchors urgency above the fold, the offer is legible, and the "TONIGHT" deadline in the subject makes the time pressure credible. The creative holds together.
- But by send three on the same offer, the differentiation has been reduced to clock position alone. The countdown timer is the only net-new element. Everything else — the green Deal Days hero, the "GET OFFER" button, the $100+ threshold — is a pixel-for-pixel repeat. Diminishing returns are real here, and a subscriber who opened the first two and didn't convert is unlikely to convert on a third identical nudge. The app-exclusive requirement also creates a genuine click barrier that "GET OFFER" does nothing to prepare the reader for.
- If there's any justification for this send, it's the countdown itself: a time-bounded urgency reminder for a consumer who opened but didn't act is textbook CRM. The execution just needed more differentiation — a featured product, a tighter hero, or at minimum a subject line that signals "this is the final reminder, not another blast."

## What's working

- **Countdown timer above the fold** — "Final Hours: 07:58:48" is the single highest-impact urgency signal in the email and is immediately visible before any scrolling.
- **Subject line density** — "$20 OFF $100+ expires TONIGHT only in the app! ⏳" packs the full offer, deadline, and channel restriction into ~48 characters.
- **Secondary CTA for clearance browsers** — the "Up to 60% Off Select Clearance / SHOP NOW" section gives a fallback for users who won't download the app.
- **Category nav strip** — "ALL SALE / MEN'S / GOLF / WOMEN'S / KIDS / OUTDOOR" provides immediate category wayfinding for anyone who lands in the email without a specific intent.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping copy, no layout failures visible.

## What's weak

- **"GET OFFER" is the wrong CTA for an app-exclusive deal** — it implies an in-browser redemption; nothing prepares the reader for the required app download until after the click.
- ***** conditions are never surfaced** — "DICK'S Deal Days App Exclusive: $20 Off Your Purchase of $100 or More***" ends with three asterisks but the exclusions are nowhere in the visible scroll area of this screenshot.
- **No ScoreCard/loyalty hook** — the June 21 "Last Chance" ScoreCard send (8/10) wove loyalty context naturally; this send drops it entirely despite being the same brand and the same audience.
- **Product tiles are generic, not conversion-focused** — a women's tank, two water bottles, and HOKA shoes are summer basics, not the kind of hero SKU that makes a subscriber think "I was going to buy that anyway."
- **Third near-identical send today** — there is no creative differentiation from the prior two; the only new information is the countdown timer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rename the CTA to "Open App & Save $20"** — makes the required action explicit before the click, reduces post-click abandonment from users expecting a web promo code.
- 2. **Feature one specific, high-intent hero product** — pick a trending SKU (On or HOKA shoes, a Stanley, a Patagonia piece) and let the $20 off feel like a real discount on something the reader actually wants, not a generic basket-size push.
- 3. **Inline the key exclusion** — one line of small copy under the offer ("Excludes team sales and clearance") prevents the *** asterisk from feeling like bait-and-switch.
- 4. **Add a non-app fallback** — a secondary offer row (e.g., free shipping on $75+ no app required) captures the segment that won't download; otherwise non-app users are completely dead-ended.
- 5. **Tighten the subject to signal "final reminder, not another blast"** — the word "TONIGHT" does work but adding a final-reminder frame would differentiate this from send one and two.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last call: $20 off $100+ ends at midnight in-app`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your $20 app deal expires in hours — don't miss it`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Countdown's at 8 hours — open the app before it's gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `App exclusive: $20 off any $100+ order, tonight only. Tap to claim.`

## Technical Audit — DICK'S Deal Days ($20 OFF $100+)

### 1. Technical Summary

The email renders structurally sound but ships with a broken JSON-LD block (invalid syntax + unfilled placeholders) that disables Gmail annotations entirely, and a CDN image path containing `_dev` that may resolve to a staging asset in production.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Click-tracking wrapper:** All links route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610000019ef0f8814ac07e3f6e96c65848/…` — standard Epsilon/Harmony ESP redirect. No issues with the mechanism itself.

**Open-tracking pixel:** `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019ef0f8814ac07e3f6e96c65848/…` is present and correctly hidden (`display:none; max-height:0; overflow:hidden`). No issues.

**Template placeholder comment left in markup:**
```html
<td id="ssl" ...>
  <img src="https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/..." style="display:none..."/>
  <!--Replace/Paste SSL Here-->
</td>
```
The `<!--Replace/Paste SSL Here-->` comment is a CMS slot marker that was not removed before send. It is inert but indicates a QA gap.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`border-collapse:separate` on layout tables:** All wrapper `<table>` elements use `border-collapse:separate; border-spacing:0`. While `border-spacing:0` neutralizes any visual difference, the standard for email layout tables is `border-collapse:collapse`. In some older Outlook versions, `separate` can introduce unexpected 1px gaps. Low-severity but worth standardizing.

**Logo image URL contains `_dev` path segment:**
```
https://dsg.images.harmony.epsilon.com/_phoenix/_dev/AEM/img/headers/brand-logo--dk…
```
The `_dev` directory in a production send CDN path is suspicious — this may be resolving to a staging/development asset rather than the production-optimized image. Requires verification that this is an intentional path naming convention, not an incorrect environment reference.

**Alt text on logo:** Truncated source prevents confirmation; must verify that `<img id="logo">` has a non-empty `alt` attribute.

**Archivo font via `@media screen` guard:** Web font is correctly gated so it does not attempt to load in Outlook/MSO clients. No issues.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Multiple unfilled JSON-LD fields sent as empty strings:**
```json
{
  "@type": "DiscountOffer",
  "description": "",          // empty
  "discountCode": "",         // empty
  "availabilityEnds": ""      // empty
}
```
```json
{
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  "image": ""                 // empty — also flagged by the inline designer comment
}
```
These fields were never populated. The `PromotionCard` block even contains a designer instruction (`// DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!`) that shipped in the production send.

**Hardcoded stale `availabilityStarts` date:**
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```
This date is over six years old. It should be dynamically injected per campaign at send time, not hardcoded. A Gmail annotation using this block would show a promotion already expired in 2020.

---

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM physical address / unsubscribe:** Source is truncated before the footer; cannot confirm presence of required physical mailing address and one-click unsubscribe link. These must be verified in the full HTML.

**Authentication headers:** Sending domain is `e.dcsg.com` (Epsilon subdomain). SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment cannot be assessed from HTML source alone — requires header inspection (`Authentication-Results` field from a delivered copy). The ESP subdomain pattern is standard but alignment with the `From:` domain `dcsg.com` must be confirmed.

**`http://` in JSON-LD `@context`:**
```json
"@context": "http://schema.org/"
```
All three JSON-LD blocks use plain HTTP for the schema.org context URI. Google's structured data guidelines prefer `https://schema.org/`.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

UTM parameters on destination URLs cannot be confirmed from the truncated source — all visible links are wrapped in `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/…` click redirects, so the final query strings are not visible. Requires inspection of the unwrapped destination URLs or a live click-through test to confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` are appended correctly and match the campaign identifier in the subject line.
