# Up to 50% off EPIC deals for summer ☀️ Shop our Memorial Day Sale today!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S Sporting Goods' second promotional email on May 22 and — by this audit series' count — at least the twelfth in roughly five days. Arriving on the Saturday morning of Memorial Day weekend, the email itself is competently assembled: a bold "UP TO 50% OFF" patriotic hero, clean category navigation chips, and a well-organized grid of product modules spanning shoes, apparel, swim, bikes, and basketball. Standalone, that's a solid send. But it does not exist standalone.
- The "EXTRA 25% Off Select Clearance" module reappears here for at least the third time this week — and running it again in the same send window as this morning's "The SALE starts now 🎆" (scored 8/10) trains subscribers to treat that discount as permanent wallpaper. The second send today also detonates any urgency the first send built. By the time a subscriber has absorbed that their inbox has received two Memorial Day emails before noon on Saturday, the campaign has become noise.
- Clear judgement: Competent creative execution undermined by a cadence problem that is now the defining story of this campaign.

## What's working

- **Hero is immediately legible**: "UP TO 50% OFF" in bold white against a patriotic red/navy/star background renders cleanly and hierarchy is obvious.
- **Category navigation chips** ("SUMMER DEALS," "SUMMER SHOP," "APPAREL," "RUNNING," "SHOES," "BIKES") make the long email scannable without requiring sequential scrolling.
- **"$100 and Under Shoes" module** is a specific, price-anchored offer — the one section that escapes the vague "up to/select" framing and gives a buyer a concrete filter.
- **Breadth of coverage**: Swim, bikes, basketball, clearance, and footwear modules mean nearly any subscriber finds at least one relevant section.
- **Clean render**: No broken images, no layout breakage, no overlapping text visible.

## What's weak

- **Second email before noon on the same day**: The morning anchor send already established the 50% off Memorial Day frame. This email recycles the same hero discount, making both sends feel weaker.
- **"EXTRA 25% Off Select Clearance" is a zombie module**: It has appeared in multiple sends this week with no new framing or deadline. Subscribers have been trained to ignore it.
- **"SHOP NOW" everywhere**: Every product module ends on the same generic CTA. There is zero category-specific pull copy — no "Shop Men's Runners," no "Find Your Bike."
- **Subject line is over-length**: "Up to 50% off EPIC deals for summer ☀️ Shop our Memorial Day Sale today!" is ~72 characters. It will be heavily truncated on most mobile clients, burying the "Memorial Day Sale today" urgency anchor.
- **Zero personalization**: Same email, same modules, same order, regardless of what the subscriber actually buys.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Hold the second send for a "last hours" window.** If two sends are necessary, the second should fire at 6–8pm ("Sale ends tonight") — not within hours of the first at effectively the same discount level.
- 2. **Give "Extra 25% Off Clearance" a hard expiration or cut it.** Without a visible deadline, it reads as permanent. If it must run, add a countdown-style label: "Clearance overlay ends midnight tonight."
- 3. **Write category-specific CTAs throughout.** Replace every "SHOP NOW" with verb + category noun. Higher click specificity = lower decision fatigue.
- 4. **Shorten and focus the subject to mobile-safe length:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Memorial Day Sale ends tonight — up to 50% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `50% off. Today only. DICK'S Memorial Day ☀️`
- 5. **Write a preheader that adds, not repeats:**
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shoes, bikes, swim — one sale, one day left.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your Memorial Day gear: up to 50% off + free ship on $49+`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Email Audit — DICK'S Sporting Goods Memorial Day Sale

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Table-based layout with MSO/IE conditional comments and Responsys click-tracking is structurally sound, but the JSON-LD structured data block contains invalid JSON (JavaScript-style comments) that will break Gmail Promotions tab enhancement parsing, and several template fields were left unpopulated before send.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**No critical issues.** Click links correctly route through the Responsys redirect domain (`app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/…`). Open-tracking pixel is present and correctly suppressed with `display:none; max-height:0px; overflow:hidden; mso-hide:all`.

Minor: The tracking pixel `<img>` is missing an explicit `alt=""` attribute. While functionally hidden, some screen readers may announce a missing alt on a visible-in-text-render fallback.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**MSO conditional stylesheet — stray quote character:**
```css
div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
```
The `"` immediately before `}` is invalid CSS. Outlook's parser will likely drop the entire `div` rule, which could affect spacing/line-height overrides in Outlook 2016+.

**`role="presentation"` on layout tables:** Correctly applied — no issues.

**Font loading:** `@font-face` for Archivo is wrapped in `@media screen`, preventing download in MSO clients. Correct fallback chain (`Arial, sans-serif`) is present.

**Truncated logo `<img>` tag:** The source cuts off at `src="https://dsg.images.harmony.ep…"` — cannot confirm whether `alt` text is present. If omitted, screen readers will announce the filename or URL.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**JSON-LD structured data block contains JavaScript-style comments — these are invalid JSON:**
```json
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
  // Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
  "@context": "http://schema.org/",
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": ""
}
```
`//` comments are not valid JSON. This causes a parse error for the entire `<script type="application/ld+json">` block — all three objects (Organization, DiscountOffer, PromotionCard) fail to render in Gmail's structured data pipeline.

**Unpopulated template fields sent to production:**
- `DiscountOffer.discountCode` → `""` (empty)
- `DiscountOffer.availabilityEnds` → `""` (empty)
- `PromotionCard.image` → `""` (empty)
- `DiscountOffer.description` → `""` (empty)

**Hardcoded stale date:**
- `"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"` — this is a 6-year-old static date, clearly a template default never replaced with the campaign date.

**Stale year comment:** `<!--DKS Email Marketing 2025-->` in `<head>` — sent in 2026.

---

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**HTML source is truncated** — the footer, physical mailing address, and unsubscribe link cannot be confirmed from the provided source. These are required under CAN-SPAM § 5(a)(5) and must be present.

**Authentication headers** are not evaluable from HTML source alone (SPF/DKIM/DMARC live in envelope/transport headers). Sending domain `e.dcsg.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain — alignment should be verified via `dig TXT _dmarc.dcsg.com` and checking that the DKIM `d=` value aligns with `e.dcsg.com` or `dcsg.com`.

No issues found in the visible HTML regarding unsubscribe link placement or label — but the truncation prevents a definitive pass.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM Params, Landing Page Alignment)

Responsys redirect URLs (`/T/v610000019e517360908367b0f4bbc786c8/…`) handle UTM injection server-side at click time — UTM parameters cannot be validated from the HTML source. Confirm in the Responsys tracking configuration that `utm_source=email`, `utm_medium=email`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content` are set per link group.

The landing domain for redirected clicks should resolve to `www.dickssportinggoods.com` — verify no redirect chain sends traffic through an intermediate domain that strips UTM params before hitting the analytics tag.
