# Fresh deals for fall? Yes, please! Shop up to 50% off this week  💸  👉
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-27**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S weekly-deals template again. A giant white "UP TO 50% OFF" sits on an autumn-leaf hero, with "This Week's Deals" and a red Shop Now button underneath. It's competent and the eye lands on the number first. But it's nearly identical to the 10/26, 10/25 and 10/24 weekly sends, and only the subject line changed.
- The body is a long, crowded scroll. After the hero there's a row of category pills and then a run of product blocks: running shoes, Nike fleece, World Cup soccer balls, YETI, NCAA, Walter Hagen golf and a second "Up to 75% Off" clearance hero. A product grid closes it out. Every block has its own green Shop Now button, so nothing is the clear second step after the hero. The 75% clearance block also repeats the 10/25 "Stop scrolling!" send, which dilutes the 50% hero.
- Verdict: a solid, safe send that is fatiguing as a subscriber. This is at least the fourth near-identical "up to 50%" email in four days. It would benefit more from a distinct angle than from more polish.

## What's working

- The "UP TO 50% OFF" hero is bold and readable, and the autumn imagery fits the season.
- The red Shop Now button directly under the hero is easy to find.
- The ScoreCard points and "Order Today. Sport it Tomorrow." strip at the top gives loyalty and fulfilment value without crowding the hero.
- The product blocks are specific and varied (running shoes, fleece, soccer balls, YETI, golf), so there's something for most shoppers.
- Callouts like "Just Launched" and "Only at DICK'S" add a little freshness and exclusivity.

## What's weak

- The cadence is stale. The hero and layout match the 10/26 "worth a closer look" and 10/25 "conjured up some DEALS" sends almost exactly.
- The email carries two hero banners, 50% and 75%, plus six or more offers at different discount levels. This splits the message.
- There are about 10 identical Shop Now buttons and no hierarchy among them. The generic CTA copy says nothing about the destination.
- The product tiles have no prices, no ratings and no sale tags. The "Check out these picks" grid shows names only, with no savings.
- The fine print is small and dense, and the "up to" language hides what the typical discount is. The "Reg. $55.00–$104" golf detail is tiny.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one story per send. Lead with a single category hero (running shoes or fleece, with the real discount) instead of the generic "up to 50%" banner, and drop the second 75% hero.
- 2. Add sale prices and a strikethrough regular price to the product grid, plus ratings on the top picks.
- 3. Differentiate the CTAs. Make the hero button a verb plus noun ("Shop Fall Deals") and the product buttons category-specific ("Shop Running Shoes").
- 4. Reduce the cadence of near-identical weekly deal sends, or at least change the hero creative week to week.
- 5. Cut the body to about 4 blocks and move the fine print into a clearer, readable "Details" link.

- **Subject Alt B:** `This week only: up to 50% off fall gear`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Nike, Brooks & HOKA running shoes on sale, plus fleece from $50 and under.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Order today, pick up in an hour. New deals on shoes, fleece and fan gear.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **[FAIL] Plain-text part is 9 characters.** It is effectively empty, so text-only clients and some spam filters get no usable alternative.
- **12 images have no alt text (QA-confirmed):**
  - Content images: `shoes1027stwd6`, `nikefleece1027stwd6`, `soccerball1027stwd6`, `1027-outdoor-olive-camo-banner`, `2ncaa1027stwd6b` and `golf1027stwd6`.
