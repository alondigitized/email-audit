# FINAL HOURS! Up to 70% off our exclusive brands ends tonight!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S fifth consecutive "up to 70% off" send in four days, and the creative team clearly knew it. The answer this time is a live countdown timer ("Final Hours: 27:59:58") planted right below the header, which is the right instinct — it reframes a stale offer as a real deadline. The "24-HOUR SALE" hero is clean and typographically dominant, the brand logos (DSG, CALIA, VRST) are readable, and the render is tight.
- The problem is beneath the hero: the email devolves into six or seven separate deal modules — On-Cloud, shorts, alpine shirts, drinkware, a 50% off banner, and a clearance grid — each with its own "Shop Now" button. Instead of one intensified close, the recipient gets a department-store flyer. The countdown timer creates urgency at the top; the scroll depth destroys it.
- Bottom line: the right mechanic (live countdown, FINAL HOURS framing) is grafted onto the wrong structure (a sprawling multi-section browse email). A genuine "final hours" email should feel like one door closing, not a new catalog opening.

## What's working

- **Countdown timer is the strongest creative addition in days** — "27:59:58" makes the deadline feel real and measurable in a way "ends tonight" text alone does not.
- **24-HOUR SALE hero is visually clean** — large type, brand logos, and "Up to 70% Off" all land above the fold without competing elements.
- **Specific brand callouts (DSG, CALIA, VRST)** — brand-name clarity is better than the generic "select clearance" copy that earlier sends leaned on.
- **Product grid at the bottom** (water bottles, camp chairs, coolers) gives tangible reasons to click beyond the hero discount.

## What's weak

- **Offer fatigue is now a structural problem** — this is the fifth near-identical "70% off" email since July 3; the countdown timer buys goodwill back but only partially.
- **Too many parallel CTAs dilute urgency** — each deal section competes with the hero; the email can't decide whether it's a closing-bell push or a browse campaign.
- **"Our exclusive brands" in the subject is vague** — subscribers have to guess whether this is DSG-label gear or the name-brand collab sale.
- **The 50% OFF banner mid-scroll contradicts the 70% hero** — a subscriber who sees 50% after being promised 70% feels confused about which number to trust.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill all but two deal modules.** For a genuine final-hours email, lead with the countdown, one primary offer (the full 70% off), and one secondary product category. Six modules reads as browse, not urgency.
- 2. **Anchor the countdown above the 70% headline**, not tucked between the logo and hero — make the time pressure the first thing the eye hits.
- 3. **Reconcile the 50%/70% split.** Either remove the 50% section or frame it clearly ("Items marked 50% off have additional codes — everything else is 70% off"). Mixed discount signals erode trust.
- 4. **Add one specific product hero.** Show the highest-value clearance item (a shoe, a jacket) with its before/after price. Price anchoring converts better than category browsing.

### 5. Compliance

**JSON-LD contains JavaScript-style `//` comments — this is invalid JSON syntax:**
```js
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
  // Sample image is 538x138, 3.9 aspect ratio
  "@context": "http://schema.org/",
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": ""
}
```
JSON does not allow `//` comments. Any strict parser (including Gmail's structured data renderer) will reject the entire `<script type="application/ld+json">` block. This silently kills all three schema objects (Organization, DiscountOffer, PromotionCard) in Gmail's inbox preview system.

**CAN-SPAM / unsubscribe** — HTML is truncated; footer not visible. Cannot confirm physical mailing address or one-click unsubscribe are present. Requires full-source review.

**Sending domain** is `e.dcsg.com` (ESP subdomain). SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment cannot be assessed from HTML — verify with a live delivery header check (e.g., `Authentication-Results` header in a test send).

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The redirect wrapper URLs do not surface UTM parameters before the redirect:
```
https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610000019f392431c6bd7069f4bbc780a0/6ba92c6f...
```

Run a live click trace to confirm whether the final destination (dickssportinggoods.com) receives `utm_source=email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=...`. If not, this send will appear as direct traffic in analytics, not email.
