# ⏳$20 OFF $100+⏳ expires TOMORROW (only in the app!)
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S ninth email in roughly two days, and the creative is a direct reprise of yesterday's app-exclusive $20 off $100+ send (July 10, 7/10) — same offer, same channel mechanic, now with an expiration clock stapled to the subject. The urgency strategy is the right call: turning a standing offer into a "expires TOMORROW" moment is textbook drip sequencing. The problem is execution. The hero section is competent — green banner, bold "$20 OFF," clear QR code and app store badges — but the email doesn't stop there. It immediately pivots into a full editorial digest: 60% off clearance, Crocs, H&M collabs, Adidas shoes, Select Mens, golf clubs, kayaks, must-haves. Seven distinct offers crammed below the fold.
- The email works as a two-part hybrid — urgency close-out on the app offer up top, weekly deals digest below — but the combination is muddled. A subscriber trying to act on the TOMORROW deadline has to scroll past a kayak section to find the app download. The hierarchy says the $20 offer is primary, but the body says "and also literally everything else we sell."
- Verdict: this is a tactical improvement over the standard clearance blast (the deadline creates a genuine reason to open), but the bloated scroll below undercuts the urgency. It reads like two emails stapled together.

## What's working

- **Deadline urgency is real and visible.** "TOMORROW" in subject + ⏳ emoji bookends land cleanly; the hero banner repeats "App Exclusive" which reinforces the channel gate.
- **Hero hierarchy is clean.** "$20 OFF" in white on green is the first thing the eye hits; the QR code and App Store / Google Play badges below give clear next-action paths.
- **App channel differentiation.** Having a QR code in-email is smart — reduces friction for someone on desktop reading before bed who wants to scan and download.
- **60% clearance module is the second-strongest hook.** Red "SALE" tags, specific categories (Shoes, Tops, etc.), and "Up to 60%" framing are all visible and legible.
- **No visible render bugs.** Images load, text doesn't overlap, layout holds on this render.

## What's weak

- **Too many offers, too many sections.** Crocs, Adidas, H&M collab, golf clubs, kayaks, "Must-haves for an epic summer" — this is at least 7 distinct product stories below the fold that dilute the TOMORROW urgency.
- **The "expires TOMORROW" energy evaporates the moment you scroll.** By the second module the deadline is forgotten; nothing below the hero reinforces the closing clock.
- **Golf clubs and kayaks next to each other signal zero segmentation.** These audiences don't overlap meaningfully. Someone opening for the $20 off gets a kayak ad.
- **App Store / Google Play badges are small.** On a rendered desktop view they're fine, but on a narrow mobile preview the QR code + badge stack is tight — the path to action could be clearer.
- **Ninth email in ~48 hours.** Even with a fresh urgency hook, cadence fatigue is real; open rates on this send are likely suppressed regardless of creative quality.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Strip the digest, send a pure close-out.** The TOMORROW hook is strong enough to carry a single-offer email. Remove everything below the 60% clearance band or save it for a separate send. A shorter email increases the chance the subscriber actually opens the app.
- 2. **Repeat the deadline deeper in the scroll.** If the digest stays, add a "Offer expires midnight tomorrow" footer banner near the bottom — every scroll depth should feel the urgency.
- 3. **Personalize the digest to the subscriber's most-clicked category.** Golf clubs for a golfer, shoes for a sneakerhead. What's shown is random-assortment retail.
- 4. **Add a secondary mobile CTA button** ("Open in App →") just below the QR code. Not everyone will scan; a deep-link tap-through button converts more mobile readers.
- 5. **Trim cadence or suppress this to non-openers only.** This subscriber has received 9 emails in 48 hours — a fatigue-filter segment would lift per-send engagement meaningfully.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$20 off $100+ — last day in the app`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Midnight tomorrow: your $20 app deal expires`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Tap to download, scan the QR code, or open the app — offer ends tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `This one's app-only. $20 off any $100+ order. Done by midnight tomorrow.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard SFMC deployment with a critical JSON-LD validity failure that silently kills Gmail structured-data rendering, plus a CSS syntax error in the MSO conditional block that corrupts Outlook div styles. All other issues are moderate.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**SFMC click-tracking confirmed** — all links wrap through `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610000019f4dd8a6c7be9e44369fb45280/…` with a `__dU__` encoded redirect parameter. This is expected behavior; no bare untracked links detected in the visible source.

**Open-tracking pixel** placed in `<td id="ssl">` with `display:none; max-height:0px; overflow:hidden; mso-hide:all` — correct suppression technique.

**`<!--Replace/Paste SSL Here-->`** comment remains in production send. This suggests the SSL (subscriber-level tracking pixel slot) was not populated. If SFMC's send-time optimization or individual open tracking requires a separate pixel here, it is missing.

**Logo link destination** resolves through SFMC redirect to an unconfirmable final URL in the truncated source. Cannot verify landing page alignment without full redirect chain.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS syntax error in MSO conditional block:**
```css
div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
```
There is a stray `"` before the closing `}`. This is an invalid CSS declaration that will cause Outlook's rendering engine to drop the entire `div` rule, potentially breaking layout in Outlook 2016–2021.

**Web font `src` lists only `woff2`** — no `woff` fallback. Inconsequential in practice (web fonts don't load in Gmail/Outlook), but Apple Mail and some iOS clients that do load web fonts will work correctly with woff2-only.

**`alt` attribute** on the logo `<img>` is not visible in the truncated source. Cannot confirm presence. If missing, screen readers and image-off renderers get no label for the primary brand mark.

**`<img>` src appears truncated in the provided source** (`wid=248&hei=…`) — if this reflects the actual sent HTML rather than source truncation, the Scene7 image URL is malformed and the logo will fail to load.

**`role="presentation"` on layout tables** — present and correct.

**`<html lang="en">`** — present. Good.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**JSON-LD `DiscountOffer` block has unpopulated merge tokens:**
```json
"description": "",
"discountCode": "",
"availabilityEnds": ""
```
Empty strings for `discountCode` and `availabilityEnds` mean Gmail's promotional tab annotation (deal badge, expiry countdown) will not render. The email subject references "expires TOMORROW" — this urgency is entirely lost in the inbox view.

**`availabilityStarts` is hardcoded to a 2020 date:**
```json
"availabilityStarts": "2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"
```
This is a stale template default that was never replaced with a dynamic send-date value.

**`PromotionCard.image` is empty string** — the Gmail promotional image card will not render.
