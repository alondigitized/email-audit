# Clearance deals now
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- This is the third DICK'S | Clearance sub-sender email in three days, and it's the weakest of the batch. The June 10 sends both headlined up to 60% off — this one rolls back to a hero offer of "Extra 25% Off" clearance shoes. That is a visible offer regression from the same sub-account, and any subscriber who got those June 10 emails will notice it. The creative is structurally identical to every prior clearance send: red hero, stacked category modules, green SHOP NOW buttons, footer. There's no reason for the eye to stop.
- The Father's Day tack-on at the bottom is the most puzzling choice. DICK'S proper already sent a dedicated Father's Day email on June 11 that scored 9/10 — a well-executed send with strong seasonal branding. Shoehorning that same module into a clearance email dilutes both messages. This isn't a clearance email with a Father's Day bonus; it's two half-finished emails stapled together.
- The subject line "Clearance deals now" is three words that tell me nothing I couldn't infer from the sender name. It is the weakest subject line in the entire DICK'S history reviewed here. This email does enough to be functional, but it adds no reason to prefer it over an inbox archive.

## What's working

- **Hero offer is front and center.** "EXTRA 25% OFF" in bold white on red reads instantly; no guesswork about what the email is for.
- **Module diversity.** Fan shop, golf, women's luggage, DSG family clothing — multiple entry points for different shopping intents in one scroll.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds across the full render.
- **Father's Day urgency module.** "Get It By Father's Day" with a deadline framing is the one time-bounded signal in the email, even if it belongs in a different send.

## What's weak

- **Offer regression from June 10.** The same sub-sender ran "up to 60% off select clearance" twice two days ago. "Extra 25% off" looks like a step backward, not a new deal.
- **Subject line is close to useless.** "Clearance deals now" could be auto-generated. No %, no category, no urgency that lands.
- **No preheader copy visible.** The top of the render shows no explicit preheader; the inbox preview is almost certainly leaking "View in browser" junk.
- **Father's Day module is misplaced.** It undercuts the clearance message and duplicates the June 11 Father's Day dedicated send (9/10) with no new reason to act.
- **Cadence fatigue.** Three near-identical clearance emails from this sub-sender in 72 hours, with a weaker offer each time, is a trust-erosion pattern.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the subject with the actual discount and category.** "Extra 25% off" is a real offer — put it in the subject line with a specific hook, not buried in the hero.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 25% off clearance shoes — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Clearance just got cheaper: extra 25% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shoes, fan gear, golf + DSG family clothing. All marked down further.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack savings on shoes, fan gear, and last-minute Father's Day gifts.`
- 2. **Pull the Father's Day module or make it the connector.** Either remove it entirely (it's been covered) or reframe the whole email as "Clearance gifts for Dad" — one coherent story, not two.
- 3. **Add a visible expiry date.** Every module says "SHOP NOW" with no deadline. Even a soft "through Sunday" would give a reason to click today instead of later.
- 4. **Rotate the hero creative.** Five consecutive clearance emails with the same red-banner format has trained subscribers to skip on sight. A lifestyle image (dad + kid in gear, World Cup fan gear closeup) would break the scroll pattern.
- 5. **Suppress this sub-sender cadence.** Three emails in 72 hours from the clearance sub-account with flat or declining offers is burning list goodwill. Consider a 48-hour hold between sends, or merge into the main DICK'S account with a clearance module.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound for cross-client rendering (proper MSO conditionals, `role="presentation"` tables, responsive breakpoints), but contains three categories of confirmed defects: invalid JSON-LD that will break Gmail structured data parsing, a stale 2020 template date left in the schema, and at least one unfilled designer placeholder committed to production.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Epsilon/Harmony redirect chain confirmed.** All links route through `app.dickssportinggoods.com` with long opaque tokens (e.g., `https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/v610000019ebb596b4ec1ce85f4bbc782e8/...`). No bare destination URLs are visible in the source — standard for this ESP, but means broken redirect infrastructure would silently 404 all CTAs with no fallback.

**Logo image URL truncated in source** (`https://dsg.images.harmony.epsilon.com/_phoenix/_dev/AEM/img/headers/brand-logo--dk…`). If the truncation reflects the actual delivered URL (not just audit tooling), the logo will render as a broken image.

**Tracking pixel missing `alt` attribute:**
```html
<img src='https://app.dickssportinggoods.com/O/v610000019ebb596b4ec1ce85f4bbc782e8/...'
     style="display:none; max-height: 0px; ..."/>
```
No `alt=""` present. Some strict email clients flag or block images without alt attributes.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**MSO conditional CSS has a stray quote causing a parse error:**
```css
div {background-color:inherit; font-size:0; line-height:100%; margin:0; padding:0;"}
                                                                                  ^
```
The closing `"` before `}` is invalid CSS. Outlook's parser is lenient here, but it's a syntax defect that could cause unpredictable rule-dropping in strict renderers.

**Google Fonts loaded via `fonts.gstatic.com` using `v7` of Archivo** — this is an older versioned URL. Google Fonts versioned paths are stable, so no active breakage, but the version may not include newer Unicode ranges added since `v7`.

**`<link rel="icon">` in `<head>`** — most email clients strip `<head>` entirely; this has no effect and can be removed.

No issues found with table structure, MSO column wrapping, or responsive breakpoint declarations.
