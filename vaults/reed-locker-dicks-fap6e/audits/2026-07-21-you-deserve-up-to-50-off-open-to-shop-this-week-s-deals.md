# You deserve up to 50% OFF! Open to shop this week's deals
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- This is DICK'S sixth "UP TO 50% OFF" weekly-deals blast in roughly four days, and it reads like it. The creative is structurally identical to the July 19 "Up to 50% OFF starts with this week's DEALS" send (7/10 here) — same hero discount, same category-module stack, same product carousel at the bottom. The only material change is a manipulative subject line: "You deserve up to 50% OFF!" is the kind of hollow flattery that erodes trust rather than building it, and the appended "Open to shop" instruction reads as desperation.
- Inside the email the execution is clean: the "UP TO 50% OFF" hero lands immediately, Nike Deal Days still has its own module (their third appearance in the inbox this week), and six department lanes (Golf, Outdoor, Team Sports, Crossbows, Shop Gear, Fitness) give the subscriber somewhere to go. But kitchen-sink layout and near-identical cadence means this email is being sent to everyone rather than to someone.
- The verdict: competent infrastructure, exhausted strategy. DICK'S is burning list equity with a daily fire-hose of near-identical discount emails. The individual email execution is fine; the campaign logic is not.

## What's working

- **Hero discount is immediately legible.** "UP TO 50% OFF" dominates the fold in large, high-contrast type — no guessing required.
- **Category swim lanes add structure.** Golf, Outdoor, Team Sports, Crossbows, Shop Gear, Fitness give different subscriber segments a natural entry point without hunting.
- **No visible render bugs.** Images load, layout holds, text is crisp throughout.
- **Nike Deal Days module persists as a named-brand anchor.** Calling out a specific brand (30% off Nike) is more motivating than generic category copy.

## What's weak

- **"You deserve up to 50% OFF!" is the worst subject line in this series.** Manufactured flattery + explicit open instruction ("Open to shop") is a dark pattern that signals a struggling campaign.
- **Sixth near-identical email in four days.** A subscriber who received the July 18, 19, 20, and 21 sends has seen this creative three to four times already; the odds of this one landing are negligible.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is the laziest available option.** After six category lanes, every CTA resolves to the same generic label — no pull toward a specific department or urgency moment.
- **No loyalty or member hook visible.** ScoreCard pricing or member-exclusive access would differentiate this from the open web; nothing like that appears.
- **Hero imagery does not signal a department.** The outdoor lifestyle shot is aesthetically fine but doesn't commit to a season-specific category (back-to-school? clearance? summer outdoor?), making the hero feel interchangeable with any prior send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the vanity subject; lead with the real hook.** "You deserve" is noise — cut it. The Nike Deal Days angle or a category-specific hook (back-to-school, golf clearance) would give the subscriber a reason that isn't "we're saying nice things at you."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Nike Deal Days + 50% off clearance ends soon`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Golf & outdoor clearance: up to 50% off this week`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop Nike, golf gear, outdoor, fitness and more — deals expire Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `6 departments, up to 50% off — your category is in here`
- 2. **Segment or suppress the daily blast.** Subscribers who opened the July 19 or July 20 send and didn't click golf/outdoor should not receive a sixth identical golf/outdoor module. A basic click-suppression pass per department would dramatically improve relevance.
- 3. **Make CTAs department-specific.** Replace every "SHOP NOW" with the category name: "Shop Golf Deals," "Shop Outdoor," "Shop Nike." Costs nothing, adds specificity, and improves click attribution.
- 4. **Surface a deadline.** "This week's deals" implies urgency but doesn't deliver it. A visible expiry ("Ends Sunday, 7/27") anchors urgency concretely and makes the offer feel real rather than evergreen.
- 5. **Pick a hero story.** Six lanes with equal visual weight is a wall. One hero department — e.g. back-to-school team sports — with secondary lanes below would give the eye somewhere to commit.

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This is DICK'S sixth "UP TO 50% OFF" weekly-deals blast in roughly four days, and it reads like it. The creative is structurally identical to the July 19 "Up to 50% OFF starts with this week's DEALS" send (7/10 here) — same hero discount, same category-module stack, same product carousel at the bottom. The only material change is a manipulative subject line: "You deserve up to 50% OFF!" is the kind of hollow flattery that erodes trust rather than building it, and the appended "Open to shop" instruction reads as desperation.

But kitchen-sink layout and near-identical cadence means this email is being sent to everyone rather than to someone.

The verdict: competent infrastructure, exhausted strategy. DICK'S is burning list equity with a daily fire-hose of near-identical discount emails. The individual email execution is fine; the campaign logic is not.

## 4. What I'd change

1. **Kill the vanity subject; lead with the real hook.** "You deserve" is noise — cut it. The Nike Deal Days angle or a category-specific hook (back-to-school, golf clearance) would give the subscriber a reason that isn't "we're saying nice things at you."
   - **Subject Alt A:** `Nike Deal Days + 50% off clearance ends soon`
   - **Subject Alt B:** `Golf & outdoor clearance: up to 50% off this week`
   - **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop Nike, golf gear, outdoor, fitness and more — deals expire Sunday`
   - **Preheader Alt B:** `6 departments, up to 50% off — your category is in here`

2. **Segment or suppress the daily blast.** Subscribers who opened the July 19 or July 20 send and didn't click golf/outdoor should not receive a sixth identical golf/outdoor module. A basic click-suppression pass per department would dramatically improve relevance.

3. **Make CTAs department-specific.** Replace every "SHOP NOW" with the category name: "Shop Golf Deals," "Shop Outdoor," "Shop Nike." Costs nothing, adds specificity, and improves click attribution.

4. **Surface a deadline.** "This week's deals" implies urgency but doesn't deliver it. A visible expiry ("Ends Sunday, 7/27") anchors urgency concretely and makes the offer feel real rather than evergreen.

5. **Pick a hero story.** Six lanes with equal visual weight is a wall. One hero department — e.g. back-to-school team sports — with secondary lanes below would give the eye somewhere to commit.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Critical: Invalid JSON-LD block.** The `<script type="application/ld+json">` block contains JavaScript-style single-line comments (`//`), which are not valid JSON:

```json
{ // GIF & WEBP images are not supported and will be filtered out.
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  // DESIGNER, ADD YOUR PROMO IMAGE URL BELOW!
  "image": ""
}
```

JSON does not support `//` comments. This causes a hard parse failure — Gmail's structured data renderer will silently discard the entire block, and any Gmail Promotions tab annotations (logo, deal badge, expiry date) will not render.

**Stale/empty structured data fields** (all within the now-broken JSON-LD block):

| Field | Value | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| `DiscountOffer.availabilityStarts` | `"2020-03-06T06:00:00-04:00"` | 6-year-old stale date — unfilled placeholder |
| `DiscountOffer.availabilityEnds` | `""` | Empty string |
| `DiscountOffer.discountCode` | `""` | Empty string |
| `DiscountOffer.description` | `""` | Empty string |
| `PromotionCard.image` | `""` | Empty string — designer instruction not executed |

Even if comments were stripped, all five fields above would need values for Gmail to surface a promotional annotation.

**UTM continuity to site:** Cannot confirm from source whether Acoustic appends UTM params post-redirect. If not, email-sourced sessions will not be identifiable in site analytics.

---

### 7. Recommendations

1. **[P0] Strip all `//` comments from the JSON-LD block before any future send** — these invalidate the entire schema block and suppress Gmail Promotions tab rendering.

2. **[P0] Populate or remove all empty JSON-LD fields** — `availabilityEnds`, `discountCode`, `description`, `PromotionCard.image` should either be populated with live values or the schema types should be removed entirely if not used.

3. **[P0] Fix stale `availabilityStarts` date** — `2020-03-06` is clearly a template default that was never updated; this should be set to the actual campaign start date.

4. **[P1] Confirm UTM injection at Acoustic redirect layer** — validate that `app.dickssportinggoods.com/T/...` appends `utm_source=email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=...` before the 302 to DSG.com. If not, add UTMs to all destination URLs directly.

5. **[P1] Verify `alt` text on logo `<img id="logo">`** — source was truncated; confirm `alt="DICK'S Sporting Goods"` or equivalent is set.

6. **[P2] Update template year comment** — `<!--DKS Email Marketing 2025-->` should be `2026` or removed; stale comments can cause confusion in QA.

7. **[P2] Confirm CAN-SPAM footer is present** in the non-truncated email — physical address and one-click unsubscribe link are legally required.
