# Big savings for bigger summer adventures
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- This is REI's second 4th of July email on June 26 — sent the same day as their campaign launch "Our 4th of July Deals have arrived 😎" (scored 8/10 in this series). The structure is near-identical: same "4th of July Deals" hero, same "UP TO 50% OFF" callout, same multi-category layout. The one genuine differentiator is a dedicated Garmin block leading the body with specific dollar savings on three named GPS models — $100 off the Descent G2, $50 off the inReach Mini 3. For outdoor tech buyers, that's legitimately worth a second open.
- The problem is the subject "Big savings for bigger summer adventures" completely buries that Garmin angle. From the inbox, this looks indistinguishable from the morning send. A subscriber who already opened today's launch email has zero signal this one carries new value. The specific product savings — the one thing that justifies a second send — are invisible until you click in.
- The email itself executes cleanly: strong visual hierarchy, specific savings, functional CTAs, no render bugs. But two same-day sends for the same campaign headline is a cadence mistake that will inflate unsubscribes regardless of offer quality. The Garmin deal deserved its own send with its own subject line.

## What's working

- **Garmin block is the email's best asset** — specific model names ($100 off Descent G2, $50 off inReach Mini 3) and clear BUY NOW CTAs are far more compelling than generic percentage callouts
- **Three distinct discount mechanisms** (dollar-off on Garmin, 25% off Osprey packs, up to 50% off clearance footwear) give different buyer types a hook without the email feeling scattered
- **Visual hierarchy holds** — 4th of July hero lands first, category blocks follow in logical priority order, REI Co-op perks anchor the footer without crowding the offers
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no empty placeholder content visible anywhere in the scroll
- **REI Co-op membership footer** reinforces loyalty value without taking over the screen

## What's weak

- **Subject line wastes the Garmin specificity** — "Big savings for bigger summer adventures" is indistinguishable from the same-day launch email and gives subscribers zero reason to re-open
- **Second same-day send on the same campaign** — a subscriber who received "4th of July Deals have arrived" this morning sees this as noise before they've even scrolled the first email
- **No visible offer expiration** — "4th of July" implies July 4, but no explicit "ends July 4" or "sale ends Sunday" appears in the render; urgency is soft when it should be easy
- **SHOP NOW CTAs on Osprey and footwear are generic** — the BUY NOW buttons on the Garmin section feel transactional; the pack and footwear sections drop to less specific copy
- **Socks section undercuts premium positioning** — the slide from Garmin GPS watches to bundled socks in one scroll is a jarring altitude drop that makes the email feel longer than it needs to be

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the subject with the Garmin deal** — the only real justification for a second same-day send is a product-specific angle that subscribers didn't see this morning; bury it and you've eliminated the rationale for sending at all
- **Subject Alt A:** `Save $100 on Garmin this 4th of July`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$100 off Garmin Descent G2 — 4th of July sale`
- 2. **Write a preheader that handles urgency** — if the subject takes the product angle, the preheader should close with the deadline
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sale ends July 4 — plus 25% off Osprey and 50% off clearance footwear`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Deals end Sunday — Garmin, Osprey packs, clearance footwear, and more`
- 3. **Add an explicit expiration line in the hero** — "Offers valid through July 4" costs one line of copy and converts ambient campaign energy into real urgency without a redesign
- 4. **Upgrade the Osprey and footwear CTAs** — "Shop Osprey Packs" and "Shop Clearance Footwear" over "SHOP NOW" reduces friction and signals the destination before the click
- 5. **Cut or move the socks section** — drop it to a footer clearance module or remove it entirely; the premium outdoor positioning built by Garmin and Osprey shouldn't be diluted by a bundle-and-save socks pitch
