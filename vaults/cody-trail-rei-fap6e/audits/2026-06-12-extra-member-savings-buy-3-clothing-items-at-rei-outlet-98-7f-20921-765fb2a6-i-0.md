# EXTRA member savings: buy 3+ clothing items at REI Outlet
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- This is a member-exclusive clothing deal from REI Outlet — "EXTRA 20% OFF" on 3+ clothing items with a visible coupon code in the hero. The member-gating is the right instinct: REI's co-op loyalty program is a genuine differentiator, and surfacing "JUST FOR MEMBERS" above the fold makes the offer feel earned. The hero is clean, the brand logos (The North Face, Marmot) act as quality anchors, and the render is solid.
- The problem is that the email can't decide what it is. Halfway down, it pivots hard into a gift card cross-sell ("Give a gift. Get a gift.") that has nothing to do with the clothing deal. These two offers compete, and the result is that neither lands cleanly. The June 5th "Price drops—up to 50% off" send was weaker on member framing but stronger on message discipline — single theme, one through-line, scroll-to-CTA.
- Verdict: competent bones, unfocused execution. The member angle earns it, but the dual-offer structure and the absence of any deadline leave real conversion on the table.

## What's working

- **"JUST FOR MEMBERS" hero framing** — exclusivity is front and center, makes the discount feel like a privilege, not a clearance push.
- **Coupon code prominently boxed** — code is displayed in its own styled block in the hero, low friction to copy at checkout.
- **Brand logos as credibility anchors** — The North Face and Marmot visible in the hero give the deal a quality signal before the subscriber scrolls.
- **"Coupon-worthy picks" labeling** — smart copy that connects the code to a curated product grid rather than dumping the shopper on a category page.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds across the full scroll.

## What's weak

- **Two-offer structure splits the email's purpose** — clothing deal + gift card promo in a single send; a subscriber who opened for outerwear gets derailed by the gift card section before hitting the product grid CTA.
- **"SHOP NOW" repeated six times with no differentiation** — product grid shows six items, each with the same generic CTA and no price, brand name, or any hook underneath.
- **3+ item threshold competes with the headline** — "EXTRA 20% OFF" reads like a flat member discount on first glance; the qualifier is visible but creates a subtle bait-and-switch feeling.
- **No deadline anywhere visible** — unlike the May 23 coupon send (which had a countdown timer), this offer floats with no urgency. The code feels good indefinitely.
- **"BECOME A MEMBER" CTA appears twice** — once in the hero and once in the gift card section; acquisition messaging inside a member-exclusive email is contradictory and signals internal goal-stacking.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop the gift card section or send it separately** — this is the single highest-impact change. The gift card promo, likely timed to Father's Day, deserves its own send; it dilutes the clothing deal and vice versa.
- 2. **Add a hard expiration to the code** — "Code expires June 16" alone would lift urgency without a countdown timer. No deadline means no reason to act today.
- 3. **Replace generic "SHOP NOW" in the product grid** — each tile should carry the brand name or a price floor (e.g., "Marmot fleeces from $55") to turn passive grid browsing into an active buying signal.
- 4. **Reframe the threshold as a positive mechanic** — instead of "BUY 3+ CLOTHING ITEMS," try "Pick 3, pay less — your member code is waiting." Same requirement, but frames it as a game rather than a condition.
- 5. **Consolidate to one "BECOME A MEMBER" CTA** — keep it in the gift card section if that stays; remove it from the member-exclusive hero where it reads as confused targeting.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Members: 20% off when you grab 3+ clothing items`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your member code: 20% off 3+ REI Outlet clothing — ends soon`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop The North Face, Marmot & more — use code OUTWEAR20 at checkout`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack summer layers and save — offer ends June 16`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — incorrect `lang` attribute:**

```html
<html dir='auto' lang='und' ...>
```

`lang='und'` means "undetermined." Screen readers (NVDA, VoiceOver, JAWS) use this attribute to select pronunciation and text-to-speech engine. It should be `lang='en'` for an English-language email. This will cause incorrect or degraded TTS behavior for visually impaired recipients.

`dir='auto'` is acceptable but marginally weaker than an explicit `dir='ltr'` for a Latin-script English email — not a hard defect, but worth locking down.

**`<title>` tag:**

```html
<title>REI</title>
```

Some clients (Yahoo Mail, Outlook web) display the `<title>` as the visible subject or tab label. "REI" is acceptable but overly generic; no action required unless sender policy mandates subject alignment.

**CSS observations (no defects, one note):**

```css
@media only screen and (min-width:320px){
```

Hiding the scrollbar globally on all webkit viewports ≥320px is aggressive and affects every mobile client. Not a rendering bug, but suppresses the scrollbar in Gmail's iOS in-app browser — worth noting if UX complaints surface.
