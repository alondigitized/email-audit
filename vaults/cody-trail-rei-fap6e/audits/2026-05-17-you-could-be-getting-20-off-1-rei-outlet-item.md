# You could be getting 20% off 1 REI Outlet item
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- This is REI Outlet riding the coattails of the Anniversary Sale that launched two days ago (May 15 — the email I scored 9/10). The framing is smart: while the main REI list is still buzzing from the big sale, the Outlet channel is sliding in with a behavioral hook — "you could be getting 20% off" — implying you left something on the table. That's a good instinct. The problem is execution fragments it. The hero promises one personalized offer, then the email immediately layers on two more percentage-off blocks (racks, footwear) that dilute the "just for you" premise and make it feel like a batch promo wearing a personalization mask.
- Structurally the email is clean — no render bugs, clear REI Outlet branding, and the 20% starburst badge in the hero does the heavy lifting. The free shipping bar at the top is a nice value anchor. But the coupon section mid-email is a near-duplicate of the hero, and the competing offers (20% + 20% on racks + 25% on footwear) mean there's no single "click here, do this" moment.
- Bottom line: the concept is right, the personalization signal is underused, and the multi-offer structure undercuts the urgency that "just for you" tries to build.

## What's working

- **Behavioral subject line hook** — "You could be getting" implies eligibility, not a batch blast; that's the right psychological frame for an outlet coupon.
- **REI Outlet as distinct sender** — differentiates from the main REI anniversary emails; outlet shoppers skew deal-motivated, so the channel match is on-point.
- **Free standard shipping bar** anchors perceived value before the offer lands.
- **25% off footwear section** is the most specific, category-owning block in the email — trail and clean shoes shown together is a real signal for outdoor runners.
- **No render bugs** — product thumbnails, layout, and starburst badge all render cleanly.

## What's weak

- **"Could be getting" is passive** — it should be declarative ("Your 20% coupon is ready"); the conditional framing softens urgency right in the subject.
- **Three competing percentage-off blocks** (20% hero, 20% racks, 25% footwear) kill focus; the "just for you" promise evaporates when the same email pushes three unrelated categories.
- **Hero product grid is a category sampler** — tent, sunglasses, jacket, shoe thumbnails without a clear hero product; no single thing you can act on immediately.
- **No visible expiry date in the hero** — if there's a deadline, it's buried below the fold; the urgency signal is wasted.
- **Cadence is getting dense** — this is the sixth REI/REI Outlet email in 17 days; even a willing subscriber starts filtering at this volume.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the subject declarative and ownership-framed** — the single biggest lift available. "Could be getting" is a hedged invitation; "is waiting for you" or "is yours" converts passive to active.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 20% REI Outlet coupon is waiting`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Grab 20% off 1 Outlet item — your call`
- 2. **Collapse to one primary offer with a visible deadline in the hero** — kill the racks block; let the footwear 25% off sit as a secondary section. Two offers max.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Outdoor gear at outlet prices — coupon inside, expires soon`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `One item, 20% off — use it before it goes`
- 3. **Feature one hero product, not a 4-up thumbnail grid** — pick the best-margin item in the outlet, give it a name and a clear CTA button. The carousel approach is a category browse, not a conversion play.
- 4. **Show the expiry date in the hero** — even "Offer ends May 20" stamped near the starburst would convert lookers who need a push.
- 5. **Cut the mid-email coupon-delivered section** — it re-explains the hero offer and stalls momentum. Trust the hero to do the job.
