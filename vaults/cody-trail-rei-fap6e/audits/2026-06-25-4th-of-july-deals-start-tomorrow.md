# 4th of July Deals start TOMORROW!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- This is REI's campaign-launch email for their 4th of July sale — sent June 25, a responsible nine days after the last send, giving the inbox some breathing room after the May Anniversary Sale barrage. The bones are solid: outdoor-relevant hero, a clear "up to 50% OFF" badge, named products (REI Co-op Sahara & Nano Sun Shirts, specific Garmin models), and a multi-category structure that moves from apparel to gear to accessories. REI knows how to build a sale email.
- The single most damaging thing in this render is the countdown timer showing **0 : 00 : 00** — all zeros. For an email that says deals start *tomorrow*, a zeroed clock either reads as "already over" or "broken page." That's not a cosmetic issue; it's a trust signal that will lose clicks from subscribers who pause on it. Every previous REI email in the audit history avoided this problem precisely because they didn't lean on a live countdown.
- The subject line earns its keep on urgency ("TOMORROW!") but wastes the persona-targeting opportunity — "4th of July Deals" is category-agnostic. Given that REI's list skews toward active outdoor subscribers, a subject that calls out even one product category (Garmin, sun shirts, hiking) would sharply improve open relevance.

## What's working

- **"Up to 50% OFF" badge** is visible in the hero on mobile without scrolling — discount depth is communicated immediately.
- **Named products lower in the scroll** (REI Co-op Sahara & Nano Sun Shirts, Garmin Descent G2, Instinct 3S) give specific anchors that feel like real deals, not filler padding.
- **Multi-category CTAs** (SHOP SHOES, SHOP SHIRTS, SHOP SOCKS, SHOP NOW) give subscribers multiple entry points without visual clutter.
- **Garmin co-branding block** is a clean, co-op-style partnership section that adds credibility and specific dollar savings ($50, $75 off named SKUs).
- **Nine-day cadence gap** after the May Anniversary Sale exhaustion shows deliberate calendar management — this doesn't feel like the fifth "LAST CHANCE" in a week.

## What's weak

- **Countdown reads 0:00:00** — the most damaging visible element; on mobile it looks like the sale ended or the module failed to load.
- **"BUILD YOUR CART" CTA** is generic for a campaign launch — no product category or urgency in the button copy itself.
- **Hero sub-copy ("Save on sale, clearance and REI Outlet items")** is three channels crammed into one vague line; no specific product promise at the hero level.
- **No explicit end date stated** — "4th of July" implies July 4 but the email never says "ends July 4" or "through July 4," leaving urgency soft.
- **Socks bundle section** (save 15% on 2+ pairs) feels tacked on after the Garmin block — low-glamour product positioned after a premium tech brand, which deflates momentum.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill or fix the countdown timer.** If the dynamic element can't render reliably, remove it and replace with a static "Sale ends July 4" text lockup. A broken counter costs more than no counter.
- 2. **Rewrite the hero CTA to name a category**: "SHOP OUTDOOR DEALS" or "SHOP UP TO 50% OFF" — anything that carries the discount into the button copy.
- 3. **Add an explicit close date to the hero**: "Deals run through July 4" in the sub-copy below the headline.
- 4. **Move the Garmin block up** — it has the most specific dollar savings ($50–$75 off named SKUs) and is the strongest mid-email hook. Socks bundle should follow apparel, not premium tech.
- 5. **Subject: call out a product, not just a holiday.** "4th of July" tells the subscriber when, not what.
- **Subject Alt A:** `REI 4th of July: Garmin, sun shirts, up to 50% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Gear sale starts tomorrow — up to 50% off at REI`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Garmin watches, sun shirts, hiking socks — deals run through July 4`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop REI Outlet + sale items through the holiday weekend`
