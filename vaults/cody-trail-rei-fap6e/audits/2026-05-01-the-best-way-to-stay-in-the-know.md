# The Best Way to Stay in the Know
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-01**
## Executive summary

- This is a standard welcome/signup confirmation email anchored by a 15% off coupon. The offer is the clear star of the email and it's presented upfront — that's the right call. However, the subject line completely buries the lead: "The Best Way to Stay in the Know" says nothing about the coupon, making it a self-defeating first impression. The email's structure is logical (coupon → account setup → product recs → preferences → store), but it runs long and loses energy well before the fold. REI has brand equity to spend here; this email spends it on generic structure rather than warmth or surprise.
- The mechanics work — coupon is visible, CTAs exist, product recommendations are present. But the subject line is likely costing meaningful open rate, and the email's length and module density dilutes the primary action (use the coupon, create an account).

## What's working

- **Coupon is front and center.** 15% off with the code displayed prominently is the right hierarchy. New subscribers expect a reward; delivering it visually at the top rewards that expectation.
- **Multi-stage onboarding logic.** Account setup → product discovery → preferences → store finder is a sensible progression for a new subscriber. REI is attempting to onboard, not just pitch.
- **Opt Outside brand moment.** Placing the "Opt Outside" section reinforces REI's identity differentiation. For a new subscriber, this is a decent brand imprint.
- **Member benefits module.** Calling out 100% satisfaction, 1-year returns, and 5% card rewards reminds the recipient of REI's value proposition beyond the immediate coupon.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is completely disconnected from the offer.** The single most actionable reason to open this email — a 15% coupon — is invisible in the subject line. "Stay in the Know" is newsletter framing for a discount email.
- **Too many CTAs competing for attention.** CREATE ACCOUNT, CUSTOMIZE YOUR SETTINGS, FIND YOUR NEAREST STORE, and the product grid all ask for something. With no clear visual hierarchy between them below the hero, each one diminishes the others.
- **Product recommendations module feels under-merchandised at this scale.** The gear grid (jackets, gear) is small and generic — no personalization signal, no clear tie to the coupon ("pair these with your 15% off"). It's a missed opportunity to drive an immediate click.
- **Email runs too long.** By the time we reach Opt Outside, member benefits, and the footer, this email has asked the reader to process six distinct sections. For a welcome email, that's overbuilt.
- **Manage Email Preferences CTA is oddly prominent.** Placing "Customize Your Settings" as a full-width module with its own section header mid-email nudges new subscribers toward opting down before they've had a reason to stay engaged.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject line to lead with the coupon.** "Your 15% off coupon is inside" or "Welcome — here's 15% off your first order" would materially improve open rate.
- 2. **Trim to three sections max for the welcome.** Hero (coupon) → Account setup → Product recs. Push preferences and store finder to a follow-up onboarding email.
- 3. **Connect the product grid to the coupon.** Add "Use your 15% off on these" as a header above the gear grid to create a direct conversion path.
- 4. **Move email preferences to the footer or a second email.** Presenting it as a full module this early is a churn accelerant.
- 5. **Test a shorter, warmer hero.** "Thanks for signing up!" is transactional. Something like "You're officially an REI insider" sets a different tone and earns more goodwill.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1.0` blocks user zoom (confirmed)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0,
  minimum-scale=1.0, maximum-scale=1.0" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale` and `minimum-scale` constraints.

**Duplicate conflicting `color-scheme` meta tags (confirmed)**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light dark">       <!-- line 1 -->
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light, dark">      <!-- line 2 -->
```
Same meta name appears twice with inconsistent value formatting (`"light dark"` vs `"light, dark"`). The second declaration wins in most parsers. Consolidate to one: `content="light dark"`.

**Malformed `font-weight` in `@font-face` (confirmed)**
```css
/* REIStuart-Bold */
font-weight: 600;
font-weight: bold;   /* duplicate — overrides the numeric value */
```
`bold` maps to 700, not 600. Whichever weight the designer intended, only one declaration should be present. This will cause incorrect weight matching in clients that do render web fonts.

**Web fonts blocked by major clients**
All three custom faces (`Arial-Black`, `REIStuart-Semibold`, `REIStuart-Bold`) load from `https://notices.rei.com/assets/...`. Gmail strips `<style>` blocks entirely; Outlook (Windows) ignores `@font-face`. Fallback stack not visible in the truncated source — confirm a safe system-font fallback is declared for every rule that references these families.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Preheader copy contains a typographic error that could indicate a broken merge token**
```html
<span style="display: none; ...">Plus, here's a special coupon-just for you </span>
```
`"coupon-just"` reads as a compound word. If the intended text was `"coupon — just for you"` or `"coupon code just for you"`, the em-dash or intervening word was dropped. If this was meant to be a merge token (`%%COUPON_CODE%%`) that rendered empty and left the hyphen dangling, this is a personalization failure that shipped to the entire list.

**No first-name token visible in preheader.** "just for you" is generic — not confirmed as an issue, but worth verifying against the send spec.
