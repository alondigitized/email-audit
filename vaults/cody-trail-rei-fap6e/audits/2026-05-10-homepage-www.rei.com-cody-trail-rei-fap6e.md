# Homepage snapshot · www.rei.com · 2026-05-10
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-10**
## Executive summary

- REI is doing what REI does best — leading with lifestyle over price. The camp kitchen hero with YETI, Zempire, and OXO Outdoor is exactly the kind of curated gear-nerd content that made me join the co-op in the first place. The homepage is clearly pushing a late-spring "get outside and cook better" story for May, which tracks seasonally — but as a dividend-card-carrying member I'm immediately scanning for my co-op hook and it isn't anywhere above the fold.

## What's working

- **Camp kitchen hero with brand callouts (YETI, Zempire, OXO Outdoor):** Signals quality and curation. These aren't random brands — they're ones I actually research before buying. The product selection alone tells me REI did homework.
- **"Cooking, covered." headline:** Short, confident, gear-forward. Exactly the no-BS copy register I expect from REI, not some aspirational lifestyle brand trying too hard.
- **"Shop camp kitchen" CTA:** Dark green, pill-shaped, above the fold. One tap and I'm in the category. No ambiguity.
- **Top nav: Trade-In, Classes & Events:** Both are in my regular rotation. Seeing "Trade-In" up top reminds me I have old gear sitting in the garage. "Classes & Eve[nts]" is cut off but I know it's there — that's a co-op differentiator no other outdoor retailer can match.
- **Impact Report banner:** As a co-op member I actually care about the stewardship angle. It reinforces why I buy here instead of Amazon.

## What's weak

- **Zero member/dividend visibility:** I've been a member for six years. My dividend just accrued. REI knows this and I'm getting a generic homepage that a non-member would see. No "Your dividend: $XX" nudge, no "Member Sale" badge — nothing.
- **No offer above the fold:** Camp kitchen gear is expensive. A "Members save 20% this week" or even a free-shipping callout would close the gap between browse and buy. Right now there's no financial reason to shop today vs. next week.
- **No people in the hero:** The camp scene is gorgeous but it's food and gear, no human. For a lifestyle brand the size of REI, a shot of someone like me actually cooking at a lakeside camp would land harder emotionally.
- **Partially visible second card (running shoe, pink):** Whatever that second carousel panel is, it's getting cropped. If it's a women's running shoe, that's a rough pivot from the camp kitchen story — feels like two different audiences fighting for the same scroll.
- **No Garage Sale / re/Supply callout:** REI's used gear program is a genuine differentiator. For a dividend-driven member who buys once and repairs twice, not seeing it in the above-fold experience is a missed co-op culture signal.

## Recommendations

- **Add a logged-in member ribbon:** A thin bar just below the nav — "Welcome back, Cody. Your 2025 dividend: $XX. Shop now." REI has the data. Use it.
- **Inject a member pricing or active offer into the hero subhead:** Change "Elevated camp meals start with better gear" to "Members save 15% on camp kitchen this week — upgrade before your next trip." One line, immediate reason to tap.
- **Swap the stock-food-only shot for a lifestyle scene with a person:** Show someone plating actual backcountry pasta on a YETI cooler lid at dusk. Same gear, human warmth, co-op culture baked in.
- **Surface a re/Supply or Trade-In teaser below the hero:** A single strip — "Trade in your old camp stove. Get credit toward a new one." — would speak directly to how I actually shop.
