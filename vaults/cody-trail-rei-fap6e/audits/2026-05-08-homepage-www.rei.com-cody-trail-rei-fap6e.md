## What's working

- **"Cooking, covered." headline** — short, punchy, confident. It reads like a co-op member who knows their gear, not a big-box marketing team.
- **Brand trio (YETI + Zempire + OXO Outdoor)** — putting three respected names right on the hero validates the quality message. I trust YETI. I'm curious about Zempire. That combo makes me want to tap.
- **"Shop camp kitchen" CTA** — specific enough to not feel like a trap. I know exactly where I'm going.
- **2025 Impact Report banner** — this is actually REI's superpower and they're using it. I care about their sustainability story. "We made our mark on our planet, communities & lives outside" is my language.
- **Trade-In in the top nav** — one of the first things I see. REI leading with Trade-In signals they still take the circular economy seriously. That's a quiet loyalty signal for people like me who have gear to trade.

## What's weak

- **No member/dividend hook anywhere** — I co-own this place. There's no "Member Deals," no "Your REI Dividend," no "Members save X%." That's the single biggest miss for me and every other co-op member landing here.
- **No visible offer or price incentive** — camp kitchen is a considered purchase. Without a member price or even a free-shipping callout, there's nothing pulling me to tap *now* vs. next weekend.
- **No identifiable person in the hero** — hands and gear, no face. REI's best content shows real people in real terrain. A camp stove flatlay works on Instagram; on a homepage it feels a little catalog.
- **Partially visible second panel (pink shoe)** — I can see the edge of what looks like a running shoe carousel item. Camp cooking → running shoe is a jarring tonal jump if that's what's next. Makes the homepage feel unfocused.

## Recommendations

- **Add a member badge to the hero CTA** — swap "Shop camp kitchen" for "Shop camp kitchen — members save 10%" or add a small "REI Member Exclusive" pill next to the CTA. One token and you're speaking directly to the 22M+ co-op members who actually convert.
- **Surface a free-shipping threshold in the utility bar** — even "Free shipping on orders over $50" in small text between the search bar and hero would boost conversion intent immediately.
- **Replace the flatlay with a lifestyle shot** — put a person (late-20s to mid-30s, rugged casual, not performatively athletic) actually cooking at a campsite. The gear can still be hero; give it human context.
- **Tighten the campaign focus** — if the second carousel is a running shoe, either cut it or make it a coherent spring story ("gear up for everything outside"). A 34yo backpacker doesn't want a pivot to footwear without a bridge.
