# Just dropped 🔥 Up to 50% off at REI Outlet
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- This is a post-Anniversary-Sale outlet clearance drop from REI Outlet — a different sub-brand than the main REI Anniversary Sale blitz that just ended. The "Just Reduced" framing is smart timing: the sale just wrapped, gear is moving to outlet, new markdowns just dropped. The hero is clean and punchy — a bold "JUST REDUCED / UP TO / 50% OFF" over a dark background, with three recognized brand logos (Nemo, Mountain Hardwear, Gregory, ALPS Mountaineering) anchoring credibility instantly.
- The middle section is where the email loses steam. Six product tiles in a 2-column grid with no prices, no product names, and identical "SHOP NOW ›" links offer zero signal — the subscriber has to guess what's in those images, and most are thumbnail-scale and unclear. If you landed here from the subject line expecting specific deals, this grid delivers nothing to act on.
- Net judgement: a solid hero undercut by a merchandise grid that does all the work wrong. Strong enough to click through once for the 50% off promise, but the in-email experience burns trust before the click.

## What's working

- **Hero contrast and offer clarity** — "50% OFF" in massive white type on dark blue is readable at a glance; no ambiguity about the offer.
- **Brand logos as anchors** — Nemo, Mountain Hardwear, Gregory, ALPS Mountaineering immediately communicate premium outdoor gear, not generic clearance.
- **"Just Reduced" framing** — Timing feels fresh and credible, not recycled from the Anniversary Sale urgency spam.
- **Footer trust signals** — 100-day return policy badge and the REI Co-op Mastercard "Earn 5%" callout add legitimacy without cluttering the body.
- **No render bugs** — Clean layout, no broken images, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **Product grid is completely uninformative** — Six tiles with no product names, no prices, and no brand labels. The subscriber can't act on what they can't read.
- **No prices anywhere in the body** — "Up to 50% off" in the hero but zero specific price points below it; the promise is unverified until you click.
- **No urgency** — After a week of countdown timers and "deals end tomorrow!!!" messaging from main REI, this email has nothing — no "limited stock," no expiry date, nothing.
- **CTA is generic** — Both "SHOP NOW" buttons and the grid links are identical; there's no differentiated anchor for someone who wants to shop a specific category or brand.
- **No member-specific hook** — The footer mentions the Mastercard offer but the body treats everyone as anonymous; the Anniversary Sale fatigue from recent sends makes it easy to tune this out.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add at least one price or percentage to the product grid tiles** — Even "Was $189 → $94" on one hero product doubles the click signal. Right now the grid is decorative.
- 2. **Name the products or categories in the tiles** — "Nemo sleeping bag," "Gregory pack," "Mountain Hardwear jacket" — anything to orient the eye and give a reason to click a specific tile instead of the generic "SHOP ALL THE DEALS" button.
- 3. **Add a light urgency line** — "New drops sell fast — outlet sizes go first" costs nothing and is honest; it's credibly different from the countdown-timer spam of the past week.
- 4. **Replace the duplicate "SHOP NOW" CTA with category-specific buttons** — "Shop Packs," "Shop Apparel," "Shop Footwear" above the grid so a shopper can self-select without wading through all 50% off inventory.
- 5. **Surface a specific hero product** — One featured item (image + name + was/now price) above the grid would anchor the whole email and give subscribers a concrete reason to click rather than a vague promise.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New markdowns: Nemo, Gregory, Mtn Hardwear — up to 50% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `REI Outlet just dropped fresh cuts — up to 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Nemo, Mountain Hardwear, Gregory and ALPS Mountaineering gear just reduced — while sizes last.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh outlet markdowns on packs, apparel and footwear — no code needed, up to 50% off.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — `lang="und"` on `<html>` tag (confirmed)**
```html
<html dir="auto" lang="und" ...>
```
`lang="und"` (undetermined) disables language-aware text rendering in screen readers and can produce incorrect hyphenation, font selection, and pronunciation in assistive tools. For English content this must be `lang="en"`.

**Issue 2 — `dir="auto"` on `<html>` tag (confirmed)**
`dir="auto"` forces the browser to infer text direction per paragraph, which is appropriate for multilingual user-generated content but not for a static English promotional email. Use `dir="ltr"` to prevent any bidi-algorithm surprises in Gmail and Outlook.

**Issue 3 — Missing `<meta name="x-apple-disable-message-reformatting">` (confirmed)**
This meta tag is absent from the `<head>`. Without it, Apple Mail on iOS may upscale small text and break the intended responsive layout.

**Issue 4 — Scrollbar suppression (minor)**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width:320px) {
  body::-webkit-scrollbar { display: none !important }
}
```
Hides the native scrollbar on all WebKit clients above 320 px wide. This affects webmail renderers (Gmail in Chrome, Apple Mail WebKit) and can disorient users scrolling a long email. Likely a framework artifact; should be scoped out or removed.

**Issue 5 — Emoji in subject line**
`🔥` in the subject renders correctly in modern clients but is stripped or rendered as `[?]` in older enterprise mail gateways (Lotus Notes, some Outlook on-prem). Not blocking, but worth noting if deliverability to corporate domains matters.
