# Essential travel packs from The North Face
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- This is a full-funnel product-showcase email for The North Face travel and everyday packs, sent under the REI umbrella. The hero is confident — "Packs to go farther" with a lifestyle image — but the email quickly becomes a long scroll of pack vignettes ("For however you A to B," "Long-day ready," "Trail to travel," "Travel like a pro") that feel like a catalog page rather than a curated send. The REI Outlet 40% off footer does most of the conversion heavy lifting, but it's buried so far down that most readers never reach it.
- The brand history here matters: after a July wave of aggressive discount emails (up to 60% off, member coupons, brand-stacking), REI has pivoted hard to editorial storytelling. That's a smart cadence break — but the execution is soft. There's no single hero product, no price anchoring above the fold, and the CTAs ("Shop All Travel Backpacks," "Shop Now" repeated four times) blur together. This reads like a brand-awareness send masquerading as a conversion email.
- Judgement: solid creative bones, but too many competing narratives and no price signal until the very bottom. It'll drive browsing, not buying.

## What's working

- Hero lifestyle shot is strong — muted earthy tones, premium outdoor feel, immediately positions TNF packs as aspirational travel gear.
- Section headers ("For however you A to B," "Long-day ready," "Trail to travel") give the scroll a readable editorial rhythm.
- REI Outlet 40% off banner at the bottom is visually distinct and delivers a concrete discount — the only real offer in the email.
- Multiple product colorways shown per section adds useful variety without feeling chaotic.
- Brand authority signals (REI member footer, Opt Outside, 100-year guarantee) reinforce trust.

## What's weak

- No price or discount signal above the fold — a reader who bails after the hero has zero conversion incentive.
- Four near-identical "Shop Now" CTAs with no differentiation — the eye stops reading them as calls to action.
- The REI Outlet 40% off offer is the only concrete deal, but it's placed last, after four scroll-heavy product sections.
- Body copy is small and dense under each product block — unreadable at mobile size, which is where most of this send is consumed.
- Subject line ("Essential travel packs from The North Face") is descriptive but has no offer, urgency, or hook — weak open driver following a month of discount-driven subjects.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the 40% off offer to the hero.** "Up to 40% off The North Face packs — shop REI Outlet" as a sub-headline directly under the hero image. The discount exists; bury it and you waste it.
- 2. **Consolidate to one or two hero products** with specific prices shown (e.g., "Base Camp Voyager Duffel — $89, was $149"). Four product vignettes with no prices is a catalog, not an email.
- 3. **Differentiate CTA copy** per section: "Shop Travel Packs," "Shop Daypacks," "Shop Duffels" — each tied to its section's product type. Generic "Shop Now" repeated is dead weight.
- 4. **Cut body copy under product images by 70%.** One punchy line per product is enough; the current paragraph blocks are skipped.
- 5. Tighten the subject to lead with the offer or a sharper hook.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 40% off The North Face packs at REI Outlet`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The North Face travel packs — built to go farther`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Daypacks, duffels, and carry-ons — shop the REI Outlet sale`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Find your pack for every trip — 40% off at REI Outlet`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`lang="und"` on `<html>` element** — `lang="und"` ("undetermined") tells screen readers and translation engines the language is unknown. For an English-language email this must be `lang="en"`. Evidence: `<html dir="auto" lang="und" ...>`

**`dir="auto"` on `<html>` element** — `dir="auto"` causes bidirectionality to be inferred from content characters. For a left-to-right English email, this should be `dir="ltr"`. Using `auto` can misfire if the first substantive character the parser reads is from a non-Latin encoding (e.g., a product name in a URL or alt text string). Evidence: same tag as above.

**Breakpoint collision risk** — desktop column widths are applied at `min-width:480px` while a second block of display-toggle rules fires at `min-width:768px`. Any screen between 480–767 px receives column percentage widths but not the desktop/mobile visibility class overrides, which could produce layout gaps for tablet clients. Evidence: `@media only screen and (min-width:480px)` vs `@media only screen and (max-width:768px)` / `@media only screen and (min-width:768px)`.
