# A ton of savings ends soon!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-06**
## Executive summary

- This is REI's Labor Day sale winding down under real urgency — a live countdown timer (37:01:15), "thru Sep 7" stamped in the hero, and copy that literally says "Save before summer ends." It's a legitimate deadline-driven send, not the recycled mega-percentage template REI's been leaning on all summer, and it's doing real work stacking three distinct offers: 25% off packs/tents/footwear, up to 50% off footwear clearance, and the members-only extra 20% off one item (LABORDAY26) that first showed up 2026-08-29.
- The problem is there's too much of it. By the time a reader scrolls past the countdown hero, the 25%-off pack/duffel block, the six-tile "check these savings out" grid, the 50%-off footwear callout, AND the members 20%-off block, the single most time-bound offer (the countdown) has been buried under four subsequent offers competing for the same click. This is the "greatest hits" problem the 2026-09-01 "best deals" digest had, just with a genuinely better hook up top.
- Verdict: strong subject/hero combo, weak follow-through — the countdown creates urgency the rest of the email immediately dilutes.

## What's working

- Live countdown timer (37:01:15) directly under a clear "LABOR DAY SALE THRU SEP 7" banner — concrete, credible urgency.
- Hero photo (hikers, mountains, golden light) matches end-of-summer/outdoor positioning and renders cleanly.
- Product-specific tiles (Osprey packs, Gregory gear, MSR tents, La Sportiva footwear) give a real "what would I actually buy" browsing path, not just a generic banner.
- Free standard shipping banner up top adds a second concrete offer with no minimum-order friction called out.
- No render bugs — every tile, button, and price callout displays cleanly with consistent brand red.

## What's weak

- Five distinct offer blocks (shipping, sale hero, packs 25%, tent/footwear grid, footwear 50%, members 20%) stacked vertically with no prioritization — nothing tells the eye which offer matters most.
- The 50%-off footwear clearance section, which is objectively the strongest number in the email, is buried past the fold under three other offers.
- Repeated "SHOP THE SALE" / "SHOP NOW" CTAs point to the same general sale rather than to the specific category just shown, weakening each individual product block's pull.
- Subject "A ton of savings ends soon!" is vague — "a ton" undersells that there's a specific 50% off number and a hard Sep 7 date sitting right in the email.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead the subject and preview with the strongest number in the body — 50% off footwear — instead of vague "a ton of savings," and cut the email down to 2-3 offer blocks max (countdown hero, top category grid, one clearance callout) rather than five.
- 2. Move the 50%-off footwear clearance block up directly under the countdown hero — it's the best offer in the email and it's currently the fifth thing shown.
- 3. Differentiate the CTAs — "Shop Packs & Duffels," "Shop Footwear Deals," "Get 20% Off" — instead of three generic "Shop the Sale" repeats.
- 4. Trim the six-tile "Check these savings out" grid to the top 2-3 movers; a wall of near-identical 25%-off tiles reads as padding once the countdown has already made the case for urgency.

## Technical Audit — REI "A ton of savings ends soon!" Email

**1. Technical Summary**
Email is a standard MJML-generated multi-part HTML/table layout, but automated QA returned a 7% pass rate (24 issues, 4 warnings), driven primarily by link-probe timeouts and a systemic doubled-domain merge error affecting all mobile-deep-link CTAs.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- 11 of 12 probed material links failed with connection timeouts on `mlinks.rei.com` redirect infrastructure (e.g. `https://mlinks.rei.com/z/c/24132/bb812b33ee0e413889f78975e2c73b39/a7e62eb7ae79451c9883740edea7d58e` — "SHOP THE SALE"). This pattern across nearly every CTA (Shop Now ×6, Shop the Sale, hero link, unsubscribe) suggests either the redirector was unreachable at scan time or the probe was rate-limited/blocked, not necessarily that links are broken — but it should be re-verified.
- **Doubled-domain merge error** confirmed in 10 URLs: mobile deep-links via `mobile.rei.com/AkCd/` embed a full `af_r` param and `deep_link_value` param that both duplicate the same path, e.g.:
  `https://mobile.rei.com/AkCd/?af_r=https://www.rei.com/c/mens-clothing/f/scd-deals?cm_mmc=...&deep_link_value=/c/mens-clothing/f/scd-deals?cm_mmc=...`
  The `af_r` and `deep_link_value` query strings are not URL-encoded — they contain raw `?` and `&` characters that collide with the outer query string, meaning `cm_mmc` and downstream params are being parsed incorrectly by the outer URL and likely truncated or misrouted by AppsFlyer's `af_r` handler. This affects every nav/CTA link routed through the AppsFlyer OneLink (`mobile.rei.com/AkCd/`) redirector — footwear, packs-and-bags, outlet-coupon, mens/womens-clothing, Osprey, Gregory links all show the identical malformed pattern.
- Two of the malformed CTA URLs are exact duplicates appearing twice each (`c/packs-and-bags/f/scd-deals...CTA1`, `c/footwear/f/scd-deals...CTA1`, `s/outlet-coupon-eligible-items...CTA1`), indicating duplicate `<a>` tags/link zones pointing to the same destination.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- 8 images missing `alt` text, including tracking pixels (`o.gif` ×4, `eo` beacon) and two content images (`8rATxf4...`, `zvqsalcN5...`). Tracking pixels are low-risk, but the two content images should have descriptive alt text for screen readers and image-blocked clients.
- 2 images served over plain `http://` (`sp.rei.com/q/...`) rather than `https://` — likely to be blocked or flagged by mail clients enforcing mixed-content/TLS policies (Outlook, Gmail proxy).
- One `mi.rei.com` pixel URL has an empty path segment (`/p/cp//o.gif`), appearing twice — malformed tracking pixel URL, likely a missing campaign/segment ID in the merge.
- Extensive MSO/Outlook conditional comments and `mj-column-per-*` responsive classes present — standard MJML output, no structural rendering issues found in the visible source.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
No unresolved merge tokens (e.g. `{{...}}`, `%%...%%`) found in the visible HTML source. The empty-path pixel (`/p/cp//o.gif`) is the one artifact consistent with a failed personalization/segment-ID substitution — flagged under Rendering above.

- Plain-text alternative is 76% raw URLs (11,098/14,636 chars), which is a common spam-filter risk signal and also makes the text version effectively unreadable/unusable as a fallback.

**7. Recommendations**

2. Fix the AppsFlyer OneLink construction: URL-encode the `af_r` and `deep_link_value` parameter values so nested query strings (including `cm_mmc`) don't collide with the outer URL's query string. This is the single highest-impact fix — it affects 10+ CTAs.
3. Deduplicate the repeated identical CTA links (`packs-and-bags`/CTA1, `footwear`/CTA1, `outlet-coupon`/CTA1).
4. Fix the malformed `mi.rei.com/p/cp//o.gif` pixel URL (empty segment ID) — likely losing tracking data for that placement.
5. Add `alt` text to the two content images; switch the two `http://sp.rei.com` image sources to `https://`.
6. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers and SPF/DKIM authentication are actually present on the raw sent message (independent of this relay's capture), given CAN-SPAM and deliverability implications.
7. Trim the plain-text alternative to reduce URL-to-text ratio for spam-filter and accessibility purposes.
