# Our 4th of July Deals have arrived 😎
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- This is REI's official launch email for their 4th of July sale — sent June 26, the day after the teaser "4th of July Deals start TOMORROW!" (scored 8/10 on June 25). The bones are solid: a strong hero with a summer outdoor image, a specific brand deal (20% Off Outdoor Research Clothing), a product grid with 10-30% off categories, and a member cross-sell at the bottom. The visual system is clean and on-brand.
- The problem is context: a subscriber who opened yesterday's teaser already knows the sale launched. This email needs to do more than announce arrival — it needs to reward the click with a differentiating offer or sharper creative, and it doesn't. "Our 4th of July Deals have arrived" is the least interesting possible subject for day-two of a sale campaign.
- The Outdoor Research brand spotlight is the strongest card in this deck. That's a specific, credible deal for an REI audience. Everything else reads like a standard sale grid.

## What's working

- The **Outdoor Research 20% Off** section is a targeted, brand-specific offer that gives the email a real hook beyond the generic "up to 50% off" umbrella.
- **"Up to 50% off" badge** in the hero is immediately visible — no digging required to understand the offer tier.
- **Product category grid** ("Can't-miss deals") with individual discount percentages gives browsers something to scan without committing to a click.
- **Member cross-sell** ("Join the co-op today. Save tomorrow. $30 reward") is well-placed for non-members and doesn't interrupt the main offer flow.
- REI's **visual identity** is consistent — teal accent, outdoor photography, clean layout — exactly what a subscriber expects.

## What's weak

- **Day-two arrival fatigue**: yesterday's teaser already announced the sale; today's email says essentially the same thing. No escalating offer, no new hook.
- **Subject line is flat** — "Our 4th of July Deals have arrived" is a press release headline, not a sales line. It names the event without giving any reason to click.
- **Hero copy is vague** — the fine print under "4th of July Deals" is too small to read in this render, which means the supporting message is lost on mobile.
- **No hard deadline visible** — "4th of July" implies a cutoff, but no specific date or countdown is shown, which undercuts urgency.
- **Category discounts in the grid are inconsistent** (10%, 20%, 25%, 30%) — the range is honest but diffuse. No single hero product or category claims the spotlight.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Outdoor Research deal in the subject** — it's the most specific, actionable offer in the email and it's buried below the fold.
- 2. **Add an explicit deadline** — "Ends July 4" in the hero banner converts the implied urgency into a concrete reason to act now.
- 3. **Cut the hero fine print** — if body copy under the hero headline is unreadable at mobile scale, it's dead weight. Move the key message into a legible subhead.
- 4. **Differentiate this email from the June 25 teaser** — one easy lever: lead with a new category or brand spotlight not featured yesterday.
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% off Outdoor Research + up to 50% off for the 4th`
- **Subject Alt B:** `4th of July sale is live — ends July 4`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus deals on paddleboards, footwear, and more through July 4.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `20% off all Outdoor Research Clothing, now through the 4th.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `lang="und"` on `<html>` element**

`lang="und"` means "undetermined." Screen readers (NVDA, VoiceOver) and translation engines use this attribute to select the correct voice profile and language rules. An English-language email should declare `lang="en"`. This is a confirmed defect in the source.

**No issues found:**

**Minor observation:**

Applied at ≥320px. This suppresses the scrollbar in WebKit-based email clients (e.g., Apple Mail on iOS). Uncommon but not harmful; worth flagging if QA testers report unexpected scroll behavior on long emails.
