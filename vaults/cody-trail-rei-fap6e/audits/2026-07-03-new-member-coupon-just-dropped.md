# NEW member coupon just dropped!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- This is REI Outlet's member-exclusive coupon drop — a deliberate creative pivot after seven straight emails of "X% off" blanket messaging. The $20 off $100+ hero is concrete and the "JUST FOR MEMBERS" banner earns its placement. The personalized picks section below the fold is a smart loyalty signal that most brands in this cadence don't bother with.
- The problem is timing and completeness. This is the eighth email in roughly eight days from the REI/REI Outlet umbrella, and there's no visible deadline on the coupon itself — a direct contrast to the June 29 "4 days only" and July 1 "ends tomorrow" urgency that made those sends work. Without a deadline, "just dropped" is pure hype that should be "expires Sunday" or equivalent. The 4th of July Deals module at the bottom (up to 50% off) undercuts the coupon narrative and makes this feel like two emails stitched together.
- Net judgment: above-average for a day-after-holiday email. The member pivot was the right call. The execution needed a deadline and a tighter single narrative.

## What's working

- **"JUST FOR MEMBERS" banner** immediately frames this as exclusive, which is exactly the right hook after a week of mass-broadcast sale emails.
- **$20 off $100+ is a concrete dollar offer** — cleaner and easier to grasp than a percentage when you're close to the threshold.
- **Personalized picks section** breaks the generic-grid pattern; it signals REI is paying attention to purchase/browse history, which earns goodwill.
- **Multiple discount tiers (30%, 40%, 25%)** give the email legs for recipients not triggered by the hero coupon alone.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds.

## What's weak

- **No expiration date on the coupon** — "just dropped" implies freshness but gives no reason to act now vs. next week.
- **4th of July Deals module at the bottom** dilutes the member-exclusivity narrative; it looks like a standard promo append rather than a coherent email.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is generic** — on a dollar-off coupon, "Use My $20" or "Claim My Coupon" would drive far higher intent.
- **Email length** — five distinct content modules after the hero is too much; the personalized picks and the 25%-off MSR gear section are competing for attention below the fold.
- **Heavy cadence cost** — this is the 8th email in 8 days from the REI umbrella; subscriber fatigue is real, and the lack of urgency makes skipping easy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an expiration date to the hero** — "Your $20 coupon expires July 6" converts "nice to have" into act-now. Every prior email in this series (June 29, July 1) used deadline language; dropping it here breaks the pattern that was working.
- 2. **Rewrite the primary CTA** from "SHOP NOW" → "Claim My $20" or "Use My Coupon" — ties the button directly to the member benefit.
- 3. **Cut or move the 4th of July Deals module** — if you must keep it, put it after the member offer section with a clear visual separator labeled "Also ending this weekend" so it reads as additive, not contradictory.
- 4. **Trim to three modules max**: hero coupon → personalized picks → one clearance grid. The MSR rental gear section at 25% off is the weakest and should go.
- 5. **Swap "NEW" all-caps in subject** — it reads as a spam signal; trust the concept without the shout.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your member coupon: $20 off $100+ at REI Outlet`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Coupon inside: $20 off your next REI Outlet order`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Expires July 6 — grab $20 off $100 or more, just for Co-op members.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Members only: spend $100, save $20. Here's your code.`
