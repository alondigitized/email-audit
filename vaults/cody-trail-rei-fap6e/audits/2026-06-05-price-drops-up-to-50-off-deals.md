# Price drops—up to 50% off deals
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- This is a solid, no-frills clearance email that leans on "UP TO 50% OFF" three times in a single scroll. The bones are right: strong hero, recognizable brand logo placement (Patagonia, Arc'teryx, Columbia in the second panel), member benefits in the footer. It does the fundamentals without embarrassing itself.
- The problem is creative repetition. REI runs the identical template three times — red box, "UP TO 50% OFF," a generic sub-headline, "SHOP THE DEALS" button — and differentiates sections only by a vague label ("Past-season styles," "Past-season jackets"). After the May 29 REI Outlet "Up to 50% off" drop, this is the second "50% off" email in a week under the REI umbrella. A subscriber notices.
- The email is competent but forgettable. Nothing pulls you toward a specific click; everything points at the same general sale destination.

## What's working

- Brand logo cluster (Patagonia, Arc'teryx, Columbia, REI) in the second hero panel is the single most persuasive element — name-drops that convert for gear enthusiasts.
- Top nav bar (Men's Deals / Women's Deals / All Deals) lets readers self-select without scrolling.
- All product images render cleanly — no broken images, no blank boxes.
- Footer member benefits (1-year returns, 5% Mastercard rewards) reinforce value beyond the headline discount.
- Subject line at 32 characters is clean and mobile-safe.

## What's weak

- Three near-identical "red box + 50% off + SHOP THE DEALS" units make the email feel like copy-paste rather than a curated edit.
- Product thumbnails are small and generic — no specific item, no original vs. sale price callout, nothing to anchor desire.
- Zero urgency with a deadline: "Hurry to save big" in the jackets section is the closest thing to a time signal and it's hollow.
- No personalization — nothing tells you this was selected for outdoor/gear buyers vs. the entire email list.
- The three separate CTAs ("SHOP THE DEALS" ×3) likely route to the same or nearly the same destination, making the differentiation meaningless.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse to one "50% off" hero, then differentiate.** Three red boxes dilute each other. One strong opener, then distinct category lanes (jackets, footwear, layers) with separate CTAs.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline to at least one section.** "Ends Sunday" or a countdown (one that actually runs, unlike the zeroed-out timer from the May 24 send) would convert hesitating buyers.
- 3. **Show price.** Original vs. sale pricing on even one hero product ("$349 → $174") is the single highest-lift change available.
- 4. **Swap generic CTAs for category-specific ones.** "Shop Patagonia Sale" or "Shop Sale Jackets" beats three identical "SHOP THE DEALS" labels.
- 5. **Lead the preheader with brand names.** "Patagonia, Arc'teryx, Columbia — past-season gear, now up to 50% off" is a harder-working sentence than whatever is loading behind the hero.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Arc'teryx, Patagonia, Columbia—up to 50% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Past-season price drops you'll actually want`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Patagonia, Arc'teryx, Columbia and more — marked down hard, while supplies last.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Jackets, layers, footwear from top brands — past-season, deeply discounted.`
