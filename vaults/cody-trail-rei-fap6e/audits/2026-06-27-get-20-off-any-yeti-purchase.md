# Get 20% off any YETI purchase 🧊
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- This is REI's fourth 4th of July email in three days — and the smart play here is differentiating with a brand spotlight rather than another generic "4th of July Deals" blast. Leading with "Members get 20% off any full-price YETI purchase" is the right call: YETI almost never goes on sale, so 20% off for members is a genuine, defensible deal that lands differently than the clearance-percentage language that's saturated this series.
- The problem is the email can't commit to the bit. Below the YETI hero sits a full 4th of July deal grid, a "UP TO 50% OFF Clearance" block, a "25% Off" apparel section, and a summer adventure gear shelf — four separate messages competing with the one that earned the open. YETI gets the subject line and the hero, then the email immediately pivots to everything else REI is running right now.
- Verdict: the YETI angle is the right differentiation move at this point in a fatigued series, but the email body doesn't respect that decision. It reads like the 4th of July template with a YETI hero swapped in, not a genuinely YETI-curated send.

## What's working

- **YETI member deal is a real hook.** 20% off any full-price YETI is the kind of offer REI regulars know is uncommon — it earns a click for that reason alone.
- **Hero clarity.** The cooler image, the "Members get 20% off" lockup, and the Shop Now button are the first thing you see; hierarchy works.
- **Subject differentiates from the series.** After two days of "4th of July" subject lines, naming YETI specifically cuts through the inbox noise from the same sender.
- **Member loyalty is front and center.** The co-op angle is always REI's strongest weapon; surfacing it in the hero headline is correct.

## What's weak

- **The email contradicts itself.** The subject promises a YETI story; the body delivers a full-catalog promo. The clearance and 25%-off blocks undercut the premium YETI positioning.
- **"Full-price" qualifier is load-bearing but undersized.** It's visible, but a YETI fan who clicks expecting 20% off a sale item will bounce; this deserves more visual emphasis.
- **No explicit deadline on the YETI offer.** The 4th of July context implies a window, but the hero copy gives no closing date — and YETI deals are rare enough that a "through July 4th" line would close significantly more orders.
- **Cadence is stressed.** This is the fourth REI email in three days. Even a strong offer like YETI 20% off is fighting subscriber fatigue from the June 26 "Big savings for bigger summer adventures" and "Our 4th of July Deals have arrived" same-day double-send.
- **"Shop Now" CTA is doing too much generic lifting.** On a YETI-branded send, "Shop YETI" or "Get 20% off" would reinforce the hero promise at the button level.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to the YETI story top-to-bottom.** Cut the clearance block and the 25%-off section from this email — send those in a separate blast. A focused YETI email with 6-8 product tiles (coolers, drinkware, bags, soft coolers) would convert at a higher rate than a catch-all promo with a YETI hat on.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the hero.** "Through July 4th" or "This weekend only" in the hero subhead would push procrastinators to act now instead of coming back and forgetting.
- 3. **Upgrade the CTA copy.** Change "Shop Now" → "Shop YETI" on the hero button. It costs nothing and reinforces the offer at the click moment.
- 4. **Surface the full-price qualifier more boldly.** Put it right under the discount lockup in a contrasting color — something like "On all full-price YETI gear" — so no one is surprised post-click.
- 5. **Rest tomorrow.** After four emails in three days, a June 28 skip day would let the July 3 urgency email land harder.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Members: 20% off YETI — this weekend only 🧊`
- **Subject Alt B:** `YETI coolers, drinkware, bags — 20% off for members`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `YETI rarely goes on sale. Your co-op membership changes that through July 4th.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Full-price YETI, 20% off. Stock up before the deal ends.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `lang="und"` on `<html>` element:**
```html
<html dir="auto" lang="und" ...>
```
`und` (undetermined) is the IETF tag for unknown language. For an English email this should be `lang="en"`. Screen readers use this attribute to select the correct voice profile; `und` causes fallback behavior that varies by client.

**Issue — `dir="auto"` instead of `dir="ltr"`:**
`dir="auto"` derives text direction from content heuristics. For a fixed-language English email, `dir="ltr"` is explicit and safer — `auto` can misfire if the parser hits a non-Latin character early (e.g., the 🧊 emoji in the subject line leaking into preheader text).

**Issue — Conflicting breakpoint cascade:**
Two separate `@media` blocks target `max-width: 768px` and `min-width: 480px`. At viewport widths between 480–768px both blocks are active simultaneously. The 768px block hides elements with `.mh` (`display:none`) while the 480px block restores column widths — this ordering creates a specificity race that varies by client CSS engine. Gmail App (Android) and Apple Mail handle this differently.

**Issue — Scrollbar suppression:**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width:320px){
  body::-webkit-scrollbar{display:none!important}
```
This applies inside an email context (webview or browser-rendered preview). Hiding the scrollbar is atypical and can disorient users viewing the email in a browser or webmail client. Has no effect in native email clients but is harmless there.

**Cannot assess:** `alt` text on images — source truncated before `<img>` elements.
