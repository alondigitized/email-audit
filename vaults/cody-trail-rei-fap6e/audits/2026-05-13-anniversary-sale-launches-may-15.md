# Anniversary Sale launches May 15 👀
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- This is a pre-sale hype email for REI's Anniversary Sale, launching May 15 — and the countdown timer is the smart centerpiece. With roughly 1 day and 20 hours left on the clock, the hero has genuine urgency baked in, and the member bonus card offer (appear to be a $100 instrument) gives both existing members a reason to prep their carts and non-members a concrete incentive to convert before the sale starts. For REI's audience this is familiar territory executed cleanly.
- The problem is that REI stuffed a full brand newsletter into what should be a tight event teaser. By the time you scroll past the four-step member enrollment walkthrough, a gift card module, a co-op rewards reminder, and a hotel brand called RUTECAMP ("Where Boutique Hotel Meets Nature Escapes"), the countdown urgency has entirely evaporated. Each module is individually defensible; together they murder focus.
- The sale launches in two days, the member bonus card is the lever — this email should be five seconds of scroll, not five distinct editorial sections. Strip everything that isn't "what's coming May 15 and why you should be a member to access it."

## What's working

- Countdown timer is the right hero: a live "1:20:54" clock creates tangible, specific urgency that static sale copy never achieves.
- Member Bonus Card offer is concrete and explained — the four numbered steps make the redemption path feel simple rather than conditional.
- Dual-CTA structure (MEMBER EXCLUSIVE / BECOME A MEMBER) correctly addresses both member and non-member segments in one email.
- Free shipping banner at the top sets expectation immediately — no buried shipping threshold later.
- Render is clean: no broken images, no layout breaks, strong outdoor photography throughout.

## What's weak

- The RUTECAMP hotel partnership module is completely misplaced — it undercuts the urgency the countdown built and asks the reader to context-switch from "buy outdoor gear" to "book a hotel."
- Gift card section is mid-funnel content dropped into a top-of-funnel pre-sale send; it splits cognitive load at the wrong moment.
- Zero product teasers — a pre-sale email is the ideal format to surface 3-4 hero products dropping May 15 and seed desire before the cart opens.
- "MEMBER EXCLUSIVE" CTA is a label, not an action; it doesn't tell me what I'm clicking through to do.
- Email length is excessive for a pre-sale announcement — by module five, the reader who would have clicked has already left.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut RUTECAMP and the gift card sections entirely.** Save both for a standalone send during the sale window when urgency isn't the primary asset.
- 2. **Add 3–4 product teasers below the countdown** — hero items that drop on May 15, linked to category pages. Pre-sale desire is the most underused lever in event emails.
- 3. **Rewrite the primary CTA** from "MEMBER EXCLUSIVE" to something active: "Preview Member Deals" or "See What's Coming Thursday."
- 4. **Collapse the four-step walkthrough** into a single bold line: "Members: your $100 bonus card activates at checkout on May 15."
- 5. **Add a calendar/reminder micro-CTA** ("Add to Calendar") for members who are warm but not ready to browse — a zero-friction conversion that earns the open.
- **Subject Alt A:** `REI Anniversary starts Thursday — members go first`
- **Subject Alt B:** `2 days. Your $100 member bonus card is waiting.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Member bonus card + free shipping unlocks May 15. Here's how it works.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The sale opens Thursday. See which drops to watch.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`lang="und"` — confirmed defect.**
```html
<html dir="auto" lang="und" ...>
```
`und` means "undetermined." Screen readers (NVDA, VoiceOver) and email clients that use `lang` for hyphenation/text-to-speech will either fall back to system language or misread content. Should be `lang="en"` (or `en-US`).

**`dir="auto"` — minor risk.**
Acceptable but `dir="ltr"` is more explicit and avoids bidirectional heuristics that can misfire in mixed-content contexts (e.g., product names, prices).

**Fractional column widths — layout fragility risk.**
Three columns use irrational percentages:
```css
.mj-column-per-23-11046511627907 { width: 23.11046511627907% }
.mj-column-per-26-16279069767442 { width: 26.16279069767442% }
.mj-column-per-27-61627906976744 { width: 27.61627906976744% }
```
These sum to ~100% but floating-point rounding in older Outlook/WebKit renderers can cause a 1 px overflow that wraps the last column. This is a known MJML artifact — no fix needed unless column-wrap is observed in QA screenshots.

**MSO pixel density declared — correctly.**
```xml
<o:PixelsPerInch>96</o:PixelsPerInch>
```
Correct value; no issue.

**Webkit scrollbar suppressed globally at 320 px.**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width:320px) {
  body::-webkit-scrollbar { display: none !important }
```
This hides the browser scrollbar on essentially all mobile viewports. Unusual for an email; has no rendering impact inside mail clients but could cause confusion if the email is previewed in a WebView.
