# Selected sale offers end tomorrow!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- This is the deadline-closer in REI's 4th of July campaign — a "time is running out" send on July 5th, with a countdown timer showing just over an hour left. Structurally, it's a kitchen-sink email: four distinct offer blocks stacked below the timer hero (Columbia, a kayak, REI member $20 coupon, and an REI Outlet block at the bottom). That breadth is both the email's strength and its weakness — there's something for almost any REI subscriber, but no single offer owns the email.
- The member coupon block is the most actionable unit here, and it echoes the July 3 member coupon drop (scored 8/10 in this series) almost verbatim. Subscribers who acted on July 3 will feel déjà vu; those who didn't may finally convert under deadline pressure. That's a defensible re-send strategy, but the visual hierarchy buries the coupon below two other offers.
- The countdown timer is doing the heaviest lifting. Without it, this email is a weak reprise of content already sent multiple times this week. With it, there's at least one reason to feel urgency. The subject line is vague — "Selected sale offers end tomorrow" adds almost nothing over what was already sent June 29 and July 1 — and the overall cadence (this is at least the 8th email from REI/REI Outlet in ~10 days) is beginning to degrade the brand's signal-to-noise ratio.

## What's working

- **Countdown timer is prominent and immediately visible** — `1:09:14` in large digits anchors the urgency in a way no copy line could match.
- **Columbia 25% off block is clean** — brand-name + specific discount percentage is a legible, skimmable offer unit.
- **REI member coupon block carries over the clearest value prop** — "$20 off $100+" with the "MEMBERS GET" callout is concrete and repeatable.
- **REI Outlet "Save $20" footer ties the two brands together** neatly and gives a second conversion path for deal-seekers.
- **No visible render bugs** — images load, text doesn't overlap, layout holds on what appears to be a mobile-optimized width.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is generic and stale** — "Selected sale offers end tomorrow" is identical in structure to the July 1 "ends tomorrow" send; a subscriber who's seen 8 emails this week has no reason to open this one over any prior send.
- **Visual hierarchy is unfocused** — four offer blocks compete equally; the eye has no obvious first stop after the timer.
- **Kayak/outdoor gear block feels disconnected** — a single product image sandwiched between Columbia and the member coupon with no clear price or discount stated visibly.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is approximately the 8th REI/REI Outlet email in 10 days; the member coupon in this send is a near-duplicate of the July 3 coupon drop, which will frustrate members who already redeemed it.
- **Hero copy "Time's running out" is generic** — says nothing about what specifically is ending; a subscriber has to read deeper to understand what sale this covers.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the subject with the specific offer, not the clock.** "Time's running out" and "ends tomorrow" are the same vague urgency every retailer uses. Name the product category or the discount tier to earn the open.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Columbia 25% off ends tonight — plus your $20 member coupon`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last hours: 4th of July sale + $20 off for members`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Countdown is live. Grab Columbia, kayaks, and REI Outlet deals before midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your $20 member coupon expires with the sale — use it now.`
- 2. **Promote the member coupon to the first offer block.** It's the most differentiated unit (member-exclusive, concrete dollar value) and the most likely to drive a click from the engaged segment. The Columbia block is generic enough to be second.
- 3. **Give the kayak/outdoor block a visible price or discount.** If a product image has no stated discount in the render, it reads as filler. Either add a discount badge or cut it and tighten the email to three blocks.
- 4. **Reconsider the REI + REI Outlet dual-brand footer stacking.** Two separate brand footers at the bottom fragments the CTA. If Outlet already had its own dedicated email this week (it did — July 1 scored 8/10), this block is redundant.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `lang="und"` (undetermined):**
```html
<html dir="auto" lang="und" ...>
```
`lang="und"` tells screen readers and translation engines the language is unknown. For an English-language email, this should be `lang="en"`. Screen readers like JAWS and NVDA use this attribute to select voice profile; "und" falls back to OS default, which may mispronounce content.

**Issue — `dir="auto"` combined with `lang="und"`:**
`dir="auto"` instructs the client to infer text direction from content. With no declared language, some clients (notably Gmail on Android) may trigger RTL detection on ambiguous characters. Recommend `dir="ltr"` for a left-to-right-only English email.

**Issue — `p,a{margin:0!important}` applied globally at 320px+:**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width:320px){p,a{margin:0!important}}
```
This zero-margin override fires on virtually every device. If any paragraph-level spacing relies on default `<p>` margins, it will collapse. Verify intentional.

**No issues found:** Outlook compatibility block (AllowPNG, PixelsPerInch=96), `<meta charset="utf-8">`, viewport meta, `img` reset styles, and `border-collapse` table resets are all correctly implemented.
