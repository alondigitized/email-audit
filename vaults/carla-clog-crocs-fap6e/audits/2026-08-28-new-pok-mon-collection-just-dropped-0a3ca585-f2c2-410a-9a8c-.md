# New Pokémon collection just dropped
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-28**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean licensed-collaboration launch — Pokémon x Crocs, a Poké Ball Classic Clog with four enamel Jibbitz charms (Pikachu, Eevee, Psyduck implied plus more shown in the charm grid) and a Poké Ball graphic print on the strap. It's a genuine creative swing, in the same vein as the NFL (8/27), LEGO NINJAGO (8/26), and Shoe Tattoos (8/24) launches — Crocs is clearly leaning hard into licensed collab drops this month, which is a smart cadence break from the "2 for $50" discount grind that dominated most of August.
- Visually it's well-built: hero shot with the Poké Ball graphic and Pikachu charm clearly visible, then a detail-callout section (charm zoom, graphic print zoom) that does real production storytelling, then a charm-variety grid teasing more characters. The problem is there's no offer anywhere — no price, no launch-day incentive, nothing but "just dropped." For a fandom this big, that's a missed opportunity; Pokémon collab shoppers convert on novelty and scarcity, and this email doesn't lean into either explicitly (no "limited edition," no price, no "while supplies last").
- Verdict: strong creative, weak conversion mechanics. This will get opens on subject/brand recognition and Pokémon-fan curiosity, but the click path to purchase-intent is softer than it should be — no price anchor, no urgency, and CTA copy is repeated three times without variation.

## What's working

- Poké Ball graphic print and Pikachu/Eevee charms are immediately legible in the hero — strong nostalgia/fandom hook at first glance.
- Product-education layout (product shot → charm zoom callout → strap graphic callout) clearly explains what's new about the style, which matters for a $60+ novelty clog.
- Charm-variety grid (three more characters in colored tiles) teases collectibility without cluttering the hero.
- Clean creative break from the discount-bundle cadence Crocs has been running — consistent with the brand's recent pattern of licensed launches (LEGO NINJAGO 8/26, NFL 8/27).
- Copyright line ("©2026 Pokémon/Nintendo/Creatures/GAME FREAK") signals this is a real licensed product, not a knockoff — builds trust.

## What's weak

- No price or offer anywhere in the email — a launch-worthy collab like this should anchor a price or at least tease "starting at $X."
- No urgency or scarcity signal ("limited release," "while supplies last") despite Pokémon collabs historically selling out fast — a real lever left unused.
- "Shop Pokémon" CTA copy is repeated verbatim twice; missed chance to vary CTA language or push to a specific product page vs. a collection.
- Subject line "New Pokémon collection just dropped" is generic — doesn't call out the Jibbitz charms or Poké Ball design detail, which is the actual differentiator.
- Free shipping banner at top is generic evergreen copy, not tied to this launch — feels like a template default rather than a considered pairing.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a price or "starting at $XX" line near the hero CTA — collab drops without a price anchor lose purchase-intent clicks even when curiosity is high.
- 2. Add scarcity language ("Limited-edition release" or "While supplies last") near the hero — Pokémon collabs are inherently drop-culture; use that.
- 3. Vary the two "Shop Pokémon" CTAs — make the second one product-specific, e.g. "Shop Poké Ball Clog," to reduce redundancy and sharpen intent.
- 4. Tease the additional charm characters by name in copy (the grid shows three more silhouettes but doesn't name them) — naming builds collector desire.
