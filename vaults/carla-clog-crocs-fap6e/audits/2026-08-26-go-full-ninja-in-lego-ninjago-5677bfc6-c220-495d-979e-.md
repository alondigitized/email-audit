# Go Full Ninja in LEGO® NINJAGO®
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-26**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuine LEGO® NINJAGO® collaboration launch — a licensed kids' clog featuring Lloyd's masked face detail and lightning sole graphics, plus a matching Jibbitz charm pack. It's a clean creative swing that breaks from the discount-mechanic grind Crocs has been running (8/22, 8/23's "2 for $50"), and it lands in the same lane as the recent product-drop cadence (Café Slipper 8/19, Metallic Buckle Saturday Sandal 8/21, Shoe Tattoos 8/24). The green neon-streak hero art is genuinely eye-catching and on-brand for a kids' collab.
- The problem is targeting, not execution: there's no discrete offer beyond the boilerplate free-shipping banner, and this is a kids'-only product with no adult crossover — for a general subscriber, or anyone without a kid in the NINJAGO age bracket, there's nothing here to click. Multiple sections (Powered to Perform, Feel the Energy, Ninja Unite) essentially repeat the same "shop the clog" message three times with progressively less new information, which drags the email longer than the offer merits.

## What's working

- Strong, cohesive hero visual — neon green streaks, LEGO NINJAGO branding, and product shot are well-composed and immediately readable.
- Product storytelling callouts (Lloyd's masked face, lightning sole detail) give a concrete reason to care about the design, not just generic "new drop" copy.
- Clear CTA hierarchy — "Shop LEGO® NINJAGO®" repeated as a consistent, high-contrast pill button after each section.
- Footer navigation (Shop Clogs / Jibbitz / Sale / Sandals) gives an easy out for non-NINJAGO shoppers.
- No render bugs — clean layout, no overlapping text or broken images.

## What's weak

- No offer at all beyond the standard free-shipping banner — nothing time-bound, no price, no incentive to act now versus later.
- Three near-identical "shop the clog" sections (Powered to Perform / Feel the Energy / Ninja Unite) repeat the same CTA with diminishing new content — feels padded.
- This is a kids'-exclusive product with zero signal for adult or general shoppers — no crossover styling, no "shop the adult line too" nudge.
- Subject line doesn't convey it's a collab/licensed product drop — "Go Full Ninja" reads vague without the LEGO NINJAGO context that only appears once opened.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email from three "shop the clog" sections down to two — merge "Powered to Perform" and "Feel the Energy" into one hero block, since they say the same thing.
- 2. Add a real incentive — even a modest kids'-line discount or bundle price (clog + Jibbitz pack together) would give the click a reason beyond curiosity.
- 3. Put "LEGO® NINJAGO®" in the subject line itself — the license is the whole hook and it's currently buried.
- 4. Add a one-line "for ages X+" or size-range callout since this is a kids' product and parents need that fast.
