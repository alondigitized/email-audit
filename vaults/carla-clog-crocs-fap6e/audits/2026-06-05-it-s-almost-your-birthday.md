# It’s almost your birthday 🥳
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- This is a triggered birthday email, and the mechanics are solid: subject, hero headline, and offer block all point at the same occasion. The "$15 off your next purchase of $50 or more" is a real, specific discount — not a vague "savings event" — and the promo code is clearly displayed with copy instructions. Clean render, no bugs.
- The problem is the bottom two-thirds of the email. After the birthday offer block, the email reverts entirely to a generic catalog send: trending styles grid, lookbook CTA, Afterpay module, category navigation footer. None of it carries any birthday framing. The emotional warmth evaporates immediately after the first scroll. Compared to Crocs' June 4 styling guide ("Need help styling your Crocs?"), which sustained its editorial tone throughout, this one abandons its premise halfway down.
- The birthday occasion is one of the highest-intent triggers in CRM. This email earns a strong open, a mediocre click, and a wasted opportunity to build loyalty.

## What's working

- Birthday hook is executed with alignment — subject, hero, and offer all stay on the same occasion.
- "$15 off $50+" is specific and useful; the promo code is prominently displayed with clear redemption instructions.
- Free shipping banner top-of-email adds a clean secondary value prop without competing with the hero.
- No render issues — images load, layout holds, no overlapping text.
- Subject line at ~29 characters is short, punchy, and mobile-friendly.

## What's weak

- Everything below the offer block is generic catalog content with zero birthday framing — the personalization stops at the fold.
- The primary CTA is "Shop Now" — exactly the same as every non-birthday Crocs email; a wasted opportunity to use copy like "Claim Your Birthday Offer."
- No expiration date on the offer — "almost your birthday" implies urgency but the email never closes the loop with a concrete deadline.
- Hero image is festive (balloons, colorful outfit) but shows no Crocs product — the celebration visual doesn't connect to what you'd actually be buying.
- No social proof or reviews anywhere; the June 4 "Your next favorite color just dropped" send used a quoted review with measurable effect.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the primary CTA** from "Shop Now" to "Claim Your Birthday Offer" — the single change with the highest immediate lift.
- 2. **Add an expiration date** to the offer block: "Valid through [birthday + 3 days]." Creates credible, bounded urgency without feeling pushy.
- 3. **Replace or re-frame the trending styles grid** — drop the generic catalog section or add a "birthday picks for you" headline above it to maintain the personalized frame.
- 4. **Show a Crocs product in the hero** — put a clog or slide front and center in the birthday scene so the celebration visual is also a product visual.
- 5. **Add a warm closing line** before the footer — something in Crocs' voice, like "Make it a memorable one. (Ideally in Crocs.)"

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header not detected.** The AgentMail relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. This is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policies (enforced February 2024) for commercial email. If the header is present upstream but stripped by the relay, that is a relay configuration issue. If absent entirely, Crocs is out of compliance.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) not detected.** One-click unsubscribe (the machine-readable POST mechanism) is missing or not relayed. Required for Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender compliance alongside the `List-Unsubscribe` header.

**Authentication-Results header not found.** SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unverifiable through the current relay. The sending domain `crocs-email.com` should be DKIM-signed; inability to confirm this is a relay gap, not necessarily a Crocs-side failure — but it must be verified.

No physical address check was possible from the truncated source, but CAN-SPAM requires a valid postal address in the body.
