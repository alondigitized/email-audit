# Extra 30% off clearance is wrapping up!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- This is Crocs' last-call execution on the Extra 30% Off Clearance run that dominated July 1–4, took a one-day creative break on July 5 (the "Comfort That's Built for Men" pivot), and comes back July 6 with a deadline angle: "wrapping up." The hero copy — "ONLY HOURS TO SAVE!" — is the most urgent language of the entire clearance series, which is the right instinct. The two-module structure (clearance + 40% accessories cross-sell) is clean, and the Refer-a-Friend module below adds genuine value above the fold of the social/footer section.
- The problem is that "only hours" without a specific timestamp is promotional vapor. Every subscriber who has opened a Crocs email this week has already seen this mechanic four times. The single change that would make this send convert — "ends tonight at midnight" or "ends July 6 at 11:59 PM" — is conspicuously absent. The clock is ticking but the clock face is blank.
- Verdict: technically competent last-call email with the right urgency instinct but the wrong execution. Adding a hard deadline is the only thing standing between this and a genuinely strong send.

## What's working

- "ONLY HOURS TO SAVE!" is the sharpest urgency language in the clearance run — a real upgrade over the passive "Croc yeah—" energy from earlier in the week
- Two-module layout (hero clearance + accessories cross-sell) is scannable; accessories at 40% off is a distinct offer, not just a repackage
- Subject at 39 characters is clean and mobile-safe with a natural "ending" hook ("wrapping up!")
- "Shop the Sale" button sits high in the visual hierarchy — no buried CTA
- Refer-a-Friend (Give 25%, Get 25%) is a smart brand-building add below the primary offer
- No specific deadline anywhere in the email — "only hours" without a time is impossible to act on with conviction; subscribers have been burned by this language before
- Same white Classic Clog product image used across multiple sends — zero creative refresh signals that this is a final-call moment
- "FREE SHIPPING ON QUALIFIED ORDERS" as the preheader wastes the inbox opportunity to amplify the urgency message
- "Shop the Sale" CTA copy is the same generic label used throughout the series; nothing anchors the click to a specific category or action
- No personalization or segmentation hint — this reads as a full-list blast, which compounds cadence fatigue

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline in the hero** — "ONLY HOURS TO SAVE! Sale ends tonight at midnight" turns vague urgency into an actual trigger. This is the single highest-impact change.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA** — "Shop Clearance Before It's Gone" or "Grab Clearance Styles" — anything that makes the click feel like it resolves the urgency, not just navigates to a page.
- 3. **Set a real preheader** — push the deadline into the preheader so inbox-level urgency is front-loaded before the open.
- 4. **Swap the product shot** — a multi-product clearance rack image or a "Last Chance" overlay on the shoe would visually distinguish this from the prior four sends.
- 5. **Surface a specific clearance count or discount floor** — "Over 200 styles, Extra 30% off — ends tonight" converts better than an unbounded clearance offer.

## Technical Audit — Crocs "Extra 30% Off Clearance" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound with proper MSO/VML conditionals, responsive media queries, and preheader suppression implemented correctly. Primary concerns are HTTP image sources in the footer, missing authentication headers via the AgentMail relay, and absent one-click unsubscribe headers required under CAN-SPAM/RFC 8058.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**32 tracking links skipped by QA probe** — all click-redirect URLs route through a tracking domain (likely `click.crocs-email.com` or similar). This is expected behavior, but destination URLs could not be validated for broken links or UTM integrity. Manual spot-check of 2–3 destination URLs is recommended before send.

No raw broken links detected in the probed set.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources — 7 images affected:**

All footer images (App Store badge, Google Play badge, Find Your Store, Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, YouTube) are served over `http://`:

```
src: http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/9c36c0ef-...png
src: http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/13246762-...png
src: http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/c4df71ec-...png
src: http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/e18949ff-...png
src: http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/80f68cd8-...png
src: http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/51d8144e-...png
src: http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/1187ef58-...png
```

Mail clients that enforce mixed-content blocking (Apple Mail with "Protect Mail Activity", some corporate Exchange/Outlook configurations) will block these images silently. Swap all 7 to `https://`.

**Missing alt text — 1 pixel/tracking image:**

```
src: https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&c_emailopened=Yes&...
```

This Adobe Audience Manager open-tracking pixel has no `alt=""`. While a 1×1 pixel doesn't need descriptive alt text, the attribute should be present as `alt=""` to satisfy accessibility validators and avoid screen readers announcing a broken image.

**Custom font fallback** — `TT Crocs` is loaded via `@font-face` from `media.crocs.com`. Clients that block remote CSS (Gmail Web, some Outlook versions) will fall through to system fonts. No fallback stack is declared in the `@font-face` rules. This is a display degradation risk, not a breakage.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens detected in the truncated HTML source. Preheader text ("Hours left to save on this amazing sale. Shop now.") is hardcoded — no dynamic personalization tokens present in visible sections.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** via AgentMail relay. This header is required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders (≥5k/day) and strongly expected by major ISPs. If the header exists at the ESP layer but is stripped by the AgentMail relay, this is a relay capture gap — not necessarily a send-side defect. Confirm header presence in raw SMTP headers from a live send, not the relay-captured copy.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not detected** (RFC 8058). Without this, Gmail's one-click unsubscribe button in the UI will not function. One-click unsubscribe has been required by Gmail/Yahoo since February 2024 for bulk senders.

Physical mailing address and unsubscribe link presence could not be confirmed from the truncated HTML, but these are standard CAN-SPAM requirements — verify they appear in the email footer.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM validation blocked** — all 32 links route through a click-tracking redirect and were not probed. Cannot confirm UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are present or consistent on destination URLs. Spot-check recommended.

**Adobe Audience Manager pixel present** (`demandwarecrocs.demdex.net`) — confirms open-event tracking is wired. No issues with the pixel URL structure itself beyond the missing `alt=""` noted above.
