# You forgot your 15% OFF Welcome Offer! 🛍️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- This is a behavioral-trigger re-engagement email — Crocs firing an "offer expiry reminder" for the 15% first-purchase welcome discount that was originally introduced in the May 20th catalog send. The email is structurally solid: free shipping banner, strong hero, product grid, social proof, loyalty upsell, and BNPL — all in the right order.
- The single biggest problem is timing, and it is severe. Crocs sent "50% off" Memorial Day sale emails on May 20th and again on May 22nd. Nudging the same subscriber to redeem a 15% welcome discount the very next day — while the 50% sale is presumably still active — makes no strategic sense. Any subscriber who noticed the 50% offer will ignore or feel actively misled by a 15% "save it before it's gone" reminder. The behavioral trigger was never suppressed for a live promotional window.
- The email itself, stripped of that context problem, is a competent welcome offer with no major render flaws. But contextually, it is the wrong email sent at the worst possible moment.

## What's working

- **Hero is immediate**: "15% Off" is large and legible without scrolling; the black CTA button contrasts well against the lifestyle image.
- **Product grid diversity**: The "Redeem Your Offer Now" section shows Classic Clogs across a range of colorways — reduces choice paralysis, increases the odds something lands.
- **Crocs Club placement**: Loyalty membership section is correctly sequenced mid-funnel; it gives a value-add to a subscriber who still hasn't converted.
- **Afterpay section reduces price friction**: BNPL visible near the bottom is a clean reason-to-buy for anyone hesitating.
- **Star-rating social proof**: The "Our Best Selling Classic" 5-star block adds credibility right before the charm upsell.

## What's weak

- **15% offer competing with 50% Memorial Day sale**: This is the fatal context failure. Two consecutive "up to 50% off" emails make 15% feel like a demotion, not a perk.
- **No visible expiry date**: "You forgot" implies urgency but nowhere in the render does the offer state when it expires — the urgency is manufactured, not credible.
- **"Shop Your Style" CTA is vague**: Verb + noun, but the noun is generic. For a 15% welcome offer, "Claim Your 15% Off" or "Shop Now — 15% Off" lands harder.
- **Email length is excessive**: Six distinct sections (hero, product grid, club, preferences, social proof, charms, Afterpay, app) dilutes focus from the primary conversion goal — redeeming the welcome offer.
- **"Put Down Your Preferences" section**: The survey/preference module mid-email is a distraction inside a conversion-focused send; it belongs in an onboarding email, not an offer reminder.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress this trigger during the 50% Memorial Day window.** The automation fired at the worst possible time. The fix is a suppression rule: don't send the 15% welcome re-engagement if a site-wide promo (≥20% off) is live. This is the only change that actually matters strategically.
- 2. **Add an explicit expiry date to the hero.** "Expires Sunday midnight" beats "You forgot" as a conversion driver. Make the deadline visible in the first screen.
- 3. **Rewrite the CTA from "Shop Your Style" to "Claim Your 15% Off."** The offer is the point — put it in the button.
- 4. **Cut the email to three sections max**: hero + product grid + social proof, with one clean CTA. Remove the preferences survey, the app download, and the Afterpay block (the BNPL can live on the cart page).
- 5. **Acknowledge the Memorial Day sale tension**: If the 50% sale is still live and the welcome 15% has stacked rules, say so. If they're mutually exclusive, state it honestly so subscribers trust the brand.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 15% off expires at midnight — don't miss it`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Still thinking it over? Your welcome offer ends soon`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your first-purchase discount is still waiting — expires Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Grab 15% off before your welcome offer disappears for good.`

### 5. Compliance

**Two List-Unsubscribe header gaps (high priority):**

- `List-Unsubscribe` header not found — required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders (>5k/day) as of February 2024.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found — required for RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe compliance. Gmail actively demotes senders missing this.

Both gaps are attributed to the AgentMail relay not surfacing or forwarding these headers. Crocs' sending infrastructure (Salesforce Marketing Cloud / Demdex stack) almost certainly sets these upstream — confirm whether the relay is stripping them before attributing the issue to Crocs.

**Authentication-Results** header not found — SPF/DKIM pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from the captured copy. Same relay-stripping caveat applies.

Physical address and CAN-SPAM footer not visible in the truncated source — assume present but should be verified against the full HTML.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All links are wrapped in `click.crocs-email.com` redirects with opaque query strings (base64/encrypted), so UTM parameters — if appended — are not inspectable from the source. The QA probe skipped these links. **Continuity cannot be confirmed or denied without following the redirect chain** to verify UTM params (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) land on crocs.com pages.

The `campaignID=us_x_trigger_welcome-15peroff_reminder` in the Demdex pixel matches the email's trigger logic (welcome series, 15% offer, reminder send). Internally consistent.
