# ⏳ Limited-Time Offer: 30% Off + Free Shipping
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-02**
## Executive summary

- This is a plain, loud sitewide-sale email. The yellow hero puts "Take 30% Off AND Get Free Shipping!" in heavy black type, so the offer is clear in under a second. The "Shop the Sale" pill button sits right under it. Nothing is broken, and everything above the fold works.
- The creative is thin. The black Baya clogs are the same shoe Crocs used in the 9/26 "Flash Sale: 30% Off Baya Clogs" and the 9/27 "30% Off Baya Clog - Ends Tonight!" sends. The same 30% is back for the third time in a week, now as a sitewide "limited-time" offer with no new reason to act. "Hurry, this offer won't last!" gives no date. The real deadline, Wednesday Oct 7 at 11:59pm MT, is only in the fine print at the bottom.
- My verdict is that it's competent and forgettable. It would convert on price alone. A subscriber who saw the Baya sale emails earlier this week gets no new reason to click.

## What's working

- The headline puts both benefits, 30% off and free shipping, in one line of large type.
- The hierarchy is clean: eyebrow, headline, one-line body, black pill CTA, then the sale image.
- The "Shop the Sale" button has strong contrast against the yellow and is easy to find.
- The yellow-to-orange gradient with the "SALE" ring is eye-catching and says "discount" without needing to be read.
- The black "Free Shipping on Qualified Orders" bar at the top reinforces the offer before the logo.

## What's weak

- "Hurry, this offer won't last!" is vague urgency. The Oct 7 deadline is buried in the fine print.
- "Exclusions Apply" sits right under the CTA, and the footer says "select styles only." The offer reads broader than it is, and a click could end in disappointment.
- The hero image is two black Baya clogs with no model, no lifestyle context and no other styles. It doesn't show what is on sale.
- The "SALE" lettering ring partly disappears behind the clog. It's busy and cuts into the product.
- The 30% Baya offer ran on 9/26 and 9/27. Running it again with no new angle feels like discount fatigue.
- The referral banner, app badges and store locator below the hero add clutter that competes with the single sale CTA.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the real deadline in the hero, for example "Ends Oct 7," so the urgency is credible and visible. Replace "Hurry, this offer won't last!" with that.
- 2. Replace the single-shoe image with a grid of 3–4 sale styles, each linked to its product page. Use at least one lifestyle shot, so the email shows what is actually discounted.
- 3. Move the exclusions into one short line next to the offer, such as "30% off select styles." That is more honest and sets the right expectation.
- 4. Link the CTA to a specific collection, such as "Shop 30% Off Clogs," instead of the generic sale page.
- 5. Move the referral, app and store-locator blocks below a stronger product section, or cut them for sale sends.
- 6. Give this send a reason to exist, either a segment or a new angle such as "Last chance," so it doesn't repeat the Baya sale emails.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Crocs are 30% Off (Shipping's Free Too)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Clogs, sandals and slippers included. Select styles, ends Oct 7 at 11:59pm MT.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free shipping on qualified orders is stacked on top. Shop before the sale ends.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible source is a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (ExactTarget) build with a preheader, dark-mode and Outlook hacks, and an open pixel. It has 7 insecure `http://` image sources and a malformed tag, and the compliance and authentication checks are inconclusive because of the relay and the truncated HTML.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Skipped probes:** 20 tracking links on click-redirect domains were skipped, so destination status and redirect chains are unverified. This is a coverage gap, not a confirmed defect.
- **Malformed markup:** the open pixel is closed with a stray `</custom>`: `<img src="https://click.crocs-email.com/open.aspx?IZWU6A7S2P2ULAS5UKI3AITSZI.10200&d=10200&bmt=0" width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>`. This is invalid HTML. Parsers will generally ignore it, but it is sloppy template output.
- **Unescaped ampersands:** the open-pixel `src` contains raw `&` characters. Browsers tolerate this.
- **Third-party pixel:** there is a Demdex (Adobe Audience Manager) pixel at `https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&c_emailopened=Yes&d_dpuid=1c110d5f7d8fbc329b0a8ea915c05b3c631d53c`. It adds a second tracking domain, and the `d_dpuid` value looks truncated or malformed.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Insecure images:** seven images load over `http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/`:
  - App Store: `9c36c0ef-…png`
  - Google Play: `13246762-…png`
  - Find Your Store: `c4df71ec-…png`
  - Instagram: `e18949ff-…png`
  - Facebook: `80f68cd8-…png`
  - TikTok: `51d8144e-…png`
  - YouTube: `1187ef58-…png`

  These can be blocked or flagged as mixed content in some clients. They are all footer or utility images, so they are low severity. The same host should serve HTTPS.
- **Missing alt text:** the Demdex `event` image has no alt attribute. It is a tracking pixel, so `alt=""` with `width="1" height="1"` would be correct, and the impact is minimal.
- **Alt text present:** the open pixel has `alt=""`, and the images listed above all have alt text.
- **Responsive and client handling:** `viewport`, `format-detection`, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, an MSO conditional block and `@media (max-width: 639px)` rules are all present.
- **Title:** the `<title>` is just "Crocs".

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found in the visible HTML. No unresolved tokens such as `%%…%%` or `{{…}}` appear. The source is truncated, so the body was not checked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post:** both were not found. The QA tool says the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is unconfirmed rather than a verified violation. Without these headers, Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements (RFC 8058 one-click) would not be met. Check the raw headers from the original delivery.
- **Authentication-Results:** it was not found, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC are unknown. The sending domain `crocs-email.com` matches the tracking domains (`click.` and `image.`), which is consistent with aligned branded sending, but this is not verified.
- **Footer unsubscribe link and postal address:** both are outside the truncated HTML, so they could not be verified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Cannot be assessed. The visible HTML contains no destination hrefs, and all 20 links are wrapped in `click.crocs-email.com` redirects that were not resolved. UTM parameters and landing-page alignment with the "30% Off + Free Shipping" offer are unverified.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Switch the seven `http://image.crocs-email.com/...` images to `https://`.
2. Remove the stray `</custom>` after the open-pixel `<img>`.
3. Add `alt=""` to the Demdex pixel, and confirm the `d_dpuid` value isn't truncated.
4. Verify `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results` from the raw message before treating them as defects. If AgentMail strips them, capture headers another way, such as a direct SMTP test inbox.
5. Raise the HTML truncation limit, or store the full source, so footer compliance, merge tokens and hrefs can be audited.
6. Resolve the 20 click-tracking redirects with a follow-redirect probe to check final URLs, UTMs and landing-page status.
