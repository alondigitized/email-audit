# Ending soon! Up to 50% off!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- This is the urgency-close chapter of Crocs' Memorial Day campaign — "ENDING SOON," "ending tonight," a yellow hero, and the now-familiar "Up to 50% off Select Styles" hook. Executional alignment is solid: the subject, hero badge, and body copy all reinforce the same message. The visual hierarchy is clean, "Shop the Sale" is unambiguous, and the three-offer stack (main sale + Jibbitz deals + Buy 2 Take 30%) gives the email density without confusion.
- The problem isn't this email in isolation — it's what's happened around it. The brand already fired a nearly identical "Ending soon! Up to 50% off!" subject today (the `-479e961d` artifact in the vault), and this one appears to be a near-duplicate second send. Combined with "Last chance for the comfort you'll live in." also landing today (scored 8/10), Crocs has now sent **three emails on the same day** around the same terminal urgency beat. That cadence destroys open rates on the second and third sends regardless of creative quality.
- The email itself is a competent urgency close. The send strategy is the problem.

## What's working

- **Hero urgency is tightly aligned**: "ENDING SOON" badge, "ending tonight" body copy, and the subject all say the same thing — no mixed signals.
- **Three distinct offers in one scroll**: main sale, $3 Jibbitz singles, and the Buy 2 / Take 30% stacking deal give different entry points for different buyer intents.
- **"Shop the Sale" CTA is clean**: one button, one action, no competing links fighting for attention in the hero.
- **Free shipping banner leads above-the-fold**: visible context before the offer even loads.
- **Refer-a-Friend placement is smart**: anchored at the bottom as a low-pressure retention play after the deals have done their job.

## What's weak

- **Duplicate send penalty**: this subject — "Ending soon! Up to 50% off!" — already shipped today. A second send of near-identical creative kills deliverability reputation and trains the list to ignore future urgency signals.
- **"Select Styles" buried in the hero subhead**: the asterisk-level caveat appears under the main headline but above the CTA — subscribers who click expecting 50% off everything and find fewer eligible items will bounce.
- **Three CTAs fracture attention**: "Shop the Sale," "Shop These Deals," and "Refer a Friend" each pull in a different direction; the Jibbitz and BOGO sections dilute the urgency-close thesis.
- **Zero personalization**: no first name, no "your size," no category hint — this reads as a batch blast at exactly the moment subscribers are most fatigued by identical Crocs mail.
- **No visual countdown or hard deadline beyond "tonight"**: "ending tonight" is credible but vague — a clock widget or an explicit time (e.g., "midnight ET") would convert fence-sitters.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the second send entirely.** If this is a list re-engagement on non-openers, suppress the openers and update the subject — do not blast the full list twice with the same subject on the same day. This is the single highest-impact change.
- 2. **If the re-send is unavoidable, give it a hard deadline**: body copy says "ending tonight" but no clock, no timezone, no hour. Lock it: "Sale ends at midnight ET — here's what's still in stock."
- 3. **Pick one offer and own it**: the Jibbitz deal and Buy 2 / Take 30% are good, but placing them below the hero on a urgency-close email splits the click. Lead the whole email with one CTA; move accessory offers to a secondary column or drop them entirely on this send.
- 4. **Swap "Select Styles" for the category caveat inline**: "Up to 50% off shoes, sandals & clogs" sets honest expectations before the click without hiding the limitation.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Tonight only: 50% off ends at midnight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last hours — 50% off your size`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your cart is waiting. Sale closes at 11:59 PM ET — don't miss it.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `These styles are still in your size. Sale ends tonight.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility


This Adobe Audience Manager open-tracking beacon has no `alt` attribute. Technically low-risk for a 1×1 pixel, but will trigger accessibility linters.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

### 5. Compliance

**Issue — List-Unsubscribe header not captured**
The AgentMail relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. This header is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policies (enforced since February 2024 for senders >5k/day). If the header exists upstream but is stripped by the relay, it needs to be verified at the ESP level. If it is genuinely absent, this is a deliverability and compliance risk.

**Issue — List-Unsubscribe-Post absent (RFC 8058)**
No `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` header detected. Gmail and Yahoo both require one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders. Without this, Gmail may render its own unsubscribe prompt that bypasses the sender's preference center, and Yahoo may apply reputation penalties.

**Note:** Both header warnings are flagged as potentially relay-capture artifacts. Verify raw headers at the ESP (not via AgentMail) before treating as confirmed absent.

**No issues found** with physical address presence or unsubscribe link in body (assumed present in full HTML; not contradicted by truncated source).
