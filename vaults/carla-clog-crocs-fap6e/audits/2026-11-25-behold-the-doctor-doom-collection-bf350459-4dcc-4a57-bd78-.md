# Behold. The Doctor Doom collection.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-25**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, single-purpose product drop. It's a Marvel x Crocs Doctor Doom collab, sent in the middle of Black Friday week. The hero is strong: two armored green-and-silver clogs on a dark textured backdrop, with the Avengers: Doomsday logo up top. The eye lands on the shoes first, which is right.
- It also breaks the pattern. The last six audited sends (Nov 21–24) were stacked-discount Black Friday blasts: "Up to 60% off + an extra 25% off?" and "Your cart deserves a little treat." This one has no percentage, no code and no deadline. For a fan or collector it's a welcome change from the promo noise. For a deal-driven subscriber it gives no reason to act today. The only incentive is a "Free shipping on qualified orders" banner, and the fine print undercuts it with "economy shipping is applied."

## What's working

- The hero image is striking. The armored clogs are shot dramatically, and the Marvel Studios and Avengers: Doomsday lockup signals a licensed collab immediately.
- There is one message and one CTA. "Shop Doctor Doom" is a solid black pill with clear verb-plus-noun copy.
- The headline "Source of Supremacy" has personality. It fits the Doom theme and the Crocs voice.
- The layout is simple and uncluttered, with a clear hierarchy from logo to hero to headline to button.
- The free-shipping banner sits at the top, so some value is visible even without a discount.

## What's weak

- There is no price, no discount and no deadline. In the middle of Black Friday week, nothing in the email says why to buy now.
- The body copy is generic. "Celebrate the legacy of Doctor Doom's reign in this unique armored style" gives no price, sizing, availability or stock-limit detail.
- Only one product style is linked, and the second clog in the hero isn't called out. There is also no social proof or reviews.
- The footer is heavy for a short email. Refer a Friend, Contact Us and Store Locator come before the category buttons, and the four category buttons (Clogs, Jibbitz, Sale, Sandals) pull attention away from the single CTA.
- The shipping banner is vague. "Qualified orders" with no threshold, and the fine print says economy shipping, so the benefit reads as unclear.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a scarcity or timing hook, such as "Limited run" or "Drops today", and a price. Collab drops sell on exclusivity, and this email has neither.
- 2. Name the two styles in the hero and give each its own CTA or tap target. That would raise product-level click intent.
- 3. Say whether the Black Friday promo applies. If it doesn't, say "Not part of the sale, but worth it" to set expectations. If it does, show the discounted price.
- 4. Cut the footer link block above the fold of the footer, or move the category buttons up to replace it. Put the "Shop Sale" button first, since it's the strongest draw this week.
- 5. Make the shipping banner specific, for example "Free shipping over $X", or drop it.

## Technical Audit: Crocs, "Behold. The Doctor Doom collection."

### 1. Technical Summary
The source is a standard ESP-built (Salesforce Marketing Cloud / ExactTarget) template with MSO conditionals, responsive CSS and a preheader. It has one confirmed markup defect, a stray `</custom>` tag. The QA run reported 0 hard issues and 3 warnings, all header-related and unverified. The HTML was truncated, so footer, unsubscribe and link-level checks are incomplete.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Malformed tag after the open pixel (confirmed).** The ExactTarget pixel is `<img src="https://click.crocs-email.com/open.aspx?qs=…&d=10212&bmt=0" width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>`. `</custom>` has no opening tag. Browsers ignore it, but strict parsers and some clients may not.
- **Demdex pixel with no alt (QA WARN).** `https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&c_emailopened=Yes&d_dpuid=1c110d5f…` is an Adobe Audience Manager pixel. Add `alt=""` to it.
- **Per-recipient ID in the pixel URL.** `d_dpuid=…` is a hardcoded identifier. It's expected for this kind of pixel, but it sends a recipient-linked ID to a third party on open.
- **Click tracking not verified.** QA skipped 16 tracking links on the click-redirect domain, so destinations and status codes are unchecked. Redirect health is unknown.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Present and correct:**
  - `lang`/`xml:lang="en"`

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` not found (WARN).** QA notes the header may not be captured by the AgentMail relay. This is unconfirmed, not a verified defect.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` not found (WARN).** The RFC 8058 one-click header may be missing for the same reason. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require it, so check the raw message from the ESP.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found (WARN).** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown through the relay. The From domain, `crocs-email.com`, matches the click and tracking domain, which is consistent with aligned sending, but I can't confirm that.
