# Don't miss out on your special birthday offer!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Crocs' third birthday-sequence email in 13 days — a "party's almost over" expiry nudge following the June 5 teaser and the June 12 actual birthday send. That sequencing logic is sound. Birthday triggered → actual-day drop → last-chance reminder is a standard three-part lifecycle play. The problem is that it landed on the same day as the "Extra 25% off makes it easy 😎" blast (June 18), meaning a subscriber who opened their inbox today got two Crocs promotional emails with competing offers. The birthday $15/$50 is actually a better deal (30% equivalent at min spend) than the 25%-off sale, but the collision undermines both.
- Inside the email, execution is clean. The hero headline "The Party is Almost Over... But we still have a Birthday treat" does the expiry framing correctly. The offer is specific and the promo code is displayed front-and-center. Trending styles and category grid give real browsing scaffolding below the fold. No render bugs.
- The core miss: zero deadline specificity. "The Party is Almost Over" is pure FOMO vibes but provides no anchor date — which is exactly what you need in a last-chance reminder. Without a visible expiry, the urgency feels cosmetic.

## What's working

- **Hero copy nails the expiry frame.** "The Party is Almost Over..." is better birthday-sequence writing than most brands produce — it acknowledges the occasion without re-celebrating it.
- **Offer is prominent and unambiguous.** "$15 off your next purchase of $50 or more" with the full promo code displayed above the fold is exactly what a birthday reminder should do.
- **Trending styles section adds real browse value.** Named products (Classic Clog, Women's Saturday Slide, Women's Metallic Buckle Saturday Sandal, Be Happy Chain) give the subscriber a reason to scroll beyond the hero offer.
- **Visual hierarchy works.** Banner → logo → hero → offer → CTA → products — the eye follows the funnel naturally.

## What's weak

- **No expiration date is visible anywhere.** "Almost Over" with no date is empty urgency — last-chance emails need a hard deadline to convert.
- **Second email on June 18 creates offer confusion.** The 25%-off sale email went out the same day; a subscriber comparing the two now has to do math to understand which is better.
- **The promo code requires manual entry.** "Enter code in cart at Crocs.com" is friction — a click-to-copy or auto-apply link would reduce drop-off at checkout.
- **"Shop Now" is repeated four times.** The Trending Styles CTA, Lookbook CTA, and category grid all compete with the primary offer CTA — too many equal-weight calls to action.
- **Hero image is birthday-party-themed, not shoe-product-led.** Three people in colorful dresses is the vibe but it's 200px of prime real estate that shows no actual Crocs product.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a visible deadline to the hero block.** "Offer expires [day], [date]" immediately below the promo code would convert this from vague urgency to credible urgency. That's the single highest-lift change.
- 2. **Suppress the sale email on birthday-sequence days or bundle them.** Sending two promotional emails in one day hurts both opens and brand perception. Either hold the sale blast 24 hours or combine the birthday offer into the sale creative with a "plus, your birthday bonus" module.
- 3. **Replace hero party image with a product hero.** The birthday occasion has already been established across two prior emails — now lead with the shoe people might actually buy.
- 4. **Make promo code one-click.** Replace "enter code in cart" with a pre-applied link or at minimum a copy-to-clipboard interaction.
- 5. **Consolidate CTAs.** One primary "Shop with Your Birthday Code →" button; the category grid can stay but the body CTA count should drop to one.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your birthday deal ends [Day] — $15 off inside`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last chance: $15 birthday treat, code inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `$15 off any $50+ order — don't let it expire unused`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Code: HB1FFN… expires [Day]. Here's what to grab.`
