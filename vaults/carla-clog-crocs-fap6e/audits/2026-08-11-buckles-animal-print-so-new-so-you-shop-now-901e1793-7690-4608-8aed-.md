# Buckles? Animal Print? So New. So You. Shop Now.

## Executive summary

- This is a genuine two-product launch send — the Classic Buckle Ballet in bold red hero position, then the Classic Animal Ballet (leopard print) as the secondary story — and it's the cleanest, most confident creative Crocs has run all week. No discount stacking, no "extra 25% off" gimmick; just new silhouette, new print, clear CTAs. - The layout does the work: bold red hero with diagonal callout copy ("bold shade. bolder buckle. same icon.") lands the eye immediately, followed by a clean product-benefit callout treatment (annotated leopard-print shoe with "Ballet flat inspired design" / "Made with Iconic Crocs Comfort™"). Shop Now." is a run-on of four separate thoughts crammed into one line, and it buries the actual news (a new silhouette, Mary Jane/ballet flat style) behind vague adjectives. Solid execution, mediocre subject line — worth a rewrite before the next send in this series.

## What's working

- Bold, high-contrast red hero with the shoe shot large and centered — impossible to miss on open.
- Diagonal handwritten-style copy ("bold shade. bolder buckle. same icon.") is distinctive and on-brand playful.
- Annotated product-benefit graphic (ballet flat design + Crocs Comfort callouts) is a smart way to explain a new silhouette visually.
- Two clear, distinct CTAs ("Shop Buckle Ballet," "Shop Classic Ballet") tied directly to the two products shown.
- No discount-stacking fatigue — a real product story after several promo-heavy sends this week.

## What's weak

- Subject line stacks four fragments (Buckles? Animal Print? So New. So You. Shop Now.) with no single clear hook — reads cluttered, not curious.
- "new" badge on the hero is a small pill in the bottom right that's easy to miss against the busy red background.
- No price, sizing, or availability info visible anywhere in the body — pure top-of-funnel awareness with no purchase-decision detail.
- No social proof or reviews for what is a brand-new silhouette, which is exactly where shoppers would want reassurance.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split the subject into one clear headline instead of four fragments — lead with the actual news (new silhouette) rather than generic adjectives.
- 2. Add a starting price or "$XX" under at least one hero CTA — new-launch emails with zero price anchor lose conversion-minded clickers.
- 3. Enlarge or reposition the "new" badge so it's not lost against the red background — top-left corner or larger scale would read faster.
- 4. Add a one-line review/rating snippet under the leopard print product ("4.8★ — comfort you know, now in animal print") since it's leaning on the familiar-comfort story anyway.
