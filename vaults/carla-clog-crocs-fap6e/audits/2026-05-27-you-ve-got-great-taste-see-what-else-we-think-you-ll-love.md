# You’ve got great taste...see what else we think you’ll love
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- This is a post-sale "recommendation" email dressed up with flattery — the subject promises personalization ("great taste...we think you'll love") but the body delivers a generic Classic Clog catalog: feature callouts, a 15% welcome offer (already sent twice in the last five days), four clog variants in a grid, Jibbitz personalization, and two editorial style sections. There's no actual recommendation algorithm signal visible — no "based on your browse history" or product-specific logic. The flattery is hollow.
- That said, this is the smartest editorial reset Crocs has attempted since the Memorial Day blast cycle. After five consecutive discount hammers and a pivot "Matching set" send on 5/26, this email backs away from urgency-driven promo and tries to rebuild brand relationship. The execution is competent — clean render, clear CTAs, decent product photography — but the content doesn't back up the subject line's personalization promise.
- The 15% welcome offer reappearing here (also the centerpiece of the 5/23 re-engagement send) confirms this is still targeting unconverted first-time subscribers. That's a valid behavioral trigger, but burying the offer in section two — below a full feature-callout hero — means the converting hook lands after the fold on mobile.

## What's working

- Clean, unbroken render throughout — no layout bugs, no broken image gaps, no lorem ipsum.
- Feature callout design around the Classic Clog hero (ventilation, easy clean, color range, comfort) gives new-to-brand subscribers real product information.
- "Personalize Your Classics" section with the colorful outdoor Jibbitz lifestyle shot is the strongest creative unit — high visual energy, differentiating message.
- "Shop Classic Clogs" CTA is specific and actionable — verb + noun, not "Discover."
- The two editorial style sections ("Laid Back Street Style," "Double Denim Dreams") add value without screaming discount, appropriate for the post-sale cooldown moment.

## What's weak

- Subject promises personalization; body delivers a generic catalog — no "because you viewed X" or category signal visible anywhere.
- The 15% offer is buried in section two, not the hero — a converting subscriber has to scroll past a product-feature block to reach it.
- All four clog variants in the grid are shown in black, against a white background — visually monotonous and undersells the color variety that the hero callout claims ("4000+ Colors").
- "Store Locator" CTA in the Laid Back Street Style section is a weak, low-intent call to action for an email subscriber — sends them off the conversion path entirely.
- No urgency mechanism anywhere — the 15% offer has no expiry shown, which deflates any reason to click now vs. later.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Honor the subject line promise** — add a visible "Picked for you" or "Because you browsed Classics" hook in the hero section. Even one personalized product tile would make the "great taste" framing land.
- 2. **Move the 15% offer to the hero** — the welcome discount is the strongest conversion lever in the email; it should be visible without scrolling, ideally replacing the feature-callout section as the primary CTA.
- 3. **Add color to the product grid** — show the Classic Clog, Bayaband, and Kids' Clog in their most popular colorways, not all-black. The copy promises 4000+ colors; the grid contradicts it.
- 4. **Kill the Store Locator CTA** — replace with "Shop The Look" or a direct product link consistent with the email's conversion goal.
- 5. **Add an offer deadline** — even "offer valid through Sunday" would lift urgency without changing the discount mechanics.

### 7. Recommendations

1. **Fix HTTP image sources (priority: high)** — Replace all 7 `http://image.crocs-email.com/...` `src` values with `https://`. Same host, protocol swap only. These will render broken in clients that enforce mixed-content blocking.

2. **Add alt text to product recommendation images (priority: high)** — iGoDigital slots `i/1` through `i/4` should carry descriptive alt text (product name + color at minimum). Set `alt=""` on the Demdex pixel explicitly.

3. **Verify List-Unsubscribe headers at MTA (priority: high)** — Capture a raw header dump from a live Gmail/Outlook inbox to confirm whether `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` are present at send time. If missing at the MTA, add them to the SFMC send configuration. Required for Gmail bulk-sender compliance (Google's February 2024 enforcement).

4. **Verify SPF/DKIM via direct header capture (priority: medium)** — Confirm `Authentication-Results` shows `dkim=pass` and `spf=pass` from a live inbox. `email@crocs-email.com` sending domain should align with the `d=` tag in the DKIM signature.

5. **Add redirect-following to QA link probe (priority: medium)** — Resolve `click.crocs-email.com` redirects to inspect final destination URLs for UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` at minimum) and correct landing page.

6. **Audit iGoDigital fallback behavior (priority: low)** — Confirm what renders in recommendation image slots if the iGoDigital endpoint returns no results for the persona. Static fallback product images or a collapsed layout should be defined in the template.

7. **Plain-text body URL ratio (priority: low)** — Plain-text alternative is 81% URLs (10,075 / 12,400 chars). Spam filters weight this negatively. Trim tracking-redirect URLs from the plain-text version or use shortened human-readable URLs there.
