# Spotted: The Dylan Platform Slide.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-08**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuine product-launch email — the Dylan Platform Slide in a new cheetah-print colorway — and it's the strongest creative Crocs has sent this week. "She's on the prowl" is a real hook tied to the leopard pattern, the hero image is a clean, single-product shot with no clutter, and there's no discount language dragging it into the same bundle-percentage well as 9/4, 9/1, and 9/7. It sits alongside 9/6's Echo Element and 9/2's Realtree Boat Shoe as evidence the brand can do product storytelling when it wants to.
- That said, it's a straightforward "new drop" email with no urgency, no price anchor, and no social proof — there's genuinely nothing pulling a fence-sitter toward the click beyond "this looks cool." The "Recommended for You" block undercuts the launch by immediately surfacing four generic black clogs that have nothing to do with the cheetah slide just introduced. Solid, not exceptional — a clean single-product send that could convert harder with a size/fit or availability cue.

## What's working

- Hero copy ("she's on the prowl") ties directly to the leopard-print product, a real creative concept rather than a generic banner.
- Single hero product shot, no overlapping offers or competing CTAs at the top — unlike 9/4 and 9/7's stacked-discount layouts.
- "Spotted Here First" framing signals novelty/exclusivity for a new colorway launch.
- Primary CTA ("Shop Dylan Platform") is specific and sits right under the product description.
- No render bugs — clean layout, all images and buttons rendered properly (a contrast to 9/5's overlapping-text issue).

## What's weak

- No price shown anywhere for the featured product — a shopper can't gauge affordability before clicking.
- "Recommended for You" immediately below the hero pushes four unrelated black/tan clogs, diluting focus right after the launch pitch.
- No urgency or scarcity cue ("get 'em while you can" is vague, not a real deadline) despite copy implying limited availability.
- No social proof (reviews, ratings, "sold out risk") to build confidence in a brand-new colorway.
- Afterpay block and four-button nav ("Shop Clogs," "Shop Sale," etc.) crowd the email with competing paths that dilute the single-product focus.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a price under the product name in the hero section — shoppers won't click through blind on a launch item.
- 2. Cut or move the "Recommended for You" block below the fold, past a second CTA — right now it competes with the hero product instead of supporting it.
- 3. Replace vague urgency ("get 'em while you can") with a real signal — "Limited cheetah-print run" or restock/limited-quantity framing, if true.
- 4. Add one social-proof line under the CTA — star rating or "our #1 requested colorway" if available.
- 5. Trim the bottom nav row — four buttons plus Afterpay plus refer-a-friend is a lot of competing asks for what should be a focused single-product send.
