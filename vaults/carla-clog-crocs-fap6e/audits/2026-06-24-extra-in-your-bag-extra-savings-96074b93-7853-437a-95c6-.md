# Extra in your bag = extra savings!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- This send lands 24 hours after Crocs' June 23 email that ran the identical 20%/30% tiered-pairs mechanic. The "Cue the Fireworks" July 4th wrapper and the new 40% Off Accessories section are genuine creative additions — but subscribers who opened yesterday get the same hero offer with fresh wallpaper. The multi-section layout (tiered pairs → work styles strip → accessories → referral) is ambitious and technically clean; the problem is prioritization, not production.
- The subject "Extra in your bag = extra savings!" is a pun that only resolves once you're inside the email (more pairs = higher discount tier). At inbox level it's opaque and low-urgency — it tells you nothing about the fresh accessories offer buried below the fold, which is the single most compelling thing in this send.
- Verdict: Good bones, wrong hierarchy. Burying the freshest, deepest offer (40% accessories) below the fold while replaying yesterday's hero is a structural mistake a reorganized layout could fix next send.

## What's working

- "Cue the Fireworks" seasonal hook is timely and on-brand — July 4th is ~10 days out, giving this a legitimate calendar peg the June 23 send lacked
- 40% Off Accessories is a new, deeper discount not seen in recent Crocs sends; the red section is visually distinct and the CTA ("Grab them now") is specific
- Refer-a-Friend module ("Give 25%, Get 25%") adds utility value and differentiates this from a straight promo blast
- Render is clean throughout — no broken images, no layout breaks, no overlapping text

## What's weak

- Hero offer (20%/30% tiered pairs) is an exact repeat of June 23 — one day later, same mechanic, higher fatigue
- Subject "Extra in your bag = extra savings!" is too punny to resolve at inbox — the deal is better than the teaser
- The freshest offer (40% off accessories) is below the fold; it should be leading or co-leading
- "Exclusions Apply" appears twice with no specificity — "Select Styles" is a trust tax on both CTAs with no payoff
- No urgency signal anywhere: no deadline, no countdown, no "ends July 4" — the fireworks theme is decoration, not motivation

## Recommendations

- 1. **Promote accessories to co-hero or hero.** The 40% off accessories offer is genuinely new and deeper. Either lead with a dual headline ("20-30% on Pairs + 40% on Accessories — Fireworks Sale") or swap it into the top position.
- 2. **Add a deadline.** "Offer ends July 4" ties the seasonal hook to actual urgency. Right now the fireworks motif is cosmetic.
- 3. **Rewrite the subject to reveal the deal:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Fireworks sale: 40% off accessories + buy-more savings`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Buy more, save more — plus 40% off accessories`
- 4. **Set an explicit preheader that complements, not repeats:**
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New: 40% off accessories + 20-30% off pairs through July 4`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The more you buy, the more you save — accessories too`
- 5. **Cut or elevate the yellow work-styles strip.** "25% OFF SELECT WORK STYLES!" sits between two fully-fleshed sections but has no CTA of its own — it reads as an afterthought. Either build it out into its own module or cut it.

## 1. Overview

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected.** The QA relay did not capture this header. If absent at the MTA level, this violates Gmail and Yahoo's bulk-sender requirements (enforced since Feb 2024 for senders > 5k/day). Requires confirmation at the ESP level — may be a relay capture gap rather than a true absence.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected.** One-click unsubscribe will not function in supporting clients (Gmail, Apple Mail). Same caveat as above — verify at the ESP.

**`Authentication-Results` header not captured.** SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown via the relay. If SPF/DKIM are failing, this email is at high risk of landing in spam or being rejected outright by Gmail/Yahoo under current bulk-sender policy.

Physical address and unsubscribe link are assumed present per CAN-SPAM (not visible in truncated HTML but standard for Crocs sends — verify in full source).
