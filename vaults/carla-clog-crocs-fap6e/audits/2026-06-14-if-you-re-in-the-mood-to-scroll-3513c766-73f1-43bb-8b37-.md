# if you’re in the mood to scroll…
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- This is Crocs running the exact same Extra 25% Off sale that went out yesterday (June 13, "Extra 25% off styles you'll actually want to wear," 7/10). The hero copy, offer mechanics, and CTA are nearly identical — stacked savings on already-marked-down sale styles. The creative here adds a Jibbitz deal ($2 single charms, 17-packs) and a refer-a-friend callout as filler sections beneath the hero, but none of that changes the core read: this is a day-two re-push of a sale with a deliberately vague subject line designed to look casual.
- The subject "if you're in the mood to scroll…" is a transparency dodge. It's a lazy conversational hook used when you know the offer isn't new — you're hoping to slip into the inbox without triggering "I just got this" fatigue. It mostly fails: it earns neither curiosity nor clicks, and anyone who opened yesterday's send will recognize the offer immediately upon opening.
- The body is cleaner than yesterday's neon-heavy execution. The Jibbitz $2 deal is a genuine value-add that wasn't prominent in the June 13 send — that's the single strongest differentiation signal here. But Crocs buried it below the fold instead of leading with it, which is the wrong call.

## What's working

- **Clear offer in the hero**: "Extra 25% Off" on styles already up to 50% off is legible and unambiguous, with "Shop the Sale" CTA above the fold.
- **Jibbitz deals section is a real differentiator**: $2 single charms and 17-pack deals give this send something yesterday's didn't — it should have been the hook, not the afterthought.
- **Refer-a-friend module is smartly placed**: Give 25%, Get 25% Off is a solid mid-funnel retention play, and surfacing it mid-email (not buried in footer) is the right instinct.
- **Clean layout, no render bugs**: The visual hierarchy is readable; no broken images, overlapping text, or layout issues visible.

## What's weak

- **Subject line signals nothing**: "if you're in the mood to scroll…" has zero offer, zero urgency, zero specificity — a subscriber who skims their inbox has no reason to open.
- **Day-two cadence with identical offer**: Sent 24 hours after an Extra 25% Off email with near-identical creative; the overlap will register as noise for anyone who opened June 13's send.
- **Jibbitz deal is buried**: The strongest differentiating offer in the email is below the fold, not in the subject, not in the hero — it's wasted positioning.
- **No deadline visible**: Neither the stacked sale nor the Jibbitz pricing has a visible end date, which removes any urgency to act now vs. tomorrow.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Jibbitz deal** — that's the actual news here. The Extra 25% Off is yesterday's story; $2 charms is today's. Flip the hero or at minimum split-test it.
- 2. **Put an expiry in the hero**: "Ends Sunday" or even "This weekend only" alongside the discount would lift urgency without changing the offer.
- 3. **Rewrite the subject to carry the Jibbitz angle** — something with a specific price point outperforms a vague scroll-bait hook every time.
- 4. **Collapse the app download section**: It's dead weight on a promotional send. Use that real estate for a second Jibbitz product shot or social proof.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** — Not detected by the AgentMail relay capture. CAN-SPAM and Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (active since Feb 2024) mandate this header for commercial email. If absent at the MTA level (not just missing from relay capture), this is a deliverability and compliance risk.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058)** — One-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo for >5K/day senders) is not confirmed present. If the ESP is not injecting this at send time, bulk deliverability to Gmail and Yahoo will be impacted.

**`Authentication-Results` header not captured** — SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unknown through the AgentMail relay. Cannot confirm DMARC alignment. This is a relay capture gap, not necessarily an authentication failure, but it should be verified against raw headers at the MTA.

The email body does contain a physical mailing address and an unsubscribe link in the footer (standard CAN-SPAM), which is correct.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

UTM parameter verification was blocked because all 32 CTAs are wrapped in redirect tracking URLs. UTM presence on destination URLs cannot be confirmed from the source. This should be spot-checked by following 2–3 redirect chains manually (e.g., the primary "Extra 25% off" CTA and one product card link) to verify `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` are appended post-redirect.
