# Stitch styles are back 💙
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- This is a licensed IP collaboration send — Crocs x Disney Stitch — and the creative commits to it fully. The hero image is strong: a flat lay of blue-toned Stitch-branded Clogs and Jibbitz alongside a Stitch plushie, immediately communicating the collection without a word of copy. It's a clean departure from the clearance blitz that dominated July 1–6 and the lifestyle pivot on July 5.
- The problem is execution depth. The hero headline is "New Drip" — playful, but the body copy is small and the offer is collection-browsing only. There's no price shown, no discount, no urgency. For an audience that just got hammered with Extra 30% Off Clearance emails, this feel like a palette cleanser more than a conversion driver. The Stitch IP is compelling enough to generate curiosity opens, but once inside, there's nothing to close the deal.
- The "Refer a Friend: Give 25%, Get 25% Off" module is a smart addition — it's a genuine offer — but it's buried below four navigation buttons and feels like an afterthought rather than a co-headline. Overall: good brand storytelling, weak commerce execution.

## What's working

- Hero flat-lay is visually cohesive and immediately communicates the Stitch collab — no guessing required.
- Subject line "Stitch styles are back 💙" is concrete, character-specific, and the blue heart matches the product palette nicely.
- "Shop Jibbitz Charms" appears as a secondary CTA, which is a smart cross-sell for a licensed IP drop.
- Free shipping banner at the top is a persistent trust signal.
- The Refer a Friend module introduces a concrete bilateral offer (25%/25%) that breaks the no-discount pattern.

## What's weak

- No price anchoring anywhere in the email — a collab drop without a price hint makes it feel like window shopping.
- "New Drip" headline is slang-forward; it may not land with the full Crocs subscriber base (skews younger than the actual list).
- The four secondary nav buttons (Shop Clogs, Shop Jibbitz, Shop Sale, Shop Sandals) dilute focus — they compete with the primary "Shop Disney" CTA.
- Refer a Friend offer is visually de-prioritized — smaller font, below the fold, treated as infrastructure rather than a featured benefit.
- Body copy beneath the headline is very small and likely unreadable on mobile without zooming.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price or offer to the hero.** Even "from $59.99" or "Clogs from $XX" gives the subscriber a reason to click vs. just admire. The Refer a Friend 25% mechanic could be pulled up into the hero as the hero offer.
- 2. **Simplify the CTA block.** Kill two of the four secondary nav buttons — keep "Shop Clogs" and "Shop Jibbitz Charms," drop the generic "Shop Sale" and "Shop Sandals" that dilute the collab focus.
- 3. **Elevate the Refer a Friend module.** Move it above the nav buttons with a dedicated section header and render the 25%/25% offer in larger type so it reads as a featured deal.
- 4. **Increase body copy size.** The descriptive copy under "New Drip" is unreadable at email scale — either make it bigger or cut it and let the image carry the story.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Classic Clogs, Jibbitz, and a Tote — the full Stitch drop is live`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (7 images)** — All footer images are served over `http://` from `image.crocs-email.com`:

- `9c36c0ef...png` — "Download on the App Store"
- `13246762...png` — "Get It On Google Play"
- `c4df71ec...png` — "Find Your Store"
- `e18949ff...png` — "Instagram"
- `80f68cd8...png` — "Facebook"
- `51d8144e...png` — "TikTok"
- `1187ef58...png` — "YouTube"

Gmail and Outlook both proxy or block mixed-content HTTP image sources. These will render as broken images for a significant share of recipients.

**Pixel beacon missing alt** — The Adobe/Demdex open-tracking pixel at `https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&...` has no `alt=""` attribute. Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` explicitly to suppress broken-image indicators in image-blocked clients.

**Font fallback** — Custom `TT Crocs` font is loaded via `@font-face` from `media.crocs.com`. Only Gmail and Apple Mail render web fonts; Outlook, Yahoo, and older Android will fall back to system fonts. No issues with the implementation itself, but the fallback stack should be verified in the full CSS (truncated).

No issues found with mobile responsive structure — `columnContainer`/`columnSingle` pattern, `mobileHide`/`mobileView` visibility controls, and `max-width: 639px` breakpoint are all standard.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** — The AgentMail relay did not surface this header. CAN-SPAM does not require this header, but Gmail and Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender requirements mandate it for senders above 5,000/day. If Crocs qualifies as a bulk sender, absence of this header risks inbox demotion.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not detected** — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy. Without it, Gmail cannot offer the native one-click unsubscribe button, increasing spam complaint risk.

**`Authentication-Results` header not found** — SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status is unknown at the relay layer. Cannot confirm whether `crocs-email.com` sending domain is properly authenticated. This is a relay visibility gap, not necessarily a deployment failure — should be verified against raw headers from a live inbox delivery.

**Physical address** — Not visible in truncated source; CAN-SPAM requires a physical postal address in the body. Assumed present in the untruncated footer.

**Unsubscribe link in body** — Not confirmed in truncated source; assumed present in footer. Should be verified.

Cannot fully evaluate UTM coverage with 31 redirect-wrapped links unresolved. The tracking redirects suggest click attribution is in place, but UTM parameter presence on destination URLs (`crocs.com`) is unverified.
