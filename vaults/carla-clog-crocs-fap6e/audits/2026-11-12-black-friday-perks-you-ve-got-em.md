# Black Friday Perks? You've Got 'Em! 💜
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-12**
## Executive summary

- This is a bare, text-only Black Friday early-access send. "BLACK FRIDAY EARLY ACCESS" fills the dark purple hero. "GET 2 FOR $50" and a white "Shop Early Access" pill sit below it. The eye lands on the headline, then the offer, then the button, so the hierarchy works. It's the same 2-for-$50 beat Crocs ran on Nov 10 ("Skip the Line: 2 Pairs for $50") and twice on Nov 11 ("2 for $50. Need We Say More? 👀" and "Still Thinking About 2 for $50? 👀").
- The weak point is that it has no product at all. There's no clog, no Jibbitz and no person, only type on a gradient. The Nov 11 sends at least had a hero Baya clog. The giant headline spends the most space on "Black Friday Early Access", which says nothing the shopper doesn't already know. The offer sits underneath at about a third of the size. The deadline ("Offer ends November 14th, 2026 at 11:59pm MT") is buried in the legal text. It's the strongest urgency in the email and no one will see it.
- The subject promises "perks" in the plural, but the body delivers one offer. Judgement: clean and readable, but it's the fourth 2-for-$50 send in three days and the least visually interesting of them. It will get a tap from deal-seekers, and everyone else will skim past it.

## What's working

- The "GET 2 FOR $50" line is specific, legible and easy to understand at a glance.
- The white "Shop Early Access" pill has strong contrast on the dark purple, and the button copy is a clear verb plus noun.
- A single hero panel with one CTA leaves no layout bugs and nothing competing for attention.
- The black bar at the top ("FREE SHIPPING ON QUALIFIED ORDERS") adds a second incentive without cluttering the hero.
- Purple suits the holiday mood and sets this send apart from the green panels of Nov 10 and Nov 11.

## What's weak

- No product imagery. The email shows nothing a shopper could want, so the click depends entirely on the discount.
- The hero headline is much larger than the offer. "Black Friday Early Access" is the biggest text, and "2 for $50" is secondary.
- The Nov 14 deadline is hidden in the small print instead of sitting near the CTA.
- "Exclusions Apply." appears right under the button with no hint of which styles are in or out. That undercuts trust.
- This is the fourth 2-for-$50 message in three days, with no new creative angle or reason to act now.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the deadline into the hero. Use "Ends Nov 14" or "3 days left" directly under "2 for $50", and make the offer at least as big as the headline.
- 2. Add a row of three to four eligible clogs, each linked to its product page, with the sale price shown. This is the biggest lift for click-through.
- 3. Shrink "Black Friday Early Access" to a small eyebrow label above the offer.
- 4. Replace "Exclusions Apply." with "Select styles" next to a "See eligible styles" link.
- 5. Add a reason this send is different from the last three: a different offer layer, new colors, or a gift angle.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your early access: 2 pairs, $50, 3 days left`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Pick any 2 eligible styles for $50. Online and in-store through Nov 14.`

# Technical Audit: "Black Friday Perks? You've Got 'Em! 💜" (Crocs)

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a Salesforce Marketing Cloud (ExactTarget) send with a sound responsive and Outlook scaffold. It has one confirmed markup defect: a mismatched closing tag on the open-tracking pixel. The compliance and authentication warnings come from the AgentMail relay and can't be confirmed as real defects. The HTML source was truncated, so the body links and footer were not available to audit.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Malformed open pixel (confirmed).** The pixel is `<img src="https://click.crocs-email.com/open.aspx?qs=…&d=10213&bmt=0" width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>`. The closing `</custom>` doesn't match any opening tag. Strict parsers and some clients may drop or misparse the pixel and the wrapper `<div>`. Open counts could be under-reported.
- **Third-party pixel without alt (QA-flagged).** `https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&c_emailopened=Yes&d_dpuid=…` is an Adobe Audience Manager beacon with no `alt` attribute. The QA check is accurate, but the impact is low because the image is a hidden tracker. Adding `alt=""` would clear the warning.
- **Unverified links.** 12 tracking links on click-redirect domains were skipped by the HTTP probe, so their destinations and status codes were not validated.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Present and correct:**
  - `lang` and `xml:lang="en"`.
  - `viewport`, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` and `format-detection` meta tags.
  - MSO `OfficeDocumentSettings` and the Outlook `sup` fix.
  - The Gmail hack `u + .email-body #new-gmail-hack`.
  - `font-display: swap` on the `TT Crocs` fonts, with a 639px mobile breakpoint.
- **Generic title.** `<title>Crocs</title>` doesn't describe the email, which affects "view in browser" and screen-reader context. This is minor.
- **Not verifiable.** The content images and their alt text fall in the truncated portion, so I can't assess them.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`, `[FIRSTNAME]`) are visible in the available source. The `d_dpuid` value in the demdex pixel is populated, not a literal placeholder.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post not found (QA WARN).** The QA note itself says the relay may not capture these headers. This is unconfirmed, not a verified defect. It needs checking against the raw message (Gmail "Show original") or a direct ESP send.
- **Authentication-Results not found (QA WARN).** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The sender is `email@crocs-email.com`, a dedicated subdomain, which is a good sign. The check needs the raw headers.
- **Footer unsubscribe link and postal address.** The source was truncated before the footer, so I can't confirm either.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not verifiable from the available data. The links are wrapped in `click.crocs-email.com` redirects and were skipped by the QA probe, so UTM parameters and landing-page destinations can't be assessed. The preheader promises "Get 2 pairs for $50", and a click-through test would be needed to check that the offer carries over to the landing page.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Fix the open pixel.** Replace `</custom>` with a proper `/>` or `</img>`. Without the fix, the open pixel may be silently lost in some clients.
2. **Add `alt=""` to the demdex beacon**, or move it to a CSS or background tracker.
3. **Inspect the raw message headers.** Check `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC in `Authentication-Results`. This resolves all three QA warnings.
4. **Re-run the audit on the full HTML.** That covers the footer, body links, alt text and UTM parameters.
5. **Follow the redirects in a browser-based check.** That validates the final destinations of the 12 skipped tracking links.
6. **Make the `<title>` descriptive.** Set it to something like the subject line.
