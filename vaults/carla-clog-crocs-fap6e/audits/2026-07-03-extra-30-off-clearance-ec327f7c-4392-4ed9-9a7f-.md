# Extra 30% off clearance 👀
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- This is the third consecutive Extra 30% Off Clearance email in as many days. July 1 was the first shot, July 2 ("Croc yeah—") was the repeat with a punchier line, and now July 3 is a near-identical send with one meaningful addition: a 40% Off Accessories module below the hero. That accessories block is the only new structural element, and ironically it's the stronger offer — 40% beats 30% — but it's buried beneath the by-now-exhausted clearance story.
- The creative itself is clean. Dual offers, verb-forward CTA copy, good contrast, no render issues. The "SALE-ON-SALE!" badge is still a clever signal. But three hits of the same mechanic in 72 hours means the list has been trained to ignore this email at the subject line.
- Verdict: a well-built email on a completely spent mechanic. Accessories should be leading, not tagging along.

## What's working

- Dual-offer structure (30% clearance + 40% accessories) gives subscribers two separate reasons to click
- "SALE-ON-SALE!" badge signals stacked discounts clearly — good cognitive shortcut
- "Grab them now" and "Shop the Sale" are both verb+noun — no vague "Discover" language
- Clean render throughout — solid contrast, no broken images, no layout issues
- Refer-a-Friend (Give 25%, Get 25%) adds a third value layer, even if it's below the fold

## What's weak

- Third identical clearance push in three days — list fatigue is now the dominant campaign variable, not creative quality
- 40% off accessories is the stronger number but it's buried; subscribers who scroll-skim stop at the hero
- No deadline or urgency anywhere — "Sale-on-Sale" implies finite but nothing confirms it
- No product specifics — no trending clearance styles, no anchor pricing, no inventory signals to make the discount feel real
- Referral module appears without a creative bridge, tacked on after two sale blocks with no transition

## Recommendations

- 1. **Flip the offer order** — lead with 40% off accessories (the stronger number) and let clearance play the secondary role. This alone differentiates the send from the prior two.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline** — "Through July 4th only" or a countdown converts "Sale-on-Sale" from implied urgency to actual urgency. This is a holiday week; use it.
- 3. **Show two or three specific products** with struck-through original prices — clearance without an anchor price is just a banner, not a deal.
- 4. **Rest the list** — no amount of subject-line optimization recovers from a 3-peat. One day off between this and the next promo would recover more engagement than any copy tweak.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your July 4th cart upgrade is in here 🎆`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Two deals stacked. Accessories at 40% off, clearance at 30% — through the 4th.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Finishing touches, 40% off. Sale-on-sale clearance too. Ends soon.`

## Technical Audit — Crocs "Extra 30% Off Clearance" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a well-structured MSO/VML compatibility layer and standard responsive patterns. Primary technical concerns are mixed-content HTTP image URLs in the footer and missing authentication/unsubscribe headers at the relay level.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (7 images blocked on HTTPS clients):**
All footer images are served over `http://` from `image.crocs-email.com`:

- `9c36c0ef...png` — "Download on the App Store"
- `13246762...png` — "Get It On Google Play"
- `c4df71ec...png` — "Find Your Store"
- `e18949ff...png` — "Instagram"
- `80f68cd8...png` — "Facebook"
- `51d8144e...png` — "TikTok"
- `1187ef58...png` — "YouTube"

Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all block mixed-content HTTP images when the email is loaded over HTTPS. These icons will render as broken images for the majority of recipients. Fix: update `src` to `https://image.crocs-email.com/...` (the domain appears to support HTTPS).

**Missing alt text — pixel tracker:**
```
https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&c_emailopened=Yes&d_dpuid=...
```
The open-tracking pixel has no `alt` attribute. This is a minor accessibility gap; tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` explicitly to suppress broken-image indicators in some clients.

**Custom font fallback:** `TT Crocs` is loaded via `@font-face` from `media.crocs.com`. Most email clients (Outlook desktop, older Gmail) will silently fall back to system fonts. This is expected behavior, not a defect, provided the fallback font stack is defined in the inline `font-family` declarations.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header — not captured:**
The QA relay did not observe this header. This is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policies (enforced since Feb 2024 for senders >5k/day). If the header is present at the ESP level but stripped by the AgentMail relay, it needs to be confirmed via raw header inspection at the inbox. If absent entirely, this is a deliverability risk.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) — not captured:**
One-click unsubscribe (required by the same Gmail/Yahoo policy) depends on this header pairing with `List-Unsubscribe`. Same caveat as above — relay capture gap vs. genuine absence is unresolved, but must be verified.

**CAN-SPAM physical address:** Not verifiable from the truncated source. Should appear in the footer.

**Unsubscribe link in body:** Not confirmed in truncated source, but standard for this ESP. Needs spot-check on full render.
