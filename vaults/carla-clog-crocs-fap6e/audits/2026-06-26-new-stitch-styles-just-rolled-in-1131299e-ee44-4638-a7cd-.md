# New Stitch styles just rolled in 🌊
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- After three straight days of the Buy-2/Buy-3 tiered discount hammer (June 23, 24, 25), this email is a welcome creative reset — a product launch story, not a promotion. The Disney Stitch collaboration gives Crocs an earned reason to show up in your inbox without a discount, and the "Surf, Sun, Stitch" headline does the job cleanly. The hero image, the Disney Stitch lockup, and the "Shop Disney" CTA all cohere into a single legible story.
- The problem is that it stops there. Beyond naming Classic Clogs, Jibbitz charms, and an exclusive tote bag in the body copy, the email doesn't show you the product. The hero is a lifestyle crop — no clear product shot grid, no price context, no "here's what's in the collection." For a launch email, that's a miss. You're invited to the party but not shown what's inside.
- Net read: a much-needed creative break that earns its inbox slot, but stops short of driving a click with conviction. It's a solid 7 — would be an 8 with one product image row.

## What's working

- **Clean creative break from the discount loop.** After four consecutive promo-mechanic emails, a licensed collab launch lands as fresh, not fatigued.
- **"Surf, Sun, Stitch" headline.** Tight, on-season alliteration that ties the Disney IP to the summer moment without forcing it.
- **Disney Stitch brand lockup.** The co-brand badge gives the email immediate visual authority and signals licensed collaboration, not generic product.
- **Four secondary shop buttons.** Clogs / Jibbitz Charms / Sale / Sandals give the scroll-down reader somewhere to go beyond the hero CTA.
- **Refer-a-Friend mechanic is correctly positioned secondary.** 25% give / 25% get is a genuinely good offer and belongs below the hero, not competing with it.

## What's weak

- **No product imagery beyond the lifestyle hero.** A Disney collab launch email needs to show the actual shoe colorways — there is no product grid or detail shot visible.
- **"Shop Disney" CTA undersells specificity.** Every Disney licensee says "Shop Disney." "Shop Stitch" or "Surf the Collection" is sharper and character-specific.
- **No urgency signal or limited-quantity cue.** Licensed collabs often sell through fast — the email gives no reason to act today vs. next week.
- **Free shipping banner is the only concrete offer.** After three days of 20-30% off, landing on "here's a new style" with no introductory incentive feels like a step back in conversion intent.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a 2–3 product image row below the hero.** Show the Classic Clog colorways and the Jibbitz charm designs. This is the single highest-leverage change — launch emails that don't show the product leave conversion on the table.
- 2. **Sharpen the CTA copy.** Change "Shop Disney" → "Shop the Stitch Collection" so it ties directly to the character and the moment.
- 3. **Add a scarcity or launch signal.** One line: "Limited edition — get yours before they're gone" anchors the newness as a reason to act now.
- 4. **Leverage the promotion history.** After three Buy-2/Buy-3 emails, a "Buy 2 Stitch styles and save" intro bundle offer would bridge the discount-trained audience into the new launch without undermining the collab premium.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources — 7 confirmed failures:**

All footer assets are served over `http://` from `image.crocs-email.com`. Modern email clients (Gmail, Outlook 365, Apple Mail with strict security) will block mixed-content images or silently fail to load them.

Affected images:

- `13246762...png` — "Get It On Google Play"
- `c4df71ec...png` — "Find Your Store"
- `e18949ff...png` — Instagram
- `80f68cd8...png` — Facebook
- `51d8144e...png` — TikTok
- `1187ef58...png` — YouTube

All seven share the same base path: `http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/`

**Missing alt text — 1 confirmed:**

The Adobe Audience Manager pixel fires as an `<img>` tag with no `alt` attribute:
```
src: https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&...
```
Tracking pixels don't need descriptive alt text, but `alt=""` should be explicitly set to prevent screen readers from reading the raw URL aloud.

**Font loading:** Custom `TT Crocs` font is loaded via `@font-face` from `media.crocs.com` with `font-display: swap`. This is correctly implemented — fallback stacks will render on clients that block external fonts.

**Outlook rendering:** MSO conditional comments, `PixelsPerInch: 96`, and `AllowPNG` are all present. Superscript fix (`sup{font-size:100%}`) is correctly conditionally applied.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**`List-Unsubscribe` header — not detected.** The QA relay did not capture this header. If absent at the MTA level, Gmail and Yahoo (both of which enforce [bulk sender requirements](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126)) will suppress the one-click unsubscribe UI in the message chrome. This is a deliverability risk for bulk sends.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header (RFC 8058) — not detected.** One-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy for >5k/day sends) depends on this header being present alongside `List-Unsubscribe`. Its absence means recipients must navigate a multi-step unsubscribe flow, which increases spam complaints.

**Authentication-Results — not captured.** SPF and DKIM pass/fail status for `crocs-email.com` cannot be confirmed from the relay snapshot. This is likely a header-capture limitation of the AgentMail relay rather than an actual authentication failure, but should be verified against raw SMTP logs.

**CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link:** Present in the email body (standard ESP footer — visible in preheader/structure). No issues found in what was provided.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The 32 click-tracked links were skipped by the HTTP probe, so UTM parameter coverage across all CTAs cannot be confirmed. The tracking redirect domain is the ESP's own click-wrapper, which is expected — but final destination URLs and their UTM payloads are unverified.

**Risk:** If the ESP click-tracking wrapper strips or fails to append UTM parameters before redirecting, attribution in GA4/analytics will be lost for all 32 links. This is common when redirect chains are misconfigured at the ESP level.
