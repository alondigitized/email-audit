# Which One Will You Wear?
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a colorway-expansion email for the Crocs Classic Clog, leaning on the "new colors" messaging that Crocs has hit repeatedly across July. The subject "Which One Will You Wear?" sets up a choice dynamic, but the email doesn't fully deliver on that tension — there's no side-by-side comparison or decisional framing in the body. What you actually get is two sequential product blocks with similar visual structure, followed by a four-way CTA grid, a referral module, and a footer.
- The creative is pleasant enough — the top block spotlights earthy, muted colorways and reads cleanly on mobile. The second section pivots to the Crocs Classic Sandal with a "Five Styles. One Season." angle, which is a thematically different product story jammed into the same email. That split-focus weakens both pitches.
- No offer, no discount, no urgency. For a mid-July email with zero promotional hook and a subject that leans on indecision rather than desire, this is a gentle engagement play at best. It won't move units the way the July 8 Disney Stitch or July 14 Kids Crafted Clog sends did.

## What's working

- Top hero image is sharp and styled well — earthy clog colorways are laid out attractively against a neutral background with a clear "new colors" badge.
- "Your Favorite Clogs, All Glowed Up" headline is punchy and personality-forward, matching Crocs' established voice.
- Free shipping banner at the top is immediately visible and sets a positive tone without cluttering the main creative.
- The referral module ("Refer a Friend: Give 25%, Get 25% Off") is a smart inclusion — it's a concrete, mutual-benefit offer that adds value below the fold.
- CTA grid at the bottom (Clogs / Jibbitz Charms / Crocs Style / Sandals) gives subscribers multiple entry points without being visually overwhelming.

## What's weak

- The subject's "which one" promise is never fulfilled — the body doesn't show a direct side-by-side or force a pick, making the subject feel like a tease that doesn't land.
- No offer or discount anywhere in the primary product blocks — this is purely a new-colorway awareness push with no conversion incentive.
- Second section pivots to a different product (Classic Sandal / Styled Clog) mid-email; the "Five Styles. One Season." tagline contradicts the top section's clog focus and muddles the narrative.
- CTA copy is generic ("Shop Crafted Clogs," "Shop Styled Styles") — neither of those is specific enough to drive urgency.
- The lower product section is noticeably smaller and less visually commanding than the hero, making it feel like an afterthought.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Deliver on the subject's premise.** If the subject is "Which One Will You Wear?", the email should open with a true side-by-side: two or three colorways, a quick call-out of what each one is best for, and two distinct CTAs ("I'm a Sage person" / "I'm a Clay person"). Make the choice real.
- 2. **Add a lightweight offer.** Even "Free Shipping on any new color" (already in the banner — just reinforce it in the hero) or an early-access framing would give subscribers a reason to act now vs. later.
- 3. **Cut or separate the sandal section.** The Classic Sandal story belongs in its own email or a clearly labeled secondary section — not jammed in as a near-equal peer to the hero clog block.
- 4. **Sharpen CTA copy.** "Shop New Hues" beats "Shop Styled Styles." Verb + specific noun, not a vague category.
- 5. **Lean into the referral module more.** The "Give 25%, Get 25%" is the most concrete offer in the entire email — it should appear higher up or be teased in the preheader.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New palette drop: Same Croc, 6 fresh colors`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Sage or Clay? Pick your new hue`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New colorways just landed — free shipping on every pair`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your go-to clog, now in shades you haven't tried yet`
