# OoOooh! New clogs. Shop Toy Story
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-28**
## Executive summary

- The June 28 Toy Story 5 send is Crocs' second collaboration product-launch email in three days, mirroring the Stitch drop on June 26. The creative is warm and well-executed: an orange-toned hero with character-specific clogs (a brown Woody-evoking style, a green alien clog), followed cleanly by a Jibbitz charms section. No product price discount anchors this email — free shipping in the top banner and a Refer-a-Friend 25%/25% panel are the only "offers." This is a brand-equity and discovery play, not a conversion hammer.
- The cadence question looms. After the Buy-2/Buy-3 discount barrage of June 23–25, the June 26 Stitch launch was a genuine creative reset. Then June 27 snapped back to the identical tiered discount formula. Now June 28 offers another collaboration launch. The pattern feels reactive — alternating between discount hammering and IP drops — rather than building a coherent arc: discovery email → follow-up with a collection-specific offer in 48 hours would have given this more teeth.
- The email works as a brand touchpoint. It doesn't work as a sales driver. Crocs is leaving conversion on the table by launching a new collection with no price hook to pull the fence-sitters.

## What's working

- Hero image is vibrant and immediately communicates the Toy Story 5 IP — the toy-on-table art direction is playful, warm, and on-brand
- "Shop Disney and Pixar's Toy Story 5" is an unambiguous, specific CTA — no vague "Discover" or "Learn More"
- Two-section structure (clogs first, Jibbitz charms second) sells both product lines without competing with each other
- The Refer-a-Friend panel at the bottom is a smart loyalty add that doesn't muddy the hero message
- Clean render — no broken images, overlapping text, or layout issues

## What's weak

- No price anchor on the featured product — a new collection launch without even a "starting from $XX" or intro offer leaves the conversion loop open
- "OoOooh!" appears in both the subject and the hero headline verbatim — the subject-to-body echo wastes the open moment instead of deepening the hook
- No urgency or scarcity signal — "new to the collection" is enough to browse, not enough to buy today
- The Jibbitz charms section transitions abruptly — there's no narrative bridge connecting the clogs story to the character charms below it

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a launch offer or price anchor to the hero.** Even "New from $59.99" or "Free shipping on every Toy Story style" (making the generic free shipping specific to this drop) signals value and closes the "I'll come back later" loop.
- 2. **Kill the subject-to-body echo.** The subject said "OoOooh!" — the body opening doesn't need to repeat it. Use that headline slot to lead with the IP hook: "Woody. Buzz. And the alien crew. Now on your feet."
- 3. **Add urgency.** "Limited launch edition" or "Get them while the gang's all here" costs nothing to write and meaningfully lifts urgency-driven clicks.
- 4. **Plan the follow-up before you send.** A discovery email like this needs a 48-hour follow-up: "Love what you saw? Buy 2 Toy Story styles, get 20% off" — turn the interest this email opens into revenue.

## Full review
## 1. Overview

The June 28 Toy Story 5 send is Crocs' second collaboration product-launch email in three days, mirroring the Stitch drop on June 26. The creative is warm and well-executed: an orange-toned hero with character-specific clogs (a brown Woody-evoking style, a green alien clog), followed cleanly by a Jibbitz charms section. No product price discount anchors this email — free shipping in the top banner and a Refer-a-Friend 25%/25% panel are the only "offers." This is a brand-equity and discovery play, not a conversion hammer.

The cadence question looms. After the Buy-2/Buy-3 discount barrage of June 23–25, the June 26 Stitch launch was a genuine creative reset. Then June 27 snapped back to the identical tiered discount formula. Now June 28 offers another collaboration launch. The pattern feels reactive — alternating between discount hammering and IP drops — rather than building a coherent arc: discovery email → follow-up with a collection-specific offer in 48 hours would have given this more teeth.

The email works as a brand touchpoint. It doesn't work as a sales driver. Crocs is leaving conversion on the table by launching a new collection with no price hook to pull the fence-sitters.

## 3. What didn't

- "OoOooh!" appears in both the subject and the hero headline verbatim — the subject-to-body echo wastes the open moment instead of deepening the hook

1. **Add a launch offer or price anchor to the hero.** Even "New from $59.99" or "Free shipping on every Toy Story style" (making the generic free shipping specific to this drop) signals value and closes the "I'll come back later" loop.

3. **Add urgency.** "Limited launch edition" or "Get them while the gang's all here" costs nothing to write and meaningfully lifts urgency-driven clicks.
4. **Plan the follow-up before you send.** A discovery email like this needs a 48-hour follow-up: "Love what you saw? Buy 2 Toy Story styles, get 20% off" — turn the interest this email opens into revenue.

## Technical Audit — Crocs "Toy Story" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound with proper responsive scaffolding and Outlook conditional comments, but has 7 HTTP image src violations in the footer, a missing `List-Unsubscribe` header chain, and an unauthenticated relay path that leaves SPF/DKIM status unverified.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (7 violations)** — footer images served over `http://` from `image.crocs-email.com`. Modern clients (Gmail, Outlook.com, Apple Mail) block mixed-content or proxy-strip HTTP assets:

Fix: update all 7 `src` values to `https://`.

**Open pixel missing alt text** — the Adobe Audience Manager event beacon at `https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&c_emailopened=Yes&d_dpuid=...` has no `alt=""` attribute. Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` explicitly to suppress screen-reader noise and avoid client-inserted placeholder text.

**Custom font fallback** — `TT Crocs` is loaded via `@font-face` with woff2/woff sources from `media.crocs.com`. No system-font fallback stack is declared in the `font-family` rule. Clients that block remote fonts (Outlook desktop) will fall to the browser default serif, not a controlled fallback.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** — QA relay did not capture this header. CAN-SPAM and Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (Feb 2024 enforcement) mandate a `List-Unsubscribe` header. If the ESP injects this at send time and the AgentMail relay strips it, the header's presence in live delivery is unverified by this audit.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` absent** — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe header not found. Gmail and Yahoo require this for bulk senders (>5k/day). Same caveat: may be ESP-injected and relay-stripped.

**`Authentication-Results` missing** — SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown because the AgentMail relay did not forward this header. Live deliverability posture for `crocs-email.com` cannot be confirmed from this capture.

Physical mailing address and unsubscribe link presence in body cannot be confirmed from the truncated source, but are required by CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) and §5(a)(3).

32 click links are behind redirect tracking domains — UTM parameter presence cannot be confirmed without following redirects. No direct `crocs.com` destination URLs are visible in the truncated source to verify UTM attribution chain independently.
