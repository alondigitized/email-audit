# Extra 25% off makes it easy😎
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Crocs' third deployment of the "Extra 25% off" offer in six days — the same sale ran June 13 and June 14. The email is technically clean: bold hero type, unambiguous CTA, no render bugs. But "THIS SALE GOT AN UPGRADE" is marketing-speak for "we're running the same sale again," and any subscriber who opened the two prior sends will sense it immediately.
- The one genuinely new element is the Jibbitz section — $2 single charms, $7 packs — a specific, low-barrier upsell that the earlier sends didn't surface. That's a real differentiator. The problem is it's buried below the fold, where most mobile readers won't reach it.
- Solid execution, stale pitch. The brand has hit "Extra 25% off" so hard this week that this send is fighting prior-send fatigue before the first scroll.

## What's working

- **"THIS SALE GOT AN UPGRADE" + "Extra 25% Off"** — bold hierarchy, the eye lands there immediately
- **"Shop the Sale"** is a clean verb+noun CTA with no ambiguity
- **Jibbitz deal** ($2 single charms / $7 packs) is specific pricing that adds a low-entry upsell angle
- **Free shipping banner** at the top reinforces value without competing with the hero
- **No render bugs** — clean layout throughout, no overlapping elements or broken images

## What's weak

- **Third identical offer in six days** — the cadence signals desperation, not exclusivity
- **No urgency or deadline anywhere** — "makes it easy" is passive; there's no reason to act today over tomorrow
- **Jibbitz section is below the fold** — the best differentiator from prior sends is where most people won't get to
- **Referral section ("Give 25%, Get 25% Off")** crammed below the sale module dilutes the single-action message
- **No lifestyle shot or on-body imagery** — same floating-clog-on-white visual treatment as the June 13 and 14 sends

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline to the hero** — "Ends Sunday" or "Today only" transforms passive interest into urgency without any creative lift
- 2. **Lead with the Jibbitz deal as the differentiator** — "Sale + $2 Jibbitz charms" gives subscribers who already ignored the June 13 send a concrete new reason to click now
- 3. **Pull the referral section into its own dedicated email** — it belongs in a separate send, not stapled to a sale blast
- 4. **Swap the product render for an on-body or lifestyle image** to visually distinguish from the two identical prior sends
- 5. **Set an explicit preheader** — the current layout likely leaks "FREE SHIPPING ON QUALIFIED ORDERS" as inbox preview text, which repeats rather than complements the subject
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 25% off + $2 Jibbitz charms — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Sale ends soon: extra 25% off + $2 charms`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sale styles already up to 50% off, now stacked — free shipping included`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `$2 single charms, $7 packs while the extra 25% lasts`

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not captured** — QA relay did not surface this header. If absent at send time, Yahoo Mail and Gmail will suppress the native unsubscribe UI, and the send risks deliverability penalties from both providers. Needs verification against the actual SMTP envelope.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058)** — One-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy for >5k/day senders) depends on this header pairing with a `List-Unsubscribe` mailto/URL. If Crocs qualifies as a bulk sender, this is a compliance gap, not just a warning.

**`Authentication-Results` header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status cannot be confirmed via the AgentMail relay. The sending domain `crocs-email.com` should be verified to have DKIM signing and a DMARC policy in place. No direct evidence of failure, but cannot confirm pass.

CAN-SPAM physical address: not visible in the truncated source — should be confirmed present in footer.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Cannot confirm UTM parameters** — 32 links were skipped by the HTTP probe. UTM tagging on click-redirect chains cannot be verified from source alone. Should be spot-checked against resolved destination URLs for `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` presence.

No issues found with landing page alignment based on available evidence.
