# Easy math: more styles = more off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- After three consecutive Buy-2/Buy-3 sends (June 23–25), Crocs gave the list one day's breathing room with the Stitch product launch on June 26. June 27 immediately returns to the identical 20%/30% tiered mechanics — and while the June 26 break prevents this from being a literal fourth-straight-day repeat, any subscriber who opened all three of those sends will recognize the offer the moment the email loads. The subject line ("Easy math: more styles = more off") is the most interesting creative choice here — indirect, mildly clever, under 50 chars — but the body immediately collapses the intrigue by serving the same hero offer they've already seen.
- Two genuinely new elements exist: a Refer-a-Friend module ("Give 25%, Get 25% Off!") that makes its first appearance in this brand history, and a "25% Off Select Work Styles!" badge on the hero image. Neither surfaces in the subject or hero copy. The accessories cross-sell (40% Off) carried over from the June 24 send.
- The email is clean, renders perfectly, and the visual hierarchy is solid. The problem isn't craft — it's that the creative reset Crocs earned with the Stitch email gets immediately spent returning to an offer the list already knows cold.

## What's working

- **"Easy math" subject is the strongest framing this mechanic has had** — implies the deal without spelling it out, and breaks the pattern of the June 23–25 blunt-number subjects.
- **Free shipping banner** is the first thing a subscriber sees; it's a real conversion sweetener that costs nothing to surface.
- **Refer-a-Friend module is genuinely new** and the most relationship-building element Crocs has put in an email in this entire streak.
- **"25% Off Work Styles" badge** opens a sub-segment (healthcare, culinary) that the prior six sends completely ignored.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, all images load, CTA buttons are clearly readable.

## What's weak

- **The body immediately cashes out the subject's intrigue.** "Easy math" creates curiosity; "20% Off 2 Pairs, 30% Off 3 Pairs" delivers the exact deal subscribers have seen three days prior, erasing any earned novelty.
- **Work Styles buried in a small badge** — a potentially high-value sub-segment gets a badge near the shoe instead of a dedicated section or subject mention.
- **No expiry date anywhere.** No deadline in hero copy, no banner countdown — the strongest available click trigger is absent for the fifth or sixth consecutive send.
- **"SALE SALE SALE" watermark** around the shoe competes visually with the clean headline above it; the repetition reads as desperation rather than energy.
- **Refer-a-Friend falls below accessories and app download** in the scroll order, burying the most differentiated module in this email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a visible expiry date to the hero sub-copy.** "Stock up on your favorites and save today!" → "Stock up — offer ends [date]." This is the single highest-lift change available and costs nothing to implement.
- 2. **Segment the Work Styles audience and send them a dedicated email.** Healthcare workers and chefs are a distinct cohort; 25% Off Work Styles as the hero of a targeted send would outperform this general blast for that group and relieve cadence fatigue on the main list.
- 3. **Move Refer-a-Friend to the second module**, above accessories. It's the only genuinely new element and should be rewarded with prime real estate.
- 4. **Retire or significantly tone down the "SALE SALE SALE" watermark.** The headline already does the work; the watermark adds visual noise without adding information.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Work styles, weekend clogs, sandals — buy more, save more`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `The buy-more math still works (+ new styles added)`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `20% off 2 pairs, 30% off 3 — plus 25% off Work Styles today`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Refer a friend: you both get 25% off — and the buy-more deal is back`

### 1. Technical Summary

Solid foundational HTML with appropriate Outlook/MSO conditionals, responsive CSS, and preheader suppression. The main actionable issues are mixed-content HTTP image sources in the footer and missing authentication/unsubscribe headers at the relay layer.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources — 7 footer images at risk of blocking:**

All social/app-store icons in the footer are served over `http://` from `image.crocs-email.com`:

- `http://image.crocs-email.com/.../9c36c0ef...png` — "Download on the App Store"
- `http://image.crocs-email.com/.../13246762...png` — "Get It On Google Play"
- `http://image.crocs-email.com/.../c4df71ec...png` — "Find Your Store"
- `http://image.crocs-email.com/.../e18949ff...png` — Instagram
- `http://image.crocs-email.com/.../80f68cd8...png` — Facebook
- `http://image.crocs-email.com/.../51d8144e...png` — TikTok
- `http://image.crocs-email.com/.../1187ef58...png` — YouTube

Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all block mixed-content HTTP images by default. These icons will render as broken images for a significant portion of recipients.

**Missing alt text — open pixel:**

`https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&c_emailopened=Yes&d_dpuid=1c110d5f7d8fbc329b0a8ea915c05b3c631d53c` has no `alt` attribute. Tracking pixels are intentionally invisible, but an empty `alt=""` should be explicit to satisfy accessibility linters and avoid screen reader noise.

**Custom font fallback** — `TT Crocs` is loaded via `@font-face` with woff2/woff sources from `media.crocs.com`. No system-font fallback stack is declared alongside it. Clients that block remote fonts (Outlook desktop, some corporate environments) will fall back to the browser/client default rather than a controlled sans-serif.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not found** — the AgentMail relay did not surface this header. CAN-SPAM requires a functional unsubscribe mechanism; Gmail and Apple Mail additionally use `List-Unsubscribe` to surface the native unsubscribe UI. If the header is present at the ESP but stripped by the relay, this is a monitoring gap. If it's genuinely absent, it's a compliance and deliverability risk.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found** — one-click unsubscribe as required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (enforced since Feb 2024 for >5k/day senders) is unconfirmed. Crocs at volume almost certainly qualifies.

**`Authentication-Results` header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown due to AgentMail relay not forwarding the header. Cannot confirm authentication posture from this capture.

Physical mailing address and unsubscribe link are expected in the footer (not visible in truncated source) — standard for Crocs; no confirmed absence.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**31 links through click-redirect** — UTM parameter presence and correctness cannot be confirmed without resolving the redirect chain. The QA probe deliberately skipped these. Attribution hygiene (source/medium/campaign/content) is unverifiable from this audit.

No issues found with link structure in the visible HTML head/preheader section.
