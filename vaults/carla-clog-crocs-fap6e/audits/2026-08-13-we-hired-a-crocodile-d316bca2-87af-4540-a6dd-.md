# We hired a crocodile.
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-13**
## Executive summary

- This is a pure brand-personality send — no product launch, no discount, just a mascot introduction. Crocs is leaning fully into its "unhinged internet brand" voice: a plush crocodile mascot named Niles sitting at an office desk, framed as a new "hire," with a CTA that literally reads "Time to Eat." It's a gamble that only pays off if the recipient already has affection for the brand's meme energy — there's no product hook, no offer, and no reason to click beyond curiosity about a social media bit.
- The "Recommended for You" grid and shop buttons at the bottom feel bolted on, like a mandatory commerce module attached to what is otherwise a social-follow campaign. This isn't a "did I miss a sale" email, it's a "does this brand's humor land for me" email — that's a much narrower bet than anything in the recent brand history (buckle ballet launch, Mellow 2 clog, back-to-school).
- Judgement: a fun, low-effort brand-building send, but weak on business impact — no offer, no urgent reason to open, and the primary CTA drives to Instagram-adjacent brand content, not a purchase.

## What's working

- The mascot image is well-produced, on-brand, and immediately legible as a joke ("crocodile in an office cubicle").
- "NILES CROCS" sticker/tag graphic is a nice touch that reinforces this as a character-introduction moment.
- Copy is short, punchy, and reads in the brand's established playful voice ("Trying to figure out what motivates a crocodile is hard").
- Fallback commerce content (4 products + 4 shop-category buttons) is present in case the joke doesn't land, so the email isn't a total dead end for purchase intent.
- Free shipping banner up top is a small added incentive layer even though it's not the focus.

## What's weak

- No product, no discount, no urgency anywhere in the hero — the entire top half of the email sells a social media follow, not a purchase.
- Primary CTA "Time to Eat" is cute but vague — it's unclear without reading copy first that it links anywhere useful (presumably to the Instagram/TikTok handle, not the shop).
- The "Recommended for You" section feels disconnected from the hero story — black clogs and a sandal have zero narrative link to "we hired a crocodile."
- Subject line "We hired a crocodile." has no offer, product, or urgency signal — it's a curiosity play with no real payoff for someone who isn't already invested in Crocs' social presence.

## Recommendations

- 1. Give the mascot bit a commerce tie-in — e.g., a limited-edition Niles Jibbitz charm or a "meet Niles" landing page that leads into a real product, so the joke converts curiosity into a click that matters.
- 2. Make the primary CTA explicit about where it goes — "Time to Eat" should either say "Follow @nilesthecroc" or "Meet Niles" so recipients know they're leaving the shop funnel.
- 3. Cut or shrink the generic 4-product grid — if there's no thematic link, it reads as filler; either theme it (all-black colorway like Niles) or drop it in favor of more mascot content/video.
- 4. Add urgency or a light offer somewhere (even a small "Jibbitz 2-for-1" tie-in) so the email isn't 100% brand equity with zero purchase incentive.
