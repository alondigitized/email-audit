# Crocs at Work™: New Styles Out Now!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a workwear-segment send with a strong, concrete offer. The tiered "Buy 2, Get 20% Off / Buy 3, Get 30% Off / Buy 4, Get 40% Off" block on yellow is the best thing in the email. It's clear, and it rewards bigger baskets. It's a smarter mechanic than the flat "30% off + free shipping" run from Oct 2–7 and the "Up to 50% Off" send on Oct 11.
- The structure is muddled, though. The hero is a mop-on-checkerboard-floor photo with a small shoe. It sells "work" but not the offer. The subject says "New Styles Out Now!" while the body is really a multi-buy promo. The email also has two near-identical "Shop Crocs At Work™" buttons, a feature strip, and a four-button grid. The reader gets many exits and no single hero product.
- Overall it's a competent, on-brand send that matches the 6/10 range of the recent Crocs sends. It will land best with people who wear Crocs on a shift. Everyone else gets a promotion that doesn't mention itself in the inbox.

## What's working

- The tiered discount block is large, heavy black type on yellow. The three lines are easy to read at a glance.
- "Stock Up Now" matches the multi-buy mechanic and is a stronger CTA than a generic "Shop Now".
- The three-circle feature strip (closed-toe protection, grip sole, easy-clean material) gives real product reasons to buy. The sole shot makes the traction claim visible.
- The "Free shipping on qualified orders" top banner adds a second incentive without crowding the hero.
- The footer is tidy, and the fine print states a real end date (Mon, Oct 19, 11:59pm MT).

## What's weak

- The hero image is a mop and a checkerboard floor, with the clog small and cropped in the corner. The product isn't the hero.
- The deadline appears only in the legal fine print. The offer block has no visible "ends Monday" urgency.
- Two identical "Shop Crocs At Work™" buttons plus four secondary pills (Clogs, Jibbitz™ Charms, Sale, Sandals) split attention. No specific product is linked.
- "Select styles" and "Exclusions Apply." are vague. The email never says which styles qualify, which hurts trust.
- There is no social proof or review content. Oct 14's "The Comfiest Crocs I've Owned" showed Crocs does this well.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead the subject with the offer. The multi-buy is the reason to open. Put the tiered discount in the subject and keep "Crocs at Work" as the qualifier.
- 2. Move the offer block above the fold, or put "Buy 2+, save up to 40%" as an overlay on the hero. Crop the hero tight on the clog so the product is the subject.
- 3. Add a visible deadline inside the yellow block, for example "Ends Monday, Oct 19". Don't leave it in the footer.
- 4. Replace the duplicate "Shop Crocs At Work™" buttons with a CTA pointing to one specific bestselling work style. Name the qualifying styles in the offer block.
- 5. Add one review line or star rating under the feature strip, for example "Non-slip, rated by nurses and line cooks".

# Technical Audit: "Crocs at Work™: New Styles Out Now!"

## 1. Technical Summary
The source is a well-structured, Salesforce Marketing Cloud (ExactTarget) email with Outlook/Gmail/Apple Mail hardening and a mobile media query. There are no blocking defects. The confirmed issues are one malformed tracking-pixel tag and one third-party pixel with no `alt`. The HTML was truncated, so the body, footer and links could not be inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Malformed markup on the open pixel.** The tag is `<img src="https://click.crocs-email.com/open.aspx?TTQUT5CZM2OE3PEFDCF3VOGCAE.10199&d=10199&bmt=0" width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>`. The stray `</custom>` closing tag has no opening tag. Most clients ignore it, but it is invalid HTML and could trip strict parsers or sanitizers.
- **Raw `&` in attribute values.** The pixel URLs use `&` instead of `&amp;`. This is tolerated in practice but is not strictly valid.
- **19 tracking links were not probed.** They sit on click-redirect domains (likely `click.crocs-email.com`), so destination status, redirect chains and final URLs are unverified.
- **Third-party Adobe Audience Manager pixel.** QA found `https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&c_emailopened=Yes&d_dpuid=1c110d5f…`. This is a second open-tracking beacon alongside the SFMC one. It is not a defect, but it is an extra third-party request that Apple MPP and image-blocking clients may handle differently.
- **Leftover template comment.** `<!--webfont code goes here-->` is still in the source. It is harmless.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `alt` on the demdex pixel (QA WARN).** The `<img>` has no `alt` attribute. Add `alt=""` and `width="1" height="1"`. The SFMC pixel already has `alt=""`.
- **Generic `<title>`.** `<title>Crocs</title>` is the same for every send and does not describe this email.
- **Good practices confirmed in the source:**
  - `lang="en"` and `xml:lang="en"` are set.
  - There is a viewport meta tag and a mobile `@media (max-width: 639px)` block.
  - Apple data-detector and Gmail `u + .email-body` hacks are present.
  - The preheader is hidden with `display:none !important`.
  - The Outlook conditionals (`OfficeDocumentSettings`, `sup` fix) are in place.
  - Web fonts use `font-display: swap` with woff2 and woff sources, but the fallback stack could not be verified in the truncated source.
- **Not verifiable.** Image `alt` text, `role="presentation"` on layout tables, and link text were not visible in the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`, `[%…%]`) appear in the visible source, and the `d_dpuid` value in the demdex pixel is resolved. The body was truncated, so tokens further down could not be checked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found (QA WARN).** QA flags that the AgentMail relay may strip them. This is **unconfirmed**, and it is not evidence that Crocs omits them. Check the raw headers at the receiving mailbox.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found (QA WARN).** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The sender is `email@crocs-email.com`, which matches the tracking domain `click.crocs-email.com`, so the domains are consistent. Alignment is unverified.
- **Footer not inspected.** The unsubscribe link and physical postal address are not in the truncated HTML, so they are unverified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. All 19 links were skipped as tracking redirects, and no final URLs or UTM parameters were captured. No misalignment is evidenced.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Remove the stray `</custom>` after the SFMC open pixel and encode `&` as `&amp;` in pixel URLs.
2. Add `alt=""` to the demdex pixel `<img>`.
3. Make `<title>` specific to each send, for example the subject line.
