# Welcome to Crocs Club! Here’s 15% OFF 💚
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-11**
## Executive summary

- This is a solid welcome-series email doing exactly what welcome emails should do: lead with the discount to justify the signup, then introduce the catalog. The 15% off hero lands above the fold, the Crocs Club framing gives it a loyalty feel, and the social proof section earns its place. For someone who just joined, this email answers the right question — "what can I get and why should I care?"
- The weakness is length. By the time you hit the "Try Them on for Size" / app-download section, you've lost most shoppers. The welcome series shouldn't be trying to do acquisition, retention, personalization, catalog browse, *and* app install in one send. That dilution costs clicks.
- Clear pass on execution, average on targeting. Good enough to drive conversions from the top 30% of openers; dead weight for the rest.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline to the 15% off offer.** "Expires in 7 days" or "Valid through May 18" would lift conversion meaningfully. The current email has zero time pressure.
- 2. **Cut the "Try Them on for Size" / app section entirely.** App acquisition is a separate funnel. Don't compete with yourself in the welcome email.
- 3. **Make "Shop Your Style" more specific.** Replace with "Shop Clogs" or "Shop Sandals" — it's a stronger click signal. Better yet, A/B two CTAs: one for Clogs, one for Sandals, based on any onboarding signal.
- 4. **Move the preference module higher** — above the product grid rather than below it. You'll get better data if you ask before they start browsing.
- 5. **Rewrite the subject to hint at the expiry** — creates urgency without deception.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 15% off is waiting — expires soon`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Crocs Club is official. Use your 15% today.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus free shipping on your first order — don't let it expire.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop clogs, sandals, and slides with your welcome discount.`

## Full review
## 1. Take

This is a solid welcome-series email doing exactly what welcome emails should do: lead with the discount to justify the signup, then introduce the catalog. The 15% off hero lands above the fold, the Crocs Club framing gives it a loyalty feel, and the social proof section earns its place. For someone who just joined, this email answers the right question — "what can I get and why should I care?"

The weakness is length. By the time you hit the "Try Them on for Size" / app-download section, you've lost most shoppers. The welcome series shouldn't be trying to do acquisition, retention, personalization, catalog browse, *and* app install in one send. That dilution costs clicks.

Clear pass on execution, average on targeting. Good enough to drive conversions from the top 30% of openers; dead weight for the rest.

---

## 2. What stood out

The hero gets the job done: "15% Off" in large type, followed by "Your Next Order," a code visible below it, and a clear "Shop Your Style" button. Free shipping is announced at the very top of the email as a secondary hook — a smart double-incentive for a welcome send. The "Welcome To The Crocs Club" section below the hero transitions smoothly into a product grid that shows the breadth of the lineup, which works well for someone still learning the catalog.

The "Pair Down Your Preferences" module is genuinely clever — asking new subscribers to tell you what they like is better than guessing, and surfacing it prominently in the welcome email is the right timing. The two five-star reviews ("Comfortably and cute" and "Versatile") are short, credible, and placed right before a secondary CTA. The "Most Popular Looks" grid with Jibbitz-decked clogs is vibrant and visually loud in a way that matches the brand's playful personality.

The misses: no expiration on the 15% offer anywhere visible in the scroll, which removes urgency. The app-download section at the bottom is a pure distraction in a first-contact email — it belongs in email 3 or 4 of an onboarding sequence, not here.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Crocs Club Welcome (15% OFF)

### 1. Technical Summary

A standard welcome/trigger email built on a table-based layout with VML Outlook fallbacks and Adobe Campaign (Demdex/demandware) infrastructure. Core rendering structure is sound, but the email has several infrastructure-level issues: HTTP image sources that will break in modern clients, missing unsubscribe headers, and unauthenticated relay delivery.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**35 tracking links skipped** — all clicks route through `click.crocs-email.com` redirect domain. QA could not probe destinations; broken final URLs would not be detected.

**Pixel tracker missing alt text:**
```
src: https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&c_emailopened=Yes&...
```
The open-tracking 1×1 pixel at the top of `<body>` has no `alt=""`. Some clients will render broken image chrome in its place.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not found** — QA relay did not capture this header. If the header is absent at delivery, one-click unsubscribe will not appear in Gmail/Apple Mail. Required for bulk senders under Google and Yahoo's 2024 sender requirements.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found (RFC 8058)** — One-click POST unsubscribe is not confirmed present. Required alongside `List-Unsubscribe` for Gmail bulk sender compliance.

**`Authentication-Results` header not found** — SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown because the AgentMail relay did not forward or synthesize these headers. Cannot confirm authenticated delivery; this should be verified at the MTA level directly.

**CAN-SPAM physical address:** Not visible in truncated source — should be confirmed present in the email footer.

**Plain-text body is 82% URLs** — `12047 / 14743` characters. Most ESPs use plain-text part as a spam signal input. A near-empty plain-text body with mostly tracking links is a deliverability risk.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All CTAs route through `click.crocs-email.com` redirect links, so UTM parameters are injected server-side at click time. The redirect chain itself could not be probed (35 links skipped), so UTM coverage and landing page alignment cannot be confirmed from this audit alone.

The `campaignID` value encoded in the tracking pixel is:
```
campaignID=us_x_trigger_welcome-15peroff_signupconfirmation
```
This is consistent with the email subject and send context — no mismatch detected at the pixel level.
