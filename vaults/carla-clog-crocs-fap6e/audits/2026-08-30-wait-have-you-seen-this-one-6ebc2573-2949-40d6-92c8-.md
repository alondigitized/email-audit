# Wait, have you seen this one?
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-30**
## Executive summary

- This is a "TikTok made me buy it" social-proof play, and it's a smart deviation from the collab-launch grind Crocs has been running (Pokémon 8/28, NFL 8/27, NINJAGO 8/26, Shoe Tattoos 8/24). The hero shot — cozy blanket, gold jewelry, Crafted clog styled like a boot with a woven trim — is genuinely the best product photography I've seen in this brand's recent sends: lifestyle, texture, warm-weather-adjacent styling that makes the shoe look like a fashion object, not just a clog.
- But the email buries its lede. "Wait, have you seen this one?" promises something specific and viral; the body delivers three completely different offers (Crafted collection, $30 off $100, Shoe Tattoos) with no connective thread back to the subject's hook. The $30/$100 banner is the strongest commercial asset here and it's sandwiched awkwardly between two unrelated product pushes rather than leading.
- Net: strong creative, unfocused execution. Worth opening, mediocre at converting past the first CTA.

## What's working

- Hero image is genuinely scroll-stopping — styled lifestyle shot with jewelry/cozy blanket context, not a flat product photo.
- "From Your Feed to Your Feet" headline smartly ties the TikTok hook from the subject line into on-page copy.
- $30 off $100 banner is a clear, high-contrast block with unambiguous value and a distinct "Shop and Save" CTA.
- Four-CTA footer row (Clogs / Jibbitz / Sale / Sandals) gives multiple easy exits for browsers who didn't want Crafted or Shoe Tattoos specifically.
- No render bugs — clean layout, no overlapping text or broken images.

## What's weak

- Subject line ("have you seen this one") never pays off in the body — no TikTok video embed, view count, or influencer/UGC content, just a static product photo.
- Three unrelated offers (Crafted, $30/$100, Shoe Tattoos) stacked with equal visual weight — no hierarchy signaling which one actually matters most today.
- "Shoe Tattoos" block repeats verbatim from the 8/24 send with no new angle — same product, same framing, five days later.
- $30 off $100 discount is real but generic — not tied to the "viral" Crafted product it's sandwiched next to, so the offer and the hook don't reinforce each other.
- Preview/subject pairing leans on curiosity-bait ("have you seen this one") without specificity — risky given inbox fatigue from five near-identical sends this week.

## Recommendations

- 1. Commit to one story: lead with Crafted + the $30/$100 offer combined into a single hero block ("Crafted is going viral — get $30 off $100 to try it"), and drop Shoe Tattoos from this send entirely — it doesn't need a second outing five days after its own launch email.
- 2. Add a concrete social-proof element to the hero — a "X million views on TikTok" badge or a UGC-style creator quote — to actually earn the subject line's claim.
- 3. Move "Recommended for You" personalization signal higher; it's currently the last thing before the footer despite being the most individually-relevant content block.
- 4. Tighten the subject to name the product so it stops relying purely on curiosity.
