# Happy Birthday! 🥳
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- This is the actual-birthday send in what appears to be a deliberate two-part sequence — Crocs already deployed "It's almost your birthday 🥳" on June 5. That earlier send scored 8/10 and the description praised it for having "subject, hero headline, and offer block all pointing at the same occasion." The problem with today's email is that it doesn't live up to that description: the birthday framing holds for the first two sections (hero + offer block) and then evaporates entirely. Below that, it's a generic June promo — sandals grid, lookbook module, Afterpay upsell, category navigation. The occasion is abandoned the moment it's established.
- The offer itself ($15 off $50 with a visible code) is the strongest element. It's honest, specific, and placed early. But subscribers who opened the June 5 "almost birthday" email already saw essentially the same deal. Receiving an identical discount framed as a "special birthday gift" seven days later flattens the perceived value of both sends.
- Judgment: The bones are right but the execution is padded. Strip out four of the six modules below the fold and this becomes a genuinely good triggered send.

## What's working

- **Concrete, honest offer:** "$15 off your next purchase of $50 or more" with a visible promo code and "Exclusions Apply*" — no buried conditions, easy to act on.
- **Offer block placement:** The pink discount section appears immediately below the hero image; the deal is findable without scrolling.
- **Clean render:** No broken images, overlapping text, or layout failures anywhere in the email.
- **Sandals grid is seasonally relevant:** Four slides and sandals in June is a legitimate reason to browse — the product selection earns its place.
- **Free shipping banner is a real benefit:** The top strip adds incremental value and is a credible incentive alongside the code.

## What's weak

- **Birthday theme disappears after the second section:** The lookbook, Afterpay module, and category navigation blocks have zero birthday context — it reads like a standard promo with a personalized header bolted on.
- **Hero image isn't birthday creative:** Two models in colorful summer outfits with florals reads as June launch content, not birthday celebration. There's no visual cue (confetti, gifting motif, etc.) that this is a triggered occasion email.
- **No differentiation from the June 5 send:** The offer structure is identical. The subscriber who opened "It's almost your birthday" already primed for the same code/discount — today's send feels like a reminder, not a celebration.
- **Email is too long:** Six modules after the offer block produce decision fatigue. The birthday CTA competes with a lookbook CTA, an Afterpay CTA, and four category CTAs.
- **Preheader almost certainly leaks the utility bar:** "FREE SHIPPING ON QUALIFIED ORDERS*" is the first visible text and very likely surfaces in inbox preview — irrelevant to a birthday message.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the email by ~60%:** Hero + offer block + one sandal product pick + one "Shop Now." Birthday sends should feel like a gift, not a catalog drop.
- 2. **Differentiate the offer from the June 5 tease:** Even a marginal upgrade ("an extra $5 because it's the real day") makes the birthday feel earned rather than recycled.
- 3. **Add birthday-specific visual language to the hero:** Confetti, a wrapped clog, a celebratory animation — anything that visually signals "this is not a regular promo."
- 4. **Write a real preheader:** The current render almost certainly leaks the free-shipping banner. Replace it with a deadline-aware birthday hook.
- 5. **Remove the Afterpay and category navigation blocks:** Both are better suited for a homepage-style send. They pull attention away from the one action this email should drive.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your birthday gift from Crocs 🎁`
- **Subject Alt B:** `It's your day — here's $15 off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `$15 off any $50+ order, just for your birthday — expires soon.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `A little something from us. Use your code at checkout.`
