# Super Mario™ is here! 🍄
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Crocs' second licensed IP collab in eight days — the July 8 Disney Stitch send being the direct predecessor — and the creative here is meaningfully stronger. The blue Classic Clog hero image is the best visual this brand has sent in the current audit window: a Mario mushroom-Jibbitzed shoe against a warm brick backdrop reads instantly as a collector drop, not a clearance push. The "Pipe Dreams" headline earns its Nintendo license with real wordplay. The execution has a clear point of view.
- Where the email falls short is the commercial layer. There is no price, no scarcity signal, and no urgency — this is a pure product-introduction send aimed at fans who already want it. The referral mechanic (Give 25%, Get 25% Off) adds transactional depth but sits below the fold. The secondary Yoshi section undercuts the hero's momentum with a generic headline ("Flutter into Fun") and mushroom-prop set dressing that reads as filler.
- Bottom line: strong creative foundation for a collab launch, but the absence of any purchase incentive or scarcity hook means it converts only the already-converted.

## What's working

- Hero blue clog is immediately readable as a collector item — the mushroom Jibbitz pop against the brick backdrop without crowding the silhouette.
- "Pipe Dreams" headline is the sharpest licensed copy Crocs has sent in this audit window — it rewards the reader who gets the Mario reference.
- "Includes 6 exclusive Jibbitz™ charms" callout with a labeling arrow makes the bundle value explicit without requiring fine print.
- "PVC dimensional fins" product detail label signals attention to material specifics — right signal for a collector audience.
- Referral CTA (Give 25%, Get 25% Off) is well-placed for a collab drop where social sharing is a natural behavior.

## What's weak

- No price visible anywhere — collab audiences are price-anchoring fast; the absence creates friction before clicking.
- No scarcity or deadline language — collab drops sell out, but this email gives no reason to act now.
- "Flutter into Fun" headline on the Yoshi section is generic and breaks the Mario-voice established by "Pipe Dreams."
- Four-button nav row (Shop Clogs / Shop Jibbitz™ Charms / Shop Sale / Shop Sandals) dilutes the collab's focus — "Shop Sale" especially conflicts with the launch framing.
- Yoshi Classic Clog secondary image is propped with mushrooms in a way that reads staged rather than editorial; the hero's restraint is absent here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price anchor and a scarcity signal in the hero** — "Starting at $XX · While Supplies Last" directly below the "Shop Super Mario™" CTA converts fence-sitters without redesigning the email.
- 2. **Rewrite "Flutter into Fun" to something Mario-specific** — "Go Green for Yoshi" or "Pick Your Player" maintains the IP voice and differentiates the Yoshi clog from the blue hero.
- 3. **Cut "Shop Sale" and "Shop Sandals" from the bottom nav** — a collab drop should not route traffic to clearance; trim to two buttons (Shop Super Mario™ / Shop Jibbitz™ Charms).
- 4. **Pull the referral mechanic up one section** — on a collab send, "Share this with a friend, you both get 25% off" is a natural ask; bury it and you lose the impulse.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Super Mario™ Clogs just dropped — while they last`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Pipe Dreams: Mario x Crocs is here 🍄`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Yoshi Classic Clog + 6 exclusive Jibbitz™. Shop before they warp away.`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `6 exclusive Mario Jibbitz™ charms, PVC fins, limited run. See the drop.`
