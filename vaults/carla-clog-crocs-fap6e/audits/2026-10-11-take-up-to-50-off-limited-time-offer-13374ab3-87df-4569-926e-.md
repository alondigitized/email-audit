# Take Up To 50% Off - Limited Time Offer
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-11**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, loud sale email, and it's a step down in specificity from the 30% off + free shipping run. The lime hero with a heavy black "Up to 50% Off!" is the first thing you see, and the black "Shop the Sale" pill is unmistakable. The pink Baya clog against the lime and the repeating "sale" type is on-brand and eye-catching. But "Up to 50% off select styles" with "Exclusions Apply" is the vaguest offer Crocs has sent in this stretch. It also arrives right after the Oct 10 Crafted Lace Clog launch and the Oct 2–7 30% + free shipping campaign.
- The "sale sale sale" graphic partly collides with the hero shoe. The top "sale" and the third "sale" in the right-hand column are clipped by the photo frame. That reads as intentional collage, not a render bug, but it does make the lower half of the email decoration, not product. There's no price, no named style and no product grid. The pink clog isn't labeled or linked to a specific item, and nothing shows what 50% off actually buys. The only concrete numbers are the Jibbitz $2 / $7 pack deal, and that's buried in a thin lavender strip under the hero.
- Verdict: a functional, on-brand clearance blast that will get opens from existing fans. It gives a shopper little reason to click beyond "there's a sale." Its fine print is the real deadline (Tuesday, Oct 13), and the body never mentions it. A mid-tier send.

## What's working

- The hero hierarchy is strong. "Up to 50% Off!" in oversized black on lime is read first, with no competition.
- The "Shop the Sale" button is a high-contrast black pill that's easy to find and tap.
- The Jibbitz line is the most concrete offer here: "$2 single charms, $7 packs". It gives a second, cheaper entry point.
- The pink Baya clog on a pink-orange gradient is a vivid, on-brand product shot, and the lime and pink palette is distinctive in an inbox.
- The "Free shipping on qualified orders" banner at the top is a good, quiet value add.

## What's weak

- "Select Styles" and "Exclusions Apply" make the headline offer feel hedged. "Up to" combined with both qualifiers gives no sense of what's discounted.
- There are no prices, product names or product grid. The shopper can't see a single discounted item.
- The hero image has no label and no link target I can see, and the "sale" text overlaps the photo frames. The bottom half is mostly decoration.
- The deadline (Oct 13) appears only in the legal fine print. The subject says "Limited Time" but the body shows no countdown or date.
- The Jibbitz deal strip is easy to miss, and it's a separate offer that splits focus from the 50% message.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a product grid of 3–4 named, priced items (original and sale price) below the hero, in place of the second "sale sale sale" half. Show real discounts, not a graphic.
- 2. Move the deadline up into the hero: "Ends Tuesday, Oct 13" under the headline, with a countdown if possible. Right now the only date is in the footer.
- 3. Replace "Select Styles" with category entry points like "Clogs from $X", "Kids", "Sandals", each as its own CTA.
- 4. Promote the Jibbitz $2 / $7 deal to a visible card with charm imagery and its own button.
- 5. Cut the "Exclusions Apply" line under the button and move it to a small asterisk note, or state the exclusions plainly.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 50% off clogs, ends Tuesday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Baya clogs + $2 Jibbitz, 3 days only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus $2 charms and $7 packs. Sale ends Oct 13 at 11:59pm MT.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free shipping on qualified orders. Our best clog deals of the season.`

# Technical Audit: Crocs, "Take Up To 50% Off - Limited Time Offer"

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible head and top of the body are mostly well built for email. They include Outlook/MSO fixes, Apple and Gmail hacks, and a mobile breakpoint at 639px. There is one malformed tag, one tracking pixel without an `alt` attribute, and several compliance and authentication checks that could not be confirmed. The HTML is truncated and 13 links were skipped by the QA probe, so the body, footer and link-level checks are largely unverified.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **13 tracking links not probed.** QA skipped them as tracking/click-redirect domains. The visible open pixel is `https://click.crocs-email.com/open.aspx?...&bmt=0`, which looks like a Salesforce Marketing Cloud (Exact Target) pattern. Final destinations, redirect health and link validity are unverified, but this is not a defect.
- **Malformed markup on the open pixel.** The `<img ... alt="">` is followed by a stray `</custom>` closing tag with no matching opener. Most clients ignore it, but it is invalid HTML and could cause quirks in strict parsers.
- **Third-party pixel.** `https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&c_emailopened=Yes&d_dpuid=...` is an Adobe Audience Manager open-event beacon. It is expected tracking, but it duplicates the SFMC open pixel and adds a second third-party request.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `alt` on the demdex pixel.** QA flagged the image with no `alt` attribute. For a tracking pixel the fix is `alt=""` plus `width="1" height="1"`. Without it, some screen readers may announce the URL.
- **Positives confirmed in source:**
  - `lang="en"` is set on `<html>`.
  - Viewport, `format-detection` and `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` meta tags are present.
  - MSO `OfficeDocumentSettings` and the superscript fix are included.
  - The `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` reset and Gmail `u + .email-body` hack are included.
  - The preheader is hidden with `display:none` and padded with zero-width characters.
- **Web fonts.** `TT Crocs` loads from `media.crocs.com` with `font-display: swap`. The fallback font stack is not visible in the truncated source, so it can't be verified.
- **Generic `<title>`.** `<title>Crocs</title>` is not descriptive. It is low severity because it rarely surfaces in email clients.
- **Body content not reviewable.** Image `alt` text, table roles and contrast fall in the truncated portion.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (e.g. `%%...%%`, `{{...}}`) appear in the visible source. The truncated portion is unchecked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found.** QA notes the AgentMail relay may strip or not capture them. This is unconfirmed, not a verified defect. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require one-click unsubscribe, so check the raw headers on the original message.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown, again likely a relay capture limit. The sending domain is `crocs-email.com`. Alignment with the visible From domain can't be verified from this data.
- **Footer not visible.** The unsubscribe link, physical postal address and sender identification are not in the truncated HTML, so they are not assessed.
- **QA summary inconsistency.** It reports "3 warning(s)" but lists 4 `[WARN]` lines. This is a QA tooling counting quirk, not an email issue.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. Visible links are wrapped in `click.crocs-email.com` redirects and were skipped by QA, so final landing URLs and UTM parameters are not observable. No evidence of mismatch was found.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Remove the stray `</custom>` after the open pixel `<img>`.
2. Add `alt=""` to the demdex beacon (`demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?...`). Consider dropping it if it duplicates the SFMC open pixel.
3. Re-run QA against the raw `.eml` or original headers (not the relay copy) to verify `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment.
4. Resolve the 13 tracking links by following redirects (a HEAD/GET with redirect following) to confirm final URLs return 200 and carry the expected UTM parameters.
5. Re-audit with the full, untruncated HTML to check the footer (unsubscribe link, postal address), alt text, merge tokens and link integrity.
6. Make the `<title>` descriptive (low priority).
