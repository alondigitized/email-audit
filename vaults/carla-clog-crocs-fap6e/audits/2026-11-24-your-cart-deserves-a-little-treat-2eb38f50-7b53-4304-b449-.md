# Your cart deserves a little treat.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-24**
## Executive summary

- This is the eighth Black Friday send from Crocs in about ten days, and it's a decent one. It's also the first to give the stacked discount a real code. The hero reads "UP TO 60% OFF + AN EXTRA 20% OFF (ALMOST) EVERYTHING! USE CODE COZY20", with a black "Shop Black Friday" button below it. The earlier sends said "extra 25% off" with no code. The mechanic is now clear, but the extra drops from 25% to 20%, and the email doesn't say so.
- The subject, "Your cart deserves a little treat.", is the same abandoned-cart framing I flagged on 11/22 with "Your Cart's Getting a Big Deal". The hero pays it off better this time. The testimonial bubbles ("I deserve a treat", "My cart has zero regrets!") echo the subject and put social proof on a lined clog. Below that, the body is a pile: a Grinch clog strip, a $30 doorbuster banner, a TikTok Shop strip, and an Afterpay block. Several competing offers dilute the hero.
- Overall it's a solid 6-7. The hero and the code are good. The cadence, the stacked secondary modules, and the "(ALMOST) EVERYTHING" hedge hold it back.

## What's working

- The hero offer is unmissable: "60% OFF" in large black type, with the code "COZY20" set on its own line.
- The code gives a concrete action. Compared with the vague "extra 25% off" earlier in the week, it makes the discount feel real.
- The testimonial bubbles with five-star ratings add social proof to the hero, and they tie back to the "treat" subject.
- The "Classic clogs starting at $30" banner is a strong price anchor. "Prices Straight from the Past" is a fun frame, and the red-on-pink creative stands out.
- The Grinch Classic Lined Clogs strip names one product at 45% off, a good seasonal hook.

## What's weak

- The cadence is relentless. This is at least the eighth near-identical Black Friday promo since 11/14, and the 11/21 and 11/22 sends ran the same 60% + extra-percent offer.
- "(ALMOST) EVERYTHING" plus "Exclusions Apply" under the button undercuts the headline. The fine print is long, and the real end date, Sunday 11/29, appears only in the footer.
- Every button goes to a category or generic destination ("Shop Black Friday", "Shop All Doorbusters"). No product is shown with a name, a price, or a sizing cue.
- The lower third is cluttered. The TikTok Shop strip is a stray element with no CTA, and the Afterpay block has no tie to the main offer. The tan Afterpay box clashes with the rest of the palette.
- The hero has no urgency. "Offer ends Sunday" is buried, and there is no countdown or "ends tonight" line.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the deadline into the hero ("Code COZY20 · ends Sunday 11/29") and drop "(ALMOST)" for a plain "extra 20% off select styles."
- 2. Cut the TikTok strip and shrink the Afterpay block into a one-line footer note. Lead the second half with one hero product instead.
- 3. Have the hero button deep-link to the lined-clog collection shown in the image, with copy like "Shop Lined Clogs", not the generic "Shop Black Friday".
- 4. Pull the "$30 Classic Clog" and "Grinch 45% off" modules up into one two-up row of named products with prices, so the email reads as a product grid instead of a stack of banners.
- 5. Vary the cadence. Suppress or segment people who got the last three sends, or give them a "last chance" variant.
- **Subject Alt A:** `COZY20: extra 20% off, ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Lined clogs + 60% off. Your cart approves.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use code COZY20 at checkout. Classic Clogs from $30 while they last.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Carol, Melissa and Steve all went for the lined clog. Offer ends 11/29.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The ExactTarget/Salesforce Marketing Cloud send has a standard responsive structure with a preheader and an open pixel. The only confirmed defects are a stray `</custom>` tag and an alt-less third-party Demdex pixel. The header and compliance warnings come from the capture pipeline. They are not confirmed defects in the email. The HTML was truncated, so the body, footer and CTA links could not be inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** 21 links on `click.crocs-email.com` (ExactTarget click-redirect) were skipped by the HTTP probe. Their destinations and status codes are unknown.
- **Malformed markup:** the open pixel is closed with an invalid tag: `<img src="https://click.crocs-email.com/open.aspx?qs=…&d=10213&bmt=0" width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>`. `</custom>` has no opening tag. Clients tolerate it, but it is invalid HTML.
- **Third-party pixel:** a Demdex (Adobe Audience Manager) pixel fires on open: `https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&c_emailopened=Yes&d_dpuid=1c110d5f…`. It is a second tracking beacon on a different domain from the ESP pixel. It also gets blocked by Apple Mail Privacy Protection and by image blocking, so open-based audience data will be undercounted.
- The open pixel's `qs` token is long and opaque (it encodes the subscriber, job and list). That is normal for SFMC and not an issue.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt (confirmed by QA):** the Demdex `<img>` has no `alt` attribute. It is a hidden tracking pixel, so the practical impact is low, but it should have `alt=""`. The ESP open pixel correctly uses `alt=""`.
- **Invalid markup:** the stray `</custom>` above, and a `<style>` block placed directly inside `<body>` (before the preheader). Gmail and Apple Mail accept this, but it is not valid HTML.
- **Preheader:** `.preheader` is hidden with `display:none !important`. The padding buffer (`&#847; &zwnj; &nbsp; &#8199; &shy;`) is a standard technique and works.
- **Outlook:** the VML/Office namespaces and `<!--[if mso]>` PNG/DPI settings are present, so no issue.
- **Mobile and dark mode:** the `@media (max-width:639px)` utilities are present. No `prefers-color-scheme` or `color-scheme` meta was found in the visible portion, so dark-mode handling is unconfirmed.
- **`<title>`:** the value is a generic `Crocs`. This has a minor impact on web-view and accessibility context.
- **Web fonts:** TT Crocs loads from `media.crocs.com` with `font-display: swap`. The fallback stack is not visible in the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found in the visible source. No unresolved tokens such as `%%…%%`, `{{…}}` or `[FirstName]` appear. The `d_dpuid` value in the Demdex URL is populated, but the truncated source can't confirm whether it is a real ID or a merge output. Body and footer personalization were not inspected.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Not confirmed as defects:** the QA warnings for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) and `Authentication-Results` all say the headers "may not be captured by AgentMail relay". These are visibility gaps in the test harness. Verify them against raw headers from a direct-delivery copy before treating them as failures.
- **Sender alignment:** the From domain `crocs-email.com` matches the click and tracking domain `click.crocs-email.com`. This is good for DKIM/SPF alignment, but the signing result is unverified.
- **Footer:** the unsubscribe link and physical postal address are not in the truncated source, so CAN-SPAM body requirements could not be checked.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. All 21 links are wrapped in SFMC redirects and were not probed. The visible source contains no UTM parameters or destination URLs. The preheader promises "an extra 20% off (almost) everything", and landing-page alignment with that offer could not be verified.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Remove the stray `</custom>` after the open-pixel `<img>`.
2. Add `alt=""` to the Demdex pixel. Consider `style="display:block"` and explicit `width="1" height="1"`.
3. Move the `<style>` block that sits directly inside `<body>` into `<head>`.
4. Re-run the header checks (`List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, SPF/DKIM/DMARC in `Authentication-Results`) on a copy that preserves raw headers.
5. Resolve the 21 SFMC redirects with a follow-redirect probe. Confirm that they return 200, that the final URLs carry UTM parameters, and that they land on a page matching the 20% offer.
6. Re-run the audit on the full, untruncated HTML to check the footer (unsubscribe link, postal address), dark-mode support and the font fallback stacks.
