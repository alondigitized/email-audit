# Don't Miss Out On Your 15% OFF Welcome Offer! 💸
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- This is a welcome/triggered email delivering a 15% off first-order code — a mechanically correct welcome flow, but landing right on the heels of Crocs' Memorial Day campaign that was offering "up to 50% off." That sequencing problem is the central issue here: a new subscriber who glimpsed the May 27th "Up to 50% off" subject line before signing up just received a welcome offer that is less than a third as deep. The optics are bad even if the timing is automated.
- The email itself is structurally overcrowded. The 15% hero is clean, but Crocs then layers on a product grid, a preference-center prompt ("Pair Down Your Preferences"), a lookbook section, and an AR try-on pitch — all before the footer. Four distinct value propositions means zero memorable ones.
- The execution is competent — no broken images, clear brand identity, free-shipping banner at top — but the offer feels like a template email that forgot to check the promotional calendar before firing.

## What's working

- Hero section is unambiguous: "TAKE 15% Off Your Next Order" with a visible promo code and a single "Shop Crocs" CTA lands cleanly above the fold.
- Free shipping banner at the very top reduces purchase friction immediately.
- Category shop links ("Shop Clogs," "Shop Croc Sandals," "Shop Platform Sandals") give product-aware subscribers a direct path rather than dumping to the homepage.
- The "Try Them on for Size" AR section with App Store / Google Play links is a smart conversion play that differentiates from the brand's typical promo emails.

## What's weak

- 15% off feels weak immediately after the Memorial Day "Up to 50% off" cadence — a new subscriber comparison-shopping could easily find a deeper deal via the sale landing page.
- Four separate content blocks after the hero (product grid, preference center, lookbook, app download) dilute focus and make the email feel like an internal kitchen-sink brief, not a designed subscriber experience.
- "Don't Miss Out" FOMO in the subject with a 💸 emoji and "OFF" in caps are mild spam triggers and don't match the elevated creative tone Crocs showed in the May 30th "I can't believe these are Crocs" send.
- No expiration date is visible on the 15% code — the fine print says "limited time" but there's no specific deadline, which softens the urgency and gives no reason to act today over tomorrow.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a real deadline to the hero.** "Valid through June 5" changes the urgency from theoretical to actionable. One line under the code.
- 2. **Cut the email to two sections.** Hero (15% off + code + CTA) and one secondary block (either the app or the lookbook — not both). Welcome emails should have one job.
- 3. **Acknowledge the post-sale context.** Something like "Welcome — the big sale just ended, but your 15% is always on" reframes the offer as membership value rather than a consolation prize.
- 4. **Pull the preference center to send #2.** "Pair Down Your Preferences" is a good idea but it competes with the conversion ask. Send it 24 hours after the welcome as a standalone.
- 5. **Rework the subject line.** The current subject has FOMO clichés and an emoji that clash with the creative brand voice Crocs demonstrated May 30th.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your welcome gift: 15% off, no catch`
- **Subject Alt B:** `15% off your first Crocs — here's the code`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use it on clogs, sandals, Jibbitz — anything. Expires June 5.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Code inside. Free shipping included. Grab it before it expires.`

### 5. Compliance

**Missing `List-Unsubscribe` header** — required by Google and Yahoo for bulk senders (>5K/day threshold) since February 2024. Absence means Gmail may demote or block delivery without user-controlled suppression.

**Missing `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header (RFC 8058)** — one-click unsubscribe is mandatory for the same Google/Yahoo policy. Without this, recipients using Gmail's "Unsubscribe" button get a degraded or non-functional flow.

**`Authentication-Results` header not found** — SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unknown through the AgentMail relay. Cannot confirm DMARC alignment. This is a relay capture gap, not necessarily a sending failure, but it should be verified against raw headers from a live test send.

**Plain-text body is 80% URLs** (9,902 of 12,316 chars) — a strong spam-filter signal. Most ESPs generate plain-text automatically from HTML; if this is auto-generated, the template structure (heavy click-tracking links) is the root cause.

CAN-SPAM physical address presence cannot be confirmed from the truncated HTML — verify footer contains a valid postal address.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All destination URLs are behind the `click.crocs-email.com` redirect layer. UTM parameters (if any) would be appended at redirect resolution time and are not inspectable in the raw HTML. QA did not probe redirects, so it is unknown whether:

- The `campaignID=us_x_trigger_welcome-15peroff_lastcall` value maps to a GA4 or Adobe Analytics campaign

**Recommendation:** probe one representative `click.crocs-email.com` URL through to its final destination to verify UTM passthrough and landing page state (welcome offer banner live, 15% discount active in cart).
