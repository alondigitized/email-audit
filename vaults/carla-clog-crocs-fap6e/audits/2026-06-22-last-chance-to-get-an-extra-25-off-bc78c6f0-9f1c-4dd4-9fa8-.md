# Last chance to get an extra 25% off!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Crocs' **fifth deployment** of the "Extra 25% Off" offer in nine days — June 13, June 14, June 18, June 20, and now June 22. The June 20 send already declared "before it's officially over…" and yet here we are, 48 hours later, with "Last chance" and "ONLY HOURS LEFT!" blazing in the hero. That false urgency is the single biggest problem in this email: not the design, not the copy, not the product — just the calendar.
- The visual execution is actually the best version of this campaign. Hot-pink hero with bold knockout type, a clean "Shop the Sale" CTA, and two secondary modules (Jibbitz deals, referral program) that add genuine cross-sell value without cluttering the scroll. If this were the first or second deployment of the offer, scores would be materially higher.
- Verdict: a competent email undermined by a promotional strategy that has cried wolf too many times. The list has been trained not to believe the urgency, which is the worst outcome for a "last chance" frame.

## What's working

- **Hero visual** — neon pink background with the pink clog pops immediately; no question what the offer is within half a second.
- **Offer math is visible** — "extra 25% off sale styles already up to 50% off" stacks the value clearly in the body copy.
- **Jibbitz secondary module** — "$2 single charms, $7 packs" is specific and price-forward; gives non-footwear buyers a reason to click.
- **Referral module** — "Give 25%, Get 25% Off" is a concrete, symmetric offer that doesn't feel tacked on.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, no broken images, no overlapping text, free-shipping banner renders correctly.

## What's weak

- **Hollow urgency** — "ONLY HOURS LEFT!" on the fifth send of the same promotion is not believable; the offer has been expiring for nine days.
- **No new creative hook** — identical hero structure and offer to the June 18 and June 20 sends; nothing signals this email is different.
- **No product specificity below the fold** — the hero links to a sale page, not a featured product or curated edit; it's a broad invitation, not a pull.
- **App download module buried mid-email** — it interrupts the shopping flow at an odd moment and isn't connected to any offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill or restructure the urgency claim** — if the sale genuinely ends tonight, show a countdown timer or a specific timestamp ("Ends tonight at midnight PT"). If it doesn't, drop "ONLY HOURS LEFT!" — a subscriber who got the June 20 "it's almost over" email will clock the lie immediately.
- 2. **Pivot the hero to a specific product** — five "shop the sale" emails have run; this one should feature a bestselling style with a name, colorway, and direct link. That's the only meaningful differentiation left.
- 3. **Move the Jibbitz module to the hero** — "$2 charms" is a genuinely low-resistance entry point; leading with it on the fifth "Extra 25% Off" send would re-engage list members who already passed on footwear.
- 4. **Add sizing or availability signal** — even "Limited sizes remaining in Classic Clog" would create legitimate scarcity without manufactured time pressure.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 25% off ends tonight — here's what's still in stock`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$2 Jibbitz + 25% off — your last real shot`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sale styles already up to 50% off. Stack the savings before midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Classic Clogs, sandals, slides — still in your size? Check now.`

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not found** — QA flagged this as likely an AgentMail relay capture issue, not necessarily a missing header at the envelope level. However, this cannot be confirmed without inspecting the raw SMTP headers. If the header is genuinely absent, CAN-SPAM and Gmail/Yahoo sender policy compliance is at risk.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found** — one-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender guidelines (>5k/day). Same caveat as above re: relay capture.

**`Authentication-Results` not found** — SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unknown. If DKIM is failing, Gmail will suppress the sender name and flag the message. Needs verification at the MTA/DNS layer independently of this relay.

Physical mailing address and unsubscribe link appear present in the footer (standard ESP compliance block) based on the source structure; no issues detected there.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

UTM parameters cannot be fully audited because all 32 CTA links pass through the ESP click-redirect wrapper and were skipped by the HTTP prober. UTM coverage is unknown. Recommend a manual spot-check of 2–3 decoded destination URLs to confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` are present and consistent with the send.
