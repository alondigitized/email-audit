# The horror has arrived...
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-24**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuine collab launch — Halloween/Michael Myers-themed Crocs, tying into the Lionsgate Horror franchise — and it's the most distinctive creative Crocs has sent in this stretch, easily beating the recycled "Extra 50% Off" green banners from earlier in the week (9/19–9/20). The hero shot is moody and on-theme: dim concrete backdrop, "LIONSGATE HORROR" in dripping red type, two genuinely creepy character-print clogs (one Halloween mask/knife motif, one slasher-red puzzle pattern) lit like a horror poster.
- But it stumbles right where it matters: there's no price, no discount, no "new drop" urgency framing, and "This Collection Slays" is a flat, overused pun that undersells a collab this visually strong. The CTA "Shop Lionsgate Horror" is clear but generic — it doesn't tell you these are limited-edition or drop-dated, which is exactly the kind of collab that benefits from scarcity messaging.
- Judgement: strong visual identity, weak commercial mechanics — worth opening for the novelty, only a maybe on the click.

## What's working

- The Lionsgate Horror x Crocs collab is a real, recognizable IP tie-in — distinct from Crocs' repeated generic sale creative this month.
- Hero image is moody, high-production, thematically consistent (concrete horror-movie set backdrop, dripping blood-red logo type).
- Two distinct clog designs are clearly visible with enough close-up detail to see the character print/hardware.
- "Shop Lionsgate Horror" CTA button is high-contrast and unambiguous.
- Legal/trademark line under the hero photo signals this is an authentic licensed release, not a knockoff.

## What's weak

- No price, discount, or "limited edition/drop" urgency anywhere in the body — nothing to click toward beyond "look at these."
- "This Collection Slays" is generic filler copy; it wastes the section that should name the actual product or hook (e.g. "Michael Myers has arrived").
- No product name/style callout under the two clogs — a shopper can't tell what to search for if they leave the email.
- No social proof, sizing/fit info, or "selling fast" signal for what's clearly a hype/collab drop that would benefit from both.

## Recommendations

- 1. Name the collab explicitly in the body copy — swap "This Collection Slays" for something that states what it is (e.g., "Michael Myers Has Arrived") so the value is legible without relying on subject-line intrigue.
- 2. Add scarcity/urgency: "Limited-edition drop" or a date ("Available now — while it lasts") since horror-collab hype items sell on FOMO, not discount.
- 3. Label the two clogs individually (style names) under the hero so viewers know what they're looking at before they click.
- 4. Add a price or "Starting at $X" — collab drops still convert better with a visible price anchor even without a discount.
