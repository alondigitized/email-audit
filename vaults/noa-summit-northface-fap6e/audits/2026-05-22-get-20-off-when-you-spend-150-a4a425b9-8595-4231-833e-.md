# Get 20% off when you spend $150+.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- This is The North Face's fifth promotional email in nine days, landing two days before the Memorial Day 5.25 deadline — and it pivots the conversion goal mid-campaign. The top hero is a loyalty recruitment play: "MEMBER EXCLUSIVE | Spend $150+ Get 20% Off" with a "Join Now" CTA pointing non-members to the XPLR Pass signup. Below that sits the same "Up to 25% Off Select Gear" offer from the May 16 send, now playing second fiddle behind a loyalty gate.
- The structural problem is split intent. One email is trying to do two things — recruit loyalty members and drive Memorial Day sale traffic — and neither conversion gets full creative commitment. Worse, the spend-threshold offer (20% off $150+) is a regression from the clean, threshold-free 25% off promoted on May 14 and May 16. Subscribers who've been tracking this campaign will notice the worse math, and existing XPLR Pass members are being shown a "Join Now" CTA that doesn't apply to them.
- The render is clean, the deadline is present, and the brand mark is strong. But this send feels like a late-stage list squeeze rather than a meaningful final push.

## What's working

- Hard deadline ("ENDS 5.25") appears twice — sets urgency without feeling frantic
- The 25% sale offer is still present below the fold for subscribers who skip the loyalty gate
- Layout is clean with zero render bugs or broken images
- Subject line math is immediately parseable — no ambiguity about what the offer is

## What's weak

- Split CTAs ("Join Now" for loyalty + "Shop Sale" for the sale) fragment conversion intent — neither button owns the email
- The 20% off with a $150 spend threshold is a weaker offer than the threshold-free 25% off promoted May 14–16; subscribers who've been watching will notice the downgrade
- Existing XPLR Pass members hit a "Join Now" CTA that's wrong for them — the hero is non-member copy served to the full list
- Blue jacket on the model is the most compelling visual but is unnamed, unlinked, and completely wasted as a click entry point
- Fifth email in nine days; cadence fatigue is measurable at this stage and this send will underperform the May 16 peak

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to one conversion goal.** The Join Now + Shop Sale split halves both click rates. If the priority is loyalty recruitment, make the XPLR Pass value prop the full hero and move the sale to a secondary block. If the priority is the sale (it should be, given the 5.25 deadline), lead with Shop Sale and reduce the loyalty ask to a footer callout.
- 2. **Segment the loyalty block.** Send the XPLR Pass recruitment hero only to non-members. Existing members should see a hero that reads "Your exclusive 20% kicks in at $150 — here's what's worth it," not an invitation to join a program they're already in.
- 3. **Put the deadline in the subject.** The "Ends 5.25" urgency is buried inside the email. A last-chance subject with "ends Sunday" or "ends tomorrow" is the highest-leverage change for open rate on a final Memorial Day send. The May 16 send scored a 10 partly because the deadline was front-loaded.
- 4. **Name and link the jacket.** The blue jacket model is the most eye-catching image in the email — give it a product name and a direct PDP link. That's a free additional click entry point.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Members: 20% off $150+ — ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Spend $150, save 20%. Ends 5.25.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Memorial Day sale still on — up to 25% off select gear while it lasts.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Not a member yet? Join XPLR Pass and unlock 20% off this weekend.`

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This is The North Face's fifth promotional email in nine days, landing two days before the Memorial Day 5.25 deadline — and it pivots the conversion goal mid-campaign. The top hero is a loyalty recruitment play: "MEMBER EXCLUSIVE | Spend $150+ Get 20% Off" with a "Join Now" CTA pointing non-members to the XPLR Pass signup. Below that sits the same "Up to 25% Off Select Gear" offer from the May 16 send, now playing second fiddle behind a loyalty gate.

The structural problem is split intent. One email is trying to do two things — recruit loyalty members and drive Memorial Day sale traffic — and neither conversion gets full creative commitment. Worse, the spend-threshold offer (20% off $150+) is a regression from the clean, threshold-free 25% off promoted on May 14 and May 16. 1. **Commit to one conversion goal.** The Join Now + Shop Sale split halves both click rates. A last-chance subject with "ends Sunday" or "ends tomorrow" is the highest-leverage change for open rate on a final Memorial Day send. The May 16 send scored a 10 partly because the deadline was front-loaded.

4. **Name and link the jacket.** The blue jacket model is the most eye-catching image in the email — give it a product name and a direct PDP link. That's a free additional click entry point.

## Technical Audit — The North Face "Get 20% off when you spend $150+"

### 1. Technical Summary

Three CSS authoring defects will silently break font rendering across all clients; compliance headers (List-Unsubscribe, Authentication-Results) are absent or unverifiable via the AgentMail relay, creating CAN-SPAM and deliverability risk. All other categories are clean or low-severity.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**27 click-redirect links skipped** — All CTAs route through `e.thenorthface.com` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud redirect). Destination URLs and UTM completeness cannot be verified from the relay-captured source.

**Plain-text body is 78% URLs** — QA measured 5,430 of 6,956 plain-text characters as URLs. Spam filters (SpamAssassin `URI_RATIO`, Postmark scoring) penalize ratios above ~60%. This is likely caused by an auto-generated plain-text part that dumps raw redirect URLs instead of human-readable text with inline links.

No broken or malformed `href` values visible in the truncated source.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[High] Missing `font-family:` property keyword in two CSS rules**

```css
/* As written — bare string, not a valid declaration */
table, td { 'HelveticaNeue-Medium', 'Helvetica Neue Medium', ... }
h1, h2, h3, h4, h5, h6 { 'HelveticaNeue-Medium', ... !important; margin: 0px; }
```

Both rules omit `font-family:` before the value. All email clients will silently drop these declarations; tables and headings will fall back to the client's default serif/sans. Needs `font-family:` inserted.

**[High] Malformed `style` attribute on `<body>` — missing semicolon after font-family stack**

```html
<body style="font-family:..., sans-serif margin: 0; padding: 0;">
```

`sans-serif` is not followed by `;` before `margin`. The CSS parser treats `sans-serif margin` as a continuation of the font-family value, causing `margin: 0` and `padding: 0` to be silently discarded. Affects the full-body layout in strict CSS parsers (Apple Mail, newer Gmail).

**[Low] Spacer image missing `alt` attribute**

```
src: https://image.e.thenorthface.com/lib/.../TNF-spacer-100px_01.jpg
```

No `alt` attribute present. For a spacer, the correct fix is `alt=""` (empty string) — screen readers will skip it. Absence triggers WCAG 1.1.1 violations and may increase spam score in some filters.

**[Info] Unclosed `@media` block in non-Outlook conditional**

The `<!--[if false]><!--> ... <!--<![endif]-->` block at line ~65 contains an `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)` rule whose closing `}` for the `*[class*="show"]` block is absent in the captured source (may be a truncation artifact — flag for verification against full source).

### 5. Compliance
