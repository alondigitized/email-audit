# The Official Performance Apparel Partner of U.S. Ski & Snowboard
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-12**
## Executive summary

- This is a brand-partnership storytelling email, not a promotional send. The North Face is announcing (or reminding subscribers of) its role as the official performance apparel partner of U.S. Ski & Snowboard — leaning heavily into the "Every mountain. Every medal." hero line and athlete credibility. The visual execution is tight: dark navy palette, clean product photography, and a readable hierarchy from hero to product callouts to footer Shop Now buttons.
- The fatal flaw is timing. This lands May 12 — deep into spring, with ski resorts closed across the US. A partnership announcement that belonged in November or January is now just noise. Without any offer, discount, or time-bound reason to act, the "Shop Now" CTA asks a lot from a reader who just put away their ski gear two months ago.
- Solid brand work, wrong month. It will get opens from loyal TNF fans and almost nothing from casual subscribers.

## What's working

- **Hero copy lands fast.** "Every mountain. Every medal." is legible, punchy, and fits the premium athletic positioning in two seconds flat.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean.** The email reads top-to-bottom without confusion — hero, product 1, copy block, product 2, CTA.
- **Product specificity mid-scroll.** Named products ("Unisex US Ski Relaxed Hoodie," "Unisex US Ski Relaxed Base Tee") appear with product images, not just a generic collection link.
- **No render bugs.** Images load, text is legible, no layout breaks or overlapping elements visible.
- **XPLR Pass loyalty callout in footer** gives members a nudge, even if understated.

## What's weak

- **Off-season send.** A ski performance email in mid-May is a misfire on calendar. The audience's mental model for ski gear is firmly dormant.
- **Zero offer.** No price reduction, no XPLR member exclusive, no free shipping — just "Shop Now" into the void. There's no transactional hook.
- **"Shop Now" CTA is too generic.** Used twice, both times identically, both pointing to what appears to be a collection rather than a specific product. It undersells the product copy that precedes it.
- **Subject reads like a press release.** "The Official Performance Apparel Partner of U.S. Ski & Snowboard" is 64 characters of declarative corporate language. It doesn't give the reader a reason to open beyond brand loyalty.
- **Partnership story is thin.** The copy block mentions 2022 and 2024 Olympics but doesn't show athlete faces or quote anyone. The proof point is present but not humanized.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Reframe for spring/summer end-of-season clearance.** If this collection has inventory to move, lead with a 20–30% off close-out on ski gear. That gives the off-season timing a purpose and makes every other element work harder.
- 2. **Replace both "Shop Now" buttons with product-specific CTAs.** "Shop the Relaxed Hoodie" or "Get the US Ski Base Tee" matches the product name visible just above.
- 3. **Add an athlete face and quote.** One photo of a named US Ski athlete + a one-liner creates the human trust signal the partnership story needs.
- 4. **Surface XPLR Pass benefits above the fold.** If members get early access or a discount, say so in the hero section — not the footer.
- 5. **Tighten and rewrite the subject:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Gear the US Ski team trusts — now 25% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `End-of-season: US Ski collection, official kit`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `We've outfitted the US team since 2017. Now you can wear it.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The Olympic-backed hoodie and base tee, clearance priced.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Bug 1 — Missing `font-family:` property name in two CSS rules (silent drop)**

```css
/* As written — invalid, silently ignored */
table, td {
    'HelveticaNeue-Medium', 'Helvetica Neue Medium', 'Helvetica Neue', Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif;
}
h1, h2, h3, h4, h5, h6 {
    'HelveticaNeue-Medium', 'Helvetica Neue Medium', 'Helvetica Neue', Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif !important;
}
```

Both declarations start with the font value string with no `font-family:` property name. Every CSS parser will discard these rules. Tables and headings will fall back to the client's default serif/sans-serif instead of Helvetica Neue.

**Bug 2 — Missing semicolon in `<body>` inline style**

```html
<body style="font-family:'HelveticaNeue-Medium', ... sans-serif margin: 0; padding: 0;">
```

The `;` after the font stack is absent, so `margin: 0` is parsed as a continuation of the `font-family` value and discarded. The `margin`/`padding` resets on `<body>` do not apply inline (the `<style>` block rule with `!important` compensates, but the inline intent is broken).

**Bug 3 — Unclosed `@media` block in conditional style block**

The `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)` rule inside the `<!--[if false]><!-->` block appears to lack a closing `}` before `</style>`. If confirmed, all CSS rules after the media query in that block would be scoped inside it in non-Outlook clients, breaking `.hide`/`.show` responsive toggling.

**Bug 4 — Invalid table nesting**

```html
<table bgcolor="#f4f4f4" style="width:100%...">
  <!-- missing <tr><td> -->
  <table id="backgroundWrapper" ...>
```

The outer wrapper `<table>` nests `<table id="backgroundWrapper">` with no intervening `<tr><td>`. This is invalid HTML; strict-mode clients and accessibility tools may reorder or drop content.

**Accessibility:** HTML is truncated — `alt` attribute completeness on images cannot be fully audited. The missing `font-family` on `table`/`td` also affects screen reader font inheritance in some clients.

| `List-Unsubscribe` header | **MISSING** | QA WARN: "Header may not be captured by AgentMail relay" |
| `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) | **MISSING** | QA WARN: one-click unsubscribe not supported |

The missing `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are a **policy violation** for senders exceeding 5,000 messages/day to Gmail or Yahoo (enforced since February 2024). Without these, mailbox providers will not surface the native unsubscribe UI, increasing spam complaint rates and risking deliverability demotion.

The `Authentication-Results` gap means SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment cannot be confirmed. The sending domain is `e.thenorthface.com` (an ESP subdomain); DKIM alignment against `thenorthface.com` should be verified directly against the received headers.
