# Explore best-sellers for campus and beyond
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-14**
## Executive summary

- This is a best-sellers roundup — hero image of two people lounging in campus gear, then a long scroll of individual products with star ratings and customer quotes, segmented by Women's/Men's/Bags with "Shop" CTAs breaking up each section. It's a fundamentally different structure from the last five sends (the "$150+, 20% off" backpack promo and the campus bag pushes) — no discount anywhere, no urgency, just "tried, true and loved by all." That's a deliberate change of pace after a stretch of near-identical promotional emails, and the social-proof format (star ratings, real-sounding quotes) is a nice trust-building beat this brand hasn't used before in this history.
- But there's no offer at all, which caps how hard this email can pull compared to the discount-anchored sends that scored 7-8. It reads more like a catalog page than a marketing email — good for browsing, weak for converting someone who wasn't already shopping. Given the brand has been leaning hard into backpacks and campus gear all summer (July 20, July 31, Aug 6/7/10), this send is a reasonable palate-cleanser but not a strong performer on its own.

## What's working

- Clean, consistent product-card layout — image, star rating, product name, customer quote, color swatches repeated identically for every item, easy to scan on mobile.
- Real-sounding customer quotes ("I love how well it layers and keeps me dry," "I've never used shorts with such good elasticity and performance") add credibility no prior email in this history has used.
- Section breaks with dedicated CTAs ("Shop Women's Best Sellers," "Shop Men's Best Sellers," "Shop Backpacks") give the eye clear rest points and multiple entry paths.
- Hero image and headline ("Campus favorites") land the theme immediately without clutter.
- XPLR Pass loyalty banner is present at both top and bottom, consistent with brand identity.

## What's weak

- Zero concrete offer — no %/$ off, no free shipping, nothing — a hard drop-off from the 20%-off run this brand was just on.
- The email is extremely long (11 products deep) with no way to jump to a category from the top; a mobile reader has to scroll past the entire women's section to reach men's.
- Star ratings show no numeric rating or review count, just stars and one quote — less credible than a "4.8 (312 reviews)" treatment would be.
- Every product card is visually identical (image, stars, name, quote, dots) — after the third one it starts to blur together with no visual hierarchy distinguishing "best of the best" from the rest.
- No urgency or reason to act now — nothing timestamps this as a "this week" email.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a real offer or at minimum a value hook (e.g., "Free shipping over $50" or a loyalty-tier callout) — this is the single biggest lever missing versus the brand's higher-scoring recent sends.
- 2. Add a jump-nav or shorten the list to top 3-4 per section with a "See all best-sellers" link instead of 11 full cards — cuts scroll fatigue significantly.
- 3. Show numeric ratings + review counts next to the stars to strengthen the social proof that's already half-built here.
- 4. Vary the visual treatment for the top 1-2 "hero" bestsellers (larger image, badge like "#1 rated") so the eye has somewhere to land first.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Real reviews, real favorites — shop what's trending this term`
