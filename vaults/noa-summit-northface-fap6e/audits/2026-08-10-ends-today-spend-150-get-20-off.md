# Ends today: Spend $150+, get 20% off
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-10**
## Executive summary

- This is the third send of the exact same "Spend $150+, get 20% off" backpack promo in five days — August 6, then a same-day repeat on August 7 with an added urgency word, and now this. The creative is completely unchanged: the same four backpacks (Recon, Hot Shot SE, Jester, Borealis) in the same order, same annotated-callout layout, same black hero block. The only variable across three sends has been the subject line getting incrementally more urgent — "Spend $150+" → "Don't miss it" → now "Ends today."
- The execution itself is still clean and on-brand: clear hero offer, strong annotated product diagrams, consistent black/white/loyalty-badge branding, no render bugs. But this is the exhaustion point for that creative. A subscriber who opened the July 31 or August 6 version has now seen this exact layout for the third time in ten days with zero new information except a countdown clock. The "Ends today" framing is doing real work to justify one more open, but if it's not actually the last day, this pattern erodes trust in future urgency claims.
- Judgement: solid standalone execution, weak as the third beat of an identical campaign. The deadline framing earns this one a click from people who were on the fence, but the brand is spending its urgency credibility on a stale creative.

## What's working

- "Ends today" is a genuinely strong, credible urgency lever — much sharper than August 7's softer "Don't miss it."
- The hero offer (Spend $150+, Get 20% Off) is immediately visible with no scrolling, in bold white-on-black type.
- Four distinct backpacks with clear "Best for:" use-case labels give shoppers an easy way to self-select without reading paragraphs.
- Annotated feature callouts (laptop sleeve size, water-repellent finish, suspension system) are specific and useful for a purchase decision.
- No layout or image-render issues — everything is crisp and aligned.

## What's weak

- Zero creative variation from the August 6 and August 7 sends — same four products, same order, same photography, same layout.
- "Ends today" contradicts the brand's own recent pattern (this exact offer already "ended" once on August 7's subject line and reappeared) — if it doesn't actually end today, this undercuts future urgency claims.
- No price shown on any product — shoppers can't gauge whether they'll clear the $150 threshold without leaving the email.
- No countdown timer or explicit end time/date to back up "Ends today," which is a missed opportunity given this is the strongest lever in the email.
- Cadence problem: this is a near-identical creative for the third time in five days, which risks fatigue/unsubscribes more than it drives incremental clicks.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap the creative for this final "ends today" push — even reshuffling to lead with a different backpack or adding a real-time countdown timer would break the repetition and justify the third send.
- 2. Add a visible countdown timer or explicit cutoff time (e.g., "Ends tonight at 11:59pm ET") to make "Ends today" concrete and credible rather than a repeated generic urgency phrase.
- 3. Show prices on at least the hero product so shoppers can see how close they are to the $150 threshold at a glance.
- 4. If this truly is the last chance on this offer, say so explicitly in the body copy ("last chance," "final hours") to differentiate it from the two prior sends.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last chance: 20% off ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Final hours: $150+ = 20% off backpacks`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Grab a Recon, Jester, Hot Shot SE or Borealis before midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your backpack pick, 20% off — offer closes tonight.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit: The North Face — "Ends today: Spend $150+, get 20% off"

### 1. Technical Summary
Email uses a standard Bronto/Oracle-style responsive table layout with MSO conditionals; core compliance headers (List-Unsubscribe, DKIM/SPF authentication) were not detected, and a tracking pixel is wrapped in a clickable anchor rather than served as a bare `<img>`.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Pixel wrapped in a live link**: the honeypot/tracking pixel (`https://image.e.thenorthface.com/lib/fe3811717164047a741772/m/1/honeypot_pixel.png`) is nested inside `<a href="https://click.e.thenorthface.com/?qs=ABB7InYi...">` with `target="_blank"`. A 1×1 tracking pixel does not need to be clickable — if this `<a>` wrapper unintentionally extends over adjacent content in some clients, it risks accidental navigation on tap.
- 35 tracking/click-redirect links were present but skipped by the automated HTTP probe (expected behavior for redirect domains — not itself a defect, but destinations are unverified).
- Plain-text alternative is 79.8% URLs (6,130/7,684 chars) — QA flagged this as a deliverability risk; excessive link-to-text ratio in the plain-text part is a common spam-filter signal.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- `honeypot_pixel.png` is missing `alt` text (confirmed by QA). Low visual impact (image is 1×1 and hidden), but screen readers/accessibility scanners will still flag it.
- Preheader text contains a long run of `&nbsp;&zwnj;` padding characters — functions as intended (pushes preview text) but adds noticeable weight to the very top of the DOM; no functional defect.
- Extensive MSO/Outlook conditional comments (`<!--[if gte mso 9]>`, `<!--[if false]><!-->`) and Yahoo/Samsung-specific overrides are present and correctly structured — no malformed conditional tags found in the visible source.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No merge/personalization tokens (e.g. `%%FIRSTNAME%%`, `{{ }}`, `[[ ]]`) are present in the visible HTML source. No issues found in the truncated portion reviewed — cannot confirm the state of the body content beyond this excerpt.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header not found** — required for one-click unsubscribe support in Gmail/Yahoo and best-practice for CAN-SPAM; may be a relay-capture artifact (AgentMail) rather than a true absence, but unconfirmed.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found (RFC 8058)** — without this, one-click unsubscribe cannot function even if List-Unsubscribe is present.
- **Authentication-Results header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown via the relay; cannot confirm sender authentication from available data.
- Physical mailer address/unsubscribe footer link were not visible in the truncated HTML — cannot confirm CAN-SPAM footer compliance from the excerpt provided.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- The primary CTA link routes through `click.e.thenorthface.com/?qs=ABB7...` (opaque Bronto-style encoded redirect token), so UTM parameters and final landing-page URL cannot be verified from the source alone — the 35 skipped tracking links fall into this same category.
- No directly-exposed destination URLs with UTM query strings are present in the visible source to check against a landing page.

### 7. Recommendations
1. Remove the `<a>` wrapper around the honeypot pixel, or confirm it's intentional tracking behavior — a bare `<img>` is standard for pixels.
2. Add `alt=""` to the honeypot pixel (empty alt is correct for a decorative/non-content tracking image).
3. Verify List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post headers are actually being sent at the MTA level — the current audit trail cannot rule out relay capture loss vs. true absence.
4. Confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment via a raw header capture outside the relay, since Authentication-Results wasn't observable here.
5. Reduce the plain-text part's URL-to-text ratio (currently ~80%) by adding descriptive surrounding text, to lower spam-filter risk.
6. Resolve/expand the `click.e.thenorthface.com` redirect chain to confirm final landing-page URL and UTM parameters match campaign intent.
