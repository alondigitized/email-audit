# Homepage snapshot · www.thenorthface.com · 2026-05-16
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- TNF is running hard on Memorial Day sale mode right now — the discount message hits twice before I've even touched the nav, and the XPLR Pass prompt is practically standing in the doorway. It reads like a strong, confident brand that knows how to move product during a sale window, but the homepage is set up for acquisition, not for a returning member who's already in the ecosystem and actually wants to find technical gear fast.

## What's working

- **Double-reinforced sale message**: "Up to 25% Off Select Gear / Ends 5.25" appears in both the sticky top banner AND the hero. I read it twice before I've done anything. That repetition is doing real work — I'm already calculating whether my wishlist items are in scope.
- **Deadline specificity**: "Ends 5.25" is concrete. Not "limited time" — an actual date. I'll plan around that.
- **Landscape photography quality**: That canyon shot is genuinely stunning. High-production, full-bleed, no clutter. This is the visual shorthand for why I buy TNF over a cheaper alternative — the brand earns premium positioning even in a discount moment.
- **XPLR Pass placement for non-members**: The 10% offer in the modal is well-timed for someone who hasn't joined yet. Strong acquisition moment.

## What's weak

- **Modal doesn't recognize me as a member**: I'm already XPLR Pass. Seeing "Join Now" feels like the site has no memory of me. This should flip to a member-state message ("10% earned on every purchase" or "You're earning points on this sale") for logged-in Pass holders.
- **Hero CTA is physically blocked**: The XPLR modal sits directly on top of the primary button. I can see the white button shape; I cannot read a single word of its copy. That's the main conversion action for this page and it's invisible.
- **"Select gear" is doing zero work**: Nowhere above the fold does it say whether Nuptse, Summit Series, Denali, or technical layers are actually in the sale. That vagueness makes me skeptical rather than excited.
- **Hamburger-only navigation**: No category strip, no quick-links. I'm one tap from the homepage and still two or three taps away from any actual product category. On mobile that friction compounds fast.
- **Zero social proof**: No review counts, no bestseller badges, no "our top-rated jackets" hook. Nothing that tells me what's worth prioritizing in the sale.

## Recommendations

- **Swap the XPLR modal for a member-state CTA**: For logged-in Pass members, suppress the join prompt and show a "Your points balance: X" or "10% is already applied — start shopping" message in the nav or a small banner. Reserve the join modal for new visitors only.
- **Specify what's actually on sale in the hero**: Add a single line under the headline — "Nuptse, Denali, Summit Series & more" — that specificity turns a generic sale into a purchase intent trigger for someone like me.
- **Add a two-tile category strip below the hero**: "Jackets & Vests" and "Technical Gear" (or Summit Series) at minimum. Let me tap through in one step without opening a hamburger menu on a sale day.
- **Test a layered lifestyle shot alongside the landscape**: The canyon is beautiful but it shows no product on body. A shot of someone my age in a Summit Series + Nuptse layering system would move this from aspirational to covetable.
