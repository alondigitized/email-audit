# Homepage snapshot · www.thenorthface.com · 2026-06-11
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so TNF is leaning fully into the aspirational outdoor lifestyle right now — four-panel editorial grid, no headline text, pure visual storytelling. It reads like a brand that trusts its imagery to do the work, which honestly I respect, but it also leaves me with zero directional pull above the fold. There's a rewards nudge at the very top which is a nice touch, but the page is missing the thing I actually need: a reason to tap.

## What's working

- **Rewards banner placement:** It's the literal first pixel I see, which is smart — as an XPLR Pass member I'm primed to respond to this. Even if it's just a login nudge, it signals the brand knows I have a relationship with them.
- **Top-right panel (trail runner with hydration vest):** This image is basically me on a Saturday. Young woman, smiling, in a light layer with a run vest and bandana — I would wear every single piece in that frame. It's the most compelling panel on screen.
- **Four-panel grid layout:** Showing multiple use cases (dog walk, trail run, layering close-up, casual outdoor) in one swipe is efficient. I don't have to wonder if TNF still does the stuff I care about.
- **No modal interruptions:** The page loaded without a pop-up demanding my email. The X on the rewards banner is visible and dismissible. I appreciate that.
- **Photography quality:** Earth tones, natural light, mountain horizon in the background — this is proper aspirational content, not a studio white-backdrop situation.

## What's weak

- **Zero hero copy:** There is no headline, no tagline, no campaign name, nothing. I have no idea what the narrative thread is — is this a new collection? A summer campaign? A sustainability story? The images are doing 100% of the work and that's a lot to ask.
- **No primary shop CTA anywhere above the fold:** I would have to tap the hamburger menu just to start navigating. That's friction. I know where to go, but a first-visit Millennial who crosses from Patagonia doesn't.
- **Rewards banner undersells the program:** "Check your status" is table stakes. Tell me my points balance. Tell me I'm 200 points from a tier unlock. Make me feel something about XPLR Pass instead of just reminding me it exists.
- **Bottom-left panel is the weakest creative:** A person with their face buried in a hood, completely obscured — cool editorial choice for print, but on mobile it communicates nothing. I don't see a product I want to buy, I see a photo concept.
- **No new-arrivals or category signal:** I track the seasonal drop calendar. I want to know if the Summit Series new colorways just landed or if the PFC-free Hydrenalite is back in stock. Nothing here tells me that.

## Recommendations

- **Add a single headline over the hero grid** — even four words like "Trail Season Is Here" or "Summer Layers, Zero PFC" would give the page a spine and give me a reason to scroll. The imagery earns it; the copy just needs to show up.
- **Replace the rewards banner copy with a live hook:** "You have X points — redeem now" or "Members earn 2x through June 15" converts a passive announcement into an action trigger. You already have my account data; use it.
- **Add a sticky or below-fold CTA row** with 3 category tiles (Women's Trail, Summit Series, New Arrivals) — single-tap navigation to my world. The hamburger menu is a dead end for impulse browsing.
- **Swap the bottom-left hooded-face panel** for a product-forward shot showing the actual layer — collar detail, zipper, something tactile. The mystery-face look doesn't move units on mobile.
