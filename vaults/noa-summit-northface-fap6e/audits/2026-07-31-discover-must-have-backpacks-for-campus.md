# Discover must-have backpacks for campus
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-31**
## Executive summary

- This is The North Face's second bag-focused send in eleven days — following July 20's "Campus-ready bags built for off-grid adventures" (8/10) — and the sixth campus-themed email in the past 24 days. The execution itself is clean and on-brand: a strong lifestyle hero, five well-organized product cards each with capacity/feature specs and a customer quote, and consistent XPLR Pass loyalty framing top and bottom. Nothing is broken, nothing looks stale creative-wise.
- The problem is category fatigue, not execution. A subscriber who opened the July 20 bags email is now getting a near-identical shopping experience — different specific packs, same "here are five backpacks, pick one" structure — less than two weeks later. There's also no discount, promo code, or urgency anywhere in the render; this is a pure browse-and-shop send leaning entirely on product merit and loyalty membership.
- Judgment: well-built, poorly sequenced. If this ran as the only backpack email of the back-to-school push it'd be strong; running it right behind the July 20 bags send makes it feel like the same email twice.

## What's working

- Hero copy ("Packs that got your back") ties directly to the campus/backpack use case with a clear "All Backpacks" CTA button.
- Each of the five products shows color-swatch options, a customer quote, and a "BEST FOR" spec list (capacity, laptop sleeve size, suspension system) — genuinely useful shopping detail.
- Star ratings appear on the Borealis and Recon cards, adding social proof where present.
- A secondary "Find Your Store" CTA is dropped mid-scroll, giving a non-purchase-ready reader another action.
- XPLR Pass loyalty banner (points, free shipping) bookends the email, reinforcing membership value without being pushy.

## What's weak

- No concrete offer anywhere — no %/$ off, no BOGO, no promo code. It's a pure catalog scroll.
- Star ratings are inconsistent: Jester, Hot Shot SE, and Women's Surge Luxe show no rating stars while Borealis and Recon do, which reads unfinished.
- Zero urgency or time-bounding — nothing signals why a reader should act today versus next week.
- This is the second dedicated bag/backpack send in 11 days after July 20's bags email, which will read as repetitive to anyone who opened both.
- Subject line ("Discover must-have backpacks for campus") uses the generic, low-curiosity verb "Discover" rather than naming a hook (a bestseller, a price, a feature).

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a real hook to the hero or subject — a specific offer, a "new for fall" flag, or a loyalty-points multiplier — since right now nothing differentiates this from July 20's bags email or justifies opening it a second time in the same category.
- 2. Fix the inconsistent star ratings — either show them on all five product cards or drop them entirely; the mix looks like a template error.
- 3. Lead with the strongest single product (e.g., Borealis, which already has both stars and a strong quote) as a full-width hero card instead of five equal-weight cards — gives the eye somewhere to land first.
- 4. Space this out from the July 20 bags send in future planning — if backpacks need two touches during back-to-school, differentiate them by angle (e.g., "new colorways" vs. "most durable") rather than repeating the same five-card format.
- 5. Add a small urgency or scarcity line ("Back-to-school bestsellers — while sizes last") to give a reason to act now.

- **Subject Alt B:** `New backpack colorways just landed for campus`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `5 packs built for heavy textbooks, laptops, and long days`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Earn XPLR Pass points on every backpack, all week`
