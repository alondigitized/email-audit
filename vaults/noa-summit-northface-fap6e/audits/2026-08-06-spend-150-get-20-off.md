# Spend $150+, get 20% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-06**
## Executive summary

- This is The North Face's return to bags after the brief single-product detour of August 4's "Introducing Offtrail Ultra" (7/10, trail running shoe launch). It's structurally closer to July 31's "Discover must-have backpacks for campus" (9/10) — same four-pack layout of Recon, Hot Shot SE, Jester, and Borealis — but now wrapped in a sitewide "Spend $150+, get 20% off" threshold discount instead of a pure editorial pitch. The mechanic is new for this stretch of sends; the backpacks are not.
- The execution is clean and on-brand: bold black hero block, XPLR Pass member-exclusive framing top and bottom, tidy product grid with no render bugs. But the strategy is thin — this is now the brand's third or fourth backpack-forward email in about three weeks, and the hero copy leads entirely with the discount mechanic rather than any product story, which makes it feel like a generic sitewide-sale template dropped onto last month's product lineup.
- Solid, safe, and slightly fatigued. It'll perform on the strength of the loyalty discount alone, not on creative differentiation.

## What's working

- The offer is stated with real numbers ("$150+", "20% off") and a hard end date ("ENDS 8.10") right in the hero — no ambiguity about what's on the table.
- "MEMBER EXCLUSIVE" framing plus the XPLR Pass badge top and bottom gives loyalty members a reason to feel specifically targeted.
- Clean visual hierarchy: black hero block anchors the eye immediately, followed by a consistent product grid (image, "Best For" tag, name, one-line pitch).
- Fine print ("*Sale excluded. Additional exclusions & terms apply") is visible in the hero, not buried at the bottom — reduces bait-and-switch feel even if the exclusions themselves are vague.
- Two clear CTAs — "Shop Now" for the discount, "Shop Backpacks" for the category — with no broken images or layout issues.

## What's weak

- Hero copy never mentions backpacks, campus, or any category — it's a generic discount block sitting on top of a bag-specific product grid, so subject and hero both undersell what's actually being sold.
- This is the brand's fourth-plus bag-focused send since July 20 (July 20 bags, July 22 campus best-sellers, July 31 backpacks, now this) — same four products reappearing again with no new creative angle.
- No pricing, sizing, or availability info on any of the four backpacks — just a tag line and one sentence, which won't help anyone compare or decide.
- No social proof or reviews anywhere, despite Borealis literally being pitched as "a back-to-campus essential" — that claim would land harder with a rating or review count next to it.
- "Shop Now," the primary CTA, is generic — it doesn't tell the reader what they're shopping for, unlike the more specific "Shop Backpacks" further down.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the category in the hero. Change the headline to lead with what the discount unlocks — e.g. pair "Spend $150+, Get 20% Off" with a subhead like "On backpacks, apparel & more" so the offer and the product grid feel like one campaign, not two stapled together.
- 2. Swap "Shop Now" for a specific CTA — "Shop the Sale" or "Shop $150+ & Save" — so the primary button does more work before the reader even scrolls.
- 3. Retire or refresh the four-pack product lineup — Recon/Hot Shot SE/Jester/Borealis has now run in at least two recent sends; rotate in a fifth product or add a star rating/review count to justify showing the same four again.
- 4. Tighten the exclusions language — "Additional exclusions & terms apply" reads vague; naming the categories that are excluded (or confirming "most items included") builds more trust than a blanket disclaimer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `XPLR Pass exclusive: 20% off $150+`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Backpacks + 20% off — ends 8.10`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Recon, Jester, Borealis & more — all eligible through 8.10.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your XPLR Pass discount is live. Shop bags before it ends.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Invalid CSS declarations (missing property name):**
  - `table, td { 'HelveticaNeue-Medium', 'Helvetica Neue Medium', ... sans-serif; }` — missing `font-family:` before the value list. This rule is invalid and will be dropped by all rendering engines.
  - `h1, h2, h3, h4, h5, h6 { 'HelveticaNeue-Medium', ... !important; margin: 0px; }` — same issue; the font-family value has no property name, so only `margin: 0px` will apply.
- **Inline body style missing a semicolon:** `style="font-family:'HelveticaNeue-Medium', ... sans-serif margin: 0; padding: 0;"` — no `;` terminates the font stack before `margin: 0`, so `margin` and `padding` are parsed as part of the (invalid) `font-family` value and will not apply to `<body>`.
- **Malformed/non-standard conditional comment:** `<!--[if true]> ... <![endif]-->` wraps MSO-specific rules (`mso-line-height-rule`, `mso-table-lspace/rspace`, `li` indent, `.desktop-content` display). `[if true]` is not valid Outlook/Word conditional-comment syntax (valid forms are `[if mso]`, `[if gte mso 9]`, `[if !mso]`, etc.). This looks like an unresolved ESP template variable rather than an intentional MSO gate, meaning these Outlook-targeted rules may not render as intended in Outlook desktop.
- **Tracking pixel missing `alt` text:** `honeypot_pixel.png` (`https://image.e.thenorthface.com/lib/fe3811717164047a741772/m/1/honeypot_pixel.png`) has no alt attribute. Low practical impact since it's a 1×1 tracking pixel not meant to be visible, but flagged per QA.
