# Keep the wind out—and warmth in.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- This is a product-showcase email for The North Face's wind/waterproof shell lineup — five specific jackets presented in alternating image-copy rows, with the Memorial Day sale banner surfacing only at the very bottom. The strategic logic is sound: use the sale window to drive discovery of specific performance outerwear, not just blast "25% off" again. The problem is execution. The hero communicates functional benefit ("keep the wind out") but buries the discount until after you've scrolled past five jacket cards. Anyone who doesn't scroll far enough sees no price signal at all.
- Coming off two near-identical Memorial Day blast emails on May 14 and May 16, this pivot to product storytelling is the right move — but the email is too long and too fragmented to close anyone. Five separate products with no ranked priority, no comparative pricing, and no single hero product to click dilutes the send.
- Verdict: a defensible creative direction executed without enough editorial discipline. The brand is sending into fatigue (three emails in four days) with its weakest call-to-action of the week.

## What's working

- **Subject line is genuinely distinctive.** "Keep the wind out—and warmth in." is evocative, product-specific, and a clean break from two consecutive "25% off" hammer subjects.
- **Shell-focused lineup is seasonally credible.** Wind/waterproof jackets in mid-May (pre-summer hiking season) is the right product category to feature.
- **Loyalty program footer visible.** TNF Peak® logo appears at the bottom — at least acknowledges the membership tier.
- **Sale banner at the bottom provides a price hook** for anyone who does scroll through — ties this email back to the running Memorial Day offer.
- **Alternating image/copy layout** creates visual rhythm down the scroll; none of the product images appear broken.

## What's weak

- **No price or offer in the hero.** The above-fold experience is pure brand/functional copy with zero price signal — a subscriber who bounces after the hero has no reason to click.
- **Five jackets, five equal weights.** There's no editorial hierarchy — no "this is the one" hero product. The eye doesn't know where to go.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** Third North Face email in four days. The subject pivots the creative, but the audience is already conditioned to the Memorial Day drum — this lands soft.
- **Product copy is illegible at mobile-preview scale.** The body copy under each jacket name renders very small in the screenshot; on an actual mobile inbox, detail copy likely drops below comfortable reading size.
- **No urgency signal until the very bottom.** The May 25 deadline visible in prior sends is absent from the hero — a missed lever when the sale has 8 days left.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the sale callout into the hero.** Add "Up to 25% off through May 25" as a subhead or badge on the hero image. This is the single highest-impact change — it converts a brand/storytelling email into an action email without changing the creative direction.
- 2. **Pick one hero jacket.** Feature the Polartec WINDFORCE® or HiCo Devils Thumb as the editorial lead with a full-width CTA, then surface the others as secondary. One product to click > five products to consider.
- 3. **Add a deadline line above the bottom CTA.** "Sale ends May 25 — shop the full wind shell lineup" gives the scroll-to-bottom reader an urgency nudge.
- 4. **Trim to three jackets max.** Five product rows with body copy is newsletter length, not email length. Cut the two weakest performers from the lineup to tighten the scroll.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Built for the ridge. 25% off this week only.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `5 shells designed to keep the elements out — on sale now`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — The North Face "Keep the wind out—and warmth in."

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email contains two confirmed CSS parse bugs that silently drop font-family declarations across most elements, and a malformed `<body>` style attribute that defeats margin/padding resets. Compliance header capture is incomplete via the AgentMail relay, and the plain-text body's URL density is a deliverability risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Tracking links not probed (25 links)**
All 25 click-redirect URLs were skipped by the HTTP probe. Destination integrity, UTM parameter presence, and link rot cannot be confirmed from this audit. Manual spot-check is required.

**Plain-text body — URL density**
`6341 / 7884 chars (80%)` of the plain-text part are URLs. This exceeds the 70% threshold flagged by spam filters (Barracuda, SpamAssassin `URI_NO_ANCHOR` class rules). The plain-text alternative should contain readable copy, not a dump of redirect URLs.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Bug 1 — `font-family` missing property name in `table, td` and heading rules**

```css
/* AS WRITTEN — invalid, silently ignored */
table, td {
  'HelveticaNeue-Medium', 'Helvetica Neue Medium', 'Helvetica Neue', Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif;
}
h1, h2, h3, h4, h5, h6 {
  'HelveticaNeue-Medium', ... !important;
}
```

Both rules list font values as bare strings without the `font-family:` property name. Every CSS parser discards these declarations. Neither tables/cells nor headings will inherit the intended typeface — clients fall back to their own defaults.

**Fix:** Prepend `font-family:` to both declarations.

---

**Bug 2 — Missing semicolon in `<body>` inline style**

```html
<body style="font-family:'HelveticaNeue-Medium', ..., sans-serif margin: 0; padding: 0;">
```

There is no `;` between `sans-serif` and `margin`. The inline style parser merges `sans-serif margin: 0` into a single invalid token; `margin: 0` and `padding: 0` are never applied to `<body>`. This can cause unintended spacing in clients that enforce body margin (older Android Gmail, Samsung Mail).

**Fix:** Insert `;` after `sans-serif`.

---

**Bug 3 — Unclosed `@media (max-width: 480px)` block**

The `<!--[if false]><!-->` conditional style block's 480 px media query ends mid-rule in the truncated source. If the closing `}` for the media query is absent in the live send, styles declared inside leak into the global scope in WebKit-based clients (iOS Mail, Apple Mail), potentially hiding or mis-sizing desktop-only elements.

**Verify:** Confirm the corresponding `}` is present after the `.showMobile`/`*[class*="show"]` rules.

---

**`align-content: center` on non-flex containers**

`align-content: center` appears on `.disclaimer` and `.showMobile`. This property only applies to flex/grid containers; it has no effect on `<td>` or block elements in email layout. Use `text-align: center` instead.

---

**No `alt` text confirmed in truncated source**
The source was truncated before `<img>` tags were visible. Verify all images carry descriptive `alt` attributes — images-off rendering (common in Outlook and corporate environments) requires it for accessibility and CAN-SPAM image-text balance.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not captured**
`[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found — Header may not be captured by AgentMail relay`

This header is required for Gmail's bulk-sender one-click unsubscribe UI (Google's Feb 2024 enforcement). Its absence in the captured headers — even if present at send time — means the relay is stripping or not forwarding it. Confirm the header is present in raw SMTP delivery to a non-relay inbox.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not captured**
`[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found`

Required by Gmail and Apple Mail 16+ for machine-readable one-click unsubscribe. Without it, Gmail degrades the unsubscribe experience and may suppress the email for high-complaint senders. Same relay-capture caveat applies.

**SPF/DKIM authentication unknown**
`[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found`

Cannot confirm DMARC alignment. Sending domain is `e.thenorthface.com` (ESP subdomain). Verify DKIM `d=` tag aligns with the From header domain for DMARC pass. If not aligned, Gmail and Yahoo's 2024 bulk-sender policy will route to spam.

All 25 CTAs use click-redirect domains and were not probed. UTM parameter coverage (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content`) cannot be confirmed. Because all links pass through a redirect layer, a missing UTM on any link will result in direct/none attribution for that click in GA4.

**Action required:** Manually follow 3–5 representative CTAs through the redirect chain and verify final landing URL contains full UTM parameters.
