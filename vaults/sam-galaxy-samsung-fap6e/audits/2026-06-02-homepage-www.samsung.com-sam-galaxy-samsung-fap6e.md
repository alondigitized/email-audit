# Homepage snapshot · www.samsung.com · 2026-06-02
**Score:** 2/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- Samsung's homepage is currently invisible to me — a full-screen cookie consent wall is the entire above-the-fold experience. Behind it I can just make out a colorful illustrated scene with what looks like a washing machine, a tablet, and some kind of sporty lifestyle graphic, but I can't interact with any of it until I click through three buttons. For a Galaxy loyalist who opened this to check pre-order status or trade-in options, this is a wall between me and the thing I care about.

## What's working

- **Modal renders cleanly** — the three-button layout (Configure / Accept All / Decline All) is unambiguous. I know exactly what each button does.
- **"Decline All" is present** — as a privacy-aware millennial, I appreciate they don't hide the opt-out. It's right there, same visual weight as "Accept All."
- **Colorful illustrated backdrop** — the sliver of content I can see behind the modal looks vibrant and energetic. There's clearly a real homepage worth uncovering.

## What's weak

- **Modal completely blocks the homepage** — I came to check out the Galaxy lineup, not read a cookie policy. This is the worst possible first impression for a returning loyalist.
- **No preview of value behind the gate** — the modal has no headline, no offer teaser, nothing to tell me why I should care about accepting. I'm being asked to decide before I've seen anything.
- **Cookie text wall is dense** — the consent copy is four long paragraphs on a small mobile screen. No one reads this. It creates cognitive fatigue before I've even seen a product.
- **Behind-the-modal content is indeterminate** — the illustrated graphic I can partially see looks more like a playful kids campaign than a Galaxy Z Fold / S-series launch. If Samsung is pushing appliances right now, that's not my visit intent.

## Recommendations

- **Reduce the modal to a slim bottom bar** — show the homepage, let me feel the brand energy, then ask for consent in a dismissible banner at the bottom. This is a legal requirement, not a landing page.
- **Add a one-line value prop to the modal header** — even "Great deals on Galaxy devices" above the consent copy gives me a reason to hit Accept All and get in.
- **If there's a Galaxy trade-in or pre-order campaign active, surface it in the background** — make the blurred-out hero show something I recognize so the friction of the modal feels worth it.
- **Test a "Remember my choice" checkbox** — returning users like me shouldn't see this wall every visit. Samsung accounts could tie consent to login so loyalists get a clean experience.
