# Homepage snapshot · www.samsung.com · 2026-05-13
**Score:** 2/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- I landed on samsung.com and the first thing I see is a cookie consent modal taking over the entire screen. Behind it, I can just make out a dark shot of what looks like a Galaxy S-series camera array — which tells me the brand is pushing its flagship camera story — but I genuinely cannot see any of the homepage content, copy, offers, or navigation because this modal is eating everything. For a brand that should feel like home to me, this is a frustrating cold shoulder.

## What's working

- **Cookie modal itself renders crisply** — the three-button layout (Configure / Accept All / Decline All) has a clear visual hierarchy with the primary blue "Accept All" standing out. No overlapping text, no layout breakage.
- **Samsung wordmark is immediately visible** at the top of the modal, so I know I'm in the right place.
- **Camera array teaser** (bottom half of screen, barely visible) — even through the overlay, that multi-lens camera cluster screams Galaxy S25 Ultra or similar flagship. For me, that's a Pavlovian signal that there's something worth seeing if I can just get past this wall.

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage is blocked.** This is the central problem. No hero, no offer, no nav, no product — just a legal wall. I'm a returning Galaxy loyalist who came here to check pre-order details or trade-in windows, and I'm immediately treated like a suspect.
- **"Configure" as the first button** is confusing UX — it looks like the primary action but it's the most friction-heavy option. Most users (including me) will feel nudged rather than served.
- **Zero brand energy above the fold.** For a company going up against Apple's ultra-polished mobile experience, leading with a grey cookie modal is a brand-perception own-goal.
- **Privacy copy is dense and small.** The paragraph at the top is a wall of legalese — the kind of thing that makes even a technically comfortable 32-year-old skim and tap "Accept All" on autopilot just to get past it.

## Recommendations

- **Delay the cookie modal by 2-3 seconds or use a slim banner at the bottom** instead of a full-screen takeover — let the hero load first so returning shoppers can orient before hitting the legal wall.
- **Swap the consent layout** so "Accept All" is the first button (or use a standard browser-style bottom bar) — the current order (Configure → Accept All → Decline All) buries the fastest path to the site.
- **Let the background hero breathe** — even a semi-transparent modal would let me see the Galaxy campaign behind it, reinforcing brand energy while I make the consent choice.
- **Add a "You were browsing Galaxy S25 Ultra" returning-user nudge** somewhere near the modal for logged-in users — Galaxy loyalists like me trade in annually and a personalized hook would immediately differentiate from Apple.
