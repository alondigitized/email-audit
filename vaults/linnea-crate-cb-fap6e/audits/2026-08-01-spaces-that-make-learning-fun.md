# Spaces that make learning fun →
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-01**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids organizes this one around age brackets — 1-3, 4-6, 6+ — which is the right instinct: a parent can jump straight to their kid's stage instead of wading through a mixed collection dump like the 2026-08-01 "Shop furniture you'll love now & forever" send that stitched four unrelated collections together. Each age section gets its own staged room photo, two product tiles, and a specific CTA, which reads clean at a glance.
- The problem is what comes after the three age sections. This email doesn't stop at furniture — it keeps going into a "more for back-to-school" grid, a $3,500 sweepstakes banner, a "we love these for you" recommendation row, a credit-card cross-sell, and an app-download pitch. That's six distinct content blocks and asks in one send, and by the time a reader hits the sweepstakes they've already scrolled past three full furniture pitches. The core "up to 30% off" offer from the masthead never resurfaces as a price or percentage on any actual product tile, so the discount stays abstract.
- Solid structural idea, badly overloaded execution. This should have been two emails: one tight age-segmented furniture pitch, and a separate send for the sweepstakes/credit-card/app cross-sells.

## What's working

- Age-segmented sections (1-3, 4-6, 6+ years) let a parent self-select instantly instead of scanning a mixed grid.
- Each section pairs a real staged-room photo with matching product tiles, giving concrete visual context for how the furniture actually looks in a kid's space.
- Section CTAs are specific ("Shop Play Tables + Chairs," "Shop Book Nooks," "Shop Desks + Chairs") rather than a generic "Shop Now" repeated everywhere.
- No visible render bugs — images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, layout holds together despite the length.
- Back-to-school framing is well-timed for early August.

## What's weak

- No pricing or percentage badge appears on any individual product — the "up to 30% off" headline offer is stated once at the very top and never reinforced at the point of decision.
- The email keeps going well past the furniture pitch into a sweepstakes, a recommendation row, a credit-card offer, and an app download — six stacked asks with no single obvious "primary" CTA.
- Two "New Arrival" tags in the same section but nothing distinguishing which item is the better deal or bestseller.
- The $3,500 sweepstakes, a genuinely strong curiosity hook, is buried after three full furniture sections instead of leading or getting its own send.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into two sends: a tight "shop by age" furniture email (just the three sections) and a separate email for the sweepstakes/credit-card/app content. This is the single biggest lift — right now six asks compete for one click.
- 2. Put a visible price or "% off" tag directly on each product tile so the top-banner discount is reinforced where the decision actually happens.
- 3. Pick one primary CTA per email rather than seven equally-weighted buttons (three "Shop" buttons, "Shop Back to School," "Shop All Furniture," "Enter Now," "Learn More," "Download").
- 4. If keeping the sweepstakes in this send, move it above the fold or right after the hero — it's a stronger curiosity driver than the third furniture section it currently follows.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Back-to-school furniture, sorted by age`
- **Subject Alt B:** `30% off desks, book nooks & play tables`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop play spaces, book nooks, and study zones — plus a $3,500 sweepstakes inside.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `From toddler play tables to teen desks, up to 30% off through back-to-school.`
