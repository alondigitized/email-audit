# Get inspired: fresh finds for kid spaces
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-13**
## Executive summary

- This is a category-browse email, not a promo — "new arrivals for every kid's space," then bedroom, nursery, and playroom sections each ending in a "Shop All [X]" button, followed by a "we love these for you" recommendation grid and a credit-card cross-sell. It's essentially a catalog index dressed as an email: broad, comprehensive, and completely offer-free.
- Compared to the brand's recent sends — the back-to-school email (7.00/10) that led with one real hero product, or even the holiday first-look (6.00/10) that had a clear seasonal hook — this one reads flatter. There's no price, no discount, no urgency, nothing time-bound; it's four different room categories stacked into one long scroll with near-identical "Shop All" CTAs repeated four times, plus a loyalty card pitch and app download bolted on at the bottom.
- It's competently produced — clean product photography, consistent styling, no render bugs — but it's a low-intent "browse when you're ready" send, not a click-driver. Fine as a mid-cadence catalog touch, weak as a standalone send.

## What's working

- Clear category structure: bedroom → nursery → playroom → "easy playroom updates," each visually distinct and easy to scan.
- Product photography is consistent and appealing — realistic room vignettes (bunk bed scene, nursery corner, playroom shelving).
- "Bestseller," "Ships Free," and "GREENGUARD Gold Certified" badges on individual products add real, specific trust signals (safety-conscious parents care about GREENGUARD).
- No visible render bugs — text, images, and buttons all display cleanly through the full scroll.

## What's weak

- Zero concrete offer anywhere in the email — no %/$ off, no sale, nothing beyond "Shop Now."
- Four separate "Shop All [category]" CTAs dilute any single primary action — there's no hierarchy telling the eye where to go first.
- The hero says "500+ quality-crafted pieces" — a stat, not a reason to click today.
- Credit-card upsell ("the card you love just got better," Redeem Points) and app-download module at the bottom feel bolted-on and unrelated to kid-space browsing.
- Subject and hero are generic ("fresh finds," "new arrivals") — nothing seasonal or urgent, despite the mid-September timing when back-to-school/fall framing would fit (as the brand did in its 7.00/10 back-to-school send this same day).

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one hero product or a real offer instead of a generic "new arrivals" banner — the brand's best-performing recent send (back-to-school, 7.00/10) worked specifically because it opened with a single real product, not a category index.
- 2. Cut to one primary CTA above the fold and demote the other three "Shop All" buttons to plain text links — four identical buttons of equal visual weight is CTA competition, not hierarchy.
- 3. Tie the send to a seasonal moment (fall room refresh, back-to-school bedroom, etc.) rather than evergreen "every kid's space" copy.
- 4. Move the credit-card and app-download modules to their own dedicated send — they're diluting a room-shopping email with unrelated asks.
