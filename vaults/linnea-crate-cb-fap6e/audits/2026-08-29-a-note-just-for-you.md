# A note just for you!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-29**
## Executive summary

- This is a plain-text, letter-style send from Crate & Kids — no hero image, just a "Hi friend" salutation and a wall of underlined-link copy stacking five different Labor Day categories (nursery furniture, study furniture, bedding, rugs, decor, toys) before a single grey CTA button. It reads like the Crate & Barrel "Just for you: a note about Labor Day" letter format from 2026-08-28, reused for the kids sub-brand — so the "personal note" device is now a recognizable template rather than a genuine break from the mega-sale cascade.
- The subject "A note just for you!" promises intimacy but the body is just another Labor Day discount list with an exclamation point, which undercuts the premise. Below the fold it reverts to the standard modular stack (app download, Design Desk, credit card, store locator, category footer) seen across every recent Crate & Kids/Crate & Barrel send this week. Net: a mild format change dressed up as personalization, running the same underlying promo.

## What's working

- No render bugs — clean typography, working logo, legible link-styled offers.
- Copy is organized by room/need (bedroom, study, bedding, rugs, decor, toys) rather than a generic banner, which helps a parent scan for their specific project.
- The letter format visually differentiates this send from the loud red-banner mega-sale template dominating the brand's recent history.
- Footer fine print (exclusions, card issuer disclosures, sweepstakes rules) is present and not hidden.

## What's weak

- The CTA button ("Shop The Labor Day Event") is a washed-out grey-on-grey pill that barely registers against the background — weakest button contrast of any recent send.
- Subject line "A note just for you!" is a bait — the content is not personalized at all, just another discount list; this is the second time this week the brand has used the "personal note" device for the same promo.
- No hero image or product photography anywhere in the top half — for a kids' furniture brand, skipping visual proof of the product is a missed opportunity, especially compared to the Jenny Lind collection send that scored 6/10 on imagery.
- Five different offers (50%, 30%, 60%, 50%, 60%) compressed into dense paragraph text makes it hard to identify the single best deal at a glance.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix CTA contrast — swap the pale grey button for a solid black or brand-red button so it's the clear visual anchor; right now the eye has nowhere to land.
- 2. Add one hero product image (a styled kids' room) above the fold — the letter format doesn't need to mean zero imagery, and this is a furniture brand.
- 3. Lead with the single strongest offer (60% off bedding/decor) in the first line instead of burying it third in a five-item list.
- 4. Stop reusing "a note" framing for standard promo content — either write an actual personalized note (segment by purchase history) or drop the device so it doesn't erode trust.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Their room glow-up starts at 30% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Bedroom, study, bedding & decor — all discounted through the sale`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus Double Rewards on everything for their space`
