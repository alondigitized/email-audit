# NEW MARKDOWNS! Shop up to 60% off →
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Barrel is back to blasting sale urgency — two days after the smart 10%-off loyalty email (5/28, scored 8.00) that felt like a deliberate brand reset, this send reverts hard to the markdown-fire-hose playbook. The hero is clear and the offer is the sharpest thing in the email: "up to 60% off The Summer Clearance Event" lands immediately, the product grid is clean, and the embedded Crate & Kids cross-sell is a smart use of vertical real estate.
- The problem is above the fold on mobile — the inbox, not the email. The sender name "Crate & Barrel SALE" is an altered display name that will feel unfamiliar or even suspicious to anyone not looking at the domain, and the ALL CAPS "NEW MARKDOWNS!" in the subject line is a spam-filter flag before a subscriber even taps. The email itself executes fine; it's the inbox signals that erode trust built by the cleaner sends earlier this week.
- The cadence is the deeper concern. This is the fourth C&B family email in three days (alongside two Crate & Kids sends on 5/28 alone). The 5/28 loyalty email felt like a reset — this one walks it back.

## What's working

- **Hero clarity**: "up to 60% off / The Summer Clearance Event" is immediately readable, visually dominant, no ambiguity about what the email is for.
- **Product grid navigation**: Category tiles (Outdoor, Cookware, Lighting, More Furniture) let scanners self-route without reading a word of copy.
- **"Shop All Markdowns" CTA**: Clean, specific button copy — a verb plus a noun, not vague "Discover" or "Explore."
- **Crate & Kids embed**: The sub-brand block ("we love these for you") is well-integrated — cross-sells without hijacking the main message.
- **App upsell block**: The "It's all on the app" module is polished and feels native, not tacked on.

## What's weak

- **Sender name "Crate & Barrel SALE"**: Altered from the usual display name — inbox-level trust hit before the email is even opened.
- **ALL CAPS "NEW MARKDOWNS!"**: A recognized spam-filter and fatigue trigger; undermines the brand polish visible inside the email.
- **No urgency anchor**: "NEW" implies freshness but there's no deadline date, countdown, or quantity signal. "Up to 60% off" without "ends Sunday" or "while supplies last" is just ambient noise at this point in the sale cycle.
- **Cadence overload**: Fourth send in three days from the C&B family. The 5/28 loyalty email was the right move; reverting to a markdown blast the next morning burns goodwill fast.
- **No member callout**: The 5/28 send explicitly rewarded members ("10% off even on furniture"). This one treats everyone identically — a missed segmentation opportunity that flattens engagement for the most valuable subscribers.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the sender name** — restore it to "Crate & Barrel" (drop "SALE"). The brand name is the asset; the word SALE in the sender slot reads like a phishing tell and increases unsubscribes.
- 2. **Kill the ALL CAPS and add a hard deadline** — "New markdowns hit: 60% off ends Sunday" is more credible and more specific than shouting.
- 3. **Segment the member audience** — show member-exclusive early access or a stacked loyalty benefit for the subscribers who opened the 5/28 loyalty email. Don't let one send erode the positioning of the prior.
- 4. **Consolidate the Crate & Kids cross-sell into a footer module** — the full sub-brand block midway through the email splits attention from the clearance message.
- 5. **Add at least one hero product with a before/after price** — "$399 → $199 sectional" converts better than a category grid for a markdown event.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Zoom disabled via viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents users from pinching to zoom. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[HIGH] Global text-size-adjust disabled**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applied to `*` (all elements), not just `body`. Blocks OS-level font scaling for low-vision users. Scope to `body` only and use `100%`, not `none`.

**[MEDIUM] Conflicting image max-width media queries**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
The `640px` rule is declared *after* the `768px` rule. At viewport ≥768px both apply; CSS cascade gives `max-width: 640px` to the 640px rule (last-wins), overriding the 768px rule's `600px`. This is logically inverted — larger viewports end up with larger max-width than intended. Reorder so the wider breakpoint is last, or consolidate into one rule.

**[LOW] No dark mode support**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Explicitly blocks dark mode rendering. Images and background colors may invert poorly in dark-mode clients (Apple Mail, Gmail app). Consider `content="light dark"` with tested dark-mode overrides.

**[LOW] Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
The tag appears four times with overlapping values — once combined (`telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no`) and then individually for `date`, `address`, and `telephone`. The redundant entries add noise but cause no functional error. Consolidate to one.
