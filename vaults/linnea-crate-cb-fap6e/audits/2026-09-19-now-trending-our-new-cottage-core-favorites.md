# Now trending: our new cottage-core favorites →
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-19**
## Executive summary

- The subject line promises "cottage-core favorites," but nothing in the rendered email is cottage-core — the hero is a "Sharland England for Crate&kids" heritage collection shoot, followed by a Venus Williams tennis-collab push, a Halloween shop teaser, a Stokke sale banner, and a Nuna stroller cross-sell. This is a subject/body mismatch a subscriber would notice within one scroll, and it undercuts trust before the good content even gets a chance.
- Once you look past that mismatch, the email itself is a long, busy multi-offer scroll: heritage bedroom collection, nursery, playroom, Venus Williams collab, Halloween shop, Stokke discount, then a stroller upsell, credit card pitch, and app download — six or seven distinct asks in one send. There's also a visible render glitch in the hero image (a soft white cloud/smudge floating over the crib on the left) that looks like a broken transparent PNG rather than an intentional design element.
- The strongest section by far is "up to 20% off Stokke bestsellers" — it has a real discount, a specific hero product (Tripp Trapp in olive green), urgency ("limited time"), and a clean CTA. If this had been the lead instead of buried two-thirds down, this would be a notably stronger send.

## What's working

- The Stokke banner is the clean, well-executed template every other section should follow: specific product, specific discount, credible urgency, clear CTA.
- The Venus Williams collab section reuses a proven celebrity hook (per brand history, Crate & Kids has run this play before) with a strong lifestyle photo of a joyful kid.
- Product tiles throughout show clear before/after pricing (e.g., Nuna PIPA strikethrough $1350 → $1010), which builds trust in the offer.
- The Halloween teaser is well-timed for late September and uses a charming, personalized prop (a basket with "Jackson" embroidered).

## What's weak

- The subject line ("cottage-core favorites") does not match anything visible in the email — the actual lead story is a heritage/traditional English collection, not cottage-core.
- There's a visible white blur/smudge artifact over the left side of the hero image, next to the crib — looks like a broken or misplaced graphic element.
- The email crams in at least six distinct campaigns (heritage launch, Venus Williams, Halloween, Stokke sale, stroller upsell, credit card, app download) with no prioritization — nothing anchors as "the one thing to click."
- The hero section has no discount or urgency at all — pure brand storytelling up top, while the actual offer (Stokke) is buried far down the scroll.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the subject/body mismatch first** — either lead the email with cottage-core product (if it exists elsewhere in the campaign) or rewrite the subject to match what's actually here (the Sharland England heritage launch or the Stokke sale, whichever is the real priority).
- 2. **Move the Stokke discount banner to the top**, directly under the logo — it's the only section with a real, time-bound, priced offer, and it's currently buried six sections deep.
- 3. **Cut the email in half** — pick two priorities (e.g., heritage launch + Stokke sale) and move Halloween, Venus Williams, credit card, and app-download into separate sends; this is a "4th near-identical stacked-offer email" problem per the brand history pattern.
- 4. **Fix the hero image artifact** — the white smudge next to the crib reads as a broken asset and undercuts the "heritage made modern" premium positioning.
