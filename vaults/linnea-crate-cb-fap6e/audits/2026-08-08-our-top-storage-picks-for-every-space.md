# Our top storage picks for every space
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel doing a full-catalog storage sweep — seven separate furniture collections (Raffael, Ventana, Mota, Calypso, Keane, Campagna, Siena) stacked one after another, each with its own hero shot, customer-quote pull-line, and "Shop Now" button. The subject promise — "our top storage picks" — is delivered honestly for the first two-thirds of the email: it's genuinely all cabinets, consoles, dressers, and étagères, which is more disciplined than the 2026-08-07 "Summer Sale" grab-bag (5.0) that buried a storewide sale under five unrelated promos.
- Where it falls apart is the back third: after seven storage collections, the email pivots into a Cosentino countertops cross-sell, a bedding-basics push, and a Design Desk "$500 giveaway" appointment pitch — none of which are storage. That's the same category-drift problem that dragged the Summer Sale email down, just less severe here because it happens after the core promise is already fulfilled rather than instead of it.
- The bigger structural issue is repetition fatigue: seven nearly-identical collection blocks (image, italic review quote, generic "Shop Now" button, product names) with no differentiation in CTA copy or visible pricing. Nothing here tells me why Ventana beats Mota, or gives me a price anchor to click toward. It's a competent, on-brand catalog scroll — solid mid-pack, not a standout.

## What's working

- Subject and hero headline ("our most-loved storage") are aligned and deliver on the storage theme immediately.
- Each collection includes a genuine customer review pull-quote, which functions as real social proof throughout the send.
- Visual consistency is strong — same layout rhythm, clean product photography, no broken images or overlapping text visible.
- Collections link to specific named product lines (Raffael, Ventana, Mota, etc.), not just a generic "shop all" homepage dump.
- Material/craft details surface in product names (e.g., burl wood, acacia), giving some texture beyond stock photos.

## What's weak

- No visible price, % off, or discount anywhere in the storage section — just "Shop Now," so there's no urgency or value hook to click on.
- Every single CTA button says "Shop Now" — seven times in a row with zero differentiation makes the email feel templated rather than curated.
- The email drifts off-topic in its back half (countertops, bedding, Design Desk sweepstakes) after "storage picks" was the entire premise — same genre error as the low-scoring Summer Sale send.
- It's an extremely long, single-speed scroll — seven collections deep before any variety in format, which risks fatigue well before the reader reaches the cross-sells.
- Two separate "win $500" sweepstakes mentions appear in unrelated contexts (Mota collection area, then again near Design Desk) without a shared thread, reading as bolted-on rather than integrated.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the send after the storage collections end — drop Cosentino, bedding, and the Design Desk sweepstakes into their own dedicated email. This keeps the subject's promise intact and shortens the scroll dramatically.
- 2. Vary CTA copy per collection ("Shop Ventana," "Shop Mota Consoles") instead of seven identical "Shop Now" buttons — cheap fix, immediate lift to click intent.
- 3. Surface at least one price point or "starting at $X" per collection, mirroring the 2026-08-07 nursery bestsellers send ("starting at $30") that scored 9.0 — concrete pricing is the single biggest lever missing here.
- 4. Trim to 3-4 hero collections instead of seven; move the rest to a "see more storage" link — reduces fatigue and keeps the top of the email doing the heaviest lifting.
- 5. If the sweepstakes stays, give it one clear placement and framing tied to storage purchases, not two disconnected drop-ins.

## Technical Audit: Crate & Barrel — "Our top storage picks for every space"

**1. Technical Summary**
Standard multi-vendor ESP template (Cheetah Mail/Marigold-style `mail.crateandbarrel.com` infrastructure + Scene7 image CDN) with heavy MSO/Outlook conditional markup and mobile media queries. Automated QA returned 0 hard issues and 4 warnings, primarily around missing alt text and unverified compliance headers.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- 82 tracking/click-redirect links were present but skipped by the automated HTTP prober by design (redirect-domain heuristic) — link destination health (404s, broken redirects) is **unverified**, not confirmed-clean.
- Multiple third-party tracking pixels fire beyond the standard open pixel: `sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif` (LiveRamp identity resolution, 5 sequential calls `n=1`–`n=5`), `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/...?cp_tp=v` (view-through conversion pixel), and 7 `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png` engagement pixels plus one `p/up/*/o.gif` open pixel. No functional issue confirmed, but this is a lot of concurrent pixel load — flagging for awareness.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- 45 images are missing `alt` text across both CDNs (`image.mail.crateandbarrel.com` product/lifestyle images and `s7d5.scene7.com/is/image/Crate/...` hero/product shots), e.g. `202504_CB_Storage_Hero_Primary`, `202504_CB_Storage_Ventana_Cabinet`, `202504_CB_Storage_Rule`. With images blocked (default in Outlook/many mobile clients), the email renders with no fallback text.
- Head contains a leftover template placeholder: `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` — appears to be an unfilled/unremoved build artifact (also has a typo, "IMPUT" for "INPUT"), harmless but suggests the build wasn't fully cleaned before send.
- MSO conditional VML/Outlook fixes and responsive `@media` breakpoints (460px, 640px, 768px) are present — standard defensive rendering coverage, no issues found there.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
No unresolved merge tags (e.g. `{{firstname}}`, `%%FIELD%%`) observed in the visible portion of the source. The `mi_u=` hash parameter on tracking pixels is a resolved subscriber token, not a broken merge field. Note: HTML was truncated for this review, so body content beyond the visible portion wasn't inspected.

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
- `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) headers were not detected. **Caveat from the QA tool itself: these may simply not be captured by the AgentMail relay rather than genuinely absent** — this needs verification against the raw SMTP headers at the source, not just the relayed copy, before treating it as a real compliance gap. If genuinely absent, this fails Gmail/Yahoo's 2024 bulk-sender one-click unsubscribe requirement.
- `Authentication-Results` (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) header also not found, same relay caveat applies — SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is **unknown**, not confirmed-failing.

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
Cannot be confirmed — all 82 outbound links route through click-redirect domains and were excluded from the automated probe, so post-redirect UTM structure and landing-page destinations were not captured in this pass.

**7. Recommendations**
1. Verify `List-Unsubscribe`/`List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results` directly against raw sender headers (not the AgentMail-relayed copy) to confirm whether the compliance gap is real or a relay artifact.
2. Add descriptive `alt` text to the 45 flagged images, particularly the Scene7 hero/product images, for accessibility and images-off fallback.
3. Remove the leftover `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT...]-->` placeholder comment before next send.
4. Plain-text version is 70%+ URL characters (15,793/22,520) — reduce link density or shorten redirect URLs, as this ratio is a common spam-filter trigger.
5. Follow up on the 82 skipped tracking links with a manual or allow-listed probe to confirm no broken redirects, since this pass provides no signal either way.
