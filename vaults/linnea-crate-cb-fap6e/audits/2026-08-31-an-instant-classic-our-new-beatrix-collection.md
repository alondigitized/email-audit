# An instant classic: our new Beatrix collection ♥️
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-31**
## Executive summary

- This is a rare non-Labor-Day-hero send from Crate & Kids — the subject leads with a genuine product story ("our new Beatrix collection") instead of the seventh red discount banner this week, and that's the right call after so much promo fatigue. But it immediately undercuts itself: the very first thing visible in the render is a "Double Rewards is on! / Up to 60% off The Labor Day Event" strip sitting above the Beatrix hero, so the "new collection" premise gets stepped on by the same sale messaging before you even scroll.
- Below the fold this reverts hard to the familiar cascade — nursery collection, "more new fall arrivals," a full Labor Day category grid, a second Double Rewards banner, a sweepstakes tile, "we love these for you" products, a credit card pitch, and an app download footer. It's an enormous, kitchen-sink email; the Beatrix launch gets maybe two screens of real estate before drowning in the same generic mega-sale furniture the brand has run daily.
- Net: a good subject idea, poorly protected in the layout. If this is meant to be a launch email, it needed to stay a launch email longer before folding back into the sale template.

## What's working

- Subject and preview both lead with a specific named collection ("Beatrix") rather than another generic %-off headline — a real change from the last six sends.
- Hero image is a styled, on-brand bedroom vignette that clearly shows the product story.
- "Meet the Beatrix nursery collection" section reuses the same product family across bedroom and nursery, which reads as a coherent launch, not just SKU dumping.
- Clean modular grid layout throughout — no overlapping text, broken images, or obvious render bugs.

## What's weak

- The "Double Rewards is on! Up to 60% off The Labor Day Event" strip appears directly above the Beatrix hero, diluting the "instant classic" narrative before it starts.
- The email pivots into the exact same Labor Day category grid (rugs, lighting, storage, bedding) seen in the brand's prior sends this week — same offer copy, same visual template.
- At this length (roughly a dozen distinct sections), the Beatrix collection is a small fraction of total content — it stops being a "collection launch" email and becomes another mega-sale email with a new hero bolted on.
- A second Double Rewards banner, a $1,000 sweepstakes tile, and a credit-card pitch all compete for attention in the lower half, adding to fatigue rather than reinforcing the launch.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the Labor Day banner from above/near the hero — let the Beatrix launch breathe for at least the first two sections before any sale messaging appears.
- 2. Trim the email significantly: Beatrix bedroom + nursery, one clear CTA, done. Push the Labor Day category grid, second Double Rewards banner, sweepstakes, and credit-card pitch to separate sends — this single email is trying to be a launch announcement and a mega-sale in one body.
- 3. Add a concrete offer or launch hook tied to Beatrix specifically (e.g. "new arrival," "limited release," or an intro price) so the collection isn't competing purely on story against the sale's %-off numbers.
- 4. Given six near-identical Labor Day sends already this week (per brand history), use this slot to actually differentiate — a true editorial/lookbook treatment for Beatrix would stand out far more than folding it into the same cascade template.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New: the Beatrix bedroom collection is here`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Meet Beatrix — our newest nursery + bedroom line`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Vintage-inspired pieces for the whole home, just launched.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Bedroom to nursery — see the full Beatrix lineup.`
