# Shop it first: our Venus Williams collab, only on our app!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-14**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuinely newsworthy hero — a real celebrity collab (Venus Williams x Crate & Kids), with a clean phone-mockup visual that immediately signals "this is app-exclusive and new." But the email buries the lede almost instantly: after one screen of collab hero, it pivots to Design Desk consultations, then a generic "we love these for you" product grid, then a credit card pitch, then the app download again, then Design Desk again. The Venus Williams story gets one shot and is gone — no product shots of the actual collab, no price, no category info, nothing to click into beyond "download the app and go find it yourself."
- That's the core problem: the CTA isn't a click, it's an app-store detour. For a subscriber who opened specifically because of "Venus Williams," landing on "download our app" with zero visual proof of what's actually in the collection is a letdown. The rest of the email is Crate & Kids' now-familiar kitchen-sink structure (compare to 2026-09-13's back-to-school and kid-spaces sends) — Design Desk, generic reco grid, credit card, app plug, Design Desk again — recycled almost verbatim across recent sends.
- Judgement: strong hook, weak payoff. The celebrity name is the best subject/hero pairing this brand has run this week, but the email fails to capitalize on it with any actual product content.

## What's working

- The phone-mockup hero with "VENUS WILLIAMS for Crate&kids" is a clear, well-composed visual that reads instantly, even at a glance.
- A recognizable celebrity name creates real curiosity that generic "new arrivals" copy can't.
- The green banner reinforces exclusivity/urgency ("be the first," "only on our app") right under the hero.
- Clean single CTA button in the hero section ("Download the Crate & Barrel App") without competing clicks nearby.

## What's weak

- Zero product imagery from the actual Venus Williams collection — no items, no price, no category (apparel? nursery? furniture?) shown anywhere.
- The primary ask is an app download, not a shoppable link — high-friction for anyone who just wants to browse from their inbox.
- The email immediately dilutes the collab news with unrelated Design Desk, generic product recs, and a credit card pitch — five different asks competing for attention.
- Repeats the same Design Desk module twice (mid-email and again near the bottom) — visibly redundant scrolling through the same pitch.
- "TheDesignDesk" wordmark is stylized in a way that reads visually cluttered against its photo background at a glance.

## Recommendations

- 1. Show at least 2-3 actual product shots from the Venus Williams line in the hero module — a name alone with no visible product kills conversion intent immediately.
- 2. If the collab truly is app-exclusive, make the CTA "Preview the Collection" (linking to a lookbook or teaser page) rather than a cold "Download the App" — give people something to see before asking them to install software.
- 3. Cut one of the two Design Desk placements — running the identical module twice in one email reads as filler, not intentional.
- 4. Move the credit card pitch and generic product grid to a separate send — this email should be 100% about the collab to protect the hook.
- 5. Add a launch date/time-bound line ("Available in-app starting [date]") to convert curiosity into urgency.
- **Subject Alt A:** `First look: Venus Williams x Crate&kids, app-only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Venus Williams designed this for your kid's room`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `See the pieces before anyone else — only in the Crate & Barrel app.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Tennis star, tiny rooms: the collab drops first on our app.`
