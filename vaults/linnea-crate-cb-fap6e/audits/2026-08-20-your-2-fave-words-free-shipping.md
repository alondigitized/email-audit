# Your 2 fave words: FREE SHIPPING!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-20**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids running a single-benefit free-shipping send — "it. all. ships. free." is the hero, and the whole email backs it up with a clean grid of category tiles (kids bedding, baby gear, cuddles, curtains, throw pillows) each tagged "ships free." It's the most single-minded, offer-clear layout I've seen from this brand family in the recent run — no competing message, no confusion about what the deal is.
- The problem is the offer itself: free shipping isn't a discount, and after 2026-08-20's "up to 60% off clearance" from the sibling Crate & Barrel line, this reads like a step down in value. It's also creative-heavy rather than product-specific — most of the email is soft lifestyle photography with small "ships free" labels rather than concrete SKUs, prices, or a single hero product to click into.
- Solid execution, weak offer. This will get opens on subject line curiosity but won't convert as hard as the clearance send.

## What's working

- The hero states the benefit twice, in copy and in the button ("it. all. ships. free." / "Shop All Free Shipping") — no ambiguity about the offer.
- The category grid (bedding, gear, cuddles, bath, curtains, throw pillows) is visually consistent — same crop ratio, same "[category] ships free" caption pattern, easy to scan.
- Two secondary modules (backpack bundles, bedding fabric finder) add real utility beyond the free-shipping message, useful for a back-to-school-timed send.
- The product grid near the bottom shows real SKUs with prices (Nuna PIPA car seat, BabyLetto rocker, dresser) — this is the one place concrete purchase intent lives.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, layout is orderly top to bottom.

## What's weak

- Free shipping alone is a weak lead offer compared to this brand's recent 60%-off clearance send — it doesn't feel like an event, just table stakes.
- The hero image is a soft, cropped lifestyle shot (a striped blanket over a chair) with no visible product or model — it doesn't clearly signal "kids/baby" until you scroll into the grid.
- No urgency or deadline anywhere in the email — free shipping with no "ends Sunday" or similar reads as evergreen, not a reason to act today.
- The email is long and repetitive before reaching anything with a price tag — six near-identical "ships free" tiles push the priced product grid far down the scroll.
- Card/rewards program plug ("the card you love just got better") and app-download CTA compete for attention against the primary free-shipping message, diluting hierarchy.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pair the free-shipping message with a real threshold or deadline (e.g., "Free shipping, no minimum — today only") to convert it from a permanent perk into a reason to act now.
- 2. Move the priced product grid ("we love these for you") up near the top, directly under the hero, so the eye hits a real SKU with a price before six repetitive category tiles.
- 3. Replace the abstract hero shot (striped throw on a chair) with an actual nursery/kids-room scene featuring a baby or child — matches this brand's demographic better and creates warmth.
- 4. Cut the tile grid from 6 categories to 3-4 — bedding, gear, and one seasonal pick — to shorten the scroll before the CTA repeats.
- 5. Drop or move the card/rewards and app-download blocks below the product grid; right now they're stealing hierarchy from the core offer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free shipping, zero minimum. today only.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Everything for their room — ships free`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New bedding, gear & bundles — no minimum, no code needed`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the nursery essentials on your list, free shipping included`
