# The prices you’ve been waiting for: Up to 50% off Black Friday!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-26**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, competent Black Friday catalog send, but it's one more of the same. The hero is strong: "Biggest sale of the year! Up to 50% off Black Friday + free shipping," with a red Shop Now button over a cozy nod-chair photo. Below it sits a long 2x4 grid of "up to X% off" category tiles, then a sprawl of secondary modules.
- After the hero the email stops prioritizing. The grid has no single hero product. It's followed by a baby-gear editorial block, the personalization shop, a Babylyte & Namesake tile, a credit-card promo, an app-download pitch, a design-services pitch and a store locator. Each module dilutes the others. The fine print at the bottom is a dense wall of exclusions.
- For a subscriber in about day three of the Black Friday blitz, this is background noise. It follows "Black Friday just got even BETTER!" and "Happy Thanksgiving! 🦃" from the same day. Nothing here marks it as different. Verdict: solid execution, low marginal value.

## What's working

- The hero states the offer plainly: up to 50% off, free shipping, with a visible Shop Now button.
- The "585,267 people are shopping Black Friday now" banner at the top adds social proof and urgency.
- The tile grid labels each category with a discount, such as "up to 30% off toys" and "up to 50% off top kids furniture," so the depth of the deal is clear at a glance.
- Photography is warm and seasonal (holiday stockings, a wreath, a cozy nursery), and no images appear broken.
- The "Kiwi Striped Glider" module (Sale $525, Reg $1,099) is the one concrete product with a price and a named button.

## What's weak

- The subject promises "the prices you've been waiting for," but the body shows only "up to" ranges. The price you've been waiting for is never shown.
- Roughly eight sections follow the hero, with five or more different CTAs (Shop Now, Shop the Sale, Shop Baby Gear, Learn More, Download Now, Book a Free Appointment). The email has no single dominant ask.
- The urgency is generic. There's no end time, only "Don't miss out," and the same promise has run for days.
- "Exclusions apply. Orders $79+" under the hero hedges the headline offer, and the legal block at the bottom is a dense wall of exclusions and conditions.
- The credit-card, app and design-services promos are off-message for a Black Friday shopper and push the email very long.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to the hero, the category grid and one product row (glider plus two or three more with actual sale prices). Move the credit card, app, design services and store locator to a non-sale send. This is the single biggest lift.
- 2. Replace the "up to" tile labels with real "from $X" or "was/now" prices on at least the top four tiles, so the subject's promise is kept.
- 3. Give the urgency a real deadline, such as "Ends Monday 11:59pm PT," and put it directly under the hero.
- 4. Vary the creative from the last three days. This send reuses the same chair hero and category grid, and a different angle would signal that it isn't a repeat.
- 5. Change "Shop Now" to a category-specific verb and noun, such as "Shop Nod Chairs 30% Off."

- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call: kids' furniture up to 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping on orders $79+. Ends Monday at midnight PT.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Nursery, playroom and bedding deals from $29. See what's still in stock.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The sampled source is a standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional ESP template, with `lang="en"`, VML/MSO fallbacks and a mobile breakpoint at 460px. The confirmed problems are widespread missing `alt` text (about 29 images flagged) and a viewport tag that disables zoom. Header-based compliance and authentication checks are inconclusive because the relay didn't capture the headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **70 tracking links were not probed.** They are click-redirect domains, so the QA tool skipped the HTTP check. Their destinations are unverified, so I can't confirm they resolve or that there are no broken links.
- **Five LiveRamp pixels are in the body.** The URLs are `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=dbe8da71…&n=1` through `&n=5`, all with the same hashed identifier `s=`. This is third-party tracking beyond the ESP's own pixels.
- **ESP open and impression pixels** are present at `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif?mi_u=…` and `https://dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/e7de2cc8-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…`. They are first-party hosts, so tracking runs on a brand-owned subdomain.
- No broken or malformed links are confirmed in the truncated source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `alt` on content images.** About 20 images served from `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/…` (`.jpg` and `.gif`) lack alt text. That includes `592f13e9-…jpg`, `2635bc5e-…jpg` and animated `91ea068d-…gif` and `ac698ed7-…gif`. One more is on Scene7, `s7d5.scene7.com/.../2026_1105_CBK_PersonalizedGiftShop_Seconda…`, and one is on `.../m/47/bbf6f9d4-…png`. With images blocked, or for screen readers, these show nothing or a filename. This is the most consequential finding.
- **Tracking pixels lack alt.** The `rlcdn.com`, `o.gif` and `dv.` pixels are reported too. These should carry `alt=""` as a decorative image, which is a lower-severity fix.
- **Zoom is disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` blocks pinch-zoom, a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Unescaped ampersand in the title.** `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` uses a raw `&` under an XHTML doctype. It should be `&amp;`. Parsers tolerate it, so the impact is low.
- **Leftover template placeholder.** The comment `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` shows the template wasn't cleaned up. There is no rendering impact.
- **Mixed or duplicated meta tags.** There are three `format-detection` metas, and both `http-equiv` Content-Type and `<meta charset>`. They are redundant, with no functional impact.
- **Text-size CSS.** `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` and `html{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` conflict with `body{...:100%}`. They prevent user text scaling on iOS, which is a minor accessibility regression.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`, `[FIRST_NAME]`) are visible in the provided excerpt. The source is truncated, so the rest of the body wasn't checked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found.** QA notes the AgentMail relay may strip these headers. This is unconfirmed, so don't count it as a sender defect. Check the raw `.eml` or the original delivery path.
- **`Authentication-Results` is absent.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The From domain `mail.crateandbarrel.com` is aligned with the brand domain, but alignment can't be checked without headers.
- **The footer can't be checked.** The truncated HTML doesn't reach the unsubscribe link or the physical postal address, so neither can be verified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not verifiable from the data provided. The 70 tracked links were skipped, and the excerpt contains no resolved destination URLs or UTM parameters. No issues are confirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to all content images. Use `alt=""` for the pixels: `rlcdn.com/448796.gif`, `mi.…/o.gif` and `dv.…/o/e7de2cc8…`.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta, and drop the `text-size-adjust:none` rules.
3. Change the title to `Crate &amp; Barrel`, and remove the "IMPUT HERE" placeholder comment and the duplicate meta tags.
4. Re-run QA against the original raw message, or follow a few redirects manually. That would confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, SPF/DKIM/DMARC, tracked-link destinations and UTM carry-through.
5. Capture the full HTML, including the footer, to check the unsubscribe link, the postal address and any merge tokens. Treat those items as unverified rather than failures until then.
