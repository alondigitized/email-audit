# Final weekend! Up to 30% off dining tables, chairs & more →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-25**
## Executive summary

- This is a solid dining-furniture sale email, but the creative doesn't match the subject. The subject says "Final weekend!". The hero says "the Thanksgiving dining room event, up to 30% off" and has no deadline or countdown. The offer is clear and the hero is strong: a styled dining room with a big headline and a "Shop Now" button. A four-tile grid for tables, seating, storage and lighting follows.
- The rest of the email loses focus. After the dining modules it runs a kitchen-deals block with Sale/Reg pricing, a "fall deals edit," a product grid, a credit-card promo, an app download, a design-desk upsell and a store locator. The dining story that the subject sets up is diluted by about the halfway point.
- Verdict: a competent send that is better than the brand's recent catalog-style emails. It would be stronger if the "final weekend" urgency appeared in the body and the lower third were cut.

## What's working

- The hero is clear. A large lifestyle dining photo, a bold "up to 30% off" headline and one button put the offer first.
- The four-tile grid (tables, seating, storage, lighting) matches the "tables, chairs & more" subject and gives shoppers a way into each category.
- The kitchen block shows concrete sale and regular prices (for example $349.95 against $499.95), which makes the discount credible.
- There are two specific CTAs ("Shop The Dining Furniture Event" and "Shop All Dining Furniture"), and they sit near the hero.
- The seasonal Thanksgiving framing fits the date, and the design looks consistent and trustworthy.

## What's weak

- "Final weekend" is never repeated in the body. There is no end date, countdown or "ends Sunday" line, so the urgency in the subject isn't backed up.
- The email is very long. The kitchen deals, fall edit, "we love these for you" grid, credit card, app, design desk and store locator all dilute the dining message.
- The text on several tile images is weak. "20% off select lighting" and the "Thanksgiving Hosting Guide" banner overlay busy photos and are hard to read.
- The fine print is dense and sits in small type at the bottom. The exclusions and the "up to" qualifiers are easy to miss.
- The tiles have no product names, prices or sale prices, so the "up to 30%" claim never becomes a specific piece of furniture.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the deadline match the subject. Put "Ends Sunday" or a countdown in the hero and on the primary button ("Shop dining before it ends").
- 2. Cut everything after the dining modules except one secondary block, and put the app, card and design-desk promos in a slim footer strip. That keeps the email to the one dining story the subject promised.
- 3. Add two or three hero products with sale and regular prices under the dining tiles, so shoppers see real numbers.
- 4. Fix the text overlays on the tile images with a solid or darkened label band, especially on lighting and the hosting guide.
- 5. Align the subject and hero wording. Use either "Thanksgiving dining" or "final weekend", not both.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Thanksgiving table, up to 30% off →`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dining tables, chairs, sideboards & lighting. Sale ends Sunday night.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Hosting this year? Free shipping on select styles, plus 20% off lighting.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML source is a Movable Intelligence template on an XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype. It has 30 images without alt text and several template and markup defects. The automated compliance and authentication warnings are most likely artifacts of the AgentMail relay. Only the head of the source is available, so body links, footer and UTM parameters could not be checked.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Probing:** 67 tracking links were skipped (click-redirect domains), so none were checked for resolution, redirect chains or destinations.
- **Shared identifier:** the `mi_u` value on `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png` and `.../up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif` is `dbe8da718e2997cce014546f16c5722eaa3c322f28d40f0a8386f8fd1015f5a8`. The same 64-character hex string is passed as `s=` to `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif`, a LiveRamp domain. A per-recipient identifier is therefore going to a third party from the email body.
- **Duplicate pixels:** the LiveRamp pixel fires five times (`n=1` to `n=5`) with the same `s=`. This adds requests with no apparent benefit.
- **Open pixel:** `https://dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/1510ebef-...?mi_cid=...&mi_mid=...` is a separate open-tracking pixel with no extension.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt (30 images):** the QA check flags 30 images, which is the main accessibility finding.
  - Content images on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/` and `/m/47/` (e.g. `bbf6f9d4-...png`, `ef861136-...jpg`) have no alt text. These need real descriptions, especially any that carry offer text.
  - The scene7 asset `2026_0920_CB_ThanksgivingGuide_Tertiary-` also has no alt text.
  - The seven `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png` images are dynamic modules, probably product or recommendation content. They also have no alt text.
  - The five `sr.rlcdn.com` pixels, `o.gif` and the `dv.` pixel are tracking pixels. They should carry `alt=""` explicitly.
  - The check cannot tell a missing `alt` attribute from `alt=""`.
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="..., maximum-scale=1" />` prevents pinch-zoom, and the CSS sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` in several places. Both hurt low-vision users.
- **Invalid markup:**
  - The `<title>` contains an unescaped `&` (`Crate & Barrel`) under an XHTML doctype.
  - `<meta charset="utf-8">` is unclosed alongside the XHTML-style `http-equiv` tag, so the head mixes HTML5 and XHTML syntax.
  - `format-detection` is declared four times with overlapping values.
- **Conflicting rules:** `@media (min-width:768px){img{max-width:600px}}` and `@media (min-width:640px){img{max-width:640px}}` both match at 768px and wider. The later rule wins, so the 600px cap is dead code.
- **Template leftover:** the comment `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` was never removed.

## 7. Recommendations
