# The high chair they'll use until middle school (really!) in our exclusive color→
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-20**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuine single-product hero email — a rare departure from Crate & Kids' usual roundup format — spotlighting the Stokke Tripp Trapp high chair in a new "olive green" exclusive colorway. The hero is clean, the brand story block ("Stokke has challenged the ordinary...") adds credibility, and the product photography (baby in the chair, detail shots of the tray, adjustable seat) is genuinely strong. But after the strong open, the email sprawls into the same long catalog scroll seen in every recent send: "even more from Stokke," a Venus Williams collab teaser, a Halloween teaser, a 20% off Stokke bestsellers banner, then a generic "we love these for you" product grid and a credit card pitch.
- The subject line is the best thing about this email — "the high chair they'll use until middle school (really!)" is specific, funny, and benefit-driven, a clear step up from the vague/thematic subjects in prior sends like "Now trending: our new cottage-core favorites." It's undercut by the layout burying the actual offer (20% off) six screens down, after multiple unrelated diversions.
- Net: strong creative concept, weak execution discipline. This should have been a tight 3-4 panel single-product email; instead it reverts to the brand's chronic over-stuffing habit.

## What's working

- Subject line is concrete, benefit-led, and has personality — a genuine outlier for this brand's history.
- Hero product shot (baby seated in the olive Tripp Trapp) is well-composed and on-brand for a baby/parenting audience.
- Detail insert callouts ("New & exclusive Stokke Tripp Trapp high chair... with a comfy customizable seat") support the single-product story with real product education.
- Clean color palette (olive green, cream, black) feels premium and cohesive through the top third of the email.

## What's weak

- The email abandons its own single-product premise almost immediately — by the third screen it's back to a multi-item Stokke grid, then unrelated Venus Williams and Halloween teasers.
- The actual discount (20% off Stokke bestsellers) is buried deep in the scroll with no visual priority, despite being the most concrete purchase incentive in the email.
- "we love these for you" grid at the bottom is generic cross-sell filler unrelated to the high chair story, diluting focus exactly like recent sends ("Nuna, Doona & more great baby gear," 2026-09-19).
- Halloween teaser mid-email is a jarring seasonal non-sequitur dropped into a baby-gear/high-chair narrative.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after the Stokke story + "even more to love from Stokke" section — move the 20% off banner, Venus Williams, Halloween, and "we love these for you" content to separate sends. A focused 4-panel email converts better than a 12-panel scroll, and this brand has run the kitchen-sink format five times in the last audit window already.
- 2. Move the "up to 20% off Stokke bestsellers" offer up near the hero — pair the exclusive-colorway story with the actual discount so readers don't have to scroll 5 screens to find a reason to buy now.
- 3. Add a size/price/availability detail near the hero CTA (price point, ship date) since high-chair buyers care about fit and cost before clicking "Shop the Tripp Trapp."
- 4. Drop or relocate the Halloween teaser — it breaks the coherent baby-furniture narrative and reads like an afterthought.
