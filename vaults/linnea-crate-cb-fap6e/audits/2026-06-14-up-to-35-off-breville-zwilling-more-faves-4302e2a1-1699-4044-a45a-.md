# Up to 35% off Breville, Zwilling & more faves!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- This is C&B's first explicit branded-kitchen sale email after a run of furniture editorials and lifestyle pieces — and it earns the category shift. Naming Breville and Zwilling in the subject does real work: these are aspirational kitchen brands with specific price anchors in consumers' heads, which makes "up to 35% off" feel tangible rather than generic. The hero render is clean, the Breville appliance takes clear visual priority, and the offer is visible without any scroll on mobile.
- The main drag is what happens below the fold. A furniture/living room lifestyle block appears mid-scroll in what should be a focused kitchen send, and there's no visible deadline anywhere in the email — "up to 35% off" with no end date is the kind of offer that's easy to bookmark-and-forget. The cadence context also matters: C&B has been sending multiple times per day (three sends on June 13 alone), so subscriber fatigue is real.
- Solid execution on a targeted premise, undercut by split focus and no urgency lever.

## What's working

- Named brands (Breville, Zwilling) in the subject create a specific, price-anchored offer that feels credible and queryable.
- Breville appliance hero renders cleanly — the visual hierarchy lands exactly where it should.
- The 35% off headline is visible immediately; no hunting for the offer.
- Category pivot after a heavy run of furniture/editorial sends feels fresh rather than repetitive.
- Clean rendering — no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout artifacts visible.

## What's weak

- A furniture/living room lifestyle block appears lower in the email, splitting the message — a subscriber who opened for Breville gets confused by sofa content.
- No visible deadline anywhere in the email; without urgency, "up to 35% off" is easily deferred indefinitely.
- "More faves!" in the subject is conversational to the point of feeling slightly off-brand for C&B's typically considered voice.
- The "up to" qualifier hedges the 35% — feels honest, but the email doesn't counteract it with a specific hero price point (e.g., "Was $299, now $199").
- C&B is currently sending at a very high cadence (multiple per day this week), which dilutes the signal of this otherwise clean send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard end date to the hero** — "Sale ends Sunday" above or below the 35% headline is the single highest-leverage change. It converts a browsable offer into an actionable one.
- 2. **Anchor one hero price** — Show the Breville's was/now price in the hero section. Named brands have known retail prices; showing the actual savings converts faster than a percentage alone.
- 3. **Cut or relocate the furniture block** — If it must appear, move it to a clearly labeled "While you're here" section at the very bottom, not mid-scroll where it competes with the kitchen story.
- 4. **Sharpen the subject line:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `35% off Breville, Zwilling & more — sale ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Breville moment: up to 35% off kitchen faves`
- 5. **Rewrite the preheader to add specificity and urgency:**
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Smart Oven Air, Zwilling knives, Le Creuset & more. Shop before it sells out.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Breville, Zwilling & kitchen essentials — all on sale this week only.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Crate & Barrel "Up to 35% off Breville, Zwilling & more faves!"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has four hard infrastructure failures on webfont delivery and two missing compliance headers that violate 2024 Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender requirements. Accessibility coverage is near-zero: 33 images lack `alt` attributes, including both product images and tracking pixels.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Four webfont assets return HTTP 403**

```
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/1e07d600-3787-4517-bd3d-b140f9e54ec2.woff2
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/7ec25fd5-2752-4d84-b307-af3faabc21bb.woff2
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/aaa41944-25b3-4265-80b2-5564efe9b2d7.woff2
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/fd5f8f50-d539-4a7d-b893-99501d746ef2.woff2
```

The font files are hosted on `crateandbarrel.com` with what appears to be referrer or origin restrictions. Email clients do not send a `Referer` header matching the web origin, so the request is rejected and the email falls back to system fonts in every client that respects `@font-face`. Fix: serve webfonts from a CDN or subdomain with `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` and no referrer guard, or use a third-party font host (e.g., Adobe Fonts, Google Fonts) with email-safe delivery.

**[INFO] 22 tracking/redirect links skipped**

Click-tracking links could not be probed. UTM and destination validation is deferred to section 6.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Template placeholder comment left in production HTML**

```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```

A build-time placeholder (with typo "IMPUT") was shipped verbatim. This is harmless to rendering but indicates the send was not run through a pre-flight template linting step.

**[FAIL] 33 images missing `alt` attributes**

All 33 flagged images have no `alt` attribute at all (not even `alt=""`). This affects two distinct categories:

- **Product/content images** (22 `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com` JPGs + 1 GIF, plus 1 `971aa10f` JPG): render as broken images in image-blocked environments; screen readers have nothing to announce.
- **Tracking pixels** (6× `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/`, 5× `sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif`, 1× `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/o.gif`, 1× `dv.crateandbarrel.com` impression tracker): must carry `alt=""` explicitly to suppress screen-reader announcement.

**[WARN] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

Disables user zoom on mobile — a WCAG 1.4.4 failure. Remove `maximum-scale=1` or replace with `maximum-scale=5`.

**[WARN] Global `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`**

```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }
```

Applied to all elements, not just `body`. This suppresses OS-level text scaling for users who need it. Scope to `body` only.

**[INFO] Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**

The head contains one combined tag and three additional single-attribute tags covering the same directives (`telephone=no`, `address=no`, `date=no`). Redundant but not rendering-breaking.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unfired merge tokens detected in the visible HTML. The subscriber identifier in tracking URLs (`mi_u=dbe8da718e2997cce014546f16c5722eaa3c322f28d40f0a8386f8fd1...`) appears to be a populated hash, not a literal token placeholder. No issues found in this category.

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**

QA reports the header was not captured by the AgentMail relay. This header is required by CAN-SPAM (indirect, via best practice) and is a hard requirement under Google and Yahoo's February 2024 bulk-sender policy for lists >5,000/day. If the ESP is injecting it downstream, confirm it survives the relay. If not, it must be added at send time.

**[FAIL] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent**

One-click unsubscribe per RFC 8058 is mandatory for Gmail/Yahoo bulk senders. Without `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, Gmail will show its own unsubscribe UI rather than the brand's managed flow, and Yahoo may apply deliverability penalties.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` not captured**

SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown through the current relay path. Cannot confirm the email authenticates as `crateandbarrel.com`. If DMARC is in `p=reject` or `p=quarantine`, a relay that breaks DKIM alignment will cause inbox-level failures.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 22 CTAs use click-tracking redirect URLs and could not be probed. UTM parameter coverage and landing page alignment cannot be confirmed from this audit. A manual spot-check of two or three redirect chains (e.g., "Shop Breville," "Shop All Sale") is needed to verify:

- `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` are present on final destination URLs
- Redirects resolve to in-stock sale pages, not generic homepage

The webfont 403 failures do not affect landing pages.
