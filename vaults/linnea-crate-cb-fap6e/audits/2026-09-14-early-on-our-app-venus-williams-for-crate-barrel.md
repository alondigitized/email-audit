# Early on our app: Venus Williams for Crate & Barrel!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-14**
## Executive summary

- This is a repeat of yesterday's "Shop it first" Venus Williams collab email (2026-09-14, 6.00/10) — same phone-mockup hero, same tennis-racket imagery, same "app exclusive" hook, just retitled. That's two sends in one day pushing the identical collab with near-identical creative, which reads as cadence fatigue rather than a fresh beat. The hero itself is still strong: a real celebrity name, a clean product shot, and unambiguous app-exclusivity messaging that would stop a scroll.
- But the email sprawls badly after the hero. It stacks a Design Desk pitch, a "we love these for you" six-item product grid, a $500 sweepstakes app pitch, a credit card rewards pitch, and another Design Desk repeat — five distinct asks competing with the one thing the subject promised. The actual collab (what does Venus Williams' line even look like beyond one dress?) never gets shown again after the hero. This is a subject-line bait email: open expecting a collab lookbook, get a general-purpose Crate & Barrel circular.

## What's working

- Hero visual is genuinely strong — a real celebrity, a styled dress-on-model shot inside a phone mockup, tennis rackets as a clever visual nod to Venus Williams.
- "Be the first to shop our collab... only on our app" is a clear, specific value prop for the primary CTA (Download the Crate App).
- No render bugs — clean layout, no overlapping text or broken images, consistent brand typography throughout.
- Footer nav grid and social icons are complete and unbroken.

## What's weak

- This is the second near-identical Venus Williams app-exclusive email in the brand history within 24 hours (see 2026-09-14 "Shop it first" at 6.00/10) — same creative concept, diminishing returns.
- Only one product from the actual collab is shown (the dress) — no lookbook, no range, no sense of what's actually being sold.
- Email dilutes into an unrelated grab-bag: Design Desk (twice), generic sofa/dresser grid, $500 sweepstakes, and a credit card pitch — five competing CTAs after the hero.
- The "we love these for you" product grid has zero connection to Venus Williams or the collab — it undercuts the exclusivity narrative right after the hero sold it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email down to two modules: the Venus Williams hero/app-download CTA, and a short footer — kill Design Desk, the generic product grid, the credit card pitch, and the sweepstakes teaser. Let the collab breathe.
- 2. Show more of the actual collab — 3-4 product shots (not just the dress) so recipients see what "shopping first" actually gets them.
- 3. Since this is a same-day repeat of yesterday's collab email, differentiate the angle — e.g., "now with X new pieces" or "24 hours left before it's live everywhere" — otherwise this reads as an accidental duplicate send.
- 4. Move "Download the Crate App" CTA button higher/closer to the hero image itself so it's visible without any scroll on mobile.
