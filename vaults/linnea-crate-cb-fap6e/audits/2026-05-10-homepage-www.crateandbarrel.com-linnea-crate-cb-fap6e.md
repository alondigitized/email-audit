# Homepage snapshot · www.crateandbarrel.com · 2026-05-10
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-10**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Barrel is running a spring outdoor furniture sale, and they're clearly betting on that to be the story right now — big hero, warm landscape photography, "up to 30% off" as the lead. The brand reads immediately premium and tasteful. But landing here as someone who just registered, I'm getting stopped at the door by an email capture modal before I can even take in the page — that's a friction tax before I've had a single reason to trust them with my inbox.

## What's working

- **Rewards banner at the very top** — "Earn 10% Back in Rewards Dollars" is a legit hook for someone like me who is about to spend real money on a registry. I see it before anything else.
- **"the spring refresh event: up to 30% off" promotional strip** — the event framing (not just "sale") feels elevated, which matches the brand.
- **Outdoor furniture hero photography** — that warm golden-hour patio shot is genuinely beautiful. Even though outdoor isn't my main category, the aesthetic tells me this brand's visual direction aligns with what I'd want on my registry.
- **"Tabletop & Bar" in the secondary nav** — it's right there, one tap from wherever I land.
- **"built-to-last bestsellers" module** just entering the frame at the bottom — the confidence in quality messaging resonates with my registry mindset (I'm buying for keeps, not trends).

## What's weak

- **The immediate email capture modal** — I literally just opened the site and I'm being asked for my email AND mobile number before I've seen a single product. That's two required fields before I get to shop. It makes me feel like a lead, not a customer.
- **Hero is outdoor furniture, not registry-adjacent** — as a recently registered person, I opened this app thinking dinnerware, serving pieces, bar tools. The outdoor push doesn't speak to where I am in my life right now.
- **No registry entry point visible above the fold** — "Wedding Registry" should be surfaced prominently for someone like me. I see a "Personalization Shop" tab in the top nav but it's buried in a row of options; nothing is shouting "your registry is here."
- **No people in the imagery** — the patio shot is gorgeous but abstract. No lifestyle context of someone actually using the space or the pieces.
- **Fine print density in the modal** — the SMS legal block under the form is a wall of tiny text. It reads like liability, not transparency.

## Recommendations

- **Kill the cold-open modal or delay it until scroll depth ~40%.** Let me see the site first. An immediately-triggered modal asking for email + phone on first visit is the single biggest conversion killer on this page.
- **Add a "Complete Your Registry" persistent entry point** — a sticky or hero-adjacent CTA that routes registered users directly to their list. Right now that journey requires me to already know where to go.
- **Swap one hero tile to tabletop or kitchen** — a shot of a beautifully set dinner table with walnut accents and ceramic pieces would land squarely for me and every other registry shopper scrolling in May.
- **Surface the free-shipping threshold** — I can see 10% back in rewards, but I have no idea what free shipping looks like. One line in the promo strip ("Free shipping on orders over $X") would reduce hesitation before I even reach checkout.
