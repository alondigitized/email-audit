# NEW! 1500+ fresh finds, inspired by heritage designs
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Barrel is running the new-arrivals chapter it opened yesterday with Crate & Kids' "One day early: Be the first to shop 1500+ new arrivals" — and today's send is the main-brand equivalent. The visual execution is genuinely strong: the hero leans into the "heritage" brief with dark-wood vignettes, warm brass, and editorial lighting that earns the subject-line promise. The email looks premium and renders cleanly.
- The problem is structural, not visual. This is a pure brand-editorial send with no discount, no deadline, and no reason to click today rather than next Tuesday. After five days of countdown timers that trained subscribers to wait for deals, pivoting to "inspired by heritage designs" with zero offer is tonally right but commercially thin. The reader finishes the scroll having admired the photography and done nothing.
- The deeper issue: "1500+ new arrivals" is appearing in both this send and yesterday's Crate & Kids email. A subscriber to both brands — a very common overlap — sees the identical number twice in 24 hours. The freshness cue evaporates.

## What's working

- Hero vignette earns the "heritage" promise — warm amber tones, dark wood, styled room context, editorial and premium.
- Visual hierarchy is coherent top to bottom; each section flows without clutter or layout breaks.
- Tone reset is appropriate — no fake countdown timers, no "FINAL HOURS," a clean palate-cleanse after last week's urgency blitz.
- Subject line numeral ("1500+") anchors the editorial story with a concrete scale signal.

## What's weak

- No discount, no offer, no hook for action — the email gives subscribers every reason to admire and no reason to buy now.
- "1500+" used again within 24 hours across the brand family (Crate & Kids ran it yesterday); the scale signal loses impact on any subscriber who received both.
- "Inspired by heritage designs" is abstract; the subject-line back half doesn't tell you what categories or what's actually new.
- No loyalty/Crate & Barrel Club callout visible — a missed chance to make members feel the send is for them.
- Preview text unknown (not surfaced in the render); without an explicit preheader, the first crawlable text in-body (likely navigation copy) becomes the inbox preview — wasted line.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a soft offer to convert the scroll.** Even "complimentary shipping on new arrivals" or "free shipping through Sunday" turns an admiration-only email into a buying trigger. No new arrivals email needs to be sale-priced; it just needs a reason to click today.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject back half.** "Heritage designs" is brand-speak. Replace with what the categories actually are: furniture, lighting, tableware. Give the reader something to self-select on.
- 3. **Differentiate from yesterday's Crate & Kids send.** Use a different scale hook or a different headline entirely — the "1500+" collision trains subscribers to tune out the number.
- 4. **Inject a loyalty callout.** "Club members: early access to new arrivals" in the hero or a standalone module would lift click rates for the most valuable segment.
- 5. **Set an explicit preheader.** Whatever is rendering as preview text in the inbox needs to be a continuation of the subject, not nav copy or "view in browser."

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dark woods, warm brass, and ceramics built to last — shop what's just in`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta (line ~7):** Prevents user zoom. This violates WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is enforced even further by the global `*{ -webkit-text-size-adjust:none; }` rule. Combined, these two rules aggressively block text scaling on mobile.

- **Conflicting image max-width media queries:**
  ```css
  @media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }  /* declared first */
  @media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }  /* declared second — wins at 768px+ */
  ```
  At viewports ≥768px, both rules match; cascade order means the 640px query (`max-width: 640px`) overrides the 768px query (`max-width: 600px`). If the template container is 600px, images can bleed 40px past the container on wide displays. Reverse the declaration order or consolidate into a single rule.

- **Developer placeholder comment left in production:**
  ```html
  <!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
  ```
  Typo ("IMPUT"), empty placeholder, and internal workflow instructions are exposed in the delivered HTML.

- **Internal structure comments exposed to recipients:**
  ```html
  <!----- MI styling start-->
  <!----- CLIENT styling start----->
  <!--V2-->
  ```
  These are template-authoring artifacts. Not harmful, but they leak internal versioning and vendor tooling conventions.

- **`format-detection` meta tag duplicated:** The combined `content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no"` tag is followed by three separate single-directive tags repeating `telephone=no`, `address=no`, and `date=no`. Redundant but harmless.
