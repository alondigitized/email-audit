# New backpacks in every size, now starting at $39!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-05**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids doing what it does best when it stays in its lane: a single-category, size-guided backpack push timed exactly to back-to-school. The subject promises "$39" and "every size," and the email delivers — hero banner leads with "back-to-school sale: up to 20% off," then the body methodically walks small → medium → large backpacks, each with its own "what fits inside" list and named product options (Scoops, Shark School, Leopard Floral). That's a genuinely useful structure for a parent trying to size a backpack for a specific kid.
- Where it loses points is the back half. After a clean, on-topic "make it a bundle" CTA block, the email pivots hard into bedding, nursery furniture, a stroller/car-seat system, and a credit-card pitch — none of which has anything to do with backpacks or back-to-school. That's the same "guide masquerading as an ad" problem flagged in the 2026-08-05 pizza-party send (7.00/10): a well-executed top half diluted by an unrelated bottom half stapled on for cross-sell.
- Net: strongest, most literally on-target subject-to-content match seen from this brand this week — a clear step up from the nursery/review sends and the grilling-guide bait-and-switch. Ships as a strong 8-9 range send; the padding at the bottom keeps it from being a 10.

## What's working

- Hero delivers exactly what the subject promises — "the backpack size guide" with a visible "Shop Now" CTA, no bait-and-switch.
- Small/medium/large segmentation with per-size "what fits inside" checklists (folders, lunch box, tablet, etc.) is genuinely useful, sizing-anxious-parent content.
- Named product callouts (Scoops, Shark School, Leopard Floral, Dino Party Bundle) give the eye specific things to click, not just a generic "Shop Backpacks."
- $39 starting price and "ship free" are stated plainly and match the subject line verbatim — no ambiguity.
- Clean render throughout — no overlapping text, broken images, or layout breaks across the full 12,000px length.

## What's weak

- The email is nearly 3x longer than the backpack content requires — bedding, a nursery dresser, a glider recliner, and a stroller/car-seat bundle are all crammed in after the backpack sections, none of it relevant to a backpack-shopping visit.
- Small asterisks trail "up to 20% off" and "ship free" with no visible resolution in the render — flags exclusions the reader can't check without leaving the email.
- Hero CTA copy is generic ("Shop Now") despite sitting under a specific, well-branded headline — a missed chance to say "Find Their Size."
- No urgency or end-date anywhere — "back-to-school sale" with no deadline reads as always-on, not time-bound.
- No reviews or social proof on any backpack shown, despite this being a considered, fit-sensitive purchase.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the email at "make it a bundle."** Everything from the bedding/nursery/stroller block onward belongs in a separate send — it dilutes an otherwise tightly-targeted backpack email and risks the reader losing the thread before reaching the actual CTA buttons.
- 2. **Replace "Shop Now" in the hero with a specific CTA** — "Find Their Size" or "Take the Size Quiz" — to match the "size guide" promise in the headline.
- 3. **Resolve or drop the asterisks** on "up to 20% off" and "ship free" — either state the exclusion inline in small text or remove the asterisk if there isn't one.
- 4. **Add a deadline** to the sale banner ("ends Sunday") — turns a passive discount into a reason to act this week.
- 5. **Add one social-proof line** near the size-guide hero ("Rated 4.8★ by 2,000+ families") — backpacks are a fit/durability purchase, and this email has zero trust signal.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Backpacks for every size kid, starting at $39`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Find their perfect backpack fit — up to 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Small, medium, or large — our size guide makes it easy.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Machine-washable, durable, and ready for day one.`

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 86 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the automated HTTP probe (redirect domains, not independently verified) — link destinations unconfirmed.
- Open-tracking pixel `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png` appears **6 times** with identical `mi_u` params — likely one fire per template module; redundant beacon load.
- LiveRamp beacon `sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif` fires **5 times** with sequential `n=1`–`n=5` params — repeated third-party identity-sync calls in a single send.
- View-tracking beacon at `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/c1f73597-3512-4757-8b92-1f2e8b945839?mi_mid=...&cp_tp=v` present.
- Full CTA `href` values are not visible in the truncated HTML, so destination/redirect-chain integrity can't be confirmed from source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 43 `<img>` instances flagged with no `alt` attribute (not even `alt=""`), spanning product imagery (`image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/...jpg/.gif`), a Scene7 asset (`s7d5.scene7.com/.../CK_F26_BackpackGuide/...`), and tracking pixels. Content images need descriptive alt text; tracking/spacer pixels should use `alt=""` rather than omitting the attribute (screen readers may otherwise announce the raw filename/URL).
- Three separate `format-detection` meta tags (`telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no` plus individual `date=no`/`address=no`/`telephone=no` tags) are redundant — should be a single consolidated tag.
- Correct use of `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, `color-scheme`/`supported-color-schemes` (light), and MSO `xml:namespace` fallbacks for Outlook.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No merge tokens (e.g. `{{first_name}}`, `%%FIELD%%`) are visible in the captured `<head>`/style section. Body content was truncated before delivery, so personalization coverage cannot be confirmed either way from the available source.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable — the HTML source was truncated before CTA links/footer, and all 86 outbound links were skipped by the tracking-domain probe rather than resolved, so UTM parameters and landing-page alignment can't be verified from this data.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to the ~37 content images; set `alt=""` on the tracking/spacer pixels instead of omitting the attribute.
2. Consolidate the three duplicate `format-detection` meta tags into one.
3. Reduce duplicate beacon calls — 6x `mi.crateandbarrel.com` pixel and 5x `sr.rlcdn.com` beacon in one send adds unnecessary third-party requests.
4. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results` directly at the MTA/raw source level to rule out relay stripping vs. genuine absence before treating these as compliance defects.
5. Re-audit link/UTM continuity against the full (untruncated) HTML — current source cuts off before body CTAs and footer.
