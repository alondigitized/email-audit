# Want exclusive sneak peeks and sale alerts?
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- This is a hybrid email doing two jobs simultaneously and excelling at neither. The hero pitches "get on the list & get 10% off" — a list-acquisition/preference capture play — but this lands in the inbox of someone who is *already subscribed and receiving emails*. The existential question — why is Crate & Barrel asking me to join a list I'm clearly already on? — goes unanswered and erodes trust in the premise instantly.
- Below the hero, the email pivots into a fairly standard product scroll: a summer nugget-ice-maker feature, a "we love these for you" personalized grid, an app promo, and a Design Desk callout. Five distinct jobs in one send is a creative budget problem. None of them get enough real estate to land.
- The timing also stings. Yesterday's send led with "Up to 60% off the Summer Sale!" — the brand's single biggest value proposition of the season. Today's headline offer is 10% for joining a list. For a subscriber who saw both, this reads as a sharp downgrade, not a complement.

## What's working

- **Hero legibility** — "get on the list & get 10% off" is large, high-contrast, and immediately scannable; the offer is visible in under a second.
- **Seasonal product peg** — the nugget ice maker section earns its placement; it's genuinely summer-appropriate and product-specific, not just a lifestyle filler shot.
- **Clean rendering** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breaks visible anywhere in the email.
- **Sender recognition** — Crate & Barrel's logo and brand identity are immediately identifiable.
- **Subject line length** — 44 characters clears the mobile-safe threshold with room to spare.

## What's weak

- **Identity crisis at the hero** — subscribers who receive this email are already on the list; "get on the list" is literally false for them, which undermines the entire opening.
- **10% after 60% feels like punishment** — yesterday's Summer Sale blast (audited July 12) led with a dramatically larger discount; today's list-join incentive looks stingy by comparison.
- **CTA copy mismatches the action** — "Shop Now" follows "get on the list," but these are different verbs. If the goal is preference capture or sign-up confirmation, the button copy should say so.
- **No urgency anywhere** — no deadline on the 10% offer, no expiry on the ice maker feature, no "ends Sunday" anywhere; without a clock, nothing compels action over "I'll look later."
- **Subject buries the actual offer** — "Want exclusive sneak peeks and sale alerts?" doesn't mention 10% off, which is the only concrete hook in the email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Resolve the identity crisis first.** If this is a preference-update email for partially-opted-in subscribers, the hero should say "Update your preferences — and unlock 10% off." If it's genuinely for non-subscribers, it shouldn't go to active subscribers. Fix the audience segmentation before touching the creative.
- 2. **Put the 10% in the subject line.** The offer is the hook; burying it in the hero while the subject asks a vague question means the discount only works for people who open, not people who need convincing.
- 3. **Collapse to one job.** Kill the nugget ice feature, the app promo, or both. A preference/list-capture email and a product editorial are two separate sends; combining them dilutes both.
- 4. **Add an expiry.** "10% off expires in 48 hours" turns a passive offer into a reason to act now.
- 5. **Fix the CTA copy.** "Join the List" or "Claim My 10%" beats "Shop Now" when the ask is about email preferences, not a cart.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Claim your 10% off — join the insider list`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 10% off is waiting (sneak peeks inside)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Exclusive sale alerts + early access — opt in and save.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `First to know. First to save. Here's 10% to start.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Conflicting `img` max-width media queries — rule ordering bug**

```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```

Both rules fire at desktop widths (≥768px). Because the `640px` rule appears *later* in the stylesheet, CSS cascade gives it precedence — so images cap at `640px` even on wide viewports where `600px` was intended. The rule order is inverted. Fix: move the `min-width: 768px` block after `min-width: 640px`, or consolidate into a single rule.

**[MEDIUM] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta blocks user zoom**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

Prevents recipients from zooming on mobile. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[MEDIUM] `* { line-height: 100% }` universal reset**

Applied via the global `*` selector. A `100%` line-height (relative to font-size) on body copy (typically 14–16px) produces cramped, nearly unreadable text in clients that don't override it. This reset is inherited from the legacy MI reset block and conflicts with the intent of the `.f*` utility classes defined in the same `<style>`.

**[LOW] `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` in desktop stylesheet**

```css
html { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }
```

The desktop `<style>` block sets this to `none` (disables adjustment entirely) rather than `100%` (preserves it). This can cause text to render at unexpected sizes in WebKit-based clients like Apple Mail. The MI reset above correctly uses `100%`; the client stylesheet overrides it with `none`.

**[LOW] Dark mode not supported**

```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```

No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` rules present. Apple Mail, Gmail Android (dark mode), and Outlook 2021+ will force-invert or apply their own dark treatment, potentially making white text on white backgrounds invisible. Explicitly declaring `light` without a dark fallback opts the email out of system-managed dark mode without providing a manual one.
