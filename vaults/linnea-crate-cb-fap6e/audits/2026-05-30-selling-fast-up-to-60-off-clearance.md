# Selling fast: up to 60% off clearance!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- The email opens with a genuinely strong hero: warm floral photography, bold "up to 60% off + new markdowns" copy, and a crystal-clear "Shop Clearance" CTA — exactly what the subject line promised. For a Crate & Kids subscriber scanning for deals, that first fold is doing its job.
- Below the fold, the send falls apart into at least five competing blocks: bedding fabric, summer gear, a product recommendations grid, an app download push, and TheDesignDesk editorial content. Each section is individually competent; stacked together they dilute the clearance urgency established at the top and turn a sale email into a catalog browse. The "selling fast" urgency cue in the subject has no follow-through — there's no deadline anywhere in the body copy.
- The deeper issue is cadence. This is the sixth email from the Crate & Barrel / Crate & Kids family in three days, and it's the second clearance-forward send in 48 hours after the 5/29 Crate & Barrel "NEW MARKDOWNS! Shop up to 60% off →" blast (scored 9/10). Subscribers on both lists are getting déjà vu. The hero is good enough to overcome that for motivated deal-seekers; the open rate probably isn't.

## What's working

- Floral hero is visually arresting and immediately conveys a premium clearance moment, not a liquidation dump
- "Shop Clearance" is the most specific, unambiguous primary CTA this sender family has shipped across the reviewed sends — verb + category, no guessing
- "New! gear up for summer" gives the send a seasonal hook that differentiates from the prior week's generic markdown language
- Product recommendation grid adds a discovery layer for shoppers not committed to clearance browsing

## What's weak

- "Selling fast" is a stock urgency cue with no specific deadline — no "ends Sunday," no "June 1" cutoff, nothing credible to act on
- Five content sections below the hero scatter attention; the clearance message is effectively abandoned by mid-scroll
- No personalization anywhere — no name, no category signal, no "because you browsed X"
- TheDesignDesk editorial block at the bottom is a tonal mismatch in a clearance email; it belongs in a different send type
- Sixth email in three days from this sender family; open rates will reflect the list fatigue

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline in the hero** — "ends Sunday" or "June 1 only" anywhere in body copy. "Selling fast" has been trained into noise. A real date is the single highest-ROI change available.
- 2. **Cut to three sections max** — hero clearance → one curated product moment (summer gear OR the product grid, not both) → footer. Drop bedding banner, app promo, and TheDesignDesk.
- 3. **Anchor a hero product** — one stroller or crib at a crossed-out original price next to the clearance price would outperform the abstract "up to 60% off" claim every time.
- 4. **Differentiate from the 5/29 C&B blast** — since Crate & Barrel already hit the joint file with markdowns, this C&K send needs a separate angle: kids-exclusive items, deeper cuts, or new additions to clearance.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Strollers, cribs & summer gear — new markdowns added today`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta (line 7):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents user zoom. Violates WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Should be removed or changed to `user-scalable=yes`.

**Conflicting `img` max-width breakpoints:**
Two media queries set conflicting image constraints:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
```
On viewports ≥ 768px, images are capped at 600px even if the container is 640px wide. The 768px rule overrides the 640px rule — images will appear narrower than the container on wide desktop clients.

**Mobile breakpoint at 460px:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 460px)
```
iPhone 14/15 default viewport is 390px logical pixels; this breakpoint fires correctly. However, `.showmobile` hardcodes `width: 414px` — this exceeds the 390px viewport on current-generation iPhones and will force horizontal scroll.

**Dark mode:**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Dark mode explicitly disabled. Not a bug, but Apple Mail and Outlook will still force inversion on some elements — no dark-mode override CSS is present to control this.
