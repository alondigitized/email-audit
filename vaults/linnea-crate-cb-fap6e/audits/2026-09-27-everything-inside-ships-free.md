# EVERYTHING inside ships free →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-27**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, promise-matching send — the subject says "EVERYTHING inside ships free" and the hero literally reads "it. all. ships. free!" over a soft green nursery/baby-gear shot, immediately backed by an 8-tile grid (kids bedding, baby bedding, kids gear, baby gear, cuddlies, bath, curtains, throw pillows) all tagged "ships free." That's a strong, disciplined open two-thirds.
- Where it falls apart is exactly where Crate & Barrel Kids always falls apart per the brand history — after the free-shipping block, it pivots into a Venus Williams collab, a bestselling bedding sale, an "ending soon" up-to-20%-off essentials sale, a Nuna travel system push, a "we love these for you" product grid, a credit card pitch, an app download push, and a design-service CTA. That's seven distinct offers stacked after the one the subject promised, echoing the same sprawl called out in "Your no-stress holiday season starts…now!" (2026-09-26, 7/10) and "Make this holiday extra special!" (2026-09-26, 6/10).
- Verdict: excellent top, bloated bottom — the free-shipping story deserved to be the whole email, or at minimum the sale/collab/Nuna/credit-card/app stack needed to be trimmed to one follow-on offer instead of five.

## What's working

- Subject and hero are a near-verbatim match ("ships free" appears in both), so there's no expectation gap on open.
- The 2x4 category grid under the hero is genuinely useful — it shows the breadth of what ships free without forcing a click just to find out.
- "Shop All Free Shipping" CTA button appears twice (hero + after grid) in matching sage green, high contrast against white.
- No render bugs — every image, button, and text block displays cleanly with no overlaps or broken placeholders.
- The Venus Williams collab section and Nuna travel system section each have their own clear hero image and single CTA, so even mid-email they don't feel cluttered individually.

## What's weak

- Too many competing offers after the lead story: collab launch, bedding sale, "ending soon" 20% off, Nuna exclusive, product grid, credit card, app download, design service — a subscriber has no idea what this email is actually "about" by the bottom.
- The "ending soon! up to 20% off the best baby essentials" banner duplicates urgency language already used in the hero's eyebrow line ("ending soon! up to 20% off the best baby essentials →"), so it reads like recycled copy rather than a second reason to click.
- Credit card and app-download CTAs this deep in a kids' free-shipping email are pure friction — nobody opened this for a credit card pitch.
- The free-shipping offer itself never states a minimum order or expiration date anywhere visible, so "ends soon" has no credible deadline attached.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after the free-shipping grid and CTA — end on "Shop All Free Shipping" and drop the collab/sale/Nuna/product-grid/credit-card/app stack into a separate send or next week's email. This is the single highest-impact fix; the brand history shows every one of these sprawling sends scores lower than the focused ones.
- 2. If the sale and collab must stay, keep only one (the 20%-off essentials sale is more relevant to "ships free" shoppers than the Venus Williams collab) and cut the rest.
- 3. Add a real deadline to the hero ("free shipping ends Sunday 9/28") — bare "ending soon" isn't a credible constraint.
- 4. Move the credit card and app-download blocks to a dedicated lifecycle email, not a free-shipping promo.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free shipping on everything — nursery, gear & more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Ends soon: free shipping, zero exceptions`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `No minimum, no exclusions — just add to cart.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Bedding, strollers, bath, and more — all ship free right now.`
