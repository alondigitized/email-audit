# A registry (and perks!) you’ll love
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-03**
## Executive summary

- A registry-acquisition email from Crate & Kids targeting expectant parents. Well-produced with a calm lifestyle aesthetic, clear nursery category focus, and a logical funnel toward starting a registry. The fatal flaw for a general list send: if the recipient is not currently pregnant or planning a shower, this email has essentially zero relevance. The subject line is soft and benefits-vague, the offer is unclear (registry "perks" vs. a hard discount), and there's no personalization or segmentation signal to justify why this landed in my inbox today.

## What's working

- **Clean production quality.** The lifestyle photography is warm and aspirational — neutral nursery tones, soft lighting, premium feel consistent with Crate & Barrel's positioning.
- **Logical structure.** The email moves sequentially: hero (why register) → registry checklist/product categories → product recommendations → CTA. The funnel is coherent.
- **Category breadth on display.** Multiple product modules (furniture, bedding, décor) signal the full assortment without feeling cluttered.
- **Brand voice is consistent.** Soft, premium, lifestyle — no discount-first hard-sell that would undercut the brand.

## What's weak

- **No hard offer.** "Perks" is doing a lot of work without ever being defined in the visible hero. Registry completion discount? Cash back? Gift from Crate? The vagueness kills urgency.
- **Zero personalization.** No name, no life-stage signal, no "Congratulations" hook that would confirm this was sent to the right person at the right time.
- **Subject and hero misaligned on value.** The subject promises "perks you'll love" but the hero appears to lead with registry inspiration/lifestyle, not the perk itself. The reader has to do mental work to connect them.
- **Relevance risk is high.** Sending a registry email to someone not in a shower/baby moment is pure list fatigue. There's no fallback offer (gift-giving for a friend, gifting occasion) to salvage the send for non-pregnant recipients.
- **Product discovery is below the fold.** Multiple scrolls required to reach specific product modules, which reduces click depth for anyone who isn't already motivated.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the perk, not the lifestyle.** Put the specific benefit (e.g., "20% off completion discount" or "$50 welcome bonus") in the hero, not buried in a sub-section.
- 2. **Segment or personalize the send.** If triggered by a life-event signal (baby shower search, registry browse), say so. "We noticed you're exploring baby registries" converts better than a cold send.
- 3. **Add a gift-giver hook.** A second CTA ("Shopping for a friend?") captures the non-pregnant segment and keeps the email useful without a separate send.
- 4. **Sharpen the subject.** "Registry perks" is the buried lede — surface the specific reward in the subject or at minimum replace "perks!" with a quantified hook.
- 5. **Compress the scroll.** Move a specific hero product (e.g., the top-rated crib or stroller) above the fold alongside the registry CTA.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` / sender name mismatch**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
Sender is "Crate & Kids." Some email clients (Gmail browser, Outlook 365) surface the `<title>` as the window/tab label. This creates a brand inconsistency.

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks pinch-to-zoom on iOS. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Conflicting desktop image max-width media queries**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
Both target "desktop." At ≥768px the 600px rule overrides the 640px rule (cascade order), making the 640px rule unreachable at the widths it was likely intended for. One of these should be removed or the breakpoints reconciled.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
The tag appears three times: once as a combined value, then individually for `date=no` and `address=no`. Redundant but harmless; should be collapsed to one declaration.

**Duplicate `.ReadMsgBody` / `.ExternalClass` rules**
Both classes are declared in the MI styling block and again in the client styling block with identical selectors. No functional impact, but increases CSS parse weight and signals copy-paste template assembly without cleanup.

**DOCTYPE / meta charset inconsistency**
Document declares XHTML 1.0 Transitional (`-//W3C//DTD XHTML 1.0 Transitional//EN`) but uses an HTML5-style bare `<meta charset="utf-8">` (no closing slash, not `http-equiv`). The `http-equiv` version immediately above is sufficient; the second `<meta charset>` is redundant.
