# NEW ITEMS ADDED! Up to 50% off bestselling furniture →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's tenth-plus contact in the 4th of July sale sequence, now dropping on July 2nd with a "NEW ITEMS ADDED" hook stitched on top of the same furniture catalog format that's been running since at least June 26. The hero is a handsome navy sectional presented against a clean, dark lifestyle shot — the visual hierarchy works and the 50% off claim lands immediately. But the creative is exhausted: C&B hit the exact same "new items added / just got bigger" refresh angle on June 29th and again on July 1st, and a subscriber who's been paying attention will recognize the pattern rather than the novelty.
- The email is a catalog dump — sofas, dining, storage, accent pieces — which is fine for a warehouse-sale format. The problem is that "up to 50% off bestselling furniture" has been true every single day of this campaign. Nothing in this send gives a reason to click *today* specifically. The deadline is implied (4th of July) but never stated in body copy that's visible without scrolling. For a subscriber who already opened the June 30th and July 1st C&B emails and didn't buy, this one moves the needle very little.
- The email renders cleanly and the product photography is consistent with C&B's elevated aesthetic. This isn't a bad email — it's a fatigue problem dressed up as a new send.

## What's working

- **Strong hero visual** — the navy sectional in a dark-background lifestyle shot creates an aspirational anchor; the eye lands on the sofa and the "up to 50% off" claim together.
- **Clean column layout** — the multi-product grid below the hero is well-organized, clear product tiles, no cramped text.
- **Offer is specific enough** — "up to 50% off bestselling furniture" is a concrete discount bracket, not vague "savings."
- **Brand consistency** — typography, color palette, and logo treatment are unmistakably C&B; trust is pre-loaded.

## What's weak

- **"NEW ITEMS ADDED" is a recycled refresh** — C&B used the same structural hook on June 29 and July 1; a repeat subscriber reads through it.
- **No visible deadline** — the 4th of July is implied by the campaign context, but the hero and above-the-fold copy don't state a hard end date, draining urgency.
- **ALL CAPS subject-line opener** — "NEW ITEMS ADDED!" reads as a shout and edges toward spam-trigger territory.
- **Catalog dump without a lead product story** — the hero sofa isn't named or called out with a specific price; it's a mood image, not a deal anchor.
- **App download block feels out of place** — mid-email real estate used for an app promo dilutes the purchase focus on a sale-day send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a visible countdown or hard date in the hero** — "Sale ends July 4th" in the hero block converts the implied deadline into a real one. That single change lifts urgency for every reader.
- 2. **Name and price the hero sofa** — "Hudson Sectional — now $1,299 (was $2,199)" turns the hero from a mood board into a deal. Give readers a reason to click *that specific image.*
- 3. **Cut or move the app block** — on a sale-day send this is noise; push it to a post-purchase or non-sale email.
- 4. **Differentiate the subject from prior sends** — "new items added" has been used twice in 72 hours; this send needs a sharper hook.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends Friday: 50% off the sofa you've been saving`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last days — up to 50% off furniture, now with new picks`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sale wraps July 4th. Bestselling sofas, dining, and storage — all discounted.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied via wildcard selector (confirmed):**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```
The `body` correctly sets `100%`, but the `*` wildcard overrides it for all child elements. On iOS Safari this can cause text to render smaller than intended when the device auto-adjusts layout. Should be `100%` everywhere.

**Conflicting `img` `max-width` rules — 600px rule is dead code (confirmed):**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
At ≥768px both queries fire; the 640px rule appears later in the stylesheet and wins via cascade. The 600px declaration is never applied. At exactly 640–767px only the second fires. Net effect: images are always capped at 640px on desktop, never 600px. Likely unintentional.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta (confirmed):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents user zoom on mobile — an accessibility failure (WCAG 1.4.4). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Dark mode suppressed entirely (confirmed):**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Apple Mail and Gmail Android dark mode users will receive no dark-adapted styles. Acceptable as a deliberate choice, but should be documented as intentional.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags (confirmed):**
`format-detection` appears four times — once combined and three times as individual declarations. Redundant but harmless; clean up for maintainability.

**Duplicate `charset` declarations (confirmed):**
Both `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` and `<meta charset="utf-8">` are present. Redundant; keep only the `http-equiv` form for maximum XHTML client compatibility.
