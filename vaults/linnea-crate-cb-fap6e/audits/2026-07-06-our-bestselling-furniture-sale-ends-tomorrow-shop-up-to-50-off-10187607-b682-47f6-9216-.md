# Our bestselling furniture sale ENDS TOMORROW! Shop up to 50% off →
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's final-push send for the July Warehouse Sale — the "ENDS TOMORROW" follow-on to yesterday's "3 DAYS LEFT! Up to 60% off" urgency blast. The countdown timer leading the email is the right call and the lifestyle photography is polished. But the email has a real problem: yesterday's send headlined "up to 60% off" and today's drops that claim to 50%. A subscriber who opened both will feel like the headline discount is being walked back just as the deadline tightens — a trust bleed the brand can't afford on the send that needs to close holdouts.
- The second problem is length. This close to deadline the email should be lean. Instead it runs through dining, living, outdoor, a design-services upsell, and a product-recommendation grid — five distinct asks when the moment calls for one. Subscribers who opened yesterday already know the sale exists. This send needs to close, not catalog.
- Execution is solid otherwise — imagery is aspirational, no visible render bugs, and "ENDS TOMORROW" is credible urgency. But the discount regression and sprawl hold it back.

## What's working

- Countdown timer at the very top creates immediate, visceral urgency before the subscriber reads a word.
- "Bestselling furniture" framing is more credible than a generic "sale" claim — it implies the popular items, not clearance.
- Aspirational lifestyle room photography gives high-ticket furniture real environmental context, making a purchase feel less abstract.
- Including a design-services upsell at this stage is smart — big-ticket furniture shoppers are more likely to want it, and the sale creates the natural entry point.

## What's weak

- Discount headline regresses from 60% (yesterday's "3 DAYS LEFT" send) to 50% today — the wrong direction as urgency peaks.
- Email is too long for a countdown send; five distinct content sections diffuse the "act now" message.
- No visible social proof — "bestselling" is asserted but not demonstrated (no star ratings, review counts, sold-out flags).
- Subject line is 66 characters and clips on most mobile clients.
- "ENDS TOMORROW!" all-caps combined with exclamation point and → arrow stacks into mild panic-send signals.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the discount regression first.** If 60%-off inventory is gone, say so explicitly — "up to 60% off is nearly sold out, 50% still live." Silence reads as bait-and-switch to anyone who opened yesterday's send.
- 2. **Cut to two sections max.** Pick the two highest-conversion categories (sofas/seating + dining) and remove the rest. Outdoor, design services, and the recommendation grid belong in a different send.
- 3. **Add social proof to the hero.** One aggregate rating ("4.8★ from 12,000+ reviews") on the hero item earns the "bestselling" claim in the subject line.
- 4. **Trim subject under 50 chars** — lose the panic signals while keeping the concrete offer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Furniture sale ends tomorrow — up to 50% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last day: bestselling sofas & dining, up to 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dining, living, outdoor — your last chance at sale pricing.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the bestsellers before they go back to full price tonight.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Accessibility failure (WCAG 1.4.4). Recommendation: remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` on `*` selector**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applied globally, overrides user font-size settings across all elements. Should be scoped to `body` only.

**Conflicting image `max-width` breakpoints**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
The 640px rule appears after the 768px rule at equal specificity. For viewports 640–767px the cascade resolves to `max-width: 640px`, not `600px`. Likely a copy-paste error — consolidate or verify intent.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
`telephone=no` and `address=no` declared twice each (once combined, once individually). No functional impact; template hygiene issue.
