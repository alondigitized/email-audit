# Don’t miss this: Up to 60% off the Summer Sale!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's Summer Sale blast — a category-wide price-reduction email that has done nearly everything right structurally and very little right strategically. The hero visual (a moody coastal rocks-and-ocean scene) is arresting and breaks from the typical in-home product staging. But the email pivots immediately into category-browsing mode rather than anchoring on a hero product or story. The result is a well-executed catalog scroll that asks the subscriber to do all the work of discovery.
- After a heavy July send cadence from the Crate family — soccer kids bedroom, washable rugs, recipe inspo, and the nursery chair all landing in the last two days — this feels like the fifth distinct pitch in three days. The Summer Sale framing is seasonally correct, but there's nothing in the creative that makes this feel curated for *you* versus blasted to the entire list.
- The subject — "Don't miss this: Up to 60% off the Summer Sale!" — is functional but thin. "Don't miss this" is the email equivalent of "Hey" in a cold message: soft urgency with no new information. For a brand that showed genuine creative range with "Found: the 1,000 hour nursery chair →" just today, this is a step back to mass-blast defaults.

## What's working

- **Hero visual is distinctive.** The moody coastal scene earns a second look and sets a premium aesthetic that most sale emails abandon in favor of product grids.
- **Category grid is genuinely useful.** Bed & bath, outdoor, kitchen, entertaining, dining & living, rug, and all-sale links give a subscriber a fast entry point without hunting.
- **"We love these for you" product cards add tangibility.** Individual items with visible prices make the sale feel real rather than abstract.
- **Top-of-email shipping offer.** The "$15 off + free shipping on orders $99+" banner gives a secondary hook for subscribers not swayed by percentage discounts alone.
- **Card/loyalty module is well-placed.** The "card you love just got better" section adds member value without cluttering the hero.

## What's weak

- **"Don't miss this" is content-free urgency.** No deadline, no inventory signal, no specific product in jeopardy — it signals a blast, not a send.
- **No visible end date anywhere in the email.** Without a hard stop, "up to 60% off" reads as permanent, which kills click-now motivation entirely.
- **Four competing CTAs dilute the primary action.** Hero "Shop Now," category "Shop the Sale," new-arrivals "Shop Now," card "Learn More," app download, and TheDesignDesk signup is too many asks in one scroll.
- **The new-arrivals module contradicts the sale message.** "1500+ new heritage-inspired finds" mid-email puts the subscriber in two incompatible buying modes: clearance and discovery simultaneously.
- **Zero social proof.** No bestseller badges, star ratings, or review counts on any of the "we love these for you" product cards.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline to the subject line and hero.** "Ends Sunday" or "72 hours left" transforms this from a catalog into a time-pressured decision. Without it, everything that follows lacks urgency.
- 2. **Lead with one hero product, not the category grid.** Pick the single best-performing summer sale item, give it a before/after price, then use the category grid as the secondary layer. Today's nursery chair email proved a specific anchor outperforms a grid on its own.
- 3. **Kill or move the new-arrivals module.** It belongs in a separate send or below the footer. A subscriber in sale mode is not in discovery mode.
- 4. **Collapse to two CTAs maximum.** "Shop the Sale" (primary) plus one supporting action. Drop the app download and TheDesignDesk signup from this send entirely.
- 5. **Add a deadline strip below the hero.** Even a single copy bar — "Sale ends Sunday, July 13" — lifts urgency without restructuring the template.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 60% off ends Sunday — shop the Summer Sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Summer Sale is live: 60% off before it's gone`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping $99+ + new markdowns added daily`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Every category, deeply discounted — shop before Sunday`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Conflicting `img` max-width media queries**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
The 768px rule is declared after the 640px rule. On screens ≥768px both rules match; the last-declared wins (600px). On screens 640–767px only the 640px rule fires (640px). This is probably the intended behavior, but the ordering is confusing and fragile — a future edit could silently flip the cascade.

**No dark mode support**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Forces light rendering in Apple Mail and iOS Mail. No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` override exists. Background `#e6e7e8` and any hardcoded white content cells will invert unpredictably in clients that ignore this meta (Yahoo, Gmail Android).

**Aggressive universal `line-height: 100%` reset**
```css
* { line-height: 100%; }
```
Applied to every element. This collapses multi-line text and can make body copy illegible in some Outlook versions where `mso-line-height-rule: exactly` is also set on the same selector.

**`<title>` uses bare `&` in XHTML context**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
XHTML 1.0 requires `&amp;`. A strict XML parser will reject the document. Most email clients tolerate it, but it's a spec violation.

**Wrong generic font family for Times New Roman**
```css
.mso-font-fix-times_new_roman { font-family: 'Times New Roman', sans-serif; }
```
The fallback should be `serif`, not `sans-serif`. No functional impact in Outlook (the class exists for VML fallback), but it's a latent correctness bug.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
The combined declaration on line 1 already covers all signals:
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
```
Three separate single-attribute declarations follow redundantly. No client behavior impact, but dead markup.
