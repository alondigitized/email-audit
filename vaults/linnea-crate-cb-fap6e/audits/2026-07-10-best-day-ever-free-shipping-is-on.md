# Best day ever! FREE shipping is on →
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids' free-shipping announcement — the sister send to today's Crate & Barrel "STARTS NOW: Free shipping on orders over $59!" which scored 5/10 on the same day. The two emails are running in tandem and the creative strategy is nearly identical: a lifestyle hero, a product grid, and a utility offer designed to unstick carts. The problem is the same problem that sibling send had: free shipping is a friction-reducer, not a demand generator, and this email doesn't build desire before flashing the threshold.
- What's different — and arguably worse — here is the subject line. "Best day ever! FREE shipping is on →" is pure hyperbole that undersells the actual offer. The email body is long and multi-section (new arrivals grid, personalized picks, Design Desk promo, app download), which dilutes the single message that should be screaming: free shipping starts now, here's what to buy with it.
- The bottom half of the email is doing too many jobs at once. By the time a reader gets to the Design Desk and app download CTAs, the free-shipping hook has completely evaporated. A focused free-shipping send this is not.

## What's working

- Recognizable Crate & Kids branding — logo, color palette, and lifestyle photography are all consistent and clean.
- The "we love these for you" personalized section is a smart inclusion; signals this isn't a generic blast.
- Category nav bar (Storage, Lighting, Bedding, Furniture, Rugs) lets motivated shoppers self-route quickly.
- The "500+ new fall arrivals" callout ties the free-shipping offer to a reason to browse — contextual, not just a coupon.

## What's weak

- "Best day ever!" is meaningless hyperbole in the subject — a reader who just got this from Crate & Barrel two hours ago with a clearer subject will feel the letdown instantly.
- "FREE" in all caps in the subject is a spam-signal risk on aggressive inbox filters and reads as shouty in a brand that positions itself as elevated.
- The email is far too long — Design Desk, app download, and the footer editorial all compete with the free-shipping CTA and dilute it.
- No visible deadline for the free-shipping offer in the hero; urgency from the subject ("starts today") isn't reinforced in the body.
- The hero image reads as a living room lifestyle shot, not a Crate & Kids-specific nursery or kids' room shot — misses the brand's core emotional territory.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to be concrete** — "Best day ever!" earns zero purchase intent. Tell me what the threshold is and tie it to the moment. The sibling Crate & Barrel send was cleaner.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the hero** — "Free shipping through [date]" anchors urgency in the body, not just the subject.
- 3. **Cut the email in half** — kill the Design Desk and app-download sections for this send. A free-shipping email needs one CTA and a tight product grid. Save the Design Desk cross-sell for its own dedicated send.
- 4. **Swap the hero image** — show a kids' room or nursery, not a generic living room. Crate & Kids has a distinct emotional register; use it.
- 5. **Match the Crate & Barrel threshold copy** — if both sends have the same $59 threshold, say "$59" explicitly in the hero so the offer is immediately scannable.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `No minimum. Shop new fall arrivals + all your favorites.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Brand mismatch in `<title>`**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
Sender is "Crate & Kids" but the title references the parent brand. Affects browser tab, screen readers, and some preview pane renderers.

**Conflicting `img` max-width media queries**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
At ≥768px both rules fire; the 640px rule is declared last so it wins — overriding the intended 600px cap. Images can render up to 640px on wide viewports. Rule order should be reversed, or the 640px breakpoint dropped.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents user zoom on mobile — WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA) failure.

**Aggressive `*` reset**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; max-height:1000000px; line-height: 100%; }
```
`line-height: 100%` on every element can collapse multi-line text in certain clients. Safer to scope this to `body` and table elements only.

**Unreplaced template placeholder comment**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
Exposed in rendered source. Typo ("IMPUT") confirms this is an unfilled template slot. If no custom font is needed, the comment should be removed; if a font is needed, the `@font-face` / Google Fonts import is missing.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta**
`format-detection` appears four times (one combined attribute, then three separate tags for `date`, `address`, `telephone`). Redundant but not harmful; should be consolidated to one tag.
