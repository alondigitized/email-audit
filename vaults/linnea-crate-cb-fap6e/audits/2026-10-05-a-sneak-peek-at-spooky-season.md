# A sneak peek at spooky season 👻→
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-05**
## Executive summary

- This is two campaigns in one email. The subject and hero promise a Halloween "first look". The second banner, from "The Semi-Annual Bedroom & Nursery Event", gives the only real discount ("Up to 50% off"). The same event is also a green strip pinned above the hero. The Halloween hero is strong. A black cat and ghost bag, an orange pumpkin treat bag and a bunting garland make a charming, on-brand image. Under it, "Shop Halloween" is a clear orange button.
- For a home-furniture reader like Felix, the Halloween kids' content is off-target. The sale that matches that interest is squeezed into a strip at the top and a mid-email banner that says "Select styles", so it never gets a clear moment. The "we love these for you" grid then mixes a $1,350 stroller, hair bows, a countdown calendar, plates and a mirror, with no clear logic. Compare this to the 2026-10-04 "New markdowns added! Up to 60% off →" send, which led with one offer and one button. This one splits attention and is weaker for it.
- Judgement: a pretty, seasonal send that works as a lookbook, but it's unfocused for this persona. It would score higher if it led with either the Halloween story or the sale, not both.

## What's working

- The hero image is festive and eye-catching, with a clear "first look: the halloween shop" headline and a visible "Shop Halloween" button.
- Personalizable items ("Personalize It" on the treat bag and hooded towel) and the "Selling Fast!" tag on the throw pillows add useful product cues.
- The product grid shows prices, including a markdown ($72.00 → $53.78 on the bow knobs), so shoppers can judge value without clicking.
- The send has a seasonal tie-in, since it lands in early October.
- The Crate Card and "The Design Desk" free-appointment blocks give secondary conversion paths, and the render has no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- The first thing under the logo is a sale banner for a different campaign, which competes with the Halloween hero and muddies the message.
- The "Semi-Annual Bedroom & Nursery Event" is repeated mid-email with only "Select styles", so the 50% offer is vague and sits below the fold.
- The "we love these for you" grid has no theme. A stroller, cabinet knobs, a calendar, plates and a mirror don't read as personalized to a home-furniture buyer.
- The email is very long, with Crate Card, app download, Design Desk and store-locator modules stacked at the bottom. That dilutes the primary action.
- The legal and fine-print block is dense and long, which undercuts the "up to 50% off" claim.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one story. Either send a pure Halloween email with the sale removed, or lead with the sale and use Halloween as the secondary section. Splitting them costs the most clicks.
- 2. If the sale stays, move the "Semi-Annual Bedroom & Nursery Event" into the hero with a specific number and end date, and name the categories ("up to 50% off bedding & rugs, through Sunday").
- 3. Rebuild the "we love these for you" grid around one theme. A Halloween home-decor grid or a furniture grid would both read as more personal than this mix.
- 4. Cut the bottom modules (app, Crate Card, Design Desk) to one or two. Test dropping the Crate Card promo from promotional sends altogether.
- 5. Make the subject say something. "Sneak peek" is vague, so name the product or the deal.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Meet our Halloween treat bags 🎃 + up to 50% off bedding`
- **Subject Alt B:** `First look: the Halloween shop is open 👻`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Personalized treat bags, cat towels & ghost pillows, plus up to 50% off bedding and rugs.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Pumpkin treat bags from $34.95. Personalize yours before they sell out.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a Marketo-style template (Movable Ink `mi.crateandbarrel.com` and `dv.crateandbarrel.com` plus click-redirect links) that passes 57% of automated QA with 0 errors and 3 warnings. The main confirmed issues are missing alt text on 12 content images and `maximum-scale=1` in the viewport meta. The compliance warnings are unverified because the relay may have stripped the headers. The HTML source was truncated, so the footer, unsubscribe link, preheader and merge tokens could not be inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **65 tracking links were not probed.** The QA tool skipped them as click-redirect domains, so destinations, redirect chains and 4xx/5xx status are unverified.
- **Five LiveRamp pixels:** `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=dbe8da71…15f5a8&n=1` through `n=5`. The `s=` value is 64 hex characters, which looks like a SHA-256 hash, possibly of the recipient's email address. I can't confirm this from the truncated source. If so, it is a third-party identity-sync pixel that makes five requests per open and exposes a hashed identifier to a data broker. It should be covered in the privacy policy and consent language.
- **Six identical Movable Ink calls:** `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png?mi_u=dbe8da71…`. These are dynamic-content image requests, each carrying a per-recipient `mi_u` hash. If the service is slow or blocked, those slots will render empty.
- **Open and view pixels:** `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif?mi_u=…` and `https://dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/112ad0df-68d8-4bbf-b3dc-54966bc75bcc?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…`. Neither has `alt`, and the first-party `dv.` pixel has no file extension. These are expected tracking beacons, not defects. They only add noise to the alt-text warnings.

- **Tracking pixels (12 of the 24 warnings)** have no `alt` or `alt=""`. These should be given `alt="" width="1" height="1"` so they don't inflate the accessibility count.

- The Outlook conditional blocks (`mso-table-lspace`, `<o:PixelsPerInch>`) and `lang="en"` are present, so no issues there.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe not found (WARN).** The QA note says AgentMail may not capture this header. I can't say whether the sender omitted it or the relay stripped it.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post not found (WARN).** The same caveat applies. RFC 8058 one-click support is unverified, and Gmail and Yahoo require it for bulk senders.
- **Authentication-Results not found (WARN).** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The From domain `mail.crateandbarrel.com` is a subdomain, so alignment can't be confirmed.
- **Footer not verified.** The physical postal address and the visible unsubscribe link both fall in the truncated section.

None of these three is a confirmed defect. All depend on raw headers that the pipeline did not capture.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. All 65 links go through click-redirect domains and weren't resolved, so UTM parameters and landing destinations are unknown. No landing-page checks were possible.

2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
3. Fix the capture gap for the three header warnings. Pull the raw message with full headers to check `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results`. Mark them as "unverifiable" in the QA rule if the relay is known to drop them.
4. Resolve a sample of the 65 redirect links with a follow-redirects probe, so UTM and landing-page continuity can be audited.
