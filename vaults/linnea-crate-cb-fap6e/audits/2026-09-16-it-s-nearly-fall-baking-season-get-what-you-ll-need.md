# It’s nearly fall baking season! Get what you’ll need →
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-16**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel doing content marketing instead of a straight discount push — a full "fall baking" recipe hub (apple spice cake, pumpkin pie, cheesecake bars, pumpkin bread, maple cupcakes) each paired with the bakeware/tools needed to make it. It's a smarter creative concept than the last two sends in this brand history (the 5.00/10 "bestsellers roundup" and "top 10 kitchen finds" scrolls) because the product tie-ins feel earned by the recipe rather than just crammed into a list.
- But it inherits the same structural problem as those prior sends: it's an enormous vertical scroll with six-plus separate "shop" CTAs before you even reach the Venus Williams collab banner (yes, still running — the fourth appearance of that creative in this brand history) and then bedding, a Halloween teaser, and an app pitch. There is no discount, no urgency, and no single dominant CTA — just cumulative volume.
- Net: strong seasonal hook, well-photographed food, but it's trying to be five emails at once. Judgement — a competent seasonal content play dragged down by scope creep and zero-offer economics.

## What's working

- The hero image (bakeware laid out with fresh bread) is genuinely appetizing and immediately legible as "fall baking."
- Each recipe card pairs the food photo with the exact tools needed (e.g., pie dish + cake stand for apple spice cake) — a real content-to-commerce link, not a generic catalog dump.
- Star ratings appear under product SKUs, giving light social proof.
- No render bugs — clean grid alignment, no overlapping text or broken images anywhere in the scroll.

## What's weak

- Zero concrete offer anywhere — no %/$ off, no free shipping, nothing that would move a browser to a buyer today.
- Way too many competing CTAs (Shop Now, Get the Recipe ×5, Shop All Recipes, Shop Bakeware, Shop Electrics, Get More Recipes, Shop Fall Recipes, Shop Bedding Basics) — there's no single primary action.
- The Venus Williams collab banner is jammed into the middle of a baking email with zero topical connection — it's the same creative from three prior sends in this brand history, now appearing in an unrelated context.
- The email keeps going well past baking into bedding and a Halloween tease ("so scary, it's chic") — scope creep that dilutes the "get what you'll need for baking" promise made in the subject.
- Subject line has no urgency or specific offer — "nearly fall baking season" is a soft, generic seasonal tease rather than a reason to open now.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after the six recipe cards and the "more fall baking must-haves" grid — drop Venus Williams, bedding, and the Halloween teaser entirely; make three focused sends instead of one bloated one.
- 2. Add one concrete offer tied to the baking theme (e.g., "20% off bakeware this week") — right now there's no reason to buy today versus bookmarking a recipe.
- 3. Pick one primary CTA for the hero ("Shop Bakeware") and make the recipe CTAs secondary/smaller so the eye has a clear first action.
- 4. Give the subject a specific, time-bound hook instead of a vague seasonal tease.
- **Subject Alt A:** `6 new fall recipes + the bakeware to make them`
- **Subject Alt B:** `20% off bakeware, just in time for pie season`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Apple spice cake, pumpkin cheesecake bars, and the pans that make them easy.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your fall baking shopping list, sorted by recipe.`
