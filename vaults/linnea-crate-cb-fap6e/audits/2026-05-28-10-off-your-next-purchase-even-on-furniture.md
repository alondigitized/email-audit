# 10% off your next purchase—even on furniture.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Barrel's first post-Memorial-Day send is a pivot from five days of escalating sale urgency to loyalty-building — and the concept is sound. The 10% offer with "even on furniture" is the sharpest piece of copy C&B has sent in this entire reviewed cycle: it directly names the exclusion customers have been trained to expect and flips it into the entire pitch. That's a genuinely clever setup.
- The execution, however, buries that strong lead. What follows the hero is a kitchen-sink of five competing strategies: text/newsletter signup, a Design Desk consultation push (again — the same push from yesterday's 6/10 "You're invited!" send on 5/27), a Keene collection 10th anniversary spotlight, personalized product recommendations, and an app download ask. Subscribers who opened to claim a furniture discount have to navigate four unrelated modules before reaching anything that looks like a purchase path.
- The Design Desk appearing twice — mid-email and again in the footer — is the clearest editorial failure here. It signals this email wasn't pruned for focus; it was assembled by module-add. The 10% off is a good enough hook to anchor a clean single-CTA send. This isn't that.

## What's working

- "Even on furniture" in the subject is the best copy in this entire Memorial Day cycle — it preempts the #1 objection before the email even opens.
- The hero lifestyle image holds C&B's elevated post-sale editorial register; no jarring urgency graphic after a week of red banners.
- The "we love these for you" product grid surfaces specific items with prices — a frictionless shopping path that doesn't require a signup.
- The "new! The card you just got better" loyalty module adds relationship continuity at the right moment in the sale cycle.
- Clean render throughout — no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breakage.

## What's weak

- Five competing objectives destroy focus: text signup, design consult, Keene anniversary, product recs, app download — the email doesn't commit to a single win.
- The 10% offer is gated behind a text or newsletter signup, adding friction at the exact moment a motivated furniture buyer is ready to click through.
- The Design Desk module appears twice with near-identical copy — a clear sign of no editorial pass; the 5/27 "You're invited!" send (6/10) already made this ask.
- The app section's framing — "including your extra 10% off" — implies the 10% is app-exclusive, which directly contradicts the email hero's text/newsletter signup framing.
- No expiry on the 10% offer; there is zero urgency to act now rather than next week.

## Recommendations

- 1. **One email, one job.** Strip to the 10% offer + product recs. Cut Design Desk to a single footer text link (or hold it for its own send). Cut the Keene module. Cut the app section. The subject earned the open — let the body deliver.
- 2. **Remove the signup wall.** If this is an acquisition email designed to grow the text list, own that frame entirely: make the signup the hero action and design around the value exchange. If it's a retention/purchase email, skip the signup gate and deliver the discount directly to the subscriber.
- 3. **Carry "even on furniture" into the body.** The subject's strongest line disappears the moment the email opens. Feature a furniture piece with a "10% off — yes, really" label or callout badge to reinforce the claim.
- 4. **Add an expiry.** "Valid through June 1" or "This week only" converts passive interest into same-session action — every prior send in this cycle used urgency effectively.
- 5. **Kill the duplicate Design Desk module.** One mid-email placement is enough; the footer repeat wastes real estate.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[ISSUE] Conflicting `img` max-width rules across breakpoints**
Two media queries set contradictory values:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
The 640px rule is declared after the 768px rule. For viewports 640–767px, both match and the last declaration wins (`640px`). For viewports ≥768px, the 640px rule still wins by cascade order — the `600px` cap is effectively dead. Images will render at up to 640px on all desktop clients, which may not match the intended 600px layout width.

**[ISSUE] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">
```
Prevents user zoom. Violates WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Some email clients (Gmail iOS, Apple Mail) expose this to the WebView and will block pinch-zoom for low-vision readers.

**[ISSUE] No dark mode support**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Explicit light-only declaration. Apple Mail and Outlook for Mac will not apply system dark-mode inversion, but images with white or light backgrounds will appear as hard-cutout artifacts against the dark chrome. At minimum, dark-mode background overrides (`prefers-color-scheme: dark`) should be considered for the outer body/wrapper.

**[ISSUE] Unresponsive viewport band (461–639px)**
Mobile rules fire at `max-width: 460px`; desktop rules fire at `min-width: 640px`. Devices rendering at 461–639px (e.g., large phones in landscape, small tablets) receive no responsive overrides and fall through to fixed-width desktop layout.

**[MINOR] Universal `line-height: 100%` via `*` selector**
```css
* { line-height: 100% }
```
Overrides computed line-height on all descendants. Can collapse multi-line text in certain clients and breaks accessible text spacing. Prefer explicit per-element declarations.

**[MINOR] Duplicate `format-detection` meta declarations**
`format-detection` appears four times (once combined, then three individual `telephone`, `address`, `date` entries). Redundant but harmless; suggests a template merge error.
