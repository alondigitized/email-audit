# You need to see the latest designs from KitchenAid
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's "top 10 kitchen finds this fall" roundup, and KitchenAid gets top billing in the subject but is just one of ten products in an extremely long vertical scroll. The hero does its job — a moody blue KitchenAid stand mixer shot with "new! KitchenAid Design Series Luminaire" — but after that it's a relentless product-after-product cascade (Le Creuset, De'Longhi, Ninja, Caraway, Campanha, JURA, GreenPan) each with its own "Shop Now" button, which dilutes any single CTA and turns this into a catalog rather than a curated pick.
- Compared to the brand's recent Venus Williams run (9/14–9/15, which climbed from 5-6 up to 8.00/10 once it committed to one hero story), this email regresses to the same "bestsellers roundup" format as the 9/14 "Inside: Quality-crafted bestsellers" send (5.00/10) — same room-by-room/product-by-product scroll, same "Shop Now" spam, same lack of a single decisive offer. The subject over-promises a KitchenAid feature and the body under-delivers by burying it under nine other appliances.
- Judgement: functional but unfocused. It'll work for browsers already in a kitchen-upgrade mindset, but the subject/body mismatch and sheer length will cost opens and clicks from anyone expecting a KitchenAid-centric story.

## What's working

- The hero visual (blue stand mixer on a styled shelf) is well-lit and on-brand, and it matches the subject's KitchenAid callout directly below the top nav.
- "new!" and "new + exclusive!" badges are used consistently to signal freshness across the ten featured items.
- The closing "It's all on the app" panel with a phone mockup and a $500 sweepstakes tease adds a secondary hook for app installs.
- Clean, consistent product photography throughout — no broken images or layout breakage visible.

## What's weak

- Ten "Shop Now" buttons in one email is CTA overload — nothing is primary, so the eye has nowhere confident to land after the hero.
- The subject promises "the latest designs from KitchenAid" but KitchenAid is one card among ten; most of the email is unrelated appliances and cookware.
- No visible price, discount, or bundle offer anywhere in the scroll — every card is "new/exclusive" framing with zero pricing signal, unlike the "Reserve Pro" or "chance at $500" hooks that at least imply value.
- The email is exhaustingly long for a scroll-and-decide format; by the time a reader hits Ninja SLUSHi or GreenPan, the KitchenAid hook from the subject is a distant memory.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into two emails: a KitchenAid-only "new Design Series Luminaire" launch (matching the subject) and a separate "fall kitchen roundup" catalog email — don't force a subject that only covers 10% of the content.
- 2. Cut the product count in the roundup version to 4-5 hero items max, each with more breathing room, rather than ten stacked cards.
- 3. Add at least one visible price or offer (e.g., "starting at $XXX" or a bundle discount) — right now nothing here signals value, only novelty.
- 4. Move the "$500 sweepstakes / app-exclusive" panel higher — it's a strong hook currently buried at the very bottom after an exhausting scroll.
- 5. Tighten the subject to match actual hero content if keeping the multi-product format.
