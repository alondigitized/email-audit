# ICYMI: Our new Venus Williams kids collab! 🎾
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids running an "ICYMI" reminder for its Venus Williams collab — a smart move since a re-send to non-openers of a collab launch is exactly the kind of email that earns its keep. The room-by-room tour format (bedroom, playroom, study space) is the same well-organized template the brand leaned on for the 09-17 "mood board" and "birthday shop" sends, and it holds up: consistent green/navy tennis-court palette, tennis-ball and racquet motifs woven into styling, and a clear "championship-worthy decor" through-line that ties the celebrity name to an actual design point of view rather than just slapping a name on generic furniture.
- Where it falls down is exactly where a collab email needs to be strongest: there's no single hero shot of Venus Williams with the product up top, no visible price or discount anywhere in the render, and the "ICYMI" urgency promised in the subject never shows up in the body — no "collection is almost gone" or timestamp cue. The Venus photo doesn't appear until roughly two-thirds of the way down, which buries the actual reason someone opens an ICYMI email.
- Solid execution, weak targeting of the "you missed this" premise. Middle-of-the-pack, in line with the brand's recent 5-6 range rather than the 8/10 mood-board send.

## What's working

- Consistent tennis-inspired styling throughout (racquet decor, tennis balls as accent props, green/cream/navy court palette) reinforces the collab concept visually, not just in copy.
- Room-by-room structure (bedroom → playroom → study) gives the email a clear narrative arc and multiple distinct "Shop the Collab" entry points.
- Italic pull-quotes ("the room I always dreamed of as a kid," "diving into the opportunity...") add a design-magazine tone that fits the brand voice.
- Venus Williams portrait section, when it finally appears, is a clean, well-lit lifestyle shot that humanizes the collab.
- Recommended products carousel at the bottom (with visible prices) gives a concrete next step for scrollers who didn't click a hero CTA.

## What's weak

- No visible price, % off, or any offer anywhere in the hero or body sections — this is pure lookbook, no purchase incentive.
- The "ICYMI" urgency from the subject line has zero visual reinforcement in the body (no "still available," no countdown, no scarcity cue).
- Venus Williams' name/face doesn't appear until near the bottom of a very long email — for a collab email, the celebrity should anchor the hero, not show up as an afterthought.
- The email is extremely long (nine-plus full sections before reaching product prices), which is a lot of scrolling before any concrete price signal shows up.
- Repeated "Shop the Collab" CTA text throughout with no variation makes the sections blur together on a skim.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move a Venus Williams hero shot (with product) to the very top, replacing or pairing with the current opening room shot — the subject promises a "collab," the hero should deliver the face immediately.
- 2. Add a visible offer or urgency element (e.g., "collection is going fast" banner, or a specific % off) near the top — right now there's no reason to act now versus later.
- 3. Trim the room-by-room sections from five to three; the format is repetitive at this length and risks losing scroll-through before reaching the Venus content and prices.
- 4. Vary CTA copy per section ("Shop the Bedroom," "Shop the Playroom," "Shop the Study") instead of repeating "Shop the Collab" — it reinforces where the reader is in the story and reads less templated.
- 5. Pull one or two product prices up into the hero/body sections instead of only in the bottom carousel, so price-conscious scrollers see value earlier.
