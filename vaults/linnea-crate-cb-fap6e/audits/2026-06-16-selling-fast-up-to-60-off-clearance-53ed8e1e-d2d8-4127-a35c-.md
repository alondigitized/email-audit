# Selling fast: up to 60% off clearance!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a clearance blitz from Crate & Kids — "up to 60% off + new markdowns" in the hero is the strongest price offer the brand has put forward across the sends audited this week, and the visual is big and instantly legible. If you're a parent with nursery items on a wishlist, that hero is enough to drag you in.
- The problem is everything below it. The email runs five distinct campaigns in sequence: the clearance offer, a lilac color story, a personalized product picks row, a credit card pitch, an app download push, and a free design consultation CTA. Each might be a defensible standalone send; stacked here, they hollow out the clearance urgency by the time the reader reaches the footer. Compare this to the June 14 C&K quick-ship email — that one had one idea and one CTA. This one has six.
- Verdict: a legitimately strong offer buried under unrelated marketing noise. The hero does its job; the rest of the email undermines it.

## What's working

- **Hero offer reads instantly**: "up to 60% off + new markdowns" needs zero parsing — the discount is the first thing the eye lands on.
- **Subject line is punchy and mobile-sized**: "Selling fast" creates scarcity, and at 38 chars it clears the mobile truncation window cleanly.
- **Product tiles include visible pricing**: The "we love these for you" row gives shoppers a real price anchor before they commit to a click.
- **Lilac color story is visually cohesive**: The nursery scene in purple tones gives a secondary editorial reason to browse beyond pure clearance.
- **Clean render throughout**: No broken images, overlapping text, or layout breakage visible across the full email.

## What's weak

- **Five competing CTAs below the hero**: Clearance → Lilac → Credit card → App download → Design Desk. Each one dilutes the next and diffuses purchase intent.
- **Credit card pitch is tone-deaf in a clearance email**: Interrupting a discount-motivated parent with a financial product kills shopping momentum at exactly the wrong moment.
- **"Up to 60% off" is the oldest hedge in retail**: No floor percentage means savvy shoppers assume one crib qualifies at 60%, the rest are 25%. Erodes trust.
- **No hard deadline**: "Selling fast" is vague urgency. A specific date ("ends Sunday") would actually close the loop.
- **App download competes with the conversion goal**: A parent ready to buy clearance nursery furniture does not want a detour through the App Store first.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the credit card and app sections from this send** — those are separate audience moments and belong in dedicated emails, not mid-clearance.
- 2. **Add a hard end date in the hero**: Swap "Selling fast" copy for "Ends June 22" or equivalent — both in the subject and as a banner near the CTA button.
- 3. **Surface a floor percentage**: "30–60% off clearance" builds more trust than "up to 60%" and still leads with the ceiling.
- 4. **Collapse to two CTAs max for this send**: Primary = Shop Clearance, Secondary = Shop Lilac. Everything else cut or deferred.
- 5. **Default the product tiles to clearance items**: "We love these for you" should surface discounted SKUs first to reinforce the email's one job.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends soon: 60% off clearance + new markdowns`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your nursery wishlist — now up to 60% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns added — cribs, gliders & more on sale now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Clearance + new markdowns: shop before it sells out.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional layout with Movable Ink (MI) dynamic content and LiveRamp retargeting. Four custom webfont assets return 403s, 26 images carry no alt text, and three compliance-relevant headers were absent from the AgentMail relay capture.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Broken webfont assets (4 × HTTP 403)**

All four `@font-face` woff2 files served from `crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/` return 403 Forbidden:

```
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/1e07d600-3787-4517-bd3d-b140f9e54ec2.woff2
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/7ec25fd5-2752-4d84-b307-af3faabc21bb.woff2
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/aaa41944-25b3-4265-80b2-5564efe9b2d7.woff2
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/fd5f8f50-d539-4a7d-b893-99501d746ef2.woff2
```

Clients that load remote fonts (iOS Mail, some webmail) will fall back to the system stack silently. The `@font-face` declarations in `<head>` are dead weight and should either be fixed or removed.

**22 click-tracked links not probed**

All outbound links pass through a click-redirect domain and were skipped by the HTTP probe. Destination URLs and UTM coverage could not be verified (see §6).

**Tracking pixel duplication**

`mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png` fires six times with an identical `mi_u` token. This is likely intentional for MI dynamic-content rendering, but warrants confirmation that it is not a template copy-paste error inflating open counts.

`sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif` fires five times (`n=1` through `n=5`), which is standard LiveRamp audience sync behavior — no action needed.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**26 images missing `alt` text** — confirmed by QA. Affected assets span product photography, GIFs, spacer images, and the MI tracking pixel. Partial list of content images:

```
bbf6f9d4-86cc-437a-aa80-391067b74306.png  (header/logo area)
68910f1a / 75ad9bd7 / 62b7b090 / 5429e84a / 87a83d3f / e5169222 / 5d324a1d / c044610c / 971aa10f (product shots)
2026%5F0315%5FCBK%5FClearance%5F01%5F05.gif  (hero GIF)
33367d69 / 62b8e289 (animated GIFs)
```

Alt-blocked environments (Outlook by default, some corporate proxies) will render blank boxes for every content image. Spacer/pixel images should use `alt=""`, not omit the attribute.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

This prevents user zoom on mobile — an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4). Should be removed or replaced with `user-scalable=yes`.

**Duplicate `<meta name="format-detection">` tags**

`telephone=no`, `address=no`, and `date=no` each appear twice (once in a combined tag, once standalone). Redundant but harmless; clean up to reduce parse noise.

**`<title>` brand mismatch**

```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```

The sending domain/brand is `Crate & Kids`. The `<title>` is used by some screen readers and accessibility tools as document context. Should read `Crate & Kids`.
