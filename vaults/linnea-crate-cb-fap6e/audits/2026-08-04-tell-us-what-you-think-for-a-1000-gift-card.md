# Tell us what you think for a $1000 gift card
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-04**
## Executive summary

- This is the third $1,000 review-sweepstakes email Crate & Barrel has sent today under a different subject line than the "Write a review. Enter for a chance to win $1,000. Find out how →" send already logged at 8.00/10 for 2026-08-04 — same offer, same August 31 deadline, same "Write a Review" CTA, just re-headlined as "Tell us what you think for a $1000 gift card." A subscriber who opened both in the same inbox session would immediately clock the duplication.
- Past the hero, the email sprawls into an unrelated grab-bag: a summer-produce recipe push, a 15%-off Aspen collection sale, a six-item product grid, a new credit card pitch, an app download ad, and a design-consultation offer. None of it reinforces the review ask — it dilutes it. The hero itself is clean and the CTA is unambiguous, but by the time a reader scrolls past corn on the cob and dining tables, the sweepstakes hook has been forgotten.
- Given the same-day duplication and the unfocused body, this reads as a lower-effort send than the other Crate & Barrel emails logged today. Worth flagging to the sender as cadence risk, not a creative win.

## What's working

- The hero is clean and legible: bold "$1000" callout, three-star icon, and a clear one-sentence offer explanation with a hard deadline (August 31).
- Two clearly differentiated hero CTAs ("Write a Review" / "Shop New Arrivals") give the reader an immediate next step either way.
- No visible render bugs — images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, product grid is evenly aligned with visible pricing.
- The lower modules (produce, sale, product grid, card, app, design service) are each internally well-formatted even if the overall sequencing is scattershot.

## What's weak

- This is a near-duplicate of the same-day "Write a review. Enter for a chance to win $1,000. Find out how →" send — same sweepstakes, same deadline, different headline. Sending the identical offer twice in one day under a disguised subject reads as spammy cadence, not a genuine second touch.
- The body has no thematic throughline: review sweepstakes → summer produce recipes → 15% off sale → product grid → credit card → app → design service. Seven unrelated asks in one email crowds out the primary CTA.
- No actual review content (star ratings, testimonial quotes) appears anywhere in an email whose entire premise is "tell us what you think" — a missed chance to show social proof.
- Fine print is scattered across multiple modules (asterisks on the review copy, the 15% off, the shipping line) without a single consolidated terms callout, which reads as slightly evasive even though nothing is hidden outright.

## Recommendations

- 1. Don't send this the same day as the other review-sweepstakes email — pick one subject/creative and hold the second for a follow-up later in the week to different non-openers.
- 2. Cut the email down to the sweepstakes hero + one supporting module (e.g., the product grid) and move produce/card/app/design-service pitches to their own sends — a single-purpose email will convert the review ask better than a kitchen-sink one.
- 3. Add a visible star-rating strip or a pulled customer quote near the hero to make "tell us what you think" feel like it's building on real reviews, not just soliciting them.
- 4. Consolidate all sweepstakes terms into one clearly-labeled line near the CTA instead of scattering asterisks through the email.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your review could win you $1,000`
- **Subject Alt B:** `2 minutes, 1 review, $1,000 up for grabs`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Rate what you bought — sweepstakes ends August 31.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `No purchase required. Enter before August 31.`

# Technical Audit — Crate & Barrel "$1000 Gift Card" Email

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is built on a legacy XHTML Transitional table-based template (Cheetah/MessageIntegrity-style ESP markup) with standard MSO/Outlook conditional comments and mobile media queries; core rendering scaffolding is sound, but the message fails multiple deliverability/compliance signals and ships with widespread missing alt text.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 57 tracking/click-redirect links were present but skipped by automated HTTP probing (opaque redirect domains), so destination validity could not be confirmed programmatically — flagged as unverified, not broken.
- Multiple tracking pixels are embedded: `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png` (6 instances), `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif`, `sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif` (5 sequential instances, `n=1`–`n=5`, LiveRamp/RampID sync), and `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/9b24b056-...` (DoubleVerify). This is a heavy pixel load (12 total tracking beacons) typical of an ESP + ad-verification + identity-resolution stack.
- No malformed or duplicate `href` values could be confirmed from the truncated source; full link audit would require the untruncated HTML.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on all content images** — confirmed via QA: 7 distinct content images (`bbf6f9d4...png`, `7f976451...gif`, `b5b3598f...jpg`, `91e28e1d...jpg`, `2b148ce7...jpg`, `97b139b7...jpg`, `c11ea49e...jpg`, `971aa10f...jpg`) plus all tracking pixels lack `alt` attributes. For content images this degrades screen-reader and image-blocked rendering (Outlook default); tracking pixels correctly having no alt is not an issue, but content images should carry descriptive `alt`.
- Template uses `<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD XHTML 1.0 Transitional//EN">` — an outdated doctype convention still common in ESP boilerplate; not itself a bug given MSO conditional fallback is present.
- `img { display:block; }` and `border:0` resets are correctly applied, standard defensive CSS for cross-client image rendering.
- Minor: HTML contains a leftover template placeholder comment `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` (typo: "IMPUT"). Cosmetic/inert (inside an HTML comment, no functional impact) but indicates the template wasn't fully cleaned before send.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No merge tags, personalization placeholders, or unresolved token syntax (e.g. `{{first_name}}`, `%%FIELD%%`, `[FNAME]`) were visible in the provided HTML excerpt. No issues found in the visible portion; note the source was truncated, so tokens later in the document could not be checked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header not found** — either absent from the message or not captured by the AgentMail relay. CAN-SPAM does not mandate this header (an in-body unsubscribe link suffices), but its absence prevents one-click unsubscribe in Gmail/Yahoo/Outlook inbox UI.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found (RFC 8058)** — one-click (POST-based) unsubscribe is not confirmed supported. Combined with the missing List-Unsubscribe header, this is a deliverability risk given Gmail/Yahoo's 2024 bulk-sender requirements mandate one-click unsubscribe for high-volume senders.
- **Authentication-Results header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status could not be verified via the AgentMail relay. This is a QA/tooling visibility gap rather than confirmed evidence of an actual auth failure; flagging as unverified, not a failure.
- In-body unsubscribe link presence/wording could not be confirmed from the truncated HTML excerpt provided.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Cannot be assessed: all 57 outbound links route through tracking/click-redirect domains that were skipped during probing, so query-string UTM parameters and final landing-page destinations are not visible in the data provided. No conclusion drawn — flag as unverified rather than pass/fail.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to the 7 content images identified above; leave tracking pixels alt-less (correct as-is).
2. Confirm List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post headers are actually being sent (may be a relay-capture gap rather than a true absence) — verify against raw SMTP headers outside the AgentMail relay.
3. Independently verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC status via a raw header capture, since Authentication-Results wasn't observable through this pipeline.
4. Re-run link/UTM validation against the unredacted final HTML (post click-tracking resolution) to confirm landing-page continuity.
5. Remove the leftover `[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT...]` template comment during final QA pass.
