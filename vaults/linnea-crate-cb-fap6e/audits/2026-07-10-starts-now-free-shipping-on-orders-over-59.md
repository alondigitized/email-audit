# STARTS NOW: Free shipping on orders over $59!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- This is a sitewide free-shipping threshold email — a utility send designed to clear basket hesitation rather than inspire purchase intent. The hero is a tall vase with foliage on a wood table: clean, on-brand, but functionally decorationless. The offer ("free shipping on orders over $59") sits in a small-type callout below the brand bar, surrounded by a four-image category grid, a sofa hero feature, a "we love these for you" recs block, and then app/newsletter promotion modules that bury the offer further. By the time you reach the fold, the email has lost its thread entirely.
- Crate & Barrel's last two strong sends — yesterday's Wüsthof/KitchenAid deals drop (6/10) and the 1500+ new arrivals send (8/10) — showed the brand can execute tight, offer-led layouts. This one regresses to a department-store catalogue format where no single product or hero offer earns visual authority. The free-shipping message is the subject, but the email doesn't structure itself around it: the category grid, the sofa moment, the recs block, and the app download all compete equally.
- Bottom line: this is a passive promotional email that won't drive incremental clicks. It functions as a brand touchpoint, not a conversion email.

## What's working

- Free-shipping offer is stated clearly in the sub-header callout with a legible threshold ($59) — no fine-print ambiguity visible.
- The hero image is clean, high quality, and consistent with C&B's neutral/editorial aesthetic.
- Category grid below the hero gives multiple entry points (tableware, textiles, kitchen tools, etc.) so different buyer types have somewhere to go.
- The sofa "living room setup" section acts as a visual anchor mid-scroll and gives the email a second moment of product specificity.

## What's weak

- The free-shipping offer — the entire reason this email exists per the subject line — is set in small, non-bold type and visually outranked by the hero image, category grid, and the sofa module. The email promises a deal and then makes you hunt for it.
- No urgency signal anywhere visible. "STARTS NOW" in the subject implies a time window, but the email body never shows when this ends — no deadline, no countdown, no expiry copy.
- The "we love these for you" personalization block is generic-looking: product thumbnails with prices but nothing that signals actual personalization.
- App download / The Design Desk / newsletter join modules stack at the bottom and dilute the email's singular purpose. This is a shipping-offer send, not a platform onboarding email.
- No CTA on the hero unit itself — the "Shop Now" button is visible but buried well below the fold after the category grid.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the free-shipping offer the hero.** Replace the vase lifestyle image with an offer-first hero: large typographic treatment of "FREE SHIPPING" over $59 threshold, one CTA button ("Shop now — free shipping included"), and nothing else above the fold. The creative can still be on-brand without burying the offer.
- 2. **Add a deadline.** If "STARTS NOW" is true, end time must also exist. Add "Offer ends [date]" visibly in the hero unit. Right now the urgency is in the subject and completely absent in the body — a jarring disconnect.
- 3. **Collapse the bottom modules.** App download, Design Desk, and newsletter join should be stripped or moved to the footer. One email, one job.
- 4. **Anchor the category grid to the offer.** Add a copy line above the grid ("Here's what ships free today") so the grid reads as offer proof, not a generic browse prompt.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free shipping on everything over $59 — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your cart ships free this weekend ($59+)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop furniture, kitchen, and more — no shipping cost over $59`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Ends Sunday: free shipping on orders $59 and up`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Viewport blocks user zoom — WCAG 1.4.4 violation**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom on iOS Safari. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[MEDIUM] Universal selector overrides text-size-adjust fix**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
The `body` rule below it sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%`, but the universal selector (`*`) has already overridden it on all child elements. This can cause text to render at unexpected sizes on mobile. Scope the none-value rule to the elements that actually need it rather than `*`.

**[LOW] Conflicting `img` max-width media queries**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
At viewport widths 640–767 px, both queries match; source order makes `max-width: 600px` (the 768px rule) override the 640px rule at ≥768px, but at 640–767px the `640px` rule wins. Likely unintentional — the two breakpoints should be deduplicated or the ranges made exclusive.

**[LOW] Dark mode not supported**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Only light scheme declared. Images with white backgrounds will render as white blocks in dark-mode Apple Mail. If dark-mode images exist, `color-scheme` should include `dark` and `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` overrides should be added.
