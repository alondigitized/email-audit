# NEW MARKDOWNS ADDED: Up to 60% off clearance
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-20**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's clearance email, and it's the most direct, offer-forward send I've seen from this brand in the recent run — a clean beach-sunset hero with "up to 60% off clearance" front and center, no ambiguity about what this email is for. Compare that to 2026-08-19's "TheDesignDesk" makeover send (7.00/10) or the kitchen-gadget crawl (5.00/10) — this one skips the editorial framing and just says "here's the sale," which is honestly refreshing after a week of soft lifestyle pitches.
- The problem is everything after the hero. The email immediately pivots into a category grid (furniture, outdoor, kitchen, tabletop, lighting, decor, rugs, bedding) that IS the clearance shop, but then keeps going for a full scroll into unrelated full-price merchandising — Clean Kitchen line, JURA espresso machine, "the new fall table," a Crate Card ad, and TheDesignDesk pitch again. The clearance offer that earned the open gets buried under five more campaigns.
- It's a solid subject/hero combo dragging a bloated, unfocused body. Score it a middling send: strong top, wandering bottom.

## What's working

- Hero is unambiguous: bold "new markdowns added! up to 60% off clearance*" over a clean coastal image, single "Shop Now" CTA.
- The category grid right below the hero (furniture, outdoor, kitchen, tabletop & bar, lighting, decor & pillows, rugs, bedding & bath) gives shoppers a fast way into the actual sale by department.
- "Shop All Clearance" button caps the clearance section cleanly before the email moves on.
- No visible render bugs — images loaded, text is legible, layout is consistent with prior sends from this brand.

## What's weak

- The clearance offer is only the first ~20% of the email; it then bolts on Clean Kitchen, JURA espresso, "the new fall table," a product grid, Crate Card ad, app download, and TheDesignDesk — none of which are clearance and all of which dilute the subject line's promise.
- No specific dollar or percentage callouts on the category tiles themselves — "up to 60%" is asserted once in the hero and never reinforced with a real markdown price anywhere in the clearance section.
- The asterisk on "up to 60% off clearance*" signals fine print exists but it's not visible near the offer — a minor trust ding.
- By the time a reader hits JURA espresso machines and fall tablescapes, the clearance framing from the subject line is long forgotten — this reads like two or three emails stapled together.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after "Shop All Clearance" or immediately follow it with actual clearance product tiles (with struck-through prices) instead of pivoting to full-price Clean Kitchen/JURA/fall-table content — a clearance email should sell clearance, not use the subject line as a bait-and-switch into new arrivals.
- 2. Add real markdown pricing (e.g., "was $899, now $399") to at least the top category tiles or a "starting at" price — right now there's no proof of the discount anywhere below the hero.
- 3. Move the asterisk disclosure inline near the hero offer, even just "*select styles," so the discount claim doesn't feel unbacked.
- 4. If cross-sell content (JURA, fall table, Crate Card) is a must-keep for revenue reasons, move it to a separate send rather than diluting a clearance-branded email.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `New styles just hit clearance — shop by room before they're gone.`
