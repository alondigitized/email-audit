# 10% off your next purchase—even on furniture.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- This is a customer retention/email-preference email — the hero is built around a "get on the list → 10% off" incentive that typically goes to cold website visitors or lapsed non-subscribers. Sending it to an active subscriber creates instant read confusion: the recipient IS already on the list, which means the primary CTA ("get on the list & get 10% off") is a dead end for the exact person who received it. The subject's "even on furniture" hook is the strongest copywriting in the send — furniture exclusions are a known frustration in the home goods space, and flagging the exception earns trust before the email is even opened. But the promise in the subject doesn't match what the hero delivers.
- The Father's Day module does real work. June 6 with a firm June 13 ship deadline is genuinely useful information, and it's the most actionable section in the email by a distance. Had the 10% off hero been cleanly segmented or reframed for an existing subscriber, this would be a solid mid-week send.
- Net verdict: a strong subject line glued to the wrong audience segment, partially rescued by a timely Father's Day push and a recognizable brand. The offer is real; the activation is broken.

## What's working

- "Even on furniture" in the subject is a trust-building, category-specific exception that earns the open — furniture exclusions are so common that calling it out explicitly signals a genuinely better deal.
- Father's Day deadline (order by 6/13) is both timely and specific — June 6 + 7-day ship window is the kind of calendar-useful information that converts browsers to buyers.
- Product recommendation grid ("we love these for you") creates genuine visual browse-ability with real products and prices visible.
- Crate & Barrel identity is airtight — logo, typography, and lifestyle photography are immediately on-brand with no render issues.

## What's weak

- "Get on the list & get 10% off" is being served to an existing subscriber — the primary offer is inapplicable to its own audience, which creates distrust rather than action.
- The top clearance banner ("up to 40% off the summer clearance event!") is the first visible element and almost certainly the inbox preheader — it competes with and undercuts the 10%-off-on-furniture subject hook before the reader is past the fold.
- Five distinct content modules (clearance bar, list sign-up, Father's Day, product recs, app promo + TheDesignDesk) fight for attention; no single offer dominates the scroll.
- This is the third C&B email today (washable rugs, outdoor 30% off, now this) — cadence fatigue is real for a recipient who hasn't self-selected into high-frequency sends.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Re-segment or reframe the hero for existing subscribers.** The 10% offer is compelling; the "join the list" framing is broken for this audience. Reframe to "Your exclusive member discount: 10% off, furniture included" — same offer, correct premise.
- 2. **Kill or suppress the clearance banner from the top position.** It's cannibalizing its own email's subject line by surfacing a competing offer as the inbox preview text. Move below the Father's Day module or cut it entirely from this send.
- 3. **Promote the Father's Day module to hero position.** Order-by-6/13 with a countdown-style callout is the most time-sensitive element and deserves first-fold real estate.
- 4. **Collapse the email to two sections max.** List sign-up OR Father's Day OR product recs — pick one story per send given the current send cadence.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your member discount is live—10% off, furniture included`
- **Subject Alt B:** `No furniture exclusions. Your 10% off is ready.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use it on the dining table you've been eyeing. No strings attached.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Father's Day ships by 6/13—grab 10% off while you're here.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
This prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile, violating WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Should be removed or changed to `maximum-scale=5`.

**Issue — aggressive global text-size-adjust override:**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applied via the universal selector in the MI base styles. This suppresses OS-level font scaling for users with visual accessibility needs. Standard practice is `100%` on `body`, not `none` on `*`.

**Issue — duplicate `format-detection` meta tags:**
The combined tag and three individual tags create redundancy and could cause parsing inconsistency in older WebKit clients:
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<!-- ...then later: -->
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
The duplicates (date, address, telephone) should be removed; the combined tag is sufficient.

**Cannot assess** — `alt` text coverage on images requires full source.
