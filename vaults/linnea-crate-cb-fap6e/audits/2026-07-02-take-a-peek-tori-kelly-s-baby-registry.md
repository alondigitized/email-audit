# Take a peek: Tori Kelly’s baby registry →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- This email is a hard editorial pivot — smack in the middle of Crate & Kids' most aggressive 4th of July sale sequence, they drop a celebrity baby registry reveal featuring Tori Kelly. No discounts, no urgency, no price anchors. Just aspirational nursery lifestyle photography and a "here's what a celebrity picked" framing. The creative is genuinely beautiful: warm cream tones, lifestyle photography that matches the brand's premium register, and a clean multi-section product grid that doesn't feel desperate.
- The strategic tension here is real. The July 1st "Yes, furniture is ON SALE now!" send was an 8/10 heavy-discount email hammering the warehouse sale. Sending an editorial celebrity piece the very next day creates cognitive whiplash — subscribers who opened expecting another deal will land in content-marketing territory with no clear path to savings. The brand is essentially breaking its own sale momentum to insert earned media.
- The judgment call: this email works better as a standalone editorial than as day-11 of a sale sequence. A subscriber who doesn't know or care about Tori Kelly gets nothing from it. One who does gets a genuinely fun browse moment, but zero incentive to buy today.

## What's working

- **Hero photography is excellent** — soft, warm nursery aesthetic matches Crate & Kids' premium positioning perfectly.
- **Celebrity registry hook is differentiated** — a sharp visual and conceptual break from the discount-catalog format that's dominated recent sends.
- **Clean layout with no visible render bugs** — the product grid sections are well-spaced, text doesn't overlap, images load.
- **Subject line is specific and legible** — naming Tori Kelly directly creates real curiosity, and the "take a peek" framing is inviting without feeling pushy.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer or pricing visible anywhere** — in the middle of a warehouse sale, sending a no-discount editorial email leaves money on the table; even a "registry picks, now on sale" hook would bridge the two worlds.
- **No urgency signal** — nothing time-bound, no inventory language, no event tie-in; easy to close and forget.
- **Celebrity relevance is narrow** — Tori Kelly has a dedicated fanbase, but she's not a universal name; subscribers unfamiliar with her have no emotional hook.
- **Multiple product sections dilute focus** — the scroll depth required to see all categories works against mobile click rates; no single hero product anchors the CTA.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Tie the registry to the active sale** — the single biggest lift would be adding a "her picks are in the warehouse sale" sub-headline in the hero. You get the editorial cachet AND you convert the browse into a transactional moment.
- 2. **Add one anchor price to the hero section** — even "from $XX" below the hero copy gives subscribers a reason to click beyond curiosity.
- 3. **Compress the product grid to one featured category** — pick the highest-converting registry category (likely cribs or nursery seating) and let the rest live on the landing page.
- 4. **Add a time hook** — "her registry, through July 4th" would create urgency without undermining the editorial tone.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `The exact pieces she chose for her nursery — with 4th of July pricing on select styles.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `From her crib pick to the rocker everyone's buying — now through July 4th.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users from pinching to zoom on mobile. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Issue 2 — Conflicting desktop `img` max-width rules**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
The 640px block is declared after the 768px block. At viewport widths ≥768px, both rules match and specificity is equal, so `max-width: 640px` wins (last declaration). The 600px rule is dead code. One of these should be removed.

**Issue 3 — Aggressive `*` selector text-size override**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```
Applying `none` globally (rather than `100%`) suppresses iOS font scaling entirely. Declared twice: once in the MI block and once in the client block. Use `100%` on `body` only.

**Issue 4 — Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
The `format-detection` attribute appears four times: once as a combined value and three more times individually (`telephone`, `date`, `address`). Redundant — keep the single combined declaration.

**Issue 5 — Dark mode not supported**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Forces light-only rendering. No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block is present. Not a defect per se, but noted as a deliberate exclusion for ~50% of Apple Mail users.
