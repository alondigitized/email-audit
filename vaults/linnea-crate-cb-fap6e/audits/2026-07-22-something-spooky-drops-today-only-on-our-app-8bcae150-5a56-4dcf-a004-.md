# Something spooky drops TODAY, only on our app! 🎃
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- This is a split-personality email with two unrelated promotional hooks crammed into one send: a Halloween app-exclusive "first drop" teaser (the hero) and a live "up to 50% off Summer Sale" offer. Neither benefits from sharing space with the other. The Halloween reveal is deliberately vague — "something spooky" — which kills specificity in exchange for curiosity, and then asks you to download an app as the primary action rather than shop. That's a high-friction ask that most recipients will bounce off.
- The Summer Sale 50%-off section buried below the hero is actually the stronger promotional hook, but it's treated as a secondary module rather than the lead. The product grid ("we love these for you") suggests some personalization logic is in play, but the items shown are generic living-room furniture that doesn't connect to either the Halloween or Summer Sale narrative.
- It's July 22. Halloween is 100 days away. The timing creates cognitive dissonance — it reads like a calendar miscommunication rather than an exciting early reveal, and it competes with the brand's own active Summer Sale. One email, two conflicting urgencies: this should have been two separate sends.

## What's working

- **"TODAY" urgency in the subject line** is the single strongest signal — it earns opens from subscribers who scan for time-sensitive promos.
- **App-exclusive framing** creates genuine scarcity and FOMO; being "first" to shop is a real differentiator.
- **50% Summer Sale offer is concrete and credible** — the discount depth is visible and the CTA ("Shop Now") is clean.
- **No render bugs** — the email is visually clean, images loaded, layout holds.

## What's weak

- **Halloween in late July is deeply off-season** — this lands as a curiosity oddity, not as excitement. Compare to the back-to-school flash sale from earlier today (scored 8/10): that email had seasonal alignment; this one doesn't.
- **"Download the App" is the primary CTA** — asking a subscriber to leave their inbox, go to an app store, install an app, and authenticate is the highest-friction ask possible. Most won't do it.
- **Two competing offers dilute both** — the Halloween drop and the Summer Sale fight each other for attention. Neither gets full visual real estate.
- **"Something spooky" deliberately withholds the product** — curiosity-gap hooks work well in subjects, but when the body *also* withholds (requiring an app download to see the item), the payoff never arrives in-email. The loop doesn't close.
- **No product shown for the Halloween drop** — even a silhouetted or partially obscured item would anchor the teaser without fully revealing it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Separate this into two emails.** The Summer Sale is a live promotional event with 50% off — it deserves its own send. The Halloween app drop is a different campaign. Running them together dilutes both.
- 2. **Show *something* from the Halloween drop** — a silhouetted candlestick, a ghost throw pillow, *anything* that makes the mystery feel concrete rather than vaporware. The current hero image is atmosphere with no product signal.
- 3. **Add a "Remind me" or "Early access list" CTA as an alternative to the app install** — not everyone will install the app, but they might drop their email to get pinged when it launches in-browser.
- 4. **Lead with the Summer Sale if keeping both** — 50% off is the most purchase-ready hook; put it in the hero and demote Halloween to a secondary teaser module.
- 5. **Remove the Design Desk section** — this is the third Design Desk appearance in the brand history; it's turning into wallpaper.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Halloween's coming early — app-only first look inside`
- **Subject Alt B:** `👻 We're dropping Halloween early. Today, app only.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: Summer Sale up to 50% off, no app needed.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `First to see it = first to shop it. Open to claim your spot.`

`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom. Fails WCAG 2.1 Success Criterion 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

Applied via the universal selector — overrides browser accessibility settings for all elements, not just the targeted reset on `body`. Scope this to `body` only.
