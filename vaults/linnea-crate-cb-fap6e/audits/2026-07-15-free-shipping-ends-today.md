# FREE SHIPPING ends today!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- This is the Crate & Kids version of the same free-shipping deadline sprint that Crate & Barrel sent *earlier the same day* — which itself was the escalation of July 14's "FREE SHIPPING ends tomorrow!" send. Three deadline emails in under 36 hours across two sub-brands, same mechanic, same cadence. For any subscriber who gets both brands, this lands as noise by the third hit, not urgency.
- The email itself is competent. The hero is clean: the plush toy photography is on-brand for Crate & Kids, "ends today! free shipping" is readable above the fold, and the fall arrivals teaser ("500+ new fall arrivals, starting at $29!") adds genuine catalog depth that the sibling Crate & Barrel send lacked. The "we love these for you" personalized grid is a genuine asset.
- The weakness is the offer itself: the subject drops the minimum threshold ("FREE SHIPPING ends today!" vs. the Crate & Barrel version's explicit "$59+"), making the offer feel simultaneously less specific and potentially more appealing — but also less honest for customers who'll discover a threshold at checkout.

## What's working

- Hero product shot (soft plush in blue/gray tones) is warmly on-brand and appropriate for the kids vertical — better demo product than the Crate & Barrel version.
- "500+ new fall arrivals, starting at $29!" gives a price anchor and inventory signal; the specific number builds credibility.
- "we love these for you" personalization module adds stickiness and a reason to scroll past the hero.
- Layout is clean and renders without obvious bugs — no broken images or text overlaps visible.
- Email length is earned — each module adds a distinct reason to engage (urgency → new arrivals → personalized picks → app → design services).

## What's weak

- "FREE SHIPPING ends today!" subject omits any minimum-order threshold — creates inbox expectation mismatch if a threshold exists at checkout.
- Third shipping-deadline email in 36 hours (across both brands); cadence fatigue is real for dual subscribers.
- "Shop Now" CTAs are generic across all modules — nothing specific pulls you to a single product or category.
- The fall arrivals module is a strong hook but below the fold on mobile — it deserves higher placement.
- No loyalty or member pricing callout anywhere visible, missing an obvious engagement hook for CB2/Crate loyalists.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lift the fall arrivals module above the deadline hero** — or merge them: "500+ new arrivals ship free today" is a stronger combined hook than either alone.
- 2. **State the threshold in the subject** — "FREE SHIPPING ends today (no min!)" or "FREE SHIPPING on $59+ — ends tonight" eliminates checkout surprise and sets accurate expectation.
- 3. **Make one CTA specific** — "Shop fall storage, from $29" beats a fourth "Shop Now."
- 4. **Suppress or delay for subscribers who already received the Crate & Barrel ENDS TODAY send** — or reframe the message to emphasize the kids-specific catalog, not the repeated shipping mechanic.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Free shipping ends tonight — 500+ new kids arrivals`

- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `500+ new arrivals just landed — bedding, storage, and more from $29`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Wildcard `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applying to `*` is overly broad and suppresses OS-level text scaling for users who need it. Should be scoped to `body` only.

**Conflicting `img` max-width media queries — dead code**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }  /* declared first */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }  /* declared second, wins on ≥768px */
```
At viewport widths ≥768px both queries match; the later `640px` rule wins in cascade, making the `768px` rule dead code. If `max-width: 600px` on large screens was intentional, reverse the declaration order or use a more specific selector.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<!-- then separately: -->
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
Three redundant declarations after the combined one. Not harmful, but noisy and indicates template drift.

**No dark mode CSS declared**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Dark mode is supported by Apple Mail, Gmail app, and Outlook. Opting out entirely means forced light mode with no dark-mode image or color alternatives. Consider at minimum adding a `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block.
