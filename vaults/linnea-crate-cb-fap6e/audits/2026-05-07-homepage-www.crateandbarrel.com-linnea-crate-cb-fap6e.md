# Homepage snapshot · www.crateandbarrel.com · 2026-05-07
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-07**
## Executive summary

- Okay, Crate & Barrel is doing the spring outdoor push hard — earthy rattan, warm tones, 30% off patio furniture. The aesthetic is genuinely my thing, so it doesn't feel wrong, but I came here thinking registry and tabletop, and an email-capture modal is the first thing I see. The brand feels elevated and on-season, but this homepage is selling me outdoor furniture when I'm mentally still in "what dinnerware goes with our walnut dining table" mode.

## What's working

- **"Wedding Registry" utility link** — it's small but it's there, and for me right now it's the most relevant link on the page; one tap to my actual use case
- **"Earn 10% Back in Rewards Dollars" banner** — I'm full-price tolerant but a 10% back program makes me feel smart about spending full price; this is a good hook for my mindset
- **Outdoor hero aesthetic** — the warm walnut-and-rattan vibe mirrors exactly what I'm trying to do with my space; even if I'm not buying patio furniture today, this makes me trust the brand's taste
- **"the spring refresh event: up to 20% off" sub-banner** — feels current and not manufactured-urgency; I'd click to see what's included
- **"built-to-last bestsellers" section** — the language "built-to-last" speaks directly to my full-price, quality-over-quantity mindset

## What's weak

- **The immediate email-capture modal** — this is the first thing I interact with on the page; before I've even seen what's on sale, I'm being asked for my email AND phone number. The asterisk on "10% off full-price items\*" leading to SMS fine print feels like bait-and-switch; I tapped "Maybe Later" in my head immediately
- **No tabletop/dinnerware content above the fold** — "Tabletop & Bar" is in the nav, but nothing in the hero or visible modules speaks to registry-relevant categories. The homepage is fully committed to outdoor furniture, which is fine for some visitors but misses my intent
- **No social proof above the fold** — no review counts, no "bestseller" badge on actual products, no "over 10,000 registered couples chose this" registry signal; for a full-price purchase, I want reassurance
- **"Shop Sofa Essentials" / "Shop In-Stock Sofas" CTAs in bestsellers** — I'm not shopping sofas today and this rail doesn't pull me in

## Recommendations

- **Surface a "Registry Picks" or "Most-Registered Items" module above the fold** — even a secondary strip or tab below the hero saying "Top Registry Picks This Season" would catch me; I'm here with registry intent and nothing is speaking to it
- **Delay the email modal by one scroll or add a registry-specific prompt** — instead of a cold email grab, trigger it after I click into a category, or swap the CTA to "Save 10% on your registry" to make the value exchange feel relevant
- **Add a tabletop or kitchen lifestyle shot** to the homepage carousel or secondary editorial slot — even one image of minimal dinnerware on a walnut table would instantly tell me this page is for me
- **Show one concrete trust signal on featured products** — a star rating or "4,200 reviews" badge on the bestsellers module would go a long way toward making full-price feel justified
