# New & bestselling bunks and loft beds →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids running a category-scoped catalog for bunk and loft beds — "two kids, one room: find their sleep solution" is a genuinely sharp, need-based hook for a family sharing a bedroom, and it's followed by three named hero products (Grayson storage loft bed, Hampshire bunk bed, Finn bunk bed) each with a clean lifestyle shot and its own CTA. That's a real improvement in specificity over the "Beautiful & bestselling for every room" catalog-grid sends I've seen from this brand recently (2026-08-14, 5.00/10) — this one has an actual organizing idea instead of just being a wall of SKUs.
- Where it falls apart is length and focus: after the three bunk-bed heroes it keeps going — bedding, storage, a Jenny Lind furniture collection, a "personalize it" module, a "we love these for you" grid, a credit card pitch, and an app download — nine or ten distinct offers stacked in one email. A parent shopping specifically for a bunk-bed solution gets exactly what they came for in the first three screens, then has to scroll through unrelated cross-sell for a while. No prices are shown near the hero beds (just "Starting at $34" way down for a different category), and there's no discount, sale, or urgency anywhere — this is a pure merchandising email, not a promo.
- Solid, well-targeted opening; bloated, unfocused back half. **6/10.**

## What's working

- Subject and hero headline directly name the shopper's problem ("two kids, one room") rather than generic bestseller language.
- Three named, specific bunk/loft bed products each get their own image, name, and "Shop now" CTA — easy to scan and compare.
- "Exclusive!" tags on the Hampshire and Finn beds signal retailer-exclusive assortment, a real differentiator.
- Clean, consistent product photography with kids' bedroom styling that matches the category.
- No visible render bugs — images, text, and buttons all display correctly.

## What's weak

- The email keeps going well past the bunk-bed theme (bedding, storage bins, Jenny Lind collection, credit card, app) — the "two kids, one room" premise doesn't hold for the last two-thirds of the send.
- No price is shown on any of the three hero bunk/loft beds, which is the single most important buying signal for furniture this size.
- No offer or urgency at all — pure catalog browsing, which will underperform against the brand's sale sends (e.g. 2026-08-14's "Up to 60% off," 6.00/10).
- The "personalize it" and credit-card modules feel bolted on and dilute an otherwise well-themed email.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after the third bunk-bed hero (Finn bunk bed) and the "more faves for shared spaces" module — split bedding/storage/Jenny Lind/credit card/app into a separate send. This alone would make it feel intentional instead of catalog-dumped.
- 2. Add starting prices directly under each of the three hero bed names — "no price shown" is the biggest miss for big-ticket furniture.
- 3. If there's any current promo (financing, % off, free shipping on bunk beds), surface it in the hero band — right now this reads stale next to the brand's recent 60%-off sale send.
- 4. Tighten the subject to match the sharper hero copy rather than a flat product-listing subject.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Two kids, one room: 3 bunk beds that actually work`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Exclusive bunk & loft beds for shared kids' rooms`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Space-saving sleep solutions, starting under $500`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Grayson, Hampshire & Finn — new this week, only at Crate & Kids`
