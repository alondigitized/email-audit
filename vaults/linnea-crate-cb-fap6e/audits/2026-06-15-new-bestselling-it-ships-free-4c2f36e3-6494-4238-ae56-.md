# New, bestselling & it ships free…
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- This is a multi-block editorial catalog email from Crate & Kids that leans on three subject-line hooks — newness, bestseller proof, free shipping — without fully delivering any of them in the body. The hero image is a clean, well-styled bedroom scene with soft neutrals that look seasonally appropriate, but the email unfolds as a long product grid scroll rather than a single focused proposition. The "ships free" promise buried in the subject never gets reinforced visually as a primary message inside the email, which wastes the one concrete hook the brand led with.
- The bigger problem is cadence. This is the fourth email across Crate & Barrel / Crate & Kids in roughly 24 hours, and yesterday's Crate & Kids send ("Need it now? Shop quick-ship furniture") already played the shipping-as-benefit card. Recycling a logistics hook the very next day dilutes both sends.
- The email is competently produced but lacks editorial conviction. It reads like a catch-all product dump dressed up in nice photography rather than a send with a clear reason for existing today.

## What's working

- Hero photography is clean and high quality — a calm, well-lit bedroom scene with a coherent neutral palette that instantly signals the brand.
- Subject line is short (34 chars) and mobile-safe with no spam triggers.
- Multi-section product grid is visually organized with consistent image sizing and spacing — no obvious layout breaks.
- "Bestselling" as a social-proof signal in the subject line is the right instinct, echoing the June 14 sofa send that leaned on the same hook effectively.

## What's weak

- "Ships free" is the most concrete offer in the subject but gets no visible headline reinforcement or hero call-out inside the email body — the value proposition evaporates on open.
- CTA buttons are small and hard to distinguish at mobile scale; copy is unreadable in the render, likely defaulting to generic "Shop Now" language.
- No price anchoring anywhere visible — no sale percentage, no clearance, no tiered discount — makes this a pure-editorial send with a misleading subject that implies a deal.
- Cadence red flag: yesterday's Crate & Kids send led with fast/free shipping as the hook; this send repeats it. A subscriber who opened both will notice the recycled angle.
- The email is very long with many product sections and no clear editorial thesis tying them together — "new + bestselling + free shipping" is three different strategies at once.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make free shipping the hero inside the email.** The subject line earns the open on a logistics promise — a bold banner or hero headline ("Free shipping. No minimum.") should greet the reader immediately to validate the click. Right now the subject and body are telling different stories.
- 2. **Pick one editorial angle and commit.** Either "New arrivals" or "Our bestsellers" — not both. The June 14 sofa send worked because it had a clear thesis (social proof). This send tries to be three things and lands as none.
- 3. **Enlarge and sharpen CTAs.** Buttons need to be legible at mobile render scale with specific copy (e.g., "Shop Bestselling Beds" not "Shop Now").
- 4. **Trim the product grid.** A shorter email with a hero product or tight curation of 4–6 items performs better than a catalog scroll when there's no active sale.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

**a. Conflicting `img` `max-width` media queries**
Two rules are both active on screens ≥ 768px but disagree:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
The 768px rule is declared last, so on viewports ≥ 768px the cap regresses from 640px to 600px — images shrink as the viewport *widens*. Intended max should be 640px at all desktop widths; the 768px block should be removed or consolidated.

**b. `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks pinch-to-zoom on iOS/Android. This is an accessibility failure (WCAG 1.4.4 — Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**c. `*` selector with `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none`**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applied globally via the wildcard selector. Combined with the zoom lock above, users with visual impairments cannot scale text. Should be scoped to `body` only with value `100%`, not `none`.

**d. Dark mode not declared**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Opts out of dark mode entirely. Apple Mail and Outlook will still attempt dark mode auto-inversion on unprotected elements. If dark mode is intentionally unsupported, background colors on all containers must be explicitly set to prevent inversion artifacts.

**e. Duplicate CSS class definitions**
`ReadMsgBody`, `ExternalClass`, and associated rules are defined in two separate `<style>` blocks. The second definitions override the first silently. No functional breakage observed from what's visible, but increases parse weight and creates maintenance risk.
