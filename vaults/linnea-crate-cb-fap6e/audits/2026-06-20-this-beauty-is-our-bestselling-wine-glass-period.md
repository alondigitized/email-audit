# This beauty is our bestselling wine glass. Period.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Barrel earns a quiet editorial moment here — breaking from the 4th of July Warehouse Sale drumbeat that's dominated since June 17 to spotlight a single hero product. The subject line is confident and refreshingly un-promo: "This beauty is our bestselling wine glass. Period." lands with conviction, and the hero render delivers on that promise with clean photography and visible star ratings as lead social proof. It's a trust-building send, not a discount push.
- The problem is that the email doesn't commit to the premise. After the strong wine glass hero, it sprawls — tableware sets, flatware, and what looks like living-room furniture all follow below the fold. The hero editorial concept gets diluted into a catalog. A single-product confidence play and a multi-section browse email are two different strategies; this tries to be both and fully executes neither.
- Judgment: smart pivot away from sale fatigue, undercut by structural discipline. A good idea that needed an editor.

## What's working

- **Subject line earns its confidence.** "This beauty is our bestselling wine glass. Period." is concrete, short, and uses a declarative full stop as punctuation — rare and memorable in an inbox full of %-off subject lines.
- **Star ratings are front-and-center in the hero.** Social proof as the lead proof point for a bestseller claim is exactly right; the visual choice validates the copy.
- **Clean hero photography.** The wine glass render is elegant — white space, good lighting, premium feel consistent with the brand and the product.
- **Cadence break creates relevance.** After three-plus consecutive 4th of July Warehouse Sale sends, this non-sale email earns opens on contrast alone.

## What's weak

- **The email doesn't commit to its own premise.** It leads with a single-hero product concept but then continues through multiple unrelated product categories — tableware, flatware, and even furniture at the bottom. The editorial throughline evaporates.
- **No visible offer or pricing in the hero.** This is framed as a product hero, but there's no price, no sale callout, no urgency — nothing to convert. Opens become browses, not buys.
- **CTA copy is too small to read at mobile scale.** Whatever the button says below the hero doesn't register visually — it's there, but not commanding.
- **Cross-category lower sections reduce focus.** The furniture section at the bottom, in particular, feels like it belongs in a different email — the tonal shift from wine glass editorial to living-room browse is abrupt and unmotivated.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Strip everything below the wine glass sections.** The multi-category append is killing the editorial punch. One product, one category, one story. If the brand wants a catalog send on the same day, that should be a separate send.
- 2. **Add a concrete anchor price or social proof stat.** "Our bestselling wine glass" is stronger with a number: "#1 rated with 4,000+ reviews" or "starting at $X" — give the reader a reason to click beyond curiosity.
- 3. **Make the CTA button read at a glance.** "Shop the glass" or "Get the bestseller" — a verb + noun that mirrors the subject line's confidence.
- 4. **Use the preheader to deliver the proof point the subject teases.** The subject stakes the claim; the preheader should cash it: the review count, the price, or the specific glass name.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Rated 4-stars by thousands. Our bestselling wine glass.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Meet the wine glass we can't keep in stock.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `4,200+ five-star reviews. See why it never leaves our bestseller list.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The Camille wine glass. Elegant, lead-free, and perpetually sold out.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CONFIRMED] Unresolved template placeholder in `<head>`**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
This is a build-time authoring comment that shipped in production. The misspelling ("IMPUT" vs "INPUT") confirms this is inherited boilerplate, not intentional. No functional breakage, but indicates the template was not fully cleaned before send.

**[CONFIRMED] Conflicting `img` max-width at desktop breakpoints**
Two `@media` rules target overlapping viewport widths:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
At viewports ≥ 768px, both rules fire. The `640px`-breakpoint rule appears later in the stylesheet and wins the cascade, capping images at **640px**, not 600px. If the template container is 600px wide, images may overflow or render at unexpected sizes.

**[CONFIRMED] User zoom disabled on iOS**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom in Safari/iOS. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) requires content to be resizable up to 200% without loss of functionality.

**[CONFIRMED] Global text-size-adjust disabled**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applied to all elements via the universal selector. This suppresses browser-level font scaling for users who have increased system text size. The intent (prevent Outlook auto-scaling) is better achieved by scoping to `body` or specific table cells.

**[CONFIRMED] Duplicate `<meta>` declarations**
`format-detection` appears four times — once as a combined value and three times individually:
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
`charset` is also declared twice (`http-equiv` + direct `charset` attribute). Neither causes functional breakage but indicates duplicate template merging.

**[CONFIRMED] `.showmobile` hardcoded to 414px**
```css
.showmobile { width: 414px !important; }
```
This fires at the `max-width: 460px` breakpoint but targets iPhone 6/7/8 logical width specifically. On devices with narrower viewports (e.g., 360px Android), this will cause horizontal overflow.

**Light-mode only declared**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Dark mode is explicitly excluded. Some clients (Apple Mail, Gmail Android) may still force dark-mode inversion on images and backgrounds, which can produce unintended results if the email was not tested against forced dark mode.
