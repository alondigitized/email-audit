# Up to 35% off: the best time to shop Ninja, Fellow & more!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's electrics editorial send — a deliberate break from the 4th of July Warehouse Sale drumbeat that's dominated the inbox for a week, pivoting to a kitchen brands spotlight anchored on Fellow and Ninja. The brand-naming in the subject is the smartest move here: it functions as a purchase-intent filter, pulling in people who already care about those brands and sidelining everyone else. The visual structure works — hero appliance image leads, then a product grid cascades below — and the render is clean.
- The problem is the discount signal is comparatively weak. The warehouse sale sends were screaming 50–60% off; this one caps at 35%, and for premium electrics brands like Fellow, even that may be fiction (Fellow rarely discounts). A subscriber who opened the June 21 "Up to 60% off" Warehouse Sale send — which scored a 9 — will feel the discount regression immediately. The email also runs long with repetitive section breaks that dilute the single-brand urgency established in the subject.
- Verdict: a solid electrics editorial with a credible brand hook, undermined by a discount figure that can't compete with the sale emails still fresh in the recipient's memory.

## What's working

- **Brand-name drop in subject** — calling out Ninja and Fellow specifically is rare and correct; it acts as a purchase-intent trigger for kitchen gear enthusiasts.
- **Clean render** — multi-section layout holds together well; no broken images or layout drift visible.
- **Visual hierarchy in the hero** — the appliance imagery leads cleanly before the product grid, which gives the eye a logical path.
- **Category focus is coherent** — the email stays in kitchen electrics throughout rather than scattering into furniture or linens mid-scroll.
- **Sender name discipline** — "Crate & Barrel SALE" continues to signal deal-mode to inbox scanners.

## What's weak

- **35% off feels like a demotion** — immediately after the 4th of July Warehouse Sale sends cresting at 60% off, this cap reads as a step backward for the same brand.
- **Fellow at a discount is a credibility stretch** — Fellow is a premium DTC brand with tight MAP policies; saying "best time to shop Fellow" risks reading as inflated if the actual Fellow discount is shallow.
- **No deadline visible** — the sale period for the warehouse event was time-anchored; this send has no visible end date, which strips urgency.
- **Long scroll, thin density** — the email's lower half appears to dissolve into smaller product tiles that don't carry the hero's energy; lots of visual real estate spent on secondary products.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline.** A line like "Sale ends Sunday" would rescue the urgency entirely missing from this send. This is the single highest-impact change.
- 2. **Lead with the Fellow or Ninja product specifically** — a hero shot of a Fellow Stagg EKG or Ninja Creami with the discounted price shown creates a product-level pull that a generic "up to 35% off" does not.
- 3. **Acknowledge the sale context explicitly.** A line like "These brands are in our 4th of July Sale — rarely on promotion" reframes the 35% as special rather than weak.
- 4. **Tighten the subject** — it's over 50 characters and clips on mobile.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Fellow, Ninja, Vitamix — up to 35% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Rare deals on the kitchen brands you want`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fellow EKG, Ninja Creami, and more — sale ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `These brands don't go on sale often. This weekend they do.`

## Technical Audit — Crate & Barrel | "Up to 35% off: Ninja, Fellow & more"

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has four hard-broken webfont requests (403) and missing authentication header capture via the relay, which together represent the primary infrastructure failures. Accessibility posture is poor with 50+ images lacking alt text, and two compliance headers are absent from the relayed message.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Broken webfont requests (4 × HTTP 403)**
All four custom font files return 403 Forbidden, meaning Crate & Barrel's custom typeface does not load in any client that respects `@font-face`:
- `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/1e07d600-3787-4517-bd3d-b140f9e54ec2.woff2`
- `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/7ec25fd5-2752-4d84-b307-af3faabc21bb.woff2`
- `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/aaa41944-25b3-4265-80b2-5564efe9b2d7.woff2`
- `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/fd5f8f50-d539-4a7d-b893-99501d746ef2.woff2`

The font `@font-face` import comment placeholder (`<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->`) was left unfilled in the production HTML — the typo "IMPUT" confirms this is a raw template artifact, not intentional. Fonts fall back to system stack silently.

**Tracking links (22 skipped)**
22 click-redirect URLs were not probed. No broken destination links can be confirmed or ruled out from this audit. UTM parameter coverage on those destinations is also unverified.

**Third-party tracking pixels**
Three tracking domains fire on open:
- `mi.crateandbarrel.com` (Movable Ink open pixel — 6 instances)
- `sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif` (LiveRamp identity resolution — 5 instances with `?n=1` through `?n=5`)
- `dv.crateandbarrel.com` (device/view pixel)

These are expected for enterprise ESP stacks but represent personal-data collection points that should be disclosed in privacy policy.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Alt text: 50 images missing `alt` attributes**

Every product image and all decorative GIFs lack alt text. This breaks the email for screen readers and renders it content-free when images are blocked (default in Outlook, many corporate clients). Affected examples:
- `31ea28b4-2d64-4123-a33c-4511a20ac4c4.jpg` — product image (no alt)
- `ccceec39-1c00-4459-a214-b3aa0bfa6e5d.gif` — animated element (no alt)
- `ae314b10-e7df-42d5-8722-315e643d7c27.gif` — animated element (no alt)
- Tracking pixels (`o.gif`, `448796.gif`, `f3c0d22992a01946.png`) also lack `alt=""` — these should carry empty alt, not omit the attribute entirely.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
`telephone=no` and `address=no` appear in both a combined meta and individual metas — redundant but harmless.

**Template comment left in production**
`<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` is visible in the HTML `<head>`. This is a build/QA process failure — production HTML should not contain unfilled template comments.

**CSS media query conflict**
Two `@media` rules targeting desktop images conflict:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
The 640px breakpoint rule is declared after and overrides the 768px rule on screens 768px+ wide, capping images at 640px rather than 600px. Likely unintentional.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** — not captured by the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to surface the unsubscribe button natively; its absence increases spam classification risk on those clients.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe not supported or not relayed. Google and Yahoo both require this for bulk senders (>5k/day) as of February 2024. If volume thresholds are met, this is a deliverability compliance violation.

**`Authentication-Results` header absent** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status unknown due to relay gap. Cannot confirm sender domain `mail.crateandbarrel.com` is properly authenticated from available data.

CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link presence cannot be confirmed from the truncated HTML source, but this is expected to be present given Crate & Barrel's compliance posture.
