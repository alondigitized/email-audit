# The bestselling favorites your kitchen needs (like, now) →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel running its "top 5 bestsellers" numbered-listicle format for kitchen — electrics, cookware, cutlery, bakeware, tools & prep — each with a star rating, a hero shot, and its own "Shop Bestselling X" CTA. It's a cleaner, more disciplined structure than the multi-collection furniture sweeps (compare the 2026-08-08 "storage picks" send, 6.00/10, which stacked seven unrelated furniture lines), because every section stays inside one category with real social proof (star ratings) attached to each pick.
- Where it falls apart is the same place every recent Crate & Barrel send falls apart: it doesn't stop at the bestsellers. After the fifth numbered item it keeps going — a Godinger bakery collab, a back-to-school $5000 sweepstakes with TheDesignDesk, a "we love these for you" grid, a credit card pitch, and an app-download banner. The bestseller concept (tight, credible, comparison-shopping-friendly) gets buried under the same grab-bag tail this brand hits in nearly every send this week.
- Judgement: solid top half, bloated bottom half. This scores a notch above the brand's average multi-category sweeps because the top-of-email actually commits to one focused idea, but it doesn't reach the discipline of the 9.00 "Bestsellers for your nursery" send because it doesn't know when to stop.

## What's working

- Numbered "01–05" bestseller format gives clear scannable structure and mirrors how a shopper actually compares kitchen categories.
- Star ratings on each hero item are visible social proof, rare in this brand's other sends.
- Subject/hero payoff match — "bestselling favorites your kitchen needs" lands directly on a 5-item kitchen bestseller list, no bait-and-switch.
- Clean, on-brand product photography with consistent styling (wood tones, neutral kitchen scenes) throughout.
- Each section has its own distinct, specific CTA ("Shop Bestselling Kitchen Electrics," "Shop Bestselling Cutlery") rather than one generic button.

## What's weak

- Email doesn't end at the bestseller list — it tacks on a bakery collab, a sweepstakes, a "we love these for you" grid, a credit card pitch, and an app banner, diluting the focused premise.
- No visible price or %-off anywhere in the bestseller section — "bestselling" is a merchandising claim, not an offer, so there's no concrete incentive to click now.
- The $5000 sweepstakes banner competes visually with the bestseller CTAs for attention partway through the scroll.
- "we love these for you" section has no clear personalization logic shown — reads as generic filler after five specific numbered picks.
- Very long single-column scroll with no visible offer until the very bottom (credit card $50 mention) — most of the email is discovery, not conversion.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after section 05 (tools & prep) or after the bakery collab — the sweepstakes, "we love these," and credit card blocks should be a separate send; this alone would make it read like the 9.00-scoring nursery bestsellers email instead of another long scroll.
- 2. Add a price or "starting at $X" under at least the hero and 2-3 of the numbered items — right now there's zero pricing signal despite the offer-rubric expecting one.
- 3. Move the sweepstakes ("enter for a chance to win $5000") out of the middle of a bestseller list — it breaks the category focus and should either lead or be cut.
- 4. Add a one-line "why it's a bestseller" trust cue (e.g., "10,000+ sold" or "#1 in Cookware") next to the star ratings to strengthen the social-proof angle already in place.
- 5. Tighten the subject for mobile and drop the informal aside:
- **Subject Alt A:** `5 kitchen bestsellers, starting at $30`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Our most-loved kitchen picks →`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Top-rated electrics, cookware & cutlery, chosen by shoppers like you`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `See what's selling out in the kitchen dept.`
