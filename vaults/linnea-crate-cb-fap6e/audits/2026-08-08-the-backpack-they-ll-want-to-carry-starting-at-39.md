# The backpack they’ll want to carry, starting at $39!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids doing an actual product-focused send — a genuine "backpack size guide" that walks small/medium/large fit by age, with each size showing exactly what fits inside (lunchbox, water bottle, folders, tablet). That's a real category-specific hook, not a catalog dump, and it lands closer to the 2026-08-07 "Bestsellers for your nursery" 9.0 than the grab-bag sweeps this brand also runs.
- Where it falls apart is discipline: after three tight size sections it pivots into "make it a bundle," then a completely unrelated "500+ new fall arrivals starting at $29" banner, a nursery giveaway, an "we love these for you" cross-sell grid, a credit card pitch, and a DesignDesk plug. The backpack promise from the subject line gets buried under five more offers by the time you're two-thirds down.
- The size-guide structure itself is genuinely well-executed and the strongest creative idea in recent Crate & Kids sends — the problem is the brand can't resist tacking on unrelated inventory after a good idea, which drags this from an 8-9 down into the 6-7 range.

## What's working

- The size-guide format (small/medium/large with "what fits inside" icon lists) is a real utility hook that directly answers the parent's actual question — will this fit my kid's stuff.
- Each size tier has its own hero lifestyle shot showing a kid actually wearing/using the backpack, reinforcing scale.
- "Make it a bundle" cross-sell (bundle + backpack + back-to-school) is at least adjacent and logically placed right after the size sections.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, layout holds together end to end.

## What's weak

- The email keeps going well past the backpack story — fall arrivals, a $500 nursery giveaway, unrelated cross-sell products, and a credit card pitch all appear after the backpack content is done, diluting the one good idea.
- "500+ new fall arrivals, starting at $29" is a jarring category switch with zero visual connection to backpacks or back-to-school.
- The nursery sweepstakes banner has nothing to do with backpacks, kids old enough to carry one, or back-to-school shopping — it reads like a template block bolted on.
- No urgency or deadline anywhere in the backpack content — "starting at $39" is a price anchor, not an offer with a clock on it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after "make it a bundle" — end on the backpack/back-to-school story and drop the fall-arrivals banner, nursery giveaway, and cross-sell grid entirely. This is the single biggest lift: it turns a diluted 8-section email into a tight, single-purpose send.
- 2. Add a real time-bound offer to the backpack story itself (e.g., "20% off backpacks through Sunday") — right now the only offer is a starting price with no urgency.
- 3. Move the "shop all bundles / shop all backpacks / shop back to school" CTA row higher, right after the last size tier, so it's the natural next click instead of getting lost before the fall-arrivals detour.
- 4. If fall arrivals or the giveaway must stay in rotation, send them as a separate email — don't stack four unrelated offers onto one good idea.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Small, medium or large? A backpack that actually fits`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `See what fits inside each size before you buy.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Lunchbox, tablet, folders — matched to their grade.`
