# We’re giving away a $500 Gift Card! Learn more →
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-20**
## Executive summary

- This is a review-solicitation dressed up as a giveaway: the actual mechanic — "write a review, get entered to win a $500 gift card" — is a review-farming play, not a real prize giveaway, and it's not entirely clear from the hero alone. The hero itself is oddly styled: a two-star rating graphic sits above "want to win a $500 gift card?" like a placeholder that got left in, which reads as a render bug even if it's intentional review-UI iconography. Below the fold it reverts to the exact same long-scroll catalog pattern seen in nearly every other Crate & Kids/C&B send this week — collab teaser, sale banner, product grid, credit card cross-sell, app pitch, design service pitch, footer nav.
- The offer clarity is genuinely weak: is the prize $500, and is the sale "up to 20% off," and is there also a credit card reward program — three competing "asks" stacked in one email with no single dominant CTA. This dilutes what should be a simple, high-curiosity send.
- Judgement: functional but unfocused — the concept (reviews-for-prize) is fine as content, but execution buries it under too many competing offers and one visual element (the star rating) look glitchy at first glance.

## What's working

- The $500 gift card hook is a strong scroll-stopper and differentiates from this brand's recent run of plain catalog sends.
- Clear deadline ("Contest ends September 30!") gives credible urgency.
- Product grid shows real prices, including a visible markdown ($1350 → $1010 on the Nuna stroller), which supports the "up to 20% off" claim.
- Venus Williams collab callout is a genuine exclusive-content hook distinct from the generic listings around it.

## What's weak

- The two-star rating icon directly under the header reads as a rendering glitch on first glance — a giveaway hero shouldn't visually resemble a bad review.
- Too many competing offers in one email: gift card contest, 20% off sale, new credit card, app download, free design consult — no single visual hierarchy winner.
- The primary CTAs ("Write a Review" / "Shop Bestsellers") are muted gray-on-gray buttons with low contrast, easy to miss against the beige background.
- This is at least the 6th near-identical long-scroll structure from this brand in a week (per brand history) — cadence fatigue is real here.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split the review-contest concept into its own short, single-CTA email — kill the sale/card/app add-ons entirely for this send; a $500 prize deserves to be the whole story, not one panel of six.
- 2. Fix the star-rating graphic or replace it with a trophy/gift icon — anything that doesn't read as "2 out of 5 stars" at a glance.
- 3. Make "Write a Review" the only high-contrast button on the page (solid black/dark green) and demote or remove "Shop Bestsellers" so there's one clear action.
- 4. Add a personalization hook — "Rate items you've purchased" — since this is a review ask, referencing actual past purchases would lift both open and click rates.
- 5. Reduce structural repetition vs. recent sends — this brand has run near-identical layouts five nights running; a genuinely different template for the contest would stand out more.
