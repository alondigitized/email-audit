# New look, almost no wait
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's second email on 5/30 — landing the same day as the clearance blast ("Selling fast: up to 60% off clearance!", scored 9/10) — and it takes the opposite approach: a delivery-speed editorial hook ("New look, almost no wait") rather than a discount hammer. The gamble is real. Promising a fast home refresh is a credible emotional angle for someone staring down a summer gathering or a long weekend. The outdoor furniture hero at the top delivers on that premise visually.
- But the email immediately undermines itself. After a clean hero it sprints sideways into category navigation tiles, then a summer bedding editorial, then a "we love these for you" furniture grid, then an app download push, then TheDesignDesk. Five narrative threads in one scroll. The subject line set up a coherent story — *fast delivery on new arrivals* — and the email abandons it by the second section. A subscriber who opened expecting to browse fresh pieces with quick ship windows gets an extended catalog dump instead.
- The context from brand history makes this worse. This is roughly the sixth or seventh send from the C&B / Crate & Kids family in three days. Even a strong subject line fights against inbox fatigue at that cadence. The result is a send that had a good idea, good creative in the hero, and then buried both under a kitchen-sink layout.

## What's working

- **Hero image is genuinely strong** — warm outdoor lifestyle shot with the "a new look by next week" headline lands the brand promise cleanly and seasonally.
- **Subject line length is mobile-friendly** — 24 characters clears the 50-char threshold with room to spare; no truncation risk.
- **Loyalty hook present** — the app section surfaces loyalty rewards, which is a useful reminder for a member audience.
- **Summer bedding editorial** — "find your dream sheets" is timely and category-appropriate for the season.
- **No visible render bugs** — layout appears clean, no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **Zero price signal** — the entire email never shows a discount, a threshold offer, or a free-shipping callout; without one, "almost no wait" has to carry all the motivation alone.
- **Five competing CTAs fracture focus** — outdoor furniture, category tiles, bedding guide, personalized recs, and the app are all fighting for the click; there is no dominant primary action.
- **Hero promise abandoned immediately** — the fast-delivery/new-arrivals angle disappears after section one; the bedding guide and product recs aren't framed around ship speed at all.
- **Cadence is exhausting** — sixth-ish email from this sender family in three days; even a subscriber who likes C&B has diminishing patience for yet another send today.
- **"We love these for you" section feels generic** — the product thumbnails at this render size don't communicate any preference signal, undermining the personalization language.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one story and tell it all the way through.** The delivery-speed angle ("new look by next week") is genuinely good. Every section should support it: outdoor furniture that ships fast → "also arriving quickly" bedding → app to track your order. Cut the design desk and generic category tiles. One narrative = one click = one conversion.
- 2. **Add a ship-speed signal to the hero CTA.** "Ships in 3–5 days" or "In your home before the weekend" directly beneath the hero button would make the subject line pay off. That's the one copy change that lifts this the most.
- 3. **Drop one email today.** Two C&B sends on 5/30 is one too many — the clearance email already captured the high-urgency buyer; this one should have shipped on 5/31 when there's breathing room.
- 4. **Give the CTA buttons a specific room/use case** — "Shop Outdoor" or "Find Your Sheets" beats a generic "Shop Now" across five different sections.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `maximum-scale=1` blocks zoom:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on iOS/Android. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize text). Remove `maximum-scale=1` or replace with `maximum-scale=5`.

**Issue — Global `line-height: 100%` override:**
```css
*{... line-height: 100%}
```
Applying `line-height: 100%` universally via the `*` selector collapses multi-line text in some clients (particularly Samsung Mail, older Android). Scope this to `td, p` instead.

**Issue — Conflicting `img` `max-width` media queries:**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
Due to source order, on viewports ≥768px the 600px rule wins. On 640–767px viewports the 640px rule applies — images are wider on *smaller* screens than on larger ones. The 768px block should be ordered last or removed in favor of a single breakpoint.

**Issue — Mobile fixed width mismatch:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 460px) {
  .showmobile { width:414px !important; }
}
```
A 460px breakpoint activating a 414px fixed-width element creates a 46px gap where content can overflow on 415–460px devices. Align the fixed width to 100% or match the breakpoint.

**Minor — Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags (x4):**
One combined tag plus three individual repeats. Harmless but indicates template sloppiness.

**Minor — Duplicate `charset` declarations:**
Both `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">` and `<meta charset="utf-8">` present. Redundant; remove the HTTP-equiv form.

**Minor — `.ExternalClass` rules declared twice** across the two `<style>` blocks. Last-write wins, no functional breakage, but adds parse weight.
