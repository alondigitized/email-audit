# New deals JUST hit our 4th of July Warehouse Sale!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth "new items added / just got bigger" refresh in the 4th of July Warehouse Sale sequence from Crate & Barrel — the June 26 "NEW DEALS! The 4th of July Warehouse Sale just got bigger 🎆" (5/10) used the identical playbook, and this send copies it beat for beat with swapped copy. The "JUST hit" hook is the same freshness gambit as "just got bigger" twenty-four hours ago. For anyone who has been watching their inbox, this reads as the warehouse sale equivalent of a car dealership calling back twice in one day.
- Inside the open, the email is actually well-constructed: a clean hero with specific product imagery (tableware, upholstery), a clearly labeled "Top picks from our warehouse sale" mid-section, legible product tiles with visible prices, and a single directional CTA button. The visual hierarchy is fine — eye lands on the offer, scrolls through product choices, hits the button. No render bugs visible.
- The problem is purely one of cadence and signal fatigue. By send number four of the same campaign in roughly 48 hours, the "NEW" and "JUST" urgency language is spent. Even if the SKUs are genuinely new, the subscriber's inbox is trained to ignore these.

## What's working

- **Clean hero with specific product imagery** — tableware/ceramics and upholstered furniture pieces anchor the sale visually rather than relying on abstract sale copy alone.
- **Legible product tile grid** — items shown with visible prices; no broken images or empty slots.
- **Single directional CTA** — one button, clear placement, no competing links fighting for the click.
- **"Top picks from our warehouse sale"** section label does useful editorial work — it positions C&B as curating, not just dumping.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout top to bottom in the render.

## What's weak

- **Same subject gambit as yesterday** — "JUST hit" mirrors "just got bigger" from the June 26 send; any subscriber who opened that one has already been inoculated against this hook.
- **"JUST" in all-caps is a soft spam signal** — it triggers pattern-matching that depresses inbox trust, especially on Apple Mail/Gmail.
- **No visible deadline specificity in-body** — "4th of July" is the implicit clock, but nothing in the body copy says "sale ends July 4" or a countdown. The urgency is ambient, not concrete.
- **TheDesignDesk / app content at the bottom dilutes the email's job** — this send has one job (warehouse sale conversions); the lifestyle/content module at the bottom is off-brief.
- **No loyalty or member pricing signal** — a subscriber who is a CB2 or Crate & Barrel member has no reason visible here to believe they're getting preferential access.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rest the "new deals added" refresh cadence** — sending the same hook on back-to-back days kills the signal. This email should not have gone out. If new SKUs genuinely need promotion, roll them into a consolidated end-of-sale "last chance" send on July 3 instead.
- 2. **Add an explicit deadline line in the hero** — something like "Sale ends July 4 at midnight" converts the ambient holiday urgency into a hard CTA trigger.
- 3. **Replace "JUST" all-caps with a specific product count** — "47 new pieces just landed" beats "New deals JUST hit" on both spam scoring and specificity.
- 4. **Cut the TheDesignDesk / app footer module** — it belongs in an editorial send, not a conversion email. This is wasted scroll.
- 5. **Add a member-pricing badge** — even "Members save an extra 10%" would give the loyalty segment a reason to click over a non-member.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue A — Conflicting desktop `img max-width` rules (confirmed bug)**

Two overlapping `min-width` media queries are declared in reverse specificity order:

```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }  /* declared first */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }  /* declared second — wins */
```

On screens ≥ 768px, both rules match and the cascade resolves to the last-declared value: `640px`. Images will render 40px wider than the apparent 600px design intent on all desktop clients.

**Issue B — `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

Prevents user zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Many ESPs flag this in deliverability checks.

**Issue C — `webkit-text-size-adjust` conflict**

```css
html { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }   /* in client stylesheet */
body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; }   /* in base stylesheet */
```

The `html`-level `none` disables iOS auto-sizing entirely, overriding the `body` rule for most elements. This is intentional for some email designs but combined with `maximum-scale=1` leaves mobile readers with no text resize mechanism at all.

**Issue D — Global `line-height: 100%`**

```css
* { line-height: 100%; }
```

Applied universally, this sets computed line-height equal to font-size on every element. WCAG 1.4.12 requires line spacing of at least 1.5× font-size for body text. The override in client styles does not reset this for paragraph content.

**Issue E — No dark mode support**

```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```

Explicitly opts out of dark mode. Apple Mail and recent Gmail will still force-invert unprotected images. Without `prefers-color-scheme` media query handling, this is opt-out without mitigation, not opt-out with fallback.

**Issue F — Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**

Four declarations cover the same attributes:

```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```

Redundant; harmless functionally but indicates copy-paste template hygiene issues.
