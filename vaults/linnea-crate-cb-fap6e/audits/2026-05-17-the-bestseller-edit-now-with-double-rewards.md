# The Bestseller Edit: Now with Double Rewards!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- This is an editorial-format "bestseller curation" email — a deliberate pivot away from the blunt discount messaging that has defined the Crate & Barrel family's Memorial Day window. The hypothesis is sound: after eight straight "X% off + Double Rewards" emails, an aspirational "here's what everyone's buying" angle should feel fresher. Execution is cleaner than most in this run — professional lifestyle photography, multi-category sections (living, dining, bedroom, kitchen), and no visible render bugs.
- The problem is structural. Double Rewards without a hard % off anchor is a weaker offer hook than the brand has been running all week, and a subscriber who opened "Up to 50% off everything" on May 15 will notice the regression. The email is also long — seven or eight scroll-able sections — which diffuses the click rather than concentrating it. "The Bestseller Edit" is a good editorial label, but editorial without urgency is browsing fodder, not buying fuel.
- Bottom line: a thoughtful creative departure that lands slightly flat on offer substance. The concept is worth evolving; this version doesn't fully close the loop between "here's what's popular" and "here's why you should buy it today."

## What's working

- **Editorial framing is earned.** "The Bestseller Edit" is a genuine departure from the sale-speak cadence; it signals curation, not desperation.
- **Multi-category spread catches different intents.** Living room, dining, bedroom, and cookware in one email means a subscriber at any stage of a home project has a reason to scroll.
- **Clean render.** Lifestyle photography is polished, images load correctly, no overlapping text or layout breaks visible.
- **Double Rewards is prominent.** Loyalty multiplier is called out in the subject and (presumably) the hero, which gives loyalists a tangible hook.

## What's weak

- **No % off anchor.** Every prior Memorial Day email from this brand led with a discount number ("Up to 50% off," "Up to 40% off," "Up to 30% off"). "Double Rewards" alone is weaker and will read as a step backward.
- **Nine emails in 72 hours.** Cadence fatigue is real; this is the ninth or tenth touchpoint in a single promotional window. Open rates on this send will suffer regardless of creative quality.
- **Multi-category = no hero product.** Unlike the May 16 rug-specific or outdoor-specific sends — which had a clear category hero — this email has no single focal point a shopper can act on immediately.
- **Long scroll, diffuse CTAs.** Seven-plus sections with "Shop" buttons across multiple categories spreads click intent thin. No single CTA dominates.
- **Urgency is absent.** The subject and visible body copy don't communicate when Double Rewards ends. Memorial Day weekend implies a deadline, but the email doesn't state it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard offer number to the hero.** "Double Rewards on our bestsellers" paired with a sub-head like "Plus, up to 40% off select styles" gives this email a concrete hook and prevents the discount regression perception.
- 2. **State the deadline explicitly.** "Ends Monday" or a countdown sub-head converts the always-on Double Rewards into a time-bound reason to act now.
- 3. **Pick one hero category.** Lead with the single bestselling item or category (presumably something in living room given the hero image) and put the multi-category grid lower. Force one click before branching.
- 4. **Tighten the subject.** The current subject is pleasant but low-stakes — it could run any week. Tie it to Memorial Day or the weekend deadline.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Our bestsellers — Double Rewards ends Monday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The pieces everyone's buying this Memorial Day`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Top-rated across every room — plus, earn 2x Rewards before the weekend ends`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Dining tables, sofas, cookware — shop what's flying off shelves`
