# Make your morning easier with these NEW coffee & espresso machines →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- This is a new-arrivals product showcase from Crate & Barrel for coffee and espresso machines — no price hook, no sale, just "look at these new things." Coming after the June 14 Breville/Zwilling kitchen sale (which scored a 7 and did earn its promotion by naming recognizable appliance brands), this send feels like a step backward: same category territory but without the discount momentum that made that email land. The multi-block catalog format shows several machines but never commits to a single hero product, which means the eye has nowhere to anchor.
- The subject line is doing the heavy lifting here — "coffee & espresso machines" is concrete and category-relevant — but the body fails to reward the open. There's no price, no urgency, no standout reason to act now versus browsing later. It reads like a product-launch email that forgot to tell you why today is different from any other Tuesday.
- Verdict: competent execution of a low-ambition brief. The brand knows how to render clean email; it just didn't give this one anything to work with.

## What's working

- **Sender identity is instantly clear** — Crate & Barrel logo, clean header, no doubt about who sent it.
- **Subject line names the category explicitly** — "coffee & espresso machines" does real category work for anyone who cares about the kitchen vertical.
- **Render is clean** — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds across the visible width.
- **Product variety is present** — multiple machines shown across the email body, giving the reader genuine browse value.
- **Natural cadence break** — after a heavy run of furniture and sofa emails, shifting to kitchen appliances is the right editorial move.

## What's weak

- **No price or discount signal anywhere** — the June 14 Breville/Zwilling email set a price-expectation baseline in this category; a follow-up with zero offer feels like a step down.
- **No single hero product** — the multi-block catalog format scatters attention; nothing earns the prime visual position.
- **Subject is too long for mobile** — 67 characters, and the arrow glyph at the end is decoration, not information.
- **"NEW" in all-caps reads slightly promotional** — minor spam-filter concern, but also just not necessary when the subject already says "new."
- **No social proof or urgency** — no star ratings, no "selling fast," no deadline; everything is equally available forever, apparently.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with one machine as the hero with a price.** Pick the Breville or De'Longhi that's trending, feature it large with an MSRP and either a member price or a "free shipping on orders $X+" threshold. The June 14 Breville send proved that naming a real brand at a real price converts better than "look at new stuff."
- 2. **Tighten the subject below 50 characters and drop the arrow.** The arrow adds length without adding click signal.
- 3. **Add a time element.** Even "new this week" or "just arrived — limited quantities" gives the email a reason to exist today rather than next week.
- 4. **Add one proof point per machine** — a single sentence of spec that matters (size, brew volume, steam wand, programmable) gives the coffee buyer something to forward or decide on.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Conflicting `img` max-width media queries (rendering bug):**

```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }  /* declared first */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }  /* declared second — overrides at ≥768px */
```

At viewport widths ≥768px, both rules match; CSS cascade gives the later rule priority, so `max-width: 640px` wins. If the container is 600px wide, images can overflow by up to 40px at desktop widths.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta (accessibility):**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

Prevents user pinch-zoom on mobile — WCAG 1.4.4 violation. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` on universal selector (rendering):**

```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none; ... }
```

Overrides the `body { -webkit-text-size-adjust:100% }` rule set two lines earlier, suppressing iOS auto-resizing globally. The `body` rule should be sufficient; remove the `none` value from `*`.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags:**

`format-detection` is declared four times (once combined, then three times individually). Redundant but harmless; template hygiene issue.

**No dark mode support:**

```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```

Dark mode explicitly disabled. Apple Mail and Gmail dark mode will force-invert colors. This is a deliberate choice but worth flagging — no `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` override is present.
