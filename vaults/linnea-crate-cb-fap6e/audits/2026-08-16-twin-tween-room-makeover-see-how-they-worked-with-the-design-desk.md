# Twin tween room makeover! See how they worked with The Design Desk →
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-16**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids running an editorial-style content play instead of a straight catalog send — twin tween sisters (Rumi and Juno) each get their own bedroom designed via "The Design Desk," complete with real quotes from the kids ("the fluffier sheep is so soft, it is like a marshmallow"). It's a genuinely different format from the run of catalog crawls this brand has been sending (compare 2026-08-15's dining/living/bunk-bed roundups), and the personal narrative device is a smart way to make product feel aspirational rather than transactional.
- The problem is the email buries its actual commerce under a LOT of storytelling before you get real shoppable urgency. There's no discount, no price, no "sale" language anywhere in the hero or body — this is pure inspiration content with "Shop Room" / "Book a Free Appointment" CTAs, which is a fine strategy but scores very differently than a promo email. It also runs extremely long (three full bedroom stories stacked before you even reach the generic "we love these for you" grid), which is a lot of scroll for a subscriber to get through on mobile.
- Judgment: strong creative concept, weak on urgency/offer — this will read well to a design-curious parent already loyal to the brand, but it won't convert casual browsers the way a "25% off" subject would.

## What's working

- The twin-sister narrative ("twins tell all: designing their dream rooms") is a distinctive hook this brand hasn't used recently — it stands out against the catalog-crawl cadence of 08-14/08-15.
- Real kid quotes under each room ("i wanted to try something new and minimalist," "the fluffier sheep... like a marshmallow") add authentic personality that a generic product grid can't.
- Each bedroom section is followed by a clean shoppable product strip (bed, dresser, rug, etc.) with matching CTAs — the inspiration-to-shop path is logically laid out.
- "Book a Free Appointment" is repeated consistently as a secondary CTA, reinforcing The Design Desk service angle throughout rather than just once.
- No visible render bugs — images, text, and product tiles all render cleanly.

## What's weak

- No discount, price, or offer visible anywhere in the email — a subscriber scanning for "why open this" gets zero concrete incentive.
- The email is excessive length (three full room stories + generic grid + credit-card pitch + app pitch) — most readers won't scroll to the bottom, so the "we love these for you" and credit card sections are likely wasted real estate.
- Visual hierarchy is diffuse — with three near-identical bedroom sections back to back, nothing distinctly says "look here first"; it reads more like a blog post than an email built to convert.
- The credit card cross-sell ("new! the card you love just got better") and app download pitch at the bottom feel bolted-on and off-theme against the editorial content above.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one hero room (not three) and move the other two bedrooms to a "see more room makeovers" link — this cuts scroll length dramatically and gives the eye a single clear focal point.
- 2. Add a concrete incentive tied to the story — e.g., "book a free Design Desk appointment this week and get 15% off your first order" — the free-appointment CTA has no urgency or dollar value attached right now.
- 3. Move "Book a Free Appointment" above the fold as the primary CTA if that's the real conversion goal — right now "Shop Room" and "Book a Free Appointment" compete equally, diluting both.
- 4. Cut the credit card and app-download blocks from this send — they undercut the editorial tone and are better suited to a dedicated email.

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This is Crate & Kids running an editorial-style content play instead of a straight catalog send — twin tween sisters (Rumi and Juno) each get their own bedroom designed via "The Design Desk," complete with real quotes from the kids ("the fluffier sheep is so soft, it is like a marshmallow"). It's a genuinely different format from the run of catalog crawls this brand has been sending (compare 2026-08-15's dining/living/bunk-bed roundups), and the personal narrative device is a smart way to make product feel aspirational rather than transactional.

The problem is the email buries its actual commerce under a LOT of storytelling before you get real shoppable urgency. There's no discount, no price, no "sale" language anywhere in the hero or body — this is pure inspiration content with "Shop Room" / "Book a Free Appointment" CTAs, which is a fine strategy but scores very differently than a promo email. It also runs extremely long (three full bedroom stories stacked before you even reach the generic "we love these for you" grid), which is a lot of scroll for a subscriber to get through on mobile.

Judgment: strong creative concept, weak on urgency/offer — this will read well to a design-curious parent already loyal to the brand, but it won't convert casual browsers the way a "25% off" subject would.

## 2. What worked

- The twin-sister narrative ("twins tell all: designing their dream rooms") is a distinctive hook this brand hasn't used recently — it stands out against the catalog-crawl cadence of 08-14/08-15.
- Real kid quotes under each room ("i wanted to try something new and minimalist," "the fluffier sheep... like a marshmallow") add authentic personality that a generic product grid can't.
- Each bedroom section is followed by a clean shoppable product strip (bed, dresser, rug, etc.) with matching CTAs — the inspiration-to-shop path is logically laid out.
- "Book a Free Appointment" is repeated consistently as a secondary CTA, reinforcing The Design Desk service angle throughout rather than just once.
- No visible render bugs — images, text, and product tiles all render cleanly.

## 3. What didn't

- No discount, price, or offer visible anywhere in the email — a subscriber scanning for "why open this" gets zero concrete incentive.
- The email is excessive length (three full room stories + generic grid + credit-card pitch + app pitch) — most readers won't scroll to the bottom, so the "we love these for you" and credit card sections are likely wasted real estate.
- Visual hierarchy is diffuse — with three near-identical bedroom sections back to back, nothing distinctly says "look here first"; it reads more like a blog post than an email built to convert.
- The credit card cross-sell ("new! the card you love just got better") and app download pitch at the bottom feel bolted-on and off-theme against the editorial content above.

3. Move "Book a Free Appointment" above the fold as the primary CTA if that's the real conversion goal — right now "Shop Room" and "Book a Free Appointment" compete equally, diluting both.
4. Cut the credit card and app-download blocks from this send — they undercut the editorial tone and are better suited to a dedicated email.
