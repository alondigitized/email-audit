# We’re proud to celebrate love.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's Pride Month brand-values send — "We're proud to celebrate love" is the clearest possible Pride Month activation, and the timing (June 5) is intentional. The email leans into elegant lifestyle imagery of tableware, glassware, and home goods styled around celebration, with a product grid below the hero. There's no price promotion anywhere visible; this is pure brand-positioning territory.
- The problem is this lands on the same day as the social-proof editorial ("★★★★★ | 'Exactly what I've always wanted'") already sent by Crate & Barrel. That earlier send was a stronger-performing editorial because it had actual customer review copy doing emotional work. This one has the values statement but no concrete offer and no obvious urgency hook — the net effect is a warm, harmless, forgettable send.
- Good brand citizenship. Mediocre email. The aesthetic is right; the activation is shallow.

## What's working

- **Subject line lands the Pride Month beat cleanly** — "We're proud to celebrate love." reads naturally, not forced, and the lowercase styling matches the editorial tone.
- **Hero image is elegant and on-brand** — tableware and glassware styled for celebration; nothing garish or off-tone.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no layout breaks, no overlapping text visible at any product tier.
- **Seasonal relevance** — June + "celebrate love" is well-timed; the brand isn't late or awkward about it.

## What's weak

- **No offer** — there's no price, no discount, no free-shipping hook, nothing to give the subscriber a reason to act beyond browsing.
- **Fourth email in the family today** — the C&B/Crate & Kids inbox footprint on June 5 is now sustainability editorial → quality editorial → social proof → this. Cadence fatigue is real.
- **CTA copy is illegible at render size** — the button(s) below the hero appear to be present but the copy can't be read, suggesting they're small or understated, not dominant.
- **No registry or gifting specificity** — "celebrate love" in June could mean Pride, weddings, anniversaries. The email doesn't commit to any of them, which dilutes the targeting.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick a lane — Pride or registry** — "Proud to celebrate love" with a registry tie-in ("Help them build a home they love") or an explicit Pride angle (a curated Pride edit) would convert brand sentiment into a browsable category. Right now it's neither.
- 2. **Add one concrete action** — even a soft hook like "Registry picks for June celebrations" with a clear category CTA would give the subscriber a destination.
- 3. **Move this to tomorrow** — the social-proof email sent today was a stronger Crate & Barrel send. Stacking two editorial C&B emails on the same day with no offer on either dilutes both.
- 4. **Make the CTA button visible at a glance** — it needs to be a high-contrast block button with legible verb-noun copy ("Shop Celebratory Gifts" or "Build Your Registry").

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Conflicting desktop `img` max-width breakpoints** — two media queries set contradictory rules:

```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```

Because both are `min-width`, the 640px rule fires first in the cascade at ≥640px viewports. The 768px rule then overrides it, capping images at **600px on wider viewports and 640px on narrower ones** — the opposite of the likely intent. The 768px block should be removed or the two values reconciled.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — suppresses pinch-to-zoom on mobile, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) failure and will trigger accessibility flags in some inbox audits.

**`* { line-height: 100% }` in MI styles** — the universal selector is too broad; it collides with the client stylesheet's more specific `line-height` declarations and can produce collapsed text rendering in Gmail Android and Samsung Mail.

**Dark mode** — `<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">` and `<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">` explicitly opt out of dark mode. No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` overrides present. Apple Mail and Outlook for iOS will force-invert colors unless overrides are provided.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags** — `telephone=no`, `address=no`, and `date=no` each appear twice (once in a combined tag, once standalone). Redundant but harmless; remove dupes for cleanliness.
