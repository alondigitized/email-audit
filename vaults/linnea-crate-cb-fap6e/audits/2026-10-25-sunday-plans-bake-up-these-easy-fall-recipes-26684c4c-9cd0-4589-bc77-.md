# Sunday plans: Bake up these easy fall recipes 🥧
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-25**
## Executive summary

- This is a recipe-led content send. A "Sunday plans" subject opens onto a fall-baking edit that carries a 30% KitchenAid offer. The editorial is strong. The offer is a banner line and a few price-cut modules, and the body doesn't sell it hard.
- It is a very long scroll. The structure is hero, apple spice cake, pumpkin pie, cheesecake bars, apple pie bars, pumpkin bread, maple pumpkin cakelets, a "more fall baking must-haves" grid, then a design-tour and TheDesignDesk block. The back half drifts into a "fall deals" tile, a sweepstakes and an app promo. The baking story loses focus by the end.
- Judgement: a pleasant, on-theme send that gets people baking more reliably than it gets them buying. It is a 6–7 for anyone who bakes, and it dilutes the offer.

## What's working

- The subject and hero agree. "Easy fall recipes for a cozy weekend" matches the "Sunday plans" framing.
- The 30% off KitchenAid electrics and attachments line is a concrete offer, and the milkshake mixer shows Sale $349.95 against Reg. $499.99.
- The food photography is appetizing, and the full-bleed brown maple pumpkin cakelets panel breaks up the white modules well.
- "Get the Recipe" is a clear verb-and-noun CTA, and each recipe has its own button.
- The pie dish at $79.95 against $111.96 and the 5-star ratings give the shopper a reason to buy.
- The "more fall baking must-haves" grid (bakeware, linens, tools, mixers) gives clear category exits.

## What's weak

- The email is about 12,000px tall, so it is far too long for a baking story.
- The 30% off offer sits at the top as small text, not as a focal point, and there is no end date.
- Recipe CTAs and shop CTAs are stacked together ("Get the Recipe" over "Shop Electrics"), so the two jobs compete for the click.
- The lower sections (TheDesignDesk consult, $5,000 sweepstakes, app download, "fall deals") switch topic and bury the bake-along idea.
- The same send repeats the TheDesignDesk pitch twice.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to the recipes plus one clear offer block. Move the design consult, sweepstakes and app promo to other sends.
- 2. Make the 30% off KitchenAid offer the hero headline, with an end date, and put a single "Shop KitchenAid" button right under it.
- 3. In each recipe module, link the recipe and the product used (such as the mixer or pie dish) with a price. Don't stack two equal-weight buttons.
- 4. Add urgency, such as "ends Sunday" or "before Thanksgiving".
- 5. Check cadence. Crate & Barrel sent six emails across 10/24–10/25, including "Final weekend! Up to 30% off dining tables, chairs & more →". This send adds to that pile and should have a clear job.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a Marigold/Cheetah-style XHTML Transitional template with MSO conditionals and a mobile breakpoint at 460px. The main confirmed issue is about 50 images with no `alt` attribute. The header-level compliance and authentication checks couldn't be verified from the data supplied.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Tracking links not verified.** The QA run skipped the HTTP probe on 84 links because they sit on tracking/click-redirect domains. Final destinations, redirect chains and 404s are unconfirmed.
- **Repeated third-party pixel.** `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=dbe8da71…&n=1` through `n=5` appear five times. This is a LiveRamp sync pixel, each carrying the same recipient hash. The five copies add requests without adding data.
- **Recipient identifier in image URLs.** `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png?mi_u=dbe8da718e29…` and `o.gif?mi_u=…` carry a per-recipient hash in the query string. The `mi_u` value is shared with the LiveRamp pixels, which links the recipient to a third party.
- **Open-tracking pixels.** `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif` and `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/b390ce7b-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…` are tracking pixels. Images-off clients won't register opens.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt on about 50 images.** The count is from the QA list, and each flagged image has no `alt` attribute.
  - Content images from `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/…` (for example `09a93d8e-…jpg`, `57d8ab35-…jpg`, and roughly 30 more).
  - The hero video fallback `s7d5.scene7.com/…2026_1024_CB_FallBaking_REMIX_hero_vid`.
  - The animated GIFs `6bcb89df-…gif` and `e92eca67-…gif`.
  - The `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png` images.
  - Several of these appear to be content or linked images. With images blocked or on a screen reader, the recipe and product content is unreadable.
- **Pixels should use `alt=""`.** The tracking pixels (`o.gif`, `448796.gif` ×5, `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/…`) should carry an explicit empty `alt=""`. A missing attribute can make screen readers announce the filename.
- **Zoom is disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="…maximum-scale=1" />` blocks pinch-zoom on some clients, which fails WCAG 1.4.4.
- **Duplicate and redundant meta tags.**
  - Both `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" … charset=utf-8>` and `<meta charset="utf-8">` are present.
  - `format-detection` is declared four times, with two conflicting variants: `telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no` and the individual tags.
  - This is harmless, but it is template bloat.
- **Unescaped ampersand in `<title>`.** `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` is invalid under the declared XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype. It should be `&amp;`.
- **Leftover template placeholder.** `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` is a typo left in production source.
- **Desktop image rule.** The rule `img {max-width:640px}` at `min-width:640px`, followed by `max-width:600px` at 768px, can fight the desktop container width. The ordering is also inverted: the 768px rule comes first in the source, so the 640px rule wins at wider viewports. This is a minor risk, and I couldn't confirm it from the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The visible source has no unrendered merge tokens (no `%%…%%`, `{{…}}` or `[FIRSTNAME]`). The `mi_u` / `mi_cid` / `mi_mid` parameters are resolved.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` not found** (QA WARN). The QA note says the header may not be captured by the AgentMail relay, so this is unconfirmed.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found** (QA WARN). Same caveat. One-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders, so check the raw message.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment could not be assessed. The From domain `mail.crateandbarrel.com` and the image/tracking subdomains are consistent, but that isn't proof of alignment.
- **Footer unsubscribe link and physical address.** Neither is in the truncated source, so I can't confirm or deny them.
- **Plain-text part is mostly URLs.** 17,148 of 23,968 characters (about 71.5%) are URLs. The plain-text alternative is dominated by raw tracking links, which can hurt spam scoring and readability.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
No confirmable issues. All 84 links are wrapped in click-redirect domains and were not resolved, so UTM parameters and landing-page alignment couldn't be checked. This is a limit of the data, not a finding.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to all content, product and hero images. Use `alt=""` on every tracking pixel and spacer.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Check the raw message headers for `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, plus SPF/DKIM/DMARC results. These are likely relay artifacts, but they are unconfirmed.
4. Resolve the tracking-redirect chains in a follow-up QA pass to verify destinations and UTM carry-through.
5. Collapse the five `rlcdn.com` pixel calls to one if possible, and review passing `mi_u` to a third party.
6. Escape `&` as `&amp;` in the `<title>`, and strip the duplicate meta tags and the `IMPUT HERE` comment.
7. Trim the plain-text part so the readable copy isn't buried under tracking URLs.
