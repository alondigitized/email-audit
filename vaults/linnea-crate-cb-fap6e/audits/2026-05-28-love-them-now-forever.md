# Love them now & forever ♥️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- "Love them now & forever" is Crate & Kids' third post-Memorial-Day content pivot in two days — joining the 5/27 design consult email and the "one-of-a-kind designs" send. The brand is clearly executing a deliberate reset from the week of urgency blasts, and the editorial instinct is right. The problem is the execution: this subject line is pure sentiment with zero purchase hook, landing in an inbox that just received multiple emails from the same sub-brand in 48 hours. Fatigue is real.
- The email body shows a long, warm multi-module layout — nursery and kids bedroom scenes with a soft cream/wood palette, multiple product sections, and teal CTA buttons throughout. It scans as brand-affirming rather than action-driving. No visible discount, no urgency, no loyalty reward. That's a legitimate creative choice for post-sale recovery, but the subject line doesn't earn the open, and the body doesn't reward a reader who does open.
- The email sits in an awkward middle ground: too emotional/vague to drive a click, not editorial enough to genuinely delight. Compare it to the 5/27 SMEG spotlight from C&B (scored 6/10) — at least that had a focused story. Here the multi-section format diffuses attention across too many products simultaneously.

## What's working

- **Warm palette is consistent with the brand reset** — cream, soft wood tones, and natural light imagery signal quality and longevity without repeating the sale creative
- **Multiple product sections create discovery depth** — a reader with specific needs (crib, dresser, bed) has multiple entry points to something relevant
- **Teal CTA buttons are visually distinct** from the surrounding content and appear consistently throughout
- **Render is clean** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no obvious layout failures visible in the screenshot

## What's weak

- **Subject line "Love them now & forever ♥️" is all feeling, zero function** — no product category, no offer, no reason to open over the dozens of other emails in the inbox
- **No concrete offer is visible anywhere in the render** — after a week of 50-60% off urgency, a full-price email with no loyalty hook feels like a step backward without explanation
- **Email volume fatigue risk is high** — this is the 3rd Crate & Kids email in two days; the cadence signal almost certainly hurts open rates
- **Multi-section format dilutes the hero moment** — the eye has nowhere specific to land because every section asks for equal attention

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the subject to a concrete product or reason to buy today.** "Love them now & forever" could survive as a *theme* inside the email, but the subject should earn the open — a specific product, a new arrival, a reason to act this week.
- 2. **Add one loyalty or member hook in the body.** The 5/28 C&B email ("10% off your next purchase—even on furniture") was smart for exactly this reason. Crate & Kids could mirror that with a member reward or free-shipping reminder to justify the no-discount send.
- 3. **Pick a hero product and lead with it.** Choose one piece (the hero nursery look, one new crib or dresser) and let it dominate the first screen before cascading into supporting products.
- 4. **Trim to 3 sections max.** Long multi-module emails after a sale week read as clearing inventory. Focus breeds clicks.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

**a. Conflicting `img` max-width media queries**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
Both rules apply above 768px, but cascade order means the 768px rule (600px cap) wins over the 640px rule. For viewports 640–767px only the 640px cap applies. This is almost certainly unintentional — the 768px block should declare 640px or the rules should be consolidated.

**b. Wildcard `text-size-adjust: none`**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; }
```
Applying `text-size-adjust:none` globally via `*` suppresses OS-level font scaling for users with accessibility needs. Should be scoped to `body` only, not `*`.

**c. `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks pinch-to-zoom on iOS/Android — an accessibility failure for low-vision users. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**d. `line-height: 100%` on wildcard selector**
```css
* { line-height: 100%; }
```
A computed value of 100% resolves to the element's own font-size, not the standard 1.5× reading default. This collapses line spacing on multi-line text blocks. Should be removed from the wildcard and set explicitly per text element.

**e. Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
The combined tag already covers all cases:
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
```
Three subsequent single-value tags (`date=no`, `address=no`, `telephone=no`) are redundant noise. Remove the duplicates.

**f. `<title>` mismatch**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
Sender brand is "Crate & Kids" (`crateandkids@mail.crateandbarrel.com`). Title should read `Crate & Kids` to match the sub-brand and avoid confusion in screen readers / tab labels.

**g. Unfilled template placeholder comment**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
Developer scaffold comment with a typo ("IMPUT") was not removed before send. Harmless but indicates incomplete template cleanup.

**h. `color-scheme` locked to `light` only**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
No dark-mode CSS is declared. Apple Mail and Gmail dark mode will force-invert or re-color elements unpredictably. Intentional if dark mode was out of scope, but should be a documented decision.
