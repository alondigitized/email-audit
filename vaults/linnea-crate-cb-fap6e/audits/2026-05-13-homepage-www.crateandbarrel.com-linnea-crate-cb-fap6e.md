# Homepage snapshot · www.crateandbarrel.com · 2026-05-13
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- The first thing that hits me isn't the homepage — it's a modal asking for my email *and* my phone number before I've scrolled an inch. Once I mentally dismiss that, Crate & Barrel is clearly in spring-clearance mode, pushing outdoor furniture hard with up to 30% off front and center. The brand aesthetic is unmistakably warm and aspirational — exactly what I expect — but the editorial momentum is pointed entirely at outdoor, not at the registry lane I'm shopping, which makes this feel like I arrived on the wrong floor.

## What's working

- **"Earn 10% Back in Rewards Dollars" top bar** — first thing I read, and as someone building a relationship with the brand through my registry, rewards feel directly useful
- **Yellow urgency strip** — "last chance! up to 30% off the spring refresh event" explicitly names Tabletop in the body copy ("Shop Furniture, Tabletop, rugs, decor & more"), which is exactly where I'd shop
- **"Wedding Registry" in the secondary nav** — I can see it without scrolling; for a recently registered person, this is a direct on-ramp
- **Primary nav** — "Tabletop & Bar" and "Kitchen" are one tap from the hero, no digging required
- **"Built-to-last bestsellers" section** — the glimpse of walnut shelving and warm living room content peeking below the modal is the most compelling thing on the page for me personally
- **Brand aesthetic** — the outdoor lifestyle photography is warm, textured, grown-up; it looks like a home I'd want, not a catalog for someone else's house

## What's weak

- **The modal is a door slam** — requiring both an email and a phone number before I've seen a single product is aggressive. The phone field is marked required. I'm clicking "Maybe Later" without thinking twice.
- **The entire hero is outdoor furniture** — not one mention of tabletop, dinnerware, or registry in the above-fold imagery or hero copy. The urgency strip covers it, but the hero doesn't.
- **No registry momentum hook** — I just registered. A "check what's new for your registry" or "see recently added" pull would have grabbed me immediately. Instead, I'm looking at patio loungers.
- **Both CTAs route me away from my category** — "Shop the Sale" and "Shop All Outdoor" are the only button-level actions visible in the hero; neither leads me toward dinnerware or walnut anything.
- **"Last chance!" with no end date** — the urgency copy on the yellow strip has no specific deadline, which makes the scarcity feel manufactured rather than real.

## Recommendations

- **Add a second hero tile (or swap to a carousel) featuring a tabletop lifestyle shot** — stoneware bowls, a walnut serving board, a Sunday-morning brunch table. The spring refresh covers it in the banner copy; the hero should show it.
- **Strip the modal to email-only** — remove the phone number field entirely. Single-field capture converts better, and demanding a phone number from a first-time visitor (or a new registrant who's already in the system) reads as extractive rather than welcoming.
- **Drop a "Your Registry" welcome card below the fold** — even a small persistent module showing registry item count and recently purchased gifts would close the loop for registrants and create genuine return-visit momentum.
- **Reorder the urgency strip copy** — put Tabletop first: "Shop Tabletop, Furniture, Rugs, Decor & more." Category order in that strip signals intent and redirects arriving registrants before they bounce.
