# What to buy: back-to-school bundles
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-17**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids running a tight, single-focus send for once — everything above the fold is backpacks, and it stays disciplined about it. The hero photo (kid dragging a colorful backpack + rolling luggage set) is on-brand and the "4-in-1 backpack bundle" diagram (backpack + lunch box + water bottle + pencil case, all matching prints) is the smartest merchandising idea I've seen from this brand this cycle — it's a real cross-sell, not just a grid of unrelated SKUs.
- Where it falls apart is the back half: after the clean backpack section it sprawls into a "focus zone" desk setup, a celebrity registry plug (Nicole Brydon Bloom & Justin Theroux), a "we love these for you" grid of cribs/strollers/car seats that have nothing to do with back-to-school, a credit card pitch, an app download push, and a design consultation offer. That's six unrelated asks stapled onto a backpack email, which is the same bloat problem flagged in the 2026-08-17 "Quality-crafted new arrivals" send (7.00/10) — this brand keeps defaulting to the kitchen-sink template even when it has a genuinely good, focused hook up top.
- Net: strong hero and strong offer mechanic, undermined by a body that gives up on the subject's promise two screens in. Worth opening, worth one click on "Shop Bundles," but the long tail dilutes what should've been a crisp, high-converting single-offer email.

## What's working

- The 4-in-1 bundle diagram (backpack, lunch box, water bottle, pencil case labeled on one visual) makes the value prop instantly legible without reading copy.
- "Ships free" is repeated on every product tile (backpacks, duffel bags, lunch boxes, water bottles) — a consistent, visible incentive.
- Clear category-specific grid (shop backpacks / duffel bags / lunch boxes / water bottles) gives four concrete entry points, not just one CTA.
- Hero image is seasonally on-point (kid walking with backpack + rolling bag) and matches back-to-school intent exactly.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, buttons are legible.

## What's weak

- The email pivots hard after "shop back-to-school" into desk furniture, then a celebrity registry, then baby gear (cribs, strollers, car seats) — none of that belongs in a back-to-school backpack email.
- No visible price or percentage discount anywhere in the backpack section — "starts at $30" is the only number shown, so there's no real "offer," just a product showcase.
- Subject says "backpack bundles" but hero headline says "backpack bundles" while the email subject text given is "back-to-school bundles" — minor mismatch in specificity between what's promised and what's delivered as you scroll (bundle framing disappears after the first two sections).
- Six distinct CTAs stacked in one email (Shop Now, Shop Bundles, Shop Back-to-School, focus zone Shop Now, registry, credit card Learn More, Download Now, Book a Free Appointment) dilutes the primary action.
- The celebrity registry block and credit card pitch have zero visual or thematic connection to back-to-school, creating a jarring mid-scroll tone shift.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after the backpack/lunch box/water bottle grid — end on "Shop Back-to-School" as the single CTA. Move the focus-zone furniture, registry, baby gear, credit card, and app plug into separate sends; this alone would turn a 7-CTA catalog crawl into a focused, high-intent conversion email.
- 2. Add a real, visible discount or bundle price ("Bundle for $XX, save $YY") next to the 4-in-1 diagram — right now there's a smart bundle idea but no proof it's actually cheaper than buying separately.
- 3. Move "ships free" language up to the hero band as a headline badge, not just repeated fine print under each tile, so it registers as the offer, not a footnote.
- 4. If the "we love these for you" baby-gear grid must stay for cross-sell reasons, retitle it and separate it clearly (e.g. a divider + "New parent? Start here") so it doesn't read as a non sequitur inside a backpack email.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Backpack bundles: everything matches, ships free`
- **Subject Alt B:** `4-in-1 backpack bundle — save on the whole set`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Backpack, lunch box, water bottle, pencil case — one print, one order.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Coordinated back-to-school sets, starting at $30, free shipping.`
