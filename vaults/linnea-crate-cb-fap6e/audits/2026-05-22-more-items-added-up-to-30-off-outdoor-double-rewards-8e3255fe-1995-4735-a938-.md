# MORE ITEMS ADDED! Up to 30% off outdoor + Double Rewards!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's outdoor-category slice for Memorial Day — the same playbook the brand used effectively with rugs on 5/20 (8/10). The timing is genuinely excellent: it's the Friday of Memorial Day weekend and outdoor furniture is exactly what people are shopping for. The hero is clean, aspirational, and immediately communicates the offer.
- The problem is the offer anchor. "Up to 30% off outdoor" lands against a backdrop of "up to 60% off" emails this same subscriber received yesterday. Outdoor is getting a noticeably smaller discount, which risks reading as a second-tier deal just as the brand has trained its list to expect 50-60% off everything. The "MORE ITEMS ADDED!" hook — appearing here for at least the third time this week — has spent its urgency.
- Competent execution on a smart category angle, undercut by a weaker discount ceiling and a recycled refresh hook that no longer surprises.

## What's working

- **Timing is dead-on.** Outdoor furniture on the Friday of Memorial Day weekend is as well-timed as a C&B send can be — patios are literally being set up this weekend.
- **Clean visual hierarchy.** The hero patio seating image loads first, large and unambiguous — no hunting for the point of the email.
- **Multiple category entries.** Outdoor seating, dining, entertaining, and smaller decor sections give subscribers several browsing hooks.
- **Double Rewards prominently placed.** The loyalty mechanic sits alongside the discount, giving members a compound reason to act.
- **No visible render bugs.** Clean load, no broken images, no overlapping text across any section.

## What's weak

- **30% cap undercuts the week's narrative.** After "up to 60% off" Memorial Day emails yesterday, 30% outdoor reads as a downgrade, not an addition.
- **"MORE ITEMS ADDED!" is worn out.** This hook has appeared three or more times this week across C&B sends — the urgency has fully evaporated.
- **No explicit deadline.** Neither the subject nor visible body copy anchors when the sale ends, defusing any time-pressure the offer could carry.
- **Email runs long without editorial spine.** Multiple stacked product sections continue past where a natural exit point should exist.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Contextualize the 30% honestly.** Don't let it land unannounced against a 60% backdrop. A line like "Outdoor pricing, finally here" or framing it as a separate category drop makes the 30% feel intentional, not stingy.
- 2. **Add a visible end date.** "Ends Monday" in the hero or above the primary CTA turns a vague sale into a real deadline.
- 3. **Retire the "MORE ITEMS ADDED" hook.** It's been used too many times this week. Lead with the season and the category instead.
- 4. **Trim to two sections + a grid.** Hero seating, outdoor dining, then a tight "Shop All Outdoor" grid. Cut the rest.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Patio season starts here: up to 30% off outdoor`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your patio. 30% off. Ends Monday.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New outdoor drops just in time for the long weekend — shop seating, dining, and more.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Earn Double Rewards on every outdoor purchase this weekend only.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text: 40+ images** flagged across all content images on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com` and all tracking pixels. This is a blanket omission — no `alt` attribute present on any `<img>` tag per QA scan.

- Functional images (product shots, CTAs rendered as images) must carry descriptive alt text.
- Decorative spacers and tracking pixels must carry `alt=""` (empty string) to suppress screen reader output.
- Images-off rendering (default in many corporate Outlook environments) will produce a content-free email with no text fallback for any image-based CTA.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` — this prevents user zoom on mobile, which violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is blocked by iOS accessibility settings in some configurations.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags:** `format-detection` appears three times (consolidated once in the first meta, then again as separate telephone/address/date tags). Redundant but harmless.

**`<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` comment left in `<head>`:** Unreplaced template placeholder comment is visible in source. No functional impact but indicates incomplete template cleanup.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**`List-Unsubscribe` header: not captured** — QA reports the header absent at the relay layer. If the header exists on the originating MTA but is stripped by the AgentMail relay, Gmail and Yahoo Mail will not surface the native one-click unsubscribe UI (required for bulk senders under Google/Yahoo 2024 sender requirements for >5k/day volume).

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058): not captured** — Same cause. Without this header, one-click unsubscribe (POST-based) is non-functional in compliant clients, which can elevate spam complaint rates.

**`Authentication-Results` header: not captured** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status is unverifiable from the relay snapshot. If these are present upstream and stripped, there is no audit gap from a sending-domain perspective; however, the pipeline cannot confirm authentication for this send.

**Action required:** Confirm whether `crateandbarrel.com` publishes a DMARC policy at `_dmarc.crateandbarrel.com` and that `mail.crateandbarrel.com` is an aligned SPF/DKIM signing domain. The relay stripping these headers is a monitoring blind spot.

**CAN-SPAM physical address:** Not verifiable from the truncated source — confirm a valid postal address appears in the email footer.
