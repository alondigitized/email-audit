# See our new & exclusive Nuna clothing collection!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**

- This is a clean new-collection launch email introducing a Nuna x Crate & Kids clothing line — the editorial hook ("new & exclusive") is the whole play here. - What holds it back is the context it lands in: this is the third email from the Crate & Barrel family on May 30 alone (the clearance blast scored 9/10, the TV editorial send also went out today). - **"New & Exclusive" positions this above routine promotion** — the exclusivity signal gives a subscriber real reason to click versus another clearance blast.
- **Lifestyle photography is warm and demographically precise** — parent + young child, neutral palette, soft light; matches the C&K subscriber perfectly.
- **Nuna is a premium, trusted brand in this category** — existing Nuna gear owners are a natural ready audience for a Nuna apparel extension.
- **Product grid is tightly edited** — a focused capsule of soft neutrals rather than a firehose of SKUs; easier to parse on mobile.
- **Nuna gear cross-sell at the bottom is smart sequencing** — reminds subscribers of why they trust Nuna before asking them to extend that trust to clothing.

## What's weak

- **No offer or incentive anywhere visible** — a collection launch with no introductory pricing, no bundle, no early-access perk gives a subscriber no urgency to click now versus later (or never).
- **Third email from the C&B family today** — the cadence math (clearance blast, TV editorial, this) makes this easy to skip; brand-history fatigue is real by send #3.
- **Subject line is 53 characters — just over the mobile-safe threshold** — "See our" is padding and the line will truncate on many Android lock screens.
- **CTA button copy isn't legible at mobile screenshot resolution** — likely generic ("Shop Now" or "Shop Collection"), which is a missed specificity opportunity.
- **No social proof or newness signal** — "new & exclusive" without a launch date, limited quantities, or any "just dropped" framing feels soft.

- 1. **Add a soft scarcity or launch signal** — "Now live: limited first run" or "Just dropped — first looks" in the hero would convert a discovery email into an action email without needing a discount.
- 2. **Cut "See our" from the subject** — leads with a weak verb. "New & exclusive: the Nuna Clothing Collection" or "Nuna makes clothes now — only at Crate & Kids" lands harder.
- 3. **Don't send this on the same day as the clearance blast** — holding this 24–48 hours would give it room to breathe and lift open rate meaningfully.
- 4. **Sharpen the CTA** — "Shop the Nuna Capsule" beats "Shop Now" because it mirrors the exclusive language used in the hero.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` disables user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile; WCAG 1.4.4 failure. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Global `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `*`**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Blocks iOS automatic text resizing for all elements. The later client CSS block correctly applies it only to `body`; the earlier rule on `*` undermines that.

**Conflicting desktop `img` max-width queries — cascade bug**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }  /* earlier */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }  /* later — wins at 768px+ */
```
At ≥768px viewports both rules match; the 640px rule is declared last and overrides the 768px rule, so `max-width: 640px` applies everywhere instead of the intended 600px cap.

**`<title>` mismatch — wrong brand**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
Email is from "Crate & Kids"; the title tag names the parent brand. Affects screen reader announcements and some preview pane renderers.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags (4×)**
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
The combined tag is sufficient; the three trailing singles are redundant noise.

**Duplicate `<meta charset>`**
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta charset="utf-8">
```
Both are present. One is sufficient; some parsers may flag the redundancy.

**Global `line-height: 100%` on `*`**
```css
*{... line-height: 100%; ...}
```
Applied universally, this collapses multi-line text in several email clients (notably older Outlook). Typically scoped only to `.ExternalClass` children.

**Duplicated `.ReadMsgBody` / `.ExternalClass` rules across two `<style>` blocks**
Rules for both selectors appear in both the first and second `<style>` block with partially different declarations. Cascade order depends on block position; no explicit conflicts exist here, but dead rules add parse weight and maintenance risk.
