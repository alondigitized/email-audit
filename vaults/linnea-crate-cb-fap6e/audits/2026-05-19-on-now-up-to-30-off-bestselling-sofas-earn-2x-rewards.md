# ON NOW! Up to 30% off bestselling sofas + earn 2x Rewards!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's sofa-specific arm of the Memorial Day campaign — the same "ON NOW!" creative device and 2x Rewards mechanic that anchored the broader 60%-off send from yesterday, now narrowed to a single category with a much shallower discount ceiling. The editorial hook ("Bestselling Sofas and How to Style Them") is smart for a high-consideration purchase: it gives the shopper a reason to browse beyond just hunting a deal. But the execution pulls in two directions at once — if you opened yesterday's "up to 60% off everything" email and now see "up to 30% off sofas," the math reads as a step backward even though sofas are always lower-discount.
- The email is long — four or more distinct sofa collection sections, each with its own swatches and CTA — which fits the category (sofas need comparison) but asks a lot of mobile scroll before anything clicks. The color swatch treatment is genuinely useful and differentiates this from a standard blast.
- This is the third C&B-family email on May 19 alone (alongside "Have you seen everything that earns Double Rewards?" and the "1000s of items" send). At that cadence, inbox fatigue is the single biggest risk to open rate, regardless of creative quality.

## What's working

- **Editorial framing is category-appropriate.** "How to Style" positions Crate & Barrel as a design authority, not a liquidator — smart for sofas where confidence, not urgency, closes the sale.
- **Color swatches per collection** give the reader actionable detail without clicking through; this is a meaningful click-qualifier for a high-ticket item.
- **Subject is precise.** "Up to 30% off bestselling sofas" tells you the category, the ceiling, and the mechanic in one line — no guesswork.
- **2x Rewards layered on top of the discount** gives members a clear reason to act now vs. waiting; the dual-incentive is well-executed at the copy level.

## What's weak

- **30% cap vs. 60% sitewide creates a discount downgrade feeling.** A subscriber who saw yesterday's sitewide 60%-off email will anchor to that number; 30% on sofas reads as worse, not as "still a deal."
- **"ON NOW!" urgency hook is recycled.** This is at minimum the second "ON NOW!" subject in this Memorial Day window; the second use of the same device cuts its urgency signal roughly in half.
- **The email is too long.** Four-plus sofa collections with swatches and individual CTAs creates scroll fatigue on mobile before any purchase intent crystallizes.
- **No end date visible.** "ON NOW!" implies fleeting, but without a visible deadline ("ends Monday" or a countdown) the urgency stays abstract.
- **Third send in one day from the C&B family.** Volume alone erodes engagement regardless of content quality.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a visible expiry date to the hero.** "Ends Monday" or a countdown bar turns "ON NOW!" from a mood into a real deadline. Single highest-lift change — no other element drives the sofa shopper to act today.
- 2. **Reframe the discount relative to sitewide.** Something like "sofas rarely go this low" or "30% off — our biggest sofa sale of the year" reclaims the anchor rather than letting 60% sitewide shadow it.
- 3. **Lead with one hero sofa, not a catalog.** Cut to two sections max above the fold; let the email link to a landing page for the full collection. Reduces scroll tax and focuses intent.
- 4. **Retire "ON NOW!" for this window.** It ran its highest-impact send already. Try something category-specific for this iteration.
- 5. **Surface an end date in the preview/preheader** so inbox-level urgency is reinforced before the open.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Sofas up to 30% off — biggest sale we run all year`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 30% off the sofa you've been watching`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sale ends Monday. Free shipping + 2x Rewards on every style.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Style guides + 30% off — find the sofa that fits your room.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — 40 images:** Every product image, promotional image, and all tracking pixels lack `alt` attributes. Images confirmed missing alt:
- 31 content images on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com`
- 6 MessageGears pixels (`f3c0d22992a01946.png`)
- 5 LiveRamp pixels (`448796.gif`)
- `o.gif` (open tracker), `94c6a8b7...` (dynamic beacon)

For tracking/beacon pixels, `alt=""` is acceptable and expected. For product/promo images, missing alt breaks screen-reader compatibility and renders the email content-free when images are blocked (common in corporate environments).

**Conflicting desktop media query breakpoints:**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
Both rules match on viewports ≥768px. The 640px rule appears second and wins the cascade, overriding the 600px constraint. Result: on true desktop viewports the 768px rule is dead code. Likely unintentional.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile — a WCAG 2.1 Level AA failure (Success Criterion 1.4.4).

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags:** `telephone=no` and `date=no` are each declared twice (once in the combined tag, once standalone). Harmless but indicates copy-paste template debt.

**Unfilled template comment left in `<head>`:**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
This is a production deployment of an unfinished template placeholder — minor, but visible in source inspection.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header: NOT FOUND**
Google and Yahoo's February 2024 bulk sender mandate requires this header for senders exceeding 5,000 messages/day. Its absence (or non-capture by the AgentMail relay) is a deliverability risk — Gmail may suppress or junk-folder the email. Evidence: QA `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found`.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header (RFC 8058): NOT FOUND**
One-click unsubscribe is now required by the same Google/Yahoo mandate for bulk senders. Missing header means Gmail's native "Unsubscribe" button cannot trigger a machine-readable one-click unsub — users must click through to a web form instead.

**`Authentication-Results` header: NOT FOUND**
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown for this capture. The sending domain is `mail.crateandbarrel.com` via MessageGears (`mi.crateandbarrel.com`). If SPF/DKIM are properly configured at the DNS/ESP level (likely, given Crate & Barrel's scale), this is a relay-capture gap rather than an actual authentication failure — but it cannot be confirmed from this audit.

**CAN-SPAM physical address:** Cannot verify presence without full HTML source — standard requirement but truncation prevents confirmation.

Final destination URLs and UTM parameters could not be verified because the QA probe skipped redirect chains. No direct-load destination URLs were exposed in the captured HTML for independent verification.

The `mi_mid` and `mi_u` parameters on the MessageGears beacon URLs are correctly structured for per-send, per-subscriber attribution. No issues found in what was observable.
