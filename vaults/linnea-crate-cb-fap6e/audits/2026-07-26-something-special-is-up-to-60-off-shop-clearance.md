# Something special is up to 60% off! Shop clearance →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-26**
## Executive summary

- This is the same kitchen-sink pattern Crate & Barrel has run before, just executed slightly better. The subject promises "something special" plus "up to 60% off clearance," and the hero delivers on that immediately — a clean beach photo, a matching "up to 60% off" ribbon, and a "Shop Now" button, so at least subject and hero agree this time (unlike the 07-25 "10% off" send that opened with a mismatched 60%-off banner).
- But the coherence stops at the category grid. After "Shop All Clearance," the email pivots hard into a Lupita Nyong'o home-makeover editorial for TheDesignDesk, a $5,000 sweepstakes, a "we love these for you" product grid with no connection to clearance, a Crate Card rewards pitch, and an app-download push — none of it clearance-related. A subscriber who opened for the discount scrolls through four unrelated marketing units before the email is over.
- Net: strong top, unfocused everything after. This lands in the same 6-7 range as the brand's better sends — good enough hero, undercut by scope creep in the body.

## What's working

- Hero is a single, uncluttered offer: "up to 60% off clearance" with one clear "Shop Now" CTA.
- Category grid (furniture, outdoor, kitchen, tabletop & bar, lighting, decor & pillows, rugs, bedding & bath) gives a legible way to jump straight to a department.
- "Shop All Clearance" gives a second, redundant-but-reinforcing CTA right after the grid.
- No visible render bugs — images load cleanly, no overlapping text, no broken layout.
- Crate Card rewards section is a legitimate value-add for existing loyalty members.

## What's weak

- The ribbon ("up to 60% off The Summer Sale") and hero headline ("up to 60% off clearance") say the same number for two different promotions, which is confusing about what's actually on sale.
- Over half the email — the Lupita Nyong'o design story, the sweepstakes, "we love these for you," the credit card pitch, and the app download — has nothing to do with the clearance offer that got the subject line's attention.
- No urgency or deadline anywhere; "While quantities last" is the only soft push, which is vague and low-credibility.
- The "we love these for you" product grid shows prices but no indication these items are actually part of the clearance/sale being advertised.
- Sender name "Crate & Barrel SALE" reads like a spam-adjacent label rather than the brand's normal identity.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email down to one job: clearance. Drop the Lupita Nyong'o editorial, sweepstakes, and Crate Card pitch into separate sends — they dilute the offer this email was opened for.
- 2. Fix the ribbon/headline redundancy — pick either "Summer Sale" or "Clearance" as the single framing, not both stacked at 60% off.
- 3. Add a real deadline ("Ends Sunday," "Through 8/9") to replace the vague "While quantities last."
- 4. If "we love these for you" stays, label it clearly as clearance-eligible or cut it — right now it reads like an unrelated catalog insert.
- 5. Rename the sender to plain "Crate & Barrel" — the "SALE" suffix reads as a spam signal, not a differentiator.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Clearance just dropped: up to 60% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 60% off clearance — while it lasts`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns across furniture, rugs, and decor.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Clearance ends soon — shop before it's gone.`
