# First look: Check out our Halloween shop now →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids' first Halloween send of the season, and it leads with genuine charm — a cozy window-nook hero shot with pumpkin/ghost/cat treat bags, a bunting banner spelling out "TRICK OR TREAT," and warm, styled lighting. It's the most tonally distinct hero in this brand's recent run (compare to the straight catalog crawls of 08/17). The "first look" framing plus personalization callouts (monogram the treat bag, the towel, the pillow) gives it a reason to click beyond generic seasonal filler.
- Where it falls apart is discipline: after a tight three-product Halloween strip, the email pivots hard into nursery furniture ("500+ new arrivals, starting at $29"), then a generic "we love these for you" grid mixing a backpack, gold knobs, a wall mirror — most of which have nothing to do with Halloween — then a credit card pitch, an app pitch, a design-service pitch, and a store locator. The Halloween story is over by the third screen; everything after is unrelated cross-sell bolted onto a seasonal subject line.
- Solid hero, undisciplined body. Worth opening, only worth clicking if you stop scrolling after the first fold.

## What's working

- Hero image has real seasonal atmosphere (window light, bunting, styled treat bags) rather than a flat product-on-white shot.
- "first look: the halloween shop" copy matches a "new collection just dropped" curiosity hook.
- Personalization is called out directly under each of the three lead products (Personalize It / Personalize It / Selling Fast!), which is a concrete, specific hook.
- Clear single CTA button ("Shop Halloween") sits directly under the hero headline, no scrolling needed.
- "Selling Fast!" on the cat & ghost pillows adds credible urgency without being spammy.

## What's weak

- The email abandons its own theme almost immediately — nursery furniture and a "we love these for you" grid of non-Halloween products dilute the "first look" promise.
- No pricing shown on any of the three hero Halloween products (treat bag, hooded towel, pillows) — a fit/price-conscious parent has to click through blind.
- The "we love these for you" grid is generic, unpersonalized cross-merch (backpack, drawer knobs, mirror) that reads like filler, not a follow-through on the Halloween hook.
- Credit card, app download, and design-service blocks stack back-to-back at the bottom, turning a seasonal launch email into a kitchen-sink send.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after the Halloween product strip (or right after "Shop All Halloween") and either end there or replace the nursery/generic-grid blocks with more Halloween SKUs (costumes, decor, tableware) — the subject promised a Halloween shop, so give a full one, not three items.
- 2. Add prices under the three hero products (treat bag, towel, pillows) — no-price product tiles kill click confidence, especially with "Selling Fast!" urgency stacked on top.
- 3. Replace "we love these for you" with a themed module like "spooky season faves" pulling only Halloween/fall SKUs — right now it undercuts the "first look" narrative.
- 4. Move the credit card / app / design-service blocks to a standard footer position they already occupy in every send (per brand history) — fine to keep, but don't let them compete with the seasonal hook for attention.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The Halloween shop is officially open →`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New: pumpkin treat bags, costumes & spooky decor`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Personalized treat bags, costumes and decor for trick-or-treat season`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Monogram it before it's gone — Halloween favorites are selling fast`
