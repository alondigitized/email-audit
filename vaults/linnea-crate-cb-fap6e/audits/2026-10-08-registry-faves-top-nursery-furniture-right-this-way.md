# Registry faves: top nursery furniture right this way →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-08**
## Executive summary

- This is a nursery-furniture send from Crate & Barrel Kids, tied to the "Semi-Annual Bedroom & Nursery Event." The top banner says "Up to 30% off," and the hero reads "everything you need for your nursery" with a "Shop Now" button. The subject's "Registry faves" and "nursery furniture" match the hero's nursery framing. Below the hero, the email is a tidy category tour with a headline, a line of copy and a button per section. The sections are cribs and bassinets, nursery chairs, dressers, on-the-go gear, "complete the room," and a second "up to 20% off Babyletto bestsellers" block.
- The weakness is length and focus. The render is heavily downscaled (680x12000 shown at 113x2000), so I can read structure, headlines and button labels but not fine print or prices. Compared with the focused "Furniture on sale: Up to 50% off right now!" send from 2026-10-07, this one is less urgent and more diffuse.
- Overall it's a competent, on-brand seasonal catalog with a mid-strength offer. It's relevant for an expecting parent but too sprawling to drive a decisive click.

## What's working

- The subject and hero both focus on nursery and registry, so the message is consistent.
- The "Up to 30% off" event banner gives a concrete price hook at the very top.
- The category sections use a repeatable pattern of headline, one line of copy and a specific button, such as "Shop Cribs & Bassinets" and "Shop Dressers." This makes the email easy to scan.
- The lifestyle photography is warm, and the crib, chair and dresser images show real rooms.
- The product grid near the bottom ("we love these for you") shows named items, which gives shoppers a direct path to a product.

## What's weak

- The email is extremely long, with at least eight CTAs. The main offer gets diluted and the bottom half is mostly secondary content.
- The 30% offer is only in a small banner, and a second 20% Babyletto offer appears lower down. Two different discount levels with no clear hierarchy is confusing.
- The "first look: the Halloween…" block, the credit-card promo and the app and design-service ads at the bottom pull attention away from the nursery and registry story.
- At this scale the large photo sections have very little copy, and one tall dark crop (partial letters visible) looks like a wasted block of space. I can't verify whether it's a bug.
- Nothing in the render gives a deadline for the event, so there's no urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to a hero plus 3-4 sections and state the offer in the hero itself: "Up to 30% off nursery furniture" with a "Shop Nursery Furniture" button. Don't leave it in a thin banner.
- 2. Make the offer hierarchy clear. Show one primary discount and fold the Babyletto 20% into that section as a supporting line.
- 3. Remove the Halloween, credit-card, app and design-service blocks from this send, or move them into a compact footer row. They dilute a registry-intent email.
- 4. Add a deadline, such as "Event ends Oct 20," and one trust signal, such as ratings or "GREENGUARD Gold Certified" near the product CTAs.
- 5. Add a registry hook near the top, such as "Add to your registry and get a completion discount." This is what the subject promises.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is built on a standard ESP template (Marketing Cloud-style `image.mail.` and `mi.` hosts). Its main confirmed defects are about 50 images flagged for missing alt text and accessibility-hostile viewport and text-size settings. The QA run scored 57% with 0 failures and 3 warnings. The header warnings look like relay artifacts, and the HTML was truncated before the footer and body links, so those areas can't be assessed.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified:** 91 links went through tracking/click-redirect domains, so the HTTP probe skipped them (`91 tracking link(s) skipped`). Destination status, redirect chains and broken links are unknown.
- **Tracking pixels:** These were flagged as images without alt text.
  - `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?...&n=1` through `n=5` (5 LiveRamp pixels)
  - `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png?mi_u=...` (6 repeats)
  - `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif`
  - `https://dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/81bdf1cd-...?mi_cid=...&mi_mid=...`
- **Repeated pixels:** The same `f3c0d22992a01946.png` pixel appears 6 times, and the rlcdn pixel appears 5 times with only `n` changing. This adds third-party requests and image-blocking overhead without clear benefit. It is not a defect on its own.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (confirmed):** About 50 `<img>` elements were flagged. Roughly 40 are content images, for example:
  - `https://image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/eeb8adae-...jpg`
  - Scene7 animated GIFs: `2026%5F0902%5FCBK%5FBabyAwarenessCTV%5FheroAnimati...`, `...gliderAnima...`, `...gearAnimati...`
  - Spacer `25%5FMI%5FBottom%5FSpacer%5F40px%5FWhite`
  - The first two images, `bbf6f9d4-...png` and `eeb8adae-...jpg`, are likely header or hero images.
  - If the product and hero images carry the message and have no alt text, image-blocked and screen-reader users get nothing. Spacers and pixels need `alt=""` rather than a missing attribute.
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` prevents pinch-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 concern).
- **Text resize disabled:** `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` and `html { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none; }` stop iOS text scaling. The `.f10` class sets 10px text on mobile.
- **Invalid markup:**
  - `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` has an unescaped `&` under an XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype. It should be `&amp;`.
  - `<meta charset="utf-8">` is not self-closed, while the doctype is XHTML.
- **Leftover template placeholder:** `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` is still in the source. It is harmless but sloppy.
- **Animated GIFs:** Three Scene7 animated assets are used (hero, glider, gear). Outlook desktop shows only the first frame, and I can't confirm from the source that the first frame carries the message.
- **Duplicate meta:** Several redundant `format-detection` tags are declared. This is benign.
