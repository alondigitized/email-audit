# Free shipping on 100s of items. Don’t miss out!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-02**
## Executive summary

- The subject promises "free shipping on 100s of items," but the hero banner that actually opens the email leads with something else entirely — "back-to-school sale: up to 20% off." That's the first thing a reader sees, not the offer they clicked to see, and it's a self-inflicted mismatch that costs trust in the first three seconds.
- Past the hero, the email does deliver on the subject with a genuinely well-organized "ships free" grid (baby bedding, kids bedding, gear, bath, curtains, all clearly tagged), which is the strongest section of the send. But then it keeps going: a $5,000 sweepstakes module, a "50% off summer sale" module, a "we love these for you" product grid with real prices, a Crate & Barrel credit card pitch, an app download push, and a design-service booking CTA. This is the same "everything merged into one email" problem flagged in the 2026-08-01 furniture send (7/10) — except here it's worse because the very first thing you see doesn't even match what you opened the email for.
- Solid execution on individual modules, undermined by a scattered structure and a hero that contradicts the subject line. Call it a 7-8: competent but unfocused.

## What's working

- The "ships free" product grid is genuinely well done — eight clearly labeled categories (baby bedding, kids bedding, baby gear, throw pillows, baby bath, kids bath, crate cuddlies, curtains) in a clean two-column layout.
- "Shop All Free Shipping" is repeated as a CTA both above and below the grid, so the reader never has to hunt for it.
- The "we love these for you" product grid shows real prices with strikethrough discounts (not vague "shop now" copy), which builds purchase intent.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, layout holds together end to end.
- Consistent Crate & Kids / Crate & Barrel visual identity throughout (color palette, typography, footer nav).

## What's weak

- Hero banner contradicts the subject line — "back-to-school sale: up to 20% off" leads instead of the free-shipping message the recipient opened the email for.
- Every "ships free" claim carries a dagger (†) footnote, signaling exclusions that aren't spelled out anywhere visible in the render — undercuts the "100s of items" honesty.
- Way too many competing asks stacked in one send: free shipping, 20% off, 50% off summer sale, a $5,000 sweepstakes, a credit card pitch, an app download, and a design consultation booking. No single offer gets to be the hero.
- No social proof anywhere — no star ratings, review counts, or "X sold" signals on any product.
- No credible deadline on the actual shopping offers; the only visible date is buried in the sweepstakes fine print.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the hero match the subject: open with the free-shipping message and the product grid, then push back-to-school and summer sale further down. Right now the two competing offers cancel each other out.
- 2. Cut the email in half. Free shipping + the product grid is a complete send on its own; move the credit card, app download, and design-service booking into separate, focused emails.
- 3. Resolve the dagger footnotes into a one-line visible caveat under the hero ("Free shipping on orders $49+, some exclusions apply") so the offer reads as honest rather than hedged.
- 4. Add a real deadline to the free-shipping offer if one exists — "ends Sunday" beats "don't miss out" for urgency that's actually credible.
- 5. Add star ratings to at least the "we love these for you" grid — those products already show price, they just need the social-proof layer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free shipping is here — shop 100s of kids' favorites`
- **Subject Alt B:** `No minimum, no catch: free shipping ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Bedding, gear, bath & more — all ships free this week`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock up for back-to-school and skip the shipping fee`
