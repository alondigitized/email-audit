# SMEG's retro style in an only-at-Crate, modern neutral
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-26**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuinely focused product-drop email — SMEG's Mocha colorway launch, and it stays on that story for the first two-thirds of the send before sliding into the usual Crate & Barrel grab-bag (fall decor sale, dining furniture, kitchen odds and ends, credit card pitch, app download). The hero does its job: a moody, well-lit shot of the Mocha toaster/kettle/mixer trio with a clean "SMEG's bestsellers, now in Mocha" headline and a single "Shop Now" button, no clutter.
- Where it loses me is the "drench your kitchen in color" section — three products shown, only one visibly has a price-adjacent CTA, and the influencer tie-in ("all Mocha everything with @ethanrode") is a nice touch but buries the actual purchase path behind a "Watch on IG" link that takes me off-email entirely. Compare this to the 2026-09-25 furniture markdowns send, which led with one bold, unambiguous offer the entire way down — this one starts strong then diffuses.
- Net: solid top-of-funnel creative for a specific, recognizable partnership (SMEG is a named, desirable brand within Crate & Barrel's catalog), but it's not a strong direct-response email — there's no discount, no urgency, and it fragments attention after the fold.

## What's working

- Hero section is clean and on-message: one product family (SMEG Mocha), one headline, one CTA button ("Shop Now"), no overlapping elements.
- SMEG is a recognizable, desirable sub-brand — this reads as a real collab/exclusive ("only-at-Crate") rather than generic housewares.
- Star ratings and "Ships Free" badges are visible on the "more faves from SMEG" product tiles, adding credibility.
- Color-coordinated design (warm mocha/tan palette matching the product line) carries through the top of the email consistently.

## What's weak

- No discount, price cut, or promo code anywhere in the SMEG section — this is a full-price style/aesthetic play, which caps urgency and click incentive.
- The email pivots hard after the SMEG content into unrelated categories (fall decor sale, dining furniture, kitchenware, credit card, app) — same "trying to do too much" pattern flagged in the 2026-09-26 clearance and holiday-kickoff sends.
- The influencer callout ("@ethanrode") routes to Instagram, a dead end that pulls the click away from checkout rather than toward it.
- Bottom third (dining, "we love these for you," credit card, app download) has no throughline to the subject line at all — a reader who opened for SMEG gets steadily less relevant content the further they scroll.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after "Shop All Kitchen" — end on the SMEG story instead of pivoting into fall decor/dining/credit card. A tighter, single-purpose email will outperform this diluted version on click-through even without adding an offer.
- 2. Add a concrete incentive to the SMEG section — even a modest "free shipping over $X" callout or early-access framing for the Mocha drop would lift both open and click scores; right now it's purely aspirational.
- 3. Replace the "Watch on IG" link with a "Shop Ethan's Kitchen" product link — keep the influencer credibility but route the click to product, not to a different platform.
- 4. Make "everything Mocha" section tiles individually clickable to product with visible price — right now it's unclear if prices are shown at all in that middle block.
