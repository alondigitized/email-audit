# The holiday decor shop is now open 🎄
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-31**
## Executive summary

- This is a seasonal launch email whose subject and top banner disagree. The subject announces the holiday decor shop opening. The first thing in the render is a banner reading "last chance! the Thanksgiving dining room event up to 30% off." It is stale promo language sitting above a Christmas hero, and it is the same dining-room event the brand pushed in the "Last call! Up to 30% off The Dining Room Event ends TONIGHT" send earlier today.
- Below the banner the email is good. The "everything to deck your halls" hero is warm and aspirational. A tidy category grid covers garlands, stockings, ornaments, pillows and candlelight. A garland feature and an ornament feature follow. The "what's your holiday aesthetic?" style block gives it some editorial personality.
- The email then runs long. It adds a Black Friday signup, a glassware product grid, the Crate Card, the app, Design Desk and a store locator. That is roughly eight modules for one message, and the decor story gets diluted. This is a polished, on-brand send that tries to do too much, with no holiday offer to give the "now open" news any pull. The image is heavily downscaled, so I can't verify fine print or small copy.

## What's working

- The hero ("everything to deck your halls") is cozy, on-season and clearly tied to the subject.
- The six-tile category grid is easy to scan and links to specific decor types.
- The "what's your holiday aesthetic?" module adds a point of view instead of just listing products.
- The Black Friday signup block gives subscribers a reason to stay engaged.
- The render is clean, with no visible broken images or overlaps.

## What's weak

- The top banner promotes the expired Thanksgiving dining-room event with "last chance," which undercuts the holiday launch and confuses the message.
- There is no decor offer anywhere. The only visible discount is the 30% off dining, which is irrelevant to the subject.
- Three near-identical "Shop… Christmas" buttons appear (Shop Holiday Decor, Shop All Christmas Decor, Shop All Christmas), which adds clutter without adding choice.
- The "we love these for you" product grid shows glassware, not decor, so it breaks the theme.
- The bottom third (Crate Card, app download, Design Desk, store finder) is a long tail of brand modules that pushes the email into scroll fatigue.

## Recommendations

- 1. Remove the stale Thanksgiving dining banner, or replace it with a real holiday message such as free shipping thresholds or a decor-specific offer. This single change would lift the email most.
- 2. Cut the email to hero, category grid, one featured collection and one CTA. Move the app, Design Desk, card and store modules to a separate send.
- 3. Make the "we love these for you" grid show decor, such as ornaments, stockings and garlands, with prices.
- 4. Consolidate the repeated "Shop All Christmas" buttons into one.
- 5. Add a credible reason to act now, such as fast-ship cutoff dates or limited-run styles, because "now open" has no urgency.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's HTML source has code-level hygiene problems: 32 images with no `alt` attribute, a viewport tag that blocks zoom, and a leftover template placeholder. The header checks (List-Unsubscribe, Authentication-Results) came back inconclusive because of the relay, and 65 tracked links were not probed. The QA run reports 0 hard failures and a 57% pass rate.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified destinations:** 65 links were skipped as tracking/click-redirect domains, so none of their final destinations or status codes were checked. This is a coverage gap, not a confirmed defect.
- **Per-recipient identifier in asset URLs:** The `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png?mi_u=dbe8da718e29…` and `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/59bc3b93-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…` URLs carry a 64-hex recipient token. If the email is forwarded or archived, the token travels with it.
- **Third-party pixels:** Five `sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=…&n=1…5` beacons (LiveRamp) load on open, alongside the `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif` open pixel. They add extra third-party requests per open. No broken links were confirmed.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt (32 images):** The `alt` attribute is absent entirely, not just empty.
  - Content and hero images: 17 on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/{1,47}/*.jpg|png|gif`, plus the hero GIF `s7d5.scene7.com/.../2026%5F1016%5FCB%5FHolidayDecor%5FGrid%5Fhero.gif`. With images blocked, these render as blanks, and screen readers announce filenames or nothing. This is the most significant finding.
  - `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png`: 7 images.
  - Tracking pixels: `sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif` (×5), `o.gif`, and `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/59bc3b93-…`. These should carry `alt=""` so assistive tech skips them.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` prevents pinch-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4).
- **Invalid markup under the XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype:**
  - `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` has an unescaped ampersand.
  - `<meta charset="utf-8">` is not self-closed.
  - Two `<meta http-equiv>` / `<meta charset>` declarations duplicate each other.
  - `format-detection` is declared four times, with conflicting values on the first one.
- **Template residue:** The comment `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` (misspelled) shipped in production.
- **Global resets:** `p{margin:0 auto; text-align:center}` and `*{line-height:100%}` are applied globally. They can override per-element styling in some clients, but I can't confirm a visible effect from the truncated source.
