# We’re OBSESSED with these kitchen gadgets
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-19**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel doing a straightforward category crawl dressed up as a "we're obsessed" moment — kitchen tools, cutting boards, dish towels, mixing bowls, storage — each with a clean product shot and "Starting at $X" price anchor. It's competent, on-brand, and visually calm, but it's also the least differentiated format the brand has run this week: no single hero offer, no discount, just "shop this category" repeated six times down a long scroll.
- The one spark of personality is the "meal prep, but make it ASMR" section with a UGC-style video-still callout — that's the only beat that feels like it's trying to earn a click rather than just present inventory. Everything else, including the "no such thing as too many dish towels" and "the new fall table" sections, reads as filler between the top and the app-download / DesignDesk footer blocks that close nearly every recent C&B send.
- Compared to the $19.95 Howell & Harrier collab (8/10) or even the sofa refresh (6/10), this is a weaker send — there's no price anchor in the hero, no urgency, no single unmissable CTA. It's a solid catalog page, not an email built to convert.

## What's working

- Clean, consistent product photography throughout — no render bugs, no broken images, no overlapping text.
- Clear section-by-section structure (utensils, cutting boards, dish towels, table) makes it easy to skim even though it's long.
- "Starting at $X" price anchors appear under most sub-sections, giving at least a soft entry point per category.
- The ASMR meal-prep video-still is a genuine attention-grabber and breaks up the catalog monotony.
- "we love these for you" personalized product grid near the bottom with visible prices is a nice touch for a browsing/loyalty signal.

## What's weak

- No concrete offer anywhere in the hero — subject promises "OBSESSED" excitement but the top of the email is just a product shot and "Shop Now," no % off or deal.
- Hero has no price, no urgency, no differentiator versus just landing on the kitchen category page.
- The email is very long with six+ near-identical "photo + short blurb + Shop Now" blocks before any variety — visual fatigue sets in fast.
- Multiple unrelated categories bolted onto the bottom (fall table, back-to-school luggage, credit card upsell, app download, DesignDesk) dilute the "kitchen gadgets" premise the subject sets up.
- No social proof, reviews, or ratings visible anywhere in a lifestyle category that usually converts well on reviews (e.g., "4.8 stars, 2,000+ reviews").

## Recommendations

- 1. Give the hero a real offer or a single standout hero product with a price — right now "the kitchen tools we can't live without" plus a plain "Shop Now" button undersells the subject's enthusiasm. Anchor it the way Howell & Harrier did ("starting at $19.95").
- 2. Cut the send in half — six sequential "category photo + blurb + Shop Now" blocks is too much; pick the 3 strongest (utensils, cutting boards, ASMR meal-prep) and cut mixing bowls/shakers/dish towels or fold them into a single grid.
- 3. Move the ASMR meal-prep block up near the top — it's the only creative differentiator and it's currently buried mid-scroll after four other blocks.
- 4. Drop the off-topic tail (fall table, back-to-school luggage, credit card) from a "kitchen gadgets" email — save them for their own sends; they contradict the subject's promise and add scroll fatigue.
- 5. Add a review/rating snippet on at least one hero product to build trust before the CTA.

- **Subject Alt B:** `The $12 tool that fixed our meal prep`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cutting boards, mixing bowls & the towel upgrade you need`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus: the ASMR-worthy gadget everyone's talking about`
