# 20% off ALL kids desks!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- This is a tightly-scoped, single-SKU-category sale email: 20% off all kids desks, with an "up to 90% off select storage" secondary offer and a "35% new fall arrivals" tertiary offer stacked beneath it. The creative is clean and on-brand — lifestyle imagery at the hero, grid of product tiles below. The headline offer is unambiguous and the CTA button is immediately visible without scrolling.
- Where it loses me is the offer dilution. Three separate promotions — desks, storage, and fall arrivals — compete for attention in a single email. The desk offer is the headliner but the "up to 90% off" storage callout is visually louder when you start scrolling, and the fall arrivals block adds a third register entirely. None of the three get the depth they deserve. Crate & Kids' cadence right now (this is the second email today, following the fairy-tale Fall 2026 lookbook) also risks subscriber fatigue — two sends in one calendar day from the same brand sub-account.
- That said, this is a better-than-median promotional execution. The offer is concrete, the visuals are clean, and it doesn't try to hide the discount in fine print. For a parent furnishing a kid's room or homework space, this is genuinely actionable.

## What's working

- **Headline offer is specific and prominent:** "20% off ALL kids desks" is unambiguous — no minimum, no exclusion caveat visible in hero.
- **Strong lifestyle hero image:** The study/desk room scene is aspirational and on-target for the category; draws the eye immediately.
- **Product tile grid below the fold:** Gives the reader concrete options to click rather than just dumping them on a homepage.
- **Secondary "up to 90% off" callout:** High-discount number adds stop-scroll power even if the category scope is vague.
- **Brand sender attribution is clear:** "Crate & Kids SALE" sender display name is unambiguous and brand-consistent.

## What's weak

- **Three competing offers dilute each other:** Desks at 20%, storage at 90%, fall arrivals at 35% — no clear hierarchy beyond the hero. A single-offer send on desks would be stronger.
- **Fall arrivals block feels out of place:** The fairy-tale fall lookbook was already sent today (July 16 send, "Inspired by fairy tales"); repeating the fall pitch same-day cheapens both sends.
- **"Up to 90% off select storage" — "select" is doing heavy lifting:** Without a product count or category qualifier, "select" reads as bait-and-switch risk.
- **No visible deadline or expiry:** "20% off ALL kids desks" has no end date shown in the hero or sub-copy, removing urgency entirely.
- **Two sends in one day from the same sub-account:** Following the fall lookbook earlier today, this risks inbox fatigue and unsubscribes.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop the fall arrivals block entirely.** It already got its own dedicated email today. Every scroll-inch spent on it dilutes the desk CTA.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the hero:** Even "This weekend only" or "Ends Sunday" in the subhead converts fence-sitters. The 20% offer floats without a time anchor.
- 3. **Narrow "select storage" to something credible:** Replace "up to 90% off select storage" with a specific count ("60+ items") or category ("bins, baskets & cube organizers") to neutralize bait-and-switch perception.
- 4. **Swap the secondary offer order:** Lead desks → then specific storage items → cut fall arrivals. Three tiers is one too many.
- 5. **Subhead copy under the hero:** Add a "Shop now — sale ends [date]" sub-CTA directly under the hero button.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Kids desk sale — 20% off, all styles`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Back-to-school ready: 20% off every kids desk`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus up to 90% off storage — ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Every kids desk on sale. Find their perfect study spot.`

## Technical Audit — Crate & Kids "20% off ALL kids desks!"

**1. Technical Summary**

Four web font requests return 403, degrading typography for all recipients. No authentication headers were captured by the relay, leaving SPF/DKIM status unverifiable and one-click unsubscribe non-functional.

---

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**

| Severity | Issue | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| FAIL | 4 web font files return HTTP 403 | `crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/1e07d600-...woff2`, `7ec25fd5-...woff2`, `aaa41944-...woff2`, `fd5f8f50-...woff2` |
| INFO | 22 click-redirect URLs not probed | Tracking domain wraps prevented HTTP status verification — destination link health is unconfirmed |

The 403s on font files mean all clients that attempt to load custom fonts (primarily Apple Mail, Outlook on Mac) will fall back to system fonts. The font `@font-face` declarations are present in `<style>` but the CDN is blocking the fetch — likely a missing `Origin` / `Referer` header policy on the font CDN, or the files have been moved/deleted.

---

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**

**Alt text:** 30 images are missing `alt` attributes, including all product imagery, both open/read tracking pixels (`o.gif`, `f3c0d22992a01946.png` ×6), and third-party retargeting pixels (`sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif` ×5). This breaks the email entirely in image-blocking clients (Outlook default, many corporate environments) and fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.1.1.

Notable missing-alt images:
- Hero/product shots: `bbf6f9d4-...png`, `2856f177-...jpg` through `971aa10f-...jpg`
- Animated GIF (likely promo banner): `d99d15dc-...gif`, `30cf0d67-...gif`
- Scene7 animated GIF: `CK%5FF26%5FBackToSChool%5F4x5%5F...gif` — product content, needs descriptive alt

**CSS conflicts:** Two `@media` blocks both target desktop but with different breakpoints (`min-width: 768px` sets `img { max-width: 600px }` while `min-width: 640px` sets `img { max-width: 640px }`). Because 640px is declared after 768px in source order, the 640px rule wins for viewports 640–767px — the narrower cap will clip images on mid-size tablets.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta:** `format-detection` appears three times in `<head>` (consolidated value first, then individual `date=no`, `address=no`, `telephone=no` declarations). Redundant but not harmful.

**5. Compliance**

| Severity | Issue | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| WARN | `List-Unsubscribe` header absent | Not captured by AgentMail relay — one-click unsubscribe per RFC 8058 non-functional |

The missing `List-Unsubscribe-Post` is the highest-risk compliance gap: Gmail and Yahoo now require `mailto:` + `https:` one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders (>5k/day). If the header is genuinely absent (not just dropped by the relay), this email is non-compliant with current inbox provider requirements and risks deliverability suppression.

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity**

The `<title>` tag reads `Crate & Barrel` rather than `Crate & Kids` — minor brand inconsistency, not a functional issue.

**7. Recommendations**

**P1 — Fix before next campaign:**

4. **Probe the 22 tracking links:** Run a redirect-following HTTP check against click-wrap URLs to verify destination pages return 200 and are not 404/301-looping.
5. **Confirm UTM tagging:** Validate `utm_source=email&utm_medium=crate-kids&utm_campaign=<campaign_id>` (or equivalent) on all CTA links in the full HTML.
