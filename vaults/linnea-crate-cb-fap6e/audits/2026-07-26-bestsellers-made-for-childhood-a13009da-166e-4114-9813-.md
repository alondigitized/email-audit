# Bestsellers made for childhood →
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-26**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids running the exact same kitchen-sink playbook flagged in the Crate & Barrel parent-brand emails from 7/25 and 7/26 — except here it's worse because three separate campaigns are stacked into one send. The header ribbon pitches "back-to-school sale: up to 20% off," the hero underneath pitches "quality furniture built for childhood / Shop Bestsellers," a giveaway block pitches a $4,199 sweepstakes, and then a banner two screens later pitches "up to 50% off summer sale." None of these match the subject line, which promises none of the above and just says "Bestsellers made for childhood."
- What actually works well is the collection storytelling in the middle third — Jenny Lind, Classic, Bodie, and Hampshire each get a hero shot, a one-line value prop, a "starting at $X" price anchor, and a dedicated CTA, plus one genuinely nice touch: a real customer quote about the Hampshire dresser holding up for six years. That's the strongest content in the email and it's buried under three unrelated promo threads competing for attention above and below it.
- Net: solid product photography and a well-built mid-section, undercut by an unfocused offer structure that doesn't deliver what the subject promised. Consistent with the brand's pattern — score accordingly.

## What's working

- The hero delivers on brand ("Crate&kids" wordmark over a lifestyle baby photo) and the primary CTA ("Shop Bestsellers") matches the subject line's promise.
- Each of the four collection blocks (Jenny Lind, Classic, Bodie, Hampshire) has a clear hero image, price anchor, and dedicated CTA — easy to scan and shop by collection.
- A real customer testimonial ("We've had this dresser for 6 years now! It's heavy duty and durable...") on the Hampshire block adds credible social proof that generic promo copy can't.
- "we love these for you" grid shows a live markdown ($54.00 → $43.20) with star ratings on at least one item, giving concrete purchase signals.
- No render bugs — clean layout, no overlapping text or broken images across the full 12,000px scroll.

## What's weak

- Subject says "Bestsellers made for childhood," but the email opens with a back-to-school ribbon, then pivots to a summer sale banner mid-scroll — three different campaigns, none of which is the subject's promise.
- The offer story never resolves: is this 20% off BTS, 50% off summer sale, or a bestsellers roundup? A reader can't tell what the actual deal is.
- The $4,199 giveaway block, the Crate Card ad, the app-download ad, and TheDesignDesk ad are four more asks stacked after the product content — by the time a reader reaches "we love these for you," they've been pitched five different things.
- Dense fine print at the bottom (exclusions, sweepstakes eligibility rules) is the kind of buried-condition pattern that erodes trust in the "up to" offers up top.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one offer and lead with it end to end. If it's the bestsellers story, drop the BTS ribbon and the mid-email summer sale banner — or split them into separate sends. Cadence-wise, this brand already sent BTS and summer-sale messaging on 7/25 and 7/26; recombining them here just reads as leftover inventory, not a fresh campaign.
- 2. Move the customer testimonial and star ratings higher — that's the most persuasive, differentiated content in the email and it's currently the sixth section down.
- 3. Cut the ancillary asks (Crate Card, app download, TheDesignDesk) to one, placed after the product story, not stacked three deep.
- 4. Add a real deadline to the summer sale banner ("ends Sunday") — right now the discount has no urgency attached.
