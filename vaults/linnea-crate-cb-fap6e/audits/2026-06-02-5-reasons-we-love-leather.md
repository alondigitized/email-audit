# 5 reasons we love leather →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's leather category editorial — a "5 reasons" listicle formatted like a brand-voice magazine piece rather than a promotional send. The structure is familiar: numbered sections with reasons-to-believe copy, a product image for each, and a CTA button after each block. The creative is clean and warm, leaning on rich tan/caramel leather photography that makes the product genuinely desirable.
- The problem is that this is pure brand storytelling with no commercial mechanic. No price. No offer. No time pressure. Compare this to the 06-01 outdoor furniture email ("Up to 30% off. Your oasis is calling") which had all three — this one has none. It reads more like a catalog interstitial than an email designed to convert.
- The cadence concern is real: this is the second Crate & Barrel send today that overlaps with sofa/seating territory. The morning "sofa raves" email was also about upholstered seating. Sending two soft-furnishing-adjacent emails in one day without a shared campaign arc is subscriber-fatigue territory.

## What's working

- Hero photography is strong — warm caramel tones, a curved/sculptural sofa form, and natural light make the product feel luxurious and aspirational.
- Numbered editorial format creates easy scan-ability and gives the reader a sense of progression through the email.
- Each section pairs a written reason with a product visual, which keeps the email from feeling like a wall of copy.
- Subject line "5 reasons we love leather →" is short (27 chars), concrete in format, and the arrow is a consistent brand styling signal subscribers recognize.
- No visible render bugs — images load, layout holds, text doesn't overlap.

## What's weak

- Zero commercial offer — no discount, no "shop the collection at X% off," no free shipping threshold. The email has no reason to click today vs. any other day.
- Heavy cadence overlap with this morning's "sofa raves" send; two seating-adjacent emails in one inbox day dilutes both.
- CTA buttons appear generic ("Shop" or similar) — a verb-noun CTA specific to leather would lift click intent.
- No pricing or product detail visible — leather grade, hide type, lead time, or care story would reward someone who's already curious about buying leather.
- No loyalty or member pricing signal for those in C&B's rewards program.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a commercial hook.** Even a soft one — "free leather care kit with any leather sofa order" or "complimentary swatches, shipped to you" — gives readers a concrete reason to click now rather than appreciate the email and move on.
- 2. **Swap "5 reasons we love leather" for a reason-to-act frame.** The current editorial angle is brand voice; a slight reframe toward the reader's hesitation ("Is leather right for your home?") creates curiosity and makes the listicle feel useful rather than promotional.
- 3. **Make CTA copy specific to the action** — e.g., "See all leather sofas" or "Request a leather swatch" rather than a generic shop button.
- 4. **Stagger cadence** — this should not land on the same day as a sofa social-proof email. The leather editorial belongs on a standalone day or as part of a clearly sequenced leather week.
- 5. **Add one product detail per section** — leather grade (top-grain, full-grain), care instructions, or durability claim in-body. This is the kind of information that moves a consideration-stage buyer.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Viewport blocks user zoom:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-zoom — a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) violation and an accessibility flag for visually impaired users.

**Conflicting image `max-width` media queries:**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
The 640px rule fires first in cascade order; the 768px rule then overrides it for wider viewports, producing a narrower max-width (600px) on larger screens than on 640–767px screens. Likely unintentional — images will appear constrained on desktop.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`** appears in the client CSS block (`html { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none; }`). Using `none` (rather than `100%`) hard-suppresses text scaling on iOS Safari, overriding user accessibility settings.

**Dark mode not supported:**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` rules present. On Apple Mail and iOS Mail (which account for ~50%+ of opens in typical retail lists), this forces light rendering with no dark-mode adaptation — white backgrounds may invert unintentionally in forced-dark clients that ignore `supported-color-schemes`.
