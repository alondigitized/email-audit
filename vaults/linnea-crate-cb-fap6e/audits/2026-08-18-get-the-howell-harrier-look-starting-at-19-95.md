# Get the Howell & Harrier look, starting at $19.95
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's strongest hero section in recent memory — "statement pieces starting at $19.95" paired with a clear price anchor and a genuinely differentiated collab story (Howell x Harrier) gives the top of the email real focus, unlike the straight catalog crawls in the 2026-08-17 sends. The lifestyle shot of two women on the new sofa, the "designed to be jaw-dropping" vanity section with customer-style quotes from "Laura" and "Tiffany," and clean product tiles (Wedgie Glass, Colette Resin) all read as a deliberate campaign moment, not filler.
- But it doesn't stay disciplined. After the collab wraps up, the email sprawls into kitchen tools, a Smeg toaster plug, a $5,000 sweepstakes, a free design-service pitch, an unrelated "we love these for you" grid, a credit card upsell, and an app download banner — six additional asks stacked below a strong opener. That's the same kitchen-sink pattern flagged in the 2026-08-17 "Quality-crafted new arrivals" send (7/10), just with a better top half this time.
- Judgment: the hero alone would be an 8-9. The full scroll drags it back to a solid 6-7 — a good top-of-funnel unit buried in a catalog.

## What's working

- Clear price anchor ("$19.95") paired with an unambiguous CTA ("Shop the New Collab") right at the top, visible without much scrolling.
- The Howell x Harrier collab has an actual creative concept — lifestyle photography, named product tiles, two customer-voice quotes — rather than a plain grid.
- No render bugs: images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, layout holds together through the entire long scroll.
- Product tiles under the vanity section include material detail (glass, resin) that a shopper can act on.

## What's weak

- The email has at least seven distinct offers/asks (collab, kitchen tools, Smeg toaster, sweepstakes, design service, generic "we love these" grid, credit card) — no single-focus discipline after the hero.
- The Smeg toaster and "clean kitchen" sections have zero visual or thematic connection to the Howell x Harrier collab that the subject line promised — a reader who opened for the collab hits an unrelated product pivot mid-scroll.
- The credit card pitch ("new! the card you love just got better") and app download banner near the bottom feel like bolted-on filler competing with the actual collab message.
- Per brand history, this is another Crate & Barrel send in the same week as three other 5-7 scored emails — cadence is heavy, and this one doesn't obviously justify a fourth open.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the email to the collab.** Everything from "clean kitchen" downward (kitchen tools, Smeg, sweepstakes, design service, generic product grid, credit card, app) belongs in separate sends. A tight Howell x Harrier-only email would let the strong hero and vanity sections carry the whole message instead of getting diluted.
- 2. **Add urgency or scarcity to the collab.** Nothing here signals it's limited or new-this-week beyond the "new" framing — a "collab drops today" or item-count callout would sharpen the CTA.
- 3. **Move the price point into a real hook, not just a floor.** "$19.95" as a low anchor is good, but pairing it with what's actually included at that price (e.g., "starting at $19.95 for the Shell Tray") would make the number feel more concrete.
- 4. **Drop the credit card and app-download blocks from collab-focused sends** — they read as reflexive footer inventory rather than intentional cross-sell.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Howell & Harrier is here — pieces from $19.95`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New collab alert: Howell x Harrier, starting at $19.95`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Limited-run pieces designed with Howell & Harrier — shop before they're gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Statement decor, real prices: the new collab starts at $19.95.`
