# FREE SHIPPING ends tomorrow!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- This is a transactional urgency email — free shipping ending tomorrow — grafted onto what is essentially a fall-arrivals catalog send. The hero does its job: "ends tomorrow / free shipping" lands above the fold with a clear Shop Now CTA, and the urgency is real. But below the fold the email loses the plot quickly: fall arrivals, a "we love these for you" product carousel, an app download push, and The Design Desk all compete for attention. One compelling reason to click turns into five indifferent ones.
- The sender name "Crate & Kids SALE" is doing extra work this week. Looking at the brand history, Crate & Kids sent a 9/10 Summer Sale blast just two days ago, followed by The Design Desk editorial (4/10) and a list-acquisition email (7/10) on the 13th. Subscribers who stayed through all of those are now getting a fourth touch in three days. Frequency fatigue is real, and this send does nothing to earn its place in an inbox that's already seen three Crate & Kids emails this week.
- The "fall arrivals" framing in a mid-July email is a curious choice — not wrong by retail calendar standards, but it sits awkwardly next to a summer shipping promo. The creative is competent but the strategic stack feels unfocused.

## What's working

- **Hero urgency is hard to miss.** "Ends tomorrow!" is the kind of deadline that earns a same-day open response.
- **Free shipping is a genuinely low-friction offer.** No complex exclusions visible, no minimum order amount buried — it reads as clean.
- **Navigation bar accelerates discovery.** Shop Storage / Lighting / Kids Bedding / Kids Furniture / Lounge Chairs gives task-oriented shoppers a shortcut past the hero.
- **Starting price anchor ("starting at $29") is smart.** It lowers the perceived barrier to browsing the fall arrivals section.
- **"We love these for you" signals personalization.** Even if it's editorial rather than algorithmic, the framing builds trust for a parent-buyer persona.

## What's weak

- **Four competing value propositions dilute the primary CTA.** Free shipping, fall arrivals, curated product recs, app download, and Design Desk all ask something different of the reader.
- **"FREE SHIPPING" all-caps in subject is a mild spam signal.** It may clip deliverability scores and reads as shouted rather than earned.
- **"Fall arrivals" in a summer shipping promo is dissonant.** The copy doesn't bridge the seasonal pivot — it just drops two unrelated messages side by side.
- **"Shop Now" is the weakest possible CTA copy.** Every Crate & Kids email this week uses it. It carries no category signal and no urgency.
- **Cadence is breaking down.** This is the fourth email in three days from the Crate & Kids / Crate & Barrel family. The goodwill from the 9/10 Summer Sale blast is getting eroded fast.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse to one CTA.** Strip the app download, Design Desk footer section, and the fall arrivals secondary block. Run this as a pure "free shipping ends tonight" send — lean, fast, one click. The multi-section layout is killing focus.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA button.** Replace "Shop Now" with "Claim Free Shipping" or "Shop Before Midnight." Add the deadline to the button, not just the hero text.
- 3. **Bridge the seasonal pivot.** If fall arrivals are in the email, say it explicitly: "New fall arrivals just landed — and free shipping makes now the time." Don't make the reader connect the dots.
- 4. **Lowercase the subject line.** "Free shipping ends tomorrow" reads less spammy and more like a friend's text than a broadcast alert.
- 5. **Consider a rest day tomorrow.** The cadence this week is aggressive. The Summer Sale blast two days ago was a 9/10 — that's the send that earns the click. Don't spend the goodwill on a shipping-reminder follow-up delivered at the same frequency.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free shipping ends at midnight →`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last chance: free shipping on everything`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `500+ new fall arrivals just landed — ship them free before tomorrow.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your cart ships free today. After midnight it doesn't.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta** (confirmed):
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 failure. Should be removed or set to `maximum-scale=5`.

**Global text-size-adjust suppression** (confirmed):
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Disables OS-level font scaling for all elements. Combine with the above and visually-impaired users on mobile have no recourse to increase text size.

**Conflicting image `max-width` media queries** (confirmed):
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
On screens ≥ 768px both rules apply; the `600px` rule wins (last in cascade) because it's declared after `640px`. The `640px` breakpoint rule is effectively dead on desktop. Unify to a single breakpoint.

**Unfilled template placeholder comment** (confirmed):
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
<!---->
```
Suggests a custom font `@font-face` block was intended but never injected. If a web font is referenced elsewhere in the stylesheet, clients will fall back silently. Verify font stack is complete or remove the placeholder.

**`<title>` brand mismatch** (confirmed):
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
Email is sent from `Crate & Kids`; title renders as "Crate & Barrel" in browser-based email clients and screen reader document announcements. Should be `<title>Crate & Kids</title>`.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags** (confirmed — three separate declarations):
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
Redundant; collapse to a single combined declaration to reduce parse overhead.
