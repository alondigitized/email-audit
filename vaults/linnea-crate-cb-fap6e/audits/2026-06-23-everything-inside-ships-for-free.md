# Everything inside ships for FREE!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids' sitewide free shipping drop — a deliberate soft beat in the middle of an aggressive promotional calendar. After a full week of warehouse sale emails escalating to "up to 60% off," dropping to "everything inside ships for FREE" is a quieter value prop that trades savings for convenience. The offer is clear, the render is clean, and the hero message is playful. But free shipping is table stakes for a brand that runs these regularly, and without a deadline, a stacked offer, or a single hero product to anchor intent, the email settles for being functional rather than compelling.
- The product grid does real work — stroller, bedding, nursery furniture, bags — showing assortment breadth in one scroll. But that breadth scatters attention rather than focusing it. This email should have been built around the stroller, where free shipping is actually a meaningful unlock (heavy item, high shipping cost, high consideration purchase). Instead it leans on a generic yellow bag as the hero.
- Net read: competent but forgettable. It earns a slot in the cadence as a break from the sale drumbeat, but it won't drive outsized performance.

## What's working

- **Hero message is immediately legible.** "it. all. ships. FREE!" in playful lowercase/caps contrast reads on-brand and lands the offer in under two seconds.
- **Product variety signals assortment depth.** Stroller, bedding, dresser vignette, and bags in one scroll communicates range without chaos.
- **Clean render, no layout bugs.** White space is generous, photography is professional, no broken images or overlapping text visible.
- **Trusted sender.** Crate & Kids carries parent-brand equity — low unsubscribe risk for an active subscriber.

## What's weak

- **No deadline, no urgency.** Free shipping with no expiry feels permanent. Subscribers mentally file this as "I'll use it whenever" and don't return.
- **All-caps FREE in the subject.** A dated spam-signal move the brand's copy quality doesn't need. "Everything inside ships free" is cleaner.
- **Stroller buried in the grid.** The highest-consideration item visible — likely a Bugaboo or premium equivalent — is the one product where free shipping is a genuine unlock. It's not the hero.
- **CTAs are generic "Shop" buttons.** No product-specific copy, no verb+noun action pairing — they blend into the layout.
- **No deadline stacking with the ongoing sale.** The June 23 Crate & Barrel send (35% off Ninja, Fellow & more) ran the same day. A subscriber in both lists got two Crate family emails today; the Crate & Kids version has the weaker offer and no reference to the warehouse sale still running next door.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline.** "Free shipping ends Sunday" or "Free shipping through July 4" instantly creates a reason to act now rather than bookmark-and-forget.
- 2. **Make the stroller the hero.** "Your new stroller ships free — and so does everything else" turns a commodity offer into a specific unlock for the most considered purchase in the email. Build the hero image around it.
- 3. **Stack with the warehouse sale.** The 4th of July sale is still live. "Up to 60% off + free shipping through July 4" is a better composite offer than either standalone — align or cross-link with the Crate & Barrel send.
- 4. **Rewrite the CTA buttons.** "Get Free Shipping" or "Shop Baby Gear Free" mirrors the offer and adds intent signal. "Shop" alone is a placeholder.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Free shipping on everything — ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your stroller (and everything else) ships free`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `No minimum. No code. Free shipping on every order through July 4.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack it with the sale — free shipping on all orders right now.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` brand mismatch**
`<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` — sender is "Crate & Kids." Title populates the browser tab when preheader/open occurs in webmail; this is the wrong brand.

**Unfilled template placeholder shipped in production**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
<!----> 
```
Both lines are visible in rendered source. "IMPUT" is a typo for "INPUT." The trailing empty comment `<!---->` is a second artifact from the same block.

**Conflicting `img` `max-width` breakpoints (ordering bug)**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
The 640px query appears *after* the 768px query in the stylesheet. On viewports ≥768px both rules match; the later rule (640px max) wins due to cascade order, overriding the intended 600px cap. Images on wide-viewport clients (Gmail web, Outlook.com) render up to 640px instead of the intended 600px.

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). No functional email reason to restrict scaling.

**Global `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `*` selector**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Disables OS-level text scaling for all elements, not just the body. Amplifies the zoom issue above.

**`color-scheme` locked to light only**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Apple Mail and iOS Mail will not attempt dark mode reflow. No `prefers-color-scheme` media query present. Acceptable as a deliberate choice, but worth flagging if dark mode support is desired.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
One combined tag and three individual tags cover the same attributes (`telephone`, `address`, `date`). Redundant; the combined tag alone is sufficient.
