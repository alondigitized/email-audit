# This interior designer made our bestsellers look STUNNING
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-16**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel running an influencer/designer collab — Lisa Pollock, a Florida-based interior designer, walks through her "home makeover" using C&B bestsellers, and it's a longer, more editorial send than the straight catalog crawls this brand usually runs (compare to yesterday's "bedroom bestsellers, up to 25% off" send, which led with a hard price anchor). The lifestyle photography is genuinely strong — real rooms, real styling, a living human walking through a space — and it's a nice change of pace from stacked product grids. But it commits the same sin as the Crate & Kids "Design Desk" send from this cycle: no price, no percentage, no discount anywhere in the visible render, so all that emotional investment in "her vision" dead-ends into vague CTAs like "Shop Her Picks" and "Shop Living Room."
- The email is also long — living room, dining room, "come shopping with Lisa," fall tabletop, cookware, "we love these for you" grid, credit card pitch, app pitch, Design Desk footer — and by the time a reader scrolls that far the initial designer hook has fully evaporated. It reads like four separate email ideas stapled together rather than one coherent story.
- Judgment: nice art direction, no commercial spine. Without an offer, this is a nurture/brand-affinity play, not a conversion email — fine as one piece of a broader cadence, weak as a standalone send.

## What's working

- Hero shot of Lisa Pollock in the styled space with a named designer and clear "home makeover" framing gives the send a distinct identity versus C&B's usual product-forward layout.
- Section-by-section pacing (living room → dining room → "shopping with Lisa") mimics a real home tour, which is a smart structural idea for a design-led brand.
- Individual product call-outs under each room shot (Gather 86" Sofa, Herning Accent Chair, Floor Coffee Table, etc.) give shoppers a way to buy the exact pieces shown, not just the room.
- "we love these for you" grid at the bottom shows real prices ($1,199.00, $2,199.00, etc.) with clean product photography — the one place price transparency actually shows up.
- No visible render bugs — images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, layout holds together end to end.

## What's weak

- Zero discount, price anchor, or urgency anywhere in the top two-thirds of the email — "Lisa Pollock's home makeover" and "a living room with style that lasts" are pure inspiration copy with no reason to act today.
- The email tries to do too much: designer story, fall tabletop, cookware ("crafted-to-last cookware"), credit card upsell, and app download all compete for attention in one send, diluting the Lisa Pollock narrative it opened with.
- CTAs are generic and repetitive — "Shop Her Picks," "Shop Living Room," "Shop Dining Room," "Shop Bestsellers," "Shop Now" — none of them tell you what you'll actually get for clicking.
- The credit card pitch ("new! the card you love just got better... earning Reward Dollars") appears with no visual separation from the design content above it, so it reads as a jarring tonal shift mid-scroll.
- Subject line ("This interior designer made our bestsellers look STUNNING") promises a visual wow moment but the partial caps ("STUNNING") is a mild spam-adjacent tic C&B doesn't usually lean on.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real offer in the hero band — even a modest "up to 25% off Lisa's picks" turns this from a lookbook into a sellable email; yesterday's bedroom send proved C&B already has the discount to lend it.
- 2. Cut the email roughly in half — end at "come shopping with Lisa" / "Shop All Bestsellers," and spin fall tabletop, cookware, credit card, and app promo into separate sends. This send is trying to be four emails.
- 3. Replace generic CTAs with product-specific copy — "Shop the Gather Sofa" instead of "Shop Her Picks" — so each click has a clear destination in the reader's head before they tap.
- 4. Drop the credit card block entirely from this send or move it to the very end after a divider — it currently breaks the designer narrative mid-story.
- 5. Tone down "STUNNING" in the subject to sentence case to match C&B's usual restrained voice and avoid a spam-flag signal.

- **Subject Alt B:** `A designer's picks, now up to 25% off`
