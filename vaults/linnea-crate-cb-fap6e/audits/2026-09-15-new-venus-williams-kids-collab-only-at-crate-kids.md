# NEW Venus Williams kids collab 💜 Only at Crate & Kids!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids' full unveiling of the Venus Williams collab — the "app-exclusive teaser" from 9/14 and the "shop it first" nudge have now turned into the real, full-line launch email, and it's the best-executed of the bunch. The hero is a tight macro shot of hands weaving a bookcase, with "VENUS WILLIAMS × Crate&kids" laid over it and a single unambiguous "Shop the New Collab" button — no hedging, no buried lede this time.
- The body is a long, well-paced room-by-room scroll (bedroom, playroom, study, gallery wall) each anchored by a styled vignette, a named hero product, and a repeated CTA — genuinely strong lifestyle merchandising. But there is zero discount, urgency, or loyalty hook anywhere in this email; it's pure brand storytelling for a full-price collab, capped off with an unrelated bestsellers grid and a credit-card pitch that dilutes the collab's momentum right at the close.
- Given this is now the fourth-plus Venus Williams-themed send in roughly 48 hours across the C&B family (per brand history: 9/14 "Shop it first" 6/10, 9/14 "Early on our app" 5/10, 9/15 "unmatched style" 6/10), fatigue is a real risk even though this particular execution is the cleanest of the set. Strong creative, no commercial hook, bad timing relative to cadence.

## What's working

- Hero immediately establishes "who" (Venus Williams) and "what" (Crate&kids collab) with a single clear CTA, no scrolling needed to find it.
- Each room vignette pairs a lifestyle shot with a specific named product (e.g., "Compton Natural and Dyed Green Wood Kids Bookcase by Venus Williams"), so shoppers aren't guessing what they're clicking into.
- The pull-quote block ("In designing a kid's room, it needs to be calming...") adds a designer-voice credibility layer that's new for this campaign.
- Consistent green/tennis motif (rackets, "LOVE" wall art, striped rug) ties the whole scroll together without feeling repetitive.
- Clean render throughout — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum.

## What's weak

- No discount, promo code, free shipping, or any commercial incentive anywhere in the email — it's a pure lookbook for full-price goods ($1,499 dresser, $1,450 stroller system).
- This is at least the fourth Venus Williams-branded email in two days across the Crate & Barrel family; nothing in the copy acknowledges or varies the format enough to avoid feeling like more of the same drop.
- The email drags on well past the collab itself into a generic "bestsellers" cross-sell grid and a Crate Card ad — neither has anything to do with Venus Williams or kids, and they dilute the close.
- No social proof (star ratings, review counts) anywhere, despite this being a big-ticket furniture purchase decision.
- Subject line emoji (💜) plus "Only at Crate & Kids!" exclamation nudges toward promotional tone without actually delivering a promotion.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one concrete incentive** — even a modest "extra 10% off collab launch week" or free-shipping banner would convert this from a lookbook into a converting email; right now there's no reason to buy today vs. next month.
- 2. **Cut the length** — drop the bestsellers grid and Crate Card pitch entirely, or move them to a separate send; a 12,000px scroll for a single collab is fatiguing and buries the strongest room shots.
- 3. **Vary this from the last three Venus Williams sends** — since the audience has already seen "Shop it first," "Early on our app," and "unmatched style" this week, lead with something new (e.g., a limited-quantity note, a "collab sells out fast" cue) rather than another straight product tour.
- 4. **Add a star rating or "as seen on" proof point** to at least the hero bookcase, given the price point.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Tennis-inspired bedrooms, playrooms, and study spaces — designed with Venus Williams.`
