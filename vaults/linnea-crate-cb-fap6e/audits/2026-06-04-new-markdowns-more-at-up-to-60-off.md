# NEW markdowns! More at up to 60% off →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids' "new markdowns added" follow-up to the Summer Clearance Event Crate & Barrel launched earlier this morning. The hero reads cleanly — "up to 60% off + new markdowns" with a "Shop Now" CTA is visible at zero scroll — and the branded content sections (TheDesignDesk editorial, app download with extra 20% off, card promotion) give the email more scroll depth than a pure promo blast. The email is technically competent.
- The problem is context. This is the second email from the Crate & Barrel family today, and it is leaning on the same "up to 60% off" ceiling that the Summer Clearance launch already announced. "NEW markdowns" needs to prove its claim — the hero doesn't show what's newly cut vs. what was already discounted, so the urgency feels manufactured rather than earned.
- The Crate & Kids sub-brand framing does carve out a distinct lane: all imagery is nursery-and-kids focused, which works for a parent subscriber. But that segmentation sharpens the demographic target in a way that also narrows relevance — a Crate & Barrel subscriber without kids is going to feel like this is someone else's email. Verdict: functional for a parent with active purchase intent, soft for everyone else.

## What's working

- Hero offer ("up to 60% off + new markdowns including Furniture") is legible at first glance, no scrolling required
- "Shop Now" CTA button is clearly positioned and visible against the hero
- TheDesignDesk section breaks up pure promo content with a differentiated service hook — free design consultation adds brand equity beyond the discount
- App download section with "extra 20% off" gives loyalty subscribers a tangible reason to download, layering a second offer on top of the sale
- Category navigation shortcuts below the hero let a shopper route directly to their department without hunting

## What's weak

- "New markdowns" claim goes unproven in the hero — no callout of which categories or price points are newly cut, making the urgency feel hollow rather than specific
- Cadence is strained: Crate & Barrel already sent a Summer Clearance launch email earlier today; a second email from the Crate & Kids sibling on the same day with identical discount language reads like a retread, not new news
- "NEW" in all-caps in the subject is a mild spam-signal and looks overexcited in a crowded inbox
- The card promotion ("new! the card you have just got better") interrupts the purchase scroll with a financial product pitch that belongs in its own send or in the footer
- Product items in "we love these for you" are too small to convey prices or product names on mobile — the section occupies real estate without doing selling work

## Recommendations

- 1. **Prove "new" in the hero.** Add a "Just dropped:" sub-headline with 2–3 specific categories or item types that are newly marked down. "New markdowns" earns nothing without a list.
- 2. **Add a sale end date.** Even "Ends Sunday" in the hero sub-copy triples urgency without feeling dishonest. Nothing pins this sale to a close date anywhere visible.
- 3. **Move the card promotion to the footer.** A financial product cross-sell mid-scroll kills purchase momentum; it works better as a footer module or standalone email.
- 4. **Enlarge the product grid.** The "we love these for you" tiles need legible prices and names on mobile — if a shopper can't read what it costs without zooming, the section is decorative, not selling.
- 5. **Rewrite the subject to remove "NEW" all-caps and add specificity.**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Just added to the sale: up to 60% off kids furniture`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New markdown drops: cribs, strollers, nursery faves`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh cuts just landed — shop before they're gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New prices on nursery, furniture, bedding, and more.`
