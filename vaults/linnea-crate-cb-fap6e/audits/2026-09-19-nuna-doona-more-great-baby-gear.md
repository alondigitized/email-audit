# Nuna, Doona & more great baby gear
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-19**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids running a category-roundup email — "the very best baby gear" — that stacks strollers, car seats, high chairs, bassinets, and travel gear into one long scroll, capped off with a named-brand strip (Nuna, Doondolla/Doona, Stokke, BabyBjörn) and a time-limited 20% off sitewide banner. It's the same long-scroll catalog playbook this brand has run all September (see the 2026-09-19 dining-room and 2026-09-18 fast-shipping sends) — just swapped into the baby vertical instead of home furniture.
- The strength here is real specificity: named products (Nuna DEMI, Doona Liki Trike S3, Stokke Beat high chair), price ranges under thumbnails, and a genuine brand-recognition play with logos for Nuna/Doona/Stokke/BabyBjörn. The weakness is structure — nine-plus stacked sections with near-identical "Shop X" buttons bury the one actual offer (20% off) two-thirds of the way down, and there's no single hero moment up top to anchor the eye.
- Judgment: solid content, mediocre execution of hierarchy. A subscriber with a baby-gear need will find their category eventually, but the email asks for a lot of scrolling before it gives a reason to act now.

## What's working

- Category-first structure (strollers → car seats → high chairs → travel gear) mirrors how a parent actually shops, not a random grid.
- Named, recognizable products with visible price points (Urbo Stroller, Nuna DEMI, Doona Liki Trike S3) rather than generic stock photos.
- Brand-logo strip (Nuna, Doona, Stokke, BabyBjörn) builds trust for a category where parents care deeply about safety-certified brands.
- The 20% off + "limited time only" banner is a clear, legible offer with a visible "Shop the Sale" button.
- Clean, consistent product photography — neutral backgrounds, no visible broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- No single hero offer at the top — the email opens with "the very best baby gear" and a lifestyle stroller shot, not a deal, so there's nothing to anchor first-glance attention.
- The 20% off banner is buried after five separate product sections; anyone who doesn't scroll misses the only real incentive in the email.
- Nearly every section repeats the same "Shop [Category]" button pattern, which flattens visual hierarchy — nothing tells the eye where to land first.
- This is at least the fourth long-scroll catalog send from this brand family in a week (dining room, fast-shipping, Venus Williams collab, now baby gear) — cadence risk for anyone tracking multiple Crate & Barrel/Kids threads.
- No urgency detail on the sale (no end date shown), so "limited time only" reads as filler rather than credible urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the 20% off sitewide banner to the top of the email, above the stroller section — right now the only real offer is competing with six sections of pure browsing before a reader ever sees it.
- 2. Add a specific end date/time to "limited time only" (e.g., "ends Sunday") to make the urgency credible instead of generic.
- 3. Cut the number of "top-rated" sections from five to two or three, or convert the rest into a single scannable grid — the repetition of full-width sections with identical CTAs dilutes rather than reinforces.
- 4. Differentiate CTA copy per section ("Shop Strollers," "Find Your Car Seat," "See High Chairs") instead of the same "Shop All ___" pattern repeated — small change, but it breaks visual monotony.
- 5. Lead the subject/preheader with the sale, not the brand list, since that's the actual conversion lever in this send.
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% off Nuna, Doona & top baby gear — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your registry favorites are on sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Strollers, car seats & high chairs from the brands you trust`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Limited time: 20% off the best baby gear`
