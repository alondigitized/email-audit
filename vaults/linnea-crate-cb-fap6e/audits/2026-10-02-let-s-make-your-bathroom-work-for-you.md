# Let’s make your bathroom work for you →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-02**
## Executive summary

- This is a long, editorial "Design Desk" bathroom guide. It's a well-built email, but it tries to be four emails at once. The hero is a calm bathroom vanity with "breathe new life into your bathroom" and a "Book a Free Appointment" button. Below it are three numbered steps (foundation, essentials, natural elements), each with three products and a "Shop Now" button. The scroll then keeps going into more design services, plush towels, a decor event, a storage event, a registry-style "we love these for you" grid and an app promo. I'm working from a heavily downscaled render of a very tall image, so small text such as product names and fine print is hard to make out.
- The strongest part is the content. The numbered structure, the star ratings and the "Exclusive" and "Bestseller" tags on products give it a real reason to exist beyond a discount. The weakness is focus. The first thing you see is a banner for up to 20% off sofas and sectionals, which has nothing to do with a bathroom subject line. A big offer then reappears further down for the decor event and storage. The bathroom story is diluted by the sofas and the multiple promotions.
- Compared with the other sends today, it's the same pattern as "Is your tween asking for a new room?". That was also a Design Desk editorial, and it also scored 6. Crate & Barrel is sending a lot today, and this one is content-first and offer-light. I'd call it a decent, brand-consistent send held back by length and a mismatched top banner.

## What's working

- The three-step "foundation, essentials, natural elements" structure gives the scroll a clear logic.
- Product tiles show star ratings and "Exclusive" and "Bestseller" tags, which add social proof and a reason to click.
- The bathroom photography is cohesive, warm and aspirational, and it matches the subject line.
- The "Shop All Bath" and "Book a Free Appointment" buttons are visible and sit near the hero.
- The Rewards Dollars callout ("Get 10% back in Rewards Dollars") adds a member benefit.

## What's weak

- The top banner promotes sofas and sectionals at up to 20% off, which contradicts the bathroom subject line.
- The email is extremely long (about 11,800 px), with several unrelated promo blocks after the bath content: decor event, storage event, registry-style picks and an app promo.
- There is no single clear offer for bathroom products. The discounts are for other categories.
- Nothing is time-bound, so there's no reason to click today.
- The two top CTAs, "Book a Free Appointment" and "Shop All Bath", compete with each other.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace the sofa banner with a bath-relevant offer, such as a towel or bath-accessory discount, and cut everything after the bath sections plus one closing CTA. This is the single biggest lift.
- 2. Pick one primary CTA. I'd use "Shop Bath Essentials" and demote the appointment booking to a text link.
- 3. Add a deadline to the offer, for example "this weekend only" or "ends Sunday".
- 4. Keep one secondary event module, not three.
- 5. Put the best-reviewed bath product in the hero with its price, so the click lands on a specific product.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email uses a standard table-based XHTML Transitional layout with `lang="en"` and VML/MSO fallbacks. The QA run found no hard failures, but 46 images have no alt text, and the unsubscribe and authentication headers could not be verified (57% pass rate, 3 warnings).

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 81 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are click-redirect domains, so the destinations are unverified. This is a coverage gap, not a confirmed defect.
- Five identical `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=dbe8da71…&n=1` to `n=5` pixels (a third-party LiveRamp-style sync) are in the body. Each carries an `s=` identifier and has no alt attribute.
- Additional open-tracking pixels are `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif?mi_u=…` and `https://dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/4549bcc2-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…`. Neither has alt text.
- The source contains an unfilled template placeholder: `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->`. It is harmless, but it points to a missed QA step.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (confirmed, 46 images):**
  - About 31 are content images on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/*.jpg|gif`, plus `.../m/47/bbf6f9d4-…png`.
  - One is the Scene7 image `20250117_CB_DesignDesk_Bath_Renderings_PI…`.
  - Seven are `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png` dynamic Movable Ink images.
  - Seven are tracking pixels.
  - Content images with no alt text give screen readers nothing, and with images blocked the email shows only blanks.
- `<meta name="viewport" content="…maximum-scale=1">` disables pinch-zoom on mobile, which is an accessibility problem.
- The global rule `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; line-height:100%}` and `html{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` prevent text scaling in WebKit clients.
- `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` has an unescaped `&`, which is invalid under the XHTML doctype. The title is also generic and does not describe the email.
- Minor: the `format-detection` meta is declared three times with overlapping values, and `.ReadMsgBody` and `.ExternalClass` are defined twice.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tokens (such as `%%…%%`, `{{…}}` or `[FIRST_NAME]`) appear in the visible source or QA output. This is limited to the truncated source.
- The `mi_u=` parameter on the Movable Ink image URLs is populated with a hash, which shows personalization resolved correctly.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058):** Both were not found. The QA tool says the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is unconfirmed. If the headers are really absent, that conflicts with Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements.
- **`Authentication-Results`:** Not found, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment are unknown. The sender domain `mail.crateandbarrel.com` matches the image host `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com`, which is consistent but not proof.
- The in-body unsubscribe link and physical address could not be checked because the source was truncated before the footer.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
No issues found that can be confirmed. Links go through tracking redirects (81 skipped), so UTM parameters and landing pages cannot be evaluated from the available data.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to all 39 content images, and `alt=""` to the 7 tracking pixels so assistive tech skips them.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta and drop `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none`.
3. Escape the title (`Crate &amp; Barrel`) and make it specific to the email.
4. Delete the leftover `IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT` comment.
5. Re-check the raw message headers directly. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` and `Authentication-Results` (SPF, DKIM and DMARC pass) before treating the compliance warnings as real.
6. Resolve a sample of the redirect links manually to confirm destinations and UTM tagging.
7. Review whether the five `rlcdn.com` pixels are needed. They add third-party requests and a privacy surface.
