# We’re giving away a $1,000 Gift Card! Learn more →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- This is a sweepstakes-lead email from Crate & Kids anchored on a $1,000 gift card giveaway, but the giveaway hook is almost immediately abandoned as the email pivots into a shopping digest: editorial lifestyle imagery, a "500+ new fall arrivals" promo, a curated product grid, a credit/loyalty card enrollment push ("enter the card you love and get golden"), an app download, and TheDesignDesk service. That's seven discrete messages in one scroll — none of them reinforcing each other. The giveaway subject line creates a specific expectation that the email body does not fulfil; the "prize" gets one hero block and then disappears.
- The bigger problem is cadence. This is the **third email from Crate & Kids today** — following the kids' room lighting guide and the collab reveal, both sent July 21. Even the best creative burns goodwill at that pace, and this send is not the best creative.
- The gift card angle had legs. A $1,000 sweepstakes is genuinely attention-grabbing, but it needed to own the entire email. Instead, it's buried under a shopping newsletter that has nothing to do with why the subscriber opened.

## What's working

- **Specific dollar amount in the subject** — "$1,000" is concrete and scannable; it earns the open even if the body doesn't deliver.
- **Fall arrivals section lands seasonally** — "500+ new fall arrivals, starting at $20" gives a clear reason to browse now.
- **Hero render is clean** — no broken images or layout issues visible in the screenshot; the email displays properly.
- **Product grid has breadth** — stroller, armchair, and storage items give different shopper types an entry point.

## What's weak

- **Seven CTAs, zero focus** — giveaway, dream room, fall arrivals, product recs, credit card, app download, design desk; the email can't decide what it wants the subscriber to do.
- **"Enter the card you love (and get golden)" is unexplained** — this section reads as a credit card enrollment pitch that appears without setup, and it actively competes with the giveaway framing in the subject.
- **Third email today from this brand** — the cadence alone suppresses open and click rates; no individual email can overcome that exhaustion signal.
- **Giveaway has no deadline or mechanics visible** — no "ends X date," no "enter here," no entry count or odds language. The sweepstakes feel like a hook, not a real offer.
- **"Learn more →" CTA copy is weak** — it describes the action, not the benefit. A $1,000 prize deserves a more compelling button.

## Recommendations

- 1. **One email, one job.** The giveaway is compelling — build the entire send around it. Entry mechanics, what you can buy with $1,000, past winner or community story. Cut the fall arrivals, app, and design desk to the footer or a separate send.
- 2. **Give the sweepstakes a deadline.** "Enter by July 28" changes the entire urgency posture and gives the subject line a real hook.
- 3. **Kill the credit card section or promote it separately.** The "enter the card" copy collides with the giveaway framing in a confusing way; a standalone credit card email earns better attribution.
- 4. **Rewrite the CTA from "Learn more" to "Enter to win →"** — verb + noun, action-oriented, matches the prize framing.
- 5. **Hold one of the three July 21 emails.** This send should have replaced the lighting guide or the collab email, not stacked on top of them.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issues found:**

| # | Issue | Evidence |
|---|-------|----------|
| 1 | **Viewport blocks user zoom** | `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` — `maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-zoom; WCAG 1.4.4 failure |
| 2 | **Conflicting desktop `img` max-width rules** | Two `@media` blocks both set `img { max-width }` — `min-width: 768px` sets `600px`, `min-width: 640px` sets `640px`. At 640–767px viewport width both fire; cascade order determines outcome, likely unintentionally |
| 3 | **`line-height: 100%` on universal selector** | `* { ... line-height: 100% }` — overrides inline `line-height` on any element, including buttons and headings. Known to collapse text in some clients |
| 4 | **`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `*`** | Forces no text scaling globally, not just on body. Prevents user accessibility overrides |
| 5 | **`showmobile` hardcodes `width:414px`** | `.showmobile { width:414px !important }` — device-specific pixel value; breaks on Plus/Pro/Max iPhone sizes and Android flagships |
| 6 | **No dark mode CSS** | `color-scheme: light` declared but no `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block. Will invert poorly in Apple Mail dark mode |
| 7 | **Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags** | `telephone=no`, `address=no`, and `date=no` each appear twice across two separate `<meta name="format-detection">` tags — redundant but harmless; indicates template sloppiness |
