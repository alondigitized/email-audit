# Our top 3 collections for their bedroom, playroom & more
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids' collection-showcase email — "top-rated collections for every kind of room" leads into three separate furniture lines (Bodie, Finn, Hampshire) each with its own moody room shot, GREENGUARD-certified badges, and product grid. It's a fundamentally different structure from the clearance/markdown sends I've seen from this brand this week (the 60%-off cascade, the bedroom-refresh sale) — this one is aspirational and browse-driven, not offer-driven, and there's no discount visible anywhere near the top three collections.
- The execution is clean — each collection has consistent styling (room photo, star ratings, certification badges, "Shop X" button) and the nursery/kids-room styling is on-persona. But there's no price anchor, no urgency, and no single hero CTA — by the time you hit "Shop All Furniture," you've already scrolled past three near-identical sales pitches. Then it kitchen-sinks further with a back-to-school banner, a "$500 gift card" sweepstakes pitch, and a "we love these for you" grid, diluting the top-3-collections premise the subject promised.
- Judgement: solid product photography and on-brand execution, but this is a mid-funnel browse email dressed up with too many bolted-on asks. Fine for a loyalty/nurture send, weak for driving urgency or a click today.

## What's working

- Each of the three collections (Bodie, Finn, Hampshire) has a clear, distinct lifestyle photo that sets context (room style, color palette).
- GREENGUARD Gold Certified badges and star ratings are visible on every product tile — real trust signals for a nursery-furniture buyer.
- Consistent "Shop [Collection]" buttons make the CTA per-section unambiguous.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, layout is consistent top to bottom.
- Room styling matches persona (nursery, kids bedroom, playroom) demographically appropriate.

## What's weak

- No discount, price, or offer of any kind near the hero or the three collections — this is pure browse content, weaker on the Business Impact "concrete offer" signal than the brand's clearance sends.
- Three back-to-back near-identical collection blocks (photo → bullets → grid → button) make the email extremely long with no differentiation in urgency or value between them.
- After the three collections, the email piles on unrelated asks — back-to-school banner, "we love these for you" grid, a sweepstakes plug for TheDesignDesk — none of which relate to "top 3 collections."
- No single clear hero CTA — by the fourth "Shop" button, visual hierarchy has flattened and nothing stands out as the one thing to click.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a price or intro-offer anchor to the hero ("starting at $X" or a collection-specific price break) — right now this is style-only inspiration, nothing pushes toward converting today.
- 2. Cut to one hero collection with a strong CTA, and demote the other two to a smaller "also new" module below — three full-length blocks dilutes the "top 3" promise into three separate mini-emails.
- 3. Remove or move the back-to-school banner, sweepstakes plug, and "we love these for you" grid to a separate send — they're unrelated to the subject line and stretch an already-long scroll.
- 4. Feature the "500+ new arrivals starting at $29" line higher — it's the only concrete price signal in the whole email and it's buried mid-scroll.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `GREENGUARD-certified furniture for every room, new arrivals from $29`
