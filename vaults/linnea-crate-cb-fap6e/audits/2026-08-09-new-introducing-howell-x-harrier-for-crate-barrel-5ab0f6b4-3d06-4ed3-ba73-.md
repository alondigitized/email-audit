# NEW! Introducing Howell x Harrier for Crate & Barrel
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-09**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's designer-collab launch — Howell x Harrier, a furniture and decor line from Tiffany Howell (Laure Home) and Leanne Ford. It's a genuine premium moment: a real "hero" collab email, not another catalog sweep. The styling is cohesive — checkerboard floors, warm neutrals, a consistent set design across every room shot — and it reads as editorial rather than promotional, which fits a designer collab.
- Where it falls apart is the same place every C&B send this week has: it never stops. After the collab intro (which alone would be a strong, disciplined email), it keeps rolling into "upgrade your morning routine" (espresso machines), a Le Creuset Dutch oven callout, a generic "we love these for you" furniture grid, and a credit card pitch. The collab — the actual news here — gets diluted by four unrelated appended modules by the time you reach the bottom.
- Compared to 2026-08-07's "Bestsellers for your nursery" (9.00/10, tight and disciplined) versus 2026-08-07's "hosting essentials" sweep (8.00, but a full-catalog stack), this lands in between: a strong hero concept let down by C&B's habitual bolt-on modules.

## What's working

- Clear, on-brand hero: "howell x harrier for Crate&Barrel" lockup with a single "Get Lost in the Look" CTA up top.
- Consistent styling across every collab room shot (checkerboard floor, warm wood tones, brass accents) — feels like a real photoshoot, not stock imagery.
- Designer quotes ("We gravitate to trends. Chic, always." —Tiffany / "This is what our fashion line would look like." —Leanne) add authenticity and a point of view.
- "shop the full collab, starting under $100" gives a concrete price anchor before the collection grid.
- Multiple well-placed "Shop the Collab" CTAs reinforce the primary action without being redundant within the collab section itself.

## What's weak

- The email doesn't end where the story ends — coffee makers, a Dutch oven deal, generic furniture picks, and a credit-card pitch are all bolted on after the collab, burying the actual launch under unrelated merchandising.
- No overall discount or launch incentive on the collab itself — "starting under $100" is a price point, not an offer, so there's nothing urgent pulling a click.
- Subject line ("NEW! Introducing Howell x Harrier for Crate & Barrel") leads with "NEW!" which reads as a stock template opener rather than the more evocative designer-collab language used inside.
- Very long single-column scroll (this screenshot alone spans ~12,000px) — by the time a reader hits the Le Creuset or credit card blocks, they've fully left the collab mindset.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email at the collab. Everything after "shop the full collab, starting under $100" (espresso machines, Dutch oven, "we love these for you," credit card) should be a separate send — it dilutes what is otherwise C&B's best creative concept this week.
- 2. Add a real hook to the collab itself — a launch-week perk (early access, free shipping, or a limited quantity note) since "starting under $100" alone isn't urgent.
- 3. Lead the subject with the designer names, not "NEW!" — subscribers who follow Leanne Ford or Laure Home will respond to the name recognition more than a generic "NEW!" flag.
- 4. Tighten the preheader to name a hero product (e.g. the Dutch wide-arm sofa or the credenza) instead of leaving it generic/absent.
