# Homepage snapshot · www.crateandbarrel.com · 2026-05-04
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-04**
## Executive summary

- I'm in full registry mode right now and Crate&Barrel is one of my top two stores, so I came in expecting to feel immediately seen — and what I got instead was a modal asking for my email *and* my phone number before I could even read the hero. Once I mentally closed that, the page is clearly pushing outdoor furniture hard for the season, which is fine aesthetically but basically zero relevance to why I'm here. The brand looks premium and confident, but right now it's talking to patio-shoppers, not me.
- **8/10**
- \+ Returning-shopper hook: "Earn 10% Back in Rewards Dollars" top strip — immediately relevant to me building a registry
- \+ Concrete offer above fold: modal "Save 10% off full-price items\*" and "40% off" outdoor strip both visible
- \+ Primary CTA above fold: "Shop All Outdoor" button visible behind the modal
- \+ No render bugs: clean layout, no broken images or placeholder text
- \+ Demographic signals match: aspirational, modern, minimal aesthetic — millennial-coded throughout
- \+ Current campaign/season: outdoor push in early May is correct and timely
- \+ Loyalty/membership: "Earn 10% Back" visible without any scrolling
- − Hero copy/imagery does not explicitly speak to my focus: outdoor patio furniture, not tabletop, dinnerware, or walnut indoor
- − Visual hierarchy: the modal covering the page collapses the hero experience before I can engage
- − Offer feels honest: asterisk on "10% off full-price items\*" with a required phone number field and dense fine print feels extractive

## What's working

- **"Earn 10% Back in Rewards Dollars" top strip**: First thing I see, and as a full-price-tolerant registry shopper accumulating rewards on a $2K+ purchase, this actually matters to me.
- **"Wedding Registry" and "Registry Shop" in utility nav**: These are exactly where I'd expect them, no hunting. This store knows who half its customer base is.
- **"Tabletop & Bar" in main nav**: One tap from where I actually want to be. The category names are specific enough to be useful.
- **Malibone outdoor photography**: Even if outdoor furniture isn't my immediate mission, the image quality here is stunning — warm terracotta, natural materials, full-bleed composure. It makes me trust the brand's taste.
- **"built-to-last bestsellers" section (partially visible)**: "Shop Sofa Collections" with that warm, walnut-adjacent imagery reads as the kind of long-term purchase mindset I have right now. Good signal.

## What's weak

- **The modal is the whole page**: I land and immediately have to deal with a two-required-fields form with a mobile phone number ask and a paragraph of legal text. The asterisk on the discount and the forced phone input make this feel like a privacy tax rather than a welcome.
- **The hero is completely wrong for me**: If CB knows I have a registry (and they do, I set it up here), there is no reason the hero is showing me patio cushion colors. Not one piece of dinnerware, not one walnut serving board, nothing from my wishlist category.
- **Zero registry personalization above the fold**: CB is a leading wedding registry destination. The hero could do so much more — "What your guests are buying right now" or "Most-registered tabletop pieces this month" would hit differently for someone at my exact life stage.
- **No shipping, returns, or trust signals visible**: I can't see a free-shipping threshold anywhere, no return policy mention, no review counts. For a full-price purchase on tabletop I need more reassurance than just good photography.

## Recommendations

- **Surface a registry-aware hero tile alongside the seasonal hero**: A split hero or secondary module — "Building your registry? Shop this season's most-wanted tabletop" — would speak directly to a massive and highly motivated segment without sacrificing the outdoor campaign.
- **Replace the intrusive full-screen modal with a bottom drawer or sticky banner**: "Get 10% off — enter your email" in a dismissible bottom bar converts nearly as well and doesn't hijack the experience before I've seen a single product. Drop the required phone field entirely on first touch.
- **Add free-shipping threshold to the rewards strip**: "Free shipping on orders $49+ · Earn 10% Back in Rewards Dollars" — putting both in that top bar answers two purchase-decision questions before I even hit the nav.
- **Build a "Trending on registries" or "Registry bestsellers" rail**: A social-proof module surfacing what newlyweds are actually adding would be magnetic to someone like me right now and costs almost nothing to implement editorially.
