# Not sure which sheets? Let us help.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-13**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids running an educational/consultative angle instead of a discount push — "find their perfect bedding fabric" with a four-way comparison (percale, gauze, washed cotton, jersey) rated on material, breathability, and feel. It's a genuinely different beat from the "up to X% off" sends that dominate this brand's recent history, and the fabric-guide structure is well-executed: each section has a star rating, three attribute callouts, and a real customer quote. The problem is there's no offer anywhere in this email — no price, no percent-off, no urgency — so while it's useful content, it gives almost nothing to click on urgently.
- The visual execution is clean and on-brand (soft pastels, real lifestyle photography, a genuinely cute kid-in-bed shot for the jersey section), but the email is extremely long — nine-plus scroll sections before the "bedding essentials collection" wrap-up — which is a lot to ask of an inbox skim. Compared to 2026-08-13's "Up to 25% off bedroom furniture, bedding & more" (7.00/10) which led with a single sale price, this one trades urgency for depth. It'll work for someone actively shopping for kids' sheets and undecided on fabric; it won't move an impulse click.

## What's working

- Clear organizing device: four fabric types, each scored on the same three attributes (material, breathability, feel) — genuinely helpful for a comparison-shopping parent.
- Real customer quotes under each fabric section add credibility ("these sheets are so soft and comfortable and versatile").
- Consistent "Shop All [X]" CTA pattern under every section keeps the ask simple even as content varies.
- Strong photography — the cozy cloud washed cotton section with the kid peeking out from under covers is the most emotionally engaging image in the email.
- Clean top banner sets expectation immediately: "find their perfect bedding fabric."

## What's weak

- Zero price or discount signal anywhere in the email — no "% off," no sale price, nothing time-bound. This is a pure content play with no urgency lever.
- Nine-plus stacked sections make this one of the longest sends in this brand's recent history — most readers won't scroll past section three or four.
- The "$29 finishing touches" and "fall 2026 lookbook" blocks at the very bottom feel bolted on, unrelated to the sheets-comparison narrative that structured everything above.
- Subject promises help choosing sheets, but the email reads more like a static buying guide than something that "helps" — no quiz, no "answer 3 questions" personalization hook.
- With four near-identical section templates in a row, visual fatigue sets in — the layout doesn't vary enough to sustain attention through the full length.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a price or bundle offer to at least one fabric tier (e.g., "organic cotton starting at $39") — content-only sends underperform on click without a concrete number to anchor to.
- 2. Cut the email to the top two fabric types plus a "see all fabrics" link — nine sections is too much scroll for a promotional email.
- 3. Turn the "find their perfect fabric" premise into an actual interactive quiz link in the hero, matching the subject's "let us help" promise literally.
- 4. Move the $29 finishing touches and fall lookbook content to a separate send — they dilute the fabric-comparison focus here.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Which sheets fit your kid? Take our 30-second quiz`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Percale, gauze, or jersey? Here's how to choose`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `4 organic cotton fabrics, compared by feel and breathability`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Real parent reviews on our softest sheet sets`
