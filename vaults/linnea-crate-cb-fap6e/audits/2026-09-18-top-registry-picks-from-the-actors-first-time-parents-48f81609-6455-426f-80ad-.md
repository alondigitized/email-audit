# Top registry picks from the actors & first-time parents →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids running a celebrity-registry content play — "Nicole Brydon Bloom & Justin Theroux's registry faves" — dressed up as a curated nursery tour rather than a straight sale. It's a smart hook for the persona (first-time parents love borrowing taste from people they recognize), and the quote-card format ("cozy for long feeds," "adds just the right whiff of whimsy") does real work making each product feel chosen rather than merchandised. The banner still leads with "up to 20% off the best baby essentials," so the discount isn't buried, just secondary to the story.
- Where it falls down is credibility and cadence. Justin Theroux and Nicole Brydon Bloom are not a couple as far as any public record shows — pairing two unrelated actors as "first-time parents" sharing one registry reads like a mail-merge error, not a genuine celebrity partnership, and that will cost trust with anyone who notices. It also lands one day after "The birthday shop is OPEN" and two days after two other Crate & Kids/Crate & Barrel sends in the same week — the cadence is getting heavy for the inbox. Net: creative concept is above average, execution has a credibility problem that undercuts it.

## What's working

- Clear masthead-to-hero flow: logo → offer banner → black-and-white lifestyle photo → celebrity hook, in that order, so the eye has an obvious path.
- The "TheBabyRegistry" partner lockup with the ampersand gives the collab visual weight without a busy design.
- Product cards pair a styled room photo with a first-person quote ("cozy for long feeds") and a specific product name (Seesaw Cream Boucle Nursery Chair) — that's concrete, not generic.
- Each section ends with its own "Shop Registry Picks" CTA, so scrolling readers always have a nearby next step.
- No visible render bugs — images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, layout holds together end to end.

## What's weak

- The central premise (two actors as a couple/co-parents) reads as implausible or mismatched — this is the single biggest trust risk in the email.
- The offer itself ("up to 20% off," with an asterisk) is vague — no specific percentage or category callout, so it's a weaker hook than the celebrity story around it.
- Design Desk cross-sell ("our designers can help you too — it's 100% free") interrupts the registry narrative rather than closing it, diluting the single message.
- This is the second registry/gifting-adjacent Crate & Kids email in three days (after "The birthday shop is OPEN," 2026-09-17) — fatigue risk for anyone getting both.
- "we love these for you" personalized product strip at the bottom is generic stroller/car-seat thumbnails with no styling or quotes, a visual step down from the curated cards above it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix or reframe the celebrity pairing — if this is two separate registries being cross-promoted, say so explicitly ("two takes on the nursery, from Nicole Brydon Bloom and separately, Justin Theroux") rather than implying a shared registry; if they are actually partners, nothing to fix, but as rendered it reads as a mismatch that a sharp subscriber will flag.
- 2. Put a real number in the hero band — swap "up to 20% off" for the actual top-line discount or a specific category ("20% off nursery furniture") to give the click a firmer reason.
- 3. Move the Design Desk block to the very end (it's already near the bottom) and cut it to one line + button so it doesn't compete with the registry story for the second CTA slot.
- 4. Add a one-line customer proof point under the hero (review count or rating) — the product cards have zero social proof, which is a missed trust signal for the highest-consideration items in the catalog (cribs, gliders).
