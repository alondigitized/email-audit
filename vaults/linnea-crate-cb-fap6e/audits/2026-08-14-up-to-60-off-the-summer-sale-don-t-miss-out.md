# Up to 60% off The Summer Sale! Don’t miss out →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-14**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's site-wide sale email, and it's a real departure from the recent run of catalog and single-product sends I've seen from this brand — "Up to 60% off" with a hero coastal image and a clean category grid (bed & bath, kitchen, rug, outdoor, decor, furniture clearance, tabletop clearance) is the most conversion-focused open I've seen from them in this batch. Compare to 2026-08-13's "The easiest update: new bedding!" which scored a 4 for having zero discount or urgency anywhere in 12,000px — this one leads with the number in the subject and repeats it as the first thing you see below the fold.
- Where it falls apart is everything after the sale grid. The email keeps going for what looks like another 10+ screens — a Levain bakery collab, a Howell x Harrier $1000 sweepstakes, The Design Desk consultation pitch, six "we love these for you" product cards, a credit card pitch, an app download pitch — none of which reinforces the sale. It reads like five separate campaigns got stapled together behind one subject line, which dilutes the one clear offer that got someone to open in the first place.
- Verdict: strong hook, sale-first hero and grid are well executed, but the email overstays its welcome and buries its own CTA under unrelated asks by the third scroll.

## What's working

- Hero image is clean, on-brand, and the "up to 60% off The Summer Sale" headline is legible against the coastal photo with no overlap or contrast issues.
- Category grid (bed & bath, kitchen, rug, outdoor, decor & lighting, furniture clearance, clearance, tabletop clearance) gives a clear self-select path immediately below the hero — better structured than the flat catalog scroll in prior sends.
- Two CTA buttons ("Shop Now," "Shop the Sale") appear early and are visually consistent — black pill buttons, white text.
- No render bugs: images loaded, text is crisp, no lorem ipsum or broken layout anywhere in the scroll.

## What's weak

- The sale offer is abandoned almost immediately — by the third section the email pivots to a bakery collab, then a sweepstakes, then a free design service, none of which mention the sale or a discount.
- No specific percentage is tied to any category — "up to 60%" in the hero is the only number in the entire email; a shopper can't tell if rugs are 60% off or 10% off until they click through.
- Six-item "we love these for you" grid shows full prices with no markdown, sale badge, or strikethrough — visually contradicts the "up to 60% off" promise right in the body.
- Email is extremely long for a sale send; the sale-specific content (hero + category grid) is compressed into the first ~15% of the scroll, everything after is competing, unrelated asks (credit card, app, design service, sweepstakes).

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email by 60-70%: hero, category grid, and one more "shop the sale" reinforcement, then stop. Move the bakery collab, sweepstakes, Design Desk, and credit card pitches to their own sends — this is what's diluting an otherwise strong open.
- 2. Put a discount percentage or badge on the "we love these for you" product cards, or drop that section entirely — full-price products directly under a 60%-off hero undercuts the offer's credibility.
- 3. Add urgency to the hero or subhead (an end date or "ends Sunday") — nothing in the visible email tells the reader when this sale closes, which costs the "credible deadline" signal on click-through.
- 4. Move the second CTA higher — right now "Shop Now" under the hero and "Shop the Sale" under the grid are close together; consolidate to one strong CTA immediately after the grid instead of stacking with all the unrelated sections that follow.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 60% off ends soon — shop the Summer Sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `60% off rugs, furniture & more (today only-ish)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Bed & bath, kitchen, outdoor, rugs, decor — all on sale now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Save up to 60% across the categories you shop most.`
