# Try these recipes with your summer farmers market finds →
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel going full editorial content mode — a recipe-inspiration send that bridges summer farmers market produce to their kitchen product catalog. The conceit is smart: corn, beets, and summer vegetables give them a reason to show cutting boards, salad bowls, Dutch ovens, and utensils without leading with a discount. The visual execution is clean and on-brand, with strong food photography that feels seasonally appropriate in mid-July.
- The problem is strategic: this is the fifth email in two days from the Crate/Crate & Kids family, following four sends on July 10 alone (free-shipping announcements, a lighting editorial, a kids' category deal). A content pivot after that cadence is actually the right call — but this email has no offer, no CTA that drives to a specific product page with urgency, and no loyalty hook. It reads as a brand awareness play dressed up as a content send.
- The bottom line: well-shot, seasonally timed, and a relief after yesterday's promo barrage — but it has almost nothing to make a reader click beyond curiosity. For a brand that just hit you with four emails in one day, "here are some recipes" is a weak close.

## What's working

- **Seasonally sharp.** Farmers market, corn, beets, summer salads — the timing is tight and the visual content earns the July 11 date.
- **Strong food photography.** The hero taco/corn image and the colorful salad spread are genuinely appetizing; they don't look like stock.
- **Content cadence break.** After four promo emails on 7/10, shifting to editorial is the right instinct — it reduces list fatigue pressure.
- **Product integration is organic.** Kitchen tools and cookware appear to be embedded alongside recipes rather than bolted on at the bottom, which is the right structure for a content email.

## What's weak

- **No offer anywhere visible.** No percentage off, no free shipping threshold, no members pricing — a pure content email with zero commercial hook.
- **No urgency.** Nothing time-bounds the content or the products; there's no reason to act now vs. next week.
- **Cadence context kills it.** Arriving the morning after four emails from the same family, even a well-executed content send feels like inbox noise. The arrow-in-subject-line motif ("→") is now the third straight C&B subject to use it.
- **Subject is too long for mobile.** At ~57 characters including the arrow, it will truncate on most phone lock screens before "finds" appears.
- **No social proof or reviews.** For a recipe send that's implicitly selling kitchen gear, a single "4.8 stars, 2,400 reviews" callout on a hero product would bridge content to commerce meaningfully.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one product CTA with a real offer.** Even "Free shipping on the Dutch oven in today's recipe" turns this from a content dead-end into a revenue play. Right now there's nothing to click that creates urgency.
- 2. **Trim the subject under 50 characters.** The arrow suffix is a C&B signature but costs characters on mobile. Cut it or shorten the body copy.
- 3. **Tie one recipe to a specific, linked product bundle.** "Make this corn salad → see the bowl, the server, the tongs" with a direct link to a curated collection is more compelling than generic product rows.
- 4. **Throttle the cadence or merge this with the next send.** Five emails in two days from the same sender family is the real problem no creative fix solves.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CONFIRMED] Conflicting image max-width media queries will produce incorrect sizing at 640–767px viewports:**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
```
At 640–767px, the 640px rule fires; at 768px+, it is overridden to 600px — meaning images *shrink* as the viewport widens. The 600px rule looks like it was meant to constrain the container, not imgs, or the breakpoints are inverted.

**[CONFIRMED] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta blocks zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users with low vision from pinch-zooming. Should be removed or set to `maximum-scale=5`.

**[CONFIRMED] Global `*` rule applies `text-size-adjust: none` and `line-height: 100%` to all elements:**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; max-height:1000000px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly; line-height: 100%}
```
`line-height: 100%` on the universal selector can cause text overlap in multiline spans; `text-size-adjust: none` suppresses the browser's mobile readability correction more broadly than intended (should be scoped to `body` or container tables only).

**[CONFIRMED] Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags — three redundant declarations:**
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">      <!-- duplicate -->
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">   <!-- duplicate -->
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no"> <!-- duplicate -->
```
Harmless but adds unnecessary weight and signals sloppy template hygiene.
