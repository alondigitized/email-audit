# ON NOW! Up to 60% off outdoor!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's outdoor sale email — "up to 60% off outdoor" — dropping a day after July 17's nearly-identical "Summer's heating up! Shop up to 60% off →" send. The creative is clean and on-brand: a moody hero shot of dark outdoor dining furniture anchors the headline offer, a grill sweepstakes block adds a second engagement mechanic, and a light-furniture lifestyle section rounds out the scroll. As a standalone email, it works well. As the second "60% off" email in under 24 hours from the same brand, it suffers from serious cadence fatigue — the moment a subscriber sees it, their gut says "this again."
- The subject "ON NOW! Up to 60% off outdoor!" is functionally fine but the all-caps opener is noise, not urgency. The outdoor specificity is a genuine improvement over yesterday's category-omnibus send, but that advantage evaporates when the inbox shows two virtually identical 60%-off claims side by side.
- The email itself is well-executed. The cadence is the problem.

## What's working

- **Hero visual is immediate and on-brief** — dark teak/metal dining set reads "outdoor" at a glance; no ambiguity about what's on sale.
- **Outdoor specificity helps** — unlike yesterday's broad summer sale, this email is scoped to outdoor category, which focuses the reader's intent.
- **Sweepstakes block adds a secondary hook** — the grill/BBQ imagery and giveaway mechanic gives non-shoppers a reason to click even if they're not ready to buy.
- **Clean render, no visible layout breaks** — multiple sections stack neatly with consistent type treatment and button styling throughout.
- **Multiple outdoor price points implied** — from dining sets to what appears to be outdoor seating, the breadth gives multiple entry points without fragmenting the theme.

## What's weak

- **Cadence collapse** — this is the second "up to 60% off" email in one day from Crate & Barrel; subscribers who opened yesterday have learned this offer has no real deadline, which is the fastest way to train inbox blindness.
- **ALL CAPS "ON NOW!" is inbox spam-signal energy** — the urgency is performative; there's no actual end date visible anywhere in the email to justify the alarm.
- **The sweepstakes block dilutes purchase intent** — entering a grill giveaway and buying outdoor furniture at 60% off are different mental modes; forcing both into one email risks neither converting.
- **"Up to 60% off" lacks a specific anchor** — no hero product with a before/after price, no "was $599, now $239" moment to make the discount feel real and tangible.
- **No deadline visible** — "ON NOW!" implies time pressure but no end date or clock appears anywhere; without it, the urgency reads as a generic campaign header.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Set a hard deadline and show it** — add "Ends Sunday" or a countdown to make "ON NOW!" mean something. Without a date, subscribers learn to wait because nothing ever actually expires.
- 2. **Anchor with one hero product at a specific discount** — lead with a single aspirational piece (e.g., a dining set, "Was $1,299 → $649") before opening to the full category. A specific number builds credibility that "up to 60%" can't.
- 3. **Separate the sweepstakes from the sale** — either give the giveaway its own email (it earned one — see the well-received 7/17 $200 sweepstakes treatment) or relegate it to a slim footer mention so it doesn't split the email's conversion focus.
- 4. **Kill the ALL CAPS subject opener** — urgency should come from the offer structure, not punctuation style.
- 5. **Respect the 24-hour gap** — if a 60%-off outdoor sale needs two sends, make the second a "last chance" with explicit justification, not a clone.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus enter to win a Napoleon grill — no purchase necessary.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sale ends Sunday. Shop outdoor dining, seating & more.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Conflicting `text-size-adjust` rules:**
- `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` (universal selector, first `<style>` block)
- `body{-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%}` (second `<style>` block, also in `html` rule)
- Universal `none` will win in most parsers due to specificity tie-breaking on cascade order, suppressing user-agent text scaling in iOS Mail.

**Competing desktop image max-width breakpoints:**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
At ≥768px, both rules match. The 768px rule is declared last and wins, capping images at 600px on desktop — contradicting the 640px container expectation set elsewhere. If the email container is 600px, this is harmless; if 640px, images will be clipped.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Disables user zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 violation for subscribers who rely on pinch-to-zoom.

**`line-height: 100%` on `*`:**
Universal `line-height: 100%` applied to all elements. This is a known cause of clipped descenders and tight line spacing in Gmail and Apple Mail, particularly for multi-line text blocks.

**Duplicate CSS rule blocks:**
The following rules are declared in two separate `<style>` blocks, which is redundant and increases payload size:
- `.ReadMsgBody { width: 100%; }` — appears twice
- `.ExternalClass * { line-height: 100%; }` — appears twice
- `body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; margin: 0; }` — appears twice
- `table td { border-collapse: collapse; }` — appears three times

**Missing preheader text:** Not visible in provided source. If absent from the body, inbox preview will render the first visible HTML content (often navigation or legal copy) as the preheader.
