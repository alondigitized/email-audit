# Can’t wait to get home
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-27**
## Executive summary

- This is the same kitchen-sink playbook flagged repeatedly in this brand's recent sends (7/25, 7/26 x3), and it's arguably the most stacked yet: hero → living room → entryway → dining → kids → free design service → a Lupita Nyong'o home-tour feature → a $5,000 sweepstakes → a six-item product grid → a credit card pitch → an app download push, all in one scroll. The subject, "Can't wait to get home," is a warm, editorial line that promises a mood, not an offer — and then the email opens with a thin "up to 60% off The Summer Sale*" ribbon that has nothing to do with that mood, asterisk and all.
- The execution quality is genuinely clean — no broken images, no overlapping text, consistent black-and-white type system, real prices on every product tile ($749–$2,499). But there's no single story here; it's nine campaigns wearing one "from" line. A reader who opens for "can't wait to get home" gets a sale banner, then a design-service upsell, then a celebrity home tour, then a sweepstakes, then a credit card ad — the emotional hook from the subject line is abandoned by the second screen.
- Verdict: well-produced, poorly edited. This needs to be split into 2-3 focused sends rather than one everything-email.

## What's working

- Zero render bugs — clean typography, consistent black pill CTAs (mostly), sharp product photography throughout.
- Product grid ("we love these for you") shows real prices ($1,799–$2,499) and specific named SKUs (Basque dining table, Anneli king bed) — genuine product detail, not vague teasers.
- The sweepstakes has a real, credible deadline ("book an appointment... now through August 3") — the only section in the whole email with concrete urgency.
- CTA copy is consistently specific across sections: "Shop Living Room," "Shop Entryway," "Shop Dining Room" — verb + destination, not generic "Discover."

## What's weak

- Subject promises a warm homecoming feeling; the actual email leads with an unrelated "up to 60% off" sale ribbon, then pivots through eight more unrelated campaigns — no coherent thread.
- The main "60% off" offer carries an asterisk with no visible fine print in view — a soft honesty ding per this brand's recurring pattern.
- Nine distinct campaigns (rooms, design service, celebrity feature, sweepstakes, credit card, app) in one send dilutes every individual CTA; nothing gets to be the hero.
- No reviews or social proof anywhere in the render, despite six named products with prices — an easy trust signal left on the table.
- CTA button color is inconsistent: black pills everywhere except the sweepstakes section, which switches to olive green — a small but visible break in the design system.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split this into two or three sends.** The design-service/Lupita/sweepstakes content is strong enough to be its own email; the room-by-room shop content is strong enough to be its own; bolting the credit card and app pitch onto both is unnecessary. This is the single highest-leverage fix — it's what every recent audit of this brand has flagged.
- 2. **Make the subject match what's actually first in the body.** Either lead the email with the "can't wait to get home" room content and drop the sale ribbon, or change the subject to reference the sale so the open and the first screen agree.
- 3. **Resolve or remove the asterisk on "up to 60% off."** If exclusions apply, at least gesture at them ("select styles") rather than leaving a bare asterisk with no visible resolution.
- 4. **Add a star rating or review count to at least the top 2 product tiles** — cheap trust signal that's currently absent everywhere.
- 5. **Standardize the CTA button color** — the olive-green sweepstakes button breaks the black-pill pattern used everywhere else.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 60% off — welcome yourself home`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your living room, entryway & more: up to 60% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: free design help and a shot at $5,000`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New arrivals for every room, now on sale`
