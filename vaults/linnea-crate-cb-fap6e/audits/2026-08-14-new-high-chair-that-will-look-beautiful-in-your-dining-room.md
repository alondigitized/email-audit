# New high chair that will look beautiful in your dining room
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-14**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids running a single-product launch for the Stokke Tripp Trapp high chair in a new "olive green" colorway — a genuinely premium, well-branded hero moment. The subject line ("New high chair that will look beautiful in your dining room") undersells what's actually a strong, specific hook — "new & exclusive: Stokke Tripp Trapp in olive green" — buried in the hero image instead of the subject text.
- The top third is excellent: co-branded Stokke lockup, lifestyle photo of a toddler in the chair, clear "Shop Now" CTA, followed by a legitimate brand-education block (50+ years, 100,000+ reviews, adjustable cushion). But after that strong open, the email sprawls into eight more unrelated modules — other Stokke gear, fall arrivals, a sweepstakes, a credit card pitch, an app plug, a store locator — which dilutes the single-product focus that made the top so effective. Compared to 2026-08-13's "Up to 25% off bedroom furniture" send (7/10, disciplined single-offer hero), this one has a stronger visual hero but far worse follow-through.
- Judgement: strong hero creative undercut by subject-line mismatch and an overloaded, unfocused body.

## What's working

- Hero photography is premium and on-brand — real lifestyle shot of a baby in the chair, olive green colorway clearly visible.
- "new & exclusive" framing plus the Stokke co-brand lockup signals genuine product news, not a generic promo.
- Trust-building copy block (50+ years, 100,000+ reviews, adjustable growth) gives real social proof directly under the hero.
- Primary CTA ("Shop Now") appears twice in the first screen, unambiguous and easy to tap.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text is legible, layout holds together end to end.

## What's weak

- Subject line completely omits the actual product name and the "olive green" / exclusive angle that's the whole story — a huge missed specificity opportunity.
- The single-product story dies fast: by module 4 you're into "even more from Stokke," then fall arrivals, a $5000 sweepstakes, a credit card offer, and an app pitch — nine distinct asks in one email.
- No price or discount anywhere — for a premium high chair, even "starting at $XXX" would help set expectations before the click.
- The sweepstakes and credit-card blocks compete visually with the hero product for attention despite being unrelated to the send's stated purpose.

## Recommendations

- 1. Rewrite the subject to surface the actual news: name the product and the exclusive colorway instead of generic dining-room language.
- 2. Cut the email after the Tripp Trapp + "even more from Stokke" modules; move fall arrivals, sweepstakes, credit card, and app promo to a separate send or a footer-only mention.
- 3. Add a price point near the hero CTA — premium high chairs invite sticker shock, so setting it early builds trust.
- 4. Tighten preview text to name the color/exclusivity so subject + preview aren't doing the same generic job.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New & exclusive: Tripp Trapp in Olive Green`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Stokke's iconic high chair, now in olive green`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The adjustable chair that grows with your child — first look at the new colorway`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `100,000+ 5-star reviews. Now in an exclusive new shade.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **29 images missing `alt` text** per QA scan, spanning both content images (`image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/...`) and tracking pixels. Content images without alt text fail WCAG 1.1.1 and degrade the experience for screen-reader users and image-blocked clients (default in Outlook/many corporate mail clients).
- Template includes standard multi-client resets (Outlook MSO conditionals, `ExternalClass` fixes for Outlook.com, `-webkit-text-size-adjust` for iOS auto-resize prevention, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`) — indicates reasonably mature cross-client hardening.
- `color-scheme`/`supported-color-schemes` both set to `light` only — no dark-mode-specific handling declared, which can cause inverted/washed-out rendering in clients that auto-dark-mode HTML email (e.g., Outlook.com, some Gmail dark mode heuristics) despite the explicit opt-out.
