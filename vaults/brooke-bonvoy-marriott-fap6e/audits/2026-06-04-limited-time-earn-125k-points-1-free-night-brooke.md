# Limited-Time: Earn 125K Points + 1 Free Night, Brooke
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- Ten days after Marriott failed to convert Brooke on the 100K Bonus Points credit card pitch (May 25), they're back with a sweetened offer: 125K points plus 1 free night added to the headline. The escalation is transparent — this is a textbook "didn't click? here's 25% more" retry — but the execution is strong enough that it doesn't feel like a copy-paste resend. The offer hierarchy is clean, the $95 annual fee is disclosed in the hero (not buried), and the Silver Elite status angle adds a second conversion hook for status-minded members.
- The email is long: four distinct sections before the footer cycling through the card offer, status benefits, annual perks, and then two alternative cards. Most conversions will happen in the first viewport, and Marriott correctly front-loads the best offer there. The trailing "no annual fee" card option late in the scroll, however, undercuts the premium pitch by giving price-sensitive readers an easy off-ramp before they've committed.
- Verdict: the best-executed send in Brooke's Bonvoy drip to date. The escalated offer, honest fee disclosure, and consistent CTAs all do their jobs. Length and a vague urgency deadline are the only meaningful liabilities.

## What's working

- **Offer escalation is legible.** 125K vs. the prior 100K, with "1 Free Night" added to the headline, signals a real upgrade — not a resend.
- **Fee disclosed in the hero.** "$95 annual fee" sits immediately below the headline. That's rare honesty and reduces perceived bait-and-switch risk.
- **Multiple CTAs, not spammy.** "APPLY NOW" appears at least three times, but each is anchored to new content (card offer, status tier, annual perks) — not naked repetition.
- **Silver Elite status as secondary hook.** "Get Silver Elite Status Upon Approval" targets a different conversion motive (status vs. points), broadening the pitch without doubling the length.
- **Alternative card options at bottom.** Showing the no-annual-fee and business card keeps non-converters in the funnel with a lower-commitment offer — smart funnel design even if the placement is late.

## What's weak

- **No hard deadline in the body.** "Limited-Time" is in the subject, but no visible expiration date appears inside the email — urgency is stated, not demonstrated.
- **"APPLY NOW" CTA is generic.** For a $95 card with a big bonus, benefit-first copy ("Claim 125K Points") would convert better than an application-form prompt.
- **Email is very long.** Four major sections require significant scrolling; most users in a real inbox will never reach the business card offer.
- **The no-annual-fee card late in the scroll undermines the hero.** It implicitly tells price-sensitive readers to skip the $95 card and take the free version — a separate non-converter flow would capture them without cannibalizing the premium CTA.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard expiration date next to the first CTA.** "Offer ends June 30" kills the "I'll think about it" deferral. "Limited-Time" in a subject line is wallpaper by now; a date inside the body is real urgency.
- 2. **Rewrite the primary CTA to "Claim Your 125K Points."** Mirrors the reader's actual reward, not the process of applying.
- 3. **Move the no-annual-fee card to a separate non-converter drip.** Reserve it for a day-3-after-non-click email; don't let it dilute the premium pitch.
- 4. **Trim to hero + status + 3 key perks.** Collapse or cut the "Explore Annual Boundless Benefits" icon grid — it adds length without driving the one action this email wants.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Brooke, your 125K points offer expires soon`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New offer: 125K points + a free night, just for you`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Earn more than ever — plus Silver Elite status the day you're approved`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Our highest welcome offer yet. Here's what you get.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Mixed HTTP/HTTPS on font sources (blocker in secure contexts)**

Two `@font-face` declarations use plain HTTP while all others use HTTPS:

```
url('http://cache.marriott.com/aka-fonts/MarriottDigital/AldineforMarriott-Roman.woff')
url('http://cache.marriott.com/aka-fonts/MarriottDigital/AldineforMarriott-Italic.woff')
```

Secure webmail clients (Gmail via HTTPS, Outlook.com) will block these as mixed content, silently falling back to `Times New Roman`. All other font variants on the same host correctly use HTTPS.

**Invalid CSS font format identifiers**

`format('otf')` is not a recognized CSS `@font-face` format string — the spec value is `format('opentype')`. Appears in at least four declarations (Aldine-Light, Aldine-Light-Italic, Swiss-Light, Swiss-Regular). Most browsers tolerate this but it is non-conformant and may cause silent load failures in stricter parsers.

**SVG font format included**

`format('svg')` is present in all multi-format stacks. SVG fonts are deprecated (removed from Chrome/Firefox, never supported in Edge/IE11+). Harmless but adds unnecessary payload.

**Outlook DPI / VML block**

`<o:PixelsPerInch>96</o:PixelsPerInch>` present — correct fix for Outlook 120 DPI scaling. No issues.

**`format-detection` meta**

`telephone=no, date=no, address=no, email=no` — correctly suppresses iOS auto-linking. No issues.
