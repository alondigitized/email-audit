# Brooke, Imagine Where 125K Points + 1 Free Night Can Take You
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Marriott's second bite at the exact same apple. June 4 delivered "Limited-Time: Earn 125K Points + 1 Free Night, Brooke" and failed to convert. June 12 brought two lifestyle pivots — Tours & Activities and the "Wild Side" discover email — which felt like a deliberate retreat from the hard sell. Now, 11 days after the first failed conversion attempt and just three days after the lifestyle pivot, Marriott is back with the identical offer at the identical price. No escalation. No expiration date. Just a reframed subject.
- The email itself is technically competent but chronically long. Four distinct content blocks — Silver Elite status, the Boundless Benefits icon grid, a repeated CTA, and a Q&A section — all fight for attention below the fold. The hero is clean and the offer is undeniably specific, but a subscriber who skipped this on June 4 is not re-engaging because the subject swapped "Limited-Time" for "Imagine."
- The subject line shift is the tell. Moving from urgency ("Limited-Time") to aspiration ("Imagine Where") is a defensible A/B strategy — but only if the body delivers a new emotional hook. It doesn't. Aspirational framing requires aspirational content: destination imagery, a story, a stat. What Brooke gets instead is the same card benefits breakdown she already declined.

## What's working

- **Offer specificity is airtight**: 125K Points + 1 Free Night appears in the hero, repeated in a second CTA block, and is never buried — a skimmer can absorb the proposition in under two seconds.
- **Annual fee is disclosed in the hero**: "$95 annual fee" appears alongside the bonus, which signals honesty and prevents the bait-and-switch feeling that kills trust on financial product emails.
- **Loyalty benefits are fully enumerated**: Silver Elite Status, 15 Elite Night Credits, Free Night Award, and the full Boundless Benefits suite are all present — someone seriously evaluating the card has every signal they need.
- **"APPLY NOW" button is unambiguous**: Clear verb, no hedging, no "Discover More" vagueness.
- **No render bugs**: The email renders cleanly with no broken images, overlapping text, or layout failures visible.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer evolution from June 4**: Same 125K, same 1 Free Night, same $95 fee. A non-converter has been handed the same ask with no new reason to act.
- **Urgency was dropped with nothing replacing it**: June 4 had "Limited-Time" in the subject. This email has "Imagine." The aspiration frame requires a destination or a story to work — instead, the body is a benefits table.
- **The lifestyle interlude makes this feel like a bait-and-switch**: Two warmth-building lifestyle emails followed immediately by an identical card pitch signals that the June 12 emails were filler, not strategy.
- **Email is too long for a second-touch conversion attempt**: Repeating the full Silver Elite and Annual Benefits sections when Brooke already saw this content on June 4 adds friction, not persuasion.
- **No deadline anywhere in the email body**: Without a credible expiration — even a soft one — there is no mechanism to force a decision now rather than never.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Give her a reason June 4 didn't**: Add a statement credit, a limited-time application window ("Apply by June 22"), or one specific destination peg ("Use your free night at one of these 10 summer properties"). Without a new hook, this is noise.
- 2. **Cut the email by half**: Keep the hero offer block and the Silver Elite status section. Drop the full Boundless Benefits icon grid and the Q&A. A non-converter is not re-converting because of a feature checklist.
- 3. **Commit to the aspirational frame or abandon it**: "Imagine" in the subject demands destination content in the body — a hero image of where 125K points takes you, a property callout, a mileage-equivalent. If the team won't rebuild the body, use the urgency subject instead.
- 4. **Add one social proof line**: "2M+ Bonvoy cardholders earned their free night last year" or similar. Social proof is missing entirely and is the single cheapest click-lift available.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Mixed-content HTTP font URLs (confirmed issue):**

Two `@font-face` `src` declarations use `http://` rather than `https://`:

```
url('http://cache.marriott.com/aka-fonts/MarriottDigital/AldineforMarriott-Roman.woff')
url('http://cache.marriott.com/aka-fonts/MarriottDigital/AldineforMarriott-Italic.woff')
```

All other font URLs in the same file correctly use `https://`. Gmail webmail and Outlook.com block mixed-content resource loads, so `Aldine-Regular` and `Aldine-Regular-Italic` will silently fall back to `Times New Roman` in those clients. The `.woff2` variants immediately above each declaration are HTTPS and would load first in supported clients, but the `.woff` fallback (targeted at older clients) is broken.

**Non-standard `format()` hint (confirmed issue):**

Six `@font-face` blocks use `format('otf')` — this is not a valid CSS font format keyword. The correct value is `format('opentype')`. Most clients ignore the hint and load by MIME type, but it may cause silent skip in strict parsers.

Affected declarations: `Aldine-Light`, `Aldine-Light-Italic`, `Swiss-Light`, `Swiss-Regular` (all OTF entries).

**SVG font format (low severity):**

`format('svg')` is included in four `@font-face` blocks. SVG fonts have had no meaningful client support since ~2016 and add unnecessary payload. No functional rendering impact.

**Outlook DPI fix:** Correctly implemented — `<o:PixelsPerInch>96</o:PixelsPerInch>` present.

**`x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`:** Present. ✓

**`format-detection`:** Disables telephone, date, address, and email auto-linking. ✓
