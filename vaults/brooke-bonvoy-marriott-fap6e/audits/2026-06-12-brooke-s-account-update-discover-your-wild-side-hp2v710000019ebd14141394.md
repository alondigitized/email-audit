# Brooke's Account Update: Discover Your Wild Side
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- This is a content-rich lifestyle send that continues the strategic pivot Marriott began on June 12 with the Tours & Activities email — moving away from the credit card hard-sell that dominated May 25 through June 4. The hero is the strongest visual Marriott has put in front of Brooke since her welcome: a red-rock desert landscape paired with "Find Your Natural Habitat" is evocative, on-brand for summer, and immediately signals adventure. That's the email's peak.
- The subject line is doing two incompatible jobs. "Account Update" is a functional hook that earns opens precisely because people feel obligated to read account notifications. "Discover Your Wild Side" is a lifestyle campaign tagline. Bolted together, they read as if a transactional trigger and a marketing copy brief were merged at the last minute without resolving which one this email actually is. One subverts the other.
- More critically: if this landed the same day as the June 12 Tours & Activities send, Marriott has emailed Brooke twice in one calendar day after already hitting her seven times since May 17. That's a frequency problem no amount of good hero photography overcomes. The email is also long to the point of unfocused — account news, dining content, a partner offer, an automotive section, an editorial magazine block, and a full hotel brand logo grid all compete for a single click. The result is a well-produced email that asks for everything and makes it easy to do nothing.

## What's working

- The red-rock desert hero is visually arresting and perfectly matched to summer adventure travel — best visual in the Marriott series so far.
- "Find Your Natural Habitat" is a clean, memorable headline that earns the lifestyle positioning.
- The "Your Account Just Got a Makeover" section adds genuine subscriber utility — one legitimately useful reason to open beyond a promotion.
- Clean render: no broken images, no layout breaks, no visible placeholder text.
- Personalization in the subject line ("Brooke's") gives the account update framing an air of necessity.

## What's weak

- Subject line tonal collision: "Account Update" (transactional) and "Discover Your Wild Side" (lifestyle) cancel each other's intent.
- No concrete offer anywhere visible in the email — no %, no $, no BOGO — which makes it hard to motivate a click after five promotional emails primed Brooke to expect a deal.
- The dining/food section ("Don't Forget to Pack Your Palate") is a thematic non sequitur inside an outdoor adventure email.
- The full hotel brand logo grid at the bottom adds substantial scrolling length with zero conversion value.
- If this is the second Marriott email on June 12 (same date as Tours & Activities), frequency is excessive and erodes the good work that send did to re-engage Brooke.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Resolve the subject line identity crisis** — commit to either an account notification (clean, functional) or a campaign email. Mixing both signals "I don't know why I'm emailing you" and trains Brooke to distrust future "Account Update" subjects.
- 2. **Add one concrete summer offer** — even 500 bonus points on outdoor resort stays this summer gives the lifestyle content a conversion hook. Content without offer is brand spend, not revenue.
- 3. **Cut to three sections max** — hero + offer + one editorial. Drop the automotive block and the dining section; they belong in separate sends.
- 4. **Kill the hotel brand logo grid** — it adds scroll weight and is never what converts a Bonvoy member. Reserve footer real estate for the actual loyalty benefits reminder.
- 5. **Don't batch with same-day sends** — separate the account update into a transactional trigger, send the lifestyle campaign separately.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Brooke, your account has a new look — plus summer adventure picks`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Find your natural habitat this summer, Brooke`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your Bonvoy dashboard got an upgrade. Here's what's new — plus the outdoor stays worth booking now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Red rocks, open trails, and a refreshed account view waiting for you.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

**Mixed HTTP/HTTPS font references** — two `@font-face` declarations load `.woff` fallback files over plain HTTP:
```
http://cache.marriott.com/aka-fonts/MarriottDigital/AldineforMarriott-Roman.woff
http://cache.marriott.com/aka-fonts/MarriottDigital/AldineforMarriott-Italic.woff
```
Gmail and Outlook.com will block these under mixed-content rules. The `.woff2` variants are HTTPS and will load first, so visual impact is low, but these HTTP references should be upgraded.

**Duplicate `@font-face` for `Aldine-Light`** — two declarations share the same `font-family: 'Aldine-Light'` name with no distinguishing `font-weight` or `font-style`:
```css
/* Declaration 1: tt0804m.woff / tt0804m.woff2 */
/* Declaration 2: tt0805m.woff / tt0805m.woff2 */
```
The second silently overrides the first. Likely intended to declare a bold or italic variant — the missing descriptor means one weight/style is unreachable.

**CSS hover transitions won't render in email** — `.img-hover-zoom img { transition: transform 1s ease; }` and `.img-hover-zoom:hover img { transform: scale(1.1); }` are inert in every major email client. Not a breakage, but dead code in this context.

**`section { display: inline !important; }`** — this global reset will affect any `<section>` elements in the rendered DOM and could conflict with client-injected markup. Low risk but non-standard.

**Developer comment left in production HTML:**
```html
<!--UPDATED 11/12/25-->
```
Discloses internal update cadence and template date. Should be stripped before send.

**Typekit dependency** — `Good-Karma` font relies on `https://use.typekit.net/yft3kyd.css` loading successfully. Typekit stylesheets are blocked by most email clients; the declared fallback (`'Brush Script MT', serif`) will be used instead in practice. This is expected behavior, but if the Typekit kit ID (`yft3kyd`) expires or is misconfigured, the link request will 404 silently.
