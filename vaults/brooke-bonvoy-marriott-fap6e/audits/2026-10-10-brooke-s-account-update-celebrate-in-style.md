# Brooke's Account Update: Celebrate in Style
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-10**
## Executive summary

- This is a Marriott Bonvoy newsletter-style catch-all. The subject says "Account Update," but the body is a long scroll of unrelated modules. A destination hero ("A Destination for Every Celebration") is followed by a 4,500-bonus-point stay offer. After that come three "Offers Made to Delight" cards (Vacations by Marriott, Aloha 75K points, a resort getaway with 5,000 daily points) and an NFL Super Bowl LXI sweepstakes. The rest is MGM Collection, Homes & Villas, and a Traveler magazine block. The member bar at the top shows "0 points" and "0 nights this year." That bar is the most honest part of the email, and it also shows how little this recipient has engaged.
- The subject does a lot of the damage. "Brooke's Account Update" suggests a statement or a transactional message. It then pivots to "Celebrate in Style," which has no offer or product in it. The body doesn't match the promise. There is no real account update beyond the member bar, and the "celebration" theme is a loose holiday frame around about eight separate pitches.
- My judgement is a competent, polished, but unfocused send. The best offer (75K bonus points for Aloha stays, book by Nov 23) sits in the middle of the page, and the hero pushes a generic "Book Now." Compared with the 9/30 "Act Fast to Get up to 55% Bonus Points" send, which had one clear offer, this one buries the value.

## What's working

- The personalized member bar shows the recipient's name, tier, points and nights, which makes the email feel account-aware.
- The hero is a warm, aspirational beach image that sets the celebration tone immediately.
- The bonus-points offers have concrete numbers and deadlines (4,500 points through Nov 22, 75K points for stays booked by Nov 23 and completed by Dec 30, 5,000 daily points).
- The layout is clean, with alternating image and text blocks and no broken images or overlapping text.
- The Super Bowl LXI sweepstakes adds a distinct, timely hook for sports fans.

## What's weak

- The subject line promises an "Account Update" that the body never delivers, and "Celebrate in Style" is vague.
- Too many competing modules (about 8–10 CTAs) dilute focus, and the page is very long.
- The hero CTA is a generic "BOOK NOW" with no offer attached, while the real points offers sit below the fold.
- The legal and sweepstakes fine print at the bottom is dense and long. It undercuts the honest, clean feel of the top.
- The email is mostly lifestyle imagery. Nothing in it speaks to the recipient's zero-points, zero-nights status, such as a first-stay incentive.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make one offer the hero. Replace "A Destination for Every Celebration / BOOK NOW" with the 75K bonus-points Aloha offer or the 4,500-point stay promo, with the number in the headline and the deadline on the button.
- 2. Use the member data. Brooke has 0 points and 0 nights, so lead with a "Start earning" first-stay offer instead of generic celebration copy.
- 3. Cut the page to three modules (hero offer, one secondary offer, one sweepstakes or brand block) and move the rest to a "More ways to earn" link.
- 4. Make the subject match the content, and put the offer in it.
- 5. Write real preheader copy that adds the deadline or the mechanic instead of leaving it blank.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Brooke, earn 75K bonus points on your next stay`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 0 nights this year? Earn 4,500 bonus points`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Book by Nov 23 for Aloha stays through Dec 30. Plus 5,000 bonus points a day at select resorts.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Register by Nov 22 and start earning on your first stay.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's links are all wrapped in `email-marriott.com/T/...` redirect URLs. 12 of the 62 links were probed, and all 12 failed (16% pass rate). Most of the failures are 403s that are probably bot-blocking by the tracker. Two relative redirect targets, a missing plain-text part, and missing `List-Unsubscribe` and auth headers are the more credible issues.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **403 on every probed tracked link (10 of 12).** This includes "find & reserve", "Book Now", "explore the collection", "Marriott Bonvoy" (x2), the two "A family making memories on vacation." image links, and UNSUBSCRIBE (`.../0cebb7cc3bcb43e70000021ef3a0bcf8/...`). A uniform 403 across one redirect domain points to bot or UA filtering by the ESP click-tracker, not 10 separately broken links. It can't be confirmed from this data, and the final destinations were never reached.
- **Relative redirect targets.** Two tracked links resolve to relative paths, so the probe errored with `unknown url type`:
  - "» Register Now" → `/sign-in.mi?returnTo=%2Floyalty%2Fpromotion.mi%3Fpromotion%3DWL26`
  - "MY ACCOUNT" → `/loyalty/myAccount/default.mi?enrollmentSourceCode=INCK&nck=1028103536&ck=12021304&lk=1000301947`

  If the tracker really returns a relative `Location`, clients would resolve it against `email-marriott.com`, not `marriott.com`. That would send both links to a dead path. This needs a manual check with a real browser UA.
- **Very long tracking URLs.** The `__F__` payloads run to roughly 500 characters or more. This is a minor risk for clients that truncate or mangle long URLs.
- **Probe coverage.** 50 links were skipped, so footer, utility and social links are unverified.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Tracking pixels have no `alt` attribute.** `sli.marriott.com/imp?s=127820400` through `...404`, `s=1101484`, `1101485`, `1101487`, `1101488` and `email-marriott.com/O/.../0cebb7cc3bcb43e700004c5a42963aa1` are all affected. They should carry `alt=""` so screen readers skip them.
- **`mgm.gif` has no `alt`.** It appears twice, at `media.4at5.net/email_domains/mar/24083/images/mgm.gif`. If it's a content image, it needs descriptive alt text.
- **Mixed `http://` font URLs.** `@font-face` rules reference `http://cache.marriott.com/aka-fonts/.../AldineforMarriott-Roman.woff` and `-Italic.woff`. Some clients block or warn on these.
- **Wrong XHTML namespace.** The root element uses `xmlns="https://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml"`. The namespace is `http://`, and the `https` form is non-standard. It's harmless in practice.
- **Typekit stylesheet.** `<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://use.typekit.net/yft3kyd.css">` is stripped by most clients. The fallback fonts (Times New Roman, Arial) are declared, so this is handled.
- The HTML is truncated, so the rest of the markup, such as table roles and media queries, couldn't be checked.
