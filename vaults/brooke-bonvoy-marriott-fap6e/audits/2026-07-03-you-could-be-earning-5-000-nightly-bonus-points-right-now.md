# You Could Be Earning 5,000 Nightly Bonus Points Right Now
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- Marriott is running the June 26 New Orleans playbook again—destination utility rather than another credit card push—but Richmond is a harder sell than the Crescent City, and this email doesn't do the work to earn that ask. "Richmond Is Rewarding You This Summer" could appear on a city tourism poster; there's no hook that tells Brooke why Richmond, why now, why her. The deeper problem is the passive subject construction: "You Could Be Earning" is the exact same framing Marriott used on June 30 for the credit card push, redeployed here just three days later. That cadence risks the email reading as background noise before it's even opened.
- There are actually two real offers stacked here—5,000 bonus points per night for August stays, plus a 10% rate discount for July bookings—which is genuine value. But the July rate discount is buried below the fold with a secondary CTA ("CLAIM YOUR SAVINGS") while the August points bonus takes the hero slot. That's backwards: the July offer closes July 28, making it the immediately actionable hook. The August offer can support rather than lead.
- The email is clean, renders without obvious visual breakage, and the Bonvoy brand earns the open. But this reads as a template swapped onto Richmond rather than a campaign built around the destination.

## What's working

- Two distinct offers (5,000 points/night + 10% rate cut) give two independent reasons to act—points stackers and deal-seekers both have a lane
- Specific hotels named inline (Richmond Marriott, Courtyard, Residence Inn) with exact book/stay dates—no ambiguity about what's being offered
- "CLAIM YOUR SAVINGS" is a better CTA than "COUNT ME IN"—more specific about the reward, which is ironic since it's buried in the secondary block
- Aerial Richmond cityscape gives the hero a distinct visual identity; it's not a stock-photo couple on a beach
- Footer brand grid and social links are clean; no broken images visible in the render
- "You Could Be Earning" is passive conditional framing—same construction as the June 30 credit card subject—and gets weaker every time it appears
- No personalization: June 30, June 15, and June 12 all opened with "Brooke," making its absence here feel like a segmentation error, not a deliberate choice
- Preview "Book Early, Stay in August: Leave with More Points Than You Came With" echoes the subject's points theme rather than adding a new hook (rate discount, destination angle, urgency)
- Richmond gets zero destination sell—no landmark, no seasonal event, no reason Brooke should want to be there this summer specifically
- The July urgency (book by July 28) is structurally subordinated to the August offer despite being more time-sensitive

## Recommendations

- 1. **Flip the offer order and personalize:** Lead with the July rate, which is actionable this week. "Brooke, Save 10% in Richmond — Book by July 28" is a cleaner, more urgent hero than a future-tense points pitch.
- 2. **Sell Richmond in one line:** Give the hero copy a reason. "Jazz Brunches. Riverwalk Runs. 5,000 Bonus Points a Night." One local hook changes the destination from "wherever" to "somewhere worth going."
- 3. **Rewrite the primary CTA:** "COUNT ME IN" is brand-speak for nothing. Replace with "Book Richmond + Earn Points" or "Lock In 10% Off" — self-describing actions convert better.
- 4. **Fix the preview:** It should surface the rate discount and a deadline that the subject doesn't mention, making the inbox unit as a whole tell a richer story.
- 5. **Retire "You Could Be Earning" until August at earliest:** It was just in market three days ago. Repeating the construction this quickly collapses distinct campaigns into one blurry message.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Book by July 28 for 10% off — or stay in August to stack nightly bonus points`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Three Richmond hotels, two offers — one could save you money this month`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[BUG — confirmed]** Viewport meta tag has a malformed `initial-scale` value:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial scale=1.0" />
```
`initial scale` (with a space) is not a valid property. The correct value is `initial-scale=1.0` (with a hyphen). This will cause the viewport directive to be silently ignored on some mobile clients, potentially breaking responsive layout.

**[BUG — confirmed]** Invalid `vertical-align` CSS on `sup` and `sub` elements:
```css
sup { vertical-align: text-top .6em; }
sub { vertical-align: text-bottom .9em; }
```
`vertical-align` accepts a single keyword or length, not two values. The `.6em` / `.9em` tokens are dropped by CSS parsers, leaving only the keyword. Intended offset behavior will not apply.

**[MINOR]** `robots` meta uses non-standard token spelling:
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
Standard directive is `noindex` (no space). Functionally irrelevant for email rendering but indicates copy-paste error in the template header.

**[MINOR]** `<title></title>` is empty. Intentional by convention to suppress client display, but some accessibility tools surface this as a missing document title.
