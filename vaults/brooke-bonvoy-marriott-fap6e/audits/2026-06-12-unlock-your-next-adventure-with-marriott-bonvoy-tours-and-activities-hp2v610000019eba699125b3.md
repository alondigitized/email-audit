# Unlock Your Next Adventure with Marriott Bonvoy Tours and Activities
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- This email marks a deliberate gear shift after two weeks of aggressive credit-card conversion attempts. Marriott failed to get Brooke on the 100K points card (May 25), escalated to 125K points plus a free night (June 4), and now — presumably still no conversion — has pivoted entirely to the Tours & Activities marketplace. The subtext is clear: monetize the member through a lower-commitment product.
- The execution is visually strong — a gorgeous night shot of Rome anchors a clean catalog of three featured experiences — but operationally thin. "Earn or Redeem Points on Experiences" is a utility description, not an offer. There's no bonus, no discount, no deadline, and for the first time since May 25, no "Brooke" in the subject. That name-personalization pattern was a consistent thread through every conversion email; dropping it here feels like an unforced regression.
- The email will inform a member who's already thinking about a Rome trip. It won't create a trip out of thin air. Content emails can work, but this one leaves the conversion lever untouched.

## What's working

- Hero photography earns its keep: the Rome night cityscape immediately signals premium travel without a single word of copy.
- Earn/redeem framing reinforces member value without requiring a hard transactional pitch.
- Three geographically distinct experience cards (Rome, a water tour, Pompeii) give visual variety and let the destinations self-select interest.
- Partner logo grid at the bottom reads as marketplace breadth and supplier credibility.
- Clean render throughout — no broken images, no overlapping elements, no layout breaks.

## What's weak

- "Brooke" is absent from the subject for the first time since May 25 — breaks a proven personalization pattern with no apparent upside.
- No promotional hook in the hero: the June 4 "125K points + 1 free night" sent a reason to act; this send does not.
- Subject line is 68 characters — will truncate on most mobile clients, likely dropping "Activities" entirely.
- CTAs are scattered per-card rather than anchored by a single prominent hero button, which diffuses intent.
- Zero urgency signals anywhere in the visible email — no deadline, no limited availability, no countdown.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a redemption hook to the hero.** "Use 5,000 Points to Unlock a Roman Tour" turns "earn or redeem" from a product description into a reason to act today. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Restore the name.** "Brooke" in the subject drove the two highest-scoring sends in this brand history. No reason to abandon it here.
- 3. **Cut the subject to under 50 characters.** 68 chars bleeds off mobile entirely.
- 4. **Attach a deadline.** "Bookable with points through July 31" creates urgency the catalog format currently lacks.
- 5. **Add a single hero CTA button** before the card grid — "Browse All Experiences" — so there's one clear action before the catalog begins.
