# Brooke, Your Next Stay Starts When You Earn 100,000 Bonus Points
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-20**
## Executive summary

- This is the Marriott Bonvoy Brilliant American Express card acquisition pitch again. It is the third near-identical card send in two weeks, after "Brooke, What Would You Do With 100,000 Bonus Points?" on 10/6 and "Earn 100K Points + Automatic Elite Status" on 10/13. The render opens on a clear hero, "Earn 100,000 Bonus Points†", with a card image and an APPLY NOW button. A points-math graphic ("See How Your Points Add Up") follows. The email then lays out earn rates and premium benefits in a long, tidy scroll.
- The structure is good for a credit-card send. Offer, proof, benefits and a repeat CTA are all present. The weakness is the screenshot's extreme length. Roughly the bottom third is dense legal fine print, which makes the offer feel heavier and less honest than it needs to. The subject also frames this as "Your Next Stay," but the body is a credit-card application. That mismatch will feel like bait to a subscriber who expects a hotel promo.
- Verdict: a competent but repetitive acquisition send. It is solid on clarity and weak on freshness and cadence. A 5-6 is fair.

## What's working

- The hero states the offer in large type: 100,000 bonus points, with an APPLY NOW button right under it.
- The points-math module (current points + 100,000 welcome offer = potential total) makes the value concrete.
- The benefits section is skimmable. Free Night Award, $300 dining credit, Platinum Elite status and 25 Elite Night Credits are each broken out with an icon.
- The CTA repeats near the bottom with the same offer headline, so a scroller doesn't have to hunt for it.
- The Bonvoy branding is consistent and recognizable.

## What's weak

- The subject says "Your Next Stay," but the email is a card application pitch. The mismatch is not disclosed until the body.
- The fine print is extremely long and dominates the lower third. It undercuts the "honest offer" feel.
- Cadence is an issue. This is the third near-identical card send since 10/6, so the creative and offer are fatigued.
- The hero's key terms ($ spend threshold and time window) sit in small gray text, so the hook is hard to evaluate at a glance.
- There is no travel imagery or lifestyle context. It reads as a generic bank ad, not a Bonvoy stay.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the subject honest and specific about the card and the spend requirement, and don't send a third card email in two weeks.
- 2. Put the spend threshold and timeframe in the hero, next to the 100,000 figure.
- 3. Add a first-person, stay-based value line, such as "That's about X free nights," to connect points to travel.
- 4. Collapse the fine print into a linked "Terms" section or a short summary.
- 5. Rotate the creative so it doesn't repeat the 10/13 "Automatic Elite Status" angle.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML source is mostly sound: it has a viewport meta tag, MSO conditionals, and a `lang` attribute. Several automated checks failed, though. The email has no plain-text part, one tracked link has a relative redirect target, and the QA probe got 403s on every tracked link it tested, including UNSUBSCRIBE. Many of these failures may come from the QA environment rather than the email (see below).

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Every probed tracked link returned 403.** The affected links are "find & reserve", "» find & reserve", both "Marriott Bonvoy" links and "UNSUBSCRIBE". All sit on `https://email-marriott.com/T/v71000001a15a1077dc969dfd369fea47bc/...`. A uniform 403 across different link IDs points to bot or user-agent filtering on the click-tracking redirector rather than dead links. This is unconfirmed. Real recipients may be unaffected, but the QA data can't verify the destinations.
- **The "MY ACCOUNT" link failed with `unknown url type: '/loyalty/myAccount/default.mi?enrollmentSourceCode=INCK&nck=1028103536&ck=12193688&lk=1000301947'`.** The redirect target is a relative path with no scheme or host. If the redirector returns it as a relative `Location` header, or the underlying href is relative, the link can't resolve outside a context that supplies a base domain. This is the only link failure that is not a 403, so it is the strongest candidate for a real defect.
- **Link coverage is partial.** 12 links were probed and 32 were skipped, so the footer, utility and social links are unverified.
- **Tracking URLs are very long.** The `__F__` payloads run to several hundred characters. Some clients and plain-text renderers truncate or wrap URLs this long. Nothing in the data shows this causing a failure.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **One image has no alt text.** It is `https://email-marriott.com/O/v71000001a15a1077dc969dfd369fea47bc/d7d3e299730f4b9600004c5a42963aa1`. The `/O/` path suggests an open-tracking pixel, so the accessibility impact is probably minimal. Adding `alt=""` would still clear the warning.
- **Plain-text part is missing.** The QA check reports 0 chars. See section 5.
- **Two `@font-face` rules load fonts over insecure HTTP.** They are `Aldine-Regular` and `Aldine-Regular-Italic`, which point to `http://cache.marriott.com/aka-fonts/MarriottDigital/AldineforMarriott-Roman.woff` and `...-Italic.woff`. Clients that block mixed content will drop these fallbacks. The `woff2` sources are HTTPS.
- **The font `src` lists use invalid format hints.** For example, `format('otf')` should be `format('opentype')`. Browsers ignore the invalid entry, so there is no functional impact.
- The `Aldine-*` classes are overridden to `Times New Roman` in the `gt mso 9` conditional, so there is an Outlook fallback.
- The HTML was truncated, so the body markup (tables, `role="presentation"`, the preheader, dark-mode CSS) could not be audited.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The subject renders "Brooke," with no unresolved tokens, and none appear in the visible source. The body was truncated, so token rendering there is unverified.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **The List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) headers were not found.** The QA tool notes the AgentMail relay may strip them. This is inconclusive. Check the raw message at the source. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules expect one-click unsubscribe.
- **The plain-text part is missing or too short (0 chars).** This is a deliverability concern and a low-severity spam-filter signal. It could also be a relay capture artifact. Confirm against the raw MIME.
- **The Authentication-Results header was not found, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown.** The relay is expected to remove it. Nothing here shows an authentication failure.
- **The in-body UNSUBSCRIBE link exists.** The probe returned 403 on it (`.../d7d3e299730f4b960000021ef3a0bce7/...`). This is likely bot filtering, but the unsubscribe flow wasn't functionally verified.
- The physical postal address could not be checked because the footer was truncated.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **Final landing pages and UTM parameters could not be assessed.** All links go through the `email-marriott.com/T/` redirector, and the 403s kept the QA probe from resolving the destinations. No UTM parameters are visible in any exposed URL.
- **The one exposed destination, MY ACCOUNT, uses first-party identifiers rather than UTM parameters.** These are `enrollmentSourceCode=INCK`, `nck`, `ck` and `lk`. This path is relative, as noted in section 2.
- **"Find & reserve" lands on an unknown page.** It is a primary CTA, and whether the destination matches the 100,000-bonus-points offer is unverified.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Fix the relative redirect target for MY ACCOUNT.** Make it an absolute `https://www.marriott.com/loyalty/myAccount/default.mi?...` URL. This is the one confirmed link defect.
2. **Re-probe the tracked links with a real mail-client user-agent, or from allow-listed IPs.** That would show whether the 403s are bot filtering. Include UNSUBSCRIBE and "find & reserve" so the final URLs and UTM parameters can be checked.
3. **Add a plain-text MIME part,** if the raw message confirms it's absent.
4. **Verify `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` on the original message.** Check SPF, DKIM and DMARC there too, since the relay hides them.
5. **Switch the `http://cache.marriott.com/...woff` font sources to HTTPS.** Change `format('otf')` to `format('opentype')`.
6. **Add `alt=""` to the tracking pixel (`/O/...`).**
7. **Re-run the audit on the full, untruncated HTML.** Body markup, the footer address and any other merge tokens still need review.
