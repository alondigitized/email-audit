# Start Earning Now: Get up to 4,500 Bonus Points per Stay.
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-25**
## Executive summary

- This is Marriott Bonvoy's generic bonus-points registration email — earn 1,500 points on 2+ night stays, plus 3,000 more at resorts, September 22 through November 22. It's clean and on-brand but utterly untargeted: aerial overwater-bungalow shots, "worldwide" participating properties, and a featured property in Tanzania give this zero connection to any specific persona, trip, or location. Compare it to the 9/18 "Brooke's Account Update: 4,500 Points are Waiting" send — same 4,500-point mechanic, same registration CTA, but that one at least had a name in the subject and felt personal; this one strips even that out and reads as a mass blast.
- The visual execution is solid — striking hero image, clear CTA buttons repeated twice, generous whitespace — but there's no urgency beyond a two-month window, no loyalty-tier callout, and no product specificity (which hotels, what price). It's a perfectly fine "check the box" registration reminder that will get lost in a crowded Bonvoy cadence. Judgment: mediocre, forgettable send — a 5/10 email dressed up with premium photography.

## What's working

- Hero image (overwater bungalows) is striking and immediately signals luxury/resort travel.
- Two identical, high-contrast "REGISTER NOW" buttons make the CTA impossible to miss.
- Offer math is spelled out clearly in body copy (1,500 + 3,000 bonus points, date range).
- Clean brand header with account/reserve shortcuts, consistent Bonvoy visual identity throughout.
- Full brand-family footer builds trust/recognition for anyone in the loyalty ecosystem.

## What's weak

- No personalization — no name, no tier status, no reference to past stays (unlike the 9/18 "Brooke" send).
- Featured property is Le Mersenne Zanzibar, Tanzania — a random, unexplained destination with no connection to likely traveler geography.
- No visible fine print or minimum spend clarity beyond "participating properties" — leaves the offer feeling vague.
- Large dead whitespace gap between the hero image and the body copy paragraph breaks visual flow.
- No urgency framing beyond a generic 2-month window — nothing that creates a "act now" feeling.

## Recommendations

- 1. Personalize with member name and current points/tier status in the hero copy — this is the single biggest lift, since the brand's own history shows named sends ("Brooke's Account Update") consistently score higher.
- 2. Swap the random Tanzania featured property for one geographically relevant to the recipient's home market or stay history.
- 3. Tighten the dead space between the hero image and body text to keep momentum toward the CTA.
- 4. Add a countdown/urgency cue ("42 days left to register") rather than just stating the date range.
- 5. Surface tier-specific bonus math (e.g., "as a Titanium Elite member, you'd earn X") to make the offer feel earned, not generic.

## Technical Audit

1. Technical Summary
The email fails on link integrity, plain-text delivery, and compliance headers — 88% of programmatically checked elements failed, including the unsubscribe link itself and the primary CTA.

2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All probed links route through a redirect/tracking wrapper at `https://email-marriott.com/T/v71000001a0d889e299c81ced6e965fc958/...` with `__dU__`/`__F__` tokenized params (appears to be an Ongage-style ESP click tracker).
- 5 of 12 probed links return **403 Forbidden**: both "find & reserve" / "» find & reserve" CTAs, both "Marriott Bonvoy" header/logo links, and the **UNSUBSCRIBE** link itself.
- 4 links resolve to relative paths the redirect target doesn't handle (`unknown url type`): `/sign-in.mi?returnTo=...` (×3, "Register now" and "Go to the promotion registration page.") and `/loyalty/myAccount/default.mi?...` ("MY ACCOUNT"). These indicate the tracking redirect is decoding to a path-only URL missing its scheme/host — the destination `mi` endpoints are likely resolved by a base-href or a different mail-client context that the QA fetch doesn't replicate.
- Net effect: the primary conversion path (CTA → booking) and the compliance-required unsubscribe path are both unreachable in automated verification.

6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)

- Landing page alignment cannot be confirmed: the two most relevant destinations ("find & reserve" and "Register now") both fail before reaching a landing page (403 / unresolved path respectively), so continuity is broken at the first hop rather than at a content-mismatch level.

7. Recommendations
