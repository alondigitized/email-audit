# Explore More With the Best Rates, Brooke
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- Three days after the welcome email, Marriott Bonvoy sends Brooke a follow-up that reads less like a compelling offer and more like the second slide of an onboarding deck. The hero pushes app download, the second section sells the hotel portfolio broadly, and the third nudges social follows — three separate jobs in one email, none executed with urgency. For a new member who just joined on May 17th, this is a reasonable sequencing choice, but the execution is diffuse enough that nothing lands.
- The copy promises "best rates" in both the subject and the body, yet there is no rate, no discount, no dollar figure, and no expiration date anywhere in the scroll. That is the central problem: the subject sets up a value proposition the email body never delivers. A subscriber who opens expecting a rate benefit will feel misled by the time they reach the footer's wall of brand logos.
- Visually it's clean and on-brand — no render issues, coherent hierarchy — but clean and forgettable are the same thing when there's no hook.

## What's working

- Clean, uncluttered layout with good white space — nothing fights for attention.
- Personalization via "Brooke" in the subject line is appropriate for a new-member flow.
- The misty-mountain hero image in the "A Hotel for Every Occasion" section is genuinely aspirational and well-chosen.
- "EXPLORE HOTELS" CTA is the strongest of the three — specific enough to be actionable.
- The 30-brand portfolio callout gives a concrete reason to consider Marriott over a competitor.

## What's weak

- "Best Rates" in the subject is a promise the email body never substantiates — no percentage off, no member rate, no expiration.
- Three CTAs (Download the App / Explore Hotels / Get Inspired) dilute focus; none gets enough emphasis to drive a click.
- "Inspire Your Wanderlust / Get Inspired" pointing to social channels is a low-value ask for a brand-new member who hasn't even booked a stay yet.
- "More Tips to Optimize Your Membership Are Coming Soon" teases future emails rather than delivering immediate value — a weak closer.
- The phone-mockup hero is a stock-feeling visual; a real property photo or a lifestyle shot would do more emotional work.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Honor the subject line promise.** If you're going to say "best rates," show a rate. Even "Members save up to 25% at 9,000+ hotels — yours this week" would turn a vague claim into a reason to click.
- 2. **Consolidate to one CTA.** Pick "EXPLORE HOTELS" and remove the app download and social prompts. Those belong in a separate, dedicated send.
- 3. **Swap the phone mockup for an aspirational destination photo.** The app is a means, not a destination. Show Brooke the place she wants to be.
- 4. **Give the footer tease a date or a subject line hint.** "More tips coming soon" is filler. "Look for your first points-earning tips Friday" converts passive notification into anticipation.
- 5. **Add a lightweight urgency layer.** A "rate available through Sunday" or "this week's member deal" costs nothing to include and gives Brooke a reason to act now vs. closing the tab.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully audit — source truncated.** No CTA `href` values, tracking pixel URLs, or redirect chains are visible in the provided excerpt.

**Confirmed issue — poll rollover image references third-party CDN:**
```css
.poll:hover .pollButton {
  background-image: url("https://media.4at5.net/email_domains/mar/264715/images/poll_btn_rollover.png");
}
```
`media.4at5.net` is an ESP asset host (Epsilon/Alliance Data). If the ESP relationship changes, this asset becomes a dead link with no fallback. The image should be mirrored to `cache.marriott.com` or another Marriott-controlled CDN.

**CSS hover is non-functional in email:** The `.poll:hover` rule is stripped by Gmail, Outlook, and iOS Mail. The rollover state will never render for the vast majority of recipients — this is dead CSS weight.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `format('otf')` is an invalid format hint:**
```css
url('https://cache.marriott.com/.../tt0804m.otf') format('otf')
```
The correct CSS `format()` hint for OpenType is `'opentype'`, not `'otf'`. Browsers that strictly validate this string may skip loading the font file entirely. Affects all four `@font-face` blocks.

**Issue — `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on universal selector:**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
`none` disables user-initiated text zoom on WebKit/Blink. This overrides accessibility preferences and can cause rendering failures in Samsung Mail and older Android WebView. The override is partially corrected by `.body-fix { -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; }`, but any element outside `.body-fix` inheritance retains `none`. Should be `100%` universally, not `none`.

**Issue — SVG font format included:**
```css
url('...tt0804m.svg') format('svg')
```
SVG fonts are deprecated and unsupported in all current browsers. This adds unnecessary HTTP requests if a browser attempts to resolve the format hint. Should be removed.

**Issue — inconsistent font filename convention:**
```
tt0804m.otf   ← no underscore
tt0804m_.ttf  ← has underscore
```
The TTF filenames for Aldine fonts have a trailing underscore (`tt0804m_.ttf`, `tt0805m_.ttf`) while all other formats do not. If this is a typo in the HTML rather than the actual filename on the CDN, those TTF files will 404 silently.

**Cannot audit — alt text on images:** No `<img>` tags visible in the truncated source. Alt text coverage is unverifiable.
