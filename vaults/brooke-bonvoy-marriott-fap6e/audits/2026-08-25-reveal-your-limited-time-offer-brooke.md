# Reveal Your Limited-Time Offer, Brooke
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-25**
## Executive summary

- This is the exact same 150,000 Bonus Points + $250 Statement Credit card offer that ran on 2026-08-12, just re-skinned with a "Reveal Your Limited-Time Offer" scratch-off gimmick and swapped in "Brooke" personalization. The hero copy, benefit list (2X points, free night award, $300 Brilliant Dining Credit, elite status), and the CTA structure are essentially identical to two weeks ago — the only new element is the "reveal" framing device, which doesn't survive as static rendered copy (there's no interactivity in an image, so it just reads as a slightly redundant headline: "Earn 150,000 Bonus Points" directly under "Reveal Your Limited-Time Offer").
- The execution itself is clean — legible hierarchy, a real hotel-pool hero image, a clearly labeled Amex card art, and a well-organized benefits stack. But this is the second card-acquisition send in two weeks hitting the identical 150K+$250 number, following the 125K welcome-offer send on 08-18 and the 150K send on 08-12. That's repetitive-offer fatigue territory, and the "reveal" mechanic reads as a stale email gimmick applied to non-interactive copy rather than a genuine urgency driver.
- Net: well-produced but not novel. Brooke has now seen this exact card pitch at three different point values inside three weeks; the fourth iteration needs either a materially better number or a different angle to keep cutting through.

## What's working

- Clean visual hierarchy — hero headline, "LIMITED-TIME OFFER" badge, and card art are the first things the eye lands on.
- Amex card art is rendered clearly with a visible "APPLY NOW" button directly beneath it.
- Benefits are itemized with numbered icons (Free Night Award, Dining Credit, Elite Status) making scannability easy.
- Multiple aspirational property photos ("Where Will You Go") ground the offer in real destinations rather than abstract points.
- No render bugs — images load, text doesn't overlap, layout is intact top to bottom.

## What's weak

- The "reveal" concept (subject: "Reveal Your Limited-Time Offer") has no payoff in the static render — it just becomes a redundant second headline over the same 150K number already seen on 08-12.
- Third near-identical card offer in under three weeks (125K on 08-18, 150K on 08-12, 150K again here) — cadence fatigue is a real risk for Brooke.
- "Where Will You Go With Your Free Night Award?" section uses generic stock-style destination shots (Amman, Bali?, Fort Lauderdale) with no connection to Brooke's actual travel history or Elite status shown in prior sends.
- Fine print block at the bottom is dense and long relative to the visual offer — feels like standard Amex/Marriott boilerplate rather than a tailored disclosure.
- "Terms and Conditions" repeated twice as a header with no distinguishing content preview, which reads as filler.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the "reveal" gimmick copy for the static/image fallback — if there's no interactive scratch-off in the actual rendered email, replace "Reveal Your Limited-Time Offer" with something that pays off immediately, e.g., naming the exact number in the subject itself, since that's already the strategy that scored best (08-12, 8/10).
- 2. Space out card-acquisition sends — three sends in three weeks at 125K/150K/150K trains Brooke to wait for value creep rather than act; either escalate meaningfully or switch to a stay-based/property-based send for a cycle (the 08-07 JW Marriott Anaheim send scored 9/10 by pairing a real loyalty perk with a specific property).
- 3. Tie the "Where Will You Go" destination imagery to Brooke's actual stay history or wishlist properties instead of generic stock shots — even a light "Popular with Elite members near you" framing would help.
- 4. Trim the T&C footer or move the densest legal block below a "see full terms" link so the visual offer isn't followed by a wall of gray text.

- **Subject Alt B:** `150K Points + $250 Credit — Reserved for You, Brooke`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus a free night award and 2X earning on every stay.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your Bonvoy Brilliant™ Amex application takes minutes.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary

This email uses Epsilon Harmony/Salesforce Marketing Cloud (`email-marriott.com`) redirect tracking with heavy DPI-encoded link wrapping; automated QA shows the majority of tracked links returning HTTP 403 and no usable plain-text fallback, driving the pass rate down to 23%.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **6 of 12 probed material links return HTTP 403**, including the two primary CTAs ("find & reserve" / "» find & reserve"), both "Marriott Bonvoy" logo/header links, and the **UNSUBSCRIBE** link — evidence: all four resolve through `email-marriott.com/T/...` wrapped redirects and fail at the tracking-domain hop before ever reaching the destination.
- **MY ACCOUNT link fails with a scheme error**: `unknown url type: '/loyalty/myAccount/default.mi?enrollmentSourceCode=INCK&nck=1028103536&ck=10966414&lk=1000301947'` — the destination URL embedded in the redirect payload is a relative path (`/loyalty/...`) rather than an absolute URL, so the resolver cannot follow it.
- 33 additional links (footer/utility/social) were deprioritized and not probed by the QA pass — unverified, flagged as coverage gap rather than confirmed pass.
- All probed links share the same tracking domain (`email-marriott.com`) and campaign ID `v71000001a038fba4cdb380136e96c65848`, consistent with a single ESP send; the 403s point to a tracking-domain/redirector outage or link-wrapping misconfiguration at send time, not a per-link issue.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **4 images missing `alt` text**, confirmed in source/QA: one tracking pixel/banner image (`4c9c099d3e6a493300004c5a42963aa1`) and three benefit icons (`icon_more-points_top.png`, `icon_room-upgrades_top.png`, `icon_late-checkout_top.png`) served from `images.harmony.epsilon.com`. Screen readers will announce nothing for these elements.
- Extensive `@font-face` declarations (Aldine/Swiss families) load from `cache.marriott.com`; two font URLs use plain `http://` instead of `https://` (`AldineforMarriott-Roman.woff` and `AldineforMarriott-Italic.woff`), which will be blocked or flagged as mixed content in clients that render remote content over HTTPS.
- MSO/Outlook conditional comments (`<!--[if mso]>`, `<!--[if gt mso 9]>`) and `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` are present, indicating standard multi-client rendering guards are in place — no issues found here.

- **`List-Unsubscribe` header not found** — either absent or not captured by the AgentMail relay; cannot confirm RFC 2369 compliance from available data.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found (RFC 8058)** — one-click unsubscribe via mail client cannot be confirmed as supported.
- **`Authentication-Results` header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown from this data; cannot confirm sender authentication.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)

- No UTM parameters are present on any observed link; instead, all tracked links use Salesforce Marketing Cloud/Epsilon-style opaque redirect tokens (`__dU__`, `__F__`) rather than standard `utm_source`/`utm_medium`/`utm_campaign` query params. This is consistent with SFMC/Journey Builder tracking but means landing-page attribution depends entirely on the redirect resolving — which is currently failing for the primary CTAs (§2), breaking email-to-site continuity end-to-end for this send.
- Landing page destinations (post-redirect) could not be verified since the tracked links 403 before reaching `marriott.com`/Bonvoy destination pages.

1. **Priority — fix the 403s on tracking redirects**, especially the UNSUBSCRIBE and both primary CTA links; verify the Epsilon Harmony redirect/tracking domain (`email-marriott.com`) is live and correctly resolving `__dU__`/`__F__` tokens before next send.

3. Add `alt` text to the 4 flagged images (1 tracking/content image + 3 benefit icons).

5. Add a non-empty **plain-text MIME alternative** (currently 0 chars) — required for deliverability and accessibility, and a CAN-SPAM/RFC best practice.

7. Confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC are passing at the sending MTA — no `Authentication-Results` header was observed via the relay, so authentication status is currently unverifiable and should be checked directly against mail server logs.
