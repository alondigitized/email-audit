# Enter for a Chance to Win One Night With Myles Smith
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-28**
## Executive summary

- This is a sweepstakes activation email — a full creative break from Marriott's recent cadence of points-earning and credit-card conversion attempts. The hook is a celebrity experience prize: "One Night With Myles Smith," set at Starbucks Reserve Roastery NYC on August 14. The grand prize bundle (roundtrip flights for two, a two-night Renaissance New York Chelsea Hotel stay, $1,000 to spend, plus VIP concert access and a meet-and-greet) is well-structured and genuinely aspirational. The secondary prize — access to an intimate Myles Smith show for two — gives a second entry tier that widens appeal.
- The problem is persona fit. Brooke is a Marriott Bonvoy loyalist primed for hotel stay rewards, not a sweepstakes player. There's no direct booking offer, no points accelerator, no loyalty member advantage here — just a chance to win. For a subscriber who received a destination-utility parking deal as recently as June 26, this feels like a hard creative pivot toward a different audience segment. The entry mechanic (link Marriott Bonvoy + Starbucks Rewards) is clever cross-promotion but adds friction for anyone not already using both.
- On execution, the email looks clean and renders well. The prize structure is legible, the CTA is repeated three times, and the how-to-enter flow is concise. But the overall lift is constrained by the sweepstakes format itself — most recipients won't enter, and there's no fallback offer to capture those who don't.

## What's working

- **Grand prize breakdown is crystal clear** — flights + hotel + $1K spend + VIP meet-and-greet laid out as distinct line items, visually scannable.
- **Repeated CTA ("LINK + ENTER NOW")** — appears three times across the scroll, reducing drop-off at different read depths.
- **Cross-brand mechanic surfaces Starbucks partnership** — linking both loyalty accounts is smart co-promotion and genuinely low-effort for someone already in both ecosystems.
- **"Here's How You Can Get In" section is fast** — three icons, two steps, 30 seconds to enter. Friction reduction is well-executed.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, readable type, no broken images or text overlap.

## What's weak

- **No fallback offer for non-entrants** — if Brooke doesn't want to enter a sweepstakes, there's nothing else to act on. Zero booking nudge, zero points accelerator, zero stay discount.
- **Myles Smith is niche** — a UK pop/folk artist. Persona match is unclear; this requires the recipient to care about this specific artist to feel urgency.
- **Sweepstakes odds language in fine print feels discouraging** — "must be 21+" and odds notice at the very bottom are standard but visible, adding a transactional chill.
- **Starbucks Rewards linkage is a prerequisite, not a bonus** — recipients who don't have (or want) a Starbucks account hit a wall immediately. That's a silent disqualifier that isn't called out until mid-scroll.
- **No urgency signal on the deadline** — "Sweepstakes ends July 14" appears in small fine-print text, not in the hero or CTA. Missing the tension that a countdown would deliver.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface the deadline in the hero or CTA button** — "LINK + ENTER NOW — Ends July 14" turns passive curiosity into timed action. Right now the deadline is buried in fine print.
- 2. **Add a no-win consolation offer** — even a "enter for a chance to win, or book a stay at Renaissance New York Chelsea Hotel and earn 2X points" secondary block retains the booking intent for non-entrants.
- 3. **Call out the Starbucks requirement upfront** — put "You'll need a Marriott Bonvoy + Starbucks Rewards account" directly under the headline, not mid-scroll. Surprises kill entry rates.
- 4. **Lean into Myles Smith's moment** — one line of social proof ("over 1B streams," "sold-out global tour") before the prize block. If recipients don't know who he is, the prize reads as generic.
- 5. **Reframe subject as experience, not contest** — the current subject ("Enter for a Chance to Win") is low-conversion framing; lead with the experience.
- **Subject Alt A:** `NYC concert + hotel + $1K — Brooke, enter by July 14`
- **Subject Alt B:** `One Night With Myles Smith in NYC — your entry is 30 seconds`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Grand prize: flights, Renaissance Hotel stay, VIP meet-and-greet, and $1,000 to spend.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Link your Marriott Bonvoy + Starbucks accounts. Ends July 14.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Bug — duplicate `@font-face` for `'Aldine-Light'`:**
```css
/* Block 1 */
@font-face { font-family: 'Aldine-Light'; src: url('...tt0804m.woff') ... }
/* Block 2 — no font-style descriptor, different source files */
@font-face { font-family: 'Aldine-Light'; src: url('...tt0805m.woff') ... }
```
Block 2 overwrites Block 1 because neither carries a `font-style: italic` or `font-weight` descriptor. One of these is almost certainly the italic or bold variant with a missing descriptor — the intended variant will never load.

**Bug — hover CSS effects dead in majority of clients:**
```css
.poll:hover .pollButton { background-image: url("...poll_btn_rollover.png"); }
.img-hover-zoom:hover img { transform: scale(1.1); }
```
`:hover` and CSS `transform` are supported only in Apple Mail and Outlook.com (Chromium). Gmail (web + app), Outlook desktop, Samsung Mail, and most mobile clients strip or ignore these rules. The poll button rollover image and the hero zoom effect are non-functional for the majority of recipients. The static fallback image must carry the full intended message without the rollover state.

**Aggressive text-size suppression:**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applying `none` to the universal selector blocks user zoom and override on all WebKit-based clients, including iOS Mail. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) is violated for users who rely on system font scaling. Replace with `-webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%` on `body` only, which is the standard email pattern and is already present on `.body-fix`.

**VML/MSO configuration:** Correctly structured. `<o:AllowPNG/>`, `PixelsPerInch`, and `DontUseAdvancedTypographyReadingMail` are all present and valid.

**`<section>` reset:** `section { display: inline !important; }` — unusual but a known defensive pattern against Gmail's block-level reflow of semantic tags. Not a bug.
