# Brooke, up to 50% Bonus Points = More Joy
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-05**
## Executive summary

- This is Marriott's "buy or gift points" seasonal promo, and it's a weaker cousin of the card-acquisition emails this brand has been running lately. The 2026-08-03 "60,000 Bonus Points + No Annual Fee" send worked because the math was dead simple and free (sign up, get points). This one asks the recipient to spend real money to buy points, and it buries the actual mechanics behind a login wall before you can even see what you'd pay.
- The headline promise ("up to 50% Bonus Points") doesn't match what's actually shown on the page — the two visible tier boxes cap out at 35%, with the real 50% tier hidden in the fine print requiring a 40,000+ point purchase inside a 150k–200k annual cap. That's a credibility gap a recipient will notice on a second read. Combine that with a vague "REVEAL OFFER" CTA and an odd unfinished-looking image strip beneath the hero, and this reads as a mid-tier send — serviceable branding, but it asks for more trust and more scrolling than it earns.
- Verdict: this will get opened on brand recognition and personalization alone, but it's a soft click — the "reveal" gate and the headline/tier mismatch will cost it conversions versus Marriott's stronger free-bonus sends.

## What's working

- Clear personalization ("Brooke,") in both subject and body.
- A concrete, dated offer window (Aug 4 – Sept 9) gives the promo a real shelf life.
- The tiered bonus-points boxes (30% / 35%) are visually clean and easy to scan.
- Aspirational hammock/beach photography is on-brand and consistent with prior travel-themed sends.
- The property carousel and brand-logo wall at the bottom reinforce portfolio breadth for members deciding where to redeem.

## What's weak

- The hero image area has a large dead-white gap next to the couple photo, followed by a thin, blurry, seemingly cropped strip of another image — reads as a broken/incomplete render, not a design choice.
- Headline says "up to 50% Bonus Points" but the only tiers actually displayed cap at 35% — the real 50% tier is buried in the terms and conditions and requires a much larger purchase. That's a bait-and-switch optics problem.
- "REVEAL OFFER" is a vague, low-information CTA — it doesn't tell the recipient what buying points actually costs before making them log in.
- Login is required (membership number + password) just to see pricing — high friction before any value is shown.
- This is the second "bonus points" framing from Marriott in recent weeks (following the 125K/150K card pushes) — cadence is starting to blur together even though the mechanism differs.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the headline/tier mismatch first — either show a 50% tier box on the page or change the headline to "up to 35%" so the promise matches what's visible without a login. This is the single biggest trust risk in the email.
- 2. Replace "REVEAL OFFER" with an action-specific CTA like "See My Bonus %" or "Calculate My Points" — tell people what happens next.
- 3. Fix or remove the broken-looking image strip beneath the hero — either extend the photo full-width or cut it entirely; the current partial crop looks unfinished.
- 4. Surface at least indicative pricing (e.g., "points from $X per 1,000") before the login wall, so recipients can self-qualify before authenticating.
- 5. Space this out from the recent run of point-bonus messaging, or explicitly differentiate it as "buy points" vs. "earn points" in the subject so it doesn't blur with the card offers.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Brooke, buy points before Sept 9 and get up to 35% more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your points just got 35% more valuable, Brooke`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Buy or gift points now through September 9 — bonus tiers inside.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Turn a beach daydream into a booked stay with bonus points.`

# Technical Audit — Marriott Bonvoy "Up to 50% Bonus Points" Email

## 1. Technical Summary
The email fails the majority of automated link checks (17% pass rate, 12 failures) due to broken tracking redirects and malformed destination URLs, and is missing both a plain-text MIME part and standard unsubscribe headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **10 of 12 probed tracking links failed.** Most return HTTP 403 when resolved through the Epsilon/Harmony click-tracking domain `email-marriott.com/T/...` — affected CTAs: "find & reserve" (x2), "» Buy Points", "Marriott Bonvoy" (logo/header, x2 instances), "Reveal offer", the hero image link (`CS0635963-q2-points-26_rab.jpg`), and **"UNSUBSCRIBE."**
- **2 links resolve to relative paths, not absolute URLs**, causing "unknown url type" errors:
  - `MY ACCOUNT` → `/loyalty/myAccount/default.mi?enrollmentSourceCode=INCK&nck=...&ck=...&lk=1000301947`
  - `Marriott Bonvoy` (brands nav, 2 instances) → `/marriott-brands/explore-our-brands.mi?enrollmentSourceCode=INCK&nck=...&ck=...&lk=1000421047`
  
  This indicates the tracking redirect is terminating in a path fragment rather than a fully-qualified destination — either a base-domain misconfiguration in the ESP redirect table, or the destination was authored as a relative link by mistake.
- **Unsubscribe link itself is broken (403)** — this is also a compliance issue (see §5).

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **5 images missing `alt` text**, all decorative/content-bearing images with no fallback text for screen readers or image-blocked clients:
  - `c5e3d898cc10482300004c5a42963aa1` (tracking pixel — expected, low priority)
  - `CS0635963-Q3-Points-2025_icon_star.png`
  - `CS0635963-q3-points-2026_hero_d.gif` (appears twice, both instances missing alt)
  - `CS0635963-q2-points-26_rab.jpg`
- **Mixed-content font resource**: one `@font-face` src uses plain `http://` instead of `https://`:
  `url('http://cache.marriott.com/aka-fonts/MarriottDigital/AldineforMarriott-Roman.woff')`. In HTTPS-rendered mail clients/webviews this asset will be blocked or flagged, silently degrading to the fallback (`Times New Roman`).

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
`{{FirstName}}`-style token appears correctly resolved to "Brooke" in both the subject line and `<title>` tag. No unresolved merge tags observed in the provided source. Note: source was truncated, so body-content personalization beyond the title could not be fully verified.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Unsubscribe link returns 403** when probed — a functional CAN-SPAM violation if reproducible for end users (opt-out mechanism must work).
- **`List-Unsubscribe` header not found** — no in-client one-click unsubscribe available (mailbox providers increasingly require this for bulk senders).
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found** — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe unsupported.
- `Authentication-Results` header not found — flagged WARN only, likely an artifact of the AgentMail relay not capturing/forwarding SPF/DKIM results rather than an actual authentication failure.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Cannot fully verify UTM/query-param alignment against landing pages because the majority of tracking links 403'd before reaching a final destination. The two links that did resolve past the redirect layer landed on **relative paths** (`/loyalty/myAccount/default.mi`, `/marriott-brands/explore-our-brands.mi`) rather than qualified `marriott.com` URLs — this breaks the redirect chain and would 404 or error in most mail clients/browsers that don't have a base href context to resolve against.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Re-verify the Epsilon/Harmony click-tracking redirect table for this campaign — the 403s and relative-path resolutions suggest a broken or expired tracking link generation, not a probe artifact (10/12 links affected is too high to dismiss as a fluke).
2. Fix the unsubscribe link specifically — this is a compliance risk independent of the other broken CTAs.
3. Add `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers to meet CAN-SPAM/RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe expectations.
4. Add a plain-text MIME part (currently 0 chars) — required for CAN-SPAM and improves deliverability/spam scoring.
5. Add `alt` attributes to the 4 content-bearing images lacking them.
6. Change the `http://` font URL to `https://` to avoid mixed-content blocking.
