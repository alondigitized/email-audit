# Offer Ends Soon: Earn 125K Points + 1 Free Night, Brooke
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- This is Marriott's fifth push of the exact same 125K Points + 1 Free Night credit card offer in seven weeks. The creative lever this time is a countdown timer — a concrete, visible deadline (13 days, ~13 hours at render) that replaces the narrative framing of prior sends. The strategic bet is that urgency converts fence-sitters who ignored the June and early-July versions. That's a reasonable hypothesis for a fifth-touch email.
- The execution, however, makes a puzzling regression: the July 14 subject included "$100 in Airline Credits" as a differentiating value signal and scored an 8. This subject drops that entirely and substitutes generic deadline language ("Offer Ends Soon"). The $100 airfare credits still appear in the body in their own section, but they're invisible at the inbox impression — which is the only place you get to make a first read.
- Long email, lots of scroll. The Silver Elite section, the 17x/6x/2x benefits grid, and a second card image repeat material Brooke has now seen four times. The countdown timer is real, the offer is transparent ($95 fee disclosed in the hero), and the layout is clean — but there's no new proof point, no new creative angle, and no new reason to convert for anyone who's been saying no since June.

## What's working

- **Countdown timer is the strongest creative choice here** — a real, ticking deadline (13:13:19:56) is more compelling than vague "limited time" copy and doesn't feel manufactured.
- **$95 annual fee disclosed in the hero**, not buried — that's honest framing that builds trust with a sophisticated travel audience.
- **Points arithmetic is shown explicitly** (breakdown of how 125K is reached), which reduces skepticism around a round number.
- **Silver Elite status section** is a named, tangible perk that distinguishes the card from a generic points vehicle.
- **$100 Airfare Credits section** adds a second use-case hook (flights, not just hotel nights) for members who already have points to burn.

## What's weak

- **"$100 in Airline Credits" was dropped from the subject** — a direct regression from July 14's version, which had that signal at the inbox impression level. This send trades a value signal for a deadline flag with no compensating lift.
- **Fifth repetition of an unchanged offer** — no new property, no new benefit unlocked, no social proof — kills incremental conversion for anyone already opted out mentally.
- **First CTA appears after several paragraphs of body copy**, meaning a high-intent reader has to scroll past setup before reaching "APPLY NOW."
- **Email is extremely long** — Silver Elite, benefits grid, airfare credits, and a second card visual all compete; the email would land harder at half the length.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Restore the airline credits to the subject line** — the July 14 version proved this works; dropping it here is a step backward. Bundle deadline AND value in the same line.
- 2. **Put a second "APPLY NOW" above the fold**, immediately below the countdown and offer headline, before any benefits copy — high-intent readers shouldn't have to scroll to convert.
- 3. **Cut the email by 40%** — eliminate the second card-image block, collapse the icon grid into a single-line summary, and keep Silver Elite and Airfare Credits as the only two supporting beats.
- 4. **Add one new proof point** — a single real member quote, a stat ("Free night redeemed at the W Maldives"), or a featured property tied to the deadline. Anything that signals this is a different email, not a resend.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

**Mixed-content HTTP font URLs (medium severity)**
Two `@font-face` declarations load `.woff` fallbacks over plain HTTP:
```
url('http://cache.marriott.com/aka-fonts/MarriottDigital/AldineforMarriott-Roman.woff')
url('http://cache.marriott.com/aka-fonts/MarriottDigital/AldineforMarriott-Italic.woff')
```
All other font variants (`Aldine-Light`, `Aldine-Light-Italic`, `Swiss-Light`, `Swiss-Regular`) correctly use `https://`. Gmail and Apple Mail block mixed-content resource loads — these two woff files will silently fail and fall back to Times New Roman. Fix: prefix both with `https://`.

**Invalid CSS format hint (low severity)**
Multiple `@font-face` declarations use `format('otf')`:
```css
url('https://cache.marriott.com/.../tt0804m.otf') format('otf')
```
The CSS specification defines this value as `'opentype'`, not `'otf'`. Email clients that strictly parse format hints will skip this source entry. The WOFF/WOFF2 entries above it in the stack will catch the load, so visual impact is minimal — but the OTF files are never fetched as intended.

**SVG font format declarations (low severity)**
All six `@font-face` blocks include an SVG font fallback:
```css
url('https://cache.marriott.com/.../Swiss721BT-Light.svg') format('svg')
```
SVG fonts were removed from Chrome in 2017 and are unsupported in every major email client. These entries add source-list payload with zero rendering benefit.
