# Act Fast to Get up to 55% Bonus Points ⏰
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-30**
## Executive summary

- This is a straightforward buy-points promotion from Marriott Bonvoy. The hero is "Get up to 55% Bonus Points While They Last," and a salmon banner above it reads "LIMITED AVAILABILITY — BUY POINTS NOW." It is a cleaner and more honest send than the 9/26 and 9/29 credit-card acquisition emails in this thread. It asks for one thing, buying points, and it explains the deal in the body.
- The hero image of a couple cycling past yellow buildings and lanterns is pretty, but it says nothing about buying points, so the eye goes to the image before the offer. The three-tile row ("Act fast," "Once gone, the offer will be reduced," "Don't wait") is mostly pressure copy that repeats itself. The most useful detail is buried in a plain paragraph: the annual purchase limit rises to 200,000 points. The second block, "Buy Points and Redeem Them Your Way," is generic lifestyle content with a weak text link.
- Overall it is competent and on-brand but not persuasive. It has urgency and no math. It never shows what 55% is worth in dollars or nights, and the fine print says the actual bonus percentage varies with the quantity purchased. Since the headline says "up to," that gap matters.

## What's working

- The hero headline is large and legible, and the salmon "LIMITED AVAILABILITY" banner above it makes the urgency obvious.
- It is personalized: "Brooke, your Marriott Bonvoy® membership…" and the masked member number (XXXXX2045) make it read as a member-specific offer.
- There are two clear, well-styled CTAs: a solid black "BUY POINTS" button and an outlined "GIFT POINTS" button. The hierarchy between them is sensible.
- The "buy up to 200,000 points, 50,000 more than the usual limit" line is a real, concrete hook for heavy buyers.
- The layout is clean and uncluttered, with no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- The hero image is a lifestyle shot with no link to points or the offer. It takes up a lot of the first screen while the CTA sits far below it.
- The three tiles say the same thing three ways: hurry, the offer will shrink, don't wait. They give no new information.
- The 55% is never turned into a concrete example, such as "Buy 50,000, get X bonus" or a cost per point.
- The "Buy Points and Redeem Them Your Way" block is filler, and its "» BUY POINTS" link is small and easy to miss.
- The deadline is only in the body copy ("through November 23"). The banner and headline use vague phrases like "While They Last" and "Limited Availability." The dense terms block also admits the bonus percentage varies by quantity.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a concrete example above the fold, for example "Buy 50,000 points, get 27,500 bonus," with the price shown. This is the biggest lift, because it turns vague urgency into a decision.
- 2. Move the primary "BUY POINTS" button up under the headline, and replace the tile row with a small tier table (2,000+ points = X%, 50,000+ = Y%) so "up to" is honest.
- 3. Swap the hero for a redemption-focused image with a points overlay, and state the real deadline ("Ends Nov 23") in the banner.
- 4. Cut the lifestyle block, or replace it with a real redemption example such as "50,000 points = 4 nights at a Category 4 hotel."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Brooke, buy points with up to 55% bonus — ends Nov 23`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 55% bonus on points, plus a higher 200K limit`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Buy 2,000+ points by Nov 23 and the bonus is yours. Annual limit now 200,000.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your limit just rose by 50,000 points. Here's how the bonus tiers work.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
Marriott's Epsilon-tracked email has a 0-character plain-text part and images with no alt text. All 12 probed links failed, but most failures are likely bot-blocking or probe limits rather than confirmed breakage, and the truncated HTML and relay-stripped headers leave the compliance items unverified.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **403 on 10 of 12 probed tracked links.** These include `find & reserve`, `Buy points`, `Gift points`, `Marriott Bonvoy` (x2) and `UNSUBSCRIBE`. All are wrapped as `https://email-marriott.com/T/v71000001a0f2afc.../<link-id>/1a6776a5-48dc-483f-a2c5-b76b65db46fa?__dU__=...&__F__=...`. A uniform 403 from a click-tracking domain most likely means the tracker rejects the QA probe's user agent or IP. It does not confirm the links are dead for real recipients.
- **Relative redirect targets.** `MY ACCOUNT` and both `Marriott Bonvoy` brand links (`.../bcd2/...`, `.../bcd3/...`) fail with `unknown url type: '/loyalty/myAccount/default.mi?enrollmentSourceCode=INCK&nck=1028103536&ck=11839246&lk=1000301947'` and `'/marriott-brands/explore-our-brands.mi?...&lk=1000421047'`.
  - The tracker returned a path-only `Location` with no scheme or host. A browser would resolve it against `email-marriott.com`, not `marriott.com`.
  - This could send recipients to a wrong host, or it may be a response given only to the probe. It needs a manual check with a real browser UA.
- **Duplicate destinations.** Two `Marriott Bonvoy` links resolve to the same `explore-our-brands.mi` target, and `find & reserve` and `» find & reserve` are separate tracked URLs (`bcc9` and `bcc6`). Duplicates are normal for per-placement tracking, so this is not an issue.
- **16 links were not probed.** Footer, social and other utility links are unverified.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Plain-text fallback is empty.** `[FAIL] Text version is 0 chars`. This is a confirmed issue: it hurts deliverability and gives text-only clients and screen readers no alternative.
- **Missing alt text.** These images were flagged:
  - `q4-points-26_hero.gif` (twice)
  - `q4-points-26_mod-1.gif`, `mod-2.gif` and `mod-3.gif`
  - the tracking pixel `email-marriott.com/O/v71000001a0f2afc.../1a6776a548dc483f00004c5a42963aa1`
- The hero and module GIFs are content images. Since the visible text is `find & reserve` and `Buy points`, any promotional copy baked into the GIFs is inaccessible when images are blocked. The tracking pixel should carry `alt=""`. The QA check doesn't distinguish a missing `alt` from an empty one, so I can't confirm that one.
- **Mixed-content font URLs.** The `@font-face` blocks in `<head>` reference `http://cache.marriott.com/aka-fonts/.../AldineforMarriott-Roman.woff` and `-Italic.woff` over plain HTTP. They sit alongside HTTPS `woff2` sources, so the impact is minor.
- **Invalid font format hint.** `Swiss-Bold-Condensed` and `Swiss-Black-Condensed` declare `format('otf')`, which is not a valid value (`opentype` is). Browsers will skip that source, and the effect is negligible.
- **External Typekit stylesheet.** The head loads `https://use.typekit.net/yft3kyd.css`. Most email clients ignore it, and the fallback stacks are defined.
- MSO conditionals, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, `format-detection` and viewport meta are present. No issues found there.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The visible HTML shows no unresolved tokens (`{{ }}`, `%%`, `[[ ]]`). The `nck`, `ck` and `lk` query parameters are resolved Epsilon identifiers. The HTML is truncated, so tokens in the body can't be ruled out.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) not found.** The QA note says the AgentMail relay may strip them. This is unverified, not a confirmed defect. Check the raw message headers at the receiving inbox.
- **Authentication-Results not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown for the same relay reason.
- **No physical address detected.** The HTML is truncated, so the footer wasn't fully scanned. For a sender of this size the address is very likely present, but the check can't confirm it.
- **In-body unsubscribe link returned 403.** The `UNSUBSCRIBE` link (`.../bcd7/...`) failed the probe. This is likely the same bot-blocking as the other links, but it should be verified by hand.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **No visible UTM parameters.** The links are opaque Epsilon redirects, so campaign attribution happens server-side through `__dU__`, `__F__`, `ck`, `nck`, `lk` and `enrollmentSourceCode=INCK`. This is a design choice, not a defect.
- **Landing-page alignment can't be assessed.** The redirect chains returned 403 or a relative `Location`, so the final destinations were never reached. The `MY ACCOUNT` (`/loyalty/myAccount/default.mi`) and brands (`/marriott-brands/explore-our-brands.mi`) targets are plausible, but they were not resolved to a full URL.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Add a plain-text MIME part.** Include the offer copy and the key CTAs (find & reserve, buy points) with their URLs.
2. **Add descriptive `alt` text to the five GIFs.** Set `alt=""` on the open-tracking pixel.
3. **Recheck the failed links.** Retry with a browser-like UA, and follow the relative-`Location` redirects manually. If they really resolve to `email-marriott.com/loyalty/...`, ask the ESP to emit absolute `https://www.marriott.com/...` redirect targets.
4. **Confirm the headers on the raw message.** Look for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment for `email-marriott.com`. Confirm the footer postal address in the untruncated HTML.
5. **Fix the font declarations.** Use `https://` for the `.woff` sources and change `format('otf')` to `format('opentype')`.
6. **Fix the QA tooling.** Add a browser-like UA and redirect handling for relative `Location` headers. The current results (11% pass rate) overstate the problems because of probe artifacts.
