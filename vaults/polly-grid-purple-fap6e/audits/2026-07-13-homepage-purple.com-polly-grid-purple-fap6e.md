# Homepage snapshot · purple.com · 2026-07-13
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- This is Purple doing what Purple does best — leading with the science before showing me a single product shot. "Start the year with an unfair advantage" paired with footnoted clinical stats is peak Purple energy, and honestly, it's the reason I'm a convert. Right now they're running a Back to School angle, which is a slightly weird fit for me personally at 39 but the pricing hook ("Queen mattresses from $999") is concrete enough to stop my thumb.

## What's working

- **The science copy block** — "90% improvement in reported sleep quality¹ … sounds like cheating, doesn't it?" lands with me because I've read these studies in the Purple emails and the footnote superscripts make it feel honest, not hype.
- **"Shop + compare" CTA** — "compare" is doing heavy lifting here. For someone who researched this mattress obsessively before buying, an invitation to compare is exactly right.
- **"Less pain. Better sleep." brand tagline** — still the most efficient six-word pitch in sleep retail. Functional promise, no filler.
- **Dual CTA structure** — one action-oriented ("Shop + compare") and one educational ("Learn what makes us different") neatly funnels both the ready buyer and the still-skeptical shopper. I've been both.

## What's weak

- **"Back to School" framing** — the purple banner is eye-catching but the message skews toward dorm rooms, not adults upgrading a master bedroom. It makes me feel like I wandered into a campaign aimed at someone else.
- **No bundle pricing visible** — the whole reason I budget my Purple purchases is to wait for the mattress + pillow + sheet set deal. Zero signal of that above the fold means my #1 conversion trigger isn't here.
- **Text-dominant above the fold** — no grid imagery, no GelFlex visual, no egg-crate cross-section. I know the product, but a first-timer has nothing to anchor the science to.
- **No review count or social proof** — I trust the footnotes, but a star rating or "over 1M sleepers" would reinforce for anyone not already sold.
- **Referral bar, not loyalty bar** — "Refer & get $100" is a new-customer play. As an existing customer I want to see my rewards or a "returning customer? here's what's new."

## Recommendations

- **Swap the Back to School angle for a returning-customer rail** or at minimum add a "Bundle & save" banner alongside the promo — one line: "Mattress + Purple Pillow + SoftStretch Sheets from $X" triggers my actual buying pattern.
- **Add one GelFlex grid visual above the fold** — the science copy works but it's floating in white space. A single cross-section render of the grid next to the stat block would ground the claim visually without a full hero redesign.
- **Surface a review count in the hero section** — even a small "★ 4.8 · 50,000+ reviews" anchored near the CTA raises confidence at the exact moment I'm deciding whether to tap.
- **Personalize the top promo bar for recognized visitors** — if Purple has my account, replace "Refer & get $100" with a loyalty balance or a "back again? Your saved items →" link. That's a one-line change that signals this brand knows me.
