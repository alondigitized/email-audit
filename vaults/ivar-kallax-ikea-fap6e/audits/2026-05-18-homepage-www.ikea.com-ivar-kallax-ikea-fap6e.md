# Homepage snapshot · www.ikea.com · 2026-05-18
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- This is exactly what I expected to find when I typed ikea.com — a clean, no-nonsense mobile homepage that gets straight to the categories I actually care about. IKEA is leading with discovery right now: storage, sofas, and the promise of "Family price" somewhere below the fold. It's honest, it's functional, it's very Scandinavian — but it's also missing the urgency that would make me open my wallet today rather than just add things to my list.

## What's working

- **Category tile layout** — "Storage & organization" and "Sofas & armchairs" are the two things I search every single visit. Seeing them front and center without any digging is exactly right. One tap and I'm in.
- **Search bar with camera icon** — That visual search icon is underrated. I've photographed random furniture at a friend's place and traced it back to IKEA. Smart to surface it immediately.
- **"IKEA Family price" badge on the lifestyle image** — Even if it doesn't spell out the savings, it signals that logging in before checkout has a reason. I'm already a Family member, so this is a quiet nudge that works on me.
- **Lifestyle image at the bottom** — The living room shot with the wood coffee table and the layered textiles is aspirational without being pretentious. That's my apartment in a year and I know it.
- **Location set to Burbank, CA** — Seeing my city and zip code (91801) means stock checks and store directions are already contextual. That matters when I'm planning a showroom run for Swedish meatballs AND a KALLAX unit.

## What's weak

- **No hero headline — zero copy above the fold** — Between the logo and the category tiles there's a search bar and… nothing. No tagline, no reason to be here today vs. any other day. It's all navigation and zero narrative.
- **"IKEA Family price" badge gives no numbers** — It's a tease. Tell me I save 15% on this specific coffee table or I'll forget about it by the time I get to checkout.
- **No primary action button** — Category tiles are fine for browsing, but there's no "Shop the spring collection" or "See what's new in storage" button that would pull me into a curated funnel.
- **No people in the visible content** — The category tile images are product-only (dresser on white background, sofa on white background). No one who looks like me assembling a shelf or hosting in their small apartment. I know IKEA's lifestyle shots exist — they just aren't here.
- **Partially visible third category tile** — Something that starts with "Ou..." (probably Outdoor) is cropped at the right edge. It's a scroll hint but it looks a little sloppy at first glance.

## Recommendations

- **Add a headline strip between the logo bar and the search bar** — Even one line: "New in storage — just landed." Five words, costs nothing to ship, gives me a reason to tap instead of just search.
- **Replace the product-on-white category tiles with in-context lifestyle thumbnails** — Show the KALLAX unit actually stuffed with records and sneakers in a studio apartment, not floating on white. That's the Reddit hack aesthetic that converts me.
- **Put a specific IKEA Family discount next to the badge** — "Family members save 10% this week" is actionable. "IKEA Family price" is abstract. I shouldn't have to log in to find out if the savings are real.
- **Swap the third partially-cropped tile for a "New arrivals" or "What's trending" card** — If outdoor furniture is genuinely in season, give it a full tile. If not, use that slot for a recency hook.
