# Make this summer legendary with these best-selling gifts
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-22**
## Executive summary

- This is a "Top 5 Summer Favorites" listicle send — a departure from the relentless "30% off sitewide" flash-sale format 1-800-Flowers has hammered nearly every day this month, and a much cleaner execution than the Aug 15 "$13.80 credit" birthday send. The 25% off + code SUMMER25 banner up top is clear and legible, the numbered 1-5 format gives the scroll a sense of structure, and there's no render breakage anywhere in this long email.
- The problem is focus, not execution. This is a generic seasonal catalog scroll — roses, fruit, an arrangement, a beach towel/snack bundle, and cookies — with no single hero offer or product driving the click. By the time a reader hits "Shop by Category" nine products deep, the 25% off code from the top of the email is a distant memory with no reinforcement. It's competent design wrapped around a diffuse, low-urgency offer.
- Verdict: better creative than the fatigued flash-sale reruns, but still a mid-pack send — pleasant to scroll, nothing compels a click.

## What's working

- Clean numbered "Top 5" format gives visual rhythm and breaks up what would otherwise be a flat product dump.
- The 25% off + promo code banner is legible and sits above the fold before any scrolling.
- Product photography is bright, appetizing (the fruit box, the cookies) and on-brand for summer.
- "Shop by Category" grid at the bottom gives browsers a fallback path if none of the 5 featured gifts land.
- Celebrations Passport membership callout is a legitimate secondary CTA for loyalty-minded subscribers.

## What's weak

- No single clear hero CTA — one "SHOP NOW" button sits at the very top, then the reader scrolls through 5 products and 9 category tiles with no repeated buy button until the very bottom.
- The 25% code (SUMMER25) is never restated near any individual product — a reader who scrolls past the header banner has no reminder the discount even applies.
- "Top 5 Summer Favorites" is a vague, low-urgency framing — no scarcity, no deadline, nothing that says act now vs. browse later.
- Category grid (Birthday, Romance, Sympathy, Plants, etc.) dilutes the "summer" theme the subject line promised — half the tiles aren't seasonal at all.
- This is a fourth distinct creative approach in two weeks (flash sale → birthday credit → flash sale → now listicle) — a subscriber who's opened multiple sends will register brand fatigue via inconsistent messaging, not a coherent campaign.

## Recommendations

- 1. Repeat the SUMMER25 / 25% off callout as a sticky or repeated element near each of the 5 products (or at minimum immediately below the "Top 5" header) — right now the offer is disconnected from the products it applies to.
- 2. Add a deadline to the offer ("ends Sunday," "through Labor Day") — zero urgency signals anywhere in this render.
- 3. Cut the "Shop by Category" grid down to just summer-relevant categories (Summer, Best Sellers, Same-Day) — Sympathy/Birthday/Romance undercut the "legendary summer" subject line.
- 4. Add a second "SHOP NOW" button roughly halfway down (after item 3) so engaged scrollers don't have to scroll back to the top to convert.
- 5. Pick one hero product (the fruit box or the arrangement) and lead with it large, rather than treating all 5 as equal-weight — right now nothing pulls the eye toward a single "buy this" moment.
- **Subject Alt A:** `25% Off Summer Favorites — Ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Top 5 Summer Gifts, 25% Off With SUMMER25`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh flowers, fruit, and treats — all 25% off through Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Our 5 best-selling summer gifts, picked for you.`
