# Find Your Favorite, Then Save Up to 20%
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-09**
## Executive summary

- This is the sixth send in about two weeks built on the "Playing Favorites / This or That" concept. The render is clean and the olive and rust banner is seasonal. The offer is the weak point. "Up to 20% off* with code FLOWERSALE" sits in a thin top bar, and the hero is a script headline with no number in it. The 11/05 send ("40% off red roses + a vase on us") put its offer in the hero and scored 7. This one asks the reader to work out the deal.
- The two vs. comparisons are a fun device, but they lead to a vague "Shop Fall" button and not to the four bouquets shown. The "checkbox" labels (Autumn Fields™, Graceful Fall™) look tappable but aren't a clear call to action. The category grid and app badges at the bottom are generic filler. A subscriber who got "Moody or Neutral? 💐" on 11/04 and "20% Off. Now the Only Hard Part Is Choosing." on 11/07 is looking at the same bouquets and the same concept again.
- Judgement: well made but repetitive, with a buried and capped offer. It's a middling send at around 5/10.

## What's working

- The hero is easy to read: a large script headline, a clear "SHOP NOW" button and good contrast on the dark olive background.
- The vs. pairs (Moody vs. Neutral, Farmhouse vs. Penthouse) are scannable and the product photography is rich and seasonal.
- The code FLOWERSALE is stated in the top bar, so the reader doesn't have to hunt for it.
- The layout is clean, with no broken images or overlapping text.
- The category grid gives a quick way into birthday, sympathy, same-day and other needs.

## What's weak

- The offer is buried in a small top banner, and "up to 20%*" is hedged by an asterisk and fine print.
- The hero repeats the same "Playing Favorites" creative the subscriber has seen several times since 10/23.
- All four product buttons say "SHOP FALL". They are identical, generic and don't link to the specific bouquet shown.
- No prices, ratings or reviews appear anywhere, so there's no concrete reason to prefer one bouquet over another.
- The bottom third (category grid, app badges, long legal text) dilutes the focus and adds length.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the offer in the hero, for example "20% Off with code FLOWERSALE". Don't leave it as a thin banner above a script headline.
- 2. Retire the "Playing Favorites" creative for a week and lead with a single hero product and a hard deadline. The offer ends 11/26, so use that date or run a countdown.
- 3. Give each vs. pair a specific CTA, such as "Shop Autumn Fields" and "Shop Graceful Fall", and link each bouquet to its own product page.
- 4. Add prices and a star rating under each bouquet. The 11/03 "Gifts Rated 5 Stars" email worked because of social proof.
- 5. Cut the category grid and app badges, or move the app badges to the footer. Add a Thanksgiving-delivery cutoff message instead.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use code FLOWERSALE at checkout. Ends 11/26.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Pick your side: moody, neutral, farmhouse or penthouse.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional layout with dark-mode meta tags and responsive media queries. The automated checks flagged three problems: insecure `http://` tracking pixels, missing unsubscribe and physical-address signals, and a URL-heavy plain-text part. The HTML source was truncated, so the footer and most link targets could not be inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Insecure pixel hosts:** three beacons load over `http://`, so clients that block mixed content may drop them and under-count opens or conversions:
  - `http://view.atdmt.com/FLO/view/361227669/direct/01/`
  - `http://pixel.mathtag.com/event/img?mt_id=155437&mt_adid=103507&…`
  - `http://pixel.mathtag.com/event/img?mt_id=155438&mt_adid=103507&…`
- **Redirect links not verified:** 27 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are click-redirect domains (e.g. `click.dv.1800flowers.com/o/…`). Their destinations are unconfirmed.
- **Duplicate beacon:** `https://bounceexchange.com/tag/em/1622.gif` appears twice, which may double-fire the tag.
- **Pixel stack:** `atdmt.com`, `mathtag.com`, `bounceexchange.com`, `mi.1800flowers.com/p/up/…/o.gif` and `click.dv.1800flowers.com/o/…` are all loaded in one message. That is a lot of third-party requests, and each is a point of failure or blocking.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="… maximum-scale=1" />` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (resize text).
- **Missing alt text on tracking pixels:** the QA check flags the beacon images above. These are 1×1 trackers, so they should carry `alt=""` explicitly. The source gives no evidence that any content image lacks alt text.
- **Forced header colours:** `th {color:#000000 !important}` and `th a {color:#000000 !important}` sit alongside `color-scheme: light dark`. If the nav `th` cells sit on a transparent background, black text could become unreadable in dark mode. This is a risk to test, not a confirmed defect.
- **Text-size-adjust conflict:** `* {-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` is overridden on `body` by `100%`, so behaviour varies by client. This is minor.
- **Generic title:** `<title>1800Flowers.com</title>` doesn't match the subject line. This has low impact.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (`{{…}}`, `%%…%%`, `[[…]]`) appear in the visible source.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found:** the QA tool notes the AgentMail relay may strip these headers, so this is unconfirmed. If the headers really are absent, the email lacks one-click unsubscribe, which Gmail and Yahoo require of bulk senders.
- **No physical address detected:** CAN-SPAM requires one. The HTML was truncated before the footer, so this may be a false negative. Verify against the full source.
- **SPF/DKIM/DMARC unknown:** `Authentication-Results` was not captured, because the relay strips it. The sender is `em.1800flowers.com`, which is a dedicated sending subdomain. Authentication cannot be confirmed from this data.
- **Plain-text part is URL-heavy:** 5168 of 7027 characters (about 74%) are URLs, which is above the 70% heuristic and a possible spam-filter signal. This comes from long tracking URLs.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **UTM parameters not confirmed:** no `utm_*` parameters appear in the visible source or in the flagged URLs. Links are wrapped in redirects, so final landing URLs and their parameters are unknown.
- **Campaign ID present on the pixel:** `mi_ecmp=261109_EMAIL_18F_PZ_STO_DV_12AMET_PROMO` shows a campaign identifier exists on the open pixel. It can't be linked to any landing-page parameter without resolving the redirects.
- **Landing page alignment:** unverifiable, because the redirect probe was skipped.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Serve all tracking pixels over HTTPS. Upgrade `atdmt.com` and `mathtag.com` or remove them if they are legacy.
2. Add `alt=""` to all tracking pixels and remove the duplicate `bounceexchange.com/tag/em/1622.gif`.
3. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
4. Check the raw `.eml` for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC results, since the relay may have stripped them. Also confirm the footer contains a postal address.
5. Resolve a sample of the 27 redirect links in a browser-like probe to confirm landing pages and UTM or campaign parameters.
6. Check the `th` forced black text in dark-mode clients, such as Apple Mail and Outlook.com.
7. Shorten the plain-text URLs or add more descriptive text to bring the URL share below 70%.
