# Turn up the sunshine—double blooms inside ☀️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- This is the third "Double Blooms" execution from 1-800-Flowers in fourteen days — May 19 ("For Love This Big… Double the Flowers"), May 27 ("Double up on your favorite roses at no extra cost"), and now June 2 with summer-themed dressing over the same 2X mechanic. The creative team has slapped a new coat of paint — "Summer of Double Blooms," seasonal yellows and sunflowers in the product grid — but any subscriber who opened the last two sends will recognize this immediately as a repeat. The mechanic isn't broken; it's just exhausted.
- The one genuinely new element here is the "20% Off Summer Classics" secondary section with a promo code. That's a different lever, and it rescues the email from being a pure copy-paste of May 27. But it's buried below five product rows, making it feel like an afterthought rather than a co-headlined offer.
- The execution itself is clean — hierarchy lands, offers are legible, no render issues. The email doesn't fail; it just underperforms because the creative is operating on borrowed equity from a mechanic that should have rotated out a week ago.

## What's working

- **Clean hierarchy.** "Summer of Double Blooms" hero reads instantly; the eye moves straight to the product grid without competition.
- **Product variety.** Five arrangements shown at price, ranging across rose, mixed, and sunflower SKUs — something for different tastes.
- **Secondary offer adds a second call to action.** "20% Off Summer Classics" with a promo code gives a price-sensitive subscriber a reason to act even if they've already seen the 2X mechanic twice.
- **No render issues.** All images load, text is crisp, no overlapping elements or broken gaps.
- **Summer framing is seasonally accurate.** The "Summer of" language lands on June 2 — not stale relative to the calendar.

## What's weak

- **This is the same mechanic for the third time in 14 days.** The 2X badge, the same $44 price point, the same rose-forward product lineup — any engaged subscriber will feel déjà vu before they scroll past the hero.
- **"SHOP NOW" is the weakest possible CTA copy.** Nothing in the button tells you what you're shopping for or what the mechanic delivers — "Get Double Blooms" or "Shop 2X Arrangements" would be materially more motivating.
- **The 20% off offer is buried.** It appears after five full product rows; most mobile users won't scroll to it, which wastes the most differentiating element in this send.
- **No deadline anywhere in the email.** Neither the 2X mechanic nor the 20% Summer Classics offer shows an expiry date, removing any reason to act today vs. tomorrow.
- **No preheader distinction from the subject.** The inbox preview likely picks up "Summer of Double Blooms" from the hero, echoing the subject's "double blooms inside" rather than adding new information.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the 20% Off Summer Classics as the hero, not the 2X mechanic.** The Double Blooms concept has run twice. Lead this send with the fresher offer; put the 2X mechanic as a secondary module below. This alone flips the narrative from "same email again" to "new offer this week."
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA from "SHOP NOW" to something mechanic-specific.** "Get Double Blooms" or "Shop Summer Classics 20% Off" ties the click to the specific offer.
- 3. **Add a deadline to both offers.** Even "Offer ends Sunday" is enough to make the decision feel urgent.
- 4. **Elevate the 20% promo code to the hero or a second hero module** visible without scrolling — right now a large portion of mobile openers will never see the freshest thing in the email.
- 5. **Vary the lead product.** The pink/red rose bouquet has appeared in both prior Double Blooms sends. Swap the hero arrangement to sunflowers or the summer meadow mix to signal visual freshness.
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% off summer classics — plus double the blooms ☀️`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New: Summer Classics at 20% off (and yes, double blooms are back)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use code SUMMER20 — five arrangements, one deal, ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Two reasons to send flowers this week — pick your favorite.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

| Issue | Evidence |
|---|---|
| `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` on `*` | `* {-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}` — `none` blocks user text-size overrides; should be `100%`. Accessibility failure on iOS. |
| Responsive gap: 419px–639px unaddressed | Mobile breakpoint is `max-width: 418px`; desktop kicks in at `min-width: 640px`. No styles govern the 419–639px range (common for large Android phones / phablets). |
| Redundant `img` max-width rule | `@media only screen and (min-width: 768px)` and `@media only screen and (min-width: 640px)` both declare `img {max-width:640px}`. The 768px block is dead code — the 640px rule already covers it. |
| Mixed `.woff` / `.woff2` for same font | All Playfair Display weights load `.woff2` except weight 900 normal: `src:url(...nuFvD...ukDT.woff) format('woff')`. Inconsistent; `.woff2` is smaller and better compressed. |
| Missing `mso-font-alt` on one variant | Playfair Display 900 weight (`nuFvD...ukDT.woff`) has no `mso-font-alt` fallback, unlike all other variants which specify `'Times New Roman'`. Outlook will pick an arbitrary fallback. |
| Duplicate `:root` color-scheme declaration | `color-scheme: light dark` is declared in two separate `<style>` blocks (lines ~7 and ~42 of source). Harmless but increases parse overhead. |
