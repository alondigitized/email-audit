# Thinking of them? Send something special (+ $13.80 credit)
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- This is a meaningful thematic break from the Double Blooms rut — after three nearly identical 2X-blooms executions across fourteen days (May 19, May 27, June 2), 1-800-Flowers pivots cleanly to a Healing Gifts angle. "Thinking of them?" is emotionally resonant copy that opens a completely different buying occasion: sympathy, get-well, support. That pivot alone is the email's biggest win.
- The structure is well-considered — a numbered 1-5 editorial list ("Top Healing Gifts") with a customer testimonial dropped mid-scroll. The $13.80 credit is oddly specific, which actually works in its favor; round discounts read as invented, while precise numbers feel earned. The fruit & sweets basket (#4) and "Hang in There" (#5) product wisely broaden the category beyond flowers-only.
- The core problem: there's no time pressure anywhere in the email. The credit is offered with no expiration, no minimum shown, no condition. Without a deadline, the whole send sits in "I'll do this later" territory — and later never comes.

## What's working

- **Thematic reset:** Healing Gifts is the first non-Double-Blooms concept in two weeks; it immediately feels fresh to a subscriber who's been hammered with the same rose mechanic.
- **Numbered product list:** The 1–5 format gives editorial authority — it reads like a curated recommendation, not a product dump.
- **Customer testimonial mid-scroll:** "The flowers were beautiful. Fresh, colorful, and fragrant... flowers can change an entire space and create a great mood." — placed exactly where scroll fatigue hits. Good placement.
- **$13.80 credit specificity:** A non-round number signals this was calculated, not invented. It builds trust versus a flat "$10 off."
- **Product breadth:** Including a fruit basket and a plush/novelty item alongside florals signals this is a gift destination, not just a flower shop.

## What's weak

- **No expiration on the credit:** The $13.80 is mentioned at the top and presumably in the subject, but no deadline is shown anywhere. This removes the primary conversion lever.
- **Occasion-less for June 6:** "Healing Gifts" is evergreen but it's not tied to any June moment. Father's Day is nine days away — there's a missed opportunity to anchor this to something real.
- **Hero CTA copy is generic/possibly stale:** The button below the hero image is hard to read cleanly; it appears to reference "FALL" or is otherwise ambiguous — wrong season for June regardless.
- **$13.80 credit terms are invisible:** How do you apply it? Min spend? Auto-applied? A subscriber has to click and discover — adding friction at the top of the funnel.
- **Subject is 58 chars:** Slightly over the ~50-char mobile cutoff; "Send something special" is padding that could be cut.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline to the credit.** "Use your $13.80 credit by Sunday" transforms this from a soft suggestion into an action item. That single change would lift conversions more than any creative refresh.
- 2. **Tie it to Father's Day.** "Know a dad who's had a rough year? Healing gifts, $13.80 credit inside" reframes the Healing Gifts concept into a specific June gifting occasion with a real deadline. The products already work for it — the fruit basket and bouquets are solid Father's Day gifts.
- 3. **Clarify the credit mechanic above the fold.** One line — "No code needed. Applied at checkout." — removes the biggest uncertainty for a first-time credit recipient.
- 4. **Fix or cut the hero button copy.** Whatever it says, it should read "Send a Healing Gift" or "Shop Healing Gifts" — specific to the occasion, not a generic fallback.
- 5. **Trim the subject by cutting "Send something special."** The credit is the hook; the sentiment is filler.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Thinking of them? Your $13.80 credit expires Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Healing gifts for someone who needs it (+ $13.80 credit)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `No code needed — credit applied automatically at checkout.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Top 5 get-well gifts, curated. Credit expires this weekend.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Non-standard mobile breakpoint — Medium risk**
`@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)` targets only iPhone SE / small screens. Standard practice is `480px` or `600px`. Android devices in portrait (360px–412px range) are partially covered, but devices between 419px–479px fall into neither the mobile nor desktop layout. `.mobile` and `.showmobile` classes also hardcode `width:414px !important`, making them fragile on any device not exactly that width.

**Duplicate, redundant media queries**
Two separate rules apply identical behavior:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width:640px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width:640px} }
```
The 768px rule is fully superseded by the 640px rule. Dead code — no functional impact, but indicates a copy-paste error.

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>` tag**
The opening `<html>` element uses `xmlns` attributes but no `lang="en"`. Screen readers (NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver on iOS Mail) use this to select pronunciation rules. WCAG 3.1.1 violation.

**Aggressive text-size reset**
`* {-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` disables automatic text scaling on WebKit clients. This can make small text unreadable on mobile without manual zoom. The safer value is `100%`, not `none`.

**`mso-line-height-rule:exactly` on wildcard selector**
`* {mso-line-height-rule:exactly}` applies to every element. This is intended only for elements where you set explicit `line-height` — applying globally can collapse line heights on dynamically-sized content in Outlook.

**Web font fallback inconsistency**
Playfair Display weights 400, 600 italic, and 400 italic declare `mso-font-alt:'Times New Roman'` (Outlook fallback). Weight **900 normal** (`nuFvD...fsukDT.woff`) and weight **900 italic** lack this declaration. If Outlook renders those elements, no fallback font is specified.

**`.woff` format for Playfair Display 900**
All other weights load `.woff2`. The 900-weight normal variant loads `.woff`:
```css
src:url(...nuFvD...fsukDT.woff) format('woff')
```
`.woff2` has ~30% smaller file size and broader modern support. Inconsistency suggests copy-paste error — the `.woff2` URL should exist for this weight.

**External font loading blocked by Gmail and Outlook**
All `@font-face` rules pull from `fonts.gstatic.com`. Gmail (web and Android) and Outlook (all versions) strip external font declarations entirely. This is expected and handled by fallbacks, but confirm every text element that uses Playfair Display or Lato has an explicit `font-family` stack ending in a web-safe font.
