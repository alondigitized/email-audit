# Mark It. Shop It. Celebrate It.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-02**
## Executive summary

- This is a calendar-themed catalog email. The "November Highlights" hero frames four moments: Thanksgiving on Nov 26, a Scorpio birthday, a romance block, and Santa's arrival on Dec 25. It is polished and seasonal. The strongest idea is the "Mark Your Calendar" framing, which gives a reason to scroll.
- The weakness is focus. The only offer, 20% off with code GLOW20, sits in a thin banner at the very top and is never repeated near any product. The hero CTA is the vague "Get Gifting." Four sections each carry a "Shop X" button, then a nine-tile category grid follows. A subscriber can't tell what to do first.
- It is also the fifth or so GLOW20-style promo in about a week. The 10/26 Halloween send used the same code, and the 10/27–10/31 sends piled on doubles, gift guides and "This or That." The creative is tidy, but it reads as more of the same.
- Judgement: a competent, pretty browse email with a buried offer and no urgency. It will likely land around 5/10.

## What's working

- The hero ties dates to occasions ("Nov. 26th" Thanksgiving, "Dec. 25th"), which gives the email a useful planning angle.
- Each section follows the same pattern: a lifestyle image, a 2x2 product grid, and a green CTA. This is scannable.
- Product tiles have names, and a few have small script callouts such as "Hand woven basket" and "Last for over a year!".
- The Scorpio birthday block is a nice bit of personalization that feels fresh.
- The footer has app download badges and a category grid for people who want to keep browsing.

## What's weak

- The 20% off / GLOW20 offer is only in the top strip. It never appears next to a product or CTA, and no deadline is shown.
- The hero CTA, "Get Gifting," is generic. Four more "Shop…" buttons plus nine category tiles compete with it.
- No prices, ratings or reviews are visible, so there is no social proof or price anchor.
- The email is very long, about 7,600px tall. Several sections (birthday, romance, Santa) have little to do with the Thanksgiving-led subject and preview.
- "He's coming to town…" is an obscure teaser for a Dec 25 section, and it makes the subject's "Celebrate It" feel scattered.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the offer into the hero: "20% off with code GLOW20" as a headline element next to a single "Shop Thanksgiving Gifts" button, with an end date.
- 2. Cut the email to two or three sections. Lead with Thanksgiving, since it is the nearest date, and drop the romance and Santa blocks to a later send.
- 3. Add prices, or "from $X," under the product tiles, plus a review count or "best seller" badge on one or two.
- 4. Replace the category grid with a single "Order by [date] for Thanksgiving delivery" line, which creates credible urgency.
- 5. Space out the sends. Rotate the offer mechanic instead of repeating the same code and layout.
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% off Thanksgiving flowers, ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your November gift calendar (20% off inside)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use GLOW20 on centerpieces, baskets and more. Order by Nov 20 for Thanksgiving delivery.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Thanksgiving, birthdays and the holidays, all planned in one place.`

## Technical Audit

# Technical Audit: "Mark It. Shop It. Celebrate It." (1-800-FLOWERS.COM)

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional email with dark-mode meta tags and mobile media queries. The QA data shows two main problems: several tracking pixels are missing alt text, and two of them load over plain `http://`. The compliance warnings (List-Unsubscribe, physical address, authentication) can't be confirmed from the data given, because the HTML is truncated and the relay may have dropped headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **23 tracking links were not probed.** They are click-redirect domains, so destination validity and redirect chains are unverified.
- **Insecure pixel sources (`http://`):**
  - `http://view.atdmt.com/FLO/view/361227669/direct/01/` (Atlas/Microsoft ad tracker)
  - `http://pixel.mathtag.com/event/img?mt_id=155437&mt_adid=103507&v1=&v2=&v3=&s1=&s2=&s3=`
  - `http://pixel.mathtag.com/event/img?mt_id=155438&mt_adid=103507&v1=&v2=&v3=&s1=&s2=&s3=`
  - Clients that proxy images or block mixed content may drop these pixels, which undercounts opens and conversions.
- **Empty query parameters:** the MediaMath URLs ship `v1=`, `v2=`, `v3=`, `s1=`, `s2=` and `s3=` with no values. That points to unpopulated dynamic fields, so the pixels may carry no useful data.
- **Third-party pixel load:** at least six tracking beacons are in the body: atdmt, two mathtag, two `bounceexchange.com/tag/em/1622.gif` and `mi.1800flowers.com/.../o.gif`. A seventh, `click.dv.1800flowers.com/o/80ca53cb-...`, is likely an open pixel. This is a privacy and deliverability footprint, and the plain-text part is 4376 of 5781 characters URLs (76%).

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text** on these images:
  - `view.atdmt.com/...`
  - both `pixel.mathtag.com` pixels
  - both `bounceexchange.com/tag/em/1622.gif` instances
  - `click.dv.1800flowers.com/o/80ca53cb-810d-4148-8e13-a64fb868cac5`
  - `mi.1800flowers.com/p/up/ada90b86b2846dd9/o.gif`

  These are 1×1 tracking images, so the real fix is `alt=""` (plus `width="1" height="1"`) so screen readers skip them.
- **Plain-text part is mostly URLs (76%).** This hurts text-only readability and is a mild spam signal.
- **Viewport:** `maximum-scale=1` in `<meta name="viewport">` blocks pinch-zoom on some clients, which is an accessibility concern.
- **Global CSS:** `* {line-height:100%; -webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` forces 100% line-height on every element and disables text-size adjustment. This can hurt legibility and user font scaling.
- **Fixed-width mobile classes:** `.mobile` and `.showmobile` use `width:414px !important`. Viewports narrower than 414px may scroll horizontally, but this is unconfirmed without a render.
- **Truncation:** only the head and CSS were visible, so body-level issues such as image `alt` on content images, heading semantics and button markup couldn't be checked.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found in the visible source. The truncated HTML didn't show any unresolved tokens. The empty `v1=`, `v2=` and `v3=` values in the MediaMath URLs are the only sign of unpopulated dynamic fields (see §2).

## 5. Compliance
All of the following are QA warnings, not confirmed defects:
- **List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** not found. The QA note says AgentMail may not relay these headers, so this is unverified. If the sender really omits them, the email doesn't meet the Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender requirement for one-click unsubscribe.
- **Physical address:** not detected by the check. The HTML is truncated, so the footer wasn't inspected. Verify manually before treating it as a CAN-SPAM violation.
- **Authentication-Results:** absent, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment couldn't be checked. The sending domain is `em.1800flowers.com` (subdomain of the brand domain).

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- **UTM parameters:** not assessable. The 23 tracking links weren't resolved and the visible HTML doesn't show final URLs. The only parameter data seen is `mi_cid`, `mi_mid` and `mi_ecmp=261102_EMAIL_18F_STATIC_DV_12AMET_PROMO` on the 1800flowers pixel URLs. That campaign tag marks a static "DV" promo send.
- **Landing page alignment:** unverified, because the redirects weren't followed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Change the atdmt and both mathtag pixel URLs to `https://`, or remove them if the vendors no longer support HTTPS.
2. Add `alt=""` to all tracking pixels and give them explicit 1×1 dimensions.
3. Populate or drop the empty MediaMath parameters (`v1`–`v3`, `s1`–`s3`).
4. Confirm that `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` are in the raw message headers. Inspect the original source rather than the relay copy.
5. Confirm the footer has a physical postal address (the check may be a false positive caused by truncation).
6. Re-run the audit with the full HTML and a redirect resolver for the 23 tracking links, to verify landing URLs and UTM carry-through.
7. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
8. Shorten the plain-text part, or cut the number of tracked links in it, to lower the URL ratio.
