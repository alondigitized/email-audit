# Don’t Miss It: Your $13.80 Credit for Their Birthday
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- 1-800-Flowers pulls the classic bait-and-switch of subject-line marketing: the subject promises "Your $13.80 Credit for Their Birthday" — a specific, personalized hook that earns an open — and then the email delivers the same "We Know Birthdays" editorial template that already ran on July 7. The credit is invisible in the hero; if it exists anywhere, it's buried in the faint top banner. The disconnect between what was promised and what was delivered is this email's defining flaw.
- The creative itself is decent — a summer birthday lifestyle editorial with a social quote, category grid, and app download. But it's been running in essentially this form for weeks. The July 7 "Summer birthday season is here!" send scored 7/10 here and looked nearly identical. Recycling the same template the week after does nothing to advance the relationship or redeem the credit that was dangled.
- The judgment: smart subject line, lazy follow-through. The credit promise was the right message — this brand just didn't bother to feature it in the body.

## What's working

- The $13.80 credit in the subject is unusually specific and personalized — a real purchase-driver if honored in the body.
- "We Know Birthdays" hero is polished and on-brand; flower imagery is clean with no visible render issues.
- The testimonial quote about summer birthdays provides genuine social proof and ties the seasonal hook together.
- Category grid at the bottom gives easy browse paths without friction.
- Brand recognition is high — sender name and logo both register immediately.

## What's weak

- The promised $13.80 credit is nowhere in the hero or body — subject-to-body disconnect is a trust killer.
- This is the second near-identical "We Know Birthdays" editorial in six days (see July 7), which signals creative exhaustion.
- There is no single, prominent CTA button tied to the credit — the call to action fragments into a category grid instead of a redemption moment.
- "Don't Miss It" opener reads generically urgent, which undercuts the personalized $13.80 hook that should be the star.
- The top banner (where the credit presumably lives) is tiny and nearly illegible on mobile.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the credit the hero.** Swap the "We Know Birthdays" editorial layout for a dedicated credit-redemption module: a clear dollar amount, an expiry date, and one button. The subject earned the open — don't waste it on a recycled template.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the credit.** "Your $13.80 credit expires July 16" creates real urgency without manufactured FOMO.
- 3. **Replace the category grid CTA with a single "Redeem Your Credit" button** that links to the birthday flowers collection with the credit pre-applied or clearly noted.
- 4. **Retire the "We Know Birthdays" creative block** for this send — or at minimum relegate it below the credit module. It's done its job; leading with it again feels like the brand isn't listening.
- 5. **Rewrite the top banner** so the credit is visible without needing to zoom.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your $13.80 birthday credit expires soon`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$13.80 off their birthday flowers — redeem now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop summer birthday bouquets and it comes right off the total.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Credit valid this week. Pick the arrangement they'll actually love.`
