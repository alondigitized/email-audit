# The Ultimate Gift Guide for Every Recipient
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-30**
## Executive summary

- This is a tidy, seasonal gift-guide send with a real offer on top: a $15 credit with code HARVEST15 on a $59.99 minimum. The "Fall in the Family" hero and the For Her / For Him / For the Kids split give the email a clear structure. The offer is a small banner above the logo, though, and the hero is mostly a Yahtzee-style game box, which has little to do with the offer.
- Compared with the 10/27 and 10/28 "double your blooms" sends, this one is calmer and more useful. It is a broad gift guide with six or more CTAs, so no single product leads. The recipient-segmented layout is a smart idea, but the rest of the email dilutes it with a marathon tote promo and a category grid at the bottom.
- Overall it is a competent, middling send. The offer is solid but under-sold, and the subject is generic.

## What's working

- The "You've unlocked a $15 credit" banner with code HARVEST15 states a concrete, personalized-feeling offer.
- The For Her / For Him / For the Kids blocks are easy to scan, and each has its own "Shop Now" link.
- The warm fall palette and styling look seasonal and consistent with the brand.
- Each block pairs a large hero image with two smaller product tiles, so the eye follows a clear zig-zag down the page.
- The product captions (Pumpkin & Amber Bouquet, Juniper Bonsai) give specific items to click.

## What's weak

- The offer is only a thin banner above the logo and is not repeated in the body. The $59.99 minimum and the asterisk make it feel conditional.
- The hero shows a game box that doesn't signal flowers, gifts or the credit. The headline "Fall in the Family" is vague.
- The "Shop Now" buttons are small text links, and the hero button is small and dark, so there is little click pull.
- The NYC Marathon tote block comes out of nowhere and splits attention. The category grid at the bottom makes the email feel long.
- The subject line is generic and gives no offer or urgency. The deadline (11/22) appears only in the fine print.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the offer in the hero: "$15 credit on gifts $59.99+" with the code in a big button, and repeat it near the middle and bottom of the email.
- 2. Use a stronger hero image, such as the pumpkin bouquet, that clearly shows flowers or gifts.
- 3. Turn the text links into real buttons with specific copy such as "Shop gifts for her".
- 4. Cut the marathon block, or move it below the category grid. Shorten the grid.
- 5. Put the deadline in the body, e.g. "Ends 11/22".

- **Subject Alt B:** `Fall gifts for her, him & the kids`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use HARVEST15 on $59.99+ through 11/22. Gifts for everyone on your list.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Bouquets, snack baskets and plush pals, all eligible for your credit.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible markup (truncated) is a standard table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional email with dark-mode meta tags and a mobile media query. The main problems are non-HTTPS third-party tracking pixels with empty macro values, several images without alt text, and compliance elements (unsubscribe header, physical address) that the QA run could not confirm. Several of those may come from the relay and the truncated source rather than the email itself.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Insecure pixels:** three images load over `http://`. They are `http://view.atdmt.com/FLO/view/361227669/direct/01/`, `http://pixel.mathtag.com/event/img?mt_id=155437&mt_adid=103507...` and `http://pixel.mathtag.com/event/img?mt_id=155438&mt_adid=103507...`. Clients that proxy or block mixed content may drop them, so conversion and retargeting tracking will undercount. Switching to HTTPS endpoints would fix this.
- **Empty macro values:** both mathtag pixels end in `&v1=&v2=&v3=&s1=&s2=&s3=`. Those parameters are empty, so either the template variables were never populated or they are unused placeholders.
- **Many third-party trackers:** the email loads pixels from atdmt (Microsoft/Atlas), mathtag (MediaMath), `bounceexchange.com/tag/em/1622.gif` (twice, duplicated), `click.dv.1800flowers.com/o/...` and `mi.1800flowers.com/p/up/.../o.gif`. The duplicate `1622.gif` fires two requests for the same data.
- **Unverified links:** 24 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are click-redirect domains. Destination validity, redirect chain length and 404s are untested.
- **Plain-text part:** 4592 of 6002 characters (about 77%) are URLs, which exceeds the 70% threshold. Long tracking URLs are inflating the text part, which can hurt spam scoring.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** `view.atdmt.com`, both mathtag pixels, `1622.gif` (twice), `click.dv.1800flowers.com/o/6f47f715-...` and `o.gif` have no `alt`. These are tracking pixels, so the impact is low, but they should carry `alt=""` and `width="1" height="1"` so screen readers skip them and layout is stable.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" ... maximum-scale=1 />` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Odd breakpoint:** the mobile rules apply only at `max-width: 418px`. Devices between 419 and 639px, such as large phones in landscape, get neither the mobile layout nor the `img {max-width:640px}` rules, which start at `min-width: 640px`.
- **Global CSS:** `* {line-height:100%; max-height:1000000px}` is a known Gmail font-boosting workaround, but the universal line-height can cause tight text in clients that honor it.
- **Dark mode:** `color-scheme` and `supported-color-schemes` are declared, and `th` is forced to `#000000 !important`. Forcing black text can become unreadable if a client inverts backgrounds. I cannot confirm this from the truncated source.
- **Fonts:** web fonts (Playfair Display, Lato) load from `fonts.gstatic.com` with `font-display: swap`. Only some `@font-face` rules have `mso-font-alt`. I could not see the `font-family` fallback stacks in the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No unresolved merge tokens (`{{ }}`, `%%`, `[[ ]]`) are visible in the supplied source. The only suspect is the empty `v1`/`v2`/`v3`/`s1`/`s2`/`s3` parameters on the mathtag pixels (see section 2). The subject line and the visible `<title>` contain no personalization.

## 5. Compliance
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post:** neither header was found. The QA tool notes the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is unconfirmed. If they are really missing, Gmail and Yahoo's bulk-sender rules (one-click unsubscribe, RFC 8058) are not met.
- **Physical address:** none detected. The HTML is truncated, so the footer was probably not scanned. Treat this as unverified rather than a confirmed CAN-SPAM violation.
- **Authentication:** SPF/DKIM/DMARC could not be assessed because `Authentication-Results` is absent. The From domain `em.1800flowers.com` is a sending subdomain. Check it against the original raw message.
- **Unsubscribe link:** not visible in the truncated source.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- **UTMs:** no `utm_*` parameters are visible. The one campaign identifier is `mi_ecmp=261030_EMAIL_18F_PZ_DV_12AMET_PROMO` on the `mi.1800flowers.com` open pixel. The CTA links route through `click.dv.1800flowers.com`, so UTM or campaign parameters may be appended at redirect time. I can't confirm this because those links were not probed.
- **Landing alignment:** can't be verified because all 24 links were skipped. Final destinations, parameter preservation across redirects and mobile landing behavior are untested.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Move `view.atdmt.com` and `pixel.mathtag.com` to HTTPS, or drop them if they are redundant.
2. Populate or remove the empty `v1`–`v3` and `s1`–`s3` parameters on the mathtag pixels.
3. Remove the duplicate `bounceexchange.com/tag/em/1622.gif`.
4. Add `alt=""` and explicit 1×1 dimensions to all tracking pixels.
5. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
6. Add an intermediate breakpoint, or raise the mobile query to about 600px, so the 419–639px range is covered.
7. Check the raw message for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, the footer address and SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment. These are unconfirmed through the relay.
8. Re-run the audit with the full, untruncated HTML. Probe a sample of the 24 redirect links by following their redirects to check landing pages and UTM carry-through.
9. Shorten the plain-text part, for example by using shorter tracking URLs, to bring URL density below 70%.
