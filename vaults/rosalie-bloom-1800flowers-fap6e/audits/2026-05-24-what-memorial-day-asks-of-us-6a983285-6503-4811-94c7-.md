# What Memorial Day Asks of Us
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-24**
## Executive summary

- This is the second editorial/cause-marketing send from 1-800-Flowers in roughly a week — last time it was Mental Health Awareness Month, now it's Memorial Day reframed as "Honoring the Fallen & Serving the Living." The format is identical: long-scroll newsletter, multiple article blocks, photos of people, no visible flower product, no offer.
- As a subscriber who joined a flower-delivery list, opening this twice in a row is the moment the relationship starts to feel off. The brand has earned the right to *one* mission email near a holiday — not a recurring magazine cadence with no commerce hook. By the time my thumb reaches the second photo block I'm wondering when they're going to actually sell me flowers for the long weekend, which is a holiday with massive floral intent (cemetery visits, hosting, sympathy).
- Verdict: the editorial respect is real and tonally appropriate for Memorial Day, but burying the commercial offer entirely on the highest-intent floral weekend of late spring is a strategic miss. A Memorial Day arrangement, a wreath, or a "send to a Gold Star family" link belongs above the fold.

## What's working

- Tone matches the holiday — somber, mission-forward, not "20% off to honor the troops" which would be worse.
- Magazine-style table of contents at the top sets expectations for a long read.
- Real photography of veterans/families reads as authentic, not stock.
- "Jim at 1-800-FLOWERS.COM" sender keeps a human voice consistent with the brand's CEO-letter format.

## What's weak

- No product, no offer, no CTA visible above the fold on the highest-intent floral holiday of the month.
- Subject "What Memorial Day Asks of Us" is preachy and demands effort to open — it asks something of *me* before offering anything.
- Second editorial-heavy send in eight days following the May 17 mental health letter — cadence is drifting from retailer to nonprofit newsletter.
- Density of small images and stacked sections makes the email feel like a fundraising appeal, which conflicts with commercial intent.
- No clear visual hierarchy — the eye doesn't land on any single hero.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a commerce module above the fold.** A single Memorial Day arrangement or wreath with "Send flowers to a veteran's family" CTA, then the editorial below. You can honor the holiday AND serve the buyer.
- 2. **Cut the editorial cadence in half.** One cause letter per holiday window, not back-to-back with Mental Health Awareness. Subscribers signed up for flowers.
- 3. **Rewrite the subject to lead with service, not duty.** "What Memorial Day asks" puts the burden on the reader; flip it to what the brand is doing.
- 4. **Trim to three sections max.** TOC + intro + one hero story + one commerce block. The current scroll is newsletter-length.
- 5. **Add a low-friction donation or "send to a Gold Star family" link** — gives mission-minded readers a click target that aligns with the editorial.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Honoring the fallen — and the families they left behind`
- **Subject Alt B:** `A flower for Memorial Day, a story for the weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `One arrangement, one family, one weekend of remembrance.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `How we're serving Gold Star families this Memorial Day — and how you can help.`
