# 20% Off Fall's Most Giftable Styles
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-31**
## Executive summary

- This is the third "Playing Favorites" / "This or That" send in about a week. It reuses the 10/23 "Pick Your Favorite Bouquet" and 10/29 "Which bouquet feels most like them?" concept almost unchanged. The subject, "20% Off Fall's Most Giftable Styles," promises a gift-focused sale. The body delivers a quiz-style head-to-head layout. The offer is real, though: a top banner reads "ENJOY 20% OFF* WITH CODE GLOW20", which is the same code as the 10/26 Halloween send.
- The layout is clean and scannable. The green-striped hero, the three matchups (Moody vs. Neutral, Farmhouse vs. Penthouse, Spooky vs. Scary) and the yellow "VS" badges make the structure obvious. The weakness is the mechanics. Each matchup has checkboxes that don't work in email, and the buttons lead to broad category pages ("Shop Fall", "Shop Halloween") rather than to the bouquet you just "picked." The 20% offer is a thin strip above the hero. It is not the focus, and the subject's "giftable" angle barely appears in the body.
- Verdict: this is competent but repetitive. A subscriber who saw 10/29 will recognize the creative immediately, and the sale hook has to do all the work.

## What's working

- The 20% off / GLOW20 banner is the first thing in the email, so the offer is visible at the top.
- The VS matchup structure is easy to read at a glance and has a clear rhythm.
- The warm fall palette and the burnt-orange CTA buttons stand out against the cream background.
- The "Shop by Category" grid at the bottom gives easy secondary routes (Birthday, Sympathy, Same-Day).
- The NYC Marathon tote block with the "Buy Now" CTA adds a distinct, concrete offer.

## What's weak

- The creative is almost identical to the 10/29 send, so it feels stale after this much repetition.
- The checkboxes under each bouquet look interactive but do nothing in an email.
- Both bouquets in a matchup share one CTA, so no single product is linked, and the shopper has to guess where each button goes.
- The offer is a small banner with an asterisk, and the terms sit in tiny footer text: "Offer ends at 11:59 p.m. (EDT) on 11/22/2026". The deadline is weeks away, so there is little urgency.
- The Spooky vs. Scary block pushes Halloween products on Oct 31 and doesn't match the "fall gift" framing in the subject.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the 20% offer the hero, for example "20% off fall gifts · code GLOW20" in large type. Move the "This or That" game below it.
- 2. Give each bouquet its own link and price, and drop the dead checkboxes.
- 3. Add a nearer deadline, such as "Ends Sunday," to create credible urgency.
- 4. Change the creative between sends so it doesn't repeat the previous week's concept.
- 5. Move the Halloween matchup out now that the holiday is over, or swap in a Thanksgiving or hostess-gift pairing.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional email with dark-mode meta tags and responsive media queries. The QA run reports a 38% pass rate with 0 errors and 5 warning groups, mostly tracking-pixel hygiene and compliance items. Several of those compliance items can't be confirmed from the data provided.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Insecure tracking pixels (confirmed):** three beacons load over `http://`:
  - `http://view.atdmt.com/FLO/view/361227669/direct/01/`
  - `http://pixel.mathtag.com/event/img?mt_id=155437&mt_adid=103507&…`
  - `http://pixel.mathtag.com/event/img?mt_id=155438&mt_adid=103507&…`

  Clients that proxy images or block mixed content may drop them, which would undercount ad-retargeting events. The `http` requests also leak the recipient's IP and user agent in cleartext.
- **Empty macro values:** both mathtag pixels have blank parameters (`v1=&v2=&v3=&s1=&s2=&s3=`). Either the values are intentionally unused or the macros failed to populate, and the HTML alone can't tell which.
- **Third-party pixel stack:** the email loads beacons from atdmt (Microsoft/Atlas), mathtag (MediaMath), `bounceexchange.com/tag/em/1622.gif` (twice), `click.dv.1800flowers.com`, and `mi.1800flowers.com/.../o.gif`. The duplicate `1622.gif` fires twice per open.
- **Unverified click links:** 33 tracking links were skipped as redirect domains, so none were checked for dead redirects, destination status, or expiry.
- **Plain-text part:** 6320 of 7755 characters (~81%) are URLs, which is above the 70% threshold. This is typically a spam-filter signal.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom restriction (confirmed in source):** `<meta name="viewport" content="… maximum-scale=1" />` prevents pinch-zoom on some mobile clients, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Missing `alt` attributes:** the QA check flagged seven images, all tracking beacons: `view.atdmt.com`, both `mathtag` pixels, `1622.gif` ×2, `click.dv…/7782dd99-…`, and `o.gif`. Add `alt=""` to mark them decorative. Their absence has no visible rendering impact.
- **Global `th` override:** `th {font-size:20px !important; color:#000000 !important}` and `th a {color:#000000 !important}` apply email-wide. In dark mode, forced black text on a dark background risks low contrast wherever `<th>` is used. I can't confirm this because the source is truncated.
- **Zoom-blocking text size rule:** `* {-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` is later overridden by `body {-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%}`. The `*` rule is redundant and conflicting, and `none` disables user text scaling in some WebKit clients.
- **Unverifiable items:** the source is truncated, so I can't check body-level alt text, `role="presentation"` on layout tables, or link text.

No issues found. No unrendered merge tokens (`{{…}}`, `%%…%%`, `[FIRSTNAME]`) appear in the visible source or QA output. The recipient identifiers in the `o.gif` URL (`mi_u=174149195`) are populated. The truncated source limits this check to the portion shown.

- **List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) not found:** the QA note says the headers may not be captured by the AgentMail relay. Treat this as unconfirmed rather than a sender defect. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require both headers, so verify against the raw message.
- **Physical address not detected:** CAN-SPAM requires one. The HTML source was truncated, so the footer is likely outside the analyzed portion. Verify before concluding it's missing.
- **Authentication-Results absent:** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown because of the relay. The From domain is `em.1800flowers.com`, a dedicated sending subdomain, but alignment can't be confirmed.
- The visible unsubscribe link and preference-center presence aren't confirmable from the truncated source.
