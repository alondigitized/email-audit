# Your FREE Flower Upgrade Ends Today 🍂🌹
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-29**
## Executive summary

- This is the last-day reminder for the "double the blooms" mechanic from yesterday's "Two Days to Get DOUBLE the Flowers 💐🍂" (9/28). The hero is strong. A red-rose bouquet sits under a giant yellow "DOUBLE" wordmark, with a "SAVE $20" badge and a "That's Amore" headline. The five small-bouquet → 2X → big-bouquet pairs are the clearest visual idea this brand has sent lately.
- The email then loses focus. Below the pairs it adds a second offer, "20% Off* Fall Best Sellers" with code BLOOM20. After that come a six-tile category grid, app download badges, a brand family strip and a wall of fine print. On a "ends today" email, that dilutes the one message that matters. The "DOUBLE UP" button copy is vague, and the "free" framing in the subject sits awkwardly next to a "SAVE $20" badge, because the customer still pays for the larger bouquet.
- Overall it's a competent, clean, seasonally right send, but it's a rerun with a deadline banner. It's better than the birthday-gift-guide rut from 9/21 to 9/26, but it isn't a step forward. I'd call it a 6.

## What's working

- The hero has a clear focal point: bold "DOUBLE" lettering, a large rose photo and a red "SAVE $20" badge.
- The before/after pairs with "2X the blooms" explain the offer without reading any copy.
- The "LAST CHANCE TO GET THIS DEAL!" banner at the very top puts the urgency above everything else.
- Product imagery is high quality, and the fall tulips and mixed roses fit the season.
- The render is clean, with no broken images, overlaps or gaps.

## What's weak

- The "DOUBLE UP" button says nothing about what you get or where it goes, and it's the only CTA above the fold.
- The 20% off BLOOM20 block competes with the $20 upgrade offer, so the customer has two deals to decode.
- The lower half, with its category grid, app badges and brand family strip, reads as generic footer and pushes past the deadline message.
- The offer is not fully honest at a glance. "Free" or "for free" sits beside a $20 discount, and the fine print is dense and small.
- This is the second day in a row on the same creative, and the sixth or seventh send in about a week, so the cadence feels heavy.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to the double-bloom story. Remove the BLOOM20 block, or move it to a one-line footer note, so the deadline has one job.
- 2. Rewrite the CTA as "Shop Double Blooms" or "Upgrade My Roses – Ends Tonight". Add a countdown or "Ends 11:59 PM ET" beside the button.
- 3. Make the "free" claim precise, for example "Get 2X the roses for $20 less". This matches the SAVE $20 badge and removes the honesty gap.
- 4. Put a price or a "from $XX" under each pair so the value is visible without a click.
- 5. Pull the deadline into the preheader, and skip a third send of identical creative once the deal has ended.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call: double your bouquet`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Five bouquets upgrade to twice the blooms until 11:59 PM ET.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Red roses, tulips & more, each $20 off. Deal ends tonight.`

# Technical Audit: "Your FREE Flower Upgrade Ends Today 🍂🌹"

The HTML source was truncated and 30 tracking links were not probed, so several compliance and continuity checks below are unconfirmed. The confirmed issues are the non-HTTPS and alt-less third-party tracking pixels and the zoom-restricting viewport and text-size CSS.

## 1. Technical Summary
The markup is a standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional table layout with VML/Outlook namespaces, a dark-mode declaration and mobile media queries. The confirmed problems are the non-HTTPS legacy tracking pixels, missing `alt` attributes on tracking images, and CSS that restricts zoom and text resizing.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unprobed links:** The 30 links on click-redirect domains were skipped by the HTTP probe, so their destinations and status codes are unverified.
- **Non-HTTPS pixels (confirmed):** Three tracking images load over `http://`. Clients that block or upgrade mixed content may drop them, which would undercount opens and conversions.
  - `http://view.atdmt.com/FLO/view/361227669/direct/01/`
  - `http://pixel.mathtag.com/event/img?mt_id=155437&mt_adid=103507&…`
  - `http://pixel.mathtag.com/event/img?mt_id=155438&mt_adid=103507&…`
- **Empty parameters:** The mathtag URLs ship with `v1=&v2=&v3=&s1=&s2=&s3=`, so no data is being passed to the pixel.
- **Duplicate pixel:** `https://bounceexchange.com/tag/em/1622.gif` appears twice, which may double-fire the beacon.
- **Many third-party beacons:** The email carries beacons from at least four systems: atdmt, mathtag, bounceexchange, and 1800flowers' own `click.dv` and `mi` open pixels. This adds request weight and privacy-filter exposure.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** The QA check found no `alt` on these images. All appear to be tracking pixels, so they should carry `alt=""` and a 1×1 size to be treated as decorative.
  - `view.atdmt.com/.../direct/01/`
  - both `pixel.mathtag.com/event/img` pixels
  - both `bounceexchange.com/tag/em/1622.gif` instances
  - `click.dv.1800flowers.com/o/529c2124-…`
  - `mi.1800flowers.com/p/up/ada90b86b2846dd9/o.gif`
- **Zoom restricted:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom in clients that honor it (WCAG 1.4.4).
- **Text resizing disabled:** The universal rule `* {-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; …}` disables text scaling. The later `body` rule resets it to `100%`, but the universal rule can still override it on descendants.
- **Global `th` rule:** `th {font-size:20px !important; color:#000000 !important}` forces black text regardless of dark mode. Any `th` on a dark background under `color-scheme: light dark` could lose contrast. The truncated source can't confirm whether that happens.
- **Generic `<title>`:** `<title>1800Flowers.com</title>` doesn't describe the message.
- **Web fonts:** Playfair Display and Lato load from `fonts.gstatic.com` with `font-display:swap` and `mso-font-alt` fallbacks. Most clients ignore `@font-face`, so this is fine as long as the fallbacks are set.
