# Double blooms + 20% off = yes please 🌸
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- After two consecutive bait-and-switch credit emails (July 13's phantom $13.80, July 14's phantom $15), this send is a genuine reset. 1-800-Flowers leads with a real product concept — double blooms, meaning visibly more flowers per arrangement — backs it with a clean product grid showing five arrangements with 2X badges and prices, then layers on a 20% off secondary offer below. Both promises from the subject line are present in the body. That alone puts this above most of the recent cadence.
- The weakness is structural: "double blooms" and "20% off Summer Classics" are two separate value propositions fighting for the same inbox moment. The email never integrates them — the 20% section sits mid-scroll as an afterthought after the 2X product showcase ends. A subscriber who stops scrolling after the first product grid never sees the discount.
- The "SHOP NOW" CTA in the hero is the single biggest missed opportunity here. "Double blooms + 20% off = yes please" is a personality-forward subject but the landing CTA is the most generic button possible. On a day when this is clearly the freshest, most honest send in two weeks, that laziness costs real clicks.

## What's working

- **Subject line delivers**: the email shows both "double blooms" (2X product badges) and "20% off" — first honest subject-to-body match since July 11.
- **2X badges on product thumbnails** immediately communicate the double-blooms concept without requiring any copy read; it scans at a glance.
- **Product grid is specific**: five named arrangements with visible prices give the subscriber something concrete to shop.
- **"Summer of Double Blooms" hero** is a clean, non-cluttered banner — better visual hierarchy than recent sends that stacked multiple competing messages in the hero.
- **Clean render**: no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breaks visible.

## What's weak

- **Two competing offers, neither reinforced**: the "double blooms" quantity pitch and the "20% off classics" price pitch are separate sections that never cross-pollinate (no "get double blooms at 20% off" moment).
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is dead weight**: after a specific, personality-driven subject, the button should be "Shop Double Blooms" or "Claim 20% Off" — not the most generic four characters in email marketing.
- **No urgency signal anywhere**: no deadline, no "offer ends Sunday," no countdown. Summer is vague; subscribers learned from the July 13–14 sends that credit offers don't expire until they already have.
- **Email is too long**: hero → five product tiles → 20% off section → orchid product → category grid → loyalty sign-up → app download. The loyalty and app sections are persistent clutter from every recent send and dilute the one clear message this email could have owned.
- **"Double blooms" concept is unexplained**: what exactly is the product — twice the stems at the same price? A limited-edition size tier? A small callout in the hero explaining the mechanics would close the loop.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the 20% off a deadline-bound offer** — add "Ends Sunday" or a specific date to the 20% off section. This is the single change with the biggest click-lift potential and costs nothing to implement.
- 2. **Rewrite the hero CTA** from "SHOP NOW" to "Shop Double Blooms" (primary) or "Get 20% Off Classics" (secondary). One hero, one specific verb phrase.
- 3. **Add a one-sentence explainer to the hero**: "Every arrangement in this collection includes double the usual stem count — same price." Removes the ambiguity that makes subscribers hesitate.
- 4. **Cut the email**: drop the Shop by Category grid and the app download section. Both exist on every send; they add no incremental value here and extend scroll depth past where most subscribers give up.
- 5. **Set a preheader that creates urgency or specificity** the subject line doesn't.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Double the blooms, 20% off — offer ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `This week only: twice the flowers, less the cost 🌸`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `More stems, less spend — your summer upgrade is here`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop double blooms before they sell out`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** prevents user-initiated pinch-to-zoom on mobile. This is an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4) and will be flagged by accessibility tools.
  ```html
  <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
  ```

- **Mobile breakpoint at 418px** is non-standard. Industry baseline is 600px; 418px only catches the narrowest devices and leaves a gap for 419–599px viewports where responsive rules don't fire.
  ```css
  @media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { ... }
  ```

- **Google Fonts loaded from `gstatic.com`** via `@font-face`. These are blocked by Gmail (web and app), Apple Mail with remote image blocking enabled, and all Outlook versions. The fallback situation is inconsistent:
  - Playfair Display variants include `mso-font-alt:'Times New Roman'` ✓
  - **All Lato `@font-face` declarations are missing `mso-font-alt`** — Outlook will fall back to a default system font (typically Arial or Calibri) without the explicit hint, potentially breaking typographic hierarchy.

- **One Playfair Display weight-900 loads `.woff` instead of `.woff2`:**
  ```css
  src:url(https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/playfairdisplay/v22/...btM.woff) format('woff');
  ```
  All other weights use `.woff2`. `.woff` is ~30% larger and lacks modern compression. Likely an authoring error; the `.woff2` variant for that weight should exist.

- **`* { line-height: 100% }` universal selector** is an extremely broad reset that overrides all inherited line-height, including custom values set further down the cascade. Can cause clipped descenders on text elements if not overridden explicitly everywhere.

- **`.mobileshow` is hidden by default** with `display:none !important; visibility:hidden !important` but the class name implies it should reveal on mobile. The truncated source prevents confirming whether a complementary `@media` rule shows it — if not present, any `.mobileshow` content is permanently hidden on all clients.

- **`supported-color-schemes` in `<meta>`** is not a valid meta name. The correct tag is `<meta name="color-scheme" ...>` (already present). The `supported-color-schemes` version is a no-op and can be removed.
  ```html
  <meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light dark">  <!-- invalid, no-op -->
  ```

- **`.appleLinks a { color:#000000 !important }`** suppresses Apple Mail's auto-detected link styling for phone numbers, addresses, and dates. If any body copy contains these, they will appear as black undecorated text and be non-obvious as interactive — accessibility concern.
