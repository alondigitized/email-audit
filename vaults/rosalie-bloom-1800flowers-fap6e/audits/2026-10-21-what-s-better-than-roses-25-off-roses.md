# What's Better Than Roses? 25% Off Roses
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-21**
## Executive summary

- This is a loud, simple offer email. The hero says "DEAL OF THE WEEK 25% OFF" in big gold type on burnt orange, with a "SHOP AUTUMN ROSES" button and a dense photo of red, orange and yellow roses. A "PLUS, A FREE VASE!" badge sits on the corner of the photo. The hero is the strongest part, and you know the offer in about a second.
- The email undercuts itself below the hero. "Behind the Bouquet" is a quality-control story with no price or product link. Then a second promo, "20% Off* Fall Best Sellers, Use Code: GLOW20," competes with the 25%. The six-tile category grid and the app-download block turn a focused rose promo into a generic catalog. The subject also asks "What's Better Than Roses?" and answers with "25% Off Roses," which reads as filler.
- Judgment: a competent, seasonal send with a clear hero and a muddy back half. It's also the second 25%-off send in two days, after "25% Off. Which Will You Choose?" on 2026-10-20, and it follows the 10/12 tiered "Save Up to 25%" and the 10/17 $15 credit. This brand rotates near-identical discounts, so a "Deal of the Week" label feels routine.

## What's working

- The hero offer is huge and legible: "25% OFF" in gold on orange, with a white "SHOP AUTUMN ROSES" button directly under it.
- The rose photography is rich and seasonal, and the warm autumn palette fits mid-October.
- The "Plus, a free vase!" badge adds a tangible sweetener beyond the percentage.
- The "Behind the Bouquet" block with the vase-styled bouquet gives a quality reason to buy ("petal count, stem height, head size").
- The layout is clean, with no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- Two discounts compete: 25% off roses at the top and 20% off with code GLOW20 in the middle. Neither is clearly the main offer.
- Only the hero says "Deal of the Week." The real end date (11/22/2026) is in the footer fine print, a month away, so there's no real urgency.
- The "Behind the Bouquet" section has no CTA, price or link. It takes up a full screen of scroll for a brand story.
- The four-product grid and the nine-tile category grid dilute the rose message. The product captions are small and grey, with no prices.
- The free-vase claim has no visible conditions near the hero. The asterisks and the dense legal text at the bottom make the offer feel less clean.
- It has the same 25%-off rhythm as the 10/12 and 10/20 sends, so it feels like a rerun.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the email about one offer. Cut the GLOW20 block, or fold it into the hero as one line ("25% off roses, plus a free vase"). Put a price or "from $__" on the featured bouquet.
- 2. Cut the "Behind the Bouquet" block down to one short line next to a second CTA, or move it below a row of rose products with prices and "Shop Now" buttons.
- 3. Add real urgency with a specific end date in the hero ("Ends Sunday") instead of burying 11/22 in the footer. Put the free-vase terms right under the badge.
- 4. Replace the nine-tile category grid with three or four rose variants (autumn mix, red, long-stem, dozen), each linking to a product.
- 5. Rotate the offer structure. After two straight 25% sends, lead with the free vase or a bundle so the inbox history doesn't read as a repeat.
- **Subject Alt A:** `25% Off Autumn Roses + a Free Vase`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Our Fall Rose Mix: 25% Off, Free Vase`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Hand-picked petals, long stems, and a free vase with your order. This week only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Farm-fresh autumn roses, 25% off through Sunday. Free vase included.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML source is a standard XHTML 1.0 table layout with dark-mode meta tags and web-font fallbacks. Real findings are limited to a missing `lang` attribute, `maximum-scale=1` in the viewport, alt-less tracking pixels, and two legacy `http://` pixels. The compliance and continuity checks can't be confirmed because the HTML is truncated and AgentMail strips headers. QA reported 0 failures and 5 warnings, with a 38% pass rate.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Click links are unverified.** 20 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe (redirect domains), so none of the CTA destinations were checked for 200 status, redirect chains or expiry.
- **Two `http://` pixels:**
  - `http://view.atdmt.com/FLO/view/361227669/direct/01/`
  - `http://pixel.mathtag.com/event/img?mt_id=155437&mt_adid=103507...` and the matching `mt_id=155438` pixel

  Mixed-content and privacy-proxy clients may block them. The `atdmt.com` (Atlas) and `mathtag.com` (MediaMath) ad-tech endpoints look legacy, and their continued operation is unconfirmed. If they are dead, the pixels only add load and a failed request.
- **Empty parameters in the mathtag pixels:** `v1=&v2=&v3=&s1=&s2=&s3=` are all blank. This is either intentional or unpopulated dynamic values. Confirm with the ESP.
- **Unexplained third-party pixel:** `https://bounceexchange.com/tag/em/1622.gif` appears twice, so the same beacon fires twice per open.
- **User identifier in a pixel URL:** `https://mi.1800flowers.com/p/up/ada90b86b2846dd9/o.gif?mi_u=174149195&...` carries `mi_u=174149195` in a URL. This is first-party and standard for this kind of beacon, but it is a persistent identifier.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **No `lang` attribute.** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" ...>` has no `lang` or `xml:lang`. Screen readers must guess the language.
- **Viewport blocks zoom.** `<meta name="viewport" content="... maximum-scale=1" />` disables pinch-zoom, which conflicts with WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **Missing `alt` attributes on 7 flagged images:**
  - `view.atdmt.com`
  - both `pixel.mathtag.com` pixels
  - `bounceexchange.com/.../1622.gif` (×2)
  - `click.dv.1800flowers.com/o/e3fed6a1-...`
  - `mi.1800flowers.com/.../o.gif`

  These are tracking pixels, so the impact is low. They should still carry `alt=""` so assistive tech skips them instead of announcing the filename or URL.
- **Global `th` rule:** `th {font-size:20px !important; color:#000000 !important}` applies to every `<th>` in the email. With `color-scheme: light dark` declared, forced black text could be unreadable on a dark background. I can't confirm this from the truncated source.
- **Generic title:** `<title>1800Flowers.com</title>` doesn't describe the message. This is minor.
- **Not assessable:** alt text on content images, link text and tap-target sizes all fall in the truncated portion.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (`{{ }}`, `%%`, `[[ ]]`) appear in the visible source or the QA output. The blank `v1`–`s3` pixel parameters above are the only possible exception.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
None of these three warnings is a confirmed defect.
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found.** QA notes the AgentMail relay may drop these headers. Verify at the sending ESP, because Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require one-click unsubscribe.
- **No physical address detected.** The source is truncated, so the footer wasn't scanned. Check the full HTML and re-run the check against it.
- **`Authentication-Results` missing.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status for `em.1800flowers.com` is unknown. Check the raw message headers upstream of the relay.
- **Plain-text part is mostly URLs.** 3824 of 5166 characters (about 74%) are URLs, which is above the 70% threshold. The long tracking URLs in the text part raise spam-filter risk and hurt readability.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Unable to verify. No click URLs are visible in the truncated source, and all 20 tracking links were skipped.
- The only campaign identifier visible is `mi_ecmp=261021_EMAIL_18F_PZ_DV_12AMET_PROMO`, on the open pixel. It has no standard `utm_source`, `utm_medium` or `utm_campaign` equivalent.

- Whether the landing page matches the "25% Off Roses" offer in the subject is also unconfirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add `lang="en"` (and `xml:lang`) to `<html>`.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
3. Add `alt=""` to all tracking pixels.
4. Upgrade the `atdmt.com` and `mathtag.com` pixels to HTTPS, or remove them if the vendors are retired. Deduplicate the double `bounceexchange` beacon.
5. Check the raw message at the ESP for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and the DKIM/SPF/DMARC results. Re-run the physical-address check on the full, untruncated HTML.
6. Resolve the CTA redirect chains with a real HTTP follow. Confirm the final landing URL, its status code, UTM retention and offer match.
7. Shorten the tracking URLs in the plain-text part to bring the URL ratio under 70%.
