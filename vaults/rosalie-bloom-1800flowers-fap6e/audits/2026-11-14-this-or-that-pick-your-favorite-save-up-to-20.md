# This or That? Pick Your Favorite & Save Up to 20%
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-14**
## Executive summary

- This is the seventh send in about three weeks built on the "Playing Favorites / This or That" concept. It follows 11/07 ("20% Off. Now the Only Hard Part Is Choosing.") and 11/09 ("Find Your Favorite, Then Save Up to 20%"). The render is clean and seasonal. The olive and rust palette suits fall, and the two bouquet face-offs (Moody vs. Neutral, Farmhouse vs. Penthouse) are visually strong. The offer is still the weak point. "Up to 20% off* with code FLOWERSALE" sits in a thin rust bar above the logo, and the hero never repeats it.
- A subscriber who has seen this exact layout several times will treat it as wallpaper. The "vs" interaction implies a poll or quiz, but both pairs link to a generic "Shop Fall" page. The checkboxes beside the product names look clickable but are decorative. There is no price, no member benefit, no deadline in the body, and no delivery cue even though Thanksgiving is close.
- Judgement: a pleasant, safe send, but fatigued and under-monetized. It deserves a 5, not a 6, because the cadence is now the biggest issue.

## What's working

- The hero is clean, with elegant script type and a single bold "Shop Now" button on the olive background.
- The two "vs" matchups are easy to scan. The yellow "VS" badge gives each a clear visual anchor.
- The bouquet photography is crisp and seasonal, with rich harvest colors that fit November.
- The "Shop by Category" grid (Birthday, Sympathy, Same-Day, etc.) gives off-ramps for other needs.
- There are no render bugs. Images load, text is legible, and the layout is tidy.

## What's weak

- The offer is only in the top bar. The hero never says "20% off," so the savings are easy to miss.
- The "Shop Fall" buttons repeat across both pairs and go to a generic destination rather than the specific bouquets.
- The checkboxes imply voting or selecting, but they do nothing. That is a mild false affordance.
- No prices, no urgency (the "Offer ends 11/26" is buried in fine print), and no loyalty or Passport benefit.
- Footer text is dense. The wine and age disclaimer is clutter in an email with no wine in the main content.
- The concept is stale, repeated from 10/23, 10/29, 10/31, 11/04, 11/07 and 11/09.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the offer in the hero: "Save 20% with code FLOWERSALE" under the headline, with the end date next to it. That is the single biggest lift.
- 2. Link each bouquet to its own product page, and make the checkbox labels real links. Show prices as "$XX, now $XX."
- 3. Retire the "Playing Favorites" template for a few sends. Switch to a Thanksgiving-delivery cutoff or centerpiece angle for something new.
- 4. Replace "Shop Fall" with specific CTAs such as "Shop Autumn Fields" and "Shop Graceful Fall."
- 5. Add a sentence of social proof or a ratings line under each pair.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Autumn Fields or Graceful Fall? 20% Off Either`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Thanksgiving Centerpieces, 20% Off Through 11/26`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Moody or neutral, farmhouse or penthouse. Use code FLOWERSALE at checkout.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Order by 11/26 and save on bouquets that arrive fresh for the holiday.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML source is mostly sound, with VML/MSO fallbacks, a viewport meta tag and web-font fallbacks. The confirmed technical issues are non-HTTPS tracking pixels, missing alt text on tracking images, zoom restrictions, and a URL-heavy plain-text part. The compliance and authentication warnings come from the relay and the truncated source, so they can't be confirmed as sender defects.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **27 tracking links were skipped** because they are click-redirect domains (for example `click.dv.1800flowers.com`). Destination resolution and link health are unverified.
- **Non-HTTPS pixels** may be blocked or trigger mixed-content handling in some clients:
  - `http://view.atdmt.com/FLO/view/361227669/direct/01/`
  - `http://pixel.mathtag.com/event/img?mt_id=155437&mt_adid=103507...`
  - `http://pixel.mathtag.com/event/img?mt_id=155438&mt_adid=103507...`
  - If these are blocked, the conversion and retargeting signal is lost.
- **Many third-party pixels** load in one send: atdmt, two mathtag, `bounceexchange.com/tag/em/1622.gif` (twice), the `click.dv.1800flowers.com/o/...` open pixel and `mi.1800flowers.com/.../o.gif`.
  - `1622.gif` appears twice, which is likely duplicate firing.
  - The mathtag URLs have empty `v1=&v2=&v3=&s1=&s2=&s3=` parameters, so the template variables aren't populated. This is a minor data-quality gap, not a visible bug.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom is restricted.** `<meta name="viewport" content="... maximum-scale=1" />` blocks pinch-zoom on some mobile clients, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Text resizing is disabled in the universal rule.** `* {-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}` is later overridden on `body` with `100%`, so the effect is mixed. The global `* {line-height:100%}` is also tight for body text.
- **Alt text is missing** on all the tracking images listed in section 2.
  - For 1x1 pixels this is a low-severity QA flag.
  - It does show the pixels aren't marked `alt=""` or `role="presentation"`.
  - Whether the content images have alt text can't be confirmed, because the source is truncated and QA flagged only tracking images.
- **The mobile breakpoint is narrow.** `@media (max-width: 418px)` leaves 419–600px viewports (large phones and small tablets) on the desktop layout. The `.mobile {width:414px !important}` rules also hard-code a pixel width.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The visible source shows no unresolved merge tokens. The source is truncated, so the body copy is unverified.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Unsubscribe headers and authentication results are not confirmed defects.** The QA reports `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) and `Authentication-Results` as missing, but notes the AgentMail relay may strip them. Check the raw message in a native mailbox before treating this as a sender failure.
- **No physical address was detected.** The source is truncated, so the footer wasn't fully inspected, and this needs manual confirmation. Large senders typically include one.
- **Plain text is 5168/7027 characters of URLs (74%).** This is above the 70% QA threshold and can add spam-filter risk. It comes from tracked URLs in the plain-text part.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **UTM parameters can't be verified.** The 27 click-redirect links were not probed, and the visible tracking parameters are in the pixel URLs.
  - `mi_ecmp=261114_EMAIL_18F_PZ_STO_DV_12AMET_PROMO` and `mi_cid` / `mi_mid` are present on the Movable Ink-style pixel, so campaign attribution exists on the vendor side.
  - Whether the landing URLs carry `utm_*` or equivalent parameters, and whether they match the "Save Up to 20%" offer, is unconfirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Serve `view.atdmt.com` and `pixel.mathtag.com` over HTTPS, or drop them if the vendors are obsolete.
2. Remove the duplicate `bounceexchange.com/tag/em/1622.gif` pixel.
3. Add `alt=""` (and optionally `role="presentation"`) to all tracking pixels.
4. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag, and review the global `text-size-adjust:none` and `line-height:100%` on `*`.
5. Raise the mobile breakpoint above 418px (for example 480–600px) and replace the fixed `414px` widths with fluid ones.
6. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and the SPF/DKIM/DMARC results from the raw headers of a message delivered directly to a mailbox, since the AgentMail relay may strip them.
7. Confirm the physical address in the full, untruncated footer.
8. Shorten the plain-text part by trimming the tracked URL length, and re-probe the 27 tracked links with a redirect-following check to validate destinations and UTM/campaign parameters.
