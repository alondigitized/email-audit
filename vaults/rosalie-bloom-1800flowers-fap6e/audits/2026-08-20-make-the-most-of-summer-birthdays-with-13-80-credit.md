# Make the most of summer birthdays with $13.80 credit
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-20**
## Executive summary

- This is 1-800-Flowers back to the birthday-credit format they ran on Aug 15 ("You've got $13.80 to spend—let's make their birthday special," 5.0/10) — same $13.80-credit mechanic, same "3 ways to gift" structure (flowers, cakes/treats, personalized gifts), just re-titled "We Know Birthdays" with a founder quote bolted on for warmth. The credit code (SUNNY1380) and $49.99 minimum are prominent up top, the hero is clean, and the product grid is well-organized by gifting occasion — this is competent, on-brand execution.
- But it's derivative of a send from four days ago, and the "$13.80 credit" hook is a gimmick that reads as fake-precise rather than generous — nobody believes 1-800-Flowers is handing out $13.80 out of the goodness of their heart, it's obviously a % off dressed up as a dollar amount. The founder-quote block ("My friends and families who have summer birthdays...") is a nice trust-building touch but is buried mid-scroll where most readers won't reach it. Net: fine creative, tired mechanic, weak differentiation from four days ago.

## What's working

- Hero is clean and legible — "We Know Birthdays" headline, clear subhead, orange CTA button, and a real bouquet photo all land without clutter.
- Credit code and spend minimum are visible immediately in the top banner (SUNNY1380 / $49.99 minimum).
- Product sections are logically segmented — flowers, cakes/treats, personalized gifts, then a "Shop by Category" grid — giving multiple entry points for different gift needs.
- The founder quote adds a human, brand-trust signal that most flash-sale emails skip.
- No render bugs — images, text, and buttons all display cleanly.

## What's weak

- This is a near-repeat of the Aug 15 "$13.80 credit" birthday email — same mechanic, same "3 ways to gift" structure, four days apart.
- $13.80 is an odd, hard-to-trust number — it reads as an obfuscated percentage-off, not a genuine credit, which undercuts the "We Know Birthdays" warmth angle.
- No visible expiration/urgency on the credit — nothing tells the reader why to act today vs. next week.
- The founder quote and product depth are buried several scrolls down; a mobile reader skimming the top 2 screens sees only the hero and first bouquet grid.
- Product tiles show name and price-free product cards with no pricing visible in this crop, so there's no visible price anchor to justify the $49.99 minimum.

## Recommendations

- 1. Retire the "$13.80 credit" mechanic for now — it was already used Aug 15 and reads as gimmicky; either go back to a straightforward %-off or lean into "free gift with purchase" like the earlier vase promos, which tested better (Aug 12, 9.0/10).
- 2. Add a real deadline to the credit ("expires tonight," "through Sunday") — as-is there's zero urgency to open this over a competing promo.
- 3. Move the founder quote or a shortened version of it higher, near the hero, to differentiate from the identical Aug 15 structure.
- 4. Show prices on the product tiles so the $49.99 minimum feels achievable at a glance.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your $13.80 birthday credit expires Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Summer birthday? Here's a gift idea (+ credit)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Flowers, cakes, or a personalized gift — all under one credit.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `SUNNY1380 unlocks $13.80 toward any birthday gift, min. $49.99.`
