# Our florist’s favorite unique varieties are 25% off
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-01**
## Executive summary

- This is 1-800-Flowers running the "Florist Picks" editorial format again — a designer persona (Mallory Green, illustrated sketch avatar) curating a handful of arrangements, structurally identical to the July 23 "My Summer Birthday Faves" and July 25 "5 Hottest Birthday Blooms" listicle sends, just re-skinned around unique/textural varieties instead of birthday. The top banner offer (25% off, code BLOOM25) is clear and consistent with the brand's now-familiar sitewide discount play from July 28-30, so at least the deal itself is legible and on-brand.
- Where it falls down is focus: five different hero products (Delphinium Meadow, Dreamy Haze, Pacific Drift, Sunflower Radiance) each get their own "Shop Now," plus a Friendship Day countdown module, plus a 9-tile category grid, plus a Celebrations Passport membership pitch, plus app download and brand-family footer — all before you'd finish scrolling. It reads like four separate marketing asks stapled into one email rather than one persuasive narrative. The florist-persona hook is a nice differentiator, but it's buried under an unusually long, low-hierarchy stack.
- Net: competent execution, on-brand, no render bugs, but this is a cadence/format fatigue problem — third near-identical "florist curation" listicle in under two weeks — and a hierarchy problem, not a creative-quality one.

## What's working

- Top banner is unambiguous: "25% OFF* WITH CODE BLOOM25" — this is the strongest, most scannable element in the whole email.
- The Mallory Green illustrated-portrait device gives this send a distinct visual hook versus the brand's usual straight product-shot format.
- Product photography is clean and consistent (glass-vase bouquet shots, no broken images, no lorem ipsum, no overlapping text).
- Category grid near the bottom (Birthday, Romance, Sympathy, Best Sellers, Plants, Same-Day, Summer, Greeting Cards, Gourmet Gifts) gives a low-friction path for browsers who don't want the curated picks.
- Celebrations Passport membership module is clearly separated with its own visual block and a specific benefit (free shipping/no service charge).

## What's weak

- No single hero — four products (Delphinium Meadow, Dreamy Haze, Pacific Drift, Sunflower Radiance) each compete for the same "Shop Now" click, diluting the primary CTA.
- The email is very long and stacks unrelated asks (florist picks → Friendship Day countdown → category grid → membership → app download → brand family) with weak visual separation between them — just thin divider lines.
- "Florist Picks" curation format is the third variant of this same editorial device in ~10 days (birthday faves 7/23, hottest blooms 7/25, now unique varieties), and this send doesn't clearly differentiate why today's picks matter now.
- The Friendship Day countdown module is visually the weakest block in the email — small text, no visible deadline/date, easy to miss between the sunflower CTA and the category grid.
- 25% off is prominent in the banner but never restated near the individual product CTAs, so by the time a reader reaches "Shop Botanical Luxury" three screens down, the offer context has faded.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut to one hero product (or at most two) and move the rest to a "More from Mallory" secondary row — right now five competing CTAs above a category grid is CTA overload; a single dominant hero would concentrate clicks.
- 2. Restate the 25% off / BLOOM25 code directly under each product CTA, not just in the top banner — offer visibility decays badly over a scroll this long.
- 3. Give the Friendship Day module real visual weight (a countdown clock treatment, a firm date) or cut it — as-is it's a weak, easily-skipped insert that breaks the florist-picks narrative without earning its place.
- 4. Shorten the email — merge the category grid and membership module into a tighter closing block; the current length (5+ full-screen sections) is a scroll-fatigue risk on mobile.
- 5. Vary the format next send — three "curated picks" listicles in under two weeks risks subscribers pattern-matching and skimming past the persona hook entirely.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- Two tracking pixels served over plain `http://` (atdmt.com, mathtag.com `mt_id=155437`) and a third mathtag pixel (`mt_id=155438`) also over `http://` — non-HTTPS resources may be blocked by mail clients enforcing mixed-content policies.

These are tracking pixels (1x1), so missing alt is low-impact for accessibility but still fails WCAG image-text-alternative checks for screen readers that announce broken/blocked images.
- Web fonts loaded via `@font-face` from `fonts.gstatic.com` (Playfair Display, Lato) with `mso-font-alt:'Times New Roman'` fallback declared only for the *regular/italic 400* weights of Playfair Display — the 600/900 weight declarations omit `mso-font-alt`, so Outlook/Windows desktop clients (which don't support web fonts) will fall back to the default MSO font rather than the intended Times New Roman substitute for those weights.
- `max-height:1000000px` and `line-height:100%` global reset combined with `.ExternalClass` fixes indicate Outlook.com/Windows Mail-specific hacks are in place — no issues found with this portion.
