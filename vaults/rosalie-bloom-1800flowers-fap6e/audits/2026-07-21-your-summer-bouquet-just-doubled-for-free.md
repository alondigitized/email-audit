# Your summer bouquet just doubled (for free) ☀️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- This is 1-800-Flowers returning to their highest-performing creative format — the "double blooms" concept — and dressing it in a summer skin. The hero immediately pays off the subject line: a coral rose bouquet with a prominent "DOUBLE" badge and a red "$29" price anchor, followed by a grid of five named summer varieties (Pink Lemonade, Assorted Blooms, Summer Meadows, Carnation Blooms, Vibrant Blooms), each with a "2X" badge. This is the same skeleton as the July 15 send ("Double blooms + 20% off = yes please 🌸"), which scored a 9, and the July 20 "DOUBLE = 💐💐" send, also a 9. The creative is proven.
- What separates this from July 20 is the bonus offer: a 20% off code for "Summer Classics" below the product grid, plus an orchid plant image to extend the offer. That layered value prop (free doubling + 20% off) is genuinely strong. The weakness is compression — at mobile screenshot scale, the product names and price points become hard to read, and the 20% off section lands below the fold after a long product scroll. Subscribers who don't scroll miss the second offer entirely.
- Clear judgement: this is the brand's best format, executed reliably, with a meaningful stacked offer. It's a 9-tier creative that could flirt with a 10 if the discount were surfaced higher in the layout.

## What's working

- **Hero immediately pays off the subject line** — "Summer of Double Blooms" headline + DOUBLE badge on the hero bouquet is locked to the promise "your summer bouquet just doubled."
- **Consistent 2X badge treatment across every product tile** — at a glance you understand the mechanic without reading copy.
- **Named summer varieties** give the grid a curated, editorial feel vs. a clearance dump.
- **$29 anchor price** in the hero sets a low mental threshold before the viewer has committed to scrolling.
- **Stacked offer** — free doubling + 20% off Summer Classics is genuinely two reasons to click, which is above average for this sender.

## What's weak

- **20% off code is buried below a long product grid** — subscribers who don't scroll past five product tiles miss the best lift signal entirely.
- **Product tile prices are unreadable at mobile scale** — the "$41," "$29" etc. badges are visible but require squinting; fine print-level text undermines the value signal.
- **No urgency mechanic visible** — "free doubling" reads as evergreen; there's no countdown, no "ends Sunday," no scarcity cue to push a decision.
- **Shop-by-Category grid at the bottom is generic filler** — it dilutes focus and doesn't connect to the summer/double-bloom narrative.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface the 20% off code in the hero** — stack "double the blooms + 20% off" as two lines in the hero block, not separated by five product tiles. This single change is worth the most.
- 2. **Add a deadline** — "Through Sunday" or "This week only" on the doubling offer. The July 15 send had the same gap; this cadence is losing urgency it could easily manufacture.
- 3. **Drop the Shop-by-Category section** — it's deadweight at the bottom of a thematic email; replace with a single "add a card" upsell that matches the summer gifting frame.
- 4. **Increase price badge size** on product tiles — they need to be legible at a glance on mobile; right now they're blink-and-miss.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Double blooms free + 20% off — summer deal inside ☀️`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your bouquet just doubled. Plus 20% off this week only.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Five summer varieties, all doubled at no extra cost. Code inside.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `20% off Summer Classics + free double blooms — offer ends Sunday.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[MEDIUM] Mobile breakpoint at 418px is too narrow**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { ... }
```
Modern iPhones (SE3: 375pt, 14 Pro: 393pt, 15 Plus: 430pt) and Android devices at 480px+ will not receive the mobile layout. Standard safe breakpoint is `480px`; `418px` leaves a gap where content renders desktop-width on phones wider than 418px logical pixels.

**[MEDIUM] Dark mode declared but no overrides present**
Both `<meta name="color-scheme" content="light dark">` and `:root { color-scheme: light dark }` declare dark mode support, but no `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block is present. Apple Mail and Samsung Mail will auto-invert or partially invert elements (backgrounds, text) without intentional overrides, producing unpredictable results.

**[LOW] Inconsistent font format: `.woff` vs `.woff2` for Playfair Display 900**
```css
/* All other weights use .woff2 */
src:url(...nuFvD...PKfsukDT.woff) format('woff'); /* weight:900, normal */
```
The weight-900 normal variant uses `.woff` (larger file). Minor inconsistency; woff2 preferred.

**[LOW] Missing `mso-font-alt` on Playfair Display weight-900 italic**
The `font-weight:900; font-style:italic` `@font-face` block has no `mso-font-alt` fallback, while other weights specify `mso-font-alt:'Times New Roman'`. Outlook will render with its default serif rather than the intended fallback.

**[LOW] `.mobileshow` class naming contradiction**
```css
.mobileshow { display:none !important; visibility:hidden !important; width:0px; overflow:hidden !important; }
```
The class is named `.mobileshow` but hides content globally. The mobile override surfaces `.showmobile` (not `.mobileshow`). If any element uses `.mobileshow` expecting mobile visibility, it will remain hidden on all clients. Verify no elements are incorrectly assigned this class.

**[LOW] Universal `line-height:100%` reset**
```css
* { line-height: 100% }
```
Applies to all elements including buttons and links. Can cause clipped descenders in certain Outlook versions and interfere with CTA button text vertical alignment.
