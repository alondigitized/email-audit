# All Favorites. All Celebration. Up to 25% Off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- This is 1-800-Flowers returning to the exact same America's 250th "Happy Birthday!" creative well they've been mining since June 29 — and on July 11, a full week after the Fourth of July has passed, it lands as a relic. The hero shows a birthday cake with patriotic flag staging, the three-tier discount (15% / 20% / 25% off), and a "SHOP NOW" button — all pulled directly from the July 1 and July 6 sends that scored 8/10 and 7/10 here. The subject line tries to disguise the recycled creative by going generic ("All Favorites. All Celebration."), but the body creative tells a different story.
- The email is structurally clean: a recognizable brand, a visible discount, a product grid, a category browse, a loyalty section (Celebrations Passport), an app download nudge, and a family-of-brands footer. Everything renders without visible bugs. But clean execution on a stale concept doesn't recover the core problem.
- The judgment is clear: this send is coasting. After five-plus patriotic sends, the brand needed to hard-pivot by July 8 at the latest — back to birthday occasions, a summer fragrance angle, anything. Sending the same America's 250th creative on July 11 is a cadence failure, not a creative one.

## What's working

- Brand recognition is instant — logo and color palette are immediately identifiable as 1-800-Flowers.
- Three concrete discount tiers (15% / 20% / 25% off) are visible in the hero without scrolling.
- "SHOP NOW" CTA is clearly differentiated and unambiguous.
- Celebrations Passport loyalty section adds a second conversion path for members near the fold.
- No render bugs — no broken images, overlapping text, or layout breakage visible.

## What's weak

- Patriotic/America's 250th creative is week-old — the July 4th moment expired before this landed (July 11), making the flag-and-birthday-cake hero feel like an unsent draft.
- Three-tier discount (15/20/25%) splits attention without clarifying what qualifies for each tier — the "up to" hedge erodes trust rather than building excitement.
- "All Favorites. All Celebration." in the subject line is brand-voice wallpaper — it says nothing about what's actually in the email.
- No single hero product — the best-sellers grid is a buffet, not a recommendation; nothing earns a click on its own merits.
- This is at minimum the fourth near-identical promotional send in two weeks; subscriber fatigue is real and visible in the creative repetition.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the patriotic creative immediately.** The July 4th window closed days ago. Replace the hero with a summer-gifting or "just because" occasion frame — the brand history shows their July 7 "Summer birthday season is here!" send knew this pivot needed to happen.
- 2. **Collapse the three-tier discount to one sharp number.** "25% off sitewide this weekend" is more actionable than a tiered structure that requires decoding. If tiers must stay, explain them in one line.
- 3. **Pick one hero product.** A single well-photographed arrangement with a name, price, and "Order by [date] for delivery this weekend" line will outperform a best-sellers grid for click intent.
- 4. **Rewrite the subject to match the post-holiday moment.** The generic "All Favorites" phrasing wastes the subject line.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Summer flowers, 25% off — treat someone today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your favorites just got cheaper (up to 25% off)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free delivery on select arrangements this week — shop best sellers`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Roses, plants, sweets — all 25% off. No occasion needed.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Mobile breakpoint is too narrow.**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)
```
418px targets iPhone SE width but leaves devices between 419–600px (most Android and iPad mini viewports) without mobile styles. Standard practice is `max-width: 600px`.

**Redundant media query.**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
The 768px block is fully covered by the 640px block and can be removed.

**`mso-font-alt` missing on Lato `@font-face` declarations.**
All six `Playfair Display` declarations include `mso-font-alt: 'Times New Roman'`, but none of the six `Lato` declarations do. Outlook will silently fall back to the system default (usually Calibri) rather than a specified fallback.

**Aggressive global `line-height` reset.**
```css
* { line-height: 100% }
```
This collapses multi-line text in certain clients (Gmail iOS, Samsung Mail). Should be scoped to `td, p` at minimum.
