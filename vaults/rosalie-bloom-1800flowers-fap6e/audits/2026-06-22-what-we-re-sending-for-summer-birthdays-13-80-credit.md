# What We’re Sending for Summer Birthdays (+ $13.80 Credit)
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- This is 1-800-Flowers' most creative send in the past month — a deliberate break from the Double Blooms template that dominated May and early June. The editorial "What We're Gifting" framing, supported by multiple customer testimonials, reads more like a product guide than a promotion. That's either smart content marketing or a missed conversion opportunity, depending on what the campaign goal is.
- The $13.80 credit is the real hook — it's specific enough to feel account-generated (loyalty reward, cart-based calculation) and lives in the banner at the very top. But the body never picks it up. The hero moves straight into editorial mode, leaving the credit dangling up top with no reinforcement. A subscriber who skims past the banner loses the offer entirely.
- Bottom line: this is a better-than-average send that would perform well on opens (fresh framing, personalized credit) but undersells the conversion on click. The testimonials are genuinely useful, but the lack of a single featured product or any deadline means the email invites browsing more than buying.

## What's working

- The editorial headline ("What We're Gifting for Summer Birthdays") breaks hard from the repetitive Double Blooms structure that ran seven times in June alone — this will feel genuinely fresh to a fatigued list.
- Three distinct customer testimonials are used as social proof throughout the body — a rare and strong trust signal for a flower brand.
- The "$13.80 credit" banner is prominently placed at the very top and makes the credit feel account-personalized rather than a generic promo code.
- The summer birthday seasonal framing is tight and timely for late June.
- Clean render — no broken images, no layout bugs, good whitespace throughout.

## What's weak

- The $13.80 credit lives only in the top banner; the hero body never restates it, so scroll-skimmers lose the offer.
- No urgency or deadline anywhere in the email — there's nothing pushing a decision.
- Three featured products with separate CTAs dilutes focus; the email can't decide what it's actually selling.
- The oddly specific $13.80 amount, with no explanation of how it was earned or what it applies to, may feel confusing rather than compelling.
- "BUY NOW" is the sole CTA — generic, not connected to the summer birthday theme or any specific product.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Bring the credit into the hero.** The headline and subtext should lead with the credit — "Your $13.80 Birthday Credit is waiting" — not bury it in a banner that's easy to tune out.
- 2. **Pick one hero product and commit.** The editorial format works, but it needs a single featured arrangement with a specific name, price-after-credit, and a direct link. The multi-product spread disperses attention.
- 3. **Add a deadline.** Even "Use your credit by Sunday" would lift click-through. There's no cost to adding it and it creates urgency that's entirely absent here.
- 4. **Make the CTA earn it.** "Send a Summer Bouquet" or "Redeem Your $13.80" beats "BUY NOW" for a birthday gifting send.
- 5. **Tighten the subject line** — 58 chars is too long for mobile; cut the parenthetical or restructure.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your $13.80 birthday credit — top summer picks inside`
- **Subject Alt B:** `We picked the best summer birthday gifts (you've got $13.80)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three arrangements our customers keep sending — and your credit expires soon.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Coastal Charm, Garden Roses, and a dipped strawberry basket — all under your credit.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
This violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Gmail on iOS and Apple Mail both honor this, preventing users from zooming in on small text.

**Mobile breakpoint at 418px misses large-viewport phones**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)
```
iPhone 12 Pro Max and 14 Pro Max have a 428–430px logical viewport. Those devices receive the desktop layout despite being mobile. The `.mobile` class also hardcodes `width: 414px`, which does not scale to narrower screens like 360px Android devices that also fall under the breakpoint.

**Google Fonts will render as fallback in most email clients**
Twelve `@font-face` blocks load from `fonts.gstatic.com`. Gmail strips external CSS references; Outlook ignores web fonts entirely. The fallback chain:
- Playfair Display: `mso-font-alt: 'Times New Roman'` is set on most variants — correct.
- All six Lato `@font-face` declarations are missing `mso-font-alt` entirely. Outlook will substitute its default sans-serif rather than a specified fallback.

**Inconsistent Playfair Display font file versions**
Four declarations reference `v21`, one references `v22`. If the CDN ever deprecates `v21` paths, those weights silently fail.

**Playfair Display 900 weight serves `.woff` instead of `.woff2`**
```css
src:url(https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/playfairdisplay/v22/...woff) format('woff');
```
All other weights use `.woff2`. This weight will load a larger file on clients that do honor web fonts.

**`line-height: 100%` on `*`**
```css
* { line-height: 100%; }
```
Applied universally, this can clip descenders in multi-line text. Should be scoped to `td` or specific containers, not the wildcard selector.
