# Save 20% on Gifts Rated 5 Stars
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-03**
## Executive summary

- This is a social-proof email. Five products each get a star rating and a real customer quote, under a "Fall-Time Greats" hero. The structure is clean and easy to scan, and the reviews give it more credibility than the brand's recent catalog and "This or That" sends. The offer is a 20% discount with code GLOW20, which is concrete, and it matches the subject line.
- The offer is too quiet, though. It sits in a thin top banner, and the hero headline is a vague "Fall-Time Greats." The only button is a single small "SHOP NOW." The five product blocks have no visible prices, no per-product buttons, and no deadline above the fold. The fine print says the offer ends 11/22, which is a long runway with no urgency. Compared with the 10/30 HARVEST15 send, which paired a code with a $15 credit, this one is less compelling. It still improves on the 10/31 and 10/29 "This or That" repeats. Verdict: solid concept, under-powered execution.

## What's working

- The five-star ratings and quotes (Dee P., Debra B., Candy, Alan K., Margie S.) give real social proof and make the subject line's "rated 5 stars" claim believable.
- The subject and the banner both say 20% off, so the offer is consistent.
- The layout is a clear single column with one product per block. The circular product frames and the warm autumn palette look cohesive and seasonal.
- The Shop by Category grid (Birthday, Sympathy, Same-Day, and so on) gives a second route for people who don't like the featured items.
- The brand is easy to recognize, and the logo and serif headline look polished.

## What's weak

- The 20% offer appears only in a small banner strip. The hero headline "Fall-Time Greats" doesn't mention the discount.
- There is one small "SHOP NOW" button at the top, and none under any product. Nothing visible links each product to its own page, and no prices are shown.
- The email has no urgency. The 11/22 end date is only in the fine print, so the offer feels evergreen.
- The quotes are uneven. "Absolutely a winner. Thank you." is thin, and the long quotes push the email to a very tall scroll.
- The Thanksgiving-themed items (Turkey Centerpiece, Pumpkin Wreath) sit alongside bouquets without a clear gifting angle, and the category grid is cramped.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the offer in the hero: "20% off 5-star gifts, code GLOW20." Make the code a visible badge and move the end date up next to it.
- 2. Add a price (with the original price struck through) and a "Shop [product]" button under each product.
- 3. Cut the email to three products and use the strongest quotes, so the hero and CTA stay closer to the top.
- 4. Add a real deadline in the hero, such as "Ends Nov 22," and run a countdown in later sends.
- 5. Differentiate this from the many "double" and "pick your favorite" sends this week by leading with the reviews angle in the preheader.
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% off the bouquets customers rave about`
- **Subject Alt B:** `5-star bouquets, 20% off through 11/22`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `"I always get comments on how beautiful it is." Use code GLOW20.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Real reviews, real savings: top-rated fall gifts, now 20% off.`

# Technical Audit: "Save 20% on Gifts Rated 5 Stars" (1-800-FLOWERS.COM)

## 1. Technical Summary
The markup is a standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional table layout with dark-mode meta tags and responsive media queries. The main confirmed issues are non-HTTPS and alt-less third-party tracking pixels, and a viewport that blocks zoom. The compliance and authentication checks are inconclusive, because the relay didn't expose the headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Not verified:** 24 tracking and click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe, so destination status, redirect chains and 404s are unknown.
- **Insecure pixels:** Three pixels load over plain `http://`:
  - `http://view.atdmt.com/FLO/view/361227669/direct/01/`
  - `http://pixel.mathtag.com/event/img?mt_id=155437&mt_adid=103507&...`
  - `http://pixel.mathtag.com/event/img?mt_id=155438&mt_adid=103507&...`

  Clients that block mixed content or proxy images over HTTPS may drop these, which would silently lose conversion and retargeting data.
- **Empty parameters:** Both mathtag pixels have empty values: `v1=&v2=&v3=&s1=&s2=&s3=`. These may be unpopulated macros or intentionally blank fields. Confirm which.
- **Duplicate pixel:** `https://bounceexchange.com/tag/em/1622.gif` appears twice, which can double-count events.
- **Plain-text part:** 4592 of 5990 characters (77%) are URLs, which exceeds the 70% threshold. This is a deliverability risk, since long tracking URLs dominate the text alternative.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` prevents pinch-zoom, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Text resizing:** The universal rule `* {-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; ...}` disables text-size adjustment. `body` later sets `100%`, but the `*` rule still applies to descendants.
- **Missing alt attributes:**
  - `view.atdmt.com/.../direct/01/`
  - `pixel.mathtag.com/event/img?...` (two pixels)
  - `bounceexchange.com/tag/em/1622.gif` (two instances)
  - `click.dv.1800flowers.com/o/cef9696c-...`
  - `mi.1800flowers.com/p/up/.../o.gif`

  These are tracking pixels, so the impact is low. Add `alt=""` so screen readers skip them, and so Outlook doesn't render alt text or broken-image boxes.
- **Positives:** `lang`-independent items such as the `<title>`, charset and `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` are present. VML and Office namespaces are declared, and `@font-face` rules have web-font fallbacks (`mso-font-alt`).

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tokens such as `{{...}}` or `%%...%%` appear in the visible source or QA output. The HTML was truncated, so the body and footer are unchecked.
- The `mi.1800flowers.com/p/up/.../o.gif?mi_u=174149195&mi_ecmp=261103_EMAIL_18F_PZ_DV_12AMET_PROMO` open pixel carries a populated recipient ID (`mi_u`). This is expected behavior, not a defect.

## 5. Compliance
- **Unverified:** The `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) headers were not found. The QA note says the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is not confirmed as a sender defect. Check the raw message headers in the original source. Gmail and Yahoo require one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders.
- **Unverified:** `Authentication-Results` is absent, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status are unknown. The sender domain `em.1800flowers.com` is a dedicated subdomain, which is good practice.
- **Not assessed:** The physical address and visible unsubscribe link are in the truncated footer. No evidence either way.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- UTM parameters and landing-page alignment **cannot be assessed**. All 24 links go through tracking redirects and were not followed, so final URLs and campaign tags (`utm_*`) are unknown.
- The only visible campaign identifier is `mi_ecmp=261103_EMAIL_18F_PZ_DV_12AMET_PROMO` in the open pixel.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Switch `atdmt.com` and `mathtag.com` pixels to `https://`, or remove them if the vendors no longer support HTTPS.
2. Add `alt=""` to all tracking pixels.
3. Remove the duplicate `bounceexchange.com/.../1622.gif`.
4. Drop `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag, and scope `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` away from `*`.
5. Check the mathtag empty `v1`–`s3` parameters are intentional.
6. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` in the raw headers. The relay may be hiding them, so don't treat this as a sender failure until confirmed.
7. Re-run link resolution that follows the tracking redirects, to capture final URLs and UTM values.
8. Review the plain-text version to cut its URL density, for example by using shorter links.
