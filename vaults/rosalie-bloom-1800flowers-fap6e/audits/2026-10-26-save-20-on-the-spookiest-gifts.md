# Save 20% on the spookiest gifts
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-26**
## Executive summary

- This is a seasonal Halloween gift-guide email with a real offer, 20% off with code GLOW20. The "Scary Good Blooms" hero is dramatic: black roses, a burnt-orange script and a dark gradient. The "Top 5" numbered layout with alternating image and text is easy to scan and fits the Halloween moment. The email reads like one of the brand's better-executed seasonal sends.
- The offer is the weak point. The 20% sits in a thin strip above the logo, in small type, and the body never repeats it. Nothing in the body says what the 20% applies to. The product tiles carry no prices, no "from $" and no per-item CTA. The fine print at the bottom ("Offer ends at 11:59 p.m. (EDT) on 10/31/2026") puts the real deadline in the smallest text, so the email has no urgency in the creative.
- It also lands in a heavy cadence. Since 10/20 this brand has sent a 25% off "This or That" email, a 25% off roses email, a $15 credit email and several editorial sends. This one is a clean, on-theme send, but it is another quiet-offer editorial that asks the subscriber to do the math. It is a solid 6.

## What's working

- The hero is on-theme and striking. The black roses, script "Scary" and dark background sell the Halloween mood immediately.
- The numbered 1-to-5 layout with large burnt-orange numerals gives clear structure and an easy scan path.
- The copy is playful and on-brand, with lines like "Black roses only come once a year" and "Halloween magic that'll last well into spring."
- Photography is strong and varied. It ranges from moody black roses to warm autumn bouquets, so it reaches several tastes.
- The "Shop by Category" grid and the app-download block give a way out for people who don't like the five featured picks.

## What's weak

- The 20% off / GLOW20 line is tiny and sits above the logo. The offer is not part of the hero, and it is never repeated in the body or in a closing CTA.
- There are no prices, no "from $XX" and no per-product buttons. The five items are only linked by image or title, and a skimmer can't tell what any of them costs.
- Only one "SHOP NOW" button appears, in the hero. The rest of the email relies on plain text links.
- The deadline (10/31, 11:59 p.m. EDT) shows only in the fine print. The email has no visible urgency, even though Halloween is five days away.
- The "NYRR x 1-800-FLOWERS Give Them Their Flowers" gift bundle banner is an unrelated mid-email promo. It dilutes the Halloween story and competes with the main offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the offer in the hero. Use a large "20% OFF with code GLOW20" badge next to "Scary Good Blooms," and repeat it in a closing banner with the deadline.
- 2. Add a price or "from $XX" line and a "Shop" button under each of the five picks.
- 3. Add a visible countdown line, such as "Ends Halloween night."
- 4. Move the NYRR bundle lower, or cut it from the send.
- 5. Spell out whether the code applies sitewide or only to the featured items.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Black roses + 20% off, just in time for Halloween`
- **Subject Alt B:** `5 spooky bouquets, 20% off through Halloween`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use code GLOW20 on midnight blooms, black roses and more. Ends 10/31.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Our top 5 Halloween bouquets, ready to ship before the 31st.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML is a structurally sound XHTML 1.0 email with a dark-mode meta and Outlook (VML/MSO) support. Its main problems are several tracking pixels with no alt text (some over plain `http://`), a plain-text part that is mostly tracking URLs, and a fixed 414px mobile width. The source is truncated, so the footer and link `href`s could not be checked.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **26 tracking links were not probed.** The QA tool skipped them as click-redirect domains, so none of their destinations are verified.
- **Plain-text part is mostly URLs.** 4949 of 6364 characters (about 78%) are URLs, which points to long redirect URLs in the plain-text alternative. That can raise spam scoring.
- **Pixel count.** The QA data lists these beacons:
  - `view.atdmt.com/FLO/view/361227669/direct/01/` (http)
  - `pixel.mathtag.com/event/img?mt_id=155437…` (http)
  - `pixel.mathtag.com/event/img?mt_id=155438…` (http)
  - `bounceexchange.com/tag/em/1622.gif` (appears twice)
  - `click.dv.1800flowers.com/o/cd0d022f-…`
  - `mi.1800flowers.com/p/up/ada90b86b2846dd9/o.gif`
- **Empty parameters.** Both mathtag pixels carry `v1=&v2=&v3=&s1=&s2=&s3=`. They may be unresolved macros, or just unused fields.
- **Recipient ID in the URL.** `mi.1800flowers.com/…/o.gif?mi_u=174149195&mi_ecmp=261026_EMAIL_18F_STATIC_DV_12AMET_PROMO` puts a recipient identifier in the URL, which is expected for an open pixel.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text.** All eight tracking images flagged above have no alt. For 1×1 pixels, `alt=""` is the correct fix. A missing attribute can make screen readers read the filename or URL.
- **Insecure image sources.** Three images load over `http://` and may be blocked or trigger mixed-content warnings: `view.atdmt.com` and both `pixel.mathtag.com` pixels.
- **Fixed mobile width.** In the `@media (max-width: 418px)` block, `.mobile` and `.showmobile` are set to `width:414px !important`. On 360px or 375px viewports these elements will overflow horizontally.
- **Zoom disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" … maximum-scale=1>` blocks pinch-zoom, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Dark-mode risk.** `th {color:#000000 !important}` and `th a {color:#000000 !important}` are hard-coded while `color-scheme: light dark` is declared. If a client darkens the background, header and nav text could be black on dark. I can't confirm this without the body markup.
- **Text size adjust.** `* {-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` is overridden on `body`, so this is minor. The `*` rule also forces `line-height:100%` on everything.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The visible source has no unresolved merge tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`). The numeric `mi_u` value is resolved.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058).** Both were not found. The QA tool notes the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is **unconfirmed**. Check the raw message headers.
- **Authentication-Results.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown for the same reason. The sender uses `em.1800flowers.com`.
- **Physical address.** None was detected. Because the HTML is truncated, the footer was probably not scanned, so this is **unconfirmed**. Verify it in the full source.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not verifiable. All 26 links were wrapped in tracking redirects and skipped, and no resolved destinations or UTM parameters are in the data. The only campaign identifier seen is `mi_ecmp=261026_EMAIL_18F_STATIC_DV_12AMET_PROMO`, which is in the pixel only. Landing-page alignment with the "Save 20%" offer can't be checked.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add `alt=""` to all tracking pixels, and give `width="1" height="1"` where missing.
2. Move `view.atdmt.com` and `pixel.mathtag.com` to HTTPS, or remove them if retired. `atdmt.com` is a legacy Atlas/Microsoft domain.
3. Check whether the mathtag empty parameters should be populated.
4. Replace the fixed 414px `.mobile` and `.showmobile` widths with `width:100% !important; max-width:414px`.
5. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
6. Add dark-mode overrides for `th` colors, or avoid forcing `#000000 !important`.
7. Shorten the plain-text part by using cleaner links or fewer tracked URLs.
8. Pull the raw message from the sending side to confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, one-click POST, DKIM/SPF/DMARC and the footer address. Then re-run the audit with the untruncated HTML and resolved redirect targets to check UTMs.
