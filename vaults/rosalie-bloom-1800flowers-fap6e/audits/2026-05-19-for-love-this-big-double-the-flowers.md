# For Love This Big… Double the Flowers 💕💕
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- This is a 1-800-Flowers "Double Blooms" campaign landing on May 19 — one day after yesterday's "33% off pink roses & lilies" clearance push. The concept is solid: a 2X-blooms mechanic with four hero products each discounted $20, plus a secondary 20% off code (BLOOMS20) for Best Sellers. The visual structure is strong — the DOUBLE BLOOMS header commands the screen immediately, and the product grid gives subscribers something specific to click.
- Two things undercut it. First, the offer mechanic is ambiguous: the hero says "twice the wonder when you double the roses for free," but never plainly explains *how* — is this BOGO, a quantity upgrade, a surprise-at-checkout? That hesitation costs clicks. Second, this is the second 1-800-Flowers email in 24 hours promoting flowers at a discount. After yesterday's clearance push on the same category, sending another promo today reads less like a campaign and more like a blitz. The brand's strong welcome email from May 11 built real goodwill; two back-to-back discount fires chip away at it.
- Mid-tier send overall. Clean execution, generous offer, but imprecise copy and poor cadence timing are leaving money on the table.

## What's working

- **DOUBLE BLOOMS hero** is visually dominant — oversized headline over a full-color floral image creates strong first-screen impact before any scrolling
- **Four specific product tiles** with consistent $20 off + 2X badges give subscribers clear, clickable choices rather than a vague "shop now" dump
- **BLOOMS20 secondary offer** (20% off Best Sellers) is well-placed below the hero without competing with it
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no layout breakage, no overlapping text anywhere visible
- **"Join the Celebration" loyalty section** is present and extends the brand relationship past a single transaction

## What's weak

- **Offer mechanic never explained** — "double the flowers for free" in the hero is intriguing but not actionable; a subscriber doesn't know what they're clicking into
- **Subject line buries the lead** — "For Love This Big…" front-loads sentiment and the actual offer hook ("Double the Flowers") arrives after an ellipsis
- **No urgency signal anywhere** — no expiration, no "limited quantities," no "offer ends Sunday"; nothing moves someone off the fence today vs. next week
- **Day-after cadence is punishing** — this is the second consecutive-day discount email from 1-800-Flowers; yesterday's 33% off clearance push on the same products makes this feel like a bombardment
- **No social proof** — no star ratings, no review counts, no customer photos on any of the four product tiles despite strong product volume

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the subject with the offer, not the sentiment.** "For Love This Big…" belongs in the preview or body, not leading the subject line. The actual hook — double flowers — is buried after an ellipsis.
- **Subject Alt A:** `2X blooms, $20 off — your bouquet just doubled`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Double the flowers, free upgrade inside 💕`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Get twice the blooms on 4 fan-favorite arrangements. No code needed.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your roses just doubled. Plus: 20% off Best Sellers with BLOOMS20.`
- 2. **Explain the mechanic in one sentence** at the top of the hero: "Buy any of these four arrangements and we'll double the blooms — no code needed." If it's BOGO, say BOGO. Clarity converts.
- 3. **Add a deadline.** "Offer valid through Tuesday, May 21" is all it takes. Without it, the $20 off and 2X mechanic are features with no urgency to act on them now.
- 4. **Respect the cadence.** After a clearance push yesterday, this needed at least 48 hours of breathing room. Consider segmenting: send the "Double Blooms" campaign to subscribers who *didn't* open or click yesterday's clearance email rather than blanketing the full list two days running.
- 5. **Add star ratings to product tiles.** Even a single line — "★★★★☆ 1,240 reviews" — per arrangement would lift click confidence materially. This brand has volume; surface it.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users from pinching to zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 violation. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Issue — Mobile breakpoint too narrow:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { ... }
```
418px misses devices rendering at 390–414px logical width (iPhone 14/15 Pro range renders at 393px). Responsive styles won't fire. Standard breakpoint should be `max-width: 480px` or `600px`.

**Issue — Redundant font format in Playfair Display 900 normal weight:**
```css
src:url(.../nuFvD-...ukDT.woff) format('woff');
```
All other weights use `.woff2`; this variant uses `.woff` only. If `.woff2` is unavailable, the stack falls back to `mso-font-alt:'Times New Roman'` in Outlook, but non-Outlook clients will load the heavier `.woff` file unnecessarily. Should be `.woff2`.

**Issue — Redundant media query:**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width:640px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width:640px} }
```
Identical rule fires at two breakpoints; the 768px query is dead code. Remove the 768px block.

**Issue — Redundant show/hide declarations:**
```css
.mobileshow {display:none !important; visibility:hidden !important; width:0px; overflow:hidden !important;}
```
`visibility:hidden` is redundant when `display:none` is already set. Minor but adds specificity complexity.
