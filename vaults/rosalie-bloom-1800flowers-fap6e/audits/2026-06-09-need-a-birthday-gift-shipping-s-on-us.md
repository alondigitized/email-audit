# Need a birthday gift? Shipping’s on us
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, occasion-focused tactical email — 1-800-Flowers betting on the "someone in your life has a birthday" universal relevance rather than personalized trigger data. "We Know Birthdays" is a confident positioning statement, and the free-shipping offer in the subject is honest and easy to understand. The editorial quote ("My friends and families who have summer birthdays are extra lucky…") is a nice thematic touch that ties the season to the occasion.
- That said, this email is playing the percentages, not a targeted list segment. There's no "your friend's birthday is coming up" signal, no pricing visible in the hero, and no deadline attached to the free shipping offer — which makes it easy to archive rather than act. After yesterday's HGTV Plants collection-launch (which felt like a brand editorial pivot), this reads as a reset back to transactional gifting basics.
- The bottom half is crowded — a "Join the Celebration" loyalty join CTA and an app download block both compete after the hero, diluting focus. The email works best for a recipient already in shopping mode; it doesn't manufacture that urgency on its own.

## What's working

- **"We Know Birthdays" headline** lands instantly — confident brand authority, zero ambiguity about the email's purpose.
- **Question-format subject line** ("Need a birthday gift?") reads conversational and avoids the promotional-email template feel.
- **Social proof quote** from a summer-lifestyle voice adds warmth and differentiates from pure product grids.
- **Free shipping offer** is clean, unambiguous, and repeated from subject into the email body (top banner).
- **Hero bouquet** is visually strong — yellow florals pop against a light background, clear hero product above the fold.

## What's weak

- **No deadline on the free shipping offer** — "Shipping's on us" with no end date removes urgency and makes the defer-until-later decision easy.
- **No pricing visible in hero** — for a gifting send, the absence of "starting at $X with free shipping" leaves the offer abstract.
- **"Join the Celebration" loyalty block** is dead weight for existing members and interrupts the purchase flow at the moment of peak intent.
- **Broad targeting, no personalization** — "Need a birthday gift?" could have been "Someone's birthday is coming up — make it easy." The difference between broadcast and signal.
- **Cadence issue:** this lands the day after the HGTV Plants editorial (June 8), which is two emails in two days. The pivot back to pure transactional gifting after that editorial feels jarring.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline to the free shipping offer.** "Shipping's on us — today only" or "through midnight" turns a passive perk into an active reason to click now. This is the highest-leverage single change.
- 2. **Put a starting price in the hero.** "Birthday bouquets from $29.99 — shipping on us" is a complete offer. Without it, the reader has to click to find out if this is in their budget.
- 3. **Cut the "Join the Celebration" block** from this email. It cannibalizes the CTA and serves a different funnel stage. Save it for a post-purchase or re-engagement send.
- 4. **Lead with the bouquet link, not a category grid.** The "SHOP BY CATEGORY" section encourages browse behavior; a birthday-gifting send should push to a curated birthday page.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Birthday flowers + free shipping — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your go-to birthday gift (shipping's free)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Bouquets from $29.99, delivered free. Don't forget them.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Summer birthdays deserve something stunning. Free shipping inside.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — prevents user zoom. WCAG 1.4.4 violation; fails Success Criterion at AA level. Should be removed or replaced with `initial-scale=1` only.
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

**`* { line-height: 100% }` on the universal selector** — applies to all elements including inline elements and replaced content. This is an aggressive reset that can collapse line spacing on multiline body copy in some clients (notably Gmail/Android). Standard practice limits this reset to `body` and table cells, not `*`.

**`mobileshow` class uses both `display:none !important` and `visibility:hidden !important`** — double-hiding removes content from the accessibility tree entirely and prevents any fallback rendering. Confirm this is intentional and not accidentally hiding meaningful content for non-mobile clients.

**Font inconsistency — `.woff` vs `.woff2` for Playfair Display weight 900:**
```css
/* All other weights: */
src: url(...woff2) format('woff2');
/* Weight 900 normal: */
src: url(...nuFvD-...PKfsukDT.woff) format('woff'); /* woff, not woff2 */
```
This will load a larger file and render differently on clients that support woff2. Likely a copy-paste error in the font stack build.

**Mixed `@font-face` version tags** — Playfair Display references both `v21` and `v22` across weight variants. Not a rendering blocker, but indicates the font stack was assembled from different generations of the Google Fonts CDN response.

**`mso-font-alt` missing on some `@font-face` declarations** — present on Playfair Display regular and 600-weight, absent on italic 900-weight and on all Lato variants. In Outlook (which ignores `@font-face`), those weights fall back to the browser default rather than the declared `mso-font-alt: 'Times New Roman'`.
