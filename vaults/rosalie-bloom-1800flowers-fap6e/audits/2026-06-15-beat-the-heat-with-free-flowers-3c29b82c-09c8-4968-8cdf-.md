# Beat the heat with free flowers
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- This is 1-800-Flowers' sixth Double Blooms execution in 27 days — following sends on May 19, May 27, June 2, June 10, and June 13. The creative template is unchanged: hero arrangement, flanking "DOUBLE" typography, 2X product tiles, and a discount code. The "Summer Delight" rename is the only meaningful creative evolution. Two days ago this same subscriber got essentially this same email under "Send Love Times Two."
- The subject line — "Beat the heat with free flowers" — is the most interesting move in this send, and it's also the most damaging. The seasonal hook is fresh and "free" is irresistible open-bait. But open the email and there are no free flowers: the hero starts at $44, and the "free" claim requires subscribers to decode it as the "extra blooms" value proposition buried in a 2X label. That mental leap is too much. A subscriber who bites on "free" and lands in a $44 purchase flow is going to feel misled, and that erosion of trust is harder to repair than a lower open rate.
- The 20% off code BLOOMS and Celebrations Passport call-out are solid signals. But they cannot fix the core problem: a promise the email body doesn't keep.

## What's working

- The "Summer Delight" framing gives the Double Blooms template a seasonally coherent reason to exist in mid-June — stronger than June 13's generic "Send Love Times Two."
- 20% off with code BLOOMS is concrete, visible, and actionable without hunting for the fine print.
- SHOP NOW is unambiguous — one click from the hero to the product page.
- The Shop by Category grid (Birthday, Romance, Sympathy, Same Day) surfaces the full catalog for browsers not sold on the hero arrangement.
- Celebrations Passport section is consistent loyalty reinforcement that rewards repeat openers.

## What's weak

- "Free flowers" in the subject has no direct match in the email body — the hero starts at $44 and the "free" framing requires the subscriber to decode it as extra blooms value. That reads as bait-and-switch.
- Sixth Double Blooms in 27 days, sent just two days after the June 13 email — at this cadence the template is exhausting goodwill regardless of the seasonal wrapper.
- No urgency signal anywhere — no code expiry, no deadline on the summer offer, nothing that answers "why click today."
- "SHOP NOW" is the weakest available CTA copy — a generic directive with no category or product direction.
- No social proof or reviews visible in a template that features multiple product SKUs.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the "free flowers" promise in the body.** Frame the Double Blooms headline as "Get the extra blooms FREE" or drop "free" from the subject entirely. Right now the subject and body are telling different stories and the disconnect costs more than the open uplift gains.
- 2. **Add code expiry.** Even "code expires Sunday" would create a credible reason to act now rather than archive and forget.
- 3. **Rewrite the hero CTA.** "Shop Double Blooms" or "Get Summer Picks" — anything with a noun that closes the loop on what the reader is clicking into.
- 4. **Rest the Double Blooms template for at least 10 days.** The next send should be something meaningfully different — a plant editorial, a same-day angle, or an occasion trigger.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Your extra blooms are free — Summer Delight from $44`

- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Double the blooms, one price — save 20% with code BLOOMS through Sunday`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — User zoom disabled:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom on mobile. This violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Issue — Mobile breakpoint too narrow:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)
```
The breakpoint fires below 418px. iPhones 11–16 have logical widths of 390–430px. At 419–430px these devices receive the desktop layout, likely breaking the mobile-optimized stack. Industry standard for email is `max-width: 480px` or `600px`.

**Issue — Dead CSS rule:**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width:640px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width:640px} }
```
The `min-width: 768px` block is entirely superseded by the `min-width: 640px` block (same declaration, narrower threshold wins). The 768px rule never applies and should be removed.

**Issue — Inconsistent web font format:**
```css
/* Weight 900 normal — loads .woff */
src:url(https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/playfairdisplay/v22/...ukDT.woff) format('woff');

/* All other weights — load .woff2 */
src:url(https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/playfairdisplay/v21/...ukDV.woff2) format('woff2');
```
The Playfair Display 900-normal variant fetches `.woff` instead of `.woff2`. woff2 is ~30% smaller and universally supported in the clients that render web fonts in email (Apple Mail, Outlook.com). This weight will load slower and degrade rendering for heavy-weight headlines.

**Minor — Redundant visibility suppression:**
```css
.mobileshow {display:none !important; visibility:hidden !important; ...}
```
`display:none` already removes the element from layout; `visibility:hidden` is redundant here and adds no cross-client value.

**Note — `lang` attribute:** Not confirmed present or absent due to truncation. The `<html>` opening tag should carry `lang="en"` for screen reader and spam filter compatibility.
