# What Happened to Halloween?
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-25**
## Executive summary

- This is the twenty-fourth straight "Jim at 1-800-FLOWERS" essay newsletter, now branded "Celebrations Pulse." The subject, "What Happened to Halloween?", promises a Halloween send, but the body has no flowers, no product, no price and no offer. It is a founder letter with a pull quote, a Halloween nostalgia story and a company-history story. Below those sit two podcast promos, a vote-for-our-podcast appeal and a Substack pitch.
- The layout is clean and well built, and the opening photos of kids in costumes are charming. But a Halloween-timed email from a flower retailer, sent the week of Oct 25 with no seasonal gift or arrangement, wastes the moment. Halloween is six days out, and the only links lead to stories, podcasts and a vote. Nothing leads to a store.
- Judgement: well produced, commercially empty. It repeats the format of 10/18 ("Something scary this way comes") and 10/11 ("The Difference a Job Can Make"). A subscriber has learned this isn't a sales email and has little reason to engage.

## What's working

- The pull quote, "The costumes have gotten fancier, and the celebrations bigger. But has the spirit of Halloween really changed?", is a decent hook that matches the subject.
- The costume-kids hero photo is warm, seasonal and eye-catching.
- The "50 Years In" section adds some brand-heritage storytelling.
- The layout is clear: centered sections with labeled eyebrows ("ONCE UPON A HALLOWEEN", "PODCASTS", "EVERY VOTE COUNTS") are easy to scan.
- The Jim McCann signature block and photo keep the personal-letter voice consistent.

## What's weak

- There is no product, price, offer or flower anywhere in the email.
- The CTAs are underlined text links ("Unwrap some Halloween memories", "Cast your vote"). The only button is "Subscribe Today", which pushes readers to Substack, off the brand's own site.
- The content is overloaded and unfocused: Halloween memories, a company anniversary, two podcasts, an award vote and a book giveaway all compete.
- The Substack box asks readers to subscribe to the same letter they are already reading.
- The "Was this letter forwarded to you?" and "Want to share a story?" blocks add length and no commerce.
- The podcast thumbnails are busy, and the headline text on them is hard to read at inbox size.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one seasonal commerce block under the hero, such as a Halloween or fall arrangement, with a price and a "Shop fall bouquets" button. This single change would lift the email the most.
- 2. Cut it to one story plus one CTA. Drop the podcasts, the vote appeal and the Substack box, or move them to a separate send.
- 3. Turn the text links into real buttons with verb-plus-noun copy.
- 4. Add a time-bound reason to act, such as a seasonal gifting cutoff or a Thanksgiving pre-order.
- 5. Keep the personal voice, but link the story to a product (for example, "the treats and flowers neighbors share").
