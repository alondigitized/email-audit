# Five of our favorite rose bouquets for summer
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- This is 1-800-Flowers stepping away from both recent formats — the escalating flash-sale urgency plays (Aug 5 "24-Hour Sale," Aug 6 "Extended One More Day," both 9.00/10) and the Mallory Green florist-persona editorial (Aug 1, Aug 4, both 6.00/10) — into a numbered "Top 5" listicle. It's a fresh structural beat for this cadence, but the underlying offer is the same 25% off / code SUMMER25 the brand has already run this window, arriving right after two straight sends built around 30% off with countdown urgency.
- The render itself is clean: five bouquets, big number treatment, no broken images or overlap, a category grid and loyalty module below the fold. But the execution has a real gap — no price appears anywhere on any of the five products, so the 25% off has nothing to anchor against. Judgment: a well-built, low-friction listicle that undersells its own offer and its own hook.

## What's working

- Numbered 1-5 layout with oversized digits makes the email fast to scan on a phone.
- The 25% off code (SUMMER25) sits in a colored banner at the very top, visible with zero scrolling.
- Product photography varies angle and styling per bouquet, with small callouts ("Farm-fresh," "New") adding texture without clutter.
- Shop by Category grid and the Celebrations Passport membership pitch extend the email past the initial offer with a second conversion path.
- Clean render throughout — no overlapping text, no broken images, footer and brand-family logos display correctly.

## What's weak

- No price is shown for any of the five bouquets — a shopper can't tell if 25% off is a real deal without clicking through.
- CTA copy is generic ("SHOP NOW" repeated, underlined product names) rather than specific per item.
- 25% off reads as a step down coming right after the Aug 5/Aug 6 sends trained subscribers to expect 30% off in a 24-hour flash format.
- Subject line carries no urgency, offer, or personalization — weaker hook than the flash-sale subjects that scored highest in this brand's recent history.
- No preview/preheader text was supplied for this send, leaving that inbox real estate blank.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a price (or "starting at $X") under each bouquet — this is the single biggest lift, since the 25% off currently has no visible number to apply to.
- 2. Sharpen every CTA to verb+noun ("Shop Sorbet Roses") instead of repeated bare "SHOP NOW."
- 3. Put a deadline on the offer in the subject or top banner — the 24-hour flash format clearly outperformed for this brand on Aug 5-6.
- 4. Write and test an actual preheader; right now that slot is empty.
- **Subject Alt A:** `5 Rose Bouquets, 25% Off — Ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Peak rose season: our top 5 picks, 25% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `From classic red to sorbet pink — shop the season's best, code SUMMER25`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Our top 5 summer roses, ranked. 25% off with SUMMER25.`
- 5. Add star ratings or a review count on at least the top 1-2 picks to build trust before the click.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 8 `<img>` tags lack an `alt` attribute: the `atdmt.com` pixel, both `mathtag.com` pixels, `bounceexchange.com/tag/em/1622.gif` (appears twice — duplicate tag), the `click.dv.1800flowers.com` redirect image, and `mi.1800flowers.com/o.gif`. ## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No unresolved merge tags (e.g. `{{FirstName}}`, `%%FIELD%%`) are visible in the available HTML. The `mi_mid` value in the click-redirect URL appears to be a resolved subscriber ID, not a broken token. Full body was truncated, so this cannot be fully verified end-to-end.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication)

- Physical mailing address: not detected in the analyzed HTML. Given the source was truncated, this could be present in an untruncated footer, but should be confirmed — CAN-SPAM requires it.
- `Authentication-Results` (SPF/DKIM/DMARC): not detected, same relay-capture caveat — inconclusive.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM / landing alignment)

However, since all 40 click-through links were skipped by the probe and their resolved destinations aren't in the available data, UTM-to-landing-page alignment cannot be confirmed in this pass.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Serve the three tracking pixels over HTTPS (`view.atdmt.com`, `pixel.mathtag.com` ×2) to avoid mixed-content blocking in HTTPS-only mail clients.
2. Add explicit `alt=""` to the 8 unlabeled tracking/pixel images to clear accessibility warnings.
3. Directly verify `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and `Authentication-Results` on the raw SMTP headers (outside the AgentMail relay) — current findings are inconclusive, not confirmed failures, but RFC 8058 compliance is increasingly enforced by Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender rules.
4. Confirm the physical mailing address is present in the untruncated footer.
5. Reduce the plain-text URL ratio (currently 71%, 7253/10121 chars) — high URL density in the plain-text alternative is a spam-filter heuristic risk.
6. Re-run link validation on the 40 skipped tracking/redirect URLs to confirm none are broken or misrouted, and to verify landing-page UTM alignment.
