# $15 credit for all the ways to say happy birthday 🎈
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- This is 1-800-Flowers recycling the "$15 credit" hook for the second time in three days — July 14 ran "Your $15 Credit—Use It for Their Birthday," and here we are again on July 16 with "$15 credit for all the ways to say happy birthday." The subject-line promise lands credibly, but if that credit only appears in the top promotional banner and not as a hero offer in the body (which is what the render shows), it's the same bait-and-switch dynamic that defined the July 13 and July 14 sends.
- The email itself is the familiar multi-section editorial: "Let Them Day Dream" florals, "Birthday Sweets," a testimonial pull-quote, "Summer Greenery," and a "Designed Just for Them" personalized gifts section. It's thorough and well-photographed, but the structure means no single product wins. The eye has nowhere decisive to land.
- The double-credit-email-in-three-days cadence is the biggest problem here. A subscriber who opened July 14 and couldn't find the $15 credit will skip this one on instinct. For everyone else, the editorial scrollfest is competent but not compelling. Net: a functional send that loses points for cadence fatigue and a repeated, underdelivered promise.

## What's working

- Testimonial pull-quote breaks up the scroll and adds credibility — specific, human language visible on-screen.
- "Birthday Sweets" is a smart category inclusion — it widens the use case beyond flowers and increases basket relevance.
- "Summer Greenery" section uses a lush hydrangea-type hero image that photographs well and feels seasonal.
- Multiple Shop Now CTAs are clearly rendered buttons throughout — no ambiguity about where to click.
- The top promotional banner is coral/pink, creating visual separation from the hero so the offer is at least present.

## What's weak

- The $15 credit appears only in the banner, not in the hero body copy — same bait-and-switch pattern as July 13 and July 14. The credit earns the open, then disappears.
- Three "$15 birthday credit" emails in four days (July 13, July 14, July 16) is destructive cadence — credibility erodes with each repeat.
- No urgency signal visible in the body — if this credit expires, the deadline isn't shown anywhere in the render.
- "Designed Just for Them" section features a young child in the product photo, which signals a different shopper mindset (parent buying for a toddler) and breaks the earlier adult-giftee frame.
- "Let Them Day Dream" is soft, abstract copy for what is essentially a bouquet product — it slows the reader at the most critical fold.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the $15 credit in the hero, not just the banner.** Rewrite the hero headline to "Your $15 Birthday Credit — Here's What It Gets You" and show 3 specific SKUs that the credit covers. Closes the bait-and-switch loop completely.
- 2. **Add an expiry date to the credit.** "Expires July 19" or similar creates the urgency the send currently lacks and gives subscribers a reason to act now vs. "later."
- 3. **Collapse to one or two product focuses.** Four editorial sections (florals, sweets, greenery, personalized) splits attention and dilutes everything. Pick the top two for this birthday angle.
- 4. **Change the subject entirely.** Two consecutive $15-credit birthday subjects have already been sent — a third on the same hook will bleed opens.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Birthday order for [Name]? Your $15 is waiting — expires Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `We built an entire birthday shop. Have you seen it?`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Flowers, sweets, and custom gifts — all under one credit.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop bouquets, treats, and personalized picks — all with your $15.`
- 5. **Remove or reposition the "Designed Just for Them" child photo.** It's demographic drift mid-scroll and signals a different purchase occasion.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — confirmed in source:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
This disables pinch-to-zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) requires content to be resizable to 200% without assistive technology. This attribute violates that criterion for low-vision users.

**Non-standard mobile breakpoint** — breakpoint fires at `max-width: 418px` (not the conventional 480px or 600px). Devices between 419–479px wide will receive the desktop layout.

**Hardcoded mobile widths** — `.mobile` and `.showmobile` both set `width:414px !important`, hardcoded to iPhone 6/7/8 dimensions. Larger Android phones in the 418–480px range will overflow or clip.

**Inconsistent web font format** — Playfair Display 900-weight normal variant loads `.woff` instead of `.woff2`:
```css
src:url(https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/playfairdisplay/v22/...fvUDV.woff) format('woff');
```
All other variants use `.woff2`. `.woff` files are ~30% larger; this is a minor load-time regression on Apple Mail, the only client that renders these fonts.

**Missing `mso-font-alt` fallback** — The Playfair Display 900-weight italic `@font-face` block lacks a `mso-font-alt` declaration, unlike all other variants which specify `mso-font-alt:'Times New Roman'`. Outlook will apply its default font rather than a controlled fallback for that weight/style combination.
