# Save 20% on Father’s Day gifts
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- This is 1-800-Flowers' Father's Day push — well-timed for June 18, a week before the holiday — built around a "World's Greatest Dad" headline with a 20% off offer and a broad product spread: food baskets, sunflowers, plants, leather goods, monogrammed items. The brand is leaning on occasion-anchor copy rather than a specific hero product, which reads as safe rather than inspired.
- The problem: just yesterday they sent 30% off Sorbet Roses with a free vase stacked on top. Today's 20% off general assortment is a regression in offer value and specificity in back-to-back sends. A subscriber paying any attention will feel the downgrade. The email is visually dense — multiple mid-page product rows followed by a full Shop By Category grid — so the primary offer risks dilution before the reader scrolls halfway through.
- Solid execution, underwhelming strategy. This email will convert gift-buyers already in the purchase window, but it doesn't pull anyone earlier or harder than the Father's Day category alone would.

## What's working

- **Subject line lands the offer immediately** — 31 characters, specific discount + occasion, no spam signals.
- **"World's Greatest Dad" hero** anchors the email to the right occasion without ambiguity.
- **Diverse product grid** (food, florals, plants, leather, monogrammed) shows range and helps gift-buyers who don't know what Dad wants.
- **SHOP BY CATEGORY section** is a useful navigational escape hatch for browsers who scroll past the hero.
- **No visible render bugs** — layout holds, images load, no broken blocks visible.

## What's weak

- **Offer is weaker than yesterday's send** — 20% vs 30% + free vase on June 17 undercuts urgency for anyone who opened both.
- **No urgency signal on the deadline** — Father's Day shipping cutoff is the real hook here; that clock is invisible.
- **Hero CTA copy not legible at mobile scale** — button exists but copy is too small to read in the render.
- **Email is very long** — four distinct product rows plus a category grid dilutes focus and buries the offer.
- **No specific hero product called out** — "20% off everything" trains subscribers to wait for a better deal, not act now.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lock in a shipping-deadline countdown** — "Order by Sunday for guaranteed Father's Day delivery" is the single most action-driving piece of copy missing here. Occasion-adjacent send without a hard cutoff leaves urgency on the table.
- 2. **Surface one hero product with the 20% math applied** — "The World's Greatest Snack Crate: $79 → $63" converts better than abstract percent-off.
- 3. **Raise the offer to match or beat June 17** — 20% after 30% feels like a pullback. At minimum, stack free shipping or a bonus item.
- 4. **Cut the email to two sections** — hero offer + shop by category. The mid-scroll product rows are noise that competes with the CTA.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dad deserves it — 20% off, ships by Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Father's Day gift sorted in 2 minutes (save 20%)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping on orders over $39 — Father's Day cutoff is Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `From flowers to food — 20% off everything Dad actually wants`

### 7. Recommendations

**Priority fixes:**

1. **Remove `maximum-scale=1`** — replace `content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1"` with `content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1"`. This is an accessibility violation with no deliverability benefit.

2. **Add `mso-font-alt` to all Lato `@font-face` blocks** — without it, Outlook falls back to the browser default (usually Times New Roman) rather than a specified safe font:
   ```css
   @font-face { font-family:'Lato'; ...; mso-font-alt:'Arial'; }
   ```

3. **Widen mobile breakpoint or use `min-width` approach** — consider `max-width: 480px` to reliably catch modern large-screen phones (430px viewport on iPhone 16 Pro Max).

4. **Scope `.appleLinks` carefully** — ensure CTA buttons are not descendants of `.appleLinks` containers, or add a higher-specificity color override on button selectors.

5. **Confirm `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header is present** — Gmail and Yahoo require `List-Unsubscribe: <mailto:...>` + `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` for bulk senders. Absence can trigger spam folder placement.

6. **Pull full HTML source** before closing this audit — body content (links, alt text, unsubscribe block, physical address, merge tokens) cannot be assessed from the truncated `<head>`.
