# Why your resolutions deserve a July check-in
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- Eight consecutive editorial newsletters from a florist. "Jim at 1-800-FLOWERS" has now sent a lifestyle/self-help email about **July resolution check-ins** — the most extreme brand drift yet. The hero is "Happy Midyear! How Are Your Resolutions Holding Up?" with goal-setting bullet points, editorial photos, and what appears to be a book/product placement block ("POWER & IMPACT"). There is not a single flower, arrangement, or gift offer anywhere in the visible render.
- The subject-brand mismatch has reached a new low. A subscriber who signed up for flower deals is now getting a motivational newsletter — and this time there isn't even a seasonal flower hook to rationalize it. July 4th weekend just passed; this was a live buying occasion for patriotic arrangements, summer parties, and hostess gifts. None of that appears here.
- This is editorial content strategy gone off the rails: eight identical editorial sends since May with no apparent commercial purpose. If a subscriber hasn't unsubscribed yet, this one will likely push them over the edge.

## What's working

- **Season anchor is real:** "Happy Midyear" is a legitimate July hook — the date is accurate and the angle is novel enough to stand out in a crowded inbox.
- **No render bugs:** The layout renders cleanly; no broken images or overlapping text visible.
- **Subject line length:** At 44 characters, it fits cleanly on mobile.

## What's weak

- **Zero commercial intent from a commercial brand:** A florist sending a goals-and-resolutions newsletter with no flowers, no offer, and no CTA back to the store is a broken subscriber expectation.
- **"POWER & IMPACT" block looks like a third-party plug:** The prominent midpage block reads as sponsored/affiliate content — damaging trust in a brand email.
- **Photos show no product:** Every image in the render is people/lifestyle photography. A 1-800-FLOWERS email without a single flower is a trust violation.
- **No offer of any kind:** No discount, no free shipping, no seasonal BOGO — the commercial core of the brand's subscriber relationship is completely absent.
- **Eight in a row:** This is the eighth consecutive editorial send since May 17 (Mental Health → Memorial Day → Graduation → Weddings → Father's Day × 2 → "Amazing Dads" re-send → now resolutions). The cadence fatigue penalty here is severe.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Reconnect the editorial hook to the product:** If Jim insists on running a "midyear check-in" angle, the bridge copy should be "give yourself a midyear reset — send someone you love flowers today" with a real offer and a shop CTA. The narrative vehicle is fine; the missing commercial landing is the problem.
- 2. **Kill or clearly disclose the "POWER & IMPACT" block:** It reads as paid placement. Either remove it or add visible context so subscribers know what it is.
- 3. **Add a seasonal offer:** July 4th just happened; summer entertaining / hostess gifts / thinking-of-you arrangements are live use cases. One hero tile with a real dollar-off would change everything.
- 4. **Break the editorial streak:** The next send must be a promotional email. Eight editorials in a row has diluted the brand's commercial identity.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh summer arrangements + a midyear offer worth opening for`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — 3 CTA images:**

| Image filename | URL |
|---|---|
| `SubscribeNow0705.gif` | `https://image.em.1800flowersinc.com/lib/fe9712727466027c73/m/1/SubscribeNow0705.gif` |
| `refer-a-friend0705.gif` | `https://image.em.1800flowersinc.com/lib/fe9712727466027c73/m/1/refer-a-friend0705.gif` |
| `ContactJm0705.gif` | `https://image.em.1800flowersinc.com/lib/fe9712727466027c73/m/1/ContactJm0705.gif` |

All three are interactive CTAs (subscribe, referral, contact). With images blocked — the default in Outlook and many corporate clients — these render as blank, unclickable areas. Alt text on anchor-wrapped images is the minimum fallback.

**CSS double-semicolon syntax error** in the `@media (max-width: 320px)` block:

```css
th a { color:#000000 !important; font-size:12px !important;; display:inline; }
```

The double `;;` is technically invalid CSS. Most parsers recover silently, but it signals a copy-paste editing error and can fail strict parsers.

**Media query boundary overlap at exactly 500px:** The `(max-width: 500px and min-width: 320px)` and `(max-width: 600px and min-width: 500px)` queries both match a 500px-wide viewport. Conflicting `th` and `.tablemobilestacked` rules from both blocks apply simultaneously; cascade order resolves it, but the intent is ambiguous and the behavior differs from 499px and 501px viewports.

**HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype** — common in high-volume commercial email for legacy client compatibility; not a defect, noted for context.

### 7. Recommendations

1. **Add alt text to all three CTA GIFs** — minimum: `alt="Subscribe Now"`, `alt="Refer a Friend"`, `alt="Contact Jim"`. Images-off rendering should degrade gracefully, not disappear.

2. **Probe destination URLs** — configure the QA pipeline to follow click-redirects (with a short timeout and allowlist for known ESP redirect domains) so broken links and missing UTM params can be caught pre-send.

3. **Confirm physical address is present in full body** — if absent, it must be added before send to satisfy CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5).

4. **Verify `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers** at the ESP/relay level — if the headers exist in the ESP send but are stripped by the AgentMail relay, fix the relay capture; if they are genuinely absent, add them to satisfy Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements.

5. **Resolve the double-semicolon CSS typo** at `max-width: 320px` → `th a` rule. Low risk, but indicates a gap in template QA.

6. **Clarify 500px media query boundary** — either shift one breakpoint by 1px (`min-width: 501px`) or merge the overlapping rules to make intent explicit.
