# Send Sunshine Their Way 🌞 Enjoy 20% Off Sitewide
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- This is 1-800-Flowers' seasonal transition email — pivoting cleanly from "Spring" (yesterday's $15 credit send) to "Summer In Bloom Sale" with a 20% off sitewide headline. That pivot is well-timed, and the 20% sitewide is the clearest, highest-value offer this brand has sent in the recent audit window — more compelling than yesterday's $15 credit and more digestible than May 19's 2X Blooms mechanic. Visual execution is tidy, the hierarchy is strong, and there are no render issues.
- The problem is everything this email *doesn't* do. It lands with no deadline, a generic "SHOP NOW" CTA, and no anchor product to convert desire into intent. Coming as the fifth email in ten days — the day after a spring clearance sale — it risks being treated as brand wallpaper rather than a genuine buying signal.
- Solid bones, forgettable finish.

## What's working

- **20% sitewide is the cleanest offer in this brand's recent run** — concrete, high-value, zero ambiguity vs. the $15 credit or 2X Blooms mechanics that required more cognitive work
- **Visual hierarchy lands correctly** — "Save 20%" and "Summer In Bloom Sale" dominate the hero immediately; the eye doesn't hunt for the offer
- **Seasonal framing is sharp** — "Summer In Bloom" on May 21 feels timely and avoids the stale spring language from yesterday's send
- **"Shop By Category" grid** gives recipients a natural browse path and expands occasion relevance (edible gifts, sunflowers, etc.)
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no gaps or obvious layout bugs

## What's weak

- **No visible deadline** — "20% off sitewide" floats without a time-bound anchor; easy to bookmark, forget, and never act on
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is too generic** — doesn't reinforce the 20% discount or name a category; a missed conversion nudge
- **Cadence fatigue is setting in** — fifth email from this sender in ten days, following a sale *yesterday*; repeat subscribers are primed to start treating this as noise
- **No hero product** — the email opens the category grid before anchoring on a single summer must-buy; browsing layouts underperform when desire needs to be created, not just captured
- **No social proof or bestseller signals** — no reviews, no "most popular" badge, nothing to reduce choice paralysis on a sitewide offer

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline** — "Through Sunday" or "48 hours only" transforms a deferrable offer into an urgent one. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA to reinforce the discount** — "Save 20% on Flowers" or "Shop the Summer Sale" beats "SHOP NOW" on clarity and click intent.
- 3. **Lead with one anchor product** — a sunflower arrangement or summer hero bouquet above the category grid creates desire before presenting choice. Single-product CTAs consistently outperform browse-everything layouts for discount emails.
- 4. **Segment or pause the cadence** — sending a spring clearance and a summer sitewide sale on back-to-back days to the same list trains subscribers to defer. Either segment (buyers vs. browsers) or let this offer breathe for 48 hours.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Summer Sale: 20% Off Every Arrangement`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh seasonal picks, all at 20% off. Shop before it ends.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sunflowers, summer bouquets, edible gifts — all 20% off sitewide.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta — confirmed defect**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom. Fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Aggressive `*` reset — moderate risk**
```css
* { line-height:100%; max-height:1000000px; ... }
```
The `max-height:1000000px` hack (anti-Gmail-clipping) is applied globally instead of scoped to the wrapper table. The `line-height:100%` on `*` can clip descenders in some WebKit clients; should be scoped to table cells, not all elements.

**Inconsistent font format for Playfair Display weight-900 normal**
```css
/* All other weights: woff2 */
src:url(...v22/nuFvD...uksukDT.woff) format('woff');
```
Every other `@font-face` declaration uses `woff2`. This one falls back to the older `woff` format, adding ~30% payload overhead for that weight on clients that attempt web font loading (Apple Mail, Outlook iOS).

**Mixed Playfair Display CDN versions — minor**
- Italic 400/900 load from `v21` and `v22`
- Normal 400/600 load from `v21`
- Normal 900 loads from `v22`

Not a rendering failure, but indicates the CSS was assembled piecemeal; a future CDN path change for v21 would silently break those weights.

**Duplicate `<style>` blocks with overlapping rules**
Two separate `<style>` elements declare `:root { color-scheme:light dark; }` and `.ReadMsgBody { width:100%; }`. No functional breakage, but increases parse time and complicates maintenance.

**`.mobileshow` triple-hidden pattern**
```css
.mobileshow { display:none !important; visibility:hidden !important; width:0px; overflow:hidden !important; }
```
Redundant — `display:none` already removes the element from layout. `visibility:hidden` and `width:0px` are dead overrides here.
