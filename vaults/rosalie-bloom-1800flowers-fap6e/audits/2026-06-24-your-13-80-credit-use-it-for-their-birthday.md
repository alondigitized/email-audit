# Your $13.80 Credit—Use It for Their Birthday 🎉
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- This is a follow-up credit-redemption send arriving two days after the June 22 "What We're Sending for Summer Birthdays (+ $13.80 Credit)" email — which already performed well enough to earn an 8/10 in this audit. The subject line is the strongest part of the email: "Your $13.80 Credit—Use It for Their Birthday" creates clear ownership and sets a transactional expectation. The body doesn't honor that promise. Instead of leading with "here's your credit, pick a bouquet," the email opens with an editorial headline ("We Know Birthdays"), a lifestyle hero image, a pull quote testimonial, and a personalized tote card — all of which push the actual redemption moment below the fold.
- The creative is clean and well-shot. The summer birthday editorial has a warm, confident voice. But this is the third consecutive birthday-themed email in three days (June 22, June 23, June 24), and it's re-raising the exact same $13.80 credit from two sends ago. The subscriber who was going to use that credit has already decided. The one who hasn't is now fatigued.
- The net result is an email whose subject line promises a transaction and whose body delivers a brand magazine. That mismatch is the main drag on performance.

## What's working

- **Subject line is the clearest in this birthday run** — "$13.80 Credit" is a concrete, scannable dollar amount with personal ownership ("Your") that stands out in a crowded inbox.
- **Hero bouquet image is genuinely appealing** — lush, colorful, seasonally right for summer birthday gifting.
- **Pull quote / testimonial section** adds editorial credibility and warmth without feeling fake.
- **Shop By Category strip** at the bottom gives a browse hook for undecided shoppers.
- **Render is clean** — no broken images, no layout breakage, no overlapping text across the full scroll.

## What's weak

- **Credit gets buried in the top banner bar, then disappears** — the subject loudly promises a $13.80 credit but the body never gives subscribers a clear "tap here to spend it" moment at the top.
- **Third birthday email in three days is cadence fatigue** — June 22, 23, and 24 all hit the same birthday theme; subscribers who care have already acted.
- **No expiration date on the credit** — "Use It" without a deadline removes the one urgency lever the subject was building toward.
- **App download CTA competes with purchase intent** — "Join the Celebration" + App Store/Google Play badges push against the revenue conversion goal.
- **Personalized tote product card (with the name "Mei") feels off-persona** — a tote bag sitting mid-email in a flower-brand birthday send reads as a distraction, not a hero.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the body with the credit redemption moment.** Drop the "We Know Birthdays" editorial header and open instead with something like: "You have $13.80 waiting. Pick the bouquet." One hero product + one bold CTA button that says "Shop & Apply Credit." This closes the gap between subject promise and body delivery.
- 2. **Add a hard expiration date — and make it visible.** "Credit expires July 1" or "Use by Sunday" turns the soft "Use It" into a real urgency lever. Put it directly beneath the headline, not buried in footer fine print.
- 3. **Cut the testimonial pull quote on this send.** It worked as editorial texture on the June 22 original. On the June 24 redemption reminder, it dilutes urgency. The reader already trusts the brand; they need a reason to act *now*.
- 4. **Kill the App Store CTA or move it to the footer.** It cannibalizes click intent from a send whose only goal is credit redemption.
- 5. **Retire the tote card or make it a hero product.** If the personalized tote is a real product you're leading with, give it a section. If not, it's dead weight in a floral birthday send.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$13.80 Credit Expires Soon — Shop Birthday Flowers`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last chance: your $13.80 birthday credit`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free credit applied automatically at checkout — no code needed.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Pick any bouquet and your credit comes off instantly. Expires July 1.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta — confirmed issue**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks user zoom in mobile browsers. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Users with low vision cannot zoom the email body.

**Aggressive global `line-height` reset — confirmed**
```css
* { line-height: 100% }
```
Setting `line-height:100%` on `*` compresses line spacing to 1em across all elements. This can make multi-line text blocks illegible (the CSS spec recommends `normal` ≈ 1.2 as minimum for readability). Applied to body copy, this is a legibility defect.

**Mobile breakpoint at 418px — narrow**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)
```
Standard mobile email breakpoint is 480px or 600px. At 418px, this catches only the narrowest handsets. Devices with viewport widths 419–479px (common Android budget phones) receive desktop layout.

**`.showmobile` hardcoded to 414px**
```css
.showmobile { width:414px !important; }
```
Hardcoded to iPhone 6/7/8 logical width. On any other device that matches the 418px breakpoint (e.g., 360px, 390px screens), this overflows or creates a fixed-width artifact.

**Font format inconsistency — Playfair Display 900 weight loads `.woff` instead of `.woff2`**
```css
src:url(https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/playfairdisplay/v22/...btM.woff) format('woff');
```
All other `@font-face` declarations use `.woff2`. `.woff` is ~30% larger and unnecessary in any client that supports `.woff2` (all modern clients). Likely a copy-paste error from an older asset reference — also note the version mismatch (`v22` vs `v21` used elsewhere).
