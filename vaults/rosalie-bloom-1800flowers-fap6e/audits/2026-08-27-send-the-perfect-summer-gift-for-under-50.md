# Send the perfect summer gift for under $50
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-27**
## Executive summary

- This is 1-800-Flowers' "Top 5 Gifts Under $50" listicle format — a clean departure from the endless "30% off sitewide" flash sales and the three-day "Double Blooms" run that just wrapped (Aug 24–26, peaking at 7.0 and sliding to 5.0). It's a numbered countdown (1-Summer Dunes bouquet, 2-chocolate dipped berries, 3-Mediterranean citrus rose, 4-Warm Sunset bouquet, 5-personalized straw beach bag) with a real "Under $50" price anchor in the hero, which is more concrete than the vague "best-selling gifts" send from Aug 22.
- The execution has one clear problem: there's no visible price anywhere in the five product blocks — no "$44.99" or "$39.99" callouts under any item, just product names and one-line taglines. The "Under $50" promise lives only in the hero banner and is never reinforced at the point of decision, so the actual offer strength is unverifiable by the time someone's scrolled past item 2. The straw beach bag also breaks the "gift" framing slightly — it's personalizable with a name (shown as "Nicole" monogram), which is a nice touch but visually the weakest photo of the five (flat product shot vs. the styled lifestyle shots above it).
- This is a mid-pack send — better than the flash-sale spam, worse than the Aug 24 Double Blooms peak, hurt mainly by the missing per-item pricing.

## What's working

- Numbered 1-5 countdown format creates scannability and a light gamified "which one do I pick" browsing experience.
- Hero band is unambiguous: "Summer GIFTS UNDER $50" with a clear "SHOP SUMMER" CTA link.
- Good product variety across the five picks (bouquet, edible gift, potted plant, second bouquet, accessory) avoids the single-SKU monotony of recent sends.
- "Shop by Category" grid at the bottom (Birthday, Romance, Sympathy, Best Sellers, Plants, Same-Day, Summer, Greeting Cards, Gourmet Gifts) gives a strong secondary navigation path for browsers not sold on the top 5.
- No render bugs — images all load cleanly, text is legible, layout holds together end to end.

## What's weak

- Not a single price is shown next to any of the five products, so the headline "$50" promise is never verified against the actual items — a shopper has to click through to find out if it's true.
- Each item's CTA is just an underlined product name link, not a button — weak visual click target compared to a real CTA button.
- Straw beach bag (#5) photo is a flat studio shot against a plain background, visually flat next to the styled lifestyle photography used for items 1–4.
- "Personalization Mall" and "Cheryl's" family-of-brands logos appear at the very bottom with zero context — dead weight for a reader who won't parse cross-brand cannibalization at a glance.
- No urgency element anywhere in the body — no countdown, no "ends [date]," unlike the Aug 19 "Last Chance" send that at least manufactured urgency with a ticker.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a real price under each of the 5 products (e.g. "$39.99") — this is the single biggest miss; the hero promises "under $50" but never proves it at the point of decision, which is the whole reason someone would open this email.
- 2. Convert each numbered product link into an actual button ("Shop Summer Dunes →") to raise click affordance instead of relying on underlined text.
- 3. Add a light urgency line under the hero (e.g. "Order by 2pm for same-day delivery") to give a reason to act today rather than browse later.
- 4. Replace or restyle the beach bag product shot to match the lifestyle photography treatment used for items 1-4, or drop it in favor of a sixth bouquet/plant pick that fits the visual set better.
- 5. Trim the family-of-brands footer or move it above the legal fine print with one line of context, since as-is it reads as clutter after the CTA is already spent.
