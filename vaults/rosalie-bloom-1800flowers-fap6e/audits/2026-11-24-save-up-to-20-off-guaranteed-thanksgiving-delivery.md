# ⏰ Save Up to 20% OFF + Guaranteed Thanksgiving Delivery
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-24**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, on-brand last-call Thanksgiving send, and it's better than most of the November run. The hero is warm and seasonal. "ALL SET FOR Thanksgiving" sits over a family-dinner photo. The copy gives a real reason to act now ("only a few days away," "last chance"). The code FLOWERSALE is in the top banner. The subject pairs the discount with a "Guaranteed Thanksgiving Delivery" promise, which is the anxiety a shopper has at this point.
- The body is where it loses focus. The hero has one CTA, but below it are two product grids with three more CTAs, then a nine-tile category block and app badges. The 20% offer is a thin banner at the very top and never repeats. The "guaranteed delivery" promise from the subject is not visible anywhere in the render. The cutoff date appears only in the fine print ("Offer ends 11/26/2026"). Compared to 11/23's "You Can Still Save Up To 20% On Thanksgiving" and 11/20's tiered $149/$99/$69 offer, this adds little new beyond the delivery angle, and the render doesn't back it up.
- Verdict: solid and credible, but it undersells its two best assets, the delivery guarantee and the deadline. It's the third Thanksgiving push in five days.

## What's working

- The hero is on-theme and emotional. The family dinner photo and script "Thanksgiving" lettering make the occasion clear at a glance.
- The code FLOWERSALE is printed in the top banner, so there's no hunt for it.
- The hero copy creates real urgency: "only a few days away" and "last chance to send that host gift."
- The flower and centerpiece grids are clean, with consistent photography and short product names. The "Fresh for Thursday" and "Center of Attention" headers are concrete.
- There are no render bugs. Images load, text is legible, and the yellow CTAs contrast well on both the red and white backgrounds.

## What's weak

- "Guaranteed Thanksgiving Delivery" is in the subject but absent from the body. There's no cutoff time, badge or banner, so the promise isn't confirmed.
- The 20% offer is a small strip at the top and is not restated near any CTA or product. The conditions are only in fine print: the end date of 11/26 and the exclusions.
- There are no prices, no sale pricing and no reviews on any product tile. The click decision has nothing concrete to hold onto.
- There are five CTA zones plus nine category tiles, and the generic "SHOP NOW" is the weakest copy. Below the hero the email turns into a catalog.
- The category tiles (Birthday, Romance, Sympathy) dilute the Thanksgiving message, and the app badges add clutter at the bottom.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a delivery-guarantee strip directly under the hero, for example "Order by Wed 12 PM ET for Thanksgiving delivery." Put the end date and a clear "20% OFF" next to it.
- 2. Make the hero CTA specific, such as "Shop Thanksgiving Centerpieces" or "Send by Thursday." Replace the generic "SHOP NOW."
- 3. Show starting prices or a "from $XX" line under the product tiles, and add a star rating to one or two.
- 4. Cut the category grid and app badges for this send, or fold them into a short link row. Keep the email to two modules plus the hero.
- 5. Put the code in a copyable button near the hero, as the 11/20 send did.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Order by Wed for Thanksgiving delivery + 20% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call: guaranteed Thanksgiving flowers, 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use code FLOWERSALE. Centerpieces and fall bouquets still arrive by Thursday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Offer ends 11/26. Send the host gift before cutoff.`

# Technical Audit: 1-800-FLOWERS, "⏰ Save Up to 20% OFF + Guaranteed Thanksgiving Delivery"

The HTML I received is truncated after the `<head>` styles, so body-level checks (footer, unsubscribe link, CTAs, alt text on content images) rest on the QA output alone. I flag items as confirmed or unverified accordingly.

## 1. Technical Summary
The `<head>` is well-formed for email (XHTML doctype, VML/Office namespaces, Outlook resets, dark-mode meta). The QA run found 0 hard failures and 4 warning groups: header capture gaps, a missing physical address, `http://` tracking pixels, and alt-less pixel images. Several of these may be artifacts of the AgentMail relay and the truncation rather than defects in the email.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Link destinations not verified.** 21 links were skipped as tracking or click-redirect domains, so none were probed. The visible click domain is `click.dv.1800flowers.com`. I can't confirm destinations, status codes or redirect chains.
- **Insecure pixels.** Two third-party pixels use plain `http://`:
  - `http://view.atdmt.com/FLO/view/361227669/direct/01/`
  - `http://pixel.mathtag.com/event/img?mt_id=155437&mt_adid=103507&v1=&v2=&v3=&s1=&s2=&s3=` (a second one uses `mt_id=155438`)
  
  Clients or proxies may block or rewrite them, so those impressions may go uncounted. `atdmt.com` is a legacy ad-network domain, and the pixel may be dead.
- **Empty macro values.** The mathtag URLs carry empty `v1=`, `v2=`, `v3=`, `s1=`, `s2=`, `s3=`. That is likely by design, but it could also mean unpopulated dynamic values.
- **Duplicate pixel.** `https://bounceexchange.com/tag/em/1622.gif` appears twice, so the same event may be double-counted.
- **Stacked trackers.** The email carries six or more third-party and first-party trackers: atdmt, two mathtag pixels, bounceexchange (twice), click.dv open pixel, and mi.1800flowers `o.gif`. That is heavy and adds load and privacy exposure.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `alt` on pixels (confirmed).** All six flagged images are tracking pixels (atdmt, mathtag ×2, bounceexchange ×2, `click.dv.../o/dc4de0a2-...`, `mi.1800flowers.com/.../o.gif`). Add `alt=""`, since they are decorative. I can't assess alt text on content images because the body is truncated.
- **Zoom disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="... maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom in some clients, a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Text resizing disabled.** `* {-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` is applied to every element. The `body` override to `100%` does not cascade to descendants, so iOS text scaling is suppressed.
- **Fixed mobile widths.** At `max-width: 418px`, `.mobile` and `.showmobile` force `width:414px !important`. On 320–375px viewports this can cause horizontal overflow. It depends on which elements use these classes, which I can't see.
- **No `lang` attribute.** `<html xmlns=...>` has no `lang`, which screen readers need to pick a pronunciation.
- **Generic `<title>`.** It reads `1800Flowers.com`, which does not match the subject. This is minor.
- **Global `th` rule.** `th {font-size:20px !important; color:#000000 !important}` forces black text on any `th`. This could break dark-mode contrast if any `th` sits on a dark background. I can't verify that from the truncated source.
- **Web fonts.** Playfair Display and Lato load from `fonts.gstatic.com` with `font-display:swap`. Only `mso-font-alt` is visible as a fallback, and the full `font-family` stacks are truncated.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unrendered tokens (`{{ }}`, `%%`, `[[ ]]`) appear in the visible source or QA data. The body is truncated, so this is only partially verified.

- **Authentication-Results** was not captured (SPF/DKIM/DMARC unknown). The sending domain is `em.1800flowers.com`. Check the raw headers from a direct delivery rather than the relay.
- **Unsubscribe link in the body** can't be assessed because of truncation.
