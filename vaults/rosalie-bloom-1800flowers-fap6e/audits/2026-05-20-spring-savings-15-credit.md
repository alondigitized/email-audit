# Spring savings: $15 credit
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- This is a kitchen-sink spring campaign from 1-800-Flowers that tries to serve three distinct occasions simultaneously — a general "Late Spring Bliss" theme, graduation gifting, and what looks like Memorial Day hero messaging ("Salute the heroes who fought for our freedom"). The fragmentation is the core problem: the subject line promises a "$15 credit" but that offer is not the hero of the email — it's buried or relegated to the tiny top banner while the scroll is dominated by product category rows with no unifying discount anchor.
- The creative itself is clean and on-brand: soft pastels, bright florals, consistent button treatment. But this is the fourth email in ten days from 1-800-Flowers (May 11 welcome, May 18 clearance, May 19 Double Blooms, and now May 20). The subscriber is fatigued before they even click. Yesterday's Double Blooms send was stronger — a single mechanic, a single CTA — and this one takes a step backward into catalog-dump territory.
- Bottom line: the subject earns the open, then the email doesn't honor the promise. The $15 credit should be the hero, and it isn't.

## What's working

- **Subject line pulls its weight** — "Spring savings: $15 credit" is concrete, 26 chars, no spam signals, and the specific dollar amount gives a real reason to open.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds together on a mobile crop.
- **Seasonal relevance** — Late Spring, graduation, Memorial Day are all timely beats for a May 20 send.
- **Brand consistency** — pastel palette, rounded SHOP NOW buttons, and overall voice are coherent with prior sends.

## What's weak

- **$15 credit goes missing** — The subject hook is a $15 credit, but it does not appear as a prominent hero offer in the email body. A subscriber who opened for that reason will scroll looking for it and feel misled.
- **Three-occasion fragmentation** — Spring bliss + graduation + Memorial Day in one scroll dilutes each message to the point of none of them landing.
- **"SHOP NOW" everywhere, equally** — Every product block has the same button with the same copy, creating zero hierarchy. The eye has nowhere to prioritize.
- **No urgency or deadline** — The $15 credit has no stated expiration. "Spring savings" implies a window but provides no credible deadline to act.
- **Fourth send in ten days** — Back-to-back daily sends on May 18, 19, 20 will spike unsubscribes. The May 19 Double Blooms was a better send; this one creates diminishing returns.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the $15 credit the hero, not the banner footnote.** Drop a full-bleed hero block: big number, clear CTA, expiration date. The subject promise must match what you see above the fold.
- 2. **Pick one occasion.** Graduation is the highest-intent seasonal beat right now; own it. Save the Memorial Day hero for a dedicated send or a secondary module, not equal real estate.
- 3. **Add a deadline.** "Offer expires May 25" turns a passive credit into an action trigger. Without it, subscribers bookmark and forget.
- 4. **Rest the list.** A single send Thursday after skipping Wednesday would have more impact than three consecutive days. Reference: the May 11 welcome had strong scores precisely because it wasn't one of six sends that week.
- 5. **Differentiate CTA copy by section.** "Send Grad Flowers" beats "SHOP NOW" every time. Match the verb to the occasion.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your $15 credit — use it on grad gifts`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$15 off: save it for the grad in your life`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Expires May 25 — grab flowers, plants, or a gift basket`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Flowers for the grad. Cookie cakes too. $15 off ends soon.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Non-standard mobile breakpoint** — `@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)` targets iPhone SE screen width specifically. Standard email breakpoints are 480px or 600px. Devices 419–479px wide (many Android phones) will receive the desktop layout, likely causing horizontal overflow.

- **Inconsistent font format versions** — `Playfair Display` references both `v21` and `v22` in the same `<style>` block:
  - `v21`: weight 400 italic, weight 400 normal, weight 600 normal
  - `v22`: weight 900 italic, weight 900 normal
  This is not inherently broken, but signals the font stack was patched without a full audit pass.

- **Degraded font compression on weight 900** — The `Playfair Display` normal weight 900 declaration loads `.woff` instead of `.woff2`:
  ```
  src:url(...nuFvD-...ukDT.woff) format('woff')
  ```
  All other faces use `.woff2`. `.woff` is ~30% larger; the inconsistency suggests a copy-paste error from an older font embed snippet.

- **`mso-font-alt` missing on Lato** — All six Lato `@font-face` declarations lack `mso-font-alt`. Outlook ignores web fonts and renders its own fallback (typically Times New Roman) unless `mso-font-alt` specifies a system font. Playfair Display correctly includes `mso-font-alt:'Times New Roman'`; Lato does not. Evidence:
  ```css
  @font-face {font-family:'Lato'; font-style:normal; font-weight:400; ...
    src:url(https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/lato/v17/S6uyw4BMUTPHvxw.woff2) format('woff2');
  }
  /* no mso-font-alt present */
  ```

- **`max-height:1000000px` on `*` selector** — This is a known Gmail clipping workaround, but applying it globally can interact with sticky/fixed elements and CSS animations in webmail clients that render a subset of CSS. Low risk for a standard promotional email.

- **Alt text on images** — Cannot assess; HTML truncated before `<img>` tags.
