# Celebrate more with Double Blooms
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-06**
## Executive summary

- This is a "Double Blooms" upgrade send. Nine bouquets each carry a "SAVE $20" badge and a small-versus-large "2x the blooms" pairing. The idea is strong: double the flowers for a $20 discount. It is easy to grasp and visually repetitive in a good way. The "DOUBLE / BLOOMS" display type bracketing the hero is a clever framing device.
- The execution is weaker than the concept. The email is very long, with nine near-identical tiles. A second, different offer (20% off Fall Favorites, code GLOW20) is stacked below them, followed by a category grid and app badges. The send makes the reader work out which deal applies to what. At the hero, only one "SHOP NOW" button is visible, and it doesn't say which bouquet it opens. It is a good catalog email and a mediocre focused promo. After 11/05's clean "40% Off Red Roses + a Vase on Us," it reads as a step down in clarity and offer strength.
- Judgement: a solid mid-pack send. The mechanic is fun, but the offer is muddled and the length buries the best content.

## What's working

- The "Double Down" hero with a large rose bouquet and a "SAVE $20" badge puts the offer first.
- The small-to-large "2x the blooms" pairing shows the value instead of just stating it.
- The consistent tile layout (badge, image pair, bold product name) makes scanning fast.
- The category grid at the bottom (Birthday, Sympathy, Same-Day) gives an exit for non-bouquet shoppers.
- The tulips, roses and lilies match the season.

## What's weak

- Two competing offers: "Save $20" on the doubles and "20% Off" with GLOW20 in the lower section. Neither is clearly the main one.
- Nine repetitive tiles make the email very long. Several are the same product in a different color, such as purple, red, yellow and pink roses.
- There is only one visible CTA, in the hero. I can't see per-tile buttons, so the tiles don't read as clear click targets.
- The hero gives no price, no minimum spend and no deadline, so there is no urgency.
- The code GLOW20 is a text line rather than a button or a visually strong element.
- The "Double Blooms" name is clever, but "Save $20" on a bouquet whose price isn't shown doesn't tell me the value.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one offer. Lead with "Double the blooms, save $20" and drop or demote the GLOW20 block. Show the price of each bouquet so the $20 means something.
- 2. Cut the nine tiles to four or five best sellers, and make each tile's product name and image its own clear link with a button.
- 3. Add a deadline, such as "Ends Sunday," in the hero. Credible urgency was missing.
- 4. Make the hero CTA specific, for example "Shop Double Roses."
- 5. Move the category grid and app badges into a lighter footer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Double the roses, $20 off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `2x the blooms, $20 less`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Nine bouquets upgraded to double size, ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fall tulips, roses and lilies, now with twice the flowers.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is structurally conventional (XHTML 1.0 Transitional, VML/Outlook namespaces, dark-mode meta, responsive media queries). The QA run reports 0 errors, 5 warnings and a 38% pass rate. The findings are mostly third-party tracking pixels with missing alt text or insecure `http://` sources. The compliance warnings could not be confirmed because the HTML is truncated and the relay may have stripped headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unprobed links:** 29 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the QA probe, so none of their destinations or redirect chains were validated.
- **Insecure pixels (`http://`):**
  - `http://view.atdmt.com/FLO/view/361227669/direct/01/`
  - `http://pixel.mathtag.com/event/img?mt_id=155437&mt_adid=103507&...`
  - `http://pixel.mathtag.com/event/img?mt_id=155438&mt_adid=103507&...`
  - Clients that block or rewrite mixed content may drop these, so conversion/retargeting tracking can be lost.
- **Empty macro parameters:** Both mathtag pixels send `v1=&v2=&v3=&s1=&s2=&s3=` with no values. These may be unpopulated template variables, or they may be intentionally blank. This can't be confirmed from the truncated source.
- **Many third-party trackers:** Pixels from atdmt.com, mathtag.com and bounceexchange.com (`/tag/em/1622.gif`, which appears twice) are in addition to first-party `click.dv.1800flowers.com` and `mi.1800flowers.com`. Each is an extra request, a possible blocker point and a privacy-disclosure surface.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (QA-confirmed):**
  - `view.atdmt.com/.../direct/01/`
  - Both `pixel.mathtag.com` images
  - Both `bounceexchange.com/tag/em/1622.gif` images
  - `click.dv.1800flowers.com/o/5ed11587-...`
  - `mi.1800flowers.com/p/up/ada90b86b2846dd9/o.gif`
  - All are tracking pixels, so the fix is `alt=""` (and ideally `width="1" height="1"`) so screen readers skip them. Without the attribute, some readers announce the filename or URL.
- **Viewport zoom restriction:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` blocks pinch-zoom on clients that honor it, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **No `lang` attribute:** The `<html>` tag in the visible source has only `xmlns`, `xmlns:v` and `xmlns:o`, with no `lang`.
- **Dark-mode color forcing:** The CSS sets `th {color:#000000 !important}` and `th a {color:#000000 !important}`, and `.appleLinks a {color:#000000 !important}`, while the email declares `color-scheme: light dark`. Black text forced with `!important` can become unreadable if a background inverts in dark mode. This is unverified because the section backgrounds are in the truncated portion.
- **Redundant text-size rule:** `* {-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` is overridden by `body {-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%}`. This is harmless but inconsistent.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No unrendered merge tokens are visible in the supplied source. The `mi.1800flowers.com` pixel carries `mi_u=174149195` and a campaign code, so per-recipient IDs are rendered. The empty `v1`–`v3` and `s1`–`s3` pixel parameters (see section 2) are the only possible unpopulated values.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication)
All three items below are QA warnings, and none is confirmed as a real failure:
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** Both were not found. The QA note says the AgentMail relay may not capture them. Verify against the raw message. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require one-click unsubscribe for high-volume senders.
- **Physical address:** None was detected. The footer is likely in the truncated HTML, so confirm it is present.
- **Authentication-Results:** Not found, so SPF/DKIM/DMARC status is unknown. The sender is `1800FLOWERS@em.1800flowers.com`, and alignment couldn't be verified.
- **In-body unsubscribe link:** Not visible in the supplied source, so this couldn't be confirmed either way.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- **Not verifiable:** The 29 click-tracking links were not resolved, so landing-page alignment and any UTM parameters can't be confirmed. No `utm_*` parameters appear in the visible URLs. Campaign attribution appears to use `mi_cid`, `mi_mid` and `mi_ecmp=261106_EMAIL_18F_PZ_DV_12AMET_PROMO` (Movable Ink-style).
- **Plain-text part:** 5522 of 6906 characters (about 80%) are URLs. This is a spam-filter risk and makes the text version hard to read.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add `alt=""` and `width="1" height="1"` to all tracking pixels listed in section 3.
2. Move the atdmt and mathtag pixels to `https://`, or drop them if the vendors don't support it. Check whether the empty `v1`–`v3` and `s1`–`s3` parameters should be populated.
3. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta, and add `lang="en"` to `<html>`.
4. Inspect the raw `.eml` for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, `Authentication-Results`, the footer address and the unsubscribe link, to settle the compliance warnings.
5. Resolve the 29 tracking redirects with a headless fetch to check destinations and final-URL parameters.
6. Rebalance the plain-text part with more descriptive copy and fewer raw tracking URLs.
7. Test dark-mode rendering with the `!important` black `th` and link colors.
