# Send Love… Times Two 🌹✨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- This is the fifth Double Blooms execution from 1-800-Flowers in 25 days (May 19, May 27, June 2, June 10, and now June 13) — and the prior send was just three days ago. The June 11 Sympathy email hinted at a creative reset, but this send snaps right back to the same mechanic with near-identical structure. That cadence problem is the dominant story here, not the execution. The email itself is technically clean: the DOUBLE BLOOMS hero lands the value prop quickly, the product cards double-badge both the $20-off discount and the 2X blooms offer simultaneously, and the secondary BLOOMS20 code gives a second conversion path.
- The subject "Send Love… Times Two 🌹✨" is thematic and pleasant but declares nothing — a subscriber scanning their inbox gets no price or offer signal before opening. The body more than compensates, leading with "TWICE THE ROMANCE. MORE BLOOMS ON ROSES. FOR FREE" in large hero copy. But the bottom half loses focus fast: the Shop by Category grid and generic category tiles feel like structural filler after a tight hero section.
- Net judgment: the offer is real and easy to act on, the design is competent, and if this were the second send of the campaign it would score higher. As the fifth, it's fighting subscriber fatigue it created.

## What's working

- **Hero subhead does the heavy lifting**: "TWICE THE ROMANCE. MORE BLOOMS ON ROSES. FOR FREE" communicates the full mechanic in one read.
- **Dual-badge product cards**: each of the four arrangements carries both a $20-off badge and a 2X badge — two reasons to click, no fine print required.
- **Secondary offer is additive**: "20% off all our best sellers / use code BLOOMS20" gives a second conversion path that doesn't cannibalize the hero.
- **Clean, high-contrast CTA**: the red "SHOP NOW" button on the hero reads instantly with no visual ambiguity.
- **No render bugs**: layout is clean throughout the visible render — no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout drift.

## What's weak

- **Cadence is the lead problem**: five Double Blooms sends in 25 days — two in three days — with only a single Sympathy pivot in between. A subscribed customer who opened June 10 has no content reason to open this.
- **Subject hides the offer**: "Send Love… Times Two" is thematic but tells the inbox nothing about price or promotion before the open.
- **No deadline anywhere**: the hero, the product cards, and the code section are all evergreen — nothing communicates urgency or when the offer expires.
- **Shop by Category grid dilutes focus**: the generic category tiles (Best Sellers, Plants, Same Day, Bundles, Centerpieces, Gourmet, Occasions) appear well below the fold and scatter attention away from both conversion paths.
- **Zero social proof**: none of the four featured products carry star ratings, review counts, or any trust signal beyond product photography.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rest Double Blooms for at least 5–7 days**: no subject line or copy optimization fixes the fatigue from a fifth near-identical send. The single highest-ROI move is a pause.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to declare the offer**: the current subject teases rather than converts — subscribers who need to open to learn what "times two" means will scroll past.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free Double Blooms on Roses — Today Only 🌹`
- **Subject Alt B:** `2X the flowers, same price — plus 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Add double blooms to any rose order at no extra cost. Ends soon.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `More roses, zero extra. Use BLOOMS20 for an extra 20% off.`
- 3. **Pin an expiration date on the hero**: "Offer ends June 15" placed directly under the subhead converts passive browsers into same-day buyers.
- 4. **Add star-rating badges to the four product cards**: review counts are the fastest trust signal available and the most glaring absence in an otherwise structured product grid.
- 5. **Cut the Shop by Category grid or collapse it into a footer link**: it's visual noise that competes with both conversion paths and pads the email length without adding offer value.

## 1. Overview

This is the fifth Double Blooms execution from 1-800-Flowers in 25 days (May 19, May 27, June 2, June 10, and now June 13) — and the prior send was just three days ago. The June 11 Sympathy email hinted at a creative reset, but this send snaps right back to the same mechanic with near-identical structure. That cadence problem is the dominant story here, not the execution. The email itself is technically clean: the DOUBLE BLOOMS hero lands the value prop quickly, the product cards double-badge both the $20-off discount and the 2X blooms offer simultaneously, and the secondary BLOOMS20 code gives a second conversion path.

Net judgment: the offer is real and easy to act on, the design is competent, and if this were the second send of the campaign it would score higher. As the fifth, it's fighting subscriber fatigue it created.
