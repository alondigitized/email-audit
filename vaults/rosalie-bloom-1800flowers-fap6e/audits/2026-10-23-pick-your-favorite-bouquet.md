# Pick Your Favorite Bouquet
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-23**
## Executive summary

- This is a polished "This or That" send with a real but quiet offer. The green-striped hero, "Playing Favorites" script headline and three head-to-head matchups (Moody vs. Neutral, Farmhouse vs. Penthouse, Spooky vs. Scary) are fun and seasonal. The photography is strong. The 20% off code GLOW20 sits in a thin strip above the logo, so the first thing the eye lands on is the headline, not the discount.
- If this feels familiar, it should. It is nearly the same creative as the Oct 20 send, "25% Off. Which Will You Choose?", which used the same three matchups. This is the fourth promo in four days, after Oct 21's loud "25% Off Roses" and Oct 22's editorial "Most Coveted Flowers" send. The offer is also weaker than that earlier version, at 20% against 25%. A subscriber would read this as a recycled concept with a smaller discount.
- Judgement: well built, but the subject is vague, the offer is buried, and the cadence is tired. It's a middling 5–6 send.

## What's working

- The hero is clean and on-brand. The script "Favorites" lettering looks premium, and the orange "SHOP NOW" button stands out against the dark green.
- The matchup layout makes each pair easy to compare. The yellow "VS" circle is a clear visual device.
- The photography is vivid and seasonal, from the harvest oranges and burgundy blooms to the green glow-in-the-dark roses.
- The copy under each matchup is short and evocative, such as "Woodland magic or morning serenity?"
- The "Shop by Category" grid gives a useful fallback for people who don't care about any of the six bouquets.

## What's weak

- The 20% off code is in a thin banner at the very top, in small type. It is the weakest element in the hero, and the deadline appears to be only in the fine print.
- This is a near-repeat of the Oct 20 matchup email, with the same pairings and a smaller discount.
- All three matchup buttons say "SHOP FALL" or "SHOP HALLOWEEN". They don't say which bouquet won or what you're shopping for. The product names also have empty checkbox squares next to them, which look like a voting mechanic that doesn't work.
- The NYRR gift-bundle banner and the category grid dilute the "pick one" idea. Below the matchups the email turns into a generic catalog.
- The footer is dense, with long legal copy and a lot of fine print.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the offer into the hero. Make "20% OFF" the headline's companion in large type, with the code and a clear deadline, instead of a strip above the logo.
- 2. Make the vote real. Give each bouquet its own "Pick this one" or "Shop Autumn Fields" link to its product page, and drop the empty checkboxes or make them a real mechanic. Right now six products share three buttons.
- 3. Don't repeat the Oct 20 creative a few days later. Change the pairings, or lead with a "winners" angle, such as "Most picked this week".
- 4. Cut the lower modules. Keep the three matchups and one category row so the email has one job.
- 5. Put a deadline in the body copy near the CTA, not only in the footer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Autumn Fields or Graceful Fall? 20% Off Both`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Which bouquet wins? Code GLOW20 inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three head-to-head matchups, 20% off with code GLOW20.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Moody or neutral? Spooky or scary? Pick a side and save.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email has sound responsive and dark-mode scaffolding. Its main code-level problems are legacy third-party pixels served over `http://` and missing alt text on tracking images. The viewport meta tag also blocks pinch-zoom. Most compliance and link findings can't be confirmed, because the HTML is truncated and the relay likely stripped the headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Insecure tracking pixels (confirmed):**
  - `http://view.atdmt.com/FLO/view/361227669/direct/01/`
  - `http://pixel.mathtag.com/event/img?mt_id=155437&mt_adid=103507&…`
  - `http://pixel.mathtag.com/event/img?mt_id=155438&mt_adid=103507&…`
  
  Clients that proxy or block non-HTTPS images can drop these, so the conversion or retargeting events won't fire. The `atdmt.com` and `mathtag.com` endpoints belong to older ad-tech platforms, and I haven't verified they still resolve.
- **Empty parameters:** both mathtag URLs carry `v1=&v2=&v3=&s1=&s2=&s3=` with no values. Either the parameters are unused, or something upstream failed to populate them.
- **Duplicate pixel:** `https://bounceexchange.com/tag/em/1622.gif` appears twice. This causes double-firing or redundant requests.
- **Unprobed links:** 33 tracking links were skipped by the QA check, so destination status codes, redirect chains and dead links are unverified.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (confirmed, 7 images):** `view.atdmt.com`, both `pixel.mathtag.com` URLs, both `1622.gif` instances, `click.dv.1800flowers.com/o/7649b1c0-…` and `mi.1800flowers.com/p/up/ada90b86b2846dd9/o.gif`. All are tracking or open pixels, so the screen-reader impact is low. Add `alt=""` and explicit `width="1" height="1"` so they're ignored by assistive tech and don't render as broken-image placeholders when images are on but the host is blocked.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` blocks pinch-zoom on some mobile clients (WCAG 1.4.4).
- **Missing `lang`:** the `<html xmlns=… xmlns:v=… xmlns:o=…>` tag has no `lang` attribute.
- **Text resizing:** the universal `* {-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` rule prevents text-size adjustment on WebKit. It is partly overridden on `body`, so the impact is minor.
- **Positives:** `color-scheme` and `supported-color-schemes` are declared in both meta tags and CSS. The MSO/VML namespaces, `.ExternalClass` fixes and `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` are present. Web fonts (Playfair Display, Lato) are loaded from `fonts.gstatic.com`. The `mso-font-alt` fallback is declared on only 3 of the Playfair `@font-face` blocks, and the Lato blocks have none. This matters only if the inline `font-family` stacks lack a fallback, which the truncated source doesn't show.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

- The open pixel `mi.1800flowers.com/p/up/ada90b86b2846dd9/o.gif?mi_u=174149195&mi_ecmp=261023_EMAIL_18F_PZ_DV_12AMET_PROMO` resolved with a populated user ID and campaign code.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Physical address:** QA flagged none detected. The truncated HTML can't confirm whether the footer carries one, so check the full source.
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058):** both are absent from captured headers. The QA note says the AgentMail relay may not preserve them. This is unconfirmed, so verify against the raw message at the original recipient. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require one-click unsubscribe for high-volume senders.
- **`Authentication-Results`:** not captured, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The sender uses the subdomain `em.1800flowers.com`, which suggests an aligned ESP setup, but there's no header evidence.
- **Plain-text part:** 6243 of 8211 characters (about 76%) are URLs. This is a spam-score risk. It comes from long tracking URLs and is common in templated promotional email.
- **In-body unsubscribe link:** not checkable in the truncated HTML.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **UTM parameters:** none are verifiable. Links go through the redirect domain `click.dv.1800flowers.com`, and the visible campaign identifier is `mi_ecmp=261023_EMAIL_18F_PZ_DV_12AMET_PROMO` (the `261023` likely encodes a send date). Final-destination URLs and any UTM parameters are hidden behind the 33 unprobed links.
- **Landing alignment:** unverifiable for the same reason.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Migrate the `atdmt.com` and `mathtag.com` pixels to HTTPS. If the platforms are retired, remove them. Populate or drop the empty `v1`–`s3` parameters.
2. Remove the duplicate `bounceexchange.com/tag/em/1622.gif`.
3. Add `alt=""` and `width="1" height="1"` to all tracking pixels.
4. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag, and add `lang="en"` to `<html>`.
5. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment on the original message, since the relay may have stripped them. Confirm the footer has a physical address.
6. Resolve the 33 redirect links with a follow-redirects probe to check destinations and UTM parameters.
7. Shorten the redirect URLs, or add more plain-text copy, to reduce the 76% URL ratio.
