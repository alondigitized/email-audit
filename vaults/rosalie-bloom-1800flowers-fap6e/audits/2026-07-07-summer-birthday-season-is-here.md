# Summer birthday season is here!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- This is 1-800-Flowers resetting the creative clock after a heavy run of July 4th / America's 250th patriotic sends by pivoting back to their strongest evergreen occasion: birthday gifting. The "We Know Birthdays" hero headline is confident brand positioning, and the mid-email lifestyle quote — "My friends and families who have summer birthdays are extra lucky, because I also have a special love for summer gifting" — signals a deliberate shift toward editorial/influencer tone versus the discount-forward cadence of the past two weeks.
- The timing is defensible: the holiday has passed, and the subscriber list needed a tonal break. But this email trades the concrete offer that made the July 1st "Save Up to 25% 🎆" send work (scored 8/10 in this audit) for brand atmosphere alone, and that is a different and harder ask. There's a promotional banner at the top that appears to carry some kind of credit or deal, but it's rendered so small relative to the editorial real estate below that it functionally disappears.
- Verdict: a clean and competent creative pivot, but it will materially underperform the offer-driven July 4th sends on click-through. The brand is coasting on equity instead of giving subscribers a reason to act today.

## What's weak

- No concrete price anchor (% off, $ credit, free shipping) anywhere in the primary hero — the email asks for a click on brand sentiment alone
- The top promotional banner appears to carry an active offer but it's visually subordinated to the editorial content below; subscribers who don't read banners miss it entirely
- The long celebrity/influencer quote sits in the dead center of the email and kills scroll momentum before the product grid
- "Summer birthday season is here!" is announcement framing, not benefit framing — it tells the subscriber what's happening, not what they get

- 1. **Surface the banner offer in the hero** — if there's a credit or discount in the top strip, repeat it as a visible pill or badge on the bouquet image. The July 1st send put "Save Up to 25%" in the subject and hero both; this send buries whatever deal exists in a banner most eyes skip.

- **Subject Alt A:** `Your summer birthday flowers — free delivery inside 🌻`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The birthday bouquet they'll actually remember`
- 3. **Move the influencer quote below the product grid** — social proof earns its keep after you've already shown the product, not as a scroll wall between hero and shop.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free delivery on birthday flowers, plants & gift baskets all summer`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Summer birthdays deserve better than a last-minute gift card.`
- 4. **Add urgency even if soft** — "Order by Sunday for weekend delivery" or "Same-day delivery available" converts the browsing intent the editorial generates into an actual action.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Mobile breakpoint too narrow:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)
```
iPhone 14 Pro Max, 15, 15 Pro Max render at 430px CSS width. `.hide`, `.showmobile`, and all responsive overrides **will not fire** on current flagship iPhones. Breakpoint should be `max-width: 480px` (or `600px` for broader coverage).

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Wildcard `line-height: 100%` causes descender clipping:**
```css
* { line-height: 100%; }
```
Applied globally, this collapses line-height on `<p>`, `<td>`, and font elements. Body text with descenders (g, p, y) will clip in several WebKit clients. This should be scoped to reset targets only (e.g., `.ExternalClass`), not `*`.

**Inconsistent `mso-font-alt` fallbacks:**
- `Playfair Display` italic/400, normal/400, normal/600 → `mso-font-alt:'Times New Roman'` ✓
- `Playfair Display` normal/900 (woff format) → **no `mso-font-alt`**
- All 6 `Lato` `@font-face` declarations → **no `mso-font-alt`**

Outlook ignores `@font-face` and needs `mso-font-alt` to select the right system fallback. Missing on Lato means Outlook will fall back to its default serif, breaking the intended sans-serif body copy.

**Redundant `img` max-width rules:**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
The 768px rule is fully shadowed by the 640px rule. Not a rendering bug, but dead CSS.

**`.mobileshow` hidden via `visibility:hidden`:**
```css
.mobileshow { display:none !important; visibility:hidden !important; width:0px; overflow:hidden !important; }
```
`visibility:hidden` collapses render but preserves layout space — the element still occupies vertical height in clients that render it. Use `display:none !important` alone.
