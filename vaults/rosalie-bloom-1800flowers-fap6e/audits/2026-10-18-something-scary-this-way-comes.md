# Something scary this way comes
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-18**
## Executive summary

- This is the twenty-third straight "Jim at 1-800-FLOWERS" essay newsletter. The subject, "Something scary this way comes," dresses it up as a Halloween send. Inside there's still no product, price or flower. It's a Halloween-themed ghost-and-monster hero, three more essay blocks, a podcast plug, and a Founder letter. The 10/11 send ("The Difference a Job Can Make") and the 10/04 send ("The fall rituals that keep us grounded") used the same format.
- The creative is polished and seasonally on-theme, and the render is clean. But the Halloween hook is a metaphor for "everyday fears," not a costume, candy or gifting offer. The one real conversion element is the peach "Subscribe Today" box. It offers a free book to a newsletter the reader is already getting, and it sits right under the first story.
- Judgement: well-produced and off-strategy. A 1-800-FLOWERS subscriber who opens this learns nothing about what to buy, and the email is more than 4,000px of scrolling to get there.

## What's working

- The Halloween framing and the ghost-in-the-window hero image are timely and eye-catching for mid-October.
- The render is clean. There are no broken images, overlaps or layout breaks.
- The sections are clearly labeled (FEAR FACTOR, 50 YEARS IN, MAKING A DIFFERENCE, PODCASTS, CLOSING THOUGHTS), so the email scans easily.
- The pull quote and signature at the top give it a personal, founder-voice feel that is consistent with prior sends.
- The "Cast your vote" CTA has a concrete action and a dated hook, with public voting opening Oct. 29.

## What's weak

- There is no product, price, discount or shopping path anywhere. For a commerce brand, this email can't produce a sale.
- The "Subscribe Today" box offers a free book to people who are already subscribers. The "limited time" language has no stated deadline.
- The CTAs are small, inline text links with an emoji pointer ("Discover what's lurking within"). The only button is the black Subscribe one, which is the weakest conversion action in the email.
- The email is far too long, with five or six competing stories. The podcast banner is large and visually dominates the middle. It isn't related to Halloween or gifting.
- The top line, "Please Click Here to Manage Your Preferences or Unsubscribe," reads as the preheader. It wastes the best inbox real estate and invites unsubscribes.
- The "50 years in" and "Anthem Award" blocks are corporate self-congratulation, not anything the reader needs.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one real Halloween offer, such as treat-box gifting, fall bouquets or candy bundles, with a price, a deadline and a button above the fold. Move the essay content below it, or cut it to a single story.
- 2. Trim the email to one hero and two or three secondary blocks. Drop or collapse the podcast and the economy blurb into a single "More from Jim" link row.
- 3. Replace the inline emoji links with real buttons. Use specific verbs and nouns, such as "Shop Halloween treats" or "Cast your Anthem vote."
- 4. Set a custom preheader so the preferences/unsubscribe line doesn't take the preview slot.
- 5. Give the Subscribe box a clear deadline, or hide it from existing subscribers.
