# Trust What Our Florists Would Send 🌹
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- This is 1-800-Flowers leaning into the editorial "florist expertise" frame they piloted successfully in the June 22 "What We're Sending for Summer Birthdays" send (8/10 here). "Our Florists' Summer Picks" is a clean concept — three sections, florist avatar, lifestyle imagery — and the layout is tight. But where the June 22 send stacked editorial framing on top of a concrete $13.80 credit, this one floats the concept without a visible offer anchor. The promo banner at the top exists, but it's so narrow it reads as wallpaper.
- The three product pods (Sunshine in a Vase, It's Always Rose Season, Don't Sleep on the Peonies) are well-differentiated and seasonally appropriate. The copy headlines are actually good — punchy, specific, personality-driven. But every CTA says "SHOP NOW," which is generic for a send that's trying to sell the florist's opinion. The editorial positioning earns attention; the execution doesn't capitalize on it.
- Net verdict: competent seasonal content email, but the authority angle in the subject gets undercut by a body that functions like a standard category browse. Scores mid-range.

## What's working

- **Three distinct product editorial sections** (Sunshine in a Vase / Rose Season / Peonies) give this email clear internal structure and something to actually scan.
- **Section copy headlines are punchy and specific** — "Don't Sleep on the Peonies" is the best copy in the email.
- **Florist avatar/portrait in the hero** makes the "trust our experts" positioning concrete; it's not just a headline claim.
- **Layout renders cleanly** — no broken images, no overlapping text, consistent color palette throughout.
- **Summer seasonality is timely** and consistent with the June cadence.

## What's weak

- **No concrete offer in the body** — the expertise angle needs an offer tethered to it, or it's just a catalog browse with a fancy frame.
- **Every CTA is "SHOP NOW"** — a missed opportunity when each section has a distinct florist recommendation; "Shop Sunflowers" or "See the Peonies" would convert better.
- **Promo banner at the top is too small to read** — it disappears visually, so whatever deal is there doesn't register.
- **No urgency signal anywhere** — neither time-bound ("This weekend only") nor scarcity ("Limited stems") — making this feel like a browse email, not a buy-now one.
- **Cadence fatigue is real**: this is roughly the 9th email in five weeks. The June 24 credit send was a logical follow-up to June 22; this June 25 send feels like one more push without a new reason.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add the florist's single best pick as a named, linked hero product** — "Valeria's pick this week: Sorbet Roses, 30% off" converts the editorial concept into a click-driver instead of a browse invitation. The June 17 Sorbet Roses send proved named-product + stacked offer works.
- 2. **Rewrite every "SHOP NOW" to match the section** — "Shop Sunflower Arrangements," "Shop Peonies," etc. Three seconds of copy work, measurable lift.
- 3. **Surface the promo banner offer in the body** — if it's 20% off or similar, put it inside the first editorial section with a code or deadline. The banner is invisible on mobile scroll.
- 4. **Add a light urgency hook** — even "Available through Sunday" would give a reason to click today vs. later (which usually means never).
- 5. **Reduce send frequency or flag the gap** — after June 22 + June 23 + June 24, a June 25 send needs to clear a higher bar to avoid training unsubscribes.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta (confirmed)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Disables pinch-to-zoom on iOS. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) violation and actively harms low-vision users. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Duplicate media query rule**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width:640px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width:640px} }
```
The 768px rule is dead — any viewport ≥768px also matches the 640px rule and gets the same value. The 768px block does nothing and should be removed.

**Playfair Display 900-weight uses `.woff` not `.woff2`**
```css
src:url(https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/playfairdisplay/v22/...btM.woff) format('woff');
```
Every other `@font-face` in this email (including lighter Playfair Display weights and all Lato weights) uses `.woff2`. The 900-weight bold file is the only outlier — it will load a larger, older format file unnecessarily. Update to the `.woff2` URL for consistency and file-size reduction.

**`mso-font-alt` missing from Playfair Display 900-weight `@font-face`**
The 900-weight `@font-face` block omits the `mso-font-alt:'Times New Roman'` descriptor present in the 400 and 600-weight declarations. In Outlook, when this weight is referenced, no fallback is declared.

**`line-height:100%` on `*` selector**
```css
* {line-height:100%}
```
This collapses line-height to the font-size value for every element. For body copy, WCAG 1.4.12 recommends line-height of at least 1.5×. Cannot confirm whether inline overrides restore this without body content, but the reset is aggressive.

**Non-standard mobile breakpoint**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { ... }
```
418px is non-standard (industry standard is 600px, with 414px being a common floor for iPhone SE/8 targets). The breakpoint fires on a 1px-wide window that no device produces. The intent appears to target ≤414px devices but the threshold is off by 4px; this is cosmetically harmless but indicates a copy-paste error.
