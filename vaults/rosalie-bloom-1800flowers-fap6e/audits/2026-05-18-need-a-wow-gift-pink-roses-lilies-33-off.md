# Need a wow gift? Pink roses & lilies 33% off
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a post-Mother's Day clearance push from 1-800-Flowers, leaning hard on a "Deal of the Week" badge to move pink roses and lilies at 33% off. The hero is visually loud — hot pink background, bold badge, and a product shot — which creates quick visual impact. The email then fans out into a category grid and a "Best Sellers" carousel, which dilutes focus considerably. For a persona that doesn't follow floral gifting closely, this feels like a bulk-blast, not a targeted send; but the mechanics (discount, CTA, product imagery) are all present and functional.
- The timing is interesting: Mother's Day just passed (May 11 welcome email in the brand history, Mother's Day likely May 10-12). Sending a "wow gift" email the week after Mother's Day means the audience is either late buyers or being primed for the next gifting occasion (graduation, Memorial Day). The email doesn't name either of those occasions, which is a missed anchoring opportunity.
- Overall this is a competent promotional send — the offer is real, the creative is clean, and the layout renders without bugs. But it's generic enough that most recipients will scroll past without strong intent to click.

## What's working

- **Hero discount is immediate and specific** — "33% off" appears twice above the fold (badge + sub-headline), so the value prop registers fast.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum; the email is visually intact across the full scroll.
- **Category grid gives optionality** — Birthday, Romance, Sympathy, etc. tiles let recipients self-select if the hero SKU isn't right for them.
- **App download CTA adds retention value** — the dual App Store/Google Play block at the bottom is a genuine loyalty play, not just filler.
- **"20% Off Best Sellers" secondary offer** is clearly called out with a code (BLOOMS), giving a second reason to click for anyone who passes on the hero.

## What's weak

- **No named gifting occasion** — "wow gift" is generic; the email doesn't say who this is for or why now, and the post-Mother's Day window cries out for a graduation or spring anchor.
- **Three competing offers in one email** — 33% off hero, 20% off best sellers with a code, and category browse — no single CTA wins, so none of them do.
- **Hero CTA copy isn't visible/distinct** — from the render, the primary CTA button on the hero is small and hard to distinguish from the surrounding pink background.
- **"Join the Celebration" middle section** is vague — the copy promises something social/community but resolves to a generic brand line, adding scroll without adding value.
- **No urgency deadline** — "Deal of the Week" implies limited time, but there's no end date or countdown anywhere in the render.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one offer and kill the other.** The 33% vs. 20% split confuses intent. Lead with 33%, cut the BLOOMS code block or move it to a PS footnote. One clear action wins.
- 2. **Name the occasion.** "Congratulations grad" or "Say it with spring" would give this email a reason to exist beyond discount clearance. The timing is perfect for graduation season — use it.
- 3. **Give the hero CTA a deadline.** "Offer ends Sunday" or "3 days left" would convert the "Deal of the Week" badge into a real urgency signal.
- 4. **Rewrite the hero sub-headline** to lead with the product benefit, not just the discount — e.g., "Fresh-cut pink roses delivered tomorrow — 33% off this week only."
- 5. **Tighten the category grid** — showing 9+ categories at once forces scroll without a clear destination; narrow to 4 most-clicked for this segment.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Grad season starts now — pink roses 33% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `33% off this week: fresh roses & lilies delivered`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Offer ends Sunday. Shop before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus 20% off best sellers with code BLOOMS`
