# Free shipping on unforgettable birthday gifts
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- This is 1-800-Flowers running their "Birthday Bliss" seasonal creative — a multi-product, editorial-style send anchored by a hero bouquet image and a free-shipping lead. The free-shipping offer is the lede in the subject line, but the email buries the actual terms (minimum order threshold, if any) and never repeats the offer value prominently in-body. What you see is a catalog dump: flowers, cookies, gourmet food baskets, a personalized gift, then a full shop-by-category grid. It's wide-net, not targeted.
- The render is clean — no broken images, no overlapping text — and the visual hierarchy is reasonable: hero bouquet at top, product sub-sections descending. But the editorial logic is muddled. "Birthday Bliss" as a headline sounds like a brand campaign name, not a benefit. The free-shipping hook from the subject line evaporates inside the email and is replaced with generic "Celebrate the Season" copy. That disconnect is the single biggest miss here.
- Coming off the back of a string of $15-credit sends (July 14, July 16) and a genuine win on July 15's double-blooms execution, this free-shipping pivot is directionally smart — but the execution doesn't earn the subject line's promise. The in-body offer never says "free shipping on this order" with a deadline or a threshold. You get the subject tease, then you're dropped into a catalog.

## What's working

- **Hero image is strong** — the pink/rose bouquet opener is well-lit, aspirational, and seasonally appropriate for summer birthdays.
- **Free-shipping subject line is a genuine improvement** over the bait-and-switch credit hook used July 13–16; this one is at least an offer a subscriber can act on.
- **Product breadth is visible** — flowers, cookies, gourmet baskets, and a personalized gift are all shown; something for every budget.
- **Shop-by-category grid** is clean and scannable; it anchors the bottom of the email well.
- **No render bugs** — layout holds, images load, no lorem ipsum or broken elements visible.

## What's weak

- **Free-shipping offer disappears inside the email** — the subject promises it, the body doesn't echo it with a callout, minimum, or deadline.
- **"Birthday Bliss" headline is brand-speak, not benefit copy** — it tells the reader nothing actionable and wastes the hero position.
- **Cookie/gourmet section undercuts the flower brand** — the pivot from bouquets to cookies mid-scroll dilutes the brand identity without a bridging story.
- **No urgency mechanism** — no deadline, no "offer ends Sunday," nothing to motivate a click today vs. tomorrow.
- **Personalized gift tease is vague** — the personalization section shows a product but doesn't name it clearly or explain what's customizable.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Echo the free-shipping offer in-body, prominently.** Add a banner or hero subhead that says "Free shipping on orders $X+" with an end date. The subject earns the open; the body has to close it.
- 2. **Replace "Birthday Bliss" with a direct benefit headline** — something like "The birthday gift that arrives fresh" or "Free shipping · Delivered tomorrow."
- 3. **Pick a hero product and commit to it.** The July 15 double-blooms send worked because it had a single, concrete idea. This email tries to sell flowers, cookies, gourmet baskets, and personalized gifts simultaneously. Cut to two sections max.
- 4. **Add a credible urgency signal** — "Order by 5 PM for next-day delivery" is something 1-800-Flowers can actually fulfill and is far more compelling than no deadline.
- 5. **Remove or anchor the cookies/gourmet section** with a clear reason (e.g., "When they can't decide between flowers and cake — send both"), otherwise it reads like a random upsell.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free shipping today — birthday flowers delivered fresh`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Send birthday flowers free — order by 5 PM`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `No minimum. No catch. Just same-day and next-day delivery options.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Pick a bouquet, skip the shipping fee — offer ends tonight.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Real issues confirmed in source:**

- **Duplicate `img` max-width rule** — Both `@media only screen and (min-width: 768px)` and `@media only screen and (min-width: 640px)` declare identical `img { max-width: 640px }`. The 768px rule is dead code; it is always overridden by the 640px rule in any client that parses both. Remove the 768px block.

- **Non-standard mobile breakpoint** — Responsive block triggers at `max-width: 418px`, targeting only iPhone SE-class devices. Standard practice is 480px or 600px. Anything between 419px and ~599px wide (many Android phones, landscape phone, tablet small) receives neither the desktop nor the mobile layout. This is a real gap.

- **`.mobileshow` hides with both `display:none` and `visibility:hidden`** — `visibility:hidden` retains layout space in some clients (notably older Outlook.com). If the element is truly hidden, `display:none` alone is sufficient. The extra `visibility:hidden` risks blank whitespace in partial-support clients.

- **Font version inconsistency** — Playfair Display variants span `v21` and `v22` across `font-weight` values; the `weight:900 / style:normal` variant loads `.woff` (not `.woff2`), inconsistent with all other declared variants which use `.woff2`. The `.woff` reference may unnecessarily inflate parse time in clients that support `.woff2`.

- **Two separate `<style>` blocks in `<head>`** — Technically valid but Gmail and some Outlook Web versions strip the second block's rules. Rules split across both blocks (`color-scheme`, `.sharpen`, `th` styles) may be lost. Consolidate into one `<style>` block.

- **`line-height: 100%` on `*` selector** — Applied globally including `<td>` and `<img>`. This is aggressive; it can collapse text line-height to the element's font-size in clients that interpret it literally, particularly in small-text footers.
