# Save $13.80 on all kinds of birthday gifts
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-10**
## Executive summary

- This is 1-800-Flowers running a genuinely new format for the first time in this batch: a "3 Ways to Gift" birthday guide, structured as a numbered gift-guide editorial rather than the escalating-percentage flash sale from the Aug 3-6 run or the Mallory Green florist-persona format from late July/Aug 1-8. The credit-code mechanic ("$13.80 credit, code SUNNY1380") is a fresh offer construction too — a specific dollar credit against a stated $49.99 minimum rather than a flat sitewide percentage, which reads as more calculated and less generic than "30% off everything."
- The execution is strong: three clear numbered sections (Birthday Chic / Keep it Classic / Playful Gifts), each with its own CTA button and two product examples, capped by a 9-tile shop-by-category grid and a loyalty membership plug (Celebrations Passport). It's dense but organized, and the "Animated Birthday Cupcake ... it really sings to you!" and "Party Pooch" novelty items add some personality that pure flower sends lack.
- The weak point is the subject/offer framing — "$13.80" is an oddly specific, low-feeling number for a lead hook, and the $49.99 minimum spend to unlock it isn't visible anywhere in the hero, only buried in the footer fine print. That's the kind of soft bait-and-switch signal the rubric penalizes. Solid creative, mediocre subject strategy.

## What's working

- Clear numbered gift-guide structure (1/2/3) gives the eye an obvious path through a long email.
- Each section has its own specific CTA ("SHOP LUXURY," "SHOP TRADITIONAL," "SHOP WHIMSICAL") tied to a distinct product mood, not one generic button repeated.
- Novelty/personality touches (singing cupcake, Party Pooch plush) differentiate this from a standard rose-bouquet send.
- Shop-by-category grid at the bottom gives non-birthday shoppers an exit ramp (Sympathy, Plants, Same-Day, etc.).
- Hero is legible without obvious render bugs — code and credit amount both readable at a glance.

## What's weak

- The $49.99 minimum spend needed to redeem the $13.80 credit is not shown in the hero or anywhere near the CTA — only in dense footer legal text, which is a fine-print/bait-and-switch signal per the rubric.
- "$13.80" is a strange, unmemorable number to lead a subject line with — it reads like a randomized promo code artifact rather than a deliberate offer.
- Very long, scroll-heavy layout (three full sections + category grid + app download + brand family) for a single occasion (birthday) — no visible urgency or deadline anywhere to compel faster action.
- No pricing shown on any individual product tile, so the shopper can't tell if $13.80 off is meaningful until they click through.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the $49.99 minimum directly under the credit callout in the hero ("on orders $49.99+") — hiding it in the footer is the single biggest trust risk in this send.
- 2. Replace the odd $13.80 figure with a rounder, more scannable offer in the subject if possible, or lean into the specificity as a personalization hook (e.g., "your $13.80 credit") rather than a plain statement.
- 3. Add a redemption deadline near the code — nothing here signals urgency, unlike the brand's recent 24-hour flash-sale sends.
- 4. Show at least a starting price on 2-3 hero product tiles so shoppers can gauge value before clicking.

- **Subject Alt B:** `3 birthday gift ideas + $13.80 off $49.99+`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email uses a heavy third-party tracking stack (ADTECH, MathTag, Bounce Exchange, DoubleVerify) with several broken/insecure pixels, and fails baseline CAN-SPAM/authentication checks per the automated QA scan.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 38 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped from HTTP probing (redirect domains like `click.dv.1800flowers.com`), so their final destinations are unverified — recommend manual click-through QA before send.
- Two MathTag pixels (`pixel.mathtag.com/event/img?mt_id=155437...` and `...mt_id=155438...`) load over plain `http://`, which many corporate mail gateways and Outlook will block, silently losing that tracking data.
- One ADTECH/Digital River pixel (`http://view.atdmt.com/FLO/view/361227669/direct/01/`) also loads over `http://` — same blocking risk.
- Bounce Exchange tag (`bounceexchange.com/tag/em/1622.gif`) appears twice in source — duplicate pixel firing inflates/duplicates that vendor's engagement counts.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 7 of 8 flagged images have no `alt` text (all tracking pixels plus the DV click image `b6ffb323-6ff9-43bd-8201-79dcb6063344` and the `mi.1800flowers.com/.../o.gif` pixel). Tracking pixels are cosmetically irrelevant, but missing `alt` on any content images (not visible in the truncated source) would hurt screen-reader users and broken-image fallback.
- Markup targets legacy Outlook/mobile clients correctly (MSO conditional namespaces, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, mobile breakpoint at 418px) — no structural rendering issues found in the visible source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found in the visible/truncated source — no unresolved merge tags (e.g. `{{first_name}}`, `%%FIELD%%`) or empty personalization blocks detected.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header missing** — no `List-Unsubscribe` header detected; may be a relay capture artifact but should be confirmed against the raw source MTA headers.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) missing** — one-click unsubscribe is not confirmed supported, a Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender requirement as of 2024.
- **No physical mailing address detected** in the truncated HTML — CAN-SPAM requires one; confirm it exists in the untruncated footer (likely truncated out of this excerpt) rather than assume it's absent.
- **Authentication-Results header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status could not be verified from this capture; flag for direct inspection of raw headers rather than the AgentMail relay copy.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- The tracking/redirect link `mi.1800flowers.com/.../o.gif?mi_u=174149195&mi_ecmp=260810_EMAIL_18F_PZ_DV_12AMET_PROMO` shows a campaign code (`260810_EMAIL_18F_PZ_DV_12AMET_PROMO`) consistent with an email-channel campaign tag, but since all 38 CTAs route through `click.dv.1800flowers.com` redirects, actual landing-page UTM parameters and offer-page alignment (the $13.80 discount from the subject line) could not be verified — recommend manually resolving 2-3 primary CTAs to confirm the discount is reflected on the landing page.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Migrate the two MathTag pixels and the ADTECH pixel from `http://` to `https://` to prevent silent blocking by mail clients.
2. Remove the duplicate Bounce Exchange pixel — one instance is redundant.
3. Add `alt=""` (empty, decorative) to all tracking/pixel `<img>` tags to satisfy accessibility linting without adding noise for screen readers.
4. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` headers are present in the actual send (not just this relay capture) — required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender rules.
5. Confirm a physical mailing address is present in the full (non-truncated) footer; if genuinely absent, this is a CAN-SPAM violation requiring immediate fix.
6. Manually resolve a sample of the `click.dv.1800flowers.com` redirect links to confirm they land on the discounted-offer page referenced in the subject line.
7. Verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment directly against `em.1800flowers.com` sending headers, since Authentication-Results wasn't captured by this relay.
