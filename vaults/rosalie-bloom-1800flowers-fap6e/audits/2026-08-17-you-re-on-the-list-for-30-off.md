# You're on the List for 30% Off
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-17**
## Executive summary

- This is the same 30%-sitewide flash-sale machine 1-800-Flowers has run all week (Aug 5, 6, 11, 12, 13), just re-skinned as "Employee Discount Days" — a faux-exclusivity gimmick since this is a mass consumer send, not an employee portal. The hero is clean and the discount is impossible to miss, but there's no product tied to the offer at the top — just a lifestyle bowl of chocolate-dipped strawberries and mixed blooms floating above generic "SITEWIDE" text, so the eye has nothing specific to click toward.
- Below the fold it's the standard grid: four SKUs, then a nine-tile category directory, then a Celebrations Passport pitch. Functional, competent, entirely undifferentiated from last week's sends. The "Employee Discount Days" framing is the only new beat, and it reads as a stretched euphemism for "another 30% off code" rather than a real occasion — no birthday, no lily-season timing, no rose-week anchor, just a discount code with a new label.

## What's working

- 30% off is stated clearly twice (banner headline, "Email Exclusive Savings" section) with a visible promo code (INSIDER30).
- Single unambiguous "SHOP NOW" CTA button placed directly under the hero offer.
- Four product tiles with names and images give a concrete browsing path immediately after the hero.
- The nine-icon "Shop by Category" grid is a strong utility net for browsers not motivated by the flash sale itself.
- No render bugs — clean layout, all images loaded, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- "Employee Discount Days" is a contrived, unbelievable premise for a public marketing blast — undermines trust rather than building urgency.
- Hero image (strawberries + loose blooms) isn't a shoppable product and doesn't match any of the four SKUs shown below it — disconnect between hero and offer.
- No countdown or explicit end date near the hero; the 72-hour window is buried in small subtext and the real deadline (11:59pm 8/19) only appears in the tiny footer disclaimer.
- Zero social proof, reviews, or star ratings anywhere in the email.
- Fifth near-identical percentage-off send in under two weeks — batch fatigue is real at this point (Aug 5, 6, 11, 12, 13 all ran comparable sitewide/rose-specific % promos).

## Recommendations

- 1. Ditch "Employee Discount Days" — it's not credible from a brand blasting millions of subscribers. Replace with a straightforward loyalty/insider frame that doesn't ask the reader to believe something false.
- 2. Put an actual shoppable bouquet in the hero image, not a lifestyle strawberry bowl — tie the visual directly to what "SHOP NOW" lands on.
- 3. Move the 72-hour deadline out of small type and into a real countdown element near the CTA — urgency needs to be visible, not footnoted.
- 4. Vary the format — this brand has now run essentially the same 30%-off template five times in two weeks; a numbered guide or single-SKU spotlight (like Aug 11's "Sorbet Roses" send, which scored 9.0) performs better and avoids promo blindness.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- Multiple `<img>` elements missing `alt` text, including the atdmt view-pixel, both mathtag pixels, the two bounceexchange pixels, and the `o.gif` mi.1800flowers pixel. For 1x1 tracking pixels this has no visual impact but fails automated accessibility scoring; decorative/tracking images should use `alt=""` explicitly rather than omitting the attribute.
- Head includes standard dark-mode and Outlook/MSO compatibility meta tags (`color-scheme`, `supported-color-schemes`, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`) and conditional MSO namespaces — no rendering red flags in the visible source.
- Custom web fonts (Playfair Display, Lato) are loaded via `@font-face` from `fonts.gstatic.com`; most email clients (Outlook, Gmail) strip external `@font-face`, so `mso-font-alt:'Times New Roman'` fallback is only defined for some weights (400) — the 600/700/900 Lato/Playfair weights have no MSO fallback declared, risking inconsistent fallback rendering in Outlook.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No merge tokens or personalization variables (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `%%FIELD%%`) are visible in the truncated source. No issues found based on available evidence — recommend confirming the untruncated body doesn't contain unresolved tokens.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- `List-Unsubscribe` header not detected — may be a relay artifact (AgentMail) rather than an actual absence; needs verification against the raw source MTA headers.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe) not detected, consistent with the missing `List-Unsubscribe` header — one-click unsubscribe cannot be confirmed as supported.
- No physical mailing address detected in the scanned content — CAN-SPAM requires a physical postal address in every commercial email; this should be confirmed against the full (untruncated) footer, as truncation may be the cause.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Serve `atdmt.com` and `pixel.mathtag.com` tracking pixels over HTTPS to prevent client-side blocking of those beacons.
2. Add `alt=""` to all tracking-pixel `<img>` tags to pass accessibility checks without visual change.
3. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are present in the raw MTA-level source (they may simply not be surfacing through the AgentMail relay) and add RFC 8058 one-click support if genuinely absent.
4. Confirm a physical mailing address is present in the untruncated footer; if absent, add one for CAN-SPAM compliance.
5. Verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC via `Authentication-Results` on the raw received headers, independent of the relay.
6. Spot-check a sample of the 26 skipped tracking/redirect links manually to confirm they resolve (HTTP 200) and land on pages consistent with the "30% Off" offer, and confirm UTM parameters are present for site-side attribution.
