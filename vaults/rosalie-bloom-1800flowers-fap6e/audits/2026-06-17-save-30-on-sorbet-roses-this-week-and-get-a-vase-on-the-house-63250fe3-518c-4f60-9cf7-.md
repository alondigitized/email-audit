# Save 30% on Sorbet Roses this week and get a vase on the house
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- A genuine creative reset after seven near-identical Double Blooms sends. The "Deal of the Week" framing with a named product — Sorbet Roses — and a stacked offer (30% off + free vase) gives this email something to say that the previous batch couldn't: *here is the specific thing, here is the specific price, here is the bonus*. That specificity earns attention even from a fatigued list.
- The email then undercuts itself by immediately introducing a second, weaker offer below the fold — 20% off Summer Favorites with a promo code. Two discount structures in one email splits focus and invites the reader to wonder which deal is actually better. The "Deal of the Week" premise promises one hero, not a two-for-one discount buffet.
- The "A Peek Behind The Petals" editorial section is interesting positioning but arrives too early, slowing the path to the primary CTA before the purchase intent is captured. Bottom line: the subject line is the best thing here — the body doesn't land as cleanly as the promise made in the inbox.

## What's working

- **Subject line is the strongest this brand has sent in the audited window** — specific product, specific discount, specific bonus, and a credible deadline ("this week") all in one sentence.
- **"Deal of the Week" hero headline** creates a clear editorial frame that justifies the send and differentiates from the Double Blooms template fatigue.
- **Stacked offer is compelling in concept** — 30% off *plus* a free vase is a better-value story than pure percentage off and gives the copy two hooks.
- **Sorbet Roses product shot in the upper-left** ties the hero directly to the subject line, preventing any bait-and-switch anxiety.
- **Category grid below** ("Shop by Category") gives browsers a path even if the hero product isn't right for them.

## What's weak

- **Two competing discount offers in one email** — 30% on Sorbet Roses (hero) and 20% off Summer Favorites (secondary) dilute each other; a subscriber can't tell which is the "real" deal.
- **"A Peek Behind The Petals" editorial section** adds copy length before the primary CTA and reads more like a content newsletter insert than a transactional send — awkward positioning.
- **CTA buttons are small and undifferentiated at render scale** — at mobile viewport width they lack the visual weight to drive thumb-taps.
- **No social proof visible** — for a named variety ("Sorbet Roses") that presumably commands attention on freshness/novelty, a single review snippet or "bestseller" badge would have closed the loop.
- **"Join the Celebration" app-download footer** is generic filler that wastes real estate at the close — any momentum built by the offer dissipates before a final buy nudge.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the 20% Summer Favorites offer or move it to a separate send.** One email, one offer. The Sorbet Roses + free vase story is strong enough to stand alone; the second discount cannibalizes it.
- 2. **Lead the editorial section with a one-line proof point**, not a teaser — something like "Just harvested and available through Sunday — here's what makes Sorbet different" converts curiosity into urgency rather than stalling it.
- 3. **Enlarge and clarify the primary CTA button** — it should read something like "Get the Deal" or "Claim 30% + Free Vase" with high-contrast color that pops against the image-heavy layout.
- 4. **Add a single review line or volume signal** near the product ("★★★★★ 4.8 · 2,400 orders this month") to justify the named-variety positioning.
- 5. **Replace the app-download footer close with a deadline line** — "Offer ends Sunday. After that, the vase goes, the 30% goes, and Sorbet Roses are back to full price."

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta (accessibility)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom. Fails WCAG 1.4.4. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Non-standard mobile breakpoint**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)
```
Standard practice is 480px or 600px. 418px is narrower than most small Android devices; content between 419–479px will receive desktop layout. Confirm intentional.

**Redundant `min-width` media queries**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width:640px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width:640px} }
```
The 768px rule is entirely superseded by the 640px rule — identical declaration, wider breakpoint. Dead code; remove the 768px block.

**`.mobileshow` uses `visibility:hidden` alongside `display:none`**
```css
.mobileshow {display:none !important; visibility:hidden !important; width:0px; overflow:hidden !important;}
```
`visibility:hidden` still occupies layout space; the `!important display:none` wins, but the `visibility` declaration is misleading and will cause the element to re-appear as a space-occupying invisible block if `display:none` is ever overridden without also resetting `visibility`. Use only `display:none`.

**Missing `mso-font-alt` on Lato `@font-face` declarations**
Playfair Display correctly declares `mso-font-alt:'Times New Roman'` as an Outlook fallback; all six Lato `@font-face` declarations omit it. In Outlook, Lato will fall back to the system default serif, likely mismatching body copy intent. Add `mso-font-alt:'Arial'` (or appropriate sans-serif) to each Lato declaration.

**Playfair Display weight-900 uses `.woff` not `.woff2`**
```css
src:url(...nuFvD-...ukDT.woff) format('woff');
```
All other weights load `.woff2`. The weight-900 variant will be ~30% larger on download and may not load in older Webkits that support `.woff2` only. Replace with the `.woff2` equivalent if available.

**`line-height:100%` universal reset**
```css
* {line-height:100%}
```
Applying `line-height:100%` globally can collapse descenders and cause text clipping in some webmail renderers (Yahoo, older Outlook.com). Scope this reset only to the elements that require it (typically `td`, `.ExternalClass`).

**External font loading (`fonts.gstatic.com`)**
Font files are loaded directly from Google's CDN. Blocked in: Outlook (all), Gmail App (Android), some corporate proxies. All font stacks have `mso-font-alt` or CSS fallbacks (except Lato, noted above), so degradation is acceptable — but confirm the `font-family` stack order is correct in the full template.

**`role="presentation"` on layout tables** — Cannot confirm from truncated source. Verify all `<table>` elements used for layout (not data) carry `role="presentation"` for screen-reader accessibility.

**`alt` text on images** — Cannot confirm from truncated source. All `<img>` elements must have `alt` attributes; product images should have descriptive alt text, not empty strings.
