# Save Up to 25% 🎆 It’s America’s Birthday
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- This is 1-800-Flowers converting their June 29 "America is turning 250!" awareness send (8/10 in this audit) into a straight discount email, timed to land four days before the actual holiday. The patriotic creative — red/white/blue palette, birthday cake imagery, "Celebrating America's 250th / Happy Birthday!" hero — carries visual continuity from the prior send, making the two-part editorial → promo cadence feel planned rather than opportunistic. The tiered 15%/20%/25% off structure is the right mechanic for a variable-AOV gifting brand.
- What undercuts it is that the tier thresholds are invisible in the hero. "Save Up to 25%" reads as aspirational — most subscribers will assume they'll qualify for the lowest tier. Without seeing what you need to spend to unlock 20% or 25%, the offer creates curiosity where it should create urgency. Pair that with "SHOP NOW" as the CTA and no single featured product, and the email asks the buyer to do more cognitive work than it earns.
- The bigger flag is cadence: this is the third 1-800-Flowers send in three days (June 29, June 30, July 1). Holiday justification covers it, but this cluster will drive some unsubscribes from moderately engaged subscribers who feel batched.

## What's working

- **Patriotic creative is deliberate.** The June 29 editorial send built the conceptual frame; this send inherits it cleanly without repeating the joke.
- **Seasonal timing is tight.** July 1 gives four days of lead time — enough for delivery confidence while maintaining the holiday close.
- **Tiered discount is the right mechanic.** Rewarding higher spend is smart for a gifting brand where order size varies dramatically by occasion.
- **Celebrations Passport loyalty block** is integrated mid-email without feeling bolted on — good placement for subscribers who haven't enrolled.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breaks across the visible scroll depth.

## What's weak

- **Spend thresholds are buried or invisible.** The 15%/20%/25% stack looks compelling but gives no signal of what you need to spend to reach the top tier. "Up to 25%" functions as a ceiling most shoppers don't expect to hit.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is pure generic.** It's unambiguous but contributes nothing — "Save Up to 25% Now" or "Shop the Sale" would carry the offer through to the click.
- **No single hero product.** The best-sellers grid offers five choices; that's choice paralysis for impulse gifting decisions. One featured arrangement with a delivery-by date closes faster.
- **No delivery deadline visible.** Flowers are perishable — "Order by July 3 for July 4th delivery" is the most motivating urgency signal in this category, and it's absent from the hero.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface one spend threshold in the hero.** Replace the abstract stack with something like "Spend $65+, save 20% — or $90+ for 25% off." One concrete number changes the frame from "maybe I'll get a deal" to "I know exactly what to put in my cart."
- 2. **Add a delivery cutoff date to the hero.** "Order by July 3 for guaranteed July 4th delivery" is more compelling than any discount copy for time-sensitive gifters.
- 3. **Feature one hero arrangement with a direct product link.** Kill the choice paralysis with a single editorial recommendation — "Our #1 July 4th Pick" with price + savings.
- 4. **Sharpen the CTA.** "Save Up to 25% Now" over "SHOP NOW" — keeps the offer prominent through the click moment.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Save 25% on July 4th Flowers — Order by July 3`
- **Subject Alt B:** `🎆 Up to 25% Off — America's Birthday Sale Ends July 4`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $90+ and save 25% — order by July 3 for guaranteed delivery`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Our most popular summer arrangements are on sale through July 4th`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **`<th>` used for layout** (`th { font-weight:400; font-size:20px !important; ... }`): Table header elements carry implicit ARIA role `columnheader`, which causes screen readers to announce layout cells as data headers. Should be `<td>` with explicit styling.

- **Missing `mso-font-alt` on some `@font-face` declarations**: Playfair Display weight-900 italic and all six Lato variants lack `mso-font-alt`. Outlook will substitute its own default serif/sans instead of the intended fallback (`Times New Roman` / system sans). Example offender:
  ```css
  @font-face { font-family:'Lato'; font-weight:900; src:url(...S6u9w4BMUTPHh50Xewo.woff2)... }
  /* no mso-font-alt */
  ```

- **Playfair Display weight-900 uses `.woff`, not `.woff2`**: All other variants load `.woff2`. The weight-900 variant loads a `.woff` file — larger, slower, and unnecessary since `.woff2` is universally supported in any client that supports web fonts at all.
  ```css
  src:url(.../nuFvD-...ukDT.woff) format('woff')
  ```

- **Mobile breakpoint at 418px** (`.contenttable`, `.mobile`, `.showmobile`): Standard mobile breakpoints are 480px or 600px. Devices between 419–479px wide will receive desktop layout at mobile viewport sizes. Most mid-range Android phones (e.g., Pixel 6a: 412px CSS width) fall in this gap.

- **Dark mode declared, no overrides implemented**: Both `<style>` blocks declare `color-scheme: light dark`, but no `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block is present in the visible source. This opts the email into dark mode rendering without any color corrections — background colors, image halos, and text contrast are uncontrolled in dark-mode clients (Apple Mail, Gmail app on Android).

- **Duplicate `color-scheme` declaration**: `:root { color-scheme:light dark; supported-color-schemes:light dark; }` appears verbatim in both `<style>` blocks. Harmless but indicative of copy-paste template debt.

- **`supported-color-schemes` in `<meta>` tag**: The correct attribute name for the `<meta>` tag is `name="supported-color-schemes"` — this is non-standard and ignored by clients. The correct signal for Apple Mail is `color-scheme` only; `supported-color-schemes` belongs only in CSS `:root`. Currently both are in both places.
