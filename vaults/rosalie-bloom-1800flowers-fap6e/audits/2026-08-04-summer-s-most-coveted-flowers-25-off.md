# Summer's most coveted flowers, 25% off
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-04**
## Executive summary

- This is the third appearance of the "Mallory Green, Floral Designer" persona format in two weeks — following the July 23 original and the August 1 repeat ("Our florist's favorite unique varieties are 25% off"). Three days after the last Mallory send, 1-800-Flowers is running the identical structural template again: illustrated sketch avatar, first-person "meet the designer" copy, a scroll of named bouquets (Delphinium Meadow™, Dreamy Haze™, Pacific Drift™, Sunflower Radiance™), then the standard category grid and Passport upsell. The only real variable is which arrangements get featured.
- The offer itself (25% off, code BLOOM25) is clear and appears in the top band, but it's the weakest version of this brand's discount messaging seen recently — no urgency, no expiration visible above the fold, and it's competing with a lot of soft editorial copy before any product or CTA shows up. Compare that to the July 29/30 "Friends & Family" sends, which led with the offer and an oversized invite treatment and scored 9/10.
- This is a repeat-creative problem more than an execution problem: the render itself is clean, but a subscriber who opened the August 1 email will recognize this immediately as the same email with new flower names, which erodes both the "editorial specialness" conceit and the incentive to open the next one.

## What's working

- Clean, bug-free render — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum.
- Product photography is strong and on-brand (the mixed-bouquet grid image in particular reads well).
- Discount code (BLOOM25) is visible and unambiguous in the top banner.
- "Shop by Category" grid gives multiple entry points beyond the single hero designer picks.
- Passport membership block is visually distinct and clearly separated from the main offer.

## What's weak

- This is a near-exact repeat of the August 1 "Florist Picks" email — same Mallory Green avatar, same "meet the designer" narrative beat, same layout — just three days later, which reads as lazy cadence to anyone who opened the last one.
- No urgency or deadline anywhere in the visible render — the offer feels open-ended, which weakens the case to act now versus the July 29/30 sends that had explicit "ends tonight" framing.
- Hero section (avatar + "Gift Like a Florist for Summer" + intro paragraph) takes a lot of vertical space before any product or price appears — a mobile reader scrolls through pure editorial copy first.
- CTA copy is generic "SHOP NOW" repeated four times with no differentiation — none of them tell you what you're getting (price, bouquet, occasion).
- No social proof, ratings, or reviews visible anywhere in the render.

## Recommendations

- 1. Retire or space out the Mallory Green format — it ran July 23 and August 1; running it again August 4 burns the "curated by our florist" premise. Save it for a genuinely new capsule collection, not a recurring content hook.
- 2. Add a visible deadline to the top banner ("25% off ends [date]") — right now the offer has no time pressure, unlike the brand's higher-scoring sends.
- 3. Lead with a product image + price before the "meet the designer" narrative — move Delphinium Meadow™ (or the strongest hero shot) above the fold, narrative copy after.
- 4. Differentiate the CTA copy per section — "Shop Delphinium Meadow," "Shop Botanical Luxury" (already does this once) — replace the plain "SHOP NOW" repeats with product-specific verbs.
- 5. Surface a star rating or "X,000+ five-star reviews" badge near the hero bouquet to add trust signal that's currently absent.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New this week: Delphinium Meadow™ + 25% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `25% off ends soon — summer's best blooms`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Meet Delphinium Meadow, Dreamy Haze & 3 more — code BLOOM25`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Handpicked bouquets, 25% off through Sunday`

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a standard MSO-conditional HTML/XHTML template with responsive media queries and dark-mode meta tags, but ships with unencrypted tracking pixels, multiple unlabeled images, and no detectable unsubscribe or sender-authentication headers in the captured transmission. Automated QA returned a 38% pass rate (0 hard issues, 5 warning categories).

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 38 tracking/click-redirect links were present but skipped by the automated HTTP prober (redirect domains such as `click.dv.1800flowers.com`, `mi.1800flowers.com`), so destination validity is unconfirmed.
- Plain-text alternative part is 70%+ raw URLs (6,824 of 9,671 characters), which is a common spam-filter heuristic trigger and indicates the plain-text version is essentially a link dump rather than readable fallback content.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 6 images are missing `alt` text (evidence: QA findings), including both functional tracking pixels and at least one content-adjacent asset (`eaf9f95e-66cc-45ca-b7d8-ab5953b4d00b` from `click.dv.1800flowers.com`). Missing alt text on tracking pixels is low-impact, but any content image without alt text degrades screen-reader and images-off rendering.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
No standard `utm_source` / `utm_medium` / `utm_campaign` parameters are visible on any sampled link; the tracking system instead uses proprietary parameters (`mi_ecmp`, `mi_mid`, `cp_t`) via the `click.dv.1800flowers.com` and `mi.1800flowers.com` redirect domains. ## 7. Recommendations
1. Serve all tracking pixels (`atdmt.com`, `pixel.mathtag.com`) over HTTPS to prevent silent blocking by modern mail clients.
2. Add `alt=""` (decorative) to tracking pixels and descriptive `alt` text to content images.
3. Add `mso-font-alt` fallbacks to the Lato `@font-face` declarations for consistent Outlook rendering.
4. Verify at the sending MTA (bypassing the relay) that `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and `Authentication-Results` (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) headers are actually present — current absence may be a relay-capture artifact rather than a true compliance gap, but should be confirmed given CAN-SPAM/RFC 8058 requirements.
5. Confirm a physical mailing address is present in the full footer (outside the truncated excerpt) to satisfy CAN-SPAM.
6. Reduce the plain-text part's URL density (currently >70%) by adding readable descriptive text alongside links.
7. Standardize tracking links with conventional `utm_*` parameters (or document the `mi_*` scheme) so email-to-site attribution can be validated end-to-end.
