# Two Days to Get DOUBLE the Flowers 💐🍂
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-28**
## Executive summary

- This is a straightforward "double the blooms for free" mechanic — $20 off five specific bouquets shown as clean before/after pairs (small bouquet → big bouquet, "2X the blooms" arrow between them). It's visually the clearest email 1-800-Flowers has sent in this recent run: no cluttered numbered-guide format, no staff-picks gimmick, just repeated product comparisons that make the value prop obvious at a glance.
- The layout is also its biggest weakness: five near-identical before/after blocks stacked vertically means the actual CTA ("DOUBLE UP") only appears once, at the very top, before any of the products are shown. By the time a reader scrolls through red roses, cherished blooms, pink roses, tulips, and assorted roses, there's no button in sight — just a second, unrelated 20% off Fall Best Sellers block bolted on afterward.
- Solid, honest offer with unusually strong visual proof of value, but the execution buries its own CTA and dilutes the "double the blooms" hook by tacking on a second incongruent promo underneath.

## What's working

- The before/after visual (small bouquet vs. 2X bouquet with an arrow) is an unusually concrete, easy-to-parse value demo — better than a generic "20% off" banner.
- "DOUBLE" appears as a bold vertical sidebar treatment on both edges, reinforcing the hero message even for skimmers.
- Five product options (roses, cherished blooms, pink roses, tulips, assorted roses) give real choice within the same offer, unlike single-SKU promos.
- "TWO DAYS ONLY!" at the very top gives credible, specific urgency.
- No render bugs — images, pricing badges, and typography all display cleanly.

## What's weak

- Only one CTA button ("DOUBLE UP") exists, placed above all five product examples — after scrolling past bouquet after bouquet, there's nothing to click.
- Five repeated before/after blocks is overkill; the format gets repetitive by the third instance and the eye stops noticing "Save $20" as a signal.
- The 20% Off Fall Best Sellers section (different offer, different code BLOOM20) is stapled onto the bottom of a "double the blooms" email, diluting the single-offer clarity that made the top half work.
- No price is shown anywhere — only "Save $20" — so a shopper can't tell if the final price is $30 or $80.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a CTA button under every product pairing (or at minimum after the 2nd and 5th), not just once at the top — right now the scroll-to-click path is broken.
- 2. Cut the product list from five pairings to two or three; keep the best sellers (red roses + assorted roses) and drop the rest, or convert them into a smaller grid instead of five full-width repeats.
- 3. Split the 20% Off Fall Best Sellers into its own send — mixing two different codes/offers in one email undercuts the clean "double" hook.
- 4. Show an actual price next to "Save $20" (e.g., "$39.99 → get 2X free") so the deal is legible without doing math.
- 5. Tie subject/preheader closer to the visual hook rather than generic flowers copy.
- **Subject Alt A:** `2 Days Left: Every Bouquet, Double the Blooms`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Same Price, Twice the Flowers — 48 Hours Only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free upgrade on 5 fall favorites, ends tomorrow`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Roses, tulips & more — 2X blooms, $0 extra`
