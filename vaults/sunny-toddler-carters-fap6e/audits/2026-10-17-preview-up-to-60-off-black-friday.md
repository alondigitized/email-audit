# PREVIEW: Up to 60% off Black Friday ‼️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-17**
## Executive summary

- This is the fifth Black Friday preview send from Carter's this week. The creative is nearly the same as the Oct 16 "Up to 60% off Black Friday preview ✔️" and the Oct 13 "(Early) Black Friday: Up to 60% off 🖤". The black starfield banner with "UP TO 60%" reads in one second and the "Shop Now" button is clear. It's a competent send, but it isn't new.
- The body has more offers than the hero. A "JUST FOR YOU" grid of denim, joggers and bodysuits has no prices and no captions. A second black banner adds "$10 OFF OUTERWEAR" with a puffer jacket row. A red "UP TO 70% OFF* CLEARANCE" block then sits below a 60% hero. That's three different offers and two different percentages, so the deal is less clear than the Oct 17 fleece PJ send ("FLEECE PJS FROM $8"), which was the best hard-priced hero this week.
- Judgement: solid execution, low novelty. A subscriber who has seen four near-identical emails will skim this one. The "PREVIEW:" subject prefix and the "‼️" emoji make the subject feel stale.

## What's working

- The black starfield hero with "UP TO 60% OFF*" and the lime flat-lay is bold and easy to read at a glance.
- The "$10 OFF OUTERWEAR" banner adds a concrete dollar offer and names a hero product, the Packable Puffer.
- The seasonal flat-lay and puffer jackets fit the October timing.
- The "Shop Now" buttons are clear and the Rewards and "ALL the perks" strip at the bottom adds member value.
- The brand carousel at the top (OshKosh, Little Planet, Otter Avenue, Skip Hop) gives easy cross-brand navigation.

## What's weak

- The "JUST FOR YOU" grid has no prices, names or labels, and it doesn't look personalized. It's nine generic denim, jogger and bodysuit tiles.
- The email carries three offers (60%, $10 off outerwear, 70% clearance), so no single deal wins.
- The 70% clearance block sits below a 60% hero. That contradicts the headline and makes the hero look like the smaller deal.
- This is the fifth near-identical Black Friday preview in five days, so it will drive fatigue.
- The legal block is long, and the asterisks on every offer suggest fine print.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one offer and one deadline. Either use a hard-price hero like "Fleece PJs from $8" again, or lift the "$10 off outerwear" banner to the top. Cut the 70% clearance block or move it to its own send.
- 2. Add prices and names to the "JUST FOR YOU" grid, or remove it. Unlabeled tiles waste the most valuable space in the email.
- 3. Vary the creative. A fifth identical hero trains people to ignore it. Use a new image or a new angle, such as a gift guide or a sizing-based hero.
- 4. Drop "PREVIEW:" from the subject line and use a specific hook with a time limit.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$10 off puffers + up to 60% off sets`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Early Black Friday: ends Sunday, up to 60% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sets, tops & bottoms from $6 — plus $10 off outerwear`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your early access ends Sunday. Shop before it sells out.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
Structurally the email is mostly sound (responsive CSS, MSO conditionals, hidden preheader). The automated QA flagged all 12 probed Attentive tracking links as HTTP 403, a missing plain-text part, and missing alt text on several images. Most compliance and authentication warnings look like relay capture artifacts, not confirmed defects.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **12 of 12 probed links returned 403.** They are all `https://carters.e.attentivemail.com/e/c/...` redirect wrappers. They cover the hero/category tiles ("Matching Sets", "Tops & Bottoms", "Carter's", "osh kosh bigosh", "little planet", "Otter Avenue", "Skiphop") and the product images (Boys Dark Wash Relaxed-Fit Classic Jeans, Kid Medium Blue Wash Straight-Leg Jeans, Baby 5-Pack Short-Sleeve Bodysuits, Boys French Terry Joggers).
- **Likely a bot-blocking false positive, not confirmed breakage.** A uniform 403 across every signed Attentive link, including the brand logo link, points to the redirector rejecting the QA prober's user-agent or IP. Per-link breakage would not look like this. It was not verified that any link resolves for a real client, so the final destinations are unknown.
- **48 links were not probed** (footer, utility, social). Their status is unknown.
- **Link text is the image alt text on several links**, e.g. "Carter's | Matching Sets | Mix, match & repeat | Shop up to 60% off* >". This is long and pipe-delimited. It is not a defect, but it is what screen readers will announce.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Plain-text fallback is missing: `Text version is 0 chars` [FAIL].** If the relay did not just drop the part, this is a multipart/alternative gap. It affects text-only clients and spam scoring.
- **Four content images have no alt text.** They are the CloudFront banners `d3ptznaucccg76.cloudfront.net/MzczMjo6U0VSVklDRVNfQkFOTkVSXzAx…` through `…0X3Yz…` (SERVICES_BANNER_01–04). These are real content images, so this is a genuine accessibility gap.
- **Three tracking pixels have no alt text:**
  - `ho.gif` (Attentive)
  - `o.gif`
  - `264477f43986d8bb.png` (`mi.carters.com`)

  The first is wrapped in a `display:none` `.pixel-wrapper`. The second and third are tracking pixels. These are low severity, but `alt=""` would be cleaner.
- **`<title></title>` is empty** in the `<head>`. This is minor, but some clients and archive views use it.
- **Google Fonts (`League Spartan`) is loaded inside `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`.** This is correct, and clients that block external CSS will fall back.
- **The source was truncated,** so rendering checks past the preheader and pixel wrapper are not possible.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **No unrendered merge tokens** (`{{ }}`, `%%`, `[[ ]]`) appear in the visible source.
- **Subscriber ID in pixel URLs.** `mi_u=1150794179` appears in the `mi.carters.com` pixel URLs, so personalization resolved at send time. It is a numeric ID in a query string, which is standard for this tooling.
- **Campaign tag:** `mi_ecmp=10_17_2026_CAR_SAT_NOON_RETARGETING` confirms this was a retargeting segment send.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found [WARN].** The check itself says the AgentMail relay may strip these. Bulk-sender requirements (Gmail/Yahoo 2024) require both, but this cannot be confirmed from the captured data.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found [WARN].** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown, which is expected via the relay. No conclusion is possible.
- **Unsubscribe link and physical address:** the unsubscribe link was reportedly always probed, but no result for it appears in the QA output. The footer (including the postal address) is past the truncation point, so neither could be verified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **UTMs cannot be verified.** Every CTA is wrapped in an opaque Attentive redirect (`/e/c/<signed token>`), and all returned 403 to the prober. Final landing URLs, UTM parameters and landing page alignment with the offer ("Up to 60% off", "$10 off outerwear") are all unconfirmed.
- **The only visible campaign identifier** is `mi_ecmp=10_17_2026_CAR_SAT_NOON_RETARGETING` on the Carter's-owned pixel domain. It is not a UTM.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Re-probe the Attentive links** with a browser-like user-agent, or follow redirects from a residential IP. This separates a prober-blocking false positive from real failures, and it also reveals final URLs and UTMs.
2. **Check the raw message source** for a `text/plain` part. If it is truly absent, add one.
3. **Add descriptive `alt` text** to the four CloudFront banner images, and `alt=""` to the tracking pixels.
4. **Set a non-empty `<title>`**, e.g. the subject line.
5. **Capture full headers before the relay** (or test via a seed inbox) to confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment.
6. **Fix the QA tool:** report 403s from known ESP click-tracker domains as "inconclusive", not FAIL. That would raise the 16% pass rate, which is currently misleading.
