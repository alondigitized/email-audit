# ❤️ Labor Day sale - Shop $5+ tees 💙
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-01**
## Executive summary

- This is the same maroon-stripe tee and "hi pumpkin" pink graphic tee that ran on 8/29's "Labor Day deals unlocked — $5 tees" send, just recomposed as a hero shot instead of a flat-lay. It's a recognizable pivot away from the sunset-gradient banner that dominated 8/25–8/30, and the "JUST FOR YOU!" grid plus the OshKosh denim block give this send real depth — nine products deep before the reader even hits the fold's end.
- The problem is the offer stack is a mess: the top banner says 50% off entire site, mid-page says tees & bottoms from $5, and further down it's Buy 1 Get 2 Free denim. Three different offers competing for attention with no hierarchy telling the reader which one applies to what. That, combined with a subject line ("Shop $5+ tees") that only maps to the second of three banners, works against clarity.
- Solid, image-rich send that's let down by an unfocused offer structure — decent click bait but confusing follow-through.

## What's working

- Hero product photography (maroon stripe tee + "hi pumpkin" pink tee) is a genuine upgrade over the folded-laundry pile that's run all week.
- "JUST FOR YOU!" personalization module with a 3x3 grid gives strong browse depth without feeling cluttered.
- OshKosh B'gosh denim section is a clean brand crossover with its own distinct visual (worn denim on newsprint) breaking up the page.
- Store locator with map and "Shop Baby/Toddler/Kid" category tiles at the bottom give multiple exit paths for different shoppers.

## What's weak

- Three competing offers (50% off site, $5 tees & bottoms, Buy 1 Get 2 Free denim) with no single clear primary CTA — the eye doesn't know where to land first.
- Subject line promises "$5+ tees" but the actual hero banner leads with 50% off site-wide, a mismatch between what was opened for and what's shown first.
- Asterisked offers ("50% OFF*", "BUY 1, GET 2 FREE*") with dense fine print stacked at the bottom — reads as a lot of exclusions for a "sale."
- Model/kid demographics skew heavily toddler-and-under for most of the grid, then jump to an older kid on a skateboard/track in the bottom tiles — inconsistent age targeting.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one hero offer and commit — either 50% off site or $5 tees, not both stacked at the top. If subject says "$5+ tees," lead the hero banner with that exact framing, then let 50% off site live as a secondary module underneath.
- 2. Cut the fine-print density — the exclusions list at the bottom (Little Planet, Skip Hop, Otter Avenue, PureSoft, Carter's Bedtime, etc.) is long enough to feel like a bait-and-switch; consider trimming what's excluded or moving the worst exclusions closer to the offer itself.
- 3. Reorder so denim (Buy 1 Get 2 Free) and the $5 tees promo aren't fighting the top banner — give each offer its own clear section with a single CTA.
- 4. Tighten age-range consistency in the product/lifestyle photography so the send reads as "for your family" rather than jumping between infant and tween.
