# Clearance from $2.99 is on! ⏳✨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- Carter's is on day eleven of a Memorial Day campaign that started May 15, and this send returns to the $2.99 clearance anchor already featured in the May 17 drop and consolidated again into the May 19 multi-section email. The new claim — "More markdowns added" — is honest language, but it's thin justification for an eleventh send to an audience that has now ignored this offer for ten days. - The one genuine differentiator is the Skip Hop x Sesame Street collab block. The Elmo and Cookie Monster backpacks pop visually against the plain clearance grid, and "S is for Sesame Street Sale" is the cleverest headline in the campaign stretch. ## What's working

- $2.99 price anchor appears immediately in the hero without scrolling — strongest signal in the email
- "More markdowns added" is specific, honest copy that gives a genuine reason to revisit
- Skip Hop x Sesame Street collab provides the only fresh creative beat — character imagery (Elmo, Cookie Monster) is visually engaging and emotionally resonant for parents of toddlers
- "S is for Sesame Street Sale" is clever, brand-consistent copywriting — the best headline in the email
- 50% off badge on the collab block is prominent and legibly placed

## What's weak

- No explicit deadline anywhere — the ⏳ emoji is soft urgency with nothing behind it; a parent who's seen ten identical emails won't be moved by a hourglass icon alone
- Clearance product thumbnails show no individual prices — the grid teases items but doesn't confirm the $2.99 price point at the item level, which is where the decision gets made
- No hero CTA button directly under "FROM $2.99 CLEARANCE" — the category links ("Shop Baby >", "Shop Toddler >", "Shop Kid >") feel like navigation, not conversion
- By day 11, the clearance framing has no new creative hook — no countdown, no "X% sold," no urgency signal that this wave is different from May 17
- Rewards program is a small footer badge only — no member-specific hook to motivate the subscriber who's been sitting on the fence

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline in the hero**: "Clearance ends midnight Sunday" or a badge like "Last 3 Days" — the ⏳ emoji alone doesn't create urgency for someone who's seen ten sends
- 2. **Show prices on the clearance thumbnails**: overlay "From $2.99" on each product tile — the price is the whole argument; force it into the grid
- 3. **Lead with the Sesame Street section**: the collab is the freshest hook in the email and would pull in parents the clearance repeat is failing to convert — swap it above the fold
- 4. **Write a real preheader**: the preview text almost certainly bleeds into navigation junk; replace it with a copy that reinforces the deadline and the collab angle
- 5. **Surface a Rewards hook**: "Earn double points on clearance through Sunday" gives members a conversion reason that non-members don't have and costs nothing creatively


Wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <!--<![endif]-->` — correctly excluded from Outlook. However, Gmail (web), all Outlook versions, and Yahoo block external `<link>` stylesheets entirely. League Spartan will silently fall back to the OS default sans-serif in the majority of recipients' inboxes. This is expected behavior for web fonts in email and is not a defect unless the design has no fallback font stack specified in inline styles.
