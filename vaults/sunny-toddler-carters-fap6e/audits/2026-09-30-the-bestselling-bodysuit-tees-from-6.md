# The bestselling bodysuit + tees from $6 🎉
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-30**
## Executive summary

- This is a quiet product-led send after a week of loud 50%-off Black Friday and Christmas blasts (09-25 through 09-29). The hero works. Four colorful long-sleeve bodysuits and tees sit on a yellow-dash background, with a clean "MUST-HAVE TEES & BODYSUITS" headline and a "Shop from $6" button. The eye lands there first and the ask is clear.
- The rest of the email drifts away from that promise. The hero sells tees and bodysuits from $6, but the "We think you'll love these" grid is mostly jeans, joggers and sleepers. Only one white bodysuit multipack appears, and none of the nine tiles shows a name or price. Then a big red "UP TO 70% OFF* CLEARANCE" block appears, so the email ends up with two competing offers. A subscriber who watched 09-29's "LAST DAY! 50% off*" will notice the subject is now about $6 tees while the body pushes a deeper 70% discount. The offer is split and the urgency is gone.
- Overall this is a competent, tidy mid-funnel send that would land around a 6. The hero is strong, but the body doesn't back it up and nothing gives a reason to act today.

## What's working

- The hero image is bright and on-brand, and the tee graphics ("Giggle Factory," "Loved by Mommy Daddy") show the product clearly.
- "Shop from $6" is a visible bordered button directly under the headline, and the price anchor matches the subject.
- The brand-family header (OshKosh, Little Planet, Otter Avenue, Skip Hop) and the "Shop It All" tiles give easy navigation by sub-brand.
- The clearance block has simple category buttons (Baby Girl, Toddler Girl, Girl, Baby Boy, Toddler Boy, Boy) that make self-segmentation quick.
- The render is clean, with no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- The product grid doesn't match the hero. It shows jeans, joggers and sleepers, with no matching tees, so the subject's promise isn't reinforced.
- None of the nine grid tiles has a product name, price or "from $6" callout, so nothing is worth clicking for.
- Two offers compete: "from $6" at the top and "up to 70% off*" clearance lower down. The clearance block is visually louder than the hero CTA.
- There is no deadline or urgency. After the "LAST DAY!" and "Ending soon" sends, this one gives no reason to act now.
- The Rewards/loyalty strip, curbside and app perks are tiny footer icons, and the "Now open near you!" store banner is only relevant to people near Commerce, CA.
- The "$6" price isn't qualified. It's unclear which items, which sizes, or whether "bestselling" is true of any specific product.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the grid match the hero: a 3x3 of the actual bestselling bodysuits and tees, each with a name and a "$6" or "from $6" price tag. This is the change that would lift clicks most.
- 2. Pick one offer. Either lead with "$6 tees and bodysuits" and drop the clearance block to a small text link, or flip the email to clearance. Don't run both at equal weight.
- 3. Add a real deadline to the hero, such as "Today only" or "Through Sunday," so the $6 price has a reason to be used now.
- 4. Move loyalty up. A "Rewards members get extra points on tees" line under the CTA would use the Carter's Rewards benefit that is currently buried.
- 5. Give the hero one link to a specific collection page instead of a generic shop-all.
- 6. Make the location banner geo-conditional, or cut it for non-local recipients.
