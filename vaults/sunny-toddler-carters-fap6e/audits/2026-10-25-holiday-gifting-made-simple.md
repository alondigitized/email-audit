# Holiday gifting made simple 🎁
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-25**
## Executive summary

- This is a pretty, offer-free gift guide. The hero is a smiling child in a knit hat holding a stack of wrapped presents on a green, starry background. The headline reads "gift guide: one list. everyone's covered." The visuals are strong and the holiday mood is right. But the only price signal in the whole email is "gifts under $25" on one tile. For a brand that has spent a week on BOGO, "up to 50% off + extra 20%" and "PJs from $9," this is a big step down in commercial pull.
- The body is a clean 2x3 grid of category tiles: baby, boys, girls, character shop, organic, and under $25. A "top-rated gifts" banner and a store locator follow. Structure is easy to scan. The tile labels are white text over busy photos, though, and the "Shop Now >" links are small. The hero button is a thin outline that doesn't stand out.
- Judgement: a decent brand and mood send that sits between the 10/20 "Watch them glow" pure-mood email (5/10) and the offer-led sends (7/10). It will earn clicks from people already shopping, but it gives nobody a reason to act today.

## What's working

- The hero is warm, on-season and visually unified, and the gift-stack photo says "gifting" at a glance.
- The six-tile grid gives clear paths by recipient (baby, boys, girls) and by theme (character, organic).
- "Gifts under $25" is the one price-anchored entry point and reads as useful.
- The header shows the sub-brands (OshKosh, little planet, Otter Avenue, Skip Hop), and the footer has rewards and curbside perks.
- The "Shop at a store near you" block is a helpful omnichannel touch.

## What's weak

- No headline offer, discount or deadline is visible anywhere. After a week of promos, this feels like a downgrade.
- The hero CTA is a low-contrast outline button, and the tile labels sit on busy photos with tiny "Shop Now >" text.
- The gift guide has no products, prices, ratings or reviews. The "top-rated gifts" banner promises social proof but shows none.
- "Gifts for baby," "boys" and "girls" are generic labels and give no sense of what's inside.
- Rewards and perks are tucked into small icons near the bottom. The long fine-print block looks like a leftover from promo sends that carry no coupon here.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a real offer to the hero, such as "Gifts from $9" or "Up to 50% off gift guide," with a deadline. This one change would lift the email most.
- 2. Make the hero CTA a solid, high-contrast button with a specific verb: "Shop gifts under $25."
- 3. Put real products with prices and star ratings in the "top-rated" block.
- 4. Move "gifts under $25" to the top of the grid, and use bolder text or a scrim behind the tile labels.
- 5. Surface rewards pricing in the body, not just the footer icons.
- 6. Trim the legal block, since there's no coupon to disclaim.
