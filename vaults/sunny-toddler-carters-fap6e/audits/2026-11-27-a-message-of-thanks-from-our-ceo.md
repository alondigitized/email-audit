# A message of thanks from our CEO
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-27**
## Executive summary

- This is a text-only letter from CEO Sharon Price John. It has no hero image, no offer and no button. The story about her kids saying "cheese!" and "doggy" at Thanksgiving dinner is charming, and the sign-off line about the dry turkey is the best line in it. It reads like a personal note, which fits the day.
- It also lands as the third Thanksgiving-adjacent send in under 48 hours. On 11/26 Carter's sent "Happy Thanksgiving from Carter's 🤎", then the 60-70% Black Friday blast. Today's letter arrives in the middle of the biggest sale of the year with almost no commerce in it. The only conversion path is one small blue link on the last line, "Explore our Black Friday deals here."
- As brand-building it's pleasant. As a revenue send it's weak. Nothing in it would pull a tired parent out of the inbox on Thanksgiving, and the one deal link is buried below nine paragraphs.

## What's working

- The CEO's voice is personal and specific. The cranberry sauce on the "fancy" dress and the crescent rolls under the table make it feel human, not corporate.
- The "first mom to serve as CEO in Carter's 161-year history" line gives a real reason to trust the brand with kids.
- The layout is clean and easy to read. The brand-family logos (OshKosh, little planet, Otter Avenue, Skip Hop) sit neatly at the top, and the body copy is well spaced.
- The handwritten signature and the cyan name give it a personal touch.
- Dropping the hard sell on Thanksgiving fits the tone of the 11/26 holiday send.

## What's weak

- There is no visual hero, product, offer or button. The only deal reference is one inline link at the very bottom, after about 300 words.
- The email never states the 60-70% off offer. A reader gets no concrete number, so there's no reason to click.
- The text runs long for a mobile read. Nine paragraphs of unbroken body copy is a lot on Thanksgiving Day.
- The footer is heavy, with large Privacy and Terms blocks that take up space the letter's closing could have used for a CTA.
- This is the third gratitude-themed send in two days, which dilutes it and cuts into the Black Friday momentum from 11/26.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a clear CTA button after the sign-off, such as "Shop 60-70% off Black Friday," with the offer number stated. This is the single biggest lift.
- 2. Cut the letter by about a third, keeping the "cheese!" and dry-turkey beats.
- 3. Add one warm family photo near the top so the email has a visual anchor.
- 4. Move or repeat the deals link above the footer divider and style it as a button.
- 5. Space the sends out. Combine this letter with the 11/26 courtesy send, or hold it until the 28th as a "thank you" follow-up with a deal.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary

The QA run is mostly noise: 10 of the 11 link failures are HTTP 403 from a click-tracker, which points to bot-blocking rather than dead links. The confirmed technical defects are the missing plain-text part and the empty `<title>`.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

A uniform 403 across every redirect-wrapped link is characteristic of the ESP blocking automated probes. It does not show that the links are dead for real recipients. Treat these as **unverified**, not confirmed broken.

The `attn_macro` value decodes to `unsubscribeLink`. A 400 may mean the probe lacks a required session or cookie, or that the token is personalized and single-use.
