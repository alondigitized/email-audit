# Season low prices 😎❤️🩵
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- Carter's Memorial Day sale rolls on, but this send is a consolidation email: the 50% off sitewide hero from May 15 returns verbatim, the $2.99 clearance from May 17 gets a row, and the $7 swim offer from May 18 gets another row. That's three separate story beats crammed into one email, four days after the flagship Memorial Day send. For anyone on Carter's list who opened even one of those prior three, this reads as a recap — not a reason to act.
- The creative itself is clean and competent. The visual hierarchy works: hero discount lands first, then a real-price product grid, then two lower-priced anchors below. The render is bug-free and mobile-legible. Carter's knows how to build an email.
- The problem is entirely strategic. The subject "Season low prices 😎❤️🩵" is the weakest subject in the recent send history — generic, no price anchor, no urgency — on what is structurally the most offer-dense email in the streak. The subject buries the lede. A subscriber fatigued by six prior sends has no reason to open this one.

## What's working

- Three-offer structure still has a logical hierarchy: sitewide 50% off → specific-price grid → $7 swim → $2.99 clearance floor.
- Real prices in the product grid ($8, $7, $9, $4, $5, $3) make the value tangible without requiring a click.
- "Shop $7" and the six category buttons (Baby Girl, Toddler Girl, etc.) give subscribers direct navigation paths — no hunting required.
- Render is clean: no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds.
- REWARDS footer maintains loyalty program visibility for members.

## What's weak

- Subject "Season low prices 😎❤️🩵" has no price, no urgency, no hook — weakest subject in this streak by a wide margin, for the most offer-dense send.
- This is the seventh consecutive promotional email; the sitewide 50% off hero is a word-for-word repeat of the May 15 send. Repeat creative accelerates unsubscribe intent.
- Three competing story beats (sitewide %, swim, clearance) dilute focus — no single offer feels urgent when three are fighting for attention.
- "Lowest prices of the season!" in the preheader almost exactly mirrors the subject, wasting that inbox real estate.
- No deadline or countdown visible anywhere — the Memorial Day urgency engine has stalled.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the subject with the hardest number.** "Season low prices" is the opposite of last week's best subjects. Pick one anchor — $2.99, $7, or 50% — and put it first, exactly as the May 17 ($2.99) and May 18 ($7) sends did.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Prices from $2.99 — final Memorial Day hours 😎`
- **Subject Alt B:** `50% off + $7 swim + $2.99 clearance: all in one`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop $7 swim, $2.99 clearance, and 50% off the whole site right now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Memorial Day pricing — lowest of the season, ending soon.`
- 2. **Add a deadline.** The May 14 send had a live countdown timer (00:12:24:47) and scored 9/10 in part because of it. A "Ends midnight tonight" line or even a static badge in the hero would re-anchor urgency that has drifted since then.
- 3. **Collapse to one offer per send.** Three offers in one email after six consecutive sends is overload. If the goal is a recap before the sale ends, pick $2.99 clearance (most surprising price) as the hero and demote the others to secondary rows.
- 4. **Differentiate the creative.** The May 15 hero ("50% OFF ENTIRE SITE & STORE") is reproduced almost identically here. Subscribers who opened May 15 will feel nothing has changed. A new product shot — or even a "final hours" badge over the same layout — signals freshness.
