# Stock up on denim! Buy 👖, Get 👖👖
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- Carter's arrives on day seventeen of consecutive daily sends recycling the exact same BOGO denim mechanic that ran two days ago (July 7: "Buy 1, Get 2! Because one pair is never enough! 👖"). The emoji-as-numbers subject line trick (`Buy 👖, Get 👖👖`) is the only meaningful creative change. The OshKosh hero image is well-executed — stacked denim, strong palette — but the moment you scroll past the BUY 1, GET 2 FREE* headline, the email stops being a denim email. A "WE THINK YOU'LL LOVE THESE!" grid of baby bodysuits and rompers, a new Besties Squad backpack launch, and a catch-all 70% off clearance block all fight for attention under a subject that promised denim.
- The subject/body mismatch is the central problem. If you opened because you needed jeans for your kid, you'll land in a template that's 75% not-jeans. The BOGO mechanic is solid and the hero communicates it clearly, but this email reads like three separate campaigns stitched together with the denim hook as cover. Returning subscribers who got July 7's email will recognize the repeat immediately.
- Verdict: competent template execution, weak editorial discipline. The template is doing more work than the strategy.

## What's working

- **BOGO hero is above the fold and crystal clear** — "BUY 1, GET 2 FREE*" over a clean denim stack communicates the offer in under two seconds.
- **OshKosh visual identity is strong** — the hero photography (rich indigo-to-light-wash denim spectrum, lifestyle-adjacent styling) earns attention.
- **Extra 20% off $50 banner** at the very top adds a secondary savings layer that rewards scrollers before they even reach the BOGO.
- **Category sub-nav buttons** (Baby Boy / Toddler Boy / Baby Girl / Toddler Girl / Boy / Girl) are a practical affordance that directs parents to their specific need fast.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, images loaded, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **Exact BOGO denim repeat two days later** — July 7 was also a "Buy 1, Get 2" denim email. Subscribers who clicked then get no new reason to click now.
- **Subject promises denim; body delivers everything else** — the "WE THINK YOU'LL LOVE THESE" grid is bodysuits and rompers, not denim. The Besties Squad section is backpacks and plushies.
- **Three competing offers with no hierarchy** — BOGO denim vs. personalized recs vs. new Besties Squad launch vs. 70% off clearance. Subscriber attention is split four ways.
- **No deadline anywhere visible** — 17 days into a clearance event with no urgency signal. "Stock up" implies abundance, not scarcity.
- **Preheader likely leaks "EXTRA 20% OFF YOUR $50 PURCHASE"** — that's a different offer than the denim BOGO in the subject, and it will confuse the inbox preview.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Dedicate this email to denim only.** Cut the bodysuits grid and the Besties Squad block. If you need to fill vertical real estate, show more denim SKUs with pricing callouts or size ranges. The Besties Squad deserves its own send with its own subject.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the BOGO.** Even "through Sunday" or "this week only" converts "nice to know" into "act now." The 17-day evergreen clearance is training subscribers to wait indefinitely.
- 3. **Rewrite the subtext under BUY 1, GET 2 FREE.** "The details on that steal are here" is passive and vague — give the exclusion constraint upfront ("select OshKosh denim, kids' sizes 4–14") so the click feels earned.
- 4. **Fix the preheader.** The "EXTRA 20% off $50" banner shouldn't be leaking as preview text when the subject is about BOGO denim.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Buy 1 pair of jeans, get 2 FREE — OshKosh denim`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your kid needs 3 pairs. Buy 1, get 2 free. 👖`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three pairs, endless outfits — OshKosh B'gosh denim, all sizes`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `BOGO denim: stock the back-to-school drawer now`

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open-tracking pixel suppressed by wrapper div.**
The Attentive pixel is nested inside a `div` with `display:none; max-height:0px; max-width:0px; opacity:0; overflow:hidden`:
```html
<div class="pixel-wrapper" style="display:none;...">
  <img src="https://carters.attentivemail.com/ss/o/u001.6x9r1fsrCn5_kOCShC5EZg/4s6/_BOr3gIDSciTfvRna8wqZg/ho.gif" ...>
</div>
```
Several clients (notably some versions of Outlook and privacy-mode Apple Mail) skip loading images inside hidden containers, causing undercounted opens. The pixel should be placed directly in `<body>` at 1×1px with only inline `width/height` suppression — not inside a `display:none` parent.

**CTA link UTM coverage — unverifiable.** HTML is truncated before any `<a href>` button elements. Confirm all CTAs carry consistent `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content` parameters.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` element.**
```html
<title></title>
```
Renders as a blank tab label in web-based clients (Gmail, Yahoo, Outlook.com) and is read aloud as empty by screen readers. Should be populated with a meaningful string, e.g. `Carter's — Buy One Get Two Denim`.

**Global link underline suppression.**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This Gmail-targeted rule removes underlines and color from all links in the email body, making linked text indistinguishable from plain text for low-vision users — a WCAG 1.4.1 (Use of Color) failure. If the intent is to prevent Gmail's blue auto-link styling, scope the rule more narrowly or ensure linked elements have a non-color visual indicator.

**Missing dark mode declarations.**
No `<meta name="color-scheme">` or `<meta name="supported-color-schemes">` tags are present, and no `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block exists in `<style>`. Apple Mail and Outlook for Mac will auto-invert the `#fff` backgrounds, which may produce unintended contrast results on images with white backgrounds.

**Google Fonts loaded from external CDN.**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=League+Spartan...">
```
Blocked by Gmail, Outlook, and privacy-proxy environments. The fallback font stack is not visible in the truncated source — confirm a system-font fallback is defined.

**Soft hyphen preheader padding.**
The preheader spacer block mixes zero-width non-joiners (`&#847;`) with soft hyphens (`­` = `&shy;`). Soft hyphens render as visible `-` in Outlook 2007–2016, potentially exposing the padding string in the preview pane.
