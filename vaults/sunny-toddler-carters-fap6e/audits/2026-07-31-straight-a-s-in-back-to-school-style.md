# Straight A's in back-to-school style 🌟
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-31**
## Executive summary

- Carter's opens strong and then loses the plot. The hero is genuinely good — a moody flat-lay of maroon/olive fall separates with "first-day fit? handled" is a confident, on-theme back-to-school hook that matches the subject line. But scroll two more screens and the email pivots hard into "THE BOO IS BACK!" Halloween skeleton pajamas, then pivots again into the exact same notebook-paper "clearance flash sale, BUY 1 GET 1 50% OFF" banner that ran as its own standalone send just one day earlier (2026-07-30, 8.00/10). That's three unrelated campaigns stapled into one email under a subject line that promises only one of them.
- Individually, each section is competently executed — clean product grids, no render bugs, legible CTAs. But stacking back-to-school + Halloween + reused clearance creative in a single send reads as "we didn't decide what this email is about," and it compounds the whiplash already flagged across the 7/25–7/29 history (Christmas PJs → clearance → jeans → back-to-school). Reusing yesterday's clearance banner verbatim is the bigger tell — this isn't new creative, it's a bolt-on.
- Net: solid top-of-funnel hero, undermined by scope creep and recycled creative further down. A subscriber who opens for "back-to-school style" gets Halloween skeletons and a reused clearance sale by the third scroll.

## What's working

- The hero image and "first-day fit? handled" headline are on-brand, seasonally correct, and visually the strongest element in the email.
- Product grid ("WE THINK YOU'LL LOVE THESE...") is clean, well-lit, and consistent — no broken tiles or placeholder gaps.
- Store locator / curbside / app / Rewards footer block is a nice loyalty touch and gives repeat shoppers a reason to stay engaged.
- CTA buttons are legible with clear copy ("Shop up to 50% off*", "Shop Now") and sit right under each section's offer.

## What's weak

- The email crams three unrelated campaigns (back-to-school, Halloween, clearance flash sale) into one send — the subject only sets up one of them.
- The clearance flash sale banner ("LIMITED TIME! clearance flash sale BUY 1, GET 1 50% OFF") is the identical creative asset used in the 2026-07-30 standalone send, one day earlier — subscribers who opened both get visibly recycled content.
- "THE BOO IS BACK!" Halloween push in the last week of July, sandwiched between back-to-school and clearance, adds more seasonal whiplash rather than resolving it.
- No urgency or deadline is stated anywhere except the vague "LIMITED TIME!" — no date, no countdown (which is arguably safer given the 7/26 send's broken 00:00:00:00 countdown bug).
- Three separate CTAs with three separate offers (50% off, 25% off, BOGO 50%) dilute the "one clear action" a marketing email should drive toward.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split this into two sends.** Back-to-school deserves its own focused email (hero + grid + one CTA); Halloween and the reused clearance banner should not ride along in the same message — this single change would do the most to fix both clarity and the "why does this look like yesterday's email" problem.
- 2. **Don't reuse the exact clearance creative two days running.** If clearance inventory still needs pushing, re-cut the banner with fresh copy/urgency framing rather than dropping in the same asset.
- 3. **Give the subject a real hook.** "Straight A's" is a cute pun but not concrete — swap in the actual offer or a sharper curiosity line, consistent with what worked in the 7/29 jeans send ("Three pairs of jeans for the price of one? 👀").
- 4. **Add a genuine, specific deadline** if clearance/BOGO urgency is the goal — a real date reads more credible than "LIMITED TIME!" alone, especially given this brand's prior countdown-timer bug.

# Technical Email Audit: Carter's — "Straight A's in back-to-school style"

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a standard MSO/mobile-responsive HTML template with Attentivemail click-tracking and Movable Ink personalization/image infrastructure; automated QA shows all 12 probed click-through links returning HTTP 403 and a missing plain-text MIME part, alongside absent unsubscribe and authentication headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **All 12 probed links fail with HTTP 403**, including the primary CTA ("Shop up to 50% off*"), the logo link ("Carter's"), and every product/category link (osh kosh bigosh, little planet, Otter Avenue, Skiphop, product images). Each failing link resolves through `https://carters.attentivemail.com/ss/c/u001...` — a single tracking redirector domain, so the 403s are systemic to that redirector rather than isolated to individual destinations.
- Open-tracking pixels are present and load via two separate systems: `https://carters.attentivemail.com/ss/o/u001.../ho.gif` (Attentivemail) and `https://mi.carters.com/p/up/745a17bbb73445ad/o.gif?mi_u=1150794179&mi_ecmp=07_31_2026_CAR_FRI_AM_ENGAGERS` (Movable Ink), both with matching campaign IDs.
- QA probing was capped at 12/46 total links (34 skipped, deprioritized as footer/utility/social); the unsubscribe link was excluded from probing by design, so its live status is unconfirmed by this pass.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 6 images are missing `alt` text: `ho.gif`, `o.gif`, `264477f43986d8bb.png`, and three CloudFront-hosted service banner PNGs (`SERVICES_BANNER_01/02/03.png`). All are decorative/tracking pixels except the CloudFront banners, which appear to carry visible content and should have descriptive alt text for screen readers.
- MSO conditional blocks (`<!--[if mso]>`) and a `@media (max-width:660px)` block are present for Outlook/desktop and mobile-stacking behavior — standard responsive-email structure, no defects observed in the truncated source.
- `<title>` tag is empty — not a rendering blocker, but reduces inbox-preview/accessibility context in clients that surface it.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No merge tags (e.g. `{{first_name}}`, `*|FNAME|*`) are present in the visible HTML source. No issues found in the reviewed portion.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header**: not found. May be a relay-capture limitation (per QA note) rather than a true absence — cannot confirm from HTML alone since unsubscribe links are excluded from link probing.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header (RFC 8058)**: not found — one-click unsubscribe cannot be confirmed as supported.
- **Authentication-Results header**: not found — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unverifiable from available data.
- **Plain-text MIME part**: missing/empty (0 chars). This is a CAN-SPAM and deliverability concern — multipart/alternative emails are expected to carry a substantive text fallback, and an empty one can also depress spam-filter scoring.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Links use Movable Ink-style campaign params (`mi_ecmp=07_31_2026_CAR_FRI_AM_ENGAGERS`, `mi_campaign=07_3...`) rather than standard `utm_source/utm_medium/utm_campaign` query params — not inherently a defect, but means downstream analytics must be reconciled through Movable Ink/Attentivemail rather than generic UTM-based tooling.
- Landing-page alignment cannot be verified: every click-through link (including the logo and primary CTA) redirects through the Attentivemail tracking domain and returns 403 before reaching a Carter's destination URL, so the final landing page and its param continuity are unconfirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Investigate the Attentivemail redirector 403s as a priority — this affects 100% of probed links, including the top CTA and logo, and would block every recipient click if reproducible outside the QA probe (versus being a bot-detection artifact of the automated check itself).
2. Add `alt` text to the three CloudFront service-banner images at minimum, since they carry visible content (tracking pixels can reasonably stay empty/decorative).
3. Populate a non-empty plain-text MIME part for multipart/alternative compliance and deliverability.
4. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are actually present on the raw sent message — current absence may be a relay-capture gap rather than a sender-side omission; verify against raw headers outside AgentMail relay.
