# Three pairs of jeans for the price of one? 👀
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-29**
## Executive summary

- Carter's leads with a genuinely sharp hook this time: "Three pairs of jeans for the price of one? 👀" is a clever, curiosity-driving way to say BUY 1 GET 2 FREE denim, and the hero photo — a stacked pile of jeans in varied washes — sells the "stock up" idea better than a flat product grid would. After the whiplash of the last two weeks (BTS → clearance → Christmas PJs three days running → clearance → BTS again, per the 7/24–7/28 history), this send at least reads as coherent: it's back-to-school denim, full stop, for the header offer.
- The problem is everything stacked underneath. Before you even reach the BOGO2 hero, there's a separate 20%-off-$50 sitewide code banner up top, and by the bottom of the email there's a third offer (up to 70% off clearance) with its own red block and six category buttons. Three distinct promotions competing for the same click is a lot to parse in one scroll, and none of them clarify whether they stack. It's a well-designed template doing too many jobs at once.
- Net: strong hook, strong hero creative, but offer-stacking dilutes what should be a clean, single-minded BOGO push. This lands a notch below the 7/25 "EARLY ACCESS" send (9.0) because that one had a single clear pivot moment — this one has three asks.

## What's working

- Subject line ("Three pairs of jeans for the price of one? 👀") is a genuine curiosity hook that still resolves to a concrete, specific offer.
- Hero photography (stacked denim, varied washes, gift-tag prop) visually reinforces "stock up" without needing copy to explain it.
- BUY 1 GET 2 FREE* headline is bold, high-contrast, and impossible to miss.
- Clean render throughout — no overlapping text, broken images, or placeholder content anywhere in the send.
- "We think you'll love these…" product grid gives real merchandise variety (bodysuits, rompers, PJs, shorts) without feeling like filler.

## What's weak

- Three separate offers (20% off $50 code, BOGO2 denim, up to 70% off clearance) compete for primary attention with no visual signal for which one matters most or whether they stack.
- The top banner (20% off code) appears before the hero, so the actual subject-line offer (BOGO denim) isn't the first thing the eye hits.
- Primary CTA copy is generic "Shop Now" rather than something denim-specific like "Shop the Buy 1 Get 2 Free Deal."
- No visible expiration date or countdown for either the BOGO or the clearance offer — both read as open-ended, which undercuts urgency.
- Fine print is heavy and only visible if you scroll to the bottom footer (one offer per day, no combining, exclusions on OshKosh/Skip Hop) — the top-of-email offers don't hint at any of these restrictions.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut to one hero offer. Lead with BUY 1 GET 2 FREE denim only above the fold; move the 20%-off-$50 code and the 70%-off clearance block into a single secondary section lower down, clearly labeled as separate/non-stacking if that's the case.
- 2. Swap "Shop Now" for a denim-specific CTA — "Shop Buy 1 Get 2 Free" — so the button itself carries the offer, not just the headline above it.
- 3. Add a visible deadline ("Ends Sunday" / "Through 8/3") near the BOGO headline — right now nothing in the render signals urgency despite three offers being time-limited promotions by nature.
- 4. Trim the fine print's blast radius — a one-line disclaimer directly under the BOGO headline ("Add 3, cheapest 2 free") would pre-empt confusion better than burying stacking rules in the footer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Buy 1 pair of jeans, get 2 free 🙌`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Denim math: 3 pairs, 1 price`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stock up before the school bell rings — deal ends soon`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus an extra 20% off $50+ with code SCHOOL20`
