# 12 Days of deals start now 🌟
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-04**
## Executive summary

- This is a strong kickoff for a new campaign. The hero is clear: "DAY 1 · 12 days of deals · $12 & UNDER," with a big "$8 super comfy lounge tops & bottoms" and a "today only!" badge. It sets up a daily-deal mechanic that gives people a reason to come back, and the "coming 11.05 / 11.06 / 11.07" teasers reinforce it. Compared with the Sleep Week sends from Nov 1–3, this is a fresh hook, not a fifth PJ discount.
- The execution is busier than it should be. The email stacks the $8 daily deal, three teased future deals, a "Shop it all" row, a PJ block, a 70% off clearance banner and a store-locator panel. The "today only" urgency competes with "up to 70% off*" clearance, and the price-match line sits under the hero as a second message. The $8 tops and bottoms are a real, concrete offer, and the product grid backs them up with crossed-out prices ($30 to $8).
- Overall: a good concept, with a hero that is clear and honest. The email loses focus below the fold. It's one of the better Carter's sends this week, but it still hedges between a single-day deal and a general sale.

## What's working

- The hero states the offer: "DAY 1," "$12 & UNDER," "$8 super comfy lounge tops & bottoms" and a visible "Shop Now" button.
- "today only!" creates credible, time-bound urgency.
- The product grid shows six items with strike-through prices ($30 to $8, $26 to $8), so the discount is easy to see.
- The "coming 11.05 / 11.06 / 11.07" teasers ($9 bodysuit pant sets, $10 OshKosh jeans, $8 character tees) give people a reason to keep opening the next sends.
- The "WE PRICE MATCH" strip adds trust.
- It's seasonal and fresh, with no stale promo language and no visible render bugs.

## What's weak

- The email has too many sections. The deal grid, teasers, "Shop it all," PJs, clearance and store panel dilute the one-deal-per-day idea.
- The "UP TO 70% OFF*" clearance block competes with the $8 hero and has an asterisk and heavy fine print.
- The "Shop Now" and "Shop Today's Deal" buttons are small, outlined and low-contrast against the bright green and white.
- The product grid tiles have no product names or reviews, and prices show no size or fit info.
- The PJ hero block recycles the Sleep Week creative from Nov 1–3 ("Festive PJs for movie marathons...").

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to the Day 1 deal, the three teasers and one footer block. Drop the PJ section and the 70% clearance block, or move them to a separate send.
- 2. Make the CTA a solid, high-contrast filled button with specific copy, like "Shop $8 Lounge Sets."
- 3. Put an end time on the "today only" badge (for example "ends 11:59 PM PT") so the urgency is credible.
- 4. Add a short "Don't miss tomorrow: $9 bodysuit pant sets" line near the top to build a habit of opening.
- 5. Add product names and star ratings to the grid tiles.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Day 1: $8 lounge tops & bottoms ☁️`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Today only: $8 comfy sets, then new deals daily`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `A new $12-and-under deal every day. Tomorrow: $9 bodysuit pant sets.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `$8 tops & bottoms today only, plus a price match guarantee.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML is a well-formed, table-based email with a preheader, a viewport meta tag, MSO fallbacks and a responsive `@media (max-width:660px)` block. The automated QA run (16% pass rate) flags 12 tracked links returning 403, a 0-character plain-text part, and many images with no `alt`. Most of the link failures are probably probe artifacts and need manual confirmation. The HTML is truncated, so findings are limited to what is visible.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **12 of 12 probed links returned 403.** All are `https://carters.e.attentivemail.com/e/c/<token>` Attentive click-redirect URLs. They cover the link texts "carter's", "osh kosh bigosh", "little planet", "Otter Avenue", "Skiphop" and 7 × "Display images to show real-time content".
- **Likely a false positive.** A uniform 403 across every Attentive redirect is the usual response to a non-browser or datacenter probe (bot filtering). It does not by itself show the links are dead for recipients. Verify with a real client or a browser-UA request before treating them as broken.
- **Unresolved destinations.** Because the redirects returned 403, the final landing URLs are unknown, so they can't be checked for 404s or redirect chains.
- **"Display images to show real-time content" appears 7 times as link text.** This looks like fallback alt or text for a live-content (dynamic) image block that was wrapped in a link. If the dynamic images don't load, recipients see this placeholder as the link label. The 7 URLs are distinct, so they are not duplicates.
- **Only 12 of 63 links were probed (51 skipped).** Coverage is partial. Footer and social links were not verified.
- **Tracking pixels.** There are two:
  - `https://carters.attentivemail.com/ss/o/.../ho.gif`, 1×1, inside a hidden `.pixel-wrapper` div.
  - `https://mi.carters.com/p/up/745a17bbb73445ad/o.gif?mi_u=1150794179&mi_ecmp=11_04_2026_CAR_WED_STO_LARGEAUDIENCE`.
  
  Both are intentional. The `mi_u=1150794179` value is a per-recipient identifier in a query string.
