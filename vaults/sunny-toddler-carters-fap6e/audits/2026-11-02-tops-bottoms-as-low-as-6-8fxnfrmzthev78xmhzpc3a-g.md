# Tops & bottoms as low as $6 ✨
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-02**
## Executive summary

- This is a catalog-style basics send that tries to do too much. The hero is a clean photo of heart-print, pink and olive joggers with "COMFY TOPS & BOTTOMS" and a "Shop from $6" button. The $6 price is concrete and matches the subject. It is nearly a repeat of the 10/30 "Comfy tops & bottoms from $6 💙" send, so a subscriber would see it as the same email again.
- Below the hero, the email splits into three pitches. There is a nine-tile grid of plain bodysuits, joggers and jeans with no prices or labels. Then comes a "Snuggly, warm & soft on skin" DreamPlush PJ block with a "Shop up to 60% off*" button. Then a red "Up to 70% off* Clearance" banner with six category buttons. The sleep-week theme from the last week of sends shows up only as a secondary block. The 70% clearance banner is the strongest offer in the email, but it sits at the bottom and is not what the subject promises.
- My judgement: serviceable but diluted. The offer ladder ($6, 60%, 70%) has no clear winner, and the product grid does nothing to sell anything.

## What's working

- The hero photo is bright and uncluttered. The heart-print joggers are cute and the "Shop from $6" button is clear.
- "As low as $6" in the subject matches the hero button, so there is no bait-and-switch.
- The red 70% clearance banner has strong contrast and is the most eye-catching block. The Baby/Toddler/Girl/Boy buttons let you self-segment.
- The gingerbread and bow-print DreamPlush PJs are seasonal and well photographed.
- The footer utility row (curbside, rewards, app, store finder) is tidy and builds trust.

## What's weak

- Three competing offers ($6, up to 60%, up to 70%) are spread over one long scroll, with no single hero.
- The nine-tile "We think you'll love these…" grid has no prices, names or CTAs, so there is no reason to click any tile.
- The best offer (up to 70% off clearance) is buried below the fold, near the bottom.
- The "Stellar Sleep Week" banner from the earlier sends is gone. The sleep theme is reduced to one line of copy and the PJ block feels tacked on.
- "From $6" and the asterisked percentages have no visible detail above the fold. The exclusions are in small print at the very bottom.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one story. Either lead with the 70% clearance for urgency, or keep the $6 basics and drop the PJ and clearance blocks. Don't run both.
- 2. Add prices and product names under every grid tile, and link each tile to its own product page.
- 3. Add a deadline, for example "ends Sunday" or "Sleep Week ends 11/5". Nothing here has a deadline.
- 4. Move the clearance banner higher if it stays, and make the primary CTA copy specific, such as "Shop joggers from $6".
- 5. Differentiate this send from the 10/30 basics email with a new product, or a new price or reason to buy now.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is built on a standard Stripo/Bee-style table layout with Attentive click-wrapping. Its automated pass rate is 16%, because every probed tracked link returned 403 and the plain-text part is empty. The 403s are probably an artifact of the probe, so they need manual verification.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **12 of 12 probed `carters.e.attentivemail.com/e/c/...` links returned 403.** They include "Shop from $6", "carter's", "osh kosh bigosh", "little planet", "Otter Avenue", "Skiphop", the hero image, and four product images (e.g. "Boys French Terry Joggers - Black").
  - All failures are on the same redirect host, and they hit every link type. That points to the ESP click-tracker rejecting the automated probe (bot or user-agent filtering), not 12 separate dead links.
  - This is **unconfirmed**. The QA data can't show whether real recipients get a 403. Re-test with a browser-like user agent or a real click-through before calling the links broken.
- **The final destination URLs are not visible.** The redirect tokens are opaque and the source is truncated. Landing-page validity can't be assessed from this data.
- **Tracking pixels:**
  - `carters.attentivemail.com/ss/o/.../ho.gif` is the open pixel. It is inside a hidden `div.pixel-wrapper` and has `alt=""`, `width="1"`, `height="1"`.
  - A second pixel, `mi.carters.com/p/up/745a17bbb73445ad/o.gif?mi_u=1150794179&mi_ecmp=11_02_2026_CAR_MON_STO_ENGAGERS`, carries a numeric user ID (`mi_u`) in the query string. It is a third-party-style tracking pixel, which is worth noting for privacy review, but it isn't a defect.
- **Link coverage:** 47 of 59 links were skipped by the probe, so footer, social, and most utility links are unverified.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Empty `<title></title>`** is confirmed in the `<head>`.
- **Missing alt text.** Per QA, 8 `<img>` elements lack alt text:
  - `o.gif` (the mi.carters.com pixel).
  - Five CloudFront images: the `MzczMjo6Q0FSXzEwMjkyNl9TRUNPTkRBUllfNS5qcGc...` secondary banner and `SERVICES_BANNER_01` through `_04`.
  - Two more entries in the QA list: `264477f43986d8bb.png` and the hero GIF.
  - The hero GIF (`MzczMjo6Q0FSXzEwMjkyNl9IRVJPXzguZ2lmOjoy...`) is also linked. The QA tool reported its link text as `[image: MzczMjo6...]`, which means its accessible name falls back to the base64 filename. That is the most impactful of these, since a screen reader would announce it as the link name.
  - `ho.gif` is a false positive. The source shows `alt=""`, which is correct for a hidden tracking pixel. The hidden `o.gif` is likely the same case, but its tag isn't in the truncated source.
- **No plain-text alternative.** QA reports "Text version is 0 chars". Text-only clients and some spam filters rely on that part. The relay may have dropped it, so confirm against the raw MIME.
- **Mobile and Outlook handling looks sound:** viewport meta, an `@media (max-width:660px)` block, and MSO conditional comments are all present. The Google Fonts `<link>` is wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`, so the web font falls back gracefully.
- **Preheader** is hidden with `display:none`, padded with `͏` and soft-hyphen characters, and in the correct position.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The visible source has no unrendered tokens (`{{ }}`, `%%`, `[FIRST_NAME]`, etc.). The source is truncated, so the body copy beyond the preheader and header wasn't inspected.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found.** The QA tool itself notes that the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is unconfirmed rather than a violation. For a bulk sender, these headers are expected by Gmail and Yahoo.
- **`Authentication-Results` is absent.** SPF, DKIM, and DMARC status is unknown. The tool attributes this to the relay.
- **The footer isn't in the truncated source.** The in-body unsubscribe link and the physical postal address can't be verified. The QA summary says an unsubscribe link was always included in the probe set, but no unsubscribe failure is listed.
- **Sender:** `carters.email@marketing.carters.com` is on a subdomain of the brand domain, which is aligned. No issue found there.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. All links are wrapped in Attentive redirects, and no destination URLs or UTM parameters are exposed in the source or QA output. The only campaign identifier visible is `mi_ecmp=11_02_2026_CAR_MON_STO_ENGAGERS` on the mi.carters.com pixel.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Re-probe the tracked links** with a real-browser UA, or follow one manually. The 403s are likely bot filtering, but that is unproven.
2. **Add descriptive `alt` text to the linked hero image** and the CloudFront banners. Use `alt=""` for decorative or tracking images.
3. **Populate `<title>`.**
4. **Ship a multipart/alternative plain-text part.** First check whether the relay is dropping it.
5. **Capture raw headers** (`List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, `Authentication-Results`) from the original message, not the relay copy, so compliance and authentication can be properly audited.
6. **Resolve redirect chains** in QA to record final URLs and UTM parameters, and to check landing-page continuity.
