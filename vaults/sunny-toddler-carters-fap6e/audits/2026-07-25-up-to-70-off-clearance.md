# 🚨 Up to 70% off* clearance! 🚨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-25**
## Executive summary

- Carter's leans hard into its clearance identity here, and the creative execution itself is clean — bold red hero, clear category segmentation (Baby Girl/Boy, Toddler Girl/Boy, Girl/Boy), and a nicely styled PJ lifestyle shot lower down. But this send lands the same day as the 9.00/10 "EARLY ACCESS - 50% off Christmas faves" email in the brand history, and that's the real story: two emails, same day, one screaming clearance-floor pricing and the other pushing holiday early access. That's a jarring subscriber experience regardless of how good either individual email looks.
- Judged on its own, this is a serviceable but unremarkable clearance blast — the category-button hero is a genuinely useful piece of UX, but the product grid below it shows nine items with zero names, prices, or links differentiated from "click the whole grid," and the fine-print block at the bottom undercuts the "up to 70% off" headline the moment a reader scrolls that far.
- Verdict: functional, on-brand, but cadence-damaging when read against the same-day Christmas send, and the mid-email product grid is a missed opportunity to actually merchandise the clearance.

## What's working

- The red hero block with "UP TO 70% OFF* CLEARANCE" is unmissable and impossible to misread at a glance.
- Six category buttons (Baby Girl, Toddler Girl, Girl, Baby Boy, Toddler Boy, Boy) let a parent jump straight to their kid's segment — smart, low-friction segmentation.
- The "PJS FOR ALL-NIGHT COMFORT" section is genuine lifestyle merchandising with real product benefit copy (2-way zips, elastic waist) and a "Shop from $10" price anchor.
- Store locator block with address and "Get Directions" is a nice local-relevance touch for anyone near the Commerce, CA location.
- Loyalty section (Rewards, cardholder perks, curbside, app) is present and visible for anyone already enrolled.

## What's weak

- The nine-item product grid has no visible prices, names, or size info — just photos, so it reads as filler rather than merchandising.
- The offer headline is "up to 70% off" but the footer carries a dense wall of exclusions/disclaimers, which chips away at how honest the 70% number feels.
- Too many competing CTAs (6 category buttons + "Shop from $10" + "Get Directions") dilute what the single primary click should be.
- Subject line bookends with 🚨 emoji and an exclamation point — a classic spam-trigger pattern, and it doesn't carry a real deadline despite the alarm styling.
- Sent the same day as "EARLY ACCESS - 50% off Christmas faves" per brand history — two contradictory price-and-season messages in one day is a real subscriber-trust cost.

## Recommendations

- 1. Coordinate send cadence so clearance and Christmas-early-access don't land the same day — pick one story per day; this is the single biggest lever here since it undermines both emails' credibility.
- 2. Add product names and prices under each grid image so "We think you'll love these" actually functions as merchandising, not a mood board.
- 3. Collapse the CTA count — keep the six category buttons as the primary navigation, but drop or de-emphasize "Get Directions" so it doesn't compete visually with the shopping path.
- 4. Move at least the top-line exclusion (e.g., "excludes final sale") near the hero itself instead of only in the footer wall of text, so the 70% claim reads as more transparent up front.
- 5. Drop the siren emoji bookending in the subject — it reads as an urgency gimmick without a real deadline attached.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Carter's Clearance: up to 70% off baby & kids`
- **Subject Alt B:** `70% off clearance is here — shop by size`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns on PJs, bodysuits, and rompers — starting at $10.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop clearance by category: baby, toddler, girl, boy.`
