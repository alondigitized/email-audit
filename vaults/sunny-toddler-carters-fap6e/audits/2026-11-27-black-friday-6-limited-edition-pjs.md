# Black Friday: $6+ limited edition PJs ❄️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-27**
## Executive summary

- This is a solid Black Friday send, and its strength is that the subject and hero say the same thing. "$6+ limited edition PJs" in the subject matches the pink hero: a mom and two kids in candy-cane pajamas, "black friday exclusive," "from $6 limited edition family PJs," plus "$3 cozy socks to match." The eye lands on the price and the faces first. A red "FREE SHIPPING ON ALL ORDERS*" bar sits above the hero.
- The problem is that this is nearly a repeat of the 11/25 send, "$6+ Limited edition PJs for the fam 🌙," which had the same pink hero and the same offer. Carter's also sent the 60-70% off blast on 11/26 and a CEO letter earlier today. A subscriber has now seen the PJ offer twice in three days. The lower half is useful (four $2-$9 product tiles, a clearance block, a price match), but the layout is busy and the single hero offer gets diluted.
- Verdict: a well-executed send that adds little new. It works as a reminder but won't earn many extra opens.

## What's working

- The hero ties directly to the subject. Pricing is large ("$6", "$3") and the family photo is warm and on-brand.
- There is one clear "Shop Now" button right under the hero, and it is easy to see on pink.
- The four product tiles each carry a specific price ("From $2 per piece", "From $9", "From $8", "From $4") and a specific verb-plus-noun link such as "Shop Multipack Bodysuits".
- The free shipping bar at the top and the "we price match" band lower down lower the risk of buying.
- The "Up to 70% off* clearance" block has quick category buttons (Baby Girl, Toddler Boy, and so on) for people shopping a particular child.

## What's weak

- The PJ offer is a near-duplicate of the 11/25 send, and the hero reads as stale.
- The product tiles don't show PJs, even though the PJs are the subject's headline. The first tiles are bodysuits and outfit sets, so the click path drifts from the promise.
- The "Shop Now" button is a small gray-on-white box in a pink hero and looks understated next to the big price copy.
- The email is long. The clearance block, price match, gift bag and services row all compete, and the fine-print disclaimers at the bottom are dense.
- The asterisks on free shipping, 70% off and the gift bag push the real terms down into small print.

## Recommendations

- 1. Change the hero to something new for the third send of the weekend, such as a Cyber Monday-bound "last chance" countdown, or show the specific PJ prints. Don't resend the same family shot.
- 2. Make the first product tile a PJ set so the subject's promise carries into the grid.
- 3. Make the "Shop Now" button a saturated red or white with bold text, and label it "Shop $6 PJs."
- 4. Cut the lower blocks (clearance, price match, gift bag) to one or two so the email shortens.
- 5. Put the key terms for free shipping and the gift bag in readable text near the offer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last call: $6 family PJs + $3 socks`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Matching PJs from $6 ends tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus bodysuits from $2 and free shipping on every order.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Limited-edition prints are going fast. Grab your sizes.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML is a standard Attentive-sent, Mailchimp/Beefree-style table layout with a preheader, `lang="en"` and responsive CSS. The QA run found two likely real defects: an empty `<title>` and a 0-char plain-text part. Most other QA failures (all 12 link probes returning 403, missing headers) are probably artifacts of the probe or the AgentMail relay and can't be confirmed from the data.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **All 12 probed links returned 403.** These include CTAs ("Shop Multipack Bodysuits >", "Shop Outfit Sets >", "Shop Character Styles >", "Shop Tops & Bottoms >"), brand tiles (Osh Kosh, Little Planet, Otter Avenue, Skiphop), the logo, and the hero.
  - Every one is an `https://carters.e.attentivemail.com/e/c/...` redirect.
  - A uniform 403 across every link on one tracking domain points to bot or WAF blocking of the automated probe. It does not show the links are broken for real recipients.
  - Destination URLs and final status were not resolved, so the true landing pages are unverified.
- 42 links were skipped, so coverage is partial.
- Tracking pixels are present: `carters.attentivemail.com/ss/o/.../ho.gif` (1×1, hidden wrapper) and `mi.carters.com/p/up/.../o.gif?mi_u=...&mi_ecmp=11_27_2026_CAR_FRI_AM_LARGEAUDIENCE`. Both are a normal setup.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Empty `<title></title>`** in `<head>` (confirmed in source). Fill it in.
- **Plain-text part is 0 chars** (`[FAIL] Plain-text fallback missing`). The email is HTML-only, which hurts text-only clients and spam scoring. It could be a relay capture limit, but treat it as real until the raw MIME is checked.
- **Missing alt text on content images (real issue).**
  - The hero/secondary tiles `MzczMjo6Q0FSXzExMjUyNl9TRUNPTkRBUllfMi1M…` / `…2-1R` (the Left/Right images) and `…My1M…` / `…My1S…` have no alt text.
  - So do the four `SERVICES_BANNER_01–04` images from `d3ptznaucccg76.cloudfront.net`.
  - Several of these are wrapped in links. The probe reported the link text as `[image: MzczMjo6Q0FSXzExMjUyNl9TRUNPTkRBUllfMi1M]`, so screen readers and images-off clients get a filename-style label.
- **False positives:** `ho.gif` already has `alt=""` in the source, and it and `o.gif` / `264477f43986d8bb.png` are tracking pixels. Empty alt is correct for these.
- Preheader padding (`͏` and soft hyphens) is correctly hidden with `display:none`. Google Fonts is loaded inside `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`, which is correct. No other rendering defects are visible in the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unrendered tokens (`{{ }}`, `%%`, `*|…|*`) appear in the visible source. The pixel URL carries `mi_u=1150794179`, a per-recipient ID, which is expected. The HTML was truncated, so the body and footer weren't checked.

## 5. Compliance
- `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were **not found**. The QA tool itself says AgentMail may strip them. This is unconfirmed, not a verified violation. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require both, so check the raw headers at the sending side.
- `Authentication-Results` is missing, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment can't be assessed. The From domain `marketing.carters.com` and the tracking domain `attentivemail.com` should be checked directly.
- The physical address and visible unsubscribe link sit in the footer, which was truncated out of the source. They are unverified, not absent.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- **Not assessable.** Every link goes through an Attentive redirect that wasn't resolved (403), so the UTM parameters and landing-page alignment are unknown.
- The only campaign identifier visible is `mi_ecmp=11_27_2026_CAR_FRI_AM_LARGEAUDIENCE` in the open pixel.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Re-probe the redirects with a browser-like User-Agent and cookies, or resolve them via the Attentive API. This is needed to confirm real destinations and UTMs before anyone treats the 403s as broken links.
2. Add a plain-text MIME part. Confirm first that the 0 chars isn't a capture artifact.
3. Populate `<title>` and add descriptive `alt` text to the linked content images and service banners. Keep `alt=""` on the tracking pixels.
4. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, plus SPF/DKIM/DMARC, from raw headers at the source. The AgentMail relay can't validate them.
5. Fix the audit tooling: suppress the alt-text warning for 1×1 pixels and mark 403s on known ESP redirect domains as "inconclusive" instead of FAIL. The 16% pass rate is largely driven by these false positives.
