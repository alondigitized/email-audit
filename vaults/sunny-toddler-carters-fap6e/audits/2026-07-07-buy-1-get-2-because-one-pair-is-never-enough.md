# Buy 1, Get 2! Because one pair is never enough! 👖
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- Carter's arrives on day fifteen of consecutive daily sends with a genuine creative refresh — a Buy 1, Get 2 BOGO mechanic anchored to OshKosh B'gosh back-to-school denim. After a two-week clearance drum that peaked with "$2.99" anchors and "PRICE DROP" shouts, a BOGO structure is meaningfully different, and the timing (early July, back-to-school consideration window) is smart. The OshKosh B'gosh hero image — stacked denim, rich lifestyle photography — is the best creative in the recent run.
- The problem is that Carter's can't stop adding offers. The email leads with a banner for "EXTRA 20% OFF YOUR $50 PURCHASE," transitions into the B1G2 hero, runs a product-recommendation carousel, then pivots to category anchors ("Uniforms From $8+", "Bottoms From $8+"), and closes with "UP TO 70% OFF* CLEARANCE." That's four distinct promotional beats in one scroll. Subscribers don't know what they're here to buy. Each offer is individually compelling; together, they cannibalize each other.
- The B1G2 for back-to-school is the right call and the right moment. Commit to it. The clearance section belongs in a separate send — it undercuts the premium feel of the OshKosh hero and reintroduces exactly the fatigue this email was supposed to break from.

## What's working

- **B1G2 hero is a genuine departure** from the clearance-event grind; the BOGO mechanic is concrete and immediately legible.
- **OshKosh B'gosh imagery** — stacked denim, back-to-school framing — is the strongest visual in the recent run; the brand story email on July 6th set this up nicely.
- **Subject line clarity** is high: "Buy 1, Get 2!" communicates the deal instantly, and the 👖 emoji telegraphs the category without ambiguity.
- **Email renders cleanly** — no broken images, no layout breaks, product grids look tight.
- **Back-to-school timing is on-point** for early July; Carter's is ahead of the peak BTS inbox crunch.

## What's weak

- **Three competing offers** (Extra 20% off $50 banner, B1G2 hero, 70% off clearance footer) create decision paralysis and undermine the single-offer focus that makes a promotional email convert.
- **"Shop Now" CTA is dead copy** — every Carter's email says "Shop Now." "Build Your Bundle" or "Shop B1G2 Jeans" would be specific to this mechanic.
- **Zero urgency signal** — no deadline, no "ends Sunday," no countdown anywhere. A BOGO deal with no expiration reads like a permanent fixture, not an event worth clicking today.
- **OshKosh B'gosh handoff is opaque** — the sender is "carter's" but the hero stars OshKosh; not every subscriber knows these are the same company. A light "Carter's | OshKosh B'gosh" co-brand in the header would close the gap.
- **The clearance section actively undercuts the hero** — dropping "UP TO 70% OFF* CLEARANCE" below a premium-feeling B1G2 denim story reintroduces the exact positioning Carter's had been running from all week.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the clearance section from this email entirely.** The July 6 vintage brand story and this B1G2 send are building OshKosh equity; the 70% clearance block undoes that in a single scroll. Move it to a dedicated send.
- 2. **Add a deadline.** "Offer ends Sunday" or "3 days only" in the hero banner costs nothing and lifts urgency materially. Right now there's no reason to click today versus next week.
- 3. **Rewrite the primary CTA.** Replace "Shop Now" with "Shop B1G2 Jeans" or "Build the Back-to-School Bundle" — specific copy outperforms generic across virtually every A/B test in promotional email.
- 4. **Drop the Extra 20% off $50 banner or absorb it into the hero.** If the BOGO already implies deep savings, the stacked banner dilutes the lead. If you keep it, make sure the math is clearly better than the BOGO alone.
- 5. **Add a co-brand signal.** One small "Carter's & OshKosh B'gosh" lockup below the Carter's logo prevents subscriber confusion without significant redesign.

