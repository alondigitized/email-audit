# 🎊 Labor Day sale: Tees & bottoms from $5 🎉
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-03**
## Executive summary

- The maroon-stripe tee and "hi pumpkin" graphic tee heroing this send is the exact same product pairing that ran on 8/29's "Labor Day deals unlocked — $5 tees" and 9/1's "Shop $5+ tees" — this is the third outing for this specific photo composition in under a week, just with the pink-orange gradient banner and "Labor Day SALE" starburst reattached. The subject-line offer ("Tees & bottoms from $5") is honest and matches the on-image copy, and the layout below the fold does real work — a "Just for you" grid of jeans, bodysuits, and rompers, a Halloween-costume push, and a "Shop It All" age-segmented row (Baby/Toddler/Kid) — but none of it is new creative, it's the standard Carter's template scaffolding.
- Where this send actually breaks from the pack is the store locator module near the bottom ("Labor Day Sale near you," 100 Citadel Dr., Commerce, California with a Get Directions button) — that's a genuinely new element not seen in the last several audited sends, and it's a smart local-relevance play if the recipient is near a physical location.
- Net: solid, on-brand, zero render bugs, but creative fatigue is now the dominant story for Carter's tee-focused sends specifically — third repeat of the same two garments in six days.

## What's working

- Hero image is a clean, real product photo (no lorem ipsum, no broken assets, no overlapping text).
- Offer is stated twice and consistently — "50% OFF* SITE & STORE" in the banner and "TEES & BOTTOMS FROM $5" as the primary headline — no bait-and-switch feel.
- The "Just for you" 3x3 grid gives concrete, browsable product variety (jeans, layette, bodysuits, sleepwear) beyond the hero.
- Age-segmented CTAs (Shop Baby / Shop Toddler / Shop Kid) give a clear next click for parents shopping multiple sizes.
- New store-locator block with an address and "Get Directions" CTA adds local relevance not seen in recent sends.

## What's weak

- Third consecutive reuse of the same maroon-stripe/"hi pumpkin" tee pairing (8/29, 9/1, now 9/3) — subscribers who opened those will recognize this instantly as a repeat.
- Two competing offers stacked at the top (50% off site & store, then "from $5" tees/bottoms) creates mild hierarchy confusion about which deal is the actual hook.
- Fine print block at the bottom is dense with exclusions (Little Planet, Skip Hop, Otter Avenue, PurelySoft, Carter's Bedtime, swimwear, licensed styles, select outerwear, sneak peek, doorbusters all carved out) — undercuts the "50%" headline for anyone who reads past the fold.
- "Prep for Spooky Season" Halloween push is well-executed but is a third theme competing with Labor Day tees and the "Just for you" grid — no clear single priority.

## Recommendations

- 1. Rotate the hero product photography — pull two different SKUs (e.g., the sage-stripe zip-up or paper-airplane PJ set used in the 8/31 and 9/2 PJ sends) so tee-focused shoppers aren't seeing the third identical flat-lay this week.
- 2. Pick one headline offer and subordinate the other — lead with "$5 tees & bottoms" as the specific, credible hook and drop "50% OFF SITE & STORE" to a smaller supporting line, since two big numbers at once dilutes both.
- 3. Move the store locator module higher (right under the hero) for subscribers near the Commerce, CA location — geo-relevant CTAs convert better placed before generic grids, not buried near the footer.
- 4. Trim the exclusions list or summarize it as "some exclusions apply, details at checkout" with a link, rather than a dense paragraph block that erodes trust in the 50% headline.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Tees from $5, bottoms too — Labor Day`
- **Subject Alt B:** `🎉 $5 tees + 50% off everything (Labor Day)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New fall colors, same $5 price — shop before sizes sell out`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus: find your nearest Carter's store for in-person deals`

## Technical Email Audit — Carter's Labor Day Sale

**1. Technical Summary**
The email is built on a standard MJML/BEE-style responsive template with heavy click-tracking redirection (Attentivemail) and pixel-based engagement scoring (mi.carters.com), but automated QA shows every probed content link returning HTTP 403 and no usable plain-text fallback.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- All 12 probed material links resolve through `carters.attentivemail.com/ss/c/...` redirect wrappers and **every one returned a 403** (Broken link), including the primary CTA ("Tees & Bottoms from $5 | ... | Shop Now"), category links (Shop Baby, Toddler, Kid), brand links (OshKosh B'gosh, Little Planet, Skip Hop, Otter Avenue), the Labor Day Sale banner link, and the header logo link ("Carter's"). This is total click-through failure across the sampled set — either the tracking domain is blocking the QA probe's UA/IP (bot/anti-scraping protection on Attentive's redirect service) or the links are genuinely dead. Given 12/12 failures, an infrastructure/auth-wall cause on the tracking redirector is more likely than 12 independently broken destination URLs, but this needs manual verification with a real browser since automated probes to Attentive-wrapped links can false-positive on bot detection.
- Two open/view tracking pixels present: `carters.attentivemail.com/ss/o/.../ho.gif` (Attentive open pixel) and `mi.carters.com/p/up/...&mi_ecmp=09_03_2026_CAR_THU_AM_ENGAGERS` (Movable Ink personalization/open pixel) — both structurally standard, no issues found in their markup.
- 33 additional (footer/utility/social) links were deprioritized and not probed by QA — coverage gap, not a confirmed defect.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- 7 images missing `alt` text: `ho.gif`, `o.gif`, `264477f43986d8bb.png`, and 4 CloudFront-hosted banner images (`...SERVICES_BANNER_01.png` through `_04_v3.png`). Tracking pixels having no alt is standard/acceptable, but the 4 content banner images lacking alt text is a real accessibility gap for screen readers.
- Media query breakpoint at `max-width:660px` with `.mobile_hide`/`.desktop_hide` toggle classes present; MSO conditional comments (`<!--[if mso]>`) included for Outlook desktop compatibility — no structural issues found in the truncated markup.
- Google Fonts (`League Spartan`) loaded via `<link>` outside MSO conditional — will fail silently in Outlook (expected/standard fallback pattern, not a defect).
