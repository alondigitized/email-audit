# Back to school prep starts now! 📚
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- Carter's returns to back-to-school territory with a clean "first day favorites" hero anchored at "SCHOOL FAVES FROM $5" — the creative is the strongest of the recent BTS sends, with a calm, aspirational layout that contrasts well against the dashed-red clearance blitz of July 15–16. The "SHOP IT ALL" three-column split at the bottom is the most useful navigation Carter's has put in an email this month.
- The problem is the lede: this subject line — "Back to school prep starts now! 📚" — is a word-for-word copy of the July 13 send that scored 9/10 in this audit. Subscribers who opened that email four days ago will feel the inbox stutter; those who didn't open it get another chance but Carter's loses the compounding effect of a fresh hook. Worse, the clearance flash block ($3.99, red dashes, "LIMITED TIME") reappears mid-email for the third consecutive day — now buried inside a BTS story email, it reads as filler, not a deal.
- The bones are good. The execution is undermined by copy recycling at the subject level and creative recycling mid-body. Carter's has the right seasonal narrative; it needs to stop cannibalizing it with yesterday's clearance leftover.

## What's working

- **Hero is the clearest BTS creative to date** — "first day favorites" with clothing flats over muted backgrounds is calm and aspirational; the "SCHOOL FAVES FROM $5" anchor is prominent and immediately legible.
- **"SHOP IT ALL" section** — three columns (Baby / Toddler / Kid) with lifestyle imagery give subscribers a clean landing-point by category; the most functional navigation Carter's has included in recent sends.
- **Product recommendation grid** — "WE THINK YOU'LL LOVE THESE!" personalization block adds relevance and visual texture without overwhelming the primary message.
- **Carter's Rewards visible in footer** — loyalty branding is present and legible, a consistent trust signal.
- **Render is clean** — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds from hero to footer.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is a verbatim repeat of July 13** — four days between identical subjects is too short; a subscriber who opened the 9/10 send last week sees no reason to re-engage.
- **Clearance flash block ($3.99, "LIMITED TIME") is now in its third consecutive email** — appearing July 15, 16, and now July 17 inside a BTS-themed send, it breaks the narrative and the "limited time" urgency has lost all credibility.
- **"Shop Now" CTAs are generic** — every button in this email says "Shop Now"; no button reflects the BTS story ("Shop School Faves," "Find Your Fit") or the clearance block specifically.
- **No price anchor on the clearance CTA** — the clearance block headline says "FROM $3.99" but the button just says "Shop Now" — a wasted opportunity to carry the price into the action.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Retire the recycled subject.** The July 13 send already claimed this phrase. A fresh hook tied to the "first day favorites" creative would dramatically reduce subscriber fatigue.
- **Subject Alt A:** `First day favorites — school faves from $5`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The $5 faves your kid will actually wear`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Comfy back-to-school essentials built for the wear AND the re-wear.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the looks every first day needs — starting at $5.`
- 2. **Cut or rethink the clearance block.** If it must appear, reframe it as a closeout-before-school-starts moment ("Grab the deals before the hallways fill up") rather than a floating "limited time flash" that has appeared every day this week. Better: move it to its own standalone send and let this email be a pure BTS hero.
- 3. **Specificity on CTAs.** Replace all "Shop Now" buttons with copy that matches the block: "Shop School Faves," "Shop Clearance," "Shop Baby," etc. — one clear verb + noun per section.
- 4. **Lead the preheader with the price.** "From $5" in the first five words of the preheader gives the inbox row an immediate reason to open without repeating the subject.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**

```html
<title></title>
```

Empty titles are read aloud by screen readers as the document name fallback (often the filename or "untitled"). Should contain a meaningful subject-line equivalent.

**Missing `alt=""` attribute syntax on open pixel**

```html
<img src="https://mi.carters.com/...o.gif" ... alt>
```

`alt` without `=` is valid HTML5 but nonstandard. The Attentive pixel correctly uses `alt=""`. Normalize to `alt=""` for consistency and strict parser compatibility.

**Google Fonts loaded via `<link>` inside conditional comment**

```html
<!--[if !mso]><!--><link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=League+Spartan..."><!--<![endif]-->
```

Gmail (both web and mobile) strips `<link>` tags from the `<head>`, so League Spartan will not load for Gmail users. The CSS stack should specify a web-safe fallback in the same rule that references `League Spartan`; verify that `font-family` declarations in the body include fallbacks like `Arial, sans-serif`.

**No issues found** with MSO VML conditionals, `role="presentation"` on tables, or responsive media query structure.
