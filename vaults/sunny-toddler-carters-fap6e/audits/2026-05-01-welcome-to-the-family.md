**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-01**
## Executive summary

- **Sender:** carter's `<carters.email@welcome.carters.com>` · **Date:** 2026-05-01
- A competent but generic welcome email that does the basics right — warm tone, brand introduction, app push — without doing anything memorable. The dual-brand structure (Carter's + OshKosh B'gosh) is smart cross-sell for a new subscriber who may not know the family, but the email leans heavily on photography and light on compelling reason-to-act right now. ## What's working

- **Hero image is emotionally resonant.** The sleeping baby photo hits the right emotional register for a new parent subscriber — warm, aspirational, brand-aligned.
- **Dual-brand introduction ("Shop our family of brands").** Surfaces OshKosh B'gosh early, which is a natural cross-sell and increases catalog breadth perception.
- **App module is well-placed.** Showing phone mockups with app store badges near the bottom is standard but effective — good timing after the subscriber is already engaged reading.
- **Clean visual hierarchy.** The email isn't cluttered; each section has breathing room.

## What's weak

- **No welcome offer or first-purchase incentive.** The most important lever in any welcome email — a discount, free shipping, or exclusive perk — is absent. This is a significant missed conversion opportunity.
- **CTA hierarchy is unclear.** The red button mid-email appears to be the primary CTA, but it's surrounded by competing modules. It doesn't visually dominate the page until you're halfway through.
- **Zero personalization.** No name, no reference to how they signed up, no "here's what to explore first" direction. Feels mass-market from line one.
- **OshKosh section feels like a tonal pivot.** The brand shift mid-email can confuse a first-time subscriber who came for Carter's specifically.
- **"SHOP IT ALL" module is too generic.** A grid of products without editorial curation or a clear "start here" message asks too much of a brand-new subscriber.

- 1. **Add a welcome offer.** Even a modest 10-15% off or free shipping on the first order changes this from an awareness email into a conversion email. It's table stakes in this category.
- 2. **Elevate the primary CTA earlier.** Put one clear button in the hero — above the fold — rather than relying on a mid-email red button after two brand sections.
- 3. **Introduce the brands more deliberately.** One sentence on "Carter's for everyday essentials, OshKosh for active play" would contextualize the dual-brand structure instead of dropping the subscriber into a brand switch without explanation.
- 4. **Curate the product grid.** Replace "SHOP IT ALL" with "Start with our top picks for [age/season]" — even generic age ranges (newborn, toddler, big kid) would increase perceived relevance.
- 5. **Test a name in the subject line.** "💙 Welcome to the family, [First Name]" is a trivially easy A/B test that consistently lifts open rates for welcome emails.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Google Fonts external stylesheet will not load in most email clients:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=League+Spartan…" rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail, Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo strip or block external `<link>` stylesheets. The font will silently fall back to the client default. If fallback fonts are not declared in the inline `font-family` stack, rendering will be inconsistent. Confirm that all `font-family` declarations include a system fallback (e.g., `'League Spartan', Arial, sans-serif`).

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some email clients (notably Samsung Mail) surface the `<title>` value as the preview or tab label. An empty title is a minor but avoidable gap.

**MSO/VML conditionals are present** — Outlook table-rendering guards look structurally correct from what is visible.

**`role="presentation"` on all layout tables** — correct.

**`#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }`** — targets Outlook Web App wrapper, suppresses link auto-styling. Good practice, no issue.
