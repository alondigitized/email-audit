# 📓 Back-to-school stock up from $5 📓
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- Carter's finally breaks the notebook-paper/hopscotch-chalk merry-go-round with a legitimately new hero: a lavender studio shot of stacked denim on a stool, anchored by a "BUY 1 GET 2 FREE denim" badge and a clean "back-to-school stock up" wordmark. It's the best-looking hero this brand has sent all week — simple, seasonal, on-message.
- The problem is what happens after the hero. This send stacks three distinct offers — BOGO2-free denim, "from $5" backpack essentials, and up to 70% off clearance — into one email, plus a 9-tile product grid, a 3-tile "shop by age" block, and a store locator. It reads more like a home-page dump than a single, decisive send. Compared to yesterday's clean 50%-off-everything email (8/6, 9.00/10) or the well-shot 8/4 flat-lay send, this one dilutes a genuinely strong hero with too many competing asks.
- Cadence is the bigger issue: this is at least the seventh back-to-school-flavored email from Carter's in eight days. The creative refresh buys some goodwill, but a subscriber who's seen "50% off entire site," "up to 50% off styles," and two BOGO clearance sends this week will feel the fatigue regardless of how the hero looks.

## What's working

- New hero creative (denim on a stool, lavender backdrop) breaks the notebook-paper/chalk repeat streak that's dominated the last several sends.
- The "BUY 1 GET 2 FREE denim" badge is a strong, specific offer that stands out visually against the softer backdrop.
- "Shop Baby / Shop Toddler / Shop Kid" photo tiles give clear, low-friction segmentation for different shopper needs.
- Footer perks row (curbside, cardholder perks, app points, Rewards+) reinforces loyalty value without cluttering the main flow.
- Store locator block with a real address and map thumbnail is a nice, differentiated touch most retail emails skip.

## What's weak

- Three separate offers (BOGO2 denim, "from $5," 70% off clearance) compete for attention with no clear primary CTA — the email doesn't tell the reader what to click first.
- The "BUY 1 GET 2 FREE denim" headline offer has real restrictions (OshKosh B'gosh only, first item at MSRP) buried in small print at the very bottom, far from the badge that promotes it.
- Two generic "Shop Now" buttons with no product specificity — nothing links to an actual item, size, or fit detail.
- The 9-tile "we think you'll love these" grid has no labels, prices, or captions, so it reads as filler rather than curated merchandising.
- This is the seventh back-to-school-themed send in eight days per the brand history — the fresh hero doesn't offset offer fatigue at this frequency.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one hero offer and lead with it exclusively — either the BOGO denim deal or the clearance markdown, not both plus a "from $5" subhead. A single, unambiguous ask will out-convert three diluted ones.
- 2. Move the BOGO2 exclusion (OshKosh only, MSRP) into a one-line disclaimer directly under the badge, not buried in bottom-of-email legal copy — it currently reads as a bait-and-switch.
- 3. Add prices or at least category labels under the 9-tile product grid; unlabeled tiles waste the strongest real estate in the email.
- 4. Cut the "SHOP IT ALL" age-segment block or the clearance block — running both plus the grid makes this feel like three emails stitched together.
- 5. Space this out from the last two "50% off entire site" sends (8/5, 8/6) — three offer-led emails in three days is the kind of cadence that trains subscribers to stop opening.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Denim: Buy 1, Get 2 Free 🎒`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Backpack essentials start at $5`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus up to 70% off clearance, new markdowns just added.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock up on denim, tees & more before the bell rings.`
