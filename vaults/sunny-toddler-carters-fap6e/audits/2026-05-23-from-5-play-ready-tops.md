# From $5 play-ready tops ✨
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- This is Carter's ninth send in nine days, and it's the smartest pivot in the Memorial Day stretch: a product-specific story ($5 tees and tanks) instead of another sitewide blowout. The crab-graphic hero tee is charming, "TEES & TANKS ON REPEAT" lands a clean benefit headline, and "Shop from $5" is the most decisive CTA Carter's has put in a hero all month. For a parent who buys Carter's for kids' basics, this email is directly on target.
- The execution is undercut by two choices: the $2.99 clearance module bolted to the bottom is copy-pasted from the May 17 send and has shown up in every email since, and there's no urgency signal anywhere in the email. A parent who likes the tops story has no reason not to close the tab and return later — which they won't.
- The bigger liability is cadence. Nine emails in nine days means this lands into an exhausted inbox. The open rate ceiling is already low before a single word of copy does its job.

## What's working

- **$5 in both subject line and hero button** — the price is impossible to miss whether a subscriber opens or doesn't; the subject-to-CTA throughline is unusually tight for a Carter's send.
- **"TEES & TANKS ON REPEAT" headline** — "repeat" speaks to the parent's actual behavior (buying basics in multiples), not to a sale event.
- **Six-up product grid** — showing variety across prints, solids, and colors gives a parent a real reason to browse rather than dismiss after seeing one item.
- **Clean scroll path** — hero → grid → clearance is logical and uncluttered; nothing fights for attention at the top.
- **Crab graphic tee hero** — a specific, appealing product in the hero is more persuasive than a generic lifestyle shot; kids' character and graphic apparel is Carter's strength.

## What's weak

- **$2.99 clearance module is recycled creative** — it has appeared in every Carter's email since May 17 (six sends in a row); bolting it here splits the story between two unrelated value propositions.
- **Zero urgency** — no end date, no "while supplies last," no countdown anywhere in the email; nothing pushes action today over tomorrow.
- **Solo hero product undersells the breadth** — a single tee in the hero doesn't communicate "we have 50 styles starting at $5" the way a three-up flat-lay would.
- **Nine-email cadence is the real ceiling** — regardless of creative quality, the open probability is materially dragged down by the volume since May 15.
- **Preview text not legible in render** — whatever preheader is set isn't visible as intentional copy; the inbox preview is carrying the subject line alone.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the $2.99 clearance module entirely** — this email has one job (sell $5 tops); the clearance story belongs in its own send or the footer. Keeping it here is the single biggest diluter of the message.
- 2. **Add a visible deadline to the hero** — "Offer ends Sunday" or "Limited sizes" turns a "maybe later" into a "right now."
- 3. **Replace solo hero tee with a three-up flat-lay** — a graphic tee, a striped tank, and a solid tee at $5/$6/$7 tell the range story more convincingly than one product.
- 4. **Surface loyalty in the body, not just the footer** — if Carter's Rewards members get points on basics, say it mid-email ("Earn Rewards on every $5 top") rather than hiding the logo in a footer badge.
- 5. **Rewrite subject to lead with quantity or variety** — specificity beyond just price converts better.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Play-ready tops from $5, mix & match all summer`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Graphic tees, striped tanks, and solid basics — all starting at $5. Pick your favorites before sizes run out.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The tops they'll wear on repeat. Easy styles from $5, perfect for stocking up.`
