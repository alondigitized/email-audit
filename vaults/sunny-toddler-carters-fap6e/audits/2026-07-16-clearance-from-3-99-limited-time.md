# Clearance from $3.99 🎉 LIMITED TIME
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- This is yesterday's email with the word order reversed. Carter's sent "LIMITED TIME CLEARANCE FLASH FROM $3.99 🚨" on July 15 and today it's "Clearance from $3.99 🎉 LIMITED TIME" — same dashed-red-border hero, same $3.99 floor, same clearance theme. The only substantive new element is the **Little Planet** sub-brand block mid-email, featuring a clean rugby-stripe look and "a fresh start" framing. That section is genuinely charming and could have anchored its own send.
- The product grid ("WE THINK YOU'LL LOVE THESE!") is the email's weakest section — small thumbnails, no price callouts visible at render size, no clear threading of the clearance narrative. A subscriber who opened July 15's email is staring at the same creative, same price anchor, now receiving it as their third clearance-focused email in four days.
- Judgment: Carter's just burned subscriber patience for a send that had almost nothing new to say. The Little Planet section deserved better than a supporting role in a copy-paste clearance reprise.

## What's working

- **Hero is instantly readable.** Dashed red border, "clearance flash," "$3.99" — even at small render size the value prop is clear before a single word of body copy is read.
- **Little Planet section breaks the monotony.** The striped-shirt toddler and "a fresh start" framing give the email a story beat; the product styling is warm and on-brand.
- **No render bugs.** Clean layout, images loaded, CTA buttons render correctly on both sections.
- **Subject price anchor is specific.** "$3.99" in the subject does the heavy lifting — a concrete number beats "big savings" every time.

## What's weak

- **This is a verbatim creative repeat of July 15.** Same dashed-border clearance flash hero, same $3.99 floor, same structural layout — subscribers who opened yesterday get zero new signal.
- **"WE THINK YOU'LL LOVE THESE!" grid adds noise, not decision-making fuel.** Nine-plus tiny thumbnails with no visible prices or categories; at this density the grid disappears.
- **"LIMITED TIME" has no date.** The urgency claim appears twice (subject + hero badge) but anchors to nothing concrete — no end date, no hour countdown, no inventory signal.
- **Little Planet is buried.** The most visually distinctive section is below the fold; a casual opener never sees it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the clearance reprise; lead with Little Planet.** Move the "a fresh start" toddler hero to the top. Clearance is the sub-plot ("Little Planet styles starting from $3.99"), not the headline.
- 2. **Add a credible deadline to "LIMITED TIME."** Even "Ends Sunday" would convert the vague urgency into a real reason to act now versus next week.
- 3. **Cull the product grid or add prices.** Show five hero products with visible price-after-clearance or remove the grid entirely. Nine anonymous thumbnails do not drive clicks.
- 4. **Give the subject a new hook.** Yesterday's subject already spent the $3.99 angle; today needs something earned.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Fresh styles, clearance prices — ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Organic cotton looks for less — Little Planet styles now in clearance`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the new Little Planet drop before it's gone`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Google Fonts `<link>` will silently fail in most clients:**

```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=League+Spartan:wght@400;500;600;700&display=swap" rel="stylesheet" type="text/css">
```

Gmail (web + Android), Outlook (all versions), and Apple Mail on iOS strip or ignore external stylesheet `<link>` tags. The conditional comment wrapping (`<!--[if !mso]><!-->...<![endif]-->`) blocks it for Outlook only. The declared fallback chain in inline styles needs to be verified — if body copy uses `font-family: 'League Spartan', sans-serif` without the generic fallback resolving to Arial/Helvetica, text will revert to the client default rather than the declared stack.

**Empty `<title>` tag:**

```html
<title></title>
```

Some clients (Samsung Mail, older iOS) surface the `<title>` value as a tooltip or window label. Not a deliverability issue, but a missed opportunity to reinforce context.

**VML + MSO conditional comments are present and structurally correct** — Outlook compatibility appears properly handled.

**640px fixed container width** — standard and appropriate. Mobile breakpoint fires at `max-width: 660px`. The 20px gap between container width and breakpoint trigger is intentional padding for the breakpoint but worth confirming visually at exactly 641–660px viewport widths.
