# Shop clearance from $3.99! 💥
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- Carter's is back at the clearance well — again. "Shop clearance from $3.99! 💥" is the third subject-line variant of the same $3.99 clearance hero in four days: July 15's "LIMITED TIME CLEARANCE FLASH FROM $3.99 🚨," July 16's "Clearance from $3.99 🎉 LIMITED TIME," and now this. The dashed-border hero, the "LIMITED TIME!" badge, the "clearance flash FROM $3.99" lockup — it's the same creative, resent. If you're a real subscriber tracking this brand, you have seen this email twice already this week.
- The one genuine addition is the "BESTIES SQUAD" mid-body section — a kawaii-style backpack and accessories launch tied to back-to-school, with a "Pack & go with new kid-friendly sidekicks for school (and beyond)" line. That's fresh product news, and it partially redeems the send. The "SHOP IT ALL" category tiles below (Uniforms from $5, Bottoms from $5, Dresses from $8) are clearly BTS-aligned. But Carter's buried the new story underneath recycled clearance creative that most engaged subscribers have already scrolled past twice.
- The judgement: a structurally sound email carrying stale cargo. The BESTIES SQUAD product launch deserved to be the hero, not a mid-body section propping up a third run of the same clearance promo.

## What's working

- **"BESTIES SQUAD" is fresh and timely.** The kawaii-style character backpacks with the "pack & go for school" copy are genuinely new product news — a BTS product launch, not another clearance repromote.
- **Category floor prices are visible and specific.** Uniforms from $5, Bottoms from $5, Dresses from $8 — scannable and actionable for a parent building a school wardrobe on a budget.
- **No render bugs.** Layout is clean at this resolution — no broken images, no overlapping text, no empty content blocks.
- **Visual hierarchy is functional.** Eye lands on the clearance hero first, then moves cleanly to the product grid, then BESTIES SQUAD, then category tiles.

## What's weak

- **Third run of the same clearance hero.** The dashed-border "clearance flash FROM $3.99" creative is visually identical to July 15 and July 16. Subscribers with >25% open rates have already seen this twice.
- **BESTIES SQUAD is buried.** The only genuinely new product story is mid-body, after the subscriber has already had to scroll past a hero they've seen twice this week.
- **Subject ignores the actual news.** The backpack launch is the one thing a subscriber hasn't been pitched before — the subject line doesn't mention it at all.
- **"LIMITED TIME!" has lost all urgency.** Having used urgency language in each of the last four sends, the phrase has become ambient noise. No deadline is visible anywhere.
- **Product grid ("WE THINK YOU'LL LOVE THESE!") is generic.** Nine items with no price callouts — adds visual bulk without a clear job to do between the clearance hero and the BTS section.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the BESTIES SQUAD.** Make the kawaii backpack launch the hero — it's the one piece of content subscribers haven't already received. The clearance promo can live as a secondary strip, not a full-width hero.
- 2. **Kill the "WE THINK YOU'LL LOVE THESE!" grid or give it prices.** Nine unlabeled product images between the hero and the BESTIES SQUAD dilute attention. Either cut it or add individual price callouts.
- 3. **Add a deadline to "LIMITED TIME."** Even "Ends Sunday" would restore credibility. Right now it's the fourth "LIMITED TIME" email in a row with no visible end date.
- 4. **Rewrite the subject around the new product, not the clearance repromote.**
- **Subject Alt A:** `New! Besties Squad backpacks 🐱 + clearance from $3.99`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Pack & go: new school sidekicks from OshKosh + $3.99 clearance`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Meet the Besties Squad — kawaii character backpacks for the first day of school.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New BTS backpacks just dropped + uniforms from $5, dresses from $8.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag:** `<title></title>` — Outlook on Windows and some webmail clients surface the title in the window/tab bar. Should be populated (e.g., the subject line) to avoid blank tab labels and to aid screen readers.

**Google Fonts external request:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=League+Spartan:wght@400;500;600;700&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
Wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`  so Outlook skips it — correct. However, Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook on Mac will make this external request, which (a) leaks open data to Google and (b) fails silently in corporate environments with strict CSP. The declared fallback `Arial, Helvetica Neue, Helvetica, sans-serif` is present and sufficient.

**Preheader padding uses two distinct invisible-character classes:**

- U+00AD (SOFT HYPHEN) — `­` × ~200

Mixed use of both classes is unusual and may trigger spam heuristics on some filters that scan for excessive non-printable characters. Standard practice is one class only (typically `&zwnj;` or `&shy;`).
