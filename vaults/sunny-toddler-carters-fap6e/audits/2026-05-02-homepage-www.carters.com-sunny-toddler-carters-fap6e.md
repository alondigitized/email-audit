# Homepage snapshot · www.carters.com · 2026-05-02
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-02**
## Executive summary

- Carter's is leading with a summer sale push — 40% off with a splashy swim-focused banner screaming urgency. For me, it reads as a brand that knows its audience shops on autopilot and responds to discounts, so it went straight for the promo. The vibe is warm and energetic, but the hero image I can see is of a tween or teen — not a toddler or baby — which is a weird misfire for a brand whose core customer is me.
- **6/10**

## What's working

- **"Splash flash: 50% off* UPF 50 swim! Ends Monday"** — UPF 50 is a legit mom-magnet feature. I specifically look for sun-protective swim for my kid, and naming the spec (UPF 50) rather than just saying "swimwear" earns instant credibility with me.
- **"UP TO 40% OFF*" hero headline** — Blunt, big, and exactly what I scan for before I even decide to scroll. No mystery, no puzzling lifestyle copy I have to decode.
- **Urgency framing ("Ends Monday")** — Creates a real reason to act today instead of adding to my "later" pile.
- **Prominent search bar** — I know exactly what I want (Carter's 2T pajama sets), so a search bar front-and-center saves me from navigating a category tree.
- **Cart icon visible in nav** — Muscle-memory UX; I tap the cart icon before I tap anything else.

## What's weak

- **The lifestyle image below the hero features what looks like a tween or adult** — I'm here for my toddler. Showing me a teen girl in a white smocked top is a vibe mismatch. Carter's main audience is 0–8 and I expect to see tiny humans in sunhats immediately.
- **"The real MVPs of summer" subhead is too cute, not informative** — I want to know: is this girls' swim? boys'? babies'? all of it? This tagline tells me nothing useful.
- **No loyalty/rewards callout above the fold** — I am a CouponStacker. I stack Carter's Cash on top of sale pricing and I want to see my points balance or a "earn rewards on this purchase" nudge front and center. Buried or absent.
- **Orange-to-yellow gradient hero feels a little fast-fashion** — Carter's brand equity is in "trusted, soft, safe." This gradient reads more like a Shein flash sale. The warmth works; the execution feels slightly off-brand.
- **Asterisk on "40% OFF*" with no fine print visible** — The caveat is real and I'm going to assume it excludes exactly what I want (clearance pajamas). Not seeing the terms upfront is mildly annoying.

## Recommendations

- **Swap the tween lifestyle image for a toddler swim scene** — A 2-year-old in a rash guard with a sun hat converts me. A teen in a white smocked top does not. Shoot-or-select a hero image that matches the actual buyer's kid.
- **Add a loyalty-balance or "earn X Carter's Cash" callout in the hero** — Even a small badge ("Earn rewards on every purchase") near the CTA makes coupon-stackers like me feel seen and creates a stickier reason to buy now.
- **Expand the hero subhead to name the category** — Change "The real MVPs of summer" to something like "Baby & Toddler Swim — UPF 50, up to 40% off" so mobile scanners know in one glance exactly what's discounted.
- **Surface the fine print asterisk meaning** — A tiny "excludes doorbusters, valid on select styles" line directly below the CTA is honest and prevents cart abandonment when the discount doesn't apply at checkout.
