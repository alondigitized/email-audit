# ENDS TODAY: EXTRA 20% off w/ code 👀👉
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- Carter's sends a countdown-driven stacked-discount email: up to 40% off plus an extra 20% off with a code, underscored by a live timer reading 00:12:24:47. For a parent buying baby or toddler clothing, the category fit and offer mechanics are squarely on-target. The countdown is the strongest asset — it communicates urgency before you read a single word.
- The problem is what comes after: Skip+Hop co-brand, a textile recycling CTA, a 70% clearance blowout, a store locator, and a Carter's Rewards plug all compete for attention in the same scroll. Each section individually makes sense; together they drain the urgency the timer built. By the time you reach the clearance banner the "ENDS TODAY" energy is long gone.
- Competent, well-targeted, seasonally appropriate — but bloated enough to blunt conversion. A loyal Carter's parent will engage; anyone with mild intent will lose focus mid-scroll and bail.

## What's working

- Live countdown timer (00:12:24:47) conveys urgency before any copy is read
- Stacked offer ("UP TO 40% OFF PLUS, EXTRA 20% OFF") is scannable at a glance in the hero
- Pastel spring palette and baby/toddler product photography are seasonally and demographically precise
- Carter's Rewards section acknowledges the membership relationship without feeling like an afterthought
- "ENDS TODAY" in the subject pairs with the in-email countdown — cross-channel consistency

## What's weak

- Five-plus distinct content sections (Skip+Hop, recycle, clearance, store locator, rewards) fragment the urgency the timer creates
- Emojis 👀👉 in the subject add spam signal without adding meaning — the words already carry the urgency
- "UP TO 40% OFF" qualifier allows the real savings to be far lower than the hero implies; paired with a code requirement, the total offer structure is complex
- "Shop Now" appears as the CTA across multiple sections with no differentiation — nothing tells you *what* you're shopping for in each block
- The 70% clearance section reads like a second email grafted onto the bottom — it competes with rather than reinforces the hero offer

## Recommendations

- **1. Cut to one offer narrative.** Run the countdown, hero discount, and 3–4 specific product callouts at the discounted price, then close with the code. Strip the Skip+Hop, recycle, and clearance blocks into their own sends. One email, one conversion action.
- **2. Put the code in the subject or preheader.** "w/ code" is anonymous and creates a friction step in the reader's head. Name the code upfront.
- **3. Differentiate the CTAs by section.** Replace all generic "Shop Now" with category-specific copy: "Shop Baby Sale," "Shop Toddler Clearance," "Get 20% Off." Verb + noun, not verb + adverb.
- **4. Drop the emojis.** "ENDS TODAY" already carries the urgency. The 👀👉 pair adds noise and mild deliverability risk with no payoff.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stack your savings: code SUMMER20 takes another 20% off already-reduced baby and toddler styles.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Google Fonts — confirmed issue.** Font is loaded via `<link>` in `<head>`:
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=League+Spartan...">
```
This is blocked or ignored in Outlook (all versions), Gmail (Android/webmail), and Apple Mail with remote content disabled. No `font-family` fallback stack was observed in the inline CSS. If the template relies on League Spartan for heading weight, affected clients will render a system fallback with potentially broken layout metrics.

**Empty `<title>` tag** — `<title></title>` is present but empty. Screen reader users navigating the browser-view version of the email will get no document title.

**MSO/VML conditional blocks** — Present and correctly structured. `<o:PixelsPerInch>96</o:PixelsPerInch>` and `<o:AllowPNG/>` are set. `mso-table-lspace:0; mso-table-rspace:0` applied to all tables.

**`role="presentation"`** — Applied to all layout tables in visible source. Correct.

**Responsive breakpoint** — `@media (max-width:660px)` with `.stack .column { width:100%; display:block }`. Standard stacking approach; no issues.

**Preheader padding** — Uses a mix of `&#x200D;` soft hyphens and Unicode non-breaking space characters for inbox preview truncation. Functional but the mixing of two padding strategies (zero-width joiners + `&shy;`) is unnecessary; one type is sufficient.
