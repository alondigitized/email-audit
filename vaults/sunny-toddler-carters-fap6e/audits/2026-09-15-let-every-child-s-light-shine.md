# Let every child’s light shine ✨
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Carter's blending its new brand-campaign push with the same discount catalog structure it always runs — and the seams show. The subject and top masthead promise "let every child's light shine," but the actual hero the second you open the email is a PJ ad ("for a stellar sleep," Shop From $9) that has nothing to do with the subject line. The "let every child's light shine" campaign section doesn't show up until you scroll past two full commerce blocks.
- Worse for cadence: this brand's own history shows "Ready. Set. GLOW ✨" already went out earlier today (2026-09-15, scored 7.00/10) as its own send, and that exact "ready. set. glow. / Watch Now" block reappears inside this email too. A subscriber who opened both sends today is seeing the same campaign beat twice in one day, wrapped around a different discount promo.
- Visually it's clean — no render bugs, good product photography, legible CTAs — but the message architecture is confused: three different campaigns (sleep, bestsellers, "light shine") are stacked with no throughline, and the subject line oversells a story the email doesn't lead with.

## What's working

- Clean render throughout — no overlapping text, broken images, or layout breaks.
- The bodysuit product shot (pink "Huddles & Cuddles" + navy print) is genuine, well-lit product photography that matches its "Shop from $6" CTA.
- "Our North Star" swing/backlit photo is genuinely warm and on-campaign for the subject line, when you finally reach it.
- Footer does real work — Rewards, store locator, curbside, app callouts are all present and scannable.

## What's weak

- Hero image and copy ("for a stellar sleep," PJs) completely contradict the subject line ("let every child's light shine") — a reader clicking in from that subject sees an unrelated sleep promo first.
- Too many competing CTAs (Shop From $9, Shop from $6, See What's New, Watch Now, Shop Baby/Toddler/Kid, Get Directions) with no single clear priority action.
- The "ready. set. glow." block duplicates a campaign this brand already sent standalone today (2026-09-15 "Ready. Set. GLOW ✨"), risking fatigue for anyone who opened both.
- "Watch Now" CTA under "ready. set. glow." doesn't link to a product — it's a soft video ask buried between two hard discount CTAs, which will underperform next to them.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one story per send. Either lead with the "light shine" brand campaign (move the North Star/swing image to the hero) or lead with a discount promo — don't force both into one email with a mismatched subject.
- 2. Cut the duplicate "ready. set. glow." block entirely since it already had its own dedicated send today; reusing it here just dilutes both.
- 3. Consolidate to one primary CTA above the fold that matches the subject line's promise, then let secondary CTAs (bodysuits, shop-by-age) follow below.
- 4. If keeping the PJ hero, retitle the subject to match it rather than promising a brand-campaign story the email doesn't open with.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New PJs, sweet dreams — from $9`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Tonight's the night for better sleep (PJs from $9)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Soft, cozy sleepwear for every kind of sleeper — plus bestselling bodysuits from $6.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Their new favorite PJs just landed. Shop from $9 before bedtime.`

2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All 10 probed content links resolve through `carters.e.attentivemail.com/e/c/...` redirect wrappers and return **403 Forbidden**, including the masthead ("carter's"), all shop CTAs ("Shop Baby," "Shop toddler," "Shop Kid"), hero/banner links, and both service-banner images. This affects 100% of probed material links (10/10 FAIL).
- Two open/impression tracking pixels are present and load from separate domains: `mi.carters.com/p/up/745a17bbb73445ad/o.gif?mi_u=1150794179&mi_ecmp=09_15_2026_CAR_TUE_PM_ENGAGERS` and `carters.attentivemail.com/ss/o/u001.../ho.gif` — these are unrelated to the broken click links and loaded fine per QA (no FAIL flagged on them).
- Given the 403s are uniform across every wrapped link regardless of destination or campaign parameter, this points to a systemic issue with the Attentive redirect service or link-signing (e.g., expired/invalid signature, geo/bot-block on the fetch client, or wrapper misconfiguration) rather than isolated dead links.

- No issues found with viewport meta tag or responsive `@media (max-width:660px)` breakpoints in the visible source.
