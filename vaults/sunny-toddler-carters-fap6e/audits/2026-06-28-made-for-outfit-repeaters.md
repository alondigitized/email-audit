# Made for outfit repeaters 😉
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-28**
## Executive summary

- This send tries to do three things at once: tease a summer-ready product assortment, announce two new character-IP collabs (Skip Hop × CoComelon and Skip Hop × Blippi), and recycle the $2.99 clearance block that has now appeared in every Carter's email since June 23. The collab news is genuinely fresh and the strongest reason to open — but the subject line ("Made for outfit repeaters 😉") is a cute pun that buries the lead entirely. A subscriber scanning their inbox at 7am has no idea they're about to see a Skip Hop × Blippi drop.
- The visual execution is clean but structurally scattered. The "READY FOR SUMMER?" grid at the top isn't a hero — it's a holding pattern before the real hook. By the time the collabs section arrives, the reader has already passed through nine product thumbnails with no narrative thread connecting them. The $2.99 clearance at the bottom is starting to feel like furniture: it's been here six sends in a row, and at this point it reads as filler rather than urgency.
- Verdict: a legitimately interesting collab announcement buried under a clever-but-vague subject, an unfocused product grid, and a clearance event that has worn out its welcome. The creative team wrote a great punchline for the wrong email.

## What's working

- **Skip Hop collab section is visually clear** — the CoComelon and Blippi logos are immediately recognizable to the target parent, and the two-column layout makes the "2 NEW COLLABS" announcement land.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, images loaded, no overlapping text or broken tiles.
- **$2.99 price anchor is still concrete** — even if exhausted, the orange clearance block communicates an unambiguous price floor at a glance.
- **Carter's near you + REWARDS** — store locator with map and the loyalty block are legitimately useful bottom-of-email real estate.
- **Short subject line** — "Made for outfit repeaters 😉" at ~29 characters is mobile-optimal length and avoids spam signals.

## What's weak

- **Subject line doesn't set up the actual email** — the pun references "repeating" but never hints at new collabs, clearance, or summer — the reader gets a surprise they didn't ask for.
- **$2.99 clearance block is now a zombie** — this is its fifth or sixth appearance in consecutive sends (first surfaced June 23); any credible deadline has long since evaporated.
- **"READY FOR SUMMER?" grid has no editorial point of view** — nine small product tiles with no callout, no bestseller label, no hero product; it's a browse grid, not a hook.
- **CTA copy is generic** — both "Shop Now" buttons are identical and interchangeable; neither tells you whether you're shopping collabs or clearance.
- **No preview text visible in webview** — the inbox preheader likely falls back to nav-bar junk ("little planet | OshKosh") which wastes the open.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the collab news, full stop.** The Skip Hop × CoComelon / Blippi drop is the freshest content in this email by miles. It should be the hero, not the third section. Kill the "READY FOR SUMMER?" grid or push it below the collab reveal.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to announce the collabs** — the pun is charming but sacrifices specificity entirely.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Skip Hop × CoComelon & Blippi are here 🐛`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New collab drop: Skip Hop × Blippi + CoComelon`
- 3. **Fix the preheader** — if it's leaking nav text, a real preheader line would lift open rate meaningfully.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Two character collabs just landed — shop before they're gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New: CoComelon + Blippi drops your kid will actually lose it over`
- 4. **Retire or hard-deadline the $2.99 clearance block.** Add "Ends Sunday" or cut it. At day 5, it's background noise. If it must stay, make it a secondary CTA with an actual end date.
- 5. **Differentiate the CTA copy.** "Shop Collabs" vs. "Shop Clearance" — two distinct actions deserve two distinct labels.
