# ICYMI: 50% off entire site & store 🔥
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- This is a resend — Carter's is recycling the Memorial Day 50% off sitewide offer that's been running since May 15, wrapping it in an "ICYMI" subject line to justify the re-drop. The bones are structurally the same email that scored a 9/10 on May 15 and reappeared in the May 19 consolidation send: flat-lay hero, "50% OFF* ENTIRE SITE & STORE," product price grid, BOGO outfit section, $2.99 clearance row. It's competent.
- The problem is that this audience has been getting Carter's Memorial Day emails every single day for eight days straight. The ICYMI wrapper is a legitimate tactic for cold segments who never opened — it's a poor fit for anyone engaged enough to have opened even once, because they've already seen this exact offer repeatedly. The "last chance" energy that ICYMI is supposed to manufacture isn't felt when there's no visible deadline anywhere in the body.
- On balance: a technically solid email that's been deployed one time too many for most of this list. The timing (actual Memorial Day weekend, May 22) is the strongest argument in its favor — genuine last-day urgency is real and available, just not activated.

## What's working

- **50% off hero is immediate.** The headline lands above the fold, the flat-lay imagery reinforces it, and the "Shop Now" CTA is directly underneath — no hunting required.
- **Price anchoring in the product grid.** Specific price points ($6, $7, $8, $10) visible without scrolling make the 50% claim feel credible, not abstract.
- **Three-offer layering (sitewide + BOGO + clearance) covers multiple purchase intent modes** — a parent looking for outfit bundles, a deal hunter, and a clearance browser all find a hook.
- **Subject line length and clarity.** At 36 characters, it renders clean on mobile; the offer scope ("entire site & store") leaves no ambiguity.

## What's weak

- **No deadline anywhere in the body.** ICYMI only works as urgency if there's a visible "ends Sunday midnight" or equivalent. Without it, the implied scarcity evaporates.
- **"ICYMI" reads as a tell for re-subscribers.** Anyone who's been opening Carter's emails this week recognizes this is a resend — it can feel dismissive of their engagement history.
- **Three concurrent offers dilute the primary message.** 50% sitewide + BOGO tops & bottoms + $2.99 clearance in one scroll creates offer confusion; a reader who came for 50% off may leave less sure what the deal actually is.
- **"Shop Now" CTAs are generic throughout.** With this many sections, differentiated CTA copy ("Shop Baby Clearance," "Build an Outfit") would help each row convert on its own.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline to both the subject and hero copy.** "ICYMI" without "ends tonight" or "ends Monday midnight" is half an urgency tactic. The Memorial Day hook is real — use it. Hero subhead should read "Sale ends Monday midnight" in the same type treatment as the 50% headline.
- 2. **Pick a primary offer and make the others clearly secondary.** If the story is sitewide 50% off, put the BOGO and clearance below a visual divider labeled "More ways to save." Don't compete with your own hero.
- 3. **Differentiate the CTA copy per section.** "Shop Baby Deals," "Build an Outfit," "Shop Clearance" — each section converts better when the CTA matches what the reader is looking at.
- 4. **Suppress this resend to anyone who opened the May 15 or May 19 send.** They've seen it; a new segment angle (e.g., "you browsed but didn't buy — still 50% off") would convert better than blanket ICYMI.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends Monday: 50% off the entire site & store 🔥`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last day — entire Carter's site 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Baby, toddler & kids — lowest prices of the season end tonight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus $2.99 clearance + buy 1, get 3 free tops & bottoms`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**External Google Fonts will not load in most clients:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=League+Spartan...">
```
The `<link>` is gated behind `<!--[if !mso]>` (protecting Outlook), but Gmail, Yahoo, and most webmail clients strip external stylesheets entirely. No inline fallback font stack is declared in the visible source. `League Spartan` will silently fall back to the browser/client default serif or sans-serif — verify the fallback renders acceptably.

**Preheader padding uses mixed invisible character types:**
```html
͏ ͏ ͏ ... ­ ­ ­ ...
```
Two distinct character classes are used together — U+034F (Combining Grapheme Joiner) and U+00AD (Soft Hyphen). Mixing both is non-standard; U+034F alone is the more reliable approach. Some Android clients have been observed rendering soft hyphens as visible dashes. Recommend standardizing to U+034F or zero-width non-breaking spaces only.

**Empty `<title>` element:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Low severity, but VoiceOver on macOS/iOS announces the window title when an email opens in a browser fallback. A descriptive title improves accessibility for screen-reader users.
