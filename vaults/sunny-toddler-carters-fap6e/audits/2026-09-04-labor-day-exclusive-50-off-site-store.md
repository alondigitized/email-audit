# Labor Day exclusive ❤️ 50% off* site & store
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-04**
## Executive summary

- This is the same pink-orange sunset gradient hero and folded-laundry-pile photo that's carried nearly every Carter's send this week — it ran 8/25 through 8/30 and is back again on 9/4, still unchanged. That's now seven-plus days on one visual, and the "Just for you" grid stacking nine near-identical product tiles (three jeans, three onesie multipacks, three more) does the email no favors — it reads cluttered rather than curated.
- Where this send does earn points is structure: it's the most complete Carter's layout I've seen from this brand this week, bundling the 50%+20% offer, a real Halloween sub-campaign ("Prep for Spooky Season"), tiered price points ("Tees from $5," "Bodysuits from $6"), and even a store-locator/map block for the Commerce, CA location. But the folded-laundry hero photo itself is stale creative doing a lot of the same work it did five sends ago, and the offer stacking (50% + extra 20% with code, all footnoted with dense fine print) tips toward feeling like a bait-and-switch rather than a clean discount.
- Verdict: solid mechanics, tired hero. This is a "shop the sale" email that leans on breadth of content rather than a compelling single image to earn the click.

## What's working

- Clear, high-contrast hero banner states the offer immediately: "50% OFF SITE & STORE" plus a distinct extra-20%-with-code callout.
- Multiple entry points beyond the main sale — Halloween sub-section, tiered "Must-Have Deals," and a local store/map block — give shoppers several reasons to click in.
- No render bugs: images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, layout holds together end to end.
- The Halloween skeleton PJs and pumpkin-graphic flat-lay is a fresh, seasonally relevant image distinct from the recycled hero.

## What's weak

- Hero photo is the same folded-laundry pile that's run essentially unchanged since 8/25 — six-plus sends on one image is fatigue territory.
- "Just for You" grid is nine tiles with minimal differentiation (three near-identical jean/onesie shots) — visual noise with no clear hierarchy of what to click first.
- Offer stacking ("50% off + extra 20% off with code") paired with a wall of exclusion fine print below reads as less honest than a flat, single discount.
- No visible urgency/deadline in the hero itself — "Labor Day Sale" implies time-boundedness but no explicit end date or countdown is shown.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace the hero photo — six-plus days on the same folded-laundry pile is the single biggest drag here; swap in the Halloween skeleton-PJ flat-lay (already in this email) as the lead image, since it's the freshest and most seasonally relevant asset Carter's has.
- 2. Cut the "Just for You" grid from nine tiles to 4-6 and add a one-line label per row (e.g., "Denim," "Bodysuits") so the eye has an anchor instead of scanning a flat product wall.
- 3. Add an explicit end date/time to the hero ("Ends tonight" or "Through 9/7") to convert the implied Labor Day urgency into something concrete and clickable.
- 4. Simplify the offer to one headline number where possible, or move the extra-20%-with-code detail into a secondary banner so the hero isn't doing double discount math.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- All 12 probed links resolve through `https://carters.attentivemail.com/ss/c/u001....` redirect wrappers and returned **403 Forbidden** — including the header logo ("Carter's"), the primary hero CTA ("Labor Day Sale | 50% Off The Site And Store..."), nav links ("osh kosh bigosh," "little planet," "Otter Avenue," "Skiphop"), and 4 product image links.
- 403s on a redirect-tracking domain are frequently caused by bot/UA filtering (Attentivemail blocking non-browser probes) rather than a genuinely broken destination — this should be confirmed with a real browser session before treating as a hard link break, but as reported it is a 100% failure rate across all sampled links and warrants verification.
