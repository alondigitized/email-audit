# $10 Two-piece fleece PJs 🌠
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-13**
## Executive summary

- This is Day 10 of the 12 Days of Deals, and the hero does its job. A toddler in dinosaur-print fleece PJs sits under a "today only!" badge, a "DAY 10 / $12 & UNDER" banner, and a big "$10 two-piece fleece PJs" with a Shop Now button. The eye lands on the price and the photo first, and the subject line matches the hero exactly.
- The email is better focused than the Nov 12 send ("HOLIDAY STYLES - Up to 60% off 🎁"), which tried to do three jobs. Below the hero, though, it still stacks a "70% off clearance" block, two teaser tiles for Day 11 ($7) and Day 12 ($4), a category grid, a $5 Rewards banner and a store-locator block. It is a long scroll for a one-product offer. The red "up to 70% off*" clearance panel is also louder than the green hero in saturation, so it competes with the daily deal.
- Judgement: a solid, clear daily-deal send that is diluted by promo stacking. The day-by-day structure is a real hook, because it gives subscribers a reason to come back. It would be stronger if it did less after the hero.

## What's working

- The hero is a single concrete offer: "$10 two-piece fleece PJs," with a "today only!" badge and a warm, smiling toddler photo.
- The subject matches the hero, so there is no mismatch between inbox and body.
- The "get ready for upcoming deals" tiles ("coming 11.14 – $7 fleece & sleep & play pjs", "coming 11.15 – $4 graphic tees & bodysuits") give the 12 Days series a reason to keep opening.
- The mint "WE PRICE MATCH" strip directly below the hero lowers purchase anxiety.
- The layout renders cleanly, with no broken images or overlapping text, and the category tiles (Baby Girl, Toddler Boy, etc.) are easy to tap.

## What's weak

- The red "up to 70% off*" clearance panel is the second-loudest element and pulls attention from the $10 PJs, as in the Nov 12 send.
- Both hero CTAs say a generic "Shop Now," and "Shop Today's Deal" is the other button. None names the product.
- The banner reads "$12 & UNDER" directly above a "$10" price. The series branding and the day's deal are visually muddled.
- The body is very long. There are at least six distinct modules before the footer, and the asterisked offers ("up to 60%*", "70%*", the $5 reward) lead to dense legal copy.
- The "Shop Now" button in the hero is small and low-contrast (white on a photo) compared with the headline.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the clearance block, or shrink it to a one-line text link under the hero. Let Day 10 be the only loud thing, and keep the Day 11 and Day 12 teasers.
- 2. Rewrite the hero CTA as "Shop $10 fleece PJs" on a solid, high-contrast button.
- 3. Drop "$12 & UNDER" from the banner on days when the hero price is lower, or lead with "DAY 10 of 12" so the price is the only dollar figure in the hero.
- 4. Move the $5 Rewards banner up or fold it into the price-match strip. Loyalty value is currently buried below the fold.
- 5. Add a sizing or availability cue to the hero, such as "Sizes NB–5T" or "Matching family sets."

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML is a standard table-based, responsive build (viewport meta, `lang="en"`, MSO conditionals, `role="presentation"` tables). Automated QA failed 12 of 12 probed links with a uniform 403 and found no plain-text part. Neither result can be treated as a confirmed customer-facing defect without the caveats below.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **All 12 probed links returned 403.** This includes the primary CTA ("Up to 60% Off | Holiday styles | Shop Now"), "Tops from $5 >", "Bottoms from $7 >", "Sets from $12 >", the brand logos, and the "Display images to show real-time content" link. Every one is a `https://carters.e.attentivemail.com/e/c/...` redirect.
- **A uniform 403 across every link on one ESP redirect domain points to bot or datacenter filtering of the QA probe.** A broken-link problem would be unlikely to hit every link identically. It is unverified whether real recipients are affected. Re-probe with a browser-like UA and residential egress before treating it as a real failure.
- **Final destinations are unresolved.** Because each probe stopped at the 403, the QA run could not follow redirects to the carters.com landing pages. Destination correctness is unverified for all 12 probed links, and 44 further links were skipped.
- **The first failing link has an empty link-text label in the report.** It has an anchor wrapping an image with no accessible name (see section 3).
- **Three tracking pixels are present:**
  - `carters.attentivemail.com/ss/o/.../ho.gif`
  - `mi.carters.com/p/up/745a17bbb73445ad/o.gif?mi_u=1150794179&mi_ecmp=11_13_2026_CAR_FRI_AM_LARGEAUDIENCE`
  - `mi.carters.com/p/rp/264477f43986d8bb.png?mi_u=...`
  
  The `mi_u` value is a per-recipient identifier. This is expected behavior, not a defect.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Empty `<title></title>`:** confirmed in the source. This is minor, but it affects web-view and some accessibility contexts.
- **Four banner images served from `d3ptznaucccg76.cloudfront.net` are flagged as missing alt text** (`..._SERVICES_BANNER_01.png` through `_04_v3.png`, per the base64 filenames). They are not visible in the truncated source, so I'm relying on QA data. If they are the CTA banners, the links they wrap have no accessible name, which matches the empty link text noted above.
- **The `ho.gif` and `o.gif` pixel warnings look like false positives.** The `ho.gif` markup shown has `alt=""`, which is valid for a decorative image. The `mi.carters.com` pixels were not visible in the truncated source, so their `alt` could not be confirmed.
- **Positives, no action needed:**
  - Hidden preheader with padding characters.
  - `-webkit-text-size-adjust`.
  - Dark-mode and Apple data-detector overrides.
  - A mobile breakpoint at 660px.
- **Web font:** the Google Fonts `<link>` is wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`, which is correct. Fallback stacks could not be verified in the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unrendered merge tokens (`{{ }}`, `%%`, `[FNAME]`) appear in the visible source or the QA output.

## 5. Compliance
- **Plain-text part is 0 chars (QA FAIL).** This is a deliverability risk. It may also be an artifact of how the relay captured the message, so check the raw MIME for a `text/plain` part.
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found.** QA notes the relay may strip these headers, so this is **unconfirmed**. Verify against the original message headers. One-click unsubscribe support is a Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirement.
- **`Authentication-Results` is absent.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is **unknown**, not failed. The relay hides it.
- **Unsubscribe link and physical address:** the QA report says an unsubscribe link is always probed, but no result is shown for one. The footer is truncated, so presence of the unsubscribe link and postal address can't be confirmed from this data.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- **UTM parameters cannot be evaluated.** All destination URLs are hidden behind Attentive's encoded `/e/c/` redirects, and the probes did not resolve them. This is unverified, not a failure.
- **The only visible campaign identifier is `mi_ecmp=11_13_2026_CAR_FRI_AM_LARGEAUDIENCE` on the `mi.carters.com` pixels.** It shows a send-date and segment convention, but no UTM data.
- **The "coming 11.14" and "offer valid 11/…" links** point to date-bound offers. Their landing pages couldn't be checked because of the 403s.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Re-run link QA with a realistic browser UA or an allowlisted egress. Resolve the final redirect targets and check them for UTMs and for 200 status on carters.com.
2. Inspect the raw `.eml` for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, the `text/plain` part, and the `Authentication-Results` and `DKIM-Signature` headers. Only then confirm or dismiss the compliance and deliverability warnings.
3. If confirmed, add a populated plain-text alternative.
4. Add descriptive `alt` text to the four CloudFront banner images, especially the linked ones. Give the logo and brand links accessible names.
5. Populate `<title>` with the subject or campaign name.
6. Fix the QA tooling so `alt=""` decorative pixels aren't flagged, and so a uniform 403 across one redirect domain is reported as "probe blocked" rather than a broken link.
