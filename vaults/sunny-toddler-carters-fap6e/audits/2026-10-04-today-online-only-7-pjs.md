# ⏰ Today online only - $7+ PJs
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-04**
## Executive summary

- This is the strongest Carter's PJ send I've seen in this run. The hero is clear: a black-and-lime "Black Friday preview" block with "PJs FROM $7 / Today only online!" over a stack of holiday sleepwear. A live countdown timer sits above it (about 13 hours left), so "today only" is believable. It also fixes the problem in the 10-02 "Cuddly-soft PJs from $9" send, where the subject promised PJs and the hero showed something else. Here the subject, hero and product grid all say PJs.
- The email then loses focus. After the PJ grid it runs a "chill era" lifestyle block with no price, a red "UP TO 70% OFF* CLEARANCE" banner, and a store-opening block ("Now open near you!"). The clearance banner is big enough to compete with the $7 hook. The nine-tile product grid has no prices, names or reviews, so the $7 claim is never tied to a specific item. Another PJ send in the same week as 10-02 "Cuddly-soft PJs from $9" and the 10-02 lounge send makes the cadence feel repetitive. The new hook is a lower price ($7 vs. $9) plus urgency.
- Verdict: a solid, on-offer send that is better aligned than its recent neighbors. It would score higher with one offer, priced product tiles and a tighter second half.

## What's working

- The hero states the offer at a glance: "PJs FROM $7" in large yellow type on black, with "Today only online!" beneath.
- The countdown timer at the top gives credible, visible urgency that matches the "Today" in the subject.
- The subject, hero and grid all agree on PJs. The 10-02 mismatch is gone.
- The "Shop Now" button is clearly visible on the hero image.
- The photography is festive and seasonal (gingerbread, "Let it snow", plaid), and the render is clean. I can see no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- The 3x3 product grid has no prices, product names or "from $7" markers, so the headline offer is never tied to a product.
- The 70% off clearance banner and the "New in" lifestyle block split attention in the second half, and the lifestyle block has no price at all.
- The "Black Friday preview" label in the hero is not reflected in the subject. After a week of Black Friday blasts, a reader may not tell this from the others.
- The "$7+" in the subject and "FROM $7" in the hero are ambiguous. It is unclear whether $7 is a sale price or a starting price on a few items.
- The fine print is very dense and sits below the footer, with "Savings based on MSRP or original ticketed price." The hero asterisk-free "$7" looks cleaner than the offer likely is.
- There are no models in the render, so the persona gets no visual cue about fit or age.
- No reviews or social proof appear anywhere.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price or "from $7" tag on every product tile and link each tile to its product page. This is the biggest lift, because it turns the headline into a specific, clickable offer.
- 2. Cut the email to one offer. Drop or demote the 70% clearance banner and the "chill era" block, or move them below a store-info footer.
- 3. Make the CTA more specific, for example "Shop $7 PJs" instead of "Shop Now". Add a "Sizes NB–5T" or "Matching family sets" line for fit detail.
- 4. Tighten the subject so $7 isn't ambiguous and the Black Friday angle is explicit. The clock emoji and "online only" are fine, but "Today online only - $7+" reads clumsily.
- 5. Add a review or "bestseller" badge on one or two tiles for social proof.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Black Friday preview: $7 holiday PJs`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plaid, reindeer & gingerbread sets, NB–5T. Ends at midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Early Black Friday pricing on matching holiday PJs. Online only.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is structurally sound (table layout, `role="presentation"`, `lang="en"`, viewport meta, MSO conditionals). Its main verified gaps are a **0-character plain-text part**, **empty/non-descriptive alt text on content images**, an **empty `<title>`**, and a **likely typo in the hero image's alt text (`%7` for `$7`)**. The 403 link failures and the missing header checks are probably probe artifacts rather than defects.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **12 of 12 probed links returned 403.** They were all `https://carters.e.attentivemail.com/e/c/...` click-tracking redirects. These include the hero CTA, the "Carter's", "osh kosh bigosh", "little planet", "Otter Avenue" and "Skiphop" brand links, and 6 links with the text "Display images to show real-time content".
  - A uniform 403 on every ESP redirect, including brand-logo links, points to Attentive bot-blocking the QA prober, not a broken campaign.
  - It cannot be confirmed from this data that real recipients are affected. The final landing destinations were never resolved, so the 403s say nothing about the landing pages themselves.
- **46 links were not probed.** That includes footer and social links, so footer/unsubscribe link health is unverified beyond the "always probe unsubscribe" rule in the QA note.
- **Hero link text contains `%7`.** The text reads "Black Friday preview | PJs from %7 | Today online only! | Shop Now" and appears to be the hero image's alt text. The subject line says `$7+`, so `%7` is most likely a typo. If the alt text is shown (images off, screen readers), it misstates the offer.
- **Six "Display images to show real-time content" links.** This is fallback alt text on what look like live/dynamic content blocks, and it is wrapped in tracked links. With images blocked, recipients see this text as the link label, which is not a usable link description.

- **Empty `<title></title>`** in the `<head>`. This is minor, but some clients and "view in browser" pages use it.
