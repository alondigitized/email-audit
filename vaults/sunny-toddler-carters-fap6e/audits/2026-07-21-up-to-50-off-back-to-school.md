# Up to 50% off back to school! ✨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- This Carter's send opens with the strongest back-to-school creative of the week — "first-day fit? handled" is confident, direct, and a genuine step up from the clearance-flash grind. The flat-lay hero image earns its space. But the email erodes its own credibility almost immediately: the PJs section ships with visibly garbled copy ("comforts and speeds as they are 'More Fashionable'? & faces from fun features made for simple nights") — an AI-copy failure that nobody caught before send. That alone disqualifies this from the top tier.
- There's also a discount arithmetic problem: the subject promises "Up to 50% off" but the hero CTA says "Shop up to 40% off." The 50% figure appears nowhere in the body, so anyone who opened because of that number will scan the email confused. The clearance banner then jumps to 70% — three different discount claims with no single coherent story.
- Carter's is running the same three-module template it's used all week: hero + PJs filler + clearance banner. After July 17's PJs send and four days of clearance-flash emails, there's nothing new here except the BTS hero. The hero is strong enough to rescue open-to-click for the BTS CTA; the rest of the email is noise.

## What's working

- "first-day fit? handled" is punchy and seasonal — the best BTS headline Carter's has run this week
- The flat-lay hero (backpack, flannel, look sets) is visually clean and maps directly to the subject
- The red "UP TO 70% OFF* CLEARANCE" banner with inline category-browse buttons (Baby Boy / Toddler Girl / Girl etc.) is action-oriented and easy to navigate
- "Shop up to 40% off" is a clear verb-forward CTA — not "Discover" or "Learn more"
- July 21 is dead-center in the BTS shopping window — timing is right

## What's weak

- PJS section copy is visibly garbled — "comforts and speeds as they are 'More Fashionable'? & faces from fun features" shipped with zero proofreading
- Subject claims "Up to 50% off" but hero CTA says "Shop up to 40% off" — the number doesn't match anywhere in the body
- This is at least the second PJs push this week after July 17's "Comfy & gentle PJs from $10!" — feels like padding, not curation
- "WE THINK YOU'LL LOVE THESE" grid appears to show baby/infant onesies and rompers — off-brief for a back-to-school hero story
- No urgency mechanism anywhere — no deadline, no countdown, no "ends Sunday" — the offer floats without a reason to act now

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill or fix the PJs copy** before next send — garbled AI copy in a live email is a trust-damaging brand incident, not a minor miss
- 2. **Align the discount number** from subject to CTA: if the hook is 50% off, the button must say 50% off; if it's 40%, update the subject
- 3. **Drop the PJs module entirely** this send — Carter's already ran PJs four days ago; repeating it dilutes both sends and makes this feel templated
- 4. **Replace the "WE THINK YOU'LL LOVE THESE" grid** with BTS-specific SKUs (backpacks, uniforms, sneakers) to reinforce the hero story all the way down
- 5. **Add a credible deadline** — "BTS sale ends Sunday" gives the offer a reason to click today rather than bookmark-and-forget

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` element:**
```html

```
Some clients (notably older Outlook builds and certain preview panes) display the `<title>` value as the tab/window label. An empty title is a minor but avoidable gap.

**Google Fonts `<link>` will silently fail in most clients:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=League+Spartan:wght@400;500;600;700&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail (and any client that strips `<head>`) ignores this. The `<style>` block in the truncated source does not show an explicit `font-family` fallback stack — if League Spartan fails to load, the client falls back to the user-agent default rather than a controlled web-safe font. Confirm a `font-family` fallback is declared in the inline or `<style>` rules.

**Preheader padding uses Unicode fillers that some spam filters flag:**
The preheader uses `͏` (U+034F COMBINING GRAPHEME JOINER) in addition to the common soft-hyphen `­` technique. U+034F is a known spam signal in SpamAssassin rule `UNUSUAL_CHAR`. Mixed padding strategies increase filter risk marginally.
