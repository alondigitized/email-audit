# 100s of styles from $2.99 👀
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-29**
## Executive summary

- Carter's sends its third $2.99-floor clearance email in six days. The June 23 send launched the event; June 26 injected "100s of styles added to clearance" as the refresh; today's June 29 send fuses both angles into "100s of styles from $2.99" — plus a personalization play ("PICKS FOR YOU") and a Little Planet organic block. The structure is competent: the orange hero communicates the deal immediately, the product grid feels curated, and the sub-brand section adds content depth. But the creative brief is running on fumes. A subscriber who has seen $2.99 clearance since June 23 isn't receiving new information today — they're being reminded they didn't act.
- The "PICKS FOR YOU" nine-tile grid is the only genuine upgrade over the June 26 send. It turns a broadcast blast into something that feels selectively curated. The Little Planet organic section is a tonal non-sequitur — it positions premium organic product directly below a price-floor hero, and the two narratives actively compete with each other.
- Bottom line: a competent, on-brand clearance extension dragged down by cadence fatigue, zero visible deadline, and a sub-brand detour that belongs in a standalone send.

## What's working

- Orange hero hits the $2.99 price floor in the first visible frame — no scrolling, no ambiguity
- "PICKS FOR YOU" product grid is the right mechanic to differentiate this send from the identical-premise June 26 blast
- Visual hierarchy is clean: hero → personal picks → sub-brand → lifestyle, each section clearly delineated
- Render is bug-free — no broken images, no layout gaps, consistent typography throughout
- Subject carries both quantity signal ("100s") and a specific price floor ($2.99) in under 30 characters

## What's weak

- Subject is functionally identical to June 26's "100s of styles added to clearance!" — a subscriber who remembers that send gets zero new reason to open
- No explicit deadline anywhere in the email body — "CLEARANCE EVENT" implies urgency the copy never delivers on
- Little Planet organic section follows directly after a price-floor hero; the value-narrative whiplash undercuts both stories
- All CTAs read "Shop Now" — no category-specific copy to capture different buying intent across the three content blocks
- No loyalty or rewards hook visible; for a repeat-clearance email, member exclusivity is the clearest lever to re-engage lapsed clickers

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard expiration date to the hero.** "Ends July 1" or "Last 48 hours" converts a repeat-reminder into a genuine close. The event has been running since June 23 — subscribers who haven't bought need a deadline, not another restatement of the floor price.
- 2. **Differentiate the subject from June 26.** This send owns personalization — lead with that angle instead of recycling "100s of styles." The inbox already logged that subject line six days ago.
- 3. **Cut or defer the Little Planet section.** It's a brand-equity play grafted onto a deal email. Either commit to the clearance narrative top to bottom, or send Little Planet standalone next week when it can breathe.
- 4. **Make the PICKS FOR YOU CTAs category-specific** — "Shop Her Picks," "Shop His Picks" — instead of generic "Shop Now" across every section.
- 5. **Surface the rewards angle** in the hero sub-line ("Members, your clearance access ends July 1") to give loyalty subscribers a differentiated hook.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Picked for you — from $2.99, ends July 1`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your clearance picks expire soon 👀`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `9 items we think you'll actually use — all on clearance, final days`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The $2.99 floor closes this week. These were pulled for you.`
