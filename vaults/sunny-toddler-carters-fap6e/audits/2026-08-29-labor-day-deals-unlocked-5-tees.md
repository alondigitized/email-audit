# 🔓 Labor Day deals unlocked — $5 tees
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-29**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuinely new hero for Carter's after five straight days of recycled gradients — the pink-orange sunset banner is back but now paired with fresh product photography (a maroon-striped tee and a "hi pumpkin" graphic tee flat-lay) instead of the folded-laundry pile that's run unchanged since 8/26. The "Labor Day SALE" circle badge is a nice callback to prior sends without being a literal repeat, and the subject line's "$5 tees" hook is more concrete than anything Carter's has led with this week.
- Where it falls down is coherence between the subject and the actual hero: the subject promises "$5 tees" but the hero banner leads with "50% OFF ENTIRE SITE & STORE" — the $5 tees message doesn't show up until a secondary block ("TEES & BOTTOMS FROM $5") well below the fold. That's a bait-and-switch structure even if unintentional. The rest of the email is a solid, standard Carter's grid: "Just for you" personalized-looking product rows, a seasonal Halloween push, shop-by-age tiles, and a store locator block — all clean, no render bugs.
- Net: better creative variety than the last several sends, but the subject/hero mismatch is a real problem that will cost clicks from anyone who opened specifically for the $5 tee offer.

## What's working

- Genuinely new hero photography (striped tee + pumpkin graphic tee) breaks the multi-day laundry-pile repeat.
- "Labor Day SALE" circular badge is a nice visual anchor that ties to prior creative without duplicating it exactly.
- Clean visual hierarchy: hero → specific low-price call-out → personalized grid → seasonal (Halloween) push → shop-by-age → store locator — logical top-to-bottom flow.
- No render bugs — images load, text doesn't overlap, layout is intact.
- Distinct secondary sections (Halloween prep, shop by age, local store) give the email more to do than just repeat "50% off."

## What's weak

- Subject says "$5 tees" but the hero banner and CTA lead with "50% OFF ENTIRE SITE & STORE" — the $5 tee offer isn't visually reinforced until several scrolls down, undercutting the reason someone opened the email.
- Hero pairs two different offers (50% off site-wide + Labor Day Sale badge) with no single clear CTA hierarchy — "Shop Now" is small and easy to miss against the bold gradient and price claims.
- "TEES & BOTTOMS FROM $5" section has no product image at all next to the $5 claim — just text and a generic "Shop Now" button, which undersells the specificity of the subject line's hook.
- Fine print under the hero ("*Savings based on MSRP...") is present but small enough to functionally bury the offer terms, consistent with past Carter's sends' exclusion language.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the hero visually match the subject: put "$5 tees" (or the tee/bottoms pricing) as the dominant headline with a real product shot, not buried three sections down — right now the open promises one thing and the eye lands on another.
- 2. Give the $5 tees/bottoms block an actual product image (the graphic tee from the hero would work) instead of plain text on white — a specific product photo next to a specific price is what drives the click.
- 3. Simplify the hero to one primary offer + one CTA — either lead with 50% off sitewide or lead with $5 tees, not both competing for attention.
- 4. Enlarge or bold the "Shop Now" CTA button in the hero — currently it reads like secondary text against the gradient.
