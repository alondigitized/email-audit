# Extra 20% off $50+ online ➡️ Code: LAYERUP
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-07**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth LAYERUP send in four days (10-05, 10-06, 10-07 "Code: LAYERUP 🔓", and now this one). The code banner is the only strong offer. It sits in a gradient strip above the hero, where it's easy to miss. The hero below it sells "Must-have tees & bodysuits, Shop from $6", which is a different offer. The email is pulling in two directions.
- The render is clean, with no broken images. The "We think you'll love these" grid is the weak spot. It shows jeans, joggers, white bodysuits and one footed pajama, with no prices, names or reviews. The red "Up to 70% off clearance" block is a loud second offer that competes with the 20% code. The mid-page photos of kids are warm but carry no product information.
- Verdict: competent but fatigued. A subscriber who saw the 10-05 and 10-06 sends has no new reason to open or click.

## What's working

- The gradient banner states the offer plainly: "EXTRA 20% OFF* YOUR $50+ PURCHASE" with the code LAYERUP.
- The bodysuit and tee hero is bright, and the "Shop from $6" button has clear copy.
- The red clearance block is the strongest visual hierarchy on the page, with a big "70% OFF*" and new markdowns.
- The Baby/Toddler/Kid and Girl/Boy shortcuts make browsing by size quick.
- The footer perks row (curbside, cardholder perks, in-app points, Rewards) adds loyalty reassurance.

## What's weak

- The code banner is small and sits on a busy gradient, so it gets less weight than the hero below it.
- The hero promises "from $6" while the banner promises 20% off, and neither is tied to a specific product.
- The product grid has no prices, names or ratings, so there is nothing to compel a click.
- The 70% clearance block competes with the code offer and splits the call to action.
- The creative barely changed from the 10-05 and 10-06 sends. The disclaimer block is long, and the 20% offer has several exclusions.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the LAYERUP code in a bold, solid-color button-style banner, with the code easy to copy. Add an expiry time. Four days of the same code without a deadline makes the urgency feel fake.
- 2. Make the hero a single product with a price, such as "Bodysuits $6, plus 20% off with LAYERUP".
- 3. Add prices and star ratings to the grid, and cut it to 3–6 items that actually relate to the hero.
- 4. Demote or remove the clearance block, or move it below the grid so it doesn't compete.
- 5. Vary the creative day to day. Rotate in the PJ and Black Friday preview content from 10-04, which scored well.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a standard Attentive-wrapped, Mailchimp-style table layout. The only hard failure in the QA data is a 0-character plain-text part. All 12 probed links returned 403, but that is most likely the prober being blocked rather than a broken email. Header-level compliance and authentication could not be verified from this capture.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **All 12 probed material links failed with HTTP 403.** The URLs are Attentive click-tracking redirects (`https://carters.e.attentivemail.com/e/c/<opaque token>`). They cover Shop Baby, Shop Toddler, Shop Kid, Shop from $6, Carter's, osh kosh bigosh, little planet, Otter Avenue, Skiphop, the "Extra 20% Off* Your $50+ Purchase" banner, the hero image and the jeans product image.
  - A uniform 403 on every tracked redirect points to bot or anti-scraping filtering of the QA prober (a 4xx from the ESP's click domain) rather than 12 independently dead links.
  - Treat this as **unconfirmed breakage**. The data does not show that real recipients are blocked. Re-test with a browser-like user agent or a real click-through.
- **Coverage is partial.** 12 links were probed and 49 skipped, so nothing can be said about the footer or secondary links.
- **Redirect destinations are not exposed.** Because every link goes through an opaque wrapper, the landing URLs cannot be checked for correctness.
- **Tracking pixels are present.**
  - Attentive: `carters.attentivemail.com/ss/o/.../ho.gif`, a 1x1 pixel inside `.pixel-wrapper`.
  - Second pixel: `mi.carters.com/p/up/745a17bbb73445ad/o.gif`.
  - The `mi.carters.com` host (likely a third-party personalization/image service) also serves `mi.carters.com/p/rp/264477f43986d8bb.png`. That image is probably dynamically rendered content.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Empty `<title></title>`** in the `<head>`, confirmed in the source.
- **Missing alt text, reported by QA on 8 images:**
  - Content banners: the hero GIF (`MzczMjo6Q0FSXzA5MjkyNl9IRVJPXzEuZ2lm...`), the `264477f43986d8bb.png` dynamic image, and four service banners (`...U0VSVklDRVNfQkFOTkVSXzAx...` through `...BNNEBX3Yz...`).
  - The hero's click-through link has no accessible name beyond the filename (link text reads `[image: MzczMjo6...]`).
  - Impact: screen-reader users and image-blocked clients get no label for the hero link, and likely none for the four service banners.
- **False positives on the pixels.** The `ho.gif` source already has `alt=""`, which is correct for a decorative pixel. Flagging `ho.gif` and `mi.carters.com/.../o.gif` is noise.
- **Plain-text part.** `[FAIL] Text version is 0 chars`. A missing text/plain alternative hurts deliverability scoring and affects text-only clients.
- **Structure looks sound.** `lang="en"`, a viewport meta tag, an MSO conditional block (`o:PixelsPerInch`), and a 660px media query are all present. The Google Fonts `<link>` is wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`, so Outlook skips it.
- **Preheader** is hidden correctly (`display:none; max-height:0; opacity:0`), with zero-width and soft-hyphen padding.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved tokens such as `{{ }}`, `%%`, or `[[ ]]` appear in the visible portion of the source.
- The HTML was truncated, so the rest of the body could not be checked.
- The `mi.carters.com` pixels carry a per-recipient identifier and a campaign tag in the query string (`mi_u=1150794179`, `mi_ecmp=10_07_2026_CAR_WED_NOON_RETARGETING`). This is not a defect. It is expected for dynamic content.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found.** QA notes the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is **unverified, not confirmed missing**. For bulk sends, Gmail and Yahoo require both.
- **`Authentication-Results` was not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown, and the QA note attributes this to the relay.
- **Footer unsubscribe link and physical address:** the footer is beyond the truncation point, so neither could be verified. QA says an unsubscribe link was included in probing, and it is not among the 12 failures.
- The From domain is `marketing.carters.com`, a dedicated marketing subdomain. That is good practice.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **UTM parameters cannot be verified.** Every link is an opaque `attentivemail.com/e/c/...` redirect and every probe returned 403, so no final URL was observed.
- **The offer (`Extra 20% off $50+`, code `LAYERUP`) could not be matched against any landing page.**
- Tracking identifiers are visible only on the `mi.carters.com` pixels (`mi_ecmp`), not on destination links.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Re-probe the 12 links** with a browser-like user agent or a headless browser. Confirm the 403s come from bot filtering. If they reproduce, escalate to the ESP.
2. **Add a text/plain part.** The current 0-character text version is the only definite deliverability fail.
3. **Fill the `<title>`** (for example, the subject line).
4. **Add descriptive `alt` text** to the hero image and its link, the four service banners, and the `mi.carters.com` dynamic image.
5. **Verify headers on the original message**, not the relay copy: `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment.
6. **Capture the full HTML** so the footer, unsubscribe link, postal address and UTM parameters can be audited.
7. **QA tooling:** suppress alt-text warnings for 1x1 tracking pixels that already carry `alt=""`.
