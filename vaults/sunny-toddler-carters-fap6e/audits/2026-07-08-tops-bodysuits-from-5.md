# TOPS & BODYSUITS FROM $5 🙌
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- Carter's returns on day sixteen of consecutive daily sends with a subject-line pivot: "TOPS & BODYSUITS FROM $5" shifts the $2.99 clearance anchor up to $5 and broadens the category beyond the BOGO denim from yesterday. The creative is familiar — same two-panel shop-grid format, same "WE THINK YOU'LL LOVE THESE" product carousel, same Vintage Looks / clearance footer block that's been appearing since at least July 2. The email is clean and renders without visible bugs, but there's nothing here a subscriber of sixteen consecutive days hasn't already seen in some form.
- The strongest element is the stacked banner: an Extra 20% Off $50 coupon code lives right at the top, which is a meaningful added incentive on top of the $5 floor price. But the hero headline — "for every summer plan" — is lifestyle language that does essentially no selling; the offer and the CTA buttons below it are what pull the weight. The Vintage Looks section in the lower half has shown up multiple times this week (notably yesterday's BOGO send and July 6's vintage brand story email), so it reads as recycled filler rather than a fresh reason to click.
- Judgement: a competent, workhorse promo email that would score higher earlier in the campaign cycle. At send sixteen with nearly identical structural beats, the incremental "from $5" offer isn't enough creative differentiation to meaningfully re-engage a fatigued list.

## What's working

- **Dual offer stacking** — the Extra 20% Off $50 banner sits above the hero, so the subscriber gets a coupon code *and* a $5 floor price, which together make the value feel additive rather than flat.
- **Hero CTA pair is clear and immediately visible** — "Shop Tops" and "Shop Bodysuits" appear directly under the headline with no scrolling required; category split is a smart shortcut for a mixed-gender audience.
- **Product grid is tight and relevant** — the "We Think You'll Love These" carousel shows six to eight distinct SKUs (tops, bodysuits, sets) at a glance, reducing decision friction.
- **No visible render bugs** — images load, text doesn't overlap, layout holds at this viewport width.
- **Clearance 70% Off block with tiered pricing** — the red panel with Boys / Toddler Boy / Girls price tiers appears and makes the clearance depth legible.

## What's weak

- **Hero headline "for every summer plan" does no selling** — it's lifestyle filler; the offer lives below it and the subject line does more heavy lifting than the hero copy.
- **Vintage Looks section feels recycled** — this exact creative block (floral print, "Find the second. And the third." copy) appeared in the July 6 vintage brand story email and the July 7 BOGO send; repeating it a third time in three days dilutes its impact.
- **No urgency signal anywhere visible** — no end-date, no "today only," no countdown. After two weeks of clearance, subscribers have learned the event will still be running tomorrow.
- **Price anchor is softer than yesterday** — moving from $2.99 (July 5, July 3) up to $5 is technically accurate but makes the email feel like a step back on value, not forward.
- **Footer is dense and small** — the Rewards / legal / social block at the bottom compresses into a wall of tiny text; fine legally, but it makes the email feel longer than it needs to be.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline to the 20% code** — "Code expires Sunday" or a visible "2 days left" anywhere in the email would turn the 20% banner from a passive offer into a reason to act now. That single line would lift click rate more than any creative change.
- 2. **Replace the "for every summer plan" headline with the offer itself** — something like "Tops & Bodysuits from $5 — stock up before fall." The subject already earned the open; don't waste the hero on lifestyle copy.
- 3. **Retire the Vintage Looks block for at least three sends** — it appeared July 6, July 7, and again today. Swap in a real summer-end urgency message ("summer's almost over — grab what fits") to reset the frame.
- 4. **Introduce a product-detail signal** — show UPF rating, machine-wash-easy badge, or a specific top style by name. The grid is anonymous; one callout product with a detail would sharpen the click incentive.
- 5. **Tighten the clearance tier panel** — the Boys/Toddler/Girls price tier columns are a good idea but visually small in the red block; enlarging the price numbers would make the value pop without any copy changes.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Google Fonts loaded via `<link>` tag (will not render in most clients):**

```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=League+Spartan:wght@400;500;600;700&display=swap" rel="stylesheet" type="text/css">
```

Gmail strips `<link>` elements from the `<head>`. Outlook ignores external stylesheets entirely. `League Spartan` will only render in Apple Mail and some web-based clients. The CSS visible in the source does not show a fallback `font-family` stack — if body `font-family` declarations rely solely on `League Spartan` with no generic fallback, copy will degrade to client defaults (typically Times New Roman in Outlook). Needs a confirmed fallback stack, e.g. `font-family: 'League Spartan', Arial, sans-serif`.

**Issue — Empty `<title>` element:**


Some screen readers and browser-based email clients announce the document title when the email opens in a tab. An empty title reduces accessibility for AT users.
