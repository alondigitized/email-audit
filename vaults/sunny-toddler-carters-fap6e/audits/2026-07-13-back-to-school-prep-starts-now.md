# Back to school prep starts now! 📚
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- Carter's finally breaks out of its seventeen-day clearance loop with a genuine seasonal story — back to school, anchored at "SCHOOL FAVES FROM $5" in the hero. The creative pivot is welcome and the hero image (a fan of pants in pink, white, olive, and blue) is clean and seasonally on-point for a July send. The timing is right: back-to-school shopping peaks mid-July, and this is the first time Carter's has led with an aspirational "first day" message rather than a discount mechanic.
- The problem is the email can't commit to the story. Below the hero it fragments into three unrelated sections: a "WE THINK YOU'LL LOVE THESE!" product grid, a "LATEST NEW BABY ESSENTIALS" block, and then — predictably — a full-width red 70% OFF CLEARANCE banner with category tabs. That clearance block is the same DNA as the last two weeks of emails and it undercuts the back-to-school angle immediately. A first-day-of-school story and a "shop clearance" coda are fighting each other for attention.
- The email is competent and will convert, but it's a missed opportunity. Carter's had the seasonal hook to run a focused, single-story send. Instead it hedged with carry-over clearance inventory. The net result is a medium-impact email dressed up in premium positioning.

## What's working

- **Hero image lands well** — the fanned pants in school-palette colors is a clean, visual shorthand for "back to school" without needing much copy.
- **FROM $5 anchor** in the hero is an immediate attention-hold; price-floor entry points convert well for a value-conscious parent.
- **Timely seasonal pivot** — after a string of BOGO denim and clearance blasts, this subject line actually says something new. Compare to July 12's word-for-word repeat of the July 7 "Buy 1, Get 2!" — this is a meaningful upgrade.
- **25% OFF Baby Essentials CTA** is a clean, category-specific callout with a clear button.
- **Carter's near you / rewards section** at the bottom adds loyalty continuity without being intrusive.

## What's weak

- **70% OFF CLEARANCE block** mid-email contradicts the "first day favorites" premium story told in the hero — it signals "we're still clearing old stock" and dilutes the BTS positioning.
- **"WE THINK YOU'LL LOVE THESE!" grid** is generic; the six-thumbnail rec block has no anchor to the back-to-school theme and the images are too small to be persuasive.
- **CTA copy is weak** — "Shop Now" and "Shop All" are the lowest-common-denominator options. Every button in this email says a variation of "shop."
- **No deadline or urgency mechanics** — "starts now!" in the subject implies the beginning of something but gives no reason to act today vs. next week.
- **Subject contains no price or offer signal** — the emoji helps but a subscriber can't tell from the inbox whether this email has a deal or just editorial content.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut or push the 70% clearance block to a footer strip** — the hero's back-to-school story is genuinely good; don't let clearance cannibalize it. If clearance must stay, make it a one-liner ribbon at the very bottom, not a full-width red section.
- 2. **Add a price signal to the subject line** — "from $5" is sitting in the hero copy unused in the subject; moving it up would sharply raise open rates.
- 3. **Replace generic CTA buttons** with action-specific copy: "Shop First Day Outfits," "Build the Week's Wardrobe," "Shop Baby Essentials — 25% Off."
- 4. **Add a soft deadline** — even "Shop early for the best selection" or "Back-to-school styles go fast" creates enough urgency to pull forward-looking carts.
- 5. **Tighten the product rec grid** to back-to-school relevant items only (uniforms, basics, denim) — drop infant onesies from a school-prep send.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Back to school: FAVES from $5 📚 Shop early`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Pants, tops & basics to build the whole week's wardrobe.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock up on school-year staples before styles sell out.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some email clients (notably Samsung Mail, some webmail) display the `<title>` in the tab or preview. An empty title can surface as a blank tab label or cause minor accessibility issues for screen readers that announce it.

**Google Fonts will not render in most clients**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=League+Spartan:wght@400;500;600;700&display=swap" ...>
```
League Spartan via external `<link>` is blocked by Gmail (strips `<link>` tags), Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo Mail. The conditional comment correctly wraps this with `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <!--<![endif]-->` to exclude Outlook, but Gmail will still ignore it. The fallback font stack applied when this fails is not visible in the truncated source — confirm a readable system font is declared inline on text elements.

**Preheader padding technique — cosmetic, not a bug**
The invisible preheader filler uses `U+034F` (COMBINING GRAPHEME JOINER) characters followed by soft hyphens (`&shy;`). This is a standard technique to suppress inbox preview bleed-through from body content. No action needed.

**VML/MSO compat** — `xmlns:v`, `xmlns:o`, `PixelsPerInch`, `AllowPNG`, and `DontUseAdvancedTypographyReadingMail` are all present and correctly declared. Outlook rendering groundwork is sound.
