# Summer Staples Collection ✨
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- This is Carter's brand-integrity email, not a deal email. "Summer Staples" is a collection-merchandising play — the hero sells the summer seasonal narrative ("THE MVPS OF SUMMER") with lifestyle imagery of a baby in a floral outfit, then drops into three product category tiles, then bolts on the now-perpetual $2.99 clearance orange block as a footer afterthought. There's no single commanding offer in the hero.
- The subject line and creative are completely misaligned with the last six days of Carter's sends. June 21–26 were all promotional hammers — site-wide sales, B1G2, $2.99 clearance. Pivoting to a soft "collection" email with no headline discount will feel like a bait-and-switch to subscribers who've been trained to expect urgency. The clearance block at the bottom actually undercuts the aspirational tone the hero is trying to build.
- Net judgement: this is a filler send. It exists to maintain cadence without a new deal to announce. It won't hurt, but it won't move anyone who didn't already plan to buy.

## What's working

- **Hero image is charming and on-brand** — baby in a red floral print with natural outdoor setting; warm, summer-appropriate, emotionally resonant for the target parent.
- **"SHOP IT ALL" primary CTA is visible** — clear button below the hero, no ambiguity.
- **Three product category tiles ("Bodysuits from $5," "Shorts from $5," "Rompers from $4") anchor a price floor** — the category tiles do give a price signal even if the hero doesn't.
- **$2.99 clearance block is a consistent brand anchor** — subscribers who've been trained to look for it will find it here too.
- **Navigation bar (Girls / Boys / Baby / Clearance) is present** — functional, reduces friction for anyone who wants to browse beyond the CTA.

## What's weak

- **Subject "Summer Staples Collection ✨" is completely generic** — no price, no offer, no urgency; after six consecutive deal-heavy subjects this will look like background noise in an inbox.
- **Hero copy ("THE MVPS OF SUMMER / Built for long days of figuring things out…") is brand-tone filler** — a parent scanning their inbox in 2 seconds gets zero actionable information from this.
- **No headline offer in the hero** — the $2.99 clearance is buried below the fold; the top half of the email gives no reason to scroll.
- **"Show Now" CTA under the hero copy is weak** — "Show Now" appears to be a typo/copy error for "Shop Now"; at small size this is a trust-eroding visual bug.
- **Clearance block feels bolted-on and conflicts with the aspirational tone** — the email can't decide if it's a lifestyle/collection story or a clearance push; it tries to be both and lands as neither.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with a price anchor in the hero.** Even if this is a collection email, put "From $4" or "Starters from $2.99" inside the hero. The last six sends trained this subscriber list to expect a number — give them one or they'll skip.
- 2. **Fix "Show Now" → "Shop Now."** If that CTA copy is genuinely broken, it needs an emergency fix before next send.
- 3. **Make the clearance block the hero, not the footnote.** The $2.99 anchor is the strongest asset Carter's has right now; demoting it to a bottom block wastes the main attention zone.
- 4. **Reframe subject to anchor on value, not collection name.** "Summer Staples Collection" tells the reader nothing.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Summer must-haves from $2.99`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Rompers, shorts & bodysuits — all $5 or less`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The pieces that survive everything — shop bodysuits, shorts & rompers.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus: clearance floor just dropped to $2.99. Stock up now.`
- 5. **Drop one of the three category tiles and replace with a single hero product** — a specific item with a specific price converts better than three "from $X" category buckets.
