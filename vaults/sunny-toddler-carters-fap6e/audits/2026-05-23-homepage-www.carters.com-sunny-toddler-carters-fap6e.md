# Homepage snapshot · www.carters.com · 2026-05-23
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- Oh, this is MY store. Carter's is leading hard with a Memorial Day 50% off sitewide sale right on the hero, which is exactly the kind of event I set a calendar reminder for. The brand is reading as a dependable, deal-forward kids' clothing retailer right now — not aspirational, just useful, which is honestly what I need at 11pm when I'm refilling the pajama drawer.

## What's working

- **The 50% off sitewide headline** — I don't have to hunt for what's on sale. Entire site and store, big and bold, answers my first question instantly.
- **The Memorial Day frame** — I already knew this sale was coming; seeing it confirmed means I'm in the right place at the right time.
- **The product flat-lay below the hero** — That pink bunny-ear dress and the floral top + denim shorts combo are instantly shoppable. I'm already mentally sizing up.
- **Top announcement banner** — "Shop season-low prices! 50% off* entire site & store" doubles the offer reinforcement before I even hit the hero. Smart redundancy on mobile.
- **Clean mobile layout** — No modal popups, no overlay asking for my email before I've seen a single product. I appreciate being left alone.

## What's weak

- **"Shop Now" CTA is too generic** — I'm a Carter's loyalist. "Shop Baby Deals" or "Shop Girls 2T–4T" would tap me directly into what I actually need; "Shop Now" could go anywhere.
- **No free-shipping callout above the fold** — My typical order hits the threshold easily, but I still want to see it before I start filling a cart. The absence makes me feel like I'm missing something.
- **No pajama or sleepwear signal** — Pajamas are my highest-frequency Carter's purchase and they're invisible above the fold. A tiny "Sleepwear included" note would've sealed this for me.
- **The asterisk on the offer** — Two asterisks and zero explanation of the exclusions. I've been burned before when blanket pajamas were excluded. This creates friction before I even tap through.
- **No loyalty hook anywhere in view** — I have Carter's Rewards points. A "Your rewards: $X" or even a "Members get early access" nudge would make this feel personal instead of generic.
- **Flat lay, no lifestyle context** — The clothing shot is cute but there's no mom-and-kid scenario that makes me feel seen. It's product catalog energy, not "omg that's my Saturday morning" energy.

## Recommendations

- **Replace "Shop Now" with "Shop 50% Off Baby & Toddler"** — one extra word, immediate category clarity, and it speaks directly to the person most likely to convert on a Carter's Memorial Day sale.
- **Add a free-shipping threshold strip** below the hero ("Free shipping on $35+") — it's probably already the policy; surfacing it removes a cart-abandonment hesitation without any real cost.
- **Add an asterisk callout or "Exclusions apply — see details" micro-link** directly beneath the 50% off headline — being upfront about exclusions builds trust and reduces bounce when customers hit the fine print at checkout.
- **Inject a loyalty balance nudge** near the account icon or below the CTA ("You have 200 points — worth $10") — it's a returning-customer hook that would take me from browsing to committed in one glance.
