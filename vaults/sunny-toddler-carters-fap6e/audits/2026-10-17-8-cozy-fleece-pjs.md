# $8+ Cozy fleece PJs ✨
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-17**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth Black Friday preview send this week, and it pivots on a hard-priced hero: "FLEECE PJS FROM $8" over a row of holiday-print footies. That is a much stronger concrete offer than the vague "up to 60% off" banners in the Oct 13, 15 and 16 sends. The black starfield banner, the lime-green stripe and the Shop Now button read in a second. The fleece-PJ hero is nearly identical to the Oct 15 "Fleece PJs starting at $8 💙" send, so a regular subscriber will see it as a recycled creative.
- The body weakens it. The "Just For You" grid is nine product tiles with no prices, no names and no visible links, so it's a dead zone. The same black-banner template is reused for "$10 OFF* OUTERWEAR", and a red "UP TO 70% OFF* CLEARANCE" block follows it. That makes three competing offers on one page. The hero wins, but the email loses focus and tops out as a middling-to-good send.

## What's working

- The hero states a concrete price: "FLEECE PJS FROM $8", with "Wrap them in cozy PJs for cooler days" as support.
- The Shop Now button sits over the PJ photo and is clearly visible.
- The holiday-print footies (gingerbread, plaid, Santa) are seasonal and photograph well.
- The Black Friday preview framing and the starfield banner match the brand's recent sends, so it's instantly recognizable.
- The footer has loyalty and utility icons (Rewards, curbside, app) plus a store-finder CTA.

## What's weak

- The hero is almost the same as the Oct 15 "Fleece PJs starting at $8" send, which makes it a repeat in a week full of Black Friday previews.
- The "Just For You" grid is nine unlabeled, unpriced denim, jogger and onesie tiles with no CTA. It's a long scroll that adds nothing.
- The outerwear banner and the red 70% clearance block pull attention away from the PJ offer. Three offers compete.
- The category buttons (Baby Girl, Toddler Girl, Girl, Baby Boy, Toddler Boy, Boy) are small and sit near the bottom of a very long email.
- The disclaimers are dense and the asterisks everywhere ("60% off*", "70% off*", "$10 off*") weaken the sense of an honest offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the "Just For You" grid, or make it a real PJ product row with names, prices and the $8 callout. That keeps the whole email on its one strong offer.
- 2. Drop either the outerwear banner or the clearance block, and keep one secondary offer.
- 3. Use fresh PJ creative or a distinct angle, such as matching family sets or a gift angle, instead of the Oct 15 look. Add a deadline, such as "through Sunday".
- 4. Move the size-category buttons directly under the hero so shoppers can jump to their child's size.
- 5. Add a social proof element, such as star ratings on the PJs.
