# Better bedtimes start here!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- This is Carter's seventh email in seven days, and the creative is starting to show the strain. "Better bedtimes start here!" is the most narratively coherent send in the recent run — the subject line, the "THE EVERY-NIGHT ESSENTIALS" hero, and the six-tile PJ grid all actually line up, which was not true of the June 24 "$10 PJs? A dream!" send or the June 27 "Summer Staples" brand-integrity play. When the email stays in its lane, it works.
- But it doesn't stay in its lane. The "SHOP IT ALL" section immediately below the sleepwear hero drops in bodysuits, shorts, and rompers — daywear items that have nothing to do with bedtime. The cognitive dissonance is real: you came for PJs and suddenly you're looking at shorts. Then comes the $2.99 orange clearance block, which has now appeared in every Carter's email since June 23. After seven consecutive appearances with no stated deadline, it isn't driving urgency — it's becoming furniture.
- The geo-personalized store locator (Glendale, CA) is a welcome signal that some segmentation is happening. But this email is ultimately a competent recycle of June 24's sleepwear frame with a softer subject line and stale supporting blocks. The narrative focus it almost achieves in the top third gets diluted by the two sections below it.

## What's working

- **Subject-to-hero alignment is the best in the recent run** — "Better bedtimes start here!" flows naturally into "THE EVERY-NIGHT ESSENTIALS" and a full PJ grid, no bait-and-switch.
- **Six-tile sleepwear grid is dense and scannable** — varied styles, good color range, fills the hero without feeling padded.
- **Geo-personalized store locator** (Glendale, CA address + "Get Directions" CTA) shows real segmentation effort that most promo emails skip.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds throughout.

## What's weak

- **"SHOP IT ALL" daywear section (bodysuits, shorts, rompers) breaks the sleep story** — dropping unrelated daywear directly under a sleepwear hero creates narrative whiplash.
- **The $2.99 clearance block has now appeared in every email for seven days** — with no deadline visible, it reads as permanent, not urgent. It's lost its function.
- **No price in the PJ hero** — pricing only surfaces in the "SHOP IT ALL" subgrid row; the dominant hero section has no "from $X" hook to anchor value.
- **Subject line "Better bedtimes start here!" is soft** — aspirational but non-specific; offers no price, no urgency, no product name to earn the open.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Price the PJ hero directly** — add "From $8" or "PJs from $X" as a subhead under "THE EVERY-NIGHT ESSENTIALS." The only prices visible are in the secondary daywear row. Moving a price anchor to the hero is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Kill the daywear "SHOP IT ALL" section or separate it clearly** — either cut it or put a divider with a distinct "While you're here" framing so it doesn't bleed into the sleep story.
- 3. **Deadline or retire the clearance block** — after seven appearances, it either needs a credible end date ("Event ends July 2") or it should come out of this email entirely. An undated, indefinite clearance block trains subscribers to ignore it.
- 4. **Sharpen the subject line** — price or product specificity will lift opens from subscribers who've seen seven emails this week:
- **Subject Alt A:** `PJs from $8 — better sleep starts tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your kid's sleep uniform, from $8`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Soft every-night essentials + clearance from $2.99`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the PJs they'll actually want to wear`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Not critical, but some clients (Samsung Mail, some webmail) display the title in the tab or thread header. Should be `<title>Better bedtimes start here!</title>` to match subject.

**Invalid `alt` attribute on MI pixel**
```html
<img src="https://mi.carters.com/...o.gif?..." alt>
```
`alt` without `=""` is invalid HTML4/5. Should be `alt=""`. Most parsers handle it, but strict validators and some security filters flag it.

**Google Fonts (`League Spartan`) blocked in major clients**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=League+Spartan:...">
```
Correctly gated behind `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`so Outlook skips it. However, Gmail (all variants) strips `<link>` tags from `<head>` — League Spartan will not render for Gmail recipients. Fallback chain in the CSS (`Arial, Helvetica Neue, Helvetica, sans-serif`) appears appropriate; confirm brand accepts this fallback at scale since Gmail accounts for a large share of retail email opens.

**Preheader padding technique is correct** — uses Unicode zero-width non-joiners (`͏`) and soft hyphens (`­`) to suppress body text bleed into preview. No issue.

**MSO/VML conditionals and `PixelsPerInch: 96` setting** — present and correct for Outlook rendering fidelity.
