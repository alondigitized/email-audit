# $10 PJs? A dream! 💤
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- Carter's opens with a nine-tile PJ grid headlined "THE EVERY NIGHT ESSENTIALS," then drops into a Little Planet organic section, and finally hits the now-familiar $2.99 clearance orange block from the June 23 send. Three separate offers in one scroll means none of them land cleanly. The subject promises "$10 PJs" specifically — but the body never shows a $10 price tag anywhere visible. That's a subject-to-body mismatch: you open expecting a specific deal and get a catalog page instead.
- The Little Planet mid-section is the strangest inclusion. It's organic baby basics, a different brand sub-line, sandwiched between a PJ essentials grid and a clearance floor event. It reads like a second email stapled in.
- The clearance block is the same creative as June 23's "$2.99 CLEARANCE EVENT." Running identical creative two days in a row at this send cadence (this is the sixth Carter's email since June 17) risks training subscribers to tune out. The $10 PJ angle was fresh and specific — the email should have owned it.

## What's working

- **Subject-line specificity**: "$10 PJs" is a hard number attached to a recognizable product — exactly the format that wins inbox opens.
- **PJ grid is clean**: Nine styles, good variety of silhouettes and colorways, renders without breaks — solid product merchandising.
- **Little Planet baby lifestyle image**: The baby in the striped tee and bucket hat is charming and on-brand; the organic positioning differentiates from the clearance noise.
- **Loyalty section present**: The rewards card callout at the bottom reinforces member value, even if it's buried.

## What's weak

- **$10 price never appears in the body**: The subject sells a price point; the email never delivers it. Subscribers who opened for the $10 deal can't find it.
- **Three competing offers fragment attention**: Essentials grid, Little Planet organic, $2.99 clearance — the eye doesn't know where to go.
- **$2.99 clearance is identical to June 23**: Two days later, same orange block, same event. Offer fatigue is real at this send volume.
- **All CTAs say "Shop Now"**: Generic across three sections; no button tells you *what* you're shopping.
- **Visual hierarchy inverted**: The hero is a no-price product grid; the most specific offer (clearance) is buried below the fold.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put "$10" in the hero body copy** — the subject promise must be fulfilled above the fold. Add a hero callout: "PJs from $10 — shop the essentials." This is the single highest-impact fix.
- 2. **Cut to one offer per email** — pick the $10 PJ story or the clearance event, not both plus organic. The Little Planet section belongs in its own send.
- 3. **Retire or escalate the $2.99 clearance block** — running the same creative from June 23 again signals an event that's lost urgency. Either add a hard end date or drop it from this send.
- 4. **Make CTA buttons specific** — "Shop $10 PJs →" and "Shop $2.99 Clearance →" instead of three identical "Shop Now" labels.
- 5. **Add a deadline** — "$10 PJs — this week only" would lift both open and click scores immediately.
- **Subject Alt A:** `PJs starting at $10 — tonight only 💤`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$10 PJs for every night 🌙 — shop now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Nine cozy styles. All starting at $10. Stock up before they're gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the essentials — PJs, rompers, and more from $10.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag:** `<title></title>` — Screen readers and some inbox clients announce the title. Should be set to the subject line or a descriptive value.

**`#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }` CSS rule** overrides link styling globally in Gmail's webmail view. This suppresses underlines on all anchors, which reduces accessibility for users relying on visual link indicators. Not a blocker, but worth noting against WCAG 1.4.1.

**Google Fonts loading is correctly guarded:** `<!--[if !mso]><!--><link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=League+Spartan..."><!--<![endif]-->` — Outlook will skip this and fall back to the stack (`Arial, Helvetica Neue, Helvetica, sans-serif`) as intended.

**MSO/VML conditionals** and `<o:OfficeDocumentSettings>` are structurally correct. `PixelsPerInch=96` and `AllowPNG` are present.

**Preheader padding technique:** Uses a mix of Unicode `͏` (U+034F combining grapheme joiner) and `­` (U+00AD soft hyphen) as filler — two different invisible characters. This is common but soft hyphens can render as visible hyphens in some obscure clients. Functionally fine for major clients.

No issues found with responsive breakpoint logic (`max-width:660px`).
