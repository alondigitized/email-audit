# Buy 1, get 2 free tops & shorts 🔥
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- Carter's leads June 20th with their strongest offer format of the month: Buy 1, Get 2 Free on tops and shorts. After the brand-warmth "Summer MVPs" send on the 19th, swinging back to hard-offer territory makes commercial sense — but this is send #3 in 72 hours, and cadence math is starting to work against them.
- The hero is clean and on-brief: tropical palm art, bold BOGO headline, a flatlay grid of tops and shorts that communicates variety without overwhelming. Creative hierarchy is tight — your eye lands on the offer immediately. What dilutes the impact is what follows: a "20% OFF* Select Faves" secondary callout with brand restrictions buried in line copy, then a "THERE'S STILL TIME TO RECYCLE" brand content interrupt, then the $3.99 clearance section. Three value tiers plus a mid-email mission moment is too many acts for one email.
- Solid execution on the hero. Poor discipline on what comes after it.

## What's working

- BOGO headline is immediately legible and high-impact — "BUY 1, GET 2 FREE" is effectively ~67% off, and the creative doesn't bury it
- Flatlay product grid communicates category breadth (multiple tops + shorts, multiple colorways) cleanly
- Subject line is the most specific Carter's has been in weeks: product category plus offer type in 33 characters
- Summer seasonal framing (palm trees, gradient sky) matches the tops-and-shorts category tightly
- Store-finder and Rewards sections are present, covering cross-channel subscribers

## What's weak

- Three stacked offers (BOGO, 20% off Select Faves, $3.99 clearance) compete below the fold and dilute all three
- "THERE'S STILL TIME TO RECYCLE" mid-email breaks purchase momentum — right cause, wrong placement
- Zero urgency signal anywhere in the body: the 🔥 in the subject promises heat the email never delivers
- "20% OFF* Select Faves" fine print (Little Planet, OshKosh Avenue, select Skip Hop — online only) is cramped into a single line and undercuts confidence in the secondary offer
- Third email in 72 hours following June 18's clearance push and June 19's Summer MVPs send; open fatigue is real

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut to one hero offer.** The BOGO is strong enough to carry the send alone. Push clearance and the 20%-off-select-faves tier to a separate email tomorrow or collapse them below a fold with a "More deals" label. Right now three competing messages dilute all three.
- 2. **Add a visible deadline to the hero.** "Ends Sunday" or "This weekend only" would cost one line of copy and add meaningful urgency the subject line's 🔥 currently promises but the body never delivers.
- 3. **Relocate the recycling module to the footer.** A good initiative doesn't need mid-email placement — it earns more trust as a recurring footer fixture that doesn't intercept the buying path.
- 4. **Rewrite the secondary-offer line as a button.** "20% OFF* Select Faves — Little Planet, OshKosh Avenue & select Skip Hop — online only" is too many restrictions in too little space. Replace with: `[Shop Little Planet & OshKosh B'Gosh — 20% Off]`.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open pixel wrapped in `display:none`**
```html
<div class="pixel-wrapper" style="display:none;...max-height:0px;max-width:0px;opacity:0;overflow:hidden;">

```
The tracking pixel is nested inside a hidden `div`. Several email clients (notably older Gmail webmail and some mobile clients) suppress images inside `display:none` containers, potentially suppressing the open event even when images are enabled. The pixel should be a standalone `<img>` outside any hidden wrapper, or placed immediately after `<body>`.

**Destination link domains** — not assessable from truncated source. Confirm all CTA hrefs route through the Attentive click-tracking domain and that no raw `carters.com` untracked links are present.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some clients (Outlook desktop, Apple Mail) surface the `<title>` value as a tooltip or in notification previews. An empty title is a missed signal and can trigger spam heuristics on certain filters.

**Google Fonts blocked in MSO**
```html
<!--[if !mso]><!-->
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=League+Spartan:...">
<!--<![endif]-->
```
Correct conditional guard — Outlook will fall back to system fonts. Acceptable, but verify the `font-family` stack in body text includes a safe serif/sans-serif fallback explicitly.

**Preheader padding technique**
```html
͏ ͏ ͏ ... ­ ­ ­ ...
```
Uses U+034F (Combining Grapheme Joiner) and U+00AD (Soft Hyphen) to pad preview text. This is a widely used technique but U+034F can render as visible characters in some older Android clients. Test in Gmail Android and Samsung Mail.

**Alt text on content images** — cannot confirm from truncated HTML. All `<img>` tags in product grid rows must carry non-empty `alt` attributes for WCAG 2.1 AA and screen reader compatibility.
