# ✨ Built for the best — Tops & bottoms from $6 ⭐
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-10**
## Executive summary

- This is one of the calmer, more product-forward sends in Carter's recent run — no skeleton-costume Halloween photo hiding under a fake seasonal banner, no folded-in Labor Day contradiction. It's a straightforward "new favorites" merchandising email: hero flat-lay of joggers, a product grid, category tiles, and a clearance banner. But calm doesn't mean sharp — the subject promises "Tops & bottoms from $6" and the hero only shows bottoms (three pairs of joggers/leggings), so the headline offer isn't even the picture leading the email.
- The middle of the email is a wall of undifferentiated product tiles with no price callouts per item, which drags visual hierarchy down hard. The "UP TO 70% OFF* CLEARANCE" banner near the bottom is the actual strongest offer in the email but it's buried after three separate merchandising blocks, competing with — not amplifying — the $6 headline.
- Net: solid, un-embarrassing creative, but it undersells its own hook and the layout does the offer no favors. This lands in the middle of the pack — better than the Halloween-disguise sends, worse than the "PJs from $9" hero that actually matched image to offer.

## What's working

- No render bugs — clean grid alignment, no overlapping text, no broken images.
- Sub-brand logo strip (OshKosh, Little Planet, Otter, Skip Hop) at top signals portfolio breadth for a Carter's-family subscriber.
- Clear single primary CTA button ("Shop from $6") directly under the hero headline.
- Category tiles at the bottom of the grid ("Bodysitts from $7," "Dresses from $12," "Sets from $12") give concrete entry-point pricing that the hero image lacks.
- Store locator module with actual address and "Get Directions" CTA is a nice locally-relevant touch if this is a local subscriber.

## What's weak

- Subject says "Tops & bottoms," hero image shows only bottoms (three pairs of pants/leggings) — mismatch between copy and hero visual.
- The 9-tile "we think you'll love these" grid has zero prices or product names visible, so there's nothing to anchor a click other than the photo itself.
- The $6 price from the subject line never reappears anywhere in the body — no tile, no banner confirms what's actually $6.
- "UP TO 70% OFF CLEARANCE" — far and away the biggest number in the email — is stacked after two other merchandising sections, so it's the fourth thing a scrolling reader hits, not the first.
- Pink/green/navy leggings in the hero skew toddler-girl-and-boy generic; no styling context (no kid wearing them) to help a parent picture fit or use case.

## Recommendations

- 1. Match hero to headline: either swap the subject to reference bottoms specifically, or replace the hero photo with a mixed tops+bottoms flat-lay so the $6 price has a visual home.
- 2. Add a price sticker directly on or under the hero pants photo (e.g., "Joggers from $6") — right now the $6 claim is orphaned text with no product tie.
- 3. Move the "UP TO 70% OFF CLEARANCE" banner up, directly under the hero CTA — it's the strongest offer in the email and currently loses to three lower-value merchandising blocks for scroll position.
- 4. Add visible price tags to the 9-tile product grid — even a simple "$8" overlay per tile would give the grid the specificity it's missing.
- 5. Consider swapping one hero pant for a styled-on-kid shot to make the "built for the best" durability claim concrete instead of abstract.
