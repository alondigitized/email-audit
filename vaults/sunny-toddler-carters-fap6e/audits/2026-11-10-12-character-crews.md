# $12 Character crews 🧸
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-10**
## Executive summary

- This is Day 7 of Carter's 12 Days of Deals, and the hero offer is clear. A bright green panel shows "$12 character crews" over four holiday sweatshirts (Mickey and friends, Grinch, and a red patch crew). It carries a "today only!" badge and a Shop Now button. The mechanic is the same as the earlier days (Day 3 jeans on Nov 6, Day 4 character tees on Nov 7, and so on), so it is competent and formulaic.
- The email is overloaded, though. Above the hero sit a $5 members reward banner and a banner row of sister brands. Below it come a price-match strip, a 50-60% off site-and-store block, a teaser for upcoming days, a PJ feature, a Shop It All trio, a 70% off clearance block, and a store-locator block. The "50-60% off" clearance message that carried the Nov 8 and Nov 9 sends is still here as a side block. That dilutes the daily-deal urgency.
- Judgement: a solid, on-brand daily send with a strong hero. It would be sharper if it were half as long and the hero were the only offer.

## What's working

- The hero is one clear offer. "$12 character crews" is large, with a "today only!" badge and a visible Shop Now button.
- The product photo is festive and seasonal, with Grinch and Mickey crews that suit the holiday timing.
- The "Day 7 · 12 days of deals · $12 & UNDER" tag keeps the series consistent and rewards people who follow along.
- The "coming 11.11 / 11.12 / 11.13" teaser ($8 PureSoft, $12 shoes, $10 fleece pjs) gives a reason to keep opening.
- The price-match line ("within 14 days") reduces purchase risk.

## What's weak

- The $5 members reward banner sits above the hero and pushes the "today only" offer lower. It also competes with the daily deal.
- The "Ends Wednesday! 50-60% off* site & store" block repeats the generic clearance message from Nov 5-9, so the daily-deal focus gets mixed.
- There are too many modules (PJs, Shop It All, 70% off clearance, store block). This is a long scroll with several competing CTAs.
- CTAs are generic: "Shop Now", "Shop Today's Deal", "Shop Denim >". The hero says nothing about sizing or availability.
- The "$12" hero doesn't say what the crews usually cost, so there's no savings anchor. The dense fine print at the bottom also reads like a legal wall.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to the hero, the next-day teaser, and one fallback clearance block. Remove the PJ, Shop It All and store modules on daily-deal days.
- 2. Move the member $5 reward below the hero, or merge it as a single line, so the offer lands first.
- 3. Add a savings anchor to the hero, such as "Reg. $24 → $12" and a size-range note ("sizes 4-14").
- 4. Use specific CTA copy like "Shop $12 crews" instead of "Shop Now".
- 5. Drop the generic 50-60% block on days with a strong daily deal, or alternate it so it isn't present on every send.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary

It also reports 403s on all 12 probed links, but those are Attentive click-tracking redirects, so the 403s probably come from the probe being blocked rather than from dead links.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **All 12 probed links returned 403.** Every one is a `https://carters.e.attentivemail.com/e/c/...` redirect. Examples are "Shop Denim >", "Shop Winter >", "Shop Holiday >", "Shop PJs", "ENDS WEDNESDAY! | 50-60% OFF*", the "MEMBERS GET A $5 REWARD*" block, and the header logos "carter's", "osh kosh bigosh", "little planet" and "Otter Avenue".
- **This is probably a bot block, not a dead link.** Uniform 403s on a tracking-domain redirect point to the ESP rejecting the automated client. Without a 3xx or a final destination, I can't confirm that real recipients reach a live page.
- **48 links were not probed** (per the QA INFO line), so coverage is partial.
- **Duplicate link text with different targets.** "Shop Denim >" and "Shop Winter >" each appear twice with different Attentive URLs, for example `kWym810L...` vs `zetk2jK5...`. These are probably per-placement tracking, and I can't tell whether they point to different destinations.
- **Tracking pixels.**

## 5. Compliance
- **Plain-text part is missing (QA FAIL, 0 chars).** If this reflects the actual message and not an AgentMail capture artifact, the email is HTML-only. That is a deliverability and accessibility weakness.
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found.** QA says the relay may strip them. This is unverified, not a confirmed absence. It matters because Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require one-click unsubscribe.
- **`Authentication-Results` was not captured.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown from this data. QA attributes this to the relay.
- **Unsubscribe link and physical address.** QA says an unsubscribe link is always probed, and no failure is reported for it. The truncated source doesn't show the footer, so the postal address could not be checked.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- **Not verifiable.** Every CTA is wrapped in an Attentive redirect that returned 403 to the probe, so the final URLs, UTM parameters and landing pages were never observed.
- The only visible attribution in the source is `mi_ecmp=11_10_2026_CAR_TUE_AM_ENGAGERS` on the `mi.carters.com` pixel.
- Whether `utm_source`, `utm_medium` and `utm_campaign` survive on the destination is unconfirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Re-probe the tracking links with a browser-like User-Agent and cookies, or follow redirects with a real client. Record the final URL and status to confirm the 403s are a bot block and to check the UTMs.
2. Add descriptive `alt` text to the cloudfront banner images and `264477f43986d8bb.png`. Use `alt=""` only for purely decorative images.
3. Confirm with Carter's or the ESP that a multipart plain-text part is sent. Check the raw MIME source, not only the AgentMail capture.
4. Check the raw headers for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and `Authentication-Results` (SPF, DKIM, DMARC alignment), since the relay may have stripped them.
5. Populate `<title>` with the subject or campaign name.
6. In the QA tooling, don't flag `alt=""` on 1×1 pixels and don't count Attentive-redirect 403s as broken links without a retry. Both are likely false positives that inflate the issue count (16% pass rate).
