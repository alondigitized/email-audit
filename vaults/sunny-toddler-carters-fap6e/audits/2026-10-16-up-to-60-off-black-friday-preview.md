# Up to 60% off Black Friday preview ✔️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a solid but repetitive Carter's send. The black starfield "Black Friday preview / UP TO 60% OFF*" hero over a lime flat-lay reads in one second, and the "Shop Now" button is clear. It's the same creative as the 10/13 send, "(Early) Black Friday: Up to 60% off 🖤", which I scored 8. A subscriber who got that one, then "Fleece PJs starting at $8 💙" on 10/15, sees the same banner a third time in four days.
- The weak part is below the hero. The unlabeled "Just for You" block is a 3x3 grid of jeans, joggers and bodysuits with no prices, names or links I can tell apart. It looks like a placeholder for personalization, and nothing explains why these items were picked. The two product features (5-Star Sleep & Play from $7, Bestselling Bodysuits from $2) and the red "UP TO 70% OFF* CLEARANCE" block are better, but they sit well down the scroll.
- Verdict: competent and on-brand, but the sale has no deadline and the creative is recycled. It will perform as a reminder, not as a driver.

## What's working

- The hero is instantly legible. "UP TO 60% OFF*" in lime on black has the highest contrast in the email, and "Sets, tops, bottoms & more—from baby to big kid" sets the scope.
- The "Shop Now" button is clean and visible at the bottom of the hero.
- The two feature blocks carry concrete entry prices: "Shop from $7 >" and "Shop from $2 Per Piece >". Those are the most compelling details in the email.
- The red 70% clearance block has four clear segment buttons (Baby Girl, Toddler Girl, Girl, Baby Boy, Toddler Boy, Boy). Those make it easy to shop by size and gender.
- The render is clean. I see no broken images, overlaps or layout breaks. The loyalty and curbside icon row and the "Find a Store" block are tidy.

## What's weak

- Nothing is time-bound. "Preview" and "Black Friday" imply a date, but no end date or countdown is shown. Compare the 10/12 "TODAY ONLY" send, which had a live timer.
- The "Just for You" grid has no labels, prices or category context. Nine near-identical navy and black product shots read as filler.
- The hero is a flat-lay of mixed clothing, so the click has no single product to land on. This is the same hero as 10/13.
- The 60% and 70% numbers sit in different places with different asterisks. The footer exclusions are dense, and "Select styles" qualifies the 60% claim.
- The "Shop Now" CTA is generic.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a deadline. A single line under the hero, such as "Preview pricing ends Sunday," gives the offer a credible reason to act now.
- 2. Rotate the hero. Reuse of the 10/13 creative makes this look automated. Lead with the sleep and play or bodysuit feature, which has a $7 entry price, or swap in a new seasonal image.
- 3. Label the "Just for You" grid with product names and prices, or cut it down to a 3-item row with a clear heading.
- 4. Change the CTA to a specific one, like "Shop 60% off sets & sleepers."
- 5. Move the $7 and $2 features directly under the hero and put the grid below them.
