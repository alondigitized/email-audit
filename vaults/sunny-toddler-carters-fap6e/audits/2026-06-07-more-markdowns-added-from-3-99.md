# More markdowns added from $3.99 🔥
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- Carter's ran this same $3.99 clearance floor two days ago — "CLEARANCE FROM $3.99" on June 5 scored a 9/10 — and today's send is structurally nearly identical: same teal hero palette, same price anchor, same category tabs. The sole hook differentiating this send is "more markdowns added," which is a legitimate reason to return to a subscriber's inbox, but only if the email actually shows what's new. It doesn't. The product grid presents generic clearance items with no indication of which styles just dropped in price, leaving the headline hook unsubstantiated in the body.
- The second problem is editorial bloat. Below the clearance section, the email pivots to "Latest New Baby Essentials" and a three-card "Top Tips for Parents" blog module. A subscriber who opened on the $3.99 promise is in bargain-hunting mode. Laundry tips are noise. The email earns the open, then loses the thread.
- Net judgment: a competent but diminishing sequel to a stronger send. The $3.99 floor still anchors the hero cleanly, but the "more markdowns" hook needs receipts — call out what's newly reduced, or the subject line reads as a soft retargeting nudge, not a genuine new drop.

## What's working

- $3.99 price floor is in the hero immediately — no hunting for the offer
- Six gender/age category tabs (Baby Girl → Boy) let the subscriber self-segment in one tap
- Teal hero is visually consistent with Carter's established clearance identity — brand recognition is instant
- Carter's Rewards badge maintains the loyalty layer without a hard sell
- Subject line under 50 characters; emoji use is restrained compared to the 🚨❤️ stacking on June 5

## What's weak

- "More markdowns added" is the headline hook but no newly-reduced items are surfaced in the body — the claim goes unproven
- Product grid thumbnails show no individual prices — the $3.99 floor is stated but per-item value is invisible
- Baby Essentials lifestyle section and blog tips are off-funnel for a clearance send — clearance subscribers aren't in discovery mode
- Second near-identical clearance send in 48 hours; cadence fatigue is real for an active subscriber
- No urgency mechanism — no deadline, no "limited sizes," no sell-through language

## Recommendations

- 1. **Prove the "more markdowns" claim** — dedicate a "Just Added" module showing 4–6 specific newly-reduced items with before/after prices. The hook is good; the body needs to pay it off.
- 2. **Add a deadline** — "Ends Sunday" or "While supplies last" converts the implied urgency of 🔥 into something actionable.
- 3. **Put prices on the product grid** — showing $3.99, $5.99, $7.99 directly on thumbnail cards is the single highest-lift change for clearance click-through.
- 4. **Cut the baby essentials and blog sections** — run a separate, lower-frequency editorial send; don't blend it with bottom-funnel clearance.
- 5. **Raise the entry offer** — "from $3.99" is the same floor as June 5. Consider showcasing a deeper category ("toddler tops from $3.99") to give the second send a fresh angle.
