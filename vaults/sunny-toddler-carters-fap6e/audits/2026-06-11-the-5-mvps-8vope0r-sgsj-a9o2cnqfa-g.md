# The $5 MVPs 📣
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- The biggest story with this send is a subject-body mismatch that undermines everything else. "The $5 MVPs 📣" promises a curated roster of $5 hero items — but the email opens on a 60% off + Extra 20% (code EXTRA20) hero, with no $5 price floor surfaced as a lead narrative. A subscriber who clicked expecting $5 picks gets deposited into the standard Carter's clearance machine. That's a trust-eroding gap, and after a week of near-daily sends, subscribers are already primed to be skeptical.
- The production itself is competent and sometimes better than that. The teal hero is clear, the "HANDPICKED FOR YOU" personalized grid shows genuine effort at relevance, and the SKIP*HOP milestone-play section earns its real estate. But the 60% + Extra 20% code feels recycled — Carter's already put "Code: EXTRA20" directly in the June 9 subject line, which scored 9/10. Running the same code a day later, now buried under a $5 MVP promise that doesn't resolve, is a step backward.
- This email could be a 9 in execution but the subject kills its credibility. Until the $5 offer is either the hero or removed from the subject, this send is at odds with itself.

## What's working

- Teal hero block with a double-stacked discount (60% off + extra 20%) is visually dominant and impossible to miss
- "HANDPICKED FOR YOU" personalized product grid adds perceived relevance above a generic browse grid
- SKIP*HOP integration earns its section with milestone development framing, not a cold product push
- 70% OFF clearance organized by category tab (Baby Girl, Toddler Girl, Girl, Toddler Boy, Boy) makes navigation intuitive
- REWARDS badge in the Carter's near-you section keeps loyalty quietly visible

## What's weak

- Subject "The $5 MVPs" promises $5 items as the headline story; the email body leads with 60%+20% off and never surfaces a $5 hero
- EXTRA20 code is buried in fine print under the hero — Carter's literally put this same code in the June 9 subject line; using it again as a footnote is a downgrade
- Five-plus competing CTAs (hero Shop Now, product grid links, SKIP*HOP Shop Now, five clearance category tabs, store locator) dilute click intent and leave no single primary action
- No visible deadline or scarcity signal on the 70% clearance — the category that most rewards urgency gets the least of it
- "Shop Now" used verbatim across every CTA — zero verb+noun specificity throughout

## Recommendations

- 1. **Resolve the subject-body mismatch first.** Either promote $5 items to the hero block (and label them explicitly), or drop "$5 MVPs" from the subject entirely and lead with the clearance story. Right now it reads like a bait-and-switch regardless of intent.
- 2. **Collapse CTAs to one primary action.** The 70% off clearance category grid is the strongest commercial unit — give it a single "Shop [Category]" CTA and demote the rest.
- 3. **Add a credible deadline.** "Ends Sunday" or "Tonight only" in the hero turns a compelling discount into an actual decision point.
- 4. **Retire EXTRA20 from this send.** Subscribers who opened June 9 already have this code. Running it again two days later signals desperation, not generosity.
- 5. **Write CTAs with specificity.** "Shop $5 Picks" or "Browse 70% Off Baby Girl" beats "Shop Now" every time.
