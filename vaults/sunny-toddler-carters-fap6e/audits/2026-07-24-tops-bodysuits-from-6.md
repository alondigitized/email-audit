# TOPS & BODYSUITS FROM $6 🙌
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- Carter's pivots hard from the back-to-school editorial run of the past week to a straight price-floor clearance push. "Tops & Bodysuits From $6" is a functional subject — specific, scannable, honest — but the email behind it tries to carry three distinct offers at once: summer tops/bodysuits at price-point, an OshKosh Otter Avenue denim block, and a 70% off clearance module in red at the bottom. None of them get enough room to breathe, and the visual experience ends up feeling more like a catalog dump than a campaign.
- The hero photography is warm and seasonally fine ("for every summer plan"), and the dual CTA buttons — "Tops from $6" / "Bodysuits from $7" — are among the clearest CTAs Carter's has run this week. But after yesterday's "Straight A's" send, which was the creative high point of the cycle, this email feels like a step backward: the brief editorial ambition is gone, replaced by a three-way split that would make any of these offers land harder as a standalone.
- The 70% clearance module at the bottom is the most aggressive offer here and probably the one most likely to drive a click — but it's buried below the fold. That's backwards.

## What's working

- "Tops from $6" and "Bodysuits from $7" as CTA button copy are specific, price-anchored, and unambiguous — best button copy this brand has run in several sends.
- Hero photography is warm and summery; the flat product stack reads cleanly at mobile widths.
- The product grid ("WE THINK YOU'LL LOVE THESE...") gives enough visual variety to prompt category browsing.
- The 70% clearance red block is high-contrast and immediately readable — strong visual signal even if it's in the wrong position.
- OshKosh Otter Avenue module offers cross-brand coverage for dual-label shoppers without feeling bolted on.

## What's weak

- Three separate offers (price-floor tops/bodysuits, OshKosh, 70% clearance) compete in a single scroll with no hierarchy; the email has no single through-line.
- The 70% clearance — the most compelling hook — is buried below the product grid instead of being the lead.
- Subject line is fully ALL CAPS, a mild spam-filter and trust signal, especially coming off a week of mixed-case creative.
- No deadline, urgency cue, or "ends tonight" language anywhere in the email body.
- No personalization, no loyalty/rewards mention above the footer — after a week of higher-effort sends, this reads as a mass blast.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one offer and lead with it.** 70% clearance is the biggest hook; if that's the intent, make it the hero and push tops/bodysuits as the secondary module below. The current structure buries the lede.
- 2. **Add a deadline.** Even "through the weekend" or "ends Sunday" in the hero copy would lift urgency materially — right now there is none.
- 3. **Fix the subject capitalization.** Mixed-case with a number reads more credibly and avoids the ALL CAPS spam flag.
- 4. **Collapse to two modules max.** OshKosh Otter Avenue and the clearance block can share a footer strip; the main body should be tops/bodysuits or clearance, not both plus a third lane.
- 5. **Surface rewards in the hero or CTA area**, not just the footer — member exclusives or early-access framing would lift click intent for loyalty subscribers.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Tops from $6, bodysuits from $7 — summer must-haves`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 70% off clearance + tops from $6 🙌`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Mix-and-match summer staples, prices starting at $6.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Ends Sunday: clearance up to 70% off + new summer arrivals from $6.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` is blank. Screen readers (NVDA, VoiceOver) and some email clients surface this as the document label. Should be populated — e.g., `Carter's | Tops & Bodysuits from $6`.

**Google Fonts via external `<link>`**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=League+Spartan:wght@400;500;600;700&display=swap" ...>
```
Wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <![endif]-->` so Outlook skips it — correct. However, Apple Mail Privacy Protection and some corporate proxies block third-party font requests. No `font-family` fallback stack is visible in the truncated CSS; if `League Spartan` fails to load, the rendered fallback is browser default (typically Times or serif). Confirm a system-font fallback is set in inline styles on text elements.

**`role="presentation"` on all tables** — correctly applied throughout. No issue.

**`lang="en"` on `<html>`** — present. No issue.

**Tracking pixel `alt=""`** — correct for a decorative/invisible element.

**Preheader padding technique** — uses Unicode `U+034F` (Combining Grapheme Joiner) and soft-hyphen (`­`) characters for filler. This is a valid and widely used spacer pattern. No issue.
