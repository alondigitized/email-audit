# 100s of styles added to clearance!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- Carter's is back at the clearance well for the fourth time in four days. The June 23 send launched the $2.99 floor; this June 26 email's job is to extend the event by announcing new inventory. That's a legitimate brief, and the creative executes it cleanly: pink gradient hero, large price anchor, visible CTA, and category entry-points below ("Bodysuits from $5 >", "Shorts from $5 >", "Rompers from $8 >"). The email is technically sound.
- The problem isn't the execution — it's the cadence. A subscriber who has opened every Carter's email this week has now seen: June 20 Buy 1 Get 2, June 21 site-wide 50% off, June 22 Buy 1 Get 2 again, June 23 $2.99 clearance launch, June 24 PJs + $2.99 clearance again, June 25 B1G2 with Picks For You, and now this. "100s of styles added" is the freshest hook Carter's has used this week, but the reader's inbox fatigue is very real.
- The "PICKS FOR YOU" grid — called out negatively in the June 25 audit — returns here again, showing six items without prices. Below a clearance hero, that's a trap: a subscriber clicks a recommended item and finds it's regular price. The category tiles handle this better by anchoring a from-price to each. The email is competent; the campaign is tired.

## What's working

- **$2.99 price anchor in the hero** is the single strongest signal in the email — immediate value communication, front and center.
- **"100s of styles added"** is genuinely fresh copy relative to the June 23 send — it implies new inventory rather than just re-promoting the same event.
- **Category tiles with from-prices** (bodysuits $5, shorts $5, rompers $8) give shoppers a clear, navigable hierarchy below the hero.
- **Clean render throughout** — no broken images, no layout breakage, consistent brand palette.
- **Subject line at 34 characters** is mobile-friendly and unambiguous about what the email contains.

## What's weak

- **"PICKS FOR YOU" tiles have no prices** — directly below a $2.99 clearance hero, a grid of unmarked items creates confusion about what's actually on clearance.
- **No urgency mechanism in the body** — "while supplies last" lives only in the fine-print exclusions block; nothing in the visual hierarchy communicates scarcity or deadline.
- **Rewards badge is decorative** — appears in the footer with no member-exclusive benefit, early access callout, or bonus-points offer tied to the clearance event.
- **"Shop Now" is the weakest possible CTA** — generic verb, no noun, gives the reader no destination signal.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Price or label every PICKS FOR YOU tile** — add a clearance price, a "CLEARANCE" badge, or cut the section entirely. Showing unmarked items below a price-anchored hero is the fastest way to create a bad post-click experience.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline or scarcity signal in the body** — "New styles just added — shop before they're gone" or a banner with a sale-end date converts the implied urgency of "clearance" into an actual prompt to act.
- 3. **Give Rewards members a reason to click that non-members don't have** — "Carter's Rewards members earn 2× points on clearance items" takes one line and differentiates the list.
- 4. **Tighten the hero to one headline** — "FROM $2.99 CLEARANCE EVENT / 100s of styles added" is two competing lines. Collapse to: "100s of New Styles From $2.99."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Just added: clearance styles from $2.99`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New clearance drops — grab them from $2.99`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh bodysuits, shorts & rompers — new styles hit clearance today`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New inventory just hit the $2.99 clearance event — shop before it's gone`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed `alt` attribute on Marigold open pixel:**
```html
<img src="https://mi.carters.com/..." width="1" height="1" alt>
```
`alt` with no value (`alt>`) is invalid HTML. Should be `alt=""`. Some validators and strict parsers will reject this; the Attentive pixel correctly uses `alt=""`.

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
VoiceOver and NVDA read the `<title>` when the email is opened in a browser fallback. Should contain the subject line or a meaningful description.

**Google Fonts external load:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=League+Spartan...">
```
Wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <!--<![endif]-->` to exclude Outlook — correct. However, Gmail (webmail) strips `<link>` tags from `<head>` entirely, so League Spartan will silently fail there. The declared fallback stack (`Arial, Helvetica Neue, Helvetica, sans-serif`) in inline styles will catch this, but confirm it's applied on all text blocks, not just some.

**Responsive breakpoint:** `@media (max-width:660px)` — appropriate for a 640px desktop container.

**`role="presentation"` on all layout tables:** Present throughout — correct practice.
