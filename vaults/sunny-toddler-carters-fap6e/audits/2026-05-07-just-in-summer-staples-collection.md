# JUST IN! Summer Staples Collection ✨
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-07**
## Executive summary

- A seasonally appropriate children's clothing email from Carter's that does several things well — recognizable brand, timely summer theme, and a real discount — but undermines itself with competing messages. The subject line promises "just in" new arrivals; the email body devotes its most visually dominant module to clearance. That tension dilutes both stories and weakens the click case for either.

## What's working

- **Strong seasonal hook.** The hero photo — a toddler in a summery outdoor setting with "Summer Staples" text — lands immediately and is on-trend for May.
- **"Ready for a Vacay?" / Pool Edit module.** A themed editorial segment like this earns attention. It has a name, a clear CTA, and curated imagery rather than a generic product dump.
- **Clearance math is credible.** 70% off is a number that stops scrolling. Category buttons (Baby Girl, Toddler Girl, Girl, Baby Boy, Toddler Boy, Boy) reduce friction for shoppers with specific kids to buy for.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no layout collapse, no junk text visible. The email displays as intended.

## What's weak

- **Subject/body mismatch.** "JUST IN!" implies new arrivals, but the most visually dominant module is clearance. These are opposite propositions and they compete without resolution.
- **Divided visual hierarchy.** Three distinct stories — summer staples, pool edit, and clearance — get roughly equal weight. Nothing clearly wins the eye and tells the reader what to do first.
- **No urgency.** Neither the new arrivals nor the clearance offer has a visible deadline, count, or "while supplies last" call-out that would motivate a same-session click.
- **No social proof.** Zero reviews, ratings, or "bestseller" badges visible anywhere in the email.
- **Fine print asterisk.** The "70% OFF*" asterisk implies exclusions but fine print isn't legible at this render size. That reduces trust on the offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one story per send.** Either "Just In Summer Staples" or "Clearance up to 70% off" — not both. If clearance is the bigger commercial priority, lead with it and change the subject line.
- 2. **Add a deadline to clearance.** Even a loose "ends Sunday" or "limited sizes" creates the urgency currently absent.
- 3. **Bring the 70% off line higher.** If you keep both modules, move the clearance offer above the Pool Edit so the most conversion-driving message appears earlier on mobile.
- 4. **Replace "Shop Now" with something specific.** "Shop Summer Staples" or "See New Arrivals" converts better than the generic CTA currently on the hero.
- 5. **Surface one hero product with a visible price.** A single item + price + "was/now" comparison in the new-arrivals section would anchor the value immediately.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Google Fonts load will silently fail in Gmail:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=League+Spartan:wght@400;500;600;700&display=swap" ...>
```
The tag is wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <!--<![endif]-->` which correctly excludes Outlook. However, Gmail (web and Android) strips `<link>` tags for external stylesheets unconditionally — League Spartan will not render there. The visible CSS does not declare a fallback `font-family` stack in the truncated portion. If content paragraphs or buttons reference League Spartan without a system fallback, Gmail subscribers will receive the browser default (typically serif). **Needs verification of inline font-family fallbacks on all text elements.**

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Minor: some clients (Outlook on macOS, some webmail) surface the `<title>` value as the window/tab label. Not a hard failure but a hygiene gap.
