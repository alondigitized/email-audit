# Lowest prices OF THE SEASON ❤️🩵
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-24**
## Executive summary

- Carter's hits day ten of the Memorial Day push with a "Lowest Prices of the Season" subject that re-skins the 50% off sitewide offer that has now been running for nine straight sends. The hero is identical to multiple prior emails — "memorial day sale 50% OFF ENTIRE SITE & STORE" over the same lifestyle baby-grid mosaic — but the body adds a tighter "Lowest Prices of the Season" $4–$5 product row, a swim/UPF callout, and the $2.99 clearance block in a punchy red. That's three offers stacked vertically, which gives the email more shoppable surface than yesterday's $5 tops send.
- The render is clean — clear hierarchy, real product imagery, member-pricing footer, store locator block. But by sound, the subject is recycled language: "lowest prices OF THE SEASON" was literally the section header in the May 19 consolidation email. Subscribers who've been through this cadence are getting the same hero, the same 50%, the same $2.99 clearance for the tenth time.
- Verdict: structurally a strong email, but creatively exhausted. The "lowest prices" claim loses meaning when every send for two weeks has made the same promise.

## What's working

- Hero offer is unambiguous — 50% off entire site & store, Shop Now button visible above the fold.
- Three distinct shoppable blocks: $4/$5 lowest-prices grid, swim/UPF row, $2.99 clearance — gives multiple entry points.
- $2.99 CLEARANCE in white-on-red is the strongest visual moment in the email and gendered shop links (Baby Girl/Toddler Girl/Girl/Baby Boy/Toddler Boy/Boy) make the click obvious.
- Real product photography throughout — no lorem ipsum, no broken images, prices ($4.50, $5.00, $2.99) read clean.
- Store locator + Rewards block at the bottom does omnichannel work without crowding the offer.

## What's weak

- This is the tenth consecutive Memorial Day send and at least the fifth time this exact 50% hero has appeared — fatigue is real.
- "Lowest prices OF THE SEASON" was literally a section header in the May 19 email; using it as a subject now feels recycled, not escalated.
- Subject + hero are redundant — both say 50% off sitewide. Wastes the inbox real estate on the same claim twice.
- Swim/UPF block is buried in the middle with no price anchor visible in the hero band — the $7 swim moment from May 18/21 was sharper.
- "More markdowns added!" is asserted but the email doesn't tell me what's new vs. what I've already seen.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with what's actually new.** Ten days into the sale, "lowest prices" is noise. Lead with the $4 lowest-price tee row or the swim UPF restock — something a returning subscriber hasn't already declined four times.
- 2. **Cut the redundancy between subject and hero.** If the hero is the 50% off banner, let the subject sell the $2.99 clearance moment or the $4 tee — give me a reason to open beyond "still 50% off."
- 3. **Date-anchor the urgency.** Memorial Day weekend is the natural deadline — say "ends Monday" in the subject or hero. Right now nothing tells me this ends.
- 4. **Tighten the stack.** Three offer blocks works; four would be a wall. Hold the swim row for its own send and let $2.99 clearance breathe.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New markdowns from $4 — Memorial Day ends Monday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$2.99 clearance just got bigger ❤️🩵`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Final weekend of the sale — shop the new $4 tees, $5 shorts, and $2.99 clearance.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `More styles added to $2.99 clearance — plus 50% off everything else.`
