# ❣️ Best-selling bodysuit - $5 only ❣️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-17**
## Executive summary

- This is a well-organized, multi-offer send that actually delivers what the subject promises — a genuine hero shot of five folded/hung bodysuits (stripe, star-print, solid navy, cream) with a clean "from $5" starburst callout up top. Compared to the 2026-09-13 and 2026-09-12 sends that opened strong and then muddied the message with unrelated grids, this one keeps a coherent through-line: bodysuits → jeans/pants → sleep & play → clearance. It's basically the same "$X and under" catalog structure Carter's runs every week (see 2026-09-14 bodysuit send), just re-skinned with a denim-and-neutrals product grid instead of last week's.
- The email is long — five distinct sections plus a price-match banner and a store-locator banner before the footer — which dilutes the bodysuit focus fast. By the third scroll you're looking at jeans and clearance, not the "best-selling bodysuit" the subject sold. It's competent, on-brand, bug-free execution, but it's cadence fatigue territory: this is essentially the fourth near-identical "$X off basics" structure this week (09-12 PJs, 09-13 tops/bottoms, 09-14 bodysuits, now 09-17 bodysuits again), and a subscriber would notice the repeat.

## What's working

- Hero image genuinely matches the subject line — real bodysuits, no bait-and-switch.
- Orange "from $5" starburst is a strong, scannable focal point in the top-left corner.
- "Shop from $5" CTA button is clear, high-contrast, and sits right under the value prop copy.
- Secondary sections (jeans/pants grid, sleep & play, clearance) are visually distinct with their own headers, so the email doesn't feel like a wall of product tiles.
- Price-match banner and "we price match" reassurance add a credible trust signal not common in prior sends.

## What's weak

- The email drifts off-message almost immediately: "Lowest Prices of the Season" jeans/pants grid has nothing to do with bodysuits, same pattern that hurt the 09-12 PJ send.
- No urgency or deadline anywhere in the body — "clearance" and "lowest prices of the season" are evergreen phrasing, not time-bound.
- Five distinct offers (bodysuits, jeans, sleep & play, 70% clearance, price match) in one email dilutes which CTA a reader should actually click.
- Brand history shows this exact "$X and under" formula running nearly every day this week — a subscriber opening five near-identical Carter's emails in a row will start ignoring them.
- The "up to 70% off" clearance block uses a size/gender picker as its only CTA (no direct "shop clearance" button), which is more friction than the earlier straightforward buttons.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the email in half.** Lead with bodysuits + one supporting category max (sleep & play, since it's thematically closer to "baby basics"), and drop the jeans/pants grid entirely — it's the section that breaks the promise made in the subject.
- 2. **Add real urgency.** Nothing in this email is time-bound; even a soft "this week only" on the $5 bodysuit price would lift both open and click likelihood given the rubric penalizes evergreen language.
- 3. **Give clearance its own send.** "Up to 70% off clearance" is a strong enough offer to be a standalone email rather than a buried mid-scroll block competing with the bodysuit hero.
- 4. **Vary the creative structure week over week.** Given four "$X and under" catalog sends in five days, swap in a different format (e.g., a styling/outfit story like the 09-15 "GLOW" campaign push) to avoid fatigue.
- 5. **Make the clearance CTA a single button**, not six category buttons, to reduce decision friction.
