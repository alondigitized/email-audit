# For our very best 💙
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-21**
## Executive summary

- This is the same blue-gradient "REWARDS WEEK — UP TO 50% OFF + 4X POINTS" hero that ran yesterday (2026-08-20, "Rewards week is here ✨") with zero visual changes — same sparkle motif, same layout, same "Shop Now" outline button. The only new framing is the subject line, "For our very best 💙," which reads like a VIP/loyalty-tier re-send rather than a fresh creative, and nothing in the body actually acknowledges that framing — there's no "because you're a top member" copy or name personalization anywhere in the render.
- Below the repeated hero, the email actually earns some credit: a clean 9-product grid, a genuinely well-shot OshKosh B'gosh denim macro photo for "BUY 1, GET 2 FREE," and a bold red 70% off clearance block with gender/age shopping buttons (Baby Girl, Toddler Boy, etc.) that give a clear next click. So the email isn't badly built — it's just a lazy repackage of yesterday's hero glued onto a decent lower-funnel section.
- Net judgement: functionally fine, strategically weak. The "very best" subject over-promises a personalized VIP send and under-delivers with a recycled hero — that mismatch will read as bait to anyone who noticed yesterday's nearly identical email.

## What's working

- The OshKosh denim close-up photo for "Buy 1, Get 2 Free" is genuinely well-composed and distinct from the brand's usual flat product shots.
- Product grid (9 items) is clean, evenly spaced, and gender/category-diverse (jeans, rompers, bodysuits).
- The red 70% off clearance block has clear, tappable category buttons (Baby Girl/Boy, Toddler Girl/Boy, Girl/Boy) — a concrete next action.
- No render bugs — images load, text is legible, no overlap.

## What's weak

- The hero is a verbatim repeat of the 2026-08-20 "Rewards week is here" send — same gradient, same sparkle, same copy stack, same button. A subscriber who opened both emails on consecutive days will notice.
- Subject line "For our very best 💙" implies segmentation/exclusivity that the body never delivers — no member name, no tier callout, no "because you're a Rewards VIP" message.
- Three offers stacked with three different visual languages (blue hero, denim macro, red clearance block) with no hierarchy signaling which one is the actual headline — it reads like three separate emails taped together.
- "BUY 1, GET 2 FREE*" has no visible fine print in the body (asterisk resolves only in the tiny footer disclaimer), which risks feeling like a bait offer once the exclusions are read.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Stop re-sending the identical hero two days running.** If Rewards Week is still live, at minimum swap the hero photography or copy angle so the email doesn't look like a duplicate to anyone with both in their inbox.
- 2. **Make the subject line true.** Either build an actual VIP-segment hero (member name, points balance, "you've earned $X in rewards") to match "For our very best," or drop the VIP framing and use a subject that matches what's actually in the email (the denim BOGO or the 70% clearance, which are the freshest elements here).
- 3. **Pick one hero offer and subordinate the rest.** Right now Rewards Week, BOGO denim, and 70% clearance compete equally — lead with whichever has the best margin/inventory need and downsize the others into a single scroll section.
- 4. **Surface the Buy 1 Get 2 Free exclusions near the offer**, not buried in the footer, so the discount doesn't feel like it's hiding a catch.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your Rewards are stacking up 💙 4X points inside`
- **Subject Alt B:** `VIP access: Buy 1, Get 2 Free denim + 70% off clearance`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus new markdowns just added to clearance.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `OshKosh denim: get 2 free when you buy 1.`
