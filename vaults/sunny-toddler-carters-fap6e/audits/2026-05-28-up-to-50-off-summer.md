# UP TO 50% OFF SUMMER ☀️😎
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- One day after Carter's executed its best send of the Memorial Day stretch — the May 27 "baby must-haves" editorial that scored 9/10 by abandoning discounting entirely — this email snaps straight back to blunt discount mode. The goodwill from that pivot is spent in a single send.
- The render packs four distinct stories into one scroll: a summer 50%-off sale, a Skip+Hop co-brand section on activity centers, a $20 bonus card gift-card promo, and a 70%-off clearance event. None of them dominates. The subject shouts "UP TO 50% OFF SUMMER" in all caps, the hero green section delivers a clean seasonal opener, and then the email immediately breaks focus with a completely unrelated baby gear collab and a competing clearance headline that undercuts the summer freshness framing.
- The bones are fine — Carter's is relevant, the summer seasonal pivot is timely, and the product photography is bright and clean. But this send reads like three emails stapled together, and the ALL CAPS subject wastes the equity that careful May pacing had quietly been building. **The email is competent but unfocused.**

## What's working

- The green hero is seasonally fresh — it cleanly signals the Memorial Day palette is done and summer is here.
- The 50% OFF headline reads in under a second; "Shop Now" sits directly beneath it with no friction.
- Gender × age category buttons (Baby Girl / Toddler Girl / Girl / Baby Boy / Toddler Boy / Boy) make self-segmentation effortless — a strong click-flow design.
- The $20 Bonus Card offer is a smart lifecycle play that rewards gift-card buyers; it shouldn't be buried mid-email, but the mechanic itself is good.
- Skip+Hop co-brand earns its real estate — the milestone-play story is editorial and breaks the discount monotony, echoing what made May 27 work.

## What's weak

- The May 27 editorial reset is burned in 24 hours; a subscriber who appreciated the break from discounting now gets right back into "UP TO X% OFF" shouting.
- Three competing hero offers (50% summer, 70% clearance, $20 bonus card) create no single answer to "what is this email about?"
- ALL CAPS in the subject line is a mild spam signal and cheapens the tone Carter's had been trending toward in the final MDW sends.
- No deadline on either the summer sale or the clearance event — the urgency mechanics that drove May 26's "1 MORE DAY" success are completely absent.
- The 70% clearance headline at the bottom actively undermines the summer-freshness story at the top; a parent reads "up to 70% off clearance" and mentally prices down everything above it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one story.** Either summer 50% off (new, aspirational) or 70% clearance (value, endcap) — not both in the same send. The bonus card becomes a footer upsell, not a section.
- 2. **Add a deadline.** "Through Sunday" or "this week only" is enough. The MDW run proved hard deadlines lift opens and clicks; open-ended promos train wait-and-see behavior.
- 3. **Anchor to a specific product.** The May 23 $5-tees send worked because it had a concrete hero item. "Summer Ready Tops, Shorts & More" is forgettable. A single $9.99 swim set or $12.99 rash guard in the hero is shareable and scannable.
- 4. **Drop the ALL CAPS subject.** Title case or sentence case reads higher quality in a crowded inbox.
- 5. **Move Skip+Hop earlier** or spin it into its own send — it's a genuinely different purchase decision (durable goods vs. apparel) and gets lost sandwiched between two discount sections.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Summer tops & shorts from $9 — up to 50% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer wardrobe is ready (up to 50% off)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New arrivals for beach days, splash pads, and everything in between.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
A blank title surfaces as "Untitled" in some desktop clients (Outlook on Windows) and assistive technology. Should match the subject line or a short descriptive label.

**Issue — Google Fonts loaded via `<link>` (will fail in most clients):**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=League+Spartan:wght@400;500;600;700&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail (web + Android), Yahoo Mail, and Outlook all strip `<link>` tags or block external stylesheets. `League Spartan` will never render for the majority of recipients. The conditional comment `<!--[if !mso]>...<![endif]-->` correctly suppresses this in Outlook, but Gmail/Yahoo are not gated. This is harmless only if a safe system-font fallback stack is declared on every styled element — that cannot be confirmed from the truncated source.

**Positives:**
- `lang="en"` present on `<html>` — correct for screen readers
- `role="presentation"` on all `<table>` elements — good
- Viewport meta tag present
- Full MSO/VML conditional block present for Outlook rendering
- Tracking pixel `alt=""` — appropriate

**Cannot confirm from truncated source:** alt text on content images, color-contrast values, link underline suppression for AT.
