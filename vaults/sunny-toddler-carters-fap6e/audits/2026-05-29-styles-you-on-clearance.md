# Styles you 🫶 on CLEARANCE
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- Carter's fires another clearance missile — "UP TO 70% OFF*" in a bright red hero — the day after yesterday's 50%-off push and two weeks into a Memorial Day discount marathon. The escalation logic is clear: if 50% didn't close you yesterday, maybe 70% does today. What's interesting is that the brand splits this send in two: the clearance bomb up top, then a full Otter Avenue editorial ("NEW! GOLDEN SUMMER" / reversible styles) below. The two halves don't really talk to each other, and the email suffers for it.
- The render is clean and the navigation strip — Baby Girl / Toddler Girl / Girl / Baby Boy / Toddler Boy / Boy — remains the most underrated UX element Carter's uses consistently. It does real targeting work inside the body. But the subject "Styles you 🫶 on CLEARANCE" leaves the 70% number on the table, which is a real miss when the hero is screaming it.
- Judgement: a competent but fatigued send. Subscribers who have opened every Carter's email since May 15 have now seen essentially the same clearance hero more than a dozen times. The Otter Avenue insert tries to inject freshness but reads more like a calendar conflict than intentional creative.

## What's working

- **70% off is a genuine escalation** from yesterday's 50% — there's real signal value for bargain shoppers who have been waiting for the floor.
- **Age/gender navigation strip** (Baby Girl → Boy) lets subscribers self-select instantly without hunting; this is consistently Carter's best in-email UX.
- **Otter Avenue "reversible" callout** adds a functional product story ("REVERSIBLE FOR STYLE") that stands apart from pure discount language.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds top to bottom.
- **Carter's Rewards** is visible in the footer, giving loyalty members a secondary reason to act.

## What's weak

- **70% not in the subject line** — the biggest offer in two weeks of sending is buried below the fold of the inbox.
- **Two-message body** — clearance hero and Otter Avenue editorial pull in opposite directions; neither lands with full force.
- **Asterisk on 70% OFF*** with no visible inline explanation — the fine-print concern is immediate and erodes trust.
- **No deadline or urgency mechanism** — "New Styles Added" is not a deadline; there is no counter, no end-date, nothing to close the loop.
- **Subscriber fatigue is real** — this is the 13th-or-so discount email in the Memorial Day window; the May 27 editorial "Just dropped! Our latest baby must-haves" scored a deserved 9/10 precisely because it broke pattern, and this email snaps right back.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the 70% in the subject.** "70% off clearance — new styles just added" is worth more than the heart emoji. The offer is the hook; use it.
- 2. **Cut one message.** Either run the clearance email or run the Otter Avenue Golden Summer editorial — not both. The Otter Avenue reversible-skirt story is strong enough to stand alone, especially given it already headlined the May 27 World Otter Day send.
- 3. **Add a visible end-date to the clearance offer.** Even "through Sunday" or "while supplies last" gives a shopper a reason to act now vs. tomorrow.
- 4. **Resolve the asterisk inline.** State the exclusion (e.g., "excludes doorbusters") right under the hero number — don't make readers hunt for it in the legal block.
- 5. **Swap the generic "SHOP IT ALL" CTA** for category-split CTAs matching the navigation strip (e.g., "Shop Girls' Clearance" / "Shop Boys' Clearance") to drive higher-intent clicks.
- **Subject Alt A:** `70% off clearance — new styles just added`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New clearance drops: up to 70% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Otter Avenue's Golden Summer reversible styles are in the mix`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh clearance arrivals — shop by age before sizes go`
