# 🔔 All things holiday — Now up to 50% off online ✨
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-09**
## Executive summary

- Carter's has swapped its recurring Halloween-under-a-different-banner trick for a new host holiday: this time the skeleton costume, pumpkin bunting, and jack-o'-lantern hat sit under "ONLINE HOLIDAY FLASH DEALS!" instead of "Labor Day." Same photos this brand has run repeatedly this week (skeleton costume flagged 9/4, 9/5, 9/8), just relabeled again — this is now a pattern, not a one-off.
- The bigger problem is internal inconsistency: the subject promises "up to 50% off," but the only offer banner actually rendered in the body is "UP TO 70% OFF* CLEARANCE" — there's no visible 50%-off holiday callout anywhere above the fold. A reader who opens expecting a 50%-off deal has to scroll past a nine-photo grid and an unrelated OshKosh denim campaign before hitting a completely different number.
- This lands as a middling, slightly confusing send — decent visual polish, on-brand photography, but a subject/body mismatch and yet another recycled Halloween hero undermine trust in the "Holiday" framing.

## What's working

- Clean 3x3 product grid gives a fast visual scan of the holiday assortment (skeleton PJ, pumpkin romper, snowman costume, tree-themed sleeper, plaid sets).
- The OshKosh "Usher in the New Frontier" module is a genuinely different, well-shot lifestyle image (girl in mustard top + embroidered denim detail shots) — not another recycled asset.
- The "70% OFF CLEARANCE" banner is bold, high-contrast red, and impossible to miss.
- Shop-by-category buttons (Baby Girl, Toddler Girl, Girl, Baby Boy, Toddler Boy, Boy) give a clear, specific next click.
- Store locator module with address and "Get Directions" adds a concrete local-relevance touch.

## What's weak

- Subject says "up to 50% off" but the only offer banner shown is 70% off clearance — no 50%-off holiday callout is visible in the render at all.
- The skeleton-costume/pumpkin-hat hero is the same Halloween imagery this brand has now run at least four times this week under three different banners (Labor Day x3, now "Holiday").
- No single hero offer or price is visible without scrolling — the top of the email is just a product grid with no CTA or discount attached to it.
- "Holiday" as a header is vague at this point in September; paired with Halloween costumes and no holiday-specific messaging, it reads more like a placeholder header than a real campaign.
- Two unrelated campaigns (holiday grid + OshKosh denim) are stacked with no visual separation cueing that they're different offers.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the subject/body mismatch first — either put a "Holiday styles up to 50% off" banner directly under the hero grid, or change the subject to reference the 70% clearance number that's actually shown.
- 2. Retire the skeleton-costume/pumpkin-hat photo for at least the next few sends — it's been the hero or a grid tile in four emails this week across three different "occasions."
- 3. Move the clearance banner (or a holiday-specific offer banner) above the product grid so there's a concrete number visible without scrolling.
- 4. Either separate the OshKosh denim module with a clearer section break/label, or cut it — it dilutes the "holiday deals" narrative this send is supposed to be about.

- 403 on a redirect-wrapper domain typically indicates either (a) the QA fetcher is being blocked by bot/WAF protection on `attentivemail.com`, or (b) the tracked links are genuinely dead/expired tokens. Given 100% failure rate across a live ESP redirect domain, this pattern is most consistent with anti-bot blocking of the automated prober rather than actual dead links — but it cannot be confirmed from static HTML alone since the destination is opaque until resolved.
