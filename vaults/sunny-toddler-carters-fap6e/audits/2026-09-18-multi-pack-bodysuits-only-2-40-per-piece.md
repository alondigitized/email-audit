# 💙 Multi-pack bodysuits only $2.40 per piece 💙
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Carter's leading with a clean, functional value prop — "$2.40 per piece" multi-pack bodysuits — and it's one of the more visually organized sends in the recent run. The hero delivers exactly what the subject promises: a real product shot of five folded bodysuits (heather grey, navy, cream, striped, monster-print) with a bold orange starburst price callout. But the email quickly sprawls into a "we think you'll love these" grid (jeans, joggers, sleepers), then a full pajama/plush sub-campaign, then a 70% off clearance banner, then price-match and store-locator blocks — by the bottom it's lost the multi-pack thread entirely.
- Compared to 2026-09-17's "$5 bodysuit" send, this one has a cleaner hero but weaker follow-through — that email stayed on-message with a single organized offer, while this one buries its own subject line under three unrelated campaigns stacked vertically.
- Judgement: solid hero, bloated body. This will open fine on subject strength but will lose scroll-depth clicks to the competing offers below the fold.

## What's working

- Hero image is a genuine product shot — five folded bodysuits in on-brand colors/prints, not stock photography.
- The "$2.40 per piece" orange starburst is legible and matches the subject line exactly, no bait-and-switch.
- Clear primary CTA button ("Shop from $2.40 per piece") sits directly under a one-line value statement ("More outfit options, less laundry").
- Sub-brand logo strip (OshKosh, Little Planet, Carter's Arrive, Skip Hop) signals portfolio breadth without cluttering the hero.
- No visible render bugs — no overlapping text, broken images, or placeholder copy.

## What's weak

- The email stacks three unrelated campaigns (multipack bodysuits → "we think you'll love these" grid → pajama/plush push → 70% off clearance) with no visual transition, so it reads like three emails glued together.
- The 9-tile "we think you'll love these" grid has no prices, no labels, and no CTA of its own — pure filler that dilutes the hero offer.
- Clearance banner ("UP TO 70% OFF*") is visually the loudest block in the email (full-bleed red) and competes directly with the bodysuit hero for attention.
- By the time a reader reaches "Meet Their Bedtime Besties," the original $2.40 bodysuit offer is forgotten — there's no bottom recap or repeat CTA back to it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the "we think you'll love these" 9-tile grid entirely, or replace it with 3 products that are actually multipack-adjacent (add-on bodysuits, socks, hats) so cross-sell reinforces the hero instead of distracting from it.
- 2. Demote the 70% off clearance banner below the pajama section (or drop it from this send) — a single-offer email builds more trust than three competing at once, and this brand already ran a dedicated "5-Star Baby Event, up to 50% off" send on 2026-09-16.
- 3. Add a repeat CTA near the bottom ("Back to $2.40 bodysuits") so scrollers who got pulled into PJs/clearance still have a path back to the subject-line offer.
- 4. Add pack-size or price detail to the hero block itself (e.g., "5-pack / $12" total) — right now "$2.40 per piece" requires math to know total cost.
