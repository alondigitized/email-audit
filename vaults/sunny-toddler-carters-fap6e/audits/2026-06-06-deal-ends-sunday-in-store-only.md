# DEAL ends SUNDAY! 🚨 In store only
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- Carter's is running a rare channel-specific send today: the hero offer (EXTRA 20% OFF on 3+ clearance items) is in-store only, not online. That's an unusual and slightly brave bet — most Carter's emails this week have been driving to carters.com. The red banner executes cleanly, the deadline ("Ends Sunday") is upfront, and the price anchor on the Dresses & Rompers section ($8) gives a tangible floor. So far, so good.
- The problem is structural incoherence. The primary CTA is "Find A Store" — a store-locator ask — but the rest of the email below it (Dresses & Rompers, Shop Baby/Toddler/Kid) links to online shopping as if the in-store constraint doesn't exist. A subscriber who scrolls past the hero could easily click a "Shop Baby" link thinking they're in on the deal, only to find 20% off is not applied. That's the kind of misalignment that erodes trust.
- This also lands as Carter's second email today — the morning send was "New baby essentials collection" — and the third clearance-angle email in 48 hours following yesterday's "$3.99 clearance" floor-setter. The inbox is getting noisy. The deal is real, but the channel friction and cadence pile-up reduce the payoff.

## What's working

- Red hero with "EXTRA 20% OFF" and "Ends Sunday" is impossible to miss — the urgency hook is front and center.
- "Buy 3+ clearance items" condition is stated clearly in the hero, not buried.
- "Dresses & Rompers / Shop from $8" gives a concrete category + price floor for browsers.
- No render bugs — images load, layout is clean, text is legible.
- "Find A Store" button is distinct and actionable for an in-store campaign.

## What's weak

- Two Carter's emails on the same day creates fatigue; this one arrives in the shadow of the morning "baby essentials" send.
- The lower half of the email (Shop Baby/Toddler/Kid) links online, directly contradicting the in-store-only offer — mixed signals that will confuse scrollers.
- Subject "DEAL ends SUNDAY!" doesn't name the offer at all — 20% off, clearance, or even "kids" could all fit in 50 chars.
- The Rewards section at the bottom is tiny and easy to miss; in-store shoppers care most about stacking loyalty points with a discount.
- 🚨 in the subject alongside ALL-CAPS "DEAL" reads as alarm-emoji spam, which risks deliverability at the margin and lowers perceived brand tone.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Resolve the channel contradiction** — remove or clearly label the "Shop Baby/Toddler/Kid" online links as "Online prices, no extra 20%." Don't let scrollers accidentally think the in-store offer applies to clicks.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject** to include the actual offer — "DEAL" is doing no work that "20% off" couldn't do better.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 20% off clearance — in store, ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `In-store clearance blowout: extra 20% off, ends Sunday`
- 3. **Rewrite the preheader** to name the category most likely to drive foot traffic.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Buy any 3 clearance items, save an extra 20%. This weekend only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Dresses, rompers, sets from $8 — extra 20% off in store through Sunday.`
- 4. **Elevate the Rewards callout** — if members can stack points with this in-store offer, say it explicitly in the hero or directly below it, not buried in footer.
- 5. **Batch the sends** — Carter's hitting two emails in one day dilutes both. This clearance urgency email would have landed harder on its own.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag** — `<title></title>` is blank. Some clients (notably older Samsung Mail) render the title as a visible tab label or preview string. Should contain a non-empty descriptive string.

**Preheader filler uses two distinct Unicode padding strategies** — mixes `U+034F` (combining grapheme joiner: `͏`) and `U+00AD` (soft hyphen: `­`) in the same preheader div. Either approach works, but mixing them is redundant and increases raw byte count without benefit.

**Preheader and pixel-wrapper color not fully hidden** — both use `color:#333333` with `font-size:1px`. If a client strips `overflow:hidden` or `max-height:0px`, the filler characters become faintly visible. `color:transparent` or color-matching the background (`#ffffff`) is safer.
