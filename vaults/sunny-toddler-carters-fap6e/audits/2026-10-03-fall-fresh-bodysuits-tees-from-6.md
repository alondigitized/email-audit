# Fall-fresh bodysuits & tees from $6 🍁
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-03**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, on-brand Carter's send, but it repeats the 09-30 "The bestselling bodysuit + tees from $6 🎉" email almost beat for beat. The hero is four long-sleeve bodysuits and tees (Giggle Factory, Loved by Mommy Daddy) on a yellow-dash background. Under it sit "MUST-HAVE TEES & BODYSUITS" and a "Shop from $6" button. The eye lands on the product and the price right away.
- The rest of the email dilutes that focus. A "We think you'll love these" grid shows nine unlabeled, unpriced items, mostly jeans, joggers and sleepers, and none of them are tees or bodysuits. Then come the Dr. Becky / Good Inside block, which was the whole 10-01 email, and a red "UP TO 70% OFF* CLEARANCE" slab. That makes four competing messages. Only the $6 hero matches the subject.
- Judgement: competent, but a retread. A subscriber who got the 09-30 bodysuit email would see little new here. The "fall-fresh" and 🍁 framing is a thin refresh on the same $6 offer.

## What's working

- The hero is strong. The four garments are well lit, colorful and seasonal, and the layout is uncluttered.
- "Shop from $6" is a visible, clearly labeled button directly under the hero, and it matches the subject's price.
- The 70% off clearance block is high-contrast red with a simple age and gender navigation grid (Baby Girl, Toddler Boy and so on), so shoppers can self-sort.
- The footer value props (curbside pickup, cardholder perks, app, Rewards) are tidy and easy to scan.
- The render is clean, with no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- The "We think you'll love these" grid has no prices, names or labels. It looks like a recommendation module but gives no reason to click.
- The recommendations are off-theme. They are jeans, joggers and sleepers, not the tees and bodysuits the subject promised.
- The Good Inside / Dr. Becky block is a leftover from the 10-01 send. It's a content piece sitting in the middle of a promo and it pulls attention from the offer.
- The email has no deadline or urgency, and the "from $6" offer is generic.
- It stacks a second offer ("Up to 70% off* clearance") against the $6 hero, which muddies the single message.
- The fine print is a dense block of exclusions with no clear connection to which offer it covers.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the email about one thing: bodysuits and tees. Replace the mixed-category grid with six or nine tees and bodysuits that each show a price ("$6", "2 for $10") and a name. Cut or demote the Good Inside block to a footer link, or send it on its own.
- 2. Add a reason to act now, such as a weekend deadline or a price-hold date, and put it near the hero.
- 3. Move the clearance slab below the product section and shrink it. Two big offers dilute each other.
- 4. Give the CTA a product noun and a reason: "Shop $6 bodysuits & tees".
- 5. Show a real child or family in the hero. The flat-lay is attractive but anonymous.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **10 of 12 probed links returned 403.** All of them are `https://carters.e.attentivemail.com/e/c/...` click-tracking redirects. The affected links include:
  - the hero CTA ("Shop from $6")
  - the logo ("Carter's")
  - the brand links ("osh kosh bigosh", "little planet", "Otter Avenue", "Skiphop")
  - the hero GIF link
  - the product-tile links (jeans, 5-pack bodysuits, joggers)
- **This is probably bot-blocking, not a real outage.** A uniform 403 across every Attentive redirect, with no 404s or 5xx errors, points to the ESP's click domain rejecting the automated probe's user-agent or IP. The final destination URLs weren't resolved, so I can't confirm a live click-through.
- **The first link's text is empty in the QA output.** The "Shop from $6" CTA has a URL but no resolved anchor text. This is a labelling artifact, not a confirmed defect.
- **The redirect URLs are very long.** They run to roughly 400–900 characters. That is typical of Attentive and not a defect, but it makes the links hard to debug.
- **Coverage is partial.** 44 links were skipped (footer, social and utility links), so those weren't tested.
