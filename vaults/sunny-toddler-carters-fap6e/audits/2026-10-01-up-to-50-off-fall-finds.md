# Up to 50% off* fall finds ‼️
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-01**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, competent evergreen Carter's promo, but it feels generic after a week of Black Friday and Christmas noise. The hero is a dark green fabric swatch with a boxed "UP TO 50% OFF*" and "Go all in on fall" and a single Shop Now button. The offer reads instantly. A 3x3 grid of basics (jeans, white bodysuits, joggers, sleepers) follows under "We think you'll love these." There is also a "Match in every color" collage, a red "Up to 70% off clearance" block, a store-locator banner and a rewards footer strip.
- The email has too many jobs and no story. The hero is moody and abstract, with no child, no product and no fall styling. The product grid has no prices, names or links I can see, so the "50% off" never attaches to a real item. The 70% clearance block competes with the 50% hero, so there are two headline numbers. Against the 09-30 send ("The bestselling bodysuit + tees from $6"), which at least gave a concrete price, this one is a step back in specificity.
- The judgement is a safe 5. It is well built and honest about the asterisk, but it is easy to ignore. The subject's "‼️" adds little.

## What's working

- The hero offer is unmistakable: large white "50% OFF*" on dark green, with a bordered frame and a clear Shop Now button.
- The layout is easy to scan, with centered section headers and generous white space.
- The clearance block has strong color contrast, and its six category buttons (Baby Girl, Toddler Boy and so on) let the reader pick a segment quickly.
- The "Match in every color" collage is the best lifestyle moment. The stacked folded knits and the graphic sweatshirts feel seasonal.
- The rewards and curbside icons at the bottom give a quick loyalty reminder.

## What's weak

- The hero is a fabric close-up with no child or finished outfit, so it lacks emotional pull and doesn't say "fall."
- The 3x3 product grid shows no prices, names or sale badges. "We think you'll love these" claims personalization that isn't visible, since the items are generic basics.
- Two competing headline offers (50% and 70%) dilute the message.
- The store-locator banner ("Now open near you!") is generic and sits oddly between the clearance block and the footer.
- The legal copy is long and dense. The asterisk is honest, but the exclusions are buried at the bottom.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace the abstract hero with a child in a styled fall outfit, and tie the offer to a real product or category, such as "50% off jeans, sleepers and sets."
- 2. Add price and "was/now" labels to the grid products, and make each tile a distinct deep link.
- 3. Lead with one offer. Move clearance lower, or make it a clearly labeled secondary strip.
- 4. Change the grid heading to something true, such as "Fall basics, half off," unless the picks are actually personalized.
- 5. Drop the "‼️" and the double-exclamation energy. The brand is already in Black Friday teaser mode, so give subscribers a reason this send is different.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML source is mostly well-formed (VML/MSO conditionals, a preheader, a viewport meta tag, `role="presentation"` tables). However, the QA run found a 0-character plain-text part, an empty `<title>`, and no captured authentication or unsubscribe headers. All 12 probed tracked links returned 403, which is most likely bot-blocking of the probe rather than a real outage (see section 2).

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **12 of 12 probed links returned 403.** All were Attentive click-tracking redirects on `https://carters.e.attentivemail.com/e/c/...`. They covered:
  - the hero CTA ("Up to 50% Off* | Go all in on fall | Shop Now")
  - the header "Carter's" logo
  - the brand tiles: "osh kosh bigosh", "little planet", "Otter Avenue", "Skiphop"
  - six product image links, such as "Image of Boys Dark Wash Relaxed-Fit Classic Jeans" and "Image of Boys French Terry Joggers - Navy Blue"
- **These 403s are probably not a real outage.** Every probed link failed in the same way on the same redirect host, which points to the ESP's bot filtering of the QA fetcher rather than 12 independently broken destinations. I can't confirm this from the data, because the probe never reached the final landing URLs. Treat it as unverified, not as proof of a broken experience.
- **45 links were skipped** (footer, social and utility links). Their status is unknown.
- **Tracking pixels:**
  - `https://carters.attentivemail.com/ss/o/.../ho.gif` is an open pixel in a hidden `div`, at 1x1.
  - `https://mi.carters.com/p/up/745a17bbb73445ad/o.gif?mi_u=1150794179&mi_ecmp=10_01_2026_CAR_THU_STO_ENGAGERS` is a second open pixel. It is a separate tracker (the `mi_` parameters) that carries a subscriber ID in the URL.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **`<title></title>` is empty.** This is a minor issue that affects "view in browser" and some clients.
- **Alt text:** The QA check flags 9 images without alt text.
  - `ho.gif` is a false positive. The source shows `alt=""` on a 1x1 tracking pixel, which is correct for a decorative image.
  - `o.gif` and `264477f43986d8bb.png` on `mi.carters.com` are probably pixels. Verify that they are tracking pixels.
  - The cloudfront images are likely real gaps. The hero asset `MzczMjo6Q0FSXzA5MjkyNl9TQ0xfU0VDT05EQVJZXzEuanBnOj...` and four `SERVICES_BANNER_01` to `04` images have no alt text. If these carry text or links, that content is inaccessible.
- **Web font:** The League Spartan `<link>` is wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`, which is correct. I can't verify the fallback font stack because the source is truncated.
- Other rendering structures look sound: mobile breakpoint at 660px, `x-apple-data-detectors` override, `mso-hide` preheader handling.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The visible source has no unrendered tokens (no `{{ }}`, `%%`, or `[[ ]]`). The `mi_u=1150794179` value in the pixel URL is populated.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Plain-text part is missing.** QA reports the text version is 0 chars (FAIL). This is a deliverability and accessibility defect. It could in theory be a relay artifact, but QA reports it as the message's actual state.
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found.** QA says this may be a relay capture limitation. That means one-click unsubscribe support cannot be confirmed or denied. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules expect both headers.
- **`Authentication-Results` is absent.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment are unknown. Sender is `carters.email@marketing.carters.com`, and the tracking domains are `attentivemail.com` and `e.attentivemail.com`. Alignment cannot be assessed from this data.
- **Footer unsubscribe link and physical address:** The source is truncated, so I can't confirm either. QA says an unsubscribe link is always probed, but no result for it appears among the 12 reported failures.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not verifiable. All visible CTAs go through Attentive redirects that returned 403, so the final destination URLs, UTM parameters and landing-page match could not be checked. The only campaign identifier visible is `mi_ecmp=10_01_2026_CAR_THU_STO_ENGAGERS` on the `mi.carters.com` pixels.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Re-probe the redirect links with a real-browser user agent or a fetch that follows redirects, to separate probe blocking from real breakage. Capture the resolved destination and its UTMs.
2. Ensure a multipart text/plain alternative is sent. It is currently 0 chars.
3. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` on the raw message. Capture the raw headers, bypassing the AgentMail relay if possible, to verify SPF, DKIM and DMARC.
4. Add descriptive alt text to the cloudfront hero and the four services-banner images. Keep `alt=""` on the tracking pixels.
5. Populate `<title>`, for example with the subject line.
6. Improve the QA tooling: treat `alt=""` on 1x1 pixels as pass, and mark 403s from known ESP redirect hosts as "inconclusive" rather than FAIL. The 16% pass rate is inflated by these.
