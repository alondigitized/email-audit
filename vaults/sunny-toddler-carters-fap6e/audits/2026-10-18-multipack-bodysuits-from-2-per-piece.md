# Multipack bodysuits from $2 per piece 🩵
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, readable Carter's send with a clear hero. A black "Black Friday preview / UP TO 60% OFF*" banner sits above a flat-lay of four long-sleeve bodysuits. Beneath it, "Bestselling Bodysuits" and a "Shop from $2 Per Piece" button match the subject line. The $2 price is a strong, concrete hook for a parent buying basics.
- The email stalls after the hero. The "Just for You" grid is nine unlabeled tiles of jeans, joggers and white bodysuits, with no prices, names or CTAs, so it looks like filler. The OshKosh "From the Vault" block repeats the Oct 14 "OshKosh Reissued" send. The 70% off clearance block and the 60% off banner give two competing percentages. Because the Black Friday preview banner has run all week (Oct 13, 15, 16, 17), the header is now wallpaper.
- Judgement: a solid everyday send with a good subject-to-hero match, held back by a bloated lower half and a repeated Black Friday banner. It would lift with one hero, one price and a tighter body.

## What's working

- The subject and hero match. "Multipack bodysuits from $2 per piece" is echoed by "Bestselling Bodysuits" and the "Shop from $2 Per Piece" button.
- The bodysuit flat-lay is large, bright and easy to read. The product is obvious in about a second.
- The headline benefit, "Overlap shoulders made to slide over baby's head during blowouts", gives a practical reason to buy.
- The button copy is specific, with a price in it, which beats a generic "Shop Now".
- The footer has useful trust items: curbside pickup, the Rewards program, in-app points and a store finder.

## What's weak

- The "Just for You" grid has nine unlabeled tiles, with no prices, no product names and no CTA. It dilutes the $2 hook and reads as generic.
- The OshKosh "From the Vault" block repeats the Oct 14 hero. It pulls attention away from bodysuits and adds a second, unrelated message.
- Two discount claims compete: up to 60% off in the banner and up to 70% off clearance lower down. Neither is the star.
- The Black Friday preview banner is the fifth in a week (Oct 17 "PREVIEW: Up to 60% off Black Friday ‼️"), so it adds no urgency and no deadline.
- The footer fine print is long and dense. The "$2 per piece" claim has no visible qualifier, such as multipack size or sale dates.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the "Just for You" grid and the OshKosh block, or replace the grid with 3 to 4 labeled bodysuit multipacks, each with its per-piece price, so the email delivers on the subject.
- 2. Drop the Black Friday banner on bodysuit-led sends. Use that space for a deadline or a size or age range, such as "NB to 24M".
- 3. Show the multipack math, for example "5-pack $10 = $2 each", and state any end date.
- 4. Pick one discount frame per send, either the 60% off event or the $2 price, not both.
- 5. Move the Rewards and curbside perks up as a single strip, and shorten the disclaimers.

# Technical Audit: Carter's "Multipack bodysuits from $2 per piece 🩵"

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML structure is sound: table-based layout with `role="presentation"`, `lang="en"`, MSO conditionals and a hidden preheader. Three issues stand out. The multipart message has no plain-text part, four banner images have no alt text, and every probed tracked link returned 403. The 403s are most likely probe blocking, not real breakage. The HTML source is truncated, so the footer and any unsubscribe markup could not be checked.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **All 12 probed links returned 403.** They are all `https://carters.e.attentivemail.com/e/c/...` redirect wrappers. That covers the logo, brand links (osh kosh bigosh, little planet, Otter Avenue, Skiphop), the hero, and the product tiles. A uniform 403 across 12 of 12 points to bot or WAF filtering of the prober, not 12 independently broken links. **This is unconfirmed:** the destination URLs were never resolved, and the 43 skipped links were not tested.

  - One alt reads `"Bestelling Bodysuits | ..."`. "Bestelling" is Dutch for "order" and looks like a template or localization leftover in an English send.

  - `https://carters.attentivemail.com/ss/o/.../ho.gif` (Attentive open pixel).
