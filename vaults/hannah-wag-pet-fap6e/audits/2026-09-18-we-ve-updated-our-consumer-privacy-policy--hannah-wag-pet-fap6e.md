# We’ve updated our consumer Privacy Policy
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a legal notice, not marketing, and I read it in about two seconds before archiving it. There's no offer, no dog, no auto-ship anything — just a plain "we updated our privacy policy" wall of text with a "Warm regards, Your Chewy family" sign-off that feels weirdly saccharine for a compliance email. Compare this to Chewy's own "Free $30 eGift Card" send from July — that one earned my click with a real dollar figure up top. This one earns a shrug.
- I won't click, I won't forward, and honestly this is worse than the May 2nd "Your Chewy.com account" dud I dinged for being anodyne — at least that one had a subject line implying something happened to *my* account. This is just Chewy covering their legal bases and dressing it up like customer care.

## What's working

- The Chewy logo top and bottom is instantly recognizable, so I know who it's from without reading anything.
- Clean, uncluttered layout — no render bugs, nothing overlapping, nothing broken.
- Support info (phone + chat) is visible if I did need to act on this.
- It's honest about what it is — no bait-and-switch trying to disguise a policy update as a deal.

## What's weak

- Zero mention of my dogs, my auto-ship orders, or anything that tells me they know I'm an actual customer — just generic legal boilerplate.
- "Warm regards, Your Chewy family" reads tone-deaf pasted onto a privacy policy notice — that's copy for a birthday email, not compliance.
- No personalization at all — could've gone to literally any Chewy customer or non-customer.
- Nothing to click toward beyond "read our Privacy Policy" — there's no reason for me to open this over just deleting it.
- Compared to the med-refill email from June that at least tried to be useful to my life with Biscuit, this feels like pure box-checking.

## Recommendations

- 1. Don't send a "family" sign-off on a legal notice — match tone to content. Keep it brief and neutral, or skip the whole email and put a one-line notice in-app/on my next order confirmation instead.
- 2. If this must be an email, at least anchor it with a reason that matters to me — "here's what changed and why it affects your pet's data/order history" — not just a link.
- 3. Segment this out of my "y'know my dogs" email stream entirely, or bundle it as a footer note in a real marketing send so it doesn't cost me an open.
