# BAKING SHEETS 🟥 ⬜ 🟦 UP TO 70% OFF 🟥 ⬜ 🟦 
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-04**
## Executive summary

- Subject says "BAKING SHEETS." I open it, and there's a furniture grid staring back at me — chairs, shelving, outdoor pieces, that "picked just for you" layout I've seen six times this week. There are no baking sheets. None. Not even a section. It's a Fourth of July furniture sale wearing a baking sheet costume, and I've been fooled again.
- What makes it worse is that I'm also staring at the "KITCHEN MATS under $30 ✨" email from this same morning — that's the third time that subject has landed. So now Wayfair has sent me a kitchen mats repeat AND a bait-and-switch baking sheet/furniture hybrid on the same holiday. The email cadence has gone from aggressive to genuinely exhausting.
- Here's the weird thing: the actual content inside would have gotten a real look from me if it had arrived honestly labeled. I'm actively furnishing two houses. Forty-to-fifty percent off furniture on a Sunday is the kind of thing I'd normally tab over to Wayfair for. But by this point I trust the subject line so little that I'm clicking to debunk it, not to shop. I wouldn't forward this. I might mark Wayfair spam. I wouldn't click anything in the body.

## What's working

- **The 4th of July hero lands fast.** "70% OFF / FOURTH OF JULY SALE" is above the fold, no ambiguity — Pottery Barn ran the same "Extra 20% off" banner four times in 48 hours and at least stayed on-message each time.
- **Product grid is scannable.** The tile layout with 40%/50% OFF badges makes it easy to browse quickly — faster than CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS" which buried the discount logic below the fold.
- **"Picked just for you" is a credible personalization signal** — better than Article's generic sale headers, even if the actual picks skew generic.
- **Flag-colored square emoji are a clever visual beat.** It's the one creative moment in the subject line that actually fits the holiday, even if the all-caps kills it.

## What's weak

- **The subject line is a lie.** "BAKING SHEETS" is in the email exactly zero times. That's not subject-line creativity — that's bait-and-switch, and it's the kind of thing that trains a subscriber to stop trusting any subject line you send.
- **This is Wayfair email number whatever-I've-lost-count today.** Already had "KITCHEN MATS under $30 ✨" hit my inbox this morning (same subject as July 1st and July 3rd). The cadence is broken.
- **No deadline creates zero urgency.** The 4th of July is the implied hook, but there's no "sale ends tonight" or "final hours" call — Article nailed this three weeks into their sale by putting "ends Monday" right in the subject.
- **No specific product hero.** It's a grid of eight things, none of which are baking sheets, none of which have a "we think you specifically need this" message. Compare to Pottery Barn's Extra 20% off sends, which consistently lead with a hero product category and a reason to click now.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop the fake product hook entirely.** The subject should tell me what the email actually is — a 4th of July furniture and home sale. The bakeware angle might have click-baited opens for a week, but it's training subscribers to discount every subject line.
- 2. **One hero product, not a grid of eight.** Pick one category you actually think I need based on my browse history — wall-mounted shelves, outdoor furniture — and lead with it. The "picked just for you" copy needs to mean something.
- 3. **Give me a hard deadline.** "Sale ends tonight at midnight" or "Ends 11:59pm ET" turns browsing intent into decision pressure.
- 4. **Consolidate the sends.** One focused email beats four scattered ones. Wayfair has burned through five July 4th slots by 9am; CB2 sent one clean "JULY 4TH SALE IS ON" and walked away.

- **Hard-coded fixed heights on product sub-elements (mobile)** — The following rules will silently clip content if copy is longer than the reserved space:
  - `.ProductRec-stars, .ProductRec-shippingCopy { height: 16px }`
  - `.ProductRec-price { height: 40px }`
  - `.ProductRec-salesCopy { height: 28px }`
  - `.ProductRec-name { height: 48px }`
