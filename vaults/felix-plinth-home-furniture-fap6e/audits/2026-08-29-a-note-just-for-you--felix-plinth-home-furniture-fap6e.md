# A note just for you!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-29**
## Executive summary

- "Hi friend" — not "Hi Felix," not "Hi Registry," just "friend," in a text wall that's basically a bulleted spreadsheet with underlines instead of buttons. This is Crate&Kids, and I've now caught five of these in one week — I flagged the almost-identical "Inside: Quality-crafted furniture customers keep coming back for" and "Small dining space? We've got you" both today, and this one's a step backward from both. It's got zero hero image at the top, just a gray box of underlined category links stacked on top of each other, which reads more like a terms-and-conditions doc than a Labor Day sale.
- The one thing that actually caught my eye was buried two-thirds down: the $500 app sweepstakes and the Crate Card upgrade. Those are two completely different offers crammed into one email with the actual sale, and neither gets a hero shot of an actual room or nursery — just a phone mockup and a credit card graphic. For a "dream room" pitch, there's not one photo of a kid's room in this entire email.
- Would I click? No. I'd skim the bolded percentages (50% off nursery, 60% off decor), maybe bookmark it mentally, and move on — this is a "read later, forget entirely" email, not a "click now" one. Wouldn't forward it to anyone; there's nothing visual here worth sharing.

## What's working

- The percentage-off callouts are bolded and underlined so I can scan them fast — 50% nursery, 30% study, 60% bedding, 60% decor, 50% toys, all legible without zooming.
- Double Rewards stacked on top of the discount is a real incentive for someone like me who shops on the highest-rewards card.
- The Crate Card cross-sell make sense given I'm already a multi-purchase household — but see below.

## What's weak

- No hero image. A furniture/kids brand sending an all-text opener during a "dream room" pitch is a huge miss — Pottery Barn's "Great Pumpkin" send at least had a seasonal visual anchor, and even Wayfair's overstuffed "Faux Florals" email had product photography up top.
- The primary CTA button ("Shop The Labor Day Event") is a washed-out light gray pill that barely reads as clickable against the beige background — worst button-to-background contrast I've seen from this brand this week.
- Three unrelated asks jammed into one email — shop the sale, download the app for a sweepstakes, book a free design appointment, apply for a credit card — with zero visual hierarchy separating why I should care about each. Article's Labor Day email At least stayed focused on one sale, one CTA.
- "Hi friend" with no personalization when I've got an active registry and order history with this brand family feels like they don't know me at all.
- This is deal fatigue territory — CB2's "Now and Future Classics" and this week's C&B pile are all starting to blur into one indistinguishable felt-board of underlined links.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with an actual photo of a styled kid's room or nursery using the sale items — give the eye something to land on before the text wall starts.
- 2. Fix the primary CTA button contrast — make it black like the "Download Now" and "Learn More" buttons further down, which actually pop.
- 3. Split this into two sends: one focused purely on the Labor Day sale, one for the app sweepstakes/Crate Card cross-sell — trying to do all three in one email dilutes all three.
- 4. Personalize the greeting — pull from registry or purchase category data instead of "Hi friend."
