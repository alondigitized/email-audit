# Vintage vibes are here: Meet Beatrix
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids again, one day after "NEW! Meet the Beatrix collection" which I already gave an 8 — so either they're doing a two-part rollout or someone forgot to check the send calendar, because this is the same collection with a slightly moodier subject line. I don't have a kid. I have a primary and a second home mid-gut-renovation, two Wayfair carts, and a Pottery Barn list I actually use, and this is a forest-green crib and nursery wallpaper email landing in an inbox that's supposed to be about my own square footage.
- The "vintage vibes" hook is doing real work visually — that dark green dresser with the brass hardware in the top module could pass for an adult bedroom piece if you squint, and I'll admit I lingered on it for a second before remembering it's crib-adjacent. But then it nosedives into nursery wallpaper, a "we love these for you" grid of gliders and dressers, and a sweepstakes for $500 — none of which moves me because none of it is mine to buy.
- Not clicking, not forwarding — unless I forward it to my sister who actually has a nursery to fill, in which case sure, this is a decent one to send her. For me it's an instant archive.

## What's working

- The hero bedroom collection shot (green dresser, brass pulls, arched mirror) has the same "vintage but not costume-y" styling I liked in yesterday's Beatrix send — consistent creative, at least.
- Clean modular layout — each section (bedroom, nursery, fall arrivals, sale, sweepstakes, "we love these for you") is visually distinct with its own "Shop Now" button, no clutter.
- The up-to-50%-off summer sale banner is a legible break in the middle of an otherwise product-heavy scroll.
- No render bugs — images loaded, text is sharp, nothing overlapping.

## What's weak

- I'm not the buyer. Full stop. Crib, glider, nursery wallpaper, "storybook style" lookbook — none of this maps to a 36-year-old renovating two adult homes.
- This is the second Crate & Kids "Beatrix" email in as many days in my inbox — yesterday's was "NEW! Meet the Beatrix collection," today's is "Vintage vibes are here: Meet Beatrix." Same collection, same dresser photo, different subject dressing. That's the exact "same lamp, three brands, one week" fatigue I complain about, except here it's one brand doing it to itself.
- The email is enormous — bedroom collection, nursery collection, fall arrivals (six subcategories), sale, lookbook, sweepstakes, "we love these," credit card pitch, app download, showroom CTA. It's Wayfair-style everything-dumping (I gave Wayfair's 15-item pool table email a 5 for this exact sin) instead of one focused story.
- The $500 sweepstakes and Crate & Barrel credit card pitch buried at the bottom feel like filler bolted onto a nursery email, not a reason to open it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split the list or gate the send** — if I'm not tagged as a parent/nursery shopper, don't send me the Beatrix crib collection two days running. Pottery Barn's entryway email worked for me because it matched something I'm actually renovating; this doesn't, twice in a row.
- 2. **Cut the email in half** — six fall-arrival subcategories plus a sale plus a lookbook plus a sweepstakes plus a credit card plus an app pitch is Wayfair-level overload. Pick the bedroom collection or the nursery collection, not both, and drop the kitchen-sink tail.
- 3. **Don't reuse yesterday's hero photo** — if this is meant to be a distinct send, give it a distinct lead image. CB2 differentiated itself from parent Crate & Barrel's flood of emails; this send doesn't even differentiate itself from its own prior-day send.
- 4. **If cross-selling to non-parents, lead with the crossover pieces** — that green dresser reads adult-bedroom-adjacent; if there's a "this works outside the nursery too" angle, make that the header, not a scroll-six-screens discovery.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Heritage-inspired hardware, fall's moodiest palette.`
