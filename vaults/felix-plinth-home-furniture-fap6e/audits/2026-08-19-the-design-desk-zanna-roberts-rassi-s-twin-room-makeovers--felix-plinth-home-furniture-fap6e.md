## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids again. Second one from this brand family in my inbox this week already flagged as a no-go, and this one's the same problem — twin girls' bedrooms with garden wallpaper and "mystical garden cat bedroom" copy, styled by fashion correspondent Zanna Roberts Rassi. I don't have kids, I'm not decorating a nursery or a tween's room, and nothing about "backpack bundles" or a car seat sweepstakes touches what I'm actually doing, which is renovating two houses full of adult furniture.
- The photography's genuinely nice — soft, editorial, well-lit — and if I had a daughter this age it'd probably land. But that's the whole issue: it's a great email for a persona that isn't me. Compare this to the actual "ultimate bedroom makeover" send from Crate & Barrel proper that scored a 7 with me — same before/after room-reveal format, same design-service hook, but adult spaces I could actually picture in my house. This is that email's little-kid cousin.
- Not clicking, not forwarding, marking as read. If anything I'd forward it to my sister who has twins, not to a friend who's renovating.

## What's working

- The editorial photography is clean and aspirational — good natural light, styled shots, not stock-photo stiff.
- Before/after room narrative structure (garden bedroom, city-by-the-sea bedroom) mirrors the format that worked for me in Crate & Barrel's adult bedroom makeover email — so the underlying playbook is sound, just aimed at the wrong customer here.
- Clear "Shop the Room" and "Book a Free Appointment" CTAs paired under each vignette, so at least the path to buy or book is obvious if you're the target shopper.
- The named designer/celebrity hook (Zanna Roberts Rassi) gives it a magazine-feature feel rather than a flat catalog blast.

## What's weak

- Zero relevance to me — no kids, no nursery, no tween bedroom on my renovation list. Dead on arrival, same as the "designers help for FREE" Crate & Kids email I read earlier today.
- "Enter for a chance to win $1000" sweepstakes and backpack bundles bury any signal this could be for an adult homeowner.
- This is now the second Crate & Kids send today plus a Levain Bakery collab yesterday — Crate & Barrel's parent family is just flooding my inbox and most of it isn't for me. Pottery Barn's "Ready-to-Ship Seating" send at least respected that I'm mid-renovation and impatient about delivery windows; this doesn't even try.
- Long, scrolly email — three full room reveals plus a bundle promo plus a sweepstakes plus a credit card pitch before it ends. Way too much for a segment I'm not even part of.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment me out. I've now gotten multiple Crate & Kids sends — if the brand has any purchase-history or browse data on me (all adult furniture, no baby gear), suppress the kids' line entirely. This is the single highest-impact fix.
- 2. If it must go wide, lead subject/preview with the design-service hook (which is genuinely cross-audience) rather than "twin room makeovers," which instantly flags this as irrelevant to childless subscribers.
- 3. Trim the email — three full room reveals plus bundles plus sweepstakes plus credit card pitch is a lot of scroll for a low-intent audience.
- 4. Cross-sell to Crate & Barrel proper for subscribers without kids — a small "not decorating a kid's room? See adult bedroom looks" module would at least redirect me somewhere useful.
