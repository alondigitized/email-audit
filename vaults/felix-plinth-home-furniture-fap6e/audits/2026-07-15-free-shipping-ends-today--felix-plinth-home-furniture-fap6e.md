# FREE SHIPPING ends today!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- Forty-six. That's where the C&B family now sits in my July inbox after this one lands, and I need to say that before anything else because it makes everything else make sense. Crate & Barrel proper already hit me twice today with "ENDS TODAY: Free shipping on orders over $59" — same subject line, same hero, two separate sends — and now the kids sub-brand swoops in with its own version of the same message. Different sender address, same urgency theater.
- To be clear about what this email actually is: it's Crate & Kids, the children's furniture sub-brand, not Crate & Barrel main. If you didn't catch that from the logo, you might spend ten seconds confused about why there are stuffed animals and kids' room shots in what looked like a C&B promo. I knew immediately because I've been tracking these senders for months, but a casual subscriber wouldn't. The aesthetic is warmer and softer than the parent — beiges, creams, plush textures — and the category nav makes it clear fast: Kids Bedding, Kids Furniture, Kids Range. That's fine, that's honest. The problem is that it's arriving as the third "free shipping ends today" volley from the same corporate family inside a single morning.
- I'm not clicking. I might have a couple kids' rooms to eventually furnish across the two renovations, but this feels like being piled on by a brand's entire roster. Mark as read, move on.

## What's working

- The hero image is warm and editorial — soft plush textures, tasteful earth tones — which actually distinguishes C&K visually from the harder-edged C&B proper sends.
- "500+ new fall arrivals, starting at $29" is the most interesting copy in the email: a real count with a real price anchor, which is something Article pulled off well with their entryway editorial, and it's the rare thing in this send that isn't just cadence filler.
- The category nav (Storage, Lighting, Kids Bedding, Kids Furniture) is well-organized — someone who genuinely needs to shop kids' rooms can orient in under three seconds.
- The "we love these for you" personalized grid at least *attempts* to signal that this isn't a total spray-and-pray; product cards with prices are visible.
- Layout renders cleanly — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum showing through. That's table stakes but CB2 botched it on their "NEW COLLECTION" send, so it's worth noting when someone gets it right.

## What's weak

- This is the **third** "free shipping ends today" message from the C&B family today. The urgency has no teeth because I've already been told it's ending twice and the day isn't over. Pottery Barn would never stack the same offer across siblings this aggressively in a single morning.
- "FREE SHIPPING" in full caps in the subject line is the oldest spam-filter bait in the book — it reads like a 2011 promotional email from a brand I've never heard of, not a trusted catalog I'm actually on.
- The sub-brand targeting is off for me. I don't see a kids threshold anywhere in the hero or subject — the C&B proper emails at least said "$59" in the subject itself. Here I have no idea if there's a minimum.
- Article dropped a genuinely editorial, no-discount entryway email four days ago that made me want to browse. This email makes me want to check whether I can mass-unsubscribe from C&B family sends.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Coordinate cadence across sub-brands.** If C&B proper already sent the "ends today" message, C&Kids should not echo it the same morning. This is a suppression logic problem, not a creative problem.
- 2. **Lead with the fall arrivals story, not the shipping offer.** "500+ new fall arrivals, starting at $29" is a stronger hook than a worn-out shipping deadline. The offer should be in the *preheader*, not the subject.
- 3. **Drop the ALL CAPS.** "Free shipping ends today" reads fine in sentence case. The caps are sending spam signals without adding urgency.
- 4. **Show the threshold.** If there's a minimum order for free shipping, it belongs in the hero next to the CTA. The C&B proper emails at least named "$59" — this email is vaguer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Fall arrivals for kids, starting at $29`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Ships free today — 500+ new kids arrivals`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping on orders over $59 — but it's the new arrivals that caught my eye.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Storage, lighting, bedding: new for fall, and free to ship today only.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Wildcard `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applying to `*` is overly broad and suppresses OS-level text scaling for users who need it. Should be scoped to `body` only.

**Conflicting `img` max-width media queries — dead code**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }  /* declared first */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }  /* declared second, wins on ≥768px */
```
At viewport widths ≥768px both queries match; the later `640px` rule wins in cascade, making the `768px` rule dead code. If `max-width: 600px` on large screens was intentional, reverse the declaration order or use a more specific selector.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<!-- then separately: -->
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
Three redundant declarations after the combined one. Not harmful, but noisy and indicates template drift.

**No dark mode CSS declared**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Dark mode is supported by Apple Mail, Gmail app, and Outlook. Opting out entirely means forced light mode with no dark-mode image or color alternatives. Consider at minimum adding a `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block.
