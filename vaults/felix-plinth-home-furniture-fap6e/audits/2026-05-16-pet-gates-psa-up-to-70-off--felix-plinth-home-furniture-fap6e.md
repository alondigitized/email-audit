# PET GATES 📣 PSA 📣 UP TO 70% OFF 📣 
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- I've gotten two Wayfair emails today — this one showed up alongside "All under $29.99" which landed earlier this morning. Six Wayfair emails in roughly 72 hours. But this subject stopped me cold for the wrong reasons: pet gates. Three bullhorn emojis, all caps, a product category I have zero use for. I don't have a dog. I don't have a toddler. I have two patios in renovation limbo and a growing outdoor furniture wishlist. Nothing in my purchase history should be surfacing pet products.
- Opening it anyway — because 70% off anything still makes my rewards-card brain twitch — I found exactly what I expected: a standard Memorial Day clearance email. Adirondack chairs, outdoor rugs, lighting, doorbuster deals. Stuff I actually want. Zero pet gates visible anywhere in the email. That's a bait-and-switch. The subject made a specific, screaming promise and the body doesn't even acknowledge it.
- The frustrating part is that stripped of the subject, this is a perfectly serviceable seasonal clearance send. The timing is right, the Adirondack chair deal could have gotten a click. But the subject-to-body mismatch is such a clean betrayal of trust that I marked it read and moved on. No click. No forward.

## What's working

- Memorial Day clearance hero reads cleanly at the top — the seasonal timing is correct and the banner hierarchy gets my eye there first
- The Adirondack chairs section is genuinely on my list: I need outdoor seating for both properties and 25% off is real money on a full set
- "Picked just for you" adds a personalization signal, even if I'm skeptical it's real segmentation versus a merge token
- "Free & easy delivery over $35" is a low-friction win — Pottery Barn buries this behind membership tiers; the plain callout here is useful for furniture orders
- Multiple discount tiers (15% off doorbuster, 25% off chairs, higher % off in other sections) give enough variety to find an anchor that matches where I am in my budget cycle

## What's weak

- Subject says pet gates — email has none. That's the whole review right there. Everything else is secondary to this miss
- Triple emoji plus ALL CAPS in a subject line is the clearest possible spam trigger I know; I had to consciously override the archive reflex
- Six Wayfair emails in 72 hours on the same Memorial Day theme. The prior May 15 send already clocked as the "fifth email in three days" — we've lapped that
- CTAs are painfully generic: "shop all sales" dumps me on a homepage when Crate & Barrel would have given me "Shop Outdoor Dining" or a direct Adirondack category link
- Pet gates is a demographic swing-and-miss — nothing in my renovation profile should flag me as a pet gate buyer

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the pet gates subject entirely.** If this was a segmentation error — wrong template, wrong list — fix the targeting logic before the next send. If this was intentional, I can't follow the logic and neither can my inbox algorithm
- 2. **Drop the emoji count and the caps.** One emoji at most, sentence case. The PSA framing has potential — "A PSA for your patio: Memorial Day clearance, up to 70% off" — but not at this noise level
- 3. **Anchor the CTA to a real category.** The Adirondack deal is right there in the email — "Shop Outdoor Seating" with a direct link would have gotten a click from me. "Shop all sales" converts like a shrug
- 4. **Show loyalty math in the subject.** Crate & Kids put "Up to 50% off + 2x Rewards" in a single subject line and I opened it on reflex — that's the format Wayfair is leaving on the table here
- 5. **Throttle the cadence.** Six emails in 72 hours on Memorial Day is reader fatigue, not urgency. Article sent me one welcome email and I read every word
