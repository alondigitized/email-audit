# NEW pillows, all shipping free!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-22**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "$29.50 starting price" on plaid pillows stacked up on a plaid chair — that's a clean, punchy hero and it actually lands, because pillows are a cheap, low-risk add to a cart that's otherwise full of big-ticket reno stuff. But then the email just... keeps going. Eight more free-shipping categories (duvets, curtains, throws, sheets, towels, quilts, table linens, flatware), then a "Recommended Just For You" grid with a curtain rod, a swivel chair, two platform beds, towels, and sheets, then a Halloween shop banner, then a Design Crew banner, then app download, then rewards card, then Affirm. This is five emails stapled together.
- Compared to Pottery Barn's own recent sends — the "Free Design Services" one from the same day scored higher with me because it stayed focused on one vignette I could screenshot. This one dilutes that same Design Crew pitch by burying it two-thirds down the page under a Halloween module. And compared to Crate & Barrel's "newest Texas store" repeat-send I flagged, at least this isn't a duplicate — it's just unfocused.
- Would I click? Probably, on "Shop Now" for the pillows specifically, since $29.50 is an easy impulse add for the second house. Would I forward it? No — it's not clean enough to send a friend, too much scroll-fatigue before you get anywhere.

## What's working

- The pillow hero is genuinely well-shot — plaid stack on a plaid chair reads as a real room, not a product-on-white shot, and it's on-brand for fall.
- "$29.50 starting" gives me a real anchor price with zero digging, unlike CB2's vaguer "up to 40% off."
- Free shipping is the consistent thread through the whole email, which at least gives it one coherent message even as categories multiply.
- The "Recommended Just For You" grid includes a platform bed and a swivel chair — actual furniture, which is closer to my real reno spend than throw pillows.

## What's weak

- Nine free-shipping categories in a single grid before you even get to "recommended for you" is just visual noise — my eye has nowhere to land after the hero.
- The Halloween Shop and Design Crew banners feel bolted on, unrelated to the pillow/linens theme, and they push the actual loyalty/Affirm/app content way below the fold.
- No swatch or sample-ordering callout anywhere, which is the one thing that would actually get me to click on fabric-heavy items like these plaids.
- "Recommended Just For You" doesn't feel personalized at all — it's beds, a chair, sheets, towels — that's just a generic bestseller mix, not anything tied to what I've browsed.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email in half. Lead with pillows + one or two adjacent categories (throws, quilts) and save duvets/curtains/flatware for a separate send — nine categories in one grid kills hierarchy.
- 2. Move Design Crew right under the hero, not buried after Halloween — it's the stronger hook per my last read of that standalone send.
- 3. Add a swatch/sample CTA near the plaid pillows — "Order a swatch" is the actual next step for someone renovating two houses and matching palettes.
- 4. Drop or relocate the Halloween banner to a dedicated seasonal send; it breaks the free-shipping narrative here.

# Technical Audit: Pottery Barn — "NEW pillows, all shipping free!"

**1. Technical Summary**
Standard ESP-templated (Bronto/Cheetah-style) HTML email with heavy legacy markup; core rendering is sound, but tracking pixels/redirects use mixed HTTP/HTTPS and several images (including functional/content images) lack alt text.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- 54 tracking/click-redirect links detected (`click.e.potterybarn.com`, `clickcp.potterybarn.com`, `d.turn.com/r/dd/...`, `svopn.potterybarn.com`) — skipped by automated HTTP probe per QA tool policy, not independently verified as functional.
- Open-tracking pixel served over plaintext HTTP: `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` — mixed content, may be blocked by mail clients enforcing HTTPS-only image loading, and leaks the recipient email address in a URL query string over an unencrypted channel.
- Second HTTP asset: `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` — same mixed-content risk.
- Third HTTP asset: `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` — note this asset path is dated 2022, suggesting a stale/orphaned footer banner reused across campaigns.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- 40+ `<img>` elements missing `alt` text (QA-flagged), including primary content images (`remmy-swivel-chair-1-l.jpg`, `farmhouse-platform-bed-1-l.jpg`, `hydrocotton-quick-dry-towel-l.jpg`, `400-thread-count-organic-percale-sheet-set-l.jpg`) — screen readers and image-blocked clients (default in Outlook) will render these as blank space with no fallback text.
- Head contains non-standard/invalid `<style>` content: literal PHP `header()` calls (`header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); ... header("Pragma: no-cache");`) embedded inside a CSS `<style>` block — these are inert as CSS (browsers/mail clients will ignore them as invalid selectors), but their presence indicates a templating/build artifact that leaked PHP source into the compiled HTML rather than being executed server-side.
- `<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">` and `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` are present and appropriate for transactional/promotional email (prevents indexing of hosted web version, strips referrer leakage to linked domains).
- Legacy MSO/Outlook conditional comments (`<!--[if gte mso 9]>`, `<!--[if mso 16]>`) and table-based layout are correctly structured for Outlook desktop compatibility.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
- Recipient email is merge-token-populated correctly in tracking URLs (`lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` appears in both the customer-service link and the `sv_sveme` open-tracking parameter) — no unresolved token syntax (e.g., `{{`, `%%`, `[%`) observed in the truncated source.
- No visible on-page personalization (first name, etc.) in the truncated content; cannot confirm merge-token resolution beyond the email address instances shown.

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
- `List-Unsubscribe` header not found — QA tool notes this may be a relay artifact (AgentMail may not be capturing/forwarding the header) rather than a sender-side omission; cannot confirm compliance status from this data alone.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058, one-click unsubscribe) not found — same caveat; if genuinely absent, this is a CAN-SPAM/Gmail-Yahoo bulk sender requirement gap as of 2024+ enforcement.
- `Authentication-Results` header not found — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status cannot be verified from available data; flagged as unknown, not as a failure.
- Unsubscribe/customer-service link present in body (`potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=...`), satisfying CAN-SPAM's requirement for a visible opt-out mechanism in the message body itself, independent of header-level unsubscribe.

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
- Primary CTA/nav links route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirect wrapper rather than direct UTM-tagged URLs — cannot verify final landing page or UTM parameters without following the redirect chain (out of scope for static HTML review).
- Cannot confirm UTM consistency across the 54 tracking links without live resolution; recommend a follow-up dynamic crawl if landing-page attribution accuracy needs verification.

**7. Recommendations**
