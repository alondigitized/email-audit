## What's working

- The Aldine sofa in that rust leather is genuinely a color/material combo I'd stop on — matches the warm-wood direction I'm already leaning for the second home.
- Clean, consistent editorial layout — sketches up top, then product, then quote from the designer — it reads as one coherent story, not a grab-bag.
- The "Custom Is the Ultimate Luxury" / Interior Design Services block near the bottom is at least useful for someone mid-reno like me — a real appointment CTA, not just another product tile.

## What's weak

- No price anywhere on the hero products — Aldine Sofa, Aldine Chair, Turno Sconce all go unpriced, which for furniture is the first thing I want to know before I even care who designed it.
- This is back-to-back design-story content — Andreas Hansen yesterday, David Sirelli today. My brain's already filed CB2 under "look how artsy we are" and stopped expecting an actual deal.
- Way too much scroll before any offer shows up — by the time I hit "More for You" with actual prices, I'm 15+ screens in.
- "More From Our Designers" turns into a wall of portraits (Studio Ananasi, Dach & Zephir, Ross Cassidy) that all blur together — three bios in one email is a lot to ask a phone-scrolling commuter to absorb.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price or "from $X" on the Aldine Sofa and Chair right under the hero shot — that's the single biggest lift here, since gorgeous-but-unpriced furniture just makes me bounce to check Article or Pottery Barn for the number.
- 2. Cut this to ONE designer per send — either Sirelli or the "More From Our Designers" roundup, not both in the same email. Save the roundup for a dedicated "Meet the Designers" digest.
- 3. Move "Shop the Collection" up near the hero image instead of after the full editorial spread — I shouldn't have to scroll past three product stories to get a button.
- 4. Add a ship-time or lead-time note near the sofa/chair — furniture reno buyers like me are ruling things out on delivery windows as much as price.
