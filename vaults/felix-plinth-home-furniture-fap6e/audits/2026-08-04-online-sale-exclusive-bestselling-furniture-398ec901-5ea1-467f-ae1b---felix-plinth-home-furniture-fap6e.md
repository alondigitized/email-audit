# Online SALE exclusive: Bestselling furniture
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-04**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "Select Furniture Now on Sale" — this is Pottery Barn doing the thing they do best: big, clean furniture collections shot in warm, styled rooms with actual room-scene context. Benchwright dining, Aubrey bedroom storage, Calistoga bed, Livingston home office — four collections stacked top to bottom, each with a "NOW ON SALE" badge and zero percentage attached to any of them. That's the first crack: I'm mid-reno on two houses, I will absolutely stop for a dining table or a home office setup, but "on sale" with no number means I have to click through four different collection pages just to find out if this is 10% off or 40% off. Compare that to Article's "Last chance: Summer Sale ends today" (7/10, same week) — annoying badge, ENDS TODAY typo and all, but at least it told me 40% before I clicked anything.
- What actually worked on me: the "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" block lower down with the Toscana Extendable Dining Table and the Scarlett Upholstered Bed — that's better targeting than the hero collections, because it's specific SKUs, not just "browse this whole line." And I'll say it — this is a much better Pottery Barn send than the Halloween skeleton email from yesterday (5/10). No kids' gear, no seasonal gimmick, just furniture. It's sitting right between their "New arrivals inspired by heritage design" (8/10, 2026-08-02) and yesterday's dining room "get the look" send (8/10) in quality — good bones, but the vague sale terms and the credit card fine print wall at the bottom drag it down.
- Would I click? Yeah, probably into the Toscana table or the Benchwright collection, since those are real reno candidates. Would I forward it? No — nothing here is exciting enough to text a friend about, it's just a standard sale email.

## What's working

- Four real furniture collections with room-styled photography (dining, bedroom storage, bed, home office) — this hits my actual reno categories.
- "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" with specific SKUs (Toscana Extendable Dining Table, Scarlett Upholstered Bed) feels like it's tracking what I've browsed, not just blasting a generic list.
- Design Crew "Free Design Services" block is a smart mid-reno hook — I've actually considered using a service like that for the second house.
- Clean, consistent brand fonts and warm neutral palette across every section — no jarring color clashes like some of the CB2 sends I've seen this week.

## What's weak

- Every single "NOW ON SALE" badge has zero percentage or dollar figure attached — I have no idea if this is a real discount or 10% off two throw pillows.
- Rewards card blurb ("Earn 10% Back in Rewards") is buried after the app download pitch and Halloween Shop banner — that's actually relevant to how I buy (highest-rewards card) and it's nearly invisible.
- The Halloween Shop banner sandwiched in the middle breaks the furniture-sale flow — feels like the same seasonal tangent that annoyed me yesterday, just smaller this time.
- Legal/fine-print block at the bottom (Affirm, credit card terms, promotional exclusions) is a wall of gray text that makes the email feel like it's hiding something, even though the furniture content itself was straightforward.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put an actual number on the sale — "Up to 30% Off" or whatever it is — directly on the hero and on each collection badge. Right now "NOW ON SALE" with no digit is the single biggest miss; Article and CB2 both lead with the percentage.
- 2. Move the "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" credit card block up near the hero or right after the first collection — that's a real lever for someone like me who buys on the highest-rewards card, don't bury it under app-store badges.
- 3. Cut or move the Halloween Shop banner out of a furniture-sale email entirely — let sale emails stay sale emails, seasonal content should get its own send (like yesterday's dedicated Halloween email).
- 4. Add dimensions or starting price on at least the hero collections (Benchwright, Calistoga) the way the recommended section does for the Toscana table (60"-124.5") — that's the detail that gets me from "pretty photo" to "add to cart."
