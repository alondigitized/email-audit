# Expert design services, for FREE
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- I'm juggling a primary that needs the living room redone and a second home where I haven't made a single furniture decision yet. Free design consultation is genuinely useful to me right now, and Pottery Barn is one of my main shortlists. So when I opened this, I wasn't rolling my eyes at another flash sale — I was actually reading.
- The three service modules (Layer a Bed, Create an Outdoor Oasis, Storage Tips & Tricks) are well-chosen buckets. "Create an Outdoor Oasis" landed hard because I'm literally looking at outdoor furniture for the second home this week. The hero reads clean, the CTAs are unambiguous, and the "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" button near the middle of the email is the clearest single ask Pottery Barn has made in any of their last eight sends to me.
- That said, the email codes more feminine than I am — the bed styling, the soft palette, the product recommendations at the bottom — this could have run in a women's magazine with zero edits. And the "Recommended Just for You" grid at the bottom feels algorithmic rather than curated, which undercuts the whole "tailored to you" promise in the hero. Would I click? Yes — specifically the outdoor module. Forward to a friend? Maybe to my wife as a "we should book this" nudge. Mark as read without acting? Only if I already had a call scheduled.

## What's working

- **The pivot away from sale mode is the single biggest win.** After the outdoor-sale email on 5/28 (the best send of the week, genuinely), this doesn't feel like a recycled drum hit — it's a new lane.
- **"BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" is one of the clearest CTAs Pottery Barn has used all month.** Verb, noun, benefit, done.
- **Three distinct service modules give me a reason to self-select.** I land directly on "Create an Outdoor Oasis" because that's my project, not a generic "Shop Now."
- **10% Back in Rewards is surfaced near the bottom** — I noticed it, and it nudges me toward using my PB card rather than the Chase Sapphire I'd otherwise default to.
- **No sale pressure, no countdown clock, no 🚨** — after 33 emails that leaned on urgency, the absence of manufactured scarcity actually creates its own calm authority.

## What's weak

- **The aesthetic reads firmly feminine.** The bedding hero, the soft linen palette, the outdoor table styling — CB2 manages to stage neutral spaces that don't feel gendered. This skews Pinterest-mom, not dual-income couple renovating two houses.
- **"Recommended Just for You" at the bottom breaks the editorial logic.** I'm reading a service email, and suddenly I'm looking at a product grid — it feels like the email tripped and fell into a catalog. Article keeps their service-adjacent emails product-free for a reason.
- **No specificity about what the design session actually looks like.** Is this virtual? In-store? How long? I'd click faster if I knew it was a 30-minute video call and not a trip to the nearest showroom.
- **The Affirm block at the bottom is noise here.** This isn't a "buy something" email — the financing CTA is a non sequitur that dilutes the one clean ask Pottery Barn is making.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with what the appointment actually is.** One line under the hero: "30-minute virtual session, no showroom required." That single sentence resolves the friction that would stop me from booking.
- 2. **Remove the Affirm block entirely.** It belongs in product-purchase emails, not service consultation emails — it creates cognitive dissonance right when the user is primed to book.
- 3. **Gender-balance the imagery.** Stage the outdoor table with something that reads neutral or subtly male — teak + concrete, darker palette, less florals. CB2's approach to neutral staging is the benchmark here.
- 4. **Replace "Recommended Just for You" with one curated look tied to the service.** If I just saw the outdoor oasis module, show me the three pieces that an outdoor design session might help me choose — not a bedroom grid.
- 5. **Add one social-proof line.** "4,200 design sessions booked this year" or "Average session leads to $X in savings" — something that makes the free appointment feel like a popular, trusted thing, not a sales lead-gen funnel.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Book your free design session before summer fills up`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Two homes to furnish? We can help — free`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `30 minutes, virtual or in-store, zero obligation.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Our designers specialize in outdoor, bedrooms, and storage — pick your project.`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Expert Design Services" (2026-05-29)

### 1. Technical Summary

The email contains a PHP `header()` code fragment leaked into a `<style>` block, two broken images (including a cross-brand asset from `edm.westelm.com`), 34+ images without alt text, and two images served over plain HTTP. Compliance headers were not captured through the relay, leaving SPF/DKIM and one-click unsubscribe status unverified.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Cross-brand spacer image returns 403**
`https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
West Elm CDN asset used in a Pottery Barn email is broken. Will cause a missing-image gap or broken layout depending on client rendering of failed image requests.

**FAIL — Commented-out link returns 403 (low priority)**
`https://www.potterybarn.com/pages/credit-card/?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&...&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`
This URL is inside an HTML comment block (`<!-- -->`), so it is not rendered. However, QA flagged it as a live broken URL. If the comment block is ever removed to activate the credit card banner, this destination will return 403.

**INFO — 25 click-tracking links skipped**
All body CTAs route through `click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=...`. Final destination URLs and UTM parameters cannot be verified without following the redirect chain. No probe was attempted (by design).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CRITICAL — PHP code fragment inside `<style>` block**
The following text appears literally inside a `<style>` tag in `<head>`:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
This is server-side PHP that was not executed — it was included as literal text in the CSS block. CSS parsers will ignore it, so it does not break visual rendering, but it exposes a template assembly error, inflates HTML payload, and will trigger lint/validation failures. It should be removed.

**FAIL — Two images served over HTTP (mixed content)**
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

HTTPS email clients (Gmail, Outlook.com, Apple Mail) proxy or block non-HTTPS image sources. These images will likely not render for most recipients. Both are in the footer credit card banner section.

**WARN — 34 images missing alt text**
Affects all content slices (`slices_01` through `slices_07` in body, `slices_01` through `slices_18` in footer), all product images (bed, sheets, curtain rod, mattress), the `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` CC banner, and three tracking pixels. Since the email is image-sliced with no HTML text for core content, recipients with images disabled or using screen readers receive no meaningful information. The only alt text present in the truncated source is on the header logo (`alt="POTTERY BARN"`).

### 5. Compliance

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe` header not captured**
The QA relay did not capture a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Gmail and Yahoo require this header for bulk senders (>5k/day) under their 2024 sender requirements. If the header is present but stripped by the AgentMail relay, this is a monitoring gap; if it is genuinely absent, it is a compliance violation.

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not captured**
One-click unsubscribe header absent or not captured. Required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy since February 2024.

**WARN — `Authentication-Results` not captured**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status unknown. The sending domain `e.potterybarn.com` is an ESP subdomain (Salesforce Marketing Cloud pattern) — authentication is almost certainly configured, but the relay is not surfacing the results header for verification.

**CAN-SPAM — Physical address**
Not verifiable from the truncated source. Footer is image-sliced, so the mailing address (required by CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5)) may be embedded in a slice image rather than HTML text — making it unverifiable by automated checks and unreadable for screen readers.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

UTM parameters on body links cannot be confirmed without resolving the `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirects. The commented-out header banner used `cm_ite=` (Salesforce Commerce Cloud attribution) rather than standard UTM params — inconsistent with typical UTM-based analytics if the banner is ever re-enabled.

The open-tracking pixel domain is `svopn.potterybarn.com`; the click-tracking domain is `click.e.potterybarn.com` — two separate subdomains for open vs. click, which is expected for this ESP setup.
