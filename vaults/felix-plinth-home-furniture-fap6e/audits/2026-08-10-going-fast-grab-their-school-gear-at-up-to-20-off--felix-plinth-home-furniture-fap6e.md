# Going fast: grab their school gear at up to 20% off →
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-10**
## Executive summary

- Another Crate & Kids send, and this is genuinely the fifth or sixth family-branded email from this parent company in my inbox this week — I flagged the same fatigue on the "back-to-school 20% off" banner-vs-hero conflict email that landed the same day. This one's at least internally consistent this time — backpacks, duffel bags, lunch boxes, water bottles, all "ships free" — but I don't have kids, so a monogrammed backpack wall does absolutely nothing for me no matter how well it's merchandised.
- The bottom half is where it gets messy: after the kids gear, it pivots to "1500+ new heritage-inspired finds," then TheDesignDesk sweepstakes for $5000, then a "we love these for you" grid that mixes a kids backpack with a nursery chair, a dresser, and a stroller — none of which reads like anything "for" a 36-year-old renovating two houses. Then a new Crate Card pitch, then an app download push. It's four different campaigns duct-taped into one email.
- Not clicking, not forwarding — this is a delete-without-scroll for me, and if it weren't attached to a brand I already shop, I'd consider unsubscribing from the kids sub-brand specifically.

## What's working

- The hero backpack wall is genuinely well-styled — good color/pattern variety, feels like real merchandising effort.
- "Ships Free" is repeated on every product tile, which is a real, honest, low-friction perk I'd want if I were buying this stuff.
- The room-scene photo for TheDesignDesk (the moody heritage living room) is the one visual in the whole email that actually looks like something I'd want in my renovation — Article's "Bedroom reset" send hit that same editorial register better and with less clutter around it.

## What's weak

- Zero relevance to my renovation focus until you hit TheDesignDesk section, which is buried after a full screen of kids products — CB2's "Andreas Hansen, Copenhagen" send had the same one-track problem, but at least it was on-brand for design-forward furniture, not diapers-to-dressers.
- The "we love these for you" grid is a random mashup — backpack, cooler bag, car seat, dresser, nursery chair, stroller — that's not personalization, that's just "kids and furniture" mashed into one module.
- Four separate CTAs competing (Shop Kids Gear, Shop New Arrivals, Book Free Appointment, Learn More on the card, Download Now) — no single clear next step, which Wayfair's "COVERS & CARRY BAGS" email also botched by cramming too many categories into one send.
- New Crate Card pitch feels tacked onto the very bottom with zero context on rewards math — no "X% back" or point structure visible, just "earning Rewards Dollars."

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into two emails: a kids-gear send for the parent segment, and a heritage/design-desk send for the renovation crowd — I'm getting punished for being on the wrong list.
- 2. Drop the "we love these for you" grid entirely or actually personalize it based on purchase history — right now it reads like a random SKU dump.
- 3. Move TheDesignDesk section up top if this email is going to non-parents at all — that's the only thing I'd act on.
- 4. Put actual reward math on the Crate Card pitch (e.g., "5% back on every purchase") instead of vague "Rewards Dollars" language.

- **Subject Alt B:** `1500+ new heritage finds just landed`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: enter to win a $5000 room refresh`
