# Up to 30% off: Accent chairs & tables!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-27**
## Executive summary

- This is the same Crate & Barrel sale email I got a few hours ago, in a new subject line. This morning's "Accent chairs, tables & rugs are on SALE" opened with the same Thanksgiving dining room event banner over the same Brighton Wool Ivory Rug at $2,579.20, down from $3,699. Now I'm getting it again with an exclamation point. Between this, the 10/26 organization email and the Thanksgiving styles email, I've had four C&B sends in about two days. That's the "same lamp in three brands' emails" feeling, except it's one brand repeating itself.
- The offer is clear. Up to 30% off accent chairs and tables, and up to 60% off rugs, is a real hook for me because I'm furnishing two houses. The bouclé-looking chair tile is exactly what I'd screenshot for Pinterest. But the scroll runs about 7,600 pixels. It goes from sale tiles to "more ways to complete your home," then bestselling sofas, then a TheDesignDesk sweepstakes, then a Reward Dollars credit card pitch, then an app download, then a second TheDesignDesk ad. By the bottom it has forgotten the accent chair offer from the subject.
- Would I click? Probably once, on the chair tile, and only because I'm already shopping. I'd save it and not forward it, because there's nothing new here.

## What's working

- The "up to 30% off accent chairs" and "up to 60% off rugs" tiles map directly onto the subject, so the promise is kept.
- The first screen is calm and editorial. The chair-over-rug photo feels like a catalog, which is what I want from C&B.
- The rug hero shows a real price ($2,579.20 against $3,699). I can do the math without clicking.
- The "we love these for you" grid at the bottom has actual product names and prices, like the Contour sofa and a 10-piece stainless cookware set. That's the useful part.
- The credit card block mentions Reward Dollars. I pick my card by rewards math, so I noticed it.

## What's weak

- It's the same hero and rug as this morning's send. That's cadence fatigue.
- The overlay labels on the tiles ("up to 30% off accent tables," "up to 60% off rugs") are small and sit on busy photos. I'd have trouble reading them on a phone.
- There are too many CTAs: Shop The Sale, Shop All Sale, Shop Now, Book Your Free Appointment (twice), Learn More and Download Now. The eye has no single place to land.
- The sweepstakes ("enter for a chance to win up to $5000") and the TheDesignDesk blocks are another brand's pitch inside a sale email. They read as filler.
- The "more ways to complete your home" section (lighting, quick ship furniture) shares one CTA, so I don't know where it goes. The sofa section adds a "built to last & arrives fast" claim with no delivery date or details.
- I can't see any deadline. "Limited time" isn't a date. Furniture shoppers need ship windows, and there's no sample or swatch cue either.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut it to one job. Keep the accent chairs, tables and rugs sale tiles with one deadline and one CTA, and move sofas, sweepstakes, app and credit card to other sends.
- 2. Don't reuse the hero within 24 hours. If the rug has to stay, put the chair tile first, since that's what the subject says.
- 3. Make the tile labels larger and put them on a solid band, not over the photo.
- 4. Add a real end date and a ship-window line like "In stock, ships by 11/15."
- 5. Sort by "what I can still get before Thanksgiving." Pottery Barn did this with the "Furniture in stock & on its way FAST" angle. Crate has quick ship furniture and buries it.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary

The substantive findings are about 30 images with no `alt` attribute and some viewport and text-size settings that limit accessibility. The header warnings are probably relay artifacts and can't be confirmed from this data.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Links unverified:** 64 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe, so destination status, redirect chains and broken links are unknown.
- **Third-party pixels:** five `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=56aaa278…&n=1` through `n=5` requests go to a third-party domain (rlcdn.com). Each carries the same recipient-specific `s=` hash.
- **First-party pixels:** `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif?mi_u=…` and `https://dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/679d81f6-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…` are open-tracking pixels.
- **Recipient ID in URLs:** the `mi_u=56aaa2787a67…` value is embedded in image URLs, so the recipient identifier appears in image requests.
- **No dead links confirmed:** none of this is a broken link. The links just couldn't be tested.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (about 31 images).** The QA check flagged these groups:
  - 16 content images on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/…` and `/m/47/bbf6f9d4-….png`. These need descriptive `alt`.
  - 7 images on `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png`, such as `560d850e7e517ede.png`. These look like product-recommendation images, so they also need descriptive `alt`.
  - 7 tracking pixels (5× `rlcdn.com/448796.gif`, `o.gif`, `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/679d81f6-…`). These should carry `alt=""` so screen readers skip them.
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="… maximum-scale=1" />` prevents pinch-zoom.
- **Text resize blocked:** `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; …}` and `body{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` disable text scaling. The same file also sets `100%` on `body`, so the declarations conflict.
- **Invalid markup:** `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` uses an unescaped `&`, which is invalid under the XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype.
- **Duplicate tags:** `format-detection` is declared three times, once combined and twice individually.
- **Overlapping media queries:** `@media (min-width:768px){img{max-width:600px}}` and `@media (min-width:640px){img{max-width:640px}}` both match at 768px and wider. The later rule wins, so the 600px cap never applies.
- **Template leftover:** the comment `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` is still in the source.
- **Present and fine:** `lang="en"`, the Outlook VML/`OfficeDocumentSettings` blocks and `color-scheme` meta tags.
