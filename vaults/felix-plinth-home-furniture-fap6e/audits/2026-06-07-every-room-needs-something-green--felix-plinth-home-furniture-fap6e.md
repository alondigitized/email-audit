# Every room needs something green
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-two. We are at twenty-two CB2 emails since mid-May, and I need that number sitting on the table before I say a single word about this one. The fatigue is real. But — and this is grudging — the creative actually earns a second look. The green velvet sofa in that moody, shadow-heavy hero is exactly the kind of thing I've been hunting for the second home's living room: deep forest tones, elevated without being precious, the kind of piece that reads differently from every CB2 email I've seen this week. Editorial-first, not discount-first. That's a deliberate choice and I respect it.
- What I don't respect is the lack of a hook below the fold. This reads as a pure browse email — no sale, no urgency, no member pricing — which means CB2 is burning one of my remaining goodwill opens on pure brand ambiance. After the MIDSEASON SALE blast two days ago and "Fine dining. Free design advice." yesterday, I expected a more concrete reason to shop. Instead, they're giving me a mood board. Beautiful mood board. But still.
- I'd mark this read and revisit it on desktop later if that hero sofa product page is live. I wouldn't forward it — the friend I'd send this to is already on the CB2 list and has probably already deleted it.

## What's working

- **The hero sofa earns it.** Deep forest-green velvet, dark walnut legs, moody ambient photography — it doesn't look like the same lamp that showed up in three brands' inboxes this week. It looks specific.
- **Color discipline is tight.** Sage, forest green, warm amber accents — the palette holds from hero to grid, nothing clashes. Wayfair couldn't do this in a hundred years.
- **Editorial hierarchy is clear.** Eye goes hero → feature product → supporting grid, no question about where to start.
- **Subject/creative alignment is unusually honest.** "Every room needs something green" and then — green things. Blessedly literal. Compare to Crate & Kids' "You're invited! A 100% free, no-pressure design consult" which made me feel gaslit.
- **No render bugs visible.** Clean at this viewport, images loaded, text readable.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer.** No discount, no free shipping threshold, no member pricing. After MIDSEASON SALE ran two days ago, sending me a pure editorial with no transactional hook feels like a waste of frequency.
- **CTA copy is generic from what I can read.** "Shop Now" or equivalent — not "Shop the Velvet Sofa" or "See the Green Edit." Article's outdoor email last week had sharper verb-noun CTA copy on the same aesthetic register.
- **Cadence has broken trust.** Twenty-two emails in roughly three weeks. This one could be a 9/10 creative and it still has to fight through accumulated CB2 fatigue. The brand is spending equity it doesn't have anymore.
- **No product detail.** Dimensions, fabric grades, lead times for furniture — none of that is surfaced. For a sofa in the hero position, that's the only thing that would make me click instead of pin-and-forget.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the hero sofa to a specific offer or urgency signal.** Even "Ships in 2 weeks" or "New arrival — limited first run" turns a browse email into an action email. The creative is strong enough to carry a real ask.
- 2. **Sharpen the CTA copy.** "Shop the Green Velvet Sofa" or "See dimensions + swatches" — something that respects that I know what I want and just need the next step.
- 3. **Surface one piece of member context.** Even "Earn X points on this" would've reminded me I have a CB2 account with purchase history. Pottery Barn does this consistently in their warehouse sale emails; CB2 keeps forgetting I'm a known customer.
- 4. **Pull back cadence.** One less email this week and this one lands with more weight.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue A — Unremoved template placeholder (HIGH)**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
This is a production build artifact. The word "IMPUT" is also a typo for "INPUT." This comment is visible in raw source to any recipient who inspects the email. Remove before send.

**Issue B — Internal version tag leaked**
```html
<!--V2-->
```
Internal versioning comment rendered at the top of the document. Cosmetically harmless, but leaks internal workflow metadata.

**Issue C — `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom (MEDIUM)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) requires content to be resizable without assistive technology. This is a compliance risk for accessibility.

**Issue D — Global text-size-adjust suppression (MEDIUM)**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```
Applying `text-size-adjust: none` to the universal selector (`*`) overrides OS-level text scaling for visually impaired users. Scope this to `body` only, or remove it — the `body`-level rule above it already sets both values to `100%`.

**Issue E — Mobile breakpoint is too narrow (LOW)**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { ... }
```
414px targets iPhone SE/6/7/8. Most 2024–2026 Android flagships have viewport widths of 430–480px. This breakpoint misses a large segment of the Android audience; they receive the desktop layout on a phone screen.
