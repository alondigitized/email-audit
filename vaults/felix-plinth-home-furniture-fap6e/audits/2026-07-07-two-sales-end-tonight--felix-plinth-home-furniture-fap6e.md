# TWO SALES END TONIGHT
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- Thirteen. I made it to thirteen CB2 emails in seven days and here we are — "TWO SALES END TONIGHT" arriving the same afternoon as "HOURS LEFT: JULY 4TH SALE" already hit my inbox. The subject at least tells me something new: there are *two* sales. That's marginally more information than email twelve gave me. The countdown timer inside is showing under eight hours, which creates real pressure — but the hero image is wine glasses and crystal decanters for a "tabletop" category I could not care less about when I'm sourcing sofas for two houses simultaneously. The Warehouse Sale at 60% off buried below that? That's the actual email.
- Pottery Barn dropped a storage editorial on the same day — no countdown, no "JULY 4TH" anything — and I found it more actionable because it wasn't screaming. Article is four days into *their* July 4th sale cadence and already feels less exhausting than CB2's seven. CB2 is doing real damage to its own urgency currency here: when every email is "HOURS LEFT" the clock stops meaning anything.
- I'm marking this read and checking the Warehouse Sale link, but only because 60% off furniture is actually relevant to me. The tabletop hero might as well be from a different brand's list. I wouldn't forward this — I'd tell a friend to just go to cb2.com and skip the inbox entirely.

## What's working

- **Countdown timer is real** — under eight hours showing in the hero, which is the most credible urgency signal CB2 has sent in days. Unlike "HOURS LEFT" in the subject line, an actual ticking clock has specificity.
- **Two sales surfaces information** — subject line "TWO SALES END TONIGHT" is actually more useful than twelve previous variations; I didn't know there was a separate Warehouse Sale until this email.
- **Warehouse Sale gets its own section** — 60% off furniture with a "SHOP NOW" button has clear hierarchy and is the right offer for a two-home renovator like me.
- **"More For You" personalization** — the product grid at the bottom looks like actual furniture picks, which is more useful than another generic glassware shot.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, overlapping text, or layout collapses visible. CB2 at least executes technically.

## What's weak

- **The hero sells tabletop to a guy buying sofas** — wine glasses and crystal in the primary image slot are a segmentation miss. I'm in the catalog for furniture, not tabletop.
- **"JULY 4TH SALE" is still the name on July 7th** — the holiday was three days ago. Crate & Barrel called theirs "Warehouse Sale" and moved on; CB2 is still waving a flag nobody's looking at.
- **Email thirteen in seven days** — cadence has destroyed any sense of signal here. I'm conditioned to ignore CB2's subject lines now, which makes even the legitimate 60% off story feel like noise.
- **"SHOP NOW" is the laziest CTA in the industry** — Article wrote "Shop the Sale" with product categories linked. At minimum, "SHOP WAREHOUSE SALE" would tell me where I'm going.
- **No loyalty or member signal visible** — I'm clearly a known customer (personalized recs exist), but there's no "member price" or "Design Enthusiast" acknowledgment anywhere in the email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Warehouse Sale, not tabletop** — for a furniture-buying segment, 60% off furniture should be the hero image and the primary CTA. Flip the sections. The glassware imagery should be below the fold or cut entirely for this segment.
- 2. **Retire the July 4th name mid-campaign** — rename to "End-of-Season Sale" or "Warehouse Clearance" after July 5th. The holiday peg expired; the offer didn't have to.
- 3. **Make the CTA specific** — "SHOP WAREHOUSE SALE" or "SHOP FURNITURE" over the generic "SHOP NOW" on both blocks.
- 4. **Surface the "More For You" section higher** — personalized product picks are the most valuable real estate in this email and they're buried at the bottom. Bring them to mid-page, before the category grid.
- 5. **Reduce cadence or consolidate** — this email and "HOURS LEFT: JULY 4TH SALE" both hit today. One email per day during sale finales, max. Two on the same afternoon is self-defeating.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional table-based layout with acceptable ESP conventions. Three confirmed technical defects: malformed font asset URLs (double slashes), an unresolved template placeholder comment left in production HTML, and a viewport directive that breaks pinch-to-zoom accessibility.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully assess** — the HTML is truncated before any `<a href>` body links appear. The following is flagged from what is visible:

- **Font URLs with double slashes** — three `@font-face` `src` URLs contain `//` path segments that are technically malformed. While most servers handle this via redirect, it adds a latency hop and can fail on strict CDNs:
  - `https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2`
  - `https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2`
  - `https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2`
- **Cross-domain font load** — `HNW05` font family is served from `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/` (four declarations). This is an inter-brand dependency; if that origin is unavailable or rate-limits, font rendering degrades silently. Should be served from `cb2.com` or a shared CDN.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **`maximum-scale=1` in viewport** — `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.
- **`-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` on `*`** — applied globally via the CSS reset. Combined with `maximum-scale=1`, this double-locks text scaling on iOS. Standard practice is `100%`, not `none`.
- **Mobile breakpoint at 418px only** — media query triggers at `max-width: 418px` and sets `.mobile { width: 414px }`. This misses the most common modern viewport widths (375px iPhone SE, 390px iPhone 14 Pro, 430px iPhone 15 Plus). The fixed 414px width inside a max-418px query is a narrow window that may clip on some devices.
- **`max-height:1000000px` on `*`** — Gmail clip workaround hack. Functional, but confirms the email body may be long enough to hit Gmail's ~102KB clip threshold. If the full HTML exceeds 102KB, Gmail will clip the message and hide the unsubscribe footer, which is a compliance risk.
- **Alt text** — not assessable from truncated source. Must verify all `<img>` tags carry descriptive `alt` attributes.
