# Memorial Day Sale ⏳ Time is running out!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-eight. I've now opened and reviewed twenty-eight Pottery Barn Memorial Day sends since Sunday, and I need you to sit with that number for a moment.
- To the email's credit: the mechanics are solid. Red hero, "Extra 20% off" above the fold, "ENDS TOMORROW" urgency strip right below the logo — it's hitting its marks. And the "Recommended Just for You" section actually earns its name: a platform bed, a dining table, a few case pieces I've genuinely been shopping for the second home. That personalization block does more work than anything in the subject line.
- But here's where I land: I could have sketched this layout before I opened it. Same red hero, same percentage, same hourglass emoji in a trench coat. If Pottery Barn had sent one excellent, fully-merchandised Memorial Day furniture email instead of twenty-eight, I would have read every word and clicked three times. At this cadence, I'm on autopilot. I'm marking it read without clicking, and I'm not forwarding it to anyone.

## What's working

- "ENDS TOMORROW" above the logo fold is the right placement — urgency before I even hit the hero.
- "Extra 20% off" plus "new deals up to 40% off" gives a range without feeling bait-and-switch; that dual-tier framing is honest.
- The "Recommended Just for You" section surfaced a platform bed and a dining table — two items I'm actively shopping — which means the personalization engine is actually working.
- Category nav (FURNITURE / OUTDOOR / SOFAS & CHAIRS / BEDDING) is immediately scannable; I can skip straight to what I care about without reading a word of copy.
- "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" is visible — a loyalty signal that Wayfair buries in the footer and CB2 skips entirely.

## What's weak

- "Time is running out!" is the laziest urgency copy in the playbook. No date, no time, no hard stop. Crate & Barrel's "ends TOMORROW" phrasing from their send earlier this week was sharper and required fewer words.
- The subject line buries the offer entirely — no percentage, no product, no dollar figure. After twenty-seven prior sends, I've been conditioned to expect the number up front. Not finding it is a quiet disappointment.
- The "More Deals from Our Family of Brands" block drops right in the middle of the email and breaks momentum. I know Pottery Barn and Williams Sonoma are related. Rejuvenation mid-funnel reads like an ad insertion, not a curated recommendation.
- Product tiles in the recommendation block show no pricing — no sale price, no "was/now." Wayfair shows the strikethrough and discount in the grid. I shouldn't have to click blind into a PDP to find out what "up to 40% off" actually means for a specific bed.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the percentage in the subject — "Extra 20% off ends tomorrow" would outperform "Time is running out!" in every A/B variant they've ever run. This is not a hypothesis.
- 2. Kill the Family of Brands cross-promo block or drop it to the footer. I know how Williams Sonoma Holdings works. Surfacing Pottery Barn Teen mid-scroll when I'm shopping for a dining table sends me sideways.
- 3. Show at-a-glance sale pricing on the personalized product tiles — even a simple "was $X / now $Y" strip. Article does this correctly; it's table stakes at this point.
- 4. Pick one headline: it's either a 20% send or a "up to 40% off" send. Running both simultaneously dilutes both.
- 5. Cap the cadence to one email per day during sale weekends. Non-negotiable.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 20% off ends tomorrow — Memorial Day Sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Memorial Day picks: up to 40% off, final day`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New deals added. Furniture, outdoor, bedding — shop before midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Beds, tables, sofas — up to 40% off. This is the last one.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard table-based ESP-sent email (Salesforce Marketing Cloud via `e.potterybarn.com`). Two confirmed defects require immediate attention: orphaned PHP server code injected into a CSS block, and an empty `<title>` element. Truncated source prevents full compliance verification.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**UTM visibility — cannot confirm.** All clicks route through `click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=<opaque-base64>`. UTM parameters, if present, are appended server-side by the ESP redirect. No UTM params are visible in the raw `href` values, so attribution depends entirely on the redirect chain functioning correctly. If the ESP redirect ever breaks, all click attribution is lost with no fallback.

**Image host path includes "2016":**
```
https://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2016/nav/nav0.jpg
```
Likely a static asset path that has never been updated. Not a functional issue, but worth flagging as a long-term CDN dependency risk.

**Source truncated** — cannot audit all CTAs for broken or duplicate tracking parameters.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical: PHP header() calls injected into a `<style>` block.** Lines inside the third `<style>` tag are orphaned server-side PHP, not CSS:

```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```

This is a template assembly error — PHP output from an image-serving script was written into the wrong output buffer. Email clients silently discard invalid CSS, so it does not break rendering, but it indicates a broken build pipeline and the same defect may have hit other recipients or campaigns.

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some clients (Gmail web, Apple Mail) surface the title in tab or notification UI. Empty is a missed opportunity at minimum; in some clients it displays as a blank string next to the subject line.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 violation for users with low vision.

**Global `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`:**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applied via the `*` selector, this disables system font scaling across the entire email. Conflicts with WCAG 1.4.4.

**Hardcoded 414 px mobile breakpoint:**
```css
.showmobile { width:414px !important; }
```
iPhone 12/13/14 width. Clips or misaligns on smaller Android devices (~360 px viewport) and newer large-display phones.

**`role="presentation"` on layout tables** — correctly applied. No issues.
