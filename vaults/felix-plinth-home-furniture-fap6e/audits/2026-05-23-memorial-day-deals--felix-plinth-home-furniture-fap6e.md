# 🥳 🙌 🛒 Memorial Day DEALS 🥳 🙌 🛒
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- Fourteen. I need that number on the table before anything else. This is the fourteenth Wayfair email since roughly Sunday, and whoever controls the send button at Wayfair decided that what the previous thirteen lacked was *more emoji*. Six of them. In the subject line. Symmetrically arranged. I had to actually look twice to confirm it wasn't a phishing attempt that had grabbed their logo.
- Here's what makes this frustrating: I'm genuinely in the market. Two active renovations, outdoor furniture on the punch list, faucets still TBD. The 70% off tier and the "buy one Adirondack chair, get one 25% off" doorbuster down in the third section — those are real line items for me. But Wayfair has sent so many of these that my finger goes to archive before the preview even loads. The knife sets one (thirteen) and the "#kitchenmats" one (also thirteen/fourteen range) conditioned that response. I'm Pavlov's dog but the bell sounds like a promotional email.
- Compare this to what Crate & Barrel did with "This is it! Final weekend for 2x Rewards" — that landed like a closing event. Pottery Barn's "Weekend plans?" felt like a friend texting. This feels like someone set a schedule to fire and walked away. I'd mark as read without clicking. I wouldn't forward it; I'd be embarrassed to.

## What's working

- The doorbuster label — "MEMORIAL DAY EXCLUSIVE" on the Adirondack buy-one deal is the one moment of specificity in this email, and it's the only section I actually lingered on
- Discount tiers (40%, 50%, 70%) are all above the fold on mobile — no digging required
- Star ratings on the "picked just for you" product tiles add credibility without copy bloat
- Clean render throughout — no broken images, no layout chaos, text stays legible at a glance
- The "picked just for you" header at least gestures at personalization, even if the product mix doesn't prove it

## What's weak

- Six emojis in the subject line — 🥳 🙌 🛒 twice over — is what the spam folder looks like when it's trying to be festive; Crate & Barrel closed out their entire Memorial Day sale with zero subject emojis and scored better
- Fourteen sends in four days means even a 70%-off offer reads as routine; the knife sets had a concrete category, the Adirondack chair has a specific mechanic, and both were buried in the noise Wayfair created for itself
- "Shop all sales" is the laziest possible CTA — Article sent a brand-building email with no discount at all and still felt more clickable because the copy was intentional
- No rewards or card math anywhere in the email — I buy on whichever card maximizes return, and "40% off" is only half the calculation for me
- "Big-name brands, bigger savings" heading with no actual brand names visible in the body copy is the furniture equivalent of a clickbait headline

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the subject-line emojis and write one concrete offer.** Pick the best number, attach it to a category, give it a deadline date. One line, no decoration.
- 2. **Move the Adirondack doorbuster to the hero position.** "Buy one, get one 25% off" is a specific mechanic that requires thought — it belongs at the top where I'm still paying attention, not in row three after I've already seen four generic discount tiles.
- 3. **Add a hard deadline in the hero.** "Ends midnight Monday" beats "Memorial Day" when you've been running this since Thursday and the customer has no idea how much time is left.
- 4. **Surface rewards math.** One line: "Stack with your Wayfair Rewards card for up to X% back on outdoor furniture." Turns a price email into a loyalty email for a meaningful segment.
- 5. **Throttle to two sends for a holiday weekend maximum.** The opener Thursday, the closer Sunday. Everything in between is list fatigue that shows up in unsubscribes a week later.
