# Your table linen refresh starts here
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- Three. That's the Pottery Barn count today, and I'm tracking. The outdoor furniture sale landed first — the 50%-off email I'd have bookmarked even if I didn't need anything — then the Coastal Washed Quilt hit me before lunch. Now table linens. I don't hate the category. I genuinely need placemats and runners for two houses, and this is the most focused send of the three: one department, three clean subcategories, sensible CTAs. But Pottery Barn has burned through my daily engagement budget. Whatever click I might have given this got spent upstream.
- The email renders cleanly and is visually organized. The "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" block pulls in what looks like bedding shots I recognize from the Coastal Washed Quilt send — either the algorithm cross-pollinated or someone recycled an asset set. At send #3 today it reads as copy-paste regardless of intent.
- Mark as read. Possible revisit Sunday when I'm mentally in "second home dining room" mode, but I'm not clicking today.

## What's working

- Category breakdown — Table Runners, Placemats, Runners as three distinct SHOP NOW tiles — mirrors how I actually think through a table setting purchase. Useful structure.
- "SHOP ALL TABLE LINENS" as a consolidating bottom CTA is smart; gives me an exit ramp if none of the three tiles speak to me.
- Visual hierarchy is clean: hero leads, subcategories follow, recommendations trail. Nothing competes for attention.
- Loyalty and financing mention ("Earn 10% Back in Rewards" + Affirm) is the right trust stack near checkout — I run rewards math before every purchase, and I appreciate the reminder.
- Clean render across every section visible — no broken images, no overlapping text, nothing that screams "production miss."

## What's weak

- Third Pottery Barn email today. Even if the category is relevant, three sends in one day is how I build muscle memory for the unsubscribe button.
- Aesthetic skews traditional/feminine — heavy floral tablecloths and classic color stories dominate the hero. My primary and second home are both heading toward modern-transitional; this feels like it was built for my mother's holiday table, not my dining room. CB2 manages to show the same product types without looking like a 1998 catalog.
- No offer. No percentage off, no free shipping, no threshold discount. It's a pure catalog browse in a moment when every other brand in my inbox has some form of a hook. Wayfair at least tried to anchor with a number in the subject line (even if the email didn't deliver on it).
- "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" shows bedding, not table accessories. If the algorithm fired because I browsed the quilt, it picked the wrong signal — serve me napkin rings and chargers, not something from a different email I already mentally filed.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cap daily sends at two.** If outdoor furniture and table linens both have to go out May 30th, stagger them 36–48 hours or bundle into a single "Room by Room" digest. Three emails in one calendar day is cadence abuse regardless of how good the content is.
- 2. **Give the subject a reason, not just a destination.** "Starts here" positions the email as a starting point with no incentive to start today. Even "New patterns just landed" or a price anchor would add pull.
- 3. **Lead the hero with a modern/transitional option.** One contemporary option up front widens the audience without alienating the traditional buyer — just don't make floral patterns the entire visual story.
- 4. **Tie the "Recommended" block to the email's department.** Quilts do not belong in a table linen send; chargers, napkin rings, and centerpiece accessories do.
- 5. **Make the rewards feel exclusive.** "Earn 10% back" is the standard rate — a send-specific bump ("12% back through Sunday for Key Rewards members") would create urgency without fabricating a sale.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New table linen arrivals — runners, placemats & more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Set the table in either house: linens from $XX`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Runners, placemats & tablecloths in new seasonal patterns`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Key Rewards members earn 10% back on every table linen order`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP code in `<style>` block**
The following PHP `header()` calls appear literally inside the client `<style>` block and will be emitted as raw text to the CSS parser:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
This is a template/build error. It does not execute, but it corrupts the stylesheet and may cause unexpected rendering artifacts in strict parsers.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` — blank title can cause issues in some preview and tab environments.

**45 images missing `alt` text**
All sliced content images (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_13.jpg` in both the body and footer), all product recommendation images, both bottom banner images, and all three open/tracking pixels lack `alt` attributes. This breaks screen reader access to the entire email body and triggers accessibility flags in Outlook and Apple Mail.

**3 images served over HTTP (mixed content)**
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

All three will be blocked or display broken in Gmail and most modern clients due to mixed-content policy.

**Open pixel served over HTTP**
`http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` — the open-tracking pixel uses plain HTTP, which will be blocked or proxied by Gmail Image Proxy, invalidating open-rate data for Gmail recipients.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html

```
Prevents user zoom on mobile — an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4).

### 5. Compliance

The AgentMail relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. If absent from the actual SMTP envelope, this violates Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (enforced since 2024) and may cause deliverability penalties.

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not captured (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe is unverifiable. Required by Gmail/Yahoo for senders above 5k/day threshold.

**WARN — `Authentication-Results` not captured**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed. The sending domain `e.potterybarn.com` should have all three configured; inability to verify through the relay is a monitoring gap, not necessarily a sender-side failure.

**WARN — Plain-text body is 70.7% URLs**
9,924 of 14,044 characters in the plain-text part are URL strings. Spam filters (SpamAssassin, Proofpoint) penalize high URL-to-text ratios in plain-text alternatives.

**`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />`**
Referrer suppression is intentional for privacy, but confirms that any analytics relying on HTTP `Referer` headers at the landing page will receive no upstream referrer signal — UTM params in the click URL must carry the full attribution load.
