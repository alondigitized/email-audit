# Win a $1,500 Pottery Barn gift card
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-two. That's where I am with Pottery Barn in July, and this one lands on the same Sunday where they already sent me a digital catalog an hour ago. If the catalog felt like someone trying too hard, this one feels like a completely different department forgot I exist. "Win $1,500 Toward Newlywed Must-Haves" — I'm thirty-six and renovating two houses simultaneously. I am not a newlywed. I am the exact opposite of someone building a first registry. I'm someone *dismantling* accumulated stuff from two prior moves and trying to make deliberate, high-spend choices about what actually stays.
- The sweepstakes mechanic itself isn't inherently offensive — a free $1,500 toward Pottery Barn is real money and I'd spend it in forty minutes on upholstery alone. But the framing buries me. The entire middle section of this email is registry education: how to use the checklist, the complimentary app, free expert advice. I know all of this. I'm *on* their list already. The "Recommended Just For You" grid at the bottom is the only part that looks like they know who I am, and it's been pushed below two full modules of content aimed at a 27-year-old who just got engaged.
- Mark as read. If this same $1,500 sweepstakes had landed in my inbox without the newlywed wrapper, I'd have clicked to enter in under five seconds. The prize is real; the targeting is not.

## What's working

- **The prize is genuinely compelling** — $1,500 is a concrete, meaningful number and it's the first thing your eye hits in the hero. No vagueness, no "up to."
- **Clean layout, no render bugs** — text is sharp, images load, hierarchy reads top-to-bottom without any of the layout chaos that plagued the July 18 double-send.
- **Rewards callout at the bottom** — the 10% back in rewards reminder is buried but present; as a rewards card guy I at least register it.
- **"Recommended Just For You" module** — beds, linens, and what appear to be nightstands in the product grid at the bottom. That's actually useful given I'm cross-shopping nightstands right now (Wayfair's Black Friday in July email from the same day hit this far harder and earlier in their layout).

## What's weak

- **The entire newlywed framing is wrong for me** — "Newlywed Must-Haves" and "Start Building Your Registry" assume I'm pre-purchase, not a repeat buyer mid-renovation. Wayfair would never send me a first-registry explainer.
- **Two Pottery Barn emails in one morning** — the catalog landed earlier today. This cadence is broken and it makes both emails feel cheaper.
- **Registry tutorial content wastes prime scroll space** — the personalized checklist, app download, and free-advice module are three full content blocks aimed at a new customer. I've owned Pottery Barn furniture for a decade.
- **CTA after the sweepstakes is vague** — the button under the hero reads like it goes to a registry landing page, not directly to a sweepstakes entry form. That's friction I won't bother resolving.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Strip the newlywed wrapper entirely and let the prize speak to existing customers.** "You've got taste. Now win the budget to match it." That's a subject line for me, not a newlywed.
- 2. **Collapse the registry tutorial section for known customers.** I'm in their CRM. If I've made purchases, swap those three how-to blocks for product recommendations or a sweepstakes explainer with no-purchase-necessary copy.
- 3. **Lead the "Recommended Just For You" module higher** — move it directly under the sweepstakes hero. That grid earns its slot; the tutorial modules don't.
- 4. **Fix the cadence.** One email per day is already pushing it. Two emails before noon from the same brand is a mute trigger. CB2 somehow managed one clean send this week and it landed better.
- 5. **Make the CTA destination explicit** — "Enter the Sweepstakes" beats any generic "Shop Now" or ambiguous registry link.
- **Subject Alt A:** `You could win $1,500. No registry required.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$1,500 in Pottery Barn. Enter to win — no catch.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ends soon — one entry, real prize, real budget for your home.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Pick what you'd spend it on first. Then enter.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP header() calls leaked into `<style>` block (template render error):**

```css
/* inside <style> tag, not inside a PHP file context */

header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```

These are server-side PHP directives that were never executed — raw source leaked into the CSS block. They are parsed as invalid CSS and silently ignored by email clients, but their presence confirms a template assembly failure upstream.
