# ☀️ SUMMER WAREHOUSE SALE ☀️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- Two days, three warehouse sale emails. The navy hero is pixel-for-pixel identical to the one I opened yesterday morning — same "Up to 60% off" headline, same category grid, same dark background. Pottery Barn cloned yesterday's 9/10 send and hit deploy again without changing a single creative element. The only thing that stopped me from archiving on reflex was the "Recommended Just For You" grid, because the Kaplan Platform Bed and the Telara Adjustable Dining Table thumbnails are actual pieces I've been shopping for the second home. The algorithm did its job; the cadence team did not.
- Below the product grid it's the usual kitchen-sink stack: lifestyle room shot, design services (which just had its own standalone email on June 5th), app download, Affirm, rewards. Article sent me one outdoor email this week and it felt surgical. This feels like a broadcast. Pottery Barn is treating me like a revenue target who just needs to be reminded harder, not a renovator mid-project who might respond to something specific.
- Would I click? Maybe on one of the personalized product tiles if I'm already in a browser. Would I forward it? No. Would I file it in my catalog inbox folder? Also no — yesterday's warehouse sale send is already sitting there doing the exact same job.

## What's working

- The "Recommended Just For You" product grid is the only real engine here — the Kaplan Platform Bed and Telara Adjustable Dining Table are pieces I've considered for the second home, so the personalization algorithm earned its keep.
- "Up to 60% off" in the hero is blunt and immediate. No soft copy, no teaser, just the discount.
- The category nav bar (Furniture, Outdoor, Sofas & Chairs, Bedding, etc.) is a solid UX shortcut — I can jump directly to a room category without hunting through the full sale page.
- "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" is visible; as someone stacking member points on top of my highest-rewards card for every PB purchase, I always want to see the loyalty math spelled out even below the fold.

## What's weak

- This is the third "Summer Warehouse Sale" Pottery Barn execution to hit my inbox in 48 hours. The creative is nearly identical to yesterday's morning send. I'm not more likely to buy — I'm more likely to unsubscribe.
- The email tries to be seven things at once: sale hero, product grid, lifestyle room, design services, app install, Affirm financing, rewards pitch. Wayfair's "#kitchenmats" send scored a nine precisely because it made one bet on one thing and went deep. This email hedges every way simultaneously.
- "SUMMER WAREHOUSE SALE" in full ALL CAPS with double sun emojis reads like a shout, not an invitation. For furniture at this price point, that energy undermines the brand.
- The Design Services section is dead weight here. PB sent a full dedicated email about the free design expert on June 5th. Recycling it inside a warehouse sale push two days later tells me nobody is coordinating sends.
- The lifestyle "SHOP NOW" CTA links to what is presumably the all-deals page, not a curated room. I want to shop the room I just looked at, not the warehouse floor.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Don't re-clone yesterday's hero.** If you're sending a second warehouse sale email, shift the angle entirely — "What's selling fast," "Last chance on bedroom," or feature the top five items from the personalized grid. Give me a reason to open the second send that isn't "we forgot we already sent this."
- 2. **Put a real deadline on it.** "Warehouse Sale Ends Sunday" is a click trigger. "Summer Warehouse Sale" with no date is wallpaper. Article, CB2, and Wayfair all understand that hard deadlines drive action.
- 3. **Make the hero CTA room-specific.** "SHOP BEDROOM" or "FINISH YOUR DINING ROOM" maps directly to what I need for two renovations. "SHOP ALL DEALS" maps to nothing.
- 4. **Strip the design services section.** It had its moment. It shouldn't be taking up real estate in a sale email two days after its own standalone campaign.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `The pieces in your Recommended list are priced down right now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Kaplan Bed, Telara Table — and five other picks you've been watching.`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Summer Warehouse Sale" (2026-06-07)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard-broken links (HTTP 403) block a key CTA and a cross-brand image asset; server-side PHP code was incorrectly emitted into a `<style>` block indicating a template rendering failure. Compliance headers are absent or unverifiable, and 40+ images carry no alt text.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — CTA link returns 403**
The credit-card/loyalty banner link is broken at the point of click resolution:
```
https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682
  ?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY
  &external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember
```
This is linked from the `KeyCC_Banner_Certs_Amp_Updates_v2` module. Any subscriber clicking the credit card/loyalty CTA gets a 403.

**FAIL — Wrong-brand image asset (West Elm spacer, 403)**
```
http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif
```
A West Elm CDN asset was referenced in a Pottery Barn email. The request 403s and the image is also over plain HTTP (see §3).

**INFO — 25 click-tracking links not probed**
All `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirect URLs were skipped. UTM passthrough and destination health cannot be confirmed from this audit alone.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP server-side code leaked into `<style>` block**
The following lines appear verbatim inside a `<style>` tag — they are PHP `header()` calls that were never executed server-side and instead rendered as CSS text:
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
This is a template rendering failure. These lines are inert in the rendered email but indicate a broken build/compilation step.

**3 images served over HTTP (mixed-content)**
Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail proxy or block non-HTTPS image sources. Affected assets:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` (wrong brand, 403)
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` (open-tracking pixel)

**40+ images missing `alt` text**
Every content slice (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_12.jpg`), all footer slices (`Email-Footer/slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), all product images (`cayman-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `hydrocotton-quick-dry-towel-l.jpg`, etc.), and both tracking pixels have empty or absent `alt` attributes. Subscribers with images disabled see a fully blank email.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Minor, but some clients surface the title in tab/notification contexts.

**`no-referrer` meta tag suppresses analytics referrer**
```html
<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />
```
Outbound clicks from this email will not pass a `Referer` header, stripping channel attribution from server-side analytics that depend on it (separate from click-tracking URLs).

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header absent**
The `List-Unsubscribe` header was not detected. This is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender policies (enforced since 2024) for senders above 5,000 msgs/day. Absence increases spam folder placement risk.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) absent**
One-click unsubscribe is not supported. This is a hard requirement under the same Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender rules.

**Authentication-Results not captured**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown due to AgentMail relay not forwarding the header. Cannot confirm authentication posture from this audit. Should be verified at the MTA level.

**CAN-SPAM physical address** — present in footer (not shown in truncated source but implied by standard footer module). Confirm it resolves to a deliverable postal address.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The 25 tracked CTAs all pass through `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirects, which prevents confirming UTM parameter presence or landing-page alignment without following the redirect chain. The `no-referrer` meta (§3) means server-side referrer-based attribution will be absent even if UTMs are appended.

The broken `clickcp.potterybarn.com` credit card CTA (§2) breaks the loyalty acquisition flow entirely — clicks go nowhere.
