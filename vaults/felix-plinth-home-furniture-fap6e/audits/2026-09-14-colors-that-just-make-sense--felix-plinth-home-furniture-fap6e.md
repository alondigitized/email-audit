# Colors that just make sense
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-14**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "Colors That Just Make Sense" — this is CB2 pivoting from selling me furniture to selling me their *design service*, and it's the sixth email from this brand family in my inbox this week (I'm counting CB2 and Crate & Barrel together at this point, my brain has merged them into one entity). The "Design Math: The Color Equation" hero with olive/turmeric, yellow/denim, jade/oxblood swatch pairings is genuinely the most interesting visual idea any home brand has sent me lately — it's basically a mood board I didn't ask for but kind of want.
- The problem is there's no offer here. It's a soft-sell for a free design consultation, buried under three separate room-inspiration grids (living room, lighting, dining room, decor, bedroom, rugs), then a swatch program CTA, then loyalty perks at the bottom. It's well-designed but it's asking me to book an appointment or order swatches, not buy anything today, and after CB2's "THE LUXURY OF CUSTOM" swatch pitch two days ago (2026-09-13) and "Grand entrances" yesterday, this is starting to feel like the same three ideas on rotation.
- I wouldn't click today — no urgency, no discount, and I'm mid-scroll fatigue on this brand. I might screenshot the color-pairing graphic and drop it in my reno folder though, that part's a keeper.

## What's working

- The "Design Math / Color Equation" hero graphic with actual paint-chip-style swatch pairs (olive+turmeric, yellow+denim, jade+oxblood) is exactly the kind of decision-paralysis-solving content I'm renovating two houses and actually want.
- "2 Ways to Meet: In Store or Online" directly answers a real question I have about whether design help means a showroom visit or just a Zoom call.
- The room-category grid (Living Room, Lighting, Dining Room, Decor & Mirrors, Bedroom, Rugs) is clean and scannable — good use of white space, no clutter.
- "EARN 10% BACK IN Rewards Dollars every day on purchases of CB2 and Crate & Barrel" — that's useful, concrete loyalty math, unlike Wayfair's messy "picked for you" grid that buries everything.
- Swatch CTA ("Order 10 free swatches") is a real, no-cost way to reduce my color-matching anxiety across two houses.

## What's weak

- Zero price, zero percentage, zero dollar figure anywhere in this email — after CB2's own "Up to 30% off" sale two days ago, this feels like a step backward into pure branding with nothing to act on today.
- This is the third CB2/C&B swatch-or-custom pitch I've seen this week (13th: "THE LUXURY OF CUSTOM," now this) — the swatch message is repeating faster than I can act on it.
- The design-service CTA ("Book Your Free Design Appointment") appears twice but there's no indication of wait time, availability, or what a session actually costs me in time — for a guy juggling two renovations simultaneously, that's the actual blocker.
- No swatch/shipping specifics for furniture — no delivery windows like Pottery Barn's "Delivered in 1-4 weeks" callout, which is the one piece of practical info I actually need mid-reno.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real incentive in the hero — even "Book a free design session, get $50 off your first order" would convert this from inspiration into action. Right now there's nothing pulling me past looking.
- 2. Cut the swatch CTA out of this email entirely — it was the whole point of Sunday's send. Let each email own one job instead of restating three.
- 3. Add a one-line note under "Book Your Free Design Appointment" clarifying turnaround time (e.g., "sessions available this week") — removes the biggest friction for someone managing two projects at once.
- 4. Move the 10% Rewards Dollars callout up near the top — that's the most concrete, differentiating fact in the whole email and it's stuck in the footer.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Free design help, no swatches required`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Book a free session — in-store or online, this week`

## Technical Audit

1. Technical Summary

CB2 promotional email using standard MSO/Outlook + webkit table-based markup. Automated QA shows a 57% pass rate driven primarily by legacy ad-redirect image tags served over unencrypted HTTP and widespread missing alt attributes; unsubscribe/auth headers could not be confirmed due to relay limitations.

2. Link & Tracking Issues

- 61 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the automated HTTP probe (non-standard redirect domains) — link destinations were not validated and should be manually verified before send.
- 10 legacy Dotomi ad-redirect pixels load over plain `http://` (non-HTTPS): `http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?...`, `http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2437/pub1.php` through `pub10.php`. Mixed-content HTTP resources are commonly blocked or stripped by modern mail clients (Gmail, Outlook.com proxy images), which can silently break these tracking pixels.
- First-party tracking pixels (`https://mi.cb2.com/p/up/.../o.gif`, `https://dv.cb2.com/o/...`) are served over HTTPS — no issue.

3. Rendering & Accessibility

- 12+ content/product images are missing `alt` attributes, including all images under `image.mail.cb2.com/lib/fe9213727564027a72/m/1/` (e.g., `37784b8d-9ef1-4527-9117-252e45e157f5.jpg`, `f3367084-f19a-4f94-b731-52c5f7725e9b.jpg`, and 8 others). This fails WCAG 1.1.1 and degrades the experience for screen-reader users and when images are blocked by default (common in Outlook/Gmail).
- The 10 Dotomi HTTP tracking pixels and `UCMController`/`pub*.php` calls also lack `alt` text, though as 1x1 tracking pixels this is standard practice and low-impact.
- Markup includes legacy `<!--[if mso]>` / `<!--[if gte mso 9]>` conditional blocks and `.ReadMsgBody`/`.ExternalClass` resets — standard Outlook-compatibility patterns, no issue.
- `@font-face` sources reference `cb2.com`, `crateandbarrel.com`, and (relative-protocol typo) `https://www.cb2.com//Styles/...` (double slash after domain) — not fatal but is a minor URL-construction defect worth fixing.

4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

- No merge tags or personalization tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `%%FIELD%%`) are visible in the truncated source. No issues found in the visible portion.

5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)

- `List-Unsubscribe` header not detected — QA notes this may be an artifact of the AgentMail relay rather than the sender omitting it; cannot confirm compliance either way from this data.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058, one-click unsubscribe) not detected — same relay caveat applies; if genuinely absent, this is a Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender requirement gap as of their 2024 policies.
- `Authentication-Results` header (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) not found — status unknown, again attributed to relay capture rather than confirmed failure.
- Visible in-body unsubscribe link/footer was not present in the truncated HTML provided — cannot confirm CAN-SPAM physical-address/unsubscribe-link presence from available source.

6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)

- Cannot confirm UTM parameter presence or landing-page alignment — the 61 tracking-redirect links were skipped by the probe and the truncated HTML does not expose final destination URLs. Recommend manual resolution of a sample of the redirect links to verify UTM tagging and that final landing pages match email content/promo.

7. Recommendations

- Replace the 10 `http://` Dotomi ad-redirect/pixel URLs with HTTPS equivalents (or drop them if the ad network no longer requires legacy non-secure endpoints) to prevent blocked mixed-content pixels.
- Add descriptive `alt` text to all 12 content images under `image.mail.cb2.com`; keep tracking pixels alt="" (empty, not missing) per accessibility convention.
- Fix the double-slash in the `https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/...` font URLs.
- Re-run QA against the raw outbound SMTP payload (not via the AgentMail relay) to get a definitive read on `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and `Authentication-Results` — current warnings are inconclusive, not confirmed failures.
- Manually resolve a sample of the 61 skipped tracking links to confirm they carry UTM parameters and route to matching, live landing pages.
