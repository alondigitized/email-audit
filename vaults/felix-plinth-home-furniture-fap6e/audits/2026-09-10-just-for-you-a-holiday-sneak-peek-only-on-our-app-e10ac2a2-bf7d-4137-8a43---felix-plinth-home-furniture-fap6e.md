# Just for you: a holiday sneak peek, only on our app!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-10**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids again — third "holiday sneak peek, only on our app" nursery blast I've scrolled past today, and this one's even less for me than the last two since I don't have kids and Crate & Kids has zero relevance to my two-home renovation. The forest-green kids' bedroom hero is genuinely pretty (someone on their photo team is good), but then it just keeps going — a design-consult sweepstakes, a Jellycat plush restock, a nursery furniture grid with $900-$1,500 gliders and cribs, and a credit card pitch, all stacked into one endless scroll. Nothing in here is a lamp, a rug, or a sectional I'm tracking across Pottery Barn and Wayfair.
- I'm marking this read and moving on. I wouldn't forward it — the one friend I'd send a nursery chair to isn't expecting a baby — and I definitely wouldn't click, since every CTA either wants me to download an app or book a "design pro" appointment for a room I'm not furnishing.

## What's working

- The hero bedroom photography is legitimately well-styled — that green wall and warm lighting look like something out of a design mag, not a stock catalog shot.
- The "enter for a chance to win $5000" appointment offer is a real, specific incentive, even if it's aimed at the wrong shopper.
- Layout is clean and scannable — no overlapping text, no broken images, clear section breaks the whole way down.

## What's weak

- This is a kids/nursery brand emailing a childless 36-year-old renovator — the demographic mismatch is total, not partial.
- "Holiday sneak peek, only on our app" is the exact framing I already flagged on the last two Crate & Kids sends today — Crate & Barrel proper managed a sharper, more relevant "Hours left for Double Rewards & up to 60% off" send just yesterday, and this one still can't match that urgency or fit.
- The email is exhausting — hero, sweepstakes, Jellycat, six-product grid, credit card, app pitch again — Pottery Barn's "Living Room Checklist" send was one clean idea end to end; this is five ideas competing for the same scroll.
- Every real CTA (Download Our App, Book a Free Appointment) routes off-email to an app store or a consult booking, not to a product I can actually buy in two taps.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment me out of Crate & Kids sends entirely, or at minimum stop leading nursery furniture emails to a subscriber whose purchase history is living-room and primary-bedroom furniture across two houses.
- 2. Cut the email down to one offer — either the sweepstakes or the Jellycat restock, not both plus a product grid plus a card pitch.
- 3. Give the "we love these for you" grid an actual reason to exist — right now it's generic nursery bestsellers, not anything personalized to a viewer.
- 4. Stop repeating the "only on our app" hook for the third time this week — it's dead on arrival for me now.

- **Subject Alt B:** `$5000 off your next room — book a free design session`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Jellycat restock + a free design consult, no app required`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New cribs, gliders, and dressers just landed`
