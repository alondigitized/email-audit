# LOOK AGAIN: 2x Rewards + up to 50% off is happening now!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- Eight. I've now received eight emails from the Crate & Barrel family in roughly 48 hours, and whoever is running list segmentation over there has officially lost the plot. The "LOOK AGAIN" in the subject is a transparent re-engagement play — they saw I didn't click the prior kids send (the responsible-design brand essay on the 17th, which I archived in about three seconds) and decided the fix was to blast me again with the same sub-brand I've consistently ignored.
- Here's my actual problem: I don't have children. I'm renovating two homes simultaneously. The 2x Rewards offer is technically interesting — those rewards are good across the C&B family — but you're asking me to buy a crib to earn them. That's not a real trade. Compare this to the CB2 send earlier this week (NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE, 7/10): that one at least had product categories I'd actually consider. And Article's welcome email was a lesson in restraint — one message, clear brand, relevant product. This is the opposite.
- I'd mark this as read without clicking. I would absolutely not forward it. If I were a new parent, this would be a solid 8. But I'm not, and the fact that I'm getting Crate & Kids emails at all suggests the segmentation data is either stale or nonexistent.

## What's working

- **The offer is genuinely clear.** "2x Rewards + up to 50% off" is front-loaded in the hero — no hunting required, and the Memorial Day framing makes the urgency feel credible rather than manufactured.
- **Clean execution.** The email renders without layout issues, the product tiles are aligned, imagery is bright and professional. No broken images, no lorem ipsum. Better composed than the responsible-design essay they sent two days ago.
- **Social proof is visible.** There's a star-rating block mid-email with what looks like review copy — that's a real trust signal and most of their recent sends have skipped it.
- **Rewards visibility.** "Double Rewards" is surfaced prominently enough that a member immediately understands the loyalty math. The prior Memorial Day send from the main brand did this well too; the kids sub-brand carried it over.

## What's weak

- **Wrong sub-brand, full stop.** The entire email is cribs, nursery furniture, and children's decor. I renovate homes. There is no version of this send where I'm the target customer.
- **"LOOK AGAIN" is doing too much.** It's a four-character ALL CAPS re-engagement gambit in the subject line, and it reads as slightly desperate rather than clever — especially because the prior Crate & Kids send was also irrelevant to me. Looking again doesn't fix the mismatch.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is email eight in the C&B family blitz. Even if the category had been right, I'd be flagging this for volume alone. Pottery Barn sent ten in 72 hours and I'm already auto-archiving those.
- **Offer regression.** The main brand's Memorial Day email on May 17 had "up to 60% off." This sub-brand send is "up to 50% off." The drop feels like a downgrade even if the categories are different.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress from non-parent segments entirely.** If I haven't browsed or purchased kids furniture in the last 12 months, I shouldn't be receiving Crate & Kids promotional sends. No subject line rewrite fixes a category mismatch.
- 2. **If you must send to this list, cross-sell the rewards to the main brand.** Lead with "Earn Double Rewards you can use anywhere at Crate & Barrel — including our Memorial Day furniture sale." That gives me a reason to care even if I don't buy kids stuff.
- 3. **Kill the ALL CAPS re-engagement hook.** "LOOK AGAIN" is a spam signal and a guilt trip. If I didn't open the last one, that's data — not an invitation to shout louder.
- 4. **Consolidate the product tiles.** The multi-tile grid is fine for browsing but there's no single hero product anchoring the email. Article does this better: one product in the hero, everything else secondary.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your rewards work across Crate & Barrel, CB2, and Crate & Kids — shop once, earn everywhere`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Conflicting desktop image max-width rules**
Two `min-width` media queries both target desktop and set conflicting `img` max-widths:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }  /* declared first */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }  /* declared second — wins cascade */
```
Because the 640px rule is declared *after* the 768px rule, it wins for all screens ≥640px. The 600px rule is effectively dead. If the layout container is 600px wide, images will be allowed to overflow by 40px on desktop clients that honor `<style>` blocks (Gmail web, Apple Mail).

**[MEDIUM] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents user zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 failure for low-vision users. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[MEDIUM] Global `line-height: 100%` on `*` selector**
```css
* { line-height: 100%; }
```
`100%` is relative to font-size, not the element's own line-height, causing collapsed or overlapping text in some Outlook and Samsung Mail versions. Standard fix is `line-height: normal` or explicit `px`/unitless values.

**[LOW] Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
`telephone=no` and `address=no` are declared twice — once in the combined meta and again as standalone metas. Redundant; no functional impact but indicates sloppy templating.

**[LOW] No dark mode declaration**
`color-scheme: light` with `supported-color-schemes: light` forces light mode but provides no dark-mode overrides. Apple Mail and Outlook for Mac will invert background and text colors. If dark mode is intentionally unsupported, this is fine — but it should be a deliberate choice.
