# “But what’s my holiday aesthetic??” 🎄😵‍💫
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-19**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction was that this is a twelfth Crate & Barrel send in about two days, and the "hours left" free-shipping banner didn't help. It reads like the usual urgency stunt. I did read on, though, because "what's my holiday aesthetic" is a real question when I'm furnishing two houses. The body is a mood board in five named styles: modern heritage, timeless evergreen, classic cheer, frosted sage and winter harvest. Each is a big fireplace-mantel hero, a pair of product shots and a "Shop the Look" button. I'd save this to my Pinterest-adjacent folder.
- It's pretty and on-brand, but it's very long. Five near-identical mantel-and-tree scenes blur together, and I couldn't tell the styles apart without reading the numbered labels. By the time I hit the TheDesignDesk block and the app promo, I was done scrolling.
- Would I click? Probably one "Shop the Look", maybe frosted sage. Would I forward it? Only to my partner as a "which tree vibe?" text. It's better than the cookware or Kids sends from the last two days, but it's not as good as "Buy once, love for decades."

## What's working

- The numbered style names are a good hook. "1. modern heritage" and "4. frosted sage" give me something to react to, where most holiday emails just say "shop decor."
- Each section is a styled room scene followed by a product pair and one button. The pattern is consistent and the buttons are easy to find and tap.
- The free-shipping banner at the very top puts the only concrete offer above the hero.
- The TheDesignDesk block, with its free design appointment, speaks to someone doing two renovations at once. It's the most relevant thing here for me.
- The dark, moody, candlelit photography looks premium. This is the kind of image I'd drop into the second-house folder.

## What's weak

- At five sections it's too long. By style 3 I'd already skimmed past the point.
- The banner says "hours left," but I can't tell what the cutoff is or what "restrictions apply" covers. For furniture shipping I want to know whether this even counts.
- I couldn't see prices or product names under the product tiles, only small captions. That makes the grid hard to shop from, and it's all tiny on a phone.
- The "furniture that arrives before your holiday guests do" block and the "we love these for you" glassware row pull away from the holiday theme. Wayfair's pergola email stayed on one product, which is more honest.
- The app promo and the second TheDesignDesk pitch at the bottom feel like filler on top of an already long email.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut it from five styles to three and put a quiz-style "pick your look" row of five thumbnails at the top. That would answer the subject's question quickly and link each thumbnail to its section.
- 2. Say what the shipping deadline actually is, with a date and time. "Hours left" is vague.
- 3. Put prices and product names under every tile, with one hero product per style. Pottery Barn's nightstands email did this better.
- 4. Move the design appointment up and drop the duplicate at the bottom.
- 5. Drop the app promo and the generic "we love these for you" row.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `alt` (27 images listed; the last QA entry is cut off, so the count may be higher):**
  - 13 on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/…`. Examples: `bbf6f9d4-…png`, `f8e57f8e-…gif`, `971aa10f-…jpg`. These are probably content images, so with images blocked or a screen reader they have no text equivalent.

- **Dead CSS rule:** `@media (min-width:768px){img{max-width:600px}}` is always overridden by the later `@media (min-width:640px){img{max-width:640px}}`. Both match at 768px and above, and the later rule wins.
