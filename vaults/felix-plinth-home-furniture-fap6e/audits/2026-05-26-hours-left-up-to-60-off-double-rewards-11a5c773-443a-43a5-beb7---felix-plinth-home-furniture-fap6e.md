# HOURS LEFT: Up to 60% off + Double Rewards!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-26**
## Executive summary

- Here's the honest truth: I saw "Crate & Barrel SALE" hit my inbox and my thumb was already hovering over the swipe-to-archive. This is — by my count — somewhere in the range of email number five or six from the C&B family since Sunday, and I've already sat through two Crate & Kids sends that had zero business reaching a guy renovating two adult homes. So the brand has burned a lot of goodwill this week.
- But I opened it. Because "60% off" at the end of the subject got my attention in spite of everything. And once I was inside, the email is genuinely clean — dark moody hero featuring what looks like a deep-stained sideboard or entertainment console, clear "Shop the Memorial Day Sale" button, a "we love these for you" personalization block with actual furniture recommendations, and a TheDesignDesk editorial strip before the footer. The aesthetic matches what I'm hunting for. The offer is the best number I've seen all week from any sender — 60% off clears Article's 40%, beats CB2's 50%, and lines up with what Pottery Barn was doing on their 10/10 send earlier today.
- I'd click. Reluctantly, with exhausted eyes, but I'd click. I would not forward this. If a friend asked me about the C&B Memorial Day sale I'd just text them the URL directly.

## What's working

- **The 60% off headline is the best floor number in my inbox this week** — Article topped out at 40%, CB2 at 50%, and Pottery Barn's final-day send matched it; 60% stops the scroll.
- **Hero furniture is dark and architectural** — that sideboard / console reads like something I'd actually source for the second home, not a soft nursery vibe like the Crate & Kids emails that contaminated my inbox twice before this.
- **"We love these for you" personalization block** — I can see tailored product thumbnails; it signals they're pulling from browse or purchase history rather than blasting a generic catalog shot at everyone.
- **Double Rewards is meaningfully called out** — I run rewards math; seeing it explicitly repeated alongside the discount tells me the total value proposition without digging into an app.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum disasters.

## What's weak

- **"HOURS LEFT" in all-caps is cheap urgency signaling** — Pottery Barn on their 10/10 send used plain lowercase "Less than 24 hrs" and it felt more credible. Shouting at me in the subject line after six emails this week makes me distrust the clock.
- **The app promo mid-scroll kills momentum** — I just want to shop the sale; dropping an "It's all on the app, including your chance at $500" block between the product recs and the editorial footer is a speed bump I didn't ask for.
- **The lifestyle grid under the hero includes what appears to be a berry/food shot** — I'm here for furniture; entertaining-lifestyle imagery is fine as editorial texture but it reads like a mismatch against the renovation context I'm in.
- **Cadence has poisoned the well** — two Crate & Kids emails earlier this week means the C&B name triggers mild annoyance before I've even read a word. That's entirely a list-management and segmentation problem that bleeds into every metric here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop "HOURS LEFT" caps and lean on the 60% number in the subject** — the 60 is the strongest hook in the email; bury it after a yelled phrase and you waste it.
- 2. **Kill or relocate the app promo** — push it to the footer alongside the brand family logos, not interrupting the product funnel.
- 3. **Suppress Crate & Kids recipients from this send** — or at minimum deduplicate so a household that has already received five C&B family emails in 72 hours doesn't get a sixth. Pottery Barn's 29-email Memorial Day cadence is already the cautionary ceiling; don't match it.
- 4. **Add a second CTA anchored to a specific hero product** — the sideboard in the hero is compelling; give me a "Shop this sideboard" link so I don't have to wade into a sale page to find it.
- 5. **Swap the berry shot for a styled room vignette** — a living room or home-office setup reinforces the renovation use case and keeps the visual thread from the hero consistent.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Dead desktop image rule (CSS cascade bug)**
Two conflicting `min-width` media queries appear in this order:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
At any viewport ≥768px, both rules match. The 640px block appears later in the cascade and wins — the `600px` cap is never applied. The `768px` rule is dead code. On desktop, images render at 640px, not 600px as intended.

**Accessibility: `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents user-initiated zoom — a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) failure that also violates Apple App Store review guidelines when rendered in iOS mail clients.

**Accessibility: Global `text-size-adjust: none`**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```
Applied to the universal selector, this suppresses OS-level font scaling for users who rely on system accessibility settings.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
`format-detection` appears four times — once as a combined value and then three times individually:
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
Redundant tags; only the first combined tag is needed. Low severity but indicative of template hygiene issues.

**No dark mode support**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
`light` only — no `dark` or `light dark` value. Apple Mail and Samsung Mail will force-invert colors in dark mode rather than rendering any authored dark-mode styles.

**`line-height: 100%` on universal selector**
```css
* { line-height: 100%; }
```
Collapses line-height to the element's font size for every element, which can cause text in multi-line blocks to overlap. Should be scoped to specific elements.
