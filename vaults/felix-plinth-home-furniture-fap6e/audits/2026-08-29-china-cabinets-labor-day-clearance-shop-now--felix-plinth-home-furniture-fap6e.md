# CHINA CABINETS ⭐️ LABOR DAY CLEARANCE ⭐️ SHOP NOW ⭐️ 
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-29**
## Executive summary

- Subject says "CHINA CABINETS" and I scrolled through the entire "picked just for you" grid looking for one — closest thing is that blue-lit glass cabinet in the top-left tile, and even that reads more like a bar cart or curio than an actual china cabinet. Everything else in that first row is noise for me: a girl on an inflatable pool float, a crockpot, a trampoline, a beige sofa. This is the same Wayfair move I called out on 2026-08-28 with "FURNITURE CUSHIONS" — subject line as clickbait for a grid that has nothing to do with the subject.
- The good news, if there is any, is the offer stack further down — 30-70% off decor, mattresses, curtains, bathroom vanities — is at least organized into clean colored tiles instead of the sprawling everything-must-go dump from "UP TO 70% OFF end-of-summer upgrades." But I'm renovating two houses right now and dining/cabinet storage is genuinely on my list, so getting baited by a subject line that doesn't deliver a single china cabinet product shot is the kind of thing that makes me trust the sender less next time, not more.
- Not clicking, not forwarding. This is a scroll-past-and-archive email, same as the last four Wayfair sends I've logged this week.

## What's working

- The percentage-off tile grid (30% decor, 70% wall art, 55% mirrors, 50% bedding, etc.) is legible at a glance — better organized than the messy "70% OFF end-of-summer" grid from two days ago.
- Afterpay 0% APR banner is a real, concrete financing hook for someone furnishing two houses at once — that's relevant to how I actually buy big furniture.
- "Doorbuster deals" section has actual product shots with star ratings and was/now pricing visible, which is more trust-building than a text-only banner.
- Sealy mattress cross-promo at the bottom is at least targeted to a category I'd plausibly buy for a second home.

## What's weak

- Zero china cabinets anywhere in this email. The subject is the entire pitch and the body doesn't back it up — CB2's "BACK IN STOCK" emails at least deliver the product the subject promises.
- "Picked just for you" grid is a random assortment (kid's pool float, crockpot, trampoline) that has nothing to do with my renovation lists — Article's Labor Day email at least kept its hero grid in-category.
- This is my third or fourth Wayfair "Labor Day Clearance" email this week (following the faux florals and ceiling fan sends) — same purple/red palette, same "SHOP NOW" stars, same fatigue.
- No color-palette or room-styling continuity between tiles — it jumps from a glass cabinet to a sofa to a kid's toy to a crockpot with no visual thread tying it together.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix subject-to-hero alignment — if you're leading with "CHINA CABINETS," the first tile in the grid needs to be an actual china cabinet, not a blue-lit curio that only sort of qualifies. This is the single biggest miss.
- 2. Segment the "picked for you" grid by actual browsing/purchase category instead of a grab-bag — I've got dining/storage and living room on my lists at Pottery Barn and C&B; show me that, not a trampoline.
- 3. Cut the email frequency or clearly differentiate each Labor Day send by category so it doesn't feel like the same purple sale wrapper for the fifth time this week.
- 4. Move the Afterpay 0% APR banner higher — it's a stronger hook for a two-house renovator than another percentage-off tile.
