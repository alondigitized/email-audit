# ENDS SOON! Your chance to win a $1,000 Gift Card
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- Nine. That's the Crate & Barrel family count in my inbox this week, and somehow I'm looking at my *second* Crate & Kids email of the same calendar day. "Love them now & forever" already hit me this morning — I filed it at 5/10, noted the nursery mismatch, moved on. Now here's another one with a sweepstakes in the hero and a bedroom image in sage green. I don't have a nursery. My project list is an HVAC rough-in at the second house and a dining table decision at the primary. Nobody sleeping in a crib in either of them.
- The sweepstakes format is my least favorite move in the promotions playbook, and C&Kids just ran it on the wrong segment. The $1,000 number is legible and specific — I'll give them that — but the moment I'm inside the email and it's finishing-touches nursery art and summer camp gear, I'm already tabbing away. Below the hero there's a reasonably well-structured layout: a "finishing touches for their dream room" editorial block, a seasonal summer-gear grid, a "we love these for you" personalization module that clearly pulled baby products from a browse session I have no memory of, a random Crate & Barrel crossover banner, the usual app push, and a TheDesignDesk footer. That's six distinct content asks in a single send from a sub-brand that already emailed me today.
- Mark as read. Not forwarding. Not clicking. The markdowns email earlier this week — the one with up to 60% off clearance, which I scored an 8 — showed exactly what this brand family can do when it commits to a clean price message. This is not that.

## What's working

- The $1,000 figure is a concrete, scannable number that stops the thumb for a half-second before the sweepstakes format registers
- The hero bedroom image is warm and well-shot; sage green palette is genuinely contemporary and on-brand for the C&B aesthetic I recognize
- "Gear up for summer" framing is timely — the seasonal hook is appropriate even if it's not pulling me into kids gear
- The app/rewards section is a consistent beat across every send in this family; I know where to find it and it doesn't intrude
- "We love these for you" at least signals the CRM is attempting personalization, even if the product logic misfired

## What's weak

- Second Crate & Kids email of the same day is an automatic annoyance surcharge before I've even read the subject; the cadence tab is already pegged
- Sweepstakes hook does nothing for a purchase-intent shopper — this is engagement-farming, not a loyalty reward, and it reads that way
- Nursery and children's summer product is a complete miss for me; the personalization module served baby gear, not the rugs, lighting, or outdoor furniture I actually browse
- "ENDS SOON!" with no visible drop-dead date is a credibility drain; Wayfair put an actual date on their American Summer Shop urgency and it landed cleaner
- Six distinct content blocks with no connective thread — sweepstakes, nursery editorial, summer grid, C&B crossover, app push, TheDesignDesk — the email doesn't know what it's trying to do

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment before you send** — an adult renovating two homes should never receive a nursery email. One purchase-history filter catches this. The CRM knows what I've looked at; the personalization module proves it.
- 2. **Commit to one angle or kill the sweepstakes** — Pottery Barn's Chris Loves Julia collab (6/10) at least had a single coherent editorial POV. CB2's "All green everything" (7/10) committed fully to a creative direction. This email tries to be a sweepstakes *and* a nursery editorial *and* a summer gear shop simultaneously and lands none of them.
- 3. **Date-stamp the urgency** — "Ends Sunday, June 1 at midnight" costs nothing to add and removes the bait-and-switch feeling of a floating "ENDS SOON!"
- 4. **Make the gift card a purchase incentive, not a raffle** — tie it to a spend threshold and I might actually redirect my rewards math toward it; a sweepstakes I enter once and forget
- 5. **Cut the email by half** — remove either the seasonal summer block or the nursery finishing-touches block; pick a lane and own it
- **Subject Alt A:** `Win a $1,000 gift card — enter before Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer picks + a $1,000 giveaway inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New seasonal arrivals and a giveaway closing soon — here's how to enter.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the summer edit. Enter the $1,000 gift card draw while you're at it.`

**[CONFIRMED] Conflicting `img` max-width breakpoints**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
Both rules are present. On viewports ≥ 768px both fire; the 768px rule is declared later, so it wins and clamps images to 600px. On 640–767px viewports only the second rule fires (640px). The ordering is inverted from intent — the 640px rule should come first so the 768px rule properly overrides it. This is consistent behavior, but the narrower desktop cap (600px on a 768px+ screen) may cause blurring of any image intended to fill a 640px container.

<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">

The first declaration covers all five. The three individual tags below it are redundant. No functional breakage, but bloats `<head>` unnecessarily.
