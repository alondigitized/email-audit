# NEW ITEMS ADDED! Up to 50% off bestselling furniture →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- I've lost count of how many Crate & Barrel-family emails have hit my inbox in the past 48 hours. Walking through yesterday alone I clocked the kitchen counter brand email, the perfect sofa at $1,599 send, and at least two "Yes, furniture is ON SALE now!" blasts from Crate & Kids. Today the SALE sub-sender shows up with "NEW ITEMS ADDED!" — same playbook, different day. My threshold for this family is genuinely worn thin.
- That said: I'm in the market. Two houses, both mid-renovation, and the hero sofa in this email — dark navy, low-profile, clean lines — is legitimately the aesthetic I've been circling on Crate's site. The photography is doing real work. If this had landed on Monday when my inbox wasn't already saturated with C&B, I'd have clicked straight to the sofa PDP.
- As it stands I'll glance, tab it, probably forget it. I won't forward it — I'd feel embarrassed sending someone the seventh installment in a franchise. Mark as read is the most likely outcome.

## What's working

- **Hero photography is genuinely aspirational.** Dark navy sofa against a moody, architecturally interesting room reads like a West Elm shoot done with a bigger budget. That's my visual language.
- **The offer is visible immediately.** "Up to 50% off bestselling furniture" is above the fold, no scroll required — Pottery Barn's "Extra 20% off" send on July 1st took longer to surface the number than this does.
- **Layout is clean.** Multiple product pods beneath the hero give me real browsing surface — dining, seating, bedroom — without feeling like a cluttered Wayfair homepage dump.
- **Sub-sender branding ("Crate & Barrel SALE") is doing its job.** I know exactly what I'm walking into before I open.

## What's weak

- **ALL CAPS "NEW ITEMS ADDED!" is shouted, not edited.** CB2 SALE pulled off the same beat on July 1st with "New items added. One week left to shop." — lowercase, with a deadline baked in. This version feels like someone held the Caps Lock key too long and nobody caught it.
- **No deadline anywhere I can see.** "Up to 50% off" floats untethered. Article's "Shop 4th of July Sale" landed this same week and gave me a Monday-night-before-the-holiday close. This gives me nothing to act on.
- **Cadence fatigue is real and cumulative.** This is at minimum send five in the C&B family this week. The brand history here is a liability, not an asset. Even a genuinely good email struggles when it's the fifth chapter of the same novel.
- **"Bestselling" is lazy copy.** What's bestselling? Name the sofa. Name the table. Pottery Barn went generic with their hero too, but at least they had the holiday urgency to compensate.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline, immediately.** "Sale ends July 6" in the subhead would change the math entirely. Right now there's no reason to act today vs. Saturday. Article and Pottery Barn both used the July 4th weekend as a hard stop — C&B is leaving that urgency on the table.
- 2. **Name the hero product.** The dark navy sofa is doing the visual heavy lifting. Give it a name, a starting price, and a direct PDP link. "Caden sofa from $1,399" beats "bestselling furniture" every time.
- 3. **Kill the ALL CAPS opener.** CB2 SALE proved the "new items" message lands fine in sentence case. Lowercase with a deadline is more credible than ALL CAPS without one.
- 4. **Surface member pricing if it exists.** I carry the rewards card. If there's a stacked member benefit on top of 50% off, I can't find it here — and that's the one thing that would make me forward this to my spouse.
- 5. **Thin the email.** The length is working against the product. Pick three hero items, link them directly, get out. The scroll depth is approaching CB2 "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS" territory, which I already noted felt exhausting.

- **Subject Alt B:** `New: Caden sofa + 200 more pieces added to sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping on orders $49+ through Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Member pricing stacked on top — shop before Monday.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied via wildcard selector (confirmed):**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```
The `body` correctly sets `100%`, but the `*` wildcard overrides it for all child elements. On iOS Safari this can cause text to render smaller than intended when the device auto-adjusts layout. Should be `100%` everywhere.

**Conflicting `img` `max-width` rules — 600px rule is dead code (confirmed):**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
At ≥768px both queries fire; the 640px rule appears later in the stylesheet and wins via cascade. The 600px declaration is never applied. At exactly 640–767px only the second fires. Net effect: images are always capped at 640px on desktop, never 600px. Likely unintentional.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta (confirmed):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents user zoom on mobile — an accessibility failure (WCAG 1.4.4). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Dark mode suppressed entirely (confirmed):**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Apple Mail and Gmail Android dark mode users will receive no dark-adapted styles. Acceptable as a deliberate choice, but should be documented as intentional.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags (confirmed):**
`format-detection` appears four times — once combined and three times as individual declarations. Redundant but harmless; clean up for maintainability.

**Duplicate `charset` declarations (confirmed):**
Both `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` and `<meta charset="utf-8">` are present. Redundant; keep only the `http-equiv` form for maximum XHTML client compatibility.
