# Find a backpack that’s just their size →
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- Eleven. That's the number — eleven Crate & Barrel family emails in roughly 36 hours, and this one is the clearest sign yet that the segmentation engine has completely lost the plot on me. Previous sends were at least *adjacent* to what I actually buy from C&B: outdoor furniture, kitchen stuff, table styling. This one is about kids' backpacks. Colorful, well-photographed kids' backpacks, sized for a seven-year-old.
- I don't have a seven-year-old. I have two homes I'm trying to furnish simultaneously and an inbox that now reads like a C&B family newsletter subscription I never asked for. The subject line arrow glyph ("→") showed up in the exact same position it did in the plastic-in-your-kitchen send from earlier today — different brand, same template fingerprint, same cadence problem.
- The execution is technically fine. The backpacks are cute. If you *have* a kid starting school in September, this is probably a pleasant email to open. I am not that person, and C&B absolutely has purchase history to know that. Mark as read, no click, definitely not forwarding. Article has been sending me one editorial email every four or five days about living rooms and dining rooms — stuff I'm actually buying — and it's landing every time. This does not.

## What's working

- The hero image is clean and immediately communicates the category — no ambiguity about what's being sold, and the color variety of the backpacks comes across well even on mobile.
- The Crate & Kids sub-brand is visually distinct from the parent C&B sends, so it reads as intentional, not sloppy.
- Back-to-school timing in late July is correct — this isn't stale inventory copy, it's the right moment to push this category.
- No render bugs anywhere I can see — the layout is tight, product grid reads cleanly, and the CTA buttons are visible.

## What's weak

- This landed in my inbox nine emails too late to feel like a curated experience and for a category — kids' school supplies — that has nothing to do with my purchasing history with this brand.
- "Just their size" is a cute line but generic family-speak that doesn't mean anything to me; the CB2 furniture event sent this week used "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS" and at least made me feel like the copy knew who it was writing to.
- No offer visible in the hero — no percentage off, no free shipping threshold, nothing to justify the send cadence or create urgency.
- The demographic signals are entirely kids-facing (small models, primary color palette) — fine if I'm a parent, actively alienating if I'm a 36-year-old guy who bought the Finn Collection last month.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress this from anyone who has never bought in a kids/nursery category and has only purchased home furniture.** This is the single highest-leverage change — it's not a creative problem, it's a list problem. Wayfair sends me eleven emails in two days and I expect to get a few misfires; from a brand like C&B that has actual purchase data on me, this feels like a failure.
- 2. **Add a concrete offer.** If you're going to interrupt someone's inbox as the eleventh email in 36 hours, at minimum give them a reason: "20% off for Rewards members" or "free monogramming this week" buys goodwill. The plastic-reduction email earlier today at least had a $2.50 entry price point visible.
- 3. **Introduce a cross-sell pod** for customers who aren't parents — a small "while you're here, the Fall furniture preview is live" module at the bottom would salvage the open for people like me and not dilute the primary message for parents.
- 4. **Cut the cadence.** Eleven emails in 36 hours across the family is why I'm numbering them out loud. Article's four-to-five day editorial cadence makes me *look forward* to the next one. This makes me reach for the unsubscribe link.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport (confirmed)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Wildcard `text-size-adjust: none` (confirmed)**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applied to all elements via `*` selector, this overrides OS-level font scaling for users with visual impairments. Should be scoped to `body` only, and value should be `100%` not `none`.

**Conflicting desktop image breakpoints (confirmed)**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
On screens ≥768px both rules apply; the 600px cap wins (last-declared loses to specificity/cascade order here — actually the 768 rule is declared first, 640 second, so on screens ≥768px the 640px rule overrides, capping images at 640px when 600px was intended for desktop). Consolidate into a single rule or clarify intent.

**Dark mode not supported (confirmed)**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Forces light rendering in Apple Mail and iOS — no dark mode fallback defined. Low-severity but increasingly common in inboxes.
