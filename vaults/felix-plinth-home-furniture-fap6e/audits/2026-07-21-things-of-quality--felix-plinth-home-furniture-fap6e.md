# THINGS OF QUALITY
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-one. That's how many CB2 emails have landed in my July inbox, and I want to lead with that because it is the single biggest context for everything else I'm about to say. Two came yesterday — the registry push and the art collection send — both of which I actually gave credit for showing some restraint. Today's arrives with all-caps "THINGS OF QUALITY" in the subject, and my immediate reaction was a tired sigh before I even clicked.
- Here's the frustrating thing: the email itself is *genuinely good*. That camel swivel armchair in the hero is exactly the kind of piece I have a saved search for right now. The color story — camel leather into burgundy velvet into warm wood tones — is editorial without being pretentious. CB2 can clearly shoot furniture. The $5,000 REFRESH section mid-email is where my attention stalled though. Two days ago it was a $1,000 sweepstakes. Now it's $5,000. I'm starting to feel like the giveaway number is just being inflated each send rather than replaced with something of actual value to me as a shopper.
- I'd save the hero chair to my Pinterest folder. I would not forward this to anyone. The photography earns a click; the subject line and cadence fatigue almost cancel it out.

## What's working

- **The hero product is genuinely covetable.** That camel swivel chair hits exactly the contemporary-with-warmth vibe I'm trying to achieve in the second home's sitting room — CB2 made me stop scrolling.
- **Color story is cohesive and intentional.** Camel → burgundy velvet → warm neutrals flows like a mood board, not a liquidation event. This is what makes CB2 different from Wayfair's scatter-shot grid approach.
- **"THE SUMMER EDIT" framing works as sub-header.** Gives the editorial scaffolding that justifies the aspirational subject without needing a hard discount; Pottery Barn's July 20th "classic, comfortable, collected" send did something similar and scored a 9 with me.
- **No render issues.** Clean layout, images load, copy sits where it should. After Crate & Kids' birthday email chaos from yesterday, I'm grateful for a basic win.

## What's weak

- **"THINGS OF QUALITY" as a subject line is all-caps nothing.** It tells me zero about what to expect inside. It's a mood board title, not a subject line. Compare it to Pottery Barn's "New arrivals—classic, comfortable, collected" which uses lowercase and three specific qualifiers — I knew what I was opening.
- **The $5K REFRESH sweepstakes is a pattern I've now spotted.** Two days ago it was "$1K COULD BE YOURS TO WIN." Today it's $5,000. Neither gives me the thing I actually want: a specific reason to buy *this* product *today* at a price I feel good about.
- **CTAs throughout the scroll are small and undifferentiated.** From what I can see, every section has a similar-looking text link or small button — I can't easily identify which product in the "MORE FOR YOU" grid I'd click into without hunting.
- **No membership or rewards acknowledgment.** I buy via my highest-rewards card and track my CB2 spending. Article's dining room email last week made me feel like a known customer. This makes me feel like list volume.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the chair, not the editorial concept.** The camel swivel armchair is the best asset in this email; make it the CTA, name it, link it, maybe give it a current price. "The swivel chair I've been thinking about all week" is more compelling than a philosophy statement.
- 2. **Kill or contextualize the $5K sweepstakes.** If you're going to run a giveaway the day after another giveaway, at least tie the prize to something concrete — "Win a $5K room refresh: tell us which room" — so it feels like a campaign, not a number that got bumped up.
- 3. **Move to lowercase subject lines.** CB2's own art collection send yesterday used mixed-case internally and it felt more elevated. ALL CAPS in 2026 reads anxious, not editorial.
- 4. **Let the "MORE FOR YOU" grid breathe with names/prices.** From the thumbnail those products are anonymous squares. Article links product names and price points directly — that converts browsers.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks user zoom — fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[MEDIUM] `line-height: 100%` on universal selector**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; max-height:1000000px;
   mso-line-height-rule:exactly; line-height: 100%}
```
Applying `line-height: 100%` globally via `*` clips descenders and causes text overlap on multi-line elements in several clients (Yahoo Mail, older Android). Should be scoped to specific elements, not the wildcard.

**[MEDIUM] `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on universal selector**
The global `none` value (vs `100%`) suppresses automatic text scaling on iOS. Combined with the `maximum-scale=1` lock above, small type becomes unreadable on mobile without any fallback.

**[LOW] Non-standard mobile breakpoint**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) {
  .mobile { width: 414px !important; }
```
Breakpoint fires at 418px but sets `width: 414px` — targets iPhone 6/7/8 native resolution but misses the 415–418px range it claims to cover. Common email breakpoints are 480px or 600px; this custom value risks leaving some viewports in an intermediate broken state.

**[LOW] Duplicate `@font-face` declarations for "Lineto Brown Sub"**
The family is declared three times with overlapping `src` URLs — once without weight/style descriptors, and twice with `font-weight: 400` and `font-weight: 600`. The unweighted declaration will override browser matching for intermediate weights. Consolidate into a single block per weight variant.
