# Up to 60% off: It’s our annual summer warehouse sale!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- My C&B family inbox counter is sitting at 74. That matters because I counted at least four emails from the C&B family yesterday alone about this exact promotion — two "It's here! Up to 50% off" sends, the "Psst… something new just hit up to 60% off" teaser, and now this one the following morning bumping the headline number to 60%. I noticed the escalation. Was it 50% yesterday and 60% today because they decided to deepen the discount, or is this the kind of retroactive "actually our best deal all along" reframe that retailers run when day-one open rates disappoint? Either way, it creates friction before I've even scrolled past the hero.
- The email itself is competently built. Long, but laid out with intention: sale hero, category quicklinks, a "Big Finds" callout block, a solo product feature (looks like a kitchen appliance wedged in the middle, which is disorienting), an outdoor furniture push, and then — at the very bottom — an "Items just chosen for you" personalized module that's doing real work. That last piece is the most interesting thing in the email and it's buried after four other content blocks.
- I'm not clicking. Not because the deal is bad, but because I've already been in and out of the sale in my browser since yesterday. I know the offer exists. What I need is either a reason to close the loop (a deadline) or a shortcut to the thing I'm actually considering buying. This email gives me neither. Mark as read, move on.

## What's working

- **"Items just chosen for you" module** — the only moment I slowed down. Browse-history personalization at the bottom proves they have data on me; the mistake is burying it.
- **Hero offer is immediate and legible** — "Up to 60% off" lands without a mystery or click-to-reveal; the number is in the first visual beat.
- **Seasonal framing as an event** — calling it "our annual summer warehouse sale" positions this as a recurring occasion rather than a random markdown, which adds a little psychological gravity.
- **Clean render across all visible sections** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no placeholder copy. In a long multi-block email, that's actually worth noting.
- **Category breadth** — outdoor, kitchen, dining are all touched in a single send, which does cover two renovations' worth of needs even if the execution is scattered.

## What's weak

- **The cadence is genuinely punishing** — fifth send in 36 hours from the same brand family about the same sale. At that volume my inbox muscle memory shifts from "evaluate" to "bulk archive."
- **50% yesterday, 60% today** — the headline number changed overnight with no explanation. That's confusing for anyone who opened the previous send, and I opened the "Psst…" one.
- **No deadline anywhere visible** — "annual summer sale" implies it runs for a while. Without an end date or a countdown, there's zero urgency differentiating this send from the four before it.
- **The solo kitchen-appliance callout mid-email breaks the flow** — I came in for furniture. Dropping what looks like a countertop oven in the middle of an outdoor and dining push fragments the scroll-story.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface the personalized module above the fold** — move "Items just chosen for you" to the top half. It's the single thing that could convert a warm lead who already knows about the sale. CB2's "FLOOR SAMPLE SALE" email (9/10 last week) nailed this by leading with specific, scarce product; C&B should do the same using my own history.
- 2. **Print a deadline** — "Ends Sunday," "last 48 hours," anything. The Pottery Barn "NEW finishes for pieces you love" send this week didn't need a countdown because it was product-led; a sale email lives or dies by urgency.
- 3. **Suppress this segment for anyone who opened in the last 48 hours** — I'm a warm lead. Mailing me again the next morning with a slightly-different headline number treats me like an unengaged cold contact.
- 4. **Anchor one hero SKU instead of the full catalog** — Wayfair's repeated "8' X 10' OUTDOOR RUGS" sends fail for the opposite reason (stale repetition), but CB2's floor-sample approach of two products and one CTA consistently outperforms this scatter approach.
- 5. **Trim and deadline the subject line** — currently 53 characters with no time element. Under 50 and with a close date, it's a materially different open proposition.
- **Subject Alt A:** `60% off ends Sunday — your saves are included`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your warehouse picks, now up to 60% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Furniture, outdoor, kitchen — deals close this weekend.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `We pulled items from your browsing. They're on sale now.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — 28+ images (confirmed by QA).** Notably:
- All product imagery: `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/*.jpg`
- Hero image: `s7d5.scene7.com/is/content/Crate/00%5FEmail/2026/06%5FCore/...`
- Tracking pixels **also** missing `alt=""`: `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png`, `sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif`, `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/.../o.gif`, `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/...` — tracking pixels must have `alt=""` (empty, not absent) to suppress broken-image icon in image-blocking clients.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents user-initiated zoom. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, AA).

**Conflicting `img` max-width media queries:**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }  /* declared after → wins at ≥768px */
```
At ≥768px both queries match; cascade order means the 640px rule wins, making the 600px rule dead. Intended desktop cap (600px) is never applied.

**Duplicate meta tags (production noise):**
- `<meta charset="utf-8">` declared twice (once via `http-equiv`, once standalone).
- `<meta name="format-detection">` declared three times with overlapping values.

**Template artifact left in production HTML:**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
Unexecuted placeholder comment exposed in rendered source — indicates incomplete template cleanup.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header — missing (WARN):** Gmail and Yahoo have required `List-Unsubscribe` for bulk senders since February 2024. Header not captured by the AgentMail relay; if absent at send time, inbox placement is at risk.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post header — missing (WARN):** RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) is required by Gmail/Yahoo policy for ≥5k/day senders. Absent header forces multi-step unsubscribe flow, which also risks spam classification.

**Authentication-Results — not captured (WARN):** SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status is unknown through the current relay. Sending domain is `mail.crateandbarrel.com`; alignment with DMARC policy on `crateandbarrel.com` cannot be confirmed.
