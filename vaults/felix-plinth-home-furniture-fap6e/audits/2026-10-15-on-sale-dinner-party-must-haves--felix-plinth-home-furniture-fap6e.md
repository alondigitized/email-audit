# ON SALE: DINNER PARTY MUST-HAVES
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-15**
## Executive summary

- CB2 again. This is the first dining-room email I've gotten from them, and it's a good one for me. I'm furnishing two houses, and a dining table is exactly what I'd buy in a "semi-annual event." The hero is a candlelit mantel and a set table with "UP TO 30% OFF" in huge type. I understood the offer in about two seconds. It looks better than the last few Upholstery Event countdowns I got on 10/14.
- Then the email undercuts itself. A second "Warehouse Sale, up to 60% off" banner follows the 30% hero, so there are two sales with two percentages and two black buttons. The "SHOP THE SALE" button in the first banner and the "SHOP THE DINING EVENT" button right after the tiles are redundant. I'd click the dining tables tile, but I'd check the fine print first. The "30% off" is only on select items, and it can't be combined with anything.
- I'd click. I wouldn't forward it. It's a clean, on-brand sale email, but it doesn't speak to my situation of two houses and a long project. Fifteen-plus CB2 sends in about a week have made me skim.

## What's working

- The "30% OFF" is huge and white on a dark, warm photo. I got the offer without reading anything else.
- The category tiles are split into Dining Tables, Dining Chairs, Dining Storage and Entertaining. That's how I shop a dining room, and the tables tile is big enough to be tappable.
- The photography is moody and consistent: warm woods, olive velvet, dark florals. It looks like something I'd save to my Pinterest folder.
- The "Earn 10% back" block mentions Rewards Dollars on CB2 and Crate & Barrel. That's rewards math I care about.
- The "Let's Celebrate" gift list block is a smart tie-in for house warmings.
- The nav bar (New Arrivals, Furniture, Decor, Rugs, Lighting) is clean and the logo is clear.

## What's weak

- The Warehouse Sale banner (up to 60% off) competes with the dining hero. It's a second, bigger number, so it makes the 30% look weak.
- The "Dining Chairs" and "Dining Storage" labels are small and hard to read on the dark photos. They're also tiny tiles, so tapping them on a phone would be fiddly.
- No prices, no product names, no hero piece. It's all mood and categories. Wayfair at least puts a price and a struck-through number on a product (like its "$134 off" pergola email).
- The offer feels buried. The footer fine print runs through "up to 30%," "up to 60%," a 15% text signup, a reviews giveaway and exclusions, which is a lot of asterisks.
- There's no furniture shipping or delivery timing and no swatch or sample mention. For a big dining table buy, I need to know when it arrives.
- The Free Design Services pitch from 10/13's "Staying grounded in color" is missing. That one actually spoke to my two-house project.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one sale. Send the Dining Event on its own, or lead with the Warehouse Sale. Two "up to" offers in one email dilutes both.
- 2. Put one hero product in the email with a name, a price and a struck-through original price. Link it directly, not to a category page.
- 3. Add one line on delivery timing and a swatch or sample CTA for upholstered dining chairs. Pottery Barn does this better in its Kravet email with material and pattern storytelling.
- 4. Make the category labels larger and put them on a solid scrim so they read on a phone.
- 5. Move the Rewards "10% back" line up near the offer, since that's what helps me decide.
- 6. Cut the duplicate "Shop" buttons down to one primary CTA.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dining tables up to 30% off through Nov 4`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your dining room, 30% off (tables, chairs, storage)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 10% back in Rewards Dollars. Ends Nov 4.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Tables, chairs and sideboards. Hosting season starts here.`
