# Get the look: This celeb’s cozy cabin
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-three. That's where the Pottery Barn counter sits in my inbox by the time this one lands on June 17, because it came in the same morning as "Quilts with character in every stitch, from $169." Two emails before noon. I want that on the record before I say anything favorable, because the cadence is the real story and I'm not going to pretend otherwise.
- That said — the cabin aesthetic is genuinely working on me right now. I'm actively sourcing furniture for a second home, the warm neutrals and layered textiles in that hero photography are exactly what I've been pinning, and the 70% off Summer Sale banner is the first thing I see when I open it. PB's visual hierarchy is clean. The Design Crew testimonial plus the free appointment CTA is the kind of utility that Wayfair never offers and CB2 buries — it lands differently when you're mid-renovation and actually need someone to help you commit to a sofa configuration.
- What kills my momentum is the subject line bait-and-switch. "This celeb's cozy cabin" promises a name. What I get inside is "AT HOME WITH THE STYLIST" — whoever that is. The subject dangled a person; the email delivered a concept. On the 23rd email from a single sender, my trust reserves for that kind of move are thin. I'd still click through because the discount is real and I have a credit card that rewards PB spend. But I'd do it slightly annoyed, which is not a great emotional state to be buying a $900 accent chair from.

## What's working

- **70% off Summer Sale banner breaks the fold immediately.** Zero scrolling required. The discount is the hero. That's correct.
- **Cabin Comfort theming is dialed in.** The warm wood tones, natural light, layered textiles — this looks exactly like my second home moodboard. PB clearly knows their seasonal editorial calendar.
- **"Recommended Just for You" section is earning its space.** I know PB tracks browse history and I'm glad they use it. This section is the reason I don't unsubscribe despite the volume.
- **Design Crew testimonial plus free appointment CTA is genuinely useful.** "Made the dream come to life. Easy and seamless." for someone mid-renovation is a real offer, not filler. Wayfair has nothing equivalent and CB2's design service is harder to find.
- **Earn 10% Back in Rewards is visible.** I'm running the math against my rewards card every time. Seeing both options in the same scroll is useful.

## What's weak

- **The celeb hook isn't paid off.** Subject promises a person; email delivers "AT HOME WITH THE STYLIST." No name visible, no face I recognize. Curiosity gap opened, curiosity gap ignored.
- **Same-day double send.** The quilts email already hit me this morning. This is the second PB open before noon. My delete reflex is getting faster with each one.
- **"Explore the Full Collection" is the same vague CTA as everyone else.** CB2 wrote "NEW ITEMS ADDED" as a subject and still managed more specific button copy than this. "Explore" and "Discover" are the same button dressed differently.
- **No sale end date anywhere visible.** "Summer Sale: Up to 70% off" with no deadline. Wayfair always drops a countdown — even a soft "Ends Sunday" creates a reason to act today instead of later and never.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name the person or kill the subject hook.** If it's a stylist I'd recognize, show the name and face in the first screen. If it's someone I wouldn't know, rewrite the subject to lead with the offer instead of false celebrity urgency.
- 2. **Swap "Explore the Full Collection" for a category CTA.** "Shop Cabin Furniture Sale" or even just "Shop the Sale" is more specific than "Explore." I know what I'm in the market for; give me a shorter path.
- 3. **Add a sale end date.** "Summer Sale ends July 4" or "Sale through the weekend" costs nothing to add and converts the mental bookmark into an action.
- 4. **One email per day, maximum.** The quilts send was the lighter editorial one. This one had the stronger sale anchor. Pick one and hold the other for tomorrow.
- 5. **"Book Your Free Appointment" reads clinical.** "Start My Free Design Plan" or "Get a Free Room Plan" keeps the warmth the Design Crew section earned.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP code injected into CSS block (template defect):**

Inside a `<style>` tag, the following PHP function calls appear as literal text:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are server-side PHP directives that were not executed before assembly — they were pasted into the HTML output raw. Email clients will parse them as invalid CSS and ignore them, but their presence signals a broken template build step.

**Mixed content (HTTP image sources — 4 images):**

Clients with strict HTTPS enforcement will block these silently:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true...` (open-tracking pixel)

**Missing alt text:** 40+ images have no `alt` attribute, including all hero/editorial content slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_13.jpg`), product images, and the Summer Sale header. Subscribers with images disabled see a fully blank email.

**Empty `<title>` tag:** `<title></title>` — visible in some clients' tab/window chrome and read by assistive technology.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** prevents pinch-to-zoom on iOS, violating WCAG 1.4.4.
