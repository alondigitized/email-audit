# 🎉 Celebrate with an EXTRA 20% off + Beautiful table linens to elevate your next meal
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-04**
## Executive summary

- Sixty. That's the rough count on my Pottery Barn email tally for the month, and this one drops on the Fourth of July carrying the identical "Extra 20% off" badge I watched arrive on July 3rd — twice — and the day before that, and the day before that. The table linen pivot is at least *something* different from the all-category blast I got yesterday morning, and for a moment I thought maybe PB had finally segmented me into a more targeted bucket.
- Then I kept scrolling. Below the patriotic table setting is a "Recommended For You" section that slides into bedding and a "Discover New Arrivals" block with living room sofas — so we're back to the whole catalog again. The table linen framing in the hero is a nice touch for the holiday but it's window dressing on the same 20% off skeleton we've all been looking at since July 1st.
- I'm not clicking this. I've had the deal memo memorized since Tuesday. I'd mark it read, maybe screenshot the table runner section for my Saved folder since I actually do need something for the dining table at the second place — but I'm not clicking through because I don't trust the urgency anymore. Article did this right: three sends over eight days with a genuine arc. This is Pottery Barn sending the same email in five different outfits.

## What's working

- **Table linen hook is genuinely timely** — I actually need placemats and a runner for the secondary home dining table; the July 4th styling gave me a concrete visual reference for what I'm shopping for.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean** — logo → sale banner → product category → subcategory tiles; eye travels exactly where it's supposed to, no clutter.
- **Three-category breakdown** (table runners, placemats, napkins) is more useful than a single "SHOP TABLE" catch-all — it tells me what's actually in the collection.
- **No render issues** — email loads clean, images are sharp, nothing broken or overlapping.
- **Affirm mention at footer** — relevant when I'm furnishing two houses; financing info buried at the bottom still counts as present.

## What's weak

- **Fifth iteration of the same offer in four days** — the Extra 20% off lost all urgency the moment PB sent it on July 1st, 2nd, 3rd (twice), and now the 4th. CB2 had the discipline to lead with a genuinely new "July 4th Sale IS ON" creative this morning; PB just stamped emoji on the same skeleton.
- **Table linen hero is lovely but reads feminine** — the floral arrangements and classic red/white patterned linens don't signal "36-year-old guy shopping for a second home dining room"; they signal "mom setting a holiday table." Wayfair at least uses lifestyle shots with mixed-gender couples in context.
- **"Recommended for You" undercuts the editorial focus** — the brand established a cohesive "Table Linen Refresh" narrative, then immediately pivoted to bedroom bedding and sofas. If this is a table linens email, commit to it.
- **Generic CTAs throughout** — every button says "SHOP NOW." After seeing Article's "Shop the Sale — Ends Monday" button, "SHOP NOW" feels lazy.
- **Subject line is 85+ characters** — it'll get truncated on mobile at the worst possible moment, right after the discount and right before the product name.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to one category** — if this is a table linens email, kill the bedding "Recommended for You" mid-section. Let the linen narrative breathe. One well-told story beats three half-told ones.
- 2. **Differentiate the offer copy** — "Extra 20% off" for the fifth straight day needs a new frame. "Stock up before the summer entertaining stretch" or a specific deal on the runner/placemat bundle would feel like new information.
- 3. **Add a product anchor** — show me one specific table runner with a price before and after discount. Article does this with a hero SKU every time. It converts.
- 4. **Put a deadline timestamp in the email body** — "Offer ends tonight at midnight" beats an implied July 4th cutoff that I can't verify without clicking.
- 5. **Swap "SHOP NOW" for something specific** — "Shop Table Runners" on the runner tile, "Explore Placemats" on that tile. Four extra words, meaningfully higher intent.
- **Subject Alt A:** `4th of July: Extra 20% off table linens — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Set the table right — extra 20% off linens, runners & more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Tablecloths, runners, placemats — all on sale for the holiday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The dining table deserves a refresh. 20% off through tonight.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources — blocked by modern clients**
Three non-HTTPS image `src` values will be suppressed by Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook when image loading is restricted:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` (also 403)
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` (open-tracking pixel)

**PHP directives injected into `<style>` block — invalid CSS**
The final `<style>` tag in `<head>` contains literal PHP `header()` calls:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, ...");
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are server-side PHP statements that should never appear in rendered HTML. They are syntactically invalid CSS, have no effect on the email client, and indicate a template generation defect where a PHP snippet was pasted into the wrong block.

**46 images missing `alt` text**
All product imagery slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), hero banners (`S26_Top_July4Sale_FinalWeekend.jpg`, `S26_Bottom_UpTo60OffOutdoor.jpg`), product thumbnails (beds, towels, quilt), and footer slices have empty or absent `alt` attributes. Recipients with images disabled see no fallback text for any content block.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` is blank. Some clients (notably older Outlook) surface this in UI; it should at minimum match the subject line.

**Zoom disabled on mobile**
`<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` prevents user zoom on iOS, which is an accessibility regression for low-vision users.

**Global `text-size-adjust: none`**
`*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}` disables OS-level font scaling globally, compounding the zoom issue above.
