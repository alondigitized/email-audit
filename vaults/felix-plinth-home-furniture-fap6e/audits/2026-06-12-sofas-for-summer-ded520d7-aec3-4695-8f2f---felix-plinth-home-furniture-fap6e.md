- CB2's dark cinematic aesthetic actually differentiates summer differently than the competition — where Article is doing warm-wood-and-bright-linen, CB2 leans moody, and after seeing Article's "Summer hosting made simple" the contrast is useful
- The product grid does real work: multiple sofa configurations visible without hunting, which matters when I'm trying to mentally place pieces in two different floor plans with different proportions

- Layout is clean, no render bugs, imagery isn't fighting itself — this is the baseline CB2 always clears
- Brand identity is unmistakably CB2, so I immediately know the quality tier and price range I'm shopping at

## What's weak

- Zero price signal anywhere — Pottery Barn ran "NEW DEALS ADDED! Up to 70% off" in the same week and at least gave me a number; coming off CB2's warehouse sale two days ago, the mismatch between deal mode and editorial mode hasn't been bridged
- No freight or delivery callout, which is the detail that quietly kills sofa add-to-carts for me every time — a $200–300 white-glove delivery fee is the hidden variable I always have to look up manually
- No clear primary CTA anchoring the scroll; the product grid implies I can click around, but there's no single "Shop Summer Sofas" button with weight behind it
- The indoor/outdoor split is muddled — I'm shopping for two different contexts and the email blends them without giving me a navigation fork to choose a path
- The all-caps subject after 26 emails is CB2's house style, not a signal — nothing in "SOFAS FOR SUMMER" tells me whether this is new arrivals, on sale, or just a mood board

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a freight/shipping callout — "Complimentary white-glove delivery on orders over $X" would unlock the hesitation that kills every sofa purchase at the add-to-cart step
- 2. Anchor one hero product with a visible price and a single button — pick the sofa in the lifestyle shot, name it, price it, CTA it; everything else supports
- 3. Split the indoor and outdoor navigation — two buttons below the hero, two different paths, lets me self-select to what I actually need
- 4. Add a soft credibility hook, not a fake countdown — something like "Arriving before July 4th" or "Limited quantities on outdoor configurations"

```css

@media only screen and (min-width: 690px) { img { max-width: 690px } }
```
Both rules fire on screens ≥ 768px. The `100% !important` in the 768px block overrides the 690px cap, making the 690px rule partially dead. Depending on container width, images could exceed 690px on wide desktop clients.
