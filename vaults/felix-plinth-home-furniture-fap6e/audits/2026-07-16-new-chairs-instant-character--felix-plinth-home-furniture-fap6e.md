# New chairs. Instant character.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-two. I counted. And the thing is, "New chairs. Instant character." is actually the most useful thing CB2 has put in my inbox this week — which is a low bar, but credit where it's due. I'm mid-renovation on two houses simultaneously right now, and accent chairs are literally on my physical to-buy list. A three-word subject line that names the product and promises the outcome. That's it. That's what I needed.
- The email delivers on the promise: a moody hero image of a swivel chair in a styled dark room, then a clean grid of three or four chairs in different materials — a cream barrel, a cognac leather swivel, a goldenrod velvet. The "Hello, Design Aficionado" section with the 10% offer is the kind of soft-personalization that feels like CB2 knows I have the card. Good instinct.
- But here's the problem: this lands as email 22 this month, and yesterday's "JUST ADDED TO SALE" push already covered sale furniture broadly. The fatigue tax is real. I'd click — I actually would, because I need chairs — but I'd be clicking despite the volume, not because of it. I wouldn't forward this. I'd star it, throw it in the folder, and use it as a reference tab when I'm finally ready to pull the trigger.

## What's working

- **Subject line is surgical.** "New chairs. Instant character." is 30 characters, zero fluff. After CB2's all-caps "NEW COLLECTION" from last week, this reads like they hired someone with a pulse.
- **Hero image earns its space.** The moody room shot with the swivel chair anchors the email immediately — this is the aesthetic I'd pin, and the visual hierarchy doesn't make me hunt for the product.
- **Chair grid is coherent and shoppable.** Three distinct styles, clearly different materials — I can actually compare them without clicking five times. Pottery Barn would have buried two of these in a rug guide.
- **"Design Aficionado" 10% offer.** Feels like a member perk rather than a mass blast. That's the right register for CB2's positioning.
- **No clutter.** The email doesn't try to sell me kitchen canisters halfway through. Chairs, chairs, chairs. Article did this right with their entryway editorial last week — single category, maintained through the send.

## What's weak

- **The cadence is still the product.** This is a good email arriving at a bad time. The 21 that came before it have numbed me to the brand. Wayfair sent me "KITCHEN MATS under $30 ✨" three consecutive days and I barely blinked at the third one — CB2 is building toward that same immune response.
- **No pricing visible in the hero.** I can see the chairs look expensive, but I have no sense of whether "new chairs" means $399 or $1,299 before I click. Article would have anchored a starting price in the hero. That one piece of info changes whether I'm browsing or buying.
- **The product grid is small on mobile.** Three chairs in a row reads fine on desktop but on a phone those swatches compress down to thumbnail territory. I shouldn't have to zoom to tell cognac from cream.
- **No urgency signal.** "Instant character" is a lifestyle promise, not a reason to act today. The 10% offer has no visible deadline. Yesterday's "JUST ADDED TO SALE" at least implied scarcity.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor a price point in or just below the hero.** "Starting at $799 — ships in 4–6 weeks" does more for my decision-making than any lifestyle copy. Furniture buyers need shipping-window reality, not just vibes.
- 2. **Give the 10% offer a deadline.** "Through Sunday" or "48 hours only" on a member discount converts fence-sitters. Right now it floats.
- 3. **Compress the cadence.** This is an editorial problem, not a creative one. One CB2 chair email per week; this one earns that slot. The Tuesday blast and the Thursday blast cannibalize each other.
- 4. **Make the chair grid tap-targets larger on mobile.** Each chair card needs breathing room — either two-up or stacked.
- 5. **One "already own a CB2 piece" hook.** "New chairs designed to pair with the [Maeve sofa]" — if you know I've bought from you, speak to that. Crate & Barrel does this occasionally on repeat-buyer segments; CB2 almost never does.
