# Enjoy FREE shipping over $35. All day, every day.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- Two days. That's how long it took Wayfair to recycle the exact same subject line — word for word, character for character — from the July 19 send I already rated a 7. I flagged it then for being evergreen policy dressed up as a promotional email, and here it is again. I'm the kind of subscriber who keeps a mental tally of what's hitting my inbox and from whom, and this kind of repeat makes me feel like I'm getting blasted from a CRM with a stuck trigger, not marketed to.
- Inside the email: the Open Box Deals hook is actually the most relevant thing for me right now. I'm furnishing two houses simultaneously, and returned, like-new furniture at a discount is exactly the kind of deal I'm running the math on. The Black Friday in July banner (July 23–27, 80% off) gives the send a real time peg to anchor to. But then the email pivots hard into recliners — traditional-style La-Z-Boy-adjacent armchairs that have absolutely nothing to do with what I'm shopping. Recliners are not on a single wishlist across either project.
- I'd mark it as read and maybe open a tab for Open Box Deals in the background — not because the email earned it, but because the deal concept did. That's a miss. The content carried the email; the email didn't carry itself.

## What's working

- The Black Friday in July event (July 23–27, 80% off banner) creates a concrete, time-bounded reason to act — better than most Wayfair sends, which tend to run evergreen promos with no event anchor.
- Open Box Deals is smart targeting for a volume buyer; I need to fill a lot of square footage and open-box like-new at a discount is a real value proposition.
- Visual hierarchy is clean — hero lands first, recliners section below, service icons after. No hunting required.
- No render bugs: images loaded, text is legible, layout is intact.
- The Family of Brands footer (AllModern, Perigold in particular) gives me a sideways door to the aesthetics I actually shop.

## What's weak

- The subject line is a word-for-word repeat of July 19. Wayfair already blew this exact line two days ago and I noticed it then. A second identical send inside 48 hours reads like a broken automation, not a deliberate campaign.
- Recliners as the hero product category is a demographic whiff — the styles shown skew 60+ and nothing about them signals the modern renovation aesthetic I'm chasing across two properties. Article ran a home-tour email this month that understood its audience; this is the opposite.
- Both CTAs just say "Shop Now." CB2 ran "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" buttons that at least named the event. Generic copy wastes the Black Friday in July hook Wayfair actually has to work with.
- The subject promises a shipping policy, but the email delivers an event sale and a product category — those are three separate stories with no connective tissue.
- Zero product detail anywhere: no dimensions, no material callouts, no colorways. For furniture decisions I'm making across two houses, that absence means I have to click through before I even know if it's worth my time.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the recycled subject line.** The Black Friday in July event is the actual hook — use it. A shipping policy reminder is table stakes, not a reason to open.
- 2. **Unify the narrative around Open Box + BFiJ.** Lead with "Open Box pricing drops July 23" and build the recliner (or better: a broader seating/bedroom/dining grid) around that event, not as a disconnected second chapter.
- 3. **Swap the recliner hero for renovation-adjacent categories.** Nightstands, sofas, dining chairs, lighting — anything that maps to someone mid-renovation. Pottery Barn knows I'm in that headspace; Wayfair's own brand history (the July 20 nightstand send scored an 8 from me) proves they can do it.
- 4. **Make the CTAs specific.** "Shop Black Friday in July" or "Shop Open Box Deals" instead of "Shop Now" twice.
- 5. **Anchor one product with a real detail.** A material, a dimension, a colorway name. Article does this in virtually every email, and it's why I trust their furniture recommendations over Wayfair's even though Wayfair is the bigger platform.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Open Box Deals: Black Friday in July starts July 23`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Early access: up to 80% off before the July event`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Returned, like-new furniture at event pricing. July 23–27 only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop Open Box before Black Friday in July crowds the site.`
