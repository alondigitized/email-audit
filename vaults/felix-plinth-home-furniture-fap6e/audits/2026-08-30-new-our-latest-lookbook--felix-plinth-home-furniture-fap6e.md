# New! Our latest lookbook
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-30**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "The Fall Lookbook" — this is a full editorial drop, and my honest reaction scrolling through is: gorgeous, moody, zero discount. Rustic Lodge, Heritage Autumn, Indigo Blues — three fully styled rooms in a row, reclaimed wood and heritage leather, the kind of imagery that makes me want to redo the whole cabin-adjacent second house in one sitting. Then I hit "Recommended Just For You" with an extendable dining table and a platform bed and think, okay, this is at least trying to be useful to my actual punch list.
- But there's no offer anywhere. Not one percentage, not a "starting at" price except buried in the grid thumbnails. Compare that to yesterday's "Nightstands that do it all" send, which put "starting at $349" right in the grid — that one I could act on. This one is pure mood board, more like the "Molly Sims' favorites" edit from two days ago than an actual shopping email.
- Would I click? Maybe once, out of curiosity, to see the dining table pricing since that's genuinely on my list. Would I forward it? No — this is a "screenshot for the inspiration folder" email, not a "forward to my partner to decide" email. Mark as read and move on.

## What's working

- The three-room editorial structure (Rustic Lodge / Heritage Autumn / Indigo Blues) actually maps to real style decisions I'm making across two renovations right now.
- "Recommended Just For You" with the extendable dining table and platform bed feels like it's reading my actual cart/browse history, not just generic furniture.
- Rewards/Affirm/credit card blurb at the bottom ("Earn 10% Back in Rewards") is the one concrete number in the whole email and it's exactly the kind of math I care about.
- Clean, consistent brand voice — this looks and feels like Pottery Barn, no confusion about who sent it.

## What's weak

- Zero discount, zero urgency, zero price visible until you're deep into the recommended grid — Crate & Barrel's "Up to 40% off + Double Rewards" send this week just did the money math for me up top; this makes me hunt.
- Three "SHOP THE LOOK" CTAs in a row with identical styling — by the second one my eye's already glazed, no visual escalation or hierarchy pulling me toward one hero action.
- The Halloween Shop banner wedged in right after "Fall Lookbook" content is a jarring seasonal whiplash — pick a lane.
- No swatch/sample or fabric-and-finish callout anywhere, which is the thing I actually need before committing to leather or plaid upholstery on a real order.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real number in the hero — even "New arrivals, up to 20% off" would beat pure mood copy; C&B and Article both lead with the discount now and it converts better for me.
- 2. Cut two of the three "SHOP THE LOOK" room sections down to one hero room + a smaller secondary strip — right now nothing tells me which room to care about first.
- 3. Move "Recommended Just For You" up near the top — that's the section with my actual shopping intent (dining table, platform bed), don't bury it two-thirds down.
- 4. Drop the Halloween Shop insert from a fall-lookbook email entirely, or move it to its own send — it breaks the "cozy cabin" mood you just spent five sections building.
- 5. Add a swatch/sample CTA on at least one room — "Order swatches" next to "Shop the Look" would move me from browsing to actually spec'ing a room.

# Technical Email Audit — Pottery Barn "New! Our latest lookbook"

## 1. Technical Summary
Standard Responsys-templated HTML email with table-based layout and MSO conditional comments; core rendering markup is sound, but tracking-pixel/compliance headers were not captured by the relay and a handful of legacy `http://` asset references remain.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- Primary CTA and header links route through the Responsys click-tracking domain `click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=...` (base64-style opaque payload) — 61 tracking/redirect links total were skipped by the automated HTTP probe (expected behavior for redirect domains, not a defect).
- Open-tracking pixel served over plaintext HTTP: `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` — mixed-content risk, and the recipient's raw email address is passed in a URL query string in cleartext.
- Secondary click-tracking pixel: `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/c041fc7b-9e09-474c-819d-5205e92e1974?mi_mid=...` — resolves fine over HTTPS, no issue.
- Third-party pixel to `d.turn.com/r/dd/id/...` (Oracle/Turn ad-tracking) — present but not flagged as broken; note as an external data-sharing pixel, not a rendering issue.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- Widespread missing `alt` attributes: every content and slice image in the QA output (banner, `slices_01`–`slices_18`, product shots, footer spacers, tracking pixels) lacks `alt` text — 40+ instances. This fails basic email accessibility (screen readers) and leaves blank space when images are blocked.
- Two non-HTTPS image sources will be blocked/flagged by mail clients enforcing mixed-content rules:
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `<style>` block contains stray PHP-style `header(...)` calls (`header('Content-Type: image/jpeg')`, cache-control headers) sitting inside a CSS `<style>` tag — these are inert as CSS but indicate a template artifact/copy-paste error from a server-side script; harmless to rendering but should be cleaned up.
- No `<title>` text set (`<title></title>` empty) — cosmetic, affects webmail tab labeling only.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- Recipient identifier `lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` is resolved and embedded in plaintext in at least three places: the `coreemail.html?email=...` preference-center link, the `sv_sveme=` open-tracking pixel, and (likely) other Responsys merge fields. No unresolved merge tags (e.g. `{{FIRST_NAME}}`) were found in the visible source — personalization appears correctly resolved for this send.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication)
- `List-Unsubscribe` header not detected — QA notes this may be an AgentMail relay capture limitation rather than a genuine absence; cannot confirm compliance status from headers alone. A footer-based unsubscribe/preference link (`coreemail.html`) is present in the HTML body, which covers CAN-SPAM's basic unsubscribe requirement regardless of header presence.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe) not detected — if genuinely absent, this means Gmail/Yahoo one-click unsubscribe is not supported for this send, which will hurt sender reputation under bulk-sender requirements.
- `Authentication-Results` header not found — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status cannot be verified from available data; this is a relay-capture gap, not proof of an auth failure.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- Outbound links do not use standard `?utm_source/utm_medium/utm_campaign` query parameters — Pottery Barn uses Responsys's proprietary opaque `qs=` token instead, so campaign attribution is not verifiable from the URL structure alone (this is a vendor convention, not a defect).
- Preference-center link resolves to `www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=...` — same root domain as the sender, consistent domain alignment between email and landing destination.
- Product image links point to `www.potterybarn.com/pbimgs/.../*.jpg` (live production asset paths) rather than the `edm.potterybarn.com` ESP asset host used elsewhere — inconsistent asset hosting strategy, though not a functional break per the QA data.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to all content images (banners, product shots, slice images); leave decorative spacer/tracking pixels with `alt=""` rather than omitted.
2. Migrate the two remaining `http://` image sources (`Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, `slices_02.jpg`) to HTTPS to avoid mixed-content blocking in strict mail clients.
3. Remove the stray `header('Content-Type: ...')` PHP artifacts from the `<style>` block — dead code left over from a server-side template.
4. Confirm with the ESP (Responsys) that `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are actually being sent at the SMTP layer — verify via a raw header dump from a non-relay-captured test send, since the current QA gap may be a capture artifact rather than a true compliance failure.
5. Verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment directly against `e.potterybarn.com`'s DNS records rather than relying on relay-captured `Authentication-Results`.
