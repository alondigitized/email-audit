# $1K COULD BE YOURS TO WIN
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-eight. I went back and counted — this is at least the second CB2 email to land in my inbox today, following "SO MUCH NEW" from this morning, which was itself the sequel to "MORE ON SALE" and "APRES POOL" from yesterday. At a certain volume the brand becomes ambient noise, and CB2 has crossed that threshold for me in July. So the first thing I'm asking when I see "$1K COULD BE YOURS TO WIN" is: what's the catch?
- The catch is the app. They want me to download the CB2 app to enter a sweepstakes for a $1,000 gift card. I'll give them this: a grand at CB2 is a real number in my world right now. I'm mid-renovation on two houses simultaneously and I've been deferring a sectional decision for six weeks. That hero room shot doesn't hurt — warm tones, moody ambient lamp, exactly the vibe I'm trying to approximate in my primary. But the mechanic underneath is a data-capture play dressed in prize money. Download the app, sign in, maybe win. It's Publisher's Clearing House energy inside a $3,000 sofa brand.
- I'd open it — the subject is blunt enough to create a pause. I wouldn't tap "DOWNLOAD NOW." I'd scroll the "SO MUCH NEW" product grid at the bottom, which is, I'll note, the exact same section header as the subject line of the earlier email today. Someone on this team is running on creative autopilot. I'd mark it read and move on.

## What's working

- The hero room image is doing genuine heavy lifting — warm, sophisticated living room that maps directly to what I'm trying to build. It stopped the scroll.
- "$1,000" lands. Five figures register differently than "10% off." In a category where a dining table runs $1,200, this is aspirational but not absurd.
- "NOW UNTIL AUGUST 15" is a real deadline, not a fake-countdown-timer stunt. I respect the honesty.
- "DOWNLOAD NOW" is an unambiguous CTA — high contrast, verb-forward, top of the email. No buried link, no four-paragraph journey to find the button.
- The "SO MUCH NEW" product grid below the fold (Sonja Sectional, new lighting, rugs) gives me somewhere to go if the sweepstakes mechanic doesn't land. Useful fallback.

## What's weak

- The entire email is built around a mechanic that treats me like a cold prospect. I'm already on the CB2 list. I already have the relationship. Pottery Barn's send earlier this month acknowledged existing order history — this one asks me to download an app to enter a lottery like I've never heard of them.
- "SO MUCH NEW" appears as both a section header here *and* as the subject line of the CB2 email that hit my inbox hours earlier. That's either a sequencing accident or a creative department with five content buckets and no coordination between sends.
- The "Meet the CB2 App" four-icon row (exclusive offers, personalized ideas, manage orders, scan items) is onboarding copy, not a value proposition. Article would have shown me *what* the exclusive content actually is. This is an app store listing pasted into an email.
- No prices anywhere in the product grid. Thumbnails without prices are visual decoration, not conversion tools. Crate & Barrel's "dark rich woods" send last week put prices inline — I knew what I was clicking into.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Flip the mechanic for known customers.** If I'm already on the email list, I've crossed the moat. Lead with "App members get 10% off everything for 7 days — install now to activate" and let the $1K sweepstakes be the secondary hook. Turn the app install into an immediate discount trigger, not a lottery ticket. That's the version I forward to my partner.
- 2. **Kill the four-icon app explainer.** Replace it with two or three products from the "SO MUCH NEW" grid — with prices — linked directly to PDPs. Sectional, rug, pendant. That's what I came here for.
- 3. **Deconflict same-day creative.** "SO MUCH NEW" cannot be a section header in email #28 on the same day it was the subject line of email #27. That's an editorial calendar problem, not a copywriting problem, but it signals to subscribers that they're on a factory line.
- 4. **Add prices to the "MORE FOR YOU" row.** Five thumbnail images with no context, no pricing, no SKU detail — that's not personalization, that's wallpaper.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Double-slash font URLs (broken asset requests)**
Three `@font-face` `src` declarations use `//` after the domain, producing malformed paths:
```
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2
```
Most servers tolerate the double slash, but it is non-canonical and will fail on strict path routing or CDN rules that normalize URLs.

**Cross-domain font loading**
`HNW05` font variants are loaded from `crateandbarrel.com` (parent brand), not `cb2.com`. If `crateandbarrel.com`'s CDN is down or its CORS policy tightens, CB2 emails silently fall back to system fonts.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Production developer placeholder left in `<head>`**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
This is an unprocessed template comment (note the typo: "IMPUT"). It should never reach a send. Indicates a template QA gap.

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) requires content to be resizable up to 200% without assistive technology. This fails that criterion.

**Global text-size-adjust suppression**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applied via the `*` universal selector — affects every element. Overrides both OS-level and browser-level font scaling for users with visual impairments. Should be scoped to `body` only and set to `100%`, not `none`.

**Conflicting `@font-face` declarations for "Lineto Brown Sub"**
The font is declared three times:
- Once with `.eot` + `.woff2` + `.woff` sources (no weight specified)
- Once with `font-weight: 400` pointing to the Light variant
- Once with `font-weight: 600` pointing to the Regular variant

Weight 400 maps to the Light cut and weight 600 to Regular — inverted from convention. Outlook and some WebKit clients may render the wrong weight.

**Non-standard mobile breakpoint**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)
```
414px targets the iPhone SE/6/7/8 logical width. Devices with 415–479px viewports (many Android mid-range) receive no responsive overrides. Standard practice is `480px` or `600px`.
