# Just got BIGGER: Earn 2x Rewards + up to 50% off The Memorial Day Event
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-two. I've been counting. This is, by my reckoning, email number twenty-two from some configuration of the Crate & Barrel family since Sunday, and the particular sting of this one is that it's from Crate & *Kids* — a sub-brand I apparently joined at some point, presumably by clicking something on the website at 11pm while trying to find a nightstand. I don't have children. I have two half-renovated houses and an increasingly hostile relationship with my inbox.
- The sale is real — 50% off and 2x Rewards is the best number the C&B family has offered this week — but what I'm actually being sold is a ball pit. I can see it mid-scroll: a round container full of colorful plastic balls, next to a photo of a toddler, next to what looks like toy storage. The hero bedroom scene at the top is nice but clearly a kids' room. Nothing below the fold is furniture I would ever put in either of my houses.
- The email is clean, the offer is legible, the buttons work. I'm still marking it read and moving on. Would not click, would not forward — unless I'm specifically warning a parent friend that the C&B kids sale is live, which is an act of charity, not marketing conversion.

## What's working

- The 50% off + 2x Rewards offer lands immediately in the hero banner — clear numbers, no squinting required, no "up to X% on select styles" buried in a footnote.
- Render is clean on mobile: no broken images, no overlapping text, tap targets on the CTA buttons are appropriately sized.
- The Memorial Day framing is current and doesn't feel recycled — it's at least seasonally coherent, which I can't say for every brand in my inbox this week.
- The rewards callout signals I'm in the loyalty ecosystem, which creates just enough of a "this is for me" feeling before the product grid destroys it.

## What's weak

- The products are for children and I don't have children. A ball pit is the exact midpoint between "irrelevant" and "mocking me."
- This is email twenty-two from the C&B family in roughly 48 hours. The previous send — "400+ NEW DEALS ADDED" same day — already pushed the offer to 60% off, so "up to 50% off" in this subject line actually feels like a *downgrade* in the sequence.
- The subject "Just got BIGGER" is doing a lot of work with very little specificity. Bigger than what? Article sent one Memorial Day email this week, told me exactly what was on sale, and didn't make me decode a teaser.
- Zero personalization. Not my name, not my browse history, not even a nod that I might be a home renovator and not a nursery designer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pull Crate & Kids off non-parent segments entirely.** If I've never clicked a kids product, there is no behavioral signal that should put me on this list. This is a list hygiene problem, not a creative problem. Pottery Barn, for all its email volume this week, at least kept their kids list separate and didn't mix it into the main sale blast.
- 2. **Stop sending sub-brand blasts as standalone sends.** A single C&B-family digest consolidating C&B, CB2, and Crate & Kids into one email is one send instead of three. The "400+ NEW DEALS ADDED" send earlier *today* already exhausted my patience — this competes with itself.
- 3. **Rewrite the subject to anchor on the number.** "Just got BIGGER" makes me work. "Memorial Day just hit 50% off + 2x Rewards" tells me immediately whether to open.
- 4. **Add a crossover escape hatch.** A small footer link — "Not shopping for kids? See the full C&B Memorial Day sale" — would rescue conversion from mis-targeted adults. That's a five-minute engineering task that probably doubles click rate for this segment.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cribs, rugs, play furniture — everything in the event just went deeper on discount`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `2x Rewards stack on every order. Sale ends Memorial Day.`
