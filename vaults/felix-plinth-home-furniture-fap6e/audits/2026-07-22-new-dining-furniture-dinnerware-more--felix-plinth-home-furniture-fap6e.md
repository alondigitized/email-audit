# NEW dining furniture, dinnerware & more
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-six. July. I say it every time because someone at Pottery Barn HQ needs to see it in writing. And of all the ways to spend slot #36, they picked a new arrivals send with no discount. The timing is legitimately not terrible — I'm actively in the weeds on a dining table decision for the second home, and the hero photo of that round wood table with cane chairs stopped me for exactly two seconds. That's real photography doing real work.
- But two seconds is all they got, because after the hero there's no reason to keep reading. No price. No shipping offer. No member callout. Just a sequence of pretty sections — "Hardwood Dining Essentials," "Artful Accent Pieces," "Thoughtfully Crafted Furniture," "INDIGO BLUES" — stacked like catalog pages with no through-line or urgency. Article dropped a dining room email on me four days ago and it felt more actionable than this one, which is an embarrassing sentence to write about a brand that's been in the dining furniture business for decades. Wayfair would've had an end table percentage off in the first 80 pixels. Pottery Barn is asking me to shop on vibes.
- Mark as read. No click. Definitely not forwarding.

## What's working

- **Hero image earns the open** — the round wood dining table, cane-back chairs, yellow flowers, and blue and white pottery is a genuinely well-styled room shot. That's the kind of visual that makes you pause even at email #36.
- **"Hardwood Dining Essentials" copy** — naming the material at the section level is the right call for furniture buyers who actually care about construction. Better than the vague "our newest pieces" pattern.
- **"INDIGO BLUES" as a named collection** — giving a palette a name creates a mental handle for a buyer building a cohesive look. It's good catalog thinking even if the aesthetic isn't mine.
- **"Recommended Just for You" section** — at least signals they know I buy in this category, which is more than most broadcasts.
- **Clean render, no bugs** — layout holds, images load, text is legible at mobile scale.

## What's weak

- **No offer means no urgency** — this is a browse email asking me to shop without giving me any reason to do it today rather than next week or never. Article pulled off a no-discount dining room email by making the aspiration feel pointed; this one just... exists.
- **"& more" in the subject** — catalog filler. If it's hardwood dining furniture and Indigo Blues dinnerware, say that. "More" signals they couldn't prioritize, which makes me not prioritize opening it.
- **Aesthetic skews feminine** — blue and white china, florals in every hero shot, the INDIGO BLUES section — not a dealbreaker, but it reads like a broadcast send, not a segment aimed at someone like me. CB2 manages to feel gender-neutral even in editorial sends; this feels like it was pulled from a women's home audience.
- **Three separately-named "furniture" sections** — "Hardwood Dining Essentials" + "Thoughtfully Crafted Furniture" is the same thing split in two. Competing section headers dilute each other.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a number in the hero** — even "free shipping on orders over $199" or "Key Rewards members earn 10% back" turns this from a catalog browse into an actionable email. There is no reason this lives above the hero CTA.
- 2. **Kill "& more" in the subject** — name the actual categories or pick one. "New dining tables + Indigo Blues dinnerware" is more specific and still short.
- 3. **Collapse the furniture sections** — three named furniture blocks fight each other; give me one strong "Dining Room" section and one "Accent Pieces" and move on.
- 4. **Make the Recommended section work harder** — if you know my purchase history, show me one specific product with a price, not a thumbnail grid with no context.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New dining tables + Indigo Blues pottery — just in`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Hardwood dining furniture just landed (free shipping inside)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Round tables, cane chairs, blue & white dinnerware — new arrivals worth a look`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New dining room furniture, now with free shipping on orders over $199`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP code inside `<style>` block**
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
Four PHP `header()` calls appear literally inside a `<style>` tag. They are inert in HTML context and will not execute, but this is a clear signal that template assembly is pulling from a server-side image-generation script without stripping its PHP preamble. Browsers and email clients will attempt to parse this as CSS and silently discard it, but it inflates payload and indicates a fragile build process.

**`<title>` is empty**
`<title></title>` — affects tab rendering in webmail clients (Gmail browser tab, Outlook Web).

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
`<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` — prevents user-initiated zoom. This is an accessibility regression on mobile; WCAG 1.4.4 requires content to be resizable up to 200%.

### 5. Compliance

If missing from the actual SMTP envelope (not just a relay artifact), Gmail and Apple Mail will not surface the one-click unsubscribe button in their UI — increasing spam complaints and risking deliverability throttling under Google's 2024 bulk sender requirements.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post header not present**
RFC 8058 one-click POST unsubscribe is not confirmed. This is required for Google and Yahoo bulk sender compliance (>5k/day threshold).

**`no-referrer` meta tag**
`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />` — suppresses the HTTP Referer header on all outbound clicks. This is intentional for privacy but means landing page analytics cannot attribute sessions back to email as a channel without relying entirely on UTM parameters.
