# This beauty is our bestselling wine glass. Period.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- Day five of the C&B family barrage — I've stopped counting — and for the first time this week they didn't lead with a warehouse sale. That's almost enough to make me emotional. The subject line's "Period." is a punctuation choice that actually registers as a human writing something, not a campaign scheduler firing a template. I opened it. That matters given how many I've archived unread this week.
- The wine glass hero is clean, the star ratings are right there above the fold, and for about three seconds this felt like C&B doing what C&B does well: confident, well-styled editorial. Then the email kept scrolling. Dinnerware, flatware, and by the time I hit what looked like furniture vignettes near the bottom, the thread was completely gone. "Bestselling wine glass. Period." is a thesis statement. You don't end a thesis statement and then add three more chapters from different departments.
- I'd click through to the wine glass PDP — I actually need a set right now for the primary home — but I wouldn't forward this and I wouldn't call it a great email. It's a solid concept undermined by a kitchen-sink body.

## What's working

- **"Period." earns its keep** — that one word makes the subject feel authored rather than generated. It broke through the inbox noise in a way the June 19 Warehouse Sale subjects ("Up to 60% off!") did not.
- **Star ratings visible in the hero section** — Wayfair buried reviews in the flameless candle email I audited and it cost them the click. C&B put the ratings front and center on the lead product. Right call.
- **Visual hierarchy at the top is genuinely clean** — white space, centered photography, no competing CTAs fighting for the top of the email. The eye lands where it should.
- **Tone shift is welcome** — an editorial "bestseller" frame after three days of clearance-rack urgency is a palette cleanser. It reads like brand, not panic.

## What's weak

- **Doesn't stay in its lane** — the wine glass opener bleeds into dinnerware, then flatware, then what appears to be furniture. Article's Cillian Collection email last week picked one story and committed to it. This one hedges.
- **No offer, no shipping signal** — I'm buying for two houses. "Bestselling" is nice; "ships in 5 days, free on orders over $49" would close me. Pottery Barn's "On sale & ready-to-ship" email paired editorial confidence with a logistics fact and that combination is hard to beat.
- **Cadence tax is real** — even a good email pays a penalty when it's the fifth in 48 hours from the same brand family. The goodwill from the subject line doesn't fully survive that context.
- **No loyalty acknowledgment** — I have a C&B rewards account. CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" email from the same week made the same mistake. Treating me like a cold prospect when I'm a repeat buyer is a wasted opportunity.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill everything below the wine glass section** — the dinnerware, flatware, and furniture blocks dilute the story the subject line promised. One product, one CTA, done. The Article Cillian email is the template: commit to the item, let the photography do the selling.
- 2. **Add a shipping/availability line near the CTA** — two words. "In stock." That's it. For a furniture and home buyer mid-renovation, logistics confidence is as persuasive as 20% off.
- 3. **Pull one real review quote** — the star rating count is good; one sentence of customer copy is better. "I've replaced every other glass in my cabinet with this one" beats a 4.8 average every time.
- 4. **Surface rewards balance or member pricing** — even a single line ("Insider? Earn 10% back on this purchase") turns a browse into a commitment.

- **Preheader Alt B:** `The one glass our buyers rate five stars and actually reorder.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CONFIRMED] Unresolved template placeholder in `<head>`**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
This is a build-time authoring comment that shipped in production. The misspelling ("IMPUT" vs "INPUT") confirms this is inherited boilerplate, not intentional. No functional breakage, but indicates the template was not fully cleaned before send.

**[CONFIRMED] Conflicting `img` max-width at desktop breakpoints**
Two `@media` rules target overlapping viewport widths:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
At viewports ≥ 768px, both rules fire. The `640px`-breakpoint rule appears later in the stylesheet and wins the cascade, capping images at **640px**, not 600px. If the template container is 600px wide, images may overflow or render at unexpected sizes.

**[CONFIRMED] User zoom disabled on iOS**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom in Safari/iOS. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) requires content to be resizable up to 200% without loss of functionality.

**[CONFIRMED] Global text-size-adjust disabled**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applied to all elements via the universal selector. This suppresses browser-level font scaling for users who have increased system text size. The intent (prevent Outlook auto-scaling) is better achieved by scoping to `body` or specific table cells.

**[CONFIRMED] Duplicate `<meta>` declarations**
`format-detection` appears four times — once as a combined value and three times individually:
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
`charset` is also declared twice (`http-equiv` + direct `charset` attribute). Neither causes functional breakage but indicates duplicate template merging.

**[CONFIRMED] `.showmobile` hardcoded to 414px**
```css
.showmobile { width: 414px !important; }
```
This fires at the `max-width: 460px` breakpoint but targets iPhone 6/7/8 logical width specifically. On devices with narrower viewports (e.g., 360px Android), this will cause horizontal overflow.

**Light-mode only declared**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Dark mode is explicitly excluded. Some clients (Apple Mail, Gmail Android) may still force dark-mode inversion on images and backgrounds, which can produce unintended results if the email was not tested against forced dark mode.
