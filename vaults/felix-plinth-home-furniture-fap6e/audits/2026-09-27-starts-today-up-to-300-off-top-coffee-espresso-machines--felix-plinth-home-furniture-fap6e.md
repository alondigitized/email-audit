# Starts today: Up to $300 off top coffee & espresso machines!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-27**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this actually hooked me for a second — "up to $300 off top coffee & espresso machines" is a real, specific offer, and I've been eyeing an upgrade from our drip pot since we're redoing the kitchen in the second house. The Breville hero shot is clean, the price ladders are visible right under each machine, and there's a genuinely useful "$300 off a do-it-all fave in an exclusive color" callout early. But by the time I hit the fourth espresso machine — Miele, then another "20% off exclusive KitchenAid" — I'm scrolling fatigue city, and this email just keeps going. It's basically Crate & Barrel's entire coffee category dumped into one send.
- This is a step up from the SMEG send on 9/26, which at least stayed tight around one collection. This one sprawls — by the "more from the Coffee & Espresso Event" section I've already made my decision, and everything after that (fall pecan latte recipe, Caraway cookware teaser, Thanksgiving banner, "we love these for you" grid) is just noise tacked onto a single-category email.
- Would I click? Yeah, probably on the Breville hero or the "$300 off" banner up top — that's a real number on a real category I need. Would I forward it? Not to a friend, but maybe screenshot the Breville price to myself for the reno folder. Would not forward the whole email — too bloated.

## What's working

- The "up to $300 off" headline is concrete and matches the subject line exactly — no bait and switch like the 9/26 "10% off even on furniture" email that buried its real CTA under a text-signup mockup.
- Each machine card has a visible price + discount (20% off, $200 off, $100 off, 25% off) right under the product shot — easy to scan.
- Kitchen/counter styling in the photos (marble counters, warm wood) is exactly my second-house aesthetic — feels like a real kitchen, not a studio white-box shot.
- Sale end date ("Ends 9/28, 11:59pm ET") is visible on multiple cards — real urgency, not vague "limited time."

## What's weak

- This email just does not end — six-plus machines, then a totally unrelated pecan-crunch-latte recipe, then a Caraway cookware teaser, then a Thanksgiving banner. Pick a lane.
- No mention of Crate&Barrel Rewards or loyalty pricing anywhere, and I've got their card — Pottery Barn's Thanksgiving Shop send at least felt like it knew its audience segment better.
- Zero swatch/finish comparison across machines even though color/finish clearly varies (cream KitchenAid, black Breville, stainless Miele) — for a $1,500+ espresso machine I want to see finish options before I click, not guess.
- The "we love these for you" personalization grid at the very bottom is generic clearance-bin filler (candle holders, random glassware) that has nothing to do with coffee — feels bolted on, not personalized.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this to the top 2-3 machines and split the rest into a follow-up send — this is one email doing the job of three, and it's killing the hierarchy that was strong in the first three screens.
- 2. Move a loyalty/rewards callout up near the hero — I'm a cardholder, treat me like one instead of a stranger getting a mass blast.
- 3. Kill the recipe/Thanksgiving/random-product tail entirely from this send — those belong in separate emails, not stapled to a coffee machine sale.
- 4. Add a one-line finish/color chooser under each hero product ("also in stainless, matte black") since that's the actual decision blocker for expensive kitchen gear.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your espresso upgrade: up to $300 off, 2 days only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Breville, Miele, KitchenAid — sale ends 9/28 at midnight`
