# Our 4th of July Warehouse Sale just got BIGGER!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-five. Roughly. The C&B family has sent me so many emails this month that I've stopped pretending I'm tracking the count accurately. I opened this one because it said "BIGGER" in all-caps and I thought maybe they'd added a category — outdoor furniture, rugs, something new. They didn't. The email is functionally identical to the "NEW DEALS! The 4th of July Warehouse Sale just got bigger 🎆" send I already reviewed today — same campaign, same sale, same furniture photography, different subject-line caps treatment.
- That said: when I slowed down mid-scroll, the kitchen section stopped me. There's a KitchenAid stand mixer visible and I've been going back and forth on whether the second house actually needs one. That specific moment of product recognition is what separates this from the Bodie Collection spotlight earlier today, which showed me a single collection with no urgency hook and nothing I was in the market for. Here, at least the merchandise selection is broad enough that something lands.
- Would I click? One tap, into the kitchen section, then out. Would I forward it? No. Would I pin it? I would have — if I hadn't already pinned the first version of this exact email earlier today.

## What's working

- The kitchen section with the KitchenAid is the most purposeful moment in the email — a specific product I was already considering, surfaced in a seasonal sale context
- The 4th of July deadline is real and credible right now, unlike the Bodie Collection email from this same send-day, which had no urgency signal attached to anything
- Multi-category layout (dining, bedroom, living, kitchen in one scroll) is genuinely useful when you're furnishing two houses simultaneously — I don't have to go hunting across multiple sends
- Clean render throughout — no broken images, no text overlap, no layout collapse; that alone puts it ahead of some of the Wayfair sends I've been processing this week

## What's weak

- This is functionally a duplicate of the earlier "NEW DEALS!" warehouse sale email from the same day — Article pulled off a clean narrative escalation with their 4th of July follow-up; C&B just changed the subject line caps and hit send again
- No price anchor in the hero — Pottery Barn's "Extra 20% off select styles" (also June 26, scored 9/10 by me) gave me an instant mental calculation; this gives me "BIGGER" and expects me to care
- "BIGGER!" in all-caps in the subject line is a panic move that makes me trust the sale less, not more — it reads like a resend acknowledgment rather than a genuine upgrade
- No loyalty or rewards callout anywhere in the visible layout; I'm making every furniture purchase on highest-rewards-card math and a single "C&B Rewards members save an extra X%" line would change my calculus
- Product sections feel like a catalog dump rather than a curated "here's what's newly added to the warehouse sale" edit — if the sale actually got bigger, show me what's new

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a hard number in the hero** — "Up to 40% off" or "New markdowns: save up to $X" is always more actionable than "just got BIGGER." Pottery Barn does this correctly; C&B is consistently lagging on this exact signal.
- 2. **Consolidate to one warehouse sale email per day** — if you're escalating, show me what was added: a new category, a deeper cut, a countdown timer. Two nearly-identical sends in one day trains me to skip the second.
- 3. **Add a rewards/loyalty line** — even a footer callout. I need to know if stacking a C&B card gets me anything before I decide whether to open a second tab.
- 4. **Label the "new" items** — if the sale genuinely expanded, flag those products with a "just added" badge so the email justifies its own existence as an escalation.
- 5. **Sharpen the CTA copy** — "Shop the Warehouse" beats "Shop Now" on every test. Verb + noun, not a placeholder.
- **Subject Alt A:** `4th of July Sale: new markdowns just added`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Warehouse Sale expanded — here's what's new`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Kitchen, dining, bedroom — deeper cuts, ends soon.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh picks added to the sale. See what's new.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue: Conflicting desktop image max-width rules**

Two `min-width` media queries define `img { max-width }` in reverse specificity order:

```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) {
  img { max-width: 600px }   /* declared first */
}
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) {
  img { max-width: 640px }   /* declared second — overrides at ≥640px */
}
```

The 640px rule is declared *after* the 768px rule, so at any viewport ≥640px, images resolve to `max-width: 640px`. The 768px rule is effectively dead. If the design intent is `600px` at wide desktop, the declaration order must be reversed, or the 768px rule must be moved below the 640px rule.

**Issue: Unescaped `&` in XHTML title**

```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```

The document declares `XHTML 1.0 Transitional`. The `&` must be `&amp;`. Benign in practice (browsers handle it), but technically malformed and will fail strict XML parsers.
