# 3 dream bedroom scenarios
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- Twenty. And counting. The sender is "CB2 Free Design Services" which technically arrives on a different track than the main promo list, but my brain has long since stopped making that distinction — it's all CB2, it's all July, and I'm filing everything from this domain on autopilot. To their credit, this one doesn't open with a discount. It opens with "DREAM SCENARIO" in bold editorial type over a moodily lit bedroom shot, and it's pitching free interior design appointments. That's actually a reasonable break from the seven-day march of "UP TO 40% OFF" and "GET IT ON SALE."
- Here's the thing: I'm renovating two bedrooms right now and genuinely don't have a designer on either project. The "PERFECT BEDROOM PAIRINGS" grid in the middle section is exactly the kind of curated shorthand I save to a folder and revisit on a Sunday. The named combos — whatever the two pairing columns are — let me think in sets, not individual pieces, which is how I actually shop for rooms. The "3 WAYS TO MEET" structure (in-person, virtually, at home) is useful framing too, even if it's a little business-card energy.
- The problem is I've already gotten a design-services adjacent send from CB2 this week, plus yesterday's editorial "How a gallerist lives with objects" from the main list. This one isn't annoying — it's just blurring into the stack. I'd save it to the folder, probably not book the appointment today, and check back when I'm further along with the primary bedroom floor plan. Not a click, but not a delete either.

## What's working

- **"DREAM SCENARIO" hero is exactly the right tone** — aspirational without being screamy, and it pairs naturally with someone mid-renovation who has a specific vision in their head
- **The bedroom pairings grid earns attention** — named combinations let me think in room sets, not single SKUs; this is the most useful organizational content CB2 has sent me this month
- **"3 WAYS TO MEET" removes my objection before I form it** — I don't want to drive to a showroom on a weekday, so seeing "virtually" and "at home" options visible without clicking buys goodwill
- **Free appointment CTA is honest and low-pressure** — no fine print visible, no "valid on in-store purchases of $X" asterisk, which is more than I can say for the July 9th "up to 40% off" send that excluded half the sale inventory

## What's weak

- **I can't tell what the three "scenarios" actually are** — the subject promises three distinct bedroom scenarios but the email delivers a design-services pitch; Crate & Barrel's Le Creuset email last week led with exactly what it was, and I respected that directness
- **No product-level specificity in the pairing grid** — I can see paired shots but can't read the product names or prices at thumbnail size; Article's "new arrivals just landed" send last week showed piece-by-piece in a scrollable grid that actually worked on mobile
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is the 20th CB2 or CB2-adjacent email in July and the sender name "CB2 Free Design Services" doesn't differentiate enough to reset my mental counter
- **Zero urgency signal** — free design appointments presumably don't expire, but there's no "X appointments remaining this month" or seasonal hook to push me off the fence today

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the three bedroom scenarios as actual design directions** — give them names ("Warm Brutalism," "Quiet Luxury," "Raw Industrial") and let me self-select before you pitch the appointment; that's the editorial that would make me forward this to my contractor
- 2. **Add a capacity or calendar hook** — "Designers available this week in Chicago" or "Book before July 20" would convert saves-to-folder into actual bookings
- 3. **Surface one hero product with a price** — even a single pairing at "from $XXX" anchors the value proposition and gives me something concrete to screenshot for the renovation Pinterest board
- 4. **Differentiate the sender name at the list level** — "CB2 Design Studio" landing in a separate inbox tab or labeled differently would reset the cadence clock; right now it reads as email #20 not email #1

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta (line 4)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents user-initiated zoom. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Universal `text-size-adjust: none` (CSS `*` rule)**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;-ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applied to every element, not just the body. This suppresses OS-level text scaling globally, compounding the zoom issue above.

**Double-slash font URLs — 4 instances**
```
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2
```
Extra leading slash. Most servers handle this gracefully via redirect, but it adds a round-trip latency hit and is inconsistent with the single-slash declarations earlier in the same `<style>` block.

**Duplicate `@font-face` for `"Lineto Brown Sub"`**
Three separate `@font-face` blocks share the family name `"Lineto Brown Sub"` with overlapping `font-weight` values (400 appears twice). The third declaration (woff2, weight 400) overwrites the earlier combined eot/woff2/woff declaration. Likely harmless in practice but fragile.

**Cross-domain font dependency on `crateandbarrel.com`**
```css
@font-face { font-family:"HNW05"; src: url("https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/...") }
```
Four `HNW05` cuts load from the sibling brand's domain. If that domain's CORS headers don't explicitly allow `mail.CB2.com` origins (they won't for webmail clients), this font will silently fail. It also creates a delivery dependency on an uncontrolled third-party host.

**Mobile breakpoint at `max-width: 418px`**
Responsive overrides only apply below 418px. Common Android devices render at 360–412px logical width, but iPhones 14 and later are 390px+. The breakpoint catches most but misses some 420–600px viewport widths where the layout may not adapt correctly.
