# Picking the perfect rug is easy—just ask The Design Desk
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-23**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one's actually decent — for once a Crate & Barrel email lands squarely in a project I'm actively dealing with. We're doing rugs in both houses right now, and "The Design Desk" framing — a design pro breaking it down into aesthetics, practicality, size — feels like an actual answer to a question I have, not just another product dump. It's still C&B, so of course there's a wall of rugs by the fifth scroll, but at least this one has a spine: a clear three-part framework instead of just five collections stacked with no logic.
- That said, my brain is fried on this brand family. This is the sixth-ish C&B/CB2 email I've plowed through this week — I flagged "The one that checks every box" sofa email and its near-clone three days ago for the exact same "keep scrolling forever" problem, and this one, while better organized, still runs long: aesthetics, then practicality, then size, then "more ways The Design Desk can help," then reviews, then "we love these for you," then an app download pitch. That's a lot of real estate for one rug decision.
- Would I click? Probably, actually — "Book Your Free Appointment" for a design consult is genuinely useful when you're renovating two houses and don't know jute from wool-blend. Would I forward it? Maybe to my wife, since she's more hands-on with the rug picks. Not marking as read and moving on — this one earns a real look.

## What's working

- The "3 things our design pros keep in mind" structure (aesthetics/practicality/size) is genuinely useful for someone actively choosing a rug — this isn't Article's decor-vibe filler, it's decision-support.
- Room photography is consistently my style — warm neutrals, arched mirrors, textured jute, the kind of "collected" look CB2 chases but this one earns because it's tied to a real buying decision.
- "Book Your Free Appointment" as a repeated CTA gives me an actual next step beyond "buy this rug," which matters when I'm furnishing two houses and could use expert input.
- Price points are visible under each rug thumbnail — no hide-the-price nonsense like Pottery Barn's "sign up for texts" bait I flagged before.

## What's weak

- It's still long — by the time I hit "the reviews are in" and "we love these for you" I've mentally checked out; Article's entryway email proved you can make a home project feel authoritative in way less scroll.
- The "$500 sweepstakes on the app" bolt-on at the bottom feels tacked-on and dilutes what was otherwise a focused, expert-framed email.
- No swatch-and-sample callout anywhere — for a rug purchase, "order a sample first" is the single most useful CTA missing given how many of these are wool/jute textures I can't judge from a thumbnail.
- Multiple "Bestseller" tags with no actual review counts or star ratings attached — feels like a claim, not proof.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after "03. size" — drop "more ways The Design Desk can help," the reviews block, and the "we love these for you" grid, or move them to a follow-up send. This is the single biggest lift: shorter, sharper, more like Article's 3-step format.
- 2. Add a "order a free swatch" CTA next to "Shop Rugs" — rugs are a texture/color-match purchase, and right now there's zero sampling path visible.
- 3. Put actual star ratings or review counts on the "Bestseller" tags instead of an unsupported label.
- 4. Move the appointment booking CTA higher — if design consultation is the hook, don't make me scroll past three rug sections to find it twice.
- 5. Drop or separate the $500 app sweepstakes — it competes with the rug-buying intent instead of supporting it.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Wrong rug? Here's how our design pros avoid it`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Aesthetics, practicality, size — then book a free design session`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free swatches, free design help, rugs that actually fit your space`
