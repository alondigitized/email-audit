# STAIR TREADS.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-two. That's where the Wayfair counter sits since Sunday, and I need to say it out loud before anything else because it's the only real frame for this email. Email number twenty-two is "STAIR TREADS." — and here's the thing I didn't expect: the subject is actually excellent. I'm literally spec'ing out stair treads for the second home right now. The period at the end feels almost confrontational, like Wayfair looked at my browsing history and just decided to stop pretending. For exactly two seconds I felt seen.
- Then I opened it. The stair tread content is there — the hero is there — but the email immediately pivots. "Big Help." Bed upgrade. "Picked just for you." It's the same Wayfair catalog dump I've seen twenty-one times this week with a different cover slide. The subject earned the open; the body wasted it. The Memorial Day surplus banner is still running which at this point feels like the last table at a yard sale.
- I would not click through to anything except maybe the stair treads section, and even that requires me to trust that the SKUs on screen are actually in stock. I would not forward this. I'd mark it read and keep my stair tread tab open in Safari like I have been for four days.

## What's working

- **"STAIR TREADS." as a subject line is genuinely sharp** — one category, hard stop, no emoji noise, and it happens to be exactly what I'm shopping for on one of the two properties I'm actively renovating.
- **Hero section delivers on the subject** — stair tread product imagery is above the fold, which is more than Wayfair usually gives me when the subject makes a promise.
- **Memorial Day surplus banner is honest about timing** — "shop before they're gone" is credible endgame framing and doesn't feel fabricated the way yesterday's rug send did.
- **Clean layout, no render bugs** — nothing broken, images loaded, readable on mobile without zooming.

## What's weak

- **The email loses the thread immediately** — "Big Help," bed upgrade, and a generic picked-for-you grid have nothing to do with stair treads. Crate & Barrel's "FINAL DAY! Earn 2x Rewards" from the same Memorial Day window stayed on-topic for its entire scroll; this one falls apart below the hero.
- **Twenty-two emails in under a week** — the cadence alone tanks my goodwill before the email even renders. Pottery Barn sent twenty-nine and at least the last one scored a 10 from me because it was tightly crafted. This isn't.
- **No product-level detail visible on the stair treads** — dimensions, material, pile height, whether they're carpet or rubber — the stuff I actually need to make a decision. CB2's "Final hours for up to 50% off" at least showed enough product context to push me to click; this just shows thumbnail grids.
- **The "Big Help" section is bizarre mid-email filler** — whatever that section is selling (looks like it might be cleaning products from the thumbnail), it disrupts the renovation-focused reader I was when I opened.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the entire email about stair treads.** Don't pivot. If you're going to send a subject line that precise, honor it for the whole scroll. The "picked just for you" grid should show stair treads in multiple materials and styles, not random furniture.
- 2. **Surface key specs in the product tiles** — even just "Non-slip backing · fits 9" tread" would let me qualify SKUs without clicking. Article does this better on their sale emails.
- 3. **Kill the "upgrading your bed?" module** — that's a separate email. It doesn't belong here. It dilutes the coherence that the subject line earned.
- 4. **One explicit discount on stair treads above the fold** — I see the Memorial Day banner but I want to know what the stair tread category is actually discounted to. "Up to 40% off stair treads" as hero copy would be four words that justify the click.
- 5. **Drop the email frequency** — this is a product/editorial note, not a design note, but twenty-two emails in a week is actively training me to stop opening.
