# Tailor your living room to your tastes
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- Article is now on living room, and the room-rotation editorial strategy is still working for me. Four days after the dining room send — which I genuinely bookmarked because I'm mid-decision on chairs for the second home — they've pivoted to seating and specifically to Made to Order. That pitch is well-timed. I'm actively wrestling with a sectional configuration right now, and "build it your way" is the exact conversation I want to have with a sofa brand.
- Here's the rub: this email is all editorial and zero math. Not one dollar sign, not one starting price, not one "from $X." For a category where Article sofas run well into the thousands, I need a number to anchor myself before I bother clicking through. ## What's working

- **"Have it your way" lands immediately** — the hero line is punchy and customization-forward without being vague. I knew what this email was about before I read a word of body copy.
- **Category tile grid is genuinely useful** — breaking out sectionals, lounge chairs, modules, swivel chairs, and sofa beds into their own tiles gives me a real sense of what "Made to Order" actually covers. Not just a hero and a single CTA.
- **"Shop Worry Free" section is doing quiet work** — 30-day returns visible for furniture is meaningful. This isn't a throw pillow; having that reassurance on-page earns trust for a high-ticket customization purchase.
- **Layout is clean and renders cleanly** — after Wayfair's glitter-emoji Black Friday in July disaster (eleven emails, two of them today), Article's minimalist white-and-warm-gray grid is a genuine visual relief.
- **Cadence is still sane** — four sends in eleven days, each covering a different room. Pottery Barn sent me thirty-nine emails this month. Thirty-nine. Article's restraint is noticed.

## What's weak

- **No price anchor anywhere** — Made to Order is premium positioning. I have no idea if "starting" means $899 or $2,499. CB2's Furniture Event told me the ceiling upfront; Article is asking me to click blind.
- **Category tiles are small on mobile** — a 2×3 grid of tappable tiles compresses down to tight tap targets. "Shop Sectionals & Loveseats" versus "Shop Modules" sitting that close together is a mis-tap waiting to happen.
- **No lead-time urgency** — Made to Order presumably has production timelines, and that's actually a built-in close: "Order by August 15 for fall delivery" would have been a real, non-manufactured deadline for someone furnishing a home before guests arrive. They left it on the table.
- **Dining room echo is faint** — the July 21 send covered dining room seating specifically. Four days later, pivoting to living room seating almost works, but lounge chairs appear in both contexts and start to feel like the same pitch re-skinned.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a starting price in the hero** — even "From $1,299" under the "SHOP MADE TO ORDER" button pre-qualifies the click and tells me immediately whether I'm in range. Wayfair does this reflexively; Article refuses to, and it costs them.
- 2. **Add lead-time as organic urgency** — "Order by August 15 for fall delivery" is real, credible, and tailored exactly to someone in mid-renovation mode. Build that into the copy beneath the hero.
- 3. **Collapse tiles to three** — Sectionals, Lounge Chairs, and Sofa Beds are the high-traffic picks. Push Modules and Swivel Chairs to a "see all" link or a secondary row. The current grid dilutes focus.
- 4. **Sharpen the "Shop Worry Free" section** — the current paragraph is doing too much. One bolded line — "30-day returns. Free white-glove delivery." — would hit harder and be readable at a scroll.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
An empty title causes some push-notification previews and Gmail tab labels to show "Untitled." Should contain a meaningful fallback string matching the subject line.

**Issue 2 — CSS selector syntax error (trailing comma) in dark-mode block**
```css
/* Inside @media(prefers-color-scheme:dark) */
h1,h2,p,{color:#fff!important}
```
The trailing comma before `{` is invalid CSS. Parsers that aren't lenient (Apple Mail on macOS, some WebKit builds) will drop the entire rule, leaving `h1`, `h2`, and `p` text dark-colored on a black background in dark mode — invisible text.

**Issue 3 — CSS property syntax error (semicolon before `!important`) in dark-mode block**
```css
a{color:#fff;!important}
```
`!important` must be part of the value declaration, not a separate token. The correct form is `color:#fff!important`. As written, the `color` declaration is terminated by the semicolon and `!important` is an orphan token — the rule is dropped by spec-compliant parsers, leaving links uncolored in dark mode on Outlook.com.

**Issue 4 — Missing closing `}` for `@media(prefers-color-scheme:dark)` block**
```css
@media(prefers-color-scheme:dark){
  ...
  a .link{color:#fff!important}
</style>  ← media query never closed
```
The `@media` block is not closed with `}` before `</style>`. Browsers auto-recover, but strict parsers (older Outlook WebView) may discard the entire block or bleed styles into adjacent rules.

**Issue 5 — `[data-ogsc]` block closes correctly** — the Outlook.com dark-mode override block has proper closure; no issue there.
