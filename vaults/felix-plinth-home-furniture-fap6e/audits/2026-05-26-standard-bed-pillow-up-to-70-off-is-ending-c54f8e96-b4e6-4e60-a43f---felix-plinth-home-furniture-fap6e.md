# STANDARD BED PILLOW ⭕ UP TO 70% OFF IS ENDING ⭕ 
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-26**
## Executive summary

- Twenty. That's where the Wayfair tally lands since Sunday. The cabinet and drawer pulls send — which at least earned a 7 from me because it named a thing I'm literally mid-order on — was number nineteen. This is twenty.
- The subject promised a standard bed pillow at up to 70% off. I'm outfitting two master bedrooms right now so I opened, and then I scrolled past area rugs, outdoor chairs, grills, tableware, a wine cooler, a flash-deals grid, and a personalized picks row before I circled back and wondered where the pillow actually went. It's there — I think it's the white square in the top-left of the "picked just for you" grid — but it's no bigger than any of the other thirty product tiles. Pottery Barn would have put that pillow in a full-bleed hero with a 300-thread-count callout and a swatch selector. Wayfair used it as clickbait to open the warehouse door.
- The "ENDS TONIGHT" red banner at the top is doing work — it communicates urgency before I even process the layout. But the "picked just for you" framing is aspirational at best when the picks span literal grills and wine coolers in the same view. Mark as read. Maybe scan the rugs section later. Would not forward to anyone.

## What's working

- The red "ENDS TONIGHT" banner hits immediately — urgency above the fold, no hunting required.
- "Up to 70% off" is a concrete number; I don't have to guess at the discount tier.
- The flash-deals module ("24 HRS ONLY") is a decent idea — time-boxing a subset of deals within an already time-boxed email at least tries to create a priority hierarchy.
- Free delivery over $35 is visible and reassuring for a low-AOV item like a pillow, which is actually relevant here.

## What's weak

- The subject promises a pillow and the email is a department store — bait-and-switch cadence erodes trust faster than any one bad email.
- ALL CAPS subject line with circle emojis reads as spam in my inbox at volume twenty; Crate & Barrel's "Last chance! Up to 30% off bestselling furniture + 2x Rewards ends TOMORROW!" conveyed the same urgency in sentence case and still got a 10.
- "Picked just for you" is doing no personalized lifting when the personalized section includes both grills AND cabinet hardware AND decorative mirrors in the same row.
- The category tiles (area rugs, outdoor furniture, grills & more, tableware) compete directly with the hero offer — I don't know what this email is actually *about*.
- Twenty sends in under a week. The cadence is the single loudest problem and it's invisible inside any individual email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Honor the subject line.** If you put "standard bed pillow" in the subject, make the hero a bed pillow lifestyle shot with the specific discount, thread count, and a direct "Shop Pillows" CTA. One product, one click. Everything else moves below the fold.
- 2. **Kill the category-grid format for urgency emails.** When the deadline is tonight, the layout should funnel, not scatter. One offer, one hero, one button. Article's Memorial Day send wasn't clean either, but at least it didn't try to sell me grills and pillows in the same screen.
- 3. **Drop the ALL CAPS.** Sentence case plus a single urgency word ("ending tonight") reads like a brand with confidence. Full-shout reads like a clearance bin.
- 4. **Compress or kill the "Your Home, These Picks" personalization block.** It's below the flash-deals section, which means it's the fourth value proposition in a single scroll. By then I'm out.
- 5. **Cut the App Store CTA.** CB2 tried this and I said no then too. I have a cart open in a browser tab. Don't ask me to switch apps mid-shopping.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Flagged:**
- `@font-face` fallback is `mso-font-alt: "Arial"` — correct for Outlook. No system-font stack fallback in the `font-family` declaration itself (e.g., `"Sofia", Arial, sans-serif`). If the WOFF fails to load in non-Outlook clients, behavior is client-dependent.
- Media query blocks are not consolidated: `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)` appears as ~20 separate `@media` blocks instead of one. This inflates the `<style>` block unnecessarily and may hit CSS size limits in aggressive email clients (e.g., older Android Gmail).
- No `<meta name="viewport">` visible in `<head>`. Required for correct mobile rendering in some clients (iOS Mail renders correctly regardless, but its absence is a gap).
- No `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">` visible — if absent from the full `<head>`, character encoding is declared only via HTTP headers, which is a minor but real risk in forwarded/saved copies.
- `.ProductRec-name { word-break: break-all }` on mobile will break words mid-syllable in product names. `overflow-wrap: break-word` is the correct property; `break-all` is overly aggressive.
