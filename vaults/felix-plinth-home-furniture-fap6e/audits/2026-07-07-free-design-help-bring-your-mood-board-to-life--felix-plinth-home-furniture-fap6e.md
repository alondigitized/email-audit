# Free design help: Bring your mood board to life
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- Okay, palette cleanser. After eight — I counted eight — Pottery Barn sale emails in four days hammering "LAST DAY," then "HOURS LEFT," then apparently still more hours left, this one drops into my inbox from a slightly different sender: "Pottery Barn Design Services." Different enough that I actually looked twice. And the content is genuinely different too: no percentage-off stacking, no countdown language. Just: we'll send a designer to help you figure out your rooms, for free.
- Which, honestly? I needed this two renovations ago. I have a primary and a second home in various states of "Pinterest board that never resolved into real furniture." The outdoor section alone — they're showing me a styled deck with layered rugs and a sectional — that's basically my second home's back patio in aspirational form. I saved it. I am exactly the person this email was built for.
- That said, I'm still a little annoyed. The mood-board language implies they know I'm in research mode. But there's zero acknowledgment that the sale I've been hammered with all week might still be running. Do I get the extra 20% off if I book this appointment? That question isn't answered, and I'm not clicking a "book" button without knowing whether I'm leaving money on the table.
- Would I click? Probably yes, specifically on the outdoor vignette. Would I forward? Not this version, but a version that bundled the design service with clarity on whether the sale stacks — that I'd text to my contractor.

## What's working

- **The sender pivot actually read as intentional** — "Pottery Barn Design Services" vs. the usual promo sender feels like a different track, not a fatigue recycle.
- **"Mood board" in the subject nailed me specifically** — I literally have a Milanote board open for each house; this language is a direct hit.
- **The outdoor vignette is exactly right for July** — layered rugs, sectional, deck feel — seasonally precise and relevant for a second home situation.
- **Three distinct room categories (living, bedroom, outdoors) scan fast** — I could immediately map each to a room I'm actually working on.
- **10% back in rewards mention lands** — I buy on the highest-rewards card; the Pottery Barn Mastercard note is at least worth a mental note, even buried at the bottom.
- **The July 12 design class date is specific and credible** — this is actual urgency, not manufactured "HOURS LEFT" urgency.

## What's weak

- **Zero mention of the ongoing sale** — After a week of extra 20% off messaging, does this design appointment stack? Does the sale still apply? Silence here is genuinely frustrating, not just an oversight.
- **"Bring This Look to Life" as a CTA is vague** — Wayfair links me to a specific product page when I click a vignette image; this feels like it goes to a department landing page. I've been burned before clicking "Bring This Look to Life" and landing on a generic living room category.
- **"Find the Right Rug" at the bottom feels like a template module that slipped in** — The email already showed me rugs in three styled rooms. The generic rug finder module breaks the curated-service feel of the rest of the email.
- **No mention of whether the service is in-store only or remote** — I do a lot of my furniture shopping after hours online; a stylist I can video-chat is interesting; one I can only visit at a location is a scheduling negotiation I may not win.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one line explicitly connecting the appointment to the sale:** "Book now — your design session counts toward extra savings through [date]." This is the single change that would convert a save-for-later into a same-day click.
- 2. **Make "Bring This Look to Life" a deep link to the specific room's shoppable page**, not a category. Article does this well — every hero image links to a product. PB is leaving the conversion path to chance.
- 3. **Surface the remote/virtual option early** — even a "Shop in-store or via video" line under the RSVP header would address the friction.
- 4. **Cut the rug-finder module** — it undercuts the curated, service-first feel. If they want to cross-sell rugs, feature one specific rug in the outdoor vignette with a direct link.
- 5. **Mention appointment availability signal** — "Limited spots" or "Only 3 appointments left this week" would make the urgency credible rather than implying it with "Save your spots while they last."

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CRITICAL] PHP code in `<style>` block**
The second `<style>` tag opens with raw PHP function calls:

```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```

These are not valid CSS. They indicate a server-side template fragment that was not executed (or not stripped) before the HTML was assembled. Most email clients will silently discard invalid CSS declarations, but parsing behavior varies — some clients may surface this as visible rendered text or cause the style block to be dropped entirely (breaking the mobile media query that follows). This is a template generation defect and should be escalated to the build pipeline.

**[WARN] 26 images missing alt text**
All body content images (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_07.jpg`), all footer images (`Email-Footer/slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), the credit card banner images, and all tracking pixels carry empty or absent `alt` attributes. With images-off rendering (common in Outlook, corporate environments), the email presents as a blank white strip. Functional CTAs are entirely image-based with no text fallback.

**[WARN] 2 images served over HTTP**
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Mixed content (HTTP images in an otherwise HTTPS context) will be blocked by Apple Mail, Outlook on iOS, and other security-strict clients, producing broken image slots in the footer credit card banner.

**[INFO] Google Fonts `<link>` tag present**
`<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat&display=swap">` will be stripped by Gmail, Apple Mail (iOS), and most webmail clients. Montserrat will silently not load. The declared fallback chain in body text should be confirmed; the `<style>` blocks reference `verdana,arial` but body copy may not have an explicit fallback.
