# Here’s your chance to win your dream soccer setup ⚽
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-five. That's where I land after this one — twenty-five emails from the Crate & Barrel family since July 8th. And what do they reach for on number twenty-five? A soccer sweepstakes. The same household that sent me a "world-class bedroom for your soccer star" two sends ago via Crate & Kids has now pivoted the mothership — Crate & Barrel proper — to "win your dream soccer setup." The coordination is almost impressive in how tone-deaf it lands on me specifically. I'm not shopping for a soccer setup. I'm sourcing a sectional for a second home and trying to figure out if I want to do a CB2 dining table or wait for Pottery Barn's sale to deepen.
- The email itself is long. It scrolls past a "Bring home the winning setup!" hero into a grid of products that, fine, are more my speed — outdoor furniture, living room pieces, some kitchen photography that suggests entertaining. But the soccer framing is a veneer. It's a giveaway mechanic wrapped around a product catalog email, and neither half of that equation is earning a click from me today.
- I'd mark as read without clicking. I would not forward this — there's nobody in my life I'd send a "here's your chance to win a soccer setup from Crate & Barrel" to.

## What's working

- The "Bring home the winning setup!" hero copy is a decent pun — it gestures at home furnishings and sports simultaneously, which is at least a craft choice.
- The product grid below the hero is genuinely my category — outdoor and living room goods in a visual format I can skim fast.
- The email renders cleanly; no broken images, no layout chaos, no lorem ipsum leaking through. C&B's template hygiene is consistently solid.
- The lifestyle photography, particularly what looks like an outdoor entertaining spread, is aspirational in a way that matches what I'm actually planning for the second home's back patio.

## What's weak

- A soccer sweepstakes from Crate & Barrel is a category mismatch I cannot get past. Pottery Barn Teen does soccer. Crate & Kids just did soccer (email 23). This feels like the third brand in the family punching the same seasonal content calendar button without talking to each other.
- "Win your dream soccer setup" sets an expectation the body can't fully deliver on — once I'm in the email and I see it's mostly home goods with a sweepstakes tacked on, the cognitive whiplash is real.
- Twenty-five sends in four days. At this cadence, individual email quality is irrelevant — I'm developing a conditioned non-response to the sender name regardless of content.
- No pricing visible in the sections I could parse — Article's "New arrivals just landed" send (which I audited recently) had cleaner price anchoring in the product tiles, which kept me in the scroll loop longer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop the soccer theme entirely for this list segment.** Anyone on Crate & Barrel's core list who also received the Crate & Kids soccer email is now getting the same seasonal narrative twice. Segment these audiences or pick one brand to own the moment.
- 2. **Lead with the product category I actually care about.** The outdoor entertaining imagery buried in the scroll is doing more work for me than the sweepstakes hero. Put that above the fold for renovators.
- 3. **If you're running a sweepstakes, make it feel valuable — show the prize.** "Win your dream soccer setup" with no image of what that looks like is a weak pull. CB2 in their "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" send at least showed you what you were getting.
- 4. **Dial back the send frequency.** This is a send-count problem as much as a content problem. Even a great email doesn't survive cadence fatigue at 25 in 4 days.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — conflicting `img` `max-width` media queries:**

```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }  /* declared first */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }  /* declared second */
```

At viewports ≥768px both queries match; the 640px rule wins due to cascade order. The `max-width: 600px` rule is effectively dead code above 768px — images will render up to 640px wide on large screens. Intent appears to be 600px cap; rule order should be reversed or the 600px rule inlined after the 640px one.

**Issue — dark mode not supported:**

```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```

Light-only declaration will force dark-mode clients (Gmail, Apple Mail, Outlook) to invert or recolor the email without sender control. If dark mode handling is intentionally deferred, this is acceptable — but it should be a deliberate choice, not an oversight.

**Minor — duplicate `format-detection` declarations:**

```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<!-- then individually below -->
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```

Three attributes repeated individually after already being set in the combined declaration. Not harmful, but adds ~180 bytes of dead markup.
