# A $1000 gift card could be yours →
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-29**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one made me actually stop scrolling. "Rate and review your favorite product for a chance to win a $1000 Crate & Barrel gift card" — that's a real hook, not a generic sweepstakes blast, because it's tied to something I've presumably bought. Then it stacks Double Rewards (earn 10% back in Reward Dollars through September 8) on top of up to 60% off the Labor Day event, and the grid underneath actually shows sofas, hardwood rugs, kitchen brands, outdoor furniture — categories I'm mid-renovation for right now.
- The thing that annoys me is the sheer length. This is the fourth or fifth C&B-family email format I've caught this week between Crate & Barrel proper, Crate & Kids, and CB2, and this one alone has like six different CTAs stacked top to bottom — write a review, shop new arrivals, shop the Labor Day event, shop kitchen deals, book a Design Desk appointment, download the app. It's trying to be five emails at once.
- Would I click? Yes, on "Shop The Labor Day Event" specifically for the furniture grid — that's the one section that's genuinely in my lane. Would I forward it? No. Would I mark it read and move on without engaging the gift-card review CTA? Also yes — that offer needed to be its own send, not buried as line one of a scroll-forever email.

## What's working

- The $1000 gift card hook up top is a real, specific incentive tied to a review action — that's smarter than the generic "up to 60% off" subject lines CB2 and Crate & Kids keep recycling.
- Double Rewards (10% back in Reward Dollars) is loyalty-member-relevant and I'm on the highest-rewards-card program already, so this is exactly my buying behavior.
- The category grid — sofas/sectionals, kitchen, entertaining, decor/bedding, outdoor, kids — is genuinely product-forward and photographed cleanly, no lorem-ipsum, no broken tiles.
- The Design Desk section with a free in-home design appointment + $5000 giveaway entry is a smart upsell for someone renovating two houses at once — that's a section Article and CB2 don't have an equivalent for.

## What's weak

- This email is enormous — six distinct offers/CTAs competing for the same scroll, when CB2's recent "BACK IN STOCK" send (6/10, but at least focused) proved a tighter email gets opened on autopilot.
- The gift-card review ask and the Labor Day sale are two completely different asks crammed into one hero — I don't know if I'm supposed to review a product or shop a sale first.
- "top kitchen deals (they ship free!)" strike prices look like stock-photo cookware, nothing that reads as curated for someone furnishing two full homes.
- By the time I hit "we love these for you," I've already scrolled past two Labor Day CTAs and a Design Desk pitch — that section reads like an afterthought, not personalization.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into two sends: one focused purely on the $1000 review sweepstakes, one purely on the Labor Day + Double Rewards sale. Right now they're diluting each other.
- 2. Cut the email length by half — pick Labor Day event + Double Rewards as the single hero, move Design Desk and kitchen deals to a follow-up send later in the week.
- 3. Move "we love these for you" — the actual personalized picks — up near the top, right under the hero. That's the section that should be doing the work, not buried after five other CTAs.
- 4. Add a real deadline callout near the top hero, not just "Now-September 8" in small type under Double Rewards — Article's "Labor Day Sale starts now" (7/10) at least led with urgency front and center.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Double Rewards + up to 60% off starts now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Write a review, win $1000 — Labor Day event is live`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Earn 10% back in Reward Dollars through Sept 8, plus up to 60% off sofas, rugs & more.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your Labor Day picks: sofas, hardwood rugs, and kitchen deals that ship free.`
