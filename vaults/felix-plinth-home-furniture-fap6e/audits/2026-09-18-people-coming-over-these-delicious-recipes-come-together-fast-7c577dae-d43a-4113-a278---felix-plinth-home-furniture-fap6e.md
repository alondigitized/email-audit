# People coming over? These delicious recipes come together fast
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-18**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is a recipe email, not a furniture email — Crate & Barrel sending me "people coming over" hosting recipes with a salmon hero, a rosé spritz, a ricotta dip, crudités with bagna cauda, and a whole "Sunday night salmon" section. I get why they do this — recipe content sells cookware — but I'm two houses deep into simultaneous renovations right now, and not one of these five recipe blocks points me at furniture, a room, or a reno decision. It's the opposite problem I flagged on "Get a custom-crafted look with our new dining furniture" (6/10, 2026-09-17) — that one actually felt built for my renovation; this one feels built for someone hosting a dinner party in a house that's already finished.
- Buried at the bottom there's a genuinely relevant strip — "we love these for you" with cookware and serveware plus a Crate & Kids banner mixed in — and then a Venus Williams collab that's a nice trust/authority signal but zero relevance to me. I'm not opening this one twice, I'm not forwarding it, and I'm scrolling straight past the top five sections to see if the bottom third has anything I'd actually click.

## What's working

- The hero salmon shot is genuinely appetizing and well-lit — food photography here is on par with what Williams Sonoma does in this category.
- "It's all about a home you love. Get $50 off orders $250+ with code..." near the bottom is a real, specific offer with a concrete threshold — that's more concrete than most of what I see from Pottery Barn's warehouse-sale emails.
- The "we love these for you" module at least tries to personalize toward products (cookware, serveware) instead of just more recipes.
- Venus Williams collab gives a legit trust/prestige signal, distinct from CB2's usual designer-name-drop wordplay I called out before.

## What's weak

- This is a recipe/entertaining email wearing a Crate & Barrel wrapper — I'm mid-renovation on two houses, not planning a dinner party, and there's no dining-room-furniture or room-reno tie-in anywhere in the first five sections.
- Every "Get the Recipe" CTA sends me to content, not product — by the time I hit a "Shop X" button I've already scrolled past 80% of the email.
- Crate & Kids sneaks into the "we love these for you" grid again — same thing I've now flagged four times this stretch (birthday shop, clearance, baby essentials) — I don't have kids.
- The $50-off code and app-download pitch are crammed into the last two inches of a very long scroll — Article's home-office tour got its hook in immediately; this one buries its only real offer.
- No swatch/sample program, no shipping-timeline info for furniture, nothing that speaks to someone actively furnishing two homes — this brand knows I'm renovating (per last week's dining furniture send) and didn't use it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the $50-off-$250 offer to the top, above the fold — it's the only concrete, actionable discount in the whole email and it's currently the last thing anyone will see.
- 2. Cut the email in half — five stacked recipe modules before a single furniture CTA is too much scroll for a mobile inbox; two recipe blocks max, then pivot to product.
- 3. Segment me out of Crate & Kids modules — I've told this brand four times now via my clicks (or lack thereof) that I don't have kids; keep showing me this and I start ignoring the sender entirely.
- 4. Tie the hosting theme back to my actual project — "hosting for the first time in your new dining room" with a dining-set CTA would connect this content to my renovation the way last week's dining furniture email did.
- 5. Add a shipping/lead-time callout near any furniture CTA — I'm buying for two active renovations and timing is the thing I actually need to know.
