# Double Rewards & The Memorial Day Event starts…NOW!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "Double Rewards" got me. That subject line hit the one lever Crate & Barrel knows to pull with me — I do literally track which card gives me the best return on furniture purchases, and stacking that on top of a points program is the kind of math I will actually do in my head while standing in a lighting aisle. So I opened this fast.
- Inside, though, it's a lot. This email is long — lifestyle shot after lifestyle shot, dining room, living room, outdoor, entertaining, grid of products at the bottom. It's gorgeous in the way Crate & Barrel always is, but it's also clearly a "we're throwing everything at the wall for Memorial Day" send rather than a tightly curated "Felix, here's the sofa you've been eyeing" send. I'm renovating two houses at once; I don't need discovery mode, I need decision mode.
- I'd click to check the rewards terms — that's the actual hook — but I wouldn't forward this to anyone. This is a "file it in my deals folder and come back to it Saturday" email. Mark as read, revisit when I'm in a purchasing headspace.

## What's working

- **Double Rewards is the right hook for me.** I shop on rewards math. That lead is correct and I noticed it immediately.
- **Lifestyle photography is on-brand and aspirational.** Clean neutrals, modern without being sterile — this is exactly how I think about both houses, not the grandma chintz aesthetic I get from some other catalogs.
- **Memorial Day framing gives it a credible deadline.** It's a real holiday, it's mid-May, the timing makes sense. Doesn't feel like a manufactured urgency.
- **Multiple room categories shown.** I'm furnishing a primary and a second home simultaneously, so seeing living, dining, and outdoor all in one scroll is actually useful — I have items on my list across all three.
- **Clean render, no broken images or layout issues.** It loaded exactly as intended, which — after seeing some truly broken emails this week — I'm not taking for granted.

## What's weak

- **No personalization at all.** I've been a Crate & Barrel customer for years. I have online browsing history, a wishlist, past purchases. This email treats me like a stranger on their first visit.
- **The email is very long for a single offer.** The Double Rewards headline is the story, but they bury it under wave after wave of lifestyle shots. I had to remind myself what I was supposed to care about by the time I got to the product grid.
- **"starts…NOW!" in the subject line reads a little desperate.** The ellipsis-plus-all-caps combo is the kind of thing that makes me feel like I'm being managed rather than talked to. One of those moves that a 22-year-old email manager uses to juice open rates and a 36-year-old reader notices immediately.
- **No rewards math shown in the body.** If you lead with Double Rewards, show me what that means. Is it 10% back instead of 5%? Is there a dollar cap? I'm the kind of person who will close the email and Google the terms rather than just trust "double."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the body with the rewards mechanism spelled out.** One clear line: "Earn 10% back on everything through May 27." That's the click. Don't make me hunt for the math.
- 2. **Segment the product imagery to my purchase history or wishlist.** Show me the dining chair I looked at twice, not a curated room I've never expressed interest in. That's a click. A generic lifestyle grid is a scroll.
- 3. **Cut the email length by 40%.** Pick three hero categories, not eight. The cognitive load of scrolling through that many lifestyle blocks makes me less likely to act on any of them.
- 4. **Remove the "…NOW!" in the subject.** It undercuts the premium positioning Crate & Barrel otherwise earns.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your C&B Rewards just doubled — Memorial Day is on`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Double Rewards starts today. Memorial Day's here.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Earn double through May 27 — shop furniture, outdoor, and more`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack your rewards before the long weekend. Shop the event.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Inverted image `max-width` breakpoints**
Two desktop media queries fire in sequence:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
```
At viewports ≥ 768px both rules apply; the 768px rule (declared later) wins, capping images at **600px on larger screens but 640px on smaller ones**. If the container is 600px this is harmless, but the ordering means images are allowed to be wider on a narrower viewport than on a wider one — likely unintentional.

**[MEDIUM] `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users from zooming in on text, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA).

**[MEDIUM] Wildcard `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` overrides body**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; }
```
The `*` rule sets `none` on all child elements; `body`'s `100%` only applies to the `body` selector itself. All descendant elements inherit `none`, suppressing WebKit's automatic text size adjustment.

**[LOW] Unfilled font import placeholder left in send**
```html

```
Template comment with a typo ("IMPUT") shipped in the rendered HTML. No functional breakage, but exposes the ESP template structure.

**[LOW] Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
Four `format-detection` declarations appear: one combined and three individual (`date=no`, `address=no`, `telephone=no`). Redundant — only one combined declaration is needed.

**[LOW] Bare `&` in `<title>`**
```html

```
`&` should be encoded as `&amp;` per XHTML rules. Most parsers handle this gracefully, but it is technically malformed.
