# 10% off your next purchase—even on furniture.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- Seven. That's my updated count from the Crate & Barrel family across two days, and I'm opening this one with a specific kind of cautious optimism — because the subject line does something the previous six haven't: it explicitly calls out furniture. As someone currently juggling spec sheets for two different living rooms, the phrase "even on furniture" actually registers. That's not an accident. Most loyalty-discount emails from this tier bury the furniture exclusion in fine print; surfacing it in the subject is a real choice, and it got me past the lock screen.
- Inside, though, the story gets muddier. The hero is a soft "get on the list" pitch — which I'm already on, I'm reading this email — so that framing immediately feels like talking to a stranger rather than a known customer. Then the Design Desk section shows up. Again. This is at minimum the third push for free design consultations from the C&B family this week (I audited two on May 27th alone). I understand the design-consult push is a strategic priority right now, but hitting me with it on a day when the headline offer is 10% off furniture makes the email feel like two campaigns stapled together.
- Would I click? Maybe once, on the Shop Now, if the 10% is genuinely stackable on a furniture piece I already have bookmarked. Would I forward it? No. Mark as read? Probably — then come back to it before checkout on something I was already considering.

## What's working

- **"Even on furniture" is load-bearing language.** That three-word qualifier is the entire reason I opened this, and it's positioned right in the subject. For someone actively pricing sofas and dining tables, that word "even" does more work than any lifestyle photo.
- **The hero visuals are actually coherent.** Warm neutrals, natural light, layered textures — the aesthetic aligns with what C&B does well and doesn't feel like the catalog equivalent of a stock-photo dump the way some Wayfair sends do.
- **The Keene collection 10th anniversary is a genuine content moment.** It's buried mid-scroll, but it gives the email editorial credibility beyond pure promotional mechanics. Pottery Barn tried something similar with the Chris Loves Julia collab send on May 28th, which I scored a 6 — at least C&B has a legacy milestone to anchor to rather than a pure influencer play.
- **No render bugs.** After a week of auditing emails that have at least one broken-image gap or orphaned header, a clean render is worth noting.

## What's weak

- **The "get on the list" hero talks past an existing subscriber.** I'm already on the list. I know I'm on the list because you sent me this email. Framing the value prop as "join to get 10% off" when I'm a confirmed subscriber makes me feel like a cold prospect, not a known customer.
- **The Design Desk appears again, and this time it feels like padding.** Yesterday I got it twice in one day. Today it's back. CB2 ran a comparably tight editorial email ("All green everything") this week and didn't once recycle a service CTA — that email felt intentional. This one feels like a template with an un-retired module.
- **The app download CTA is ambiguous friction.** The section reads something like "It's all on the app, including your extra 10%" — which raises an immediate question: do I need the app to get the 10%? Or is the 10% already mine from being on the list and the app just unlocks *extra*? That ambiguity makes me less likely to act on either path.
- **The Keene anniversary is undersold for what it is.** Ten years on an iconic collection is a real story. But it's sandwiched between a loyalty pitch and product tiles and gets lost. This should have been the hero, with the 10% as a supporting mechanic.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Reframe the hero for subscribers, not cold prospects.** The copy should read "Your 10% is here — yes, even on furniture" not "get on the list." I'm already here.
- 2. **Retire the Design Desk module for this send.** It's the third push in two days. Give it a week. The email is about the 10% off; let it be about the 10% off.
- 3. **Clarify the app 10% vs. list 10% relationship.** One sentence of plain language: "Shop in the app or use code [X] at checkout." Ambiguous discount mechanics kill conversion.
- 4. **Elevate the Keene anniversary as the editorial anchor.** Lead with the milestone, position the 10% as a "celebrate with us" moment — that's a story, not just a promo.
- 5. **Pull in a product tile from my browse or cart history.** Article's Memorial Day send was generic but at least led with specific product categories. C&B has my data; the "we love these for you" section exists but could be doing heavier lifting.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[ISSUE] Conflicting `img` max-width rules across breakpoints**
Two media queries set contradictory values:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
The 640px rule is declared after the 768px rule. For viewports 640–767px, both match and the last declaration wins (`640px`). For viewports ≥768px, the 640px rule still wins by cascade order — the `600px` cap is effectively dead. Images will render at up to 640px on all desktop clients, which may not match the intended 600px layout width.

**[ISSUE] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">
```
Prevents user zoom. Violates WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Some email clients (Gmail iOS, Apple Mail) expose this to the WebView and will block pinch-zoom for low-vision readers.

**[ISSUE] No dark mode support**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Explicit light-only declaration. Apple Mail and Outlook for Mac will not apply system dark-mode inversion, but images with white or light backgrounds will appear as hard-cutout artifacts against the dark chrome. At minimum, dark-mode background overrides (`prefers-color-scheme: dark`) should be considered for the outer body/wrapper.

**[ISSUE] Unresponsive viewport band (461–639px)**
Mobile rules fire at `max-width: 460px`; desktop rules fire at `min-width: 640px`. Devices rendering at 461–639px (e.g., large phones in landscape, small tablets) receive no responsive overrides and fall through to fixed-width desktop layout.

**[MINOR] Universal `line-height: 100%` via `*` selector**
```css
* { line-height: 100% }
```
Overrides computed line-height on all descendants. Can collapse multi-line text in certain clients and breaks accessible text spacing. Prefer explicit per-element declarations.

**[MINOR] Duplicate `format-detection` meta declarations**
`format-detection` appears four times (once combined, then three individual `telephone`, `address`, `date` entries). Redundant but harmless; suggests a template merge error.
