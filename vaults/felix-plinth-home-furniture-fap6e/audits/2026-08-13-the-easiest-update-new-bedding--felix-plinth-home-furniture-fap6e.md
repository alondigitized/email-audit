# The easiest update: new bedding!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-13**
## Executive summary

- Third Crate & Kids email in two days, and this is the one where I actually stop scrolling for a second — "layer a cozy bed" with a real duvet-and-sheet flat lay is at least in the right zip code of what I'm doing to my primary bedroom. But then it just... keeps going. Quilts, sheet sets, baby bedding, blankets, throw pillows, a "personalize it" section, a mattress plug, dog beds and backpacks at the bottom — this is a catalog dump, not an email. I scrolled forever and never hit a price, a percent off, or a single reason to click today versus next month.
- Compare that to yesterday's Crate & Barrel send with the canopy bed and "Sale $1,899 Reg. $2,299" front and center — that one I said I'd actually read. This one has better photography honestly (that striped quilt flat-lay is nice) but zero urgency and, again, it's Crate & Kids — nursery and toddler bedding — landing in the inbox where I'm tracking grown-up furniture. I'm not forwarding this to anyone. Marking as read, moving on.

## What's working

- The "layer a cozy bed" hero shot with a real duvet + sheet combo is a clean, relevant opener for someone bedding-shopping.
- Product photography throughout is consistent and well-lit — no brand-voice whiplash.
- "Ships free" tags appear consistently under products, which at least answers one logistics question up front.
- The five-star "loved by parents" review strip near the bottom is a decent trust cue, even if it's aimed at a different shopper than me.

## What's weak

- No price, no percent off, no offer anywhere I can see — just "Shop Free" repeated over and over, which reads like shipping copy mistaken for a CTA.
- This is still Crate & Kids — baby bedding, "bestselling baby bedding," a nursery mattress ad, kids' backpacks and a dog bed at the bottom. I don't have kids' furniture on my list, full stop, same complaint as yesterday's "enchanted nursery" lookbook.
- It's a wall of sections — quilts, sheets, baby bedding, blankets, pillows, "make it extra special," bestsellers, "we love these for you" — with no hierarchy telling me where to actually click first.
- Zero urgency, zero deadline, nothing time-bound. Compare to Wayfair jumping the gun on Halloween — annoying, but at least it's trying to create a reason to act now.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with a real number. Pottery Barn's quick-ship email and Crate & Barrel's canopy bed send both anchor on a specific price or "up to 25% off" — this email has nothing, and that's the single biggest miss.
- 2. Segment me out of Crate & Kids entirely, or at minimum stop routing baby/nursery/kids content into the same inbox where I'm actively pricing a primary-bedroom canopy bed. CB2 and Crate & Barrel proper are clearly better matches for my actual cart.
- 3. Cut this from ten sections to three. Pick the hero (adult bedding), one cross-sell, one CTA. The backpack/dog-bed/mattress grab-bag at the bottom dilutes whatever focus the hero had.
- 4. Swap "Shop Free" for actual verb+noun CTAs tied to product — "Shop Quilts," "Shop Sheet Sets" — "Free" isn't a call to action, it's a shipping disclaimer wearing a button's clothes.
