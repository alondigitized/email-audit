# Psst… something new just hit up to 60% off!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- Seventy-two. That's my unofficial C&B family inbox tally for the month, and this is the *third* email I've gotten today alone about this warehouse sale. The "Psst..." subject gimmick is doing the heavy lifting here — trying to make a familiar 60%-off drop feel like a secret tip-off from a friend, which is cute, but only works if I haven't already deleted two nearly identical emails in the same inbox session. I had.
- That said, once I was actually inside the email, I'll admit the content is legitimately more interesting than the subject implies: this isn't just "more of the same sale," it's specifically flagging new items added — bedding, dining, rugs, some side pieces I hadn't seen in the earlier sends. There's a TheDesignCook collaboration section that's aesthetically on-brand and creates a tiny bit of pause. The product photography is clean and the grid layout is legible, which is more than I can say for Wayfair's "MUST-SEE DORM ROOM DEALS" emoji assault from the same week.
- But the cadence problem is real and it's drowning everything else. When Pottery Barn sent "Get the look: This celeb's cozy cabin" that same morning — one email, well-composed, strong lifestyle concept — it felt like a brand I wanted to hear from. This feels like C&B is throwing content at me until something sticks. I would not click. I would not forward. I would folder it and maybe circle back Saturday.

## What's working

- **"New items added" framing is a real differentiator** — if C&B had put that in the subject, I'd have opened faster. Actually seeing new product photography in the grid justified the send.
- **60% off is a concrete, scannable number** — it lands immediately above the first product row without hunting.
- **TheDesignCook section** adds a lifestyle break from the pure discount grid; the bedroom styling in that block is the most visually arresting thing in the email.
- **Photograph quality is solid** — the dining and bedding shots are clean enough that I'd save one to a reference folder for the second home.
- **CTA buttons are visible and consistent** — "SHOP NOW" appears at logical intervals so I don't have to scroll to hunt for an action.

## What's weak

- **Cadence is completely broken** — this is the fourth C&B-family email today on the same warehouse sale. "Psst" doesn't fix saturation; it just feels sarcastic by this point.
- **"SHOP NOW" is an inert CTA** — after Pottery Barn's "Get the Look" linking format where I can trace a specific room, generic "SHOP NOW" on a category grid reads as lazy.
- **No personalization signal** — the product mix looks like a broadcast grid, not curated to someone who's bought dining and bedroom from them before.
- **TheDesignCook placement is buried** — the most differentiated content in the email is near the bottom. If you have a collab, lead with it.
- **Offer window isn't explicit** — there's a 4th of July framing implied but I don't see a clear "ends July 4" or countdown anywhere in the visible render.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the collab, not the discount** — flip the email structure. "TheDesignCook's summer list just hit the sale" is a story. "Up to 60% off" is a ticker. One of these gets saved to a folder; one gets deleted.
- 2. **Use the subject line to actually announce what's new** — "New to sale: bedding, dining, rugs" tells me something the other four emails didn't. That's click-worthy. "Psst" is not.
- 3. **Anchor the deadline explicitly in the body** — "Prices good through July 4" with a visual deadline element would justify the urgency without relying on the "4th of July" brand assumption.
- 4. **Suppress the blast to anyone who received the prior same-day send** — Seriously, the email that does the most damage to C&B's brand equity is the second one on the same day about the same topic.
- 5. **Swap generic CTA to category-specific copy** — "Shop Bedding" / "Shop Dining" under each grid section instead of uniform "SHOP NOW."

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — blocks user pinch-to-zoom on mobile, violating WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text):
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

**Global `text-size-adjust: none` reset** — `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}` applies to every element, overriding OS-level font scaling for low-vision users.

**Forced light-mode only** — both `color-scheme` and `supported-color-schemes` are hardcoded to `light`. Dark-mode email clients (Gmail Android, Apple Mail) will invert or override colors unpredictably without a `dark` variant or explicit dark-mode overrides.

**Conflicting desktop image `max-width` rules** — two `min-width` media queries set `img {max-width}` at incompatible values. The 640px breakpoint is declared *after* the 768px one, so in cascade order the 640px rule wins at all desktop widths. The `max-width: 600px` rule is dead code:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }  /* never applied */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }  /* overrides above */
```

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags** — `telephone=no` and `address=no` appear in the combined tag *and* again as separate tags, producing three redundant declarations:
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
...
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```

**Alt text on images** — cannot verify; `<img>` tags not present in truncated source. Must confirm all product and promotional images carry descriptive `alt` attributes.
