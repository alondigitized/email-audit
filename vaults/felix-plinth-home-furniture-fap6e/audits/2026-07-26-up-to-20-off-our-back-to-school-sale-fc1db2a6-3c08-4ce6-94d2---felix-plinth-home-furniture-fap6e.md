# Up to 20% off our Back-to-School Sale!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-26**
## Executive summary

- Second Crate & Kids email in my inbox this cycle, and it's the exact same story as the last one: I don't have kids, so backpacks, lunch totes, and monogrammed pencil cases are just noise scrolling past my thumb. What almost saved it was the "create a focus zone" desk-and-chairs section halfway down — that's actually adjacent to my renovation, since I've got a home office to furnish in the second place. But then it swings right back into a nursery glider and a baby car seat under "we love these for you," which tells me their personalization engine has zero idea who I am.
- The crib/nursery furniture in the "we love these" block — a Nuna car seat, a Babyletto glider — that's about as far from my life as this inbox gets. Compare that to how CB2 handled "the most important room in the house" a few weeks back (9/10 from me): tight focus, one room, one clear offer, no whiplash between toddler gear and adult furniture in the same scroll.
- Would I click? Only on the desk/office section, and even that's a maybe. Forward to a friend? No — unless I know someone with actual school-age kids, this has zero relevance to send along. Mark as read and move on.

## What's working

- The "create a focus zone" desk vignette is genuinely well-shot and on-topic for someone furnishing a home office right now.
- "500+ new fall arrivals, starting at $29" is a real, specific price point — that's the kind of concrete anchor Pottery Barn's emails often skip.
- The upgraded Crate Card / Reward Dollars callout is a decent loyalty nudge if I were already a cardholder.
- Clean product-grid layout, no rendering bugs — images loaded, text aligned, nothing broken.

## What's weak

- Kids' backpacks, lunch boxes, and pencil cases dominate the top third — dead on arrival for a 36-year-old without kids.
- The "we love these for you" personalization block recommends a baby car seat and a nursery glider to me — that's not a targeting miss, that's targeting failure.
- Two nearly identical "Shop the Sale" CTA buttons stacked back to back with no differentiation — lazy CTA architecture.
- "Up to 50% off summer sale" in the header and "up to 20% off back-to-school" in the hero are two different offers competing for the same eyeball in the first screen — muddies which discount actually applies to what.

## Recommendations

- 1. Suppress this send entirely for subscribers without a kids/baby purchase or browse history — Crate & Kids should not be firing at Crate & Barrel's core adult-furniture list. This is the single biggest lift available.
- 2. If someone like me does get through the filter, lead with the office/desk content and drop the school-supplies hero — that's the only relevant real estate in the whole email.
- 3. Fix the recommendation engine — a baby car seat has no business in "we love these for you" for an account with zero kids' purchases.
- 4. Collapse the two "Shop the Sale" buttons into one, or differentiate them (one for back-to-school, one for the broader sale).
- **Subject Alt A:** `Fall arrivals from $29 — plus 20% off back-to-school`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your home office refresh starts here (up to 20% off)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `500+ new fall pieces, desks included, starting at $29`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Backpacks to desks — everything for the new school year`
