# BONUS deals ❤️ Extra 20% off + Shop the Memorial Day Sale!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-one. I know because I counted twenty this morning — that was the "Final weekend! EXTRA 20% off select styles" send — and then, before lunch, Pottery Barn came back with this one. Same Memorial Day sale. Same percentage. Same red button. Repackaged as "BONUS deals" with a heart emoji in the subject line, as if that changes anything. I'm mid-renovation on two properties right now. I genuinely need a sofa, an outdoor dining set, and probably a new bed frame. Pottery Barn should be winning me this week. Instead I caught myself sighing before I even tapped it open.
- The bones are fine. Category grid hits Furniture, Sofas & Chairs, Bedding, and Outdoor — which is essentially my shopping list right now — and the Extra 20% off is right at the top, legible, above the fold. The "Recommended for You" rail at least acknowledges that I'm a known customer, not a cold prospect. The rewards/loyalty callout and the Buy Now Pay Over Time block are both relevant to how I actually make purchase decisions. These are points in its favor.
- But "BONUS deals" is doing zero work. There's no actual bonus — it's the same discount that was in email twenty, and email nineteen, and email seventeen. Article sent me one email about their Memorial Day 40% off, put a real deadline on it, linked a specific sectional, and I clicked it the same day. This is attempt twenty-one from Pottery Barn and I'm marking it read.

## What's working

- Category grid (Furniture, Sofas & Chairs, Bedding, Outdoor) maps almost exactly onto what I'm actively buying for two renovations — this is correctly targeted inventory
- "Extra 20% off" headline and free-shipping threshold are both above the fold on mobile, no hunting required
- "Recommended for You" section exists — signals they know I'm a repeat customer, even if the recommendations feel generic
- Loyalty/rewards callout at the bottom is present; I do run that math before I checkout
- Clean render throughout — images loaded, no broken layout, no overlapping elements

## What's weak

- "BONUS" is empty — if there's a stacked discount or a specific category bonus, show me the math; if not, drop the word entirely
- This is the second Pottery Barn email today, same offer, and the first one already said "Final weekend" — so the urgency signal from that one collapsed the credibility of this one immediately
- Heart emoji in the subject plus BONUS in caps reads like a re-engagement blast to a lapsed list, not a send to someone who's been opening their emails all week
- No specific product in the hero — Article linked me to a specific piece with a price point; this just dumps me on a sale landing page with hundreds of items and no entry point
- The recommended products at the bottom look recycled from earlier sends — if the algorithm knows I've been browsing outdoor dining, lead with that, don't bury it

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the double-send.** One email per day, maximum. Two same-day emails with the same offer is the fastest way to train a subscriber to ignore everything from this sender — and CB2 is managing to be editorial and interesting on a fraction of this volume.
- 2. **Make "BONUS" mean something.** If this discount stacks on top of already-marked-down sale items, say "Extra 20% stacks on clearance prices" and put that in the subject. If it doesn't, drop the word.
- 3. **Put one specific product in the hero.** Article does this every single send. A sectional, a price, a single clean CTA. The category grid approach works as a secondary layer; the hero should earn the click on its own.
- 4. **Personalize the hero from browse/purchase history.** I've presumably been looking at outdoor furniture — lead with that, not a generic living-room lifestyle shot that's appeared in three of the last five emails.
- 5. **Shorten and clean up the subject.** Drop the emoji, shorten to under 50 chars, pick one message.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 20% off furniture + bedding — ends Monday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Memorial Day final prices: sofas, outdoor, bedding`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping on orders $149+. Outdoor dining from $299.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack your rewards — extra 20% ends Monday night.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard-broken CTAs (403) and PHP source code leaked into a `<style>` block are the critical defects. The email also carries a systemic alt-text gap across 38+ images and three mixed-content HTTP asset loads that will trigger security warnings in modern clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[CRITICAL] Credit card apply link returns 403**
```
https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682
  ?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY
  &external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember
```
This is the Key Rewards / credit card banner CTA — a revenue-generating placement. The server returns 403, meaning all clicks on that module land on an error page.

**[CRITICAL] West Elm CDN spacer image 403**
```
http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif
```
Cross-brand asset from the West Elm CDN. Returns 403 and is also HTTP (see §3). Likely a stale evergreen footer module that was never updated to a Pottery Barn host.

**Tracking links:** 24 click-redirect URLs via `click.e.potterybarn.com` were not probed. Destination UTM coverage cannot be confirmed from the source alone (see §6).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CRITICAL] PHP code injected into `<style>` block**

Inside a `<style>` tag in `<head>`, the following raw PHP appears as literal CSS text:

```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```

This is unexecuted PHP that leaked from a server-side template. It is inert in the browser but is technically invalid CSS. Some strict email clients may reject or partially mangle the entire `<style>` block upon encountering this, causing layout breakage.

**[HIGH] 38 images missing `alt` text**

Nearly every content image — all 13 `slices_NN.jpg` hero/body images, all product images (Westin mattress, Cayman bed, farmhouse bed, curtain brackets, sheet set, etc.), the outdoor sale banner, and all 18 footer slices — has no `alt` attribute. Subscribers with images disabled (common on corporate mail servers) see a blank email. Screen reader users get no context.

**[HIGH] 3 image sources over plain HTTP**

| Image | Source |
|---|---|
| `spacer_0.gif` | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` |
| `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` |
| `slices_02.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` |

The open-tracking pixel also fires over HTTP:
```
http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=...
```
Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook 365 will block or proxy these. Mixed content warnings may suppress the images entirely; the HTTP open pixel will produce inaccurate open-rate data as it's intercepted by Apple MPP and Gmail Image Proxy regardless.

**[LOW] `<title>` tag is empty**

`<title></title>` — some clients display this in preview or tab labels.

**[LOW] Conflicting viewport meta tags**

Two viewport directives are commented out but still present in source; the active one (`width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1`) conflicts with the `@media (min-width: 700px) { img { max-width: 650px } }` rule immediately below it. Minor but worth noting for QA hygiene.
