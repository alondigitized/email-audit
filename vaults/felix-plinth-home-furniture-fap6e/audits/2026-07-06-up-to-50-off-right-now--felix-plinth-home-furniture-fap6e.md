# UP TO 50% OFF RIGHT NOW
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- Ten. I did the count — this is my tenth CB2 email in six days. When "3 SALES ON NOW" landed on July 5th I thought that was peak exhaustion, but here we are July 6th and the hero still reads "THE JULY 4TH SALE." The Fourth of July was two days ago. The countdown timer shows a day and a half left, which is fine — extended sales are real — but calling it a "July 4th Sale" when the sender display name has already pivoted to "CB2 LONG WKND SALE" is the left hand not knowing what the right hand is doing. That split-identity thing bugs me more than the fatigue.
- Here's the thing though: the warehouse section actually stopped me. "UP TO 60% OFF" with a separate WAREHOUSE SALE block is a genuine escalation from the 50% hero. That's a beat that wasn't in yesterday's send. And the "MORE FOR YOU" product carousel — a bed, a dining table, a desk with drawers — those are things I'm literally shopping for right now for the second home. So against all odds, this email got a longer look than the previous three combined.
- I wouldn't click the hero CTA. But I might drill into the warehouse block. I would not forward this.

## What's working

- **Countdown timer in the hero** is doing real work — 1 day and change of urgency is credible and not manufactured panic.
- **Warehouse Sale escalation (up to 60% off)** is the smartest move in the email; it creates a tier above the main sale that a savvy shopper notices.
- **"MORE FOR YOU" product carousel** includes specific named pieces with prices — Harrith Oak Dining Chair, Journey Terracotta Red Velvet Accent Chair — which is actual personalization signal rather than just "SHOP ALL."
- **Category navigation grid** (SHOP FURNITURE / OUTDOOR / LIGHTING / DECOR / BATH / TABLETOP) is efficiently structured — no click-hunting required.
- **EARN 15% BACK loyalty block** is visible, relevant if you're already a member.

## What's weak

- **"THE JULY 4TH SALE" hero copy on July 6th** is stale — the sender display name is already calling it a Long Weekend sale but the email didn't get the memo.
- **Sender display name "CB2 LONG WKND SALE"** looks like a segmented list label that escaped into production; it reads like campaign metadata, not a brand.
- **"UP TO" dilutes the headline** — Pottery Barn's July 4th extended send at least stacked "extra 20% off select styles" on top of the base discount, which feels more additive; CB2's "UP TO 50%" with "UP TO 60%" in the warehouse block just makes me wonder what's actually at 50%.
- **No outdoor hero imagery** — I'm furnishing a second-home patio and "SHOP OUTDOOR" is a nav button with zero visual pull; compare that to how Wayfair led with an "8' x 10' OUTDOOR RUGS" subject when they wanted outdoor clicks (flawed execution, but at least intent was clear).
- **CB2 / Crate & Barrel / Crate+Kids footer row** — I know which catalog is which, but showing all three brands in the footer on a CB2 sale email makes this feel like a shared template rather than a CB2 send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the July 4th hero copy on July 6th** — reframe the countdown as "Long Weekend Sale Ends [Day]" to match what the sender display name already says. This single change makes the email feel current instead of like a re-send with a new subject line.
- 2. **Give the Warehouse Sale its own hero module** with a specific product image — that 60% figure is the most compelling thing in the email and it's buried below the fold in a small block.
- 3. **Outdoor module with a hero image** — even one patio shot above the SHOP OUTDOOR button would convert people like me who are in active second-home furnishing mode.
- 4. **Reduce email cadence** — the 10-send-in-6-days pace is cannibalizing click rate. Article sent four emails on the same sale and I still clicked because the gap between sends was reasonable. CB2 is burning through my goodwill.
- 5. **Consolidate the loyalty call-to-action** — "EARN 15% BACK" and "GET 15% OFF" in two separate blocks feels like two different teams wrote two different emails and mashed them together.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Warehouse clearance just opened — up to 60% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Long weekend sale: 1 day left + warehouse deals`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop the clearance floor before it closes Tuesday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your saved furniture categories, now up to 60% off.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Leaked template placeholder in `<head>`:**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
Typo ("IMPUT") and production scaffolding text present in deployed HTML. Should have been removed pre-send.

**[HIGH] Double-slash in multiple `@font-face` src URLs:**
```css
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2")
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2")
```
Three of six `@font-face` declarations use `//` after the domain. CDN or origin servers may return 404 on strict path matching; causes silent fallback to Helvetica in supporting clients (Apple Mail, Outlook on iOS).

**[MEDIUM] Duplicate `@font-face` for "Lineto Brown Sub":**
The family is declared three separate times — once with a bare `.woff` (no `format()` hint), then twice with `font-weight: 400` and `font-weight: 600`. The first declaration's lack of a format hint causes browsers/mail clients to attempt to load it even when woff2 is available, adding a redundant network request.

**[MEDIUM] Non-standard mobile breakpoint:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)
```
No current production iPhone maps to 418px. iPhone 15 Pro = 393px, iPhone 15 Plus = 430px. This breakpoint will not trigger on 430px-wide devices, causing those users to receive the desktop layout on a phone.

**[MEDIUM] `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Violates WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Blocks pinch-zoom for low-vision users. No functional email reason to prevent zoom.

**[LOW] Global `text-size-adjust: none`:**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}
```
Applied to all elements, not just the body. Prevents iOS Safari from auto-scaling small text in narrow containers. Compounds the `maximum-scale=1` issue above.

**[LOW] Cross-domain font loading (cb2.com + crateandbarrel.com):**
`HNW05` font family loads from `crateandbarrel.com` webfonts CDN while all other fonts load from `cb2.com`. Email clients that enforce same-origin or CORS restrictions will silently fall back on the Crate & Barrel font variant.
