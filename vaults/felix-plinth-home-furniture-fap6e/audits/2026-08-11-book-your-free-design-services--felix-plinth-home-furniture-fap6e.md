# Book your FREE design services
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-11**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so this is the fourteenth Pottery Barn email I've opened this week and it's actually a different beat — Design Crew, "Book a free Design appointment for a chance to win a $1500 Pottery Barn Gift Card." That's a real hook, not another 60%-off banner. I've got two houses going at once right now, so a free designer consult with a sweepstakes attached is exactly the kind of thing that should be landing in my inbox — this is the first PB send all week that actually maps to what I'm doing.
- That said, the email buries the lede under a mile of scroll. Indigo Blues, Heritage Autumn, Rustic Lodge — three separate mood boards stacked one after another before I even get to the "Recommended Just For You" product grid. It's gorgeous, moody photography, but by the third vignette section I've forgotten there was a sweepstakes at the top. Article's "Meet Ashford" collection drop did this same "show me the room story" move but kept it to one collection, one message — this one tries to do three seasons in one email and it dilutes the actual offer.
- I'd click the top CTA — "BOOK NOW" is right there above the fold with the gift card angle attached. Would I scroll through the whole thing? No. Would I forward it? Maybe to my wife since we're actually mid-renovation and a free design appointment is a real decision point for us, not just eye candy.

## What's working

- Free Design Crew appointment + $1500 gift card sweepstakes is a genuinely relevant hook for someone renovating two homes right now.
- "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" button is visible without much scrolling, with clear WHAT/WHEN/WHERE breakdown underneath.
- The virtual-or-in-store option ("In your home, virtually, or in-store") actually answers a real logistics question I have.
- Rewards footer calls out 10% back in rewards with the Pottery Barn credit card — I'm a rewards-card shopper, so that's on my radar even buried at the bottom.
- Photography is warm and well-composed, unlike Wayfair's spreadsheet-style grid-of-tiles emails.

## What's weak

- Three full mood-board sections (Indigo Blues, Heritage Autumn, Rustic Lodge) before the product grid — way too much scroll for one email, and it dilutes the actual sweepstakes offer instead of reinforcing it.
- No mention of what the "chance to win" odds or entry mechanics actually are — feels like the fine print got cut entirely rather than just tucked away.
- The in-store design class RSVP ("Decorating for Halloween," August 23) is a random tonal left turn in the middle of a design-consult pitch — doesn't belong in this send.
- Zero color-palette or fabric swatch/sample program callout, which is exactly the kind of thing that would tip me into actually booking a consult instead of just admiring photos.
- Demographic signal in the lifestyle shots skews a bit more traditional/heritage than my taste — I'm not mad about it, but it's not "young dad doing a mid-century flip" energy either.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the mood-board sections from three down to one — pick the strongest (Indigo Blues has the best photography) and move the sweepstakes CTA to repeat right after it, since that's the actual conversion point, not the last-scroll "BOOK YOUR FREE APPOINTMENT" button.
- 2. Pull the swatch/sample program front and center — if PB has a "order swatches before you book" flow, that's the trust-building step a renovator like me actually wants before committing to a design appointment.
- 3. Move the rewards card 10% callout up near the top CTA — I'm a highest-rewards-card shopper, and "book now + earn rewards" is a stronger stack than book now alone.
- 4. Drop the unrelated Halloween decorating class RSVP — it competes with the design-consult message and doesn't earn its spot in this specific send.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free design help + a shot at $1,500 (limited appointments)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your room, a real designer, zero cost`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Book virtually, in-store, or in your home — plus enter to win a $1,500 gift card.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `No fee, no obligation. Just bring your space.`

These are functioning as expected for open/attribution tracking but are flagged because they carry no `alt=""`.
- The `sv_sveme` and `email` params on the tracking/CS pixel URLs embed the raw recipient email address in plaintext in the URL (e.g. `?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`, `sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`).
