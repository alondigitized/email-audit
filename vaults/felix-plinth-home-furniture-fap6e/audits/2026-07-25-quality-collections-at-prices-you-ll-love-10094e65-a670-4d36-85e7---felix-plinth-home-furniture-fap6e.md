# Quality collections at prices you’ll love
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-25**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one's different — not a rug, not a sofa, not a coffee table. This is Crate & Kids, and I don't have kids. I don't know when I got signed up for the nursery furniture arm of Crate & Barrel, but "back to school sale up to 25% off" over a photo of a plaid bean bag chair is not what I open my inbox hoping to see when I'm trying to close out a primary and a second home renovation. Cribs, changing tables, hooded baby towels, stuffed animals called "Crate Cuddles" — none of it maps to a single room I'm actually furnishing right now.
- That said, credit where due: the execution itself is clean. Clear hero, clear pricing ($999 bed, $99.95 quilts, $49.95 towels, $199 rug), repeated unambiguous "Shop Now" buttons, no broken images, no overlapping text. If I had a toddler this would probably be a decent send. I don't, so it reads like static.
- Verdict: not opening past the first two seconds if I'm honest, definitely not clicking, and definitely not forwarding — unless it's to razz a friend who just had a baby. This gets marked read and ignored, and honestly it makes me want to check whether I can unsubscribe from Crate & Kids specifically without nuking the whole C&B family, because I only want the adult furniture list.

## What's working

- The pricing is right there on every single product tile — no "starting from mystery" nonsense, I respect that.
- Visual hierarchy is genuinely good: hero, then one product per scroll-stop, then a sweepstakes break, then cross-sell grid. Easy to skim on mobile.
- The little star-rating row up top is a nice trust cue even if I'm not the target buyer.
- No render bugs — layout held together the whole way down, unlike some of the CB2 sends I've gotten that felt half-assembled.

## What's weak

- Wrong sub-brand, wrong life stage — I'm renovating two adult households, not a nursery. This whole email might as well be from a different company.
- "Back to school sale" as the lead banner doesn't even match the product mix underneath (cribs and quilts aren't back-to-school items in any real sense) — feels like a leftover seasonal header slapped on a kids-furniture push.
- The "new! the card you love just got better" credit card pitch buried near the bottom does nothing for me — I already run everything through my highest-rewards card, and a store card doesn't beat that math.
- Given I've now logged fifteen-plus Crate & Barrel family emails this month (per my own count from the rug and sofa sends), getting a Crate & Kids send on top of that is exactly the kind of over-segmentation-gone-wrong that makes me want to trim the list, not engage with it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress me from this list entirely** — if I've never clicked a nursery/kids product in the main C&B or Crate & Kids history, don't send it. That's the single biggest lift here; no subject line fixes a fundamentally wrong recipient.
- 2. If Crate & Kids must go to broader C&B subscribers, lead with a "shopping for a gift or a nursery redo?" framing so it's honest about being outside my usual purchase pattern instead of pretending relevance.
- 3. Ditch the generic "Shop Now" CTA repeated seven times — swap in product-specific verbs ("Shop the Weston Bed," "Shop Quilts") like Article does with its room-rotation sends.
- 4. Drop or contextualize the "back to school" banner — it doesn't match cribs and stuffed animals; pick one seasonal hook and stick to it.
