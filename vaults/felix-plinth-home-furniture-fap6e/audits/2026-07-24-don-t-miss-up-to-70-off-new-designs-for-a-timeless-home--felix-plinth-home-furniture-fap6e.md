# Don't miss UP TO 70% OFF + NEW designs for a timeless home
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- Forty-one. That is the Pottery Barn count in my July inbox as of this send, and I lead with that number because there is no other honest framing for this email. Today alone, I've already had "Your first look: 1600+ NEW arrivals" and "Work with our designers for FREE" land from the same address — and now this one, which somehow tries to be both a sale announcement and a new-arrivals editorial at the same time. The subject is doing two jobs and finishing neither.
- That said, I'm not going to ignore 70% off. I am in the middle of furnishing a second home from scratch, so a real sale number gets past my fatigue filters in a way that "1600+ new arrivals" doesn't. The hero delivers a warm, layered living room that reads as aspirational but achievable — neutral linen sofa, textured throws, the kind of setup I'm actively building — and the "INDIGO BLUES" section further down breaks the grid with an actual point of view, which is more editorial effort than email #39 made. The "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" personalization layer at least implies they know I'm a customer and not a stranger.
- But here's the problem I hit every time with PB's sale emails: "UP TO 70% OFF" doesn't tell me what's 70% off. The body leads with lifestyle imagery and new arrivals copy, not with a clear sale hero. I'd have to scroll and hunt to find whether the pieces I have saved are actually discounted. At email forty-one, I don't have that patience. I'd mark it read and maybe come back — but I wouldn't click in the moment.

## What's working

- The hero lifestyle shot earns its place — neutral sofa, layered textures, genuinely looks like the living room I'm trying to build at the second home
- "UP TO 70% OFF" in the subject is the only reason I opened this at all; it's a real number and it cut through forty emails of noise
- The "INDIGO BLUES" editorial block gives the email a distinct mood, which is more than the prior new-arrivals sends managed
- "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" section signals some personalization logic is running — that's meaningfully better than another wall of undifferentiated catalog
- Clean render throughout — images loaded, nothing broken or misaligned

## What's weak

- The subject is 58 characters trying to promise a sale AND new arrivals simultaneously — those are opposite commercial messages with opposite conversion logic
- "Don't miss" is PB's laziest urgency phrase; without a deadline it's white noise by email forty-one
- The body leads with new-arrivals lifestyle copy, not the 70% off hook from the subject — that disconnect makes me feel like I was baited by a subject line and delivered a different email
- Third Pottery Barn email in a single day means none of the three can claim my full attention; Article sent one email this week and I read the whole thing
- No shipping information visible anywhere — for a furniture purchase this is always a blocker; I need to know if white glove delivery is going to add $200 to the cart before I commit

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick the sale and build the whole email around it.** The 70% off is the stronger hook; cut the new-arrivals framing from the hero entirely and lead with the discount, anchored to specific categories (rugs, seating, whatever's actually 70%).
- 2. **Put a real deadline in the email body AND the subject.** "Sale ends Sunday" would have nearly doubled my click impulse. Crate & Barrel's coffee table guide two days ago had no sale but had a clear editorial purpose — this email has a sale but no urgency shape.
- 3. **Tell me what's in the 70% bucket in the first scroll.** A single line — "Up to 70% off sofas, rugs, and case goods" — would unlock the click that "UP TO 70% OFF + NEW designs" never triggers.
- 4. **Add a shipping line early.** One sentence: "Free shipping on orders $X+" or a flat delivery rate. Wayfair is annoying but they always answer this question early; PB makes me go hunting.
- 5. **Pull back the send cadence** — not a fix available in a single email, I know, but three sends in one day cannibalizes PB's own conversion rate. The designer email from this morning was their strongest July send; this one diluted it by lunchtime.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Sale ends Sunday — up to 70% off sofas, rugs + more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `70% off is live. Here's what to grab first.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New arrivals are in. The sale price won't last.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Rugs, seating, lighting — the discounts that actually matter.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP `header()` calls inside a `<style>` block**
The following literal PHP code appears inside a `<style>` tag in `<head>`:
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
This is an unrendered server-side template artifact — the PHP was never executed, leaving raw source in the CSS. It will not crash rendering, but it is invalid CSS and represents a template/build pipeline defect.

**3 image sources served over HTTP (not HTTPS)**
Modern email clients (Gmail, Apple Mail) block mixed-content images:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

`Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` is a visible Key Rewards footer banner — if blocked, subscribers see a broken image where a CTA should be. The 2022-dated path on `slices_02.jpg` suggests a stale footer asset.

**Open pixel also served over HTTP**
```
http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=...
```
The open-tracking pixel uses HTTP — will be blocked in secure clients, undercounting open metrics.

**44 images missing `alt` text**
The full grid of content images (`slices_01–slices_13` across multiple modules), all product images, and the footer slices (`slices_01–slices_18`) have empty or absent `alt` attributes. Recipients using screen readers get no content. In image-blocking clients (Outlook default), the email renders as blank space with no fallback text.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
The header was not captured by the AgentMail relay. If it is genuinely missing from the envelope, Gmail and Yahoo (per their 2024 bulk sender requirements) will surface their own unsubscribe UI — and sustained absence can trigger deliverability penalties for senders at this volume.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent**
One-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail/Yahoo for bulk senders. Without this header, one-click unsub is non-functional. This may be a relay capture gap, but should be confirmed at the MTA level.

**Note:** Both header warnings may be artifacts of the AgentMail relay not forwarding them. Verify against raw SMTP headers before treating as a production defect.
