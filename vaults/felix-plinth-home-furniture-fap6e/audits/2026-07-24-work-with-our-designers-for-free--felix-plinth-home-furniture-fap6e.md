# Work with our designers for FREE
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- Forty. That's the Pottery Barn count in my July inbox — I know because I've been tracking it, partly for my own sanity, partly because someone at their HQ should see this in writing. And yet — I have to be honest — this subject line actually stopped me. I am mid-renovation on two properties simultaneously. There are paint chips on my kitchen counter, a furniture quote spreadsheet that needs a third column, and a browser history that is mostly Pottery Barn product pages anyway. Free design services is not a throwaway offer for me right now. It's the email I've been waiting for since email number three.
- The execution is cleaner than usual. The hero does what a hero should — names the service, explains the value in bullets I can actually skim, and drops a CTA button that says precisely what clicking it will do. The "FREE IN-STORE DESIGN CLASS / Styling Our Fall Collection / August 10 | 10AM" panel is a smart add-on: a specific date and time makes an offer feel real rather than perpetually vague. I genuinely appreciated this. Compared to the blunt "1600+ NEW arrivals" they sent this same morning, this feels like a different brand ran it.
- What drags it down is the "Recommended Just For You" grid at the bottom — a curtain rod, a linen quilt, and a quick-dry towel that have nothing to do with why I opened this. After a hero that correctly identified my exact life situation, the recommendation engine reverts to browsing history guesswork. I'll click "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT." I won't forward it — forty emails buys you zero referrals from me — but the click is real.

## What's working

- **Subject line is the sharpest Pottery Barn has sent me all month.** "Work with our designers for FREE" beats "NEW artisanal vases for your collection" (July 23) and completely humiliates this morning's "1600+ NEW arrivals" on relevance to someone mid-renovation.
- **"BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" is a genuinely good button.** Verb plus noun, no vagueness, tells me exactly what happens when I tap it — something Article occasionally fumbles with their "Explore" CTAs.
- **August 10 class with a specific time (10AM) makes the urgency feel real.** CB2 ran "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" with no date at all; this is how you add a timeline without screaming about it.
- **The dual entry point works.** Ongoing one-on-one appointments AND a one-time class covers both the committed customer (me) and the browser — neither competes with the other.
- **The 10% back in rewards callout is credible.** I run the math on rewards points — seeing that acknowledged without me having to hunt for it is a small but real trust signal.

## What's weak

- **This is the second Pottery Barn email today.** Sending a mass "new arrivals" blast and a "free design services" appeal on the same calendar day is not segmentation — it's volume management, and I feel it.
- **"Recommended Just For You" is not for me.** A Standard Curtain Rod & Wall Bracket, a Linen Quilt, and a Hydrocotton Quick Dry Towel in the same grid tell me the algorithm is reading browsing history, not renovation intent. Article has been sending me room-specific curation (dining room, then living room) that actually tracks my decision path; this feels random by comparison.
- **No deadline on the main design services CTA.** The class has August 10; why doesn't the appointment booking have any urgency language at all? "Limited designer availability" or even "book before August 10" would close that gap.
- **The Affirm section and App section are filler.** Both buried below the fold are doing no work for a customer already primed to book a design appointment. They eat scroll depth and dilute the signal.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Flip the day's send order, or kill the second send.** The design services email is the more valuable touch — lead with it, don't tuck it after a new arrivals blast. Better yet, on any day this email goes out, nothing else from Pottery Barn hits my inbox.
- 2. **Add a soft deadline to the main appointment CTA** — "designer calendars fill fast" or "book your spot before August 10" creates continuity with the class urgency already present in the email.
- 3. **Rebuild the product recommendations around my renovation signals.** If the data shows I've looked at sofas, rugs, or side tables in the last 30 days, lead with those — not accessories. Wayfair spams me with outdoor privacy screens when I've never browsed outdoor; I expect better from Pottery Barn.
- 4. **Move Affirm and the App callout to the footer** or remove them entirely. This is a service-first email; those sections belong elsewhere.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken link — credit card banner (403)**
```
https://www.potterybarn.com/pages/credit-card/?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB
  &marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner
  &cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember
```
This link is in a commented-out block in the HTML (`<!-- ... -->`), so it is not rendered to subscribers. However, it still appears in link-checker output because the raw HTML was scanned before comment stripping. **Not a live subscriber risk**, but the dead URL should be cleaned from template source to avoid confusion.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (2) — may be blocked by email clients:**
```
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg
```
Gmail, Outlook 365, and Apple Mail proxy/block non-HTTPS image sources. Both images will fail to render for a significant portion of the subscriber base. These are footer assets — the CC banner and brand banner. Fix: update `src` to `https://`.

**Missing alt text: 30+ images flagged**, including all body slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), all product images (curtain rod, swivel chair, curtains, quilt, rug, towel), the footer slices, and tracking pixels. With images blocked (the default in many clients), subscribers see no fallback text on any content block. The hero and product images are the highest-impact gaps.

**PHP headers inside `<style>` tag:**
```html
<style>
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  ...
```
This is server-side PHP code that was rendered literally into the `<style>` block rather than being executed. It is inert in the browser/email client but indicates a template rendering failure — a PHP dynamic image endpoint's output was embedded raw into the HTML. No functional breakage to the subscriber, but it's a build-pipeline defect.

**Preheader mismatch:** The hidden preheader text reads "New arrivals just landed in stores!" while the subject line is "Work with our designers for FREE." These are mismatched offers, which will hurt open-rate performance on clients that show preheader text. (Flagging as a technical continuity issue, not a copy issue.)

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found**
Not captured in the relay view. If absent from actual delivery headers, this violates Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender requirements (mandatory since Feb 2024 for senders >5k/day). Needs verification against raw received headers.

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo) requires both `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`. Absence of the `-Post` header means Gmail's unsubscribe button may route to a landing page rather than processing instantly, increasing spam complaints.

**[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found**
SPF/DKIM status unverifiable from the AgentMail relay capture. Given the sending domain is `e.potterybarn.com` (an ESP subdomain), DKIM should be present — likely a relay capture gap, but must be confirmed with raw `.eml` headers.

**CAN-SPAM physical address:** Not visible in the truncated HTML, but typically present in PB footers. Assume compliant pending full-source review.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The active body links route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirect URLs with opaque `qs=` parameters — UTM attribution is handled server-side by the redirect resolver, not visible in the HTML.
