# New from David Girelli, VUUE & more
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-15**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one's actually good, and it's CB2 doing what CB2 does best when it's not chasing me with sale banners — designer collabs. "Things of Quality" as the hero line, moody rust-velvet chair on that stone-textured background, "Covetable Frames. New Custom Fabrics. So Worth It." That's a confident, well-shot lead and it doesn't need a discount to make me stop scrolling.
- Then it just keeps going — chair after sofa after chair, each one named ("The Fitzo Chair," "The Ambrosie Sofa," "The Aldine Sofa," "The Media Chair," "The Curvo Sofa") with the designer credited (VUUE, Piet Hein Eek, Brad Cavett). That's the part that actually works on me — I've seen enough of Article's "Home tour" send and CB2's own "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS" email to know when a brand is treating me like someone who cares who designed the thing versus someone who just wants 30% off. This is the former, and it's a better use of my attention than yesterday's "Grand entrances" console-table push.
- Would I click? Yeah, probably on the Curvo Sofa in rose — that's a genuinely distinct piece, not another beige sectional. Would I forward it? Maybe to my wife if we're still deciding on the second-house living room, since it's basically a lookbook. But I will say: by the time I hit "Fall Sale Up to 30% Off" at the bottom, my brain had already CB2-fatigued — this is the fifth-plus email from this brand family this week (I flagged that exact fatigue on "Colors That Just Make Sense" two days ago), and burying the actual discount below six product spotlights feels like a missed sequencing call, not a content problem.

## What's working

- Design-collab framing ("New from David Girelli, VUUE & more") is a real differentiator — nobody else in my inbox (not Pottery Barn, not Wayfair) leads with named designers.
- Named pieces with designer attribution under each shot — makes we feel like I'm browsing a gallery, not a clearance rack.
- Consistent warm, moody staging across every shot — same lighting language as the "Fall Sale" bedroom hero I scored 7 yesterday, so the brand feels coherent this week.
- Rewards Dollars callout at the bottom (10% back on CB2 and Crate & Barrel) is relevant to me specifically since I stack rewards across both brands already.
- "More For You" grid at the bottom has actual prices visible ($1199, $349, $699, $299, $999) — no hunting for cost.

## What's weak

- The actual sale (30% off, ends tomorrow) is buried after seven full product blocks — by the time I get there I've mentally exited.
- Zero mention of swatches, fabric samples, or the custom-fabric program despite the subhead literally saying "New Custom Fabrics" — huge miss, CB2 ran a whole swatch-program email three days ago ("THE LUXURY OF CUSTOM") and didn't bother linking the two.
- This is at least the fifth CB2/Crate & Barrel-family send in my inbox this week — no acknowledgment of that, no "in case you missed" framing, just another full send.
- No shipping/delivery timeline anywhere, and for upholstered furniture like this that's the first thing I actually want to know before I click "Shop."

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the Fall Sale banner (30% off, ends tomorrow) up top or make it a persistent sticky strip — urgency plus discount is what actually gets me to click, not designer prestige alone (see: yesterday's 7/10 "Fall Sale is over tomorrow").
- 2. Link the custom-fabric callout in the hero to an actual swatch/sample CTA — you're already selling me on fabric, don't make me hunt for how to touch it first.
- 3. Cut the product list from six full-width blocks to three, and let the rest live in the "More For You" grid — six near-identical hero shots kills momentum before the sale even shows up.
- 4. Add a one-line delivery/lead-time note near the sofa CTAs — furniture is the one category where I won't click without knowing if it's 2 weeks or 12.
- 5. Acknowledge the cadence — something like "Yes, another CB2 email — here's why this one's different" would disarm the fatigue I'm already feeling five sends deep this week.
