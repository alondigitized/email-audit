# Up to 50% off ENDS TODAY! 🏃
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- Seven. That's my Crate & Barrel family email count for today, July 7th. This one — number seven, maybe eight — arrives wearing the Crate & Kids logo, and I don't have a single children's room in either renovation. I have two adult homes, a Pottery Barn watchlist, a mental map of which rugs are cross-listed across three brands, and absolutely no patience for a sub-brand pivot at 4pm after the parent store already ran a **60% off** Warehouse Sale this morning with a live countdown clock. This one says 50% off. The family de-escalated its own offer.
- The email itself isn't broken. The countdown timer is sharp, the layout renders clean, the hierarchy is clear. But I'm not the customer here. Crate & Kids is talking to a parent furnishing a nursery. I'm a 36-year-old guy figuring out which credenza clears a doorway in a second house. Same ecosystem, completely different funnel.
- Mark as read. No click, no forward.

## What's working

- The countdown timer in the hero is doing real work — same urgency mechanic the parent brand deployed this morning, and it translates cleanly to this sub-brand's layout
- "Up to 50% off" is legible and front-loaded — no hunting for the offer, it's the first thing the eye lands on
- Beach/coastal lifestyle hero image skews millennial and aspirational; aesthetically I'm not offended, even if the products underneath aren't for me
- Layout renders clean end-to-end — no broken images, no overlapping text, no orphaned modules
- Showing the Crate & Barrel and CB2 logos in the footer is a smart cross-family signal, acknowledging I live in their ecosystem

## What's weak

- This is a kids furniture email and I have zero children's rooms in either renovation — the product grid is bassinets, soft goods, and nursery storage; the entire thing is off-category for me
- The parent brand (Crate & Barrel) ran **60% off** earlier today; Crate & Kids shows up hours later at **50% off** — that's not a coordinated family campaign, that's a step backward within the same inbox
- Running man emoji (🏃) in the subject is trying too hard — CB2 and the main C&B brand don't lean on emoji like that, and it makes this read as a lower-tier send
- This is the seventh-plus C&B family email today alone; Pottery Barn, which I'm also tracking, sent **one** considered send today ("Ready-to-ship furniture") — one send, and I actually read it; the C&B cadence is eroding the brand with me
- Product thumbnails show no detail — no dimensions, no lead times, no material callout; when Article ran their 4th of July closer, they at least surfaced fabric names and a star rating; this is just a grid of price-tagged thumbnails

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress Crate & Kids to non-parents** — if I've never browsed kids categories, this sub-brand shouldn't hit my inbox. The segmentation data exists; the failure to use it is a choice that costs trust.
- 2. **Don't de-escalate the offer within the family** — if the parent brand just closed at 60%, Crate & Kids should match it ("same Warehouse Sale, same 60% off — now in kids") or reframe it as coordinated. Showing 50% after 60% reads as a clearance afterthought.
- 3. **Pull back the send cadence by 70%** — seven family emails in one day is the kind of thing that triggers an unsubscribe review. Compare Wayfair: they've been in my inbox this week on outdoor rugs, twice, and it still felt intentional. Seven C&B emails in eight hours feels like a broken drip trigger.
- 4. **Add at least one product detail to the hero** — lead time, material, or a single review quote. A dresser or crib is a considered purchase; give me one reason to trust it's worth the click.
- 5. **Fix the CTA copy** — "Shop Now" is the vanilla option; even "Shop the Warehouse Sale" tells me where I'm going.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Kids Warehouse Sale: 50% off, ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Stock the kids' rooms — Warehouse Sale closes at midnight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Furniture, bedding & nursery picks — warehouse prices through tonight only`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free shipping on orders $49+ · sale ends tonight`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` brand mismatch**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
Sender is "Crate & Kids SALE." Some clients (notably Gmail on desktop) render the `<title>` tag. This surfaces the parent brand name instead of the sub-brand, which may confuse recipients or affect tab/window labeling.

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents accessibility zoom on iOS. WCAG 1.4.4 (Level AA) requires user-agent zoom to be uninhibited. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Global `text-size-adjust: none` override**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applies to all elements, not just body/wrapper tables. This is overly broad and can suppress iOS auto-scaling for small text. Scope this to `body` or a wrapper class.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags (4 declarations)**
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
The combined declaration on line 1 already covers all cases. The three individual declarations are dead weight. Harmless but indicates template hygiene issues.

**Duplicate charset declarations**
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta charset="utf-8">
```
Both are present. Only one is needed. Keep the `http-equiv` form for broadest client compatibility; the bare `charset` form can be dropped.

**No dark mode support**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Dark mode is explicitly disabled. Apple Mail and Outlook will force invert colors on images/backgrounds. If this is intentional, no action needed — but confirm images have transparent or light-safe backgrounds.

**Mobile breakpoint is 460px, not 600px**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 460px) { ... }
```
Most email frameworks use 600px. This may cause layout breaks on devices between 460–600px wide (many Android phones). Not a blocker, but worth validating in Litmus/Email on Acid at 480px and 540px.
