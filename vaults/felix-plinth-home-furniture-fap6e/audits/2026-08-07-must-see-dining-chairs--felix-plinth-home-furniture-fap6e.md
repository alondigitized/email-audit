# Must-see dining chairs
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- Another Pottery Barn email — I want to say this is the fifth or sixth this week but I've genuinely lost count at this point, and that's the problem before I even get to the content. The chairs themselves are fine: clean product photography, a decent room shot up top with three mismatched dining chairs around a table (I get what they're going for — "mix and match," which is actually a real trend I've been eyeballing for the second house). But this is also the second "Up to 60% off" banner from Pottery Barn in two days — the 8/6 Warehouse Sale email already used that exact number, so this doesn't read like a new push, it reads like the same sale wearing a different hat.
- What actually annoys me is the "Recommended Just For You" block halfway down — a platform bed, a curtain rod, two rugs, a duvet cover. None of that is dining room. If you're going to slap "just for you" on a section, at least make it about the room I'm shopping, Pottery Barn. Wayfair's "Open Box Steals" email I looked at last month was a wall of ugly purple pills but at least every pill was a real, distinct discount category — this feels like a generic recommendation engine that didn't read the subject line of its own email.
- Would I click? Maybe, on the chairs themselves, since dining seating is genuinely on my list for the second house. Would I forward it to a friend? No — there's nothing here worth sharing, it's a catalog scroll. This one gets skimmed and archived, not acted on today.

## What's working

- The hero shot with three different dining chair styles around one table actually sells the "mix and match" idea better than a single product on white — that's smart merchandising, not just a stock photo.
- "SHOP NOW" buttons are placed consistently after every section, so I never have to hunt for the CTA.
- The rewards callout ("Earn 10% Back in Rewards") is visible near the bottom and I do have the Williams Sonoma card, so that's a real reason to buy through them over Wayfair or CB2.
- Layout is clean — no overlapping text, no broken image placeholders, everything renders the way it should.

## What's weak

- "Up to 60% off" is a rerun from the 8/6 Warehouse Sale send — if this is the same sale, say so; if it's not, give me a different number so I don't assume Pottery Barn is just recycling banner art.
- "Recommended Just For You" is a bed, rugs, curtains, and a duvet cover in a dining chair email — none of it is dining room, which makes the "for you" claim feel like a lie. Article's "Bedroom reset" send at least stayed in its lane category-wise.
- No prices or fabric/material info on any individual chair tile (Montclair, Abbey, Cline, Windsor) — for a chair I'm about to sit in every day, I want to know if it's velvet or performance fabric before I click through.
- The Halloween Shop banner ("Enter If You Dare") wedged in below the dining content is a jarring tonal left turn — I'm shopping chairs for a renovation, not pumpkin decor, and it makes the email feel like three campaigns stapled together.
- This is at least the seventh Pottery Barn email in my inbox this week (CB2's parent-company fatigue applies here too) — the cadence alone is starting to make me tune out the brand entirely.

## Recommendations

- 1. Drop or replace the "Recommended Just For You" block with actual dining room cross-sells (table linens, dinnerware, buffets — which PB already has a text-link row for further down, so just promote that instead of the bedroom stuff).
- 2. Add price and fabric/material under each chair name — "Montclair Dining Chair" tells me nothing about whether it's in budget or what it feels like.
- 3. Cut the Halloween Shop insert from this send entirely, or move dining-adjacent content only — mixing reno-shopping intent with seasonal decor breaks the single-purpose focus that made the coffee-table-styling email (7/10, 8/7) work well.
- 4. Give the sale banner a distinct number or end date from the 8/6 Warehouse Sale so repeat readers don't assume it's a stale rerun.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Mix-and-match dining chairs for your table`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Upholstered, wood, or ladder-back — shop the full collection`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Warehouse Sale pricing on dining chairs, ends soon`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Must-see dining chairs"

### 1. Technical Summary
The email is a standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional MSO/Outlook-hardened template with heavy tracking instrumentation; it passes basic rendering checks but has notable accessibility gaps, mixed-content (non-HTTPS) assets, leaked server-side code in a `<style>` block, and missing authentication/list-unsubscribe headers at the relay level.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- Primary CTA/logo link routes through a click-tracking redirect: `https://click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=ABB7InYiOjEs...` (opaque encoded payload, not independently verifiable).
- Multiple tracking/open pixels present:
  - `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` — **served over plain HTTP**, and carries the recipient's full email address as a plaintext query parameter.
  - `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/L21rdC84NjEvY2lkLzE3NDc3ODk3MzMvdC8y/cat/1` (third-party ad-tracking pixel, Turn/Amobee).
  - `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/73c8a294-180e-4b0d-8c25-172084d93c44?mi_mid=019fd9f2-dd00-7000-823a-6bb1813a67cd&cp_tp` (open-tracking beacon).
- 61 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the automated HTTP prober (expected for redirect domains — not a defect, just unverified reachability).
- `meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer"` is set, which mitigates (but does not eliminate) leakage of the email-address-bearing URLs above via the `Referer` header on outbound clicks.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **43 images missing `alt` text**, including all product/lifestyle banners (`F26_TOP_WarehouseSale.jpg`, `slices_01–18.jpg` in two different modules, `farmhouse-platform-bed-1-l.jpg`, `banks-extendable-dining-table-76-128-1-l.jpg`, etc.) and functional images (footer, nav). This fails WCAG image-alternative requirements and degrades the experience for screen-reader users and image-blocked inboxes.
- **4 assets served over plain HTTP** (mixed content, may be blocked/flagged by mail clients and browsers):
  - `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
  - `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/...` (tracking pixel, noted above)
- Cross-brand asset reference: a West Elm domain (`edm.westelm.com`) spacer image is loaded inside a Pottery Barn email — likely a stale/shared template artifact from a common ESP account, not a functional bug but worth flagging to the template owner.
- **Leaked server-side code in the `<style>` block**: literal PHP statements — `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: no-store...")` — appear as inert text inside `<style type="text/css">` in the `<head>`. This is invalid CSS (browsers will ignore it, so no rendering impact), but it indicates a template-generation error where raw PHP from an image-proxy script leaked into the compiled HTML. Recommend flagging to the ESP/template build pipeline since it exposes backend implementation details.
- Standard MSO/Outlook conditional comments, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, and responsive `@media` breakpoints (480px/650px/700px) are correctly present.

3. Migrate the 4 HTTP-only asset URLs to HTTPS, especially the `svopn.potterybarn.com` open-tracking pixel, which also currently exposes the recipient's email address unencrypted.
4. Remove the leaked PHP `header()` statements from the `<style>` block in `<head>` — report to the template build pipeline as a code-generation defect.
5. Investigate the stray `edm.westelm.com` cross-brand asset reference for template hygiene.
6. Consider adding standard UTM parameters alongside the proprietary tracking scheme if GA/GA4-based attribution is needed for this campaign.
