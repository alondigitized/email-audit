# On sale now: decor for indoors & out
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- Six. That's my CB2/CB2 email count since July 1st, and this one — landing on July 3rd — is doing the same ask as "SALE'S GOING FAST" did yesterday, and "Up to 50% off rugs" before that, and "MORE ADDED TO SALE" before that. My muscle memory almost filed it unread.
- Except the subject said "indoors & out" and I paused. That framing actually means something when you're renovating two properties simultaneously and one of them desperately needs outdoor seating. The warehouse sale at 60% off mid-scroll is the most interesting thing CB2 has shown me all campaign, and it's wasted below the fold behind a white sculptural fan piece I'd never buy. The countdown timer is doing real work — a genuine hours-left clock creates urgency that the other five emails in this sequence couldn't manufacture with all-caps subject lines. But the subject line itself is whispering "on sale now" while the email body is screaming "four hours left." That disconnect is maddening.
- Would I click? Yes — specifically into the outdoor warehouse sale. Would I forward? No. Would I file this alongside the others? Probably, unless I'm actively shopping outdoor furniture right now, which I happen to be.

## What's working

- The countdown timer ("04 : 17 : 02 : 00") delivers real urgency above the fold — this is the first CB2 send in this sequence that gives me a credible reason to act now rather than "maybe tomorrow."
- "Decor for indoors & out" is the best subject framing CB2 has used this cycle — it speaks directly to someone who has an outdoor space problem and an indoor space problem at the same time.
- The warehouse sale at "UP TO 60% OFF" on outdoor furniture is genuinely the most compelling offer in this entire sale campaign; that sectional image stopped my scroll.
- Visual hierarchy holds up — countdown, then hero, then category tiles, then warehouse, then personalized grid. The eye finds the offer without hunting.
- The "MORE FOR YOU" personalized product grid suggests they're pulling browse data; it doesn't feel completely cold.

## What's weak

- The subject line "On sale now" is graveyard urgency when there's a literal hours-ticking clock inside the email. Article landed "Up to 40% off ends Monday" and that was enough — CB2's subject undersells a much more urgent body.
- Sixth email in 48 hours. The cadence turns any good creative into wallpaper. Crate & Kids ran the same "LAST WEEKEND" headline yesterday with the same 50% framing and I processed that one in under two seconds too — the whole family has a cadence problem right now.
- The hero product — that white sculptural fan — is aesthetic-forward and completely anonymous. No name, no price, no specific link. "SHOP NOW" drops me on a category page, not a product detail. Pottery Barn at least shows me the product name on their hero images.
- "UP TO 60% OFF" is the classic hedge — I assume the median discount is around 20% until proven otherwise. Wayfair does the same thing with their "UP TO" language and I've learned to treat it as a floor price warning.
- No loyalty callout. I've given this brand real money and I'm being treated like a stranger.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the outdoor warehouse sale** — 60% off outdoor furniture is the most actionable offer for a two-home reno customer and it's buried mid-scroll. That sectional shot should be the hero, not the white fan thing.
- 2. **Match subject urgency to the countdown** — if there's a real timer running inside, the subject should say "X hours left" or "ends tonight." The gap between "on sale now" and "04:17:02" is the biggest wasted conversion moment in this send.
- 3. **Name the hero product and link it directly** — Article links to individual product pages from the hero; CB2 should too. Give me the SKU name, the sale price, and a direct link. Generic "SHOP NOW" to a category page wastes the warm click intent.
- 4. **Suppress or acknowledge cadence** — even "we've been sending you a lot this week — here's the one thing worth grabbing" framing would signal self-awareness and buy goodwill.
- 5. **Add a loyalty/rewards line** — one sentence about points, member pricing, or early access. I've earned it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `4 hours left: up to 60% off outdoor + indoor decor`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Warehouse sale ends tonight — 60% off outdoor`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sectionals, accent lighting, indoor decor — the deepest cuts of the whole sale.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your last shot before July 4th. Outdoor furniture at the best prices all year.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Unfilled template comment in production `<head>`:**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
This developer instruction was never removed. "IMPUT" is also misspelled ("INPUT"). Low rendering impact but signals the template was not fully QA'd before send.

**Issue 2 — `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Issue 3 — `*` selector applies `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` globally:**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;-ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applying `none` (not `100%`) to all elements via the universal selector suppresses browser-level text scaling, compounding the zoom issue above. Should be scoped to `body` only and set to `100%`.

**Issue 4 — Double-slash in `@font-face` src URLs:**
```css
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2")
```
Three of the six `@font-face` declarations have `//` after the domain. HTTP servers normalize this correctly, but it indicates copy-paste slop in the template.

**Issue 5 — Cross-domain font dependency on `crateandbarrel.com`:**
```css
@font-face { font-family:"HNW05"; src: url("https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/...") }
```
All four "HNW05" weight variants load from a sibling-brand domain. If `crateandbarrel.com` enforces CORS headers that block third-party requests, CB2 emails silently fall back to system fonts. Font assets should be self-hosted on `cb2.com` or a dedicated email CDN.

**Issue 6 — "Lineto Brown Light Sub" has no woff2 source:**
```css
@font-face {
  font-family: "Lineto Brown Light Sub";
  src: url("https://www.cb2.com/Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff");
}
```
Only `.woff` declared. All modern clients prefer `.woff2` for ~30% smaller payloads. The Regular weight has a `.woff2` entry; Light does not.

**Issue 7 — Mobile breakpoint at 418px is too narrow for some current devices:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { .mobile { width:414px !important; } }
```
iPhone 16 Pro Max viewport is 430px wide; this breakpoint misses it. Standard safe value is `max-width: 480px`.
