# ENDS TOMORROW! Up to 50% off beds, rugs & more
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- I know exactly what Crate & Kids is. I know it's not the main Crate & Barrel catalog — I know which catalog is which. So when I open something that says "beds, rugs & more" at 50% off, I do a quick mental check: am I furnishing a kids' room right now? I'm furnishing two adult homes simultaneously, and not a single room has a toddler in it. The categories are right, the sub-brand is wrong, and the C&B loyalty system clearly doesn't know the difference.
- What gets me is the countdown timer — that thing is the first thing my eyes hit, and for a split second I feel the urgency they want me to feel. The 50% off is a real number on a category I genuinely care about. Then the lifestyle imagery and product tiles do their thing and remind me this is kids' land. The "we love these for you" section feels like a placeholder for personalization that didn't actually happen. I got four Crate & Kids emails yesterday — beds, nursery themes, more beds — and this is number five in under 24 hours.
- I won't click. I'll archive it and maybe revisit if a specific rug from this sale migrates to the adult C&B site. I'm not forwarding it.

## What's working

- The countdown timer is the first thing I see — no ambiguity about the deadline, no hunting for when this expires
- "50% off beds, rugs & more" is a real dollar magnitude for someone buying rugs by the room across two properties
- "Shop Our Beds" and "Shop Our Rugs" CTAs are specific verbs + nouns — no "Discover," no "Explore," just direct
- TheDesignerDesk section at the bottom is the most genuinely useful thing in the email — a room visualization tool is something I'd actually bookmark when furnishing multiple rooms at once
- Clean render, legible text, no broken images or layout collapse

## What's weak

- This is the kids brand and it reads like the kids brand — the lifestyle creative and product tile selection don't translate to the adult renovation context I'm actually in
- Five emails from the Crate & Kids sub-brand in under 24 hours; Pottery Barn ran seven in 96 hours and I counted, and this is matching that pace
- No rug dimensions anywhere in the product grid — a 5x8 and a 9x12 rug look identical in a thumbnail, and size is the only thing that matters when I'm buying for a specific room
- "We love these for you" is decorative personalization, not functional — none of those selections read like they're based on my actual browse history
- The C&B flagship already sent me two "ENDS TOMORROW!" furniture sale emails today; this sub-brand version lands like a duplicate alarm from a different clock

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress kids sub-brand sends to accounts that haven't browsed kids' SKUs** — if my session history is adult beds and area rugs, I should land in the C&B flagship send, not the Crate & Kids one
- 2. **Put rug dimensions in the product tile captions** — even a "8' x 10'" badge would increase click confidence dramatically; Wayfair does this and it's the only thing their emails get right consistently
- 3. **Surface TheDesignerDesk earlier** — it's the feature most likely to generate a long session; it shouldn't be two screens down past the footer
- 4. **Cap the cadence at one sub-brand email per 48 hours** — Article managed their entire "ends today" series better than C&B is managing the sub-brand split this week
- 5. **Drop the ALL CAPS lead** — "ENDS TOMORROW!" starting the subject scans as noise after the third inbox hit; even CB2's "3 SALES ON NOW" felt less frantic

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** prevents users from zooming, which is an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4). Evidence: `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />`

- **`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied globally** via the wildcard selector `* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }`. This overrides OS-level text scaling for low-vision users and conflicts with the separately applied `body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; }`, creating a specificity battle.

- **No dark mode support declared.** `<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">` and `<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">` explicitly opt out of dark mode. No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block is present in the visible CSS. Images and backgrounds will invert or render poorly in forced-dark mail clients.

- **`format-detection` declared redundantly.** The same directives appear three times: once as a combined value and twice as individual `<meta>` tags. No functional breakage, but indicates template sloppiness.
