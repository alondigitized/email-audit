# Find a rug in a color you ❤️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-four. I stopped counting Pottery Barn emails around email twenty-two but the number matters here because this one is doing something different: after a week of sale-on-sale-on-extra-20%-off-on-sale, they pivoted to a pure browse email organized by color palette. Classic Blues, Shades of Greens, Warm Neutrals. No countdown. No sitewide discount stacked on top of another discount. Just: here are rugs, grouped by the decision you actually make first.
- Here's the thing — I actually needed this. I'm mid-selection on two rooms simultaneously, one of which is pulling greens from tile samples I locked in three weeks ago. A color-filtered rug browse is legitimately how I shop. The Custom Sized Rugs callout is also exactly where I'm at; I've got an awkward 9×12 gap in the secondary living room that nothing off-the-shelf is filling cleanly. This email landed at the right moment, doing the right thing, and I still felt almost nothing opening it because email thirty-four.
- I won't click through today — but I'll save it. That's the highest compliment I give a browse email. It goes in the folder, I pull it up when I'm on the phone with a designer, and I order when the next sale hits. Forward to a friend? Only if that friend already knows Pottery Barn. There's no hook here for someone who's new.

## What's working

- **Color-organized browsing is genuinely smart navigation.** Classic Blues / Shades of Greens / Warm Neutrals maps directly to how I actually start a rug search — by what's already in the room, not by collection name.
- **Custom Sized Rugs as a standalone section earns its space.** Most brands bury this. Calling it out in the body acknowledges that a meaningful chunk of their furniture-buying audience has non-standard rooms.
- **The hero image reads cleanly at a glance.** Warm, lived-in living room, rug front-and-center, no awkward lifestyle staging. It doesn't scream "studio shot."
- **No sale stacking, no panic urgency.** After last week's Memorial Day pile-on — which Pottery Barn ran harder than anyone in my inbox, Article included — the absence of a countdown timer is itself a creative choice that reads as confident.
- **The "Recommended Just For You" section at the bottom** shows some actual personalization signal. Wayfair's "#99 large rug" email from two days ago had zero of that.

## What's weak

- **No offer means no close.** I'm in buying mode for two rooms, and there's nothing in this email that tips me from browse to purchase. Even "free shipping on rugs this week" would do it. Article's last-chance Memorial Day send at least gave me a hard deadline to react to.
- **Subject line leaves money on the table.** The heart emoji does no work. It's soft copy on a product category that actually lends itself to specificity — a color, a room type, a price point, anything.
- **Recommended section feels disconnected.** The furniture thumbnails at the bottom don't visually connect back to the rug story I was just sold. The layout shift reads like an algorithm appended it, which it probably did.
- **Email cadence has eroded the brand.** The May 27 "NEW designs" send scored high with me because it broke the pattern. At email thirty-four, the pattern fatigue means even a good email gets less credit than it deserves.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop one anchor price in the hero.** Even "Starting at $299" does the job. CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" email — which I found genuinely more clickable than PB's comparable sends — always includes a floor price somewhere above the fold.
- 2. **Subject line needs a product detail, not an emotion.** The heart emoji signals "soft sell" when the audience is in active purchase mode. Pick a color or a type and say it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Rugs by color — find yours fast`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Custom-sized rugs for awkward rooms`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Blues, greens, neutrals — shop by the palette your room already speaks.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Non-standard dimensions? Custom sizes available with free design help.`
- 3. **Tie the Recommended section back to what I just browsed.** If I lingered on Warm Neutrals, the rec section should feel like a warm-neutral room. Right now it looks like a different email was stapled to the bottom.
- 4. **Add a "save to favorites" or "pin this rug" micro-CTA.** I save every good rug email to a folder anyway — if Pottery Barn gave me a native way to do that, they'd own the intent signal instead of my Gmail folder.
