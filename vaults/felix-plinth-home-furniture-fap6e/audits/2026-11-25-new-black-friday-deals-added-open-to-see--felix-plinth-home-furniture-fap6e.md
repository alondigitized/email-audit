# New Black Friday deals added! Open to see…
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-25**
## Executive summary

- This is the fifth Crate & Barrel family send I've gotten today, and my C&B snooze is fully engaged. The Kids arm is also the wrong arm for me. I'm renovating two houses, and nobody in either one needs a baby activity chair. The "New Black Friday deals added! Open to see…" subject is a bait-and-switch. The only new thing I could find is a "free shipping is on" strip. The rest is the same "up to 50% off" I saw in the 11/25 "NEW ITEMS ADDED!" send.
- The body is organized well. The hero is clear and the sale-category list is clean. The rug banner and the personalization shop are the closest things to my renovation. But the products are strollers, a nursery glider and a changing-table dresser. I wouldn't click, I wouldn't forward it, and I'd mark it read.

## What's working

- The free shipping strip at the top is clear: "On orders $79+, excluding furniture, rugs & select items." I appreciate the honesty about the furniture exclusion, since furniture shipping is what I actually care about.
- The sale-category grid is easy to scan, with Furniture Sale, Rugs Sale and so on as clean text links.
- The "Up to 50% off rugs" banner has a single clear offer and a "Shop the Sale" button.
- The Crate card callout ("the card you love just got better") is a loyalty tie-in. I'd read it, because I choose cards by rewards math.
- The design studio ("TheDesignDesk … 100% free") is the one piece of service content that's relevant to a renovator.

## What's weak

- The subject promises new deals, and I couldn't find anything new. The body is the same 50% off as earlier today.
- This is the Kids brand, so the hero, the product grid and the stroller picks don't fit me. Pottery Barn's Early Black Friday furniture email at least led with the furniture I was looking for.
- The personalization shop and the stockings are Christmas-gift items. They aren't what I'm buying for the renovation.
- The email is very long, and the fine print runs to several dense paragraphs. Offers are buried under exclusions like "Exclusions apply. Orders $79+."
- The "app only, chance at $500" tile feels like a gimmick and adds clutter.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put one real new deal at the top. If nothing is new, don't say "new." Name the specific item and the price.
- 2. Segment by household. Kids-category sends shouldn't go to someone with no kids purchase history. Send me rugs and lighting from the main brand instead.
- 3. Cut the length. Four product tiles that matter beat sixteen sale categories, stockings and a card ad. Wayfair's "picked just for you" grid is noisy, but it does show me products I've actually looked at.
- 4. Cap the cadence. Five sends from one family in a day is a good reason for someone to unsubscribe.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Rugs, nursery furniture and holiday stockings, plus free shipping on $79+.`

## 1. Technical Summary

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **Third-party identifier pixels.** Five `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=dbe8da718e29…&n=1` through `n=5` requests carry what looks like a 64-hex hashed recipient identifier (`s=`). The `mi_u=dbe8da71…` value appears on `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png` (6 instances) and `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif`. - **Another open pixel.** `https://dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/1cc80cf1-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…` is an extra open-tracking image.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text, content images (about 24).** These include `…/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/*.jpg` (for example `d3119bec-…jpg`, `5d4ea9f0-…jpg`, `971aa10f-…jpg`), `m/47/bbf6f9d4-…png`, `m/1/f8c48ff1-…gif`, and the Scene7 assets `2026_1124_CBK_BF_NewDealsAdded_02.gif` and `2026_1105_CBK_PersonalizedGiftShop_Seconda…`. Image-blocking clients and screen readers will get no text for these.
- **Missing alt text, spacers and pixels (about 13).** These are `25_MI_Bottom_Spacer_40px_White`, the `rlcdn` and `mi.` pixels, and the `dv.` pixel. They should carry `alt=""` explicitly. A missing attribute makes some screen readers announce the filename.

- **Dead CSS rule.** `@media (min-width:768px){img{max-width:600px}}` is followed by `@media (min-width:640px){img{max-width:640px}}`. At 768px and wider both match, and the later rule wins, so the 600px cap never applies.

- **Duplicate meta tags.** There are four `format-detection` metas and two charset declarations (`http-equiv` and `<meta charset>`).
