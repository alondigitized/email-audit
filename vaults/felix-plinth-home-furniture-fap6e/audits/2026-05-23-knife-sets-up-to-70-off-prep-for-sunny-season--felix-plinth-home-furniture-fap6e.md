# KNIFE SETS 🙌 UP TO 70% OFF 🌞 Prep for sunny season 
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- Thirteen. I just want to say that number out loud. Thirteen Wayfair emails since roughly Sunday, and this one led with "KNIFE SETS" in the subject — so I opened it half-expecting to actually buy something for the kitchen in the second house. What I found was a generic Memorial Day clearance grid: outdoor chairs, ceiling fans, what looks like a power recliner, some kind of rug deal. There are knife sets somewhere in here, I think — I can see a small product block that might be cutlery — but it's buried three or four rows down in a "Your Home, These Picks" section that looks algorithmically assembled and feels absolutely impersonal.
- The doorbuster section caught my attention briefly: buy one Adirondack chair, get one 25% off. That's actually useful — I have a back deck at the second house that needs seating. But by the time my eye landed there, I'd already mentally filed this as another Wayfair spray-and-pray blast.
- I'm not clicking. I'm not forwarding. I'm doing what I've done with the last four of these: tapping the star off, leaving it unread at the bottom of my inbox folder, and moving on.

## What's working

- The Memorial Day Clearance banner at the top is crisp — the red/white/blue flag reads instantly and the seasonal context is clear without being cloying.
- The "buy one Adirondack chair, get one 25% OFF" doorbuster is one of the only specific, product-anchored offers in the email; it's the kind of thing that could actually convert for someone furnishing outdoor space.
- Free delivery over $35 is surfaced early and clearly — I noticed it immediately and it's table stakes for furniture consideration.
- The grid layout is clean; no broken images, no overlapping text, renders fine on my screen.

## What's weak

- The subject line promised knife sets — the email delivered patio furniture and ceiling fans. That bait-and-switch energy erodes trust in a way that Article, which ran a clean "Up to 40% off ends Monday" subject last week with a body that matched exactly, would never do.
- Thirteen emails in roughly four days. Wayfair is burning my goodwill faster than any brand in my inbox right now. C&B has sent me ~29 emails this week and I'm still more likely to click them because at least the cadence feels like different categories.
- "Shop all sales" is the primary CTA — it goes nowhere useful. CB2's "Pieces worth lusting after" email linked me directly to the sale landing page; this button deposits me on a general clearance dump.
- The "picked just for you" hero grid reads as pure algorithm: a desk chair, storage furniture, a lamp, and something I can't even identify at this thumbnail size. Nothing says "we know you're renovating two houses and are in the market for outdoor seating or kitchen upgrades."
- No pricing specificity anywhere near the hero — I have to scroll past three rows to see an actual dollar amount.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Match the subject to the body.** If knife sets are 70% off, make knife sets the hero. Full stop. A kitchen block or a knife set staged on a cutting board with a real price call-out would have been the kind of hero image that earns the click. Article does this — one category, one image, one number, done.
- 2. **Cut the BOGO Adirondack offer out of the doorbuster row and promote it to the hero.** It's the most actionable item in this email for someone with a second-home deck. Hide it mid-scroll and it converts nobody.
- 3. **Add a countdown or hard date.** "Memorial Day" implies Sunday, but there's no "ends Sunday 5/25" anywhere I can see. Pottery Barn's Memorial Day emails have been using deadline copy; that's the one thing they're doing better than this send.
- 4. **Dial back cadence or segment harder.** If I'm getting thirteen emails and the last one was about electric fireplaces, something in the suppression logic is broken.

- **Subject Alt B:** `70% off knife sets. Plus a BOGO Adirondack deal worth seeing.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Outfit the kitchen and the back deck before Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free delivery over $35 — stock up before Memorial Day ends.`
