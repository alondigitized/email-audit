# Shop it first: our Venus Williams collab, only on our app!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-14**
## Executive summary

- Fifth Crate & Kids email in my inbox this week, and this one's got a genuinely interesting hook — Venus Williams collab — buried in a phone-mockup graphic that's exclusively pushing me to download an app. I don't have a kid. I don't want the Crate & Barrel app. I want a browser link to a console table.
- The one thing that stopped my thumb for a half-second was the wreath-quality art direction on that hero shot — moody lifestyle photography, same energy as the "Christmas decor" send that actually worked for me. But then it's straight into a nursery glider, a stroller travel system, a dresser, and baby registry links. This is not a "shop it first" moment for me, it's a moment to archive and move on.
- Not clicking, not forwarding — unless it's to a friend who just had a baby, and even then I'd caption it "why do they only let you buy this in an app."

## What's working

- The hero photography (phone mockup + moody tennis-court-adjacent lifestyle shot) is genuinely well-styled, on par with the wreath-wall hero I flagged from the main Crate & Barrel brand.
- "TheDesignDesk" free design consultation block is a smart, low-pressure CTA that at least gestures at furniture/home styling rather than pure baby gear — closest thing to relevant content in this whole email.
- Clean section breaks and a real footer nav (Furniture, Decor, Storage, Gear) show some brand discipline even if the featured products don't match it.

## What's weak

- The entire "we love these for you" product grid is nursery furniture, car seats, and a "Busy Baby Activity Chair" — zero relevance to a childless 36-year-old renovating two houses.
- App-exclusive gating on a name-brand collab (Venus Williams) is a hard no for me — I shop by whichever brand shows up in my inbox with the best link, I'm not installing an app for a kids' product line I'll never buy.
- The Crate Card promo ("new! the card you love just got better") is squeezed in between baby gear and another app download push — feels like three different emails stapled together.
- Baby Registry is literally in the footer nav — this is Crate & Kids reminding me for the fifth time this week that I am not their customer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this list. If Crate & Barrel/Crate & Kids know I've never clicked a nursery or stroller link, stop sending me this sub-brand — that's the single biggest lift here, and it's the same note I left on the "Newborn Essentials Shop" and "kid spaces" sends.
- 2. Kill the app-exclusivity gate on a genuine collab moment — a Venus Williams tie-in deserves a shoppable web landing page, not "download our app to see it."
- 3. If this must go to non-parent subscribers, lead with TheDesignDesk and general furniture, not the nursery grid — that's the only section with crossover appeal.
- 4. Don't bundle a credit-card pitch into a collab-launch email — it reads like leftover inventory from three separate campaigns.
