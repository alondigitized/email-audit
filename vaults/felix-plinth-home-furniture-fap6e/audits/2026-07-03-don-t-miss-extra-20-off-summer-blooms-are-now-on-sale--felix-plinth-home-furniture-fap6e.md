# Don't miss EXTRA 20% OFF + Summer blooms are now on sale!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- Fifty-nine. That's roughly where my Pottery Barn count lands for the month, and this one drops on July 3rd as — I'm not exaggerating — the third PB email I've opened today. The first one this morning was the "Final weekend" drop with the same Extra 20% off hero. The second was "Last weekend for EXTRA 20% OFF + All pillows ship free." Now it's "Don't miss EXTRA 20% OFF + Summer blooms." Same discount, different noun appended. The bloom shop pivot is clearly a real campaign angle, and honestly, the photography is beautiful — lush florals, clean white vases, that earthy-editorial PB look they do well. But I am renovating a primary home and a second home simultaneously, and fresh-cut florals are approximately item 400 on that list.
- The "Recommended Just For You" section below the fold has what looks like bedroom furniture, which is the first thing in this email that actually applies to me. But by the time I scroll there, the email has already burned its first impression on botanicals.
- Mark as read. Maybe I skim the recommended section before I close it. I don't click. I definitely don't forward it — Article's July 3rd deadline email from this morning is a better send to share if someone's asking for furniture recs.

## What's working

- The Bloom Shop photography is genuinely strong — full-bleed florals in neutral vessels feel editorial, not clip-art. PB's visual brand is doing its job.
- The 4th of July Sale callout banner at the very top anchors the discount before anything else loads. Smart hierarchy even if the offer is tired.
- "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" section below the hero is the right instinct — personalizing to furniture when the email hero is botanicals salvages relevance for someone like me.
- Loyalty rewards callout ("Earn 10% back in Rewards") near the footer is a clean, low-pressure trust signal — I do track that math when I'm making a big furniture decision.
- Render is clean: no broken images, no overlapping text, no Lorem Ipsum. The layout holds on mobile.

## What's weak

- Third PB send today. The "Extra 20% off" banner has now appeared in four emails in two days — the urgency is completely dead. "Don't miss" means nothing when I've been told not to miss the same offer since July 1st.
- The hero is all florals. I'm not buying botanicals. Compare that to Crate & Barrel's "Starting at $219: Dining chairs" email I saw this week — that subject line answered a specific question I had open. This one opens with a category I'm not shopping.
- "Summer blooms" in the subject is the first new information in days, but it pushes the audience feminine. If PB's database knows I'm buying sofas and dining tables, why is my above-the-fold about silk florals?
- The primary CTA — "Shop All Botanicals" — doesn't go anywhere near my buying intent. Article had a single "Shop Sale" button with a clear "ends Monday" deadline and I clicked it. This has three CTAs above the fold pointing at categories I'm not in.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the botanical push away from the active renovators.** If my purchase history is furniture, the hero should be the 20% off furniture angle, with botanicals as a secondary module. PB has my data. Use it the way "Recommended Just For You" already hints at.
- 2. **Kill one of today's sends.** Three PB emails on July 3rd is a deliverability cliff. I'm not unsubscribing yet, but I'm thinking about it — and that's a real signal.
- 3. **Harden the urgency.** "Don't miss" is the weakest possible urgency phrase after four days of the same discount. Article used "ends Monday" with a specific date. Copy that.
- 4. **Collapse the CTA count above the fold.** One primary button per email section. "Shop the Bloom Shop" and "Shop All Botanicals" and "Shop All Styles" are three buttons before I've seen a single piece of furniture.
- 5. **Move the recommended section higher** — or at minimum tease it in the subject line if that's the actually relevant content.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 20% off furniture — ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your picks are 20% off this weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Summer Bloom Shop added to sale — plus the furniture you've been watching`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `20% off ends soon. Your saved items are in here.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
A West Elm asset domain (`edm.westelm.com`) appears in a Pottery Barn email. The request returns 403 and is also HTTP (not HTTPS). This is a copy-paste template contamination from a sibling brand's module.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP `header()` calls inside `<style>` block — template error**
The following server-side PHP directives appear verbatim inside a CSS `<style>` tag in `<head>`:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are not CSS. They will not execute; email clients will parse them as invalid CSS and may log warnings or fail silently. This is a template generation defect — PHP output that was intended for an HTTP response was injected into the HTML document.

**3 images served over HTTP**
Email clients with HTTPS enforcement (Apple Mail, Outlook.com) will block these:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` (open-tracking pixel)

**33 images missing `alt` text** — virtually every content image in the body and footer. With images off (default in many enterprise clients), the email renders as blank space. The logo image (`nav0.jpg`) has `alt="POTTERY BARN"` — the same pattern should be applied to all CTAs and content images.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile, an accessibility regression (WCAG 1.4.4).

**Global `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`** applied via `* {}` — overrides user accessibility settings for text scaling.
