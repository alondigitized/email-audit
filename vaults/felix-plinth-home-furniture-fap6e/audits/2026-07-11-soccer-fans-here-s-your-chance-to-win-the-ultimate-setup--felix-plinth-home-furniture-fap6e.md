- This is the second soccer sweepstakes send from the C&B family today — the "dream soccer setup" email already ran; this is a retread, not a campaign, and I noticed.
- "Soccer fans" is demographic gating that doesn't match how I think of myself when I open this folder — I'm here because I need furniture for two houses, not because I follow a sport.

- Twenty-six emails in three days from this brand family means every individual send is fighting a depleted attention reserve before it even renders.

CB2's "When your bedroom is just for you" email this week didn't use a sweepstakes and still pulled me in — because it trusted I came to buy.
- 4. **Cap the cadence at the family level, not the brand level.** Pottery Barn, CB2, Crate & Kids, and Crate & Barrel hitting simultaneously is a brand architecture problem that shows up as an inbox experience problem.
- 5. **Segment "soccer fans" separately if this sweepstakes has real engagement.** The fans who want to enter will; the renovation buyers who don't will stop opening.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — `maximum-scale=1` blocks accessibility zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents users from pinching to zoom on mobile. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is disallowed in some email client audits. Remove `maximum-scale=1` or raise to `maximum-scale=5`.

**Issue 2 — Conflicting `img` max-width media queries**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
Both rules fire at ≥768px. Because the 768px block appears first in source, the 640px block (declared after) overrides it — images render at 640px on all wide viewports regardless of intent. If 600px is the correct desktop cap, the 640px rule must come before the 768px rule, or consolidate into a single breakpoint.

**Issue 3 — Global `text-size-adjust: none`**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applying `text-size-adjust: none` via the `*` selector suppresses automatic font scaling across all elements. This can degrade readability on small screens and conflicts with accessibility guidelines. Scope to specific problem elements rather than the universal selector.

**Issue 4 — Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
`<meta name="format-detection">` appears four times (once combined, then three individual declarations). The combined tag `content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no"` is sufficient; the redundant individual tags should be removed.
