# From The Design Desk: nursery inspo with our new chair
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-22**
## Executive summary

- Fourth Crate & Kids email in my inbox this week, and this one's the "Design Desk" swatch-comparison format — one nursery glider chair shown in four different fabrics with a mood board. No sale, no countdown, just design content. I don't have a nursery to furnish — I've got a bunk-bed situation for the guest room and two full houses of adult renovation to manage — so this is a total miss on relevance even though it's actually a nicer-looking email than their usual catalog dump.
- The "book a free design appointment" CTA repeated after every single fabric section is the tell that this brand wants a service relationship, not a one-off purchase, which is smart for new parents but does nothing for me. It's well-shot, on-brand, restrained — genuinely one of the better-produced Crate & Kids sends I've seen — but it's the wrong send to the wrong inbox. Not clicking, not forwarding, marking as read and moving on.

## What's working

- The one-chair-four-fabrics format is a clean, focused idea — way better than the "NEW for fall!" catalog dump from earlier this week that just threw beds, bedding, lighting, and backpacks at me all at once.
- Photography is consistent and calm — muted greens, tans, denim blue, natural linen — actual color-palette discipline across the whole send, something CB2's cluttered clearance sends usually skip.
- The mood board treatment (chair + fabric swatch + wallpaper) is a nice touch for anyone actually decorating a nursery — shows they thought about a real design workflow, not just "buy this."
- Each section links a "New Arrival," "Bestseller," and other furniture pairing — decent merchandising logic if I actually needed a glider.

## What's weak

- Wrong persona, full stop — no nursery in my reno plan, and Crate & Kids knows I've been opening their bunk-bed emails, not glider chair ones.
- Zero pricing anywhere in this email — not one dollar figure or "starting at" call-out on any fabric or the chair itself. Pottery Barn's sale emails at least lead with the number.
- No offer, no urgency, no rewards math — this is pure lookbook content, and I'm not in a browsing mood for a category I donm't need.
- Repeating the "Book a Free Appointment" CTA five separate times reads as filler when I have no reason to book anything nursery-related.
- This is now four sends this week from the same sub-brand — my inbox fatigue with Crate & Kids specifically is real at this point.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment me out of nursery-specific content entirely — if your data shows I've engaged with bunk-bed/kids-room-transition emails, stop sending glider chair content; it's a wasted send.
- 2. Put a price or starting-at figure on the hero chair — a $000 anchor gives me a reason to even consider clicking, design content alone won't move a Millennial dad mid-reno budget mode.
- 3. Cut the CTA repetition — one strong "Book a Free Appointment" at the end is plenty; five instances trains me to ignore all of them.
- 4. If this needs to go broad, pair it with a cross-sell to bunk beds or shared-room furniture, which is the actual project in my house right now.
