# Big kid bed time? Find their perfect match!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- I've opened two Crate & Kids emails today. Two. The first one — "Beautiful beds that work in any room" — already made me do the double-take of checking whether it was the same email I'd already read. This one has the exact same problem but somehow leans even harder into parent territory. "Big kid bed time?" is unmistakably about transitioning a child out of a crib or toddler bed — it is not a renovation email, it is not a design-forward adult furniture email, it is a *this-weekend-in-Target* parent errand email. I don't know how Crate & Kids segmented me into this send, but if they checked my actual order history or browsing behavior, they'd know I'm shopping for a living room sectional and a guest bedroom situation, not a twin bed with under-storage.
- The imagery is fine — warm neutrals, styled children's rooms, the kind of tasteful execution you expect from C&B's sub-brands. The countdown timer in the hero actually stopped me for a second, same as it did in the 60%-off parent brand email earlier today. But then the product grid confirmed everything: bunk beds, beds-with-drawers, low-profile kid frames. Nothing here applies to either of the two properties I'm renovating.
- Mark as read. Definitely not forwarding. If I had a seven-year-old, maybe I'd screenshot the bunk bed section for my spouse — but I'm not that person this week, and Crate & Kids doesn't seem to know or care.

## What's working

- **Countdown timer in the hero** — it's the same urgency mechanic that worked in the 60%-off C&B send earlier today, and it still registers before my brain can talk me out of it
- **Aesthetic is solid** — warm neutrals, layered bedding, the Crate & Kids look is cohesive and doesn't feel chaotic or cluttered; it's the right visual register for anyone in a design-aware household
- **Product variety is meaningful** — bunk beds, storage beds, different frame profiles; if you're shopping in this category, the range makes sense and the grid is easy to scan
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no layout fractures, reads fine at mobile width

## What's weak

- **Second Crate & Kids email today, sixth-plus from the C&B family** — I'm watching my inbox turn into a Crate & Barrel franchise; the fatigue is real and by the second send of the day from the same sub-brand, I'm reflexively reaching for "archive"
- **"Big kid bed time" is 100% parent-coded** — unlike the earlier "Beautiful beds" send which at least gestured at design relevance, this subject is explicitly about a childhood developmental milestone; if C&B's list doesn't know I'm shopping for adult spaces, this is a targeting miss, not a messaging miss
- **No adult crossover hook** — Article would have found a way to acknowledge the design-curious adult in the room, even in a kids send; this email makes no attempt to bridge the gap
- **CTA copy is generic** — "Shop Now" appears throughout; compared to the "5 top sofa collections" C&B send that led with editorial specificity, this feels like a template drop

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment or suppress** — the highest-impact fix: if I'm not identified as a parent in the C&B system, I shouldn't be on this send at all; Pottery Barn Kids and PB Teen already run separate lists from the parent brand for exactly this reason
- 2. **Lead with a design angle before the product grid** — even a single line like "The beds that look good in the room *you* designed" bridges the gap for design-forward buyers who happen to also have kids; Article does this instinctively, Crate & Kids doesn't
- 3. **Don't send two sub-brand emails in one day** — the "Beautiful beds" email and this one are four hours apart; one of them should have been held until tomorrow
- 4. **Add a specific discount callout in the subject** — the timer is doing urgency work in the body but the subject doesn't earn the click on offer alone
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to X% off kids beds — sale ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The transitional bed they'll keep for years — on sale now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Built to last from first big-kid night through the teenage years`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Find the frame that grows with them — and fits the room you already have`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` brand mismatch (high)**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
Sender is `Crate & Kids`; the title renders as "Crate & Barrel" in browser/preview tabs and is indexed by some ESPs. Should be `<title>Crate & Kids</title>`.

**Viewport blocks pinch-to-zoom (medium — WCAG 1.4.4)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents user zoom on mobile. Remove or raise to `maximum-scale=5`.

**Global text-size-adjust disabled (medium — WCAG 1.4.4)**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applied universally via the wildcard selector. Prevents OS-level font scaling for users with visual needs. Scope to `body` only and set to `100%`, not `none`.

**Conflicting `img` max-width media queries (low — dead rule)**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }  /* declared first */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }  /* declared second — wins at 768px+ via cascade */
```
At viewport ≥ 768px both queries match; the 640px rule is declared later and wins, capping images at 640px. The 768px rule (`max-width: 600px`) is dead code and will never apply. Reorder or merge into a single rule.

**Duplicate and redundant meta tags (low)**
`format-detection` is declared four times — once combined, then three times individually. `charset` is declared twice (`http-equiv` and `meta charset`). Redundant but not render-breaking.
