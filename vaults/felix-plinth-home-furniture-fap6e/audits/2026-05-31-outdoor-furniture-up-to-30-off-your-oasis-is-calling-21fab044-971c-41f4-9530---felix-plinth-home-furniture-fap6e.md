## What's working

- **Hero lands immediately** — "Up to 30% off" in large type over a well-styled outdoor scene, offer-first, no editorial mystery to decode before the value prop lands.
- **Category nav is surgical** — six outdoor-specific links above the hero image let me skip the browse and land directly on Sofas & Sectionals or Dining; Pottery Barn and Article both bury those entry points below the fold.
- **Photography earns it** — garden setting, natural light, aspirational without being unreachable. This is the exact aesthetic I'm building toward on the back patio.
- **Dual CTA structure** — "Shop Now" for the deal-motivated and "New Outdoor Entertaining" for the browse-curious; two entry points, two purchase intents covered.

## What's weak

- **This is the second identical send today** — same subject, same date, same offer. The first got an 8. This one loses points entirely on cadence. Seeing the duplicate in my preview pane triggered a skip reflex before I even opened it.
- **"We love these for you" is misfiring** — the personalized recs appear to be indoor pieces (what looks like a gray upholstered sectional, a side table) inside an outdoor sale email. My recent browse is clearly outdoor; the algorithm should know that. This is the kind of misfire that makes a subscriber feel unseen despite the "for you" label.
- **No deadline anywhere** — Article's Memorial Day emails were the best in the category specifically because they said "ends Monday." C&B's outdoor sale is a temporal void. I don't know if this is a weekend offer or a permanent markdown.
- **The card promo breaks the funnel** — "the card you love just got better / Learn More" lands mid-scroll in a sale conversion email. That's a separate ask that belongs in its own send or in the footer, not interrupting a purchase path.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress duplicate sends at the operational level.** If you're retargeting non-openers, change the subject and preview. Same-day same-subject resends erode sender reputation with real subscribers faster than any open rate optimization helps.
- 2. **Sync the recommendation engine to active session context.** When my browse history is outdoor, every module should surface outdoor. Indoor recs in a patio sale email actively undermine the "we know you" signal.
- 3. **Add a hard deadline.** "Sale ends June 2" — one line, anywhere above the fold. CB2's editorial sends don't need this but sale emails do.
- 4. **Push the card promo to footer or standalone.** Rewards card interest is real for me (I track which card clears which threshold), but not when I'm mid-funnel on a 30% off sale.
- 5. **Trim the subject** — the second clause is doing nothing the first clause hasn't already done.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA).

**Global `text-size-adjust: none` applied via wildcard**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Wildcard selector suppresses OS-level text scaling across all elements. Should be scoped to `body` only, not `*`.

**Conflicting image `max-width` media queries**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
At viewport ≥ 768px both rules apply; cascade order makes the 600px rule win, clipping images that are sized for a 640px container. The 768px block should be removed or consolidated.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags** — `telephone`, `address`, and `date` are declared twice each (once in the combined tag, once individually). Redundant but harmless.

**Alt text on images** — not verifiable from truncated source; must be confirmed separately.
