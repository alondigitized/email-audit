# The high chair they'll use until middle school (really!) in our exclusive color→
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-14**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is Crate & Kids, not the main Crate & Barrel account, and it's a Stokke Tripp Trapp launch — a new olive green colorway on the high chair that apparently grows with your kid "until middle school (really!)." I'll give it this: we don't have kids yet, but my sister-in-law has been evangelizing Tripp Trapp to anyone who'll listen, so the brand recognition is real even outside the target buyer.
- The problem is I'm not the target buyer. This is the eighth-ish Crate & Barrel-family email this week and the first one that's actually product-specific to a life stage I'm not in — no nursery, no high chair needed. Article's Ashford dining collection last week at least had a "new collection, full room story" angle I could see myself using; this is one SKU in one new color, dressed up with five separate product modules to justify the send.
- I'm not clicking, not forwarding — unless I forward it as a joke to my sister-in-law with "still team Tripp Trapp?" Otherwise this is an instant scroll-past-and-archive.

## What's working

- Olive green as an "exclusive" color is a real hook — feels distinct from the beige/blonde-wood high chair aesthetic everyone already owns.
- The hero shot of the kid in the chair against the matching olive wall is genuinely well-styled, on-brand C&B/Crate&Kids visual polish.
- "500+ new fall arrivals, starting at $29!" buried mid-email is actually the most universally relevant line in the whole send, and it's underused.
- Clean single accent color (olive/sage) running through swatches and highlight and CTA buttons — visually coherent top to bottom.

## What's weak

- I have no kid needing a high chair, so the entire hero, the "iconic high chair" section, and the Stokke bouncer/tray upsells are 100% irrelevant to me — this is a life-stage mismatch, not a taste mismatch.
- It's an absolute kitchen-sink email: Tripp Trapp, more Stokke gear, fall arrivals, a $5000 sweepstakes, "we love these for you" grid, a store credit card pitch, and TheDesignDesk — by the time I hit the $5k sweepstakes I've forgotten this started as a high chair email.
- "we love these for you" personalization module shows strollers and nursery furniture — that's not "for me," that's just generic Crate & Kids inventory, and it undercuts the idea that this brand knows who's opening its emails.
- Yet another store-card pitch at the bottom ("now the card you love just got better") — CB2 and Pottery Barn both lean on this too, and by the fourth time this week it reads as filler, not a benefit.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into two sends: a tight, gorgeous single-product Tripp Trapp launch for actual parents/gift-givers, and route non-parent segments (like me) into the fall-arrivals or sweepstakes email instead — sending me a high chair headline wastes the open.
- 2. Cut the email in half. Tripp Trapp, more Stokke gear, fall arrivals, sweepstakes, personalized grid, credit card, design service — pick two and ship the rest next week. Article's single-collection Ashford email proved restraint reads as confidence, not laziness.
- 3. If Crate & Kids does keep sending me stuff, segment the "we love these for you" module off real browsing/purchase history — right now it's just proving it doesn't know me.
- 4. Move the fall arrivals line ("500+ new, starting at $29") up near the top for segments without kids — that's the actual relevant offer buried at position five.
