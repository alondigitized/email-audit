# New markdowns are already selling fast, don’t miss out!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- I counted: this is the second C&B email today, after the SMEG confession landed in my inbox this morning. And honestly, that one was weird enough that it reset my expectations. So when "New markdowns are already selling fast" hit right after it, my first reaction was — of course it did. We're back to the script. The SMEG email was a creative palate cleanser; this is the main course they were stalling on.
- That said, the clearance angle is the *right* play for me right now. I'm actively furnishing two homes and I genuinely want to know what's been marked down. The hero banner leads with "up to 60% off Clearance" and that's the offer I care about. The "we love these for you" personalized block mid-email is the most interesting part — a dining chair and a table that are actually in my wheelhouse. If the link lands on a curated clearance page (not the entire clearance dump), I'd click.
- I wouldn't forward this. I'd drop it in my saved-email folder the same way I do with every other Crate & Barrel sale send. Mark it for a Sunday evening browse with a rewards card already pulled up. Whether I act on it depends entirely on where the links drop me.

## What's working

- **The hero offer is immediately legible** — "up to 60% off Clearance" with a Shop Now button. I don't have to hunt for the value prop; it's the first thing my eye lands on.
- **TheDesignDesk call-out is genuinely useful for a renovator** — free design help while I'm actually in a renovation is the right moment to surface that. Pottery Barn's Memorial Day blitz never mentioned their design services once.
- **The "we love these for you" section** — a table and dining chairs that look like actual furniture I'd spec. This feels more considered than Wayfair's stair-treads-as-a-hero-email moment.
- **Clean layout, no visual breakage** — the grid renders properly, images load, hierarchy holds up. CB2's "Now & Future Classics" send had the same clean skeleton; C&B matches it here.
- **Crate&Kids cross-sell feels natural** — tucked in rather than screamed, which is the right call.

## What's weak

- **Cadence is the real problem, not the content** — this is email number two from C&B today and at least the fifth from the broader C&B family since Sunday. "Selling fast, don't miss out" loses all urgency when I've been told the same thing in different fonts for 72 hours straight.
- **"Up to 60% off" is doing heavy lifting on a vague promise** — the CB2 Last Call for Memorial Day email was specific about categories and product types. This just waves at clearance broadly. What's actually 60% off? The page will tell me but the email shouldn't make me guess.
- **"Shop Now" is the laziest CTA alive** — Article's "Last chance" send at least used directional language. "Shop Clearance" or "See What's New" would at least tell me where I'm going.
- **No rewards math visible** — I buy on the highest-rewards card and I've hit Double Rewards days with C&B before. The card mention is buried at the bottom. Compare this to the "HOURS LEFT: Up to 60% off + Double Rewards!" send from yesterday: that email put the rewards front and center. This one drops it in the footer like an afterthought.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the rewards lever back in the hero** — If Double Rewards is running, say it next to the 60% off. That's the combo that makes me click immediately, not tomorrow.
- 2. **Replace "Shop Now" with a category-specific CTA** — "Shop Clearance Furniture" or "See New Price Drops" changes what I expect to land on and makes the click feel more intentional.
- 3. **Anchor the hero to a specific markdown category** — Even one callout — "New drops in dining + seating" — would differentiate this from the five other clearance emails sitting in my saved folder.
- 4. **Either skip the Crate&Kids section or make it toggleable** — If I've browsed kids furniture, keep it. If not, this real estate could show more relevant personalized picks for my renovation.
- 5. **Cadence fix at the send-schedule level** — No email tweak saves a brand that emails twice in one day. This one needed a 24-hour buffer after the SMEG send, minimum.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users from zooming in on mobile. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Conflicting `img { max-width }` media queries**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
The 640 px rule appears after the 768 px rule. At viewport widths 640–767 px, only the 640 px rule fires (correct). At 768 px+, both rules fire and the 640 px rule wins via cascade order, overriding the intended 600 px desktop cap. Swap the declaration order so the 768 px rule is last.

**`* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none }` applied globally**
The universal selector disables font scaling across all elements, which can break user accessibility preferences. Scope this to `body` or specific containers rather than `*`.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
`telephone=no` and `address=no` each appear twice (once in a combined tag, once separately). Redundant but non-breaking; clean up for hygiene.

**No dark mode support**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Dark mode is explicitly disabled. Apple Mail and Gmail will force-invert, potentially breaking white text on colored backgrounds. Not a defect per se, but a known rendering risk if the design relies on white-on-white image masking.
