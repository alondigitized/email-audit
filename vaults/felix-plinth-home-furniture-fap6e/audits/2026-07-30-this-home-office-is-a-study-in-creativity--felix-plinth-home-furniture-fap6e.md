# This home office is a study in creativity
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- Okay, CB2 pivoted hard from "40% off, ends tonight" mode into full editorial-designer-home-tour mode, and honestly it's a nice change of pace after four straight sale/urgency sends I've been tracking from them. This one's built around Aurora James' home office in some Laurel Canyon cottage — moody, plant-filled, a little bohemian, very "design magazine spread" — and since I'm mid-renovation on two houses and one of them desperately needs an office that isn't a card table, the room match actually lands.
- That said, there's no offer anywhere near the top of this thing. It's all vibes and "SHOP AURORA'S SPACE" until you scroll way, way down to the sofa CTA and the review-for-$1,000 sweepstakes. I like looking at it, but "like looking at it" and "am I buying a $1,899 side table today" are different questions, and CB2 didn't try very hard to close that gap.
- Verdict: I'd open it, I'd scroll through the pretty photos on my lunch break, I might screenshot the Klub chair for my Pinterest-adjacent folder — but I'm not clicking to buy, and I'm not forwarding it. This is a "read and file away" email, not an "act now" one.

## What's working

- The home office framing is genuinely on-topic for me right now — better than CB2's usual furniture-event blast, and closer to what Pottery Barn did with its entryway styling guide (6/10, but praised specifically for being room-specific instead of generic).
- Real named pieces with real designers attached — "Klub Chair designed by Steen Østergaard in 1962," "The Felena Vase, designed by Ross Cassidy" — that provenance detail is catnip for someone who reads the fine print on Design Within Reach before buying at CB2 prices instead.
- The photography is consistent top to bottom — same warm, moody, plant-heavy palette in every shot, no jarring stock-photo tonal shift like Wayfair's "15 things before coffee" grid.
- No render bugs, images load clean, buttons are legible on a phone-width screenshot.

## What's weak

- Zero discount, zero urgency, zero price anywhere until the "More for you" grid at the very bottom — this is basically an inspiration blog post wearing a marketing-email costume.
- The hero model is a female fashion designer in a distinctly feminine, bohemian-leaning space — nice story, but it's not really speaking to a guy shopping for a home office that needs to survive a toddler and a home-office Zoom background.
- "SHOP NOW" under the sofa section is generic CTA copy sitting right next to much better, more specific ones like "SHOP KOA" and "SHOP FELENA" — inconsistent effort.
- The $1,000 review sweepstakes and the 15%-back credit card pitch are both buried at the bottom, stacked with app-download and social icons — either one could've been a real hook higher up.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price or a specific incentive near the top — even "shop the look, pieces from $99" under the hero would give me a reason to keep scrolling instead of just admiring.
- 2. Swap "SHOP NOW" on the sofa module for something as specific as the rest of the email — "Shop the Sectional" or "Build Your Sofa."
- 3. Move the $1,000 review sweepstakes up near the fold — that's a stronger click driver than another "shop the space" button.
- 4. Add one home-office-specific detail I actually need before buying — desk dimensions, storage capacity, whether the Klub chair comes in a fabric that isn't going to show cat hair.
