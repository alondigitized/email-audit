# under $29.99
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-07**
## Executive summary

- So the subject line is just "under $29.99" — no punctuation gymnastics, no emoji shrapnel, which honestly is a nice break from the last three Wayfair sends I've torn apart today. But then I open it and it's the exact same problem I keep flagging with this brand: it's not one email, it's eight emails stapled together. Under $29.99 gets its module up top, then it's Halloween must-haves, then chef knives, then "vintage revival," then storage bins, then bedding, then a mattress "deal of the day," then a "but wait there's more" grab bag of privacy screens and massage chairs. By the time I hit the bedding section I've forgotten this was supposed to be a budget clearance email.
- The "70% OFF ENDS TONIGHT" banner up top is doing real work — that's the one moment of actual urgency in the whole thing — but it's disconnected from the "all under $29.99" module directly below it, so I genuinely can't tell if the 70% off applies to the under-$29.99 stuff or if that's a separate sale entirely. Compare that to Article's "Last call: Up to 40% off ends today" send, which I scored an 8 because the deadline and the discount were the same headline, same hero, no guessing. Wayfair keeps making me do math and inference work it should be doing for me.
- I'm not clicking. I'm not forwarding. This is a scroll-and-close, and honestly it's starting to blur together with the aquariums email and the range hoods email from earlier today — different subject, same kitchen-sink body.

## What's working

- The "70% OFF LABOR DAY CLEARANCE ENDS TONIGHT" banner at the very top is clear, bold, and has a real deadline — that's the strongest single element in the email.
- The "under $29.99" hero section is genuinely clean — four product shots (glassware, a lamp pair, a round rug, an entry table) with a clear "Shop under $29.99" button right beneath it. If the whole email had stopped there, I'd have scored this higher.
- No render bugs — images all loaded, text is legible, buttons are tappable-sized on the mobile-width render.
- The chef knife module and the vintage revival module actually have decent product photography — the knife set and the vintage-look plates look like something I'd screenshot for the second-home kitchen board.

## What's weak

- Same "wrong Felix" sprawl I flagged on the aquariums and range hoods sends today: a "$29.99" subject line should not end with recliners, massage chairs, and outdoor privacy screens eight modules later.
- The 70% off banner and the under-$29.99 hero aren't visually tied together — is the clearance sale ON TOP OF under-$29.99 pricing, or are these two different promos? I shouldn't have to guess that mid-scroll.
- Every section header ("Halloween must-haves," "chef-worthy picks," "vintage revival," "smart storage solutions," "design your dream bed") is its own mini-campaign with its own CTA button — that's five distinct asks in one email, which kills any sense of priority.
- No pricing shown anywhere below the top hero — not even the mattress "deal of the day" gets a dollar figure on it, just a bare "Shop today's deals" button on a plain bed photo.
- Zero rewards-math or member-pricing callout despite Wayfair having a loyalty program — Rewards only shows up as a tiny icon in the footer, nowhere near the actual offers.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this down to ONE theme. If it's "under $29.99," make the entire email that — pull six or eight items that hit the price point and kill Halloween/storage/bedding/recliners entirely. Right now this reads like five separate campaigns got merged because nobody wanted to schedule five separate sends.
- 2. Tie the 70% off banner directly to the under-$29.99 hero with one line of copy — "70% off = these prices" or whatever's actually true — so I'm not left guessing whether it's one sale or two.
- 3. Put an actual price or rewards-adjusted price on the mattress "deal of the day" — a bare product photo with no number on a bed is the weakest CTA in the whole email.
- 4. If Wayfair Rewards points multiply on any of this (the storage or bedding modules especially), say so inline near that module, not buried in the footer icon row.
- 5. Drop the "but wait there's more" grab-bag section entirely — recliners and massage chairs have nothing to do with a $29.99 clearance email and just dilute the one good idea at the top.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Rugs, lamps, entry tables — all under $30, tonight only`
