# On sale now and twice the rewards
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-31**
## Executive summary

- Sixteenth CB2 email of the month and this one isn't even about furniture first — it's a credit card pitch. "Double Rewards Is On, get 20% back in reward dollars" is the hero, and it's honestly a smart move to lead with because it's genuinely different from the fifteen "% off sofas" emails I've already gotten, but it's also not what I want from CB2 in my inbox. I've got the highest-rewards card I'm already using for everything, so pitching me a co-branded store card is a hard pass unless the math is spectacular, and 20% back in reward dollars (store credit, not cash) isn't spectacular.
- The category grid underneath — Living Room, Lighting, Dining Room, Decor & Mirrors, Bedroom, Rugs — is the actual useful part, and the styling on those thumbnails is genuinely good, especially that green pendant lamp shot. But it's buried under a card ad and then followed by yet another "Labor Day Sale up to 50% off" banner near the bottom, so the email is basically fighting itself over what it wants me to do. Not clicking, not forwarding. Marking as read and moving on — I've got Crate & Barrel's "Apartment Collection" from earlier still open in another tab and that one actually earned my attention today.

## What's working

- The "10% ~~strikethrough~~ 20%" visual on the reward rate is a clean way to signal an upgraded offer at a glance.
- Category grid (Living Room, Lighting, Dining Room, Decor & Mirrors, Bedroom, Rugs) is genuinely well-photographed and on-brand for my mid-century-adjacent taste.
- Clear date window ("Now through Sept 9th, 2026") up top — no ambiguity on urgency.
- Footer cross-brand mark (CB2, Crate&Barrel, Crate&kids, H, G) at least tells me who's in the family, which is more transparent than Wayfair's grab-bag approach.

## What's weak

- Leading with a credit card offer instead of product is a swing-and-miss for someone like me who already has a rewards card dialed in — I don't want another store-specific card.
- The email crams in three different asks: apply for a card, shop six categories, and shop the Labor Day sale — nothing is the priority.
- "Double Rewards" and then a separate "up to 50% off" banner later feels like two campaigns duct-taped together, unlike Article's cleaner single-offer sends.
- Fine print disclaimers stack up at the bottom (financing terms, APR-adjacent legalese) which reads more like a bank email than a furniture one.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the Labor Day sale and a hero product, not the credit card — save the card pitch for its own send or a smaller footer callout. Crate & Barrel's Apartment Collection email proved a focused hero outperforms a cluttered one.
- 2. If the card offer stays top billing, translate "20% back in Reward Dollars" into a real dollar example ("spend $500, get $100 back") — abstract percentages on store credit don't move we members.
- 3. Cut the email down to one CTA. Right now LEARN MORE, six category links, and SHOP THE SALE are all competing.
- 4. Move the category grid up top since that's the actual browsing behavior I'd use this email for.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your CB2 card just doubled its rewards`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 50% off + double rewards through 9/9`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New rooms are live — plus 20% back on every purchase`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop lighting, rugs, and decor before the sale ends`
