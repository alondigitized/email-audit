# WEIGHT BENCHES 🎉 🥳 BLACK FRIDAY IN JULY 🎉 🥳 
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- Ten. That's the Wayfair count now — and I'm leading with it the same way I have for every email in this marathon because the sheer volume is the most important thing happening here. Yesterday's sends already included a "Black Friday in July" hero, a drying racks deep-dive, an ornaments email that somehow arrived in late July, and at least two others I've lost track of. Today brings me a fresh subject line that says "weight benches" and an email that has... bedding, TV stands, rugs, and air quality appliances. I scrolled. I squinted. No weight benches.
- That subject line bait-and-switch is the whole story here. The body is actually a reasonably coherent Black Friday in July sale featuring categories I do care about — TV stands and living room furniture? I'm literally sourcing end tables right now for the second home. But Wayfair wrote "weight benches" in the subject and delivered home goods, and I noticed immediately. It either signals a segmentation error (I never browse gym equipment on Wayfair, so someone mixed up a send list) or it's a cheap clickbait trick to stand out in an inbox they've already colonized. Either way it's annoying.
- Mark as read. I'm not clicking anything when the first thing I see is a promise the email doesn't keep.

## What's working

- **"Picked just for you" banner** — it's a small thing, but starting with personalized picks rather than a generic hero at least gestures at relevance; the home goods shown in that grid are closer to my actual browsing than weight benches ever would be
- **Category discounts are scannable** — bedding up to 84% off, TV stands & living room tables up to 84% off, cooking & air quality up to 84% off; I can parse those at a glance, which Article and CB2 both do better than Wayfair usually manages
- **Affirm financing is visible** — on larger furniture pieces this matters; it shows in the doorbuster section and I noticed it
- **Star ratings on product cards** — social proof above the fold in the product grid is always welcome; CB2's "Furniture Event" email last week had the same and I appreciated it there too

## What's weak

- **Subject says weight benches, body has zero weight benches** — I'm not a gym guy and I don't shop that category on Wayfair; this is either a segmentation miss or deliberate clickbait and neither is a good look
- **"BLACK FRIDAY IN JULY" is now the fourth time I've seen that exact phrase from Wayfair in 48 hours** — the phrase has lost all urgency; Article used four days between sends and each felt like news; Wayfair is burning this theme to the ground
- **ALL CAPS + four emoji in the subject line** — the ornaments email yesterday already pushed this direction; now with two party poppers and two party faces stacked next to screaming caps it reads like a brand having a small panic attack
- **"Shop all" CTA is the weakest possible button copy** — Crate & Barrel's eclectic-vibe send this week used more directional copy; "shop all" off a doorbuster section tells me nothing about what I'm clicking into
- **84% off across every category starts to feel unserious** — bedding, TV stands, AND cooking & air quality all at 84%? Pottery Barn would never claim uniform 84% off everything; when every category hits the same ceiling it reads as a marketing number, not a real discount

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the weight benches subject entirely or actually put weight benches in the email** — pick one. If you're going to use a category hook, that category needs to be visible on open. A mismatch this obvious erodes trust faster than any discount can rebuild it.
- 2. **Rotate off "Black Friday in July" to a category-specific angle** — you've sent this campaign name four times; try something like "The TV Stand I've Been Eyeing Just Dropped" or make it the living room pull since that's what the body is actually pushing.
- 3. **Give the CTA a noun** — "Shop TV Stands" or "See Living Room Deals" instead of "Shop All." Pottery Barn and CB2 both use category-specific buttons; it's not hard.
- 4. **Throttle the cadence** — five emails on a single day is the equivalent of a sales rep who follows up every two hours. I've started reflexively archiving without opening. One great email beats four mediocre ones.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

**Web font fallback is declared but font will silently drop in most clients.**
```css
@font-face {
  font-family: "Sofia";
  src: url(https://secure.img.wfrcdn.com/st4/stores/common/fonts/sofia/sofia_reg.woff);
  mso-font-alt: "Arial"
}
```
`@font-face` in email is ignored by Gmail (all surfaces), Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo Mail. The `mso-font-alt` covers Outlook only. Gmail web/app will silently fall through to the system sans-serif — not Arial — unless Arial is explicitly declared in a `font-family` stack on each element. If brand typography depends on Sofia rendering, this is a gap on the highest-volume clients.

**Breakpoints present and structurally correct:** 480px primary, 320px secondary. Standard pattern; no issues.

**iOS-specific `@supports` hack present:**
```css
@supports (-webkit-touch-callout: none) {
  .u-HalfWidthBlock img { display: inline !important; vertical-align: top !important; }
}
```
This is a known workaround for WKWebView image stacking — expected in Wayfair templates.

**Gmail `u + div` hack present** (`u ~ div { min-width: 100vw }`): correct approach for Gmail app clipping prevention.
