# 400+ NEW DEALS ADDED: Up to 60% off + Double Rewards
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-three. At minimum. I've lost the exact count but I know it's somewhere in that range for the Crate & Barrel family since Sunday, and this one's playing the escalation card: the last two were "up to 50% off," and now we're at 60% off plus 400+ new deals added. I see what they're doing — it's the classic re-engagement hook for people who didn't bite the first or fifth or tenth time. I am, regrettably, still reading these, so the hook has some pull.
- The email itself is actually structured reasonably well. The hero is dark and bold — Memorial Day, Double Rewards, the percentage — and there's a product grid below it with furniture that's genuinely relevant to what I'm shopping right now. The "we love these for you" section with what looks like bed frames is either actual personalization or a convincing fake of it, and I'd click it just to find out. The Design Desk module keeps appearing in every C&B send this week, which tells me it's a push, but at this point it reads like wallpaper.
- Would I click? Maybe one product link, specifically whatever's in the personalized section. Would I forward it? No. Would I mark it read? Probably — I've read all twenty-two before it.

## What's working

- The offer escalation is real and visible: 60% off is a concrete step up from the 50% in earlier sends this week, and "400+ new deals added" gives me a reason to look again.
- "Double Rewards" is legible in the hero — for someone who routes purchases through the rewards card, that math actually matters.
- The product grid hits home furnishing categories I'm actively browsing: chairs, dining tables, serveware — nothing wildly off-target.
- The "we love these for you" section with what looks like bedroom furniture is the most personally relevant module in the email; it's doing real personalization work even at thumbnail scale.
- No render issues visible — the layout holds, no broken images, no obvious overlap or junk text spilling through.

## What's weak

- I've now seen The Design Desk module in approximately every C&B email this week — at this cadence it's invisible. Article does a better job surfacing editorial-style content without beating it to death.
- The app download section at the bottom is a distraction from the sale; if I haven't downloaded the C&B app after twenty-three emails, email twenty-three isn't the moment.
- The hero copy leans hard on "up to 60% off" without anchoring it — 60% off what? The lowest-stock items from 2019? CB2 was more surgical about this in their sale blasts; this feels like the umbrella version.
- The cadence itself is the biggest miss: no single email is broken, but running a parallel Crate & Kids version of the same campaign on the same day as this send (which I also got) makes the brand feel like it's spamming me, not targeting me.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse the sibling sends.** If Crate & Barrel and Crate & Kids are running the same Memorial Day event, one coordinated email with a "shop by room / shop for kids" toggle is less abrasive than two near-identical sends in 90 minutes.
- 2. **Make the hero offer specific to a category.** "Up to 60% off dining" or "60% off sofas this weekend" gives me somewhere to land. Pottery Barn's last send was pillow-specific and still annoying, but at least I knew what I was walking into.
- 3. **Anchor the "400+ deals" with one hero product and a real price.** Show me the Marin dining table at $899 down from $1,499 — that's the send that makes me pull the credit card.
- 4. **Let the "we love these for you" section lead, not trail.** If the personalization data is there, open with it. I'm more likely to click the thing that looks like it knows me.
- 5. **Kill the app CTA during a sale.** Swap it for a loyalty-tier meter: "You're X points from [status] — this weekend counts double."

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Bug — conflicting `img` max-width rules (wrong cascade order):**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }  /* declared first */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }  /* declared second — wins at 768px+ */
```
At viewport ≥768 px both rules match; the 640 px rule is declared later and wins by cascade, so `max-width: 640px` applies where 600 px was intended. Swap the declaration order (768 px block last) to correct specificity.

**Accessibility — viewport blocks user zoom:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom. WCAG 1.4.4 requires content to be resizable up to 200%. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Dark mode — explicitly suppressed:**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Forces light rendering in Apple Mail and Samsung Mail dark mode. High-contrast dark environments will fall back to client-forced color inversion, which is unpredictable. Not blocking for delivery, but notable.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags:** Four instances (one multi-value, three single-value). Harmless but produces redundant markup.

**Alt text on images** — cannot verify; requires full HTML.
