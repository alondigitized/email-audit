# Exclusive content from Todd Snyder
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-six on the dot, and CB2 sent two of them on June 12th — this one landed the same day as "SOFAS FOR SUMMER," which I also sat through. That double-tap is the first thing I'm registering before I look at anything else. The only reason I'm opening this instead of archiving it is Todd Snyder's name, because he's primarily a menswear guy and CB2 putting him in a subject line is genuinely unexpected.
- Then I open it and I realize: this is an app promo disguised as a collaboration email. The hero isn't a sofa or a leather chair — it's a smartphone mockup asking me to download the CB2 app so I can tour Todd Snyder's townhouse in some kind of virtual editorial. Meanwhile there's a 50% off banner sitting above it all. So the email is doing three things at once: telling me to download an app, selling me on an editorial townhouse tour, and also — somewhere below the fold — actually showing me the Todd Snyder collection I came here to see.
- Article's "Summer hosting made simple" send from earlier this month did one thing, said it once, and put a table in front of me. This email wishes it were that. I wouldn't click the app download. I might scroll to the product grid. I wouldn't forward this to anyone.

## What's working

- Todd Snyder is Millennial menswear shorthand for "elevated without trying too hard" — seeing his name on a CB2 collaboration is a real hook for someone like me who buys furniture the same way he thinks about a sport coat
- The product grid below the hero earns its place: warm leathers, clean silhouettes, neutral palette — exactly the tone I'm trying to hit in the second home living room
- "NOT SOLD OUT, YET" is the best copy in this entire email by a wide margin — playful, scarcity-aware, and doesn't feel desperate the way the June 13th "ALMOST OVER" phrasing did
- The 50% off banner is above the fold before any scrolling; Pottery Barn buried their 70% off language under three paragraphs of "farmhouse style" in their June 12th send, so this is at least better than that

## What's weak

- "Exclusive content" as a subject line is what I expect from a Substack or a Patreon creator — not a furniture brand pushing a collaboration with a designer I actually like; the subject actively undersells what's inside
- The primary CTA is "DOWNLOAD THE SHOP" — I am renovating two properties simultaneously and I have thirty browser tabs open; asking me to download an app as the first conversion step is a full stop
- CB2 sent two emails on June 12th; one of these should have been held for June 14th when my inbox was less noisy
- The email has three separate jobs — app promo, editorial lifestyle, and collection sale — and genuinely commits to none of them; Article doesn't do this to me, and that's part of why Article scored higher
- No shipping or lead time info anywhere visible — when I'm coordinating a renovation schedule across two addresses, "ships in 4-6 weeks" is a real purchase signal and it's not here

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the app download as the primary CTA entirely.** Link the hero image directly to the Todd Snyder collection landing page. Save the app pitch for the footer where it belongs — or make it a P.S., not the whole point.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject line to lead with the collaboration name and the sale.** "Exclusive content" sounds like I'm being upsold on a membership tier, not offered furniture at half price.
- 3. **Split the email into two sends.** The editorial/app townhouse tour and the 50% off collection sale are fighting each other for real estate. One could be a Monday brand send; the other is a Wednesday sale send. Sending both in one email on the same day CB2 already hit me with "SOFAS FOR SUMMER" is how you train subscribers to ignore you.
- 4. **Move "NOT SOLD OUT, YET" logic upstream.** That phrase is strong enough to live in the subject line or preheader. It's doing zero work buried halfway down the email.
- 5. **Add a delivery window to the product grid.** One line per item: "ships in 3-5 weeks." For a renovation buyer, that closes faster than any percentage off.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** prevents users from pinching to zoom on mobile. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
  ```html
  <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
  ```

- **Global text-size-adjust disabled** — the universal selector rule below silences OS-level font scaling across all elements, compounding the zoom block above:
  ```css
  *{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
  ```

- **Non-standard mobile breakpoint** — `max-width: 418px` targets a narrow window (iPhone SE-sized). Standard practice is 480px or 600px. Layouts may break on Android devices and older iPhones between 419–479px.

- **Cross-domain font load** — `HNW05` typeface loads from `www.crateandbarrel.com`, a sibling brand's domain, not `cb2.com`. If that domain has downtime or changes its CORS policy, font rendering silently degrades in supported clients.
  ```css
  src: url("https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/1e07d600-....woff2")
  ```

- **Double-slash font URLs** — three `@font-face` declarations under `cb2.com` contain a double-slash path segment, which causes an unnecessary redirect before the font is served:
  ```css
  src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
  ```

- **Alt text on images** — cannot confirm; source is truncated before `<img>` tags appear.
