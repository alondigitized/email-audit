# Our top Memorial Day picks (up to 60% off!)
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- Twelve. I hit twelve Pottery Barn emails in roughly seventy-two hours this week, and this one landed on top of yesterday's "EXTRA 20% off select styles" send without even a day's buffer. The email itself is one of the better-structured ones in the blitz — I'll give it that. The category tile layout actually does something useful: separate percentage-off callouts for Furniture (50%), Sofas & Chairs (25%), Bedding (40%), Home & Decor (60%), and Outdoor (40%) gives me something real to parse instead of a single vague "up to X%" hero. When you're mid-renovation on two properties simultaneously, "50% off furniture" and "40% off outdoor" are genuinely relevant numbers that I would normally act on. The "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" grid below the fold is the first personalization signal Pottery Barn has landed in this entire multi-day stretch, and it had product tiles that looked like they'd been pulled from my browsing behavior — outdoor seating, accent chairs, area rugs.
- But cadence is context, and by send twelve, even a well-executed email reads as noise. Wayfair hit me with their low-pile rugs send last week and I clicked through in under thirty seconds. Crate & Barrel's "$1.95 tabletop" send slowed me down long enough to actually read. This one? I recognized the red banner before I consciously read the subject, which means Pottery Barn has trained me to pattern-match and archive. The "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" personalization came too late — I was already thumbing past it.
- Mark as read. I'll revisit the category tile breakdown later when I'm actually in purchase mode, but I'm not clicking today.

## What's working

- The five-category tile grid with distinct, specific percentages-off is the clearest offer hierarchy PB has sent all week — my eye went straight to the Furniture and Outdoor tiles, which are exactly where I am right now.
- "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" personalizes the send in a way the subject line doesn't; the outdoor seating and accent chair tiles actually matched what I've been looking at.
- Affirm's "Buy Now, Pay Over Time" at the bottom is smart for a dual-renovation buyer who's managing cash flow across two properties — it's the only payment flexibility signal I've seen in this blitz.
- No render bugs; the layout is clean, images load, and the red CTA buttons are unambiguous on the page.
- Loyalty rewards section is visible and not buried — the rewards program call-out is at least surface-level present.

## What's weak

- Cadence has completely eroded trust in the word "top" — after twelve emails in three days, nothing feels curated, it feels desperate.
- The "Extra 20% off" is followed immediately by "select styles," which is the kind of qualifier that makes me wonder if every item I want is excluded; Crate & Barrel's "$1.95 tabletop" send last week put a real price in the subject and never had to walk it back.
- No specific end date or countdown for Memorial Day — "up to 60% off" without a hard deadline doesn't create urgency for someone who's learned to wait for the next email fourteen hours later.
- The subject "Our top Memorial Day picks" is generic and brand-centric; it reads like it's talking to everyone, not to someone who's been tagged as actively shopping outdoor furniture.
- "SHOP NOW" CTAs on every category tile is lazy button copy — Wayfair's targeted category emails at least label the button with what you're shopping.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Throttle the cadence immediately.** One email per day maximum during a sale event. The personalization work in this email is good and would have landed if it weren't the twelfth send in three days. This is the single highest-leverage change — better cadence makes every other fix matter.
- 2. **Move "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" above the category tile grid**, or make it the hero. The algorithm clearly knows I've been browsing outdoor seating; lead with that instead of a generic banner everyone gets.
- 3. **Give the Extra 20% off a hard deadline** — "through Monday, May 26" costs one line of copy and converts the urgency from implied to real.
- 4. **Sharpen the CTA copy on category tiles** — "Shop Outdoor Deals" beats "SHOP NOW" every time for someone scanning quickly on a phone.
- 5. **Rewrite the subject to lead with the strongest specific discount** — 60% off Home & Decor is the most aggressive number in the email and it's buried in a tile.

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Memorial Day Picks" (2026-05-19)

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard-broken CTAs (403) and a PHP code fragment embedded in a `<style>` block are the highest-severity defects. Accessibility coverage is near-zero: 38 of approximately 40 content images carry no `alt` text, meaning images-blocked rendering produces a blank email.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Credit card apply banner — HTTP 403**
`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember&...`
The KeyCC_Banner CTA is fully non-functional. Any recipient clicking "Apply & Buy" hits a 403.

**[FAIL] West Elm spacer asset — HTTP 403**
`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
Cross-brand asset from a WSI sibling domain embedded in a Pottery Barn email. The server is returning 403, and the URL is plain HTTP (see §3). Layout spacer will fail to load.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP code injected into a CSS `<style>` block**
The final `<style>` block in `<head>` contains raw PHP header-emission calls:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
This is server-side PHP accidentally pasted into client-side CSS. It is syntactically invalid CSS. Most clients silently discard it, but it is a template assembly defect that should be removed.

**38 images missing `alt` text**
Every content slice (`slices_01a.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), all product images, the footer credit card banner, and both open-pixel beacons carry no `alt` attribute. With images blocked (common in corporate/Outlook environments), the email renders as empty white space. Affected examples:
- `https://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2026/US/S26_Sale_MemorialDay_ZigZag2/slices_01a.jpg` — no `alt`
- `https://www.potterybarn.com/pbimgs/ab/images/dp/wcm/202606/0011/farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg` — no `alt`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` — no `alt`

**Three non-HTTPS image sources**
Will be blocked by Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook with default security settings:
1. `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
2. `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
3. `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

**Open-pixel beacon is plain HTTP**
`http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
Open-tracking pixel uses HTTP, meaning open-rate data will be under-counted in clients that block mixed content.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents user-initiated zoom on iOS, a WCAG 1.4.4 failure for low-vision users.

**Global text-size-adjust suppression**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}
```
Applied universally; removes OS-level font scaling for all elements.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` — no impact on rendering but degrades screen-reader context.

Required for bulk senders under Google and Yahoo 2024 sender policy. One-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058 `List-Unsubscribe-Post`) is also unconfirmed.

**Authentication-Results header absent**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from the captured headers via the AgentMail relay. The sending domain `e.potterybarn.com` should have a DMARC policy aligned with the `From:` domain; this cannot be verified from the current capture.

**CAN-SPAM physical address**
