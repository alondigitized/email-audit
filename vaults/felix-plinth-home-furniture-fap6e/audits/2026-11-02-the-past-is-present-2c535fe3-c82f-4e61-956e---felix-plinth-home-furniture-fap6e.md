# The past is present
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-02**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2 doing what CB2 does best, and I mostly liked it. The midcentury-design editorial is much better than the discount shouting I've had from them all week. "In Our Midcentury Era" opens a run of products with designer credits: the Loop Chair (Andreas Hansen, 1968), the Symmetric Chair, the Tule Console Table, the Diana Bed, the Evora Lamp and the Klub Chair. It reads like a catalog, and I save catalog-style emails to my inspiration folder.
- It's still the Warehouse Sale on top, though. That's the fifth send in a row to carry it, after "Final days of the Warehouse Sale" (11/01), "THIS WEEK'S STEALS" (10/31) and "THIS IS YOUR CHANCE" (10/30). The Guest Ready Sale is stuck at the bottom again. The editorial is a nice change, but nothing here is new news.
- I'd open this and scroll the whole thing. I'd probably click the Diana Bed or the console table, and I'd save the email to my folder. I wouldn't forward it. Nothing in it is a deal or a "you have to see this" moment.

## What's working

- The designer attributions and dates ("Designed by Andreas Hansen," "first designed in 1968") give me a reason to care beyond price. That's the Pottery Barn and Crate & Barrel gap: their emails are all percent-off, no story.
- The hero photography is huge and confident. The midcentury living room with the pool makes me want to renovate.
- The layout is clean, one product per band with a lot of white space. It's easy to scan on a phone, and Wayfair's tile grids can't touch it.
- The "Final Weekend / Warehouse Sale / up to 60% off" strip at the top puts the offer first without drowning the editorial.
- "More For You" at the bottom gives me real product and price points: the Foley Mink Velvet Dining Armchair at $139, the Viggo 84" dining table at $1,999.

## What's weak

- The sale banner is the same one I've had five days running. I'm in the same CB2 family snooze I get from Crate & Barrel, and that exact weariness is what makes me stop reading.
- Most of the editorial products show no prices and no per-product link. I can't tell whether the Loop Chair is in the sale or full price. Crate & Barrel's Axis sofa email at least gave me a clear pitch.
- Nothing here helps with the swatch and sample question or with delivery windows. I'm furnishing two houses, and CB2's own "order 10 free swatches" email on 10/31 was far more useful.
- The email is very long, with at least six full-bleed product bands before the "Shop the Collection" button. A button is hidden below the Klub Chair, and the small tap targets in the footer are tiny.
- The footer has no rewards math. I buy on my highest-rewards card, so seeing the Rewards 10% back, app and Crate & Barrel cross-sell in the fine-print area is no use to me. The legal text is tiny and I'd never read it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price and a "Shop" link under every editorial product, flagged "Warehouse Sale" where it applies. Right now the beautiful part of this email is a dead end.
- 2. Cut the repeated sale strip down to one line and let the editorial lead. Keep the sale to the bottom module.
- 3. Add one line on swatches or delivery ("Ships in 2 to 3 weeks, free swatches on custom upholstery"). That's what makes me commit on furniture.
- 4. Trim to four products. The email is long enough that the two final products get no attention.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email has one confirmed rendering defect: a raw merge token, `%%reviews_skulist%%`, was left unresolved. It also has many images with no alt text and 11 non-HTTPS third-party pixels. Header-based compliance and authentication checks couldn't be run because the relay didn't capture those headers. The QA pass rate was 43%.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** 70 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are click-redirect domains. Their destinations, status codes and redirect chains are unverified, so none are confirmed broken or working.
- **Non-HTTPS tracking pixels:** 11 `<img>` pixels use `http://`, which clients and image proxies may block or rewrite:
  - `http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?dtm_com=2&dtm_cid=2437&...`
  - `http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2437/pub1.php` through `pub10.php?d98e199bf5027afea04b790655b3dee2=1`
- **Third-party pixels:** These look like ad-network cookie-sync calls (Dotomi). They add 11 external requests per open, and a blocked request will not fire.
- **First-party pixels:** `https://mi.cb2.com/p/up/09aebc5b21344cf7/o.gif?mi_u=...` and `https://dv.cb2.com/o/8c7974a3-...?mi_cid=...` are HTTPS and work as expected.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe:** The QA tool did not find this header. It warns that AgentMail may not relay it, so its absence isn't confirmed on the sender side.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** Also not found, with the same caveat. One-click unsubscribe is unverified, and Gmail and Yahoo require it for bulk senders.
- **Authentication-Results:** Not found. The QA tool expects this from the relay, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment for `mail.cb2.com` are unknown.
- **Body unsubscribe link and physical address:** The truncated source doesn't show a footer unsubscribe link or postal address, so neither can be confirmed or ruled out.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not verifiable. The 70 tracking links were not probed, and no destination URLs or UTM parameters appear in the truncated source or QA output. All visible links appear to go through ESP click-wrapping, so UTM parameters would only show up in the final destination after resolving the redirects.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Fix `%%reviews_skulist%%`.** Check the SFMC data extension or AMPscript for that block, and add a fallback or suppress the block when the value is empty. Run a seed-list render test before send.
2. **Add alt text to the 10 content images.** Use descriptive text for product or content images, and `alt=""` for the tracking pixels.
3. **Move the Dotomi pixels to HTTPS or remove them.** Some of these calls are likely dead on blocking clients anyway, and 11 third-party requests per open is a deliverability and privacy cost.
4. **Remove `maximum-scale=1`** from the viewport meta tag.
5. **Verify the headers on the raw message.** Check `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and SPF, DKIM and DMARC on the original `.eml` rather than the AgentMail relay copy. Also confirm the footer unsubscribe link and postal address in the full HTML.
6. **Resolve the 70 tracking links** with a redirect-following probe. Confirm they return 200, land on `cb2.com`, and carry consistent UTM parameters.
