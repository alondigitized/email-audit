## What's working

- The first tile, "Low-profile Rugs You'll Love," does deliver on the subject line. A flat-weave rug on a wood floor is a believable product shot.
- The dog bed block ("pup approved") is a real editorial moment and is the best-looking section. It's off-topic for me, but it's the only part with a point of view.
- The Christmas tree and seasonal decor tiles carry a clear "up to 40% off" badge. That's a concrete number I can see at a glance.
- The star ratings on the "fresh-picked favorites" grid are a nice bit of social proof.
- The rewards and financing icons are a trust cue. I know the Wayfair Rewards and card setup.

## What's weak

- The subject promised rugs and the email is mostly pergolas, dog beds and shower curtains. That's a bait-and-switch.
- It's the fourth pergola push in a week: "New Pergolas for YOU" on 11/01, "Gazebos & pergolas from $100" on 11/02, "Pergolas for less" on 11/05, "New pergolas available just for you" on 11/06, and now two pergola tiles plus a four-pergola grid. I own no pergola, and I've shown no interest.
- The "7% back" credit card block takes prime space. I already pay with my highest-rewards card, so it's noise to me.
- The 70% off flash sale is at the bottom with a tiny "Shop Now" button. A tight deadline is wasted at that depth.
- The product tiles have no prices and no named products. I can't tell what's on sale, so I have no reason to click.
- There's no single CTA. It's about 20 tiles, and none is dominant.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the email about rugs, since that's what the subject promised. Use a hero rug image with a clear "Shop low-profile rugs" button and 4-6 rug tiles with prices. Move the flash sale banner up top.
- 2. Cut the pergolas. Wayfair has hammered them for a week, and they should be suppressed for anyone who didn't click the first few.
- 3. Put prices and discount badges on the product tiles, as the 40% tree tile does. Pottery Barn's "Open Box Outlet" email at least had a real hook.
- 4. Move the credit card ad lower, or drop it.
- 5. Cut the tile count to 8-10. Article's "Real homes. Really good storage." was one theme done well.
