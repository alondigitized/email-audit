# BACK IN STOCK
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- Fourteen. This is email fourteen from CB2 since July 1st, and I know because I started counting around email ten when it stopped feeling like a coincidence and started feeling like a strategy. The good news: "BACK IN STOCK" as a subject line is a genuine gear-shift from the wall of urgency prose I've been wading through. Yesterday CB2 was screaming "TWO SALES END TONIGHT" — today it's telling me something specific came back. That's a smarter hook.
- The bad news: I open it and the hero says "SEIZE the Moment." Nothing about what's back in stock, no specific product called out, no "that bench you clicked three times finally replenished." The email pivots immediately into an editorial layout — bedroom vignette, sectionals section, a big lifestyle room shot, and what looks like a $5,000 refresh promotion buried mid-scroll. The subject made a promise the body never honored.
- I'd scan it, click once on the sectionals section because I'm genuinely mid-sofa-hunt for the second property, and then close it. I wouldn't forward it. I wouldn't open the next CB2 email with anything but skepticism.

## What's working

- **Subject line is a real pattern break.** After twelve straight sale-countdown subjects, "BACK IN STOCK" actually signals something different — scarcity over discount. That's the one thing that would get me to open past the fatigue.
- **Bedroom and sectionals as lead categories is perfectly targeted.** I have two properties to furnish and these are literally active line items on my shopping list right now.
- **The editorial layout earns some trust.** Large lifestyle photography, clear section breaks — it reads more like a catalog than a coupon, which is how I actually make furniture decisions.
- **$5,000 refresh offer (if it's a real sweep or credit) is the right scale.** Home furnishing purchases aren't $50 decisions. Showing a meaningful number at least acknowledges that.

## What's weak

- **"SEIZE the Moment" vs. "BACK IN STOCK" is a broken promise.** The subject says inventory news; the hero delivers brand poetry. Those are two different emails.
- **No specific item is ever called out as "back in stock."** If the Calypso dining table I've been watching finally restocked, I'd click instantly. Generic photography tells me nothing.
- **The $5,000 offer is visually buried and unclear.** Is it a sweepstakes? A bundle? A store credit threshold? Can't tell without zooming in. Article's "ends today" last week was cleaner — one offer, explained, done.
- **Fourteen emails in eight days.** Pottery Barn managed a full editorial pivot to heritage design to break its own sale fatigue. CB2 is still blasting cadence without that kind of strategic relief valve.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name the product in the subject.** "BACK IN STOCK: The Calypso Table" or "BACK IN STOCK: 3 Sectionals You Saved" beats generic every time. Make the subject the news, not a tease.
- 2. **Align hero copy to the subject.** If the promise is back-in-stock, the hero image should show a specific item with a "Restocked" badge — not a lifestyle vignette.
- 3. **Surface the $5K offer earlier and explain it in one sentence.** If it's a giveaway, say "Enter to win." If it's a bundle, say so. Ambiguity costs clicks.
- 4. **Cut email frequency, or segment by browse/save history.** Wayfair's bath mat debacle last week proved that untargeted volume actively destroys trust. I'd accept half as many CB2 emails if they were sourced from my behavior.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta — WCAG 1.4.4 violation**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents low-vision users from pinching to zoom on mobile. Remove `maximum-scale=1` or replace with `maximum-scale=5`.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on universal selector**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applied to every element rather than just `body`. Overrides user browser accessibility settings globally.

**Duplicate `@font-face` declarations for "Lineto Brown Sub"** — three separate `@font-face` blocks use the same `font-family` name with overlapping `font-weight` ranges, creating unpredictable font resolution across clients:
- Block 1: no weight specified (eot + woff2 + woff sources)
- Block 2: `font-weight: 400`
- Block 3: `font-weight: 600`

**Malformed double-slash font URLs**
```css
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2")
```
`//` in the path (after domain, not as protocol-relative) is technically valid via HTTP redirect normalization, but can produce 301s in strict CDN configurations and adds unnecessary round-trips. Four affected URLs.

**Conflicting desktop image `max-width` rules**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 100% !important } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 690px) { img { max-width: 690px } }
```
At ≥768px both rules apply; the `!important` on 100% overrides the 690px cap, making the 690px rule a dead rule for viewports ≥768px. Consolidate into a single breakpoint.

**Non-standard mobile breakpoint**: `max-width: 418px` is unusual (vs. industry-standard 480px). Will not activate on devices with 420px–480px viewport widths (e.g., iPhone SE in landscape), leaving those users without mobile-optimized layout.

**Cross-brand font CDN dependency**: HNW05 webfonts are loaded from `crateandbarrel.com`, not `cb2.com`:
```css
src: url("https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/1e07d600-3787-4517-bd3d-b140f9e54ec2.woff2")
```
CB2 email rendering depends on Crate & Barrel's CDN availability. Any Crate & Barrel CDN incident or CORS policy change breaks CB2 email typography.
