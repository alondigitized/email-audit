# It's on! Black Friday deals & free shipping inside
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-22**
## Executive summary

- Kids again. This is the sixth Crate & Barrel Kids send in about three days, and I still don't have a nursery. I'm renovating two houses and shopping sofas, lighting and rugs. I don't need a crib, a toddler chair or a Nuna stroller. The hero is clean: "Black Friday is on! Up to 50% off + free shipping" over a festive kid-room photo with a Shop the Sale button. The grid under it is a wall of nurseries and stockings.
- The one thing that got my attention was the rug tile, "up to 50% off bestselling rugs". I would normally open the main-brand email for that. Here it's buried at the bottom of a kids grid. I'd skim it, not click, and mark it read. I wouldn't forward it to anyone. I'd only forward it to a friend with a baby.

## What's working

- The hero states the offer plainly: "up to 50% off + free shipping" with a visible Shop the Sale button.
- Each tile has its own discount (30%, 50%, 20%), so I can see the range at a glance.
- "Ships Free" labels on the bedding and gear tiles answer the shipping question up front. That matters for bulky furniture.
- The Crate Card block ("earn Reward Dollars") speaks to my rewards-card habit, even though it's far down the email.
- Photography is warm and styled, and it reads as the Crate & Barrel look I recognize.

## What's weak

- The sender is "Crate & Barrel Kids SALE", and I'm not the kids audience. This is the same Kids creative I've been getting since 11/20.
- It repeats the 11/22 "Black Friday is on! Shop our deals now →" send almost word for word. Same promo, a slightly different subject.
- The rugs tile is the only thing relevant to me, and it's tucked into a six-tile grid.
- The offer is "up to" everywhere, so I can't tell what's actually discounted. The fine print at the bottom is a dense wall of text.
- It's very long. The app-download pitch, the TheDesignDesk block and the store locator all come after the product grid. Several competing CTAs dilute the offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment by household. Send me main-brand home and furniture, not Kids. That one change would lift this more than anything else.
- 2. If Kids has to go out, lead with the best-selling item and show the actual sale price, not "up to".
- 3. Cut the lower third down to one CTA. The app, Crate Card, TheDesignDesk and store locator blocks all compete.
- 4. Add a rewards line near the top, for example "2x Reward Dollars today".

- **Subject Alt B:** `Nursery & playroom Black Friday, ships free`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email uses a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional layout from the Marigold/MI template ("V2"). It has no hard failures (0 issues, 3 warnings, 57% pass rate). The main findings are about 30 images flagged as missing alt text, a viewport tag that blocks zoom, and template leftovers in the head. Compliance headers could not be verified because the relay may have stripped them.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **78 tracking links were not probed.** They are on click-redirect domains, so destination status, redirect chains and UTM preservation are unverified. Don't read this as a pass.
- **Duplicate tracking beacons.**
  - `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png?mi_u=…` appears 6 times.
  - `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=…&n=1` through `&n=5` appear 5 times. That is a LiveRamp (rlcdn) pixel, and it fires once per n value.
  - Add the `o.gif` open pixel and the `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/102ac91d-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…` pixel, and the email loads at least 13 tracking images. This adds request weight, and it makes open-rate data noisy because the same open is counted multiple times.
- **A subscriber identifier is passed to a third party.** The `s=` value in the rlcdn URLs (`56aaa2787a6773d0f164733102dce5192f75df7c634bb941bb640686964…`) matches the `mi_u=` value in the first-party pixels. The same hashed ID is sent to a third-party data broker. Confirm this is covered by the privacy disclosure.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on about 33 `<img>` instances.** The QA check cannot tell a missing `alt` from an empty `alt=""`, so confirm against the full source.
  - Content images with no accessible name:
    - `…/m/47/bbf6f9d4-86cc-437a-aa80-391067b74306.png`
    - the scene7 hero `2026_1122_CBK_BF_DealsGrid_REMIX_Hero…`
    - the scene7 `2026_1105_CBK_PersonalizedGiftShop_Seconda…` banner
    - about 17 product or tile images at `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/*.jpg` and `.gif`
  - The hero and the deals grid are probably carrying the offer in image text. With images blocked, the offer, the CTAs and any promo terms would be invisible.
  - Tracking pixels and spacers (`25_MI_Bottom_Spacer_40px_White`, rlcdn, `mi.` and `dv.` pixels, `o.gif`) should have `alt=""` plus `width="1" height="1"`. Missing `alt` makes some screen readers read out the filename.
- **`<meta name="viewport" … maximum-scale=1>`** blocks pinch-zoom on iOS and fails WCAG 1.4.4. The CSS also sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none`, which blocks text resizing in WebKit.
- **Invalid markup for the declared doctype.**
  - `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` has an unescaped `&`, which is invalid under XHTML 1.0.
  - The head has a duplicate `<meta charset>` next to `http-equiv Content-Type`.
  - The head has three `format-detection` metas, one combined and two separate.
  - The title is generic and does not match the subject line.
- **Template leftovers in production.** The source has `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->`, an empty `<!---->`, and an empty `<!--[if !mso]><!--> <!--<![endif]-->` block. These are harmless to rendering but show an unreviewed template.
- **No issues found** for `lang="en"`, the VML/Office namespaces, the MSO `OfficeDocumentSettings` block, or the mobile breakpoint CSS. The source was truncated, so the body markup is not covered.

## 3b. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The visible source has no unresolved tokens such as `%%…%%`, `{{…}}` or `[FIRSTNAME]`. The part of the body after the truncation was not audited.

## 4. Compliance
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found.** The QA tool notes the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is unconfirmed. If the headers really are missing, Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require one-click unsubscribe.
- **`Authentication-Results` was not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown from this data. The sender is `mail.crateandbarrel.com`, a subdomain, so check alignment against the raw message.
- **The in-body unsubscribe link and the physical postal address** are not in the truncated source, so they cannot be confirmed.

## 5. Email-to-Site Continuity
Not assessable from this data. Click-through domains were skipped, and no destination URLs or UTM parameters appear in the truncated source. The email carries `mi_cid` and `mi_mid` on its pixels, which suggests the redirects tag campaign and message IDs. Whether those become UTMs on the landing page is unverified.

## 6. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to the hero, the deals grid and the product tiles. Add `alt=""` with explicit 1×1 dimensions to every pixel and spacer.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport tag. Replace `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` with `100%`.
3. Cut the duplicate pixels to one `mi.` pixel and one rlcdn call. Review whether the rlcdn pixel and its shared hashed ID are disclosed.
4. Escape `&` as `&amp;` in the title, and use a title that matches the subject. Remove the duplicate metas and the placeholder comments.
5. Check the raw message headers for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment. If the relay is stripping them, capture the headers upstream so the audit stops flagging false positives.
6. Re-run the audit with the full HTML and a redirect-following link probe, so that UTMs, landing-page status and the footer unsubscribe link and address can be checked.
