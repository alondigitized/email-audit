# The Warehouse Sale is selling fast
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-seven. That's my rough CB2 / Crate family count since mid-May, and this one lands on June 23rd — the day after "The best entryways from our saves" and four days into a streak that includes two sends on June 20 alone. I registered all of that before I even looked at the body. And yet — the Warehouse Sale with UP TO 60% OFF is the kind of number that actually cuts through when you're actively sourcing furniture for two houses simultaneously. The warm terracotta bedroom scene in the hero felt right for where my head is: deep in fixture decisions, trying to make spaces feel lived-in rather than staged.
- That said, I've played the "up to X% off" game too many times to take the 60% at face value. Pottery Barn just ran "Big news! Up to 70% off" a few days back and the items I actually wanted were 25% off at best. Same song, different catalog. What saves this one is the category breakdown underneath — discrete LIGHTING and RUGS rows that let me navigate toward what I actually need instead of dumping me into a warehouse of 50,000 SKUs the way Wayfair's "AREA RUGS." blast does.
- I'd click — but directly into the LIGHTING section, not the "SHOP THE SALE" catch-all. I wouldn't forward this to anyone. This is me, my Amex, and a pending decision on dining pendants for the primary house.

## What's working

- **60% off in big type, top of fold** — no hunting for the number; it's the first thing my eye lands on after the CB2 logo.
- **Category navigation rows (LIGHTING, RUGS)** — this is where CB2 beats a Wayfair firehose; I can self-select into what I'm actually shopping without scrolling endlessly.
- **Earthy warm palette on the bedroom hero** — the terracotta and dark canopy bed read contemporary without screaming trend-of-the-month; it's consistent with how I'd actually style a guest room.
- **"SHOP THE SALE" button is unambiguous** — one button, one job, clear copy.
- **"MORE FOR YOU" personalized grid** — in principle this is the right move; if those SKUs actually match my browse history, that's the section I'd spend the most time in.

## What's weak

- **"Up to 60% off" is doing bait-and-switch work** — every savvy shopper in this space knows "up to" means the markdown floor is probably 20–25%, and the 60% item is a $49 candleholder. Pottery Barn ran "up to 70% off" days ago and I learned my lesson.
- **Four CB2 emails in four days** — the June 20 double-send already strained goodwill; landing another one on the 23rd means I'm primed to skim rather than explore.
- **"LEAVE A REVIEW" section is dead weight** — mid-email, in a sale send, this is a momentum-killer. That real estate should be a second category row or a single hero product with the actual sale price shown.
- **Hero bedroom scene doesn't show a price** — the most powerful visual in the email has no anchor price attached to it; showing even one marked-down item in the hero would convert "nice room" into "I want that."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill "LEAVE A REVIEW," replace with a time-limited offer block** — a counter ("48 hours left on Warehouse prices") or a category CTA ("Lighting: up to 60% off — shop now") would do more than a review solicitation in a sale send.
- 2. **Put one hero product with its marked-down price in the bedroom scene** — "Dune Canopy Bed, was $2,199 / now $1,099" anchors the 60% claim and makes it feel real, not bait.
- 3. **Add an explicit end-date** — "selling fast" is filler; "sale ends Sunday" is a reason to click today instead of saving the tab and forgetting it.
- 4. **The "MORE FOR YOU" grid needs to earn its scroll** — if those items aren't calibrated to my actual browse/wishlist, they're dead pixels. Show me what I've been looking at, not what's leftover in the warehouse.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Felix, your saved items are on sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ends Sunday — shop before the best pieces go.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Up to 60% off lighting, rugs, and furniture — curated, not clearance.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta tag**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile, violating WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Users with low vision cannot magnify content.

**Global `text-size-adjust: none` on `*` selector**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Overrides OS-level text scaling for all elements. This is acceptable on `body` for layout control but applying it to `*` suppresses user accessibility preferences more aggressively than needed.

**Mobile breakpoint/width mismatch**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) {
  .mobile { width:414px !important; }
```
The media query fires at ≤418px but forces 414px width. On a 415–418px viewport this forces horizontal overflow.

**Cross-domain font dependency on crateandbarrel.com**
```css
@font-face { font-family:"HNW05";
  src: url("https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/1e07d600-3787-4517-bd3d-b140f9e54ec2.woff2")
```
Four `@font-face` declarations pull from `crateandbarrel.com` instead of `cb2.com`. If Crate & Barrel rotates or removes these assets, the font silently falls back — undetectable in CB2's own monitoring.

**Double-slash paths in cb2.com font URLs**
```css
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
```
Four occurrences. Technically resolves via server normalization but is non-canonical and will fail on strict CDN configs or if path normalization is ever removed.
