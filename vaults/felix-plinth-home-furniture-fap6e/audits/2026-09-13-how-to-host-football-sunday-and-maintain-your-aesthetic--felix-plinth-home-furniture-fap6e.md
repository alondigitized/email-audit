# How to host football Sunday and maintain your aesthetic
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-13**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is Crate & Barrel doubling down on the NFL angle for the second day in a row — yesterday it was "Text everyone to come over for kickoff" and I scored that a 5. This one's basically the same playbook: "your house, your Sunday," a bunch of NFL-licensed serveware, and a wine key with a helmet logo, but with more scroll and more repetition of the same three product shots restyled six times. I get it, they found an angle that isn't the fifth Crate & Kids toddler furniture email, and I'll credit that. But by the time I hit "cook like a pro" and then "how to host like a true fan" using nearly identical boards, cheese knives, and charcuterie photography, my eye glazes over.
- The one thing that actually stopped my scroll was "our latest collab is coming... shop first, only in app on September 15th" — that's real urgency and it's app-exclusive, which at least feels like a reason to open the app instead of just clicking through email. Also "TheDesignDesk... $5000" giveaway is a swing at the exact thing I'm mid-renovation for — free design help — but it's buried two-thirds down the email after I've already scrolled past four NFL merch blocks.
- Would I click? Maybe the "fall gathering guide" module, since that's actual hosting/tablescape content and not another cutting board. Forward to a friend? No — this is too long and too repetitive for me to bother. Mark as read and move on.

## What's working

- The NFL hosting hook is a genuinely different lane than the endless kids/holiday sends this week — Crate & Kids fatigue is real and this at least isn't that.
- "TheDesignDesk" $5000 sweepstakes and "book your free appointment" speaks directly to me — I'm renovating two houses right now, free design consults are exactly my thing.
- The app-exclusive drop teaser ("shop first, only in app, September 15") has real specificity and a real deadline, unlike Article's vague "not over yet" messaging I saw this week.
- Product photography is consistently on-brand — moody, styled, not stock-photo cheap like some of the CB2 sends I've seen.

## What's weak

- This email is exhaustingly long — by my count there are at least 8 separate promo modules before you even hit the footer, and three of them (cutting boards, wine glasses, cheese knives) are basically the same shelf restyled.
- Zero pricing or discount anywhere in the whole email — no %, no $ off, just "shop now" over and over. Pottery Barn's holiday preview at least led with delivery-speed value; this leads with nothing.
- The "we love these for you" personalization block at the bottom is generic barware/glassware, not tied to my actual renovation lists at Pottery Barn/Crate/Wayfair — feels like a template block, not real segmentation.
- Two competing CTAs fight for my attention near the bottom (the app download push and TheDesignDesk appointment) — no clear hierarchy on which one they actually want me to take.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this email in half — merge the three NFL merch modules into one carousel instead of three near-duplicate sections. Repetition is killing momentum, same problem CB2 had with its countdown-clock gauntlet.
- 2. Move TheDesignDesk $5000 giveaway up near the top — that's the strongest hook for a renovation-mode shopper like me, not something buried after four rounds of cutting boards.
- 3. Put an actual price or discount in the hero — "your house, your Sunday" with zero offer is a missed chance when Wayfair's closet-systems send at least anchored on "$59."
- 4. Tighten the app-exclusive collab tease into its own standalone send instead of one module in a wall of content — it deserves its own subject line and urgency.
