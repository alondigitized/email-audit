# Our new lookbook has arrived
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-25**
## Executive summary

- Forty-two. That's the Pottery Barn count in my July inbox as of this one, and at this point I genuinely think I should just start a spreadsheet — oh wait, I basically already have, it's called scoring these emails. This one's "The Fall Lookbook" — rustic lodge, heritage autumn, indigo blues, plaid everywhere, a guy in a flannel-adjacent living room drinking something warm probably. It's pretty. It's also July 25th and they're selling me fall in the middle of summer sale season, which is its own kind of whiplash after yesterday's "up to 70% off" blast.
- The "Recommended Just For You" block is the one thing that actually stopped my scroll — a curtain rod, bath towels, a swivel chair, a rug — because that's real personalization, not just moodboard eye candy. But the overall email reads like a catalog PDF got chopped into sections, not something built to get me to buy a specific thing today.
- Would I click? Maybe into "Recommended Just For You" out of curiosity, but I'm not buying a whole rustic lodge aesthetic off one email. Wouldn't forward it — nothing here is showroom-specific or swatch-related enough for the second home to text my contractor about. Mark as read and move on, same as most of this month's forty-plus sends.

## What's working

- The hero image is genuinely gorgeous — that lodge living room with the leather sofa and layered plaid throws is the kind of shot that makes me want to save it to my inbox folder, even if I don't act on it.
- "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" is real personalization — curtain rods, towels, a rug, a linen duvet — actual products, not just vibes, and it's the section I'd actually click through.
- The three-room breakdown (Rustic Lodge / Heritage Autumn / Indigo Blues) gives me a clear way to file this mentally even if I don't shop it — smart way to organize a big seasonal drop.
- Loyalty content is there at the bottom — 10% back in rewards via the Pottery Barn credit card and Affirm buy-now-pay-later — which at least acknowledges I might be financing a renovation, not just browsing throw pillows.

## What's weak

- There's no actual offer up top. "THE FALL LOOKBOOK / GET INSPIRED" is inspiration copy, not a deal — compare that to yesterday's Pottery Barn "up to 70% off" subject, which at least led with a number.
- It's a lookbook in July. CB2 and Article are still running current-season sends; this one feels like it jumped the gun on fall by about six weeks.
- Way too many "SHOP THE LOOK" CTAs stacked on top of each other with zero differentiation — by the third one my eye just glazes past them.
- Nothing here talks to a renovation-scale buyer — no swatch/sample program, no room-planning tool, no "shop by room size" — which is exactly the gap Crate & Barrel's coffee table guide filled better for me two weeks ago.
- This is at least the third rustic/heritage/plaid mood email from a home brand this month — my brain has officially entered plaid fatigue.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with an actual number. Swap "THE FALL LOOKBOOK / GET INSPIRED" for a real offer in the hero — even "New Fall Arrivals, 20% Off" beats pure inspiration copy with no deal attached.
- 2. Cut the "SHOP THE LOOK" repetition — vary the CTA copy per section ("Shop Rustic Lodge," "Shop Indigo Blues") so it doesn't read as one button copy-pasted four times.
- 3. Move "Recommended Just For You" up near the top — it's the strongest, most personalized section and it's buried past four scrolls of moodboard.
- 4. Add a swatch/sample CTA for the fabric-heavy pieces (the plaid chair, the indigo bedding) — I'm mid-renovation on two houses, "order a swatch" is the single most useful button you could give me right now.
- 5. Hold the "lookbook" framing until closer to September — right now it's competing with your own 70% off summer sale messaging from the day before, and that's a self-inflicted confusion.
