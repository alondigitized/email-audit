# 260+ ways to make our most laid-back sofa yours
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-21**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is Oceanside again — same sofa Crate & Barrel already put front and center on 2026-09-21's "Laid-back vibes every time you sit" send, which I scored a 7 just days ago. This time the angle is "260+ ways to make it yours," and honestly, as someone knee-deep in renovating two houses right now, this is the more useful of the two emails. The swatch grid (40 leathers, fabric options, two depths, two heights) is genuinely the content I need when I'm trying to commit to a sofa I won't regret in five years.
- That said, my inbox fatigue is real. This is the same hero product, same brand family, inside a week — and by the time I hit the Venus Williams collab banner and the "we love these for you" grid of random side tables and dining chairs, my eyes have glazed over. It's a kitchen-sink email pretending to be a configurator email.
- I'd click through to the swatches section specifically — that's a real decision-support tool. I wouldn't forward this to a friend; it's not exciting enough for that, it's just competent. Mark as read after I bookmark the fabric page.

## What's working

- The "240+ fabric options and 40 leathers" section with an actual swatch grid is exactly the customization detail I look for before dropping $2k+ on a sofa.
- "available in 2 depths and 2 heights" plus "quality craftsmanship... assembled by hand in the USA" hits real furniture-shopper concerns (fit, provenance) that Pottery Barn's cozy-textiles email never bothered with.
- The Design Desk free appointment CTA is smart for someone renovating two homes at once — free design help is a real value-add, not filler.
- "Try a swatch first" with a dedicated Get Samples button is the single best UX idea in this email — no other brand in my recent inbox (not CB2, not Article) has offered a literal free-sample CTA this cleanly.

## What's weak

- This is the second Oceanside-led email from Crate & Barrel in under a week — the brand is now doing what CB2 does to it monthly, cannibalizing its own list with repeat hero product.
- The Venus Williams collab banner is a total non-sequitur dropped mid-funnel — I was in "compare fabrics" mode and got yanked into "celebrity collab" mode with zero transition.
- "We love these for you" is generic filler — a console table, a dining set, a side chair with no thread connecting it to Oceanside or to anything I've shown interest in.
- No price anchor for Oceanside itself until deep in the email (the first send at least led with "starting at $1,899" — this one buries pricing entirely behind "Shop Oceanside" clicks).
- Crate Card / rewards blurb ("get 15% back in Rewards Dollars") is crammed at the bottom as an afterthought instead of tied to the swatch/customization moment where it'd actually move a purchase decision.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price back in the hero or swatch section — I compared prices across Pottery Barn, Wayfair, and C&B in my head instantly, and this email opted out of that comparison entirely.
- 2. Cut the Venus Williams banner from this send — it belongs in its own campaign, not wedged between "compare fabrics" and "Design Desk."
- 3. Swap "we love these for you" for actual Oceanside-adjacent accessories (throw pillows, coffee tables in matching fabric family) — right now it reads like a random assortment.
- 4. Space this out from last week's Oceanside hero send — same-week repeat is starting to feel like CB2/C&B's shared-inbox problem.
