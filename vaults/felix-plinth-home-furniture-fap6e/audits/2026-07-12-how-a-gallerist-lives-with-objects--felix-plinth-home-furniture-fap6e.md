# How a gallerist lives with objects
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- Nineteen. That's where we are in the CB2 July count, and I'll be honest — when I saw "How a gallerist lives with objects" drop into my inbox I didn't groan the way I did at email sixteen, the 40% off repeat from July 11th. The editorial framing caught me. A subject line that doesn't mention a discount, doesn't shout, doesn't end in a countdown — that alone earns a pause from me at this point in the month.
- But here's the thing: the email is doing two very different things at once and they fight each other. The top half is a genuine lifestyle editorial — the ochre velvet armchair against navy walls, the gallerist portrait with the table lamp casting that warm amber pool, the burgundy accent chair with its studied placement. This is CB2 behaving like a magazine. I am a renovating Millennial male who saves inspo to an inbox folder and has a color swatch board going for the second home. This is theoretically the CB2 I want to exist. Then the email pivots to a "$5,000" promotion section — sweepstakes energy — and then a product grid. The tonal whiplash is real. I went in for art direction and came out at a raffle ticket booth.
- I'd mark it as read and screenshot the first hero image for my mood board, but I wouldn't click. I definitely wouldn't forward it — not because it's bad, but because I can't even explain to a friend what CB2 is *actually* asking me to do right now.

## What's working

- **The subject line is the best CB2 has sent in two weeks.** No % off, no caps, no urgency bait — just a character-driven editorial hook. Thirty-five characters. Finally.
- **The hero lifestyle photography earns its real estate.** The ochre armchair against deep navy walls with layered textiles is exactly the kind of editorial shot I save to my inbox folder. Article does this well; CB2 matched it here.
- **The gallerist framing is differentiated.** After the Todd Snyder collab email on July 10th, which at least had a collaborator's name, this one builds a full character POV. That's a different gear.
- **The product grid at the bottom delivers functional density** — prices visible, items distinct, no single product repeated. That's the execution Pottery Barn's "Get the look: The layered bed" from earlier this week didn't quite nail because it kept redirecting to category pages.

## What's weak

- **The $5,000 offer section kills the editorial momentum cold.** Going from gallerist lifestyle photography to what reads as a sweepstakes or gift card promotion is a hard tonal break. Whatever that offer is, it needed to be either integrated into the narrative or cut to a footer footnote.
- **The gallerist is a woman in her 40s-50s.** The styling is beautiful but the demographic signal isn't me — a 36-year-old male. Article's new arrivals email on July 8th had more gender-neutral staging; CB2 skewed female here.
- **No specificity on the $5,000 mechanism.** From what I can see in the render, I can't tell if this is a sweepstakes entry, a gift card, or a trade credit. If I have to click to understand the offer, you've already lost me — especially at email 19.
- **No indication this is "collectors edition" content vs. permanent inventory.** The "THE COLLECTORS" badge up top is interesting but I don't know if these are limited pieces, curated picks, or just the regular catalog dressed up.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut or reframe the $5,000 section.** If it's a sweepstakes, move it to the footer. If it's a gift card promotion, give it one clean descriptive line within the editorial flow. Don't break the immersion mid-scroll.
- 2. **Lean further into the character POV throughout.** The gallerist narrative should run through the product grid — "the velvet chair she keeps near the window" not just a floating SKU and price. Pottery Barn's editorial emails gesture at this but never commit. CB2 is closer; go all the way.
- 3. **Add one or two gender-neutral lifestyle shots in the product section** — a broader range of styled hands, room owners, etc. Right now the entire email reads through one demo lens.
- 4. **At email 19 in 12 days, suppress for recently-opened non-clickers.** I've opened the Todd Snyder email and the bedroom email this week. If I didn't click, don't send me this format cold — warm it with a 48-hour gap minimum.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**A. Double-slash URLs in font `src` declarations (3 occurrences)**
```
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2
```
Double slashes may resolve on Apache/Nginx with default config, but some CDN or WAF rules will 404 them. Normalize to single slash.

**B. `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile — WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) violation. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**C. Global `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `*` selector**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;-ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applied to every element, not just `body`. Overrides user agent text scaling on iOS across all nodes. Scope to `body` only.

**D. Duplicate `@font-face` blocks for "Lineto Brown Sub"**
Three separate `@font-face` declarations share the same `font-family: "Lineto Brown Sub"` name — one with no weight/style descriptor, one with `font-weight: 400`, and one with `font-weight: 600`. The bare declaration creates an ambiguous cascade. Consolidate or rename per-weight families.
