# STARTS NOW! Up to 60% off The Memorial Day Event + 2x Rewards!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- Memorial Day week with two houses in mid-renovation — this email landed at literally the right moment. I know C&B's sale calendar; the Memorial Day event is one of maybe three times a year they actually move pricing in a way that matters. The 60% off claim plus the 2x rewards multiplier in the same message is the kind of combination that stops my thumb mid-scroll. The hero shot of that coastal living room is almost exactly the register I'm shopping for the second house — airy, neutral, natural wood — and that's not an accident.
- But the subject line is screaming at me like I don't know what Memorial Day is. "STARTS NOW!" in all caps reads as the kind of email a brand sends when they're not confident the offer speaks for itself — and this offer does. The email body is well-composed by C&B standards: clear hierarchy, rewards module, TheDesignDesk callout I'd actually use, a product grid below the fold. Nothing is broken.
- Would I click? Yes, because I want to know what's actually at 60% and whether any of it maps to the rooms I'm actively speccing. Would I forward it? No — this one goes straight into my saved-promos folder. It's mine to work through with a cart.

## What's working

- **The 2x Rewards callout is timed perfectly.** I run everything through my highest-rewards card and layer whatever program multiplier exists on top — doubling C&B rewards during a 60%-off event is real math, and I felt it.
- **The hero lifestyle image hits the right register.** That bright coastal living room with linen-toned seating and natural wood is almost exactly what I'm pulling references for on the second house. Whoever art-directed it aimed at the right aspirational ceiling.
- **TheDesignDesk module earns its spot.** I've used C&B's free design consultation before. Seeing it featured mid-email during a sale is a smart nudge — "here's the offer AND the tool to make the most of it."
- **The offer feels credible and current.** Memorial Day is this week. Not "limited time," not a vague "for a short time only" — an actual named event that I know and expect from this brand.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem-ipsum ghosts — the email is composed exactly as intended.

## What's weak

- **ALL CAPS "STARTS NOW!" is a visual slap.** It reads as urgency manufactured for someone who doesn't track sales. I do. The offer is strong enough to stand without the megaphone.
- **"Up to 60% off" is doing a lot of work.** I've clicked into Memorial Day sales before and found the marquee discount applied to three clearance accent pillows. Category-level specificity — living room, outdoor, dining — would have pulled me in faster.
- **Zero personalization.** I've bought from this brand multiple times. They know I skew toward living room and dining. A "we think you'd like these" module, or even a category tile that reflects my browse history, would have made this feel like a known-customer email rather than a broadcast.
- **The hero image goes somewhere, but I can't tell where.** The lifestyle shot is beautiful but there's no visible label on what collection this is — clicking the hero is a gamble on whether it takes me somewhere targeted or just dumps me on a generic sale landing page.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the ALL CAPS in the subject.** Rewrite with confidence, not volume — the offer earns opens on its own.
- 2. **Name the categories at 60% in the hero subhead.** "Sofas, dining tables, outdoor seating — up to 60% off" gives me a decision-making signal before I scroll.
- 3. **Add a personalized product module above the fold.** Even a segment-level one ("top picks in living room") beats the fully generic browse experience.
- 4. **Make the rewards math concrete.** "2x Rewards" is abstract. "Earn an extra $20 in rewards for every $200 you spend this weekend" is something I'll actually calculate against.
- 5. **Label the hero image's destination.** A small text callout — "Shop the Coastal Living Collection" — turns a beautiful photo into a useful link.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Memorial Day Sale starts today — 60% off + double rewards`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your C&B Memorial Day deal: 60% off, 2x rewards, this weekend only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sofas, dining, outdoor — the deepest discounts of the year, starting now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Double your rewards points on everything in the sale, through Monday.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

**3a. `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta — accessibility violation**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom. WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize text) violation. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**3b. Conflicting desktop image max-width media queries**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
At viewport widths ≥768px, both rules apply. Because the `640px` breakpoint is declared *after* the `768px` one, it wins via cascade — images cap at `640px` even on wide viewports. The `768px` rule is effectively dead. Consolidate to a single rule.

**3c. `.showmobile` hardcoded to `414px`**
```css
.showmobile { width: 414px !important; }
```
Hardcodes to iPhone X/11/12 width. On small Android devices (360px wide), this causes horizontal overflow. Use `100%` or `calc(100vw - 40px)` instead.

**3d. Dark mode explicitly disabled**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Apple Mail and Gmail on iOS will attempt an auto-invert that may distort the `#e6e7e8` background and image assets. Either add a tested dark-mode media query or accept the rendering risk — but the current opt-out with no fallback leaves dark mode clients unstyled.

**3e. Wildcard `line-height` override**
```css
* { line-height: 100%; }
```
Applies to every element including `<td>`, `<img>`, and `<span>`. Can collapse line-height on multi-line text blocks. Scope this to specific selectors.
