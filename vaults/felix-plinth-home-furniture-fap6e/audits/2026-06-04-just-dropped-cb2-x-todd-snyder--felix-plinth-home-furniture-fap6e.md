# JUST DROPPED: CB2 x Todd Snyder
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- Seventeen. We are at seventeen CB2 emails since mid-May, and I already saw the Todd Snyder app teaser yesterday. So when "JUST DROPPED: CB2 x Todd Snyder" hit this morning — the second CB2 of the day, back-to-back with last night's — my first instinct was eye-roll. But then I opened it. Because Todd Snyder is not a home brand. He makes tailored chinos and wool overcoats and things you'd wear to a whiskey bar in SoHo. The fact that CB2 handed him a whole collection called "Townhouse" — and that it shows up in my inbox while I'm literally gut-renovating two houses simultaneously — made me stop.
- The email earns the look. The hero is editorial in a way that's genuinely different from CB2's usual cooler modernism: warm cream plaster walls, a dark charcoal sofa, a staircase with shadow and depth. It feels less like a catalog and more like an apartment you'd want to steal. The "TOWNHOUSE" collection name is doing serious work for someone like me — it's not a coincidence that word lands differently when you've been on the phone with a contractor every day for six months. That $1,000 gift card promo midway down got my attention and made me scroll to understand the mechanic.
- I'd save this one to the folder and probably click through to browse. I would not forward it to a friend — this is the kind of thing I quietly steal from and then recommend in person later. But the double-send today is wearing on me, and I'm comparing this directly against the Pottery Barn x Chris Loves Julia email from earlier this week, which had cleaner hierarchy and a more obvious "this is a buy" moment. CB2 is bringing editorial atmosphere; PB brought a specific product and a clear path to purchase. I'd take a hybrid of both.

## What's working

- **The Todd Snyder name is a genuine hook.** He's a menswear guy making furniture — that's news, and news gets opens. The collaboration feels un-obvious in a good way.
- **"TOWNHOUSE" is the right collection name for my brain right now.** I'm literally renovating a townhouse. This either hit a segment or got incredibly lucky.
- **The warm palette is a real departure from CB2's usual cool-grey modernism.** Cream, warm charcoal, deep wood tones — it reads like a different brand, which is the point of a collab.
- **The $1,000 figure midway down creates a scroll reward.** I wanted to understand what it was, which means the hierarchy actually made me engage with more of the email.
- **The subject line is clean.** "JUST DROPPED: CB2 x Todd Snyder" is 31 characters, no caps abuse, specific, uses casual tone. After "MIDSEASON SALE" in full screaming caps two days ago, this feels like a reset.

## What's weak

- **This is the second CB2 email today.** Yesterday was "New collab. Only on the app." — also Todd Snyder. Two sends in under 24 hours on the same collab launch dilutes both. The app-exclusivity from yesterday feels hollow if today the same thing is in my general inbox.
- **The $1,000 offer is buried and contextless.** I can see the number but I can't tell from the email whether this is a sweepstakes, a loyalty reward, or a gift card with purchase. That ambiguity makes me cautious rather than excited — I compare this to Pottery Barn's clearance mechanic, which was annoying in volume but at least legible: "60% off, here's the link."
- **The "SHOP NOW" CTA is generic.** For a collab this specific, "Explore Townhouse" or even "Shop the Collab" would carry the moment better. Wayfair uses "SHOP NOW" too — this shouldn't feel interchangeable with Wayfair.
- **Body copy is too small to engage with on mobile.** The editorial paragraphs look beautiful at arm's length but I'm not pinching to zoom on promotional email copy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse the two-send sequence into one.** The app teaser yesterday should have been the only pre-launch email, with a clear "available everywhere tomorrow" callout. Sending two Todd Snyder emails inside 24 hours is the fastest way to train me to stop opening Todd Snyder emails.
- 2. **Make the $1,000 offer immediately scannable.** One line: "Enter to win a $1,000 CB2 gift card — no purchase necessary" or "Spend $500, get a $1,000 gift card." Either version works. The mystery doesn't work.
- 3. **Swap "SHOP NOW" for "Explore Townhouse."** Keep the brand name in the CTA — it's what differentiates this from any other CB2 send.
- 4. **Add one specific product callout.** The Pottery Barn x Chris Loves Julia email worked better than this one because it surfaced a specific hero piece and linked directly to it. The all-collection approach makes me a browser, not a buyer.
- 5. **Increase body copy size by at least 2pt.** If the editorial copy is worth writing, it's worth reading.
