# Welcome to CB2
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- So CB2 is welcoming me today — one day after they sent me their "Now & Future Classics On Sale" blast. The automation sequencing alone is enough to give me pause: why am I being welcomed to a brand that was already pitching me discounts yesterday? That reverse-order friction sits in the back of my head the entire time I'm scrolling. That said, a welcome email is a categorically different beast from a promotional blast, and after ten Pottery Barn emails in 72 hours and Crate & Kids' "LOOK AGAIN" desperation play, landing on clean, full-bleed CB2 photography genuinely felt like taking a breath.
- The content is handsome but stubbornly brand-first. "Well Made Is Always Worth It" is a tagline, not a hook. It doesn't give me a reason to click today. Compare this to Article's welcome email — which wired a real new-subscriber offer into the hero — and CB2 is burning goodwill with a placeholder manifesto. The six category tiles (Living Room Furniture, Decor & Mirrors, Bedroom, Lighting, Rugs, Pillows & Throws) are genuinely relevant for my current situation — I'm outfitting two homes simultaneously and lighting alone is a rabbit hole right now — but they just drop me to the site with no offer attached. The most compelling copy in the entire email, "EARN 50% BACK\*", is buried three screens down near the footer.
- I'd save this to my home-interiors folder for reference and move on. No click today, definitely not forwarding.

## What's working

- Full-bleed lifestyle photography is legitimately beautiful — I save this kind of reference imagery; CB2 earns that reaction
- Six category tiles above the fold are immediately functional for someone in active-renovation mode; Living Room Furniture and Lighting are exactly my two current pain points
- The "EARN 50% BACK\*" rewards callout speaks directly to how I actually shop — every furniture purchase gets routed through the highest-rewards card I own
- "SHOP NOW" CTA is clean and unambiguous — no guessing what happens when I tap it
- No render bugs; clean layout start to finish
- Getting a welcome email the day after a CB2 sale email is backwards automation — it communicates that their systems don't know when I actually joined, which chips away at the "known customer" feeling
- "Well Made Is Always Worth It" is a brand philosophy, not a welcome offer — Article baked a discount into their welcome hero and I actually redeemed it; CB2 gives me a manifesto
- "YOUR DAILY DOSE OF LUXURY / FOLLOW & SHOP OUR FEED" is dead weight in a welcome — I'm not pivoting to Instagram from an email in the first 24 hours on a list
- The asterisk on "EARN 50% BACK\*" does heavy, invisible lifting — I want to know what's excluded before I mentally incorporate this into my rewards math, and I shouldn't have to click to find out
- The CB2 app promotion takes up a substantial block of real estate that should be converting me to a buyer first, app user second

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a new-subscriber offer to the hero** — even 10% off first purchase puts me in buying mode. Article did this and I used it. CB2 leaving the hero as pure brand storytelling forfeits the best opportunity in the welcome sequence.
- 2. **Surface the rewards card messaging higher** — "EARN 50% BACK\*" buried in the footer is a wasted conversion signal for anyone like me who shops rewards-first. Move it to a dedicated banner between the hero and the category tiles.
- 3. **Fix the automation queue** — the sale email should follow the welcome, not precede it. This is a list-ops fix but it meaningfully changes how trusted the first impression feels.
- 4. **Cut the Instagram follow section** — that real estate should either extend the category tiles or host a curated "new arrivals this week" module. Social cross-sell in a welcome email has near-zero conversion for this audience.
- 5. **Resolve the asterisk inline** — add a parenthetical in the rewards copy ("on CB2 purchases with the CB2 credit card, excluding clearance") so I don't have to click to get the fine print.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your CB2 account is ready — plus 10% off your first order`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Welcome. Here's what makes CB2 worth it (and a gift).`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop living room, lighting, and decor — with your new-member offer inside.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Well made, built to last. Your first-order discount is waiting.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

| Issue | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Duplicate `<meta charset="utf-8">` | Appears twice in `<head>` — once inline, once as a standalone tag. Redundant; some parsers may trigger warnings. |
| Redundant `format-detection` metas | A single combined tag (`telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no`) is present **and** three separate individual tags for `date=no`, `address=no`, `telephone=no`. The combined tag's `email=no` and `url=no` values have no corresponding individual fallback — inconsistent and noisy. |
| Light-mode only declared | `<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">` + `<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">` — clients in forced dark mode (Apple Mail, Gmail Android) will apply their own inversion algorithm, potentially inverting background colors unexpectedly. No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` overrides present. |
| `display:none` on `.ecxhm` | `.ExternalClass .ecxhm { display:none }` hides elements with this class in Outlook.com/Hotmail. Confirm no visible content is assigned `.ecxhm` unintentionally. |

**Cannot assess from truncated source:**

- ARIA roles or `role="presentation"` on layout tables

---

### 7. Recommendations

1. **Remove duplicate `<meta charset="utf-8">`** — keep one, in the first position within `<head>`.
2. **Consolidate `format-detection` metas** — use a single tag with all values, or use individual tags consistently. Remove the hybrid.
3. **Add a dark mode media query** or at minimum test forced-dark rendering in Apple Mail. At minimum, set explicit background colors on `<body>` and outer wrapper tables so inversion is predictable.
4. **Audit `.ecxhm` class usage** — confirm it is only applied to intentionally hidden elements (e.g., preheader spacer).
5. **Verify full footer in untruncated source** — physical address, unsubscribe link, and List-Unsubscribe headers are non-negotiable for deliverability and legal compliance.
6. **Confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass** on `mail.cb2.com` via raw headers from a test send before production deployment.
