# MATTRESS FOUNDATIONS ➡️ CLEAROUT ➡️ Can't-miss PRICE DROPS ➡️ 
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-seven. That's where the Wayfair count sits as of this morning — two emails on the same Thursday, which I noticed before I even processed the creative. The area rugs email hit earlier today at #26, and now we're here with mattress foundations. The subject line drags "MATTRESS FOUNDATIONS" as the lead, which actually lands for me — I'm spec-ing beds for the second home right now. So I opened it. And then I got the whole store.
- That's the problem. Open the email and what greets you is a 4×2 grid of generic department discounts: TV stands, rugs, outdoor, appliances, kitchen — mattresses are buried in the middle as a 30% off block no bigger than the adjacent "fitness & sports" tile. The subject made a specific promise about a specific sleep product category. The email delivered a clearout flyer.
- I mark this as read. I don't click, I don't forward. I've watched Crate & Barrel earn an 8 this week on two back-to-back 60%-off clearance emails — and they actually sent me to the clearance section, not the homepage wearing a discount banner. That's the gap here.

## What's working

- **"Picked just for you" framing** at the hero at least nods toward personalization — better than a cold "Sale is on."
- **The 60% off hero tile** at the top catches the eye immediately; the color blocking on the percentage is large and readable.
- **Star ratings visible** on the product picks at the bottom — that's a small trust signal I actually register.
- **Wayfair Professional callout** at the footer is relevant; I use Wayfair Pro pricing for bulk furniture orders on the renovation.

## What's weak

- **Subject-to-body mismatch is a bait-and-switch.** "Mattress foundations" promised a focused, navigable section — I got 8 generic department tiles and had to hunt for the mattress block.
- **No hierarchy.** Every discount tile is the same visual weight. 30% off mattresses competes on equal footing with 30% off appliances; there's no anchor.
- **The arrow-emoji chain in the subject** (➡️ ➡️ ➡️) reads like a spam folder audition. CB2 has been landing in my inbox all week with cleaner subject lines and still pulled a 7.
- **Two emails on the same day, again.** Wayfair did this on the 28th with the American Summer Shop and the area rugs drop. The cadence is eroding trust faster than any offer can rebuild it.
- **"Can't-miss" copy with no deadline.** No date, no end-time, nothing credible behind the urgency language.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Build a mattress foundations landing page and link the hero directly to it** — not shop-all-sales. The subject earned a specific click; honor it.
- 2. **Kill two of the three arrows in the subject line.** One ➡️ as a separator is a style choice; three in a row is a spam signal.
- 3. **Promote the 30% off mattresses tile to hero status**, or at minimum make it the first item in the grid with a larger image and a spec-level detail (foundation type, compatibility, sizing).
- 4. **Add a "sale ends" date to the CTA button.** Article's "Up to 40% off ends Monday" from the 22nd was the gold standard this week — a six-word sentence that made me click immediately. Wayfair has the same inventory, just a weaker ask.
- 5. **Suppress this send to anyone who already received the area rugs email today.** Two emails in one day from the same sender trains the finger to unsubscribe.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your bed frame needs a foundation. We have them.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Box springs, platform bases, and split foundations — sizes in stock now.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Single breakpoint gap**
Two breakpoints are defined: `max-width: 480px` (primary) and `max-width: 320px` (footer links only). No intermediate breakpoint exists for tablets (481–768px), so layout falls through to desktop table widths on mid-size screens. This is a common trade-off in ESP-generated email but worth logging.

**`word-break: break-all` on `.ProductRec-name`**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) {
  .ProductRec-name { word-break: break-all; ... }
}
```
`break-all` breaks at any character boundary, including mid-word. `overflow-wrap: break-word` or `word-break: break-word` would be less aggressive and preserve readability for long product names.
