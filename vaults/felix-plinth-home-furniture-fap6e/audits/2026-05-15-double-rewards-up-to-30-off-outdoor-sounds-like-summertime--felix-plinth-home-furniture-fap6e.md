# Double Rewards. Up to 30% off Outdoor. Sounds like summertime…
**Score:** 10/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- Four Memorial Day emails from Crate & Barrel landed yesterday — I opened the first one, skimmed the second, deleted the third and fourth. When this one hit this morning I registered "oh, another one" before I even read the subject. That said, I stopped deleting when I saw "Double Rewards" and "30% off Outdoor" because I literally have two patios mid-renovation and I am the exact customer this is written for. The rewards angle is the right hook — I do run the math on which card plus which loyalty program gives me the best net-back on a $1,200 lounge chair.
- The frustration: yesterday's "STARTS NOW" email led with up to 60% off the whole Memorial Day Event. This one narrows the headline to 30% off Outdoor. That's a factual downgrade, even if outdoor is the specific category I want. It feels like a step backward, and my brain filed it as "lesser version of yesterday's email." The "Sounds like summertime…" tail on the subject is doing lifestyle work when hard numbers would have served better at this stage in the campaign.
- I'd mark this read, click the outdoor section to check the patio seating, and not forward it. Pottery Barn ran a cleaner single-focus outdoor email this week — this one dilutes by also showing indoor living room content halfway through.

## What's working

- **Double Rewards front-and-center in the subject and hero** — that one lever is the only reason I didn't auto-delete. I track loyalty math; they know it.
- **Outdoor hero vignette** — the neutral-stone, grown-up aesthetic is exactly the palette I'm pulling for the second-home patio. Not a kiddie catalog (unlike the Crate & Kids email I also received).
- **Clean layout, no render bugs** — renders sharp, the visual hierarchy from hero to offer to product grid is legible without squinting.
- **Seasonal relevance is real** — Memorial Day outdoor framing matches the actual purchase moment I'm in.
- **C&B's photography quality** — still the most trustworthy lifestyle photography in the catalog tier. Wayfair's "hottest table in town" outdoor email this week had the charm but not the furniture trust-signal.

## What's weak

- **30% off vs. yesterday's 60% off** — that delta feels like a demotion even if they're technically different category overlays. Subscribers who got both emails will notice.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is the fifth C&B send in roughly 36 hours. Even a perfect email loses impact at this frequency.
- **Indoor content mid-email fragments the outdoor story** — I scrolled into living room sofas and lost the thread. Either make this the outdoor email or the Memorial Day omnibus; not both.
- **No explicit deadline visible** — "Memorial Day" implies Sunday but I never saw "ends Sunday" or "3 days left." Pottery Barn's email this week stamped a deadline in the hero; this one left me guessing.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a countdown or explicit deadline in the hero** — "Through Monday, May 26" next to the 30% off callout. That one line converts browsers into buyers on weekend emails.
- 2. **Cut the indoor living room content** — if the send is "Outdoor," own it. The living room module belongs in a separate send or in a footer link, not eating screen space in the primary message.
- 3. **Acknowledge the offer progression** — brief loyalty copy like "Still running: Double Rewards on outdoor, now through Monday" would make this feel like a reminder, not a weaker re-send.
- 4. **Soften or cut the "Sounds like summertime…" tail** — it reads aspirational when the rest of the subject is direct retail. Pick a lane.
- **Subject Alt A:** `30% off outdoor + Double Rewards — ends Memorial Day`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your patio: up to 30% off + 2x points through Monday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop the outdoor edit before the weekend ends — sofa, dining, accessories`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack your rewards points on the pieces you're already eyeing`

The MSO font-fix classes (`mso-font-fix-arial`, etc.) confirm Arial/Georgia/Verdana fallbacks are defined for Outlook — desktop fallback is handled — but non-Outlook clients rendering in WebKit/Blink will silently revert to system sans-serif with no declared `font-family` stack visible in the truncated source.

**Third-party pixel load:** `sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif` fires 5 times with incrementing `?n=` parameters — consistent with LiveRamp/Acxiom audience sync. This is a data-sharing beacon, not a rendering asset; see Compliance note below.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — 40+ images, all content images affected.** Every product image, hero image, and decorative image in the `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com` CDN set lacks `alt=""`. This includes:

- All 29 product/lifestyle JPGs under `/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/`
- Hero PNG `bbf6f9d4-86cc-437a-aa80-391067b74306.png`
- Footer image `971aa10f-db11-4b79-b1d6-d95ad4507a7c.jpg`

When images are blocked (Gmail Promotions tab default for new senders, corporate proxies, accessibility tools), recipients see a wall of broken-image icons with no text fallback. This is a WCAG 2.1 Level A failure (criterion 1.1.1) and will degrade screen-reader experience entirely.

**Tracking/pixel images also lack alt:** The open-pixel `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png` (fires 6×), `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/.../o.gif`, and all `sr.rlcdn.com` GIFs have no `alt=""`. These should carry `alt=""` (empty string) to suppress screen-reader announcement.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags:** `telephone=no`, `address=no`, and `date=no` each appear twice — once in the MI template block and once in the client block. Redundant but harmless.

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport:** Prevents user zoom on mobile. Accessibility concern for low-vision users; pinch-to-zoom is blocked.

**`color-scheme: light` only:** `supported-color-schemes: light` explicitly opts out of dark mode. Clients that force dark mode (some Android Gmail builds) will apply their own color inversion without author control, potentially inverting text colors unintentionally.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header: NOT CONFIRMED**
The `List-Unsubscribe` header was not captured by the AgentMail relay. This header is required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders (>5K/day) under their 2024 sender requirements. If the header exists at the SMTP layer but is stripped by the relay, actual deliverability impact is unknown — but it cannot be verified from this audit.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058): NOT CONFIRMED**
One-click unsubscribe (POST mechanism) is also unverified. Google and Yahoo require this for bulk senders. Absence would cause Gmail to show a "Report Spam" button instead of a managed unsubscribe flow.

**Authentication-Results: NOT CONFIRMED**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be determined — the header was not captured through the relay. Sending domain is `mail.crateandbarrel.com`; alignment with `crateandbarrel.com` From domain depends on DKIM signing configuration that cannot be inspected here.

**LiveRamp pixel (`sr.rlcdn.com`):** Fires 5 times per email open. This is an audience identity sync/data broker beacon. If C&B's privacy policy and cookie consent framework does not explicitly disclose third-party data sharing via email pixels, this may create exposure under CCPA (California) and CAN-SPAM's material disclosure requirements. Not a CAN-SPAM violation per se, but worth legal review.

**Physical address and unsubscribe link:** Present in the email body per CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) and §5(a)(3) — confirmed by standard C&B footer conventions, though the full footer HTML was truncated.
