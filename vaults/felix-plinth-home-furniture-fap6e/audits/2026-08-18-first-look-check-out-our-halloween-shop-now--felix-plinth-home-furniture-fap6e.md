# First look: Check out our Halloween shop now →
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-18**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids showed up in my inbox with a Halloween shop launch, and my first thought was: wrong Felix. I don't have a nursery to furnish — I've got a primary and a second home, both very much adult spaces, and this is nursery bedding, kid backpacks, and a "Haunted House Halloween Countdown Calendar." The pumpkin treat bag and ghost bucket in the hero shot are genuinely cute styling, I'll give them that, but cute doesn't get me to click when there's zero overlap with either renovation.
- The one thing that made me pause was the "500+ new arrivals, starting at $29" nursery/kids furniture banner — not because I need it, but because it's a reminder that Crate & Barrel's parent company is now the fourth version of this same brand family hitting my inbox this stretch (I've already sat through Crate & Barrel's clearance email and the Howell & Harrier collab today alone). CB2 already griped about this exact fatigue with its own "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS" send, and now Crate & Kids is piling on with a completely different demo. This is a mark-as-read, not a forward — unless I know a friend with a toddler and a Halloween obsession, which, no.

## What's working

- The Halloween banner styling — pumpkin treat bag, ghost bucket, black cat pillow — is seasonally on-point and well shot, feels current for late August/early fall push.
- "Personalize It" callouts on the treat bags and hooded towel are a nice functional detail if I were shopping for a kid.
- The nursery furniture banner ($29 starting price) is at least a concrete price point, which beats vague "shop now" messaging.
- Layout is clean, no rendering bugs, images all loaded fine.

## What's weak

- I have no kids in my life referenced anywhere in my situation — this is Crate & Kids, a nursery/kids brand, and nothing about my renovation touch points (living room, dining, outdoor) is here.
- Eight-plus product tiles and three separate banners (Halloween, nursery arrivals, "we love these for you," credit card, app, TheDesignDesk) — this thing sprawls just like Crate & Barrel's clearance email did today, and by the time I hit the credit card pitch I've long since checked out.
- The "we love these for you" personalized grid is clearly not personalized to me — backpacks, gold knobs, kids' throw pillows. If this is supposed to be algorithmic, it missed badly.
- Another Crate-family credit card pitch mid-email — Crate & Barrel's own send today didn't do this, so seeing it here feels like noise stacked on noise.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment me out of Crate & Kids entirely, or at minimum suppress kids-brand sends for subscribers who've never bought nursery/kids product — that's the single biggest lift here, since no amount of copy tweaking fixes a fundamentally mistargeted send.
- 2. Cut the email down to one clear CTA (Halloween shop) instead of stacking nursery arrivals, personalized picks, credit card, app download, and TheDesignDesk — Article's editorial sends at least stay focused on one theme.
- 3. If cross-selling to Crate & Barrel parent-brand shoppers is the goal, lead with adult-relevant crossover product (Halloween entertaining/decor for grown-up spaces) instead of assuming everyone on the list has kids.
- 4. Drop or relocate the credit card pitch — it's the third time today I've seen a Crate-family card ask, and it reads as filler this deep into an already-long email.
