# NEW! Introducing Howell x Harrier for Crate & Barrel
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-09**
## Executive summary

- Okay, six sends in from Crate & Barrel this week and this is the one where I actually stopped scrolling. It's a designer collab — "Howell x Harrier" with Tiffany Howell of Laura Hunt/Studio Collab — and the checkerboard floor, the boucle chairs, the whole editorial spread genuinely looks like a room I'd screenshot for the primary-home mood board. This is the furniture email I've been waiting for from this brand all week, not another backpack or nursery pitch.
- That said, it's a lot. This thing scrolls forever — collab hero, three or four room vignettes, quotes from the designer, then it just... keeps going into a Le Creuset ad, an espresso machine cross-sell, "we love these for you," and a credit card pitch at the bottom. It's like they bolted their entire weekly newsletter onto a genuinely good launch. I'd click into the collab itself for sure. I would not scroll to the bottom, and I wouldn't forward it — it's nice, not "hey check this out" nice.

## What's working

- The hero shot — checkerboard floor, cane chair, warm wood tones — actually looks like a room, not a product dump. That's rarer than it should be in my inbox.
- "Six years after Tiffany Howell designed artist Laura Hunt's L.A. home, their creative partnership comes full circle" — that's an actual story, not just "NEW ARRIVALS." Reads more like Article's editorial "Bedroom reset" send than the usual sale-mode blast.
- Multiple room types shown (living, dining, bedroom) so I can picture it across both houses I'm working on right now.
- Designer quotes ("We gimmick, no trends, chic, always") add a credibility layer none of the Pottery Barn or Wayfair sale emails bother with.
- "Shop the full collab, starting under $100" gives me an actual price anchor, which most of this genre skips entirely.

## What's weak

- It just keeps going. After the actual collab wraps, there's a coffee/espresso machine section, a Le Creuset Dutch oven ad, a generic "we love these for you" grid, AND a credit card pitch — four separate campaigns stapled onto one email. CB2's "90+ fabrics" send I rated a 6 stayed disciplined and single-focus; this one doesn't.
- No pricing shown on the actual hero pieces (chair, credit table, pendant lights) until way down at "starting under $100" — I'm 15 pieces of furniture deep before I know if this is $200 or $2,000 territory.
- Text is genuinely small in the screenshot — captions like "New At A Glance: 6 Signature Styles Made to Coordinate" are borderline unreadable at a glance on my phone, and this is furniture, where scale and dimensions matter to me.
- Buried at the bottom, the credit card push ("chance at $500") feels bolted-on and cheapens what was otherwise a genuinely aspirational email.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut it in half. Ship the collab as its own dedicated email — hero, 3 rooms, shop-the-look — and send the Le Creuset/espresso/credit-card stuff as separate, focused sends. This is the single biggest lift; a tight collab email would beat this bloated one easily.
- 2. Put a price range in the hero, not six screens down. "Starting under $100" should be above the fold next to the first "Shop the Collab" button.
- 3. Add a "shop by room" jump-nav near the top since the email is long anyway — living room / dining / bedroom anchors so I can skip to what I actually need.
- 4. Increase caption font size on mobile — I shouldn't have to zoom to read what piece I'm looking at.
- 5. Drop the credit card CTA from this specific email entirely — it undercuts the designer-collab premium feel.

- **Subject Alt B:** `The Howell x Harrier collab, starting under $100`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Checkerboard floors, boucle chairs, six years in the making.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the full room — living, dining, bedroom — before it sells out.`
