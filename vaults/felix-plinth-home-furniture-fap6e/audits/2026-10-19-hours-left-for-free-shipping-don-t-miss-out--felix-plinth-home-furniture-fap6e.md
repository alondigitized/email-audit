# Hours left for free shipping! Don’t miss out
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-19**
## Executive summary

- This is the same free-shipping blast I've been getting for two days, and the Kids sub-brand isn't the sender this time. It's a mass-market Crate & Barrel send, and I'm much closer to its target. The top is a stack of Dutch ovens, then "hours left: free shipping on orders over $99!" and a wall of category links. The Thanksgiving dining room event further down, with up to 30% off, is the part that matters to me. I'm furnishing two houses, so a dining table is a real purchase.
- It still reads like a stranger's email. It's the sixteenth or seventeenth C&B send in about two days, and "hours left" has been the pitch since "ENDS TODAY!" this morning. I stopped believing the deadline a few sends ago. A $99 free-shipping threshold also means nothing to me when I'm buying furniture. A table or sideboard crosses that number the second it's in the cart. Free shipping is a stocking-stuffer hook, not a renovation hook.
- I'd open it out of habit and scroll to the dining table. I might click that one tile, but I wouldn't forward it. I'd save the table image to my Pinterest folder and move on. Wayfair's pergola email last week had one product with a visible dollar-off badge. This email makes me hunt for the offer.

## What's working

- The dining table hero is a styled room with a round pedestal table and curved chairs. It's exactly the kind of image I'd pin for the second house.
- "Up to 30% off" on the Thanksgiving dining event is a concrete number. It's the best offer in the email.
- The Reward Dollars card block, "Get 10% back in Reward Dollars," speaks to how I shop. I buy through the highest-rewards card, so it got a second look.
- The TheDesignDesk "Book a Free Appointment" block fits someone doing two renovations. A free design pro is useful.
- The "Shop All Free Shipping" button is a clear, readable primary CTA.

## What's weak

- The urgency is stale. "Hours left" after a day of "ENDS TODAY!" and "Ends soon" sends makes the whole thing feel like a fire sale that never ends.
- The shipping offer is built for $99 buyers. It doesn't mention furniture delivery, lead times, or white-glove options, which is what I'd need to know.
- The Dutch oven hero has nothing to do with the offer and sits above it. The first screen sells cookware while the real furniture event is buried a long way down.
- The "we love these for you" section is five glasses and a bowl at $6–$20. It looks personalized, but it isn't. I'm shopping for a dining set, not juice glasses.
- Sale pricing on the dining table is tiny and low-contrast, and the dense fine print at the bottom is unreadable on a phone. That makes the "up to 30% off" feel hedged.
- It's a lot of modules: eight category links, the holiday collection, the dining event, the products, the card, the app, the design desk and the store locator. Nothing leads.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one offer. Either free shipping or the dining event, not both. For me, put the dining event first with a visible price on the table.
- 2. Drop the false urgency. If the deadline is real, give an exact time like "ends 11:59 PM PT tonight." Don't send a third "hours left" email.
- 3. Make the product module reflect browse history. If I've looked at dining tables, show chairs and sideboards, not glassware.
- 4. Say what shipping means for large items, like flat-rate delivery or a free-shipping threshold for furniture.
- 5. Cut the category grid and the app block. Keep the card and the design appointment, since they suit a renovator.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dining tables up to 30% off, ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Free shipping ends 11:59 PM PT tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Tables, chairs and sideboards for Thanksgiving hosting. Select styles.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus 10% back in Reward Dollars with your Crate Card.`

The HTML source was truncated, so findings are limited to the visible head and the QA output.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **5 duplicate LiveRamp pixels.** `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=dbe8da71…f5a8&n=1` through `&n=5` use the same hash and differ only in `n`. Five identical third-party beacons add requests and cross-site ID syncing with no functional benefit visible in the source.

- **Tracking pixels without alt.** `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif` and `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/b5e0fd92-…` are open and impression pixels.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt on 26 images.** Counts by source:
  - 11 content images on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/…`, for example `bbf6f9d4-…png`, `fc2adc28-….gif`, `1c9c4d87-….jpg`, `971aa10f-….jpg`.

  - 7 Movable Ink `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png` images (`560d850e7e517ede.png`, `789e7381ff44be74.png`, and others), likely dynamic product tiles.
  - 7 tracking pixels, which should carry `alt=""`.
  
  With images off or a screen reader, the content images have no text fallback. Any that carry the offer message or are inside links are the most serious cases.
- **`<meta name="viewport" … maximum-scale=1>`** blocks pinch-zoom, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Dead CSS rule.** `@media (min-width:768px){img{max-width:600px}}` is always overridden by the later `@media (min-width:640px){img{max-width:640px}}`, because both match at ≥768px and the later one wins.
- **Head hygiene:**
  - Duplicate `<meta name="format-detection">` tags (three declarations).
  - Duplicate charset declarations (`http-equiv` and `meta charset`).
  - A leftover template placeholder comment, `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->`.
  - An unescaped ampersand in `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>`, which is invalid under the XHTML doctype.
- **Global `*{line-height:100%; max-height:1000000px}`** is a broad reset that can cause unexpected line-height behavior in some clients. This is a risk, not a confirmed bug.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (`{{…}}`, `%%…%%`, `[FIRSTNAME]`) are visible in the available source. Per-recipient values (`mi_u`) are populated.
