# Up to 50% off our biggest sale of the year! Shop Black Friday now →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-20**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction was "fine, but I've seen this exact sale already." The "biggest sale of the year" line is the same Black Friday promo Crate & Barrel sent me earlier today, with "Black Friday deals are here! Shop up to 50% off →" and "Save up to 50% on sofas, dining furniture & more" both on 11/19–11/20. The hero is the stand mixer again, with "up to 60% off + free shipping" and a Shop Now button. It's clear and on-brand, but it isn't news.
- The email is very long, about 11,000px when rendered. It runs from a tile grid to a clearance banner, kitchen deals, a Thanksgiving menu, the Design Desk giveaway, a "we love these for you" product row and an app ad. I'm renovating two houses, so the sofas and sectionals, furniture and rugs tiles and the dining room clearance banner are what I'd stop on. I'd skim to those, but I wouldn't read the rest.
- Would I click? Probably, on the sofa/sectional tile or the dining clearance banner. Would I forward it? No. Mark as read, save the dining-room shot to my Pinterest folder, move on.

## What's working

- The hero leads with the offer: "Black Friday is here! Up to 60% off + free shipping." I get the point in two seconds.
- The category tile grid ("shop 1000s of Black Friday deals") gives each category its own percentage. Sofas & sectionals, furniture and rugs line up with what I'm buying.
- The dining room lifestyle shot with the "up to 60% off clearance" banner is the strongest image in the email. It's the kind of room I'd pin.
- The Design Desk section ("enter for a chance to win $5000" plus a free appointment with a designer) is a showroom/design-service touch. Pottery Barn's recent sends skip this entirely.
- The 4-day shipping / "get it by Thanksgiving" strip at the top is credible urgency for furniture buyers.

## What's weak

- It's the same sale I've already had twice. After the 11/19 sofa/dining send and this morning's "Black Friday deals are here," a third "biggest sale of the year" is cadence fatigue.
- It runs too long and tries to do everything: kitchen, gifts, kids, a menu, a giveaway, an app ad. The furniture offer I want is competing with Le Creuset and coffee makers.
- Every button reads "Shop Now" or "Shop Black Friday." None says what I'd get, like "Shop sofas up to 60% off."
- The kitchen and food blocks (Thanksgiving menu, Le Creuset) are irrelevant to a furniture-focused renovation buyer.
- On the mobile-width render the tile captions and product row are tiny. I'd need to zoom to read prices.
- "Up to 50%" in the subject and "up to 60%" in the hero is a small mismatch, and I notice it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the length by about half and lead with furniture for browsers who've clicked sofas or dining. Move kitchen, gifts and the menu to a separate send.
- 2. Make the CTAs specific. "Shop sofas & sectionals" beats a generic "Shop Now" repeated four times.
- 3. Match the discount in the subject to the hero. Say "up to 60%" in both, or make the hero less vague.
- 4. Add rewards math, like Crate Card points or Design Desk member pricing, so I can plan which card to use.
- 5. Add a swatch or fabric sample callout near the sofa tile. For furniture, that's what actually moves me.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call: 4-day shipping ends 11/23`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sectionals, dining tables and rugs, plus free design help.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's HTML mostly follows standard ESP-built, table-based patterns (`lang="en"`, MSO conditionals, a mobile breakpoint). Confirmed problems are widespread missing image `alt` attributes (38+ listed) and a viewport meta that blocks pinch-zoom. Header-based compliance and authentication checks couldn't be verified, because the relay doesn't expose those headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Destinations unverified.** The QA run skipped the HTTP probe on 80 links because they are tracking/click-redirect domains. Final landing URLs, redirect chains and 404s are untested. The `mi.crateandbarrel.com` and `dv.crateandbarrel.com` hosts appear in the QA data.
- **Third-party pixels.** `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?...&n=1` through `&n=5` are five identical LiveRamp (rlcdn) pixels differing only by `n`. They are third-party data-sync beacons, and they are also in the missing-alt list.
- **Identifier in image URLs.** The `mi_u=56aaa2787a67…` parameter appears on `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png` and `o.gif`. It is a long per-recipient token, and the same value appears in the rlcdn `s=` parameter. It identifies the recipient, so forwarded or archived copies of the email expose it.
- **Open-tracking pixel.** `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif` is the expected open pixel.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text.** At least 38 images are flagged, and the QA list appears truncated, so the real count may be higher. About 31 are content images:
  - 23 on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/*.jpg` and `/m/47/bbf6f9d4-….png`.
  - 7 on `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png`.
  - `971aa10f-….jpg`.

  The remaining ones are tracking pixels (`448796.gif` ×5, `o.gif`, `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/380ab5d0-…`). The QA tool flags *missing* alt; it can't distinguish a missing attribute from a valid `alt=""`, so verify the pixels have `alt=""`. For the content images, missing alt means the sale content disappears when images are blocked.
- **Pinch-zoom disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` blocks user zoom, which fails WCAG 1.4.4. Combined with `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}`, text scaling is also suppressed.
- **Template leftovers and duplicates:**
  - The unfilled placeholder comment `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` shipped in production.
  - `format-detection` is declared three times with overlapping values.
  - `<meta charset>` is declared twice, once via `http-equiv`.
- **XHTML 1.0 doctype with an unescaped ampersand.** `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` should be `&amp;`. It is harmless in practice.
- **Dead CSS rule.** `@media (min-width:768px){img{max-width:600px}}` is always overridden by the later `@media (min-width:640px){img{max-width:640px}}`, since both match at ≥768px.
- **`<title>` mismatch.** `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` is generic and doesn't match the subject line. This is minor.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found in the visible source. No unrendered tokens (`{{…}}`, `%%…%%`, `[FIRST_NAME]`) appear, but the HTML is truncated, so the body and footer weren't fully reviewed.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058).** QA reports both missing. The QA output itself says the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is **unconfirmed**. It matters for Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements.
- **`Authentication-Results`.** Not captured, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment for `mail.crateandbarrel.com` are unknown.
- **Footer unsubscribe link and physical address.** Not visible in the truncated source, so they weren't verified.
- **From address.** The From domain `mail.crateandbarrel.com` matches the image and tracking subdomains, which is consistent.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
No issues confirmed. Links go through redirect domains and weren't probed, so UTM parameters and landing alignment can't be assessed. No destination URLs with `utm_*` parameters were visible in the truncated source.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to all content images (the `/m/1/*.jpg` set, `/m/47/*.png` and the `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png` set). Set `alt=""` on the tracking pixels.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta. Drop the `text-size-adjust:none` rules that suppress text scaling.
3. Re-run the QA with raw `.eml` capture, or fetch headers directly, to confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results` before treating them as defects.
4. Resolve the tracked links by following redirects, with a GET or a rate-limited probe. Then check final URLs, status codes and UTM parameters.
5. Consolidate the duplicate meta tags, remove the `IMPUT HERE` placeholder comment, escape `&` in `<title>`, and delete the dead 768px `img` rule.
6. Review the five `sr.rlcdn.com` pixels and the `mi_u` identifier in image URLs for privacy and consent compliance.
