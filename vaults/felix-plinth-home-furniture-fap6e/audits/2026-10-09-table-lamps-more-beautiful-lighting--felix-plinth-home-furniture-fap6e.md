# Table lamps & more beautiful lighting
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-09**
## Executive summary

- Finally, a Pottery Barn email with an actual product story. Lighting is a real need for me, because I'm furnishing two houses and neither has decent lamps yet. "Illuminate Every Corner" with a lineup of table lamps is the best hero PB has sent me this week. Compare that to the green "Fall Sale: Up to 70% off" strip, which I've now seen on every send since 10/06. It's wallpaper at this point.
- The rest earns its length. The lighting-by-type grid (chandeliers, sconces, pendants, outdoor, floor lamps) is how I shop, and the "Recommended just for you" block is the first sign all week that PB knows I exist. But the email is long. The rugs, curtain rods, sofa and dresser tiles, the New Arrivals banner and the Christmas Shop banner all pile on after the lamp story. Christmas Shop on October 9th, in the same email as a Fall Sale, feels sloppy.
- Would I click? Yes, probably on table lamps or sconces, and I'd save it to my inspiration folder. Would I forward it? Maybe to my partner, for the sconces only. I'd be more tempted if I weren't tired of PB's cadence. Crate & Barrel's "Just landed: The fall deals edit" at least varied its creative. This one still carries the same banner.

## What's working

- The hero is a clean lamp lineup with a plain "Shop Table Lamps" button, so I know exactly where I'm going.
- The "Petite table lamps are having a moment… dressers, entryways, or kitchen counters" copy gives me room-by-room use cases, which suits a renovator.
- The lighting-by-type grid is easy to browse. Chandeliers, sconces, pendants and outdoor each get a big image, which beats Wayfair's wall of tiles.
- The personalized "Recommended just for you" block feels like it knows my tastes. That's rare from PB lately.
- The 10% back in Rewards callout matters to a points-maximizer like me. Pairing it with Affirm is a decent nod to big renovation spend.

## What's weak

- The green "Up to 70% off" strip is identical to my last four PB sends. It's banner blindness now, and it never says what's actually 70% off. Is it lamps?
- No price or offer is attached to any lamp. I can't tell if the sale applies to lighting at all.
- The email is too long. After "Shop All Lighting" it spins off into rugs, decor, bedding, New Arrivals and the Christmas Shop. It reads like a catalog dump.
- The Christmas Shop banner in early October jars with the fall sale framing.
- Several tiles, such as "Pendants" and "Picture Lights", have tiny labels that are hard to read and tap on a phone.
- I've now had lighting from Crate & Barrel and CB2 in the same week. A same-lamp-in-three-emails problem is coming.
- The fine print at the bottom is dense and hard to read. That undercuts the "70% off" headline.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put one real offer on the lighting, such as "Up to 30% off lighting" with a deadline. Right now the hero is pretty but I don't know what I'm saving.
- 2. Cut everything after "Shop All Lighting" except the rewards line. A lighting email should stay about lighting.
- 3. Retire the generic "Up to 70% off" strip. Swap it for a lighting-specific line, and don't repeat the same banner across four sends.
- 4. Make the category labels bigger and tappable (Pendants, Picture Lights, Floor Lamps).
- 5. Add shade and finish swatch callouts, or a note on free design services. That ties back to your 10/09 "FREE design services" send, which actually spoke to my situation.
- 6. Drop the Christmas banner until it's closer to the season.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Petite table lamps for small corners`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Lighting up to 30% off, ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Table lamps, sconces, chandeliers and more, plus free design help.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Find the right lamp for your entryway, dresser or kitchen counter.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is table-based XHTML with a hidden preheader and a click-tracked structure. The source has image-accessibility gaps (about 50 images flagged for missing alt text, with a hero built from sliced images), a stray PHP snippet inside a `<style>` block, and plain `http://` image sources. The recipient address also appears in plaintext in tracking-pixel URLs. The 50% QA pass rate is mostly header-capture limits of the relay, not confirmed sender defects.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **57 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe.** They are `click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=...` redirects. Destination validity, redirect chains and UTM presence are unverified.
- **The recipient email is exposed in tracking URLs.**
  - `https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
  - `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
- **The svopn open pixel is plain `http://`.** The address travels unencrypted, and clients may block or rewrite the pixel.
- **Several tracking pixels have no `alt` attribute.** These are `d.turn.com/r/dd/id/.../cat/1`, `clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/469b30e5-...` and the svopn pixel. They should carry `alt=""` and explicit dimensions.
- **Plain-text part is 72% URLs** (10968 of 15203 chars). The long tracking redirects drive this, which is a minor spam-filter heuristic risk.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on content images.** The campaign images `0818_TUE_AM_TableLampsAndMore/slices_01.jpg` to `slices_15.jpg` have no alt, and neither do the product images (`york-slope-arm-deep-seat-slipcovered-sofa-60-108-l.jpg`, `remmy-swivel-chair-1-l.jpg`, etc.). The `Email-Footer/slices_01..18.jpg` images have none either.
  - Sliced, image-only content renders as blank blocks and is unreadable to screen readers when images are blocked.
  - The hero banners `H26_TOP_FallSale.jpg`, `F26_Bottom_NewArrivals_V1.jpg` and `H26_Bottom_ChristmasShop.jpg` are also missing alt.
- **`<title></title>` is empty.**
- **`<meta name="viewport" content="..., maximum-scale=1">`** blocks pinch-zoom, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Non-HTTPS images.**
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
  - These may be blocked or flagged as mixed content.
- **Stray server-side code inside `<style>`:** `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: no-store..."); header("Pragma: no-cache");`. It is invalid CSS and can break the following rule in strict parsers. It also leaks template internals.
- **Cross-brand and stale assets.**
  - `https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` is a West Elm spacer in a Pottery Barn send.
  - The `2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner` asset is four years old.
  - The campaign folder is `0818_...`, which may mean template reuse.
- **Layout fragility.** `<a>` wraps `<img>` with `border="0"`, which is fine, but the sliced layout has no live text fallback.
- **Google Fonts `<link>`** (Montserrat) is unsupported in Gmail and Outlook. It falls back silently, which is acceptable but gives no fallback stack in the visible source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No unresolved merge tokens (`%%`, `{{ }}`, `[[ ]]`) were found in the visible source. The recipient email is merged correctly into `coreemail.html` and the svopn pixel (see PII note above). The HTML is truncated, so tokens in the body past that point are unverified.

## 5. Compliance
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) were not found.** The QA tool notes the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is inconclusive, not a confirmed defect.
- **Authentication-Results was not found**, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC are unverified. The cause is again relay capture.
- **Unsubscribe link and physical address.** The visible footer is truncated, so the in-body unsubscribe link and postal address (CAN-SPAM) could not be confirmed from this source.
- **From domain.** `e.potterybarn.com` is a dedicated sending subdomain, which is good practice.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- **Tracking.** No UTM parameters are visible, because all links are opaque `click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=` redirects. The destination, UTM mapping and landing alignment cannot be verified from this source.
- **Direct product images.** They point at `www.potterybarn.com/pbimgs/...`, which depends on the live site's image paths staying stable.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to all content and sliced images. Use `alt=""` on spacers and tracking pixels, and set a non-empty `<title>`.
2. Remove the `header(...)` PHP snippet from the `<style>` block.
3. Serve all assets over HTTPS, in particular `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, `slices_02.jpg` and the svopn pixel.
4. Stop putting plaintext email addresses in tracking and pixel query strings. Use a hashed or opaque ID instead.
5. Drop `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
6. Replace the 2022 and West Elm assets, and confirm the `0818` folder isn't a stale-template carryover.
7. For our audit pipeline, capture the raw headers (List-Unsubscribe, List-Unsubscribe-Post, Authentication-Results) from the original message. Also resolve the redirects to verify the destinations and UTMs.
