# Prepare to want it all
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-11**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "Prepare to want it all" — that's a bold subject for a "so much new" arrivals dump, and the email delivers exactly what it promises: sofa, dining table, pendant lights, vase, rugs, one after another, no discount banner in sight until you scroll way past the product parade. It's shot beautifully — that Aldine Sofa in the wood-paneled room and the Rio Dining Table with the sculptural legs are genuinely the kind of pieces I've screenshot-saved before — but it's a wall of new arrivals with zero organizing narrative, which after CB2's 8/8 fabric-swatch send (the one that actually felt like it had a point) feels like a step backward into generic catalog-dump mode.
- What worked for me was the designer-attribution thread running through it — David Ginelli on the sofa, Studio Anania on the table, David Aguirre on the pendant — CB2 clearly wants to keep riding that "Design Legends"-adjacent credibility angle from 8/10's Andreas Hansen send. But six section blocks deep with a "SHOP NEW ___" button under each one is a lot of scrolling for someone checking email on his phone during a commute, and there's no price visible until the "MORE FOR YOU" grid at the bottom.
- I'd open this because CB2's new-arrivals sends usually have at least one thing worth seeing, but I wouldn't click past the hero unless I had five free minutes to actually browse. Not forwarding it — it's not a "hey check this out" email, it's a "here's our whole new collection" email, and those read as marketing to me, not a personal recommendation.

## What's working

- Hero shot (wood-paneled room, brass sconce, that chunky corduroy-look sofa peeking in) is genuinely gorgeous and stopped my scroll.
- Designer-name callouts (Ginelli, Anania, Aguirre) give it the same design-credibility flex CB2 ran on 8/10 — feels aspirational, not discount-bin.
- "MORE FOR YOU" grid at the bottom finally shows real prices ($299–$1199), which is the first concrete data point in the whole email.
- Clean, consistent black-button CTA styling across every section — no visual clutter, easy to scan even if it's long.

## What's weak

- Six near-identical "SHOP NEW ___" sections back to back with zero price info until the very bottom — I have no idea what any of this costs until I've scrolled past a rug spread.
- No offer, no percentage off, nothing — coming right after three sale-heavy sends this week (8/9's "STEALS," 8/9's Cubs sweepstakes), this reads oddly flat by comparison.
- The "Download the app, win $1,000" and "Leave us a review, win $1,000" blocks feel bolted on and generic — Article's Ashford dining launch last week didn't need a sweepstakes crutch to hold my attention.
- No swatch or fabric-sample callout anywhere, which is disappointing after 8/8's fabric-focused send actually got me interested in the custom-upholstery angle.
- Rewards messaging ("Earn 10% Back") is buried in the footer alongside app-store badges — if I'm a Crate & Barrel family loyalty member, that should be higher up, not competing with social icons.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one hero product and its price, not six equal-weight category blocks — Wayfair's chandelier send proved a single statement piece with a specific hook outperforms a scroll-fest.
- 2. Put prices under every product shot, not just the bottom grid — I shouldn't have to scroll to the footer to find out the Aldine Sofa costs anything.
- 3. Cut the sweepstakes blocks or move them to a dedicated send — they dilute the "new collection" story and don't match CB2's design-forward positioning from the Hansen email.
- 4. Surface the 10% Back rewards line near the top if I'm a known loyalty member — right now it's indistinguishable from footer legal text.
- 5. Bring back a swatch/fabric callout like 8/8's send — that's the one recent CB2 email that felt personally relevant to my renovation, and this one drops it entirely.
