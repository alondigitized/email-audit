# New collab. Only on the app.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- Seventeen. We're at seventeen CB2 emails since mid-May, and they've already burned one today on the "MIDSEASON SALE" all-caps blast. So when this one drops in the same inbox session with "New collab. Only on the app." I'm genuinely intrigued — Todd Snyder doing furniture for CB2 is actually the kind of thing that gets my attention. I own two Todd Snyder shirts and I've watched his aesthetic with interest. A menswear designer translating that restraint into a home collection called "Townhouse" is a legit pitch to a guy renovating two places right now.
- But then I open it and the first thing I see is the MIDSEASON SALE banner plastered across the top. It's the same banner from the email I already read from them today. So they took a genuinely interesting collab announcement and wrapped it in the sale creative they've been hammering for three weeks. The app-exclusive angle could be a power move — early access, the thrill of feeling inside-track — but when the CTA is "DOWNLOAD THE CB2 APP" instead of "Shop the collection," it immediately becomes a chore. I have the app. I don't want to be redirected to a download screen when I'm already in a browser reading your email.
- Verdict: mark as read, screenshot the Townhouse hero for my inspo folder, revisit the collection next time I'm actually in the CB2 app. I would not click through here. I would not forward this — I'd text a friend the Todd Snyder collab name instead.

## What's working

- **The collab itself is demographically dialed.** Todd Snyder to CB2 is a credible menswear-to-home arc, and "Townhouse" as the collection name is doing real work. This feels aimed at me.
- **The editorial photography reads well.** The moody, staged interior shot is consistent with the CB2 aesthetic I expect — dark tones, considered layering, nothing trying too hard to look aspirational.
- **Rewards block at the bottom is placed well.** The "Earn 10% Back" section is visible and relevant — I always buy on my highest-rewards card and knowing I'd stack points is a real signal for me.
- **Subject line breaks the pattern.** After "MIDSEASON SALE" in all-caps earlier today, "New collab. Only on the app." is short, lowercase, and doesn't shout. It earns the open even if the body doesn't deliver on it.
- **Product recs section is relevant.** The "More for You" strip shows a platform bed, marble side tables, a wood desk — all categories I'm actively buying for right now.

## What's weak

- **Two emails in one day is too much, and both share the same sale banner.** The MIDSEASON SALE hero at the top is copy-pasted from the earlier send. It reads as sloppy batching, not a coordinated campaign.
- **App-gating the collab is a genuine friction wall.** The hero CTA is "DOWNLOAD THE CB2 APP" — not "Shop Townhouse," not "See the full collection." If I have to leave my inbox, navigate to the App Store, and then come back, you've lost me.
- **Visual hierarchy is split three ways.** Sale banner, collab editorial, then review sweepstakes — the $1,000 review prompt feels like it was bolted on and competes with the collab announcement rather than supporting it.
- **No collab pricing visible.** The Townhouse collection is the hero but there's not a single price point in the editorial section. Pottery Barn's "Favorite dining table & chair combos" email, which I audited today, at least showed anchor prices in the hero. That tells me immediately whether I'm in the right tier.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the collab, drop the sale banner entirely.** Today's earlier email handled the sale. This send should be a clean collab announcement — no MIDSEASON SALE bar stealing the top. Article did this well last week when they let a kitchen island stand alone without stacking a sitewide offer on top of it.
- 2. **Replace "DOWNLOAD THE CB2 APP" with "Shop Townhouse Early."** The exclusivity angle is the hook — lean into early access, not the mechanics of downloading software. Then deep-link existing app users directly to the collection; everyone else gets a "Get the app + shop" two-step.
- 3. **Show one hero price.** One sofa or case piece with the price in the editorial image earns the click. Without it, I don't know if Townhouse is $400 side tables or $4,000 sectionals.
- 4. **Cut the review sweepstakes from this email.** It belongs in a dedicated send or a post-purchase flow, not competing with your most interesting collab drop in months.
- 5. **Sequence discipline.** Do not send two branded emails in the same day unless they're genuinely distinct campaigns. Wayfair and Pottery Barn are both doing this right now and it's killing their per-email weight. CB2 used to feel like the curated option in this stack.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Townhouse is here — app exclusive, early access`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `CB2 x Todd Snyder: a home collection from one of menswear's best. Shop before anyone else.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Introducing Townhouse — get it first on the CB2 app.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Unresolved developer placeholder in `<head>`:**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
This is a template comment with a typo ("IMPUT") that shipped in the live email. It's harmless to rendering but indicates the template was not fully sanitized before deployment.

**[MEDIUM] `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users from zooming text on mobile. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA).

**[MEDIUM] Wildcard `text-size-adjust: none` on all elements:**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applied globally rather than scoped to `body`. Can interfere with system accessibility font scaling across clients.

**[LOW] Non-standard mobile breakpoint:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)
```
418px is below the CB2 app's likely device targets. iPhone 14/15 (390px logical width) would trigger this, but devices at 420–600px get neither mobile nor a true desktop layout. Standard email breakpoints are 480px or 600px.

**[LOW] Double-slash in several `@font-face` `src` URLs:**
```css
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
```
Four of the `@font-face` rules contain `cb2.com//Styles/` (double slash). Most servers normalize this silently, but it is a malformed URL that should be corrected.
