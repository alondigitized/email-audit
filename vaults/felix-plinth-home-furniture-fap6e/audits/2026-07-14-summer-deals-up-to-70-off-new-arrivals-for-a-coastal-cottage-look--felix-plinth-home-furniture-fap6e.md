# Summer deals ☀️ UP TO 70% OFF + New arrivals for a coastal cottage look
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-two. That's where I am now with Pottery Barn in July, and I went back and confirmed it because at this point the count is the only thing making me feel like an active participant in this relationship rather than just an inbox waiting to be filled. The July 12 heart-emoji 70%-off send, the July 13 florals-and-plaids, the July 13 "up to 70% off is happening now plus decor details" — and now this, which is somehow both of those emails at once, combined into a single subject line.
- Here's what's frustrating: the coastal cottage editorial section is genuinely the right content for me right now. I'm actively sourcing for a second home. "Collected pieces and warm wood furniture, all grounded by deep indigo, natural rattan, and other comforts that turn a house into a heritage home" — that's a sentence that slows my scroll. That's Pottery Barn doing what Pottery Barn actually does well. But it's buried beneath the same 70%-off hero I've seen so many times in July that my brain has started filtering it before I consciously process the words.
- Mark as read, no click. I wouldn't forward it. If anything, the CB2 Free Design Services send earlier this week — the "3 dream bedroom scenarios" one — did this editorial work with more discipline, no discount noise, and I actually spent time with it. Pottery Barn is spending its own brand equity here.

## What's working

- **"Coastal cottage look" in the subject is the right hook** for someone actively sourcing a second home — that's specific enough to be targeting me rather than everyone.
- **The editorial body copy is strong**: "collected pieces," "warm wood," "deep indigo, natural rattan" — that's a room I can picture, which is harder to do than it looks.
- **"Recommended Just For You" product grid** signals they know something about my browse/purchase history, even if the personalization is probably shallow.
- **"Earn 10% Back in Rewards" footer module** is a quiet loyalty nudge that reminds me I have skin in the game.
- **No visible render breaks** — clean layout, images loading, text legible without zooming.

## What's weak

- **The 70%-off hero has appeared in at least four of my last five Pottery Barn opens** — it has become wallpaper; my eye jumped straight past it to the new arrivals editorial.
- **"SHOP NOW" is the laziest CTA in home retail** — even Wayfair's "$30 kitchen mats" send this week was more specific than this.
- **No delivery window, no ship date, no "in stock" signal anywhere** — for furniture, that's the difference between a browsing click and an actual purchase decision.
- **The sale hero and the new-arrivals editorial fight each other** — one says "clearance," the other says "just landed"; they're asking me to hold two incompatible shopping modes at once.
- **Twenty-two emails in fourteen days** — the cadence alone is poisoning what would otherwise be a reasonably well-executed editorial send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop the 70%-off hero entirely for this send.** Run a clean coastal cottage new arrivals email. Article pulled this exact move this week — pivoted off their July 4th sale blitz into a pure new arrivals send — and it worked because the mode shift was legible.
- 2. **Replace "SHOP NOW" with something that has a noun in it** — "Shop Coastal Arrivals," "Explore the Heritage Edit," anything that tells me what I'm clicking into.
- 3. **Anchor the editorial section to one hero product** — a specific rattan chair or indigo throw with a price and a ship date. CB2's bedroom scenarios email gave me three specific product scenarios; I knew exactly what I'd be clicking. Pottery Barn gives me a vibe.
- 4. **Add a delivery-window line to at least one product in the recommended grid** — "Ships in 3–5 weeks" is the signal that separates intent from browsing for a furniture buyer like me.
- 5. **Surface rewards math, not just the earn rate** — "You're $60 away from your next reward" makes me feel like a known customer; "Earn 10% Back" makes me feel like a prospect.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Heritage pieces and natural textures — the second-home edit you've been waiting for.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the coastal cottage look before it sells through.`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Summer Deals / Coastal Cottage" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed broken links (403), three HTTP-only image sources that will be blocked or downgraded by modern email clients, a PHP server-side code fragment leaked into a CSS `<style>` block, and pervasive missing alt text across 38+ images. Compliance headers (List-Unsubscribe, DKIM/SPF) were not observable via the AgentMail relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Credit card apply/buy CTA returns 403**
`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY...`
The Key Rewards non-member header banner link is dead. This is a revenue-bearing CTA; 403 means the Certona/Kibo personalization endpoint rejected the request at probe time. Needs resolution before send or the entire header CTA goes nowhere.

**FAIL — Cross-brand West Elm spacer image returns 403**
`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
A West Elm CDN asset appears in a Pottery Barn email. Both are Williams-Sonoma brands sharing ESP infrastructure, but the asset is on the wrong brand's domain, is HTTP-only, and is 403ing. This is a stale template fragment that was never updated for Pottery Barn.

**INFO — 24 click-tracking links skipped**
All route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` (Oracle Responsys), standard. Not probed.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP server-side code leaked into `<style>` block**
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are PHP `header()` calls inside the final `<style>` tag. Email clients parse this as invalid CSS and ignore it — no rendering breakage — but it exposes internal server-side templating details and indicates a broken build pipeline where a PHP snippet was pasted or included without rendering.

**38+ images missing `alt` text**
Affects nearly every content image: hero banners (`F26_TOP_SummerSalePartC_NoUrgency.jpg`), product grid slices (`slices_02.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), footer slices, and several product PDPs. Email clients with images off (common in B2B environments) will render blank blocks. Accessibility for screen readers is completely broken across the body.

**3 image sources over HTTP (not HTTPS)**
All will be blocked or flagged by clients enforcing HTTPS:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` (wrong brand, also 403)
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` (footer Key CC banner)
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` (2022-era asset path — likely stale)

**Open pixel exposes recipient email address in plain-text URL**
`http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
The Selligent open-tracking pixel passes the recipient's email address as a URL query param over HTTP. This is both an HTTP issue (blocked by clients) and a privacy concern — the address is visible in server logs, proxies, and referrer chains.

**Campaign/season naming mismatch in asset filename**
`F26_TOP_SummerSalePartC_NoUrgency.jpg` — the `F26` prefix (Fall 2026) in a Summer sale email suggests a recycled or mis-named asset. Not a functional break, but a production hygiene signal.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` — no impact on email rendering, but some clients and preview tools surface this.
