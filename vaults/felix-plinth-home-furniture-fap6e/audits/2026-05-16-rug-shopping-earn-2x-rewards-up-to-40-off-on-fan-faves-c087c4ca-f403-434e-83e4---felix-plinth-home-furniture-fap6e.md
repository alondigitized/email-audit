# Rug shopping? Earn 2x Rewards + Up to 40% off on fan-faves!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- I already got a Crate & Kids email this morning — the "Up to 50% off + 2x Rewards" one that hit my inbox earlier today and scored well on the rewards math alone. So when this one dropped with the exact same sender badge, my brain did the involuntary "oh, another one" thing before I even read the subject. That said, rugs stopped me. I'm literally mid-renovation on two properties at the same time, and rugs are genuinely on my list for both. The 40% off plus 2x Rewards combination is the kind of stacked incentive that makes me pull out the spreadsheet.
- But here's the problem: this is Crate & *Kids*. The lifestyle imagery I'm seeing leans toward children's rooms — patterned, playful, primary-color-forward rug tiles in the grid. That's not the vibe I'm shopping for in the primary home's living room. The second home's kids' room, maybe, but that's a narrow slice of the ask. And "fan-faves" in the subject line is just tonally off — that's parent-Instagram language, not how a grown man thinks about area rug purchases.
- Pottery Barn, for all their Memorial Day email flooding, at least speaks to me like I'm buying furniture for a real adult room. This one makes me feel like an accidental subscriber. I'd open it — rugs are live in my consideration set — but I'd bounce fast if the grid is all toddler-friendly. Mark as read, no forward.

## What's working

- **The rug angle is genuinely purchase-relevant.** I'm shopping for rugs for two renovations. This isn't a cold pitch — it lands in an active category for me.
- **Stacked offer structure is smart.** 40% off *plus* 2x Rewards is a real compound incentive, not a soft "save big." My rewards math brain responds to this.
- **Clean layout, no render issues.** The hero image flows into a product grid without anything breaking. No overlapping text, no phantom whitespace. Basic bar, but Wayfair has been failing it with emoji-stuffed subjects all week.
- **Memorial Day anchor is clear.** The seasonal hook is visible and coherent — not some vague "shop now" that could run any day of the year.

## What's weak

- **Second email from this exact sender today.** The earlier Crate & Kids send already played the 2x Rewards card. Back-to-back from the same sub-brand in one day makes me feel managed, not courted.
- **"Fan-faves" is doing the brand no favors with me.** That phrasing signals I'm in the kids-gifting funnel, not the home-renovation funnel. It reads cute when I need to read credible.
- **Product grid skews kids-room, not adult-room.** The rugs I can see in the tiles are pattern-heavy and colorful in ways that work for a 4-year-old's room, not a dining room renovation. If the mix is different deeper in the email, the hero is hiding it.
- **No size or material signal anywhere visible.** For a rug purchase — especially over 5x8 — I need at least a hint of pile height, fiber, or dimensions to click through. Article's welcome email did zero selling and still made me trust the brand more than this product grid does on spec.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment adult rugs vs. kids rugs into separate sends.** This is the single highest-leverage fix. The 40% off Memorial Day hook works for both audiences — but they need different product heroes and different voice. Right now I'm getting a kids catalog wrapper around what should be a renovation-focused rug event.
- 2. **Throttle the cadence to one send per day from each sub-brand.** Crate & Barrel and Crate & Kids should not both be in my inbox on the same day with the same rewards mechanic. Pottery Barn figured this out at send five and six — they haven't. Space it: one brand, one offer, one day.
- 3. **Add a single specific product as the hero with a visible spec.** Pick one rug — a 8x10 in a neutral colorway at 40% off — anchor the email on that item with dimensions visible. That's what I'd actually forward to my designer.
- 4. **Kill "fan-faves."** Replace with category language: "rugs your rooms need" or even just "our most-loved rugs." It's a three-word fix that shifts the tone from children's catalog to trusted home brand.

## Technical Audit — Crate & Kids "Rug Sale / 2x Rewards" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound for a table-based HTML mailer but ships with four broken webfont references (403s), a production template comment with a typo, and blanket missing `alt` attributes across all 45+ images. Compliance header capture failed entirely via the AgentMail relay, leaving unsubscribe and authentication signals unverifiable.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Broken webfonts (403) — functional degradation**
All four webfont files hosted on `crateandbarrel.com` return HTTP 403. Email clients that attempt to load them will silently fall back to the stack declared in CSS (or system defaults). The email will render, but not with the intended typeface.

```
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/1e07d600-3787-4517-bd3d-b140f9e54ec2.woff2
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/7ec25fd5-2752-4d84-b307-af3faabc21bb.woff2
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/aaa41944-25b3-4265-80b2-5564efe9b2d7.woff2
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/fd5f8f50-d539-4a7d-b893-99501d746ef2.woff2
```

Likely cause: the web CDN serving `/webfonts/` enforces a same-origin or referer policy that blocks email client requests. Fix: serve fonts from a CDN path that allows cross-origin or embed as base64 data URIs in a `<style>` block.

**22 tracked click links skipped**
All CTA and product links pass through a click-redirect domain. The QA probe could not confirm final destinations or UTM parameter presence (see §6).

**Third-party tracking pixels**
Three distinct pixel domains fire on open:
- `mi.crateandbarrel.com` (MessageGears ESP — open pixel + `rp/` render pixel, fires 6×)
- `sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif` (LiveRamp/Acxiom — data enrichment pixel, fires 5×)
- `dv.crateandbarrel.com` (separate analytics endpoint)

The LiveRamp pixel fires 5 times with incremented `?n=1…5` parameters. This is by design for LiveRamp's resolution pipeline but is worth noting as a data-sharing disclosure item.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` viewport restriction**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom in mobile browsers that respect this meta tag. This is an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4) and is blocked by iOS/Safari since version 10, but still affects some Android WebView contexts.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied globally**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```
Overrides the user's OS-level font size preferences. The safer value is `100%`, which prevents auto-scaling without stripping user control.

**45 images missing `alt` text**
Every content image (product shots, lifestyle images) and every tracking pixel carries no `alt` attribute at all. Content images should have descriptive `alt`; tracking pixels and decorative images should have `alt=""` explicitly. Current state means screen readers will announce raw filenames like `bbf6f9d4-86cc-437a-aa80-391067b74306.png`.

**Production template comment with typo**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
"IMPUT" is a misspelling of "INPUT." This is a scaffolding comment from the email builder that was never replaced or removed before send. No functional impact, but it exposes the templating workflow in the source.

**`<title>` brand mismatch**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
Sender is "Crate & Kids SALE" but the document title is the parent brand. Minor, but visible in some webmail clients that display the document title.

**Conflicting desktop `img` max-width rules**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
The 640px rule is declared after the 768px rule, so at ≥768px both apply and the 640px cap wins via cascade order. Net effect: images cap at 640px on desktop, which is consistent with the container width. Not a rendering bug, but the 768px/600px rule is dead code.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header absent**
The `List-Unsubscribe` header was not captured by the AgentMail relay. Google and Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender requirements mandate this header for senders sending >5,000/day. If the header is present in the raw SMTP envelope but stripped by the relay, the relay configuration needs to forward all RFC 2369 headers. If it was never set by MessageGears, this is a deliverability and compliance risk.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post absent (RFC 8058 one-click)**
Gmail and Yahoo's bulk sender policy requires one-click unsubscribe via `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`. Absence means Gmail will display its own unsubscribe UI, which routes complaints back rather than list removals. Verify this header is present in the MessageGears send configuration.

**SPF/DKIM authentication unverifiable**
`Authentication-Results` was not captured. Cannot confirm SPF and DKIM pass status. The sending domain (`mail.crateandbarrel.com`) would be expected to have both. This is a relay capture gap — the raw headers need to be preserved through the pipeline.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 22 CTA links are wrapped in the MessageGears click-redirect domain. The QA probe could not follow them to verify:

Manual spot-check of 2–3 redirect chains is recommended before treating click attribution as reliable.
