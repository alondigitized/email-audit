# The backpack they’ll want to carry, starting at $39!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids, again. That's five sends now this week if I'm counting right, and this one's a kids' backpack sizing guide — small, medium, large, with a little diagram of what fits inside each one. I don't have a kid. I don't have a backpack-aged human in my house. This is the same problem as the nursery email and the toddler bedding email: someone at C&B has me bucketed as a parent, and I am not one — I'm a guy mid-gut-renovation on two houses buying consoles and media walls.
- The actual execution here isn't bad on its own terms — the size guide format is genuinely clever, breaking small/medium/large by "what fits inside" with little flat-lay graphics is a smart way to help an actual parent make a decision fast. But it's wasted on me. Somewhere buried at the bottom there's a "500+ new fall arrivals starting at $29" banner and a sweepstakes for $500, which are the only two things in this whole email even adjacent to something I might click, and they're crammed in after four screens of backpacks.
- Not opening this again, not forwarding it — there's no friend in my life who needs a backpack size guide from me. This one's an immediate scroll-past-and-archive.

## What's working

- The small/medium/large sizing breakdown with "what fits inside" icon lists is a legitimately useful format — if I were shopping for a kid, this removes real guesswork.
- Clean, consistent product photography — backpacks laid flat and on-body so you can judge scale.
- The "make a bundle" cross-sell (bundle + backpack + back-to-school) is a smart AOV play for the right audience.
- No render bugs — everything loaded, nothing overlapping, straightforward top-to-bottom scroll.

## What's weak

- Wrong audience, full stop — I have no kids, and this is the third or fourth kids-category email this week (nursery makeover, kids/toddler/baby bedding, now backpacks). C&B's parent-vs-not signal is clearly not being used at all.
- The one relevant line in the whole email — "500+ new fall arrivals starting at $29" — is buried two-thirds down, after four separate backpack-size sections I'll never act on.
- Subject promises "starting at $39" but I have to scroll past a wall of backpacks before any price shows up — no hero offer visible without scrolling.
- The $500 sweepstakes and the "get the app" footer feel like leftover template filler competing with the actual backpack content for attention.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment me out of Crate & Kids sends entirely, or at minimum suppress this category if I've never clicked a kids item in months of sends — this is a targeting fix, not a creative fix, and it's the single biggest lever here.
- 2. If I am staying on this list, lead with the adult-relevant strip — the "500+ new fall arrivals starting at $29" banner should be the hero, not buried after four backpack-size sections.
- 3. Cut the redundant size-guide repetition for anyone who isn't actively parenting-and-shopping — one clear CTA to a size-guide landing page beats three full sections of near-identical content.
- 4. Pottery Barn's "Warm & Welcoming Living Room" send (8/10 in my inbox) works because it puts one clear room-based hero right up top — C&B could borrow that discipline instead of stacking four sub-sections before the offer.
