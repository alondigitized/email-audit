# Blast off to fun with an outer-space bedroom!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-20**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids, again — this is now at least the fifth "kids" email hitting my inbox this month from the Crate & Barrel family, and I'm still childless. That said, I'll be honest: this is the best-looking one of the bunch. The space-themed bedroom — astronaut helmet lamp, navy bedding, cosmic wallpaper, that woven star-shaped hanging chair — is genuinely well-shot and cohesive. If I had a kid, this is a room I'd actually want to build.
- But it's an endless-scroll email. It just keeps going — bedding, then furniture, then decor, then rugs, then backpacks, then a Halloween teaser, then a sweepstakes, then a credit card pitch, then an app download push. By the time I hit "TheDesignDesk" booking CTA at the bottom I'd completely forgotten what the subject line promised me.
- I'm not clicking, and I'm not forwarding — not because it's bad, but because it's not for me. It goes straight to archived/read. If a friend with kids mentioned redoing a bedroom, sure, I might dig this back up, but it's not compelling enough to interrupt my day and send unprompted.

## What's working

- The astronaut-helmet lamp and space wallpaper hero shot is a strong, specific visual hook — better production value than the Stokke Tripp Trapp email from 2026-09-20, which leaned on one hero product.
- Color palette is tight and consistent — navy, cream, warm wood tones carry through every section, unlike Wayfair's rocking chair email that promised one thing and showed a random pink chair.
- The woven star/moon hanging chair is a genuinely covetable piece — that's the kind of "I didn't know I wanted this" object CB2's "Now & Future Classics" email nailed with its furniture shots.
- Multiple clear "Shop Now" CTAs throughout so I always know what to tap if something catches my eye.

## What's weak

- It's way too long — this reads like four separate campaigns (bedroom, decor, backpacks, Halloween, sweepstakes, credit card) stitched into one email. Pottery Barn's cozy-throws send stayed tightly on-theme; this one sprawls.
- The Halloween teaser and the credit card offer feel bolted onto a space-bedroom email with zero visual transition — jarring.
- "Star Gear" backpack section has nothing to do with bedroom furniture — feels like inventory-clearing, not curation.
- Same fatigue problem as the $500 gift card and Tripp Trapp emails before it: I don't have kids, so no amount of polish here converts me.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this to one campaign. Ship the space bedroom as its own tight email (hero + 3-4 products) and send Halloween/sweepstakes/credit card as separate sends — this is at least three emails wearing a trench coat.
- 2. Move the astronaut helmet lamp and star chair higher — they're the most distinctive pieces and currently buried mid-scroll behind generic desk shots.
- 3. Add a rewards/loyalty mention near the top if I'm a My Place Rewards member — nothing here acknowledges me as a repeat subscriber.
- 4. Drop "Star Gear" backpacks entirely from a bedroom-furniture email, or clearly separate it as "also trending."
