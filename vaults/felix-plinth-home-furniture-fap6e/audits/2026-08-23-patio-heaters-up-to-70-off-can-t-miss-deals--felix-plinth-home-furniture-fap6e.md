# PATIO HEATERS ⭐ UP TO 70% OFF ⭐ CAN’T-MISS DEALS ⭐ 
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-23**
## Executive summary

- Subject says "patio heaters," and I had to scroll past a soaking tub, a stockpot, a kitchen sink, a chair, and a whole "Labor Day decor 30% off" tile before that heater showed up again anywhere — it's literally the first product photo under "picked just for you" and then never mentioned again. This is the same move Wayfair pulled yesterday with "On sale now: pergolas," except that one at least kept circling back to pergola and gazebo tiles a few times. Here it's a bait headline stapled onto the exact same generic "70% OFF LABOR DAY CLEARANCE" mega-grid I got from them Tuesday and from their own sibling send with the duvet inserts subject that also went nowhere near duvets.
- Nothing here is renovation-relevant to me specifically — I'm knee-deep in two houses right now, and a random stockpot, a basketball hoop, and a plant mister tile aren't helping me pick tile or lighting for either one. It reads like Wayfair fired off "whatever's on clearance" and slapped a hot-ticket seasonal keyword on top to get the open.
- Judgment: not clicking, not forwarding. Marking as read and moving on — this is the fourth near-identical "X% OFF" grid from this brand family this week and my eyes have stopped landing on any of it.

## What's working

- The top banner is at least honest about the number — "70% OFF LABOR DAY CLEARANCE" is big, red, and unmissable the second the email opens.
- Star ratings and were-now pricing show up on individual product tiles (the tub, the stockpot, the chair), which is more transparent than Crate & Kids' vague "vintage vibes" pitch with zero pricing.
- The Afterpay 0% APR banner is a real, useful trust cue for anyone financing a full furniture haul — that's a genuinely renovation-relevant detail CB2 and Article never bother to include.

## What's weak

- The subject promises patio heaters; the email delivers one heater photo and then a bathtub, a stockpot, and a kitchen sink — worse bait-and-switch than their own pergola send.
- Zero swatch/sample program mention, zero shipping-timeline detail for furniture — the one thing that actually matters when I'm trying to sequence two renovations, and Pottery Barn's "Beautiful Foundations" send at least named real collections instead of a clearance dump.
- Visual hierarchy is just tile-after-tile of unrelated categories (rugs, mattresses, basketballs, decor bins) — nothing signals "this is for the guy furnishing two houses," it signals "this is for anyone with a Wayfair account."
- This is the same red/white/blue Labor Day clearance shell I already opened twice this week from Wayfair — the fatigue is real at this point.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the subject match the content — if it's a generic clearance blast, call it that; don't cannibalize trust by promising patio heaters and burying it.
- 2. Lead the hero grid with 3-4 actual patio/outdoor heater SKUs since that's what got me to open, then transition into the broader sale.
- 3. Add a shipping/delivery-window callout for furniture items — that's the actual friction point for someone mid-renovation, not the 30th mattress banner.
- 4. Cut the category grab-bag (basketballs, stockpots, plant misters) from a subject-targeted send — split into a real "outdoor living" segment vs. general clearance.
