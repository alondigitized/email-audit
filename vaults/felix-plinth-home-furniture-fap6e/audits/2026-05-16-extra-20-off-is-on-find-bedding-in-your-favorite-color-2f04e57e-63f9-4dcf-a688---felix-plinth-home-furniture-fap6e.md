# Extra 20% off is ON + Find bedding in your favorite color
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- Seven. That's how many Pottery Barn emails I've gotten in roughly 72 hours. I know because I went back and counted after this one landed. The cadence has become background radiation at this point — my brain auto-filters "Pottery Barn Sale" the way it filters HVAC noise. That said, I opened it. Not because the subject hooked me, but because I'm literally mid-renovation on two properties and I do need to make bedding decisions before the guest suite goes live.
- The email itself is actually well-constructed. The color-grid treatment for "Layers of Color" is genuinely smart — showing me bedding by color theme rather than by SKU is how I actually shop. When I'm standing in a half-finished room with a paint swatch taped to the wall, I'm not searching "king duvet insert"; I'm searching "what goes with Sherwin-Williams Evergreen Fog." Pottery Barn is one of the few brands that gets that.
- The "Recommended Just for You" furniture section at the bottom is where it gets interesting — there's a sectional and what looks like a sideboard that I'd actually want to look at for the primary. But it's buried below the fold after a long bedding scroll, and by the time I get there, I'm fatigued. I'd mark it read and come back later. Maybe.

## What's working

- **Color-first merchandising.** The grid layout organized by color (SHOP GREEN, SHOP BLUE, etc.) maps exactly to how I browse when I'm matching rooms. Article has done similar things but never for bedding; Pottery Barn lands it well here.
- **20% off is front and center.** No hunting for the discount — the hero banner leads with it, and the SHOP NOW button is unambiguous.
- **"More Bedding Essentials" category row** (sheets, duvets, duvet inserts, blankets) is clean navigation I can actually use without clicking around.
- **Memorial Day framing is consistent** with what I've been seeing all week, so the sale feels real and not manufactured.

## What's weak

- **Bedding is not where my head is at right now.** I need a dining table, a rug for the secondary living room, and outdoor seating. Bedding is round three. The "Recommended Just for You" furniture at the bottom is more urgent for me — and it's treated as an afterthought.
- **"Your favorite color" is not personalization.** I have no color preference on file with Pottery Barn. This reads as a mail-merge placeholder that shipped without data. Crate & Barrel pulls this move too — calling something "personalized" when it's just a generic category hook is mildly insulting to anyone who's been shopping the brand for years.
- **No specific deadline visible.** The prior send ("Our Memorial Day Sale STARTS NOW") had a clearer urgency frame. This one says the extra 20% is "ON" without telling me when it turns off. That's fine for casual browsers, not for someone actively comparing before a purchasing window closes.
- **This is email seven in three days.** No single email gets to ignore that. The cadence is doing real damage to the brand signal — this would score two points higher in a vacuum.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the furniture module, not the bedding.** If "Recommended Just for You" is actually powered by my browse/purchase history, that section should be email-first, not scroll-buried. Put the sideboard and sectional above the color grid.
- 2. **Give me a deadline.** "Extra 20% off ends Monday at midnight" would double the urgency without adding words. The prior Memorial Day launch email did this better.
- 3. **Replace "your favorite color" with actual behavior data.** If I've been browsing greens and earth tones, show me the green grid first. Wayfair does this clumsily but at least they try. Pottery Barn has the data — use it.
- 4. **Trim the send cadence.** Batch the bedding and the essentials row into one email. I'd rather get one dense, curated send than four partial ones in 24 hours. Article's welcome email proved one well-composed email outperforms four fragmented ones.
- 5. **Surface material/thread count details in the grid.** For bedding at this price point, I want to know if it's percale or sateen before I click through. One line under the swatch.

- **Subject Alt B:** `The color guide for your guest room — 20% off this weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop by room palette — greens, blues, neutrals, and more.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Match your paint swatch. Extra 20% off all bedding ends soon.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed broken links (HTTP 403) block a revenue-generating CTA and a cross-brand image asset. The HTML also contains PHP `header()` calls injected verbatim into a CSS `<style>` block — dead code from a template copy-paste error — alongside pervasive missing alt text and three mixed-content (HTTP) image sources.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Credit card apply CTA returns 403**
`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&external_id=PB_header_nonmember&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember&...`
The "Apply and Buy" banner link is dead. This is a non-member acquisition CTA — a 403 means the destination URL was either expired, misconfigured in the click-tracking system, or the campaign parameter block is malformed (the `cp_tp` value is a very long base64 blob that may have been truncated or double-encoded).

**[FAIL] Cross-brand spacer image returns 403**
`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
A West Elm CDN asset embedded in a Pottery Barn email is both broken and a mixed-content HTTP reference. This is a template hygiene failure — the asset domain belongs to a sibling brand.

**22 tracking links not probed**
All click-through links route through `click.e.potterybarn.com`. Link health beyond the two confirmed failures is unverified.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP code injected into CSS `<style>` block**
Inside the final `<style>` tag in `<head>`, four PHP `header()` calls appear as literal text:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are inert in HTML — no browser or email client executes them — but they are invalid CSS that will cause parse errors in strict renderers. This is a copy-paste error from a server-side image-generation script.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` — some email clients (e.g., Apple Mail) display the `<title>` as the window/tab label when viewing in browser. Screen readers also use it.

**50+ images missing `alt` text**
The entire `BeddingColorStory1` slice set (slices_01 through slices_19), all Email-Footer slices (slices_01 through slices_18), plus product images and the Memorial Day hero (`S26_Top_MemorialDayNoUrgency.jpg`) have no `alt` attribute. Images-off rendering (common in Outlook, corporate environments) produces a completely blank email body.

**Three HTTP image sources (mixed content)**
```
http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif         ← also broken
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg
http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...  ← open-tracking pixel
```
HTTP sources are blocked by default in Gmail, Apple Mail, and most modern clients when the parent page is loaded over HTTPS. The open-tracking pixel being HTTP is particularly problematic — it will silently fail to fire in many clients, understating open rates.

**`maximum-scale=1` and `text-size-adjust: none`**
`<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` prevents pinch-to-zoom. Combined with `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}` applied globally, this fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) — a meaningful accessibility regression for low-vision users.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
Not captured by the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Yahoo enforce this header for bulk senders (>5k/day); absence risks inbox demotion or bulk-folder routing. Cannot confirm whether Pottery Barn's MTA injects it post-relay — this should be verified against raw headers at the MTA boundary.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent**
One-click unsubscribe not confirmed. Required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy since February 2024.

**`Authentication-Results` header absent**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status unknown due to AgentMail relay interception. Sender domain is `e.potterybarn.com` (via `PotteryBarn@e.potterybarn.com`); alignment with DMARC policy for `potterybarn.com` cannot be confirmed from available data.

**Open-tracking pixel over HTTP**
`http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=...`
Functionally this may undercount opens and also transmits the subscriber email address over a plain HTTP request — a minor data hygiene issue.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**`no-referrer` meta tag suppresses click referrer data**
```html
<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />
```
Any click-through to potterybarn.com will arrive with `Referrer: (none)`. GA4 and Adobe Analytics will attribute these sessions to Direct rather than Email. UTM params in the destination URLs (if present) would still work, but referrer-based attribution and session stitching are broken by this tag.

**Plain-text version is 72% URLs**
10,757 of 14,959 characters in the plain-text part are URLs. Spam filters (SpamAssassin, Proofpoint) penalize high URL density in plain-text. The plain-text version appears to be auto-generated from HTML without pruning tracking URLs, producing an unreadable wall of links.

**Cross-brand CDN dependency**
`edm.westelm.com` is referenced for a spacer asset. This creates a runtime dependency on a sibling brand's CDN for delivery of a Pottery Barn email — a fragile coupling that produced a visible failure here.
