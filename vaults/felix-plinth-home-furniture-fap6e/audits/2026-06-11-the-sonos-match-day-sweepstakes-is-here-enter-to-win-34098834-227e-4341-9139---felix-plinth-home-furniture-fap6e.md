# The Sonos Match-Day Sweepstakes is here! Enter to win →
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-nine. That's roughly where I am with Crate & Barrel, and I need you to understand what it means to be looking at email thirty-nine when you've gotten eight in a single day twice this week. So when a Sonos sweepstakes drops in my inbox from C&B, my first instinct isn't excitement — it's suspicion. *Why is a furniture brand running a Sonos giveaway?* And then I look at the living room hero shot and it actually clicks: Match-Day, Sonos sound system, the whole "winning setup" positioning. That's legitimately clever. I'm renovating two homes simultaneously and I've been pricing out whole-home audio, so Sonos is not a miss for me. It's one of the smarter non-furniture pivots I've seen from this brand.
- The problem is I don't enter sweepstakes. I buy things with my Citi Double Cash and collect points. There's no SKU link, no 15% off code, no rewards angle on this. The furniture sections below the hero are fine — patio dining set, a few seating options — but they're clearly secondary to the sweepstakes mechanic, and none of them are surfaced with enough offer specificity to pull me down the page.
- I'd mark it read and move on. Probably not forward it either, unless a friend mentioned they were in the market for Sonos. The concept is the best thing C&B has mailed me in two weeks, but it's wrapped in a format that doesn't match how I actually shop.

## What's working

- **The Sonos tie-in is genuinely smart.** Home audio during a sports/match event makes sense, and it lands well for someone buying into a new space.
- **"Bring home the winning setup" is the strongest headline I've seen from C&B this whole run.** It connects the product, the occasion, and the aspiration in five words.
- **The hero living room shot is clean and aspirational** — neutral sofa, calm palette, tasteful. It reads like a room I'd actually put together. Closer to Article's editorial quality than the chaotic back-to-school send from yesterday.
- **No render bugs.** After the rug email that duplicated twice in one day, even flawless rendering feels like a win worth noting.
- **The outdoor dining section lower in the email is genuinely relevant** — I'm building out a second-home patio and Crate & Barrel's outdoor furniture is on my shortlist alongside Pottery Barn's quick-ship outdoor collection.

## What's weak

- **Sweepstakes are not how I buy anything.** I max my rewards card on actual purchases. A giveaway entry gives me zero points, zero serotonin, zero utility. Wayfair's HOT SUMMER SALE email at least lets me add something to my cart.
- **No price, no discount, no rewards hook.** For a guy who tracks which card gives 3x on home goods, "enter to win" is a dead end.
- **The furniture below the fold is underbaked** — small images, no offer callout, no urgency. Pottery Barn's "Quick-ship furniture" email does a better job surfacing availability details that matter when you're racing a renovation timeline.
- **No deadline on the sweepstakes.** "Is here!" in the subject implies freshness but I can't tell when it ends. CB2 at least tends to put "ends Sunday" in their offer rails.
- **Thirty-nine emails.** That's not an in-email problem, but it colors everything I read from this sender now.

## Recommendations

- **1. Add a purchase-entry mechanic.** The actual lever here: "Make any $X purchase this week and get 5x sweepstakes entries." Tie the giveaway to a transaction and now I have a reason to shop AND a reason to care about the Sonos prize. Crate & Barrel has my email because I buy stuff from them — use that.
- **2. Surface one piece of furniture with offer specificity.** Pick the patio dining set and call it out: "Quick-ship for summer delivery" or "20% off outdoor seating." That's the Pottery Barn playbook and it works on me.
- **3. Add a sweepstakes close date.** Even "Enter by June 20" creates credible urgency. Right now it feels indefinite.
- **4. The subject line is doing too much.** Two sentences, one arrow, one exclamation point. Pick a lane.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Enter to win a Sonos system — Match-Day giveaway`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Win a Sonos setup. Enter before the final whistle.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus shop furniture deals made for your game-day space.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `A Sonos home theater setup — yours if you enter by June 20.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`[HIGH]` Development placeholder shipped to production**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
"IMPUT" is a typo for "INPUT." This is an unfilled template comment that reached subscribers — a production hygiene failure.

**`[MEDIUM]` Conflicting desktop `img` max-width media queries**
Two desktop breakpoints both set `img { max-width }` with overlapping ranges:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
The 768px rule is declared first and then the 640px rule is declared second. Because both fire at ≥768px and specificity is equal, the **last rule wins** — meaning images on screens ≥768px get `max-width: 640px`, not 600px. The ordering should be reversed (largest breakpoint last) or merged into a single rule.

**`[MEDIUM]` `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
WCAG 1.4.4 requires content to be zoomable to 200%. `maximum-scale=1` prevents this on iOS. Remove or raise to `maximum-scale=5`.

**`[LOW]` Global `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` override**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```
This overrides the body's `100%` value and disables the browser's automatic text scaling for small fonts — a regression for low-vision users on mobile.

**`[LOW]` Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
`telephone=no`, `address=no`, and `date=no` are each declared twice — once in a combined tag and once individually. Harmless, but signals template drift.

**`[LOW]` Dark mode not supported**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Email explicitly opts out of dark mode with no `prefers-color-scheme: dark` override. No issue if intentional, but Apple Mail and Gmail on iOS will not adapt.
