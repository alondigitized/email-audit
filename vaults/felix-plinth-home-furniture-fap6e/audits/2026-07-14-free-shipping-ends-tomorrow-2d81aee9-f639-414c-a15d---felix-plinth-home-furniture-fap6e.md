# FREE SHIPPING ends tomorrow!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- Forty. That's the number I'm going to say before anything else, because that's where the C&B family now sits in my July inbox — and the only reason I know is that I've been keeping the tally. This is Crate & Kids, the children's sub-brand, arriving with the exact same "FREE SHIPPING ends tomorrow!" hook that ran through the subject line earlier today. Not last week. Today.
- Here's the fundamental problem: I'm renovating two homes simultaneously. Adult homes. Sofas, lighting, dining tables, outdoor pieces — I'm in market for real volume. Crate & Kids is a children's brand. The hero image is a plush ivory chair that reads like a stuffed animal crossed with a club chair — delightful if you have a nursery to fill, but I don't. Then I get to "500+ new fall arrivals, starting at $29" — in July — and I start wondering if the campaign planner and the calendar manager are in the same Slack channel. Article dropped a summer entryway editorial four days ago, timed perfectly to the season. This brand is already clearing summer to make room for fall, and it's ninety degrees outside.
- I archived it. Not spam — I know Crate & Kids is a brand I opted into somewhere in the C&B ecosystem — but I didn't click, didn't forward, moved on. The one thing that genuinely caught my eye was the CB2 cross-promotion buried in the footer, which is the most relevant content in the entire email for me, and also the last thing I almost scrolled past.

## What's working

- Free shipping urgency is real and clean — "ends tomorrow" is credible, visible in the hero, requires no fine-print hunting.
- Visual hierarchy holds together: logo → hero offer → category nav → editorial section → product grid. My eye moved top to bottom without friction.
- The CB2 and TheDesignDesk cross-promotions at the bottom are actually the most valuable content for a renovator like me — someone on the C&B team understood the suite, even if they buried it.
- No render bugs — images loaded, text didn't break, layout didn't collapse. At this send cadence, that's worth noting.

## What's weak

- This is Crate & Kids. I'm renovating adult spaces. The brand mismatch was the first thing I felt before reading a single word of copy.
- "500+ new fall arrivals, starting at $29" — it's mid-July. Article waited for the right seasonal window on their last send and scored accordingly; this reads like impatience, not strategy.
- "FREE SHIPPING ends tomorrow!" appeared in my inbox earlier today with this identical subject from the same sender family. Wayfair runs high volume too, but they vary the hook — they don't serve me the same urgency play twice in one inbox session.
- The "we love these for you" personalized grid appears to be surfacing cribs and kids bins. If the algorithm has enough purchase data to personalize, it knows I've never bought children's furniture. This isn't personalization — it's a batch rec with a personalization header on top.
- Every CTA is "Shop Now" — not one specific product linked anywhere in the stack. CB2's "3 dream bedroom scenarios" from earlier this week gave me a landing page with an imagination. This gives me a category bucket.

## Recommendations

- 1. Suppress accounts with zero kids-purchase history from Crate & Kids sends, or flip the editorial to cross-category pieces (rugs, lighting, storage) that can serve adult spaces — the overlap exists, the targeting doesn't.
- 2. Pull CB2 and TheDesignDesk above the fold for any account that has shopped adult home categories; that content is the only reason a renovator clicks.
- 3. If this is a resend of an earlier same-day subject, differentiate it: a bestseller callout, a sold-out warning on a hero SKU, a countdown — anything that evolves the narrative rather than refiles the same urgency.
- 4. Drop the all-caps "FREE SHIPPING" — it reads like spam bait, which is precisely not the positioning Crate & Barrel's family brands should want.
- 5. Hold "fall arrivals" language until August at the earliest. The 500+ SKU count is impressive; the premature timing undercuts it.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Kids, nursery, and the rooms in between — everything ships free through tomorrow`

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks pinch-to-zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 failure. Remove or raise to `maximum-scale=5`.

**Global text-size-adjust suppression** (confirmed):
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Disables OS-level font scaling for every element. Combined with `maximum-scale=1`, mobile users with vision impairments have no recourse to increase text size.

**Conflicting `img max-width` media queries** (confirmed):
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
On screens ≥768px both rules apply; the `600px` declaration wins (later in cascade), making the `640px` rule dead on desktop. Consolidate to one breakpoint.

Email originates from Crate & Kids. Browser-based clients and screen readers announce "Crate & Barrel." Change to `<title>Crate & Kids</title>`.
