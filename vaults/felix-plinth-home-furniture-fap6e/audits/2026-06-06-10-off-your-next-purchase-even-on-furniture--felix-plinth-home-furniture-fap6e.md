# 10% off your next purchase—even on furniture.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- Ten. That's the C&B family inbox count in two days — and I say "family" because Crate & Barrel, Crate & Kids, and CB2 have collectively turned my inbox into a permanent C&B annexe. This one opens with a split personality: there's a top banner promising up to 60% off a summer clearance event, and then the hero pivot-pivots to "get on the list & get 10% off." Those are two different offers competing for the same eyeball in the first three seconds, and I had to read both twice to figure out which one was the actual point of the email.
- The "even on furniture" phrasing in the subject is genuinely the best thing about this send. I'm mid-renovation on two properties. Furniture discounts land differently for me right now. But then the hero tells me to "get on the list" — and I'm already on the list. I'm reading this email. The framing makes me feel like I'm seeing a subscriber-acquisition welcome flow that wandered into my inbox by mistake. Father's Day urgency (order by 6/15) is timely and real, but it's buried below the confusing hero. The product rec grid — a red KitchenAid stand mixer, what looks like outdoor seating, and an espresso machine — is scattershot.
- I'd mark this as read, not click. And I wouldn't forward it. Compare this to Pottery Barn's Summer Warehouse Sale email from yesterday that I scored 9/10 — that one had a clear hero, one offer, one CTA, and zero ambiguity about what I was supposed to do. C&B needed to pick a lane here.

## What's working

- **"Even on furniture" in the subject** — smart hook for someone actively shopping for two homes; it signals scope that generic "% off" doesn't.
- **Father's Day deadline (6/15)** — the order cutoff is real urgency and it's timely; this is the first week of June and it actually matters.
- **No render bugs** — the email lays out cleanly; images load, text doesn't bleed or overlap.
- **Summer clearance banner** — 60% off sitting in the top strip is a compelling secondary pull if you scroll past the hero confusion.

## What's weak

- **"Get on the list" copy is nonsensical to an existing subscriber** — I'm reading the email, I'm on the list; this reads like a re-engagement or acquisition piece aimed at the wrong segment.
- **Two competing offers in the first scroll** — 60% clearance vs. 10% list signup is confusing; pick one to lead with.
- **Product rec grid is unfocused** — a red KitchenAid mixer, outdoor seating, and an espresso machine is not a coherent story; Article's outdoor email last week had a tighter product narrative on a narrower SKU set.
- **App download module** — noise when I'm trying to process a discount offer; CB2's "MIDSEASON SALE" email (7/10) had zero app friction and was still lean.
- **Cadence** — this is email ten in two days from the C&B umbrella. I'm not annoyed at this email specifically, but my goodwill tank is running low.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse to a single offer and CTA.** Lead with the 60% clearance or the 10% loyalty perk — not both. The two-banner/two-message structure is the single biggest lift available.
- 2. **Rewrite the hero to acknowledge I'm already a subscriber.** "Your 10% member discount — yes, even on furniture" lands completely differently than "get on the list."
- 3. **Tighten the product rec grid to furniture and outdoor only.** For renovation-mode buyers, a KitchenAid in a furniture email is a distraction. Wayfair segments better by intent signal.
- 4. **Move Father's Day up.** The 6/15 deadline is real urgency — it should be in the top half, not buried below the confusing hero.
- 5. **Kill the app download module or move it to the footer.** It's competing with the conversion moment.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 10% member discount is live — furniture included`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Furniture, lighting, outdoor — 10% off. Here's your code.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Order by June 15 for Father's Day delivery — shop all categories at 10% off.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus: summer clearance up to 60% off while it lasts.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
This prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile, violating WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Should be removed or changed to `maximum-scale=5`.

**Issue — aggressive global text-size-adjust override:**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applied via the universal selector in the MI base styles. This suppresses OS-level font scaling for users with visual accessibility needs. Standard practice is `100%` on `body`, not `none` on `*`.

**Issue — duplicate `format-detection` meta tags:**
The combined tag and three individual tags create redundancy and could cause parsing inconsistency in older WebKit clients:
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<!-- ...then later: -->
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
The duplicates (date, address, telephone) should be removed; the combined tag is sufficient.
