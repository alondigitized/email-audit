# Just in. Already trending.
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-27**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is at least a CB2 email I don't hate. Thirty-eight — that's where the counter lands now, one after the "STEALS OF THE WEEK" roundup I gave a 7 two days ago — and this one actually breaks the pattern instead of repeating it. It's a proper new-arrivals drop with named designers (Gianfranco Frattini, Studio Aparici, David Grelli) instead of another generic "UP TO 40% OFF EVERYTHING" blast. I'm mid-reno on two houses right now, so "just in, already trending" furniture with actual provenance is closer to what I want in my inbox than the fortieth flash sale banner.
- That said, I'm not clicking through the whole thing. I scrolled past six product hero blocks before I even hit the actual discount, and by the time "THE FURNITURE EVENT UP TO 40% OFF" showed up I'd already mentally checked out. It's a long email for a phone screen.
- Verdict: I'd probably tap through to the onyx table or the oak console since those are actually relevant to the primary-house dining room I'm redoing, then bounce without buying. Not forwarding this one — my Pinterest-folder-adjacent friend group has already seen enough CB2 in their own inboxes this month.

## What's working

- The designer-collab framing (MEDA by Gianfranco Frattini, RIO by Studio Aparici) feels like it's talking to someone who actually cares about furniture provenance, not just someone chasing a coupon code — a nice change of pace after CB2's usual "STEALS OF THE WEEK" grab-bag energy.
- Each product block is one hero shot, one name, one Shop Now — clean, no clutter, easy to scan on a phone.
- "EARN 10% BACK in Rewards Dollars" and the Crate & Barrel / Crate&Kids cross-brand footer logos at least acknowledge I might already have points sitting somewhere, which matters since I put everything on my highest-rewards card anyway.
- No render bugs, no broken images, nothing overlapping — clean execution top to bottom, better than the layout hiccups I've seen from lower-tier retailers.

## What's weak

- Six full product sections before the actual offer (40% off) buries the one thing that would actually get me to buy today — Pottery Barn's quilt email at least led with a clean single premise instead of a scroll marathon.
- "Just in. Already trending." tells me nothing concrete — no category, no percentage, no product. Compare that to Wayfair's subject lines, which for all their spam-caps sins ("FLASH DEALS ENDS TONIGHT") at least tell you exactly what you're getting.
- No reviews, no star ratings, no social proof anywhere on the actual products — they're asking ME to leave a review ("WIN $1,000") instead of showing me why I should trust the Denim Blue Velvet Chair is worth $1,000+.
- Zero lifestyle shots — no rooms, no people, just product-on-white/product-on-wood. Article's editorial sends at least put furniture in a room story so I can picture it in my actual house.
- This is basically the same "new pieces just dropped" beat CB2's been running all month layered on top of a sale banner — after 37+ emails I'm numb to almost any subject line from this sender.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the 40% off Furniture Event banner immediately after the hero, not after six product blocks — that's the actual reason I'd click today, and burying it costs conversions.
- 2. Cut the individual designer-spotlight blocks from six down to three and let the "MORE FOR YOU" grid (which already has real prices) do more of the work.
- 3. Add a star rating or "X reviews" line under at least the hero pieces — asking for reviews while showing zero existing ones undercuts trust.
- 4. Put one lifestyle/room shot in the hero so I can picture the onyx table or oak console somewhere real, not floating on a studio backdrop.
- 5. Tighten cadence — I'm getting near-daily CB2 sends; even a good email like this loses impact as email #38 this month.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Limited designer drops, plus up to 40% off the Furniture Event`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Frattini, Aparici, Grelli — new arrivals just landed`
