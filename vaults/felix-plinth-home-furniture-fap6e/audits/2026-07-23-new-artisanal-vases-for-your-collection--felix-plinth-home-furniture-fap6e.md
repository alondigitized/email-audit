# NEW artisanal vases for your collection
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-eight. That's where Pottery Barn lands in my July inbox, and yes, the coffee tables email already beat them to the punch this morning on the same day — same brand, same date, two swings. I want to be fair here: artisanal vases are genuinely in my orbit. I'm styling two spaces simultaneously and I do keep a "to-buy when the furniture's in" holding folder for exactly this category. The photography is doing the work — clean, editorial, earthy ceramics with that one blue-patterned statement piece that actually stopped me.
- But here's the rub: the 60% off banner that flashes in the middle of this email doesn't appear to be on the artisanal vases. The named vases in the hero feel like full-price new arrivals. The discount seems to live somewhere else — new styles, bedroom stuff, living room content that just kind of appears below the fold like a different email got pasted in. That gap between the subject promise and the actual offer architecture is the whole problem. I didn't open this expecting a sale on other stuff.
- I'd archive it without clicking. Not delete — archive, to the folder. It's pretty enough that I might reference it in three weeks when I'm ready to actually pull the trigger on accessories. But I'm not clicking today.

## What's working

- **The photography is genuinely beautiful** — the hero vase arrangement with the blue floral piece against a neutral background is editorial-level, and the individual named tiles (Coloma Handled, Butterfly Joint, De Kastro Sculptural) give me enough to window-shop without feeling overwhelming.
- **Named products with individual CTAs** work better than a generic "Shop Vases" dump — I can see what the specific items are called, which is how I actually browse and reference things later.
- **Subject is cleanly specific** at 38 characters — no emoji pileup, no fake urgency, it just tells me what's in the email and that's fine.
- **The "artisanal" framing lands** for me in a way "decorative vases" wouldn't — it implies handcraft, limited runs, the kind of thing worth paying for.

## What's weak

- **The 60% off banner in the middle of the email doesn't appear to be on the vases** — opening on an artisanal vase subject and finding the discount is for different items (bedroom, living room) downstream is disorienting and feels like bait.
- **Vases-at-full-price with no price visibility** means I have no idea if these are $49 accent pieces or $400 statement objects — no price anchoring, no range, nothing to help me decide whether to click or save for later.
- **The recommended section and app promo at the bottom feel like a completely different email** — Pottery Barn keeps doing this multi-section kitchen-sink format and it dilutes the editorial quality of the top half.
- **Still no loyalty/Key member framing** — CB2 this week was running a 40% event that explicitly called out member pricing; Pottery Barn keeps leaving that card on the table.
- **This is the second Pottery Barn email I've opened today** (after coffee tables). The cadence is just untenable at this point.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Tie the 60% off explicitly to the vases or remove it** — if artisanal vases are full price, say "New Arrivals" and own it. If some vases are discounted, say which ones and by how much. The current layout implies discount but doesn't deliver on it for the hero product.
- 2. **Show a price range in the hero** — even "from $49" would tell me whether this is impulse-buy territory or a considered purchase. Article does this routinely and it removes the click barrier.
- 3. **Cut to one editorial section** — lose the bedroom/living room module. It's a vase email; let it be a vase email. The Chris Loves Julia send earlier this month proved a tight, single-focus email performs better than a grab-bag.
- 4. **Add member/Key pricing callout** if these are on any kind of promotion — CB2's sale emails this week made me feel like a known customer. This one treats me like a catalog subscriber.
- 5. **Cadence throttle** — two emails on the same day from the same brand is the single biggest deliverability-trust killer here.

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "NEW artisanal vases" (2026-07-23)

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**BROKEN — 403 on credit card apply CTA:**
```
https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY...
```
This is the Key Rewards / credit card apply-and-buy link in the header banner (`cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`). Any recipient clicking it gets a 403. The URL contains a CP token (`cp_tp=v3.eJy...`) that appears malformed or expired at time of QA scan.

**BROKEN — 403 on West Elm cross-brand spacer/tracker:**
```
http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif
```
This is a 1×1 tracking pixel sourced from a West Elm domain over HTTP. It returned 403, meaning the pixel will fail to fire and open tracking via this mechanism is broken. It is also a mixed-content (HTTP) resource.

**25 tracking links skipped** — click.e.potterybarn.com redirects were not probed. No broken destination URLs can be confirmed from QA data, but the credit card CTA is confirmed broken independent of the click-tracking wrapper.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (will be blocked in many clients):**
| Image | src |
|---|---|
| spacer_0.gif | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` |
| Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` |

Gmail, Outlook, and Apple Mail all block mixed-content HTTP image loads. `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` is a visible footer creative — subscribers with images enabled may still see a broken image placeholder.

**Missing alt text — 40 images flagged.** Critical functional images with no alt text include:
- `F26_TOP_SummerSalePartC_NoUrgency.jpg` — top hero banner
- `slices_01` through `slices_10` (vases product grid) — the primary content block
- `F26_Bottom_NewArrivals.jpg` and `S26_Bottom_UpTo60OffOutdoor.jpg` — secondary CTAs
- All 18 footer slice images

Screen readers will receive no content description for any product image. When images are blocked (common in Outlook), there is no fallback text for any section.

**PHP header directives in CSS block:**
```html
<style>
  ...
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
</style>
```
PHP `header()` calls are inert inside a `<style>` block but indicate a template rendering failure — these lines should have executed server-side before HTML output. They will appear as junk text in some rendering environments and suggest the email was assembled incorrectly from a PHP template.

**`<title>` tag is empty** — minor, but some clients display this as a tab/window title.

### 5. Compliance

Requires verification against raw headers from the ESP (not relay artifact).

**Authentication-Results not captured** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail is unknown. Sending domain is `e.potterybarn.com` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud). Should be confirmed at ESP level.

**CAN-SPAM physical address** — not verifiable from truncated HTML. Assumed present in footer based on standard PB template; should be confirmed in full source.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The open-tracking pixel embeds subscriber email (`lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`) directly as a query parameter in a `coreemail.html` image src — this is a site analytics / session stitching mechanism, not a compliance issue per se, but exposes PII in HTTP logs and referrer headers.

UTM parameters on destination URLs cannot be confirmed (tracking links were skipped during QA probe). The broken credit card CTA (`cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`) means attribution for that click path is lost entirely.

Cross-brand asset reference (`edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`) indicates a shared template fragment that was not updated for this PB send — West Elm tracker should not appear in a Pottery Barn email.
