# Score with our NEW NFL Game Day Collection!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- Five. That's the C&B family inbox count for today, and this one is the one that finally broke me — not because it's badly made, but because of the profound identity crisis baked into its existence. Crate & *Kids* is launching an NFL Game Day Collection. In June. The month when NFL is in the deepest part of its offseason. I renovate homes; I watch football; I even own a sofa that I'd consider replacing with something in team colors for the media room in the second property. But none of that matters here, because the rest of this email is cribs, strollers, a registry program, and a toddler Summer section — and the brand sending it is the kids sub-brand.
- The NFL hero section isn't even bad-looking. There are some upholstered chairs and glassware that, in a Crate & Barrel adult-brand email, might have actually caught my eye. I have been thinking about bar seating and lounge chairs for the finished basement. But Crate & Kids is doing the lifting here, and the product row is immediately followed by nursery content and a baby registry pitch, which is the context collapse that kills any interest I had.
- I would not click. I would not forward this. I would archive it and mentally note that the C&B family email program is becoming the Wayfair of my inbox — high-volume, low signal, training me to ignore everything they send.

## What's working

- **The NFL hero is visually clean.** The teal/navy upholstered seating and branded glassware render clearly; the product photography is sharp and the grid layout doesn't feel cluttered.
- **"SHOP GAME DAY" is a usable CTA.** It's specific — a verb and a noun — unlike the vague "Discover" buttons that CB2 occasionally leans on. I know exactly what I'm being asked to do.
- **Subject line under 50 characters.** 43 chars, renders fully on mobile, no truncation anxiety. Small thing, but it's done right.
- **No render bugs.** After the five-star email that showed up twice in one day earlier, I was half-expecting something to break. It didn't.

## What's weak

- **Wrong brand, wrong audience.** The NFL Game Day Collection has no business living in a Crate & Kids email. This should have gone out through the Crate & Barrel adult list — the one that could actually speak to me as a renovator with a media room to furnish.
- **June is not football season.** Article sent me outdoor furniture in the first week of June and that felt *perfectly* timed — they read the calendar. Launching NFL merchandise before training camp even opens is a stretch at best, a clearance dump at worst.
- **The email is four different emails stacked.** NFL hero, summer kids content, registry pitch, loyalty program — there's no coherent editorial voice tying these together. Pottery Barn's "dedicated design expert" email from earlier this week had a single idea and executed it. This has four ideas and executes none of them.
- **No price signal anywhere.** Not a sale badge, not a member price, not a "starting at" — nothing to make me feel like clicking now versus next week.
- **Cadence is broken.** This is the fifth email from the C&B umbrella today. "Responsible designs you'll feel good about" — which I logged as the fourth — was already one too many. I am actively becoming less receptive to this brand family with every additional send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Route the NFL collection to the Crate & Barrel adult list, not Crate & Kids.** The audience mismatch is the single biggest lift available. A guy thinking about a media room remodel will click an NFL seating collection from the adult brand. He won't click it buried between a stroller rec and a crib photo.
- 2. **Add a price anchor.** Even "from $49" or "chairs from $299" changes the calculus. Right now there's zero financial signal, so there's no urgency to engage today.
- 3. **Break this into two separate emails.** NFL Game Day stands alone. The kids summer and registry content stands alone. Mixed sends dilute both messages.
- 4. **Shift the send date.** August, when training camp opens, is the credible window for NFL merchandise. June sends read as inventory push, not seasonal celebration.
- 5. **Kill the daily volume.** One C&B family email per day maximum. Everything above that is burning future open rates.
