# New Chris Loves Julia + Pottery Barn ✨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-six. That's the Pottery Barn count in July, and I say it every time because it's the only honest frame. But I'll give credit where it's due — when I saw "Chris Loves Julia" in the subject, my brain actually paused. I know the CLJ brand. Julia Marcum is the design side; Chris is the guy who shows up in lifestyle shots looking like he just discovered a great couch. I follow their stuff loosely because their wood-tone layering sensibility is closer to what I'm trying to do in the second home than the white-coastal vibe Pottery Barn defaults to. So the collaboration is genuinely interesting to me — in theory.
- In practice, the email delivers the concept clearly and without drama. The hero is warm, well-lit, and shows Chris with a dog in what looks like a CLJ-styled living room — dark wood, layered textiles, intentional. Below that, the email breaks into curated segments: wood tones, a green accent palette, and what looks like a bedroom section. There's a "Recommended Just For You" block in the middle that I actually appreciated — a rare moment where Pottery Barn treated me like a customer with a purchase history rather than a cold address.
- I'd save this to my "Inspo" folder (yes, I have one — it's basically a Pinterest annex). I would not forward it. I'd click if a specific product in the wood-tone section caught my eye, but I wouldn't convert today.

## What's working

- **The CLJ hook is real signal.** "New Chris Loves Julia + Pottery Barn" in the subject tells me exactly what this is — no mystery, no "discover the newest arrivals" vagueness. That specificity is rare in this inbox.
- **Hero lifestyle shot lands.** Chris with the dog in a furnished room reads aspirational without being performative. It's the kind of image I'd pin. Warmer and more masculine-leaning than Pottery Barn's usual editorial register.
- **Wood tones section is on-brief for my project.** I'm actively shopping cabinetry and case goods for the second home, and a curated "find wood tones" section is essentially a category landing page delivered to me. Article did something similar a few days ago with a dining room focus, and I clicked that too.
- **"Recommended Just For You" in the body.** It's a low bar, but Pottery Barn clearing it matters — I've opened enough emails from them this month where every product felt like it was sold to someone else entirely.
- **No visible render bugs.** Clean layout, readable type, sections break well on mobile.

## What's weak

- **CLJ skews Julia.** The collection's brand identity is weighted toward a female shopper. I know the blog — Julia is the design engine, Chris is the likable sidekick. That's fine, but the whole email is designed through that lens, and I'm a 36-year-old guy who would've appreciated one room vignette that didn't feel like it was waiting for throw pillows.
- **No price anchor anywhere.** This is a "shop the collection" launch, not a sale. I understand that. But after the CB2 "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" email landed in the same week, and Wayfair's been hammering me with "under $30" subject lines, the absence of any dollar signal makes this feel like I'm being asked to browse a catalog, not buy.
- **CTA hierarchy collapses by the bedroom section.** The top of the email has strong directional flow. By the third category block, I'm not sure which thing I'm supposed to click. Multiple sections, multiple implied CTAs, no clear priority. Article's recent dining room email did this better — one hero, one main CTA, secondary links underneath.
- **Cadence is the elephant in the room.** This is the fourth Pottery Barn email in two days. The "Easy & FREE design services" send yesterday was actually more actionable for me than this one, and the storage email before it was also decent. Three better-than-average emails in a row does not mean I want a fourth — it means I'm closer to unsubscribing.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with one hero CLJ product, not the lifestyle shot.** Give me the specific piece Chris is sitting next to with a price. The lifestyle works as context, not as the main event. Wayfair buries the price; don't be Wayfair.
- 2. **Add a starting-at price point to the hero.** "From $X" keeps it elegant. It anchors the collection without turning into a sale email.
- 3. **Cut to two sections, not three.** Wood tones or bedroom — pick the one most likely to convert and let it breathe. The green palette section feels like a tertiary idea that diluted the wood-tones story I actually cared about.
- 4. **Swap the generic hero CTA for something collection-specific.** "Shop Chris Loves Julia" beats "Shop Now" every time when you have a named collab to trade on.
- 5. **Suppress this send for anyone who opened the design services email yesterday.** I've already signaled purchase intent. A collab launch email with no offer is noise on top of a more actionable signal.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Wood tones, new greens, and the bedroom piece everyone's going to want.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Curated for the layered home. Shop the full Chris Loves Julia collection.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources — will be blocked by most modern clients:**
1. `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
2. `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
3. `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
4. Open-tracking pixel: `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...`

The West Elm domain reference (`edm.westelm.com`) is a cross-brand asset bleed — likely a copy-paste artifact from a sibling brand template.

**Alt text missing on 38 images** — includes all content slices (`slices_01` through `slices_10` in the CLJ block), hero banners (`F26_TOP_SummerSalePartC_NoUrgency.jpg`), product images, and footer slices. In image-blocking environments (Outlook, corporate clients) the email will render as a blank column of broken icons with no readable content fallback.

**CSS anomaly in `<style>` block:** PHP-style `header()` calls appear verbatim inside a `<style>` tag:
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
```
These are server-side PHP statements that were never executed — they were pasted raw into the stylesheet. They are inert in email clients but indicate a template assembly error.

**`<title>` is empty** — minor; some clients display the title as a fallback subject line.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header not found** — AgentMail relay did not surface it; cannot confirm RFC 2369 compliance. If absent at the MTA level, Gmail and Yahoo will suppress the native unsubscribe UI, increasing spam complaints.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found** — One-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) not confirmed. Required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy for >5k/day senders.

**Authentication-Results header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail unknown through the relay. Cannot confirm deliverability posture. Given volume, DMARC `p=reject` with aligned DKIM signing on `e.potterybarn.com` is expected but unverifiable from this capture.

**CAN-SPAM:** Physical address and unsubscribe mechanism are standard for Pottery Barn; not auditable from the truncated HTML alone, but no truncation artifacts suggest they are missing.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The broken credit card CTA (`clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407...`) carries UTM-equivalent parameters (`cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`, `marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY`) — those attribution signals are lost on every click since the link 403s before any redirect.

The 25 untested tracking links are all routed through `click.e.potterybarn.com` — standard ESP click-wrapping. UTM parameter presence on destination URLs cannot be confirmed without resolving those redirects.

The `coreemail.html` beacon (`https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=...`) leaks the raw recipient email address as a plaintext URL query parameter — this address will appear in server logs, CDN logs, and any referrer headers on the destination page.
