# BATH MATS You'll Love!!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-seven. That's the Wayfair number now. And I want to be clear about what this specific email represents before I say anything else: it is the same subject line I already received yesterday. "BATH MATS You'll Love!!" hit my inbox on June 4th — I reviewed it, gave it a 6, moved on. Now it's June 5th and it's back. Whether this is a non-opener resend or just a scheduling failure, it doesn't matter — I *did* open it yesterday, and opening it today feels like Wayfair is telling me my behavior doesn't register anywhere in their system.
- The email itself is structurally identical to what I saw yesterday: a big "new price cuts / new price cuts / new price cuts" hero that doesn't mention bath mats at all, a grid of steals that spans everything from patio stones to bedding to what appears to be a dark-wood dresser, and a "Scores in Your Style" section that feels loosely assembled. The subject-to-content mismatch was annoying yesterday. Today it just confirms Wayfair views me as a number to blast, not a subscriber to inform. Compare this to the Pottery Barn Summer Warehouse Sale email I read this week — single subject, single hero, one clean CTA, felt like it was *meant* for me. This feels like it was meant for everyone.
- I won't click. I won't forward. I'll archive and quietly bump up the mental countdown to unsubscribing.

## What's working

- **"New price cuts" is a functional hook** — price movement language beats "discover" or "explore," and the bold stacked type makes it land visually at the top of the scroll.
- **"These Top Steals Are Going Fast"** is decent urgency copy — slightly better than a generic "Shop Now," even if the claim isn't credible at email 37.
- **Product grid density** works for me as a Wayfair shopper — I like being able to scan SKU variety without a click, and the grid fills the inbox preview without broken images.
- **App CTA at the bottom** is a reasonable add-on; I already have the app but the exclusive deals framing is the right hook for someone who doesn't.

## What's weak

- **Exact repeat subject line, 24 hours later.** "BATH MATS You'll Love!!" scored a 6 yesterday and here it is again. This is the single most damaging decision in the send. Article sent me an outdoor email three days after their kitchen email and even *that* felt like a tighter editorial calendar than whatever Wayfair is running.
- **The subject promises bath mats; the hero delivers a generic deals page.** The "new price cuts" hero could be wrapped around any category. If the subject says bath mats, show me bath mats above the fold — don't make me hunt.
- **"You'll Love!!" is still doing the double-exclamation spam dance.** ALL CAPS + `!!` is inbox-filter bait and it reads as discount-bin desperation. CB2 ran "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" last week — still promotional, zero exclamation marks, didn't feel like it was screaming at me.
- **No member pricing or rewards signal visible.** I run everything through my highest-rewards card and I track points. If this is a Wayfair member price-cut event, put that front and center. The Pottery Barn Warehouse Sale email made it obvious that Rewards members got first access — Wayfair didn't bother.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the repeat send, full stop.** If you must resend to non-openers, change the subject. Sending the identical line to people who already opened is a trust erosion event, not a revenue opportunity.
- 2. **Match the hero to the subject.** If the subject says bath mats, the hero image should be a styled bathroom with two or three hero bath mats, a clear "Up to X% off" callout, and a single CTA to the bath mat collection — not a generic deals grid.
- 3. **Drop the ALL CAPS + double exclamation marks.** Pick one signal of excitement. ALL CAPS is enough. The `!!` makes it look like it came from a coupon aggregator, not a brand I have a registered account with.
- 4. **Surface member/rewards pricing.** If Wayfair Rewards members get a better price, say so. That's the one thing that would make me pause over an Article or CB2 email — loyalty math.
- 5. **Add one time-bound signal with an actual date.** "Going Fast" is not urgency. "Price cuts end Sunday" is urgency.

- **Subject Alt B:** `The bath mats I keep seeing in your saves`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Up to 40% off the ones topping our charts this week`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Member pricing live — grab the ones going fast`
