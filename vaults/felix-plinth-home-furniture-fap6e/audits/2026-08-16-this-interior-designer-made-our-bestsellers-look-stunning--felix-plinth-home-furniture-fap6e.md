# This interior designer made our bestsellers look STUNNING
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-16**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is actually Crate & Barrel proper for once — not the Crate & Kids sub-brand that's been flooding me four times in two days with nursery gliders and tween rooms I have zero use for. That's already a relief. "Lisa Pollock's home makeover" with a real designer photo, a living room, a dining room, a fall table — this is furniture content aimed at an adult renovating a house, which is literally what I'm doing right now on two properties.
- That said, it's a designer-collab format I've seen from this exact family before — CB2 did "impeccable energy" designer-adjacent framing just last week — and it runs long. I scrolled through six or seven distinct sections (living room, dining room, "come shopping with Lisa," fall tabletop, cookware, "we love these for you," credit card pitch, app pitch) before hitting the footer. No percentage off anywhere. No price on most of it except the cookware ($399 discounted from $499-ish) and the four PDP tiles at the bottom.
- Would I click? Maybe, on "Shop Dining Room" since I'm actually shopping tables right now — but I'd click straight past the cookware, the credit card, and the app pitch. Forward to a friend? No. Mark as read and move on. It's competent, on-brand, and doesn't feel like a stranger-blast, but it's not urgent enough to interrupt my day.

## What's working

- Real interior designer, real room photos, no stock-photo staging — this reads like actual inspiration content, not a generic sale blast.
- "Shop Living Room" and "Shop Dining Room" are specific, category-level CTAs I'd actually use since I'm furnishing two homes right now.
- The "crafted-to-last cookware" section shows an actual price drop ($399, "Now Lower Price") — that's the one concrete offer in the whole email.
- Product grid at the bottom ("we love these for you") shows real prices per item — better than CB2's clearance blast that buried pricing behind a click.

## What's weak

- No headline offer or discount anywhere near the top — Wayfair's "pieces from $30" cookout email got me with a number in the first screen; this makes me scroll five sections before any dollar figure shows up.
- It's basically two emails stapled together — designer home tour, then cookware, then credit card upsell, then app download. Article's "editorial mode" send at least stayed focused on one theme; this one sprawls.
- The credit card pitch ("the card you love just got better") and app download block feel bolted on at the end, unrelated to the "Lisa Pollock" story that opened the email.
- "we love these for you" is generic personalization language with no actual personalization behind it — it's just four random SKUs (a dining table, a bed, a dresser, a bench), not tied to anything I've browsed.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with a real offer or price signal above the fold — even just "up to 20% off dining" near the hero — since right now nothing pulls me in until the cookware section, five scrolls deep.
- 2. Cut the email in half: ship the designer home tour as its own send, and the cookware/credit-card/app content as separate, focused sends. This one tries to do four jobs at once.
- 3. Make "we love these for you" actually personalized (based on browse/purchase history) or relabel it "new arrivals" so it's not a false personalization promise.
- 4. Move "Shop Dining Room" or "Shop Bestsellers" higher, right under the hero — that's the CTA most likely to convert someone like me who's mid-renovation.
