# Going fast: grab their school gear at up to 20% off →
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-10**
## Executive summary

- I don't have kids. I don't need backpacks with unicorn print or lunch boxes shaped like whatever "Oliver" is monogrammed on. So the entire top half of this email — hero, backpacks, duffel bags, lunch boxes, water bottles — is just noise scrolling past my thumb. This is the same Crate & Kids school-gear send I already flagged today, and it's still not for me.
- The one thing that made me pause was buried past the fold: "we love these for you" with a kids backpack sitting right next to a Nuna RAVA car seat and a Jenny Lind dresser — for $1,499. That's a real furniture price point, that's the category I'm actually in mid-renovation, and it's crammed into a grid with toddler gear like an afterthought. Then there's a design consultation CTA ("book an appointment, win $5000"), a new credit card pitch, and an app download push, all stacked one after another like they ran out of ideas for what to lead with.
- Not clicking, not forwarding. This reads like four different emails Frankensteined into one, and none of the four is actually addressed to me.

## What's working

- The Jenny Lind Wood Dresser and Nuna car seat photography is clean and on-brand — that's the one block that could've been its own furniture email.
- "TheDesignDesk" free design consultation CTA is a decent hook for someone mid-reno like me, if it weren't wedged between backpacks and a credit card ad.
- The "1500+ new heritage-inspired finds" banner has a moody, well-shot lifestyle photo that actually looks like something I'd want in my house.
- No render bugs — images loaded, text is legible, nothing overlapping.

## What's weak

- I have no kids and this is a back-to-school email; the hero, four product tiles, and both CTAs above the fold are 100% irrelevant to me.
- The email tries to be five emails at once — kids gear, new arrivals, design consult, furniture picks, credit card, app download — nothing gets real hierarchy.
- Same fatigue I flagged twice already today: this is the third or fourth Crate & Barrel-family send in my inbox this week, and it's the second one specifically pushing "back-to-school" gear I'll never buy.
- The furniture I'd actually care about (dresser, car seat) is crammed into a generic "we love these for you" grid instead of getting its own moment.
- No mention of my Crate Card rewards balance or loyalty status even though they're pitching me a card upgrade two screens down — total stranger treatment.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this. If I've never bought kids' gear, don't lead an entire email with backpacks — CB2 and Article don't waste my first screen on a category I've never touched. Lead with the furniture or new-arrivals content instead.
- 2. Pull the Jenny Lind dresser and Nuna stroller/car seat into their own "new for your home" section with real specs (dimensions, ship time) instead of a throwaway 4-up grid.
- 3. Kill the credit card pitch and app download from this specific send — that's three unrelated asks competing with the actual offer.
- 4. If TheDesignDesk consultation is the real value-add for a renovator like me, put it up top with a clear appointment CTA, not buried after two kids' sections.
- 5. Add delivery/lead-time info near the furniture pieces — Pottery Barn's "delivered in 1-4 weeks" fatigue is real, but at least they show it; here I get nothing.
