# We found you the perfect sofa
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-08**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-four. That's the CB2 count since mid-May, and I want that on the table before I say anything else — because what I'm about to say is going to sound generous and it needs context. This one is different. "We found you the perfect sofa" in the subject line from the *design services* sub-account, not the regular promo machine, actually stopped my thumb. I'm hunting for a sofa for the second home right now. I've been bouncing between Article and Pottery Barn sectionals for three weeks. So "the hunt is over" landing in my inbox on June 8th felt, for one flicker of a second, like CB2 was reading my Zillow history.
- Then I opened it and realized it's a content-forward pitch for their free interior design consultation service — not a "here's THE sofa we've pulled for you based on your past orders." The subject implies algorithmic personalization; the body delivers a how-to guide. That bait-and-switch is real and it stings a little. The "3 WAYS TO MEET" structure is genuinely useful — in-person, virtual, whatever the third option is — and I did scroll all the way to the "GET INSPIRED" section because the aesthetic is on point. But the "HOT SOLD-OUT SET" block near the bottom is the kind of dark pattern that erodes trust fast. You can't lead with "we found you the perfect sofa" and then show me something I can't buy.
- I would not click today. But I did read it, which is more than I've done with the last four CB2 promos. I might forward to my contractor because the design service angle is legitimately relevant to a dual-renovation situation. Mark as read with mild goodwill.

## What's working

- **"THE HUNT IS OVER" as hero copy** — for a dual-renovation Millennial in active sofa-search mode, that headline earns attention before I even process it consciously.
- **Sender differentiation** — "CB2 Free Design Services" rather than the generic CB2 promo address signals a different kind of email; I'm conditioned to gloss over the usual sender, so the variant actually broke through.
- **Service offer is concrete and free** — unlike the Pottery Barn "60% off" blast from earlier today that comes with seventeen asterisks, "free design services" is clean and credible, no fine-print paranoia required.
- **"3 WAYS TO MEET" structure** — giving me explicit options (in-person, virtual, presumably a chat/async path) reduces friction in a way that most competitors don't bother with; this is something Article completely ignores on their current outdoor push.
- **Visual cleanliness** — the layout scans without noise; no overlapping text, no broken image gaps, no lorem ipsum anywhere I can see.

## What's weak

- **Subject promise vs. body delivery mismatch** — "We found you the perfect sofa" implies a personalized product recommendation; instead I get a service intake funnel. That's a trust withdrawal.
- **"HOT SOLD-OUT SET" block** — showing me unavailable product while trying to sell me on a design consultation undercuts the whole premise. If you're telling me a designer will help me shop, don't simultaneously show me things I can't have.
- **No sofa specifics above the fold** — the hero image shows a living room vignette but there's no linked product, no price, no SKU. After Pottery Barn showed me actual item names and sale prices in their June 8th hero, this feels vague by comparison.
- **Cadence context kills some goodwill** — even though this sender address is distinct, my brain still tallies it against the 23 CB2 emails preceding it. The goodwill budget is thin.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Deliver on the subject line** — if you're going to say "we found you the perfect sofa," the first content block should be a specific sofa recommendation linked to a PDP, ideally with a price. The design service can be the secondary offer. Right now the funnel is inverted.
- 2. **Remove or replace the sold-out block** — swap it for an in-stock alternative or a "join the waitlist" moment; showing unavailable product in a design-service email is a credibility hole.
- 3. **Add one real urgency signal** — a deadline on the free consultation ("Book by June 15") would push me from "interesting" to "booking now."
- 4. **Lean harder into the dual-context** — "Renovating more than one space?" as a sub-headline would have me feeling seen in a way no other brand in this category has attempted.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on a standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional template with MSO conditional comment support. A developer placeholder comment was shipped in the production send, and several font-declaration inconsistencies and a narrow mobile breakpoint are the primary rendering risks.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully assess — HTML is truncated before body links appear.** The following is flagged from what is visible:

- No tracking links or UTM parameters are present in the `<head>` portion. Full link audit requires complete source.
- Font asset requests are made to two distinct origins: `https://www.cb2.com/` and `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/`. Both origins will generate separate HTTP connections; if either CDN is slow or blocked by a corporate firewall, fallback fonts silently replace brand fonts — expected behavior, but worth confirming both CDNs have consistent uptime SLAs.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Mobile breakpoint is anomalously narrow.**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { .mobile { width:414px !important; } }
```
This triggers only below 418px. Most Android devices report logical widths of 360–414px and will not trigger this breakpoint. The industry standard for email responsive breakpoints is 600px. Any device between 418px and 600px receives no mobile layout overrides.

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom. This is a WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 violation (Resize Text) and may trigger accessibility complaints.

**Global `line-height: 100%` with `max-height` hack:**
```css
*{...; max-height:1000000px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly; line-height: 100%}
```
The `line-height: 100%` on the universal selector is a known cause of collapsed line-height in Gmail's rendering engine when combined with inherited `font-size`. If any text elements set font-size in px but rely on inherited line-height, they will render too tight.

**Alt text not assessable** — HTML truncated before `<img>` tags appear in the body. Requires full source to verify.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Developer placeholder comment shipped in production:**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
This is an unstripped template artifact. "IMPUT" is also a typo (should be "INPUT"). While invisible to end users, it is visible in view-source and signals a QA gap in the pre-send checklist.

No unresolved `{{merge_tag}}` or `%%token%%` syntax visible in the truncated `<head>`. Full body requires review.
