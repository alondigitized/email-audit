# Dining tables with a seat for everyone
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-19**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is actually a smart way to run a dining category email — "Gather Together," then it segments straight into Seats up to Six, Seats up to Eight, Seats up to Twelve. That's not fluffy lifestyle copy, that's a sizing filter, and sizing is literally the thing I'm stuck on for the second house's dining room right now since I haven't decided if it's a six-top breakfast nook situation or a full twelve-person holiday table. The "Warehouse Sale: Up to 60% off" banner up top is the same offer PB ran on 9/18 with the outdoor piece, so at least the promo is consistent, not some new made-up number.
- Where it loses me a little is the back half — once I get past dining tables it dumps into curtain rods, rugs, towels, a duvet set. I get the "shop the whole home" instinct, but I opened this for a table, not a hand towel. Crate & Barrel's "Beautiful & bestselling for every room" email (8/10 from me) handled the multi-category sprawl better because it kept each section visually distinct with its own hero; this one just tacks a generic product grid onto the bottom of a good dining email and calls it "Recommended Just for You" when it's clearly not personalized, it's just merchandising filler.
- I'd click through on "Seats up to Eight" or "Seats up to Twelve" specifically — those are relevant to what I'm actually shopping for. I wouldn't forward it to a friend, but I also wouldn't archive it unread — this is a keeper for my inspo folder, better than most PB sends this month.

## What's working

- The seat-count segmentation (Six / Eight / Twelve) is genuinely useful for someone furnishing two different dining spaces like me.
- Real, warm hero photography — wood tones, linen, actual table settings, not stock-photo stiff.
- "Extendable" callout badges on the 8- and 12-seat tables answer a real question before I have to click.
- The 60% off Warehouse Sale banner matches what I saw from PB on 9/18, so it doesn't feel like a random one-off number.
- "A Chair for Every Style" section with mixed chair styles is a smart mix-and-match upsell for people (me) who don't want a matchy dining set.

## What's weak

- The bottom half turns into an unrelated home-goods dump — curtain rods, rugs, towels, duvets — that has nothing to do with why I opened a dining table email.
- "Recommended Just for You" is a lie, it's just a generic product grid, not personalization, and I can tell.
- No rewards math shown anywhere — PB has a credit card and a rewards program mentioned way down in the footer fine print, but nothing tied to this specific sale.
- No swatch/sample program mention for the wood finishes, which matters a lot when I'm trying to match two different renovation palettes across two houses.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email at "A Chair for Every Style" — end on the dining category, don't dilute a strong, focused send with towels and curtain rods.
- 2. Add real personalization signals (browsing history, saved items) if you're going to label a section "Recommended Just for You" — otherwise just call it "More From Pottery Barn."
- 3. Surface the rewards % back or financing terms (Affirm, PB credit card) near the sale banner itself, not buried in the footer — that's a real purchase-decision factor for a big-ticket table.
- 4. Add a wood-finish swatch/sample CTA near the table sections — I'm not committing to a $2k extendable table sight-unseen on finish.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your dining table, sized right (up to 60% off)`
