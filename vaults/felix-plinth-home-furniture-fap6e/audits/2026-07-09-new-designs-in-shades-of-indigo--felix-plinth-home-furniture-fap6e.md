# New designs in shades of indigo 💙
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- Email twelve. I went back and counted — twelve Pottery Barn emails since roughly July 1st, and I have not exaggerated a single one of those counts. So my default posture walking into this one is already a mild defensive crouch. But I'll give it this: "New designs in shades of indigo" is the least annoying subject line they've sent in four days. No fake countdown, no "HOURS LEFT" that turns into more hours. Just a color story. And honestly? Indigo is a genuinely useful palette right now — I've been trying to find something that bridges the traditional bones of the primary and the slightly more relaxed vibe of the second home, and navy/indigo can travel that distance.
- Inside, the email is clean and restrained. Big editorial hero. Three product modules — bedding, a rattan chair with indigo cushions, and a blue-and-white dinnerware set. Then a "Recommended Just For You" personalization block, then a Summer Sale callout buried near the footer. It reads like a catalog spread, not a sales alert. Which is a change of pace. The problem is that the one thing that would make me actually act — the sale — is disconnected from the products it presumably applies to. I can't tell if the rattan chair I'm interested in is part of the sale, or if that discount is for something else entirely.
- I'd probably click to look at the chair. I would not forward this to anyone.

## What's working

- The indigo color story is genuinely useful for a renovator — it's one of the few palettes that reads classic *and* casual depending on how you style it; I'm actively shopping for this range right now
- The rattan chair with patterned indigo cushion is the right product for me: natural material, pattern fabric, outdoor-adjacent, exactly what I'm sourcing for the second home
- "Recommended Just For You" section at the bottom is a small but real personalization signal — something Wayfair failed completely when they sent the identical 8'x10' outdoor rug subject two days in a row
- No fake urgency mechanisms — no countdown timer, no "only 3 left," no manufactured scarcity; this feels honest after weeks of "LAST DAY" emails that apparently last multiple days
- Visual hierarchy is clean and the hero image earns the scroll — no layout chaos, no broken images, crisp rendering throughout

## What's weak

- The blue-and-white china module pushes the email into territory that doesn't feel addressed to me — I'm buying rugs and chairs, not a Spode-adjacent dinnerware set, and the styling reads feminine in a way the rattan chair does not
- The Summer Sale "Up to 70% off" banner is completely decoupled from the featured products — I have no idea if the pieces shown in the color story are discounted; that connection needs to be made in the product module, not as a separate band after I've already scrolled past
- "New designs" in the subject line doesn't tell me what's actually new — is this a new collection, a new colorway in an existing line, or just a seasonal merch push? The email doesn't answer that either
- Twelfth email this week; the cadence has built a Pavlovian filter in my head where Pottery Barn = probably another variation on the same thing, and even a good send like this gets penalized by proximity to the ones that weren't

## Recommendations

- 1. **Connect the sale to the product modules** — if the rattan chair or the linen bedding is part of the Summer Sale, put that savings signal next to the product, not orphaned at the bottom; this is the single change with the highest lift because I need price before I'll click
- 2. **Lead with furniture, not bedding** — the rattan chair should be the first editorial module; bedding skews the demographic signal feminine and loses male renovators early in the scroll
- 3. **Give the collection a name or origin story** — "Indigo Blues" as a headline is evocative but vague; Article does a better job naming their drops and grounding them in a specific design moment (e.g., "Block print. Hand-dyed. New." — something that signals genuine newness)
- 4. **Throttle the send cadence** — CB2 hit 15 emails since July 1st and I noted it felt like "surveillance"; Pottery Barn is on pace to match that; at some point the individual email quality stops mattering because inbox perception degrades for the whole sender
- **Subject Alt A:** `New: Indigo collection now in stock — furniture, bedding, tableware`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The rattan chair that works in any room (now in indigo)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sale pricing on select indigo pieces — shop before styles sell out`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New colorways for chairs, sofas, and bedding — the palette that travels`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Indigo Blues" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed broken destination links (403) and a PHP template artifact embedded in a `<style>` block constitute the most critical issues. Accessibility coverage is severely degraded with 30+ images carrying no alt text.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Broken links (HTTP 403 — confirmed failures):**

- **Credit card banner link** (`clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407...`): The KeyCC_Banner component's primary CTA returns 403. Affects `external_id=PB_header_nonmember` segment. Any click on the credit card apply banner is a dead end.
- **West Elm cross-brand spacer** (`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`): 403 on a tracking/spacer GIF from a sister brand. Indicates a stale cross-brand asset inclusion. Also HTTP (see below).

**Mixed-content / HTTP image sources (3 confirmed):**

| Asset | Source |
|---|---|
| `spacer_0.gif` | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` |
| `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` |
| `slices_02.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` |
| Open pixel | `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true...` |

Gmail and Outlook proxy/block non-HTTPS images; these assets will silently fail for a significant portion of recipients.

**Tracking links:** 25 click-redirect URLs via `click.e.potterybarn.com` were skipped from probing — destination integrity unverified by QA tooling.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP code in `<style>` block — template rendering error:**

```html
<style>
  ...
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
</style>
```

PHP `header()` calls were not executed server-side before send. Some email clients will interpret or surface this as visible text. This is a template compilation failure — the PHP block was included verbatim instead of being processed.

**Alt text — 30 images with empty `alt` attribute:**

All content slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_08.jpg`), all footer slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), and all product images (`farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `belgian-flax-linen-duvet-cover-2-l.jpg`, etc.) are missing alt text. With images blocked (default in Outlook desktop), the email renders as a blank white column.

**Google Fonts external load:**

```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```

Outlook (desktop) blocks external CSS entirely. The `Montserrat` font silently falls back to the declared `verdana,arial` stack — this is expected behavior but worth noting if font rendering consistency was tested only in webmail.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

Prevents user zoom on mobile — an accessibility barrier flagged by WCAG 1.4.4.

### 5. Compliance

**Headers (captured via AgentMail relay):**

| Header | Status |
|---|---|
| `List-Unsubscribe` | Not found — may be relay artifact, but unverifiable |
| `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) | Not found — one-click unsubscribe likely absent |
| `Authentication-Results` | Not found — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail unknown |

The `List-Unsubscribe-Post` absence is a material gap: Gmail's bulk sender requirements (enforced since Feb 2024) mandate one-click unsubscribe for senders at this volume. If the header is genuinely missing (not just dropped by the relay), this is a deliverability risk with Google.

`noindex, nofollow` and `no-referrer` meta tags are present — correct for email.

CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link presence cannot be confirmed from the truncated HTML, but the footer structure (implied by `Email-Footer` slice naming) typically carries these; not flagged as confirmed missing.
