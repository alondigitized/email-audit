# An instant classic: our new Beatrix collection ♥️
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-16**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids, again — third time in two days for this sub-brand, and it's the exact problem I flagged with the "Twin Tween Room" email: I don't have kids. No nursery, no crib, no "focus zone for every age" with little desks. The Beatrix bedroom collection at the top is genuinely nice-looking furniture — that green dresser with the scalloped edges could work in a guest room — but by the time I scroll two more screens into a nursery collection with rope-inspired hardware and crib recommendations, I know this newsletter has me filed under "parent," not "renovator with two houses."
- I'm not clicking, not forwarding. If anything I forward this to my sister who actually has a toddler, not to anyone on my reno group chat. This is the same fatigue I hit with the Pottery Barn "Curated bedding looks" send and the fourth Crate & Barrel-family email this week — everyone in this ecosystem is just cycling through sub-audiences and hoping I'm one of them.
- The one thing that almost pulls me back in is the "more new fall arrivals" grid lower down — furniture, bedding, decor, lighting, rugs, storage — that's actual Crate & Barrel-adjacent breadth I'd tap through on a different day. But it's buried under two full collection blocks I have zero use for.

## What's working

- The Beatrix bedroom hero (green scalloped dresser, round mirror, layered bedding) is a legit "screenshot for the folder" shot — matches the moody-styled hero energy Crate & Barrel proper has been running all week.
- The "more new fall arrivals" category grid (furniture/bedding/decor/lighting/rugs/storage) is a clean, scannable way to route me somewhere relevant if I bother to scroll that far.
- "we love these for you" product tiles have visible prices ($929.05, etc.) — at least there's real product data, not vague lifestyle copy.
- Clear "Shop Now" buttons throughout, easy tap targets, no broken images.

## What's weak

- It's Crate & Kids top to bottom — nursery collection, crib comparisons, "create a focus zone for every age" desks — none of it applies to a childless 36-year-old renovating two adult houses.
- Two full collection blocks (bedroom, then nursery) before I even get to anything I could use — buries the one relevant section.
- The heart emoji in the subject ("♥️") and "an instant classic" framing reads more parenting-brand-sentimental than the practical reno-shopper tone I respond to.
- The "new Crate & Barrel card" promo mid-email ("now! the card you love just got better") feels tacked on and generic — no specific rewards math, just "earning Reward Dollars."
- This is my third Crate & Kids/Crate-family send in two days — same fatigue as the CB2/Crate & Barrel pileup I called out yesterday.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment me out of Crate & Kids sends entirely, or at minimum lead with the adult "fall arrivals" grid instead of two kids/nursery collections — that's the section I'd actually shop.
- 2. Cut one of the two hero collections (bedroom or nursery) — showing both back-to-back doubles the irrelevant real estate for a non-parent subscriber.
- 3. Move the credit card promo to its own send or the footer only — it's not earning its mid-email placement with no concrete offer.
- 4. If Crate & Kids must email non-parent subscribers, add a one-line "shop for your home" escape hatch near the top, not five screens down.
