# It’s back. And now it’s on sale.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- Forty-one. That's approximately where I am with the CB2 / Crate family since mid-May, and this one lands June 26 — one day after they already hit me with *both* the Floor Sample Sale (which I genuinely liked) and the July 4th Sale. The subject "It's back. And now it's on sale." reads like they're teasing a specific product or collection returning, but when I open it, it's just the same July 4th promotional umbrella I saw yesterday. Classic intrigue-bait that lands flat once the email resolves.
- That said — there's a signal in here that actually earns my attention: **"IN STOCK."** Bold. Prominent. Repeated across every category subcategory (Living/Study, Bedroom, Accent Tables). For someone actively furnishing two homes simultaneously, "in stock" is the two most beautiful words in furniture retail. The 50% off hero is fine, but the inventory callout is what makes this email useful rather than just promotional.
- Overall verdict: I read it, I probably click the bedroom or accent-table section because I actually need pieces there right now, but I don't forward this. It's a tool, not a discovery. Mark as clicked and move on.

## What's working

- **The "IN STOCK" qualifier is the real CTA** — threading it through every room category is a smart editorial choice that treats me like someone who's been burned by 16-week lead times.
- **Room-based navigation** (Living/Study, Bedroom, Accent Tables) maps directly to how I think about furnishing — I'm not shopping "furniture," I'm furnishing a specific room.
- **Dual-offer escalation works** — 50% off in the hero, then a separate Warehouse Sale module at 60% off. The step-up feels intentional and not just repeated noise.
- **Clean layout, no render bugs** — easy to scan, no overlapping text, images loaded. The dark-wood bedroom shot and the green velvet chair both look sharp.
- **Current campaign context** — July 4th framing is timely, fits the exact moment in the calendar.

## What's weak

- **The subject is a fake tease** — "It's back" implies a specific product or collection returned. When the email is just the general July 4th sale umbrella, it reads like a bait-and-switch on the hook.
- **"SHOP EVERYTHING" as a mid-email CTA** is a waste of a button — after room-specific entries, a generic catch-all loses the momentum the category segmentation just built.
- **"More for You" product grid at the bottom is tiny and low-contrast** — I can see individual items but they're small enough that I can't tell what I'm looking at without zooming. Article's recent 4th of July email did a better job making individual products large enough to feel like discovery.
- **No price anchor in the product grid** — the "More for You" items look interesting but I can't see any pricing. Wayfair and Article both surface prices inline; CB2 keeps me guessing and that adds friction.
- **Cadence** — this is the third CB2 / Crate-adjacent email in two days. The Floor Sample Sale yesterday was the stronger email; this one competes with its own recent sibling.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Resolve the subject tease** — if "it's back" refers to a specific product (a bestselling sofa? the sale itself?), name it. The subject should front-load the "IN STOCK" angle because that's genuinely differentiating.
- 2. **Surface prices in the product grid** — even a "from $X" line per item in the "More for You" section would push me from browse to cart.
- 3. **Replace "SHOP EVERYTHING" with a room-specific CTA** — after Bedroom section: "Shop In-Stock Bedrooms." After accent tables: "Shop In-Stock Accent Tables." Keep me in the lane I just self-selected.
- 4. **Collapse the Warehouse Sale module** or cut it — it undercuts the 50% hero with a 60% competing offer and makes me wonder if the first offer was worth clicking at all. Either lead with 60% or don't show both in the same send.
- 5. **Swap cadence** — one fewer CB2 email this week and this one would've landed harder. The Floor Sample Sale yesterday was the right send; this one should have waited 48 hours.

- **Subject Alt B:** `The wait is over. In-stock sale, up to 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Living room, bedroom, accent tables — all in stock and on sale`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `No lead times. These pieces ship now — up to 60% off in the Warehouse`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Unremoved template artifact in `<head>`**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
Typo ("IMPUT" vs "INPUT") and placeholder was never replaced. Harmless to rendering but confirms the template was not fully sanitized before send.

**[MEDIUM] `@font-face` weight conflict — "Lineto Brown Sub" / weight 400 declared twice with different source files**
```css
/* Declaration 1 — resolves to BrownLight */
@font-face { font-family: "Lineto Brown Sub";
  src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2");
  font-weight: 400; }

/* Declaration 2 — resolves to BrownRegular */
@font-face { font-family: "Lineto Brown Sub";
  src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2");
  font-weight: 600; }
```
Wait — weight 600 on second declaration is fine. But cross-referencing the first block:
```css
/* Earlier block — no weight declared, catches ALL weights */
@font-face { font-family: "Lineto Brown Sub";
  src: url(".../BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.eot") ... }
```
Three `@font-face` rules share the same `font-family` name with no weight on the first, then 400 and 600 on the next two. The weightless declaration will shadow or conflict depending on the client's CSS cascade. Weight-400 text may resolve to Regular instead of Light.

**[MEDIUM] Double-slash in font URLs**
```css
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2")
```
Four font `src` URLs use `cb2.com//Styles/` (double slash). Most CDNs normalize this silently, but it is a latent 404 risk if the origin is strict about path resolution.

**[LOW] `font-display: swap` applied inconsistently**
The first two `@font-face` blocks omit `font-display`; the last four include it. In email clients that honor `@font-face` (Apple Mail, Samsung Mail), the first two fonts will block render until loaded rather than swapping to fallback.

**[LOW] Mobile breakpoint mismatch**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) {
  .mobile { width: 414px !important; }
```
Breakpoint fires at ≤418px but forces a fixed 414px width — functional, but non-standard. iPhone 14/15 Pro (430px logical width) will not trigger this breakpoint and will not receive the `.mobile` width reset.
