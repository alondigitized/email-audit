# The Memorial Day Event + Double Rewards ends TOMORROW!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- Forty-five. That's where the Crate & Barrel family tally sits since Sunday, and this is the *second* "ends TOMORROW" countdown clock the mothership has dropped on me in roughly twenty-four hours. Same ticking timer at the top — 00:05:06:55 now instead of 00:05:11:41 — same "Last Chance" lockup, same Double Rewards beat. I literally audited a near-identical version yesterday. The only fresh wrinkle is a "Sale Ends 5/26" callout on a John Boos cutting board at 15% off, which is a weirdly specific hero pick to anchor an entire "everything ends tomorrow" send.
- The grid below the hero is the usual lineup — dining tables, sofas, an outdoor sectional, accent chairs — but it's all small thumbnails with no prices visible at this zoom. So the urgency screams from the top and then the rest of the email shrugs.
- Would I click? No. I already know the Memorial Day machinery. If I were going to buy a table from C&B this weekend I'd have done it Saturday. Forward to a friend? No. Mark as read and move on.

## What's working

- The countdown clock is at least *honest* — there's a real deadline, not the rolling "ends Monday" that becomes "ends Tuesday."
- The John Boos cutting board call-out with a specific dollar amount (Reg. $34.95) and percentage is the most concrete thing on the page.
- Hero copy is clear about what's ending: Memorial Day + Double Rewards, both expiring together.
- Outdoor sectional gets some real estate, which is at least seasonally appropriate for late May.

## What's weak

- I just got this email yesterday. Same clock, same headline, same offer. Article doesn't do this — their "Up to 40% off ends Monday" landed *once* and then they pivoted to brand-building.
- Hero is a render-and-leave shot of a vague living room, not a hero product I can act on. Pottery Barn's Memorial Day send had the same problem this week.
- Zero prices visible in the product grid. If you're closing the sale, show the math.
- No mention of my Rewards balance or what "Double Rewards" actually equals in dollars for me. CB2 at least gave me a category hook last week.
- Furniture shipping windows are nowhere — I'm renovating two houses on a schedule, "ends tomorrow" means nothing if delivery is six weeks.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the duplicate send. If you ran a countdown email yesterday, today's version needs a different angle — categories left in stock, top-rated picks under $200, *something*. Don't just re-spin the clock.
- 2. Put my Rewards balance and the doubled-dollar figure at the top. "Your $40 becomes $80 if you order by tomorrow" beats any clock.
- 3. Show prices in the grid. Strike-through original, sale price, done. Wayfair manages it.
- 4. Add a delivery-by-date strip. Furniture buyers need to know if "ends tomorrow" gets them the table by July or September.
- 5. Lead with the John Boos board if that's the only item with a clean discount stamp — make the hero match the hero offer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your Rewards double until tomorrow — here's what's still in stock`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last day: Memorial Day pricing + 2x Rewards (delivery by [date])`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Order by 11:59 PT 5/26 to lock in doubled points on dining, outdoor, and bed.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `15% off John Boos boards + double rewards across the site — ends 5/26.`
