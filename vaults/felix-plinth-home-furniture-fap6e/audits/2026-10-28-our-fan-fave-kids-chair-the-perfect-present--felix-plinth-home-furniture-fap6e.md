# Our fan-fave kids’ chair = the perfect present
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-28**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction: this is a nice email for somebody else's house. I'm gutting two homes, and a kids' armchair isn't on either list. Crate & Barrel Kids is dressing it up well. The five-star strip, the "#1 holiday gift" headline and the monogrammed dark green chair under a tree all look like a Pinterest pin. I'd save the picture and keep scrolling.
- The email is long and tidy. Every block has a plain "Shop All Soft Seating" button, so I always know where to tap. The best deal, "up to 60% off clearance," is buried near the bottom, behind the NFL block and the baby section. The hero itself has no price on it. Pottery Barn leads with its maroon "Extra 20% off" strip and CB2 puts a countdown clock under the nav. Both give me the deadline before I scroll. This one makes me hunt for it.
- Would I click? Only if I had a kid to shop for. Would I forward it? Maybe to a friend with a toddler, and only because of the monogram. I'd mark it read and move on. It's also the fourth Crate-family email I've had this week, and the mirror deal earlier today already ate my attention for this brand.

## What's working

- The five-star strip plus "100+ 5-star reviews" in the hero gives me social proof before I've scrolled.
- The monogram close-up ("add their name or initials with custom embroidery") is the personalization hook that makes a chair feel like a gift.
- Every section ends in a clear, specific button, "Shop All Soft Seating" or "Find Your Team," so no CTA is vague.
- The holiday-tree styling is seasonal and photographs well, so it's on-brand for a saved-emails folder.
- The "we love these for you" product row, with names and prices, is the most useful section for a shopper like me.

## What's weak

- The hero sells a kids' chair, and nothing in it speaks to a renovation or to rooms I'm furnishing.
- There's no price or deadline in the first screen. "Up to 60% off clearance" is far down the page.
- The email is a long scroll of five or six unrelated ideas: chairs, NFL, baby, a $5,000 sweepstakes, a credit card, an app promo.
- The footer is a wall of tiny fine print I can't read without zooming.
- Crate & Barrel, CB2 and Kids all emailed me this week. I'm tired of seeing the same lifestyle imagery from one parent company.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the best offer in the hero. If clearance is the real incentive, lead with a number and a date, not a lifestyle shot.
- 2. Cut the page to the chair story plus one clearance block. Drop the sweepstakes and the card promo into a separate send.
- 3. Add shipping and lead-time info for custom embroidery. A gift buyer needs to know whether it arrives by December 25.
- 4. Add a size/color swatch row under the hero so I can see options without clicking.
- 5. Cap how often the Crate family emails me and suppress duplicates of the same deal.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email has no confirmed hard failures (QA: 0 issues, 3 warnings, 57% pass rate). The main findings are about 18 content and spacer images with no alt text, a viewport setting that blocks zoom, and header-level compliance checks the relay didn't capture. The HTML source was truncated, so footer and link markup couldn't be inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **73 tracking links not probed.** QA skipped them as click-redirect domains, so destination status, redirect chains and final URLs are unverified.
- **Per-recipient hash sent to a third party.** The same hash (`dbe8da718e29…f5a8`) appears as `mi_u=` on first-party `mi.crateandbarrel.com` pixels and as `s=` on five `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=…&n=1..5` requests (LiveRamp). That means five repeated third-party beacons carrying a recipient identifier. It isn't a defect, but it is worth disclosing in the privacy policy.
- **Repeated asset.** `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png` appears 6 times. It is likely a dynamic or placeholder image, but this can't be confirmed from the truncated source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text, about 31 flagged images.** They fall into three groups:
  - **16 content images** (the `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/...` jpg/gif/png files, plus two `s7d5.scene7.com` animated GIFs: `2026%5F1020%5FCBK%5FExclusiveSoftSeating%5Fezgif2` and `2026%5F0926%5FCBK%5FClearance%5FSecondaryStory.gif`). Images-off clients and screen readers get no content, and this is the real accessibility issue.
  - **2 spacers** (`25%5FMI%5FBottom%5FSpacer%5F40px%5FWhite` and `042025%5FCBK%5FKidsBeds1%5FRule%5FSpacer%5F40px`). These need `alt=""` explicitly.
  - **13 tracking pixels** (`rlcdn` ×5, `f3c0…png` ×6, `o.gif`, `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/…`). These should also carry `alt=""`. QA can't distinguish an absent attribute from an empty one, so check the markup.
- **Zoom blocked.** `<meta name="viewport" content="…maximum-scale=1">` disables pinch-zoom. `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` appears in `*{}` and `body`, which also blocks text scaling.
- **Dead media rule.** `@media (min-width:768px){img{max-width:600px}}` is always overridden by the later `@media (min-width:640px){img{max-width:640px}}`, since both match at ≥768px with equal specificity.
- **Template residue and validity.**
  - Leftover comment `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->`.
  - Unescaped ampersand in `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>`, which is invalid under the declared XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype.
  - Duplicate `<meta charset>` and `http-equiv` charset declarations, and three overlapping `format-detection` metas.
  - Generic `<title>` that doesn't reflect the subject.
- `lang="en"` is present, and VML/MSO conditional blocks are in place.
