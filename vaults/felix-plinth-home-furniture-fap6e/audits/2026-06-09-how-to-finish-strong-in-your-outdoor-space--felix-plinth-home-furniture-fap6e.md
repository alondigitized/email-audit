# How to: Finish strong in your outdoor space
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- Article sent me an outdoor living email four days ago — June 5, "Elevate your outdoor space," which I gave a 7 and genuinely saved because the timing was right for the second property. Now it's June 9 and here they are again, same category, same product types (rugs, pillows, planters), same earthy natural palette, just repackaged as a three-step how-to. The "Finishing touches" headline is clean. The step structure — Layer up, Add signs of life, Make it work — is smarter than the typical product grid. And "SHOP SAAME BAR CART" at the bottom is the most specific thing Article has done in recent memory, which I actually appreciate.
- But I'm sitting here comparing this to CB2's "We found you the perfect sofa" from last week — a send that felt like someone actually looked at my history — and this feels like Article grabbed their June outdoor playbook and ran it twice in a row with a different subject line. There's also not a single price visible in this email. Not a "starting at," not a member price, nothing. Pottery Barn showed me sofas from $899 in their last send. Article asks me to trust and click without giving me any math. When I'm picking which tab to open on my highest-rewards card, that matters.
- Would I click? Probably on the bar cart — that's a live purchase for the second house's patio. Would I forward? No. Would I mark as read and file it next to the June 5 send? Yes.

## What's working

- **The three-step editorial structure** is genuinely useful — "Layer up → Add signs of life → Make it work" reads like advice from someone who knows outdoor finishing, not a dump of inventory
- **"SHOP SAAME BAR CART"** is a named, specific product CTA and it's the best thing in this email; a verb, a noun, a product — that's how you write a button
- **Clean render throughout** — consistent earthy palette, no broken images, readable type at mobile size, no layout jank I can see
- **No sale pressure** — after the Memorial Day drumbeat ended (Article ran four sale emails in eight days through May 25), a content-led send with no "ENDS SUNDAY" urgency is welcome
- **Current season** — June outdoor is exactly right; both projects are in mid-flight and I'm actively pricing this category

## What's weak

- **Same beat, four days later** — June 5 was "Elevate your outdoor space," June 9 is "Finish strong in your outdoor space." Unless I missed a significant reset in between, this is the same audience, the same category, and the same product types on back-to-back Thursday/Monday sends
- **Zero pricing throughout** — no "starting at," no member price, no ballpark; furniture buying without price signals means I'm clicking just to get the number, which is friction
- **Generic category CTAs for Steps 1 and 2** — "SHOP RUGS, PILLOWS & MORE" and "SHOP PLANTERS" dump me onto a category page, not a curated selection; CB2 literally told me which sofa they picked for me
- **No loyalty or rewards mention** — I'm choosing between reward cards on every purchase; even a "free shipping on orders over $X" line would help me decide to buy today versus browse

## Recommendations

- 1. **Differentiate the second outdoor email** — if you're hitting me twice in four days on the same category, the sequel needs a real angle shift: a specific bundle deal, customer room photos, a "complete the look" based on browse history, or at least a "since you viewed outdoor last week" hook
- 2. **Add price anchors per step** — "Rugs from $199," "Planters from $89," "Bar carts from $349" — that's all I need to start the mental math before I click
- 3. **Promote the Saame Bar Cart to Step 1 or the hero** — it's your most specific, most differentiated product in this send; burying it at Step 3 means half your mobile readers never see it
- 4. **Surface social proof on the named product** — even a star rating or "482 reviews" under the Saame image gives me a reason to trust the pick without opening a new tab
- **Subject Alt A:** `"Three steps to finish your outdoor space (step 3 is underrated)"`
- **Subject Alt B:** `"The Saame Bar Cart: outdoor hosting, sorted"`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `"Rugs from $199, planters from $89 — here's how to layer it all together"`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `"Step 1: a rug. Step 2: something alive. Step 3: a bar cart you'll use every weekend."`

**Issue — CSS syntax error, trailing comma in selector:**
```css

```
The trailing comma before `{` is invalid CSS. Browsers are forgiving, but certain email clients (particularly older Webkit-based renderers) will drop the entire rule silently. All three selectors lose dark-mode color forcing in those clients.

**Issue — CSS syntax error, misplaced semicolon before `!important`:**

`color:#fff;!important` is invalid — the `!important` flag must be part of the value declaration (`color:#fff!important`), not a separate token after a semicolon. This rule will be dropped by spec-compliant parsers.
