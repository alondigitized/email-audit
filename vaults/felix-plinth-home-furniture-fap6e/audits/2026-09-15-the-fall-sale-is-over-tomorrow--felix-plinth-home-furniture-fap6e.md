# The Fall Sale is over tomorrow
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-15**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "The Fall Sale is over tomorrow" — CB2 finally leads with the thing that actually gets me to click: a deadline. Big moody bedroom hero shot, warm olive velvet headboard, "FALL 30% OFF SALE" stacked huge over it. That's clean, that's clear, I know exactly what I'm getting before I scroll an inch. This is the same 30%-off Fall Sale CB2's been running all week — I saw it Saturday in "This sofa could be vintage," I saw the swatch pitch before that — so nothing here is new information for me, but the urgency framing ("over tomorrow") is the sharpest version of this pitch they've sent yet.
- Where it loses me is right after the category grid: furniture, rugs, decor, tabletop, "SHOP THE FALL SALE" button — fine, that's a real path to purchase — and then it immediately swerves into "INTERIOR DESIGN SERVICES, book your free appointment," then "WALL ART & DECOR, new & best selling." Three different offers stacked in one email with no hierarchy telling me which one matters. I'm two houses into a reno with real budget in play; if I'm going to click, it's going to be the discount, not a design-consultation booking widget sandwiched in the middle of a sale email.
- Would I click? Yeah, actually — the "SHOP THE FALL SALE" button is unambiguous and I do need rugs for the second place. Would I forward it to a friend? No, this is routine CB2 inventory, not a specific find. Mark as read after I click through to rugs.

## What's working

- The hero does one job well: sale name, discount amount, deadline urgency, all in one glance, no scrolling needed.
- Category grid (Furniture, Rugs, Decor & Mirrors, Tabletop) gives me a real jump-off point instead of a generic "shop now" to the homepage.
- "EARN 10% BACK in Rewards Dollars... at CB2 and Crate & Barrel" — I'm a highest-rewards-card guy, so any loyalty math visible in the footer catches my eye, even buried down there.
- Fine print is upfront about exclusions (furniture/lighting/decor only, some brands excluded) rather than hiding it — more honest than CB2's "custom fabrics" send from two days ago.

## What's weak

- Interior Design Services and Wall Art & Decor modules dilute the one offer I actually care about — this reads like three emails duct-taped together.
- "Over tomorrow" urgency is a little undercut by the fact that CB2 has told me some version of "fall sale ending" for what feels like the third time this week — my Article and Wayfair inboxes are running the same countdown play right now, so the urgency numbs out fast.
- No specific hero product called out (no price, no name) — just moodboard imagery, so I can't tell if anything in that bedroom shot is actually on sale or just set dressing.
- The rewards/loyalty content is shoved into the app-download footer instead of near the offer where it'd actually influence my click.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the Interior Design Services and Wall Art & Decor sections from this send entirely — a sale-deadline email should stay a sale-deadline email. Save those for separate sends.
- 2. Put a real anchor product with a price under the hero (e.g., "Aldine sofa, was $1,899, now $1,329") so there's something concrete to click toward, like their "This sofa could be vintage" send did better.
- 3. Move the "Earn 10% Back in Rewards Dollars" line up near the CTA button — that's the single strongest lever for a rewards-card shopper like me and it's currently buried in the footer.
- 4. Tighten the countdown language — "over tomorrow" needs a literal date/time ("Ends Wed 9/16 at midnight ET") since I've seen too many vague CB2/Article deadlines this month to trust bare urgency copy anymore.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last day for 30% off — Fall Sale ends tomorrow`
- **Subject Alt B:** `30% off ends Wed: furniture, rugs, decor`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Rugs, tabletop, and decor — up to 30% off through tomorrow only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Last chance: 30% off furniture and rugs, plus 10% back in Rewards Dollars.`
