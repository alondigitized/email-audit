# Up to 60% off & selling FAST + Dining room furniture from Chris Loves Julia
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- Fifth Pottery Barn of the week. I want to land there first and stay there, because that number is doing a lot of work against everything else I'm about to say. The good news: this is actually the first one this week that gave me a reason to open beyond "here's the sale again." The Chris Loves Julia hook in the subject is the most differentiated thing Pottery Barn has put in front of me since Tuesday morning's 9/10 warehouse opener. I genuinely need dining room furniture — second home has a bare room right now, primary is getting a refresh — so that combination of a named collab plus a category I'm actively shopping almost redeems the cadence abuse.
- Almost. The hero is a warm, well-styled dining scene with the collab branding clearly visible, and the "Recommended Just For You" grid below it is doing something that looks like actual personalization — I can see bedroom and living room pieces that track with what I've been clicking. The Free Design Services callout near the bottom is the kind of thing I'd screen-cap and save to my inbox folder, because I am exactly the person who would use that.
- But I'm logging this as a "save and monitor" rather than an immediate click. The fifth email in a week from a single brand, with overlapping offers, trains me to wait — something better is probably coming Thursday. I wouldn't forward this. I'd forward Article's outdoor email from three days ago before I'd forward this.

## What's working

- **The Chris Loves Julia naming is the only differentiator this week** — it's the first Pottery Barn subject since the warehouse opener that tells me *why* this email is different from the four before it.
- **Dining room category is directly relevant** — I have a bare dining room in the second home and this email landed on the exact category, which is more than C&B's kitchen email did (great email, wrong room for my current list).
- **Visual hierarchy holds** — the eye goes hero → collab branding → category grid without fighting for attention; no broken images or overlapping text.
- **"Recommended Just For You" grid** looked like it reflected my actual browsing, which builds trust that they know who I am.
- **Free Design Services callout** is the one piece of content I'd actually act on — that's a service pitch, not just a discount pitch.

## What's weak

- **Fifth email this week is conditioning me to ignore Pottery Barn** — the warehouse sale framing is identical across all of them, and I now assume the 60% off claim is the same cherry-picked items every time.
- **"Selling FAST" is vague urgency** — no deadline, no inventory count, no timestamp. Article's outdoor email didn't need faux urgency because the products justified the open.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is the laziest possible button copy** — CB2 managed "Explore the Collection" even on a low-scoring send; "SHOP NOW" on a collab email wastes the Chris Loves Julia specificity.
- **No price anchoring on the collab items** — I can see the dining room imagery but no actual prices, no "was/now," no specific piece called out. C&B's 35%-off kitchen email at least named specific brands; this is all atmosphere.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Tighten the subject to lead with the collab, not the sale** — the 60% off headline is noise by email five; Chris Loves Julia is the news.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Chris Loves Julia: The Dining Room Edit`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Felix, your dining room — Chris Loves Julia is here`
- 2. **Preheader should carry the offer so the subject can carry the story** — currently the subject is doing two jobs badly.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Up to 60% off, warehouse prices, limited time.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The dining pieces selling fastest this week →`
- 3. **Swap "SHOP NOW" for "Shop the Collection"** or better, "Shop Chris Loves Julia Dining" — one extra word, doubles the intent signal for the algorithm and for me.
- 4. **Pin one specific hero product with a price** — show me the table from the collab, show me the "was $899, now $539," and I'm clicking. Right now the dining room image is beautiful but it's art direction, not a product page.
- 5. **Cap this brand at two warehouse sale emails per week** — the cadence is the single biggest conversion killer in this inbox, and no subject line fix compensates for it.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP code artifact in `<style>` block**
The client stylesheet block contains literal PHP `header()` function calls:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are inert in a rendered email but confirm the template was not properly compiled before send — server-side PHP leaked into HTML output. This is a build/QA pipeline defect.

**33 images missing `alt` text**
Affected assets include:
- `S26_TOP_SummerWarehouseSale_NoUrgency.jpg` (hero banner — no fallback text if images blocked)
- All 18 `slices_0N.jpg/gif` in the Chris Loves Julia Dining block
- All 18 `slices_0N.jpg` in the Email-Footer block
- Product images: `cayman-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `hydrocotton-quick-dry-towel-l.jpg`, `toscana-extendable-dining-table-60-1245-l.jpg`, and others
- Tracking pixels: open-pixel at `svopn.potterybarn.com`, turn.com pixel, Sailthru open beacon

Missing alt on content images breaks the email entirely in image-off environments (common in corporate Outlook, Gmail web default). Missing alt on tracking pixels is a minor standards violation but not operationally harmful.

**4 images served over HTTP (mixed content)**

| File | URL |
|---|---|
| `spacer_0.gif` | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` |
| `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` |
| `slices_02.jpg` (CC banner) | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` |
| Open pixel | `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=...` |

Gmail and Apple Mail proxy or block HTTP image sources. The open-pixel being HTTP means open tracking will fail silently in those clients.

**Zoom disabled via viewport + CSS**
`maximum-scale=1` in the viewport meta tag combined with `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;}` prevents users from zooming on mobile — a WCAG 1.4.4 failure and an accessibility risk.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` — some clients (Samsung Mail, some Outlook builds) surface the title in notifications or the message list. An empty title degrades discoverability.
