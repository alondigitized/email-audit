# So many NEW Memorial Day deals
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- Ten. I know because I've been counting. This is the tenth Pottery Barn email in roughly seventy-two hours, and the subject line's insistence that these are *new* deals doesn't land the way they intend — at this cadence, I've developed a Pavlovian eye-roll. That said, I opened it, because I'm simultaneously renovating two properties and I have a spreadsheet open that literally has a "PB shortlist" tab.
- The email is competent. Extra 20% off is right there in the hero, the product grid is clean, and there's a "Recommended Just for You" section that at least performs personalization. But "every room" is not a strategy. Article's welcome email last week picked a lane. Crate & Barrel's Double Rewards send last cycle — which I also audited — was more aggressive *and* more focused. This one feels like Pottery Barn took everything in the sale, put it in a grid, and shipped it before someone asked whether it was relevant to me specifically.
- I'd mark it as read before clicking. If a lamp from this grid shows up in a Wayfair email this week, I'm unsubscribing from one of them.

## What's working

- **Offer is front-and-center**: Extra 20% off is visible at the hero, no scrolling required, before any product noise.
- **"RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" is a smart insertion**: Even if it's a label, it signals that the platform *knows* me. That section sits in a spot I'll actually look.
- **Memorial Day timing is on**: I'm actively mid-purchase on outdoor and bedroom pieces — a sale tied to a real deadline is actionable.
- **Clean layout, no render bugs**: Every image loaded, text is readable, the grid feels considered rather than chaotic (relative to Wayfair, which throws emoji headers and sub-$30 kitchen mats at me and calls it personalization).
- **Credit card section earns its place**: I optimize for rewards points on big purchases — surfacing the Pottery Barn card near checkout logic is relevant to how I actually shop.

## What's weak

- **"Every room" is the definition of an untargeted blast**: I'm renovating two homes and have three active shortlists. A grid that covers sofas, outdoor furniture, and bedroom dressers simultaneously is noise, not signal. Compare this to Article's welcome email — that one picked a product category and owned it.
- **No deadline date visible**: "Memorial Day" implies urgency, but nowhere in the visible copy does it say *ends May 26* or *ends Monday*. Crate & Barrel's Memorial Day Event email at least implied a specific event window. Without a date, "limited time" feels like copy, not a constraint.
- **This is the tenth email in three days**: The cadence has poisoned the well. The "NEW" in the subject is doing heavy lifting to differentiate this from the nine emails I already archived. It's not convincing.
- **Small thumbnails, no depth**: The product grid shows stamps, not products. No dimensions, no material callouts, nothing that makes me stop and say "that's the piece." For a furniture shopper in active renovation mode, I need enough detail to feel like I can act.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Break the grid into a targeted room**: Stop sending "every room" blasts. Pick the two or three categories where I've actually browsed and build the email around those. My renovation is in outdoor + primary bedroom right now — that's knowable from my session data.
- 2. **Add the deadline date explicitly in the hero copy**: "Sale ends Monday, May 26" in the subhead. One line. Converts "browse later" to "decide now."
- 3. **Give the hero a single product to anchor**: One standout piece at the top, styled in context, with a visible price. The current hero is 100% offer copy — no product. Wayfair at least shows the item.
- 4. **Slim the "Recommended Just for You" section**: Make it four items, not a second grid. Name the rationale: "Based on your recent outdoor views" or similar. That's the conversion hook.
- 5. **Throttle the list**: Nine emails in 72 hours means the 10th lands like spam. Segment by active-shopping signal and cap the send to two per 72-hour window.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Extra 20% off — plus deals just added`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Outdoor, bedroom, dining — fresh picks added before Monday's deadline.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New items just dropped in the sale. Ends Memorial Day weekend.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP header() calls leaked into `<style>` block**
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are literal PHP function calls inside a `<style>` tag. They are inert in email clients but confirm a template/build pipeline failure — the ESP renderer did not execute or strip this server-side code before output. This is the highest-priority code defect in the send.
