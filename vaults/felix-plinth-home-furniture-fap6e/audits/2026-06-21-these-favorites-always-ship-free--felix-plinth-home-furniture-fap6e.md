# These favorites ALWAYS ship free →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- Twelve. That's the C&B family count now. I know because I've been rage-logging them since the "Up to 50% off furniture = the BEST time to shop" send landed in mid-June. And here's Crate & Kids dropping a **free shipping** email on the same June 21st date that already gave me the Warehouse Sale, the Crib Guide, and the Crate & Kids July 4th send I already audited. That's four from the C&B family on one calendar day.
- I opened this one because "always ship free" is actually a subject line that earns a click from someone hauling furniture into two addresses simultaneously — free shipping on large items is real money. But the second I'm in the body, I'm staring at a yellow kids' backpack. Then kids' bedding. Then toy storage bins. Then a crib. I don't have a nursery. I said that in the Crate & Kids July 4th audit, and I'm saying it again. The segmentation on this account is either broken or they genuinely don't care who they're mailing — and the sheer volume of sends makes me lean toward the latter.
- Mark as read. No click. Definitely not forwarding.

## What's working

- **Free shipping promise in the hero is unambiguous** — "it all ships FREE!" is the right hook for anyone buying bulky home goods; I clocked it immediately.
- **4th of July sale callout mid-email** — visible "Up to 50% off" banner gives the email a second offer anchor below the fold; it's not buried.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean** — yellow backpack hero up top, then a product grid, then the sale banner. The eye actually moves through it without confusion.
- **Brand is recognizable** — Crate & Kids is a trusted sub-brand and I know what I'm getting aesthetically.
- **Under-50-character subject** — "These favorites ALWAYS ship free →" is tight and renders well on my phone lock screen.

## What's weak

- **Wrong human, wrong products** — Every hero item and product tile is children's. Backpack, crib, kids' bedding, toy storage. I have zero nurseries and no known children's rooms. The CB2 "What's old is new again" send (8/10) I audited recently felt like it was talking to me specifically; this feels like it was auto-fired to anyone in the crateandbarrel.com domain with a pulse.
- **Fourth C&B family email on the same day** — Cadence is completely broken. Article sends me maybe one a week and I actually read them. C&B sent four brands' worth of email on June 21st and they all blur together.
- **The "favorites" framing is doing nothing** — "These favorites" implies I've browsed or saved something. I haven't curated a wishlist of kids' backpacks. This is generic batch-and-blast dressed up as personalization.
- **No adult furniture or home renovation content anywhere** — Not a single sofa, rug, or lighting piece. Even a single cross-brand tile pointing me to Crate & Barrel proper would've made this relevant.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment by life stage before sending** — If I haven't browsed kids' categories or added children's products to a registry/wishlist, don't send Crate & Kids. Route me to Crate & Barrel proper. This is the highest-leverage fix and it costs a list filter, not a redesign.
- 2. **Tie the free shipping offer to categories I actually care about** — "These favorites ship free" could be a great email *if* "favorites" meant adult furniture, rugs, or lighting. That's a segmentation call, not a creative call.
- 3. **Throttle the C&B family sends** — One brand per day maximum, or a combined digest. Article manages one send a week and I read every one. Four C&B-family emails in a day is how I get to "unsubscribe from all."
- 4. **Give the free shipping offer specificity** — Does it apply to oversized items? Furniture? There's no asterisk visible but also no explicit confirmation that my 8-foot credenza qualifies. Pottery Barn's "Happy first day of SUMMER!" send at least gestured at furniture; this doesn't.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` blocks pinch-to-zoom. WCAG 1.4.4 failure; also triggers accessibility warnings in Litmus/Email on Acid.

- **Global `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`** — `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;-ms-text-size-adjust:none;}` applied via universal selector overrides OS-level accessible text scaling for all elements.

- **Conflicting responsive image breakpoints** — two `@media` rules define `img {max-width}` at overlapping ranges:
  ```css
  @media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
  @media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
  ```
  At ≥768px both fire; the 768px rule wins by source order, capping images at 600px on wide screens — likely unintentional given the 640px container context.

- **Brand/title mismatch** — `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` while the sending entity is "Crate & Kids." Some clients (notably iOS Mail preview, Gmail web tab) surface the `<title>`. Should read "Crate & Kids."

- **Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags** — the combined declaration appears first, then three individual repeats:
  ```html
  <meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
  <!-- later: -->
  <meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
  <meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
  <meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
  ```
  Redundant but harmless; indicates copy-paste template debt.

- **Dual charset declarations** — `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` and `<meta charset="utf-8">` coexist. Benign, but redundant.
