# Big news! EXTRA 20% off is ON + Outdoor sale ⛱️ Furniture & more
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- Fifty-five. That's my rough Pottery Barn email count for the month, and I'm only on July 2nd. This one drops today as the second PB send I've already opened this morning — the first was "July 4th deals, going fast" which had the exact same Extra 20% off hero. "Big news!" in the subject line is doing some serious heavy lifting for an offer I've now seen in virtually every PB send since the 29th.
- The outdoor furniture angle is legitimately where my head is right now. I'm sourcing patio furniture for the second home and haven't pulled the trigger, so a 20% off outdoor sale should stop me cold. It doesn't — because my brain pattern-matched this email before I finished opening it. The execution is clean: hero image reads fast, the "Savings for Outdoor Living" headline is direct, "SHOP ALL SALE" is an actual button with actual words. The Affirm module at the bottom is smart for high-ticket furniture. But none of that matters when I've already mentally filed the deal three sends ago.
- Contrast this with Article, who ran one July 4th sale opener on June 26th and a clean follow-up nudge — two emails total, and I actually clicked. PB is sending twice what Article sent in a single morning. I'm closing this without clicking, and I'm definitely not forwarding it.

## What's working

- "Savings for Outdoor Living" hero copy is exactly on brief for where I am — patio furniture is the active shopping mode right now
- 4th of July seasonal peg is credible and the summer outdoor photography renders cleanly at hero size
- "SHOP ALL SALE" is a real CTA — verb, noun, no vague "discover" or "explore" nonsense
- Affirm "Buy Now, Pay Over Time" module is smart for a brand whose outdoor sectionals run $2k+; reduces the purchase-decision friction on big-ticket items
- "Earn $10 Rewards" loyalty line in the footer is a small but real signal that PB sees me as an existing member, not a cold acquisition

## What's weak

- "Big news!" as a subject opener is objectively false — the news is that it's the same offer from yesterday, and the day before
- The email tries to do two jobs simultaneously — sale clearance AND "Discover New Arrivals" — and the second section undercuts the urgency of the first; CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" I reviewed this week committed to a single idea and didn't dilute it
- No product-level specifics visible anywhere in the hero — for outdoor furniture, lead time and in-stock status matter more to me than a percentage badge; Wayfair at least shows item names and price callouts on their outdoor sends
- "Recommended Just for You" thumbnails look like rugs or bedding, not outdoor furniture — the personalization module and the sale category appear to be talking to different versions of me in the same email
- Second PB email of the morning with near-identical creative. That's not a targeting win, that's a cadence failure.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the cadence to one PB send per day maximum during sale windows.** The math is not "more sends = more revenue at this point." I'm actively developing inbox antibodies.
- 2. **Kill "Big news!" from the subject.** If the offer is genuinely new or extended, say that specifically — "Extended through July 4th" is news. "Extra 20% off" for the fifth day running is not.
- 3. **Put a ship-by date in the hero.** "Ships by July 3rd" or "In stock for summer delivery" would move me faster than any percentage discount — lead time anxiety is the real purchase blocker for outdoor furniture in early July.
- 4. **Commit to one campaign per send.** Either sale clearance or new arrivals. Not both.
- 5. **Audit the "Recommended for You" module** — if the sale is outdoor furniture, the recs should be outdoor furniture, not what appears to be bedding.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Outdoor furniture: Extra 20% off ends July 4th`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Ships by July 3rd — outdoor furniture at 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Patio sets, sectionals & more — lowest prices of the summer`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free shipping on qualifying orders. Stock is moving.`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "EXTRA 20% off + Outdoor Sale" (2026-07-02)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard 403 failures (one on the primary credit card CTA, one on a cross-brand asset) directly break user journeys. A PHP server-side header block was accidentally embedded inside a `<style>` tag, confirming a templating pipeline error. 38 images lack alt text, and three image sources are served over HTTP.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Primary CTA broken (403)**
The "Apply & Buy" credit card banner link returns HTTP 403:
`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&...`
This is a revenue-bearing CTA. 403 suggests an expired or misconfigured campaign token (`cp_cid=4fcb68173ced840b`).

**FAIL — Cross-brand spacer image broken (403)**
`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` returns 403 and is also served over HTTP. This is a West Elm template artifact carried into a Pottery Barn send. If used as a layout spacer it may cause rendering gaps.

**INFO — 25 click-redirect links unverified**
All CTAs route through `click.e.potterybarn.com`. Destination URL parameters (including UTM chains) were not probed. See Section 6.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CRITICAL — PHP code injected into `<style>` block**
The final `<style>` block in `<head>` contains raw PHP `header()` calls that were never processed by the template engine:
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are not valid CSS. They will be silently ignored by email clients but confirm a broken template preprocessing step — this code was intended for a dynamic image server, not the email `<head>`.

**HIGH — 38 images missing `alt` text**
Every content image and most footer images have empty or absent `alt` attributes, including primary hero images (`S26_Top_July4Sale_NoUrgency.jpg`, all `slices_01–09.jpg` outdoor sale images, all footer `slices_01–18.jpg`). Email clients that block images by default will render a blank email with no text fallback.

**MEDIUM — 3 image sources served over HTTP**
Will be blocked or flagged by Gmail, Outlook 2016+, and iOS Mail when loaded under HTTPS contexts:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` (open tracking pixel — also HTTP)

**LOW — Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` renders as blank in browser previews and some accessibility tools.

**LOW — `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
`<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` disables pinch-to-zoom on mobile, an accessibility regression.

**LOW — Mixed seasonal asset paths**
Content images reference `S26_Sale_MemorialDay_Outdoor/` (a May campaign path) inside a July 4 email. A footer banner references `F26_Bottom_NewArrivals.jpg` (a Fall 2026 asset). These may be intentional asset reuse but are worth flagging for QA.
