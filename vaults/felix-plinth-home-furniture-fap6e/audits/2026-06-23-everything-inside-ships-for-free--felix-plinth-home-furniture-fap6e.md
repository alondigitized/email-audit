# Everything inside ships for FREE!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- Five Crate & Barrel family emails in two days, and now Crate & Kids is in the mix with a free shipping push. The subject landed fine — "Everything inside ships for FREE!" reads instantly on mobile and I'll admit my thumb hovered. But then I opened it and the hero is a mustard backpack. Not a crib. Not a dresser. A backpack. I'm in the middle of spec-ing out a kids' bedroom for the second house — the Finn Collection email two days ago was actually useful — but the merchandise story here swings from room-ready furniture to strollers to what looks like travel bags and that's a hard mental pivot to follow on a Tuesday.
- The free shipping is a legitimately good offer — furniture from C&K adds up fast on shipping and I'd absolutely factor that in before hitting checkout. The problem is the email doesn't make a convincing case for *what* to ship free. The stroller mid-email killed my intent. I'm not in baby-gear mode; I'm in "where does the bookcase go" mode. Article's bedroom storage email earlier this week did a better job staying in one lane and making the room feel achievable.
- Mark as read, check back if I'm specifically shopping kids' furniture. Would not forward — there's nothing editorial or shareable here.

## What's working

- **Sender is instantly recognizable** — "Crate & Kids" with the familiar logo reads as the C&B family brand; I didn't have to think about whether this was spam.
- **Hero offer is legible at a glance** — "ships FREE" is above the fold and the message gets through even at thumbnail size.
- **Layout is clean, no obvious render breaks** — the grid holds, the images are loading, no lorem ipsum bleeding through.
- **Kids bedroom scenes mid-email** — there are room vignettes in there that actually look furnishing-ready; if that were the lead, the email would've landed differently.

## What's weak

- **Backpack as hero is the wrong door** — opens with what reads as a back-to-school or travel accessory when I'm in renovation mode; immediately signals category mismatch.
- **The stroller is doing serious damage** — a black stroller prominently featured mid-email is aggressively baby-registry territory, not second-home finishing. Pottery Barn at least keeps its kids-item emails to furniture/bedding, which stays adjacent to what I actually shop.
- **No deadline on the free shipping** — "ships FREE" without an expiry is basically "maybe" — and after seventeen Wayfair emails this month where urgency was faked, a real deadline would actually feel refreshing here.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is the fifth C&B family touchpoint in 48 hours. Kitchen brands, Ninja/Fellow, Finn Collection, new beds and dressers, now this. I'd have appreciated a day off.

## Recommendations

- **1. Lead with furniture, suppress the accessories** — if the email is "everything ships free," then show the things where shipping cost actually stings: dressers, bookcases, cribs, upholstered pieces. A backpack and a stroller make this feel like a clearance sweep, not a confident category offer.
- **2. Add a real deadline** — "Free shipping through Sunday" changes behavior. CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" at least felt bounded; this floats.
- **3. Segment by actual intent signal** — I bought into the Finn Collection. Show me Finn Collection accessories, or what completes the room, not the stroller. Article served me "bedroom storage essentials" as a direct follow-on to a wardrobe browse — that kind of sequencing makes me feel like the brand is paying attention.
- **4. Retire the mobile app CTA for customers already shopping via browser** — bottom-of-email "download our app" blocks are wasted space for someone actively triaging from desktop.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` brand mismatch**
`<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` — sender is "Crate & Kids." Title populates the browser tab when preheader/open occurs in webmail; this is the wrong brand.

**Unfilled template placeholder shipped in production**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
<!----> 
```
Both lines are visible in rendered source. "IMPUT" is a typo for "INPUT." The trailing empty comment `<!---->` is a second artifact from the same block.

**Conflicting `img` `max-width` breakpoints (ordering bug)**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
The 640px query appears *after* the 768px query in the stylesheet. On viewports ≥768px both rules match; the later rule (640px max) wins due to cascade order, overriding the intended 600px cap. Images on wide-viewport clients (Gmail web, Outlook.com) render up to 640px instead of the intended 600px.

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). No functional email reason to restrict scaling.

**Global `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `*` selector**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Disables OS-level text scaling for all elements, not just the body. Amplifies the zoom issue above.

**`color-scheme` locked to light only**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Apple Mail and iOS Mail will not attempt dark mode reflow. No `prefers-color-scheme` media query present. Acceptable as a deliberate choice, but worth flagging if dark mode support is desired.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
One combined tag and three individual tags cover the same attributes (`telephone`, `address`, `date`). Redundant; the combined tag alone is sufficient.
