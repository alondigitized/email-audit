# Fill your cart: FREE shipping starts now!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- Fifteen. I went back and recount — this is the fifteenth email from the Crate & Barrel family of brands since July 8th. Two and a half days. And before I say one honest word about the creative, I need that number on the record, because at fifteen, even a genuinely good email doesn't land — it just gets absorbed into the fog of "another one."
- What made this one actually register is that it's from Crate & Kids, not Crate & Barrel proper. My first read was "did they just reroute their free shipping blast to a sub-brand list?" And the answer appears to be: yes. The hero is a soft nursery armchair. The category rail says "Shop Storage," "Shop Bedding," "Baby Gifts." I don't have kids. I'm renovating two houses right now — I am exactly the person who should be buying rugs and lighting and upholstered dining chairs from these people — and they sent me a baby gifts email with the same free shipping hook I've seen four times this week already.
- I'm not clicking. I'm not forwarding. I'm marking this read and quietly wondering whether the Crate & Barrel family has a single suppression list or whether every sub-brand just fires independently into the same inbox.

## What's working

- **Free shipping offer is immediately legible** — "starts today! free shipping" is right in the hero, no scrolling needed; I knew the deal in under two seconds.
- **Design Desk section** is a genuinely smart insertion — a free design consultation is something I would actually use for either house, and it breaks the cadence of pure promotional copy.
- **"500+ new fall arrivals, starting at $59"** is a concrete, scannable anchor — a real number, a real price floor, and it suggests breadth.
- **Visual layout is clean**, no render bugs I can see — the hero image loaded, text doesn't overlap, nothing looks broken.

## What's weak

- **Wrong sub-brand for me entirely** — I'm not on the Crate & Kids list intentionally. Nursery chairs and baby gifts do nothing for a renovation audience. Wayfair at least keeps its outdoor rug blasts routed to people who bought outdoor rugs.
- **Fifteenth email in roughly 60 hours** — Article pivoted from four 4th of July sale emails to a fresh new-arrivals send and scored an 8 from me. This brand sent the same free shipping hook via four different sub-brands without resetting.
- **"Fill your cart" subject framing** — that's homepage language, not segmented language. Compare to CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" — even though that email had cadence problems too, the copy at least sounded like someone understood what CB2 carries.
- **No obvious member/loyalty pricing visible** — for a customer who tracks rewards card math, I want to know whether free shipping stacks with anything. Nothing in the scan answers that.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress cross-brand sends to the same inbox** — if I've already received four free-shipping emails from Crate & Barrel this week, Crate & Kids should not be firing the same promotion twelve hours later. One coordinated family send would do.
- 2. **Segment Crate & Kids by purchase history** — I've never bought a crib. Route this to the nursery buyers, not the renovation buyers.
- 3. **Activate the Design Desk offer as the primary CTA** for this audience — that's the most differentiated thing in the email and it's buried below the fold.
- 4. **Drop "fill your cart" framing and write to the decision** — something that acknowledges the renovation mindset would have landed; the current subject is generic.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Conflicting desktop image max-width rules** — two media queries both labeled "For desktop" produce an override conflict:

```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```

For viewports ≥768px, the 600px rule wins because it appears later in the cascade. Images will be narrower than intended on large desktop clients. The 640px breakpoint rule is effectively dead for viewports ≥768px.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** blocks user zoom:

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) recommends against suppressing pinch-to-zoom. This affects users with low vision reading on mobile.

**`line-height: 100%` applied to all elements via `*` selector** can collapse multi-line text blocks. Safer values are unitless (e.g., `1.4`) or explicit `px`.
