# ON SALE: Expertly crafted furniture
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- Thirteen. I went back and counted — thirteen Pottery Barn emails, and here we are again with furniture on sale. The frustrating thing is the email itself is fine. "Up to 70% off" in the hero, a clean category grid covering outdoor, sofas, rugs — genuinely the stuff I'm buying for the second home right now. If this had been email two or three, I'd have flagged it immediately. Instead my first emotion is low-grade irritation that yesterday's send was literally "Online exclusive: Furniture ON SALE" and today's is "ON SALE: Expertly crafted furniture." Different words, identical concept, 24 hours apart.
- But I opened it. The subject cleared the bar — the word "furniture" and "ON SALE" is basically the search query I have running in my head while juggling two renovation punch lists. And once I'm in, there's real content: the category nav hits outdoor, sofas, rugs, curtains, and tabletop, which is a solid sweep of where my money is going this month. The Affirm section is actually interesting since I'm carrying two projects simultaneously. The 10% rewards callout near the bottom is why I'm still opening these at all — I have the PB card and I'm doing math every time.
- I wouldn't forward this. I'd save it to my "Shop Later" folder and maybe tap the sofas link before my next store run. That's the honest answer — low-urgency folder-file, not a click.

## What's working

- **The category grid is doing real work.** Outdoor, Sofas & Chairs, Rugs, Curtains in one visible band — that's my full punch list for the primary. Smart.
- **"Save on Furniture" sub-section is honest about scope.** "Clearance & floor models at your local store" tells me before I click what I'm actually getting. CB2's "Up to 40% off is on now" doesn't even clarify in-body what's included — this copy is more transparent.
- **10% Back in Rewards is visible.** I use the PB card specifically for this. Seeing it reminds me why this brand gets the swipe over Crate & Barrel on the same item.
- **"Recommended Just for You" section exists.** It's not doing heavy personalization work but it signals they know something about me. Better than Wayfair's "BIG summer DEALS" with emoji spam and a completely generic grid behind it.
- **Affirm financing at the bottom.** For someone carrying two renovations at once, Buy Now Pay Over Time in a furniture email is the right upsell.

## What's weak

- **Yesterday's email was also about furniture being on sale.** "Online exclusive: Furniture ON SALE" and then "ON SALE: Expertly crafted furniture" 24 hours later — I notice this. It reads like two different teams hitting send on the same campaign without talking to each other.
- **No deadline anywhere visible.** Article ran "Last chance: 4th of July Sale ends today" and I clicked it because that's a real forcing function. Pottery Barn has no date on this sale, no "ends Sunday," nothing. The urgency is borrowed from the word "SALE" itself.
- **"Floor models at your local store" creates friction for an online buyer.** If the real 70%-off furniture is in-store only, that needs to be above the fold, not buried in sub-copy. I'm going to click SHOP NOW, see online pricing at 20% off, and feel mildly duped.
- **"Expertly crafted" in the subject is wasted real estate.** That's brand-speak, not a buying signal. Thirty-five characters to tell me furniture is on sale and three of those words are "expertly crafted."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline to every touchpoint.** "Sale ends Sunday" in the hero, the subject, and the preheader. Article forced my hand on the 4th of July sale with a single date. Pottery Barn is leaving money on the table by not doing the same thing across 13 sends.
- 2. **Differentiate this send from yesterday's.** If yesterday was "Online exclusive: Furniture ON SALE," today should spotlight a specific category (outdoor, sofas) or a price anchor ("From $299") — not a synonym for the same headline.
- 3. **Surface the store-vs-online split up front.** One sentence in the hero: "Biggest savings on floor models in-store; online exclusives below." Set expectations before the click.
- 4. **Promote the Affirm section higher.** It's buried near the footer but for a dual-renovation shopper it's a legitimate consideration that could shift the purchase timing. Put it right under the hero or adjacent to the furniture CTA.
- 5. **Make "RECOMMENDED FOR YOU" earn its placement.** The product grid feels generic at this email's resolution. If the algorithm knows I've been browsing outdoor sectionals, lead with outdoor sectionals, not throw pillows.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Furniture sale ends Sunday — up to 70% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your sofa shortlist is on sale right now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sofas, outdoor, rugs — floor model deals in-store, online exclusives shipping free.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Up to 70% off, plus 10% back in rewards. Sale wraps Sunday.`

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**

**Injected PHP headers in `<style>` block (template defect):**
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are PHP `header()` calls sitting inside a `<style>` tag. They produce no visible output in email clients (silently ignored), but indicate the template was assembled by string-concatenating a PHP image-serving script with the HTML shell — a fragile build pipeline that could break if the PHP block expands.

**Missing `alt` text — 40 images** across banner, product grid, and footer slices (full list in QA findings). Worst offenders by user impact:
- `F26_TOP_SummerSalePartC_NoUrgency.jpg` — hero banner; images-off subscribers see a blank above the fold
- All 14 `S25_Sale_B12_JuneExtraOffClearanceFurniture/slices_*.jpg` — product grid completely unreadable without images
- All 18 `Email-Footer/slices_*.jpg` — footer nav inaccessible

**HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources — 4 confirmed:**
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true…` (open-tracking pixel)

All four will be blocked in Outlook, Apple Mail (Link Tracking Protection), and any client enforcing mixed-content rules.

**Open-tracking pixel is HTTP:** `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/` — open-rate data will be under-counted in any HTTPS-enforcing client.

**`<title>` element is empty** — affects tab/window labeling and some screen reader announce flows when the email is opened in a browser.

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta** — suppresses user zoom on mobile; considered an accessibility violation under WCAG 1.4.4.
