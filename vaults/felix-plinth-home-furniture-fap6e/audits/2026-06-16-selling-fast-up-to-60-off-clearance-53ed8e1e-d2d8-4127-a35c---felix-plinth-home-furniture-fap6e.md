# Selling fast: up to 60% off clearance!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- Sixty-six. That's where the C&B family counter sits in my inbox after this one lands, and I want to lead with the number because it's the only context that matters. Yesterday — June 15, same C&B ecosystem — I got "IT'S ON | Up to 60% off clearance!" from Crate & Barrel main. Today I get "Selling fast: up to 60% off clearance!" from **Crate & Kids**. The offer is identical. The headline structure is identical. The only meaningful difference is that this one is pitching me **children's nursery furniture, baby toys, and lilac kids' room color stories** — and I don't have kids. I renovate homes. There is no child in either property I'm working on. This is the C&B data team sending a kids-vertical sub-brand clearance email to a home-renovation adult buyer, and it shows.
- The email itself renders cleanly. The hero hierarchy works. The "up to 60% off + new markdowns" message is front and center, the "Shop Clearance" button is impossible to miss, and the product grid below it is organized. But none of that matters when the segment is wrong. I'm being shown cribs, baby bouncers, and lilac nursery palettes. I'd be more annoyed if I weren't already numb from the volume.
- Mark as read. No click. Definitely not forwarding — the only person I'd send a kids clearance email to is someone expecting, and that's not a conversation I'm in right now.

## What's working

- **Hero offer is immediately legible** — "up to 60% off + new markdowns" reads in under a second, and the "Shop Clearance" button is well-sized and well-placed.
- **Clean render, no broken images** — everything loaded, layout held together, no visual junk to fight past.
- **"Selling fast" creates mild urgency** — it's a soft pull, but it's better than nothing in the subject line.
- **Navigation bar is honest about the brand** — Shop Nursery, Shop Toys, Shop Big Kids makes it immediately clear what this email is, so I don't waste time hunting for adult furniture.
- **App promotion with a $500 sweepstakes hook** — low-effort entry, and it gives me a reason to install or open the app that isn't just "browse more stuff."

## What's weak

- **Fundamental segment mismatch** — Crate & Kids clearance sent to a non-parent adult male is a suppression failure, not a targeting win. Pottery Barn sent me outdoor furniture and refresh content on June 15 and it landed because it matched what I'm actually doing.
- **This is the second "up to 60% off clearance" hit from the C&B family in 24 hours** — yesterday it was Crate & Barrel main, today it's the kids sub-brand. The offer blur is real; my brain has already detuned to this headline.
- **"Color of the year: lilac!" section** — this is genuinely aimed at nursery decorators. I'm choosing paint colors for a mudroom and a primary bedroom. Lilac kids' room pushes me fully out of the frame.
- **No adult-crossover hook** — Crate & Kids does carry some items (accent pieces, baskets, storage) that could conceivably live in an adult home. Nothing in the email makes that case.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress non-parent adult buyers from kids sub-brand sends** — this is the single highest-leverage change. If purchase history shows zero kids items and the account has no nursery registry, pull the address. Article doesn't send me infant content. Wayfair at least surfaces outdoor rugs when I'm in an outdoor-buying mode. C&B has the purchase data to do this.
- 2. **Break the 24-hour same-offer cadence** — if the clearance email went out June 15 under the main brand, the kids sub-brand version on June 16 should either be suppressed entirely or carry a meaningfully different offer angle (e.g., "kids furniture for the guest room you're finally finishing").
- 3. **Give "Selling fast" an actual deadline** — "Shop by Sunday" or "Clearance ends Monday" transforms the subject from ambient pressure to a reason to act today.
- 4. **Surface the adult-adjacent product angle** — if the email must reach me, lead with storage, organization, or furniture that's plausibly home-agnostic. One hero image of a natural wood bookcase reads adult; a crib does not.

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Broken webfont assets (4 × HTTP 403)**

All four `@font-face` woff2 files served from `crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/` return 403 Forbidden:

```
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/1e07d600-3787-4517-bd3d-b140f9e54ec2.woff2
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/7ec25fd5-2752-4d84-b307-af3faabc21bb.woff2
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/aaa41944-25b3-4265-80b2-5564efe9b2d7.woff2
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/fd5f8f50-d539-4a7d-b893-99501d746ef2.woff2
```

Clients that load remote fonts (iOS Mail, some webmail) will fall back to the system stack silently. The `@font-face` declarations in `<head>` are dead weight and should either be fixed or removed.

**22 click-tracked links not probed**

All outbound links pass through a click-redirect domain and were skipped by the HTTP probe. Destination URLs and UTM coverage could not be verified (see §6).

**Tracking pixel duplication**

`mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png` fires six times with an identical `mi_u` token. This is likely intentional for MI dynamic-content rendering, but warrants confirmation that it is not a template copy-paste error inflating open counts.

`sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif` fires five times (`n=1` through `n=5`), which is standard LiveRamp audience sync behavior — no action needed.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**26 images missing `alt` text** — confirmed by QA. Affected assets span product photography, GIFs, spacer images, and the MI tracking pixel. Partial list of content images:

```
bbf6f9d4-86cc-437a-aa80-391067b74306.png  (header/logo area)
68910f1a / 75ad9bd7 / 62b7b090 / 5429e84a / 87a83d3f / e5169222 / 5d324a1d / c044610c / 971aa10f (product shots)
2026%5F0315%5FCBK%5FClearance%5F01%5F05.gif  (hero GIF)
33367d69 / 62b8e289 (animated GIFs)
```

Alt-blocked environments (Outlook by default, some corporate proxies) will render blank boxes for every content image. Spacer/pixel images should use `alt=""`, not omit the attribute.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

This prevents user zoom on mobile — an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4). Should be removed or replaced with `user-scalable=yes`.

**Duplicate `<meta name="format-detection">` tags**

`telephone=no`, `address=no`, and `date=no` each appear twice (once in a combined tag, once standalone). Redundant but harmless; clean up to reduce parse noise.

**`<title>` brand mismatch**

```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```

The sending domain/brand is `Crate & Kids`. The `<title>` is used by some screen readers and accessibility tools as document context. Should read `Crate & Kids`.
