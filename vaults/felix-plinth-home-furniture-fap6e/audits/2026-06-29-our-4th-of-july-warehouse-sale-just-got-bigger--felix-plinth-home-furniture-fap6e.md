# Our 4th of July Warehouse Sale just got BIGGER!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-29**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-eight. That's my conservative count of C&B family emails in my June inbox, and this one lands with the subject line escalation I was absolutely dreading: the sale didn't just continue, it "got BIGGER." The hero delivers some form of that same promise — a 4th of July sale splash with what looks like tableware and coastal-adjacent product imagery in the top fold. Not furniture. Tableware. I'm mid-renovation on two properties and I opened this hoping to see, I don't know, a sectional I haven't seen six times this month.
- The structure is fine — clean C&B grid, no obvious render breaks, multiple product modules working their way down the scroll. But the problem isn't execution; it's the premise. Telling me a sale I've already been notified about four times in four days just "got BIGGER" is not a news event. It's a desperation signal. CB2's warehouse sale email two days ago — the one that scored the highest in this category cycle for me — made a decision: one hero product, one room, one strong visual, done. This email is the opposite of that.
- I will mark this read, maybe toss it in my folder out of habit, and not click anything. Not forwarding this. There's nothing here that a friend needs to know.

## What's working

- C&B brand recognition is instant — logo, color palette, and grid all land as expected; no confusion about what I opened.
- The "BIGGER" escalation at least signals *something changed*, even if the payoff inside doesn't deliver meaningfully on that promise.
- The multi-module layout does let me scan quickly down the email for category fits — I could theoretically spot a furniture deal without reading copy.
- 4th of July timing is correctly reflected; this doesn't feel stale or off-season.

## What's weak

- The hero leans into what looks like kitchen/tableware, not furniture — wrong category for a two-home renovation shopper looking for sofas and case goods.
- "Just got BIGGER!" with the exclamation is a tired escalation pattern: the sale was already at 50% off, so what exactly got bigger? New items? Deeper cuts? The body does not answer this clearly above the fold.
- Cadence is catastrophically off. Four sends in one day on June 28, and now another on June 29. Pottery Barn managed to feel editorial with "Our TOP picks (up to 70% off)" — curated, specific. This feels like the sixth alarm on a snooze loop.
- Nothing in this email treats me like a known customer: no acknowledgment of category preference, no "furniture picks for you," nothing that suggests C&B knows I've been opening their emails all month.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the "BIGGER" claim to a specific product category above the fold.** If the expansion is furniture — which is what I actually care about — lead with that. A single hero room shot ("We added 200 more furniture styles") would justify the subject line and immediately serve my intent.
- 2. **Cut the send frequency or segment the audience.** Four sends on June 28 from the same sender burned most of the goodwill this email is trying to cash. A suppression rule for people who opened the warehouse sale already would've helped.
- 3. **Swap the hero product category** — tableware in the hero of an email called "Warehouse Sale just got BIGGER" reads as filler, not news. If you want me to look at tableware, send me a tableware email. Don't bury a sofa somewhere in module three.
- 4. **Make one concrete offer explicit in the first scroll** — "50% off all upholstery, 3 days only" is infinitely more clickable than "sale just got bigger," which is not a number.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Unencoded ampersand in `<title>`:** `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` — `&` must be `&amp;` per XHTML 1.0 rules. Malformed in strict parsers.

- **Duplicate `<meta name="format-detection">` tags:** `telephone=no`, `address=no`, and `date=no` each appear twice — once in the combined tag and again as standalone tags. Redundant; consolidate into one declaration.

- **`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** blocks user-initiated zoom on mobile, which is an accessibility failure (WCAG 1.4.4 — Resize Text).

- **Conflicting `img` max-width media queries:** The `min-width: 768px` rule sets `img { max-width: 600px }` but the subsequent `min-width: 640px` rule sets `img { max-width: 640px }`. At ≥768px both rules fire; cascade order means the 640px rule wins, overriding the intended 600px desktop cap.

- **`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` in global `*` selector:** Suppresses OS-level font scaling across all elements. Combine with `line-height: 100%` on `*` and this creates a readability risk on small screens.

- **Unresolved template placeholder comment:** `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` (note typo: "IMPUT") is a production build artifact. If no custom font was intentionally omitted, verify font import was not accidentally dropped. Strip comment from send regardless.
