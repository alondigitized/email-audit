# STARTS NOW: Up to 20% off our Back-to-School Sale!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- Four. That's the C&B family count in my inbox today, and I'm leading with it again because the number is the whole story. I've already sat through the Summer Sale hero, a "Don't Miss It" follow-up, and a "STARTS NOW" that somehow didn't feel like the one I'm looking at right now — and here we are again. This one is Crate & Kids pitching back-to-school, which is at least a *different* campaign than the 60%-off summer drum beat, so I'll give them that.
- The hero lands cleanly — 20% off, "Shop Now," no mystery. The product grid below it is doing real work: I can see backpacks and lunch bags, but also what looks like storage furniture and dressers, which is the part that actually made me linger. I'm literally trying to finish two kids' rooms right now (separate from the adult spaces) so the storage angle is not totally irrelevant. But the sweepstakes module buried in the middle kills the momentum dead. Article's "Create a personalized dining room" send last week had one offer, one scroll, one click destination — and it felt like a smarter brand. This feels like they stitched two different briefs together and hoped I wouldn't notice.
- Would I click? Probably the furniture section if I could get there fast. Would I forward it? No. Would I file it in my inbox folder for later? Maybe — but I'd lose it under the three other C&B emails sitting above it.

## What's working

- **Hero discount is unambiguous** — "Up to 20% off" is right in the headline, visible without scrolling, no click required to find the number.
- **The product grid earns its space** — backpacks and school supplies at the top, but storage furniture visible lower in the scroll, which is the category I'd actually buy right now.
- **Seasonal timing is real** — Back-to-School in late July is exactly when I'd be furnishing kids' rooms, not October; C&K actually timed this correctly.
- **The Design Crew loyalty section shows up** — I noticed it at the bottom; it's a light trust signal that says "we know you're a repeat customer."
- **No render bugs** — the layout looks clean at every scroll point; no broken images, no overlapping type, no lorem ipsum leftover.

## What's weak

- **"STARTS NOW:" in all caps reads like a Wayfair subject line** — Wayfair pulled the same alarm-bell opener on their Black Friday in July send; from C&K I expect better restraint.
- **Four emails, one day, one brand family** — by the time this landed I was already numb. Pottery Barn sent me one email this week (coffee tables) and it stopped me cold because it was the only one. Cadence is trust.
- **The sweepstakes block mid-email is a detour** — "$4,000 in prizes" sounds great until I realize I'm in the middle of a sale email and you just asked me to context-switch into a contest entry flow. Pick a lane.
- **"Shop Now" is doing zero work** — this is the fourth time I've read that CTA today from C&B senders. At this point it's furniture-brand white noise.
- **No deadline visible** — "STARTS NOW" tells me when the clock started, not when it stops. Article and CB2 both nail the countdown; this just floats.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the sweepstakes section entirely from this send** — or make it its own dedicated email. Right now it interrupts the sale funnel at the exact moment I'm warming up to buy furniture.
- 2. **Split the product grid** — lead with furniture/storage (my category), push backpacks/supplies lower or into a separate tab CTA. People renovating rooms don't open a kids-brand email for lunch bags.
- 3. **Add a visible sale end date** — "20% off ends Sunday" beats "STARTS NOW" every single day of the week. CB2 did "Hours left" this week and it worked.
- 4. **Rewrite the CTA** — "Shop Kids' Furniture" or "Shop Storage + Decor" beats "Shop Now" for anyone who came to this email for something specific.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Back-to-School sale is live: 20% off furniture & storage`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Set up that room right — 20% off, starting today`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Desks, dressers, storage, and school gear — all in one sale`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Kids' furniture on sale now — grab it before the 20% off ends`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**A. Brand/title mismatch**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
Email sender is "Crate & Kids SALE" but `<title>` reads "Crate & Barrel" — wrong brand. Screen readers and some email clients surface the `<title>` value.

**B. User zoom disabled**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom, a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) failure for mobile users.

**C. Text size adjustment globally suppressed**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}
```
Applied to all elements (`*` selector). Overrides OS-level accessibility font scaling.

**D. Conflicting desktop `img` max-width rules**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
Both rules fire above 768px; declaration order means `max-width: 640px` (the 640px query, declared last) wins on wide viewports — likely unintended since the 768px rule targets a narrower `600px` cap.

**E. No dark mode handling**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Dark mode explicitly disabled. Apple Mail and Gmail app (Android) users in dark mode will get forced light rendering. No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` override is present.

**F. Duplicate/redundant meta tags**
- `charset` declared twice (`http-equiv` + standalone `<meta charset>`)
- `format-detection` declared four times with overlapping attribute pairs (telephone, address, email, date, url — split across multiple tags)

These are harmless to rendering but indicate sloppy template cleanup.
