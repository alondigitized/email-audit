# Responsible designs you’ll feel good about
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-13**
## Executive summary

- This is the fifth Crate & Barrel email I've gotten today, and the second from the Kids sender. My gut reaction was to mark it read. I'm doing sofas and bedrooms for two houses, not a nursery. The "Semi-Annual Bedroom & Nursery Event" banner at the top is the only thing that nearly pulled me in, because bedroom is half my project. Then the hero says "made responsibly, made to last" and it's a crib-and-nursery room.
- Once I scrolled, it turned into a certification parade: GREENGUARD Gold, FSC-certified wood, OEKO-TEX textiles, organic cotton, wooden toys. It's tidy and it's clearly a considered story, but it reads like a sustainability explainer with a sale hiding in the header. The 10/13 "Furniture you'll have for years to come" send was the same Kids energy, and the sofa email earlier today was the one that actually fit me.
- Would I click? Probably not. Forward it? No, unless a friend was expecting a baby. I'd save the wallcovering section for the Pinterest folder and archive the rest.

## What's working

- The certification-per-section structure is easy to scan. Each block has a badge-style headline and three product tiles, so I always know what I'm looking at.
- The material proof points (GREENGUARD Gold, FSC, OEKO-TEX) are exactly the kind of detail I'd want when comparing furniture finishes. Pottery Barn doesn't give me this in its Fall Sale strips.
- The photography is calm and consistent: warm neutrals, linen, light wood. It matches the palette I'm building toward, and it's more editorial than Wayfair's pergola tile walls.
- The first-ever wallcovering collection callout is the one new thing in the email. It's a real reason to look.
- The "we love these for you" product row at the bottom makes the email feel curated rather than a bare coupon blast.

## What's weak

- The offer is a thin banner at the very top, and nothing else repeats it. I scrolled a very long email and never saw "30% off" again. Article's "Fall is for full tables" at least had one clear story, and CB2's "ONLY HOURS LEFT" at least repeated the deadline.
- It's nursery-heavy: cribs, wooden toys, a toddler with a truck, kids' bedding. That's not my project, and it's the same Kids sender that gave me the Halloween shop earlier.
- The buttons say "Shop Now" and "Learn More" over and over. They don't tell me which product, category or discount is behind them.
- There's no deadline. The only urgency is a sweepstakes entry ("a chance to win $5000"), which isn't a reason to buy a bed.
- It's very long, with eight or so sections. I lost the thread of what the email wanted me to do.
- Five sends from the same company in one day is a lot. Pottery Barn's Terms & Conditions email at least wasn't a promo.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the offer in the hero, e.g. "Up to 30% off bedroom & nursery. Ends [day]." Stating the deadline and the discount once, big, would lift this email more than anything else.
- 2. Cut the email to 3-4 sections. Lead with bedroom furniture and bedding, and move the toys and kids' items below.
- 3. Give each button a specific verb and noun, like "Shop GREENGUARD dressers" or "Shop organic cotton bedding."
- 4. Segment. If I've only clicked sofas and sectionals, I shouldn't get the Kids sender at all. Merge it into the main Crate & Barrel send.
- 5. Cap the day's cadence at two sends per brand. By the fifth, I'm in a permanent C&B snooze.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 30% off bedroom & nursery, ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `GREENGUARD-certified bedroom furniture, 30% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Solid-wood dressers, organic cotton bedding and our first wallcoverings, all part of the Semi-Annual Event.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Every piece FSC- or OEKO-TEX-certified. Event pricing ends Sunday.`

## Technical Audit

**1. Technical Summary**
The email has a large number of images without alt text (about 60 in the QA output), and the automated checks could not confirm the unsubscribe headers or authentication results. The QA pass rate was 57%, with 0 failures and 3 warnings. The HTML source was truncated, so footer, unsubscribe link and token checks are limited to what the QA data shows.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- 97 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are click-redirect domains. Their destinations and redirect health are unverified.
- Tracking pixels and beacons are present: `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif?mi_u=…` (open pixel), `https://dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/a5080397-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…`, and five `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=…&n=1..5` calls to a third-party domain (LiveRamp, rlcdn.com).
- The `mi_u` and `s=` values are identical hex strings (`dbe8da71…`), so a subscriber-level identifier is exposed in image URLs.
- One image is served from a different host (`s7d5.scene7.com/is/image/Crate/25%5FMI%5FBottom%5FSpacer%5F40px%5FWhite`), so this spacer depends on a second CDN.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- The QA run flagged missing `alt` on every image in the list. That includes content images on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/…` (`.jpg`, `.png`, `.gif`), the spacer, and the tracking pixels.
- The animated GIFs `f1db6dd8-071b-4d4b-ab5d-5549f0cf3f58.gif` and `a4c4b37d-9101-486a-8a84-701b0e46f5d8.gif` have no alt text either.
- With images blocked, or in a screen reader, the email will have no readable content unless the text is live elsewhere. I can't tell from the truncated source whether the text is live.
- The tracking pixels (`f3c0d22992a01946.png` ×6, `448796.gif` ×5, `o.gif`) should carry `alt=""`. They are decorative, and a missing `alt` attribute is the problem for screen readers, not the empty value.
- In the visible head, `<meta charset>` is duplicated alongside `http-equiv Content-Type`, and `format-detection` is declared three times. These are harmless but redundant.
- `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` has an unescaped `&`. That is an XHTML validity error under the declared XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype.
- `<html lang="en">` is present.
- The `viewport` meta sets `maximum-scale=1`, which blocks pinch-zoom on some clients and is an accessibility concern.
- The CSS rule `*{… line-height:100%}` is applied to all elements and can compress text line spacing.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
No issues found in the visible source. No unresolved merge tokens appear in the QA data or the truncated HTML. The per-recipient values in tracking URLs (`mi_u`, `mi_mid`) are populated.

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
- `List-Unsubscribe` was not found. QA notes that the AgentMail relay may strip it, so this is unconfirmed rather than a confirmed absence.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click) was not found, with the same caveat. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements expect both headers.
- `Authentication-Results` was not found, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment could not be assessed. The sender domain is `mail.crateandbarrel.com`.
- The footer, physical address and unsubscribe link are outside the truncated HTML, so I can't confirm them either way.

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
- Because the HTTP probe skipped all 97 links, I can't confirm whether UTM parameters survive the redirects or which landing pages they resolve to.
- The visible tracking parameters (`mi_cid`, `mi_mid`, `mi_u`) belong to the ESP's own tracking. No `utm_*` parameters appear in the evidence.
- The subject, "Responsible designs you'll feel good about", cannot be matched to a landing page without resolved destinations.

**7. Recommendations**
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to all content images (about 35 on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com`), and `alt=""` on spacers and tracking pixels (`mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/…`, `sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif`, `o.gif`, the `dv.` beacon, the scene7 spacer).
2. Check the raw message headers at the source mailbox, not the relay, for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` and `Authentication-Results` (spf/dkim/dmarc = pass).
3. Resolve the redirect chains for a sample of the 97 tracking links using a real GET with redirects followed, and confirm the final URLs and any `utm_*` parameters.
4. Escape the `&` in `<title>` as `&amp;`. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
5. Review whether the five repeated `rlcdn.com` pixels are needed. They add third-party requests and expose the subscriber hash.
6. Re-run the audit with the full HTML so the footer, unsubscribe link and any remaining tokens can be verified.
