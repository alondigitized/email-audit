# New! Our softest sheets ever are here →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-10**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-three. That's the C&B family tally after today, and I'm going to make that number do some heavy lifting here: **this is the sixth Crate & Barrel email I've received today**, and the immediately preceding one — "Not sure which sheets? Let us help." — was *also* about sheets. Two sheets emails in a single calendar day from the same brand. That's not a segmentation strategy, that's a content scheduling crash. I gave that one a 6 because at least it had a helpful framing. This one has to fight through the debris of that send plus four others.
- That said — the product itself is legitimate. I'm kitting out two bedrooms across two properties right now, and "softest sheets ever" is exactly the kind of product claim I'd normally stop for. The photography reads clean, the hierarchy is clear, there's a legible product grid with what look like distinct collections to compare. On a normal day with no preceding sheets email, this hits.
- But this isn't a normal day, and I'm not a normal inbox. I'm marking this read without clicking, and I'm one more C&B day like this away from hitting unsubscribe on the whole sender cluster.

## What's working

- **The "new" positioning is honest** — this reads like a genuine product launch with editorial photography, not a markdown clearance dressed up as a launch
- **Multiple collections visible in the grid** — I can see distinct fabric/linen options without needing to click through; that's the right level of browse-before-committing for a high-consideration buy like sheets
- **Clean layout, no render breaks** — after a long scroll, nothing falls apart; the footer, social links, and product tiles all hold
- **Hero photography is aspirational without being alien** — white-and-neutral palette, lived-in bedroom feel, doesn't scream "this was styled for a 22-year-old influencer"
- **Brand consistency** — C&B typography and color feel exactly right; I know where I am immediately

## What's weak

- **Two sheets emails in one day is a sequencing crime** — "Not sure which sheets? Let us help." already primed me for sheets; sending a second sheets email the same day reads like a scheduling error, not a strategy
- **No offer in sight** — this is a straight new-arrival email with no percentage off, no free shipping hook, nothing that makes me feel like clicking *now* is better than clicking next week
- **Hero copy is too soft** — I can see the headline area but the language doesn't land a punch; "softest sheets ever" is a claim that needs a proof point (thread count, certifications, something) to feel earned
- **No size/material callout visible** — I'm buying for two beds in two different rooms; I need to know if I'm looking at percale, sateen, linen — I can't tell from the scroll

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the same-day double-sheets send at the scheduling level.** This is the highest-impact fix. No copy change rescues an email that lands right after an identical one. Whoever controls the C&B send calendar needs to enforce a 48-hour same-category cooldown.
- 2. **Add a proof point to "softest" in the hero** — even a one-liner like "OEKO-TEX certified, 400-thread-count" turns a puff claim into a reason to believe. CB2's warehouse sale email I opened the same week named specific items and prices — that specificity is why I clicked there and not here.
- 3. **Give me a reason to click today, not someday** — a "New arrivals ship free this week" or a founding-member early-access window would convert me. Pottery Barn's free design services email (which I scored a 9) worked because it offered something time-specific. This offers nothing time-specific.
- 4. **Surface material/fabric type in the grid tiles** — even a small badge ("Linen," "Percale," "Bamboo") under each product lets me shop without clicking through five PDPs.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Conflicting `img` max-width media queries — cascade bug**

```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }  /* declared after */
```

At viewports ≥ 768px, both queries match. The 640px rule is declared later in the stylesheet and wins the cascade, allowing images to render up to 640px inside a 600px container. Swap declaration order so the 768px rule is last.

**[MEDIUM] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

Prevents pinch-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[MEDIUM] `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on universal selector**

```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```

The universal `*` selector overrides text-size adjustment globally, not just on `body`. Combined with the zoom restriction above, this doubly blocks user text scaling. The `body`-scoped declarations (present separately) are sufficient; the `*` rule should drop these two properties.

**[LOW] Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags — 4 declarations, redundant**

```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<!-- ... then separately: -->
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```

The first tag covers all cases. The three subsequent tags are redundant and add unnecessary head weight. Consolidate to one.
