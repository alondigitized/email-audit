# Now trending: our new cottage-core favorites →
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-19**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids again — third time this month "the England Collection" nursery furniture has crossed my inbox, and I don't have kids. "Cottage-core favorites" in the subject had me briefly thinking window shag rugs or vintage side tables for the second house, but the second I opened it: cribs, gliders, changing tables, a rocking chair swaddled in that same rust-colored slipcover fabric. This is a baby furniture email wearing a design-trend subject line to get past my filter.
- It's not badly built — the styling is genuinely nice, moody heritage-English-cottage photography, good product density — but none of it applies to me. I already flagged the last Crate & Kids send (Nuna/Doona baby gear) as a 5/10 miss for the exact same reason, and this one's worse because the subject line is actively misleading about what's inside.
- Not clicking, not forwarding, archiving immediately.

## What's working

- The "heritage made modern" hero shot has real production value — moody lighting, a curated room vignette, not a stock cutout.
- Product cards are clean and consistently laid out — each section (bedroom, nursery, playroom) has its own mini hero plus a 3-4 item grid with prices and "New & Exclusive" tags.
- "Shop the New Collection" CTA buttons repeat consistently at the end of each section so I never lose the thread of what to click.
- The Stokke 20%-off callout near the bottom is a real, specific offer with a clear percentage — better than most of what's above it.

## What's weak

- Zero cottage-core signal. This is 100% Crate & Kids nursery/baby furniture — cribs, gliders, changing tables — dressed up with a trend-chasing subject line that has nothing to do with the content.
- I don't have kids, so the entire email is irrelevant no matter how nice the photography is — same miss as the Nuna/Doona send I already reviewed this cycle.
- It's an enormous, scroll-forever email — four or five distinct room sections before you even hit "first look: the nursery" and the Stokke callout. Compare that to Pottery Barn's Workspace Refresh send, which put one hero desk and one CTA up top and let me decide fast.
- The footer cross-sells Crate & Barrel proper and "TheDesignDesk" services, which just confirms I'm getting swept into every sub-brand's list instead of being sent what's actually relevant to my two active renovations.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the subject line mismatch first — if this is nursery furniture, say so; don't borrow a design-trend term like "cottage-core" to bait opens from people like me who aren't in the market for cribs.
- 2. Segment baby/nursery buyers away from general Crate & Barrel/second-home renovators — I've now gotten three baby-furniture-adjacent sends in a row with no kids in my household. That's a data problem, not a creative problem.
- 3. Cut the email length by half — pick the one or two strongest room vignettes (the vintage-cozy nursery dresser shot is the best asset here) and drop the rest below a "see more" link.
- 4. Lead with the Stokke 20%-off deal if there's any real discount to offer — it's currently buried two-thirds down.
