# Just added to clearance
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-20**
## Executive summary

- Okay, CB2 again — I've lost count at this point, but this has to be at least the eighth or ninth send from the Crate & Barrel family this week, and now it's "Summer Clearance." No specific percentage, no dollar figure, just "new items added" over a gorgeous cutting-board-and-citrus hero shot. It's a pretty photo, I'll give them that, but I've seen this exact "moody kitchen counter" aesthetic from them like three times already this month.
- The real issue is there's no offer up top — I have to scroll all the way to the fine print at the bottom to learn it's "up to 60% off select styles." That's buried. Then it pivots into a design-consultation sweepstakes, a review sweepstakes, an app pitch, a credit card pitch, and a text-signup pitch — this is five different asks stacked in one email. I'm mid-renovation on two houses and clearance is genuinely relevant to me, but this reads like CB2 threw every marketing initiative they have into one send instead of picking a lane.
- Would I click? Maybe "Shop All" out of habit since clearance is on my radar, but I wouldn't forward this — nothing here is specific enough to send a friend. Straight to the "read later, probably never" pile.

## What's working

- The hero photo (cutting board, citrus, warm wood tones) is genuinely well-shot and on-brand for the fall renovation mood I'm in.
- "Summer Clearance" at least signals seasonal relevance — I know why I'm seeing this now.
- The category nav buttons (Furniture, Outdoor, Decor, Lighting, Rugs, Bedding & Bath) make it easy to jump straight to what I care about instead of a generic "Shop Now."
- The 10% back Rewards Dollars card benefit is something I'd actually consider — I run everything through my highest-rewards card anyway.

## What's weak

- No actual discount number anywhere above the fold — I have to hunt in the footnotes for "up to 60% off," which is exactly the buried-fine-print problem the rubric warns about.
- This is genuinely the same "distressed wood counter, moody lighting" visual language CB2's used in at least two other recent sends I've reviewed — the fatigue is real.
- Way too many competing CTAs — clearance, a $5K design refresh sweepstakes, a $1,000 review sweepstakes, the app, the credit card, and text signup, all in one scroll. Nothing gets priority.
- The "$5,000 REFRESH" sweepstakes teaser is a swing-and-a-miss for hierarchy — it's visually the biggest, boldest moment in the email, bigger than the actual clearance offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the actual number in the hero. "Up to 60% Off" belongs under "Summer Clearance," not three screens down in 8pt footer text — this is the single biggest lift available here.
- 2. Cut this down to one primary CTA. Pick clearance-shop or the design-refresh sweepstakes, not both plus three more asks below it.
- 3. Rotate the hero image — Wayfair and Pottery Barn are also leaning hard into warm-neutral kitchen/tablescape shots this month; CB2 needs something that doesn't blend into every other inbox card.
- 4. Move the rewards/app/text-signup block to its own dedicated send instead of tacking it onto every promotional email — it dilutes the actual offer.
