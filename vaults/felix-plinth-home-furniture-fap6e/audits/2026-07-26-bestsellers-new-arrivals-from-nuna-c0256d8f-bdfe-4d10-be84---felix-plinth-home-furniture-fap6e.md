# ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Bestsellers & new arrivals from Nuna!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-26**
## Executive summary

- Nuna car seats, "Plaid" strollers, high chairs — this is baby gear top to bottom, and I don't have a baby. This is the third Crate & Kids send hitting my inbox this cycle (right behind "Bestsellers made for childhood" and the back-to-school backpack one), and Crate & Barrel's parent company clearly hasn't figured out that I'm on the Crate & Barrel side for furniture, not the Crate & Kids side for nursery gear. The star-emoji subject line — five stars stacked in front of the actual words — is the kind of thing that reads like a fake-review spam trick before I've even opened it.
- The email itself is well-built, I'll give it that: clean product photography, a coherent car-seat comparison block, a $4,999 giveaway, an up-to-50%-off summer sale banner. If I were shopping for a convertible car seat this would be a genuinely useful reference email. But I'm renovating two houses, not outfitting a nursery, so none of this — the Nuna RAVA, the REVV, the EXEC, the "Plaid" high chair — has anything to do with my life right now.
- Marking as read, not clicking, not forwarding. If anything I'd forward it to my sister who just had a kid, not to anyone in my own reno group chat.

## What's working

- The car-seat trio (RAVA / REVV / EXEC) is laid out cleanly with star ratings and one clear price/feature line each — good comparison-shopping design if you're actually in the market.
- "Up to 50% off summer sale" banner is legible and sits above a clean product grid without clutter.
- The $4,999 giveaway module has real visual weight and a specific dollar figure, which at least reads as a genuine hook rather than vague "win big" copy.
- Photography is consistent, warm, kitchen-and-nursery lifestyle shots — on-brand for whoever their target parent actually is.

## What's weak

- Zero furniture, zero home-renovation content — this is 100% baby/nursery gear, and I'm not a parent, so the entire email is scroll-past noise for me.
- The five-star emoji stack in the subject line ("⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Bestsellers & new arrivals from Nuna!") reads more like a spam/fake-testimonial trick than a real merchandising subject.
- This is the third Crate & Kids email in my inbox this review cycle — same brand family fatigue I flagged on the "Bestsellers made for childhood" send and the back-to-school backpack one. At some point the repetition itself becomes the complaint.
- No segmentation signal at all — a brand that actually knew my purchase history would know I've never bought a stroller or car seat from them, and would stop sending me this vertical.
- Compare this to Article, who at least rotates rooms I actually care about (living room after dining room) — Crate & Kids just keeps mailing the same demographic mismatch on repeat.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the segmentation, not the creative.** If I've never clicked a Crate & Kids link or bought nursery gear, suppress me from this list entirely and only send me Crate & Barrel furniture/home content — the fix here is list hygiene, not copy.
- 2. **Drop the star-emoji stack from the subject line.** It reads as spammy rather than trustworthy; a plain "New Nuna car seats & strollers just landed" does the job without the fake-review vibe.
- 3. **Lead with the giveaway or the sale, not the Nuna brand intro**, if this does go to a broader Crate & Kids list — "$4,999 in prizes" is a stronger hook than "new faves & bestsellers."
- 4. **If it must reach non-parent subscribers**, add a one-line "shopping for a home instead? Shop Crate & Barrel →" module up top so people like me have an obvious off-ramp instead of just closing the email.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New Nuna car seats & strollers, back-to-school sale up to 20% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Win $4,999 in prizes: our Ultimate Back-to-School Giveaway`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Convertible car seats, strollers, and high chairs — plus enter to win.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sale ends soon: up to 50% off summer favorites from Nuna.`
