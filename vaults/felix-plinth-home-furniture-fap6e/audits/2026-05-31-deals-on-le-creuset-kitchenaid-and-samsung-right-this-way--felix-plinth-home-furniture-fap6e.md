# Deals on Le Creuset, KitchenAid AND Samsung, right this way →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-31**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-one. That's where Crate & Barrel family lands in my inbox this week after I count this one, and I want to be honest: I almost filed it on principle. But "Le Creuset" in the subject line is a genuinely good hook for someone who's about to kit out two kitchens from scratch. I opened. Then I got to Samsung. And that's where this email started losing me.
- The structure is clear enough — hero cookware, KitchenAid block, Samsung TVs, then a product scatter grid below the fold. But stacking Le Creuset and Samsung in the same email is like walking into a Williams Sonoma and finding a wall of QLED panels next to the Dutch ovens. It's disorienting. The brand has been doing a reasonable job this week on the "new look, almost no wait" and the washable rugs send — both felt like Crate & Barrel talking to me. This one feels like a mall flyer.
- I'd mark it read. I wouldn't click through to the Samsung section. I'd maybe save the Le Creuset block mentally and go back through the site directly. No forward — none of my friends need me to forward a C&B kitchen sale in late May.

## What's working

- Le Creuset and KitchenAid are exactly what I'm shopping for right now — equipping two kitchens simultaneously means I'm actively hunting deals on these brands, so the subject line did its job on me.
- The hero image is dark and editorial — moody kitchen vibes, not a white-background catalog dump. That's the aesthetic I expect from Crate & Barrel and it delivers.
- The discount percentage is surfaced immediately in the hero area — no hunting for the offer, no "ends soon" buried in body copy.
- Overall render is clean: no broken images, no overlapping text, no obvious junk preheader leaking into the body.

## What's weak

- Samsung in a Crate & Barrel sale email is actively brand-diluting. This is the same jarring move Pottery Barn pulled when they started bundling tech accessories into their bedding sends — it reads as desperation, not curation. The "TV that blends in beautifully" email from two days ago was a better vehicle for this; it earned the Samsung conversation. Here it's just bolted on.
- The subject line is doing too much. Three brand names plus a directional arrow is a subject line that sounds like a department-store circular, not a curated home goods brand. It's also well over 50 chars, which hurts on mobile preview.
- The product grid below the hero devolves into scatter — I can see small thumbnails of things that have nothing to do with kitchen or tech, which makes the whole lower half feel like a "we also sell stuff" clearance section.
- No urgency signal anywhere visible. Is this a 24-hour sale? A week? The cookware deals feel serious but I have no reason to act today versus next Thursday.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill Samsung from this email entirely**, or give it its own send. Crate & Barrel earned the TV conversation in the "blends in beautifully" email earlier this week — that framing worked because the whole email was about it. Bolting it onto a cookware sale cheapens both.
- 2. **Add a deadline.** Even "through Sunday" in the hero copy turns this from a browse into a purchase decision. CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" had the same problem — no clock, no urgency.
- 3. **Consolidate the product grid.** The lower section needs an editorial hand. One thematic row (e.g., "complete the kitchen") not a scatter of unrelated SKUs.
- 4. **Subject Alt A:** `Le Creuset is 20% off. KitchenAid too.`
