# Are these the SLIPCOVERS you wanted?
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-16**
## Executive summary

- I opened this because the subject asked me directly — "Are these the SLIPCOVERS you wanted?" — which is a decent hook since I've genuinely been slipcover-shopping for the sectional at the second house. Then the actual hero image at the top of the email is just... blank. A white box with a tiny broken-image icon sitting dead center, for what looks like a third of the whole email's real estate. That's a broken render, not a stylistic choice, and it's the worst possible way to open an email whose entire subject line promise is "here's the specific thing you wanted."
- Once I scroll past the broken hero, it's not slipcovers at all — it's a 16-tile grab bag: pergolas, gazebos, hot tubs, birdbaths, barn doors, wall clocks, cookware. Slipcovers show up twice in that pile ("Best-Selling Slipcovers" and "Must-Have Stretch Fit Slipcovers") but they're buried as two tiles among sixteen, no different in size or prominence than a birdbath. Wayfair, this is the same trick you pulled on 8/14 with "Your RECLINER STYLE = FOUND" and 8/15 with the fencing subject that had zero fencing in the body — bait a specific category in the subject, deliver an unrelated catalog dump in the body.
- Verdict: I'm not clicking. The broken hero kills any trust I had going in, and the actual slipcover tiles don't even get top billing once I find them. Not forwarding this — if anything I'd screenshot the blank hero and complain about it, not share it as inspo.

## What's working

- The subject line actually named my category and used a question format that felt personalized, which is more than the CB2/Crate & Barrel "End of Summer Sale" emails I've been drowning in this week bother to do.
- Both slipcover tiles have real, clear product photography once you scroll to them — the gray quilted slipcover and the blue stretch-fit armchair both look legit and give me a sense of the product.
- The purple "score styles you ♥" and "savings too good to pass up" section headers at least break up the wall of tiles into digestible chunks.
- Rewards/Financing/Professional/Exclusive Brands icon row at the bottom is a nice trust footer, on par with what I'd expect from a brand I already have a purchase history with.

## What's weak

- The hero image — the single most important visual real estate, right where the eye lands first — is completely broken. Blank white box, broken-image icon. On mobile this is not fixable by squinting; it's just gone.
- The subject promised slipcovers specifically; the body delivers pergolas, hot tubs, birdbaths, wall clocks, and barn doors before I even get to a slipcover tile. This is worse than CB2's vague "End of Summer Sale" because at least CB2 doesn't promise something specific and then not lead with it.
- Zero pricing anywhere. Not one percentage off, not one dollar figure. Crate & Barrel's "Gather Sectional" email at least had me screenshotting for my Pinterest folder because it committed to one hero product with a real pitch — this is the opposite, sixteen tiles and no offer math on any of them.
- There's a second blank/broken image block near the bottom footer area too — so it's not a one-off glitch, something's actually wrong with this send's image pipeline.
- No urgency, no "sale ends," nothing time-bound — compare that to the fencing email from yesterday that at least had (overblown) countdown energy.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the broken hero image before anything else ships — a blank box where your money shot should be will tank open-to-click on every single recipient, not just me. This is the single biggest lift available here.
- 2. Lead the body with an actual slipcover hero — one large image of the specific slipcover style, with a price or "% off," directly under the subject's promise. Don't make me hunt through 16 unrelated tiles.
- 3. Cut the tile count. Sixteen categories in one email reads as "we don't know what you want" — pick 4-6 tied to the subject's theme (living room refresh: slipcovers, throw pillows, coasters) instead of pergolas-to-wall-clocks scattershot.
- 4. Add a real offer — a percentage or dollar figure on the slipcover tiles specifically. Right now there's no reason to click over just browsing the site directly.
- 5. Space out the send cadence — this is my fourth or fifth Wayfair-family email this week with a different bait-and-switch subject each time; it's starting to read as spam-adjacent even before the broken image.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your slipcover picks are back in stock`
- **Subject Alt B:** `20% off the slipcover you saved`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stretch-fit, quilted, and machine-washable — see what's trending`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free shipping on orders over $35, ends Sunday`
