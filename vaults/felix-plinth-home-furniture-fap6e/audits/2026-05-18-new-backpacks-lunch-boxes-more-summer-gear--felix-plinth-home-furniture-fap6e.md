# NEW backpacks, lunch boxes & more summer gear ☀️→
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- Nine. That's where I am with the Crate & Barrel family over the past 48 hours, and this one is from Crate & Kids — the sub-brand I genuinely have no business being on. I don't have children. I've never bought anything from Crate & Kids. I renovate houses. And yet here I am staring at a grid of colorful dinosaur and floral-print backpacks like I'm prepping a seven-year-old for second grade.
- To be fair to the email itself — visually, it's competent. The yellow backpack hero is clean, the product grid is organized, and the layout doesn't break. There's even what looks like a cross-sell section lower in the scroll with some home goods, which is the only reason I kept reading at all. But the targeting is a total miss. This belongs in the inbox of someone with a kid, not someone pricing out a kitchen remodel.
- Mark as read. Would not click. Would not forward — unless it was to someone who actually has a kindergartner.

## What's working

- **Hero product is immediately clear.** The yellow/mustard backpack is a strong, single-subject hero — the eye goes right to it without ambiguity.
- **Product grid is well-organized.** The colorful array of bags and lunch boxes below the hero is visually tidy and communicates range at a glance.
- **Email renders cleanly.** No broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds top-to-bottom. More than I can say for some of the earlier Memorial Day blasts.
- **Bottom cross-sell section.** There appears to be a home goods or furniture tie-in lower in the email — smart to use the email real estate to remind a C&B subscriber what the parent brand sells.

## What's weak

- **Wrong persona, completely.** I have no kids. Backpacks and lunch boxes are as irrelevant to me as a diaper coupon. The C&B list team clearly isn't segmenting by purchasing behavior — I've never bought a kids item.
- **Ninth C&B family email in 48 hours.** I have a folder for these now. The goodwill the brand had earlier in this cycle — especially the Double Rewards announcement that actually moved me — is gone. Pottery Barn at ten emails in 72 hours was the ceiling I thought I'd seen; C&B is threatening to match it.
- **Crate & Kids sender name is a warning flag.** From my inbox, "Crate & Kids SALE" reads as a sub-brand I opted into by accident. The disconnect between who I am and what this brand sells is jarring.
- **No sale percentage in the hero.** The sender name says "SALE" but the email doesn't immediately surface a specific discount — I have to hunt for it. Compare that to the earlier "up to 60% off" email in this same cycle, which led with the number.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress this send for subscribers with zero kids-category purchase history.** This is the fix. If I've never bought a kids product, I should not be on the Crate & Kids list. This isn't a copy problem — it's a segmentation problem. Article's welcome email was a masterclass in how a brand can feel like it knows you; this is the exact opposite.
- 2. **Surface the discount number in the hero.** "SALE" is not a number. If it's 30% off, say 30% off. The "LOOK AGAIN: 2x Rewards + up to 50% off" subject from earlier this cycle did this right.
- 3. **If you're sending to general C&B subscribers anyway, lean harder into the cross-sell.** If there's a home goods section lower in the email, bring it up. At least that's why I'm on the list.
- 4. **Reduce cadence or consolidate into a roundup.** One "C&B family weekend sale" email covering Crate & Barrel, CB2, and Crate & Kids would be less fatiguing than nine separate sends.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Conflicting image max-width media queries:**
Two overlapping `min-width` rules create a contradiction:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
At 768px+, the `600px` rule fires last (source order) and overrides the `640px` rule, making the `640px` declaration unreachable at its intended desktop breakpoint. Swap rule order or consolidate into a single declaration.

**No dark mode support:** `<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">` is declared without a corresponding `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block. Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook 2019+ will auto-invert colors on affected elements, which can break background-text contrast.

**Wildcard `max-height` hack applied globally:**
```css
* { max-height: 1000000px; }
```
This GMail-rendering workaround is applied to every element. It can suppress overflow on intentionally scrollable containers and interfere with animated GIF containment. Scope it to `body` or the wrapper `<table>` instead.

**`<meta charset>` declared twice:**
Both `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">` and `<meta charset="utf-8">` are present. Redundant but harmless; one is sufficient.

**`format-detection` declared four times:**
One combined tag plus three individual tags for `date`, `address`, and `telephone`. Redundant; the combined declaration covers all five values.
