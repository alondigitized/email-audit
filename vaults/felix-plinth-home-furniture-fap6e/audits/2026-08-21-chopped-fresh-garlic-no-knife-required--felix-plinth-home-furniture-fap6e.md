# Chopped fresh garlic, no knife required 🤯
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-21**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "chopped fresh garlic, no knife required" — and then the email opens with... a hero image of hands unwrapping a bowl of leafy greens, followed by "the kitchen tools we can't live without." So the subject sold me a garlic gadget and the hero sold me a salad prep starter kit. That's a disconnect right out of the gate, and it's the kind of thing that makes me scroll past instead of stopping.
- Once you get into it, this is a genuinely dense kitchen catalog dump — utensils, cutting boards, mixing bowls, dish towels, storage — and buried two-thirds down is the actual garlic chopper (the "Chef'n Veggie Chop garlic chopper," from the ASMR section). That's a lot of scrolling to get to the thing that got me to open the email. Compare that to CB2's "Future Heirlooms Under $500" send I read recently — that one at least kept its hero promise focused on one category instead of turning into a 15-section department store flyer. This Crate & Barrel send also tries to cross-sell a 25% off bedroom refresh AND a Levain Bakery kitchen collab AND a credit card pitch, all in the same email — it's kitchen tools, then suddenly furniture, then suddenly cookies. My brain doesn't know what this email is about by the end.
- I'm not clicking, and I'm definitely not forwarding this to my wife or anyone else — it's too unfocused. I'll skim the "we love these for you" grid at the bottom since it's got the KitchenAid stand mixer and some Le Creuset, which is genuinely renovation-adjacent for me, but that's buried after ten other product blocks.

## What's working

- The "we love these for you" grid near the bottom has real anchor products I'd actually consider — KitchenAid stand mixer, Le Creuset braiser, Crate & Barrel Acacia utensils — that's the closest thing to a personalized pick in this email.
- Product photography is clean and consistent, on-brand neutral palette (wood, cream, sage) that matches what I already associate with Crate & Barrel.
- Pricing is visible directly under each product tile ("starting at $12.95," "starting at $329") — no hunting for cost, no bait-and-switch.
- The dish towel section at least has a clear single CTA ("Shop Now") without extra clutter around it.

## What's weak

- Subject promises a garlic chopper; hero image and headline are about a totally different product (salad bowls/kitchen tools broadly) — bait mismatch.
- This is basically eight separate mini-campaigns stapled into one email: kitchen tools, ASMR garlic gadget, dish towels, 25% off bedroom sale, Levain Bakery collab, "we love these for you" grid, credit card pitch, store locator. Nothing gets real visual hierarchy because everything is fighting for it.
- The 25% off bedroom refresh insert makes zero sense sandwiched inside a kitchen tools email — if I wanted bedroom furniture content I'd expect it in a separate send, not wedged between dish towels and a bakery collab.
- No mention of Crate&Barrel rewards/loyalty program benefits anywhere, despite this being exactly the kind of email where a "members save an extra X%" line would nudge me toward the card pitch that's already at the bottom.
- Design Crew / TheDesignDesk footer plug feels like a bolt-on afterthought, not integrated with the "kitchen tools" narrative at all.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one hero and deliver on the subject line — if it's "chopped fresh garlic, no knife required," open with the Chef'n Veggie Chop front and center, not a generic salad-bowl lifestyle shot six sections later.
- 2. Cut this into two emails: one focused kitchen-tools send (utensils, cutting boards, storage, the garlic gadget) and a separate one for the bedroom sale + Levain Bakery collab — those two have nothing to do with "kitchen tools we can't live without."
- 3. Move the "we love these for you" personalized grid up near the top, right after the hero — that's the strongest, most relevant content in the whole email and it's currently buried below eight other sections.
- 4. Drop a loyalty/rewards callout near the credit card pitch at the bottom — "Rewards members save 10% today" would connect the dots better than a generic new-card ad.
