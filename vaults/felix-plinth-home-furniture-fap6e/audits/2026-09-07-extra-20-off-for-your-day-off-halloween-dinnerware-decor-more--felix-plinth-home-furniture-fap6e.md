# 💕 EXTRA 20% OFF for your day off + Halloween dinnerware, decor & more 🦇🕸️
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-07**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one's actually a swerve — same burgundy banner up top, same "Extra 20% off" I've now seen ten times this week, but then it pivots hard into "Set a Table to Die For" Halloween dinnerware, and I'll admit it landed better than I expected. The moody black plates, the "ominously illustrated" copy, the candlelight shots — that's a real seasonal story, not just a category grid with a red banner slapped on it. It's giving me actual "start planning the fall dinner party" energy, which is more than I can say for the Article "last call" clone-send I keep getting.
- Then it just... keeps going. Halloween Headquarters, then straight into "Recommended Just For You" with a platform bed, curtain rods, dining tables, a mattress — none of which have anything to do with Halloween or the 20% off up top. It's like two different emails got stitched together, and by the time I hit the rewards card pitch and the Affirm financing block I've completely forgotten what the subject line promised.
- Would I click? Maybe on the Halloween dinnerware section specifically, since I'm actually mid-renovation on the second home and could use a tablescape refresh for it. Forward to a friend? No — this is still Pottery Barn's tenth email this week, and nobody needs me flooding their inbox with the same brand cadence I'm already drowning in.

## What's working

- The Halloween dinnerware section has a real point of view — "ominously illustrated plates," "signature brew in chic, shadowy glasses" — that's actual copywriting, not just "shop now."
- Black-and-white tablescape photography looks expensive and matches the moody fall aesthetic, which is a nice break from the usual bright white-walled Pottery Barn catalog shot.
- The 20% off banner is unmissable the second the email opens — no scrolling needed to find the offer, unlike CB2's countdown-timer send that buries the actual discount lower down.
- "Recommended Just For You" at least gestures at personalization instead of a generic homepage dump.

## What's weak

- The email tries to be two campaigns at once — Labor Day sale banner, then a full Halloween storytelling section, then generic furniture recs, then app download, then rewards card, then financing. By the end it's total scope creep.
- None of the "Recommended Just For You" items (platform bed, curtain rods, mattress) tie back to the 20% off or the Halloween theme — it reads like a completely different email got pasted in below the fold.
- Cadence fatigue is real: this is Pottery Barn number ten in my tracked week, right after yesterday's near-identical "up to 70% off" burgundy banner. I can sketch this header from memory now.
- The subject line promises "your day off" — some cute Labor Day tie-in — but nothing in the actual email references that; it's just the sale banner then Halloween, no connective tissue.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into two sends. Halloween dinnerware deserves its own email — it's genuinely good creative and gets diluted by being buried under a generic sale banner and unrelated furniture recs.
- 2. Cut the "Recommended Just For You" furniture grid entirely from this send, or move it to its own weekly digest — it has nothing to do with either headline story and just adds scroll fatigue.
- 3. Tie the subject line's "day off" hook to actual copy in the email — right now it's a subject-line-only gimmick with zero payoff inside.
- 4. Trim the footer stack (app download, rewards card, Affirm financing) — three separate cross-sell pitches after an already-long email is where I check out completely.

# Technical Audit: Pottery Barn — "EXTRA 20% OFF" (2026-09-07)

## 1. Technical Summary
Email uses standard ESP tracking/redirect architecture (click.e.potterybarn.com, image.e.potterybarn.com) with legacy DOCTYPE/table markup; automated checks show broken email-authentication visibility, systemic missing alt text, and one invalid `<style>` block containing raw PHP-style `header()` calls.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All hyperlinks route through `click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=...` (opaque base64 payload) — cannot verify true destination without decoding; 55 tracking/redirect links were skipped by the HTTP prober, so link-health (404s, redirect loops) is unverified.
- Open-tracking pixel `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` is served over **plain HTTP**, not HTTPS — flagged by QA as "may be blocked" by mail clients enforcing mixed-content/TLS policies, and it also leaks the recipient's full email address in a cleartext URL parameter.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Systemic missing `alt` text**: QA flagged 40+ images with no alt attribute, including all product photography (bed, curtain rod, dining tables, swivel chair, mattress) and all decorative "slices" spacer images in header/footer. This breaks screen-reader accessibility and leaves no fallback text when images are blocked (a common default in mail clients).
- Invalid markup in `<head>`: the client-styling `<style>` block contains non-CSS server-side code —
  ```
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
  ```
  This is PHP output accidentally left inside a `<style>` tag rather than executed server-side — dead code that will be ignored by renderers but indicates a templating/build leak worth fixing at the source template.
- `<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">` and `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` are present — intentional and appropriate for transactional/marketing email, not a defect.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

This is standard ESP behavior for match-back tracking, but note it as a PII-in-URL exposure point if this HTML is ever cached, forwarded, or logged by a third party (e.g., the turn.com pixel domain).
- No unresolved merge-tag artifacts (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `%%FIELD%%`) found in the visible source.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)

In-body unsubscribe mechanism (footer `coreemail.html` link) is present, satisfying CAN-SPAM's minimum requirement independent of header support.

- **Authentication-Results (SPF/DKIM/DMARC)**: not found — same relay-capture caveat; sending domain authentication status is unverified from this data and should be checked against raw headers or a dedicated deliverability tool (e.g., mail-tester).
