# 👻🎃🕸️ Halloween’s on the brain
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-01**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so this is the *second* Crate & Barrel Halloween email in my inbox today — I already reviewed "BOO! Shop Halloween before it sells out this year" from this same brand, same date, and gave that one a 7. This one, "Halloween's on the brain," opens with a genuinely pretty porch shot (wreath, pumpkins, candlelight, the whole moody twilight thing) but then just... keeps going. And going. Halloween decor, then cocktails, then hosting, then recipes, then a Design Desk $5,000 sweepstakes, then 15% off the Aspen collection, then $170 off a Le Creuset Dutch oven, then a random "we love these for you" product grid, then a credit card pitch, then an app download nudge. That's not an email, that's their entire content calendar stapled together.
- I'm mid-renovation on two houses right now, so the Aspen furniture sale and the Design Desk consult buried two-thirds of the way down are actually the parts I care about — and they're getting drowned out by pumpkin bundles and spiked cider recipes. Article and CB2 both know how to pick one lane and stay in it; this reads like C&B couldn't decide what this week's email was actually for, so they just sent everything.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I scroll far enough to hit the Aspen sale or the Design Desk booking. Would I forward it to a friend? No — nothing here is remarkable enough, and it's honestly a little exhausting to scroll through on my phone. Getting two of these from the same brand on the same day is already pushing it.

## What's working

- The hero shot is legitimately nice — that wreathed door with pumpkins on the stoop looks like something out of a shelter magazine, not a stock photo dump.
- The Aspen collection 15% off and the Le Creuset $170-off callout are both concrete, specific offers — no vague "save big" language.
- Design Desk's free design appointment is actually relevant to me right now, mid-reno on two properties, if I can just get to it.
- Brand voice stays consistent top to bottom — same fonts, same warm color palette, nothing feels like a bolted-on template.

## What's weak

- This is C&B's second Halloween send to me today ("BOO! Shop Halloween..." already hit my inbox) — that's a cadence problem, not a content problem.
- The email tries to be six emails at once: Halloween decor, cocktails, hosting, recipes, furniture sale, credit card, app download. Nothing gets to breathe.
- The stuff I'd actually act on — Aspen furniture, Design Desk — is buried past a full scroll of seasonal filler.
- No sizing, material, or dimension info on any of the "we love these for you" furniture picks, which is exactly what I need mid-renovation to make a call.
- Zero personalization — no name, no "since you were browsing X," nothing that says they know I'm a repeat furniture shopper.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into two emails: one tight Halloween/entertaining send, one furniture-and-Design-Desk send. Right now they're competing for the same 8 seconds of my attention.
- 2. Move the Aspen 15%-off sale and Design Desk appointment up near the top if this has to stay one email — that's the content actually tied to my current project.
- 3. Add real product detail (dimensions, material, lead time) to the "we love these for you" grid — Wayfair at least shows price and category tags on similar modules.
- 4. Cut the credit card pitch and app download blurb entirely from a seasonal email — that's a different funnel and it just adds scroll fatigue.
- 5. Coordinate send cadence with whatever team owns the "BOO!" email — two Halloween sends same day from the same brand reads as noise, not enthusiasm.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: book a free Design Desk session before August 3`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Aspen collection, Le Creuset deals, and Halloween must-haves`
