# Up to 60% off even more markdowns!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- Ten. That's the C&B family inbox count in two days, and I want to lead with that because it's the only thing I thought about when I saw this land. I literally had to double-check it wasn't a duplicate of this morning's "10% off your next purchase—even on furniture" email — same sender, different offer, same week. The pivot from 10% to 60% in a single morning feels like a brand that doesn't know what story it's telling.
- That said, when I actually looked at the email itself: it's one of the stronger executions in this recent flood. The "up to 60% off clearance" hero is clean, the Father's Day deadline (order by 6/12) gives me an actual reason to act today, and the "we love these for you" personalized furniture block at the bottom is the kind of thing that would make me stop scrolling. A sideboard and a dining chair — those are live projects for me right now.
- Would I click? Probably yes, but with mild resentment. I'd save it to my deals folder and come back to it tonight. I would not forward it to a friend, partly because I've already mentally cc'd three people on three different C&B emails today and the goodwill is running dry.

## What's working

- **"Up to 60% off clearance" hero is unambiguous** — no buried lede, no lifestyle copy padding, the number is the first thing your eye hits
- **Father's Day deadline (order by 6/12) is a real urgency signal** — not fake countdown energy, an actual calendar date I can act against, which is more than the "Weekend Forecast: Up to 30% off outdoor" email managed this morning
- **"We love these for you" personalized block** — a sideboard, a chair, a bench in the lower section landed in active furniture categories for me; this is the closest any C&B email this week has come to feeling like it knows my wishlist
- **Render is clean** — no broken images, hierarchy is legible, tap targets look reasonable on mobile

## What's weak

- **60% off the same morning as 10% off is brand confusion** — someone receiving both emails before noon is going to wonder why they accepted 10% on anything when 60% clearance was always an option; Pottery Barn's Warehouse Sale email managed to feel like a one-time event rather than a drip negotiation
- **"Even more markdowns" is doing nothing** — "even more" implies a prior email I should already be aware of, but it reads as padding; Wayfair, for all its chaos, at least names the categories (vacuums, outdoor, etc.)
- **The app promo block below the personalized section kills the momentum** — I'm looking at a sideboard I might actually buy, then the screen cuts to "It's all in the app, make it yours" — wrong place, wrong order
- **No visible price anchoring on any product** — clearance works harder when I can see "$349 → $149"; the product tiles here show items without prices, which means I'm clicking blind

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with one clearance hero product at a real price** — "This sideboard just dropped to $299" beats "up to 60% off" because it gives my brain a concrete number to anchor on before I commit a click
- 2. **Kill or move the app promo block** — bury it in the footer; do not interrupt a warm personalized section with a download CTA
- 3. **Add a visible price or "was/now" on the personalized product tiles** — even one tile with crossed-out pricing would lift click intent meaningfully
- 4. **Don't send a 60% off clearance email four hours after a 10% off email** — coordinate sends at the brand family level; one of these should have been held or the 10% email should have cross-referenced clearance
- **Subject Alt A:** `Clearance hits 60% off — new drops today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your personalized picks: up to 60% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Father's Day orders due 6/12 — these pieces just made it.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Clearance expanded. The sideboard you saved is in.`

### 7. Recommendations

Issues 1–5 below are repeats from the June 4 Crate & Kids technical audit. They have not been applied to the shared template.

1. **Fix `maximum-scale=1`** — replace viewport meta with `width=device-width, initial-scale=1`. Restores pinch-to-zoom; required for WCAG 1.4.4 compliance.
2. **Scope `text-size-adjust`** — remove from the wildcard `*` selector; apply only to `body`. The current rule suppresses scaling across every element.
3. **Correct media query ordering** — place `min-width: 640px` before `min-width: 768px` so the narrower breakpoint is overridden by the wider one, not the reverse. The `max-width: 600px` desktop cap is currently unreachable.
4. **Strip the font placeholder comment** — either load the intended web font or delete `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->`. Do not ship template instructions in production HTML.
5. **Deduplicate `format-detection` meta tags** — keep only the single combined form; remove the three standalone tags.
