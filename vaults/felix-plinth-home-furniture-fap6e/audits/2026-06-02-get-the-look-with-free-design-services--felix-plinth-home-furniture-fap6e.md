# Get the look with FREE design services
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- Seven. I've now received seven Pottery Barn emails in roughly 48 hours, and I walked into this one with a real inbox deficit. But the subject stopped me — free design services isn't a product push, it's a service offer. And I'm currently juggling two unfinished rooms across two renovations simultaneously, which means a free design consultation isn't a throwaway — it's something I'd normally have to ask for or pay for at a competing firm.
- The email is structured around a clear booking mechanic: hero room scene, RSVP treatment, big "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" CTA, repeat in the dining room block below, then a secondary Summer Sale hook with what looks like a June 7 deadline, and a product recommendation strip at the bottom. The visual hierarchy works. Crate & Barrel has been running cleaner email layouts lately, but this one earns its structure — the eye moves logically from the aspirational room scene to the action. No battles with the layout.
- I would click. Specifically for the design appointment, not the product recs at the bottom, which feel like they were added to make the send feel more complete rather than because they know anything about my spaces. I would not forward this. I'd drop it into my saved folder, open my calendar, and figure out whether I want a virtual consult or an in-store session.

## What's working

- **The service angle breaks the streak.** Six consecutive product-push emails leading into this — this one's asking me to *do something* other than shop. That tonal shift alone earns a second look.
- **"BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" is a real CTA.** Verb plus noun, unambiguous action, zero ambiguity. More than I can say for the "Discover" and "Shop Now" CTAs I've been wading through.
- **Room scenes match my actual brief.** Living room and dining room — both spaces I'm actively speccing for the second home. PB's room staging is always aspirational without being untouchable, which is why I'm still on their list.
- **June 7 Summer Sale deadline** gives the email some in-body urgency the subject doesn't have. Anchoring the design service to an active sale cycle is smart — it creates a reason to book now vs. bookmark forever.
- **6% Back in Rewards** is down in the footer but it's there, and I see it every time because I'm always running rewards math on furniture orders.

## What's weak

- **Email seven in 48 hours.** Even with a different sub-sender ("Pottery Barn Design Services"), my trust account is overdrawn. This is the send that needed cadence suppression the most — it's also the most genuinely useful, which makes the timing genuinely annoying.
- **"Get the look" in the subject is too vague.** What look? The coastal living room? The dining nook I can see in the email? Article manages to name a specific space ("Make your kitchen the place to be") and it lands sharper. PB had the goods; the subject line didn't commit.
- **The Summer Sale section reads mid-email like a non-sequitur.** It appears after the design service pitch and feels bolted on. If the Summer Sale is *why* I should book the consult now, connect those dots explicitly — "book before June 7, shop with a designer during the sale."
- **Product recs at the bottom are generic.** The quilt/pillow/sofa tile strip doesn't read as curated — it reads as "here's the standard recommendation module." Wayfair pulls this same move constantly and I never click it. PB should know me better than that at this point in the relationship.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress this send to anyone who received 3+ PB or PB-family emails in the prior 48 hours.** Or at minimum, send this one *first* in the cadence — it's the most valuable offer in the batch and it got buried at the end of an exhausting week.
- 2. **Name the space in the subject.** "We'll design your living room — free" or "Free consult for the space you're stuck on" converts better than "get the look," especially with a renovation audience.
- 3. **Connect the Summer Sale to the design appointment explicitly.** Something like "Book your consultation before June 7 and shop your curated list during the sale" turns two separate offers into a funnel.
- 4. **Pull one actual product recommendation into the design service pitch.** Show me the sofa the designers keep putting in a room like mine, with a specific link. That's what converts, not the generic tile strip.
- 5. **Personalize the recommendation strip.** Even "Based on your recent views" copy would shift this from generic to considered.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your free room design consult — book before June 7`
- **Subject Alt B:** `We'll design the space you're stuck on — free`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Tell us the room. Our designers do the rest — no fees, no catch.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Book your free consult and shop with a designer during the Summer Sale.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard-broken resources (403 response + cross-brand dead image) and pervasive missing alt text across 35+ images are the primary issues. Authentication/compliance headers are unverifiable at the relay layer but warrant attention.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Broken link (403) — credit card banner**
`https://www.potterybarn.com/pages/credit-card/?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`
Returns 403. However, the link is inside a commented-out HTML block (`<!-- ... -->`), so it is not live in the current send. The dead URL is residual dead code that should be removed to prevent accidental reactivation.

**Broken image (403) — West Elm spacer**
`https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
Cross-brand tracking pixel returns 403. The image has no alt text and renders as a broken image in image-blocking clients. Should be replaced with a same-domain 1×1 spacer or removed.

**25 tracking links skipped** — all routed through `click.e.potterybarn.com`. Not an error, but confirms no direct-URL probing was possible on destination pages.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**35 images missing `alt` text** — every content slice (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), all product images, the footer, and the credit-card banner image carry `alt=""`. In images-off environments (Outlook default, many mobile clients) the email renders as a blank layout. This also fails WCAG 2.1 AA for screen readers.

Affected examples:
- `slices_01.jpg` – `slices_08.jpg`: `https://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2026/US/0603_TUE_AM2_DesignCrewCoastalBlue/`
- Product images: `farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `cayman-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `marina-cane-bed-1-l.jpg`, etc.
- Footer slices: `slices_01.jpg` – `slices_18.jpg`: `https://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2026/US/Email-Footer/`

**Two images served over `http://`** (mixed content — will be blocked in Gmail, Apple Mail, Outlook web):
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

**PHP `header()` calls inside a `<style>` block** — the `<head>` contains raw PHP output-buffering directives (`header('Content-Type: image/jpeg')`, cache-control headers) embedded in a `<style>` tag. These are inert in a rendered email but indicate a template-generation defect — likely a copy-paste from an image-serving script into the email template. Cosmetically harmless but a code hygiene issue.

**`display:none !important` on preheader** — preheader text is correctly hidden visually, but the zero-width Unicode spacers (U+034F `͏`, U+00AD `­`) used for preheader padding are present and correct.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058)** — not found. One-click unsubscribe will not function in Gmail's native unsubscribe UI without this header alongside `List-Unsubscribe`.
