# Final hours for up to 50% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-26**
## Executive summary

- Sixth CB2 email in ten days. I'm not even going to pretend I don't have a running tally at this point — and this one arrives the day after their app-download plea, which scored a 4 in my head the moment I saw it. So I'm already tired when this hits.
- That said, this is mechanically one of the better ones in the streak. "Ends tonight" is credible urgency, the dual offer — 50% off the main sale, 60% off clearance — is exactly the kind of math I run when I'm sourcing pieces for two houses simultaneously, and the Double Rewards callout means I'm thinking about stacking this against my highest-rewards card. The mosaic hero reads clean and contemporary, consistent with the CB2 aesthetic I actually like.
- But I'm not clicking with enthusiasm. I'm clicking like a man who checks the fridge he just checked three minutes ago. There's no specific product, no room context, no nod to the fact that I've been in this sale since May 17th. Compare this to Pottery Barn's "LAST DAY" send which I'd give a 10 — that email linked directly to outdoor furniture and gave me a reason to land somewhere specific. This one just says "come in, the store is open." I'll click once, quickly, and probably leave without buying because nothing was surfaced for me.

## What's working

- **Dual-offer structure is strong** — 50% sale + 60% clearance gives me two entry points, and clearance is where I actually buy statement pieces.
- **"Ends tonight" is tight and credible** — not a vague "limited time," not a week-long rolling deadline. One night. That I believe.
- **Double Rewards callout is visible and placed well** — I do the rewards math. Seeing this mid-scroll stops me.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean** — SALE dominates the top, supporting offers cascade below it, product grid anchors the scroll. The eye moves logically.
- **No render bugs I can see** — images loaded, text is legible, nothing overlapping. Basic bar, but CB2 clears it cleanly.

## What's weak

- **This is email six in ten days from CB2, and it's showing** — even if this send is objectively good, I'm experiencing it through a layer of fatigue that pure email mechanics can't fix.
- **No product specificity** — the mosaic hero is atmospheric, but I can't tell what I'd actually be buying. Pottery Barn showed me outdoor sectionals. Article showed me a specific sofa with a price tag. CB2 is showing me vibes.
- **Double Rewards copy is undersized** — I can see the block, but from what I can read the reward math isn't spelled out (e.g., "earn $X back on every $100 spent"). I have to trust the headline, which I shouldn't have to do.
- **No personalization signal anywhere** — after five prior sends, I'm still a stranger. Wayfair at least tries to segment by category; I got rug emails when I was in a rug browsing phase.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface two or three specific hero products with price-before and price-after** — "This chair was $849, tonight it's $424" is the sentence that makes me forward this to my partner. Generic mosaics are forgettable.
- 2. **Spell out the rewards math** — "Double Rewards = earn $20 back on every $100 tonight only" converts better than a badge that makes me do arithmetic.
- 3. **Acknowledge the streak with a single line** — something like "You've been shopping the sale since Saturday — here's what's left" would land as self-aware and useful instead of tone-deaf.
- 4. **Move clearance above the fold on mobile** — 60% off is the more compelling number; lead with it for the scroll-resistant.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Tonight only: clearance up to 60% off + double rewards`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last hours — 60% off clearance, double rewards end tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The chair you've been watching is 50% off. Rewards double until midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Clearance goes first. Final hours to stack rewards on top.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta** — prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
This is a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) violation. Users with low vision cannot zoom. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Mobile breakpoint is too narrow** — responsive styles trigger at `max-width: 418px`:
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { .mobile { width:414px !important; } }
```
Most modern Android and iPhone Pro/Max devices have logical widths of 428–430px (iPhone 14 Pro Max = 430px). These devices will receive the desktop layout. Standard email breakpoints are 480px or 600px.

**`<title>` is generic and mismatches the subject**:
```html
<title>CB2, FURNITURE AND DECOR FOR THE MODERN HOME</title>
```
Subject line is "Final hours for up to 50% off". Some email clients surface `<title>` as a tooltip or tab label. Not a hard failure, but a missed signal.
