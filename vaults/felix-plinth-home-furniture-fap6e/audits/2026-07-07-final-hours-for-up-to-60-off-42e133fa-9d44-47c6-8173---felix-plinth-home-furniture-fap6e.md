# FINAL HOURS for up to 60% off!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- Eleven. I'm pretty sure this is eleven from the Crate & Barrel family today, and this one lands with the exact same subject line as email number ten — "FINAL HOURS for up to 60% off!" — which I already opened and already gave a six to before lunch. I genuinely had to triple-check the timestamp to confirm this wasn't a mail client glitch. It wasn't.
- Here's the thing: the email itself is fine. Countdown timer in the hero that shows under two hours left, clean product grid with actual furniture I might buy for the primary, a showroom-finder nudge, a TheDesignDesk section at the bottom. All of it is competent and on-brand. If this had landed as the *first* email of the day from Crate & Barrel, I'd have clicked through and probably tabbed into the sale. But it didn't land first. It landed eleventh, with the same subject line as the one that was already burning down the house before it.
- Mark as read. No forward. No click. I'm actually considering setting a filter at this point.

## What's working

- **Countdown timer is honest this time** — the clock read under two hours when I opened it, which is at least technically accurate. It's the one credible urgency signal in the email.
- **Visual hierarchy is solid** — the eye goes: logo → timer → "FINAL HOURS 60% off" banner → hero room shot → product grid. Nothing is fighting for attention.
- **Product grid is actually good** — furniture and accessories that map to what I'm shopping for in the second home. The scale reads right for a renovating customer, not a first-apartment buyer.
- **Showroom CTA is there** — "Find us at our 98 showrooms near you" shows up lower in the email. I actually care about this; Article and Wayfair can't offer that, and I want to see the fabric on that sofa in person before committing.
- **TheDesignDesk placement** — carrying the design-services section into a sale email is smart. Pottery Barn's free design email from earlier this week made me feel seen; Crate & Barrel slipping it in here at least remembers it exists.

## What's weak

- **Literally the same subject line as email ten.** "FINAL HOURS for up to 60% off!" appeared in my inbox at least twice today from this exact sender. I'm not exaggerating. This is a resend or a near-clone with a different UUID. The trust damage here is not abstract — it's inbox-destroying.
- **"FINAL HOURS" in all caps reads like shouting** — paired with the repeat, this is exactly the spam-signal combo that Article somehow avoided on their fourth "ends today" email of the July 4th sale. Article used lower-case urgency and kept the countdown in the body, not the subject. Different result.
- **No personalization signal anywhere** — I'm a known customer. I have a purchase history. CB2 at least gestures toward "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS" like it knows I buy for keeps. This email treats me like a cold address.
- **The product grid doesn't signal gender or renovation scale** — neutral-to-feminine styling on the hero room shot. Pottery Barn's email at least skewed the product toward the stuff I'd actually use in a primary-plus-second-home project.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Do not send the same subject line twice in one day.** Full stop. If the system is auto-generating a resend based on non-opens, the suppression logic needs to catch duplicate subject strings. This single issue vaporized whatever residual goodwill the first "FINAL HOURS" email left standing.
- 2. **Drop the ALL CAPS from the subject** — urgency works better when it sounds like a person, not a clearing-house flyer. Article ran "Last chance: 4th of July Sale ends today" and it outperformed the energy of this one purely on tone.
- 3. **Lead the product grid with big-ticket items** — I'm in the market for a sofa, a dining table, a credenza for the second home. Lead with the $800+ items on sale, not the $45 vase. Wayfair leads with the "7-piece sectional" in their sale heroes; Crate & Barrel has better product, so use it.
- 4. **Add a loyalty or member hook** — even one line: "As a member, here's your 60% off access" would have made this feel segmented rather than blasted. Pottery Barn Design Services does this better; they opened with a personal-consultation angle that made me feel like a client, not a subscriber.
- 5. **Suppress the showroom section or make it geo-specific** — "Find us at our 98 showrooms" is good content but it needs to surface my nearest location, not a national count.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags (confirmed):**
The same directives are declared redundantly — harmless but sloppy:
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
Three of the four values in the first tag are re-declared individually. Remove the three single-value tags.

**Zoom disabled (accessibility violation):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents users from pinching to zoom, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) failure. Remove the `maximum-scale=1` constraint.

**Conflicting desktop image `max-width` rules:**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
At viewports ≥ 768px, both rules apply; the 768px rule wins (later in cascade), capping images at 600px. At 640–767px, images are capped at 640px. If the email container is 600px, the 640px rule at 640–767px is unnecessary and could cause images to overflow on narrow-but-not-mobile clients. Consolidate to a single rule at the container width.

**Unfilled template placeholder (confirmed):**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
<!---->
```
Typo ("IMPUT" vs. "INPUT") and empty comment immediately after — the font import was never populated. This is a build/QA gate miss. If no custom font is needed, remove the comment entirely.

**Charset declared twice:**
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta charset="utf-8">
```
Redundant but harmless. The `http-equiv` form is for legacy clients; both can coexist, but the duplicate is noise.
