# New backpacks in every size, now starting at $39!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-05**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so this is Crate & Kids landing in my inbox, which is a Crate & Barrel sub-brand I don't remember signing up for — I'm on the Pottery Barn, Crate & Barrel, and Wayfair lists for two houses' worth of furniture, not backpacks. My gut reaction is "wrong list, wrong Felix" — I've got zero kids mentioned in anything I do, and this is a full back-to-school assault: size guide, small/medium/large backpack tiers, lunch boxes, water bottles. It's well-produced, I'll give it that, but it's aimed at a parent doing first-day-of-school shopping, not a guy mid-reno picking sconces.
- What actually stops my scroll is the "up to 50% off summer sale" banner buried two-thirds down and "TheDesignDesk" free interior design consult at the very bottom — that's the one thing in this whole email that's actually in my lane, and it's an afterthought under a pile of Jansport-adjacent backpacks. Compare this to the Wayfair "Fearless design, bold layers" send I scored an 8 on — that one opened with styled-room inspiration I'd actually stop for. This one makes me scroll past six product grids of kids' gear before I hit anything relevant.
- Verdict: not clicking, not forwarding — I'd mark this read and maybe flag it to check if I'm accidentally subscribed to the wrong C&B list. If I had a kid starting school this would probably be a solid 7-8, but for me it's dead weight.

## What's working

- The size guide up top (small/medium/large by grade level) is genuinely clear information design — if I were the target buyer I'd know exactly which backpack to shop in one glance.
- Photography is clean and consistent — same soft-neutral palette Crate & Barrel uses in the furniture sends, so brand recognition carries over even though the product category doesn't.
- "Make it a bundle" cross-sell (backpack + lunch box + water bottle) is a smart AOV play, similar to how CB2 stacks sale tiers, though here it's at least a coherent bundle rather than a confusing double-discount stack.
- The "TheDesignDesk" free design appointment block at the bottom is the one piece of content built for someone like me — should've been higher.

## What's weak

- This is a kids' backpack blast, not home goods — nothing here touches color palette planning, swatches, shipping timelines for furniture, or anything I'd actually use mid-renovation.
- Six full sections of backpack tiers before any home content shows up is a long scroll for zero payoff if you're not shopping for a school-age kid.
- The "up to 50% off summer sale" banner — the one offer that could pull me in — has no product visual, no category callout, just vague text on a lifestyle photo. Wayfair and Pottery Barn both lead harder with the actual discount number front and center.
- "we love these for you" personalization block shows... more kids' gear (backpacks, a car seat, a stroller). If this is meant to feel personalized to me, it missed by a mile.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment me out of this send entirely, or if Crate & Kids must cross-sell to Crate & Barrel's furniture list, lead with the home content (design consult, sale) and footer the backpacks — don't bury my one relevant offer at the bottom of a school-supply catalog.
- 2. Pull the "up to 50% off summer sale" block up near the top and put an actual dollar/percent figure and a product image on it — right now it's the vaguest, least-specific offer in the whole email.
- 3. If segmentation data shows no kids in the household, swap this whole send for a Crate & Barrel furniture piece — this is exactly the kind of mismatch that makes me numb to the brand family the way I already am with CB2 vs. Crate & Barrel overlap.
- 4. Move "TheDesignDesk" design appointment CTA into its own standalone module higher up — it's buried under a "$1,000 gift card" style footer treatment right now.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Backpacks for the kids, savings for the house`

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 86 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the automated HTTP probe (redirect domains, not independently verified) — link destinations unconfirmed.
- Open-tracking pixel `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png` appears **6 times** with identical `mi_u` params — likely one fire per template module; redundant beacon load.
- LiveRamp beacon `sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif` fires **5 times** with sequential `n=1`–`n=5` params — repeated third-party identity-sync calls in a single send.

- Full CTA `href` values are not visible in the truncated HTML, so destination/redirect-chain integrity can't be confirmed from source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 43 `<img>` instances flagged with no `alt` attribute (not even `alt=""`), spanning product imagery (`image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/...jpg/.gif`), a Scene7 asset (`s7d5.scene7.com/.../CK_F26_BackpackGuide/...`), and tracking pixels. Content images need descriptive alt text; tracking/spacer pixels should use `alt=""` rather than omitting the attribute (screen readers may otherwise announce the raw filename/URL).
- Three separate `format-detection` meta tags (`telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no` plus individual `date=no`/`address=no`/`telephone=no` tags) are redundant — should be a single consolidated tag.
- Correct use of `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, `color-scheme`/`supported-color-schemes` (light), and MSO `xml:namespace` fallbacks for Outlook.
