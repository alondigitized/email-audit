# Confession: We’re obsessed with SMEG
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- Okay. I did not expect this. After four C&B family sale emails in the last two days alone — each one screaming "HOURS LEFT" or "ENDS TODAY" — I open my inbox this morning and the subject line is *"Confession: We're obsessed with SMEG."* My first thought was genuinely "wait, this isn't a sale email?" My second thought was "I'm also obsessed with SMEG." So they had me at the subject.
- The rendered email is a full SMEG product spotlight — cream and sage retro appliances arranged in a grid at the top, a hero shot of a SMEG countertop oven in a kitchen setting that is exactly the kind of styled scene I'd pin, and then a cascade of supporting sections (wooden utensils, cast iron cookware, room vignettes) filling out the scroll. It's a brand-builder, not a promo. There is no headline discount, no ticking clock, no "2x Rewards ends tonight." Just: here's a beautiful Italian appliance brand and we carry it. That restraint is jarring in the best possible way after the Memorial Day blitz.
- Would I click? Yes, and I'd actually slow down and click more than one product. Would I forward it? Probably to my wife as a "look what we should get for the second kitchen" message. Would I archive it immediately? No — this is the folder-save type.

## What's working

- **The SMEG grid opener is instant recognition.** Cream toasters, sage blenders, retro kettles — I know this brand and I was already scanning product SKUs before I realized I'd scrolled past the hero.
- **Complete tone break from the sale cadence.** Pottery Barn pulled something similar in their "Pool storage, towels, umbrellas" send — they dropped the urgency theater. C&B did the same here and it lands just as well. After "HOURS LEFT" x4, silence is loud.
- **The kitchen hero shot is genuinely well-staged.** The countertop oven in its kitchen context is exactly how I think about appliances when I'm planning a renovation — not isolated on white, but *in situ*.
- **"Confession" framing is clever.** It's the brand acting like a human with a crush, which is disarming. I'd heard CB2 pull off that same wink-and-nod brand voice in "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" — this feels like C&B is borrowing from the sibling brand's playbook.
- **No render bugs visible.** Everything is crisp, grid is intact, images load, text hierarchy reads clean.

## What's weak

- **No pricing or offer in the scroll.** I renovate for a living (two homes, simultaneously). I want to know if SMEG is $400 or $900 before I invest emotional energy. Wayfair always drops the price point in the email body; C&B makes me land on a PDP to find out. That friction is real when I'm shopping comparatively.
- **The bottom half loses the thread.** After the SMEG sections, the email pivots to wooden utensils, then a dark Dutch oven, then a lifestyle room shot that feels like it's from a different campaign. It's too much. If the subject is a "confession about SMEG," the entire email should be SMEG.
- **No loyalty/rewards mention.** Every other C&B email this week hammered "2x Rewards." This one is silent on it. If I'm a Crate & Barrel member, tell me what I get for buying a $600 countertop oven today versus tomorrow.
- **The app section at the bottom is throwaway.** I'm already in the email. The "download our app" pitch is just padding that dilutes the SMEG story.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit fully to SMEG for the entire scroll.** Everything after the cast iron section should be cut or moved to a separate email. This is the "confession" email — own it. CB2 did this well with "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS" by staying thematically tight top-to-bottom.
- 2. **Show a price anchor in the email body.** Even just "from $199" under the hero product image. Wayfair does this religiously and it reduces the PDP-click-only-to-bounce behavior I default to on high-ticket items.
- 3. **Add a rewards callout.** "Earn [X] rewards points on SMEG orders" keeps loyalty-program members engaged without turning this into a sale email.
- 4. **Tighten the CTA copy.** The buttons in this email are vague from what I can read. "Shop SMEG" is fine — "Shop the Obsession" would be better and stays in the voice.
- 5. **Surface one hero SKU with a linked image.** Not just a grid — pick *one* SMEG item, give it a bigger moment, link directly to its PDP. Article does this well: they spotlight a single hero product above the fold and the click-through is intuitive.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta (WCAG 1.4.4 violation):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents user-initiated zoom on mobile. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Issue — `*` selector sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none`:**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applying `text-size-adjust:none` globally overrides OS-level accessibility text scaling. Scope this only to `body` or remove entirely — `body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; }` already appears lower in the stylesheet and is the correct form.

**Issue — Dark mode not supported:**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Declares light-only. Apple Mail and several other clients will force-invert colors or render dark backgrounds as white text on white. Either add `dark` support or explicitly test forced-dark rendering.

**Issue — Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags:**
The combined tag and individual tags are both present — `telephone=no` and `address=no` appear twice:
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
...
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
Redundant; the combined declaration is sufficient. Remove the three individual declarations.

**Cannot evaluate — image alt text:** Body content is truncated. Verify all `<img>` tags carry descriptive `alt` attributes, especially product images.
