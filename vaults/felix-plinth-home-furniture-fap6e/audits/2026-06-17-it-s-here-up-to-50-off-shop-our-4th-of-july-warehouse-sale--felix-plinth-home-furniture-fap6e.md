# It’s here! Up to 50% off: Shop our 4th of July Warehouse Sale!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- Seventy-one. That's the C&B family inbox tally after this one hits, and I want to be upfront: I got two "Starts today! Up to 60% off the 4th of July Warehouse Sale" emails from the C&B family earlier today — from what I'd assume is the main Crate & Barrel list. Now this one arrives from Crate & Kids, same date, same holiday event, but a different discount ceiling (50% vs 60%). At this point I'm not reading any of these as news — they all blur into one continuous "warehouse sale" drumbeat.
- The deeper issue: this is Crate & Kids. I don't have a nursery on the renovation list. Watching a Nuna stroller get its own hero module mid-email while I'm sourcing dining chairs and area rugs for two homes is the definition of being in the wrong zip code. The ocean-blue hero is genuinely well shot, the sale signals are loud and clear, and the email renders clean — this is a competent send. It just isn't sent to me, it's sent to someone who has a baby registry or a three-year-old. I am not that person, or at least nothing in my purchase history should suggest I am.
- Mark as read. Maybe forward to a friend who's actually expecting.

## What's working

- **Hero is clean and immediate** — "Up to 50% off" reads before any scrolling; the ocean-blue palette avoids the heavy red-promo look and actually makes the sale feel aspirational rather than panicked.
- **Crate & Kids brand is instantly legible** — this is not a confusing sub-brand sender; I know exactly what the store sells, which is more than I can say for some CB2 vs Crate & Barrel sends I've gotten.
- **4th of July hook is time-bound and credible** — unlike Wayfair's "Area Rugs from our Exclusive Brands" subject last week that promised specific and delivered generic, the seasonal peg here at least tells me *when* the urgency expires.
- **Product tiles show discount percentages** — not buried in fine print; each tile carries its own off-callout, which is the right call for a sale event.

## What's weak

- **Wrong sub-brand for my profile** — I'm on the C&B list because I'm buying furniture for two homes, not a crib or a Nuna stroller. The algorithm that sent me a Crate & Kids email instead of (or in addition to) the main C&B sale email did me no favors.
- **50% vs 60% off on the same day from the same family** — if I'm paying attention (and I am, I track this stuff), today's C&B siblings can't agree on a headline number. That inconsistency makes me trust the discount less, not more.
- **"Sign up, save 20%"** — I am already subscribed. Showing me an acquisition module feels like being handed a "Welcome, new customer!" flyer at a store I've shopped at for three years.
- **Nuna stroller as featured product** — a $600+ stroller in the hero rotation is not an impulse add-to-cart for someone sourcing lighting and soft goods across two renovation projects. Article's summer outdoor loop was at least cohesive to a lifestyle; this feels like a product dump.
- **No shipping or lead-time callouts** — Pottery Barn's "Quilts with character" send at least tells me what I need to know about fulfillment timing when I'm managing two jobs at once. This is pure "shop now," no delivery signal.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress Crate & Kids list for non-nursery purchasers** — or at minimum, don't fire this sub-brand email to me on the same day as two main C&B warehouse sale emails. The send-cadence is the biggest single fix; no creative tweak overcomes four C&B family emails in one day.
- 2. **Harmonize the headline discount across sibling brands on the same day** — 60% off from C&B and 50% from C&B Kids landing in the same inbox the same morning erodes trust in both numbers.
- 3. **Remove the sign-up module for active subscribers** — segment this out. I'm already on the list; that 20%-off acquisition block just makes me feel like I'm leaving money on the table for a deal I should already have.
- 4. **Swap stroller hero for a furniture category that straddles adult + kid** — rugs, storage, bedding — items with crossover appeal even for non-nursery renovators. CB2's "The rug that ties it all together" did exactly this: it featured a product that works in any room.
- 5. **Add a delivery/fulfillment line under the hero** — "Orders over $X ship free, arrives before July 4th" would push me toward a click far more than a stroller I don't need.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` brand mismatch**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
Sender is `Crate & Kids SALE`; title still reads the parent brand. Screen readers and some email clients surface the `<title>` as the document label.

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile — WCAG 1.4.4 failure (resize text, level AA).

**Conflicting `min-width` image cap rules (cascade order bug)**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
On viewports ≥768 px both queries fire; the 640 px rule appears later in the stylesheet and wins due to cascade order, capping images at 640 px instead of the intended 600 px. Rules should be ordered largest-breakpoint-last, or consolidated into a single rule.

**Hard light-mode lock**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Explicitly opts out of dark-mode rendering in Apple Mail and Samsung Mail. Recipients using dark mode see a forced white background; no dark-mode CSS fallback is present in the visible source.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
`telephone=no`, `address=no`, `date=no` each appear twice (once combined, once individually). Harmless but indicates copy-paste template drift.

The first is a literal editor instruction (with a typo: `IMPUT` → `INPUT`) that was not stripped before send. The second is an empty comment — a stub that was never populated. Neither causes rendering failure, but both confirm the template was not linted before deployment.
