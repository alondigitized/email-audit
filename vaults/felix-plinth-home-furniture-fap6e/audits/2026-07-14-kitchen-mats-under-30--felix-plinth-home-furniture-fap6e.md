# KITCHEN MATS under $30 ✨
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- "KITCHEN MATS under $30" lands in my inbox and I actually feel something, which is saying a lot given the volume Wayfair has been sending me. Two kitchens in simultaneous renovation — primary and second home — means kitchen mats are a genuine line item on my list right now. The subject caught me. I opened it.
- And then I got "the must-shop steals." Not kitchen mats. Open Box furniture. A dog bed. A portable AC unit. A shelf. The same scattershot editorial dump Wayfair served me on July 12 with "TRASH CANS & RECYCLING" — where I scrolled the entire email and found, as I noted then, nothing that matched the subject. This is that email again. Different subject hook, same bait-and-switch body.
- The irony is I'd probably buy a kitchen mat right now if they showed me one. Instead I got "pup approved." I don't have a dog. Mark as read.

## What's working

- **Subject line precision for my situation**: "KITCHEN MATS under $30" is three words I actually needed this week — that's not an accident, that's a legitimate targeting win.
- **Open Box Deals section**: A clear "Shop Now" button, real copy ("Save big on returned, like-new products"), no fluff — if this were the kitchen mat hook it promised, I'd be clicking.
- **Star ratings on product tiles**: Social proof in the product grid is visible and I always notice it.
- **"Cool down" section is seasonally on-point**: It's July. Showing cooling products in July is at least not a timing mistake.

## What's weak

- **The subject line and the email are two different emails**: "KITCHEN MATS under $30" promised me something specific; "the must-shop steals" delivered everything else. This is the trash cans send from July 12 with a different hat on.
- **Hero copy is generic to the point of meaninglessness**: "the must-shop steals" tells me nothing. Crate & Barrel ran "$170 off Le Creuset Dutch ovens" in their hero and I could smell the specificity from the inbox. Wayfair can't commit.
- **"Pup approved" section burned real estate on someone without a dog**: I've never once bought pet products from Wayfair. This section exists because the send is templated, not because they know me.
- **No price anchors visible in the body**: The "$30" is in the subject, but the Open Box section doesn't show a single price or percentage off in the visible render. That number does no work inside the email.
- **Cadence is completely blown**: I'm looking at my inbox and this is at minimum the fourth Wayfair email in two days. The bins/boxes email yesterday, the chicken coop before it, the trash cans on the 12th. Every open is a small tax on my goodwill and this one collected without paying anything back.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the body match the subject — show me kitchen mats**: If the subject says "KITCHEN MATS under $30," the hero image should be a kitchen mat. One product. One room. If you have inventory to clear, anchor it. Article's new arrivals send works because it does what it says.
- 2. **Give the Open Box section a price signal**: "Save big" is not a number. "Up to 60% off" or "From $19" — pick one and put it in the section header. Crate & Barrel named the dollar amount in the hero and I remembered it two sends later.
- 3. **Suppress the pet and cooling sections for non-pet, non-HVAC buyers**: Segmentation doesn't have to be surgical — just remove the obvious misses. CB2 Free Design Services sends me bedroom scenarios because they know I've browsed bedrooms. Wayfair knows my order history and still shows me dog beds.
- 4. **Pull the subject's price point into the hero copy**: "Kitchen mats starting at $19" as a hero subhead would have made this a different email entirely.
