# 10% off your next purchase—even on furniture.
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-26**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so the subject promised 10% off "even on furniture" but the actual hero of this email is a giant iPhone mockup asking me to sign up for texts. That's a bait-and-switch right out of the gate — I opened this for a furniture discount, not to hand over my phone number to another brand's SMS list. It takes real scrolling before anything resembles the promised offer, and by the time I hit "we love these for you" I've already forgotten why I opened it.
- Buried in there though is a "Busy Baby Activity Chair" sitting right next to a KitchenAid mixer and a dining table — which tells me their personalization engine has no idea I don't have kids, same problem I flagged on every Crate & Barrel Kids send this week (2026-09-26, "up to 60% off clearance," 2026-09-26 "gotta-see clearance picks"). At least this is the real C&B house feed this time, like the SMEG one from earlier today, not another Kids reskin — small mercy.
- Would I click? Maybe on "Shop New Dining Furniture" since I'm actually mid-renovation and need dining pieces, but I'd have scrolled past three CTAs to get there. Wouldn't forward this to a friend — it's a scattershot "here's our whole catalog" email, not a sharp offer.

## What's working

- The dining room section ("life happens in your dining room") actually speaks to my renovation reality — that's the one beat that landed.
- Le Creuset Dutch oven shows a real price drop ($460 → $289.95) with strikethrough — that's honest, visible math, unlike some vague "up to" language.
- Rewards messaging ("10% back in Reward Dollars across all our design forward brands") is relevant since I'm already a highest-rewards-card shopper.
- Clean, uncluttered product grid with real prices — no lorem ipsum, no broken images.

## What's weak

- The subject says "10% off... even on furniture" but the hero is a phone-and-texting signup, not the offer — total mismatch between subject and what I actually see first.
- Baby activity chair mixed into "we love these for you" — I don't have kids, and this personalization miss is the same complaint I've had all week with the Kids sub-brand sends.
- Way too many competing CTAs stacked top to bottom (Sign Up for Texts, Shop Bestsellers, Shop Now, Shop New Dining Furniture, Learn More, Download Now, Book a Free Appointment) — nothing anchors my eye.
- The fall decor banner and dining room lifestyle shot both look great but push the actual discount further down, past where most people stop scrolling on mobile.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the 10% offer as the hero — right now the subject's best asset is invisible until you scroll. Put a clean "10% off your first order, furniture included" banner up top before the texting pitch.
- 2. Fix the product recommendation feed — a baby chair next to cookware and sofas reads like zero segmentation. Pottery Barn's towels email this week was tighter because it stuck to one relevant category for a renovator like me.
- 3. Cut the CTA count in half. CB2's "Restock alert" send had the same stacking-category problem I'm seeing here — pick one primary action per section, not seven across the whole email.
- 4. Move "Shop New Dining Furniture" higher since that's the only section tied to the subject's furniture promise.
