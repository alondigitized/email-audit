# "Durable, stylish, elegant" dinnerware
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-14**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction was "this is a dinnerware email wearing a Black Friday email's clothes." The subject sells me plates, but the top of the email is a banner about getting it by Thanksgiving, and two little boxes: "last Double Rewards this year!" and "Early Black Friday is heating up: up to 30% off." The 2x rewards is the part I care about, because I put everything on the highest-rewards card. It's tiny and sits above a big salad-bowl photo, though.
- It's also a very long scroll. After the dinnerware collections (aspen coupe, marin, mercer, aspen rimmed) I get kitchen deals, a second "Early Black Friday" block, Christmas stockings, a Gift Shop banner, and then the Double Rewards message again at the bottom. That makes four or five emails stacked in one. I'm renovating two houses, so a dinnerware set is a "maybe, Pinterest folder" item. The stockings and the Gift Shop banner are noise to me. This is the same C&B family pileup I've been complaining about since 11/13. After the Kids sends on 11/14 ("Double Rewards + Up to 50% off Early Black Friday deals!" and the Oilo nursery chairs), this at least feels like the right sender for my life. I wouldn't forward it. I'd probably click once on the aspen coupe, then save the email.

## What's working

- The Thanksgiving ship-by line ("order by 11/18 at 11:59 pm PT") at the top gives me a real deadline. I'd credit it more if it were the headline.
- The "last Double Rewards this year!" box matters to me, since rewards math drives my purchases. Having it twice, top and bottom, means I won't miss it.
- The collection-by-collection layout (aspen coupe, marin, mercer, aspen rimmed) is how I browse. Each one has its own name, a one-line description and a Shop Now button.
- Star ratings under some of the plate and bowl tiles are real social proof. They help more than the pretty photos do.
- The photography is clean and neutral, with whites, greys and warm wood. It fits the palette I'm trying to keep consistent across both houses.
- The kitchen-deals row ("they ship free!") shows sale prices on espresso machines and cookware. That's a concrete offer I can evaluate.

## What's weak

- Both rewards and discount info are small and sit above the fold, while the hero is a food photo. The eye lands on the salad, not the offer.
- The email is far too long and tries to cover tabletop, kitchen, Early Black Friday, stockings and gifts. I lose interest well before the bottom.
- Every Shop Now button reads the same. "Shop Now" under aspen coupe, marin and mercer tells me nothing and doesn't separate them.
- The stockings and Gift Shop sections are irrelevant to a guy trying to finish a renovation. They also read as filler.
- The "it's on! up to 30% off Early Black Friday" tiles say "up to 30% off" but are vague about which products and what rewards math applies. Is it stackable with 2x?

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one offer. Put "2x Rewards ends soon + up to 30% off dinnerware" in the hero and cut the second "Early Black Friday" block. The offer gets repeated three times in different words right now.
- 2. Split this into two sends: dinnerware, and holiday gifts and stockings. Don't make me scroll past a Christmas stocking to find the plates.
- 3. Make the CTAs specific: "Shop aspen coupe," "Shop marin dinner sets." Add price points to each collection.
- 4. Add a swatch or sample prompt such as "Order a free sample plate" or a matching-set builder. I want to confirm the color before I buy a set for two houses.
- 5. Note rewards math in plain words: "Earn 2x rewards on this set, ends 11/18."

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's markup basics are sound (`lang="en"`, XHTML doctype, MSO conditionals, preheader-safe resets). About 52 `<img>` tags have no `alt` attribute, and the QA harness couldn't confirm the compliance or authentication headers. The HTML source was truncated, so body-level checks (footer, unsubscribe link, UTMs) were not possible.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **96 tracking/click-redirect links were not probed.** They were skipped as tracking domains, so destination validity, redirect chains and 404s are unverified. This is a coverage gap, not a confirmed defect.
- **Third-party pixel stack.** The QA data shows these tracking images:
  - `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=dbe8da71…&n=1` through `n=5`. These are five identical LiveRamp pixels, differing only by `n`.
  - `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif?mi_u=…`, the open pixel.
  - `https://dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/226ed0f0-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…`, a second open/view beacon.
- Five repeated `rlcdn.com` calls and two open beacons add requests without adding signal. The `s=` hash and `mi_u=` identifier are per-recipient values placed in image URLs. Proxy or privacy-preserving clients, such as Apple Mail Privacy Protection, will fetch them regardless of whether a human opened the email.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **~52 images flagged for missing `alt`.**
  - **Content images (38):** `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/*.jpg|png|gif`, e.g. `bbf6f9d4-…png`, `bf3053a9-…jpg`, `971aa10f-…jpg`. The QA data doesn't show whether any of these carry the primary messaging. If they do, this is a real accessibility and images-off failure.
  - **Product/recommendation images (7):** `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png`.
  - **Tracking pixels (7):** the `rlcdn.com`, `o.gif` and `dv.` beacons. These should carry `alt=""`, which is a minor fix. A missing attribute is read differently from an empty one by screen readers.
- **`<meta name="viewport" content="…maximum-scale=1">`** disables pinch-zoom, an accessibility issue (WCAG 1.4.4).
- **Conflicting `format-detection` metas.** There are three declarations: `telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no`, then separate `date=no`, `address=no` and `telephone=no`. They are redundant, and clients typically honor only the first.
- **Dead CSS rule.** `@media (min-width:768px){img{max-width:600px}}` is followed by `@media (min-width:640px){img{max-width:640px}}`. Both match at ≥768px with equal specificity, so the later 640px rule wins and the 600px rule never applies.
- **Unescaped `&` in `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>`.** This is invalid XHTML under the declared doctype. It is cosmetic in practice.
- **Leftover template comment:** `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->`. It is harmless, but it exposes template scaffolding and has a typo.

- **`Authentication-Results` not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown, again because of the relay. Verify against the raw message at the original receiving MTA if this matters.
- Footer physical address and in-body unsubscribe link are not visible in the truncated source and are unverified.

- The click-through URLs are wrapped in a redirect domain (`mi.crateandbarrel.com` / `dv.crateandbarrel.com`). That's standard ESP behavior, but final-destination alignment with the "Durable, stylish, elegant" dinnerware subject remains untested.
