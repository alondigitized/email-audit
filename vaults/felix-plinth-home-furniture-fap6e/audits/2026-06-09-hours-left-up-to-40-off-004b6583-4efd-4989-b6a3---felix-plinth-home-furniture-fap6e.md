# HOURS LEFT: Up to 40% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-five. That's the CB2 count since mid-May, and I need that number on the table before I say anything, because it is the single most important filter through which this email gets read. Fatigue is doing real work here — "HOURS LEFT" in all-caps in the subject line reads less like urgency and more like the brand screaming from the end of the hallway. I've heard this exact tone from CB2 before. Multiple times in the past week alone.
- That said: I opened it, and the hero actually stopped me. The midseason sale layout is clean — pink sectional, clear sale callout, "SHOP NOW" front and center. The visual side of this email is doing its job. What's not doing its job is the internal offer arithmetic. The subject promises up to 40% off, but the "Design Legends Sale" section mid-scroll is calling out up to 20% off. Those two numbers in the same email, without any explanation of why they diverge, make me trust the headline less. Feels like the 40% is one discontinued table and everything else is 10%.
- I'd mark this as read. Maybe click if I had 45 seconds and the sofa hero happened to match something I'm actively sourcing for the second property. I would not forward it — there's nothing here my wife wouldn't have seen in one of the other twenty-four.

## What's working

- **Hero render is clean and on-brand.** The pink/mauve sectional in natural light reads luxe without being fussy — consistent with the CB2 aesthetic I pay for.
- **"MIDSEASON SALE" is legible and unambiguous** at the top of the body; no guessing what the event is.
- **"MORE FOR YOU" personalization section** at the bottom is the right instinct — product grid feels like it's pulling from real browse/purchase data, not just popular items.
- **Visual hierarchy holds up** — sale callout, then categories, then the Design Legends secondary offer. The eye travels naturally.
- **"HOURS LEFT" subject line is under 50 chars** — at least it's not truncated on my lock screen.

## What's weak

- **"HOURS LEFT" without a specific timestamp is lazy urgency.** Hours left until midnight? Until 11:59 PM PT? Pottery Barn puts the actual deadline in the email body. CB2 doesn't bother, and it makes "HOURS LEFT" feel like a template default, not a real countdown.
- **40% subject vs. 20% Design Legends body = bait-and-switch energy.** Even if both numbers are technically accurate, the gap is never explained. That erodes trust.
- **"LEAVE US A REVIEW, WIN $1000!" mid-email breaks the purchase flow completely.** This belongs in a post-purchase transactional email, not slotted between sale sections. Article would never do this in the middle of a promotion.
- **No furniture sizing or shipping context whatsoever.** I'm actively buying furniture for two properties. A 48" table versus a 60" table matters. CB2's sofa hero gives me zero dimensions, lead time, or in-store availability — Crate & Barrel's rug email this week at least told me the size family I was looking at.
- **The ALL CAPS "HOURS LEFT" subject skims the edge of spam-signal territory** — Wayfair does this routinely and it's part of why I mentally discount their subject lines as noise.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the review solicitation mid-email.** Move it to a post-purchase flow. It fragments the funnel and signals that CB2's email team is juggling too many KPIs in one send.
- 2. **Add a specific deadline with timezone.** "Ends tonight at midnight CT" is worth more than "HOURS LEFT" in all-caps, costs nothing, and treats me like an adult.
- 3. **Reconcile the 40% vs. 20% messaging** with a clear editorial note: "Up to 40% off furniture — Design Legends up to 20% off" stacked together. Don't make me do the math.
- 4. **Surface one hero product with a dimension callout.** Even one line — "116" sectional, in stock" — is the difference between me saving this and me marking it read.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Midseason Sale ends tonight — up to 40% off`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 40% off furniture & rugs — sale ends at midnight`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Sectionals, dining, rugs, lighting — sale closes at midnight CT`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Furniture clearance + Design Legends on sale — final hours`

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully assess** — HTML is truncated before body links appear. The following is flaggable from the `<head>` alone:

- **Double-slash in font URLs (4 instances):** The `src` attributes in `@font-face` blocks use `https://www.cb2.com//Styles/...` (two forward slashes). While most servers normalize this, it is a malformed URL and should be corrected.
  - `https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2`
  - `https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2`
  - `https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2`

- **Cross-brand font CDN dependency:** Four `@font-face` `src` values pull from `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/...` (sibling brand). If that domain has a CDN outage or CORS policy change, CB2 email font rendering breaks independently of cb2.com uptime.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **Template placeholder not removed:** A raw dev comment remains in `<head>`:
  ```
  <!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
  ```
  Note also the typo: "IMPUT" instead of "INPUT." This is a pre-send QA failure — template scaffolding was shipped to recipients.

- **`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:** Prevents user-initiated zoom. This fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is flagged by accessibility validators. Users with low vision cannot zoom the email.
  ```html
  <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
  ```

- **Aggressive `text-size-adjust: none` on `*`:** The global reset `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}` disables all OS-level text scaling. Compounds the zoom issue above.

- **Global `line-height: 100%` on `*`:** Applied universally via `*{line-height: 100%}`, which collapses line height on multi-line text blocks to effectively single-spacing with no descender room. This is commonly overridden inline but increases fragility.

- **Mobile breakpoint is 418px (`max-width: 418px`):** The breakpoint triggers only on viewports ≤418px. iPhone 14/15 Pro (393px CSS width) and SE (375px) fall within it, but several mid-range Android devices at 420–430px will receive the desktop layout on a phone screen.
