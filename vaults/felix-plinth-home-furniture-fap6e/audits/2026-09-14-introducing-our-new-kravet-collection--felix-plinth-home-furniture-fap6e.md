# Introducing our NEW Kravet Collection
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-14**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "kravet. x pottery barn" — this is a collab announcement, and my first reaction is it actually looks expensive. That floral archival-print hero shot with the layered bedding is doing real work; it doesn't scream "sale," it reads like a designer showroom moment. But then it just... keeps going. Bedding, then pillows, then a quote about "patterns telling the story of a home," then a tablescape, then MORE bedding, then a sofa, then a "recommended for you" grid, then Halloween Shop, then Design Crew, then the credit card pitch, then Affirm. By the time I hit "Enter If You Dare" for Halloween decor in a fabric-collection email, I've completely lost the thread.
- This is the second Pottery Barn "textile collab" style send I've seen this stretch, and it's better shot than most of what they've sent me this week — no Molly Sims, no celebrity tie-in, which I appreciated after complaining about that exact thing on 2026-09-12. But it's also the longest email by far, and there's no price anywhere. Not one number. I'd scroll through the top third for the mood, then bail before the credit card block. Not forwarding this — my wife would ask "so what does it cost" and I'd have nothing.

## What's working

- The hero pairing "kravet." and "pottery barn" logos side by side signals a real designer collab, not just a seasonal refresh — that's catnip for someone renovating two houses.
- The archival floral bedding shot at top is genuinely well-styled, moody, layered — this is the kind of image that makes me screenshot it for my Pinterest-adjacent folder even if I don't click.
- No celebrity name-drop, no "SALE" banner shouting over the visuals — matches the restraint I praised in the 2026-09-13 "holiday preview" send.
- The swatch/fabric close-ups (the striped and patterned textile grid) are a nice touch for someone who actually cares about fabric quality, not just the finished piece.

## What's weak

- Zero pricing anywhere in this email. Not on the bedding, not the sofa, not the "Recommended Just For You" grid. Crate & Barrel's wreath-wall send last week at least didn't scream SALE either, but this one doesn't even give me a number to anchor on — I have no idea if this collection is $200 or $2,000 a yard.
- It's enormous. I counted through bedding, pillows, a quote block, a full room vignette, ANOTHER bedding shot, a sofa, then a product grid, then Halloween, then Design Crew, then rewards, then Affirm — that's five different campaigns stapled into one email. CB2 at least keeps its swatch-program pitches to one clean message.
- The "Recommended Just For You" grid feels like a bolt-on afterthought — curtain rods and a platform bed dropped in right after a moody fabric-collab narrative kills the mood completely.
- Halloween Shop showing up mid-scroll in a Kravet fabric announcement is a total non-sequitur — that's a seasonal-cadence problem, not a content problem, and it undercuts the premium feel they just spent six screens building.
- The credit card / Affirm block at the bottom is the same beat I called out on the 2026-09-12 Visa email — it's become predictable filler at this point.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this email in half. Lead with the collab story (hero + one bedding shot + the quote), end with "Shop the Collection" — kill the Halloween Shop, Design Crew, and financing blocks entirely. This is a brand-storytelling email; let it stay one.
- 2. Put a "starting at $X" price point somewhere near the hero or the first product shot. I shouldn't have to click through to find out if this is attainable.
- 3. Move "Recommended Just For You" out of this send — it doesn't belong next to a designer archive story, it dilutes the whole premise.
- 4. Add a swatch-sample CTA (CB2 does this well — "order free swatches") since this is fabric-forward and I'm renovating; let me sample the pattern before committing to a $3k sofa reupholster.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Kravet x Pottery Barn: fabric that tells a story`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New from Kravet: archival prints, now in-stock`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Order a free swatch before you commit`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Layered patterns, 200 years of textile history`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
This is an MSO-targeted HTML email (Pottery Barn "Kravet Collection" launch) with standard responsive/Outlook conditional markup; automated QA flags missing alt text on nearly all images, two insecure `http://` image sources, and absent unsubscribe/authentication headers in the captured transport data.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- Logo click-through uses a tracking redirect: `https://click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=ABB7InYiOjEs...` — standard ESP (Responsys/Oracle) click-tracking wrapper, functioning as expected.
- Open-tracking pixel present via SocialVine: `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` — served over plain HTTP (see §3) and includes the recipient email address in the URL query string in cleartext, which will leak the address to any intermediary logging that request (proxies, CDN logs, browser history).
- Second tracking pixel: `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/L21rdC84NjEvY2lkLzE3NDc3ODk3MzMvdC8y/cat/1` (Turn/Amobee) — third-party ad-tracking pixel embedded in a marketing email; confirm this is intentional and disclosed in the privacy policy.
- 59 additional tracking/click-redirect links were present but skipped by the automated HTTP probe (by design, per QA tool) — not independently verified as live.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 46 `<img>` tags are missing `alt` text (QA-confirmed), spanning hero banners, product slices, footer navigation, and both tracking pixels. Screen-reader and images-off users will see no fallback text for any content image in this email.
- Two images use insecure `http://` sources rather than `https://`, which many mail clients (Outlook, Gmail image proxy) will block or flag as mixed content:
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Not a functional bug (parsed as invalid CSS and ignored) but signals a broken build/export pipeline worth flagging to the ESP template team.
- `<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow" />` and `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />` are present — correct practice for transactional/marketing email markup.
- Responsive breakpoints via `@media` queries and MSO conditional comments (`<!--[if gte mso 9]>`, `<!--[if mso 16]>`) are structured correctly for Outlook/desktop fallback.
