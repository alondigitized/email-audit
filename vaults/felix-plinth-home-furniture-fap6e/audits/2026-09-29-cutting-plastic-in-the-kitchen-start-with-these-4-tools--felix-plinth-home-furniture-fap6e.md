# Cutting plastic in the kitchen? Start with these 4 tools
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-29**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction was "fine, but not for me." This is the seventh Crate & Barrel-family send I've waded through this week. After this morning's "Makeover reveal!" Design Desk email, which was aimed at my two-house renovation, this one is a step back to generic housewares. The plastic-free angle is a decent hook, but it's a kitchen-gadget roundup, not a renovation email. I'm not picking cabinet pulls or a backsplash here, I'm looking at spatulas.
- The layout is clean and easy to skim. There's a big header with a Shop Now button, then four numbered sections (utensils, cutting boards, bowls, storage), each with a photo, a row of products with star ratings and "Ships Free" tags, and its own shop button. It reads like a magazine spread. At 680px wide I could follow it fine, but the product captions are small. On a phone I'd have to pinch-zoom to read them.
- I'd skim it, maybe save the storage section to my Pinterest-adjacent folder, and mark it read. I wouldn't forward it, since nobody I know is asking about wooden utensils. I might click into the cutting boards if I were actually shopping.

## What's working

- The four numbered sections tell me exactly what I'm getting, and each has its own button ("Shop Utensils", "Shop Cutting Boards"). Those are verb-plus-noun CTAs that beat a vague "Discover".
- Star ratings and "Ships Free" tags on the product rows are useful for a heavy-item shopper. I care about shipping on furniture, and free shipping is a good trust cue even on small stuff.
- The photography is warm and consistent. The wood, glass, and steel palette hangs together, so it's a mood board I'd actually save.
- The roasted kale salad recipe with "Watch Now" adds some personality. It's a lifestyle beat, not just a catalog dump.
- The Crate Card / Reward Dollars block near the bottom speaks to how I shop. I always use the highest-rewards card, so I noticed it.

## What's weak

- There's no real offer. Nothing above the fold says a % or $ off. The only money message is buried in the card block near the bottom.
- The subject's "cutting plastic" framing doesn't carry through the email. The eco angle shows up in the intro copy, then it's a normal product grid, and a promo banner and app-download block at the bottom dilute it.
- The email is very long. It has four sections, a recipe, a holiday banner, a "we love these for you" grid, a card pitch, an app block, and a Design Desk block. The brief was four tools, and this is a full catalog.
- The cadence is exhausting. This is at least the seventh C&B-family send this week, after the wine glasses, the Kids storage email, and the Halloween shop. Some of the same housewares keep resurfacing.
- The holiday banner in the middle has text over a photo that I found hard to read at this size. It also jumps the theme from "plastic-free kitchen" to Christmas.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put one clear reason to act now near the top, like a member price, a bundle deal, or a free-shipping threshold. Right now it's all browsing and no urgency.
- 2. Cut the email to the four promised sections plus the card block. Move the holiday banner, app download, and Design Desk block to their own sends. Pottery Barn Design Services gave me a single free-design-help ask and it worked better.
- 3. Make the product captions larger. The names and prices are too small for a phone.
- 4. Segment. I'm renovating, so a "kitchen refresh" send with hardware, lighting, and cookware in a matched palette would fit me better. CB2's "Color Is the Word" swatch-and-room approach is what I'd want. Article's bedroom story did the same job, with fewer distractions.
- 5. Space out the family sends. Crate, CB2, and Kids shouldn't all hit me in the same week.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary

The QA data shows no hard failures, but roughly 47 images lack alt text and all 82 links are click-redirects, so link and UTM behavior can't be verified. The HTML source is truncated, so the footer and unsubscribe markup could not be inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **All 82 links skipped.** QA did not probe any of them because they use tracking or click-redirect domains, so none of the destinations were verified.
- **Third-party pixels.** Five `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=…&n=1` to `&n=5` requests fire per open. Unless the message is deliberately built this way, this is unusually heavy for a single email.
- **Other tracking beacons.**

  - `https://dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/acbba079-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…` is a second beacon.
  - Six `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png?mi_u=…` requests repeat the same asset.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on about 47 `<img>` elements.**
  - About 34 are content images, mostly `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/*.jpg`. Two are scene7 assets: `2026_0313_CB_CleanKitchen_ToolsPrepHeroVid` and `2026_0926_CB_HolidayAwareness_Anthem`.
  - The rest are tracking pixels: 6 `f3c0d22992a01946.png`, 5 `448796.gif`, `o.gif` and the `dv.` beacon. These should carry `alt=""` (the QA data can't show whether the attribute is absent or empty).
  - If the content images are absent (not just empty), the email is unusable with images blocked or a screen reader.
- **Zoom disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` prevents pinch-zoom, a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Unescaped ampersand.** `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` under an XHTML 1.0 doctype should be `&amp;`.
- **Leftover template placeholder.** The comment `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` is still in the source (with a typo).
- **Redundant markup.** There are duplicate `<meta charset>` and `http-equiv Content-Type` tags, and repeated `format-detection` metas. These are harmless.

Gmail and Yahoo require one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders, so it should be checked against the raw message.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown, and QA attributes this to the relay. The From domain `mail.crateandbarrel.com` matches the image host, which is consistent with a properly configured sender subdomain, but that is not proof.
- **Footer not inspectable.** The physical address and in-body unsubscribe link are outside the truncated source, so they can't be verified here.
- **Plain-text part is mostly URLs.** 16,885 of 23,182 characters (about 73%) are URLs, over the 70% threshold. This is a spam-filter risk, and it follows from the long tracking-redirect URLs.
