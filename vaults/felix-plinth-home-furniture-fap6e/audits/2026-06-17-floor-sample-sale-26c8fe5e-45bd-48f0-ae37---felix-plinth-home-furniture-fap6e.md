# FLOOR SAMPLE SALE
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-one. That's the CB2 count since mid-May, and I'm front-loading it because this email lands the same day as their "NEW ITEMS ADDED: Warehouse Sale" send — meaning June 17 alone delivered two CB2 emails, both featuring the Warehouse Sale as a secondary or tertiary concept. I open my dedicated inbox folder (think Pinterest, but for things I might actually buy) and find two CB2 tabs sitting next to each other. That's not a sale, that's a negotiation with my attention span that CB2 is losing.
- The floor sample concept itself is legitimately interesting to me. I'm furnishing two homes simultaneously. A showroom-quality sofa at a markdown nobody else can replicate — that's exactly my use case for a second home where matching sets don't matter. But "FIND A STORE" as the CTA flips the whole premise. I was already in click-through mode when I hit that button and had to pivot to "do I want to book a store trip on a Tuesday?" That mental gear-change costs you the conversion. I close the tab and go back to Pottery Barn's "Shop the Summer Sale" that also landed today — because I can click that from my couch.
- The warehouse sale at 60% off is buried in section three, behind the floor sample hero and a Design Legends Sale at 20% off. That's the most actionable item in this entire email for an online shopper, and CB2 decided to let two other promos stand in front of it. I'd probably click SHOP NOW on the warehouse if I scrolled that far, but the hierarchy made me work for it. Mark as read on the floor sample; maybe open a new tab for warehouse.

## What's working

- **60% off warehouse sale is the best number in the email** — Pottery Barn's same-day summer sale didn't touch 60%, and Wayfair's "MUST-SEE DORM ROOM DEALS" was furniture-adjacent, not furniture. When CB2 says 60% off, I listen.
- **"ENDS SUNDAY" on the Design Legends Sale** — only credible time-bound signal in the email, and it lands clean. Not manufactured FOMO, just a deadline.
- **"More For You" personalized product tiles** — a side table, a lamp, a shelving unit — these map to my actual renovation browse history. Someone is paying attention to what I click, and it reads like a curated shortlist rather than a clearance bin.
- **EARN 10% BACK loyalty call-out** — I run purchases through the highest-rewards card I have and I stack loyalty where I can. Visibility on this matters to me.
- **Clean render, no bugs** — polished and professional; nothing broke, no Lorem Ipsum bleeding through.

## What's weak

- **"FIND A STORE" as the hero CTA without a clear in-store-only flag in the copy** — the subject, hero text, and visual all suggest a shoppable event. "FIND A STORE" is the reveal. That mismatch between expectation and CTA is the biggest problem in the email.
- **Three concurrent sales stacked vertically with no clear hierarchy** — Floor Samples → Design Legends → Warehouse Sale. Crate & Barrel on the same day led with one thing (the KitchenAid upgrade) and executed a single concept cleanly. CB2 asked me to hold three sales in my head simultaneously.
- **Two CB2 emails, same day, same Warehouse Sale** — the other June 17 CB2 send also features Warehouse Sale content. That's not a reinforcement strategy, that's inbox pollution. Wayfair, which sits at 57+ emails in my folder, still manages to feel like each send has a distinct job.
- **"LEAVE US A REVIEW, WIN $5,000" is a non-sequitur** — it interrupts the shopping flow between the sale sections and the personalized product recs. It's a retention mechanic dropped into the wrong email at the wrong moment.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to one sale per email.** If floor samples are the hero, the email ends there — maybe a small warehouse callout in the footer. If warehouse 60% off is the real news, lead with that. Three stacked sales train subscribers to scroll past whatever came first.
- 2. **Disclose in-store-only status before the CTA.** A single line — "In-store only. Find your nearest CB2." — underneath the floor sample hero copy removes the mismatch entirely and might actually drive store visits from people who live nearby.
- 3. **Consolidate the two June 17 sends.** One email — floor samples + warehouse update — is more coherent and doesn't cannibalize either open rate.
- 4. **Pull the review module to the footer.** A "WIN $5,000" prompt belongs after checkout, not interrupting a sale email.
- 5. **Add a store-proximity hook to the floor sample CTA** — "Floor samples available at 8 locations near you" would transform "FIND A STORE" from friction into discovery.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Floor samples: showroom pieces, last weekend`
- **Subject Alt B:** `In-store only: floor sample sale ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `One-of-a-kind pieces from our showrooms — see what's near you.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `60% off warehouse + floor samples this weekend only. Find a location.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` disables pinch-to-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Remove `maximum-scale=1`. It prevents low-vision users from zooming on mobile.

**Overly aggressive `line-height` reset:**
```css
* { line-height: 100% }
```
Applied to every element globally. Multi-line text (especially in buttons and footers) can collapse or clip across Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook. Use targeted selectors instead.

**Mobile breakpoint is too narrow:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { .mobile { width: 414px !important; } }
```
iPhone 15 Pro is 393 logical px wide; most Android flagships are 360–412px. A breakpoint at 418px is borderline and will miss a significant share of devices. Standard email practice is `max-width: 600px` for the responsive pivot.

**Conflicting desktop media queries:**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 690px) { img { max-width: 690px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 100% !important } }
```
Screens 690–767px get `max-width: 690px`; screens 768px+ get `100%`. This is probably intentional for a 690px container, but the 768px rule silently overrides it for large-viewport clients and could cause images to stretch beyond the container.

**Unfilled developer placeholder comment left in production HTML:**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
This is template residue (note also the typo: "IMPUT" instead of "INPUT"). It has no rendering impact but confirms the template was not fully sanitized before send.

**No `role="presentation"` on layout tables** — minor, but screen readers will announce them as data tables. Standard practice for email is to add `role="presentation"` to all structural `<table>` elements.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Cannot confirm UTM coverage** — links are not present in the truncated source. Verify:

- No raw tracking redirect URLs resolve to 404s or redirect to a homepage instead of the sale landing page

**Font cross-origin dependency:** Body fonts are loaded from both `https://www.cb2.com/` and `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/`. A Crate & Barrel CDN outage or CORS policy change would silently break CB2 email typography. This is a structural fragility, not an active breakage.

**Double-slash in font URLs:**
```css
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
```
Multiple occurrences. Double slashes are normalized by servers but indicate copy-paste errors in the template and should be cleaned up.

**Duplicate `@font-face` declarations for the same family:**
Three separate `@font-face` blocks declare `font-family: "Lineto Brown Sub"` — one for the base, one for `font-weight: 400`, one for `font-weight: 600`. This is functionally redundant (the base declaration already loads both weights) and adds unnecessary HTTP requests on clients that render webfonts (Apple Mail, some iOS clients).
