# Our Back-to-School Sale is on! Up to 20% off
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-28**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids again — this is now the sixth of these in my inbox, and I still don't have kids, still don't plan to. Back-to-school is at least an actual seasonal moment instead of a made-up sale name, so points for that, but the email itself is a wall: monogrammed backpacks, lunch boxes, pencil cases, a desk-and-chair "focus zone," a $4,199 giveaway, 500+ new fall arrivals, a credit card pitch, an app download push, and a "TheDesignDesk" concierge banner — all before I even hit the footer. It's not one email, it's six emails stapled together.
- My gut reaction is the same one I had on the 07-28 "Early bird" send and the "organized kid's room" one before it: this brand has decided I'm a parent and won't let it go. The "create a focus zone" desk section is actually decent-looking staging, and if I did have a kid starting school it'd probably land. But I'm a guy renovating two houses full of grown-up furniture, and there's nothing here — no sofas, no dining tables, no lighting — that speaks to me. Not opening the real one, not forwarding it, marking as read and moving on.

## What's working

- The hero shot — pastel lunch box, backpack, water bottle staged on a warm wood counter — is genuinely well-styled, better than most of Wayfair's cluttered promo grids.
- "back-to-school sale: up to 20% off" is a clean, specific hero headline directly under the banner — no scrolling needed to find the offer.
- The "create a focus zone" desk/chair module is the one section with real lifestyle photography instead of just product-on-white, and it breaks up the grid nicely.
- "500+ new fall arrivals, starting at $29!" gives a concrete price anchor, which is more than CB2's vaguer "Just in. Already trending." offered.

## What's weak

- I don't have kids. Backpacks, pencil cases, lunch boxes, monogrammed water bottles — none of it applies to me, and this brand has sent me this exact premise five times now.
- The email just keeps going — sale grid, focus zone, giveaway, new arrivals, "we love these for you" product rail, credit card upsell, app download, TheDesignDesk concierge, follow-us social icons. It's kitchen-sink email design, the same sprawl problem I dinged the "top sofas" C&B send for.
- The "we love these for you" personalization rail is still kid gear (car seats, dressers, gliders) — so the "for you" personalization is aimed at a persona that isn't me at all.
- The credit card and TheDesignDesk sections feel bolted on, unrelated to back-to-school, diluting whatever focus the top of the email had.
- Monogram initials in the product photos (A.R., J.D., Morgan) are a nice real-world touch for an actual Crate & Kids shopper, but for me it's just another signal this was never built with my inbox in mind.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment me out of Crate & Kids sends entirely and route me to Crate & Barrel or CB2 — I've never clicked one of these, and this is at least the sixth in a row.
- 2. Cut the email down to one theme. Pick back-to-school-sale or the giveaway or new fall arrivals — not all three plus a credit card pitch plus an app download.
- 3. Move the desk/chair "focus zone" content higher if it stays — it's the strongest visual block and currently buried mid-scroll.
- 4. Drop the credit card and TheDesignDesk modules from a sale email; save them for a dedicated send.
