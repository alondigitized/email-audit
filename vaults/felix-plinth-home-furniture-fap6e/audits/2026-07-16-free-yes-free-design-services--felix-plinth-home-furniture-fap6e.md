# FREE (yes, free!) design services
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-six. That's where I am after this one, and this is also the second Pottery Barn email I've opened today — the rugs guide already landed earlier. What's surprising is that I'm not annoyed. The subject's parenthetical is doing something the previous twenty-five sends didn't bother trying: it sounds self-aware. "(yes, free!)" has a conspiratorial tone that reads less like copy and more like someone leaning over and going "no, seriously." That's a real move, and it worked on me.
- The email itself lands on exactly the right category at exactly the right time. I'm renovating two homes simultaneously. I've been putting off sofa decisions, bedroom layouts, and storage planning for weeks because there are too many decisions running at once. The four service cards — refresh your space, styled bed, sofa or sectional, storage solutions — read like a to-do list I've been avoiding. Whether that's behavioral data driving the layout or just Pottery Barn's standard service pitch, it doesn't matter. It hit.
- That said, I'm clicking the appointment CTA and probably not actually booking — I'll rationalize waiting until the second house drywall is done. I wouldn't forward this. The format is too utilitarian and the offers are too personal-situation-specific. It does its job. That's more than I can say for the Wayfair kitchen mats email, which I've now received three consecutive days and which has gone from funny to invisible.

## What's working

- **The subject's self-awareness cuts through.** "FREE (yes, free!)" doesn't sound like a campaign line — it sounds like a friend flagging something. That's rare after 26 emails from the same sender.
- **The four service cards are directly actionable** — sofa/sectional, bed layering, decor refresh, storage solutions. These map exactly to what I'm shopping for across both renovations right now, even if PB didn't personalize to me specifically.
- **The CTA is unambiguous.** "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" appears twice with a clear button. No "Discover" hedging. That's the right verb.
- **The 10% Rewards callout near the bottom earns its place.** I'm running rewards math on every major purchase. Connecting the free design session to points-back framing helps me see this as a funnel, not just a charitable offer. Article's editorial sends — which I scored 8/10 — don't have loyalty infrastructure at all.
- **Clean, consistent render.** No broken images, no layout drift. For a high-volume sender, consistency at this cadence is actually harder than it looks.

## What's weak

- **"Free design services" is still vague.** I see "styling tips, room refreshes, total makeovers" in the hero, but those are aspirations, not deliverables. CB2 got "New chairs. Instant character." in three words and gave me a concrete image. Pottery Barn needs something like "in-person room plan + 3D layout included" to make this feel like an offer rather than an appointment with a salesperson.
- **The Fall Collection callout at the bottom undercuts the premise.** If you're leading with a genuinely free, no-strings service, don't pivot to a shop-today seasonal plug halfway through. It makes the free offer feel like a lead-gen setup. Either trust the design services concept to carry the email or split these into two sends.
- **"Spots go fast" is not urgency.** It's the soft-FOMO equivalent of lorem ipsum. If there's an actual constraint — limited designers, a booking window, a city-specific cutoff — show me the calendar date. The copy doesn't earn the scarcity signal.
- **Affirm at the bottom is noise for me.** I'm running a rewards card to max points. BNPL messaging is actively counterproductive — it competes with my actual purchase logic and clutters the scroll below the fold.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with a specific deliverable in the hero.** Replace the current soft hero copy with something concrete: "Free in-home session — walk in with a floorplan problem, leave with one solved." Make the output tangible or the offer doesn't feel different from a sales pitch.
- 2. **Cut the Fall Collection section or make it its own email.** These two messages (free service vs. shop the catalog) fight each other. The design services email earned a full-send slot; don't dilute it.
- 3. **Add a real booking constraint with a date.** "Limited appointments this month — book by July 23" converts soft copy into a real reason to act now. If it's not true, don't say it. But if there's genuine capacity scarcity, use it.
- 4. **Connect the rewards callout to the design session, not just to purchases generally.** "Book a free session + earn 10% back on everything you buy from your plan" is much stronger than a generic rewards blurb near the footer.
- 5. **Personalize the service card order by browse history.** If I've been looking at sofas on potterybarn.com (I have), the "Choose the perfect sofa or sectional" card should be first. Behavioral sequencing here would have made this email feel specifically addressed to me rather than a lucky coincidence.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**WARN — 30 images missing `alt` text.** Nearly every image in the email has `alt=""`, including all body content slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_08.jpg`) and all footer slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`). Since the entire email is image-sliced with no HTML text fallback, recipients with images disabled see a fully blank email.

**FAIL — Two images served over HTTP (not HTTPS):**
```
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg
```
Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail proxy/block mixed-content HTTP images. These images will likely not render for the majority of recipients.

**WARN — PHP `header()` calls inside `<style>` block:**
```html
<style>
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  ...
```
This is server-side PHP code that was never executed — it was pasted raw into the HTML `<style>` tag. Email clients will treat it as invalid CSS and discard the block. The intended cache-control behavior is not applied. This is likely a template assembly bug.

**Responsive breakpoint:** The stylesheet defines `max-width: 480px` in one media query and `max-width: 650px` in another. The inconsistency means the mobile layout activates at different widths depending on which rule takes effect — potential rendering gap between 480–649px.
