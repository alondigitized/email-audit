# ENDS TODAY: Up to 20% off ALL kids desks!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- Thirty. That's where the C&B family lands in my July inbox after this one, and I want that number right upfront — because I literally reviewed this exact email this morning. Same sender, same subject, same "ENDS TODAY" urgency, same 20% off all kids desks hero. I gave the first version a 7. Now it's back in my inbox like it forgot it already visited.
- The email itself is competently built. The warm-wood desk lifestyle image is clean, the secondary storage offer adds genuine value beyond the hero, and the "we love these for you" personalization band at the bottom suggests someone's algorithm is actually trying. If this were the first time I'd seen it today, I'd probably shop. But I already processed this send. I made my decision. Whatever window C&B thinks is closing today, I already looked through it.
- Mark as read. No forward. No click — not because the offer is bad, but because I already decided hours ago, and sending it again doesn't change my math.

## What's working

- **Hero is clean and legible**: The natural-wood desk image with bright room staging reads immediately on mobile — warm, aspirational, doesn't make me scroll to understand the offer.
- **Two-tier offer structure is smart**: All desks (20% off) stacked above select storage (also up to 20%) gives the email breadth without feeling bloated — that's something the Article email I audited last week does well too.
- **"ENDS TODAY" is credibly urgent**: Unlike Wayfair's dead-inside free-shipping loop, C&B Kids earned the deadline language with a defined category and a real discount number.
- **"We love these for you" personalization band**: Four curated picks that appear to pull from my browsing — a nice touch that Pottery Barn does even better with their Chris Loves Julia collab send, but C&B is at least trying here.
- **TheDailyDesk editorial block**: I respect any brand that adds editorial content to a sale email — it makes the thing feel less like a spam blast. Faint praise given everything else, but it's there.

## What's weak

- **This is a duplicate send**: Same subject, same hero, same day. That's not urgency — that's list hygiene failure. If I'd missed the morning send and caught this afternoon one, maybe. But I didn't miss it.
- **"Up to 20% off SELECT storage" is a hedge**: After the "ALL kids desks" confidence of the hero, walking into "select" on the secondary offer feels like a bait. Pottery Barn owns their exclusions more cleanly in copy.
- **The app promo is dead weight in a sale email**: I don't need a "download the app" nudge sandwiched between a sweepstakes and editorial content. Pick a lane — is this a sale send or an engagement send?
- **Sweepstakes section mid-email breaks the conversion funnel**: The $1,000 giveaway appeared in the Crate & Kids send I reviewed yesterday too. Running it across back-to-back sends makes it feel evergreen rather than exclusive — kills the credibility of both the sale and the sweepstakes.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress the duplicate**: The single highest-impact change is infrastructural — dedupe sends within the same calendar day, full stop. No revision to creative fixes a list management problem.
- 2. **Make storage inclusive or cut it**: Either say "20% off all storage" or be specific about which lines are included. "Select" in a sale email is a conversion killer for a buyer who knows your catalog.
- 3. **Cut the app download module**: In a ENDS TODAY sale email, every module that isn't a CTA to buy is friction. Move the app promo to a low-traffic Tuesday standalone.
- 4. **Give the personalization band a category label**: "We love these for you" is fine but "Based on your wishlist" or "You saved these — they're on sale" would convert harder.
- 5. **Consolidate the sweepstakes**: One dedicated sweepstakes email per campaign, not a recurring footer module in every send.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Kids desks sale ends at midnight — your picks`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus up to 20% off storage through tonight only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your wishlist items are part of today's sale.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional promotional email rendered via Salesforce Marketing Cloud (MI platform). Four webfont assets return 403s and 25 images lack alt attributes; compliance header visibility is degraded by the AgentMail relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Broken webfonts (403) — 4 failures:**
```
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/1e07d600-3787-4517-bd3d-b140f9e54ec2.woff2
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/7ec25fd5-2752-4d84-b307-af3faabc21bb.woff2
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/aaa41944-25b3-4265-80b2-5564efe9b2d7.woff2
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/fd5f8f50-d539-4a7d-b893-99501d746ef2.woff2
```
The `@font-face` declarations reference these paths but the CDN is refusing them. Email clients that attempt to load web fonts (Apple Mail, newer Outlook on Mac) will silently fall back to system fonts. The fallback stack in the CSS (`Arial`, `Georgia`, etc.) is present in the MSO conditional blocks, so degradation is handled — but the font load attempts generate failed network requests.

**Tracking links (22 skipped):** Click-redirect URLs through the MI tracking domain were not probed; destination URLs and UTM parameter presence are unverified. See Section 6.

**Third-party tracking pixels identified:**
- `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/` — Salesforce Marketing Cloud open pixel (×6 instances)
- `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/` — MC upload/open pixel
- `sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif` — LiveRamp/RampID pixel (×5 instances, `n=1` through `n=5`)
- `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/` — unknown internal pixel with `mi_mid` parameter

The LiveRamp pixel fires 5 sequential requests, which is consistent with their segmentation use case but worth noting for consent audit purposes.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — 25 images:**

All product images and all tracking/spacer pixels are missing `alt` attributes entirely (not `alt=""` — the attribute is absent). Examples:
```
bbf6f9d4-86cc-437a-aa80-391067b74306.png  (content image)
2856f177-1a07-40af-b84b-06791ebfd97f.jpg  (content image)
d99d15dc-40b0-441a-a1e0-584687c08eda.gif  (likely spacer)
f3c0d22992a01946.png                       (tracking pixel, ×6)
448796.gif (sr.rlcdn.com)                  (tracking pixel, ×5)
o.gif (mi.crateandbarrel.com)              (open pixel)
```
Content images should carry descriptive alt text. Tracking pixels and spacer GIFs should carry `alt=""` explicitly — absence means some assistive technologies will announce the filename.

**Dark mode locked out:**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
The email explicitly opts out of dark mode rendering. This is a deliberate choice but means Apple Mail dark mode users receive the light-mode layout with no adaptation.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags:**
`format-detection` for `telephone=no`, `address=no`, and `date=no` each appear twice in `<head>` — harmless but indicates copy-paste template debt.

**`<title>` mismatch:** The `<title>` element reads `Crate & Barrel` while the sending brand is `Crate & Kids`. Matters for tab display in webmail clients (Gmail, Outlook.com) that surface the title.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header: NOT FOUND** — QA relay did not capture this header. Gmail and Yahoo require `List-Unsubscribe` for bulk senders (>5k/day); its absence or non-forwarding by the relay could affect inbox placement and triggers a compliance gap under RFC 2369.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header: NOT FOUND** — One-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) is not confirmed. Gmail has required this for bulk senders since February 2024. If the header exists upstream but is being dropped by the AgentMail relay, the relay's header forwarding config needs investigation.

**`Authentication-Results` header: NOT FOUND** — SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown. The sending domain `mail.crateandbarrel.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain, which typically carries DKIM alignment — but this cannot be confirmed through the relay as captured.

These three findings may be relay capture artifacts rather than actual sender omissions, but they cannot be cleared without direct header inspection.
