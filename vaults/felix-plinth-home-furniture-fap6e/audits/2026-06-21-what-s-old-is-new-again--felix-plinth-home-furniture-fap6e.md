# What’s old is new again
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-five. That's the CB2 tally since mid-May, and this one — landing the morning after they sent me *two* emails on June 20 — has me in inbox-exhaustion mode before I even open it. The subject "What's old is new again" is the kind of phrase that sounds clever in a brand brainstorm and communicates exactly nothing when I'm scrolling a crowded inbox at 7am. I had zero idea this was a midcentury furniture sale until I opened it.
- Once I'm inside, though? The aesthetic actually earns some attention. The warm walnut tones, the boucle swivel chair, the cane-door sideboard — that's a mood board I've been building in my head for the second home's den. CB2 doesn't always go this warm; usually it's the cool grey-and-chrome direction. The midcentury direction works for me personally, and the "IN OUR WAREHOUSE SALE" green badge tells me there's actual money on the table.
- The problem is the email is doing the work the subject line refused to do. I wouldn't have opened this on a normal day — it read like a lifestyle platitude, not a sale. I'd save this to my "revisit" inbox folder, not forward it. Not a click-out, but not a close either.

## What's working

- **The aesthetic pivot is real.** Warm wood, curves, boucle — this is the midcentury moment I've been shopping, and CB2 doesn't always land here. The photography feels cohesive, not catalog-scatter.
- **"IN OUR WAREHOUSE SALE" badge is doing heavy lifting.** The green pill in the hero anchors the browse with intent — I know this isn't just a lookbook.
- **"SHOP THE COLLECTION" is honest CTA copy.** It's a verb + noun, not a vague "Discover" or "Explore." Points for that.
- **Visual hierarchy holds.** Hero → product grid → CTA reads in the right order. Nothing fights for my eye.
- **The sideboard with the cane/mesh doors would stop me mid-scroll.** That specific piece is doing more work than any copy in this email.

## What's weak

- **The subject line is a throwaway.** "What's old is new again" is a cliché that doesn't telegraph furniture, sale, or midcentury — all three things that would have got me to open faster. Compare Crate & Kids' "4th of July Warehouse Sale: Up to 50% off is here!" — blunt, effective, clicked it immediately.
- **Five CB2 emails in four days is too many.** June 18, 19, two on June 20, now this on June 21. Pottery Barn has managed two-homes-worth of content without burning out my thumb on the unsubscribe button quite so hard.
- **No discount anchor anywhere in the hero.** The warehouse sale badge is there, but I have no idea if I'm looking at 20% or 50% off. The Crate & Kids email I got recently put "Up to 50% off" in the subject *and* the hero — that's the move.
- **"SHOP THE COLLECTION" goes to a browse, not a product.** I want to know if that cane sideboard is $799 or $1,999 before I give up a click.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the discount in the subject line and hero.** If it's 30% off, say 30% off. The warehouse sale promise without a number feels like a tease. Article managed to lead with product specificity in their Cillian collection email and it pulled me in harder than any "sale" badge.
- 2. **Slow the send cadence.** Three emails in two days is the single biggest damage to open rates here. One email for the Todd Snyder collab, one for the midcentury sale, one to close it out — that's a week, not 96 hours.
- 3. **Surface a hero product with a price.** Make the cane sideboard the anchor. Show me $899 (was $1,299) and I'm clicking through immediately.
- 4. **Add a deadline.** "Warehouse sale ends Sunday" turns a browse into a decision. Nothing in this email creates any urgency.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Developer placeholder comment in production output**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
This is an unfilled template instruction left in the sent HTML, including a typo (`IMPUT` vs `INPUT`). It exposes the ESP/template vendor's internal workflow to anyone who views source.

**Issue 2 — Conflicting `webkit-text-size-adjust` declarations**

Three declarations target the same property with different values:
- `body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100% }` (reset block, line ~13)
- `body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none }` (linked styles block)
- `* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none }` (universal selector, overrides both)

The universal `none` wins by specificity cascade, defeating the `100%` reset entirely. On iOS, this can cause text to remain unscaled even when the user explicitly requests it.

**Issue 3 — `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Some email clients strip this, but it should not be present.

**Issue 4 — Double-slash paths in font URLs**
```
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2
```
Three of the six `@font-face` `src:` values contain double slashes. Servers typically normalize these via 301 redirect, but it adds a redirect hop and indicates a template merge error.

**Issue 5 — Cross-brand font loading from `crateandbarrel.com`**

Four `HNW05` font variants are loaded from:
```
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/*.woff2
```
CB2 and Crate & Barrel share ownership but are distinct brands and domains. This creates an unnecessary cross-origin dependency — if that domain has any CDN issue or CORS policy change, font loading silently fails. The font should be served from `cb2.com` or a shared static CDN.

**Issue 6 — `font-display: swap` in email**

Used on four `@font-face` declarations. This CSS descriptor is not recognized by any major email client (Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail). It is harmless but dead code — safe to remove.

**Issue 7 — Non-standard mobile breakpoint**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)
```
The standard breakpoint for narrow mobile is 480px or 600px. 418px targets only the narrowest phones and will leave medium-small devices (SE 3rd gen at 375pt, Pixel at 393pt) rendering the desktop layout.
