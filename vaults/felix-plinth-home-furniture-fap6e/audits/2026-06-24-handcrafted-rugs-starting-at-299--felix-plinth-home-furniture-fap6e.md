# Handcrafted rugs starting at $299
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-nine. I've officially lost count, but this one lands June 24 — same calendar day as "The Summer Sale is UP TO 70% OFF," which means I opened two Pottery Barn emails before noon. And yet, if I had to pick one email from this entire Summer Sale run to keep in my folder, it might be this one. "Handcrafted rugs starting at $299" is doing something the last four sends completely failed to do: it tells me what to buy and what it costs. I have two rooms that need rugs right now — one at the primary, one at the second home — so a price anchor and a specific category is actual decision-support, not just brand atmosphere.
- The email is clean. The hero is warm-neutral lifestyle room photography — the exact aesthetic I'm pinning. Below the fold there are sectioned use-case callouts (entryway, desk area) and a "Rug Guide" module, which signals someone was thinking about a buyer at a specific purchase stage. The "Recommended Just for You" strip at the bottom is where my trust wavers — it's always a coin-flip between "actually curated" and "algorithm dumped."
- I'd click the hero image. I would not forward this. Pottery Barn's cadence has burned through my evangelism budget for the season.

## What's working

- **Price anchor in the subject** — "$299 starting" is the single most useful piece of information in any Pottery Barn email I've opened this week. After four consecutive "UP TO 70% OFF" subjects that felt like a highway billboard, a number cuts through.
- **One category, one email** — The last five PB sends tried to sell me bedding, furniture, design consultations, and rugs simultaneously. This is just rugs. That focus is useful when rugs are on the actual list.
- **Sectioned use-case layouts** — Entryway, desk area, living room. Article's "Bedroom storage essentials" email used the same structure and I spent 20 minutes on their site after; it works because it mirrors how I actually shop by room.
- **Rug Guide module** — Educational content inside a sale email is a real reason to bookmark. That's differentiation from Wayfair, which just hammers seasonal CTAs with no content layer.
- **Photography palette** — Textured, warm neutrals, styled rooms not showroom flats. Matches what I'm pulling for both houses right now.

## What's weak

- **Sale ambiguity** — Is $299 the sale price or the pre-discount floor? I can't tell without clicking through. That ambiguity is friction on a purchase decision that starts with "does this pencil out?"
- **Generic CTA copy** — Every section button reads some variation of "Explore" or "Shop Now." Wayfair's Fourth of July email had the exact same problem. If the entryway section said "Shop Entryway Rugs," I'd be more likely to tap it.
- **No size or dimension signals** — Rug shopping is fundamentally a size problem. Nothing in this email tells me whether I'm looking at 5×8s or 9×12s without clicking through and bouncing.
- **Cadence damage is already done** — The "Recommended Just for You" strip almost certainly contains something I've already scrolled past in the last 48 hours of PB sends. I'm not verifying that; I'm just skipping it on reflex.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make "$299" work in the hero, not just the subject** — The price anchor is doing all the lift from the inbox view but disappears once I'm inside the email. A clear "Rugs from $299" in the hero image earns the click. CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" at least made the sale feel intentional in the body; this one buries the number.
- 2. **Swap "Explore" for room-specific CTA copy** — "Shop Entryway Rugs" / "Shop Desk-Area Rugs." One verb, one noun, one room. Shoppable content should be treated like it.
- 3. **Add a size callout per section** — Even a single line — "available 5×8 through 12×15" — reduces the bounce rate from click-through.
- 4. **Put a deadline on the sale** — If the Summer Sale ends Sunday, say so. "Ends Sunday" is worth more open rate than a sixth email next week. Wayfair at least floated "before they sell out" on their Fourth of July send; PB isn't even doing that.
- 5. **Promote the Rug Guide above the fold** — That module is the single thing that differentiates this email from a Wayfair category blast. Lead with the editorial; let the sale be the close.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Rugs for every room — sale starts at $299`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your next rug is on sale. Starting at $299.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Handcrafted styles for entryways, living rooms, and that desk corner you keep putting off.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The Rug Guide is inside — plus Summer Sale pricing through the weekend.`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Handcrafted Rugs" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard-broken links (HTTP 403) will cause click failures for the credit card banner and a cross-brand asset reference. The email also has pervasive missing alt text across ~40 images and three HTTP-sourced assets that will be blocked by modern mail clients enforcing HTTPS.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Broken credit card apply link (403)**
`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&...`
The Key Rewards / credit card CTA returns a 403. Any subscriber clicking the "Apply & Buy" banner gets a dead end. Likely a misconfigured or expired campaign token on the `clickcp.potterybarn.com` redirect handler.

**FAIL — Broken cross-brand spacer asset (403)**
`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
A West Elm spacer GIF is referenced in a Pottery Barn email and returns 403. This is a dead cross-brand CDN reference — likely a stale copy-paste from a shared template. Additionally served over HTTP (see §3).

**25 tracking links skipped** — not probed; no action required, but confirm click-redirect domain `click.e.potterybarn.com` resolves correctly in a pre-send test.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (will be blocked by Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail in strict mode):**
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` (open-pixel tracker)

All four use `http://` — clients with mixed-content blocking will silently drop these, breaking layout slices and open tracking.

**Missing alt text — 40 images total.** Key offenders affecting content comprehension:
- `S26_TOP_SummerSale_NoUrgency.jpg` — hero banner, no alt
- `slices_01` through `slices_11` (rug product grid) — no alt on any
- `F26_Bottom_NewArrivals.jpg`, `S26_Bottom_UpTo60OffOutdoor.jpg` — promo banners, no alt
- Footer slices `slices_01` through `slices_18` — no alt on any

When images are disabled (default in many corporate mail clients), the email renders as a blank white column with no descriptive text fallback.

**Malformed CSS in `<style>` block:**
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
PHP `header()` calls were pasted directly into a `<style>` tag. These are invalid CSS and will generate parse errors. They have no effect on email delivery but indicate a template hygiene issue and may confuse some CSS parsers in rendering engines.

**Empty `<title>` tag** — no subject line fallback in the document head.

### 5. Compliance
