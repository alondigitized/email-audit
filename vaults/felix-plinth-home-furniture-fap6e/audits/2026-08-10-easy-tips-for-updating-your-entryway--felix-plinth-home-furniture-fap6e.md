# Easy tips for updating your entryway
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-10**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is the sixth-ish Crate & Barrel-family email in my inbox this week, and this one's a curveball — it's not even a sale email, it's "TheDesignDesk" content marketing dressed up as an entryway guide. Points for actually being a different creative concept than the clearance-grid emails I've been slogging through, but the whole thing is a wall of "shop this vignette" modules stacked so deep I gave up scrolling before I hit the honeymoon sweepstakes and the credit card pitch.
- The styling itself is genuinely nice — moody arched doorway, console table vignettes, contrast-textured rugs, that's exactly my Pinterest-folder aesthetic. But there's no offer anywhere. No %, no free shipping line, nothing. It's a lookbook, and lookbooks are for browsing on a lazy Sunday, not for the 40 seconds I've got scrolling this on my commute.
- I wouldn't click past the hero, honestly. I'd screenshot the console table vignette and drop it in my reno folder, then close the email. Not forwarding this one — my wife's already annoyed I keep sending her C&B links.

## What's working

- The arched-doorway hero photo is genuinely aspirational and on-brand for "warm welcome" — better mood-setting than the beach-hero clearance email C&B sent yesterday.
- "Exclusive" and "Restocked" tags on individual products (the mirror, the runner) give me a reason to believe these are worth a look, not just filler.
- The "3 ways to meet a design desk pro" block (at home / in-store / online) is a smart trust cue for someone mid-reno like me — CB2's Cubs-ticket email had nothing this practical.
- Multiple distinct vignettes (console, wall art, rug, moodboard) actually match how I shop — room by room, not by category grid like Pottery Barn's furniture-and-more dump.

## What's weak

- Zero discount, zero urgency, buried CTA logic — every button just says "Shop X Furniture," nothing tells me why to act today versus bookmarking for later.
- It's exhausting long — by the time I hit the honeymoon giveaway and credit-card pitch I'd already checked out; Article's "Bedroom reset" editorial send was half this length and made its point faster.
- The "we love these for you" personalized grid at the bottom is buried under like eight other modules — that's the one section that should've been higher.
- Credit card pitch ("now the card you love just got better") feels tacked-on and out of place next to a $23,000 honeymoon sweepstakes — mismatched offers stacked with no throughline.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this email in half — pick 3 vignettes max (console, rug, wall art) and kill the moodboard/sweepstakes/credit-card modules into a separate send. Long design-content emails need way more restraint than sale emails.
- 2. Move "we love these for you" personalized picks up near the top — that's the section that actually reflects me as a returning shopper, and it's currently below the fold after a sweepstakes.
- 3. Give me one real reason to act now — even a soft one like "book before end of month" for the free design consultation, since right now there's zero urgency anywhere.
- 4. Drop the credit card ad entirely from a design-inspiration email — it reads like two campaigns got merged.
