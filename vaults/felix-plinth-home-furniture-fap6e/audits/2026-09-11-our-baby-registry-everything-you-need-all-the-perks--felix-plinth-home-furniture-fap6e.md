# Our Baby Registry: everything you need & all the perks →
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-11**
## Executive summary

- Six. That's six Crate & Kids/Crate & Barrel family emails in my inbox this week, and this is the one that finally made me laugh out loud — "Registry Essentials to Create Your Dream Nursery" landed this morning, and now a few hours later here's "Our Baby Registry: Everything You Need & All the Perks," which is somehow the exact same email with a different subject line stapled on top. Free design appointment, free assembly, 15% off everything, the Nuna/UppaBaby/Stokke brand strip, the "$5000 sweepstakes" — I've now seen every single one of these modules at least twice this week across different sends.
- I don't have a kid. I don't have a kid on the way. I'm renovating two houses and I'm in the market for actual furniture, and Crate & Barrel's own Samsung Frame TV email from earlier this week at least understood that about me for half a second before pivoting back to the family funnel. This one doesn't even try — it's registry, registry, registry, assembly service, registry again. Straight to archive, not even a real read.

## What's working

- The nursery hero shot is genuinely well-styled — warm wood crib, that mural wallpaper, good photography, I'll give them that.
- The "let us assemble it" module is a smart value-add for anyone actually furnishing a nursery — free assembly on orders over $75 is a real, concrete perk, not vague marketing fluff.
- Brand strip (Nuna, UppaBaby, Stokke, Baby Jogger, Bugaboo, nanit, Mushie) is clean and recognizable if you're actually shopping this category.
- The "15% off everything" banner is a clear, understandable, single number — no fine print maze to parse.

## What's weak

- I have no kids — this is the sixth Crate & Barrel family send this week and every one of them assumes I'm expecting or already a parent.
- It's a wall of modules — design desk, assembly, brands, 15% off, "more perks," "more to love," finishing touches, sweepstakes, app download — nothing is prioritized, so nothing sticks.
- The subject line change from this morning's "Registry Essentials to Create Your Dream Nursery" to this one is cosmetic — same content, same offer, same everything, just reshuffled copy up top.
- Zero furniture-for-grownups content even though I'm actively furnishing two houses right now — Pottery Barn's "Free Design Services" send at least stayed adjacent to something I'd actually buy.
- The sweepstakes banner ("enter for a chance to win $5000") is buried two-thirds down after I've already mentally checked out.

## Recommendations

- 1. Suppress me from this segment entirely — if the account has zero baby-category signal after five ignored sends this week, stop sending nursery content and start sending living room / dining / patio content instead. That's the single highest-leverage fix here.
- 2. Pick one hero offer and lead with it. CB2's recent "NEW TO COVET" send worked because it had one clear focal point — this has eight competing modules and no hierarchy.
- 3. If Crate & Barrel insists on cross-selling the Crate & Kids sub-brand to non-parent subscribers, do it as one small module inside a general home email, not as a standalone six-times-a-week campaign.
- 4. Move the $5000 sweepstakes up near the top — that's actually a broad-appeal hook that doesn't require having a kid, unlike everything else in this email.
- 5. Stop reusing subject-line variants of the same static email — Article's "Patio Season's Not Over Yet" felt like a genuinely different creative this week; this felt like a find-and-replace on yesterday's send.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Win $5000 toward your home — no registry required`
- **Subject Alt B:** `15% off everything at Crate & Barrel (yes, everything)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Furniture, decor, and a shot at $5000 — for every room, not just the nursery.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free design help and 15% off, whether or not there's a crib involved.`
