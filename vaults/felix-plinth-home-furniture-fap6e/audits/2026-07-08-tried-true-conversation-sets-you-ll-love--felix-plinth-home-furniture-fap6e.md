# TRIED & TRUE CONVERSATION SETS YOU’LL LOVE >>>
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- I almost didn't open this one — and that instinct comes entirely from the weight of my Wayfair inbox this week. By my count, this is approximately email eleven or twelve since July 7th. That's after the "BATH MATS" send that had zero bath mats in it (I genuinely scrolled the entire thing — rugs, umbrellas, sectionals, zero bath mats), the "CLEARANCE ENDS TONIGHT" that apparently ran across multiple nights, and two identical 8'x10' rug subjects back to back on the same day. So I came in skeptical. The ALL CAPS subject plus the triple chevron ">>>" didn't help — it's visual shouting, and I've been shouted at all week.
- But once I got in, this is legitimately the most useful thing Wayfair has sent me in days. Conversation sets organized by seating capacity — 2, 3-4, 5, 6+ — is exactly how I actually shop this category. I have two outdoor spaces with two completely different footprints: one that needs a bistro setup, one that could handle a full sectional arrangement. Someone at Wayfair thought about how buyers filter, not just what to photograph. The "the reviews are in" section with visible star ratings lands as a real trust cue, not lorem ipsum. Clean hierarchy. No broken images.
- What kills the click is the absence of any offer. No percent off, no free shipping trigger, no member pricing. Pottery Barn's "heritage design" email this week gave me editorial tone AND a clear promotion window. CB2 creates urgency through scarcity ("BACK IN STOCK" — you know the item might be gone). This email gives me genuinely useful organization and then just... stops. I'll save it to my inbox folder for when I've measured the deck. I won't forward it to anyone.

## What's working

- **Seating-by-capacity navigation** — 2, 3-4, 5, 6+ is the mental model I actually use when shopping patio furniture. This is category-specific product intelligence, not a generic brand grid.
- **"The reviews are in" section** — visible star ratings alongside product images make this feel curated, not clearance-dumped. That's a legitimate trust signal.
- **Seasonal fit** — conversation sets in early July is exactly the right timing. This isn't a stale asset.
- **Clean render, readable hierarchy** — no overlapping text, no broken images, sections scan clearly. After this week's Wayfair batch, that counts for something.

## What's weak

- **No offer or price signal anywhere visible** — no discount badge, no free shipping threshold, no member pricing. Without a pull to act now, the whole browse becomes a save-for-later.
- **Subject ALL CAPS + ">>>"** — the triple chevron and all-caps combination looks like it belongs in a spam folder. Pottery Barn's "New arrivals inspired by heritage design" landed quietly and hit better. Lowercase or title case would have less noise.
- **"Tried & True" framing is undefined** — bestsellers? Customer-rated? Durability-tested? I genuinely don't know what criterion earned that label, which makes it land as marketing filler.
- **App download push at the bottom** — tacking "Get Exclusive offers in the App" at the bottom of a furniture browse email is conversion theater. If I had wanted the app, I'd have it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one offer** — free shipping above a threshold or a visible member discount would give me a reason to click today. Even CB2's stock-scarcity play creates more urgency than this does.
- 2. **Lowercase the subject, cut the chevrons** — the category is interesting enough to speak for itself. The all-caps shouting undermines it.
- 3. **Make "Tried & True" earn its headline spot** — replace with something that tells me the curation logic: "Most-reviewed," "4.5-star+ rated," "Bestsellers by size."
- 4. **Remove or relegate the app CTA** — if it must be there, strip it to a footer line. Don't let it interrupt the browse.
