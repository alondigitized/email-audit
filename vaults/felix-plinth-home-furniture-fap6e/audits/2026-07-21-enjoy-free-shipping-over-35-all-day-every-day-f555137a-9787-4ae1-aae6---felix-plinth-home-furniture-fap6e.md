# Enjoy FREE shipping over $35. All day, every day.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- Three. That's how many times I've now seen a variant of the "free shipping over $35, always" message from Wayfair in less than a week — and this is the second send with this subject line verbatim. Verbatim. Word for word. Not a remix, not a seasonal version — the exact same string of characters. I flagged it when I first saw it and here it is again, apparently none the wiser for the feedback.
- Opening the email, the inside is a kitchen-sink situation: "New Price Cuts" hero → "These Top Steals Are Going Fast" subhead → Open Box Deals module → a self-aware "SALES, SALES & YES, MORE SALES" banner → a benefits icon grid → an app download QR code. Every section individually is defensible; together they produce a visual tied to no single purpose. Contrast that with Wayfair's own July 20 NIGHTSTANDS send — one word in the subject, one purpose in the email, 8/10 from me without hesitation. That's the same brand. Same week. The gap between those two executions is embarrassing.
- I'm not clicking. I'm not forwarding. The Open Box Deals module is the only thing in here that actually earns a second look — that section I'll give credit to. Everything else is noise dressed up in a red price-tag graphic.

## What's working

- **Open Box Deals** is the only module doing real work — returned, like-new product at a discount is a legitimate hook for someone buying at my scale across two renovation projects, and the copy ("before they're gone") gives it actual scarcity.
- **Wayfair Rewards** is visible and prominent — the $35 icon tells me I'm a member, not a stranger, which is a small but real trust cue.
- **Family of Brands footer** is useful — AllModern, Birch Lane, Joss & Main, Perigold in one line reminds me that Perigold exists when I'm in an elevated-spend mindset. A quiet upsell that earns its real estate.
- **No render bugs** — the layout is clean, the images load, the buttons are tappable. Low bar, but met.

## What's weak

- **Recycled subject line, word for word** — I've already audited this subject. Running it again two days later without even reordering the words suggests no one is reviewing open-rate data before hitting send.
- **"SALES, SALES & YES, MORE SALES / Snag 'Em ASAP"** — this self-mocking copy style works exactly once per quarter. Here it reads like the brand is yelling at me because it has nothing specific to say. "Snag 'Em ASAP" is not a CTA, it's a slogan from a flash-sale era that Wayfair has otherwise outgrown.
- **No single product or category hook in the hero** — Pottery Barn's "New arrivals—classic, comfortable, collected" (9/10 from me last week) communicated a complete mood in five words. "New Price Cuts" communicates exactly zero.
- **"whoa! Scores in Your Style"** — visual and copy register at least ten years younger than my inbox habits. That module belongs in a back-to-dorm campaign, not a send reaching someone coordinating two renovation budgets.
- **Flat visual hierarchy throughout** — "New Price Cuts," "Going Fast," "Open Box," and the sale banner all fight at the same visual weight. My eye drifts and then bounces out.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Promote Open Box to the hero** — it's the most distinctive offer in the email and the only thing with real scarcity. Swap it to the top slot, give it a specific number of deals ("200+ open box finds, going fast"), and let the rest of the email breathe below it.
- 2. **Kill "SALES, SALES & YES, MORE SALES"** — it wastes a full module and undermines every other section's credibility. Replace with a curated category grid: lighting, accent seating, rugs — three things a renovator actually wants to browse.
- 3. **Surface Wayfair Professional** — there's a Wayfair Professional icon buried in the benefits grid. I'm buying for two properties; if I saw "Trade pricing, bulk orders, contractor discounts" as a standalone CTA, that's a real conversion moment. Right now it's a logo I almost missed.
- 4. **One primary CTA, not four** — "Shop Now," "Snag 'Em ASAP," "Get Exclusive Deals on the App" and social icons are all competing. Pick one and make it count.
- 5. **Retire this subject line** — it had one run, the performance data exists, move on.
