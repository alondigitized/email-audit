# FREE design help for any room & any project
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- Thirteen. And I want to be really clear that number is doing work here before I say anything else positive about this email. I've been tracking the Pottery Barn streak since Tuesday and this is the thirteenth consecutive send. The fact that I'm even slightly relieved to see "design services" instead of a flash sale or a new bedding pattern tells you how low the bar has gotten — but it also tells you this email picked the right moment to exist.
- Here's the honest truth though: I'm renovating a primary and a second home simultaneously, and free interior design help is genuinely the thing I should be getting from a brand I buy from. I've spent enough with Pottery Barn that they know who I am. This email is directionally correct — room-specific CTAs, a clean hero, a service pitch that has actual utility for someone in my situation. The "Recommended Just For You" product section at the bottom even tries to close the loop. But there's a structural problem: this same concept sent on email five in this streak would have had me booking an appointment. On email thirteen, I'm mostly just relieved it isn't another 40% off rug promotion.
- Would I click? Yes, actually — but grudgingly. I would not forward this. A friend would have to already be looking for design help and on my text thread asking about it for me to send it over.

## What's working

- **The service offer itself is the right bet for me.** Free design consultations are exactly what someone managing two renovation projects simultaneously needs. Article gave me beautiful product photography last week; Pottery Barn is offering me something Article can't match — human expertise.
- **Room-by-room CTA structure is smart.** Breaking it out by bedroom, living room, dining room, and entry lets me self-select into the room I'm stuck on right now rather than routing to a generic homepage.
- **"Book a Free Appointment" button copy is specific.** It's a verb plus a noun, it tells me exactly what happens when I click, and it doesn't say "Discover" or "Explore" like CB2 keeps doing.
- **Visual hierarchy lands correctly.** The eye goes hero → room category → CTA without having to hunt. Clean reading order on what appears to be a well-structured mobile layout.
- **Loyalty block at the bottom acknowledges I'm a known customer.** Seeing rewards math there is a small but real signal that they know I have purchase history with them.

## What's weak

- **Cadence is the elephant in the room.** Thirteen emails in roughly a week is not a cadence — it's noise. This exact same email concept actually ran earlier in the streak (I scored it around the free design services send I logged for June 10), so it either is a repeat or is extremely close to one. Wayfair is at 49 emails total and even they've managed to throw in an "open box" concept to break the pattern. Pottery Barn is just hammering the same beats.
- **"Any room & any project" is too broad.** The body does the room-specific work but the subject line promises everything to everyone, which reads as nobody being targeted. If they know I bought a bedroom set, lead with bedroom. If they know I browsed rugs, lead with living room.
- **No time pressure anywhere.** The offer is free design help — that should have some urgency attached to it. Is there limited appointment availability? Is this running through a certain date? None of that is communicated, so there's no reason to book today versus someday.
- **"Recommended Just For You" section undercuts the service pitch.** If you're selling me on speaking with a designer, why are you pivoting to product tiles right below? It reads like the email can't decide whether it's a service email or a product email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the frequency before anything else.** No single email fix rescues this when I'm seeing it at thirteenth-in-a-streak. The right intervention is suppressing design-service sends to people who already got this concept in the last 14 days.
- 2. **Subject line should be room-specific based on browse/purchase data.** If they know I've been looking at bedroom furniture, this subject should be "Free design help for your bedroom" — not "any room & any project."
- 3. **Add appointment scarcity or a deadline.** "Only a few slots left this week" or "Available through June 30" would give me an actual reason to click now.
- 4. **Kill the product tile section or move it post-CTA.** Let the design service be the entire ask. The product grid muddies the purpose.
- 5. **Swap or remove "Recommended Just For You" header** if the tiles aren't actually personalized to my purchase history — that phrase is a promise and if it shows generic bestsellers, it breaks trust fast.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your free design session is waiting`
- **Subject Alt B:** `We'll design the room you're stuck on — free`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Book a 1-on-1 with a Pottery Barn designer, no cost, any room`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Limited appointments available — let's tackle your hardest room first`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CRITICAL] PHP code injected into `<style>` block**
The following server-side PHP appears verbatim inside a CSS `<style>` tag:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
This is a template assembly error — a PHP snippet was concatenated into the wrong template slot. Most email clients silently discard invalid CSS, so this is unlikely to break rendering, but it confirms a build pipeline defect that should be investigated.

**[FAIL] 37 images missing alt text**
Every content image in both the body (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_08.jpg`, product images, footer slices) and several tracking pixels carry `alt=""` or no `alt` attribute. Images-off rendering (default in Outlook, common in corporate inboxes) produces a completely blank email.

**[FAIL] Two images served over HTTP**
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Mixed-content HTTP assets are blocked by default in Gmail, Apple Mail, and most modern clients over HTTPS connections. Both are footer/banner images from a 2022 campaign path — likely stale template carry-over.

**[WARN] Google Fonts `<link>` tag will be stripped**
`<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat&display=swap">` — Gmail, Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo strip external stylesheet `<link>` tags. Montserrat will not load in the majority of clients. System fallbacks (`verdana, arial`) apply.

**[WARN] Doctype mismatch**
Document declares `XHTML 1.0 Strict` but contains HTML5-incompatible patterns. Non-blocking in practice for email clients but signals a legacy template base.
