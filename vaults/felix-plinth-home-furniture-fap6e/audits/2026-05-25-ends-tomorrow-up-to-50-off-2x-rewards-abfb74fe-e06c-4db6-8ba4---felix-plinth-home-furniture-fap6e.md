# ENDS TOMORROW: Up to 50% off & 2x Rewards!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- Forty-eight. That's the C&B family tally since Sunday — and this is the fourth Crate & Kids send on the same calendar day, with a subject line *word-for-word identical* to one I already opened earlier today (the one I scored an 8). Whether this is a second blast to a different segment, a resend to non-openers, or a scheduling fumble, from my inbox it reads as one thing: they forgot they already sent this.
- But here's the detail that actually makes me put the email down: the subject says "ENDS TOMORROW" and the hero banner inside says **"ends today!"** That's not a minor inconsistency — that's two different deadlines in the same email, and when a brand builds its entire send around urgency, contradicting itself destroys the one lever it's pulling. I can't trust the deadline, so the urgency is theater.
- On pure execution, there's a competent email buried underneath all this. The sage green dresser photography is clean, the 50% off number is real, and Double Rewards is surfaced clearly — I always notice when a brand does the loyalty math in plain sight. But this is the kids sub-brand, and I am a 36-year-old guy renovating two adult homes. The crib and nursery content in the middle scroll lands like a mis-targeted ad. Mark as read, move on.

## What's working

- **50% off is a specific number** — it's in the hero, it's impossible to miss, and it's the kind of figure that earns a second look even when you're fatigued.
- **The sage green dresser photography is genuinely nice** — that piece reads as adult guest room as easily as kids room; strong product selection for the hero.
- **Double Rewards surfaced explicitly and early** — as someone who stacks loyalty points on top of rewards cards, I notice when a brand does this right instead of burying it.
- **Star ratings visible in the scroll** — for a furniture purchase, social proof matters; Pottery Barn does this well too and it works.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout at every viewport width, images loaded, nothing broken.

## What's weak

- **"ENDS TOMORROW" in the subject, "ends today!" in the body** — this is the single worst thing in the email; the urgency premise collapses the moment you scroll two inches.
- **Identical subject line to an email I opened from this same brand hours ago** — Wayfair's cabinet-pulls email stood out *because* I'd only seen it once; Crate & Kids has inoculated me.
- **Kids sub-brand, kids content, no kids in my household** — cribs and nursery blocks are not why I'm on a C&B list; Pottery Barn doesn't make me wade through infant furniture to find a side table.
- **The mid-email app push with a $500 sweepstakes** is the same playbook CB2 ran with "IT'S ALL ON THE APP" — I gave CB2 a 4 for that move; a $500 entry draw is not why I shop home furnishings.
- **Four sends in one day from one sub-brand** — Article managed all of Memorial Day week with three emails and each one felt intentional; this cadence feels like someone left a scheduler running.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the ends-today/ends-tomorrow contradiction before anything else ships.** One wrong deployment with two different deadlines is the kind of error that makes subscribers stop believing every future countdown.
- 2. **One send per day per sub-brand, maximum.** Frequency is the biggest single reason this scored below the earlier Crate & Kids sends today — each additional send cuts the signal-to-noise ratio in half.
- 3. **Segment adults/no-kids off the nursery and crib content blocks.** My purchase history on C&B is all adult furniture; a behavioral suppressant on crib and infant SKUs for non-parent buyers would lift click rates materially.
- 4. **Move the app download module to the footer.** The $500 sweepstakes hook in a full-bleed mid-email module is the same distraction CB2 used; it interrupts the purchase funnel rather than supporting it.
- 5. **If the deadline is real, make it unambiguous in both subject and hero** — "ends tonight at midnight CT" beats "ends tomorrow" in a subject and "ends today" in the body every time.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 50% off kids furniture — ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Double Rewards + half off: final hours, Crate & Kids`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Nursery, beds & storage — Memorial Day sale closes tonight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack your rewards through tonight. Up to 50% off.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Wrong `<title>` tag (confirmed):**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
This email is from the "Crate & Kids" sub-brand. Several email clients (notably Apple Mail, Outlook Web) surface the `<title>` to screen readers. This will announce the wrong brand name to assistive technology users.

**Unreplaced template placeholder comment (confirmed):**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
<!----> 
```
The comment `[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]` (note: typo — "IMPUT" should be "INPUT") was not removed before send. No font `@import` was inserted, which means either (a) the intended custom font was silently dropped, or (b) the template was not cleaned up. The empty `<!---->` immediately after suggests the insertion point was cleared but the marker comment was not.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Disables pinch-to-zoom on mobile. This is an accessibility failure (WCAG 1.4.4) for users who need to enlarge text. This pattern is common in email templates but should be flagged.

**Contradictory image `max-width` media queries:**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
The 768px rule (capping images at 600px) will always override the 640px rule (capping at 640px) on desktop, since both apply at ≥768px and cascade order favors the first rule. This means desktop images are constrained to 600px despite the 640px container. Likely unintentional — the 640px rule is dead code.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags:**
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
The combined declaration at the top covers all cases; the three individual declarations below are redundant. No functional breakage, but indicates template hygiene issues.

**No dark mode support:**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Dark mode rendering is explicitly disabled. Apple Mail (a major client for this demographic) will force-invert colors on `light`-only emails in some configurations, potentially breaking brand colors. Consider adding `light dark` and testing in Apple Mail dark mode.

**Global `line-height: 100%` override:**
```css
* { line-height: 100%; }
```
Applied via the universal selector. This collapses line height on all elements globally, which can cause text to overlap on multi-line strings in certain clients. It is overridden inline in many places, but any element that misses an explicit `line-height` inline style is at risk.
