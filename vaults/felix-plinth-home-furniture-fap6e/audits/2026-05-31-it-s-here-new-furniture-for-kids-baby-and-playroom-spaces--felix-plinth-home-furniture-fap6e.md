# It’s here: new furniture for kids, baby and playroom spaces!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-31**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-three. That's the Crate & Barrel family count in my inbox now, and I want to get that on the record before I say anything else — because context matters when evaluating a send from the kids sub-brand. Crate & Kids is a distinct catalog; I know exactly who it is. My issue isn't brand confusion, it's relevance routing. My persona here doesn't have kids explicitly in the frame, and this email leads hard with a soft-pink teen bedroom and nursery-adjacent vibes. Nothing in the hero signals "renovating guy who just needs a dresser for the second home's spare room." The demographic coding is feminine from the first scroll, and the offer structure doesn't do the work to pull me back in.
- No discount is visible above the fold. The hierarchy is functional — hero image, some room shots, product grid — but the email never gives me a reason to act right now. Article ran a Memorial Day "up to 40% off ends Monday" that had teeth. CB2's "free living room redesign" last week gave me something concrete to hold. This one just announces that new things exist. Great. They've been announcing new things twenty-three times.
- Mark as read. I'm not forwarding this to anyone — I don't have a friend who's shopping nursery right now — and I'm not clicking.

## What's working

- **Sub-brand clarity is correct.** "Crate & Kids" sender name tells me immediately which catalog this is. I didn't have to decode the sender domain.
- **Visual hierarchy is functional.** Hero image lands first, product vignettes follow in a logical scroll. No chaos.
- **Photography quality is high.** The room scenes look like actual editorial, not stock. The natural-wood dresser section especially reads like something I'd pin.
- **No render bugs visible.** Clean layout, images loaded, no overlapping text — technically sound.

## What's weak

- **Feminine coding on the hero is a mismatch.** Dusty rose bedding, soft mauve palette, clearly styled for a girl's room — I don't see myself in this email, and I'm the one who just ordered a new rug from Wayfair for a second home guest room.
- **No offer above the fold.** Compare to the Le Creuset/KitchenAid email from earlier today (same family!) which led with a clear discount hook — this one just announces "new arrivals." That's not enough for send number twenty-three.
- **Subject overloads the scope.** Kids, baby, AND playroom in one subject line is three separate purchase occasions that have nothing to do with each other. Pottery Barn's "All bedding ships free: Start layering" was eight words and a complete value proposition. This is fifteen words and a catalog section list.
- **No urgency mechanism.** "It's here" is arrival language, not closing language. Nothing drives me to act today vs. next weekend vs. never.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** Twenty-three Crate & Barrel family emails is genuinely exhausting. Even a good email lands worse in this context.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the list by lifecycle stage.** If I'm not in the nursery buyer cohort — no baby registry, no recent kids-room purchase — this should never land in my inbox. CB2's "free living room redesign" targeted me by room type. Crate & Kids has enough purchase data to not carpet-bomb the full C&B file with every kids drop.
- 2. **Lead with a specific offer or a specific product.** Pick the hero SKU — the natural-wood dresser mid-scroll is the most broadly appealing piece — and build the subject around it. "New: the Acacia dresser kids outgrow last" is a headline. "New furniture for kids, baby and playroom spaces" is a category tab.
- 3. **Add a pricing or shipping hook.** Pottery Barn leads with "ships free" and I notice it every time. Even "free assembly on select cribs" gives me a functional reason to click now.
- 4. **Reduce send cadence or create an explicit family.** A clear "Crate & Kids" monthly digest would land better than fragmented drops every few days.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **`<title>` brand mismatch** — `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` but sender is "Crate & Kids." Some clients display the `<title>` in tab labels or accessibility trees; this should read "Crate & Kids."

- **CSS media query ordering bug** — Two conflicting desktop breakpoints appear back-to-back:
  ```css
  @media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
  @media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
  ```
  On screens ≥ 768px both queries match; the later `640px` rule wins (cascade order), so the `600px` cap at the 768px breakpoint is dead code. Intended desktop image max-width is never enforced.

- **`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — Prevents user zoom. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Should be removed or replaced with `user-scalable=yes`.

- **`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on wildcard selector** — `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}` disables system-level text scaling for all elements. Should be `100%`, not `none`. Also applied redundantly on both `html` and `*`.

- **Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags** — The combined tag (`telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no`) is followed by three additional individual tags for `date`, `address`, and `telephone`. Redundant; no functional harm, but signals template slop.

- **Alt text on images** — Cannot audit from truncated source.
