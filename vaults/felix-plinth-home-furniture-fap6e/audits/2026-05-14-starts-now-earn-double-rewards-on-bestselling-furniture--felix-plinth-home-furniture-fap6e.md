# Starts now! Earn Double Rewards on bestselling furniture →
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- I know my catalogs. The sender says "Crate & Kids" right in the from line — so before I even open this, I'm already half-checked out. I'm tearing out walls in two houses right now, neither of which belongs to anyone under four feet tall. The Double Rewards angle genuinely caught my eye in the subject — I optimize for points like it's a sport — but the moment I landed in the body and saw the nursery vignettes and blush tones, I felt the classic brand-routing confusion Crate & Barrel always pulls on me. This should have gone to people with a Crate & Kids account, not to my main Crate & Barrel rewards address.
- That said, the email is clean. No broken images, no overlapping text, the hero bedroom shot at the top actually looked adult and editorial enough that I kept scrolling for a second. Memorial Day timing is right — this is exactly when I'm placing furniture orders. But once I registered the kids brand, I'd archive it without clicking. If I have any nursery items on my list someday, sure. Not today.
- Would I click? No. Would I forward to a friend? Maybe if I had a friend who just had a baby and is also a Crate loyalist — but it's not a reflex forward.

## What's working

- **Double Rewards in the subject is perfectly tuned for me** — I track loyalty math, and a 2x earn event on furniture during a renovation is exactly the hook that makes me open.
- **Hero bedroom photography is genuinely beautiful** — the styling in that first shot is editorial enough to hold my eye; it doesn't immediately scream "kids brand."
- **Memorial Day peg is well-timed** — mid-May is when I'm making furniture commitments before summer delivery windows close.
- **Layout is clean and render-bug-free** — text doesn't overlap, images load, hierarchy is easy to scan.
- **Loyalty benefits are right up top** — doesn't make me hunt for the offer; I see it immediately.

## What's weak

- **Brand routing is wrong** — "Crate & Kids" in the sender name is a hard mismatch for someone who's never bought a crib. Segmentation should exclude accounts with zero kids-category purchase history.
- **The email is long and unfocused** — after the hero, it splits into multiple product categories with separate CTAs; I lost the thread fast.
- **No end date on the Double Rewards** — "Starts now" tells me it's live but gives me zero reason to act today vs. next Tuesday, which means I'll procrastinate and forget.
- **No specific furniture item is featured** — I renovate; I would click on a specific bed frame or sofa that's actually on sale. A generic "shop furniture" CTA is friction disguised as simplicity.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Route this to Crate & Barrel proper for non-kids buyers** — or at minimum, suppress Crate & Kids sends to anyone whose order history is adult-category only. This is the single highest-impact fix.
- 2. **Add an expiration date** — "Earn Double Rewards through Monday, May 27" gives me a reason to act; "Starts now" gives me none.
- 3. **Feature one hero product** — pick a bestselling sofa or dining table with the rewards math shown inline ("$1,200 sofa = 2,400 points = $24 back"). That's the email I click.
- 4. **Tighten the email to two sections** — hero offer + one category grid, not six. The scroll depth is working against click-through.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Earn 2x Rewards on furniture — Memorial Day, through May 27`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Double points on every furniture order this weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stack points on the pieces you've been waiting to buy.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Memorial Day sale starts now — bestsellers qualify for double rewards.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Conflicting `max-width` media queries for images:**

```css

@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```

The `640px` breakpoint appears after the `768px` rule in the stylesheet. Because both match on desktop viewports and specificity is equal, the 640px declaration wins via cascade — images cap at 640px on desktop, not 600px.
