# Proof onyx belongs in every room
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-15**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one actually stopped my scroll for a second — that onyx-and-walnut round table top with the flowers is a genuinely nice hero shot, and "Onyx & Walnut" as a collection name has more personality than the usual CB2 subject-line word salad. But then I hit "Designed by Studio Anansi" with a full designer bio and headshot, and that's where CB2 loses me — I don't care who designed it, I care whether the console fits my hallway and what it costs. This is the fourth-ish CB2/Crate & Barrel-family email in my inbox this stretch (I flagged the "family snooze" thing on the CB2 sale email from 8/14 too), and Rio is a genuinely new collection at least, so it's not a total repeat like the Crate & Kids nursery emails keep being.
- I'd scroll through this one on my phone out of actual curiosity about the coffee table, but I'm not clicking to buy anything today — there's no price visible until you get way down to the "More for you" grid, and the whole top two-thirds is mood/brand storytelling, not a shoppable moment. I wouldn't forward it to a friend, but I might screenshot the Rio dining table for my own reno folder. Compare that to the CB2 "Floor Sample Sale" email yesterday which at least had a concrete in-store CTA (even if a dumb one for me) — this is prettier but vaguer.

## What's working

- The hero image (onyx table detail, dramatic dark walnut background) is genuinely the best-looking shot I've seen from this brand family this week — better than the Article "editorial mode" send that just felt like filler.
- "ONLY AT CB2" under the intro copy is a real exclusivity hook — that's a legit reason to look here vs. Crate & Barrel or Article.
- Multiple clear product shots (console, coffee table, dining table, side tables) with named SKUs, so I can tell exactly what's in the collection.
- The "$5,000 Refresh" designer sweepstakes block reappears here too, consistent with the 8/13 email — at least CB2 is being consistent about pushing that program instead of one-and-done.
- "More for you" grid at the bottom finally has actual prices, which is the first real buying signal in the whole email.

## What's weak

- Zero pricing on the entire Rio collection above the fold — I have to scroll past a designer bio and two more product shots before any dollar figure shows up anywhere.
- The "Designed by Studio Anansi" bio block is dead weight for me — I'm mid-renovation on two houses, not shopping for design pedigree. Article at least skips this kind of filler.
- Three "SHOP THE RIO COLLECTION" CTAs but they all presumably land on the same collection page, not a specific product — I want a "Shop the Console" or "Shop the Dining Table" link next to the piece I'm actually looking at.
- The onyx material — the whole subject line's hook — never gets a callout on care/durability/sealing, which matters a lot for a stone tabletop in a house with kids or a dog. Pottery Barn's "Made to Match" email at least ties product to a real use-case.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price directly under each Rio piece (console, coffee table, dining table) instead of forcing me to a landing page to find out — that's the single biggest drop-off point in this email.
- 2. Cut or shrink the designer bio block — move it to a "the story" link instead of eating vertical scroll real estate with a headshot.
- 3. Make each product CTA specific ("Shop the Console" / "Shop the Dining Table") instead of three repeats of "Shop the Rio Collection."
- 4. Add one line about onyx care/sealing — that's the actual objection stopping me from clicking on a stone table with two renovations and normal household wear in play.
- 5. Move the "More for you" priced grid up higher — it's the only part of the email that gave me a real number, and it's buried at the very bottom.
