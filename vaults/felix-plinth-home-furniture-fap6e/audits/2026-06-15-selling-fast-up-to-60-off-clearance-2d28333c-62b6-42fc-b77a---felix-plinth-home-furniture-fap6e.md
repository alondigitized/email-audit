# Selling fast: up to 60% off clearance!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- Fifty-nine. That's the C&B family counter now, and I'm leading with it because this one lands on the same calendar day as the "New, bestselling & it ships free" C&K I already reviewed — two Crate & Kids sends before dinnertime on June 15. My inbox is doing that thing where I can't tell if I'm a known customer or a warm body on a suppression list that never ran.
- The clearance hook is real — 60% off is real money when you're furnishing two houses simultaneously. I noticed it. But Crate & Kids is a kids brand, and the hero image makes no attempt to obscure that: giant yellow daisies, botanical watercolor styling, the kind of palette that says "nursery accent wall" not "primary bedroom refresh." The "gear up for summer" section below the fold shows kids bedding. The product tiles are a crib and what looks like a stroller. If I have kids, this is relevant. If the brand assumes I have kids because I clicked a C&B baby registry link eighteen months ago, that's a segmentation guess, not a relationship.
- I am not clicking. I'm filing it in the Pinterest folder — which is where C&K clearance links go to die when I don't have an immediate need — and moving on. I would not forward this one. If I were at CB2's level of email discipline right now, I'd have already archived it.

## What's working

- **The clearance offer is front and center.** "Up to 60% off + new markdowns" is in the hero, above the fold, before I do any scrolling. No treasure hunt required.
- **"Shop Clearance" is a clean, unambiguous CTA.** It does the job — verb, noun, action. Not "Discover the magic of savings."
- **The 38-character subject line is mobile-ready.** "Selling fast: up to 60% off clearance!" fits in the preview pane without getting guillotined. The urgency framing ("selling fast") is more honest than Wayfair's perennial "LAST CALL" that never ends.
- **The summer section has at least some seasonal coherence.** "Gear up for summer" is on-calendar in mid-June. Pottery Barn's June 14 70%-off send did this better with lifestyle hero shots, but at least C&K is trying.
- **No visible render bugs.** The layout holds together — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum leaking through.

## What's weak

- **Two C&K emails in one day is a cadence problem, not a clearance problem.** This one is making the same offer to the same inbox that already saw "New, bestselling & it ships free" from the exact same address today. One of these should have been suppressed or spaced out.
- **The visual language reads female and young — and not in a subtle way.** Yellow watercolor daisies, pastel nursery tones, the whole hero palette. I'm a 36-year-old guy buying furniture for two houses. I'm not the invisible demographic here, but I might as well be.
- **"Up to 60% off" is doing a lot of heavy lifting without showing me what specifically is discounted.** CB2's "100+ new items added to Warehouse Sale" gave me a count, a category anchor, a reason to believe. This says "clearance" and leaves me to go hunting.
- **No price anchoring in the product tiles.** The three product tiles in the "we love these for you" section show items with what appear to be prices, but I can't verify a before/after comparison in the render. No "Was $899, now $359" is a missed conversion moment.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Don't send two C&K emails in one day.** This is the most impactful change and it costs nothing. Batch the clearance news with the new arrivals, or stagger them by 48 hours minimum. The cadence is eroding trust faster than any creative decision.
- 2. **Anchor a specific product in the hero.** Pick one clearance item — "The Marin Crib, now $299" — and build the hero around it. Article does this every time. One specific product lands harder than a category promise.
- 3. **Add a sell-through urgency hook with a real signal.** "Only 14 left at this price" is more credible than "selling fast." "Selling fast" is marketing language. A real stock count is a real reason to click today.
- 4. **Segment the clearance by what I've browsed.** If I've been looking at nursery furniture, show me nursery clearance. If I've been in the bedding section, lead with bedding. Right now this feels like a bulk blast to the full list.
- 5. **Make the app section earn its place.** The app promo feels bolted on at the bottom, disconnected from the clearance narrative. Either tie it to an app-exclusive price or cut it.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 60% off — these won't last the week`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns on furniture, bedding, and gear — shop before sizes sell out`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Crib, bedding, gear — 60% off clearance is live right now`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta prevents pinch-zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks user zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[MEDIUM] Conflicting desktop `img` max-width rules**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
Both rules fire on screens ≥ 768px; the 600px rule wins due to cascade order, making the 640px rule dead code. On 640–767px viewports (some tablets), images are capped at 640px even inside a 600px container — risks horizontal scroll or overflow. These breakpoints need to be reconciled.

**[LOW] Overly aggressive global text-size-adjust reset**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
The `*` selector applies `none` to all elements, including body text. The safer value is `100%`, applied to `body` or `html` only. The `html` block later correctly sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` again, duplicating the rule.

**[LOW] Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
One combined meta and three individual metas declare overlapping suppression rules. Redundant but harmless in most clients; adds unnecessary head bloat.
