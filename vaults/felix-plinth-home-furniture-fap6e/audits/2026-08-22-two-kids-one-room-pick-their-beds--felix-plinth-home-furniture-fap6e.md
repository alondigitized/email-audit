# Two kids, one room? Pick their beds →
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-22**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids again — third time this week they've hit my inbox, and this time it's actually somewhat relevant to the reno since I've got a real bunk-bed situation brewing in one of the guest rooms at the second place. "Two kids, one room" is a genuinely specific hook, not the vague design-story fluff they sent Thursday. Problem is, I don't have kids, so the second this loaded I clocked it as noise — I'm scrolling for the "we love these for you" section with the dresser and desk that could actually work in an adult space, and everything above it is toddler bunk beds and backpack bundles.
- The layout is a wall of near-identical product shots stacked forever — loft bed, storage bed, bunk bed, another bunk bed — with almost no differentiation in the hero images themselves. Compare that to CB2's "Future Heirlooms Under $500" send, which at least varied the styling per shot. This just reads as the same room photographed four times.
- Not clicking, not forwarding — I'd mark as read and move on. If I actually had kids sharing a room this might land, but as a childless-by-context subscriber to the wrong sub-brand, it's scroll-and-dismiss.

## What's working

- "Two kids, one room: find their sleep solution" is a concrete, specific hook — better than the vague "Can't figure it out?" designer-help send from earlier this week.
- The "Starting at $24" price anchor on the shared-spaces module gives at least one real number to grab onto.
- The "we love these for you" grid near the bottom has genuinely adult-adjacent pieces (a glider chair, a dresser, a crib) that could tie back to the second-home nursery-ish room, which is the one part of this email actually built for a browsing parent, not a first-time shopper.
- Clean, consistent Crate & Kids visual branding — no render bugs, images all loaded fine.

## What's weak

- I don't have kids — this is the second time Crate & Kids has fired at me this week assuming I'm shopping a nursery/kids room, and I'm not.
- The whole top half is bunk-bed-after-bunk-bed with barely any visual variation — loft bed, storage loft bed, hampshire bunk bed, finn bunk bed — it blurs together fast.
- Multiple "exclusive!" tags stacked on different beds dilute the word until it means nothing.
- The $1000 sweepstakes banner mid-email ("TheDeskDesk... enter for a chance to win $1000") feels bolted on and off-brand-voice, like a different marketing team inserted it.
- Bottom third turns into a grab-bag: a credit card pitch, a design-service app pitch, then social icons — three different asks competing for the same scroll.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment me out of Crate & Kids entirely, or at minimum lead with the crossover pieces (dresser, glider, storage furniture) that work in a non-kid room — that's the only inventory here I'd actually consider.
- 2. Cut the bunk bed count from four to two and differentiate them visually — different room styling, not just different beds in the same white box.
- 3. Pick one CTA for the whole email — either the sweepstakes or the beds, not both mid-scroll.
- 4. Move the "we love these for you" adult-friendly grid up near the top for subscribers like me who've never clicked kids' furniture.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Shared room, separate beds: bunk & loft picks`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The bunk bed lineup for sibling rooms`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New storage beds, bunk beds, and space-saving finds`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Starting at $24 — furniture built for shared kids' rooms`
