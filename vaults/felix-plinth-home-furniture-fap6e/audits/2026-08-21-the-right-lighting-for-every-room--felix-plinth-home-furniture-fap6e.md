# The right lighting for every room
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-21**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "Layering With Light" — this is Pottery Barn's fourteenth-ish email to me this week, and I've now officially lost count of how many lighting-adjacent sends they've run in the past 48 hours (yesterday's was "Bright Ideas: Lighting Upgrades" at a lackluster 4/10, the day before that was the lodge bedroom send). This one is actually a step up structurally — it's organized by fixture type (pendants, chandeliers, table lamps, sconces) instead of by room, which at least reads like a coherent lighting-education piece rather than a random grab bag.
- But here's the thing: there's no offer anywhere in the first six screens. Just moody lifestyle shots and "Shop Now" buttons. I'm mid-renovation on two houses and lighting is genuinely on my list, but I need a reason to act today, not a mood board. Crate & Barrel's recliner-sofa email ("the recline you love") at least had a clear single hero product; this one spreads me across four categories with zero pricing signal until I hit rewards math way down at the bottom.
- Would I click? Maybe, on the table lamps section since I actually need bedside lamps for the second home — but I'm not opening the app for this. Forward to a friend? No, it's not a real offer, it's a catalog page in email form.

## What's working

- Splitting lighting into pendant/chandelier/lamp/sconce sections is a smarter organizing principle than yesterday's room-by-room dump — feels more like a buying guide than filler.
- The dining room chandelier shot over a wood table looks like something out of my actual second-home reno — warm, not overly styled.
- "Recommended Just For You" with the curtain rod, dining table, and platform bed feels like real personalization, not just more lighting.
- The rewards callout (10% back, Affirm financing) is genuinely useful copy for a Millennial paying for two renovations at once, even if it's buried too low.

## What's weak

- Zero discount, price, or urgency anywhere above the fold — after CB2's "Floor Sample Sale" and Wayfair's broken-but-at-least-offer-driven "FOUND: Bar Stools," this feels like the one Pottery Barn send this week with nothing to actually act on.
- Four "Shop Now" CTAs stacked identically with no visual hierarchy telling me which one they actually want me to click.
- The Design Crew Sweepstakes and Halloween Shop blocks feel bolted on and unrelated to lighting — classic same-email grab-bag syndrome.
- This is the fourth lighting-flavored email from PB this week; the cadence is genuinely fatiguing and this one doesn't differentiate itself with an offer the way it needed to.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one concrete offer — a %-off on lighting or free shipping on fixtures over $X — right under the hero. Nothing here beats CB2's floor-sample sale on clarity of "why click today."
- 2. Pick ONE hero product (the dining chandelier is the strongest visual) and make that the single primary CTA instead of four parallel "Shop Now" buttons competing for attention.
- 3. Move the 10% rewards callout up near the top — that's the strongest reason-to-buy in the whole email and it's buried after two sweepstakes/seasonal blocks.
- 4. Cut the Halloween Shop block entirely from a lighting email — it dilutes the theme and adds another scroll before I get to anything useful.

- 55 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the automated HTTP probe (by design — redirect domains aren't followed), so live-link validity for `click.e.potterybarn.com`, `clickcp.potterybarn.com`, and `d.turn.com` destinations is unconfirmed and should be manually verified before send.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
