# The NYC apartment everyone covets
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-11**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is the fourth CB2/Crate & Barrel family email in my inbox this week and the first one that's actually about *design services*, not a countdown clock. "COLLECTED, NOT EXPECTED" as the hero, paired with an actual NYC Upper West Side apartment shoot — that's a decent hook for someone mid-renovation on two places like me. But then it just... keeps going. Design services pitch, then a full product grid (sofas, dining, bedroom, lighting, rugs, mirrors), then a fall sale banner at the bottom with "up to 30% off" buried after I've already scrolled through what feels like eight sections.
- The $5,000 refresh sweepstakes up top is a nice scroll-stopper, but it's competing with the design-services CTA, the category grid CTAs, and the fall sale CTA — by the time I hit the bottom I've seen five different "book/shop/sign up" buttons and I genuinely don't know which one CB2 wants me to click first.
- Would I click? Maybe on "SOFAS & SECTIONALS" since I'm actually shopping that category for the second home. Would I forward it? No — this is a "keep it in the folder for later" email, not a "look what I found" email. Compared to yesterday's "THE FALL SALE" send (8/10, big clean hero, no clutter), this one is trying to do too much in one shot.

## What's working

- The hero apartment photography feels aspirational and specific — "an iconic Upper West Side building" is a nice real-world anchor, not generic lifestyle stock.
- No countdown clock this time — a genuine break from the "00:17:19:18" fatigue I called out in three prior CB2 sends this week.
- The category grid (Sofas, Dining, Bedroom, Lighting, Rugs, Mirrors & Decor) is clean and photographed consistently — good for someone furnishing two homes at once who needs to browse by room.
- Rewards program gets a real callout at the bottom ("Earn 10% Back... on purchases at CB2 and Crate & Barrel") — I'm a rewards-card guy, so seeing that spelled out instead of buried is a plus.

## What's weak

- Way too many competing CTAs — design consult, category shopping, and a 30% off sale all stacked in one email dilutes all three asks.
- The fall sale — the one offer with real numbers attached — is crammed at the very bottom, below six other sections. Crate & Barrel's tablescaping email at least led with actual product up top; this one makes me work for the discount.
- "2 WAYS TO MEET" (in-store or online design consult) is a nice idea but visually it's just another beige banner that blends into the design-services block above it — no real hierarchy break.
- Small nitpick: "WIN A $5,000 REFRESH" sweepstakes copy up top and the "FALL SALE up to 30% off" at the bottom are both big swings but they're never reconciled — is this a sale email or a design-services email? I can't tell what CB2 actually wants from me.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into two sends — one for the design-services/sweepstakes angle, one for the fall sale — instead of merging them. This is the single biggest lift: right now neither offer gets the real estate it needs.
- 2. Move the 30% off sale banner up near the top if it's staying in this email at all — don't make me scroll past six sections to find the actual discount.
- 3. Pick one primary CTA per email. If this is the "design services" send, cut the category product grid down to 2-3 rooms max instead of six.
- 4. Lean harder into the reward math — "Earn 10% Back" deserves a bigger callout given how much CB2/C&B know I'm spending across both stores right now.
