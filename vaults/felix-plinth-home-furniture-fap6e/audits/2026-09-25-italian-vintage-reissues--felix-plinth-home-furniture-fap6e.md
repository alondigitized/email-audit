# Italian vintage reissues
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-25**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "Time will be kind to this" — CB2 finally sent me something with an actual point of view instead of another "% off sitewide" blast. This is a designer-chair roundup built around vintage reissues — 1954 Italian, 1986 Danish, "Design Legend" call-outs on two of them — and for a guy mid-renovation on two houses, that's the kind of copy that makes me stop and actually read the names instead of just scrolling for the sale banner. Aldine, Meda, Gwyneth, Loop, Fitz, Muir — six chairs, six distinct silhouettes, styled moodily against that dark wood/brass backdrop CB2 always uses.
- That said, it's a long scroll of near-identical accent chair shots before you hit anything transactional, and by chair number four I was skimming captions rather than reading them. The Upholstery Event banner (25% off) is buried after all six chairs, which is a swing and a miss structurally — I've already seen that same sale headline from CB2 twice this week ("The Upholstery Event is on," 9/25, 7/10, and the app-first take on 9/24, 6/10), so leading with six full-price chairs before the discount felt backwards.
- Would I click? Probably, on the Loop or Fitz chair specifically — they're genuinely good-looking pieces and different enough from what Pottery Barn and Crate & Barrel have been showing me. Would I forward it? Maybe to my wife with one chair linked, not the whole email. Not deleting, not archiving to "maybe later" — this one's a keeper for the swipe file.

## What's working

- The "vintage reissue" framing (1954 Italian, 1986 Danish) gives this actual narrative weight instead of generic product-grid filler — nobody else in my inbox this week (Pottery Barn, Wayfair, Article) is doing provenance storytelling like this.
- Each chair has a distinct style tag ("'70s Inspired," "Art Deco Moment," "California Midcentury") which helps me self-sort by taste fast.
- Photography is consistent and moody — same warm wood/brass set across all six shots, so it reads as one cohesive collection, not a stitched-together grid.
- "MORE FOR YOU" personalized module at the bottom (bed frame, dining chair, side table) is a nice touch that at least gestures at knowing my browsing history.
- Loyalty/rewards line at the very bottom ("Earn 10% back" on CB2/Crate & Barrel/CB2 Kids purchases) is relevant to me since I stack rewards cards — glad it's there, wish it were higher up.

## What's weak

- Six nearly-identical chair blocks in a row with zero visual break is a lot of scroll before any offer shows up — Article's "3 Steps" entryway email (6/10) at least varies its layout every section.
- No prices anywhere on the vintage reissue chairs — for a "which one should I actually buy" decision, that's a real gap given how price-conscious I've become chasing rewards.
- The 25% Upholstery Event banner is disconnected from the chairs above it — none of the six chairs shown are explicitly tied to that sale, so I can't tell if my favorite (Loop) is even discounted.
- This is now the third CB2 email in under 48 hours hitting my inbox (this one, plus the two Upholstery Event sends 9/24-9/25) — cadence fatigue is real, my thumb is getting faster on the "read" tap.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the chair lineup from six to three or four and move the Upholstery Event banner up top or interleave "on sale" badges directly on the chairs that qualify — right now the discount and the discovery content live in two disconnected halves of the email.
- 2. Add prices (or "from $X") under each chair name — I shouldn't have to click through six times to price-compare.
- 3. Space out sends — three CB2 emails in two days is pushing me toward the unsubscribe, not the buy button.
- 4. Tie the "MORE FOR YOU" personalized rail earlier in the email, right after the hero, so it doesn't get lost after ten chair/sale sections.
