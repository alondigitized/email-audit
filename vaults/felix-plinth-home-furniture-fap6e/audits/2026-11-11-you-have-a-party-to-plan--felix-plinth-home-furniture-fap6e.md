# You have a party to plan
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-11**
## Executive summary

- This is a different CB2 send, and I'm glad. Since 10/30 I've been looking at the same "Long Weekend Sale" banner, and this email still opens with it. There's a countdown (01:06:23:35) and a SHOP THE SALE button. But under it the email actually changes. "It's dinner party season" over a moody burnt-orange dining photo is the first CB2 creative in two weeks that made me slow down. The free design appointment pitch fits my situation. I'm renovating two houses and could use a designer who isn't charging me.
- The rest wears out its welcome. The design pitch shows up twice, with two near-identical "Book your free design appointment" buttons. Then the Double Rewards block repeats what the 11/09 and 11/10 sends already told me. That's the sixteenth or seventeenth CB2 email in about two weeks. I'd click the design appointment and skip everything else. I might forward it to my partner, mostly for the dining room photos.

## What's working

- The "Dinner Party Season" hero is seasonal and lifestyle-led. It feels less like the sixteenth "up to 50% off" in a row.
- The free design services angle fits a two-house renovation. It's a service offer, not another discount.
- The tonal palette (rust, walnut, caramel) is cohesive. It's the kind of thing I'd save to my Pinterest-adjacent folder.
- The category grid (Sofas & Sectionals, Dining Room, Bedroom, Lighting, Rugs, Mirrors & Decor) lets me jump straight to what I'm shopping for.
- The "2 ways to meet: in store or online" block is clear about how the appointment works.

## What's weak

- The "Long Weekend Sale" banner is still on top, with the same countdown I've seen for two weeks. It's stale and buries the new idea.
- The two "Book Your Free Design Appointment" CTAs repeat each other. The second block mostly restates the first.
- "Get as little as one week" is vague. I can't tell whether that means a consultation, delivery, or something else.
- The Double Rewards block ("Get 10% 20% back in reward dollars") shows a strikethrough that's hard to read, and the fine print is tiny. For someone who buys on the highest-rewards card, the math should be obvious, and here it isn't.
- The long stack of photos has no prices or product names. I can't tell which table or chairs I'm looking at.
- The footer app, gift registry and text-signup blocks are filler.

## Recommendations

- 1. Drop the sale banner from this send. Lead with the design appointment as the one offer, with a single CTA.
- 2. Merge the two design blocks into one and say what the free appointment includes (floor plan, mood board, sourcing).
- 3. Make the rewards math plain. For example, "$500 table = $100 back in rewards".
- 4. Tag the dining photos with product names and prices so each image links to something I can buy.
- 5. Cut the app and registry blocks.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free designer for your dining room`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Host-ready by Thanksgiving: book a free CB2 designer`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free 1:1 design appointments, in store or online, in as little as a week.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Tables, chairs and lighting, plus 20% back in rewards through 11/17.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a Marigold/Cheetah-style template (`<!--V2-->`, `mi_` tracking params) with 59 click-tracked links that were not probed. It has widespread image accessibility gaps, 11 non-HTTPS third-party tracking pixels from Dotomi, and a viewport meta that blocks zoom. Compliance and authentication results are inconclusive. The QA run reported 57% pass, 0 errors and 3 warnings.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified destinations:** 59 links were skipped as tracking or click-redirect domains, so none were HTTP-probed. Landing URLs, redirect chains and dead links are unconfirmed.
- **Insecure tracking pixels:** 11 images load over plain `http://`:
  - `http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?dtm_com=2&dtm_cid=2437&...`
  - `http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2437/pub1.php` through `pub10.php`
  - Clients that enforce HTTPS or proxy images, such as Gmail and Apple Mail Privacy Protection, may block or rewrite these. Dotomi cookie-redirect syncs also mean 10 extra third-party requests per open.
- **Template leftover:** the head contains the comment `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->`. It is harmless but shows the template was not cleaned.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `alt` on 8 content images:** all are on `image.mail.cb2.com/lib/fe9213727564027a72/m/1/`:
  - `22672a62-…jpg`
  - `4c0bfabe-…jpg`
  - `6852260b-…jpg`
  - `5620a399-…jpg`
  - `4ba0abd4-…jpg`
  - `1a710287-…jpg`
  - `491c9da8-…jpg`
  - `345f2993-…jpg`

  With images off, or in a screen reader, these are unlabeled. I can't confirm from the truncated source whether they are linked, in which case the links would have no accessible name.
- **Tracking pixels without an `alt` attribute:** `mi.cb2.com/.../o.gif`, `dv.cb2.com/o/bc2a8ed7-…` and the Dotomi pixels. They should carry `alt=""` so screen readers skip them.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">`. This blocks pinch-zoom, a WCAG 1.4.4 and 1.4.10 concern.
- **Text resizing disabled:** the CSS sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` and `*{... line-height:100%}`. This prevents user text scaling and can clip text at larger sizes.
- **Minor:** `<title>` is the generic "CB2, FURNITURE AND DECOR FOR THE MODERN HOME" rather than the subject. The `@font-face` URLs use a double slash (`cb2.com//Styles/...`). Both are low impact.
- **Positives confirmed:** `lang="en"` is set, MSO conditionals and VML namespaces are present, and web fonts are `font-display: swap` with fallbacks (`Helvetica, sans-serif`).

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found in the visible source or QA data. The truncated HTML shows no unresolved tokens such as `{{`, `%%` or `[FIRST_NAME]`. The body past the head was not available, so this is not fully verified.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found:** the QA note says AgentMail may strip them. This is **unconfirmed, not a verified sender defect**. If real, it would affect the Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender one-click unsubscribe requirement.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found:** SPF, DKIM and DMARC are unknown. The sending domain `mail.CB2.com` can't be validated from the data provided.
- **Not verifiable:** the in-body unsubscribe link and physical postal address are outside the truncated HTML. Neither can be confirmed or flagged.
- **Privacy:** the Dotomi cookie-sync pixels (`cookieredir/2437`) are third-party ad-tech beacons. Whether they are disclosed is a policy question that can't be answered from this data.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Cannot be assessed. All 59 links were behind tracking redirects and were never resolved. The only parameters visible are the ESP's `mi_u`, `mi_cid` and `mi_mid` on the pixels. No `utm_*` parameters or landing-page content were captured, so no issue is claimed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to the 8 content images, and `alt=""` to all tracking pixels.
2. Switch the Dotomi pixels (`UCMController`, `pub1`–`pub10`) to HTTPS, or drop the cookie-sync pixels.
3. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta, and avoid `text-size-adjust:none` where possible.
4. Fix the QA pipeline so it captures raw headers (`List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, `Authentication-Results`) before the relay strips them. Then re-run the compliance and authentication checks.
5. Follow the tracking redirects to resolve final URLs, then check `utm_*` presence and landing-page match.
6. Re-run the audit on the full, untruncated HTML to verify the unsubscribe link, postal address and any merge tokens.
