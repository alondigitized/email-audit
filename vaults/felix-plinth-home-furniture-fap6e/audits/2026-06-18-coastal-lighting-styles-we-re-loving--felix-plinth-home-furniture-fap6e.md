# Coastal lighting styles we’re loving
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-six. That's the Pottery Barn count in my inbox now, and I need to say it before anything else, because this one lands the morning after the June 17 triple-send that almost broke me. The day before this email I got "Shop the Summer Sale," "Get the look: This celeb's cozy cabin," and the quilts email before noon. I had mentally exited the Pottery Barn folder. So the fact that I'm even reading this at all is because the subject word "lighting" is doing real work for me right now — I'm spec'ing fixtures for two bathrooms and a living room simultaneously and I genuinely need to make decisions.
- Visually, the email is clean. The hero image of that wicker pendant in a bright, airy dining room is the right move — natural materials, warm neutrals, it could slot into the second house I'm doing without looking like it was designed by a committee. The "Summer Sale Up to 70% off" banner at top earns the click intent. But then I get to the "Recommended Just for You" module near the bottom and it's... bedding? I'm literally looking at a coastal lighting email and PB drops a duvet in the lower third. That's the kind of personalization failure that makes me feel like a segment-of-one to their email system, not a known customer.
- I'd mark this as "read and file" rather than an active click. If I'm being honest with myself, I'd pull up the coastal lighting category on PB's site directly — probably through the app — rather than click from this email, because I want to filter by finish and shade diameter, not browse a curated editorial. Not a forward. Definitely not a reply.

## What's working

- **Hero image earns its space**: The wicker pendant in the dining room is a clean, aspirational shot — the scale reads well and it matches exactly the aesthetic direction I'm chasing for the second house.
- **Sale banner placement at top**: "Summer Sale Up to 70% off" is the first thing I see after the logo. Functional and honest — this is the signal I needed to feel like the email is worth my time.
- **Subject word choice**: "Coastal lighting" is two words that map directly to a purchase decision I'm actively making. That's rare for the 26th email from any brand.
- **Layout is clean with no render bugs**: No overlapping text, no broken images, the hierarchy flows — hero → product shots → lifestyle → recommendations. This isn't a small thing; the June 17 wave was so rushed it showed.
- **Multiple lighting contexts shown**: Pendant, table lamp, bedroom ceiling — showing range across room types is genuinely useful for someone like me balancing two full houses.

## What's weak

- **"Styles we're loving" is doing the opposite of what I need**: That phrase is soft editorial and coded female-brand-speak. Wayfair and Article use this voice constantly. I don't need to know what PB "loves" — I need to know what fits a 10-foot ceiling or what comes in matte black.
- **Recommended module kills the coherence**: Bedding showing up in a lighting email is a personalization miss. Article kept its June series on a tight outdoor furniture thread and it felt curated; this feels like a CRM algorithm on autopilot.
- **No finish, size, or shade detail visible**: I'm choosing between rattan, linen, and ceramic shades for three different rooms. There's zero spec information in any of the product shots — Crate & Barrel at least surfaces "available in 3 finishes" in the caption text.
- **No CTA specificity**: "Shop Now" is generic. I'd click "Shop Coastal Pendants" or "Filter by Room." The CTA doesn't tell me what I'm clicking into.
- **Cadence hangover**: This email would score higher in isolation. But it's the morning after a three-email day. I'm already in damage-control mode for how PB is treating my inbox.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the bedding recommendation module entirely** — or replace it with a "Lighting by Room" grid (entry, dining, bedroom) that gives me a navigation decision instead of a distraction. The Bear x C&B collab email this week proved that tight editorial focus beats broad module stacking.
- 2. **Add at least one spec line per hero product**: Even something as simple as "Available in 3 finishes · 12" shade" would give me a reason to stop scrolling and commit to a tap.
- 3. **Make the CTA destination-specific**: "Shop Coastal Pendants" or "Shop the Sale: Lighting" — not "Shop Now" into the lighting category root.
- 4. **Urgency on the sale**: The banner says "Up to 70% off" but gives me no end date. CB2's sale email this week said "Ends Sunday." That one felt actionable; this one doesn't.
- 5. **Subject line reframe toward utility over editorial**:
- **Subject Alt A:** `Coastal lighting on sale — pendants, sconces, table lamps`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The right lamp for every room (up to 70% off)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Wicker, ceramic, rattan — find what fits your space in the Summer Sale.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Dining room, bedroom, entryway — shop the coastal lighting edit now.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[FAIL] PHP `header()` calls embedded in `<style>` block**
The following raw PHP appears verbatim inside a `<style>` tag in `<head>`:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
This is server-side code that was not stripped before HTML generation. It is inert to email clients but will be treated as unparseable CSS and may cause style-block parsing to abort in strict renderers.

**[WARN] 3 images served over HTTP (mixed content)**
Email clients with HTTPS enforcement or proxy-stripping will block these:

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail/Yahoo for bulk senders since February 2024. Absence will trigger inbox suppression or placement penalties at scale.

Suppresses HTTP Referer on clicks from webmail clients that render the email in-browser. This will cause all webmail-sourced site sessions to appear as direct traffic in analytics, masking email-attributed sessions.
