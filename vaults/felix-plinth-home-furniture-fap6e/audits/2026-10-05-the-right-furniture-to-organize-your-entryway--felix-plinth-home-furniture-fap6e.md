# The right furniture to organize your entryway
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-05**
## Executive summary

- Pottery Barn again, and this is the one I'd actually keep. After a week of catalog drops and "favorites on sale" sends, this one has a single idea: entryway furniture. I'm renovating two houses, and a mudroom or entry is on both lists, so "Make an Entrance" over a bench, hooks and baskets landed. It's a tidy, editorial scroll sorted by category: console tables, storage cabinets, benches, coat hooks.
- There's no offer anywhere, though. I can't see a percent off, a free-shipping line or a deadline. It reads like a mood board with shop links. Compared with this morning's "Just in! Our new holiday catalog" send, the focus is better. But I'd rather save it to my Pinterest-adjacent folder than click now. The bottom third also drifts into a generic "Recommended just for you" grid with ghost-lit candles, a rug and a bed. That has nothing to do with entryways, and it undoes the focus.
- Judgement: I'd open it, scroll once, save it, and click through only if I'm actively shopping the entry. I wouldn't forward it. Nothing in it is urgent or shareable.

## What's working

- One clear theme. "Make an Entrance" and "Entryway Furniture" at the top tell me what this is without a scroll.
- Sorted by category (console tables, storage cabinets, benches, coat hooks), each with a short benefit line like "Close the doors to tuck away essentials." That's how I plan a room.
- Named products are shown, like the Summerville Entry Cabinet and the Aubrey Shoe Bench. I can search those and compare against Crate & Barrel.
- The styled photography is calm and consistent, with warm woods and neutrals. That's good palette cohesion for someone matching two houses.
- The "Shop All Entryway Furniture" button is a clear catch-all CTA after the category blocks.
- The "Free Design Services / Book a Free Appointment" block is useful for a two-house renovation. I'd actually use it.

## What's weak

- No offer at all. Article's clearance email told me "up to 50% off" in giant type. Wayfair's Five Days of Deals led with "up to 70% off." This tells me nothing about savings.
- The shop links are small, light-weight text ("Shop Now ›") and hard to hit on a phone. Only the one button is a real tap target.
- No prices, no dimensions, no shipping or lead time. Furniture takes weeks, and I need "ships in X days" before I care.
- The "Recommended just for you" grid is off-topic (ghost collection, curtain rod, bed, rug). It isn't personalized to an entryway shopper, and Halloween ghosts after a calm entry story is jarring.
- The footer is long and dense: an Affirm block, rewards fine print, a card promo and an app promo. It's a lot of scroll after the point is made. It's also the sixth or seventh Pottery Barn send since 9/29, so I'm in snooze mode.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real offer in the hero, such as "Entryway furniture, up to 25% off through Sunday," so the email gives me a reason to act now instead of just browse.
- 2. Add a price ("from $X") and a delivery window under each category, and make the "Shop Now" links full-width buttons.
- 3. Swap the generic recommendations for entry-adjacent items I've viewed (mirrors, rugs, runners, lighting). Don't push a Christmas Shop module into an organization email.
- 4. Cut the footer by half. Keep the design-services block and move the Affirm and rewards copy to a single line.
- 5. Don't send this the same day as a catalog drop. Space the sends out.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Entryway storage from $249, ships in 2 weeks`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Benches, cabinets & hooks: the entry, solved`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Console tables, shoe benches and coat hooks, plus free design help for the whole house.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Pick your finish now: entry cabinets and benches are in stock and ready to ship.`

## Technical Audit

# Technical Audit: Pottery Barn, "The right furniture to organize your entryway"

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional layout with sliced images and click-wrapped links. The main problems are missing alt text on roughly 55 images, three `http://` image sources (one carries the recipient's email address), and leftover server-side code inside a `<style>` block. Header-level compliance and authentication checks are inconclusive because the relay did not capture the headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Link probing:** 61 tracking links were skipped (QA: "Tracking/click-redirect domains — skipped HTTP probe"). Destination status and landing page health are unverified.
- **Link wrapping:** Links are opaque redirect tokens, for example `https://click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=ABp7ImQi...`. The final destinations can't be checked from the source.
- **Insecure open-tracking pixel:** `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` loads over plaintext HTTP. It may be blocked or rewritten by clients, which would undercount opens.
- **Multiple tracking vendors:** There are three separate pixels:
  - `svopn.potterybarn.com`
  - `d.turn.com/r/dd/id/.../cat/1`, a third-party ad-network pixel
  - `clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/d5642f50-...?mi_cid=...`
- **Cross-brand asset:** `https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` is loaded from the West Elm host. This is a hosting dependency outside the Pottery Barn domain.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** About 55 `<img>` elements have no alt, per QA. Examples:
  - `F26_Furn_B11_EntrywayFurniture/slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`, which is the entire hero and product section.
  - `Email-Footer/slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`.
  - `F26_Top_NewArrivals+InStock/slices_01.jpg` and `slices_02.jpg`.
  - Product images such as `scarlett-upholstered-bed-1-l.jpg` and `reeva-handwoven-rug-3-l.jpg`.
  - Banners `H26_Bottom_ChristmasShop.jpg` and `DesignCrewPrimaryBottomBanner.jpg`.
  
  With images blocked, the email renders as blank slices, and screen readers announce filenames.
- **Insecure image sources:**
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
  
  Both may be blocked as mixed content. The second path is dated 2022 and looks like a stale reused asset.
- **Stray server-side code in CSS:** The second `<style>` block contains `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: no-store, ..."); header("Pragma: no-cache");`. This is invalid CSS from a template leak. It can break the parse of any rules that follow it in that block in strict clients.
- **Empty title:** `<title></title>` is empty.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4).
- **Font link:** `<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat...">` is stripped by Gmail and some other clients. It is only a fallback concern.
- **Pixel alt text:** The tracking pixels (`d.turn.com`, `svopn`, `clickcp`) lack alt text. These are non-visual, so `alt=""` is the appropriate fix.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tokens (`%%...%%`, `{{...}}`) are visible in the truncated source.
- **PII in URLs:** The recipient email address (`lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`) is embedded in plaintext query strings on two requests:
  - `https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=...`, loaded as an `<img>`.
  - The `svopn` pixel's `sv_sveme=` parameter, loaded over plain HTTP, so the address is exposed in transit.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe:** Not found (QA WARN). QA notes it may not be captured by the AgentMail relay, so this is unconfirmed.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** Not found (QA WARN), with the same caveat. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules expect it.
- **Authentication-Results:** Not found, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. This is a relay limitation, not evidence of failure.
- **Body unsubscribe link and postal address:** These are not in the truncated source, so they can't be verified. Check the full HTML.
- **Plain-text part:** 11,850 of 16,066 characters (about 74%) are URLs, which is above the 70% threshold. This is a spam-filter risk and a poor plain-text fallback.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Link destinations are hidden behind `click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=` tokens, and HTTP probes were skipped. UTM parameters and landing page alignment can't be verified from the source or QA data.
- The only visible direct destination is `coreemail.html`, which is an image-beacon endpoint, not a landing page.
- Product images are hosted on `www.potterybarn.com/pbimgs/...` (the retail domain), but the image links' targets are untestable.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to all content slices, especially the 18 entryway slices and the footer. Use `alt=""` for spacers and tracking pixels.
2. Switch the three `http://` assets to `https://`. This covers `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, the 2022 `slices_02.jpg`, and the `svopn` pixel. Replace the stale 2022 banner.
3. Stop putting the recipient's email address in plaintext URLs (`coreemail.html?email=`, `sv_sveme=`). Use an opaque ID instead.
4. Remove the stray `header(...)` PHP lines from the `<style>` block.
5. Fill in `<title>` and remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
6. Re-test with direct SMTP capture to confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and the SPF/DKIM/DMARC results.
7. Shorten or rebalance the plain-text part so URLs are under 70% of it.
8. Run a redirect-following probe on the 61 tracked links to verify destinations and UTM parameters.
