# Warehouse deals up to 60% off + Inside our NEW catalog
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-17**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so the top banner says "up to 60% off" but then the whole rest of the email is... the fall catalog. It's a bait-and-switch scroll — I click in expecting warehouse pricing and instead I get "Discover new inspiration," a "Recommended Just for You" grid, and then a Halloween shop with ghosts on a porch. That's three different emails mashed into one, and none of them commit.
- That said, the "Recommended Just for You" section actually has real product — a curtain rod, a dining table, a swivel chair, a rug — and the Toscana Extendable Dining Table catches my eye since I'm actually shopping tables right now for house #2. But there's zero price shown on any of these recommended items, which is useless when the subject line promised discounts.
- I'm not clicking. If anything I'd scroll straight to "Recommended Just for You," note the Toscana table name, and go search it myself on the site rather than trust this email's links. Not forwarding — this is the fourth Pottery Barn email in my inbox this week and it's the least focused one yet, worse than yesterday's actual warehouse sale email that at least organized by room.

## What's working

- The "Recommended Just for You" grid has actual named products with photos, and the Toscana Extendable Dining Table is relevant to what I'm shopping for right now.
- Clean brand header and consistent maroon/cream Pottery Barn palette throughout — no render bugs, nothing overlapping.
- "1600+ new arrivals" with a category grid (Pillows & Decor, Bedding, Dining Furniture, etc.) gives me a real navigation path if I did want to browse.
- Rewards card callout ("Earn 10% Back in Rewards") is at least present near the footer for anyone who cards it.

## What's weak

- The subject promises "up to 60% off" but the hero is 100% catalog inspiration copy — no discount number appears until I'm long past the fold, and even then it's just a thin top banner, not integrated into the body.
- Zero prices on any of the "Recommended Just for You" items — I can't tell if the rug or the dining table is in the sale at all.
- This is a Frankenstein email: fall catalog, design classes ad, recommended products, new arrivals push, AND a Halloween shop, all crammed into one send with no single hierarchy.
- I got Pottery Barn's actual warehouse sale email yesterday (2026-09-16, "Save up to 60% across the home") that was organized room-by-room — this one repeats that headline in the banner but doesn't deliver the same structure, so it feels like a lazier reuse of the same promo line.
- Halloween ghosts on a porch this deep in a furniture-shopping email is a jarring tonal left turn — CB2's "Fall 30% Off" send stayed on-message the whole way through; this one can't decide what it's selling.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one job for this email. If it's the catalog launch, lead with that and drop the "60% off" banner — it's setting an expectation the body never pays off. If it's the sale, put actual sale pricing on the hero and on every recommended product.
- 2. Put prices (and % off) directly on the "Recommended Just for You" tiles — that's the section with my actual purchase intent (the dining table) and it's the one place with no offer info at all.
- 3. Cut the Halloween Shop section entirely from this send or split it into its own dedicated email — it has nothing to do with catalog or warehouse pricing and breaks the flow.
- 4. Move "Recommended Just for You" up near the top — it's the only genuinely personalized, high-intent content in the whole email and it's buried past the catalog hero, the new-arrivals grid, and design classes.

- **Subject Alt B:** `New Fall Catalog: 1600+ arrivals, prices inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `See what's marked down in dining, bedroom & more`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Curtain rods to dining tables — picked from your browsing`
