# Don’t miss your chance to win $1500!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-21**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "Don't miss your chance to win $1500" — the fifteenth-ish Pottery Barn email in my inbox this week, and this time it's not even a product push, it's a Design Crew sweepstakes wrapped around booking a free design appointment. My first reaction was mild déjà vu — didn't I already get an "RSVP for your free design services" pitch a few days back? — followed by actual annoyance that the sweepstakes headline is doing all the emotional work while the real ask, buried underneath, is "book a consultation before August 30th." That's a lead-gen play dressed up as a giveaway.
- The recommended-products grid at the bottom is honestly the only part that felt like it was talking to me — a Farmhouse Platform Bed and Toscana Extendable Dining Table are exactly the kind of big-ticket stuff I'm shopping for across two renovations right now. But sandwiching that between two "book an appointment" CTAs and a wall of legal sweepstakes fine print at the bottom kills momentum.
- I'm not clicking. The $1500 gift card is a nice hook but "meet with a designer to be entered" is a real time commitment for a maybe-prize, and I've got Crate & Barrel's "Future Heirlooms Under $500" email in the same inbox with an actual straightforward discount attached. Not forwarding this — there's no single hero product worth sharing with a friend, and the sweepstakes framing feels like a lower-effort send than PB's usual lifestyle-shot emails.

## What's working

- The Design Crew sweepstakes hero has a clear, bold black banner with a specific dollar amount ($1500) — that's an actual stopping point in a fast scroll.
- "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" grid has real inventory relevant to a renovation — curtain rods, dining table, platform bed, sheets — that's my actual shopping list.
- The living room styling class card (Sept 13, 10AM, in-home/virtual/in-store) gives a concrete date and format, which beats vague "book anytime" copy.
- Rewards/Affirm/app blocks are consistent with prior PB sends — I recognize this footer pattern by now, so at least the brand voice hasn't drifted.

## What's weak

- Two nearly identical CTAs stacked on top of each other ("BOOK NOW" then "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT") before I even see a product — that's redundant and makes the email feel padded.
- The sweepstakes fine print block at the bottom is dense enough that it reads as a liability document, not marketing copy — "no purchase necessary," eligibility restrictions, promotional exclusions all crammed in.
- This is the second or third "free design services / RSVP" beat from PB this week — CB2 pulled the same trick with three straight "sample sale" emails and I called it out then too; PB's now doing its own version of that fatigue.
- No urgency tension on the actual prize deadline until you're already deep in — "August 30th" is mentioned once in tiny hero text and then never reinforced near the CTA.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut one of the two "book an appointment" CTAs — pick either the sweepstakes framing or the design-crew framing, not both stacked at the top.
- 2. Move the product grid up, above the sweepstakes fold — Wayfair's Open Box email at least led with real inventory; PB is burying its best asset (recognizable furniture I actually want) under two generic booking pitches.
- 3. Trim the legal block or move it to a linked landing page — nobody's reading four paragraphs of sweepstakes terms in an inbox.
- 4. Add a loyalty tie-in — I've got the PB credit card mentioned in the footer, but there's no rewards-points angle on the design consultation itself (e.g., "cardholders get double points on any purchase from your Design Crew session").

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
Standard SFMC-templated Pottery Barn promo email; renders via table-based layout with MSO/Outlook conditionals, but ships with mixed-content image sources, no accessible alt text, and no visible unsubscribe/authentication evidence in the provided source.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All product/hero links route through `click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=...` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud click-tracking redirects) — 45 tracking links were skipped by automated HTTP probing since they are redirect-domain wrapped, so final destinations are unverified.
- Additional third-party tracking pixels/beacons present: `secure.leadback.advertising.com/adcedge/lb?site=695501&srvc=1&betr=...`, `d.turn.com/r/dd/id/...`, and `svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` (open-tracking pixel with recipient email embedded in the URL query string — flagged below under Compliance/PII).
- Stray malformed `<style>` block contains literal PHP `header()` calls (`header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: ...")`) inside the CSS text — this is server-side code leaked into the client-facing HTML `<style>` tag. It's inert as CSS (browsers ignore invalid declarations) but indicates a templating/build error upstream.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 27 of ~30 images have no `alt` attribute (QA-confirmed), including all three primary content slices (`slices_01/02/03.jpg`) and footer/product images — screen readers and blocked-image fallback states will show nothing.
- Two images are served over plain `http://` instead of `https://`: `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` and `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` — mixed content may be blocked or flagged by mail clients enforcing HTTPS-only image loading.
- Standard MSO/Outlook and Apple Mail resets are present (`x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, `.ExternalClass`, `a[x-apple-data-detectors]`), plus responsive `@media` rules for the 480px/650px breakpoints — no issues found in the boilerplate itself.
- Preheader text uses standard zero-width-space padding technique — no issues found.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tags (e.g., `%%FirstName%%`, `{{...}}`) visible in the truncated source.
- The `coreemail.html` and `svopn.potterybarn.com` tracking URLs contain a hardcoded recipient identifier: `email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` and `sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` — confirms personalization tokens resolved correctly for this send, but note the raw email address is exposed in plaintext URL query params (see Compliance).

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header**: not found in the captured headers (QA WARN). CAN-SPAM requires a working opt-out mechanism but not necessarily this header — check whether an unsubscribe link exists in the untruncated footer HTML, which wasn't included in the source provided.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058)**: not found — one-click unsubscribe per Gmail/Yahoo 2024 bulk-sender requirements is not confirmed supported.
- **Authentication-Results header**: not found — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status cannot be verified from this capture (QA notes this may be a relay-side capture gap rather than an actual authentication failure).
- **PII in tracking URLs**: recipient's raw email address is passed in cleartext as a URL parameter in two open-tracking beacons (`coreemail.html?email=...`, `svopn.potterybarn.com/?...sv_sveme=...`). This is common ESP practice but worth flagging as a data-handling/privacy consideration, not a CAN-SPAM violation per se.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Click-tracking links use opaque `qs=` SFMC redirect tokens rather than visible UTM parameters, so campaign-to-landing-page attribution can't be verified from this source — final-destination UTM alignment is unconfirmed since these links were skipped in the automated probe.
- Product image `src` URLs point directly to `www.potterybarn.com/pbimgs/.../*-l.jpg` (live site CDN), consistent with the linked product pages being on the same domain — no cross-domain mismatch observed for the sampled product images.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to all content images, especially the three hero slices (`slices_01/02/03.jpg`) and product shots — currently blank/missing across the board.
2. Convert the two `http://` image sources to `https://` to avoid mixed-content blocking in mail clients.
3. Remove the stray PHP `header(...)` statements embedded in the `<style>` block — indicates a broken include/template artifact that should be cleaned from the ESP template source.
4. Confirm (outside this HTML capture) that a valid `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header pair is actually being sent — required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender rules as of 2024; the QA gap may be a capture artifact but should be verified against raw SMTP headers.
5. Verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC via raw headers at the sending MTA rather than relying on this relay capture, since Authentication-Results was not observed.
