# WIN A CUBS GAME
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-02**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "WIN A CUBS GAME" — I'll admit this got a genuine second look, because I'm a Chicago guy mid-renovation on two houses and this is the first CB2 email all week that isn't just another discount block. Club-level tickets, a $2,000 travel voucher, on-field access, and a $1,000 CB2 gift card stapled on the back — that's a real prize, not a "win a $50 gift card" filler sweepstakes. But it's also just a sweepstakes, and I'm not entering raffles from a furniture brand no matter how good the Cubs tie-in is.
- The sofa in the hero shot is doing real work though — that's exactly my color palette for the primary house's living room, and it's a smarter hook than another "SALE UP TO 40% OFF" banner. After five-plus CB2 sends this week including the "David Girelli, exclusively for CB2" designer spotlight on 8/2, this at least breaks the pattern instead of repeating it.
- Bottom line: I'm not clicking "ENTER NOW" for a raffle, but I might scroll past it to the "SOFAS & SECTIONALS" section below, which is the actual commerce play buried under the contest. Not forwarding this — nobody I know needs a Cubs sweepstakes email. Marked read, maybe half-considered the sectional.

## What's working

- The hero sofa/room shot is genuinely my taste — warm wood, tufted brown sectional, marble coffee table, exactly the vibe I'm chasing for the renovation.
- The prize breakdown is specific and legible: tickets, travel voucher dollar amount, gift card dollar amount — no vague "amazing prizes" language.
- "SOFAS & SECTIONALS" section below the fold has a clean single hero product shot with a clear "SHOP NOW" — that's the part of this email actually built for someone buying furniture.
- Rewards Dollars callout (10% back at CB2 and Crate & Barrel) is relevant since I run everything through the highest-rewards card anyway — that's the one loyalty signal that actually lands for me.

## What's weak

- A Cubs ticket sweepstakes has nothing to do with furniture shopping — it's a play for email opens and social buzz, not a play for my renovation budget, and CB2 is treating those as the same audience.
- The "SALE UP TO 40% OFF" banner sandwiched between the sweepstakes and the sofa section is the same discount-block energy CB2's run all week — it's becoming wallpaper at this point.
- No swatch/sample program mention anywhere, no shipping-timeline callout for the sectional — for a big-ticket furniture item that's exactly the info I need before I'd click through.
- This is CB2's sixth-ish send in my inbox this week; stacking a sweepstakes on top of the usual cadence makes it feel like more noise, not less.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into two emails — the sweepstakes is a distraction bolted onto a legitimate sofa promo. Let the sectional be the whole story with real product info (dimensions, fabric options, delivery window), and run the Cubs contest as its own standalone send if it's worth doing at all.
- 2. Put a price or "member pricing" callout directly on the sofa hero — right now there's a beautiful picture and a "SHOP NOW" button but zero indication of cost, which is a bigger click-blocker than anything the sweepstakes solves.
- 3. If keeping the Cubs angle, tie it to a purchase threshold ("spend $500, get an entry") instead of a free-entry raffle — that at least connects the promotion to buying furniture instead of competing with it for attention.
- 4. Cut the second "SALE UP TO 40% OFF" banner — Article's "Last chance" sale banner at least backs up urgency with a countdown; this one is just static discount language I've already seen from CB2 multiple times this week.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus new sofas and sectionals, up to 40% off`
