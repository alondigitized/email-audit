# BATH MATS You'll Love!!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- I'll be direct: the subject line says "BATH MATS" and the body has zero bath mats in it. I scrolled the entire email — rugs, patio umbrellas, outdoor lounge chairs, what looks like a sectional, and a product grid of things that belong on a deck, not in a bathroom. This is the same trick Wayfair pulled on me with the "TUB FAUCETS ⭕ UP TO 70% OFF" email from July 6th, which opened to a generic sale page. At least *that* one I could argue was close. This one isn't even in the same room.
- I'm renovating two bathrooms right now — the primary and the second home — and bath mats are actually on my list. So I would have opened this. But the payoff is a clearance rug grid and patio furniture I didn't ask for. The "picked just for you" header is doing a lot of heavy lifting for content that clearly was not picked for me. The only reason I'd click anything in here is if one of those rugs happened to match something I'm already sourcing — not because the email earned it.
- Mark as read. Don't forward. I'd actually have more trust in Wayfair right now if they just called this "Summer Clearance" and let me self-select. The double exclamation point on a bath mat is also doing nothing for me.

- The top promo block has a visible deal badge and percentage, so the discount is front-loaded even if the category is wrong.

- The financing callout ("Financing that suits your style") is a reasonable inclusion for a home goods buyer; furniture-scale purchases sometimes do make me do the math.

## What's weak

- Subject says bath mats. Body has zero bath mats. That's a disqualifying mismatch — I've been burned by this exact pattern twice this week from Wayfair already.
- "BATH MATS You'll Love!!" with double exclamation points in ALL CAPS reads like a clearance bin sign at a strip-mall store, not a brand I'd trust with a $400 bathroom refresh.
- "Score styles you ↓" is a weak CTA — that's not a verb-noun combo, it's filler copy.
- The outdoor furniture grid is actively annoying context for someone who opened looking for bath textiles. Article's 4th-of-July email at least put the right product in the right email.
- This is approximately the tenth Wayfair in my inbox this week. The cadence alone has trained me to distrust the urgency of any individual send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put actual bath mats in the email.** If the subject is "BATH MATS You'll Love!!" the hero product should be a bath mat. Pottery Barn's recent storage email delivered exactly what the subject promised — no tricks, just product. Do that.
- 2. **Drop the double exclamation mark and the ALL CAPS.** I'm 36. I know when I'm being yelled at. CB2 manages urgency without shouting.
- 3. **Replace "Score styles you ↓" with something that earns a click** — "Shop bath mats on sale" is boring but it works. "Find your mat" with a specific % off is better.
- 4. **Collapse the outdoor section entirely** or gate it below a bath-focused hero. Right now the rug and patio grid immediately undercuts the subject line promise.
- 5. **Cool the cadence.** Tenth email in a week means the eleventh lands with negative credibility equity. Article sends fewer, with less mismatch, and I trust those more even after four sends on the same sale.
