# Nightstands starting at $349
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-27**
## Executive summary

- Okay, forty-eight. Pottery Barn is basically co-tenant in my inbox at this point, and this one actually lands closer to home than most — nightstands are a real, live line item on both my punch lists right now, not some tangential registry sweepstakes or a quilt mood board. The eight-piece grid (Sausalito, Farmhouse, Cayman, Oakleigh, Reed, Rylee, Calistoga, Summerville) is genuinely useful — that's a real comparison shop, not a single hero shot dressed up as a "collection."
- Where it loses me is the same place Pottery Barn always loses me: the email can't decide if it's a nightstand catalog or a full store flyer. By the time I hit "Recommended Just For You," "Discover New Arrivals," and then an entirely separate outdoor furniture sale, I've mentally closed the nightstand chapter and I'm just scrolling past filler. Compare that to CB2's "Just in. Already trending." send I gave a 9 — that one stayed disciplined on one idea top to bottom. This one starts disciplined and then sprawls.
- I'd click through on one of the wood-tone nightstands, probably Farmhouse or Sausalito since they'd actually match what's going into the primary bedroom. I wouldn't forward this to a friend — it's not clever or shareable, it's just a competent product page mailed to me. Mark as read after I click, not archive-and-forget.

## What's working

- "Nightstands starting at $349" as the subject is the most concrete, non-generic hook Pottery Barn has sent me all month — actual product, actual price anchor.
- Eight distinct nightstand styles laid out with names and starting prices under each — that's real comparison shopping, not one hero SKU dressed up as a "collection."
- Wood tones and finishes are shown clearly enough to gauge against what's already in my cart mentally — farmhouse vs. mid-century vs. cane-front options side by side.
- "SHOP ALL NIGHTSTANDS" as a clean button after the grid gives me a single, obvious next step if I don't want to pick from the eight tiles individually.

## What's weak

- The email doesn't stop at nightstands — it tacks on "Complete Your Retreat" (beds, dressers, sheets, duvets), then "Recommended Just For You," then a whole separate "Up to 60% off Outdoor" sale. By the third section shift I've checked out of the original offer.
- "Summer Sale: Up to 70% off" in the header banner doesn't visibly apply to the nightstands below it — none of the eight tiles show a strike-through or sale price, just "Starting at $349." That's the exact bait-and-switch feeling Wayfair's kitchen mats email gave me, where the subject-line hook wasn't actually the thing being sold.
- No individual product prices under most of the grid tiles beyond the top "STARTING AT $349" banner — I can't tell if Oakleigh is $349 or $900 without clicking through, which is friction I don't get from CB2's cleaner sends.
- Zero mention of the Pottery Barn credit card rewards math up top where it'd actually matter for a $349+ purchase — it's buried at the very bottom in tiny print instead of near the CTA where I'm actually deciding.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after "SHOP ALL NIGHTSTANDS" — kill Complete Your Retreat, Recommended Just For You, New Arrivals, and Outdoor entirely. One category, one offer, one send. This is the single biggest lift available here.
- 2. Put an actual price under every one of the eight tiles, not just the $349 floor on the hero SKU — I shouldn't have to click eight times to comparison shop.
- 3. Either kill the "Up to 70% off" banner or make it visibly apply to at least one nightstand tile — right now it reads as decoration, and after Pottery Barn's "70% off" bedding hue email two days ago, I'm numb to that number regardless.
- 4. Move the "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" card credit line up near the CTA, not buried in bottom-of-email fine print — that's the thing that actually nudges a $349-$900 purchase for someone like me.

# Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Nightstands starting at $349"

## 1. Technical Summary
Legacy table-based HTML email (XHTML transitional doctype, MSO conditionals) with functioning tracking/personalization infrastructure, but missing accessibility attributes, mixed HTTP/HTTPS asset delivery, and no verifiable unsubscribe/authentication headers in the captured transport layer.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 61 tracking/click-redirect links (`click.e.potterybarn.com`, `clickcp.potterybarn.com`) were skipped by automated HTTP probing — expected behavior for redirect-wrapped links, not itself a defect, but destination integrity is unverified.
- **PII exposure in tracking URLs**: the recipient's email address is embedded in cleartext in multiple tracking pixel/query URLs:
  - `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
  - `https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
  This is standard ESP open-tracking practice but means the address is logged in plaintext by any intermediary (proxy, forwarder, link scanner) that touches these URLs.
- The `svopn.potterybarn.com` open-tracking pixel is served over **`http://`**, not HTTPS — see item below (also flagged under Rendering).
- Additional third-party tracking pixel present: `d.turn.com/r/dd/id/...` (Amobee/Turn), outside the `potterybarn.com`/`e.potterybarn.com` domains — worth confirming this is an intentional ad-analytics partner.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **40+ `<img>` tags missing `alt` text** (QA-confirmed), including all primary product images (`slices_01–21.jpg`, product shots like `sausalito-8-drawer-dresser-66-l.jpg`, `farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg`). Screen readers and blocked-image fallback (common in Outlook/Gmail) will render this email with no textual content.
- **3 images served over `http://` instead of `https://`**:
  - `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
  Mixed-content images are commonly blocked or flagged by HTTPS-enforcing mail clients (e.g., Outlook.com, some corporate gateways).
- **Malformed `<style>` block**: the `<head>` contains literal PHP `header(...)` calls (`header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: no-store...")`) inside a `<style>` tag. This is inert (browsers/email clients will just ignore it as invalid CSS), but it indicates a template artifact/leftover server-side snippet that was never meant to ship in the HTML — worth flagging to the ESP/template owner as build hygiene.
- Web font loaded via external `<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/...">` — most email clients (Outlook, Gmail apps) strip or block external stylesheet links, so this will silently fail to Arial/default fallback in the majority of clients; not a bug per se, but confirm a web-safe fallback is set in inline styles (not visible in the truncated source).

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved/broken merge tags (e.g., stray `%%...%%`, `{{...}}`, `[FNAME]`) found in the visible source.
- One resolved personalization token confirmed live: recipient email `lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` correctly interpolated into tracking pixel URLs (see §2 for the PII-exposure implication of that same token).

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication)
- **List-Unsubscribe header: not found** (QA WARN) — may be a relay-capture limitation of the AgentMail intermediary rather than a true absence at origin; cannot confirm from headers alone.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header (RFC 8058): not found** — one-click unsubscribe support cannot be confirmed from available headers.
- **Authentication-Results header: not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unverifiable from this capture.
- Footer/unsubscribe link and physical mailing address are not visible in the truncated HTML — cannot confirm CAN-SPAM footer compliance from the provided source; flag for full-source review before signing off.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- All outbound content links are wrapped in ESP click-tracking redirects (`click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=<opaque base64/encoded blob>`) rather than exposing plain destination URLs with standard `utm_source`/`utm_medium`/`utm_campaign` params. This is normal for enterprise ESP tracking, but it means UTM alignment with the landing page **cannot be verified from the HTML alone** — the actual destination and its query string only resolve after redirect.
- The `coreemail.html` customer-service link resolves to a direct `potterybarn.com` path (no redirect wrapper) with only an `email` param — consistent, no continuity issue found there.
- No issues found in domain consistency — all tracking/image domains resolve under expected `potterybarn.com` / `e.potterybarn.com` / `edm.potterybarn.com` properties, aside from the single third-party `d.turn.com` pixel noted in §2.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add `alt` attributes to all product and banner images (highest-impact fix — currently ~40 images, including every nightstand product shot, have none).
2. Migrate the 3 remaining `http://` image assets to HTTPS to avoid mixed-content blocking.
3. Remove the stray PHP `header(...)` lines from the `<style>` block — dead code left in the template.
4. Confirm with the ESP/deliverability team whether `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are actually being sent at origin (this audit's relay may simply not be capturing them) — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is a Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender requirement as of their 2024 policies.
5. Request full (non-truncated) headers to verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC (`Authentication-Results`) and confirm CAN-SPAM footer (physical address + unsubscribe link) is present.
6. Note for content/deliverability review (not in scope here): plain-text alternative is 72% raw URLs (11,016/15,211 chars) — this materially affects spam-filter text/link-ratio scoring and is worth a separate deliverability pass.
