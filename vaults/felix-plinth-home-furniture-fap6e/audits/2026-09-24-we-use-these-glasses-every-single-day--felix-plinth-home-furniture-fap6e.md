# We use these glasses every single day
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-24**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "we use these glasses every single day" — that's a solid, low-key subject line, but once I open it, this is basically a greatest-hits mashup of everything Crate & Barrel has sent me this week wearing a glassware trench coat. It opens on drinkware, pivots to a smoothie recipe, pivots again to a full bedroom furniture pitch, then closes with a "we love these for you" grid that's just... more glasses and dinner plates. I'm renovating two houses right now, so the bedroom section actually stopped my scroll — that hero shot with the olive/rust upholstered bed is genuinely nice — but it's buried as section four in an email that promised me glasses in the subject line.
- Would I click? Maybe on the bedroom furniture, since that's a live need for the second house's primary. Would I forward it? No — nothing here is surprising enough to send to a friend, it's just C&B doing C&B things. Would I mark it read and move on? Yeah, that's the honest answer — I'll probably tap through to "Shop Bedroom Furniture" and ignore the rest.

## What's working

- The hero glassware shot (pour-over coffee into the clear mug) is clean and matches the "we use these every day" everyday-ritual framing.
- "tabletop essentials start at $1.95" is a real, specific price point I can anchor to — that's more honest than most vague "new arrivals" copy.
- The bedroom section has an actual aspirational lifestyle shot with warm, current styling (olive headboard, layered neutral bedding) that fits fall.
- "we love these for you" personalization framing, even if generic, at least gestures at knowing my browsing category.

## What's weak

- This is at least three emails jammed into one — glassware, a recipe, and bedroom furniture — with no single clear hero offer, which tanks the visual hierarchy the subject line promised.
- No actual discount or percentage-off anywhere in this email; "starts at $1.95" is the closest thing to an offer and it's for tabletop, not the glassware the subject sold me on.
- The recipe section (berry plum smoothie) is a filler beat I've seen from this brand before — it's cute but it's not moving me toward a purchase, it's moving me toward Pinterest.
- Rewards/Crate Card teaser ("get 10% back in Reward Dollars") is stuck near the bottom as a small banner instead of being used to frame the offer up top — a missed chance for loyalty math.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one hero and cut the rest — either this is a glassware/drinkware email or a bedroom email, not both. Given I'm mid-renovation, I'd lead with bedroom and make glassware the secondary block, not bury it after a recipe detour.
- 2. Put an actual number in the hero — "$1.95" tabletop pricing should be the headline offer, not a secondary nav row under a vague "refresh your go-to glassware" banner.
- 3. Cut the smoothie recipe section entirely — it doesn't sell furniture or glassware any harder than a straight product shot would, and it adds scroll length without adding intent.
- 4. Move the 10% Reward Dollars callout up near the top CTA — that's the one loyalty hook in the whole email and it's whispering at the bottom.
