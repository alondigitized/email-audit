# Our kids' room lighting guide ✨
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-five. I just said that number in the last C&B family email today — actually there have been four from the family today alone — and now here's Crate & Kids with a lighting guide for children's rooms. I do not have a lighting guide problem. I have a *frequency* problem. And the kids' room angle specifically is doing me no favors, because nowhere in my two renovation projects is there a room I'd describe as a "kids' room" right now. My second home is getting a kitchen refresh and a guest bedroom overhaul; my primary is mid-living-room. Neither of those rooms is lit by a bunny night-light.
- That said, I'll give the email this: it's clean. The layout is editorial, the photography is warm and restrained, and the hierarchy feels considered — you can see actual lifestyle shots of real rooms alongside the product grid, which is more work than Wayfair ever bothers to do. But "well-executed editorial about a room I don't have" isn't a click. It's a folder-file-for-later at absolute best.
- Mark as read. No click, no forward. Maybe I'd screenshot one fixture if something caught my eye mid-scroll, but I'm not going to shop kids' lighting this month.

## What's working

- **Layout discipline is real.** The editorial guide format — lifestyle room shot, then product grid, then another lifestyle shot — gives the eye a rhythm. Pottery Barn does this well on their best sends; this is in that tier structurally.
- **Photography palette is cohesive.** Warm neutrals, no visual noise, lamps photographed in actual room settings rather than cut out on white. That alone puts it ahead of most Wayfair category emails.
- **Subject line is clear and unambiguous** — "kids' room lighting guide" tells me exactly what I'm getting, which is more than the ellipsis-bait from the C&B collab email sent the same morning.
- **No visible render bugs.** Clean grid, no overlapping text, images loaded correctly. The execution is solid.

## What's weak

- **Wrong room, wrong moment.** I'm in two active renovations right now and neither has a kids' room on the active project list. Article's dining room send from earlier this week (8/10) landed because it mapped exactly to a real decision I'm in the middle of. This one doesn't even get close to that.
- **No offer.** This is pure editorial — there's no percentage off, no free shipping threshold, nothing that gives me a reason to act now rather than eventually. The Pottery Barn "new arrivals" send (9/10) managed to lead with aspiration AND have a clear loyalty pricing signal. This has the aspiration, not the incentive.
- **Four emails today.** Context is everything. The C&B family has now emailed me four times on July 21 alone. Even if this were the most perfectly targeted lighting email ever written, I'm in defensive inbox mode at this point.
- **No loyalty signal visible.** I buy via my highest-rewards card specifically because I'm tracking the math on big furniture purchases. If Crate & Barrel has a rewards program, this email didn't remind me of my status or tie the guide to a member benefit.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Gate this send to subscribers with a kids' category signal.** If my browse history or past purchase data shows "kids," send this. If not, sub me into the general lighting guide or living room lighting instead. Article does this better — their sends feel targeted to decisions I'm actually in.
- 2. **Add a member benefit or rewards hook.** Even a line like "Earn [X] points on any lighting purchase this week" changes the CTA from "maybe later" to "why not now."
- 3. **Collapse the cadence.** Four emails in one day from the C&B family is indefensible. This send might have scored a 7 if it arrived in isolation on a quiet Tuesday. Instead it's the fourth pitch of the day and I'm numb.
- 4. **One hero product, not a grid.** Pick the one lamp you think is your strongest editorial statement and make me fall in love with it before showing me eight options. CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS" send made this mistake too — a grid of eight isn't curation, it's a warehouse dump with better photography.
