# OUTDOOR WALL DECOR at a great price.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- Wayfair knows I'm in renovation mode — outdoor wall decor in May is exactly the kind of send I'd expect from them, and I haven't unsubscribed because they do occasionally surface something useful. But when I actually opened this, the first thing that hit me was a giant teal butterfly. Not a subtle botanical. A *butterfly*. That's a hard skew toward whoever pins cottagecore content at 11pm, and that person is not me.
- The product grid below it is more mixed — outdoor rugs, a blue side table, some items that are at least in the ballpark for a patio or deck situation. The "we think you'll ♥ these" personalization block is doing real work trying to pull me back in. But that butterfly hero costs them the first four seconds, and for a guy who's comparing finishes across two properties, first impressions matter fast.
- I'd mark this as read, maybe save it to my "catalog" folder out of habit, but I'm not clicking through. Not forwarding it either — the one person I'd send it to would laugh at the butterfly.

## What's working

- Wayfair is a trusted sender in my actual shopping rotation — this got opened on brand recognition alone.
- Outdoor category in May is dead-on seasonally; I do have two patios to sort out.
- The product grid and the personalized recommendations section show real breadth — it's not all one style.
- Sale price indicators (orange tags) are visible and legible in the grid without having to squint.
- Email renders clean — no broken images, no layout mess, nothing that made me feel like IT had a bad day.

## What's weak

- The butterfly hero is stridently feminine — I get that wall art is a big category but leading with that piece signals this email wasn't segmented for me at all.
- "At a great price" in the subject is content-free filler. I already assume Wayfair has competitive prices; that phrase does nothing for me.
- ALL CAPS in the subject feels like a clearance-bin alert, not a curated send — it's visually loud in the inbox.
- No visible shipping callout, which matters enormously for oversized outdoor items; this is a huge omission for a furniture-adjacent email.
- The personalization block is a nice touch but the selections lean heavily decorative-feminine — didn't feel like my browsing history at all.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the hero image** to something more style-neutral — a clean outdoor gallery wall with mixed metal/wood pieces, or a styled deck with a few statement pieces. Let the category speak without alienating half the audience.
- 2. **Rework the subject line** — ditch ALL CAPS and replace the generic "great price" with something concrete (a dollar threshold, a percentage, a category anchor).
- 3. **Add a shipping or delivery-date callout** — even a line like "ships in 2 days" or "free delivery on orders $35+" goes a long way for outdoor/large-format items.
- 4. **Tighten the personalization logic** — if I've browsed industrial or mid-century outdoor, show me that, not the floral-butterfly end of the catalog.
- 5. **Add a deadline** — without any urgency anchor the email has no reason to be acted on today vs. never.
