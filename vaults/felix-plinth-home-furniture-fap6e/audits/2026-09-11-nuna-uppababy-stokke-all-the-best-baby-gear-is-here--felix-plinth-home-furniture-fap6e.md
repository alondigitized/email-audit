# Nuna, Uppababy, Stokke… all the best baby gear is here →
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-11**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids, again. That's five sends from the Crate & Barrel family this week if I'm counting right, and this one's the fifth act — "the very best baby gear," Nuna/UppaBaby/Stokke name-dropped in the subject like a registry flex. I don't have kids. I'm not registering for a $900 stroller system. This is dead-on-arrival for the same reason the bedroom send and the dinnerware send were — wrong list, wrong life stage, right brand fatigue.
- Credit where due: this one at least has an actual premium-brand hook (Nuna, UppaBaby, Stokke are genuinely aspirational names, not filler), and the layout is clean, on-brand, no broken images. But it's also an absolute scroll monster — car seats, strollers, high chairs, sweepstakes, bestsellers, "shop the app" — every category crammed into one endless email instead of picking a lane.
- Not opening this on purpose, not clicking, definitely not forwarding — unless it's to a friend who's actually expecting, in which case sure, this is a decent "look what's out there" send for them. For me it's an instant scroll-past and a mental note that Crate & Barrel's list segmentation is broken.

## What's working

- Nuna/UppaBaby/Stokke name-drop in the subject is a smart trust signal for anyone actually shopping baby gear — real premium brands, not house-label filler.
- Clean, consistent visual system — soft neutral palette, generous whitespace, no rendering bugs, no lorem ipsum.
- Clear section labels ("bestselling car seats," "fan-fave strollers," "most-loved high chairs") make it skimmable even though I'm skimming right past.
- The Stokke high chair and Nuna stroller product shots are genuinely nice photography — showroom-quality, not stock-y.

## What's weak

- I have no kids. Zero. This is the same miss as the last four Crate & Kids sends I've scrolled through this week — the segmentation is either broken or nonexistent.
- The email is exhaustingly long — car seats, strollers, high chairs, new arrivals, a sweepstakes, bestsellers, a credit card pitch, AND an app download push. Pick a lane.
- No actual offer anywhere that I can see — no %, no free shipping banner, nothing. Compare that to CB2's fall sale send that led hard with a real discount; this is just a merchandise catalog with a "shop now" button repeated a dozen times.
- The $5,000 sweepstakes and "get the card, get $500 off chance" blocks feel bolted on at the bottom, diluting whatever focus the top of the email had.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the segmentation before anything else.** If I've never bought or browsed nursery/baby gear, I should not be getting the fifth Crate & Kids send this week. This is the single biggest lever — no creative tweak fixes a targeting miss.
- 2. **Pick one hero category and cut the rest.** Lead with strollers or car seats, not all four categories stacked end to end. Article and CB2 both know how to commit to one story per send instead of a full catalog dump.
- 3. **Put an actual offer up top if there is one.** Right now there's no visible discount anywhere — if this is purely aspirational/browse content, say so; if there's a promo, it's buried too deep for anyone to find it.
- 4. **Move the sweepstakes and credit card pitch into their own separate sends.** Tacking them onto a baby-gear catalog just adds noise for people who don't fit the primary segment.
