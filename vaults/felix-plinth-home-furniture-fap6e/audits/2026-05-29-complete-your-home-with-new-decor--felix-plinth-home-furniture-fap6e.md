# Complete your home with new decor
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- Article has been in full sprint mode since Memorial Day started — "Up to 40% off ends Monday" on the 22nd was the best email they've sent in weeks, the brand-builder on the 23rd was a smart reset, and the "last chance" on the 25th closed the sale loop cleanly. Now we're six days out from that peak and this is the first genuinely new-content email in the series. That timing is correct. They didn't spam me with another discount; they pivoted to new arrivals. I respect the editorial discipline.
- What doesn't land is the subject and hero copy. I'm renovating two homes at once. I have a Pottery Barn wish list with forty-two items. I've been inside a Crate & Barrel three times this month. "Complete your home with new decor" is something I could have written myself in thirty seconds and attributed to any of the twelve brands currently clogging my inbox. It tells me nothing distinctive about Article, nothing about what's actually in here, and nothing about why today is the day to look.
- Inside the email, the shelf section earns a genuine click from me — I'm actively sourcing shelving for the second home's study right now. The textiles and planters are browsable but not urgent. I'd save this to my Pinterest-adjacent folder and come back to the shelf link. No forward.

## What's working

- **"Shelf life." is the highest-signal section.** Floating shelves in natural wood, clear category CTA — this is exactly what I'm shopping for and I didn't have to scroll far to find it.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean.** "Decor values." over the lifestyle image, button below — the eye flows without friction. No competing headlines fighting for attention.
- **Sub-CTAs are category-specific.** "SHOP PILLOWS & THROWS" and the shelf CTA route me somewhere real, not a 50,000-item bucket. Wayfair has never figured this out.
- **Cohesive material language.** Warm wood, terracotta, linen textures — the email has a consistent aesthetic that doesn't feel like it was assembled from three different shoots.
- **Cadence timing is right.** Not another sale email the week after Memorial Day ended. This is a brand that understands editorial pacing.

## What's weak

- **No price anchor anywhere.** Crate & Barrel sent me two "60% off clearance" emails this same morning. When I'm in active purchase mode, a new-arrivals email with zero price signal doesn't move the needle — it just tells me to come back during the next sale.
- **The subject is indistinguishable from any home brand in my inbox.** Pottery Barn, CB2, Wayfair — any of them could have sent "Complete your home with new decor" verbatim. Nothing about it signals Article specifically.
- **The planters section ("Branch out.") needs more information.** Indoor or outdoor rated? Drainage holes? Dimensions? Without that, I'm looking at a nice photo, not evaluating a purchase.
- **The outdoor covers section at the bottom breaks the narrative.** It feels like a different email got appended. "New decor" has a coherent through-line until it doesn't.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a product-level arrival hook in the hero.** "New this week" or "Just landed — May 28" creates a time-indexed reason to look today instead of whenever the next sale hits.
- 2. **Put a price anchor in the "Shelf life." section.** Even "starting at $149" activates the mental model for a rewards-card buyer — suddenly I'm calculating points, not just admiring photography.
- 3. **Lead with shelves.** The planters are beautiful but the shelf is the closest thing to a considered purchase in here. For a renovation-phase subscriber, lead with the functional piece.
- 4. **Give the planters one line of specs.** "Indoor/outdoor. 10 in. diameter. Drainage hole included." That's three details that close the browser-to-cart loop.
- 5. **Cut the outdoor covers to its own send.** It's a different product logic — protective, functional, outdoor — and it's diluting an otherwise coherent new-arrivals story.

The trailing comma after `p,` before `{` is invalid CSS. Strict parsers (including some webmail renderers) will drop the entire rule, leaving `h1`, `h2`, and `p` uncolored in dark mode.

`color:#fff;!important` is invalid. The semicolon terminates the declaration before `!important` is parsed, making the `!important` flag inert. The `a` color declaration will still apply (without `!important`), but specificity override behavior is unreliable across clients.
