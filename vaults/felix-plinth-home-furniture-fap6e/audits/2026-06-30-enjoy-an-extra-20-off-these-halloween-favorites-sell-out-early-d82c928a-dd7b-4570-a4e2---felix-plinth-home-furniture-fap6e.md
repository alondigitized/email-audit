# Enjoy an EXTRA 20% OFF + These Halloween favorites sell out EARLY
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- Fifty-one Pottery Barn emails. That's where I am now, and this one does something I genuinely didn't expect: it split-screens the 4th of July and Halloween in the same send. On June 30. Summer hasn't technically started and I'm being asked to think about pumpkins and skeleton garlands. I get the playbook — Halloween sells out fast and Pottery Barn has trained a segment of their list to panic-buy it in July — but it makes the email feel schizophrenic. There's a sale I actually care about (the 4th of July extra 20% off is right in the hero) and then it pivots to a spooky mood board that has nothing to do with why I opened.
- The "Recommended Just For You" module underneath is the email's saving grace — I can see bedding, a bed setup, and what looks like dining or living room pieces that are actually in the zone for what I'm furnishing. That section pulled my attention back. But the structural problem is real: this email can't decide if it's a sale email or a seasonal preview, and it ends up being only 80% of either.
- I'd mark this as read and maybe click back to the sale banner, but I'm not forwarding it. It's too mixed-message to share. And the Halloween pitch on the last day of June reads desperate rather than clever, especially when CB2's "Sale of the Summer" — which I audited earlier this week — kept its energy focused on exactly one thing.

## What's working

- **4th of July hero is clean and leads with the offer**: "Extra 20% off" lands immediately above the fold, no hunting required. That's consistent with what the June 29 curated "TOP picks" email did, which I scored an 8.
- **Recommended Just For You module is doing real work**: White bedding, bed frames, living room lifestyle — those are actually the categories I'm actively shopping. The grid format is scannable.
- **Rewards and Affirm callouts in the footer**: Earn 50% Back in Rewards is visible and Affirm financing is called out, which matters when you're buying furniture for two houses at once. I notice these.
- **New Arrivals lifestyle shot is beautiful**: That neutral linen sofa image is the kind of content I save to a folder. Pottery Barn knows how to shoot rooms.

## What's weak

- **Halloween on June 30 is tonally incoherent**: I respect the sell-out-early strategy, but the transition from red-white-and-blue to dark autumnal imagery in the same scroll is whiplash. Article kept their 4th of July send single-channel and it landed cleaner.
- **"EXTRA 20% OFF" for the third consecutive day is losing meaning**: I counted June 28, June 29, and now June 30 with the same hook. The signal is depreciating. Even the June 28 duvet email — which I thought was smart for pivoting creative — was still anchored to the same offer. My brain is starting to tune it out.
- **No single product hero linked to the sale**: The 4th of July banner goes to "SHOP ALL SALE" — that's a homepage dump. Wayfair at least tied their rug push to a specific category. Give me a room or a product, not a department.
- **The "Early Halloween Shop" CTA copy is clunky**: "SHOP THE EARLY HALLOWEEN SHOP" reads like a draft. Two instances of "shop" in one button label is sloppy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Separate the 4th of July send from the Halloween preview — they can't share real estate.** This email should be one thing. If Halloween is the big idea, own it. If the sale is the lead, drop the Halloween module entirely and run it as its own send in early July when the surprise factor is higher.
- 2. **Hero CTA should land on a specific sale category, not the full sale page.** "Shop Furniture Sale" or "Shop Bedding Sale" would convert better than dumping me on the homepage. CB2 did this better in their "Sale of the Summer" send.
- 3. **Retire "EXTRA 20% OFF" as a subject for at least 3 days.** The offer is real but the repetition has killed the subject line. This is the fourth variant I've seen this week with the same anchor.
- 4. **Fix "SHOP THE EARLY HALLOWEEN SHOP" to something like "Shop Halloween Favorites."** One verb, one noun.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Halloween's selling out early — and yes, the 4th of July sale is still on`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Grab these before they're gone: Halloween + 20% off select sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns just dropped — plus, the Halloween items people screenshot every year`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Extra 20% off through the weekend, and your fall shopping list starts now`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Extra 20% Off + Halloween" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard-broken links (403) will cause click failures for the loyalty banner CTA and a cross-brand image dependency. Three images load over plain HTTP, which modern clients will block or flag as mixed-content.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**BROKEN — CTA link returns 403:**
- `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618...` — the loyalty/Apply-and-Buy banner CTA (`cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`) is dead. Clicking it yields a 403, which means the campaign token is expired, the short-link record was never provisioned, or the `cp_tp` JWT is invalid/expired.

**BROKEN — Cross-brand spacer image returns 403 (also HTTP):**
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` — a West Elm CDN asset used in a Pottery Barn send. Request returns 403. This is a stale cross-brand asset reference; replace with a hosted Pottery Barn equivalent or remove the spacer entirely.

**25 tracking links skipped** — click-redirect domains (`click.e.potterybarn.com`) were not probed. Confirm all destination URLs resolve correctly in staging before send.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP headers embedded in CSS block (source corruption):**
```html
<style>
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, ...");
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
</style>
```
Server-side PHP `header()` calls appear literally inside a `<style>` tag. This is a template rendering failure — the PHP was not executed and the raw source leaked into the HTML. These lines are invalid CSS and will cause parse errors in strict renderers.

**Alt text missing on 32+ images** — including all primary content images (hero banners, product tiles, footer slices, pixel trackers). Images-off rendering (common in Outlook, Gmail with blocked images) produces a fully blank email. High-priority items:
- `S26_Top_July4Sale_NoUrgency.jpg` — hero/top banner
- `slices_01.jpg` / `slices_02.jpg` — Halloween sneak peek content
- All 18 `Email-Footer/slices_*.jpg` images

**Three images served over HTTP (will be blocked):**
| Image | URL |
|---|---|
| `spacer_0.gif` | `http://edm.westelm.com/...` |
| `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/...` |
| `slices_02.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/...` |
| Open pixel | `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597...` |

Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook 365 block mixed-content images by default. The open-tracking pixel (`svopn.potterybarn.com`) is also HTTP — open-rate data for this send will be undercounted.

**`<title>` is empty** — `<title></title>` breaks accessibility in screen readers and appears as a blank tab label in browser-rendered previews.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header not detected** — the relay may have stripped it, but this must be confirmed. Missing `List-Unsubscribe` blocks Gmail's one-click unsubscribe UI and can trigger spam folder placement for bulk sends.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) not detected** — required for Gmail and Yahoo one-click compliance since February 2024. If absent at the MTA level (not just relay capture), this is a deliverability defect.

**SPF/DKIM/DMARC authentication status unknown** — `Authentication-Results` header not captured by the relay. Sending domain is `e.potterybarn.com`; confirm DKIM is signed for this subdomain and DMARC policy is enforced. Cannot verify from available data.

**Open pixel over HTTP** (`http://svopn.potterybarn.com`) — some privacy-focused clients (Apple MPP, Fastmail) will either block or proxy this. No direct compliance violation, but it corrupts open metrics.
