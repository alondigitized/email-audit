# Hundreds of new items added! Shop up to 60% off + Double Rewards
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-four. That's my rough count of emails from the Crate & Barrel family since Sunday, and this one lands with a detail I noticed immediately: the subject line says "Hundreds of new items added" but the hero inside says "400+ New Items Added" — which is the exact same language from the "400+ NEW DEALS ADDED" email I already got today. So this isn't actually a new creative. It's a non-opener rebroadcast with a softer subject line swap, and I opened both because I'm apparently a masochist with a well-organized inbox folder.
- Here's the thing though: the email itself is not bad. The TheDesignDesk editorial insert is a genuine differentiator — it's the kind of slow-burn content that a renovator like me actually appreciates, and C&B is one of the few brands smart enough to mix editorial into a sale blast. The personalized "we have these for you" product row feels like it's trying. Compared to Article's "The deals are in full swing" from earlier this week — which was confident and didn't try to be anything it wasn't — this feels like two emails fighting for the same pixel.
- I would not click today. Not because the offer is bad — 60% off plus Double Rewards on furniture is genuinely interesting when I'm outfitting two homes — but because I already made that decision when I saw the first version. Mark as read. No forward.

## What's working

- **The TheDesignDesk section is legitimately useful.** "Refresh your home with the design desk" and a feature table sitting in the middle of a sale email is a smart content move — I notice it, I slow down, it makes C&B feel like more than a clearance rack.
- **60% off with Double Rewards is the best combined offer I've seen this week** — Pottery Barn's "extra 20% off select styles" from this morning felt tighter and more exclusionary. This reads as broader coverage.
- **Visual hierarchy holds.** Hero first, offer legible without zooming, "Shop Now" button is findable. Clean enough to skim in a pre-meeting inbox check.
- **No render bugs.** After a week of Memorial Day blasts, clean text and no broken tiles counts as a win.
- **The "we have these for you" personalization section** is showing me furniture-category items. Whether it's truly personalized or a generic fallback, it lands closer to relevant than a food shot would.

## What's weak

- **The subject says "Hundreds," the hero says "400+."** That mismatch is visible the moment you open it. "Hundreds" is weaker, vaguer, and less motivating than the specific number. This rebroadcast should have just kept "400+."
- **Food shot in the product grid** — there's what looks like a bowl of food in the second row. I'm renovating two homes, not planning a dinner party photo shoot. That tile breaks the furniture-shopping mental model I'm in.
- **TheDesignDesk appears twice** — once mid-email, once near the footer. One placement is editorial value; two feels like padding to justify the send.
- **No expiration date visible anywhere.** "Memorial Day" is an implied deadline, but I don't know if this sale ends Sunday night or Tuesday. Wayfair's clearance email at least gestured at urgency. This doesn't.
- **Twenty-fourth email, same creative.** The non-opener rebroadcast is a legitimate tactic; hitting someone who opened email one with a slightly reworded subject for email twenty-four is just noise.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to one number — use "400+" everywhere, not "Hundreds."** The mismatch is a trust haircut on a day when you're asking me to click for the second time in 12 hours.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline.** Even "Sale ends Monday at midnight" in the hero would force a decision. CB2's editorial emails don't do this either, which is why Article ("The deals are in full swing") actually converts better on urgency — they commit to a window.
- 3. **Cut the duplicate TheDesignDesk block.** One editorial insert mid-email is editorial. Two is filler. Free up that space for a third product row with specific furniture categories (dining, bedroom, outdoor) so I can self-sort.
- 4. **The food tile needs to go.** Segment the product grid to furniture/home only for someone with my browse history.
- 5. **If this is a rebroadcast to non-openers, don't send it to people who already opened.** This is suppression hygiene, not creative — but it's the single highest-leverage fix.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sofas, dining, outdoor — the full sale, now with more to shop.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Double Rewards stacks. New arrivals added. Sale ends Monday.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Conflicting `img` max-width breakpoints**
Two desktop media queries target overlapping viewport ranges with inconsistent values:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
On viewports 640–767px, `max-width: 640px` applies (correct). On viewports ≥768px, the first rule overrides to `max-width: 600px`, shrinking images 40px below the container width. The 768px rule should be removed or its value corrected to 640px.

**[MEDIUM] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on iOS, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) failure. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[LOW] Wildcard `*` resets `text-size-adjust` and `line-height`**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; mso-line-height-rule:exactly; line-height: 100% }
```
Applying `mso-line-height-rule:exactly` globally via `*` is known to clip descenders and cause text overflow in Outlook. Scope this to specific selectors (e.g., `td`, `p`) rather than all elements.

**[LOW] Light-mode-only color scheme declaration**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Locks Apple Mail and supported clients to light mode only. If the design does not handle dark mode, this is intentional — but it should be a documented decision, not a default.
