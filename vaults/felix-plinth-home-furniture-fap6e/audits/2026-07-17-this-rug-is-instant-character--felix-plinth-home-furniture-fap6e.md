# This rug is instant character
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-three. That's where CB2 sits in my July inbox after this one, and I'm counting again because the formula is staring me in the face: yesterday it was "New chairs. Instant character." Today it's "This rug is instant character." I see what they're doing. It's a campaign arc, or they ran out of ideas and swapped one noun for another. The honest answer is probably somewhere in between.
- The email itself is a "Modern Vintage" lookbook — no sale, no price, no offer. Just a collage of rugs in earthy taupes and sage greens, a "How to Make the Room" editorial mid-section, and a "Summer Edit" grid at the bottom. Rugs are actually on my list for both houses right now, which means the timing is fine and the category is right. But CB2 keeps sending me mood boards when I need transactions. Pottery Barn's indigo color story email from yesterday at least showed me product at the bottom. This one wants me to feel something before I do anything.
- I'd open it — that subject is short enough to read in the notification shade and "this rug" implies a specific thing, not a landing page. I might even scroll through the Summer Edit grid. But I'm not clicking to a product page without a price anchor. Mark as read, save the screenshot to my folder, come back when there's a sale.

## What's working

- **"Modern Vintage" positioning is actually right for me.** I'm trying to blend some character into two houses that currently feel like empty contractor boxes. That headline does the translation work.
- **The hero image is all rug, no distraction.** The collage of five or six different styles — geometric, hand-knotted, flat-weave — functions like a quick style filter. I could clock my preference in about three seconds.
- **"Summer Edit" section at the bottom gives me a real grid.** It's the closest thing to a catalog page in this email, and that's how I actually shop rugs — by scanning options side by side.
- **Short, clean subject.** Thirty characters, no caps, no punctuation panic. It reads like a human wrote it, which is more than I can say for Wayfair's "Must-have Sofa & Console Tables" from earlier this week.

## What's weak

- **The "instant character" formula is already 24 hours old.** Yesterday's chairs email used it. Using the same tagline on a different product the very next day makes it feel templated, not editorial. Article ran four sale sends before pivoting to a home tour — at least they earned the creative reset. This feels like CB2 just swapped a noun.
- **No price. No offer. No reason to click today versus next week.** I buy via my highest-rewards card, which means I'm already optimizing the transaction — but I need a transaction to optimize. A mood board doesn't give me one.
- **The CTAs in the body appear to be small text links, not buttons.** On mobile, that's a tap-target problem. Compare to Pottery Barn's indigo email, which had visible button callouts below each section. CB2 is making me hunt.
- **No sizing or pile information visible.** I'm buying for two specific rooms with actual dimensions. Seeing a rug I like with no size context means one more click before I know anything actionable.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one price or sale hook to the hero.** Even "from $149" or "Summer Edit now on sale" changes the decision from "I'll come back to this" to "I should look now." This is the single change that moves clicks.
- 2. **Break the "instant character" pattern.** The tagline is a week old by the time this lands. If CB2 is running a rug campaign, give it its own identity — "The rug is the room" or "Start with the floor" would still be short and punchy without feeling like a chair email with the noun swapped.
- 3. **Promote the Summer Edit grid above the fold on mobile.** The editorial mid-section ("How to Make the Room") is good content, but it's delay before product. Move the grid up, or at least add a jump anchor.
- 4. **Show sizes in the Summer Edit grid.** Even "8x10 from $X" thumbnails would let me pre-qualify without leaving the email. Article does this better on their product sends — the key detail is right on the card.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — prevents users from pinching to zoom. Violates WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). This is a hard accessibility defect.
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

**Global `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `*` selector** — disables OS-level text scaling for all elements. Compounds the zoom issue above; screen-reader-adjacent users who rely on browser font scaling are affected.
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;-ms-text-size-adjust:none;...}
```

**Mobile breakpoint targets only `max-width: 418px`** — the single responsive breakpoint is calibrated for ≤418px screens. Common modern viewports (e.g., iPhone 15 Pro at 393px logical width, Pixel 8 at 412px) fall at or near the boundary. No intermediate breakpoint exists between 418px and 690px, leaving a 272px gap where layout is neither mobile-optimized nor full-width desktop.

**`line-height: 100%` applied globally** — the `*{}` reset sets `line-height: 100%`. For text elements, `100%` resolves to the element's font-size, which collapses leading on multi-line body copy. Standard for email clients is `1.4`–`1.6` on paragraph text.

**No dark mode (`prefers-color-scheme: dark`) media query** — background is hardcoded `#ffffff`. In dark-mode email clients (Gmail app, iOS Mail), images with white backgrounds may render as white boxes against a dark UI.

**Duplicate `@font-face` family name collision**:
- "Lineto Brown Light Sub" declared separately AND as weight-400 under "Lineto Brown Sub"
- Browsers may resolve unpredictably when both family names are referenced in inline styles

**Template comment left in source**:
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
Typo ("IMPUT") and unfilled placeholder comment shipped in production HTML. No functional breakage, but signals incomplete template cleanup.
