# The wine glasses you'll use for every occasion →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one's actually fine — better than fine, honestly. It's a wine glasses email that is *about wine glasses*, top to bottom, no baby nurseries, no kids' backpacks derailing my inbox. After the week Crate & Barrel's put me through — five backpack sends, a celebrity nursery, and only one kitchen email that actually landed — this feels like the brand remembering I'm a grown adult renovating two houses who might actually buy stemware.
- The "our bestselling wine glasses ever! ★★★★" line up top with the star rating is a nice trust cue, and I like that they immediately follow with "oversized and so hip" and acrylic glasses "just dropped" — there's real variety in the glass styles, not just one SKU repeated five times. But by the time I hit the amaro spritz recipe, the ginger-thyme paloma, the Lovepop bakery collab, the back-to-school section, and a $5,000 Design Desk sweepstakes, I've completely lost the plot. This is not a wine glasses email anymore, it's a full department store circular that happens to open with wine glasses.
- Would I click? Yeah, probably the "oversized and so hip" glass or the acrylic ones — I actually need glasses for the lake house bar cart. Would I forward it? No. Would I scroll all the way to the bottom? Also no — I'm out somewhere around the bakery collab.

## What's working

- Hero section is genuinely on-category: "Shop Durable Wine Glasses" with a clean product shot, no clutter.
- Multiple distinct wine glass styles shown (classic stemmed, oversized, acrylic, stemless at $2.95) — this doesn't feel like the same lamp in three emails, it's real product breadth within one category.
- The star rating on the bestseller glasses is a legit trust signal I don't usually get from this brand.
- "we love these for you" personalized product strip near the bottom is the kind of segmentation I actually want to see more of.

## What's weak

- The recipe/cocktail cards (amaro spritz, ginger-thyme paloma) are cute but derail the email — I opened this for glasses, not a happy-hour blog post.
- The Lovepop bakery collab and back-to-school section have nothing to do with wine glasses or me — this is the same scattergun problem as the backpack emails, just buried further down instead of being the whole send.
- $5,000 Design Desk sweepstakes at the bottom feels bolted on, not integrated — CB2 would never let a sweepstakes banner sit next to a decor bundle like this.
- By the app-download footer I've forgotten this was a wine glass email at all.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the length by 60%.** Wine glasses → maybe one cocktail pairing → done. Everything after the "these go great in a wine glass, too" section (recipes, bakery collab, back-to-school, sweepstakes) belongs in separate, better-targeted sends. Pottery Barn's living-room email I scored 8/10 stayed tight to one theme the whole way down — that's the model.
- 2. **Lead the stemless glasses higher.** "$2.95, buy stems and bowls separately" is the actual value story here and it's buried two-thirds down.
- 3. **Drop the sweepstakes entirely from a product email.** It reads like three different teams' campaigns got merged into one send.
- 4. **Add a shipping/breakage note for glassware** — this is stemware, fragile, and there's zero mention of how it ships or their breakage policy, which matters more here than for a throw pillow.
