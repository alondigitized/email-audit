# Furniture on sale: Up to 50% off right now!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-07**
## Executive summary

- Crate again, and it's the Kids sender for the third or fourth time since yesterday. My first thought was "I already got the wallpaper one and the Venus Williams one today." But this is the first Crate send in days that leads with a plain furniture discount instead of a mood story. "Limited time only! Up to 50% off furniture" sits right above a big shop button, and I got the offer immediately.
- I'm renovating two houses, not furnishing a nursery. Bunk beds, cribs, a glider and a kids' dresser are a stretch for me, and the Jellycat and baby bedding tiles are noise. The dresser could pass for a regular bedroom piece, so there's a little crossover. I'd skim it, maybe save the dresser to my Pinterest folder, and not forward it.
- The email is long, with at least seven product blocks, and the layout works. Pottery Barn Kids and Wayfair do this same "sale on everything" pitch, but Wayfair at least tells me up front which category the sale covers.

## What's working

- The "limited time only! up to 50% off furniture" headline is big, clean and impossible to miss.
- Each product has its own block with a name, a testimonial-style quote and a "Shop Now" button. That's the kind of detail I want when I'm comparing pieces.
- The "more ways to save" grid breaks out bedding, lighting and storage discounts without cluttering the hero. It's easy to scan.
- The "free shipping on toys $99+" banner and the Design Desk "book a free appointment" block at the bottom give me a service angle. I like the showroom and design help.
- Nothing looks broken. I see no overlapping text and no empty image slots.

## What's weak

- Cadence. The subject says "right now", but I've had a Crate-family email nearly every few hours since 10/06. The urgency doesn't feel real.
- The offer is "up to 50%", and nothing I can see tells me which pieces are actually at 50%. The deadline is "limited time" with no date.
- The content is nursery and kids furniture. Nothing is aimed at a 36-year-old furnishing two houses.
- The email is too long. Pottery Barn would have cut the Jellycat, app and credit-card blocks. Here they dilute the sale.
- There's no color or finish guidance, no swatch or sample offer, and no delivery timing for the big pieces. A crib or a bunk bed is a long-lead item, and I want to know when it arrives.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a hard end date and one anchor product with a real price in the hero. "Ends Sunday" beats "limited time only!"
- 2. Add a delivery-window line for the big pieces ("Ships in 2-3 weeks"). That would do more than another product block.
- 3. Cut the email down to the sale, the "more ways to save" grid and the Design Desk block. Move Jellycat and the app promo to a different send.
- 4. Add rewards math. If the Crate card gives extra points on furniture, put that next to the offer. I buy on the highest-rewards card, so a rewards callout would have my attention.
- 5. Space out the sends. Two Crate-family emails in a day is one too many. CB2 had the same problem with its Long Weekend Sale.

## Technical Audit

**1. Technical Summary**
The email is a large, table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional template with roughly 94 tracked links, and it passes 57% of the automated checks. The main issues are missing alt text on nearly every image, and List-Unsubscribe and authentication headers that couldn't be verified. The headers may be a limitation of the AgentMail relay rather than the sender.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- The QA run skipped HTTP probing of 94 tracking links because they use click-redirect domains. Link validity and destinations are unverified, so no broken links are confirmed.
- Tracking pixels and beacons are present in the QA data:
  - `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif?mi_u=…`
  - `https://dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/04c16fc6-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…`
  - Five `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=…&n=1..5` beacons. The `rlcdn.com` domain is LiveRamp's third-party identity-sync pixel.
- The `mi_u=dbe8da71…` token (the same value as `s=` in the rlcdn URLs) is a per-recipient identifier. It is passed to a third-party domain and repeated across multiple pixels. This is a privacy and deliverability consideration, not a defect.
- The `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png` image appears six times with the same per-recipient token. This looks like a dynamic or real-time personalization image.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- Alt text is missing on about 50 images, and nearly all content images are affected.
  - Examples: `…/m/1/6cda691d-b295-47d6-aa7c-8c52d1eec48a.jpg`, `…/m/47/bbf6f9d4-…png`, and the `.gif` files `30cf0d67-…` and `ed972f76-…`.
  - Screen readers will announce filenames, or nothing at all. If images are blocked, the email will show blank blocks.
  - Spacer image: `https://s7d5.scene7.com/is/image/Crate/25%5FMI%5FBottom%5FSpacer%5F40px%5FWhite`.
  - The six `f3c0d22992a01946.png` instances may be dynamic product or price images, so missing alt text there hides potentially important content.
  - Tracking pixels should use `alt=""`, but the QA data shows them without any `alt`.
- Where the HTML shows it:
  - `<html lang="en">` is set, which is good.
  - The `<title>` is `Crate & Barrel`. The ampersand is unescaped (`&` instead of `&amp;`), which is invalid in XHTML 1.0.
  - `<meta name="viewport" … maximum-scale=1>` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile, which is an accessibility concern.
  - The `format-detection` meta tags are declared repeatedly (`telephone=no,address=no,…`, then separate duplicates). This is redundant but harmless.
  - The `p{margin:0 auto; text-align:center;}` and `*{line-height:100%}` global rules may cause unintended text alignment and cramped line spacing. This is minor.
- The source was truncated, so I couldn't check for ARIA roles, `role="presentation"` on layout tables, or the full mobile layout.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
- No issues found in the visible source. No unresolved merge tokens such as `%%…%%`, `{{…}}` or `[FIRST_NAME]` appear in the truncated HTML or QA findings.
- One template comment is left in the production markup: `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->`. It is harmless but shows an unfinished template (and has a typo).

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
- `List-Unsubscribe` not found [WARN].
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found [WARN].
- `Authentication-Results` not found [WARN].
- Because of the relay, these can't be confirmed as sender defects. The QA note says they may simply not be captured by AgentMail. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require one-click unsubscribe, so this should be checked against the raw message headers.
- The sender domain is `mail.crateandbarrel.com`, a dedicated sending subdomain. SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment are unverified.
- The footer's unsubscribe link and physical address could not be checked, because the truncated HTML doesn't reach the footer and no QA check covered them.

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
- No UTM parameters or landing page URLs could be assessed. All 94 links go through click-redirect domains and were not resolved.
- The destination of the "Furniture on sale: Up to 50% off" links, and whether they land on a matching sale page, are unverified.
- Tracking is likely done by redirect (`mi_cid`, `mi_mid`) rather than UTM tags. That would make this a different attribution method, not a defect.

**7. Recommendations**
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to all content images. Use `alt=""` for spacers and tracking pixels, and put the offer text in alt text for dynamic images such as `f3c0d22992a01946.png`.
2. Check the raw message headers for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC results. This will show whether the missing-header warnings are real or an artifact of the relay.
3. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag so users can zoom.
4. Escape `&` as `&amp;` in the `<title>` and any other text nodes.
5. Resolve a sample of the redirect links manually or with a redirect-following probe. Confirm the final destinations and any UTM or campaign parameters, and check that they match the sale landing page.
6. Consider auditing the third-party `rlcdn.com` beacons for privacy and consent compliance. Five repeated pixels add weight and external dependencies.
7. Remove leftover template comments such as `IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT` from production sends.
