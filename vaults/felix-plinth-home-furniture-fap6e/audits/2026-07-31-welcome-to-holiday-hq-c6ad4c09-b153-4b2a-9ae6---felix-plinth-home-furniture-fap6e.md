# Welcome to Holiday HQ 🎃🦃🎄
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-31**
## Executive summary

- Okay, it's August 4th and Pottery Barn just declared Halloween-Thanksgiving-Christmas open season in one scroll, on top of a "Summer Sale: Up to 70% off" banner sitting directly above it. That's the same bait-and-switch move I flagged on the "special Halloween sneak peek 👻" send two days ago — subject says one season, hero banner says another — except this time it's not even hidden, it's the literal top strip of the email contradicting the "Holiday Headquarters" headline four inches below it.
- The "Recommended Just For You" grid is honestly the best part — curtain rods, a farmhouse platform bed, dining table, towels, a swivel chair, duvet cover — that's actually stuff that could be pulled from my browsing history since I'm mid-renovation on two houses. But it's buried under three "shop the shop" seasonal blocks I don't care about in early August, and then followed by yet another "Discover New Arrivals" and "Up to 60% off Outdoor" block, so by the time I get to Rewards and the Affirm financing pitch at the bottom I've already mentally checked out.
- Would I click? Maybe the "Recommended" row if I'm bored on the train, but I'm not opening this with intent. Wouldn't forward it — nothing here is gift-worthy or renovation-specific enough to send a friend. Marking as read and moving on.

## What's working

- The "Recommended Just For You" grid actually reads like it knows I'm furnishing houses right now — bed frame, dining table, curtain hardware, towels — that's real relevance, not filler.
- Product photography throughout is clean and consistent — no render bugs, no broken images, no lorem ipsum.
- The Affirm "Buy Now, Pay Over Time" callout at the bottom is genuinely useful for me right now given I'm furnishing two homes simultaneously, even if it's buried too low to matter.
- Rewards section (10% back) is visible and relevant since I already use a rewards card strategy for exactly this kind of spend.

## What's weak

- "Summer Sale: Up to 70% off" hero strip directly contradicts "Holiday Headquarters" right underneath it — pick a season, PB. This is the same whiplash as the Halloween sneak-peek email two days ago, and now it's back-to-back internal contradiction in one send.
- Three near-identical "Shop the [Season] Shop" blocks (Halloween, Thanksgiving, Christmas) in early August is calendar creep nobody asked for — Crate & Barrel at least commits to one seasonal bit at a time instead of stacking three.
- Zero mention of swatches, fabric samples, or in-store design consultation — the RSVP for free design services email from yesterday actually nailed that beat; this one regresses to generic catalog dump.
- The "Recommended Just For You" personalization gets undercut by a wall of generic promo blocks both above and below it — Wayfair's "Open Box Deals" send at least kept its personalization near the top instead of sandwiching it.
- No pricing, no specific discount tied to any of the seasonal shops or the "Discover New Arrivals" / outdoor blocks other than the vague top-line 70% and bottom-line 60% — feels like bait-and-switch numbers with no attached product.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the "Summer Sale: Up to 70% off" hero strip entirely or move the holiday content to its own send — don't let two contradictory seasonal messages fight for the same eyeball in the first screen.
- 2. Move "Recommended Just For You" to the top of the email, right under the hero — that's the section with actual signal, don't bury it under three shop blocks I'll scroll past.
- 3. Cut two of the three seasonal shop blocks (Halloween/Thanksgiving/Christmas) down to one carousel — nobody needs three full-width photo blocks for "it's holiday season eventually."
- 4. Add a swatch/sample CTA or design-consultation link somewhere in the body — you had it dialed in on the RSVP email, don't let it disappear the next send.
- 5. Give the outdoor and new-arrivals blocks actual numbers ("$249 sectional," "Save $80 on this rug") instead of vague percentage banners with no anchor price.

# Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Welcome to Holiday HQ" Email

## 1. Technical Summary
Standard Responsys-style (MI/`mi_mid`) transactional HTML email with heavy image reliance and ESP-wrapped tracking links; automated QA passed 57% of checks, surfacing widespread missing alt text, mixed-content image sources, and missing unsubscribe/authentication headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **47 tracking/redirect links skipped from HTTP probing** (e.g., `https://click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=ABB7...`) — destination validity cannot be confirmed from this audit; recommend manual resolution of a sample.
- **Open-tracking pixels present and non-HTTPS**: `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` — plaintext recipient email is passed as a GET parameter over unencrypted HTTP, exposing PII in transit and in any logs/referrers.
- Additional tracking beacons: `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/...` (third-party ad pixel) and `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/03a23461-.../?mi_mid=...` — both functioning as 1x1 tracking images, consistent with expected ESP behavior.
- **Invalid embedded code in `<style>` block**: literal PHP `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: no-store...")` statements are present as plain text inside a CSS `<style>` tag. This is inert (ignored by renderers) but indicates leftover/misplaced server-side code from the template source — a template hygiene bug, not a functional break.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **35 images missing `alt` text** across header, hero, product, and footer slices (full list in QA output, e.g. `slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`, product images like `farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `remmy-swivel-chair-1-l.jpg`). With images blocked by default in Outlook/Gmail, recipients see nothing but blank space — this is the highest-volume finding.
- **Empty `<title></title>`** tag — no fallback title for clients that surface it.
- Doctype/meta setup (XHTML 1.0 Transitional, MSO conditional comments, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`) is standard legacy-ESP boilerplate; no issues found there.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- Recipient identifier `lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` resolves correctly everywhere it's merged (customer-service link, open-tracking pixel) — no unresolved merge tags (e.g., stray `%%FIELD%%` or `{{token}}`) found in the sampled source.
- No name-based or product-recommendation merge fields are visible in the truncated source to evaluate further.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication)
- **List-Unsubscribe header not found** (QA WARN) — may be a relay capture artifact rather than sender omission; cannot confirm from this data alone.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found (RFC 8058)** — one-click unsubscribe cannot be confirmed as supported. This matters for Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender requirements if Pottery Barn sends at volume.
- **Authentication-Results header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown; likely stripped by the AgentMail relay rather than a genuine auth failure. Flagged as inconclusive, not a confirmed failure.
- Visible in-body unsubscribe link was not present in the truncated HTML sample provided (source cuts off before the footer's unsubscribe block) — cannot confirm CAN-SPAM visible-unsubscribe compliance from this excerpt; recommend checking the full footer.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- No standard `utm_source`/`utm_medium`/`utm_campaign` parameters observed on sampled links; tracking instead uses ESP-native encoded query strings (`qs=`, `sv_cid=`, `mi_mid=`) via `click.e.potterybarn.com`. Landing-page alignment cannot be assessed since these are redirect-wrapped and were skipped by the HTTP probe.
- One direct (non-wrapped) production link was captured: `https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` — plaintext email in a GET parameter to a live production domain, same PII-exposure concern as in §2.
- Cross-brand asset reference: `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` appears inside a Pottery Barn email — likely shared Williams-Sonoma-family ESP infrastructure, but worth confirming it's intentional and not a template leak.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to all 35 flagged images, prioritizing hero/promo banners which carry the primary message when images are blocked.
2. Convert the 3 `http://` image sources (`spacer_0.gif`, `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, `slices_02.jpg`) to `https://` to avoid mixed-content blocking in modern mail clients.
3. Remove the stray PHP `header()` statements from the `<style>` block — dead code left over from template assembly.
4. Confirm/add `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) headers to support one-click unsubscribe, required for Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender compliance.
5. Verify original SPF/DKIM/DMARC results at the source MTA, since `Authentication-Results` was not captured by the relay — do not treat this as a confirmed auth failure without checking raw headers pre-relay.
6. Stop passing the recipient's raw email address as a plaintext GET parameter on the open-tracking pixel and the customer-service link; use a hashed/tokenized subscriber ID instead.
7. Populate the empty `<title>` tag.
8. Resolve a sample of the 47 `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirect links to confirm final landing pages match campaign intent (currently unverifiable from this audit).
