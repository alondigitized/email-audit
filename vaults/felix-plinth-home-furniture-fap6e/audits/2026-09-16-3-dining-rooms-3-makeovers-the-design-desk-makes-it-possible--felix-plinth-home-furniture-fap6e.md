# 3 dining rooms, 3 makeovers. The Design Desk makes it possible →
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-16**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one actually made me stop scrolling — "let our pros handle your dining room makeover, no matter the size" — because I've got two dining rooms in play right now and zero clue how to size furniture for either. The Design Desk angle is smart: free design consult, then three actual room scenarios (small nook, open-plan, showstopper) instead of just dumping product cards at me. That's a real structural upgrade over the usual C&B blast.
- That said, it's still Crate & Barrel, and I've had five of their emails in my inbox this week between the main brand, Crate & Kids, and CB2 — so by the time I hit "we love these for you" at the bottom I'm fatigued, not motivated. And there's zero pricing anywhere in this email. Not one number. For a guy tracking rewards math across three catalogs, that's a dealbreaker for actually clicking through to buy.
- I'd click "Book Your Free Appointment" out of curiosity since design help is genuinely useful for my situation, but I would not forward this to anyone — it's too generic-looking under the good framing. Mark as read, maybe bookmark for later.

## What's working

- The "no matter the size" hook speaks directly to my actual problem — small nook vs. open-plan vs. big room — which is more segmented than Wayfair's "we bet will sell out" nothing-burger from a couple weeks back.
- Three distinct room scenarios (small nook, open dining, showstopper) with different furniture pairings feels like actual design guidance, not just a product wall.
- The Design Desk free-consult CTA is a genuinely differentiated offer — nobody else in my inbox this week (not Article, not CB2) is pitching a real human design service.
- Photography is warm and consistent — same wood tones, same styling language across all three room shots, so it doesn't feel like a stitched-together clearance email like the Pottery Barn warehouse sale did.

## What's weak

- No price anywhere — not on the hero, not on any of the three room breakdowns, not even the "we love these for you" product grid at the bottom. I can't do rewards math on furniture I don't know the cost of.
- This is at least the third C&B-family email I've opened this week (plus the Crate & Kids "Spooky season" repeat I already flagged twice), so my brain is starting to auto-snooze anything with that logo.
- "the fall gathering guide" section near the bottom is a scope-creep tangent — I'm mid-renovation, not planning a dinner party yet, and it dilutes the dining-room focus that hooked me up top.
- The bottom half (living room, bedroom, entertaining, app download, credit card pitch) turns into a grab-bag that undercuts the tight three-room story from the top half.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price range on every single product card — even "starting at $XXX" would let me triage which of the three room scenarios fits my budget before I click anything.
- 2. Cut the email after the three dining scenarios and the Design Desk CTA — kill the living room/bedroom/entertaining/app/credit-card stack, or split it into a separate send. Right now it's two emails glued together.
- 3. Lead with a specific product, not just styled rooms — Article's "Home tour: A welcoming workspace" scored well with me because it named the Torme desk and Bea chair by name; here I don't know a single SKU without clicking through.
- 4. Swap "Book Your Free Appointment" for something that signals what happens next, like "Get My Dining Room Plan" — more concrete, less like a dentist visit.
