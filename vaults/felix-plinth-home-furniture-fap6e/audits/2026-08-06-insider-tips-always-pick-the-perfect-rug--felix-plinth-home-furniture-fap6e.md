# Insider tips: Always pick the perfect rug →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-06**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so Crate & Barrel just sent me a full guide on how to buy a rug — "10 tips to always pick the perfect rug" — and honestly, this is the most useful thing they've sent me today, which is a low bar because today also brought me the chandelier edit, the Crate & Kids backpack drop, and the Crate & Kids sweepstakes. This one at least reads like actual content instead of just another product dump: furniture placement, material picks, a size cheat sheet. I'm mid-reno on two houses and one of them desperately needs living room rugs, so the topic itself isn't wrong.
- But by the time I scroll past rugs into kids' rugs, bedding, a $135-off Le Creuset Dutch oven, a bakery collab, a honeymoon sweepstakes, and a "TheDesignEdit" cross-promo at the very bottom, it stops feeling like a rug guide and starts feeling like Crate & Barrel dumped their entire content calendar into one email because nobody wanted to trim it down. Wayfair's "Fearless design, bold layers" send stayed in its lane and gave me one clear mood; this one loses the plot two-thirds of the way down.
- I'd open it — the top third is genuinely good — but I'd bail before the Dutch oven section, and I'm not forwarding this to anyone. It's not shareable, it's a scroll marathon.

## What's working

- "10 tips to always pick the perfect rug" is a real hook — it's advice, not just "shop rugs," and that's the kind of content I'd actually read mid-reno.
- The "furniture placement" and "rug size cheat sheet" diagrams are genuinely useful reference material — this is the swatch-and-sample, practical-decision-making content I wish more of these emails had.
- "Try a swatch first — they ship free" is exactly the friction-reducer I want before dropping money on a rug I haven't seen in person.
- Clean, consistent product photography throughout — every room shot looks like the same design language, unlike CB2's "marble that elevates everything" email which felt like two campaigns stapled together.

## What's weak

- This is way too long — by the time I hit Dutch ovens and a bakery collab, I've forgotten this started as a rug guide. Pottery Barn's Warehouse Sale email stayed focused and scored an 8 from me for exactly that reason.
- No price or percent-off anywhere near the rug content itself — just "Shop Now" over and over. Compare that to the $135 off Le Creuset callout further down, which is the only concrete number in the whole email.
- Kids' rugs, bedding, cookware, a honeymoon sweepstakes, and a design-quiz cross-promo all crammed into one send — this is the same sprawl problem I flagged on their chandelier email, just worse.
- The credit card teaser near the bottom ("new! the card you love just got better," "$300") is buried so far down I almost didn't screenshot past it — if that's a real member benefit, it deserves way more than a footer slot.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this into two emails: the rug buying guide (tips, placement, material, size chart, swatches) as one focused send, and split off kids/bedding/cookware/sweepstakes into something else. Right now it's five emails wearing a trenchcoat.
- 2. Put an actual number on the rug offer — % off, free shipping threshold, something — right under the hero. "Shop All Rugs" with no price signal isn't going to beat Pottery Barn's "up to 60% off" this week.
- 3. Move the credit card benefit ($300 callout) up near the top if it's real — that's a loyalty hook, not a footer afterthought.
- 4. Drop the honeymoon sweepstakes and bakery collab entirely from a rug-education email — neither has anything to do with why I opened this.
