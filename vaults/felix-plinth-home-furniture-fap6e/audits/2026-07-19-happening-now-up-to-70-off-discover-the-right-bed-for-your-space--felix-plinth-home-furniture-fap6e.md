# Happening now: Up to 70% off + Discover the right bed for your space
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-one. That's the Pottery Barn count in July now, and this is the second one landing today alone — "Don't miss our SUMMER SALE" hit my inbox earlier this morning, which remains the laziest subject line I've seen from any brand this month. So when this one showed up a few hours later, I almost reflexed straight to archive. But I didn't, because the subject actually does something the previous thirty didn't quite nail: it ties the sale to a real decision I'm actively trying to make. I'm genuinely mid-process on beds for the second house. I have browser tabs open. This email knows that.
- The "Find Your Perfect Bed" editorial hook is the thing that saves this send. Instead of just screaming 70% off again, Pottery Barn gives me two clear categories — wood frames and upholstered — with distinct shop pathways. That's useful. That's what I'd pull up on a Saturday when I'm dragging my partner through the swatching process. Article did something similar with their "Home tour: A warm welcome" send a few weeks back, and I gave that credit for the same reason: editorial-first beats discount-first for furniture categories where the decision cycle is long.
- That said, the email doesn't stop there, and that's the problem. By the time I scroll past the bed guide, I hit a "Recommended Just for You" block, then a "Discover New Arrivals" section that's pushing a sectional sofa — not beds — then an app download pitch, then Rewards messaging, then a novel's worth of legal text. The sale message and the buyer's guide and the personalization block and the new arrivals are all competing for priority at the same time. I'd click on the bed content. I would not forward this to a friend. I'd save it to my folder and circle back.

## What's working

- **The "Find Your Perfect Bed" editorial angle is genuinely useful.** Splitting wood vs. upholstered with distinct SHOP NOW paths gives me something concrete to act on, not just a sale landing page.
- **70% off in the hero is visible and unambiguous.** Crate & Barrel's dark-woods bedroom email last week buried the discount below three paragraphs of mood copy — this one leads with the number.
- **The wood bed imagery in the hero section looks aspirational but grounded** — the warm walnut finish and neutral bedding reads like something I'd actually put in the second house, not the kind of overly styled white-on-white that skews feminine and makes me feel like I've accidentally ended up on the wrong list.
- **Pottery Barn Rewards is visible in the footer.** I buy on my highest-rewards card and stack with loyalty points whenever the math works — at least I can see it's there without having to hunt.
- **No broken images or layout issues visible.** After the "SO MUCH NEW" debacle from CB2 a few weeks back where half the grid felt misaligned, a clean render is worth noting.

## What's weak

- **Two emails in one day is a choice, and it's the wrong one.** The morning send was 70% off summer sale. This is also 70% off summer sale, with some bed content bolted on. That's not a thoughtful segmentation strategy — that's queue-dumping.
- **The "Discover New Arrivals" sectional sofa block undercuts the bed focus.** If you told me this email was about beds, I believe you — until I scroll past the editorial content and land on living room furniture. Pick a lane for the send.
- **"SHOP NOW" is a dead-end CTA.** Wayfair's campus email at least made the intent clear in the button copy. "Shop Wood Beds" or "Find My Bed Type" would do more work than a generic action verb that could live on any email in any category.
- **No end date on the sale.** "Happening now" implies urgency but gives me no reason to act today vs. next Tuesday. That's a miss on credible time-binding.
- **The recommended products block feels algorithmically lazy.** I can't tell what specifically triggered those recommendations or whether they're actually in a sale tier. If they're "just for me," tell me why.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the second send or merge it.** One bed-focused email with the sale is sharper than two generic sale emails, the second of which happens to contain bed content. Batch the message once and get out.
- 2. **Add a sale end date to the hero.** Even "Ends Sunday" is enough. "Happening now" is not urgency — it's a timestamp that nobody reads.
- 3. **Make the CTA copy do the work.** Replace "SHOP NOW" with "Shop Wood Beds" and "Shop Upholstered Beds" to match the two editorial tracks. Keep the buyer's guide concept, sharpen the buttons.
- 4. **Drop the sofa new arrivals block from this send.** It dilutes the bed brief. Save it for Monday. Or roll it into a separate email I'll probably also resent receiving.
- 5. **Surface the sale tier for the recommended products.** If those thumbnails are in the 70% off sale, say so inline. If they're not, they have no business being next to a sale hero.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Two bed styles, one sale — find the right fit for your room.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The guide I wish I had when I started shopping beds. Plus: up to 70% off.`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn Email (2026-07-19)

### 1. Technical Summary

Two broken links will cause click failures for credit card CTA and cross-brand tracking pixels. The email also has widespread missing alt text (50+ images), three HTTP image sources that will be blocked by modern clients, and unconfirmed SPF/DKIM authentication status via the relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**BROKEN — Hard failures:**

- **Credit card apply CTA** returns HTTP 403:
  `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&...`
  This is the `PB_header_nonmember` / "Apply and Buy" banner link. Any click on this CTA results in a dead page.

- **West Elm spacer pixel** returns HTTP 403:
  `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
  Cross-brand tracking pixel is also HTTP (not HTTPS) — double failure.

**Skipped (unverified):** 22 tracking/click-redirect links through `click.e.potterybarn.com` were not probed. These should be spot-checked in staging before send.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources — will be blocked by Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook with image security settings:**

| Image | URL |
|---|---|
| `spacer_0.gif` | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` |
| `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` |
| `slices_02.jpg` (footer) | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` |
| Open-pixel | `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` |

**Missing alt text:** 50+ images have no `alt` attribute, including every content slice in the "Find Your Perfect Bed" section (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_16.jpg`), all footer slices, and product images. Images-off rendering will show a completely blank email for screen-reader users and clients that block images by default.

**Inline PHP headers in `<style>` block:** The `<style>` tag in `<head>` contains literal PHP header calls:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache...");
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are dead strings inside CSS — they do nothing — but indicate a template rendering error where server-side code was not executed and leaked raw into the HTML. This is a build/template pipeline bug.

**`<title>` is empty.** Minor but affects some accessibility tools.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header:** Not found via the AgentMail relay. This is likely a relay capture gap rather than a true omission (Pottery Barn's ESP almost certainly sends this header), but it cannot be confirmed from available data. **Must be verified against raw SMTP headers** before treating as clean.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** Not found. Without this, Gmail's one-click unsubscribe button will not appear — Gmail shows the header-based one-click button only when both `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` are present.

**CAN-SPAM:** An unsubscribe mechanism and physical address appear to be present in the footer (inferred from standard Pottery Barn templates; not contradicted by the HTML excerpt). No explicit violation confirmed.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters:** The 22 tracked click links route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud click-tracking). UTM decoration on final destination URLs cannot be verified without resolving the redirect chain — skipped by QA. This should be manually validated for at least the primary CTAs (sale hero, bed finder, product tiles).

**Plain-text version is URL-heavy:** 10,470 of 14,622 characters (71.6%) are URLs. This is a spam signal for plain-text filtering and indicates the plain-text version is auto-generated from HTML without cleanup rather than human-authored.

**Cross-brand asset bleed:** A West Elm domain (`edm.westelm.com`) is used for a Pottery Barn spacer image. This is a template hygiene issue — the asset should be hosted on `edm.potterybarn.com`.
