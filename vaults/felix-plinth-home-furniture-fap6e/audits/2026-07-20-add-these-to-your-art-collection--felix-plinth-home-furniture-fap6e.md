# Add these to your art collection
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-nine. That's where I am with CB2 in July, and I want to give credit where it's due: they actually broke their all-caps streak. "ADD THESE TO YOUR ART COLLECTION" is what I expected from the inbox. What arrived was sentence-case, editorial, almost calm. That alone made me pause longer than I did for "$1K COULD BE YOURS TO WIN" yesterday.
- The email itself is doing something genuine — "Art Education" as a hero concept is the kind of framing that earns a save to my folder. I'm literally choosing wall art for two houses right now. The botanical prints and the framed map are the exact aesthetic I'm wrestling with. If this arrived as email five of July, it would've gotten a real look. As email twenty-nine, I gave it thirty seconds and moved on.
- The $5,000 gift credit sweepstakes buried in the middle is the tell. Yesterday it was $1K to win, today it's $5K. CB2 is doing that thing where they layer a sweepstakes on top of an editorial concept and hope you don't notice they're running the same play twice in 24 hours. I noticed. I would mark this as read, not forward it, and not click — but I would not unsubscribe, because the art in this email is genuinely good.

## What's working

- **The subject line tone shift is real.** Sentence case after weeks of ALL-CAPS promos actually reads as quieter, more confident — closer to how Article writes, which consistently earns my attention.
- **"Art Education" as a frame is smart.** It implies curation, not clearance, which is a different mode for CB2 and matches the editorial aesthetic of the pieces shown.
- **The art itself is strong.** The botanical prints and the framed map are warm, earthy, statement-sized — exactly the pieces I'm weighing against Pottery Barn's catalog this week. The lifestyle shot showing them hung in context is more useful than a white-background product shot.
- **Warm, earthy palette is cohesive.** The amber vases and accessories in the lower grid align with the hero — this feels like one creative direction, not a product dump.
- **No ALL CAPS, no exclamation marks.** After "APRES POOL" and "MORE ON SALE," the restraint is notable and welcome.

## What's weak

- **No scale reference on the art.** For someone buying wall art for two different rooms with different ceiling heights, the size spec matters more than the style. Article surfaces dimensions in their editorial emails; CB2 makes me click to find out.
- **$5,000 gift credit sweepstakes is confusing and repetitive.** This is day two of sweepstakes-layered-on-editorial. It undercuts the "art education" positioning — either this is a curated art moment or it's a prize promotion, but it can't cleanly be both.
- **"SHOP" as the sole CTA verb.** "Shop Art Prints" or "Browse the Collection" would do more work. "SHOP" alone is the same button I'd see on a Wayfair cordless-vacuum email.
- **No ship time or availability signal.** When I'm renovating, I'm scheduling rooms. An art print that takes 8 weeks to deliver needs to say so. Pottery Barn's catalog email at least directs you to a place where you can check stock status.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a size callout in the hero or the product tile.** Even "available in three sizes" does more work than nothing. This is the single change that moves me from browse-mode to buy-mode on wall art.
- 2. **Pick a lane on the sweepstakes.** If you're doing $5K gift credit, lead with it and build the email around it. If you're doing art education, drop the sweepstakes to a slim footer strip. Having it mid-scroll in a full-width section splits the email's purpose.
- 3. **Make the CTA copy match the editorial concept.** "Explore the Collection," "Start Your Gallery Wall" — anything that carries the tone of "Art Education" instead of dropping back to generic retail.
- 4. **Show one featured piece with full context** — title, size, price, estimated ship date — before the product grid. Article does this with their home tours: one anchor, then supporting items. It's more persuasive than six equal-weight tiles.
- 5. **Reference member pricing or rewards visibility** — I'm buying on the highest-rewards card I have, and a reminder that I can stack that with a CB2 credit program (if one exists) would close me faster than a sweepstakes I won't win.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Mobile users cannot scale the email. Change to `maximum-scale=5` or remove the cap.

**Universal `text-size-adjust: none` overrides user agent accessibility settings**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applied twice (also in `body {}`). The `*` selector is overly broad and suppresses OS-level accessibility font scaling.

**Double-slash URLs trigger 301 redirects on font assets**
Three font `src:` values have `//` after the domain:
```
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2
```
Most servers handle this with a redirect, adding latency. Normalize to single slash.

**Conflicting `@font-face` declarations for `"Lineto Brown Sub"`**
Three blocks declare the same family name with overlapping weight logic:
- Block 1: no `font-weight` (defaults to 400)
- Block 2: explicit `font-weight: 400`
- Block 3: explicit `font-weight: 600`

Block 1 and Block 2 resolve to the same descriptor, pointing to different source files (`lineto-brown-light` vs `lineto-brown-regular`). Browser behavior is undefined; one declaration will silently win.

**Cross-domain font dependency on `crateandbarrel.com`**
`HNW05` (all four weights) loads from `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/`. A CDN change or downtime on that sibling domain degrades CB2 email rendering independently.

```html

```
Production artifact with a typo ("IMPUT"). Strip before send.
