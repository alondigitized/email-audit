## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids — the kids line, not even the main brand — and Venus Williams is *still* in there, tennis-adjacent yellow dress and all, this time down at the bottom of a nursery/kids furniture email. I flagged this exact hero shot on 2026-09-14 twice and again on 2026-09-15 for "Venus Williams for Crate & Barrel" — this makes round six across the family, and now it's crossed over into a product line I don't even shop for. I don't have kids, so an "ace their space" tennis-themed kids bedroom email is dead on arrival for me regardless of execution.
- The rooms themselves are actually well-styled — dark green playroom, striped tennis-ball bedding, the "championship-worthy decor" gallery wall — this is genuinely good photography. But it's buried under six or seven stacked room modules with no single clear offer, and it's from a sub-brand of a company that's already sent me five other emails this week between Crate & Barrel, CB2, and now Crate & Kids.
- Not clicking, not forwarding — deleting. If anything this makes me want to unsubscribe from the whole Crate & Barrel family, the way I've been threatening to do since the Venus Williams sends started repeating.

## What's working

- The room styling is legitimately nice — that dark green built-in playroom and the striped tennis-ball comforter have real design POV, better than the generic gray-and-white staging Wayfair usually sends me.
- "Championship-worthy decor" and the tennis-ball motifs are at least a consistent creative thread tying the Venus Williams collab to the kids assortment, unlike some of C&B's more scattered sends.
- The Design Desk callout at the bottom (free design services) is a real value-add if I were furnishing a kid's room — CB2 ran a similar "Colors That Just Make Sense" design-service pitch this week, so at least the messaging is consistent brand-wide.

## What's weak

- I don't have kids — a Crate & Kids email has zero business being in my inbox with this kind of frequency, and there's no adult/primary-home content here at all.
- Venus Williams, again. Round six this week across the C&B family. My brain fully snoozes on this hero shot now.
- No actual offer — no %, no $ off, no free shipping banner, just room after room of "shop the new collab." Pottery Barn's ghost-decoration email was a bad fit for me too, but at least it led with urgency; this just meanders.
- Seven-plus stacked "shop the new collab" CTAs with zero differentiation in copy — repetitive button spam, no hierarchy telling me where to actually click first.
- Nothing here reflects my actual renovation — no swatch/sample program, no shipping-timeline info for furniture, no showroom tie-in, which is the one lane (storage, "clean home dream") that actually worked for me from this brand on 2026-09-14.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment me out of Crate & Kids entirely — I have no kids-category signal in my purchase history; this send should never have hit my inbox.
- 2. Retire the Venus Williams hero for at least a few sends — six appearances in a week is creative fatigue, not a campaign.
- 3. Pick one hero product (the tennis-ball bedding set, say) and lead with a real price or discount instead of seven equal-weight room shots.
- 4. Cut the CTA count from seven-plus "Shop the New Collab" buttons down to one or two — right now nothing tells me where the eye should land first.
- 5. If C&B wants my attention, redirect budget toward more "clean home dream" storage/organization content like 2026-09-14 — that's the one beat in this whole family of brands that's actually spoken to my renovation.
