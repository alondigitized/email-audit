# ADJUSTABLE BEDS 🚩 UP TO 60% OFF 🚩 FINAL HOURS 🚩 
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-12**
## Executive summary

- The subject promised adjustable beds and 60% off — I open expecting a hero image of an adjustable bed frame, and instead the top of the email is a "picked just for you" grid that's basically a garage sale: an adjustable bed frame (fine), then a grill, a bathroom scale, an elliptical, a bar cart, and a dresser. That's not "picked for you," that's "picked from six different departments and thrown into a 2x2 grid." This is the fourth Wayfair email I've opened this week (gazebos, pergolas, the "Last Call" purple banner send, and now this) and the pattern is now impossible to miss — same purple, same "Sale" red tags, same star ratings with no review counts, same "shop all sales" purple banner slab in the middle.
- The one thing that's actually new here is the adjustable bed itself at $329.99 down from $679.99 — that's a real number and it's in my renovation lane, but it's buried as one of six thumbnails instead of being the hero. Compare that to CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" send, which at least kept its hero product large and singular even mid-sale-fatigue — I gave that one a 7 partly because I didn't have to hunt for the thing they wanted me to buy. Here I have to scroll past a grill and a bathroom scale to find the bed I supposedly clicked in for.
- Verdict: I wouldn't click past the bed thumbnail, and I definitely wouldn't forward this — there's nothing here specific enough to send a friend ("hey check out this scale" is not a text I'm sending). Marking as read, maybe scrolling to the mattress category banner out of curiosity since I've got a guest room to furnish, but that's the ceiling.

## What's working

- The adjustable bed price is concrete — $679.99 down to $329.99, over 50% off, exactly matching the subject line's promise.
- No broken images this time, which after three straight Wayfair sends with a blank hero rectangle is honestly a relief.
- The category discount bar (mattresses 30%, bedding 60%, rugs 60%, grills 40%) is legible and scannable.
- "Your Home, These Picks" section further down has some actually-relevant renovation categories — kitchen mats, shoe storage, cotton sheets — that fit a guy mid-reno on two houses.

## What's weak

- The hero real estate is wasted on a random six-item grid instead of leading with the adjustable bed the subject line sold me on — grill and bathroom scale have nothing to do with "adjustable beds."
- "picked just for you" is doing a lot of lying — a scale, an elliptical, and a green dresser are not a personalized picks for someone renovating two houses.
- The 0% APR financing banner in the middle interrupts the product flow and isn't something I care about — I'm buying on my highest-rewards card, not financing a nightstand.
- "FINAL HOURS" and "ends tonight" in the subject/header don't show a countdown timer or specific cutoff time anywhere in the body — same soft urgency Wayfair used in the "Last Call" send two days ago, and it's wearing thin.
- Star ratings appear on every product but with no review counts, so I can't actually gauge trust — Article at least shows real proof points when it bothers to.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the adjustable bed as a full-width hero — big image, the $329.99 price, a single "Shop Adjustable Beds" CTA. That's the whole reason I opened this.
- 2. Cut the random grid items (scale, grill, elliptical) out of the top fold entirely — if it's not a bed or bedroom-adjacent, it doesn't belong above the category discount bar.
- 3. Replace the vague "ends tonight" with an actual countdown timer or a specific time ("Ends 11:59 PM ET") — Wayfair's used this soft-urgency trick three sends running and it's lost its teeth.
- 4. Move the financing banner out of the middle of the product flow — it breaks scanning momentum and isn't relevant to a cash/rewards-card buyer like me.
