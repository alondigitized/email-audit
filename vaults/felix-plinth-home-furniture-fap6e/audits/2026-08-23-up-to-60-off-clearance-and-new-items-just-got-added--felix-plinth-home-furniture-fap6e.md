# Up to 60% off clearance—and new items just got added!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-23**
## Executive summary

- Same beach-hero clearance move as yesterday's "60% off end-of-season" send — Crate & Barrel is now running this exact playbook three times in one week, and this time they didn't even bother changing the photo composition much, just swapped the copy to "new markdowns added." My eye landed on the category grid below the fold — furniture, outdoor, kitchen, lighting — which is at least organized and scannable, but there's zero indication which of those eight tiles actually has the new stuff versus the same picked-over clearance I've already scrolled past twice this week.
- Then it just... keeps going. NFL game day collection, a dining room lifestyle pitch, six random dining pieces "we love for you," a credit card pitch, an app download push, and a Design Desk booking CTA. This is five different emails stapled together. I opened it for the clearance offer in the subject line and got a kitchen-sink newsletter instead.
- I'm not clicking. I'm not forwarding this to my wife either — if anything I'd screenshot it as a "look how much email this brand sends me" complaint. Marking read and moving on, same as the last two C&B clearance emails.

## What's working

- Leading with the offer in the hero ("up to 60% off clearance") at least matches the subject line, unlike some sends that bury it.
- The category grid (furniture, outdoor, kitchen, tabletop, lighting, decor, rugs, bedding) is genuinely useful for scanning what's on sale by room, which is closer to how I actually shop a reno.
- Product shots are clean and consistent — no broken images, no layout bugs.

## What's weak

- This is the third near-identical "clearance" beach-hero email from Crate & Barrel this week (following 8/22's rugs email and 8/23's "see what just got added" send) — the fatigue is real and I've stopped reading past the fold.
- The email bolts on completely unrelated pitches — NFL game day, dining room lifestyle, credit card upsell, app download, Design Desk booking — none of which relate to "60% off clearance." It reads like five emails merged into one.
- No specific dollar or percent amounts on any actual product, just a blanket "up to 60%" with no visible product pricing on the clearance tiles themselves.
- The dining furniture section ($1,799-$3,695 dressers and beds) has nothing to do with clearance pricing — it's full-price cross-sell shoved into a clearance email.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email down to just the clearance offer and category grid — kill the NFL, credit card, app, and dining upsell blocks entirely. Wayfair's pergola email stayed on-topic top to bottom; this one wanders after two screens.
- 2. Put actual markdown prices or a "new markdowns" badge on the category tiles so I know which room actually has fresh stock versus what I saw Tuesday.
- 3. Since this is the third clearance send this week, differentiate it — lead with "just added" items specifically, not the same generic beach photo.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New clearance drops: dining, lighting, rugs up to 60% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `We just marked down more furniture — here's what's new`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh arrivals to clearance — shop before they're gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New markdowns just landed across 8 categories`

Standard ESP-templated (Movable Ink/MI-branded) responsive HTML email with heavy tracking infrastructure; automated QA shows a 57% pass rate driven mainly by missing image alt text and unconfirmed auth/unsubscribe headers.
