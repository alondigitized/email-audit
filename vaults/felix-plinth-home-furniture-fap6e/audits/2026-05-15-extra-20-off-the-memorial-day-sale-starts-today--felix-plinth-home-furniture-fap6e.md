# EXTRA 20% OFF 📣 The Memorial Day Sale starts today
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so this is email number five from Pottery Barn in approximately seventy-two hours. My thumb has developed muscle memory for the archive gesture at this point. When I saw "Pottery Barn Sale" in the sender field, my first instinct was to scroll past — but "EXTRA 20% OFF" caught me because it reads like a new layer on top of the base Memorial Day sale that landed yesterday. That distinction matters. If this is actually an additional discount stacked on the existing promotion, that's news. If it's just rebranded packaging of the same sale, I'm annoyed.
- Looking at the email, the hero does the job cleanly. Dark background, clear offer, category nav below. The "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" grid is a nice touch — I'm actively mid-renovation on two houses, so behavioral targeting that surfaces beds and dining tables lands differently than generic category blasts. The Trancoso Expandable Dining Table caught my eye specifically because I've been looking at expandable dining options for the second home. That said, the product grid is tiny enough on mobile that I'd have to pinch-to-zoom to read specs, which I'm not going to do.
- I'd click the sale CTA, but only because I'm already deep in the PB orbit right now. I would not forward this to anyone — there's nothing surprising enough to share. And if I weren't in active renovation mode, this would be archived in under two seconds. The cadence alone is enough to make me mute this sender address.

## What's working

- **Hero offer lands immediately** — "Extra 20% off" on a dark contrasting banner is the first thing my eye hits, no hunting required.
- **Category nav is genuinely useful** — Furniture, Outdoor, Bedding, Rugs — I can jump straight to what I need without scrolling through irrelevant categories.
- **"Recommended Just For You" section** — shows behavioral targeting is working; the dining table and platform bed SKUs are actually in my consideration set right now.
- **Rewards callout present** — "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" is visible; I shop on the highest-rewards card anyway, so stacking that with the sale math matters to me.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, images loaded, nothing overlapping.

## What's weak

- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is the fifth email in roughly three days. Wayfair pulled the same move this week with their Memorial Day blitz, and I'm already annoyed at both of them equally.
- **The "STARTS NOW" vs. "starts today" framing is redundant with yesterday's send** — the previous email literally announced the sale starting; this reads like a re-announcement rather than a genuine escalation.
- **Product cards are too small to drive real consideration** — the Recommended grid shows six items, but the type is unreadable without zooming; Crate & Barrel's recent sends give product cards more real estate and price context at a glance.
- **No end date visible** — "starts today" gives me zero urgency on the close. If this ends Sunday, tell me Sunday.
- **ALL CAPS in the subject line** — "EXTRA 20% OFF" reads like my inbox is being yelled at; Wayfair did the exact same thing with their emoji-flag Memorial Day subject and it felt just as cheap.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline** — "Ends Sunday" or "48 hours only" in both the subject and the hero. Right now there's no reason to act today vs. Saturday.
- 2. **Cut the cadence** — this should have been one well-loaded send, not five. The previous "STARTS NOW" email covered the announcement; this one should have been a mid-sale reminder with a harder close mechanic (cart reminder, top sellers, limited stock).
- 3. **Make the Recommended grid actionable** — show price, savings amount, and a single-click add-to-cart or "Save for Later" on each card. The grid is doing good targeting work but losing the conversion because specs are invisible.
- 4. **Replace the App download module** — I'm already clearly a web customer; pushing app downloads mid-sale email is a waste of prime scroll real estate. Swap it for a "Top Sellers Going Fast" strip.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `New deals added — up to 60% off furniture, bedding, outdoor`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stacks with your Key Rewards. Ends May 25.`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Memorial Day Sale" (2026-05-15)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard-broken links (HTTP 403) will silently fail for recipients clicking the credit card CTA and loading a West Elm cross-brand asset. Accessibility posture is poor: 43 images lack alt text and three asset URLs use unencrypted HTTP, which modern email clients will block.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Credit card / loyalty CTA returns 403**
The "Apply and Buy" banner link resolves to a 403:
`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner&...`
Any recipient clicking the credit card join CTA hits an error page. This is the highest-priority break.

**FAIL — Cross-brand spacer image returns 403**
`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` is a West Elm domain asset embedded in a Pottery Barn send. The 403 is likely a cross-brand permission issue; additionally it is HTTP (see §3).

**INFO — 24 click-redirect links not probed**
All `click.e.potterybarn.com` links were skipped by the HTTP probe. Destination validity is unverified for those targets.

**WARN — Open-tracking pixel is HTTP**
`http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` — unencrypted open pixel; Gmail and Outlook will proxy/block it, making open-rate data unreliable.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP `header()` calls inside `<style>` block**
The following lines appear verbatim inside a `<style>` tag, not as a comment:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are PHP server-side directives that leaked into the template. They are invalid CSS and will be emitted as parse errors by all email clients. Non-breaking, but a template hygiene failure indicating the PHP layer wrote directly into the HTML rather than stripping server headers before render.

**43 images missing `alt` text**
Every product image in the `S26_Sale_MemorialDay_GraphicOverLinkFarm/` slice set, all footer images, and several banner images have no `alt` attribute. When images are blocked (Apple Mail Privacy Protection, corporate proxies), the email renders as a blank sequence of broken boxes with no textual fallback.

**3 image assets served over HTTP**
Email clients with forced HTTPS (Outlook.com, Gmail) will block or substitute these:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` — screen readers announce "untitled document" when the email is opened in a browser or forwarded.

**`no-referrer` meta tag suppresses analytics**
`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />` strips the `Referer` header on all click-throughs. Any GA4 / Adobe Analytics session that relies on referrer attribution will show as direct traffic, degrading campaign reporting.

### 5. Compliance

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
Header not captured by the AgentMail relay. If it is genuinely missing from the SMTP envelope, this violates Gmail/Yahoo's February 2024 bulk-sender requirement and CAN-SPAM best practice. Must be confirmed at SMTP level, not just HTML inspection.

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail for >5k/day senders) is unconfirmed. Absence increases spam complaint rate and risks Gmail throttling.

**WARN — Authentication-Results header not captured**
SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unknown via the relay. Sending domain `e.potterybarn.com` should show DKIM alignment; this should be verified at the MTA.

**WARN — Plain-text version is URL-heavy**
9,742 of 13,866 plain-text characters (70.3%) are URLs. Spam filters (SpamAssassin `URI_RATIO` rule) flag this pattern; it degrades deliverability on plain-text-first evaluation paths.
