# Open Box steals
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- Forty-nine. That's where we are with Wayfair, and I'll grant them this: "open box" is a concept I haven't seen from them in this streak, which makes it the most differentiated thing they've sent since the desks email on June 11 (the one I scored a 9, the one with actual hierarchy). After four emails yesterday alone — kitchen mats, the HOT SUMMER SALE, desks, dressers back-to-back — arriving at a genuinely different premise on June 12 is the bare minimum level of respect for my inbox, but I'll take it.
- The problem is they got the concept right and the execution completely wrong. Open box is *specific by definition* — returned units, floor samples, one-of-a-kind inventory at steep markdowns. The whole appeal is the hunt. You buy open box because you found a $1,400 sofa that someone returned because the leg had a scratch and it's now $480. This email gives me seventeen category rows with "up to X% OFF" percentages and a "SHOP ALL SALES" button. That's a sitemap. Article sent me "Summer hosting made simple" earlier this week — four specific product shots, a real scene, a reason to click. This is the opposite of that.
- And the hero image is a green Dutch oven. On a lavender background. In an email about open box home deals. I own two houses. I need dining room tables, vanities, bed frames. I do not need Wayfair's answer to Le Creuset. Mark as read, maybe come back to one of the category rows if I'm actively hunting something, but not clicking the CTA.

## What's working

- **The concept is perfectly targeted at a two-home renovator.** Open box = genuine discounts on real inventory, not manufactured "sale" pricing. This is exactly how I want to shop furniture.
- **The category list covers what I'm actively buying.** Beds, end tables, coffee tables, dining room sets, bathroom vanities — all live on my lists right now. It works as a useful navigation menu even if it doesn't inspire.
- **The percentage callouts are honest and visible.** Up to 70% off on area rugs and bar stools are the ceiling numbers and they're easy to find by scanning down. Pottery Barn ran the same 70% ceiling in "NEW DEALS ADDED!" and it landed — the number itself is credible.
- **The subject line survives any screen.** "Open Box steals" at 15 characters clears mobile truncation with room to spare. Clean, no gimmicks, no emoji stacking.
- **The render is clean.** Lavender palette is on-brand, text is legible at thumbnail size, no broken images. After some competitive renders that fell apart on mobile, the baseline execution here is solid.

## What's weak

- **The hero product makes no sense for me.** A green casserole dish or Dutch oven as the single featured visual — in an email about open box home deals — actively confused me. Article's lifestyle table shot from "Summer hosting made simple" sold the *experience* of what they were moving. This sells nothing relevant to my renovation.
- **Open box needs inventory urgency and this has none.** These are finite SKUs. The entire conversion hook for open box is scarcity — "only 2 left," "ships from returns center," some signal that if I wait I lose it. Wayfair's desks email from yesterday at least created a sense of momentum. This email has no closing window anywhere visible.
- **"SHOP ALL SALES" is the wrong destination button.** If I click that, I'm in the general Wayfair sale, not the open box section. CB2 made this exact error in "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" — a compelling premise with a muddled click destination. The button should say "SHOP OPEN BOX DEALS" and go *there*.
- **Seventeen text rows with zero product photography.** The desks email on June 11 — my highest Wayfair score in this entire run — had visual hierarchy and specific callouts. This email is a category index. I'm not scrolling a wall of purple rows hoping something sparks.
- **Cadence is still the slow bleed.** Four emails yesterday. I gave them a calendar-reset grace on June 12, but my goodwill tank is near empty and this email didn't refill it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show me three actual open box units with prices crossed out.** A sofa with a visible tag that says "returned — minor leg scratch — orig. $1,400, now $490." That's the whole email. That's what converts an open box shopper.
- 2. **Rename and redirect the CTA.** "SHOP OPEN BOX DEALS" → open box landing page. Not the general sale.
- 3. **Add scarcity language at the category row level.** Even "limited inventory" copy next to Dining Room Sets would change the read. Open box is not a sale — it's a clearance of specific units.
- 4. **Replace the Dutch oven with furniture or a collage of returned-but-gorgeous pieces.** A rug, a mirror, a bed frame — anything that signals "home renovation windfall" rather than "kitchen clearance."
- 5. **Surface the sub-brand angle.** AllModern and Perigold open box hits would perform differently with different segments. Put the brand mark in the email body next to a callout, not just in the footer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Open box furniture: up to 70% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Returned. Discounted. Limited stock.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Lightly returned, deeply marked down — shop open box before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sofas, beds, dining sets and more — at open box prices while inventory lasts.`
