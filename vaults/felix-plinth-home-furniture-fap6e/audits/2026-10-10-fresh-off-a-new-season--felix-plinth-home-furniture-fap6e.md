# Fresh off a new season
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-10**
## Executive summary

- CB2 again. This is at least the eighth send I've logged from them in about three days, so I opened it already tired. The top banner is "Limited time only: Shipping on us, orders of $99 or more," and it's the same free-shipping-at-$99 offer as "So we're doing a little thing" on 10/09. Below it the email turns into a long scroll of moody, expensive-looking hero tiles: the tufted leather sofa, the fluted wood bed, the green lacquered console. They look great. But I'm furnishing two houses, and a $99 shipping threshold means nothing when I'm buying a sofa.
- The "Long Weekend Sale, up to 60% off" block is the real offer, and it sits about two-thirds of the way down. It should have been the hero. The hero instead says "Just in, already trending," which is a mood, not a deal. The Davison bed hero here is basically the 10/08 "THIS BEST SELLING BED" email recycled.
- Would I click? Maybe the sale button, because up to 60% off on a sofa matters. Would I forward it? No. I'd save the leather sofa and the green console to my Pinterest-adjacent folder and archive the rest.

## What's working

- The product tiles are strong editorial. The Ambroise tufted leather sofa and the Isobel green lacquered console are the kind of thing I'd pin.
- Each hero names the designer, like Gianfranco Frattini and Mermelada Estudio. That makes the pieces feel curated, and I could use it as a research lead.
- The "Long Weekend Sale, up to 60% off" block has a clear percentage and a visible "Shop the sale" button.
- "More for you" at the bottom shows real prices, such as the Fitz swivel chair at $879 marked down from $1,099. That's the section that helps me decide.
- The "Earn 10% back" Rewards Dollars block speaks to my highest-rewards-card habit.

## What's weak

- The sale is buried. A 60%-off event sits below five full-screen product heroes, and the top banner leads with $99 free shipping instead.
- The shipping offer is the same one from 10/09's "So we're doing a little thing." It's nothing new, and it's irrelevant to big-ticket furniture.
- The hero tiles have no prices. I can't tell whether the sofa is in the sale or at full price.
- The email is very long, and most of it is category repetition: "Shop new arrivals" appears twice and there are several tiles with no price.
- Nothing about swatches, fabric samples, delivery timing or lead times. For furniture, that's the information I need before I click.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the Long Weekend Sale at the very top with the 60% number, and cut the shipping banner or move it to the footer.
- 2. Add a price or "from $X" to every hero tile, and mark which pieces are in the sale.
- 3. Cut the email to three heroes. The Pottery Barn Sale email at least repeats a single offer clearly, and Wayfair at least puts products with prices in the first screen.
- 4. Add one line on lead times and swatches for the sofa and bed.
- 5. Space out the sends. This is the same cadence problem as the C&B family sends.

- **Subject Alt B:** `The leather sofa everyone's saving`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sofas, beds and consoles marked down through Monday. Plus free shipping on $99+.`

## Technical Audit

Header-based compliance and authentication checks can't be verified because the relay didn't capture the headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Not probed:** 69 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are click-redirect domains. Destination validity, redirect chains and final landing pages are unverified.
- **Insecure pixels:** 11 Dotomi (Epsilon) tracking pixels load over plain `http://`. Clients and proxies may block them, which would silently drop retargeting and cookie-sync calls.
  - `http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?dtm_com=2&dtm_cid=2437&dtm_cmagic=654788&dtm_fid=103&dtm_format=6&cli_promo_id…`
  - `http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2437/pub1.php?d98e199bf5027afea04b790655b3dee2=1` through `pub10.php`
- **Third-party calls:** Ten `cookieredir/pubN.php` calls plus a UCM controller call add many third-party requests on open. That adds load latency and is a privacy exposure.
