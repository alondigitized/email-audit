# Just in! New deals + Refresh your outdoor space for the season
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- Twenty. I said it at nineteen and I'll say it again: that is the number of Pottery Barn emails I've opened since Tuesday, and this one — arriving June 15 with "Easy Outdoor Updates" as the hero — is fighting against its own sender reputation. Yesterday's evening send already hit me with the 70%-off promise *and* a lighting angle. This email drops the lighting, pivots purely to outdoor, and is genuinely better for the focus. The category grid — pillows, planters, umbrellas, side tables, entertaining, rugs — maps almost exactly to what I'm trying to pull together for the second home's back deck right now.
- But here's the problem: I'm a different person on email twenty than I was on email one. The outdoor content is real. The creative is clean. The offer is visible. None of that matters because my pattern-recognition for Pottery Barn subject lines has completely flatlined. The sale banner has been on for so many consecutive sends that I genuinely can't tell if it's new or a cached image from my last twenty opens. And then the "Recommended Just for You" module fires off a bed and what looks like an indoor upholstered piece — directly below an outdoor-themed email. That recommendation engine is not paying attention.
- I'd open the outdoor grid, click maybe once into side tables or rugs, and add something to a wishlist. I would not forward this. I'd mark it read and feel mild guilt about the fact that this actually was one of their better-organized sends.

## What's working

- **"Easy Outdoor Updates" hero** — the lifestyle shot reads like a real patio, not a resort catalog; I can see myself buying specific items from that scene, which is exactly what you want
- **Category grid is scannable and occasion-complete** — pillows, planters, umbrellas, side tables, entertaining, rugs covers the whole outdoor purchase journey in one view; Wayfair's "Inside: 8' X 10' OUTDOOR RUGS" send was more specific but less comprehensive
- **Sale banner + "SHOP ALL SALE" CTA lead the email** — no scrolling required, no buried offer; cleaner hierarchy than C&B's clearance emails which tend to make me read three paragraphs before I find the button
- **"Free Design Services" section placed in context** — showing this in an outdoor-themed email is smarter than burying it generically; I've actually used this service and seeing it here reminds me I should book a session for the deck layout
- **Key Rewards visible near the footer** — I run everything through my highest-rewards card and the program reminder is genuinely useful, not just filler

## What's weak

- **Email twenty in five days** — the outdoor content is legitimately relevant to me; the cadence has made it structurally impossible to feel that way; CB2 sent 28 emails over a month and I still had curiosity left by the end; Pottery Barn sent 20 in five days and I am running on fumes
- **"Recommended Just for You" fires indoor furniture below the outdoor hero** — an upholstered bed directly below a patio-and-planter email is a recommendation engine that doesn't know what mood I'm in; this module should have been outdoor-only or cut entirely
- **No specific hero product named or priced** — Article's "Summer hosting made simple" email gave me named items with visible price anchors; this email gives me eight category thumbnails and asks me to go find the deal myself
- **"Just in! New deals" burns the first half of the subject line** — meaningless placeholder copy that has appeared in some form across multiple recent sends; the outdoor half of the subject was the only thing doing real work
- **No deadline anywhere** — "Summer Sale" with no end date is ambient clearance pricing, not urgency; I've seen this banner so many times I genuinely believe it's permanent

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut or segment the "Recommended Just for You" module** — if this email is themed outdoor, every recommendation should be outdoor; showing me a bed in frame four of an outdoor email is a segmentation miss that costs trust
- 2. **Name one specific hero product with before/after pricing** — a sectional, a dining table, a planter bundle — give me something concrete to anchor the 70% claim; the rubric-free "up to" leaves me suspicious every time
- 3. **Add a hard deadline to the sale banner** — "Ends June 22" or "4 days left" transforms that banner from wallpaper into a genuine CTA; without it I'll assume the sale is permanent and delay the click indefinitely
- 4. **Suppress to one outdoor send per active renovation campaign** — this is the highest-ROI change available and it's not a creative fix; the content here is good enough to convert if it arrived once, not twentieth
- 5. **Consolidate the CTA hierarchy** — "SHOP ALL SALE" at the top, "GO TO THE SALE" in the hero, "SHOP ALL OUTDOOR" below the grid is three separate calls that dilute each other; pick one and make it the only button above the fold

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP code injected into `<style>` block (template error):**
```html
<style>
  ...
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
</style>
```
PHP `header()` calls landed inside a CSS `<style>` tag. These are silently invalid CSS in all clients — but they expose a CMS/template pipeline bug where server-side cache directives were dropped into the HTML output layer instead of being emitted as HTTP response headers.

**Three non-HTTPS image sources** — may be blocked by Gmail, Outlook 365, and Apple Mail with strict mixed-content policies:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` (open-pixel tracker)

**38 images missing `alt` text** — all `slices_NN.jpg/gif` content images, hero banner (`S26_TOP_SummerSale_NoUrgency.jpg`), product images, footer slices, and tracking pixels have empty or absent `alt` attributes. Renders as blank in image-off environments and fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.1.1.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** prevents user zoom on mobile — WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 failure:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

**Empty `<title>` tag** — assistive technologies read page title on load; a blank title degrades screen-reader experience.
