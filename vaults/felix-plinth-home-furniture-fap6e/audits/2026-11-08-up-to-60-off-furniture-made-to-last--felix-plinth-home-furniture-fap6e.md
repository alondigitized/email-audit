# Up to 60% off furniture made to last
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-08**
## Executive summary

- This is the sixth Pottery Barn send in about five days, and the subject finally speaks to my kitchen and dining reno. The hero says "Up to 60% off Furniture" over a bedroom shot, and that's a fair offer. It still feels like a catalog template with furniture stapled to the top. Two screens down I'm back in Christmas stockings, a Christmas Shop banner and a free design appointment pitch, all of which I've already seen this week.
- I'd click once, on the furniture hero, because I'm furnishing two houses. After that the email loses me. The only furniture in "Recommended Just For You" is a dining table and a rug, and I wanted sofas and storage. The subject promised furniture and the body drifted into holiday decor. I'd save it to my Pinterest folder for the Banks dining table and not forward it.

## What's working

- "Up to 60% off Furniture" is a concrete offer, and it's at the very top where I see it first.
- The top banner, "in-stock furniture delivered in 1-4 weeks," answers my biggest furniture question, which is shipping time. Wayfair almost never tells me that up front.
- The Banks Extendable Dining Table is a real product that fits my dining room project.
- The "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" card block speaks to how I buy. I pay with the highest-rewards card, so I do the math.
- The category grid (Bedding, Rugs, Lighting, Curtains) is easy to tap and scan.

## What's weak

- Furniture gets one hero image and two buttons. Everything below it is stockings, a Christmas tree and quilts. That doesn't match "furniture made to last."
- There are no furniture product tiles, sofas or storage under the 60% off hero, and no prices or sale amounts anywhere. Article's storage send showed me actual dressers and sideboards. This one made me guess.
- The "Festive Favorites Ship Free" banner is the same creative as the 11/07 "Ships free 🎄" email. The same lamp-in-three-brands annoyance applies to the same banner in two sends.
- The hero is a patterned bedroom set with no product name. I can't tell which item is 60% off.
- The footer fine print is a wall of tiny text, and the "Promotional Exclusions" disclaimer is buried at the bottom. "Up to 60%" with no sale end date doesn't feel urgent.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace the stockings and Christmas Shop blocks with a 6-tile furniture grid showing real sale prices, such as sofas, dining tables, barstools and bed frames. That fixes the mismatch between subject and body.
- 2. Name the hero product and show its sale price, for example "Bed frame, now $X," so the 60% off feels real.
- 3. Add a sale end date and an in-stock note next to the "delivered in 1-4 weeks" line.
- 4. Cut the cadence. Send fewer than six emails in five days, and don't reuse the Festive Favorites banner twice in a row.
- 5. Move the 10% rewards card block up near the hero for people who buy big-ticket furniture.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dining tables & sofas up to 60% off, in stock`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Furniture sale ends Sunday: ships in 1-4 weeks`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `In-stock sofas, beds and dining tables, delivered in 1-4 weeks.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus 10% back with the PB card on furniture.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based XHTML layout with image-sliced content. It has widespread missing alt text, three non-HTTPS image sources, and an empty `<title>`. Authentication and unsubscribe headers could not be verified because of the relay, and none of the 56 click-tracking links were probed.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified destinations.** 56 links were skipped as tracking redirects, for example `https://click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=ABp7Imxk...`. The final landing URLs, status codes and redirect chains are unconfirmed.
- **HTTP open-tracking pixel.** `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=...` is non-HTTPS, so clients or proxies that block mixed content may drop it. This undercounts opens.
- **Recipient email in image and pixel URLs.**
  - `https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
  - `sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
  - Both put the recipient's address in plaintext in a query string, where it can be logged by proxies and CDNs.
- **Plain-text part is mostly URLs.** 10,774 of 14,978 characters (about 72%) are URLs, which exceeds the 70% threshold. The long `qs=` redirect tokens are the likely cause.
