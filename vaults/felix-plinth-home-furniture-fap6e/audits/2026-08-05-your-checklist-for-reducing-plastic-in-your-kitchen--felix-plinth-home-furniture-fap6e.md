# Your checklist for reducing plastic in your kitchen
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-05**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so this is a genuine left-turn from Crate & Barrel — no gift-card sweepstakes, no third rerun of the same twin-bed hero, just "the clean kitchen checklist" with eight numbered tips running from cutting boards to dish towels. My first reaction is relief that it's not sweepstakes email #6 this week, but my second reaction is: I'm mid-reno on two houses and this is... cutting boards and towels. Not cabinetry, not fixtures, not the big-ticket stuff I'm actually shopping for right now.
- The checklist format is genuinely well-executed — clean product photography, a logical flow, a "naturally enduring materials" callout that at least gestures at durability, which I do care about. But by the time I've scrolled past the 8-item list I'm also getting an FX "The Bear" tie-in, a 15%-off Aspen collection flash sale, a $23,000 honeymoon giveaway, a credit-card pitch, an app download nudge, and a design-consultation offer. That's six different asks stacked in one email, and none of them get room to land.
- Verdict: I'm not clicking. This is a scroll-and-archive, maybe a mental note that Aspen dinnerware is 15% off if I ever need it. Not forwarding — my wife doesn't need a plastic-free kitchen checklist from a brand she already gets five other emails a week from.

## What's working

- The numbered checklist format is clean and skimmable — better organized than CB2's clearance stack-up from earlier this week where two different sale percentages competed for my eye.
- "Naturally enduring materials" and the OEKO-TEX-certified towel callout are real product specifics, not filler — more concrete than the vague "fearless design" language Wayfair leaned on in their last send.
- No render bugs, images all load, layout holds together on the scroll — basic execution is solid.
- The Aspen collection 15% off is an actual number, not a mystery "sale" banner.

## What's weak

- Kitchen consumables — cutting boards, towels, food storage — just aren't what I'm buying right now. I'm deep in a full-house and second-home renovation; this reads like it's targeting someone furnishing a starter apartment, not two households.
- Six competing CTAs in one email (checklist, Bear tie-in, Aspen sale, honeymoon giveaway, credit card, design consult) means nothing gets primary billing. Pottery Barn's "Curated new looks for a heritage home" send stayed in one lane — three room styles, one clear path — and it worked better for exactly that reason.
- Zero demographic or gender signal — no styling, no context that this is aimed at anyone in particular. Feels like a mass blast, not a "known customer" treatment, especially after the personalized-feeling review-request sweepstakes emails this brand sent me the last two days.
- The honeymoon giveaway is a jarring non-sequitur sandwiched between kitchen towels and a credit card pitch — no thematic connection to anything else in the email.
- This is at least the sixth-ish Crate & Barrel email in my inbox this week (right behind yesterday's "Pizza party on the patio" grilling send), and the grab-bag structure here makes the cadence fatigue worse, not better.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut it down to one offer.** Either this is the sustainability checklist email or the Aspen 15%-off email — not both, plus a giveaway, plus a credit card pitch. Article and Pottery Barn both stay disciplined to one story per send; this brand should too.
- 2. **Move the Aspen 15% off to the top if that's the real driver** — right now it's buried two-thirds down after eight checklist items nobody's scrolling for on mobile.
- 3. **Drop the honeymoon giveaway and credit-card block entirely from this send** — they have nothing to do with "clean kitchen" and just dilute an otherwise decent content email.
- 4. **If kitchen is the play, tie it to something reno-relevant** — "outfitting your new kitchen" angle would land with someone like me mid-renovation far better than "reducing plastic," which reads more like a sustainability newsletter than a shopping prompt.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Aspen dinnerware, 15% off — new kitchen essentials`
- **Subject Alt B:** `8 swaps for a healthier, better-looking kitchen`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: bestselling Aspen collection at 15% off, this week only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Ceramic, cotton, no plastic — the kitchen upgrade list.`
