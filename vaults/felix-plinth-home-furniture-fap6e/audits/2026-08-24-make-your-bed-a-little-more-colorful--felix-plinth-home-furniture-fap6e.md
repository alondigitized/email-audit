# Make your bed a little more colorful
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-24**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is the fifth or sixth Pottery Barn email this week, and "Comfort Meets Color" is at least a legible premise — "Make your bed a little more colorful" with a color-grid sort (greens, browns, blues, whites) is a real merchandising idea, not just another vague lifestyle shot. But my honest first reaction is: I've now seen quilts from this brand in yesterday's "Bedding Bestsellers" email, the day before's "On sale AND shipping free" email, and now here they are again in a different arrangement. My brain has started to gray these out.
- What almost got me: the color-swatch grid actually works as a way to shop bedding by palette, which is more useful than the usual "shop now" wall. But then it sprawls into rugs, curtain rods, a dining table, a towel, a Halloween shop teaser, an Affirm pitch, and a sweepstakes — by the time I hit "The Halloween Shop... Enter If You Dare" I've completely forgotten this was a bedding email. Crate & Barrel's "On now: Up to 25% off your bedroom refresh!" stayed in its lane and scored a 7 from me for exactly that discipline; this one wanders.
- Would I click? Maybe on "shop blues" since that's actually close to what I need for the guest room reno. Would I forward it? No — nothing here is surprising enough to send to a friend. Mark as read and move on.

## What's working

- The color-grid organization (greens/browns/blues/whites) is a genuinely useful way to shop bedding by palette instead of by generic collection name.
- "It all ships free" sitting right under the hero is a clean, honest offer with no asterisk hunting required.
- The "Recommended Just for You" row has actual product photography with prices implied by category (curtain rod, dining table, towel) — feels less like a generic blast.
- Quilts/duvets are shot in warm, textured close-ups that read well even in a compressed thumbnail — better product photography than CB2's floor-sample sale shots.

## What's weak

- This is bedding, then rugs, then furniture, then a Halloween shop, then an Affirm ad, then a sweepstakes — no hierarchy, just scroll fatigue. Article's "Home tour: Don't sleep on storage" stayed disciplined on one room story; this one loses the plot by the third screen.
- Zero pricing anywhere visible — not on the quilts, not on the "Recommended" grid, not even a "starting at." Wayfair's privacy screen email at least led with a product tile I could price-check without clicking.
- The Design Crew Sweepstakes and Halloween Shop banners feel bolted onto an email that started as a bedding-color story — total tonal whiplash.
- No loyalty/rewards math tied to this specific purchase — the "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" block is generic account-level messaging, not "here's what this quilt earns you."

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to bedding only — kill the Halloween Shop, the dining table, the curtain rod, and the sweepstakes. Every one of those dilutes the "Comfort Meets Color" premise and pushes the actual CTA further down.
- 2. Put a price or a "from $XX" on the four color-grid tiles. I shouldn't have to click to find out if "shop blues" means $89 sheets or a $400 duvet.
- 3. Move "It all ships free" copy closer to an actual percent-off or dollar offer — free shipping alone isn't pulling its weight against Crate & Barrel's 25%-off bedroom sends this week.
- 4. If Design Crew and Halloween Shop must stay, push them to a separate send — don't stack three unrelated campaigns under one subject line.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Bedding, organized by color you actually want`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Find your bedding color palette — free shipping`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop quilts and duvets by green, brown, blue, or white — all ship free.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New color-coded bedding, plus prices you can actually see.`
