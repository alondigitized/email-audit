# Extra 20% off + NEW Memorial Day deals
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- This is my sixth Pottery Barn email in approximately seventy-two hours, and I want to be transparent about what that does to my attention: I opened it because I'm actively shopping for two renovations and I've trained myself to at least scan Pottery Barn before archiving. But "Extra 20% off + NEW Memorial Day deals" is almost word-for-word the subject from yesterday's "EXTRA 20% OFF 📣 The Memorial Day Sale starts today" send. My literal first reaction was to check whether this was a resend before I even read the hero copy.
- Inside, the email partially recovers. The "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" module is the only thing that made me genuinely slow down — a grid of personalized product thumbnails that could actually reflect what I've been browsing. The credit card callout at the bottom is well-placed for someone like me who runs all big-ticket purchases through whatever card maximizes points on the category. These two elements feel like they know me. The rest of the email feels like the same Memorial Day wallpaper I've been scrolling past since Thursday.
- I'd click into the recommended grid and probably bounce once I realize prices aren't legible at email size. I wouldn't forward this to anyone — it doesn't feel like news at this point in the campaign. If one of those thumbnails happened to be the dining table I've been circling, my partner would get a text with the link, but that's luck, not email design.

## What's working

- **"RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" module** is the standout — actual personalized thumbnails break the template fatigue and are the one thing that can still earn a click at email six. This is something Wayfair rarely executes as cleanly.
- **Extra 20% off** is a concrete, scannable discount in the hero — I don't have to read a single line of body copy to understand the deal.
- **Outdoor furniture imagery** in the product grid is on-season and genuinely relevant; I'm sourcing patio pieces for the second home right now and the aesthetic is exactly the direction I'm looking.
- **Credit card / loyalty callout** at the bottom speaks to how I actually shop — I care about rewards math on $800+ purchases, and the fact that Pottery Barn surfaces this quietly rather than screaming it is the right call.
- **Clean render, no layout bugs** — images loaded, hierarchy is legible, nothing broken.

## What's weak

- **This is yesterday's email in a different envelope.** The offer is identical (20% off), the Memorial Day branding is identical, the category callout (furniture, bedding & more) is identical. Pottery Barn is burning goodwill at this cadence — and I'm saying that as someone who is *actively in-market* and genuinely receptive to what they're selling.
- **"NEW Memorial Day deals" in the subject promises something the hero doesn't deliver.** I was looking for a new product drop, a new category, or at minimum a threshold offer. The hero just restates the percentage. Wayfair's "All under $29.99" from earlier this week was annoying for other reasons, but at least it gave me a dollar figure I could evaluate against.
- **No deadline visible in the body.** "Memorial Day" implies something, but I can't find an explicit end date in the render. Without a date, urgency reads as manufactured, and I stop trusting the framing.
- **Product thumbnails are too small to scan.** Individual prices and product names aren't legible at email size, so the grid can't do the filtering work — it just directs traffic to the site and hopes I'll do it there.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Differentiate the hero from yesterday's send.** If you're running the same 20% off two days in a row, the email needs a new hook — a specific new arrival, a threshold ("save $250 on orders over $1,200"), a room story. Article's welcome email earlier this week had more novelty with zero discount, just because it felt like a fresh moment. This doesn't.
- 2. **Put the deadline in the hero.** "Ends Monday, May 26 at midnight" does more than "Memorial Day" as a vague timestamp. Crate & Kids ran a cleaner urgency signal on their event email — an explicit date anchors the decision.
- 3. **Promote "Recommended Just For You" above the fold.** It's the most compelling module and it's buried mid-scroll. Lead with personalization, follow with the sale offer. The personalization is your best card; don't hide it.
- 4. **Pull back on cadence.** Six emails in three days is the point where even I — renovating two houses and genuinely shopping this catalog — start hitting archive without reading. Crate & Kids hit that threshold with me on their second email in 24 hours. Pottery Barn is close.
- 5. **Add price callouts to the product grid.** Show me "$299" under each thumbnail. Wayfair does this better — even in crowded grids, the price is always visible without clicking through.

- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh additions to the sale — furniture, outdoor, bedding`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Extra 20% off + NEW Memorial Day deals"

### 1. Technical Summary

Two destination links are hard-broken (403), three image assets load over HTTP (mixed-content), and a critical template rendering failure leaked raw PHP `header()` directives into a CSS `<style>` block. Authentication and one-click unsubscribe headers are absent or unverifiable through the relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Credit card apply link returns 403**
- URL: `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember...`
- This is the primary member/Key Rewards CTA in the header banner. A 403 means the click-path is dead for all recipients who aren't already logged in or for whom the token is invalid. High-impact.

**[FAIL] West Elm spacer pixel returns 403 over HTTP**
- URL: `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- Cross-brand asset (West Elm domain) embedded in a Pottery Barn send. The 403 plus HTTP protocol means this image will be blocked by most modern email clients. Indicates a stale/copied template that was never cleaned up.

**[INFO] 24 tracking links skipped** — click-redirect domains not probed; destination integrity unverified.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CRITICAL] PHP header() directives leaked into CSS `<style>` block**

The following server-side PHP code appears literally inside a `<style>` tag in the rendered HTML:
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are not CSS — they are PHP output-control functions that were never executed by the template engine and escaped raw into the HTML. Browsers silently ignore them inside `<style>`, but this is evidence of a broken template render pipeline. Any dynamic logic in the same template block should be audited for similar failures.

**[WARN] 3 images served over HTTP (mixed-content)**
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Gmail, Outlook 365, and Apple Mail proxy or block non-HTTPS image sources. These will render as broken images for a significant portion of recipients.

**[WARN] Open/tracking pixel served over HTTP**
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=<email>`
- HTTP open-tracking pixel will be blocked by clients enforcing HTTPS, causing open-rate undercounting.

**[WARN] 38 content images missing `alt` text** — the entire product grid (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_13.jpg`), product PDPs, footer slices, and promo banners all lack `alt` attributes. Images-off rendering (default in many enterprise mail environments) produces a completely blank email.

**[INFO] Empty `<title>` tag** — `<title></title>` — has no deliverability impact but affects accessibility tree labeling in some clients.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent** — not captured by the AgentMail relay. If genuinely absent at the MTA level, Gmail and Yahoo will suppress the native unsubscribe UI, increasing spam complaints.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent** — one-click unsubscribe required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy (>5k/day threshold). Missing this header puts the send at risk of inbox demotion.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` not available** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status unverifiable through the relay. Should be confirmed at the raw header level before treating the send as authenticated.

**[INFO] `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />` is set** — suppresses HTTP `Referer` header on all link clicks. This will strip referring source data from any analytics platform that relies on referrer (Google Analytics, Adobe) rather than UTM params alone.
