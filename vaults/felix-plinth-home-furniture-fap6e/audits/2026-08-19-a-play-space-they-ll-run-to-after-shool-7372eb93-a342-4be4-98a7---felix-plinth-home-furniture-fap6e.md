# A play space they’ll run to after shool→
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-19**
## Executive summary

- Another Crate & Kids email, third one this week in my inbox after the twin-girl bedroom wallpaper pitch and the free-design-service one. Subject says "play space they'll run to after school" but the typo — "shool" — is right there for anyone to see, which is a rough look for a brand charging what they charge for a bin system. I don't have kids, so a "7 easy playroom updates" listicle is exactly the kind of email I scroll past without reading a word of the copy, just checking if there's anything remotely relevant to my renovation buried in the layout.
- The format itself — numbered 01 through 07, each with a product shot and a "Shop X" button — is at least organized, I'll give them that. It's cleaner than the CB2 "goop" collection tie-in I got flagged as gimmicky, and there's no broken rendering here like the IKEA OTP email had. But numbered playroom furniture (soft chairs, modular storage, a "go-round playroom table," bins, rugs, wicker wall storage, a snuggle lounger) is a wall of kid-specific product with zero crossover to anything I'm doing in either house.
- Marking as read, not clicking, definitely not forwarding. If anything I'd forward it to my sister who has toddlers, not to a friend I'm renovating with.

## What's working

- The numbered 01-07 structure gives it real visual hierarchy — each section has a clear hero shot, bold label, and button, so scanning is easy even if I'm not buying.
- Product photography is consistent and well-lit throughout, feels like the same shoot/palette as the room story emails Crate & Barrel does well.
- "TheDesignDesk" free consultation module reappears here too, so at least the cross-sell is consistent with what I've seen from this brand family all week.
- No render bugs — every image loaded, nothing overlapping, buttons are all legible.

## What's weak

- "after shool" typo in the subject line is a real miss — that's the kind of thing that makes me trust a brand less before I've even opened the email.
- Every single product is playroom/kid furniture — chairs sized for toddlers, a kid's table, nursery-adjacent bins — there's nothing here for someone renovating two adult homes.
- Seven back-to-back numbered sections is a lot of scrolling for a category I have zero interest in; Article's "Clocking in" home office email respected my time a lot more with one clean story.
- The stroller/car-seat "we love these for you" module near the bottom doubles down on baby gear I'll never buy.
- This is the third near-identical Crate & Kids send this week — my inbox pattern-matched it as noise before I even got past the header.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the "shool" typo immediately — it's a one-character error but it undercuts every dollar spent on this campaign's design.
- 2. Segment me out of Crate & Kids sends entirely — I'm on Crate & Barrel/CB2 lists for home renovation, not nursery/playroom gear, and three of these in a week is a targeting failure, not a cadence choice.
- 3. If cross-sell to non-parents is unavoidable, lead with the design-service angle (TheDesignDesk) rather than seven rounds of toddler furniture — that's the only piece of this email that could apply to me.
- 4. Cut the list to 3-4 hero items instead of 7 numbered sections — most subscribers, parent or not, aren't scrolling through a catalog-length email.
