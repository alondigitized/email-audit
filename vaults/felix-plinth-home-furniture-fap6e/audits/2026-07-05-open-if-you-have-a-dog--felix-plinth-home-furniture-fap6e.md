# OPEN IF YOU HAVE A DOG >>>
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- Okay — this one actually stopped me. After a week of Wayfair sending me "BAKING SHEETS" that opened to a furniture grid and "KITCHEN MATS" three days in a row with a body that was never about kitchen mats, a subject line that tells me to open it only if I have a dog is either the most cynical trick in the playbook or genuine segmentation. I opened it suspicious.
- And the hero lands. "pup approved." in script font over a cream dog bed — it's cozy, it's lifestyle-y, and it does not lie to my face. That's a low bar for Wayfair this week, but they cleared it. The 70% off banner and the "shop before they go" scarcity nudge do enough work to keep me scrolling.
- The problem is the bottom half. Below the pet section, I'm staring at a bedroom refresh grid, some outdoor lanterns, and shelving — the same ghost-of-general-home content that haunts every one of these sends. It's not as bad as the baking sheets disaster, but I can feel the editorial team ran out of dog SKUs around the fold and just backfilled with whatever the algorithm had queued. I'd probably tap the dog bed CTA. I wouldn't forward this — it's useful, not share-worthy.

## What's working

- **The hero is honest.** "pup approved." with an actual dog bed is the first Wayfair email this week where the subject line and the opening image are in the same zip code.
- **Script-font headline has warmth.** The handwritten "pup approved." reads lifestyle, not liquidation — a tone I don't usually associate with Wayfair.
- **70% OFF is visible and prominent.** Clear discount call-out gives me a number to anchor on. Article and Pottery Barn both do this, but Wayfair's discount range here is competitive.
- **"more styles to scoop up 🐾"** — the paw print emoji section header is cute and stays on-theme longer than I expected.
- **"shop before they go" creates real scarcity.** It's vague on deadline, but the implication of limited inventory on a hero product feels more plausible than their usual "FINAL DAYS" boilerplate.

## What's weak

- **The bottom third falls apart.** Outdoor lanterns, a bedroom grid, shelving — this is the same general-home backfill I've seen on every Wayfair send. Once I scroll past the paw print section, the dog promise evaporates.
- **The >>> in the subject line.** It's a cheap urgency hack. Article's "Up to 40% off ends Monday" gave me a real deadline. Wayfair's arrow cluster just reads like someone discovered ASCII art.
- **No social proof.** Crate & Barrel's "6000+ 5-star reviews" headline made me trust the sofa. I see zero review counts or ratings on any of these pet products. For a big-ticket dog bed I'm buying for a house I'm renovating, that matters.
- **Cadence is still a problem.** This is at minimum the seventh Wayfair email in roughly 96 hours. Even a good send lands with diminished credibility when I'm this oversaturated.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the general-home backfill entirely.** If the send is about dogs, it's about dogs. Fill the bottom half with pet-adjacent rugs, outdoor dog beds, entryway mats that handle muddy paws — stuff that earns the subject line. The shelving and lanterns belong in a different send.
- 2. **Add a date to the scarcity.** "Shop before they go" is weak. "Limited stock — these go fast over the holiday weekend" or a hard deadline ("offer ends Sunday") would close more carts.
- 3. **Surface one star rating.** Even "4.7★ from 1,200+ owners" on the hero dog bed would do what C&B's review count did for me last week — give me a reason to trust the recommendation.
- 4. **Kill the >>> in the subject.** It reads desperate and inches toward spam territory. The targeting hook is strong enough without it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your dog's next favorite thing`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Pup-approved picks — before they sell out`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dog beds, mats & more your pet will actually use — up to 70% off`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The dog edit: cozy, durable, on sale this weekend only`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<head>` CSS will be stripped by Gmail web client.**
The entire responsive stylesheet is declared inside `<style type="text/css">` in `<head>`. Gmail (web, not app) strips `<head>` and `<style>` blocks entirely. This means `.u-FullWidth`, `.u-MobileHide`, `.u-MobileShow`, and all 480px breakpoint rules are silently dropped for Gmail web users. Inline styles must duplicate layout-critical rules for Gmail compatibility.

**No `lang` attribute on `<html>` tag.**
The opening tag is `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` with no `lang="en"` (or equivalent). Screen readers and accessibility validators will flag this; it also affects some spam filters that check for locale signals.

**480px breakpoint is below current standard.**
Mobile media query targets `max-width: 480px`. Most modern email clients and guides use `600px` as the primary breakpoint; 480px misses a wide band of small-tablet and large-phone viewports (481–599px) that will receive the desktop layout.
