# Create a personalized dining room
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- Four days after the home tour send (which I actually liked), Article is back in my inbox with a dining room focus — and I'll be honest, the timing is almost annoyingly good. I'm literally mid-decision on dining chairs for the second house right now. The chair grid in the middle of this email, "What's your sit-uation?", made me stop cold. Nine chairs, color options visible, names underneath. That's how you merchandise a category to someone who has twelve tabs open comparing chair silhouettes.
- What I'm missing is any pricing anchor. After four consecutive 4th of July Sale emails through July 6, Article swung editorial-only — which I respect — but they've now been in my inbox eight times since June 26 with zero pricing signal this week. Pottery Barn's "New arrivals—classic, comfortable, collected" send, which I gave a 9, wove in member pricing quietly in the hero footnote. It didn't feel transactional. Article just doesn't do that, and the absence makes me feel like a browsing guest rather than a customer they want to close.
- I'd mark this as read rather than click — but I'd screenshot the chair grid and send it to my partner with "which one." That's a partial win for Article. Would I forward it? Basically yes, in the form of a photo.

## What's working

- The "What's your sit-uation?" chair grid is genuinely smart — nine chairs in multiple colors, names readable, scannable at a glance — this is how product merchandising should feel
- "Personal taste." as the hero headline is clean and non-overwrought; no exclamation point, no superlative, just confident
- The warm oak sideboard in the "Signature storage." section is the exact aesthetic we're targeting for the second house — one image, one CTA, done
- Email sections have a clear logical flow: chairs → storage → individuality → close; the eye travels without friction
- "SHOP DINING ROOM" and "SHOP SIDEBOARDS" are unambiguous CTAs — no "Discover the Journey of Furniture" wordsmithing

## What's weak

- No price signal anywhere — not in the hero, not under the chair thumbnails, not even a "from $XXX" anchor; I'm comparison-shopping against Pottery Barn and CB2 simultaneously and the absence makes me open another tab
- The "Let your individuality" section at the bottom is the softest part of the email — it's vague enough that Wayfair or Crate & Barrel could have templated this exact block; it doesn't feel like Article
- The bottom CTA block — reads like "SHOP MORO FREE" or something similar — is illegible at mobile scale; I genuinely couldn't parse it without squinting
- Eight emails since June 26. I noted back on July 6 that four sale emails in a row was pushing my patience; the editorial pivot helped, but the raw cadence is creeping toward C&B territory (25 C&B family emails in July), and that's a warning sign

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a pricing anchor under the chair grid** — even "dining chairs from $XXX" underneath "What's your sit-uation?" gives me the comparison hook I need to click through instead of opening a Pottery Barn tab
- 2. **Make each chair thumbnail a direct link** — "SHOP DINING CHAIRS" at the bottom of nine chairs is friction; individual product links per thumbnail is how a category page behaves, and this email is acting like a category page anyway
- 3. **Give "Let your individuality" a specific product hook** — a swatch count, a dimension, a bestseller badge — something that converts the soft editorial feel into a reason to click rather than scroll past
- 4. **Kill the unreadable bottom CTA** — if "SHOP MORO FREE" (or whatever it says) is about free shipping, make it a legible text row in the footer; if it's a section link, bump the font size or just cut it
- **Subject Alt A:** `Pick your chair. We'll handle the rest.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your dining room has a personality. Does your furniture?`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Nine chairs, every color, one room — find yours in under a minute`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Chairs, sideboards, and the one piece that ties the whole room together`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` — some clients (notably Gmail tabs, some Android clients) display the title tag text in the tab or notification preview. An empty title is a missed opportunity and may render as a blank label.

**Dark-mode CSS — invalid selector (trailing comma)**
```css
@media(prefers-color-scheme:dark){
  h1,h2,p,{color:#fff!important}
```
The trailing comma before `{` is malformed CSS. Parsers will drop the entire rule, meaning `h1`, `h2`, and `p` text will **not** invert to white in dark mode on any compliant client (Apple Mail, iOS Mail, Outlook.com). Evidence: line in `<style>` block, `prefers-color-scheme:dark` media query.

**Dark-mode CSS — invalid `!important` placement**
```css
a{color:#fff;!important}
```
A semicolon before `!important` is invalid. The `!important` flag will be ignored, meaning anchor color in dark mode falls back to client defaults (often blue/purple against a dark background). Evidence: same `prefers-color-scheme:dark` block.

**`[data-ogsc]` block (Outlook.com dark mode override)** — properly formed; no issues found in the visible source.
