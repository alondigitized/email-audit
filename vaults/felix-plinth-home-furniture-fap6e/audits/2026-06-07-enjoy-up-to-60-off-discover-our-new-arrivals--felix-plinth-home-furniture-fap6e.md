# Enjoy UP TO 60% OFF + Discover our NEW ARRIVALS
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- Fourth Pottery Barn this week. I want to be clear about that context before I say anything else — the first one, the warehouse sale that landed Tuesday morning, was a legitimate nine. This is the fourth. And the subject today tries to do two completely different things at once: sell me on 60% off AND introduce new arrivals in the same line. Those are not the same email. One is urgency-driven discount shopping; the other is aspirational browsing. Crashing them together just makes me trust neither.
- What saves this one from an immediate archive: the hero creative is actually different. Instead of the dark navy warehouse-sale banner I've now seen twice, this one opens with a light, coastal, natural-tone room — beige linen, wicker accents, soft afternoon light. That's a direction I'd actually save to the folder I keep for second-home mood boarding. The "Recommended Just for You" section in the body is also a genuine attempt at personalization, which I appreciate even if I'm skeptical it's doing much beyond category-level segmentation.
- I would not click. I would not forward. I'd mark it read, probably drag it into the Inspo folder on the off chance the hero image is useful later, and move on.

## What's working

- **Hero creative variety** — the light coastal palette is a real departure from the navy warehouse hero I've been staring at all week; someone made a call to swap the art and it was the right one
- **60% off is visible immediately** — doesn't make me dig, doesn't make me scroll; the discount is in the headline of the hero
- **"Recommended Just for You"** — even if it's just category logic, seeing a dedicated personalization block says this isn't a pure batch blast
- **Credit card / loyalty section at the bottom** — I buy on rewards points, so any brand surfacing its card offer gets a half-credit from me; at least Pottery Barn is consistent about it

## What's weak

- **Two messages, one subject** — "UP TO 60% OFF" and "Discover our NEW ARRIVALS" are contradictory tones; sale urgency and discovery browsing cancel each other out, and I opened with no idea what this email was actually for
- **All-caps in the subject** — "UP TO 60% OFF" and "NEW ARRIVALS" both SHOUTING; I expect that from Wayfair's ❌FINAL HOURS❌ emails, not from Pottery Barn
- **Same warehouse sale offer, again** — 60% off is the same number I've seen in every Pottery Barn subject this week; by send four, "up to 60% off" is just white noise
- **No deadline visible** — Article's outdoor email this week gave me an actual end date; Pottery Barn's perpetual "up to 60% off" makes me feel zero urgency to act today vs. Thursday

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one message and commit.** Either this is a warehouse sale email — in which case, own the urgency, put a deadline on the hero, skip the new arrivals — or it's a new arrivals reveal, in which case ditch the sale banner and let the fresh product breathe. The hybrid kills both.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the hero.** "Ends Sunday" or "72 hours left" turns "up to 60% off" from a standing offer into a reason to click today. Article does this; Pottery Barn consistently does not.
- 3. **Drop the all-caps in the subject.** Lowercase "up to 60% off" reads more trustworthy and less like a mall circular.
- 4. **Lean into the coastal creative more explicitly.** If the hero room is that good, name it — "coastal bedroom edit" or "natural materials, 60% off" gives me a category anchor instead of a vague sale.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Summer sale ends Sunday: up to 60% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh coastal pieces just in, warehouse pricing through the weekend.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your recommended picks — now at warehouse sale prices.`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "UP TO 60% OFF + New Arrivals" (2026-06-07)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed broken links and a server-side code injection into a `<style>` block are the most severe issues; the email is otherwise structurally sound but has pervasive HTTP mixed-content and missing alt text across the New Arrivals section that will degrade the experience in major clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Credit card banner CTA returns 403**
URL: `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?...&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`
Affects the "Apply & Buy" / Key Rewards Credit Card header module. Click lands on an error, not the card application.

**[FAIL] West Elm spacer GIF returns 403**
URL: `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
Cross-brand asset referenced in the Pottery Barn send. Domain is wrong (West Elm, not Pottery Barn), the asset is HTTP-only, and it 403s. Likely a copy-paste artifact from a sibling-brand template.

**[INFO] 24 tracking/click-redirect links not probed** — ESP wraps prevent direct HTTP verification; no pass/fail can be assigned to these.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CRITICAL] PHP server-side code injected into `<style>` block**
The following literal PHP statements appear inside a `<style>` tag instead of being executed server-side:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
This is a template rendering failure — the PHP was not processed before send. CSS parsers will skip the invalid declarations, but it is evidence of a broken build pipeline for this module.

**[FAIL] 13 images served over HTTP (mixed content)**
Entire New Arrivals sliced image set (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_12.jpg`) is served from `http://edm.potterybarn.com/...`. Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook.com all upgrade or block HTTP image loads. These images will likely show as broken for the majority of recipients. Additional HTTP assets:
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597...` (open-tracking pixel)
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`

**[WARN] ~47 images missing `alt` text**
All New Arrivals slices, most footer slices, all product images (`cayman-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `hydrocotton-quick-dry-towel-l.jpg`, etc.), and tracking pixels have empty or absent `alt` attributes. In image-blocking environments this renders the entire New Arrivals section invisible with no fallback text.

**[WARN] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
`<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` — disabling user zoom fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and will trigger accessibility flags in some ESPs.

**[WARN] Empty `<title>` element** — minor; no client-visible impact but some accessibility scanners flag it.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent** — QA relay did not capture it. If absent in production delivery, this violates Google/Yahoo 2024 bulk-sender requirements (mandatory for >5K/day senders) and CAN-SPAM best practice.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe not confirmed. Required by Gmail for bulk senders since February 2024.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not captured** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status unknown via this relay path. Cannot confirm authentication posture.

**[OK] `meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow"`** — correctly prevents email HTML from being indexed if mirrored.

**[OK] `meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer"`** — intentional for privacy; noted below for UTM impact.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**[WARN] `no-referrer` meta suppresses referrer header on click** — UTM parameters are embedded in the ESP click-redirect URLs (via `click.e.potterybarn.com`), so attribution should survive in analytics if the ESP correctly appends UTMs to the final destination URL. However, any direct `document.referrer` checks on the landing page will see an empty referrer. This is a known intentional trade-off but worth confirming with the analytics team.
