# New! A beautiful dining room, built for the way you live
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-11**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one's actually fine — which, after this week, feels notable. No kid backpacks, no unicorn lunch boxes, no back-to-school-vs-summer-sale banner fight. It's just dining rooms: chairs, tables, rugs, lighting, laid out collection by collection — Strand, Sullivan, Piertsan, Blaine, Bilbury. I've got two houses mid-reno and a dining table is genuinely on my list for the second home, so this is closer to on-target than anything C&B has sent me lately.
- That said, it's long. I scrolled through what feels like six or seven separate sub-collections before I hit anything resembling an offer, and even then it's "enter for a chance to win $500" from The Design Desk, not a real discount. Compare that to CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" send I gave a 7 — that one led with an actual price cut. This is all inspiration, no incentive.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I scrolled far enough to find the Sullivan table since round pedestal tables are what I've actually been saving to my folder. Would I forward it? No — it's too generic a catalog dump to send a friend, nothing screams "this is the one."

## What's working

- Clean, moody staged shots — the Strand dining room with the arched doorway and pendant light actually looks like something I'd screenshot for my reno folder.
- No family/kids branding bleeding into this one — a relief after five Crate & Kids sends this week.
- Collection names + one-line descriptors (Sullivan: "simple modern design with room for everyone," Piertsan: "completely customizable") give me a quick read on style fit without forcing me to click through.
- Furniture category is exactly my persona's focus — dining room, not decor filler.

## What's weak

- No offer up top. First screen is pure lifestyle shot and a "Shop New Dining Furniture" button — no price, no %, nothing that tells me why to act now versus bookmark for later.
- Way too many sub-collections stacked vertically (Strand, Sullivan, Piertsan, Blaine, Bilbury, lighting, rugs) — by the fourth one I've lost the thread on which table I actually liked.
- The Design Desk $500 sweepstakes and the "crafted-to-last cookware" cross-sell at the bottom feel bolted on — cookware has nothing to do with a dining room refresh, and sweepstakes entries don't move me.
- No swatch/sample program mention anywhere, and I'm mid-reno on two houses trying to match a palette — that's exactly the content I need and it's missing.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one hero product and a real price or % off, not just a mood shot — Pottery Barn's coffee table styling email at least tried an editorial angle, but neither brand is giving me numbers, and a concrete "20% off dining tables" would beat both.
- 2. Cut the collection count in half — pick two or three strongest (Sullivan and Strand, based on the visuals) and link the rest to a "shop all dining" page instead of full modules.
- 3. Move the $500 Design Desk consult higher if it's meant to be a hook — right now it's buried after seven scrolls and reads like an afterthought.
- 4. Drop the cookware cross-sell entirely from a dining-furniture email — wrong category, dilutes focus.
- 5. Add a swatch/finish-sample CTA — I'm renovating two homes and picking finishes; "order a wood sample" would be a stronger click driver for me than "Shop Now" x9.
