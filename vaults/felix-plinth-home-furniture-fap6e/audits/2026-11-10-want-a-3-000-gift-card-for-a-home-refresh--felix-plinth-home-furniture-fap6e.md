# Want a $3,000 gift card for a home refresh?
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-10**
## Executive summary

- Finally, a Pottery Barn send that isn't another plaid-ribbon Christmas Shop hero. A $3,000 gift card for a home refresh got my attention, since I'm doing two houses. Then I saw that the "prize" is a sweepstakes entry for booking a design appointment. That's a lead-gen funnel for Design Services, not a deal. It also lands the day after the nightstands email (11/10, 8/10), which was a real product send I could act on.
- The body wanders. After the sweepstakes hero comes a free in-store classes block (mantel, tree, table, bar cart), then a "Take It Home Today" store-finder, then "Recommended just for you." The recommendations are stockings, a dining table, a curtain rod, and a bed. That's the standard grab bag. The Banks and Toscana extendable dining tables are relevant to me. The stockings aren't.
- Would I click? Probably "Book a Free Appointment" is a maybe, since I do want a designer to tell me which of my two houses gets the Cayman bed. I'd save it to my inbox folder rather than act today. I wouldn't forward it. It's a sweepstakes, and I don't send those to friends.

## What's working

- The hero is clear. "Win $3,000 Toward Your Home Refresh" is big white type on a dark wreath-and-plate photo, and the white "Book a Free Appointment" button stands out.
- The in-store classes grid has specific dates and times (11/15, 11/22, 12/6, 12/13 at 10 AM), so I can plan around them.
- "Recommended Just For You" has real furniture, the Banks and Toscana extendable tables and the Cayman Platform Bed. That's closer to my renovation than the stockings.
- The 10% back rewards card block is in my wheelhouse. I buy on the highest-rewards card, so I'd run that math.

## What's weak

- The sweepstakes framing buries the real offer. There's no discount and no price anywhere in the product grid. Compare that to yesterday's "Nightstands starting at $349."
- The legal copy is huge. The sweepstakes terms, rewards disclosures and Affirm details run for most of the bottom third of the email, and the hero footnote is tiny gray text.
- Too many competing CTAs: the appointment, class RSVPs, store finder, app download, credit card, Affirm. Nothing tells me which is the point.
- The product grid is generic. Stockings next to a dining table next to a curtain rod doesn't feel "just for me." There are no swatches, no shipping timeline for the furniture and no sample program.
- The cadence is exhausting. This is at least the twelfth Pottery Barn email in about five days.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the middle. Pick one story, the appointment/sweepstakes, and drop the classes grid and store finder, or move them below the product grid.
- 2. Put a visible, plain-language entry rule next to the CTA ("Book by 1/3/27 = automatic entry") instead of the tiny footnote.
- 3. Make the product grid relevant to the design-services angle: the dining and bedroom pieces with prices and ship windows, and drop the stockings.
- 4. Add a swatch or sample CTA and a member-pricing line. Crate & Barrel pushes 2x Rewards in the headline, and Wayfair at least puts a hard percent off in the hero.
- 5. Trim the legal text and link out to the full terms.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Book a design consult, win $3,000`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Free design help + a shot at $3,000`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Book by Jan 3 and you're automatically entered. Free, no purchase needed.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus free in-store classes: mantels, trees and tablescapes.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email uses a fixed-width 650px table layout. Its main defects are a stray block of PHP `header()` calls inside a `<style>` tag, about 30 images without alt text (the four hero slices carry `alt=""`), two `http://` image sources, and recipient email addresses in plain-text tracking-pixel URLs. The QA run reports 57% pass, 0 issues and 3 warnings. The compliance and authentication warnings come from the AgentMail relay and cannot be confirmed from the data.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Destinations unverified.** 46 links go through `click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=…` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud click redirects). The QA probe skipped all of them, so landing-page status and redirect chains are unknown.
- **Insecure image sources.** Two images load over HTTP and may be blocked or flagged as mixed content:
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- **Stale asset.** The second image sits in a 2022 folder, so it is probably a reused module.
- **Dead code.** A commented-out credit-card banner block (`cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`) is still shipped in the HTML. This is minor payload bloat.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Invalid CSS.** The second `<style>` block begins with PHP statements: `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: no-store…"); …`. A CSS parser will treat that text as part of the next selector's prelude. It is likely to drop the `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` rule that follows, so iOS auto-linking of dates and addresses will not be neutralized.
- **Missing alt text.** QA flags about 30 images.
  - The hero slices `slices_01.jpg` to `slices_04.jpg` (`…/1110_TUE_AM2_DC_SweepsTIHTClassBnr/`) are each wrapped in an `<a>` with `alt=""`. That leaves the links with no accessible name, and with images off the hero content and CTA are invisible.
  - The product images `holiday-icons-crewel-stockings-2-l.jpg`, `classic-velvet-stockings-l.jpg`, `banks-extendable-dining-table-76-128-1-l.jpg`, `toscana-extendable-dining-table-60-125-l.jpg`, `standard-curtain-rod-wall-bracket-l.jpg` and `cayman-platform-bed-1-l.jpg` have no alt text.
  - The footer slices `slices_01` to `slices_18` under `/Email-Footer/` have none either.
  - `3ceb0c96-f305-4f56-a6bf-1ce072abc871.jpg` also has none.
  - The tracking pixels (`leadback`, `d.turn.com`, `svopn`, `coreemail.html`) are flagged too. For these, `alt=""` plus explicit 1×1 dimensions is the correct fix.
- **Doctype and structure.**
  - The AMPscript comments sit before `<!DOCTYPE>`. A comment before the doctype can trigger quirks mode in some older Outlook/IE-based renderers.
  - The XHTML 1.0 Strict doctype is used with deprecated `<center>`, `bgcolor` and `border` attributes.
  - The tables have no `role="presentation"`.
- **Inert responsive rules.** The mobile CSS targets `table[class="wrapper"]` and `img[class="photo"]`. No element in the visible (truncated) source has those classes, and the layout tables are hard-set to `width="650"`. The mobile rules therefore appear to do nothing, though the truncation means the rest of the markup is unchecked.
- **Web font.** The Google Fonts `<link>` for Montserrat is stripped by Gmail and Outlook. This is only a minor concern.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **No unrendered tokens.** None are visible in the available source, and the AMPscript slot comment is empty and benign.
- **Recipient email exposed in URLs.** The address is passed in clear text in pixel and image URLs:
  - `https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
  - `https://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
- These URLs can leak PII into server logs, proxies and third-party systems. Hashing the address would be safer.
- **Third-party pixels.** `secure.leadback.advertising.com` and `d.turn.com` are third-party ad-tech pixels loaded from a promotional email. This is a privacy and deliverability consideration, not a rendering bug.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Unconfirmed warnings.** QA reports `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) as not found, and `Authentication-Results` as missing. The QA tool itself says the AgentMail relay may strip these headers, so they are not confirmed defects. Raw headers from a direct inbox copy are needed to verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC and one-click unsubscribe.
- **Footer not reviewed.** The HTML was truncated before the footer, so the visible unsubscribe link and postal address could not be checked. The footer is built from image slices (`/Email-Footer/slices_01` to `slices_18`) without alt text. If the unsubscribe and address text live only in images, they are inaccessible when images are blocked.
- **Sender alignment.** The From domain `e.potterybarn.com` matches the click domain `click.e.potterybarn.com`, which is good for alignment.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **UTM parameters cannot be assessed.** All primary links are opaque `?qs=` redirects, so UTM presence and landing pages are unknown. Resolve a sample of the hero, product and footer links, including the four hero slices, to confirm the final URLs and tags.
- **Unprobed destinations.** Nothing was checked for the `$3,000 gift card` / Design Crew appointment landing page. Link-to-page alignment is unverified.
- **Cross-brand asset.** `https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` is a West Elm spacer served inside a Pottery Barn email. It adds a dependency on another brand's CDN path.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Remove the PHP `header()` lines from the `<style>` block and confirm the `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` rule works.
2. Add descriptive alt text to the four hero slices (they are linked, so empty alt is a failure), the product images and the footer slices. Set `alt=""` and 1×1 dimensions on the tracking pixels.
3. Switch the two `http://` images to `https://`, and retire or replace the 2022 `slices_02.jpg`.
4. Stop putting the raw recipient email in the `coreemail.html` and `svopn` pixel URLs. Use a hashed or opaque ID.
5. Move AMPscript comments after the `<!DOCTYPE>`, or strip them at send time. Add `role="presentation"` to the layout tables.
6. Either add the `wrapper` and `photo` classes the media queries expect or fix the selectors, and add a responsive path for the 650px layout.
7. Verify the headers from a raw inbox copy (SPF/DKIM/DMARC, `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`) and resolve the 46 redirect links to check their destinations and UTMs.
8. Delete the commented-out credit-card block to reduce payload size.
