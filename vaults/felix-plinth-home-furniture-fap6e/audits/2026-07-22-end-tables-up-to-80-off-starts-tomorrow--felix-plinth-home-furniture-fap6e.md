# END TABLES  🚩 UP TO 80% OFF starts TOMORROW 🚩  
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- Fifth Wayfair email in three days. I want to get that on the record immediately, because it's the lens through which I read everything else. Yesterday I got the nightstands Black Friday in July send — which I actually rated well because "NIGHTSTANDS" in the subject line is exactly what I'm shopping for right now. Today the subject promises me end tables at 80% off starting *tomorrow*. That's a relevant tease. End tables are on the list for the second home. So I opened it.
- And then I scrolled and found rugs. A recliner. More rugs. Kitchen mats. Whatever "deals of the day" Wayfair is pushing right now — none of which are end tables. The subject line is essentially a pre-announcement for a sale that hasn't started, wrapped around a body of completely unrelated inventory. That's not a teaser campaign. That's a bait-and-switch dressed as urgency. The 24-hour deals visible in the body don't align with what got me here.
- I would not click on anything in this email. I'd probably scan it for three seconds, find no end tables, and archive it while mildly more annoyed at Wayfair than I was this morning. I would not forward it — there's nothing here to forward. This is the email equivalent of a store hanging a 80% OFF sign in the window and then telling you the sale starts tomorrow when you walk in.

## What's working

- **"END TABLES" in the subject is the right hook.** I'm actively cross-referencing end table options across two rooms. Article's dining room email last week hit the same "oh, that's actually my problem" nerve — Wayfair got this one right at the subject level.
- **"Starts TOMORROW" creates real urgency.** Unlike the previous three sends (two of which were literally identical "FREE shipping" subjects with zero time pressure), there's an actual calendar beat here. That's progress.
- **The 80% hero is immediate and unambiguous.** Big number, high on the page, no squinting required. Crate & Barrel's "UP TO 40% OFF ENDS TODAY" email this month had the same clean hierarchy — Wayfair matches it here.
- **Black Friday in July is a legible campaign frame.** I know what I'm looking at. That's not nothing.

## What's weak

- **The body doesn't deliver on the subject.** End tables are nowhere in the visible product grid. What I see are rugs, a recliner, and what looks like kitchen mats — essentially a carryover from yesterday's kitchen mats email. That continuity feels lazy, not strategic.
- **"But wait, there's more..." is content surrender.** That's a QVC phrase, not a design decision. It signals Wayfair ran out of end-table inventory to show me and just appended their standard deals grid.
- **Five emails in three days.** This isn't a cadence issue — it's a frequency alarm. Pottery Barn and CB2 both hit my inbox a lot, but at least they vary the creative. Two of these Wayfair sends had the exact same subject line, verbatim. My mental spam filter is starting to engage.
- **No price anchoring on end tables.** If you're telling me end tables go 80% off tomorrow, show me the $300 table that'll be $60. Give me something to come back for. Right now the promise is abstract.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show me one hero end table at the sale price.** Even a single product tile — "this $349 end table hits $69 tomorrow at 9am" — gives me a reason to set a reminder and actually come back. The most impactful change by far.
- 2. **Kill the rugs grid or section it clearly.** If "deals of the day" is the B-content filler, put it below a clear divider labeled "Today's deals while you wait." Don't let it colonize a page I came to for end tables.
- 3. **Cut one email.** Seriously. This send and the kitchen mats send were both July 22. Pick one. Article sent me four emails this entire month and I've found all four relevant. Volume is not velocity.
- 4. **Anchor the countdown.** "Starts TOMORROW" is fine but "Starts tomorrow, July 23 at 9AM ET" is a calendar event. Give me something to put in my reminder app.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Web font — incomplete fallback stack**
```css
@font-face {
  font-family: "Sofia";
  src: url(https://secure.img.wfrcdn.com/.../sofia_reg.woff) format("woff");
  mso-font-alt: "Arial"
}
```
- Only `woff` format declared; no `woff2`. Minor load-time inefficiency where fonts do render.
- `mso-font-alt` covers Outlook, but no CSS `font-family` stack fallback (e.g., `font-family: "Sofia", Arial, sans-serif`) is visible in the truncated source. Gmail, Yahoo, and all webmail clients silently drop `@font-face` — if body text elements aren't specifying a system fallback inline, those clients render the browser default serif.

**Aggressive word-break on product names**
```css
.ProductRec-name { word-break: break-all; }
```
`break-all` splits at any character boundary, including mid-word (e.g., "round" → "rou-nd"). Should be `overflow-wrap: break-word` or `word-break: break-word`.

**Fixed pixel heights on dynamic content**
```css
.ProductRec-price { height: 40px; }
.ProductRec-salesCopy { height: 28px; }
.ProductRec-stars, .ProductRec-shippingCopy { height: 16px; }
```
Fixed heights at the 480px breakpoint will clip content if rendered text exceeds the allocated height — particularly risky for sale copy with longer strings or if font scaling increases on accessibility-configured devices.

**HTML size risk**
The CSS block alone is already very large before the body starts. If the full HTML exceeds **102KB**, Gmail clips the email with a "View entire message" prompt, suppressing the unsubscribe footer and any below-the-fold CTAs. Verify compiled size.
