# New! Modern heritage style for every space
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- Fourteen. I had to sit with that number for a second. Fourteen emails from the Crate & Barrel family in roughly thirty-six hours, and this one — the second heritage-themed send from the main C&B brand *today alone* — lands as a companion piece to this morning's "Just in: 1500+ ways to bring heritage style home." Same aesthetic. Same warm wood tones. Same earthy, moody palette. Just a slightly different editorial angle bolted onto what is functionally the same campaign concept. I'm not annoyed at the email itself — the photography is actually solid, the room shots are doing real work for someone actively picking finishes for two houses — but I am annoyed at the *decision* to double-tap this concept in under twelve hours.
- The visual execution is legitimately appealing to me. Warm walnut, leather seating, clay tones, lived-in rugs — this is the exact mood I'm trying to land in the second home's living room and I know it. Crate & Barrel earns a look here in a way Wayfair's "free shipping on $35" reminder absolutely does not. But the email asks me to shop an editorial concept without a single price anchor in the hero. No percentage off. No member pricing. No limited availability signal. Just vibes and "shop the style." For the way I actually buy — highest-rewards card, scanning for the moment a price makes math sense — this is a browse email, not a buy email.
- I'd save it to the folder and move on. Forward to a friend? No. Click today? Probably not. Maybe tomorrow if I'm in a room-planning headspace.

## What's working

- The hero photography earns it — dark wood bed frame, earthy layered bedding, moody ambient light. This is exactly the visual reference I'd pull if I were building a Pinterest board for the second home's master.
- The multi-room structure (bedroom → living → dining → tableware) maps to how I actually shop when I'm mid-renovation: I need *systems*, not lone pieces, and the email gestures at that.
- The warm, masculine-leaning palette doesn't alienate me the way the Crate & Kids celebrity baby nursery sends do. This is clearly aimed at an adult with design intent.
- No render bugs I can spot — clean layout, images load, text is legible at email width. After CB2's occasionally janky sends, clean execution matters.

## What's weak

- Same-day second heritage send. The morning email was "1500+ ways to bring heritage style home" — this one is "Modern heritage style for every space." These should be one campaign, not two sends in eight hours.
- Zero price signal in the hero or anywhere prominent. Article's "Home tour: A warm welcome" editorial send worked because it followed a clear sale sequence — the editorial was a *reward* for subscribers who'd already seen the discount. This one floats without an anchor.
- The CTA structure is scattered. I can see multiple small shop-the-look type links throughout, but nothing that functions as a dominant "here's the one thing to do" button. CB2's floor sample sale email had one move: get to the floor sample prices. This has fifteen moves.
- "For every space" is doing a lot of false-promise lifting. I have specific spaces. A den, a guest room, a kitchen. Tell me which one this heritage moment lives in best.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse the same-day double-send** — if morning was the breadth play ("1500+ ways"), this one needed to be the depth play: one specific room, one specific story, one clear reason to click today over tomorrow.
- 2. **Add a price entry point in the hero** — even "From $XX" or "Member pricing on new arrivals" gives someone like me a hook. Right now the whole email is window shopping.
- 3. **Pick a dominant CTA and make it earn it** — one button, above the fold, with a verb that isn't "Discover." Something like "Shop the Heritage Collection" or "Build Your Room" (if linking to a room-builder or curated bundle).
- 4. **Sequence the editorial** — Article proved you can run editorial sends if they're sequenced after offer sends. Lead with a price reason, follow with the mood piece. Not two mood pieces back to back.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New in: Modern heritage style, starting from $XX`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The heritage look — finally, for every room you're working on`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New arrivals in the warm wood and earthy tones everyone's pulling right now`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Bedroom, living room, dining — the full heritage edit is here`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Conflicting image `max-width` media queries**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
The 640px rule is declared after the 768px rule. At viewport widths ≥768px, both breakpoints fire, and cascade order means `max-width: 640px` wins on desktop — overriding the apparent intent of 600px. Either the rule order is wrong or one of these values is stale copy-paste.

**[MEDIUM] `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users from zooming on mobile. Violates WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Common in email templates but worth flagging for accessibility compliance.

**[MEDIUM] Universal `line-height: 100%` reset**
```css
* { line-height: 100%; }
```
Applies to every element. This collapses line spacing on multi-line text blocks and can cause rendering artifacts in clients that don't fully support the cascade override. Should be scoped to specific selectors.

**[LOW] Template placeholder comment left in production HTML**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
<!----> 
```
"IMPUT" is a misspelling of "INPUT." The comment and the trailing empty `<!---->` are scaffolding artifacts from the template build process that were not removed before send. Not user-visible but indicates incomplete pre-send QA.

**[LOW] Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
The combined tag and three individual override tags all appear:
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
Functionally redundant; signals copy-paste template merging without cleanup.
