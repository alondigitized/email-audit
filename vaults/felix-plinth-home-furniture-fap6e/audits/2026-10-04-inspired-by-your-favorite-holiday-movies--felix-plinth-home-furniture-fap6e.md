# Inspired by your favorite holiday movies...
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-04**
## Executive summary

- Pottery Barn again, and this one is the first in a while that made me smile. It's a holiday movie collab email: Christmas Vacation, Elf and Peanuts. The Griswold's plates and the Elf pillows are fun, and I'd save it to my Pinterest-adjacent folder. It also has no discount in it, and after the "Save on Furniture & More" send on 10/3 and the "Open Box Outlet" send on 10/2, that's a relief.
- But I'm renovating two houses. Character-licensed tableware and Buddy the Elf pillows don't help me pick a sofa, and nothing here tells me what anything costs. There's no price, no percentage and no ship date anywhere. The "Recommended Just For You" block underneath (curtain rod, Lit Ghost Collection, rug, bed, dining table) is just the usual catalog filler. It also isn't personalized, since I've been looking at two houses' worth of furniture and it shows me ghosts.
- I'd open it, scroll once, and maybe click the Christmas Vacation plates. I wouldn't forward it. I might send the Elf pillow to a friend who loves that movie, but only as a joke.

## What's working

- The "Holiday Movie Magic" header is a clear theme, and the dark red banner stands out from the pile of sale-box heroes.
- The three licensed collabs (Christmas Vacation, Elf, Peanuts) each get a big lifestyle photo and a one-line caption. It reads like a curated story, which is why I'd save it.
- Each section has its own "Shop Now" link, so I know exactly what each image goes to.
- It's a refreshing change of pace from the 10/3 "All tabletop now ships free!" and "Favorites in stock & on sale" sends, which were pure promo.
- The Design Crew "Free Design Services" banner and the 10% back card block are useful for someone doing two houses, and they're easy to find near the bottom.

## What's weak

- There's no offer, no price and no deadline. "Explore the Christmas Shop" is a browse button, not a reason to act now.
- The captions ("All for a fun, old-fashioned family Christmas") are thin. I can't tell what's in the collection or whether any of it is still in stock.
- The "Recommended Just For You" grid has nothing to do with the movie theme. The curtain rod and the Reeva rug read as generic filler, not "just for me."
- The footer is dense with tiny legal text and the card, Affirm and app blocks, so the bottom half is a wall of fine print.
- It's early October. I get that holiday is coming, but this is send number twelve-plus since 9/29, and it feels like the same brand-voice wallpaper.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put one concrete reason to click up top: a ship-by date, "new today," or a launch-day perk, so the hero isn't only mood.
- 2. Show a price or "from $X" under each collab. Tableware at $39 vs. $149 changes whether I click.
- 3. Make the recommendations use my browsing, like bedding, rugs and dining tables, instead of showing ghosts to someone who's renovating.
- 4. Cut the footer blocks down. App, card, Affirm and design services all compete for the last scroll.
- 5. Cap the cadence. Crate & Barrel and CB2 do the same thing, and I'm in a permanent family snooze.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Griswold plates + Elf pillows are here`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New: Christmas Vacation x Pottery Barn`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Limited-run Elf, Peanuts & Christmas Vacation collabs. See what's in stock.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Table, bedding and decor from the movies you rewatch every year.`
- Compared with the others in my inbox, Wayfair's "COFFEE TABLES we HAD to share" at least tells me the category. CB2's "A well-collected NYC apartment" earns the save with a story, much like this one. Article's "Home tour: A space fit for fall" was a better mood board, but it didn't have licensed collabs. This one beats all three on novelty and loses to Crate & Barrel's "Up to 20% off your living room update" on having a number.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based, image-slice-heavy XHTML template. It has widespread missing `alt` text, three non-HTTPS image requests, and stray non-CSS code in a `<style>` block. The QA run couldn't verify the authentication and unsubscribe headers or the 48 redirect links, so those items are unconfirmed rather than confirmed defects.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **48 tracked links were not probed.** They use redirect hosts such as `click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=ABB7InYi…` (the logo link, for example). Destinations are hidden behind an opaque `qs=` token, so link health is unverified.
- **Cleartext tracking pixel carries the recipient address.** `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` is served over HTTP, so the email address is exposed in transit.
- **The recipient address also appears in `https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`.** That is PII in a URL query string, which gets logged by proxies and servers.
- **Third-party pixel.** `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/L21rdC84NjEvY2lkLzE3NDc3ODk3MzMvdC8y/cat/1` is an ad-network beacon, loaded on top of the first-party `clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/b0c47789-…` open pixel.
- **Cross-brand asset host.** `https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` is a West Elm domain used in a Pottery Barn send. This is a dependency risk if that asset is moved or removed.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **About 45 images are missing `alt`.** This includes the content slices (`F26_Collabs_B11_HolidayXMASCollabRoundUp/slices_01–06.jpg`, `F26_Top_NewArrivals+InStock/slices_01–02.jpg`), the product images (`reeva-handwoven-rug-3-l.jpg`, `scarlett-upholstered-bed-1-l.jpg`, etc.), `DesignCrewPrimaryBottomBanner.jpg` and `Email-Footer/slices_01–18.jpg`. With images off, the email will be blank or unreadable. Screen readers will announce filenames.
- **The tracking pixels and `spacer_0.gif` also lack `alt`.** These are decorative, so `alt=""` is the right fix.
- **Three images use `http://`.** They are `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, `2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` and the `svopn` pixel. Some clients block these as mixed content, so the images may not render or the pixel may not fire.
- **`<title></title>` is empty.**
- **The viewport meta tag blocks pinch-zoom.** `maximum-scale=1` stops users from zooming in. Combined with `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none`, this hurts low-vision users.
- **Stray PHP code leaked into the second `<style>` block.** `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: …")` sits inside the CSS. The parser will ignore it, but it follows `.yshortcuts{…} html{…}` and can break the rules that follow in some clients.
- **The Google Fonts `<link>` (Montserrat) is stripped or blocked in Gmail and Outlook.** No fallback stack was confirmed in the truncated source.
- **The preheader contains a raw `&`** ("Peanuts, Elf & more"), which should be `&amp;`.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The recipient address is resolved correctly in the URLs above, and no unresolved tokens appeared in the visible source. The source is truncated, so the full body is unchecked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found.** The QA note says the AgentMail relay may be stripping them, so this is unconfirmed. If the headers are really absent, Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules are not met.
- **`Authentication-Results` was not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown, again likely because of the relay.
- **The in-body unsubscribe link and postal address were not verifiable.** They sit in the truncated footer. Check the full HTML.
- **The sender domain is consistent.** `PotteryBarn@e.potterybarn.com` uses the same subdomain family as the tracking hosts.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **No UTM parameters are visible in the source.** Destinations are inside the `qs=` redirect tokens, and the 48 links were skipped, so UTM and landing-page alignment can't be confirmed.
- **Product images load from `www.potterybarn.com/pbimgs/…`.** That is consistent with the site, but the click-through targets are unverified.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add `alt` text to all content images, and `alt=""` to the pixels and spacer. This is the highest-impact fix: 45 warnings of the 57% pass rate.
2. Move the three `http://` assets to HTTPS (`Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, `slices_02.jpg`, the `svopn` pixel). Stop putting the recipient email in the `svopn` and `coreemail.html` query strings, or hash it.
3. Remove the PHP `header()` lines from the `<style>` block, set a non-empty `<title>`, and drop `maximum-scale=1`.
4. Host the spacer on `edm.potterybarn.com`, not `edm.westelm.com`.
5. Escape `&` in the preheader.
6. QA tooling: capture raw headers (`List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, `Authentication-Results`) from the original message, not the relay copy. Follow the redirects on `click.e.potterybarn.com` to read the final URLs and UTMs, instead of skipping them.
7. Re-run this audit on the full, untruncated HTML to check the footer, the unsubscribe link and the remaining tokens.
