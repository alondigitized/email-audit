# The deals are in full swing
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- I've been getting destroyed by Memorial Day blasts all week — twelve from Pottery Barn in 72 hours, ten-plus from Crate & Barrel in 48, and CB2 sending me a welcome email the day *after* a sale email like the sequencing team clocked out early. Article shows up three days after their welcome send, and the restraint alone earns them goodwill before I read a single word. That cadence is doing more work than their subject line, which is frankly terrible and we'll get to it.
- Once I'm inside the email, the offer is real and directly relevant. I'm actively sourcing an outdoor sectional for the second home and a dining set for the primary right now. Up to 40% off, category tiles for Outdoor, Dining Room, and Bedroom — Article basically built this email from my current purchase queue without knowing it. The visual execution is clean: green badge, big SALE, single CTA. No render issues, no Pottery Barn-style four-hero-image pile-up, no CB2 identity crisis in the layout.
- I'm not clicking immediately because there's no specific product pulling me and no deadline creating urgency. This goes in the "browse tonight" folder — saved, not acted on. Won't forward, but I'd actually recommend Article to a friend with better taste than I'd ever recommend a Pottery Barn email.

## What's working

- **Cadence is doing heavy lifting.** Second email in three days after a welcome. After enduring the Crate & Barrel Memorial Day blitz, Article showing this kind of restraint makes me trust the brand more, not less.
- **40% is a real discount on furniture.** At the ticket sizes I'm spending — dining table, outdoor sectional — 40% off is thousands of dollars, not a hat rack sale. The number means something here.
- **Category tiles map to active decisions.** Shop Outdoor, Shop Dining Room, Shop Bedroom — three of those four tiles correspond to rooms I'm actively sourcing for. This isn't generic; this is my life in grid form.
- **Green visual hierarchy reads fast on a phone.** Badge → SALE → SHOP THE SALE is a three-step eye path. I didn't have to hunt for the offer or the button.
- **No render bugs, no broken images, no lorem ipsum.** A clean send in a week where broken Memorial Day emails have been a theme.

## What's weak

- **Subject line burns the first impression.** "The deals are in full swing" tells me nothing: no brand, no category, no number. Wayfair ran "LOW-PILE RUGS are heeere!" this week — annoying voice, but at least I knew what I was walking into before I tapped.
- **No specific product hero.** Every CTA is a category link. Pottery Barn led with a specific outdoor chair last week and I bookmarked it. Article is asking me to go browse; Pottery Barn handed me an item.
- **No deadline.** "Limited time only" and "while they last" are placeholders. I have two active purchase decisions right now — a hard date (Ends May 26) converts "save for later" into "decide tonight."
- **Footer button split dilutes focus.** "Explore What's New" and "Shop Best Sellers" sitting below the sale CTA are competing for clicks that should go to the sale. Drop them from this send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject with a number and category.** "The deals are in full swing" has zero information value. Lead with the discount or the rooms.
- 2. **Add one specific product hero above the category grid.** A bestselling outdoor sectional or a dining table at the 40%-off price anchors the browse and gives me something to screenshot for my partner.
- 3. **Put a hard end date in the email body and the preheader.** "Ends Monday, May 26" converts this from ambient to actionable.
- 4. **Cut "Explore What's New" / "Shop Best Sellers" from this email.** Those belong in a non-sale send. Here they're noise.
- 5. **Use the preheader to do the job the subject won't.** Right now the body copy is serving as the preheader. Rewrite it to be a second subject line.
- **Subject Alt A:** `40% off furniture — Article Memorial Day sale is live`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your outdoor and dining room haul: up to 40% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Outdoor, dining, bedroom — real discounts, ends May 26. No code needed.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Save up to 40% on the pieces you've been watching. Stock is moving fast.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — CSS syntax error: trailing comma in selector (dark mode block)**
```css
/* BROKEN — trailing comma before { */
h1,h2,p,{color:#fff!important}
```
This is invalid CSS. Browsers and email clients that strictly parse CSS will drop the entire rule, leaving `h1`, `h2`, and `p` text unaffected in dark mode.  
**Fix:** `h1,h2,p{color:#fff!important}`

**Issue 2 — CSS syntax error: semicolon before `!important`**
```css
/* BROKEN */
a{color:#fff;!important}
```
The semicolon terminates the declaration before `!important` is parsed, so `!important` is interpreted as an unknown token and the override is ignored.  
**Fix:** `a{color:#fff!important}`

Both errors appear in the `@media(prefers-color-scheme:dark)` block; the `[data-ogsc]` block has the correct syntax (`a{color:#fff!important}`), so Outlook.com dark mode is unaffected but native dark mode (Gmail Android, Apple Mail) is broken for these rules.

**Issue 3 — Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some clients (notably older Samsung Mail) surface the `<title>` value as a preview. Empty is acceptable but a descriptive value reduces pre-header pollution.

**Cannot confirm:** `alt` text completeness and image-off fallback colors require the full source.
