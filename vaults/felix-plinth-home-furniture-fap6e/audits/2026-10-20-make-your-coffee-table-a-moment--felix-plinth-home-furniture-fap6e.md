# Make your coffee table a moment
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-20**
## Executive summary

- My first thought was that this is a good-looking email with no reason to act today. It's the sixth CB2 send in about six days, and the only thing separating it from 10/19's "COLLECTED, NOT EXPECTED" is a coffee table in the hero. Marble or acacia, two sizes, "works for any style and space." That's a real product, and I do need a coffee table for the second house. But nothing here says what it costs or why I should buy this week.
- The "One table, three looks" run (Minimalist, Collector, Decorator) is pure Pinterest-folder material. The bowls, candle holders and sculptures are the kind of styling images I save. But the bowls and candle holders aren't the table. I scrolled past about six styled vignettes to get to the actual furniture, and the prices only show up in the "More for you" grid at the very bottom. That grid is a beds-and-dining-chairs mix that has nothing to do with a coffee table. I'd save it for the images, probably not click, and not forward it.
- Compared with the other senders: Pottery Barn's barstool email ("from $99") put a price and a category in the subject, so I knew in one glance whether it applied. Wayfair's pergola send had a $134-off badge and a struck-through price right at the top. CB2 gives me mood and no number. Article's Melo launch worked the same way, a beautiful room and a product, but at least it was one product from top to bottom.

## What's working

- The hero names one product and gives sizes and materials ("two easy sizes… marble or acacia wood"), so I know what I'm looking at.
- "One table, three looks" is a smart structure for a design-minded shopper. I can see how the same table works in different rooms.
- The photography is moody and cohesive in warm browns and amber. It matches the CB2 voice I expect, and it's the kind of image I'd file in my inspiration folder.
- The "Shop coffee tables" button shows up twice, once at the top and once after the looks, so I don't have to hunt for it.
- The 10% back in Rewards Dollars on the CB2 card is visible in the footer. That's the math I care about when I pick a card.

## What's weak

- There's no price, no "from $X" and no offer anywhere near the hero. After 10/17's Warehouse Sale at up to 60% off and 10/18's 30% dining event, this one asks me to pay full price with no hint of it.
- The three "looks" are mostly accessories (bowls, sculpture, candle holders), and the table barely appears in them. I wanted to see it in each look.
- The "More for you" grid has a king bed, dining chairs and a folding table. None of that supports the coffee-table story. It reads like a generic recommendation block.
- The email is very long for a single-product message. There's an Interior Design Services block and an app/registry stack after the products, and it all dilutes the hero.
- Sixth send in six days, and I'm in a permanent Crate & Barrel family snooze. Even a good creative gets a low-effort "mark as read."

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price in the hero ("Coffee tables from $X") and say what the sizes are. That's the single biggest lift.
- 2. Make the three looks about the table. Show the marble and acacia versions in each room, link each look to its table, and put the accessories after.
- 3. Cut or reorder the bottom third. Swap the generic "More for you" grid for coffee tables and matching side tables, and drop the registry and app blocks from this send.
- 4. Cut the cadence. One strong send per week would beat six moody ones.
- 5. Add shipping and lead-time info for the table. For furniture that's the first thing I check.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Marble or acacia: the coffee table, from $X`
- **Subject Alt B:** `One coffee table, three ways to style it`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Two sizes, two finishes, in stock and ready to ship.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `See it styled as a minimalist, a collector and a decorator.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible markup (XHTML 1.0 Transitional, `lang="en"`, charset and viewport set) is structurally sound. The QA run found 0 hard failures and 3 warnings, but the compliance and authentication checks are inconclusive because the relay didn't capture the headers. The HTML is truncated, so the body (footer, unsubscribe link, link hrefs) couldn't be inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **68 tracking links were not probed.** They are on click-redirect domains, so destination validity, redirect chains and 404s are unverified.
- **11 insecure `http://` pixels (Dotomi/Conversant cookie-sync):**
  - `http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?dtm_com=2&dtm_cid=2437…`
  - `http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2437/pub1.php` through `pub10.php?d98e199bf5027afea04b790655b3dee2=1`
  - Clients that block mixed content or proxy images over HTTPS (Gmail's image proxy, Apple Mail Privacy Protection) may drop these, so the pixels won't fire reliably.
  - Ten sequential cookie-redirect pixels add 11 extra third-party requests per open.
- **Open-tracking pixels:** `https://mi.cb2.com/p/up/09aebc5b21344cf7/o.gif?mi_u=…` and `https://dv.cb2.com/o/77f2a31a-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…` are HTTPS and look fine. Both lack alt text (see §3).

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **Tracking pixels with no alt:** the Dotomi pixels, `o.gif` and the `dv.cb2.com` pixel should use `alt=""` so screen readers skip them. Their missing alt is low severity.
- **Zoom restriction:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` disables pinch-zoom, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.

- **Malformed asset URLs:** several `@font-face` sources use a double slash (`https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/...`). `Lineto Brown Sub` is also declared with conflicting sources, mapping the light `.woff2` to weight 400. Clients that honor web fonts may fetch the wrong file or fall back, though the `Helvetica, sans-serif` fallback in `a {}` limits the damage.
- **Cross-brand font host:** `HNW05` loads from `crateandbarrel.com`, a different domain from the sending brand. It adds a dependency on a third-party host.
- **`<title>`:** it reads "CB2, FURNITURE AND DECOR FOR THE MODERN HOME", which doesn't match the subject line. This is minor.
