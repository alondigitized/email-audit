- "BACK IN STOCK" is a real, specific reason to email — not vague brand mood copy like "Now and Future Classics" from yesterday.
- The category grid (Bedroom → Coffee Tables → Sectionals → Storage → Sofas) mirrors how I actually shop CB2's site, so scanning felt efficient, not random.
- Product shots at the bottom have prices and even a strikethrough on the Espira coffee table ($579 down from $609) — that's the concrete offer signal missing from most of this brand's recent sends.
- The 10% rewards blurb at the footer is genuinely relevant to me — I've got a Crate & Barrel family card and actually track that math.

## What's weak

- Burying an "UP TO 50% OFF" Labor Day banner in the middle of a restock email muddies the whole point — is this an inventory alert or a sale? Pick one, like Article did with "Labor Day Sale starts now" (7/10) instead of blending messages.
- Zero urgency or scarcity language on the restock itself — "back in stock" items sell out again, and CB2 doesn't once say so.
- This is email twelve-plus from CB2 alone this stretch; my brain flagged sender before content, same fatigue I called out on "Now and Future Classics."
- No swatch/material callouts on the sectionals or sofas — that's the one thing that'd actually move me toward a click on upholstered pieces I can't touch first.

- 1. Split this into two emails — restock alert and Labor Day sale — so each has one clear job instead of diluting both.
- 2. Add scarcity to the restock hero: "back by popular demand, limited quantities" would push me to act instead of bookmark-and-forget.
- 3. Move the 10% rewards callout up near the hero — I'm already a cardholder, remind me before I scroll eight product categories deep.
- 4. Lead with the coffee table or sectional specifically in the hero instead of a generic seating vignette — that's the actual pin-worthy image, not the moody armchair-and-vase shot.
