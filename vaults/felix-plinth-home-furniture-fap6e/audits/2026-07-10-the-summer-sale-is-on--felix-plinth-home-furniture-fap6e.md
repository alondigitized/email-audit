# The Summer Sale is ON
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- Fourteen. I have now received — and I do keep count — fourteen Pottery Barn emails since roughly July 1st. So when "The Summer Sale is ON" lands in my inbox, my first reaction isn't excitement, it's a kind of weary recognition. This is the same sale. The same dusty-blue hero. The same "up to 70% off" number that has appeared in at least four of the last six sends I can think of off the top of my head. At this point Pottery Barn's summer sale is less a limited-time event and more a permanent atmospheric condition.
- Here's what bugs me: the email itself is not bad. The hero is clean, the category grid is scannable, and the "Recommended Just for You" section is a genuine attempt at personalization — curtain rods, a platform bed, linen curtains. For someone juggling two renovations simultaneously, that's actually the right product vocabulary. If this had landed in my inbox once, maybe twice, I'd have clicked through to the bed frame without a second thought.
- But this is number fourteen. The cadence has completely destroyed the urgency. I'll mark it read and maybe come back to the curtain rod link directly if I remember — I'm not clicking through a Pottery Barn email today out of principle.

## What's working

- **Hero legibility is solid** — "Up to 70% off" reads in under a second on a 5-inch phone screen; I didn't have to hunt for the offer.
- **Category grid is genuinely useful** — Furniture, Outdoor, Sofas & Chairs, Lighting — whoever laid this out understands that I'm not browsing, I'm sourcing. One tap to get where I need.
- **"Recommended Just for You" is the right move** — Curtain rods, a platform bed, linen curtains: at least three of those six products are things I have open browser tabs on right now. The algorithm is paying some attention.
- **Rewards section earns its keep** — I'm aware of the PB credit card and the 10% back structure matters to me because I route everything through the highest-rewards card I can find. Seeing it reinforced mid-email is a nudge I actually absorb.
- **Clean render, no layout breaks** — Every image loaded, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum ghost blocks. CB2 had a layout issue two emails ago that drove me insane; this doesn't.

## What's weak

- **Cadence has killed the word "sale"** — After fourteen emails, "The Summer Sale is ON" reads the same as "Tuesday is ON." The word has lost all charge. Compare this to Article, which at least built a genuine countdown before I felt genuinely forced to act.
- **"Hadleynbrook Quartz Dry Erase" in my recommendations** — I see what might be a dry-erase board in the personalization row. I'm renovating two houses; I do not need a dry-erase board from Pottery Barn. That's a signal the algorithm is stitching together browsing sessions in ways that don't quite cohere.
- **No deadline anywhere** — "The Summer Sale is ON" with no end date and no "ends Sunday" callout is effort-free urgency, which is no urgency. Yesterday's email about furniture on sale didn't have a hard date either. Wayfair, for all its flaws (and four outdoor rug emails this week is a lot of flaws), at least anchors their promos to a day.
- **"Discover New Arrivals" section undercuts the sale** — I'm here for the discount. Slipping a full-price new arrivals block into the middle of a sale email is a split message. Crate & Barrel does the same thing and it's just as annoying there.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a hard end date on the sale** — "Ends Sunday, July 13" in the hero would do more work than any subject line tweak. CB2's "Up to 40% off is on now" drove actual click behavior for me because there's an implicit clock on "now." Lean into it.
- 2. **Kill the New Arrivals block or move it below the fold** — Sale email should be pure sale. Hybrid messaging dilutes conversion intent. Save full-price new arrivals for a standalone send.
- 3. **Suppress emails after 3 sends on the same promo** — This is a product decision, not a creative one, but it's the highest-leverage fix available. After send #4, every incremental send is cannibalizing future open rates.
- 4. **Clean up the personalization row** — If the dry-erase board is a category error (and it is), audit the recommendation logic. Surface the platform bed and the curtain rods at the top; earn the "Just for You" label.
- 5. **Preheader should carry the deadline** — Whatever the preheader says right now, it should say "Ends Sunday — up to 70% off furniture, lighting, rugs."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Summer Sale: Up to 70% off — ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your curtain rods are on sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Furniture, lighting, rugs — up to 70% off through July 13.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `1,000s of new styles added. Ends Sunday.`
