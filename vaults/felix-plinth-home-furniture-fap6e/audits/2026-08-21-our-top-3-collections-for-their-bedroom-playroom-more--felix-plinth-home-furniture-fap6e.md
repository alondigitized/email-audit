# Our top 3 collections for their bedroom, playroom & more
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-21**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids, and it's not even a bedroom-specific one this time — it's "top-rated collections for every kind of room," which is basically the brand admitting they're just going to show me every crib and bunk bed they've got. My youngest isn't in a crib anymore, so the Bodie, Finn, and Hampshire nursery/crib collections are dead air scrolling for me. There's no offer anywhere in this one either — no %, no $ off, just "shop the collection" three times in a row, plus a back-to-school gear plug wedged in the middle like they forgot which email they were building.
- Compare that to the actual winner in my Crate & Kids history, the 8/10 "Up to 25% off your bedroom refresh" send with the Coveleur canopy bed and a real price stamped under it — that one I'd have clicked. This one has zero pricing anywhere in the hero collections, just "starting at $29" buried near the bottom for "500+ new arrivals." CB2's "Future Heirlooms Under $500" at least led with a number in the subject; this one leads with nothing.
- I'm marking this read, not clicking, not forwarding. If my kid were still crib-age I might skim the Hampshire collection for the bunk beds, but as-is this is the fourth or fifth near-identical Crate & Kids furniture-catalog dump this week and my brain has just started auto-archiving them.

## What's working

- The "5,000+ 5-star reviews" strip up top and the GREENGUARD Gold Certified badges under nearly every SKU are a real trust signal for kids' furniture — that's the one thing this brand consistently does better than CB2 or Article on nursery-adjacent stuff.
- Clean, consistent product photography with matching lifestyle room shots above each collection — good visual hierarchy per section even if the overall email is a scroll marathon.
- The "we love these for you" personalized-feeling module near the bottom at least attempts segmentation instead of just more collection blocks.

## What's weak

- No visible discount, price comparison, or urgency anywhere in the three hero collections — just "Shop Bodie / Shop Finn / Shop Hampshire." Compare to Pottery Barn's sweepstakes email, which at least had a hook ($1500) even if it wasn't a real product offer.
- Crib, bunk bed, and dresser-heavy assortment for a household past the nursery stage — same mismatch I flagged on the "Jenny Lind" send and the daisy-field markdown email; this brand still hasn't segmented me out of crib content.
- The mid-email "get back-to-school gear in time" banner is a total non sequitur dropped between two furniture collections — no lead-in, no connection to the room theme, it just breaks the scroll.
- "500+ new arrivals, starting at $29" is the only price in the entire email and it's crammed into a small banner near the footer, easy to miss on a phone.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment by kid's age/room-stage — if I've got a school-age kid, stop pushing crib collections and lead with the bunk-bed/loft/desk content instead. This is the same swing-and-miss from the last two Crate & Kids sends.
- 2. Put an actual price or % off in the hero, the way the 25%-off canopy bed send did. Three collection blocks with zero numbers just reads as a catalog, not a marketing push.
- 3. Cut or move the back-to-school banner — it doesn't belong wedged between two bedroom collections mid-scroll.
- 4. Move "starting at $29" up near the top if that's genuinely the best price point in the email — right now it's the last thing anyone will see.
