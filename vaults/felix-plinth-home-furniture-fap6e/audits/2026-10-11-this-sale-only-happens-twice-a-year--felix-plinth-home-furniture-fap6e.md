# This sale only happens twice a year
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-11**
## Executive summary

- CB2 again. By my count this is the tenth send in about five days, so I opened it already tired. This one is more relevant to me than the last few. I'm doing two houses and have swatches sitting on a table, so "Need a sign to put those swatches to good use?" got a small grin. A semi-annual upholstery event is something I'd plan around.
- The email then argues with itself. The top banner says "Up to 60% off," the next section says "Up to 25% off" on upholstery, and below that is a $5,000 sweepstakes and a $99 free-shipping threshold. The subject says this sale "only happens twice a year." But the 10/10 sends already said "2 DEALS & ONLY DAYS LEFT," and the long-weekend banner is still on top. I don't buy the scarcity. I'd click Sectionals and look, but I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- The swatch line speaks directly to someone mid-renovation. It's the most personal copy CB2 has sent me this week.
- The four category buttons (Sectionals, Sofas, Accent Chairs, Benches & Ottomans) are big and easy to tap with a thumb. They send me straight to the furniture I'm shopping for.
- The upholstery hero is moody and well shot. It looks like a showroom, and the brand voice is consistent.
- The "Book a free design appointment" CTA fits how I buy furniture. I want a human to help with a two-house palette.
- The Rewards callout ("Earn 10% back") is there. I pick my card on rewards math, so I care about it.

## What's weak

- There are too many offers. 60% off, 25% off, the $5K giveaway, free shipping at $99 and 10% back all compete. I couldn't tell the one deal I should act on.
- The "up to 25%" on upholstery is a weak offer for the big-ticket category I'm actually shopping. Sofas are where I'd want a real number, and the fine print probably hides exclusions.
- The "New & Best Selling" block shows one green sofa with no name or price, so I can't tell what it is or what it costs.
- The legal block at the bottom is a wall of tiny text. The sweepstakes and shipping terms are exactly what I'd want to read, and I'd need to zoom to do it.
- The "twice a year" claim doesn't hold up when CB2 has run a sale every other day this week.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one offer. Upholstery gets the hero and the $5K giveaway moves to the footer.
- 2. Show real sofa products with name, price and fabric options, like the one green sofa but labeled. I want lead times and swatch ordering visible too.
- 3. Cut the send frequency. Wayfair's pergola email worked because it showed one product with a price and a "$134 off" badge. Pottery Barn's "Up to 70% off" strip has the same fatigue problem as this one. CB2 should pick a lane.
- 4. Put the shipping-on-us banner in a slim top bar instead of a full section.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Swatches chosen? Sofas are on sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sectionals, accent chairs and benches, plus free shipping on $99+ through Monday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Book a free design appointment and see fabrics in person before the event ends.`

## Technical Audit

**1. Technical Summary**
The email has one confirmed rendering defect: an unresolved merge token, `%%reviews_skulist%%`, in the source. It also loads 12 non-HTTPS Dotomi tracking images, and 9 content images plus 12 tracker images have no alt text. The QA run passed 43% of checks. The compliance and authentication headers could not be verified, because the AgentMail relay may not capture them.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- 64 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are click-redirect domains. Their destinations are unverified, so I can't confirm the links resolve.
- Twelve tracking pixels point at Dotomi (a third-party ad-sync vendor):
  - 1 request to `http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?dtm_com=2&dtm_cid=2437...`
  - 10 requests to `http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2437/pub1.php` through `pub10.php`
  - 1 `mi.cb2.com/.../o.gif` open pixel
  - The 11 Dotomi requests use plain `http://`. Mail clients that block or rewrite insecure content (Gmail's image proxy, Apple Mail Privacy Protection) can drop them, so these pixels may not fire reliably.
  - The 10 `pubN.php` calls are cookie-sync redirects. They are unusual in email and add load-time overhead.
- A second tracker, `https://dv.cb2.com/o/1bf6f07f-...?mi_cid=...`, is also embedded as an image.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- Nine content images on `image.mail.cb2.com` have no `alt` attribute. Their filenames are `9769d070-...jpg`, `07503c36-...jpg`, `08bbea64-...jpg`, `4e890782-...jpg`, `6772ded4-...jpg`, `fac3667a-...jpg`, `14f705f5-...jpg`, `7573b4c6-...jpg` and `acb86340-...jpg`. With images blocked, these render as blank space, and screen readers announce the filename or nothing.
  - If any of these images carry the offer or the call to action, that content is lost for those readers.
- Fourteen tracking images lack alt text: the 12 above plus `o.gif` and the `dv.cb2.com` image. For these, `alt=""` is the correct fix, not descriptive text.
- In the visible `<head>`, `<meta name="viewport">` includes `maximum-scale=1`. This disables pinch-zoom on some clients and is an accessibility concern.
- A comment in the source reads `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->`. This is an unremoved template placeholder, and it is harmless but sloppy.
- The `@font-face` rules load fonts from `www.crateandbarrel.com` (HNW05) and from `cb2.com`, some via a double-slash path (`https://www.cb2.com//Styles/...`). Most email clients ignore web fonts, so fallbacks apply. I did not see the full fallback stack because the HTML is truncated.
- The source was truncated, so I could not check the body markup (`role="presentation"` on layout tables, heading structure, etc.).

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
- **FAIL:** The raw token `%%reviews_skulist%%` appears in the delivered HTML. This looks like a Salesforce Marketing Cloud personalization string that did not resolve at send time.
  - If it sits in a visible element, recipients see literal `%%reviews_skulist%%`.
  - If it sits in an attribute or URL, it may break a link or an image.
  - The truncated source doesn't show where it lands, so its exact impact is unconfirmed.

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
- `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found. The QA tool notes the relay may strip them, so I can't confirm a real gap. If they are truly absent, one-click unsubscribe is not supported, which Gmail and Yahoo require of bulk senders.
- `Authentication-Results` was not captured, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC are unknown. The sending domain `mail.cb2.com` is a subdomain, which is consistent with a dedicated ESP setup, but this is not evidence of a pass.
- The visible HTML was truncated before the footer. I could not verify a physical postal address or an in-body unsubscribe link, which CAN-SPAM requires.

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
- All 64 tracking links were skipped, so I could not check their redirect destinations, UTM parameters or landing page alignment.
- The `mi_cid` and `mi_mid` parameters on `dv.cb2.com` and `mi.cb2.com` suggest the tracking identifiers are ESP-specific. I saw no explicit `utm_*` parameters in the data provided.

**7. Recommendations**
1. **Fix `%%reviews_skulist%%`.** Check the dynamic content block's data source and add a fallback or suppression rule so an unresolved token never ships. Add a pre-send QA test for `%%...%%` patterns.
2. **Add alt text to the 9 content images.** Use descriptive text for products and offers.
3. **Add `alt=""` to the 14 tracking pixels.**
4. **Move the Dotomi calls to HTTPS, or drop them.** The 10 `pubN.php` cookie-sync requests are the first candidates to remove.
5. **Remove `maximum-scale=1`** from the viewport meta tag.
6. **Verify the headers in the raw message.** Check `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC in a direct inbox test, since the relay hides them.
7. **Resolve the 64 tracking links** with a click-through test to confirm the destinations and UTM parameters.
8. **Re-run the audit on the full HTML** so the footer, body markup and the token's location can be checked.
