# NEW furniture rooted in heritage style
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- I'm mid-renovation on two houses simultaneously and I'm deep in the Pottery Barn catalog right now — so a furniture email from them isn't unwelcome. The hero image is exactly their wheelhouse: bright, airy, natural materials, that coastal-meets-farmhouse thing they do well. I get what they're going for with "heritage style" and honestly the living room shot made me pause. That's a win for them.
- But this is a new-arrivals email with no offer. No discount, no threshold shipping deal, nothing. Just "here's new stuff, go shop." That plays fine if I'm in browse mode on a Sunday morning, which I sometimes am, but I'm not clicking through at inbox-check speed on a weekday. It's a catalog page dressed as an email. The "Recommended Just for You" section at the bottom hints at personalization but it comes so late, and at such small scale, that it doesn't feel like they really know me.
- Mark as read. Maybe revisit later if I'm actively sourcing dining chairs for the second house — which I actually am — but the email doesn't make me feel urgency about it.

## What's working

- **Hero photography is strong.** The living room shot is genuinely aspirational — clean light, layered textures, and the kind of mix-and-match story that makes me think "I could actually use that in the primary."
- **Pottery Barn brand recognition is automatic.** I'm on their list, I know their catalog, I trust the quality. Sender credibility is baked in.
- **"Recommended Just for You" exists.** Even if I can't feel the personalization muscle behind it, the section label signals they're at least trying not to blast-blast-blast generic.
- **Layout is clean, no broken images or obvious render bugs.** The grid reads top to bottom without fighting itself.
- **Loyalty card callout at the bottom.** I hold the PB card. Seeing it there is a quiet reminder that if I do click and buy, I'm earning something. That's not nothing.

## What's weak

- **No offer.** This is a browse-inducing email dressed up as a promotional one. For someone balancing renovation budgets across two properties, I need a reason to move now versus waiting for the semi-annual sale I know is coming.
- **"Heritage style" is doing a lot of vague lifting.** What does that mean in concrete product terms? Mortise-and-tenon joinery? Oak over pine? English roll arm? Give me something I can hold onto.
- **The product grid feels anonymous.** Multiple items, similar scale, no pricing, no names I can hold in my head. I can't even tell which piece is the lead without zooming in on my phone.
- **No male demographic signal whatsoever.** This email could have been sent to literally anyone on their list. I'm a guy shopping for two houses and it feels targeted at the aggregate "PB customer," not me.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with one hero piece, named and priced.** Pick the dining table or the sofa, call it out by name, show its actual price. "The Cassidy Extension Dining Table — now in whitewashed oak, starting at $X" gives me something to compare against my mental budget and the competing Crate & Barrel version I bookmarked last week.
- 2. **Add at least a shipping threshold.** "Free delivery on furniture orders over $X" doesn't have to be a sale. It changes the psychology from "browsing" to "this might be the moment."
- 3. **Move "Recommended for You" to just under the hero.** If the personalization engine actually knows I've been looking at dining, lead with that. Don't bury it below four other modules.
- 4. **Tighten the subject toward one concrete category.** "New in Furniture" is a department, not a hook.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Heritage oak dining just landed — new from Pottery Barn`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The new furniture collection we've been sitting on`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Solid wood, classic proportions, built to outlast the renovation.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New arrivals in dining, living, and bedroom — with PB card rewards.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP server-side code leaked into `<style>` block**

The following PHP header calls appear verbatim inside a `<style>` tag — they are invalid CSS and indicate a template rendering defect (a PHP image-serving script's header calls were not separated from the HTML output):

```css
/* inside <style> CLIENT styling END */
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```

These are inert in rendered email but will cause CSS parse failures in strict renderers and signal a template generation bug.

**49 images missing `alt` text** — includes all 16 content slice images (`slices_01.jpg`–`slices_16.jpg`), 17 footer slices, product images, and tracking pixels. Screen readers get no fallback; images-off rendering is completely blind.

**3 images served over HTTP (will be blocked by many clients)**

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent** — Not captured by the AgentMail relay. CAN-SPAM and Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements mandate this header. If missing at the MTA level (not just relay capture), the email is non-compliant.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders (>5k/day). Without it, Gmail shows a slower multi-step unsubscribe UI and may increase spam classification.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not found** — SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status is unknown via this relay. Cannot confirm alignment.

**`no-referrer` meta present** — `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />` strips the Referer header on outbound clicks in some webmail clients, which can suppress GA4 session attribution for traffic sourced from this email.

**`robots` meta present** — `noindex, nofollow` is correct for email browser-view pages.

Unsubscribe link is present in the HTML body (confirmed by footer structure); physical mailing address is expected in the footer but not visible in the truncated source — should be verified in the full render.
