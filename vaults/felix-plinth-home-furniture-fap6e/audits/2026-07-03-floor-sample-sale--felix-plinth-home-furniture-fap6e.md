# FLOOR SAMPLE SALE
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- Floor sample sale. Okay — this one actually makes me stop. After six CB2 emails since July 1st landing in my inbox, I've basically been moving through them on autopilot: open, skim, archive, move on. But "floor sample sale" is a specific event type that means something different from "MORE ADDED TO SALE" or "SALE'S GOING FAST." Floor samples are real pieces that have been living in a showroom — one-of-a-kind, immediately available, and usually marked down in a way that's actually meaningful, not the 20%-off-already-elevated-MSRP math that online sales tend to play. For someone who is actively spec'ing two simultaneous renovations and trying to move quickly on furniture decisions, that's a genuinely different conversation.
- The problem is execution. There are two heroes stacked on top of each other — one for the in-store floor sample event ("FIND A STORE") and one for the online July 4th sale ("SHOP NOW") — and they're fighting. The banner at the top of the email has a visible copy error (looks like "HOURS" is doubled), which is not a confidence-inspiring start for a brand sitting in Pottery Barn price territory. And the countdown timer (04:07:31:43) is credible because July 4th is real, but it reads the same as every other deadline email I've gotten from this brand this week.
- I'd click "FIND A STORE" on this one. The in-store floor sample angle is unique enough to earn a trip. I'd forward this to exactly nobody — it's a personal errand, not a shareable find.

## What's working

- **"Floor Sample Sale" is the first genuinely distinct hook from CB2 in three days.** Floor samples = real scarcity, real objects, no lead time. After a week of generic sale emails, this is news.
- **The countdown timer maps to a real calendar event.** 04:07:31:43 against a July 4th hook reads as credible, unlike the vague countdown timers I've seen brands abuse. Compare to Article's clean deadline arc for the same weekend — they built to it over 8 days, CB2 is doing it through volume, but the timer itself is defensible.
- **"FIND A STORE" is the right CTA for this specific offer.** In-store floor samples require a physical visit. The button actually matches the conversion path, which is more than I can say for most generic "SHOP NOW" plays.
- **The product grid ("More For You") hits the renovation sweet spot.** I can see dining chairs and a marble coffee table — exactly the spec-work I'm doing for the second home. Crate & Barrel's "$219 dining chairs" email earlier this week worked because it named a price; this grid at least surfaces the right category.
- **Loyalty 10% back is visible at the bottom.** I run the rewards math on every purchase. Seeing it called out keeps me in the ecosystem, even if I'm annoyed at the cadence.

## What's weak

- **"EXTENDED HOURS HOURS" is a visible copy error in the banner.** "HOURS" appears doubled in the header strip. It's the first thing I read after the nav bar and it reads like a template accident. Pottery Barn doesn't ship this. CB2 shouldn't either.
- **Two heroes, two asks, no decision.** The floor sample in-store event and the online 50% off sale are different conversion paths being asked of me simultaneously. Article has run one path, one CTA, one clear verb since June 26th. This email can't decide what it wants me to do.
- **The hero image tells me nothing about what's actually on the floor.** Beautiful bedroom, nice light — but which pieces are floor samples? What's available at my local store? A mood board doesn't answer the question I'm already asking before I drive anywhere.
- **Seven emails in three days has broken the urgency signal.** The countdown timer and "sale's ending" framing might have worked on email two. On email seven, my nervous system has been conditioned to ignore it.
- **"SHOP NOW" on the second hero is the laziest CTA in the deck.** Wayfair does this. CB2 is supposed to be better than Wayfair.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split this into its own send.** The floor sample event deserves a dedicated email with zero competition from the generic online sale. Two heroes halve the impact of both.
- 2. **Show real floor sample inventory.** Even three product thumbnails with "in-store only" tags and actual store-specific availability would convert me faster than a lifestyle bedroom shot.
- 3. **Fix the doubled "HOURS" in the banner copy.** One-character fix, immediate credibility recovery.
- 4. **Drop the "SHOP NOW" CTA or make it earn its place.** If the online sale must live here, give it a real verb: "Shop Chairs Under $300" or "See What's 50% Off."
- 5. **Pull inventory specificity into the subject.** Crate & Barrel's "$219 dining chairs" subject told me everything before I opened. CB2 could do "Floor samples from $99 — this weekend only" and I'd be in.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Unfilled template placeholder left in production:**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
Typo ("IMPUT") and the comment itself indicate an authoring artifact that was never removed. Harmless to render but exposes internal workflow tooling.

**Double-slash paths in three `@font-face` src URLs:**
```css
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2")
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2")
```
Most servers normalize `//` to `/`, so this likely resolves, but it is a malformed URL and will 404 on strict servers.

**Duplicate `@font-face` for "Lineto Brown Sub"** — declared three separate times with overlapping `font-weight` values (400/600 and an unweighted block). The unweighted block has no `woff2` source, only `eot` and `woff`, meaning Outlook desktop gets a different font stack than modern clients.

**Zoom blocking:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom. WCAG 1.4.4 (resize text) violation for low-vision users.

**Global text-size-adjust suppression:**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applied to `*` rather than scoped to `body`. This overrides system accessibility text scaling for every element.

**Narrow mobile breakpoint:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)
```
Targets 414px-wide devices (iPhone SE/older iPhones). Current standard phones (390–430px logical width) may miss this breakpoint, receiving desktop layout on mobile.
