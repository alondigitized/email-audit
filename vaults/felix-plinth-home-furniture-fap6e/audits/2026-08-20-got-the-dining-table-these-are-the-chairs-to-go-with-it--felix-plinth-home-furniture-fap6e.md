# Got the dining table? These are the chairs to go with it →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-20**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one actually got me — "no more matchy-matchy," dining chairs to go with a table, and this is the first Crate & Barrel email this stretch that reads like it knows I'm mid-renovation and furnishing an actual room, not just running another blanket sale. The mixed-material chair styling (rattan, ceramic, boucle) against that warm dining room hero is legitimately my taste, and pairing it against a table purchase signal is the smartest personalization beat I've seen from this brand all week.
- That said, it sprawls hard — by the time I hit "up to 25% off your bedroom refresh" and then a kitchen collab with Loeffler Randall, I've forgotten this was supposed to be about chairs. It's basically four emails stapled together. Compare that to Article's recent "Clocking in" home office send, which stayed in one lane the whole way down — tighter and more confident.
- I'd click the "Lowe: a classic for a reason" chair or the Edendale banquette — those are genuinely relevant to a dining refresh. I wouldn't forward it, but I'd screenshot the chair section into my Pinterest-adjacent folder and ignore the rest.

## What's working

- "No more matchy-matchy" is a real insight — mixing dining chair styles is an actual trend I've seen and like, not generic sale copy.
- The table→chairs framing is the first purchase-sequence personalization I've noticed from C&B this stretch — feels like they're tracking what I looked at.
- Multiple chair materials shown side by side (wood, rattan, ceramic, upholstered) gives me real comparison shopping, which is how I actually browse furniture.
- The Edendale storage banquette callout is a smart upsell for small dining spaces — relevant, not random.

## What's weak

- It completely loses the plot after the dining section — bedroom refresh, a Loeffler Randall kitchen collab, clearance markdowns, credit card pitch, an app download push. That's five different campaigns in one email.
- No visible discount percentage or dollar figure tied to the actual chairs — just "Show Now" buttons with no pricing anchor, unlike the CB2 "Now & Future Classics" send that at least led with a real sale frame.
- The Loeffler Randall kitchen collab feels bolted on with zero connection to the dining chair story two sections above it.
- By the bottom third it's basically Wayfair-style everything-dump territory — the hot tub bait-and-switch email was worse, but this drifts the same direction.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this into two sends — a tight "chairs for your table" email that stops after Edendale, and a separate one for bedroom/kitchen/clearance. The dining section alone is an 8/10 email; bolted to the rest it drags to a 5.
- 2. Put an actual price or % off next to at least one hero chair — right now nothing anchors value.
- 3. Move "book a free Design Desk appointment" up near the top — that's a stronger CTA for someone mid-renovation than burying it mid-scroll.
- 4. Drop the Loeffler Randall kitchen collab entirely from this send; it has nothing to do with dining chairs and dilutes the good targeting work done above it.

- **Subject Alt B:** `You bought the table. Here's what goes with it.`
