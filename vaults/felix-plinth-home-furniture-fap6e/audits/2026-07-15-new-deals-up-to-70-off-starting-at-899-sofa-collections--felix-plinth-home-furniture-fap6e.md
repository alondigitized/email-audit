# New deals up to 70% off + Starting at $899: Sofa collections
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-five. I stopped counting at twenty-three on July 14th, but here we are. This one lands differently than the Kids backpack email or the desk email earlier today, because I actually *need* sofas right now — the second home's living room has a single camp chair and my ambitions. So I'm not immune. When Pottery Barn puts "Sofas & Sectionals" in the hero with a grid of specific named models — Cardiff, Chatham, Buchanan, Pacific — and anchors at $899, it's doing the right thing for where I am in my buying cycle.
- But here's the problem: I have seen this grid before. Not just from Pottery Barn, but last week Wayfair ran me through a recliner carousel and CB2 did a "classics on sale" email with essentially the same beige-sofa energy. The category is right; the creative is not differentiated enough to make me feel like *Pottery Barn specifically* is the move. The "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" section buried below the fold is doing real personalization work, but it's wasted placement.
- I'd mark this as read, screenshot the Cardiff Sofa for later, and not click today. I wouldn't forward it — no one on my contact list needs a "hey look at this Pottery Barn sale" forward; that's what group chats are for and mine already has this link from someone else.

## What's working

- **Hero category is exactly right for me right now.** "Sofas & Sectionals" + a grid of real named products with real floor prices ($899 entry point) is precisely the information I want when I'm trying to scope a second-home living room budget.
- **Multiple named collections, not a vague "shop the sale."** Cardiff, Chatham, Buchanan, Pacific — each with a starting price — lets me do real comparison math without clicking anything yet. That's efficient.
- **"RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" signals behavioral targeting.** Pottery Barn is trying to close the loop with past browsing data. I appreciate the effort even if the placement is too low.
- **Clean, no render bugs.** Photos are sharp, layout holds, nothing overlaps. This renders fine on mobile, which is where I would have opened it.
- **App download nudge and social links in footer are unobtrusive.** They're not fighting the main offer for attention.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is two subject lines in a trench coat.** "New deals up to 70% off + Starting at $899: Sofa collections" — that plus sign is doing structural damage. Pick one angle and commit.
- **"Up to 70% off" is the retail hedge I've been burned by before.** One item in a clearance fabric at 70% off, everything I actually want at 20%. I've learned to discount "up to" claims heavily.
- **"RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" is below the fold and feels like an afterthought.** If you've got my behavioral data, lead with it. Article did a whole editorial entryway email last week with zero discount that felt more personal than this.
- **No deadline visible.** "New deals" tells me nothing. Is this ending Sunday? Tonight? The Wayfair recliner email I audited didn't nail this either, but at least it had a sense of volume urgency in the subject.
- **The "Discover New Arrivals" section breaks the sofa thesis.** I'm here for the sale. Don't pivot me to new arrivals at full price mid-scroll.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Consolidate the subject into one punchy line with a credible deadline.** The dual-value-prop structure is confusing and runs long at 60 characters on mobile.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Sofas from $899 — deals up to 70% off, this week only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Cardiff. Chatham. Buchanan. All on sale from $899.`
- 2. **Move "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" into the hero block, or at least the second module.** Lead with the personalized picks, then back them up with the full collection grid.
- 3. **Kill the "New Arrivals" module or move it to a dedicated email.** It breaks purchase intent mid-funnel. I'm in deal mode; don't re-introduce full-price discovery here.
- 4. **Add a real deadline with specific copy.** "Deals end Sunday" beats "new deals" every time. Even "while supplies last" on specific sofa colorways would give me a reason to act today.
- 5. **Write a preheader that does work instead of repeating the subject.**
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cardiff, Chatham, Buchanan — named sofas, real prices, ending soon.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your shortlisted sofas are on sale. Here's where to start.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard-broken links (403s) will cause visible failures for credit-card banner recipients. The email also carries PHP `header()` calls embedded in a CSS `<style>` block — invalid syntax that is dead code but signals a template assembly defect.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Broken links (FAIL — 403 responses):**

- Credit card apply/buy CTA: `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&...`
- West Elm spacer beacon: `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`

The first is a customer-facing link; a 403 means any click lands on an error page. The second is a tracking/spacer pixel cross-referenced from the wrong brand's CDN.

**25 tracking links skipped** by the QA probe (click-redirect domains) — these are expected but mean click-path integrity is unverified.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP headers injected into CSS `<style>` block:**
```html
<style>
  ...
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
</style>
```
These are server-side PHP statements that were never executed — they leaked into the HTML output verbatim. Email clients will treat them as invalid CSS and ignore them, but this indicates a template preprocessing failure.

**Mixed content (HTTP images):** Three image sources use `http://`, which will be blocked by secure email clients:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` (also 403)
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` (open-tracking pixel)

**Missing alt text:** 43 images have no `alt` attribute. Notable functional images affected include the hero banner (`F26_TOP_SummerSalePartC_NoUrgency.jpg`), all 11 sofa collection slices, and all 18 footer slices. With images off (default in many clients), the email renders as a blank white column.

**`<title>` tag is empty** — no fallback for tab/preview display.

**Authentication-Results:** SPF/DKIM status not captured. Cannot confirm DMARC alignment. Sending domain is `e.potterybarn.com`; authentication posture unverified.

**`robots` meta tag** is set to `noindex, nofollow` and `referrer` to `no-referrer` — both correct for email HTML.
