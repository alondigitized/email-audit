# Rooms for your mood board
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-31**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "Rooms for your mood board" — this is CB2 doing free design services, and honestly it's a nice change of pace from the discount-block sends I've been grading them on all week ("THIS WEEK'S STEALS" on 7/31 got a 6, back-to-back sale sends before that). This one's closer to the 7/30 home-office editorial I gave an 8 — moody, aspirational, "book a designer" energy instead of "buy now" pressure. Problem is it goes on forever. I scrolled through what feels like four different campaigns stacked in one email: design services, a "GET INSPIRED" category grid, then a 30% off bedroom+bath sale bolted onto the bottom like they couldn't decide what this email was actually for.
- I'm mid-renovation on two houses right now, so "free design services" with an in-home consult option genuinely catches me — that's the one thing Crate & Barrel's dinnerware dump (6/10, all product grid, no service angle) and Wayfair's Open Box firehose (6/10, two emails same day) aren't even trying to offer me. That's a real differentiator. But by the time I hit the sale banner at the bottom I've forgotten what I opened this for, and there's no CTA in the first two screens that isn't "book an appointment" — which is a big ask for a passive inbox scroll on my commute.
- Would I click? Maybe the design services CTA if I had 30 seconds of patience — I've been meaning to book a virtual consult for the second-home living room anyway. Would I forward it? Not this one. Mark as read and move on, but I'd bookmark the design-services angle mentally as "come back to this."

## What's working

- The hero "THIS IS LIVING" with that warm sectional living room shot actually looks like a room I'd want, not a stock catalog shot — better restraint than Pottery Barn's chaotic holiday-over-summer-sale banner stack I dinged them for.
- "3 Ways to Meet" (in-person, virtual, in-store) is genuinely useful structure — I didn't know CB2 did in-home design visits, and that's exactly the kind of showroom-integration content I want and never get from Article or Wayfair.
- The room photography throughout is consistent, muted, cohesive palette — no jarring color clashes like some of Wayfair's grid dumps.
- "EARN 10% BACK in Rewards Dollars" and the CB2 app tile at the bottom acknowledge me as a loyalty member, which is a small trust cue Crate & Barrel's dinnerware email skipped entirely.

## What's weak

- This email has zero focus — design services pitch, then a six-tile shop-by-category grid, then a 30% off sale banner, then app/rewards/referral tiles. Pick one job.
- The sale banner ("SEMI-ANNUAL BEDROOM + BATH EVENT, up to 30% off") is buried so far down I almost didn't see it — if that's the real offer, it's competing with itself against the design-services CTA above it.
- No urgency or deadline anywhere. The design appointment CTAs are evergreen, and the sale banner doesn't say when it ends — compare that to CB2's own "24 hours left" send on 7/28 that I gave an 8 for real urgency.
- The category grid ("SHOP SOFAS & SECTIONALS," "SHOP RUGS," etc.) just routes to generic collection pages — no single hero product, no price, no swatch/sample callout even though I'm actively picking finishes for two houses right now.
- Small tap-target concern: those three "BOOK NOW" buttons under "3 Ways to Meet" all look visually identical, so there's no hierarchy telling me which meeting type CB2 actually wants me to pick.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into two sends. Design services is a strong, differentiated hook on its own — let it be the whole email. Move the bedroom+bath sale to its own send with a real deadline, the way the 7/28 "24 hours left" email did it right.
- 2. Lead the "3 Ways to Meet" section with whichever option converts best (probably virtual, lowest friction) and visually demote the other two instead of three identical black buttons.
- 3. Put a real end date or expiration on the sale banner — "up to 30% off" with no clock reads like evergreen filler, not a limited event.
- 4. Swap at least two of the six shop-by-category tiles for a swatch/sample CTA ("Order fabric samples free") — I'm actively deciding on finishes and that's the one thing none of my competitor reads (Article, C&B, Wayfair) have offered either.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `See real rooms our designers styled, then book your own consult.`
