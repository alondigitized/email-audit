# A bestseller, now in new finishes
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- Six. We're at six Pottery Barn emails in roughly 48 hours, and I want you to understand what that number means by the time this one lands: I've already processed outdoor furniture, the Coastal Washed Quilt, table linens, a ships-free bedding drop, and a new arrivals summary that barely registered. My inbox folder — the one that functions as my Pinterest board for both renovation projects — is drowning in the same warm-neutral brand palette.
- Here's the thing: the actual email isn't bad. The Sausalito Collection is a real thing I might care about, and new finishes — specifically Walnut Burl and Natural White — are genuinely useful signals when you're mid-spec on a primary bedroom. That's different from "look at our outdoor furniture" and different again from "here's a quilt." But the email is doing absolutely nothing to signal that Pottery Barn knows I exist as a person, let alone a customer with two active renovation projects. It reads like a broadcast, and after six sends in two days, that's a problem.
- The "Recommended for You" block at the bottom — outdoor pieces pasted into a bedroom collection email — breaks whatever coherence the top half built. Mark as read. I'd drag the Sausalito finish reference into my renovation folder, but I wouldn't click, and I wouldn't forward.

## What's working

- **"Walnut Burl" as a specific finish name earns credibility** — that's furniture vocabulary, not marketing copy, and it signals the brand knows what they're selling to someone who's actively comparing wood tones across collections.
- **The Sausalito Collection is immediately identifiable in the hero** — zero ambiguity about what this email is, which is more than "American classics that never go out of style" managed on June 1.
- **New finishes is genuinely new information** — the first email in this entire stretch that's telling me something I didn't already know rather than rehashing the bedding sale cadence.
- **Clean visual hierarchy** — hero leads, finish options break into scannable sections, no clutter fighting for the eye.
- **Renders without issue** — no broken images, no overlapping text, clean load throughout.

## What's weak

- **Six emails in 48 hours with no discernible logic.** Article ran a similarly dense Memorial Day stretch but structured it: sale open, brand reset, reminder, last chance. Pottery Barn is just firing. By the time this landed, I was already in archive mode.
- **No price signal anywhere.** New finishes on a bestseller is interesting; new finishes with a launch offer would be actionable. Article's "Up to 40% off ends Monday" made me click in under five seconds. This one makes me think.
- **"Recommended for You" at the bottom is incoherent** — outdoor furniture in a bedroom collection email doesn't read as personalization, it reads as a content dump. CB2 has been more disciplined about keeping their sectioned emails scoped to a single use case.
- **The subject doesn't name the collection.** Teasing "a bestseller" without naming it is a fine hook if you commit to it — CB2's "Tell everyone you studied art history" worked because the personality carried the vagueness. Here it just feels like a missed opportunity to put "Sausalito" in the subject line of a Sausalito email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name the collection in the subject line.** "Sausalito" is a recognizable name in the Pottery Barn catalog. Subscribers who care will open faster; the rest will scroll past either way.
- 2. **Add a launch incentive.** Even free shipping on the new finishes, or a 48-hour early-access window, moves this from informational to clickable.
- 3. **Cut the Recommended for You block or actually personalize it.** If those outdoor pieces are genuinely based on my browse history, say so explicitly — "Based on what you've been looking at." Otherwise it's noise that undermines the Sausalito focus.
- 4. **Dial back the cadence or impose send logic.** Six emails in two days is how you train a subscriber to stop opening. Wayfair's recent "BATH MATS You'll Love!!" at least had the self-awareness to be about one thing. Pottery Barn is sending individual product lines like individual texts.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Sausalito just dropped two new finishes`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Walnut Burl or Natural White — which room?`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The Sausalito Collection expands. Same bestseller bones, two new wood finishes.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Two new finishes on our most-copied bedroom collection — see which one fits your space.`

  &external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember

**[FAIL] Cross-brand West Elm spacer — HTTP 403 + plain HTTP**

A West Elm asset (sibling brand) is used as a layout spacer. It is both returning 403 and served over HTTP. The asset should be replaced with a self-hosted HTTPS equivalent.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP code injected into `<style>` block (template bug)**
The following server-side PHP directives appear literally inside a `<style>` tag in `<head>`:
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
This is invalid CSS. The PHP was never executed — it was included as literal text, likely from a copy-paste from a server-side image script. Email clients will treat it as malformed CSS; it will not execute but will produce parse warnings in strict validators.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Affects tab labels in webmail clients and screen reader announcements.

**48 images missing `alt` text**
All 15 `slices_*.jpg` content images, all product images in the recommendations module, both footer slices sets, and the open-tracking pixel have no `alt` attribute. Affects screen readers and images-off rendering in Outlook/Gmail.

**3 image sources on plain HTTP** (may be blocked by clients enforcing HTTPS):
```
http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif         [also 403]
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg
http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true  [open pixel]
```
The open-tracking pixel on HTTP will be blocked by Apple Mail Privacy Protection and other clients that enforce mixed-content policies, causing open rate undercounting.
