# Head back to school with Pottery Barn Kids 🎒📚
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-four. That's where I land this July with Pottery Barn, and this is the one that finally made me say out loud, to no one: *I don't have kids.* This is a Pottery Barn Kids email — backpacks, monograms, lunch boxes, three girls in matching pastels standing in front of what looks like an elementary school. I am a 36-year-old man with two houses in active renovation. I have never once purchased anything from Pottery Barn Kids. I don't know why I'm on this list.
- The execution is fine. It's clean, the personalization angle ("ready. set. personalized!") is a legitimate hook for parents who want monogrammed backpacks, and the product photography is nice. None of that matters because I am not a parent with a school-age child, and Pottery Barn apparently doesn't know the difference between its own sub-brands when it comes to segmentation. The July 14th "It's FREE to work with our design experts" send? That was a 9/10 because it was targeted directly at what I'm doing. This one is the mirror image of that — same cadence slot, completely wrong audience.
- I won't click. I won't forward it. I filed it next to the "UP TO 70% OFF + coastal cottage" blast from two days ago, which is where PB emails go when they've lost the thread.

## What's working

- **Personalization pitch is smart for its actual audience** — "ready. set. personalized!" with monogrammed backpacks is a genuine differentiator vs. what Wayfair or IKEA can offer; that's a real reason to shop PBK over a generic retailer
- **Visual hierarchy is clean** — hero image → category headline → product grid → secondary editorial → app download; the eye flows top to bottom without fighting the layout
- **Seasonal timing is correct** — July BTS sends are the right calendar move; this isn't stale creative
- **No render bugs** — every image loaded, text is readable at mobile size, no overlapping elements or truncation

## What's weak

- **Complete demographic mismatch** — the hero features school-age girls in pink/lavender; I am not in this image or anywhere near it
- **Wrong sub-brand, wrong list** — I'm presumably on the main Pottery Barn list for furniture; PBK should be a separate opt-in, not a bleed-through send
- **No price anchor anywhere visible** — Article ran a zero-discount editorial and still felt useful because it had category intent; this one pushes product without a single specific offer, discount, or even a "starting at $X" hook
- **Cadence is broken** — the July 14th free design consultation email was 9/10 because it felt earned; 24 emails in 15 days means this one can't catch a break regardless of content

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress PBK from main PB list unless the subscriber has a child-related purchase history.** This is the single biggest lift — nothing else matters if I'm not a parent.
- 2. **Add a price anchor in the hero.** "Personalized backpacks, from $XX" stops the scroll for parents who are on-target; pure editorial without a number gets skipped.
- 3. **Subject should signal the sub-brand earlier** — "Pottery Barn Kids" buried at the end means I opened it thinking it was main-line PB. Put the audience signal upfront and save unqualified subscribers the mismatch tax.
- 4. **Add a deadline.** Article's entryway email had no discount and still scored an 8 because the copy had intention; this email has seasonal urgency built in (school starts in August) but doesn't name it.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Corrupted `<style>` block — PHP headers in CSS:**

The second `<style>` block in `<head>` contains PHP `header()` calls:

```html
<style>
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  ...
</style>
```

These are PHP server-side directives that were never executed — they appear to have been copy-pasted from a server-side image script into the CSS block by mistake. Mail clients will parse them as invalid CSS and discard them, but their presence indicates a template assembly error.

**Missing alt text — 30 images:**

The QA scan flagged 30 images with empty or absent `alt` attributes, including:

- `TopBanner.jpg` (hero-level content image, no alt)
- `standard-curtain-rod-wall-bracket-l.jpg`, `farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg`, and 6 other product images — no alt
- `slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg` (footer image slices, 18 total) — no alt
- Tracking pixels (`coreemail.html`, `lb`, `1`, `svopn`) — blank alt is acceptable for tracking pixels but should be `alt=""`  explicitly

The product image alts are accessibility failures. Screen reader users receive no product information.

**Mixed content — 2 images served over HTTP:**

- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Both reference `edm.potterybarn.com` over plain HTTP. Gmail and other clients that enforce HTTPS will block these images, leaving blank spaces in the footer credit card banner area.

**Responsive breakpoints inconsistent:**

Two separate `@media` queries are defined with different breakpoints: `max-width: 480px` (in the first `<style>` block using `element[class]` attribute selectors) and `max-width: 650px` (in the third `<style>` block using class selectors). The 480px block uses attribute selector syntax specifically to work around Gmail's class-stripping behavior — the 650px block does not, meaning the `.mobile-hide`/`.mobile-show` classes may not function in Gmail.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header — unconfirmed:**

The QA relay did not capture `List-Unsubscribe` or `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) headers. These are likely present at the SMTP envelope level but not surfaced through the AgentMail capture path. Gmail and Apple Mail require `List-Unsubscribe-Post` for one-click unsubscribe compliance (Google Bulk Sender policy, February 2024). This should be verified directly against raw SMTP headers, not inferred from relay output.

**Authentication-Results — unconfirmed:**

SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown; the header was not captured by the relay. Sending domain `e.potterybarn.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain (consistent with Salesforce Marketing Cloud), so authentication is likely configured — but cannot be confirmed from this data.

**CAN-SPAM:** An unsubscribe mechanism and physical address are present in the footer (visible in the HTML structure). No issues confirmed.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**No UTM parameters on any click URLs.** All 23 CTA links route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirect URLs with opaque `qs=` query strings. The redirected destination URLs are not inspectable without following the redirect chain, so it cannot be confirmed whether UTM params (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are appended at redirect time.

If UTM parameters are not injected at redirect, GA4/analytics will attribute all email traffic as direct — a significant measurement gap for a promotional send.

**Cross-brand asset contamination:** The broken spacer `edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` is a West Elm asset appearing in a Pottery Barn Kids email. This suggests shared template components are not being correctly swapped at send time, which may indicate downstream CTA or footer links also point to the wrong brand properties.
