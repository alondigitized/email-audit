# Our latest collab is serving this star’s unmatched style
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-25**
## Executive summary

- Okay, Venus Williams is doing double duty this week — Crate & Barrel AND now this one, which reads like the extended cut with the full product drop. My eye actually landed on "serving unmatched style" as a section header, which is a cute tennis callback, but by the time I got there I'd already scrolled past two new furniture pieces and three testimonial quotes. This is less an email and more a lookbook someone forgot to trim.
- The stuff that matters to me — new sectional, new bed, new dining table, new sideboard — is genuinely there, and I'm mid-renovation on two houses so a "new! Lawn Club power sectional" headline should be catnip. But it's buried in a scroll so long I lost track of what collection I was even looking at by the Monte Carlo dining table. Compare that to the Design Desk email from 9/24, which got straight to "here's how you spec custom furniture" — this one just keeps unveiling pieces like a runway show.
- Verdict: I'd skim the top third on my phone, maybe tap through on the sectional since I need one for the lake house, but I'm not scrolling to the kitchen tools section at the bottom. Not forwarding this — it's not tight enough to send a friend, it's a whole catalog.

## What's working

- The Venus Williams collab is a real, specific hook — not generic "designer picks" language, and it's consistent with the 9/25 Venus/C&B email so at least the campaign isn't schizophrenic.
- "new! Lawn Club power sectional" and "new! Meadows bed" are genuinely useful product-drop headlines for someone furnishing two houses right now.
- The tennis-ball dining chair detail and the green color story running through the shots feels intentional and on-brand, not just stock lifestyle photography.
- Customer quote callouts ("I hope this collection makes you think, I could live like this forever") add some social proof, which Article's entryway email also leaned on effectively.

## What's weak

- This is way too long — five-plus product sections stacked vertically with zero jump links or table of contents. CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" (7/10 from me) at least segmented by category up top.
- No pricing visible anywhere in what I can see — every CTA is just "Shop the Collection," which is the same vague verb Article and CB2 both lean on when they don't want to show the number.
- I'm getting Crate & Barrel fatigue — this is the second Venus Williams-themed send in two days from the same brand, and my brain already filed that story yesterday.
- Zero mention of shipping timelines or swatch/sample ordering, which is the actual blocker holding up my furniture decisions right now — Pottery Barn's Holiday Headquarters at least organized by intent, this one organizes by "look at more stuff."

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this to one hero product (the sectional, since it's the "new" flagship) with a single strong CTA, and move the rest into a "see the full collection" link — nobody needs five separate green-toned room shots in one scroll.
- 2. Put a price or "starting at $X" on at least the hero sectional — vague CTAs are why I don't click, I want to know if this fits budget before I tap.
- 3. Add a swatch/sample CTA near the sectional and sideboard — I will not buy a $3k sofa sight-unseen on fabric, and this brand has the infrastructure to offer samples.
- 4. Since this is the second Venus Williams email in 48 hours, differentiate the subject/preview hard from the 9/25 send so it doesn't read as a resend.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New from Venus Williams: the Lawn Club sectional is here`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your green era starts with this sectional (new!)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: the Meadows bed, Monte Carlo table, and Legacy sideboard — all new this week`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the full Venus Williams x Crate&Barrel drop before it sells out`
