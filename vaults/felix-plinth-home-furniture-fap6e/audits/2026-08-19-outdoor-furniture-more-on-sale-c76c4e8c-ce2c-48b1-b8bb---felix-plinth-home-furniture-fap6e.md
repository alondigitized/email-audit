# Outdoor furniture & more ON SALE
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-19**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "Savings for Outdoor Living" — up to 60% off — and this actually lands right in my lane, because I'm renovating both houses and the second home has a patio that's been bare since June. The hero shot of the Westport Bistro Chairs on that stone patio is genuinely nice, and scrolling down I get three real outdoor sets — Torrey, Indio Metal, Indio Wood — each labeled clean, no guessing what I'm looking at. That's more restraint than Pottery Barn's usually shown me this week.
- Then it falls apart. "Recommended Just For You" throws a curtain rod, a dining table, a swivel chair, sheets, and a rug at me — none of it outdoor, none of it the thing the subject line promised. Then a Design Crew Sweepstakes I've already seen (2026-08-18), then a full "Halloween Shop" section with a ghost display on a porch — it's August 19th, I haven't even ordered outdoor cushions yet and you're pivoting me to Halloween decor two sections later. Then the App Store pitch, then the Affirm/credit card rewards pitch. That's five different asks stacked in one email.
- I'd click Shop All Sale for the outdoor stuff specifically, since that's live shopping I actually need. I would not forward this — it's not tight enough to send a friend, it's Pottery Barn doing five campaigns in one send again.

## What's working

- The outdoor furniture hero and three product blocks (Torrey, Indio Metal, Indio Wood) are genuinely on-category for a guy furnishing a patio mid-renovation.
- "UP TO 60% OFF" is a real, visible number, not vague savings language.
- Clean product labeling under each shot — I know exactly what I'm looking at without hunting.
- The lifestyle shot (dinner table set outdoors) sells the outcome, not just the product — that's the kind of image that actually gets me picturing my own patio.

## What's weak

- "Recommended Just For You" is not for me at all — curtain rods and percale sheets have nothing to do with outdoor furniture; this brand does this "personalization" section wrong constantly.
- The Halloween Shop insert mid-email is jarring — I'm buying furniture, not costumes, and it's a full seasonal pivot buried two scrolls in.
- Too many CTAs stacked back to back: Shop All Sale, Design Crew Sweepstakes, Halloween Shop, App download, 10% Rewards card — by the fourth ask I've tuned out.
- The sweepstakes block is a repeat of the exact one from 2026-08-18 ("Book a FREE appt. for a chance to win $1500") — same offer, same week, no new information.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email down to outdoor furniture only — kill Halloween, kill the rewards-card pitch, kill the repeat sweepstakes. One category, one CTA, sent clean. That's the single biggest lift here.
- 2. Fix "Recommended Just For You" to actually pull from outdoor/patio browsing history, not random indoor SKUs — right now it reads as broken personalization, not real personalization.
- 3. Move "Shop All Sale" higher, right under the hero — right now I have to scroll past three product blocks to find the actual button.
- 4. If Halloween has to run, send it as its own email next week, not stapled to an outdoor furniture sale.

# Technical Audit: Pottery Barn — "Outdoor furniture & more ON SALE"

## 1. Technical Summary
This email is a standard multi-table EDM build with heavy tracking/redirect infrastructure and multiple open-tracking pixels; automated QA passed only 57% of checks, driven almost entirely by missing `alt` attributes and a handful of insecure (`http://`) image sources.

- Additional third-party tracking calls present in source: `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/L21rdC84NjEvY2lkLzE3NDc3ODk3MzMvdC8y/cat/1` (Turn/Amobee data pixel) and `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/c4f7d21a-362e-4217-96bb-25c175356a5c?mi_mid=01a01908-c400-7000-b34a-3e0bb3dc4df7&cp_tp` (open/click tracking, MailIQ/Silverpop-style params).
- Open-tracking pixel served over plaintext HTTP: `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` — the recipient email address is passed in cleartext in a GET query string over an unencrypted connection, which is both a security/PII-leak concern and a likely image-blocking target for corporate mail gateways.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **33 images missing `alt` text**, including primary content banners (`F26_Top_NewArrivals.jpg`, `slices_01–07.jpg`, `F26_Top_DesignCrewSweeps_01a/b.jpg`, `F26_Bottom_HalloweenShop.jpg`), product images (`banks-extendable-dining-table...`, `remmy-swivel-chair...`, `toscana-extendable-dining-table...`, `400-thread-count-organic-percale-sheet-set...`, `custom-capitola-hand-tufted-wool-rug...`), and all footer slice images. With images blocked by default in most mail clients, the email will render as a near-blank shell with no fallback text for screen-reader or images-off users.
- Head contains malformed/invalid CSS: raw PHP-style `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');`, `header("Cache-Control: ...")`, `header("Pragma: no-cache")` statements are embedded inside a `<style>` block (not inside a `<?php ?>` tag or comment). These are not valid CSS and will be ignored by mail clients, but indicate a templating/build artifact that leaked into the shipped HTML.
- `<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">` and `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` are present — expected for transactional/marketing EDMs, not a defect.
- Responsive behavior relies on two overlapping/conflicting `@media` breakpoints (`min-width:700px` and `min-width:650px`) both targeting `img{max-width:...}` — functional but redundant; the 650px rule's `!important` will always win over the 700px rule at widths ≥700px, making the 700px block dead code.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tags (e.g. `%%`, `{{`, `[FIRSTNAME]`) are present in the visible truncated source.
- Recipient identity is passed as a literal, resolved email address in tracking URLs (`sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`, and in `coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`) rather than an opaque token/hash — this is a personalization implementation, not a broken merge, but it does mean PII travels in plaintext query strings (see §2/§5).

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive (or explicitly empty `alt=""` for purely decorative slices/spacers) `alt` attributes to all 33 flagged images, prioritizing primary content banners and product shots.
2. Migrate the three remaining `http://` asset URLs (`edm.westelm.com/spacer_0.gif`, `edm.potterybarn.com/.../Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, `.../slices_02.jpg`) to HTTPS.
3. Remove the stray `header(...)` PHP statements from the `<style>` block in `<head>` — dead code from a leaked build artifact.
4. Confirm at the originating MTA (not just the AgentMail relay) that `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are actually sent, given Gmail/Yahoo's bulk-sender one-click-unsubscribe requirement.
5. Move the open-tracking pixel off plaintext HTTP (`svopn.potterybarn.com`) to HTTPS to stop transmitting the recipient's email address in cleartext.
6. Consolidate the two overlapping `img{max-width}` media queries (650px vs 700px breakpoints) to remove dead CSS.
