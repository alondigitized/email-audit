# New! Our beautiful take on fairy tale style
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-04**
## Executive summary

- This is another Crate & Barrel family send, roughly the thirteenth since 11/1. I was already in snooze mode after the 11/04 "ENDS TODAY" accent chair email and the Ooni pizza one on 11/03. The sender name here is Crate & Barrel Kids, so I almost swiped it. I opened it because the nursery and bedroom photos are pretty, and I'm mid-renovation.
- It's a lookbook, and a nice one. Sage, plum and cream bedrooms with a dark green wall, wallpaper and scalloped bedding all look polished. But it's a very long scroll with five or six "Shop the Look" buttons and no single offer. I'd save a couple of images to my Pinterest-adjacent folder and not buy anything. My kids' rooms aren't the project that's eating my budget, so this isn't aimed at what I'm shopping for.
- I wouldn't forward it. I'd mark it read. The Kids sub-brand is a different list from the Crate & Barrel emails I actually act on.

## What's working

- The photography is strong. Each room has a mood and a clear story, like the dark green wall with plum bedding. That's what I'd screenshot for a mood board.
- Each room is its own block with a short caption and its own "Shop the Look" button. It's easy to scan and tap.
- There's a markdown block ("100s of new markdowns", which looks like up to 60% off). It's the only price hook, and it's one I'd actually notice.
- The product grid near the bottom shows prices and names, so I could judge value without clicking.
- The Design Desk "Book a Free Appointment" block is a useful showroom-style service. It's the closest thing here to how I really shop.

## What's weak

- The email is very long and has no clear lead offer. The eye lands on pretty rooms, not a reason to act today.
- It carries too many unrelated modules: a $5,000 sweepstakes, a gift section, a credit card promo, an app download, strollers and the Design Desk. It reads as a catch-all.
- The hero overlay text is hard to read against the busy bed photo.
- It's a Kids email, and nothing in it speaks to a 36-year-old renovating two houses. There are no swatches, shipping times or lead times for the furniture shown.
- No deadline anywhere I could see. After "ENDS TODAY" earlier, this one has no urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one offer or one hero collection (the Beatrix bedroom) and cut the other modules. Put the markdown block second, not near the bottom.
- 2. Put furniture shipping times and swatch/sample availability under the bed and dresser shots. That's what I need before I'd buy.
- 3. Cut the scroll length by half. Pottery Barn's "New arrivals with timeless holiday charm" lookbook did the same job in fewer panels.
- 4. Segment it. If I've never bought from Kids, this shouldn't be on top of the main Crate & Barrel stream. Wayfair at least ties its holiday flash sale to one clear headline.
- 5. Make the hero text legible with a solid band or a darker overlay.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's HTML uses a table layout with Outlook/VML scaffolding. The QA run passed 57% of checks, with 0 errors and 3 warnings. The main confirmed problem is widespread missing `alt` attributes on about 40 images. The compliance and authentication warnings can't be confirmed because the headers weren't captured and the HTML source was truncated.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links.** 83 tracking links were skipped because they are on click-redirect domains and the HTTP probe was skipped. Destination validity, redirect chains and final landing URLs are unverified.
- **Repeated third-party pixels.** `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=dbe8da71…&n=1` through `n=5` fires the same LiveRamp pixel five times with the same hashed ID `s`. The `n` value is the only thing that changes. This adds avoidable third-party requests.
- **Subscriber ID in image URLs.** `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png?mi_u=dbe8da71…` (6 instances), `…/o.gif?mi_u=…` and `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/e62ae65b-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…` carry per-recipient identifiers. This is normal for open and personalization tracking, but the same subscriber hash is also sent to a third party (`rlcdn.com`).

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text.** The QA run flagged about 40 `<img>` elements with no `alt` attribute, and the list appears truncated. Content images include:
  - `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/*.jpg` (about 24 files, for example `8045c9df-…jpg` and `f7fce13f-…jpg`)
  - `…/m/47/bbf6f9d4-….png`
  - Scene7 assets: `2026_0710_Kids_StorybookLookbook_Her…`, `2026_1002_CBK_GiftGuide_SecondaryStory.gif`, `20261101_CBK_Fall_Clearance_PM_v3_Tert…`

  With images blocked or read by a screen reader, the content is lost. Screen readers fall back to the filename or URL.
- **Tracking pixels.** The pixels (`o.gif`, `448796.gif` ×5, `f3c0d22992a01946.png` ×6, `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/…`) also lack `alt`. Use `alt=""` on them, plus `width="1" height="1"` where not already set.
- **Zoom disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="… maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile, which is an accessibility failure.
- **Invalid `<title>`.** `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` has an unescaped `&` under an XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype. It should be `&amp;`.
- **Leftover template markup.** The source has an unresolved template comment, `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->`. It also has duplicate or conflicting `format-detection` and charset meta tags (`<meta http-equiv="Content-Type">` plus `<meta charset>`, and several `format-detection` tags). These are harmless in practice but sloppy.
