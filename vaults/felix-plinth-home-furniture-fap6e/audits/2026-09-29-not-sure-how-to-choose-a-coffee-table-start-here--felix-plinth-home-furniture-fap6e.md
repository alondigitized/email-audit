# Not sure how to choose a coffee table? Start here →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-29**
## Executive summary

- This is the first Crate & Barrel send this week that matches what I'm doing. I'm mid-renovation on two houses and I need a coffee table for the second one. A "how to choose" guide is more useful to me than another sale blast. The layout is a buying guide with three steps (shape, material, size), and each step has product tiles under it. That's how I actually shop, and the tiles show Bestseller and New Arrival tags plus star ratings.
- But the coffee and espresso banner sits above the hero and pulls against it. It promises "up to $300 off" on machines, and the same offer comes back halfway down with a JURA espresso feature. I opened this for coffee tables, not espresso. The email is also very long, with a cabinet section, a "get organized" block, a "we love these for you" grid, an app pitch and a Design Desk booking block after the guide. It reads as three emails stitched together.
- I'd click, mainly the "Shop Coffee Tables" button. I'd save it to my inspiration folder. I wouldn't forward it. It's a decent guide, but I'd also want to compare against Pottery Barn and CB2 before buying.

## What's working

- The guide structure of shape, material, then size matches how I'd narrow down a table. Each step has a clear heading.
- The product tiles carry Bestseller and New Arrival tags and star ratings, so I can spot the safe pick fast.
- The lifestyle room photos are warm and neutral. They fit the palette I'm working with in the second house.
- Each step has its own "Shop Now"-style button, so I never have to scroll back up to act.
- The Design Desk block at the bottom is a real showroom-style service. It's relevant to someone furnishing two homes.

## What's weak

- The espresso banner at the very top competes with the coffee table hero. "Coffee" appears twice, and it confuses the message.
- The JURA espresso block and the "get organized event: up to 25% off" block are unrelated to the subject. The 25% offer isn't tied to coffee tables, so it's unclear what it covers.
- It's very long. The size-guide step and the accent tables and chairs grid feel like filler by then.
- This is another Crate & Barrel send after the SMEG toaster/kettle one and the football hosting one on 9/28. The coffee and espresso event has now been pushed at me in two different emails.
- At phone size, the small gray captions and product names under the tiles are hard to read without zooming.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the espresso banner out of the top slot, or drop it from this send. Let the coffee table guide own the hero, and send the espresso event separately to people who buy kitchen items.
- 2. Put one concrete furniture offer next to the coffee table CTA. Right now the coffee tables have no visible price or savings. Something like a member price or free shipping on tables would give me a reason to act.
- 3. Cut the bottom third (organized event, "we love these for you", app pitch) or tighten it to one block. Keep the Design Desk booking, since it's the strongest closer.
- 4. Add a swatch/sample or showroom cue for the materials section, since I'm matching finishes across rooms. Also add a delivery-time note for furniture.
- 5. Make the captions and prices bigger so they read on mobile.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional layout with `lang="en"`, an MSO/VML fallback, and 77 tracked links. It has widespread missing `alt` attributes, a zoom-restricting viewport meta tag, and several third-party tracking pixels. Header-based compliance and authentication checks couldn't be verified. The HTML source was truncated, so link targets, footer content and merge tokens were not fully inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Not verifiable:** 77 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are click-redirect domains. Their destinations, and whether any are broken, are unknown.
- **Third-party pixels:** Five `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=<hash>&n=1..5` beacons are present. They are LiveRamp pixels carrying a hashed subscriber identifier in the `s=` parameter. This is a privacy and disclosure consideration, not a functional defect.
- **Other beacons:** These are `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif?mi_u=...` (open pixel) and `https://dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/a49b2c19-...?mi_cid=...&mi_mid=...`.
- **Per-recipient parameters:** `mi_u=dbe8da71…` appears in the image URLs. These are unique per recipient, so the images can't be cached or shared across recipients.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** The QA check flagged 40+ images (the list was truncated). Around 29 are content images on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/*.jpg|gif|png`, for example `bbf6f9d4-…png` and `7a932067-…jpg`. With images blocked, these render as empty space, and screen readers announce the filenames or nothing.
- **Other images without alt:** Seven `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png` images (`560d850e7e517ede.png` and others) are also flagged. Because they are served from a recommendation-path URL, they may be product tiles. If so, they need alt text.
- **Decorative beacons:** Tracking pixels (`o.gif`, `448796.gif`) should have `alt=""`, and currently have no `alt` at all. That is low severity.
- **Viewport zoom:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on some clients, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Text scaling:** `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` (in `*{}` and `html{}`) disables user text scaling in WebKit.
- **Markup validity:** `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` has an unescaped ampersand. It should be `&amp;`. The duplicate `format-detection` meta tags are redundant but harmless.
- **Mobile layout:** The mobile breakpoint is `max-width:460px`, which is narrow. Larger phones (e.g., 480px+ landscape or Plus/Max models) fall back to the desktop layout, and `.mobile{width:460px !important}` may force horizontal overflow on narrower ones.
- **Positives:** `lang="en"`, `color-scheme` meta tags, the MSO conditional blocks and `role`-less table layout are all in place.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (`{{…}}`, `%%…%%`, `[FIRSTNAME]`) are visible in the available source. The only leftover template text is an HTML comment, `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->`. It isn't rendered, but it does show the template wasn't cleaned up.

- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** Both were not found. The QA tool itself notes these headers may not be captured by the AgentMail relay. **This is unconfirmed and not evidence that the sender omitted them.** Verify against the raw message.
- **Authentication-Results:** Not found. This is expected via the relay, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The sending domain is `mail.crateandbarrel.com`. Do not treat this as a failure.
- **Footer:** The visible unsubscribe link and physical postal address could not be verified, because the source is truncated before the footer.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. All 77 links go through click-redirect domains and were not resolved, so UTM parameters and landing pages could not be checked. The only tracking identifiers visible are ESP-style `mi_u`, `mi_cid` and `mi_mid` parameters.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Add meaningful `alt` text to all content images.** This covers the ~29 images on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com` and the 7 on `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/`. Use `alt=""` on the tracking pixels.

3. **Escape the ampersand in the title** (`Crate &amp; Barrel`).
4. **Verify the raw headers** (`List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, `Authentication-Results`) on a direct-to-inbox copy, not the AgentMail relay copy.
5. **Follow the tracked redirects** in a separate pass to check destinations and UTM parameters.
6. **Review the third-party pixels.** Confirm the LiveRamp `rlcdn.com` beacons are covered by the privacy disclosures.
7. **Consider raising the mobile breakpoint above 460px** to cover larger phones.
