# SO MUCH NEW
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-seven. That's where this puts CB2 in my July inbox — I went back and counted. Yesterday was "MORE ON SALE" at 26. The day before that was "APRES POOL" at 25. Today we get "SO MUCH NEW," which is the subject line equivalent of opening the door and yelling "things exist." At least "FLOOR SAMPLE SALE" told me something. At least "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" had a noun that meant something.
- Here's the aggravating part: when I actually opened the email, the hero stopped me cold. That warm terracotta sofa against the tone-on-tone wall is genuinely handsome — CB2 still photographs furniture better than Pottery Barn's cluttered staging and better than Crate & Barrel's increasingly catalog-stiff editorial. And then below the hero, pendant lamps in warm amber glass — which is exactly what I've been pinning for the primary's dining zone. The content is doing work. The wrapper is phoning it in.
- The $5,000 Refresh sweepstakes in the middle tells me the email team knows they need an engagement hook but didn't have a real offer to put here. That's fine as a tactic — but it's not a reason to click, and it's not a reason to buy. I'd mark as read, save a mental note about the pendants, and close it. No click today.

## What's working

- **Hero image earned the scroll** — the warm terracotta sofa in that tone-on-tone editorial room is the best single product shot I've seen from the CB2/Crate family all month. Objectively better staging than Pottery Barn's summer sale hero.
- **Pendant lamp section is exactly my category** — I have a dining room to light and that amber glass section read my wishlist. Article's editorial sends name their collections; CB2's photography here rivals that quality.
- **Palette is locked and intentional** — browns, terracotta, amber from hero to footer. No jarring tonal switches. Consistent in a way Wayfair's promotional blast emails never are.
- **Clean layout, no visible render issues** — images loaded, text didn't overlap, nothing broken.
- **$5,000 Refresh section is at least clearly labeled** — the sweepstakes is visible as a distinct section with a headline. It's not hiding in the fine print.

## What's weak

- **"SO MUCH NEW" tells me nothing** — On July 18 they expanded a sale. On July 17 they pointed me at a rug with character. Today I'm told that newness has occurred. Wayfair's subject lines are repetitive but at least "FREE shipping over $35" is a fact I can act on.
- **No price, no discount, no expiry** — CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" from a few weeks back had an actual offer. This email asks me to browse with no reduction in friction and no urgency at all.
- **The sweepstakes is more work than a click-to-buy** — entering a contest requires more cognitive effort than pressing a cart button. At email 27, I need less friction, not a form.
- **"Come Find Us. We're Good." is footer content masquerading as a content block** — the store locator section breaks the browse momentum right when the product grid should be closing the deal.
- **Cadence fatigue is terminal** — this is a structural problem, not a single-email problem, but at 27 sends in July I am genuinely speed-reading CB2 the same way I now bulk-dismiss Wayfair. The brand is conditioning me to not open carefully.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Give me nouns in the subject** — "New this week: pendants, sofas, storage" is 38 characters and tells me three things. "SO MUCH NEW" is 11 characters and tells me zero.
- 2. **Lead with or feature the pendant collection explicitly** — that amber glass section is the highest-differentiation content in the email. Make it a named hero block with a collection name and a direct link, the way Article does when they spotlight a product family.
- 3. **Trade the sweepstakes for a time-bounded offer** — 15% off new arrivals for 72 hours, or free delivery on first new arrivals item this week. Give me a reason today is different from tomorrow.
- 4. **Add a freshness signal to each product section** — even "Added this week" under the section header reduces my skepticism that "new" means Q1 inventory.
- 5. **Move the store locator to the footer** — "Come Find Us. We're Good." is a brand tagline, not a content block, and it doesn't belong above the product grid.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users with low vision from zooming. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied to all elements**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Overrides user font size preferences system-wide. Should be `100%` on `body` only, not `none` on `*`.

**Mobile breakpoint at `max-width: 418px` is too narrow**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { .mobile { width: 414px !important; } }
```
iPhone 15 Pro Max is 430px wide — this breakpoint won't fire on it. Standard safe value is `600px`.

**Double-slash in three font URLs**
```
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2
```
Technically malformed. Most servers tolerate it, but it indicates a copy-paste error in the template.

**`font-display: swap` has no effect in email**
Used in four `@font-face` blocks — this is a browser loading hint that all email clients ignore. Noise, not functional harm.

**`line-height: 100%` on `*` selector**
```css
* { line-height: 100%; }
```
Causes tight line spacing on multi-line text. Should be scoped to `p, td`, not universal.

**`max-height: 1000000px` on `*` selector**
Standard Gmail image-scaling hack but applied globally — can clip content in clients that respect it literally.
