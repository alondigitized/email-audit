# 🎃 Selling fast: Halloween costumes, bags & more  →
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-15**
## Executive summary

- This is the fifth Crate & Barrel family email in my inbox today, and the Kids sender is back for a second time. I'm renovating two houses, and a Halloween costume and trick-or-treat basket email isn't what I'm shopping for. I don't have a kid at the costume age on this list. My reflex was to swipe it into the C&B pile with the dining room event, the holiday magic send and the stripes email.
- In fairness, it's a tidy, well-built page. The hero says "selling fast! the halloween shop," and each section is labeled: baskets, bedding, costumes, decor. Under each one are three products with names and a "Shop All" button. The clearance block ("up to 60% off") is the only real price hook, and it's buried in the bottom third. The one blurry bit is the small print on the tiles, which I couldn't read at this size.
- Would I click? Probably not. Would I forward it? Only to a friend with small kids, and only because the Jellycat block and the pumpkin bedding are cute. For me it's mark-as-read.

## What's working

- The hero is clear. A kid at a table in a Halloween setting, "the halloween shop," and a button I can tap with a thumb.
- Each section follows the same pattern: headline, three products, one button. It scans fast, and the pumpkin cat basket is a good lead item.
- The "up to 60% off clearance" block is a real price hook, and so is the free-shipping banner at the top.
- The Jellycat "back in stock" block is a good urgency beat. It gives the "selling fast" subject something to back it up.
- The "we love these for you" grid at the end adds some personalization signal.

## What's weak

- The strongest offer, up to 60% off, sits near the bottom. The hero has no price and no percent off. Wayfair puts "$134 off" on the first pergola tile, and I'd take that over an unpriced hero.
- It's a Kids send on the same day as Crate & Barrel's Thanksgiving Dining Room Event, which led with up to 30% off. This one has nothing for my renovation, no swatches, no furniture and no rewards math.
- It's very long. Halloween has about two weeks left and this email has six or more sections. Even then, the "shop everything" CTAs blur together.
- The credit card block ("the card you love just got better") and the app block pile up at the bottom. They feel like filler after a long scroll.
- Cadence is the main problem. Five sends in one day is the same wall of C&B family emails that CB2 and Wayfair have trained me to tune out.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the price in the hero. Lead with the clearance percent or a concrete "from $X" on the costumes, and don't make me scroll 80% of the email to find the deal.
- 2. Cut the length by half. Keep baskets, costumes and clearance, and drop the credit card and app blocks unless they're in a dedicated send.
- 3. Segment this one. Send it to households with kids, not to me. I'm on lists for lighting and dining, and I'd rather see that. Cadence matters more than any single creative here.
- 4. Add an actual deadline, like "Ships by Oct 31, order by Oct 24." "Selling fast" with no date isn't credible urgency.
- 5. Put a rewards or card tie-in in the first screen. I buy on the highest-rewards card, so "earn 5% back" with the Crate card would register with me. It's currently buried.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based XHTML Transitional template (`lang="en"`, MSO/VML conditionals, 460px mobile breakpoint). It has no confirmed hard failures (QA: 57% pass, 0 errors, 3 warnings). Authentication, unsubscribe headers and all 96 tracked links could not be verified, and alt-text coverage is poor. The HTML source I received is truncated, so body and footer findings are limited to the QA output.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Link destinations are unverified.** QA skipped the HTTP probe on 96 tracking and click-redirect links, so I can't confirm any of them resolve. This reflects what the audit could check, not a defect in the email.
- **Per-recipient identifiers are in image URLs.** The `mi_u=56aaa278…` parameter appears on `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png` (6 times) and `…/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif`. `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/aed9429b-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…` carries campaign and message IDs.
- **Third-party pixels.** `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=56aaa278…&n=1` through `n=5` is five LiveRamp pixels. The `s=` value is a 64-hex string, which is consistent with a hashed identifier. I can't confirm what it hashes.
- **Duplicate pixel.** The same `f3c0d22992a01946.png` is requested 6 times, which is redundant.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on about 60 images.** The QA check flags every `<img>` it found without alt text. Most are content images from `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/*.jpg`, `.gif` and `.png`, for example `6cda691d-b295-47d6-aa7c-8c52d1eec48a.jpg` and `b8e4ab5a-65be-4620-a4ff-f1ab08aedc11.gif`. Scene7 assets are also affected: `20260916_CBK_HalloweenLaunch_Teriary_Socia…`, `2026_0926_CBK_Clearance_SecondaryStory.gif` and several `25_MI_Bottom_Spacer_40px_White` spacers. With images blocked, these render as blank space. The tracking pixels and spacers should carry `alt=""` instead of omitting the attribute. I can't tell from QA whether any of these already use an explicit empty alt.
- **Pinch-zoom disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="…maximum-scale=1" />` blocks zoom on mobile, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Text resizing blocked.** `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; … line-height:100%}` and `body{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` disable text scaling.
- **Invalid markup under the XHTML doctype.** `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` has an unescaped ampersand, and `<meta charset="utf-8">` is not self-closed.
- **Redundant meta tags.** There are three overlapping `format-detection` tags. The first already sets all five values. The later `date=no`, `address=no` and `telephone=no` tags duplicate it, and a second charset declaration is also present.
- **Leftover template placeholder.** The comment `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` is still in the head, and `<title>` is a generic brand string.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unrendered tokens (`{{…}}`, `%%…%%`, `[FNAME]`) appear in the visible source. The source is truncated, so the body wasn't checked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) are not found.** QA says this may be a capture limitation of the AgentMail relay. I can't confirm whether the sender sets them. If they are absent in the original message, Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements are not met.
- **`Authentication-Results` is not captured.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC are unknown. The From domain `mail.crateandbarrel.com` and the image/tracking subdomains are consistent, but that proves nothing about alignment.
- **Footer not reviewed.** The unsubscribe link and physical postal address are not in the truncated source, so I can't confirm them.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not verifiable. All 96 tracked links were skipped, and the visible source shows no resolved destination URLs or UTM parameters. The visible asset filenames suggest reused creative, such as `20260916_CBK_HalloweenLaunch` and `2026_0926_CBK_Clearance`, against a 2026-10-15 send. Whether the landing pages match is unknown.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add `alt` to every `<img>`: descriptive text for hero and product images, and `alt=""` for spacers and tracking pixels.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport tag and drop `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` from the `*` and `body` rules.
3. Check the raw `.eml` headers directly (not via the relay) for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC results.
4. Resolve a sample of the redirect links with a follow-redirects probe to check the final URLs and UTM parameters.
5. Escape the title as `Crate &amp; Barrel` and self-close the meta tags. Clean up the duplicate meta tags and the template placeholder comment.
6. Review whether sharing per-recipient identifiers with `rlcdn.com` is covered by the privacy disclosures.
7. Capture the full HTML so the footer and body links can be audited.
