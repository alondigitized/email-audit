# The chicken coop everyone LOVES. See why →
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- Sixty-eight. That's my rough Wayfair count, and I'm staring at a chicken coop. An actual chicken coop. Not a metaphor, not a style play — a wooden structure for poultry. I'm in the middle of sourcing sofas and pendant lights for two houses and Wayfair's algorithm decided I needed to know about livestock housing. I don't have chickens. I'm not getting chickens. My HOA would have opinions about chickens.
- To be fair, once I scrolled past the bewildering hero, the email revealed a more recognizable Wayfair chassis — a "Big Help. Small Price Tags." deal-grid section, a fountain spotlight (okay, that's actually interesting for the backyard of the second house), and a "ready to relax?" section anchored by a green outdoor chair that I would legitimately click. But I had to work for it, and most people — including me on a busy Thursday — don't scroll past a chicken coop to find patio furniture.
- Mark as read. Not forwarded. Not clicked. If that green chair had been the hero, we'd be having a different conversation.

## What's working

- **Specific subject, honest delivery.** "The chicken coop everyone LOVES. See why →" is exactly what's in the email — no bait-and-switch like the June 22nd "COFFEE MAKERS" disaster that opened to a 70-category dump. I didn't feel lied to, just baffled.
- **"Ready to relax?" section is legitimately relevant.** That green outdoor chair and the surrounding content is the kind of backyard-furnishing play that actually maps to what I'm shopping. It's just buried.
- **The fountain spotlight.** A standalone section on outdoor water features is smarter than it looks — summer, outdoor living, and it's a considered purchase I'd actually research. CB2's "Make your dinner party guests jealous" email from June 23rd would never feature a fountain, and they're worse for it.
- **Clean layout, no render bugs.** No overlapping text, no broken images, consistent Wayfair purple. After some of the emoji-bombed subject lines in the recent run, I'll take a visually coherent email.

## What's weak

- **The hero is wrong for any subscriber not actively raising poultry.** A chicken coop as the lead product in a home-goods email is a segmentation failure. Even if this is wildly popular on Wayfair, it belongs in a targeted "outdoor structures" email to buyers who've browsed that category — not blasted to the full list.
- **"Big Help. Small Price Tags." is vague.** No specific percentage, no anchor price, no category called out. After sixty-eight emails from this brand, I've learned that Wayfair's deal-grid sections can mean 10% off a $12 candle holder or 40% off a sofa. I need a number to care.
- **Social proof is asserted, not shown.** "People love this" is in the hero copy but I don't see a star rating or review count anywhere near the chicken coop. Article's recent "Bedroom storage essentials" email didn't have that problem — the brand voice felt trusted without needing to announce it.
- **No rewards or loyalty signal.** I run everything through my highest-rewards card and cross-check Wayfair's loyalty perks. Nothing in this email tells me whether there's member pricing or a stacking opportunity. Pottery Barn's "Discover bestselling furniture, ready to ship" at least surfaced that layer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the hero to the outdoor relaxation or fountain content.** The chicken coop belongs as a deep-scroll tile for relevant segments only. Lead with what the majority of your home-renovation subscriber base actually cares about in late June — outdoor living, patio furniture, shade solutions.
- 2. **Put a specific number in "Big Help. Small Price Tags."** "Up to 40% off" or "starting at $29" turns a vague section header into a scannable reason to scroll.
- 3. **Add a review count to any hero product.** If the coop truly is beloved, show me 4.7★ (2,841 reviews) next to it. That's the actual selling point — not the assertion.
- 4. **Surface a loyalty/rewards callout near the primary CTA.** Even one line: "Earn 5% with Wayfair Rewards" is enough to change the rewards math calculation for buyers like me.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Big Help pricing hits outdoor living this week — see what's marked down`
