# American classics that never go out of style
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-01**
## Executive summary

- Five. That's where I am with Pottery Barn. In roughly thirty-six hours I've already processed outdoor furniture, the Coastal Washed Quilt, the table linen refresh, and a ships-free bedding play — and now this lands with what I can only describe as a vibe email. "American classics that never go out of style" is not a sale. It's not a deadline. It's not even a category drop. It's Pottery Barn wrapping itself in heritage language and hoping I'll nod along. I respect the brand-equity angle in theory, but I've been trained over the last day and a half to expect a discount when a Pottery Barn address hits my inbox, and this one delivers nothing of the sort.
- The creative itself is fine. The hero image is warm and classically staged — a tan living room scene that actually suits what I'm trying to accomplish in the second house. The dining/entertaining vignette below the fold has visual pop with the red florals. There's a "Recommended for You" section near the bottom which is the most personalized thing in the entire send. But there's no offer, no urgency, no reason to click today rather than Thursday. This is a catalog in email form.
- I'd mark it read. I would not click, and I'd definitely not forward this — Crate & Barrel ran a tighter version of the "timeless outdoor classics" story last week with 30% off attached, which I actually sent to my contractor. This one has none of that teeth.

## What's working

- **Warm, coherent visual tone** — the hero living room and the dining table vignette both feel cohesive; no random product dumps, no garish sale overlays fighting the photography
- **"Recommended for You" section exists** — the personalization module near the bottom is the right instinct; it's the one moment this email acknowledges I might be an actual customer with purchase history
- **Subject line length is clean** — 44 characters, no ALL CAPS, no exclamation marks; it doesn't embarrass itself in the inbox
- **No visible render bugs** — layout holds, images load, nothing broken or overlapping from what I can see

## What's weak

- **Zero offer** — after four sale emails in a row, a zero-discount lifestyle send with no CTA hook feels like a bait-and-switch on my attention; Crate & Barrel managed "timeless + 30% off" in the same week
- **Fifth email in thirty-six hours** — this is a cadence problem that makes every individual email weaker; by this point I'm reading Pottery Barn on autopilot and skimming faster each time
- **"American classics" is too broad for where I am** — I'm mid-renovation on two homes; I need room-specific signals, not a brand thesis; Article's Memorial Day sprint at least told me *which* room to think about
- **No visible price anchoring** — even a "starting at $X" or a single hero product with a price would give me a reason to pause; the Coastal Washed Quilt email from May 30 did this right ("Starting at $179") and that's exactly the format that works on me

## Recommendations

- 1. **Attach an offer** — even free shipping on orders over $X changes the calculus entirely; a lifestyle editorial without any purchase incentive is a hard sell after conditioning me with five days of promos
- 2. **Narrow the hero to a room** — "American classics for your living room" or "The classics your dining room keeps" would land better for someone actively furnishing specific spaces; the current framing is too catalog-generic
- 3. **Surface a price in the hero** — one hero product with a price or a "starting at" anchor gives me an immediate reason to engage; without it, I'm just looking at beautiful photography I could find on Pinterest
- 4. **Throttle the cadence** — this should have been held until Monday; five emails in 36 hours is the fastest way to train a subscriber to stop reading
- 5. **Strengthen the recommended module** — it's down near the footer; if you're going to personalize, lead with it or at least give it more visual real estate
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your American classics wishlist — save on them now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The pieces that last: classics from $X`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Timeless picks for the living room, dining room, and bedroom — shop the edit`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The furniture that looks right ten years from now — a few we think you'll keep`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "American Classics" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard broken links (HTTP 403) will result in a dead CTA and a failed image load. A PHP header block leaked into a `<style>` tag indicates a template rendering defect, and 33 images ship without alt text — effectively an accessibility blackout for image-off rendering.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Credit card banner CTA returns HTTP 403**
`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&...`
This is the "Apply & Buy" / The Key credit card module. The destination is unreachable at send time; the click is dead for all recipients.

**[FAIL] Cross-brand spacer image returns HTTP 403**
`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
A West Elm CDN asset is embedded in a Pottery Barn email. It is also HTTP (not HTTPS — see §3). The 403 suggests the asset was removed or the cross-brand reference was never valid.

**[INFO] 22 click-redirect links were not probed**
All pass through `click.e.potterybarn.com`. Destination URL integrity and UTM parameter presence cannot be confirmed without resolving the redirects.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CRITICAL] PHP code leaked into `<style>` block — template rendering defect**
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
Server-side PHP directives appear verbatim inside a `<style>` tag. This is inert in the browser but confirms the template was not rendered through a PHP execution layer before send — a build/export pipeline failure. Any dynamic PHP blocks in this template are silently broken.

**[HIGH] 33 images missing alt text**
Every content image in the body, all 18 `slices_*.jpg` content tiles, the footer image slices, the CC banner, the open-tracking pixel, and the loyalty footer image have `alt=""` or no `alt` attribute. Image-off clients (Outlook default, many corporate environments) render a blank email.

Notable missing-alt images:

- `S26_Bottom_UpTo50OffOutdoor.jpg` — promotional bottom banner
- `W26_Bottom_GeneralDCBottomBanner.jpg` — DC bottom banner
- `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` — loyalty footer

**[HIGH] 3 image sources served over HTTP (not HTTPS)**
Will be blocked by default in Gmail, iOS Mail, and most modern clients:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent from captured message**
QA notes this may be a relay capture gap, but if genuinely missing, this violates Google/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (enforced since Feb 2024) and risks deliverability to both providers.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent**
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is required for bulk senders at Google and Yahoo. Missing this header means Gmail will use a slower mailto: fallback or suppress the unsubscribe UI entirely.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` not captured**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown via this relay. Cannot confirm `e.potterybarn.com` sending domain is properly authenticated.

**[WARN] Plain-text part is 72% URLs (10,937 / 15,111 chars)**
The multipart/alternative plain-text version is essentially a URL dump. This is a strong spam-filter signal and provides no usable fallback experience for plain-text clients.

CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link appear present in the HTML footer (standard Pottery Barn footer module observed). No issues detected there.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />`** is set in the email `<head>`. Any direct image or resource requests from this email to potterybarn.com will strip the `Referer` header — this is expected email hygiene. However, combined with click-through links (which pass through `click.e.potterybarn.com`), UTM attribution depends entirely on the redirect preserving parameters. Since the 22 tracking links were not resolved, UTM integrity is unverified.

The CC banner link (403 above) breaks the Apply & Buy flow entirely — any attribution for that CTA is lost.
