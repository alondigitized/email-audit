# NEW backpacks, lunch boxes & more summer gear→
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- I know this sender — Crate & Kids is the sub-brand off Crate & Barrel, and yes, I'm subscribed to the parent. But the second I open this thing I'm staring at backpacks and lunch boxes. I don't have a kid starting school this fall. I have two houses mid-reno and a very long list of things I actually need from Crate & Barrel proper — accent chairs, throw pillows, a credenza. This email is barking up the entirely wrong tree for me personally.
- That said, I can appreciate what they're doing. The layout is clean, the product photography is solid, and there's a coherent seasonal hook (summer → back to school pipeline). The colorful grid of lunch boxes and bags is actually pretty appealing visually — it's the kind of thing I'd have pinned to my kids' room board if I had one. The hero section with the yellow bag reads confident and brand-consistent.
- But I would not click. I'd probably archive it in my "catalog" folder out of habit, not interest. I wouldn't forward it either — none of my friends are in a "back to school" moment right now.

## What's working

- **Brand recognition lands immediately** — Crate & Kids clearly reads as the Crate & Barrel family, so I didn't mark it spam or feel like I was phished
- **The product grid is visually satisfying** — the multi-color lineup of backpacks and lunch boxes is well-shot and creates a genuine sense of variety without being chaotic
- **Seasonal coherence** — "summer gear" + backpacks is a real seasonal moment; they're not peddling a January clearance in May
- **Clean layout, no render bugs** — nothing overlapping, no broken images I can see, easy to skim vertically on mobile
- **The lower section appears to cross-sell into home** — there's what looks like a bedroom vignette lower in the email, which is the one moment that almost pulled me back in

## What's weak

- **Wrong persona, wrong moment** — I'm renovating two homes; I have zero interest in kids' backpacks right now and this email makes zero attempt to know that
- **No offer hook visible** — I don't see a clear discount or promotion percentage in the hero; it reads as a "new arrivals" announcement, which is low urgency for me
- **No loyalty or rewards signal** — I buy on rewards cards and track spend; if there's a Crate & Barrel member benefit attached to this, they buried it
- **The subject line arrow glyph (→) reads slightly try-hard** — minor, but as someone who tracks which brands do what, this feels like a tactic borrowed from DTC startups, not Crate & Barrel's usual register

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment harder** — anyone who hasn't browsed kids on the site or clicked a kids email in 90 days should be getting the Crate & Barrel home send, not Crate & Kids. This is a list hygiene / segmentation problem more than a creative one.
- 2. **Put an offer in the hero** — even "Free shipping on orders over $75" in the hero banner would give me a reason to click beyond "look at cute bags." New arrivals alone don't move Millennial men.
- 3. **Lead with the home crossover if one exists** — if that lower bedroom section is real product, bring it up. A "shop the kids' room" angle that includes furniture or bedding would have hooked me.
- 4. **Drop the glyph from the subject** — it adds length and reads like noise; use the character budget for specificity instead.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Back-to-school bags are here — new for summer`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New lunch boxes, backpacks & kids' room picks`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh colors, durable picks — free shipping on orders over $75`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The bags they'll actually want to carry. Shop new arrivals.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta restricts user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA) — preventing zoom is a known accessibility failure. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied globally**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Using `none` rather than `100%` can suppress iOS font scaling entirely, breaking text legibility on small displays.

**Conflicting image `max-width` media queries**
Two desktop breakpoints define `img` max-width with overlapping ranges:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
```
At ≥768px viewports the 600px rule wins, which is narrower than the 640px container — images may render smaller than intended on desktop.

**Duplicate `<meta>` declarations**
`format-detection` is declared four times (once consolidated, then three individual `date=no`, `address=no`, `telephone=no` entries). `charset` is declared twice (`http-equiv` and `<meta charset>`). No rendering breakage, but dead markup weight.

**`<title>` brand mismatch**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
Sender is `Crate & Kids`; the parent brand name in `<title>` will surface in some clients' tab/window chrome and screen reader announcements.
