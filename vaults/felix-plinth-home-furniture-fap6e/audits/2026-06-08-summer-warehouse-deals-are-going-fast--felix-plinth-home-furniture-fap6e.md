# Summer Warehouse deals are GOING FAST
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-08**
## Executive summary

- Six. That's where we are now. Six Pottery Barn emails in roughly 72 hours, all orbiting the same Summer Warehouse Sale creative. And I want to be precise about that, because the email itself — taken in isolation — is not bad. The hero is a warm, well-lit dining room that actually maps to what I'm trying to do in the second house. The "Recommended Just For You" grid below the fold is a real attempt at personalization. The Affirm block at the bottom is the kind of financing call-out I actually appreciate when I'm staring down a $1,400 sectional decision.
- But we are so deep into cadence fatigue at this point that the goodwill evaporates before the email fully loads. The subject line swapped in "GOING FAST" urgency — all caps — and that read exactly like what it is: a brand that's been running the same 60%-off message all week and is now turning up the pressure instead of the creativity. Wayfair did this to me with the outdoor rug subject four times in a row; I unsubscribed from that thread mentally. Pottery Barn is getting close.
- Would I click? Maybe the "Recommended Just For You" section if one of those product cards lands right — it's the first genuinely personalized module I've seen in this run. But I'm not clicking the hero. I've seen that hero. I know exactly where it goes. I'm marking this read and moving on.

## What's working

- **The personalized grid is real.** "Recommended Just For You" with specific product cards — what looks like a dining bench, a pendant, a bed frame — is the first signal this week that Pottery Barn knows something about me rather than just blasting a category.
- **Dining room hero maps to my actual project.** Warm neutrals, wood tones, linen-adjacent upholstery — this is exactly the palette I'm working toward in the second home. Strong category match even if the creative is stale.
- **Affirm placement makes sense.** Financing a floor-lamp-and-rug order doesn't make sense; financing a $900 dining table does. Putting it at the bottom where the consideration happens is the right call.
- **The $15 rewards call-out.** Small, but visible. I'm already working my rewards card math every time I buy furniture — seeing the loyalty layer acknowledged keeps me in the ecosystem.
- **Clean render, no layout breaks.** After four emails, if anything was going to fall apart it would have by now. It didn't.

## What's weak

- **Same hero. Again.** The navy-then-dining-room creative has rotated so many times this week I could describe it without opening the email. That's a trust tax — at some point I start assuming the sale isn't actually moving merchandise.
- **"GOING FAST" is empty pressure.** No deadline, no inventory counter, no specific item callout. Just vibes urgency in all caps. Article's outdoor email last week gave me a specific "limited quantities" call-out on a sectional SKU I was watching — that's how you manufacture urgency that lands.
- **Design Services block is on autopilot.** Same "Free Design Services / Any project big or small / BOOK NOW" block that appears in every single Pottery Barn email. At this point it's visual filler.
- **No specific end date on the warehouse sale.** Crate & Barrel's kitchen sale email — the one that scored an 8 — had a clear "ends Sunday" line above the fold. This has nothing. "Going fast" without a date is a non-event.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the hero or change it entirely.** If you're sending email six of a warehouse sale, the creative has to show something new — a new room, a new category, a new product story. The dining room has done its job. Move to the bedroom, the outdoor space, or lead with a specific bestseller.
- 2. **Put a deadline in the email.** One line. "Sale ends Sunday, June 15." Suddenly the subject line urgency is real.
- 3. **Elevate the personalized grid above the fold.** "Recommended Just For You" is the most compelling module in this email and it's buried below the category nav. Swap the positions — lead with the personal shelf, follow with the category grid.
- 4. **Retire the Design Services block or make it contextual.** Tie it to whatever's in the personalized grid: "Love the dining look above? Book a free session to pull the room together." Right now it's just a standing fixture.
- 5. **Drop the App Store badge from a sale email.** Wrong moment for app acquisition. Save that for a post-purchase or onboarding flow.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Warehouse prices on the dining pieces you've been eyeing`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `60% off through June 15 — the dining bench you viewed is still available`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your recommended picks are in the sale. Free shipping on orders $99+`

**WARN — Open pixel served over HTTP**
`http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=...`
Open-tracking pixel uses HTTP. Gmail and Apple Mail proxy all images over HTTPS; an HTTP pixel may be blocked or silently upgraded, causing open rate discrepancies.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP `header()` calls inside `<style>` block**
The following literal PHP code appears inside a CSS `<style>` tag — it is syntactically invalid CSS and serves no function in an HTML email:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
This is a copy-paste artifact from a server-side image-serving script. It should be removed.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` — some email clients display the title tag in the tab or preview pane. Should be populated or omitted.

**3 images served over HTTP (mixed content)**
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Gmail's image proxy and Apple Mail will either block or attempt HTTPS upgrades on these. The 2022 path on the third URL suggests a stale asset reference.

**40+ images missing `alt` text**
Every content slice (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), all product images, footer images, and tracking pixels lack `alt` attributes. Subscribers with images blocked see a fully blank email. Fails WCAG 2.1 §1.1.1 (screen reader users receive no content).

Affected examples:
- `slices_01.jpg` through `slices_12.jpg` — primary content area
- `standard-curtain-rod-wall-bracket-l.jpg`, `farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `hydrocotton-quick-dry-towel-l.jpg` — product images
- `S26_Bottom_UpTo50OffOutdoor.jpg`, `W26_Bottom_GeneralDCBottomBanner.jpg` — promotional banners
