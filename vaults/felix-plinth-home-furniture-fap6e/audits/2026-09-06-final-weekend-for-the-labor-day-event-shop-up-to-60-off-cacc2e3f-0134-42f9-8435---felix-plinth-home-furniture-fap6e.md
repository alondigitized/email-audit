- That said, this is basically the same skeleton I just reviewed from Pottery Barn's "BONUS deals" send — hero banner, category tile grid, "we love these for you" personalized strip, app download pitch, social icons, footer nav — just recolored navy instead of burgundy. ## What's working

- The hero photo (coastal bluff, sunset) reads premium and adult — no toddler gear in sight, unlike every Crate & Kids email today.
- "Final weekend! Up to 60% off The Labor Day Event" is genuine deadline urgency, and it's specific about the timing (through 9/8) down in the Rewards banner.
- Category grid covers real reno categories I care about — rugs, outdoor furniture, bedding, sofas — not a single generic "shop now."
- The "we love these for you" section with actual SKUs (Asante dresser, Bargas dining table, Barrett sofa) and prices is the personalization touch Pottery Barn's email skipped entirely.
- Design Desk / free designer consultation callout is a real value-add for someone renovating two houses — that's a feature I'd actually use.

## What's weak

- Rewards banner ("Double Rewards is on! Earn 10% back...") is visually identical in tone and placement to what Crate & Kids has hit me with four times today — brand-family fatigue is real and this doesn't dodge it.
- Grid tiles all say vague ranges ("up to 40% off," "up to 60% off") with no single hero product or price called out until you're deep into the email — CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS" send at least led with a bolder single statement.
- Two nearly-identical CTA blocks for the same Rewards card offer (one after the grid, one lower down) — that's redundant real estate that could've shown me more product.
- Footer is a wall of legal text and nav links that pushes the actual unsubscribe/account info way down — not a dealbreaker but it's dense for a mobile scroll.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the duplicate Rewards CTA block — one mention up top is enough; use the reclaimed space to push the rugs or outdoor furniture category higher, since those are the two collections most people renovating right now will actually filter by.
- 2. Put a real number in the hero instead of "up to 60% off" — Article's "Up to 40% off ends Monday" felt more honest than a vague ceiling percentage; give me the rug or sofa category's actual floor discount.
- 3. Move "TheDesignDesk" free $500 sweepstakes higher — that's genuinely differentiated content none of my other reno-catalog emails today are running, and it's buried mid-email.
