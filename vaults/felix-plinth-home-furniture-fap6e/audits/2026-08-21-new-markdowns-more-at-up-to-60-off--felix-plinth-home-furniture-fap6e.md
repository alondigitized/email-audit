# NEW markdowns! More at up to 60% off →
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-21**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids in my inbox — again — and this time it's "NEW markdowns! More at up to 60% off." My kid's not a baby anymore, we don't need a nursery glider or a Nuna stroller travel system, so most of this scrolls right past me. That said, "up to 60% off" is a real number stamped right up top, not buried, and the daisy hero photo at least looks like summer clearance instead of another staged nursery shoot. But five screen-lengths in I'm hitting backpack bundles, a $5,000 designer sweepstakes, a new store credit card pitch, and an app download nudge — this reads like four different emails stapled together, not one clean send.
- This is the third-plus Crate & Kids/Crate & Barrel-family email I've logged this week (the bedroom-refresh one scored 8, the recliner-sofa one scored 5), and my Crate & Barrel fatigue is now bleeding into Crate & Kids too. Compare that to Article's home office send I logged — one room, one story, no clutter. This one's trying to sell clearance, backpacks, a sweepstakes, and a credit card in a single scroll, and none of it lands as hard as it should.
- Verdict: I'm not clicking. This isn't my life stage (no baby gear, no kids backpacks), and even if it were, the email's too scattered to pull me toward one CTA. Marking as read, not forwarding — nothing here is worth sending to a friend.

## What's working

- "Up to 60% off + new markdowns" is a real, specific offer sitting right at the top — no scavenger hunt needed like Wayfair's "FOUND: Bar Stools!" disaster where I had to dig for actual product.
- The category nav strip (Furniture, Rugs, Kids Bedding, Baby Bedding, Decor & Lighting, Final Sale) is clean and scannable — no overlapping text, no broken images.
- Product grid under "we love these for you" has real prices ($54, $1300, $1499, $899, $1450, $159) — Crate & Barrel's bedroom email did this well too, and it's consistently their strongest move.
- No render bugs anywhere in this — everything loaded, nothing's stretched or cut off, which honestly beats Wayfair's broken-image mess.

## What's weak

- This isn't targeted at me at all — backpack bundles, baby cribs, nursery gliders, strollers. I'm a 36-year-old dude renovating two houses, not shopping for a newborn.
- Way too many competing asks in one email: clearance sale, backpack bundles, $5,000 designer sweepstakes, new credit card, app download. Pick one.
- The "TheDesignDesk" $5,000 sweepstakes section feels like a bait to get me to book a design appointment — buried mid-scroll where I'd have already checked out.
- Product recommendations (Nuna car seat, baby glider, baby dresser) show zero personalization to an actual bedroom-and-second-home renovation shopper like me — this is generic new-parent inventory, not "we love these for you."
- Sender name "Crate & Kids SALE" in all caps in the from-line reads spammy before I've even opened it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into two emails — a clean clearance/markdown send and a separate backpack-bundle/back-to-school send. Stapling four campaigns together dilutes all of them.
- 2. Cut the credit card and app-download blocks entirely from a clearance email — those belong in a dedicated lifecycle send, not fighting for space with 60% off.
- 3. If this list is going to non-parent subscribers like me, segment it — a renovating-second-home Crate & Barrel shopper shouldn't get Crate & Kids nursery product at all.
- 4. Move the $5,000 designer sweepstakes up near the top if it's meant to drive appointments — right now it's dead weight in the middle of a sale email.
