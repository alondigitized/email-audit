# NEW JUST IN
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- This one drops on June 30 and I'm at roughly forty-eight CB2 and Crate family emails for the month — yesterday's "SALE OF THE SUMMER" is still sitting unread two spots up in my inbox. So "NEW JUST IN" in the subject line is a particular kind of audacity. But I'll give CB2 this: when I opened it, the Art Deco Rug and the French Lou Pendant stopped my scroll. Those are exactly the kinds of pieces I'm hunting for the second home's living room, and the July 4th 50% off banner at the very top of the email means I'm doing rewards-card math before I even hit the product grid.
- The problem is this email is trying to do two jobs at once and executes neither fully. "OF THE MOMENT" editorial energy up top, July 4th sale banner also up top — which campaign is this, exactly? Are these new arrivals included in the 50% off? If yes, that's a genuinely compelling email. If no, why is the sale banner the first thing I see? I shouldn't need to click through to figure out what you're actually selling me today.
- I'd screenshot the Terracotta Vase for my mood board folder, stare at the Art Deco Rug for thirty seconds, and then close without clicking — unless I could confirm the new pieces are actually in the sale. Forward to a friend? No. Mark as read? Probably.

## What's working

- **50% off July 4th banner is front and center** — clear number, credible time peg, doesn't make me dig for the offer.
- **The Art Deco Rug and French Lou Pendant are legitimate gets** — these are renovation-purchase-level pieces, not filler, and CB2 gets credit for putting them in an editorial format rather than a discount grid.
- **The "FOR YOU" personalized section at the bottom** shows CB2 has my browse history — this is several rungs above Wayfair's "⭐⭐⭐ UP TO 70% OFF" spray-and-pray.
- **Dark editorial photography is consistent and on-brand** — it doesn't look like a flash-sale flyer even while running 50% off, which is hard to do.
- **Terracotta Vase shot is exactly the kind of object-interest still I'd screenshot** for a mood board.

## What's weak

- **"NEW JUST IN" after three consecutive SALE urgency emails is tonal whiplash** — June 28's "WAREHOUSE SALE," June 29's "SALE OF THE SUMMER," now this. CB2 can't decide if this week is a clearance event or a editorial moment and I'm paying the price.
- **No prices visible on any of the product shots** — for someone who buys on rewards-card math, I need to see whether 50% off the Art Deco Rug means $300 or $1,800 before I commit a click.
- **Two campaigns running simultaneously with no hierarchy** — is the July 4th sale the point, or are these new arrivals the point? Article's "4th of July Sale starts now" from the same day made a single clean choice and it landed better.
- **Cadence fatigue is genuinely real** — four CB2/Crate emails in four days has trained my thumb to skip without guilt.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick a lane, then lead with it in the subject.** If the new arrivals are 50% off for July 4th, that's a genuinely powerful subject. If they're not, pull the sale banner and commit to the editorial format. The split is killing both angles.
- 2. **Show sale pricing inline on the product editorial shots.** Even "Was $X / Now $Y" in small type under each product name converts a scroll-past into a click for a buyer running rewards-card math in their head.
- 3. **Give one product the full hero treatment.** The Art Deco Rug with a room-scale image, real dimensions, and a price point could carry this email alone. Four named products splits attention and earns zero clicks.
- 4. **Rest the cadence.** One fewer send this week would have made this one land harder.
- **Subject Alt A:** `July 4th: 50% off the new arrivals`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Art Deco Rug. Now 50% off.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New pieces just landed — and they're included in the sale.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `French Lou Pendant, Art Deco Rug, Terracotta Vase — all on sale through the 4th.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom** (WCAG 1.4.4 failure):
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Users with low vision cannot zoom. Change to `user-scalable=yes` or remove `maximum-scale`.

**Universal `text-size-adjust:none` is too broad:**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;-ms-text-size-adjust:none; ... }
```
Applying this to the `*` selector suppresses OS-level font scaling across all elements. Scope it to `body` only.

**Template comment artifact left in production:**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
Typo ("IMPUT") and unfilled placeholder shipped to subscribers. Should be removed in the build process.

**Mobile breakpoint mismatch:** Media query fires at `max-width: 418px` and sets `.mobile { width: 414px }`. iPhone 15/16 Pro Max logical width is 430px — those devices will not trigger the mobile layout. Recommend `max-width: 430px` minimum.

**Malformed double-slash font URLs** (cosmetic, but technically invalid):
```css
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
```
Three `@font-face` declarations contain `//` path segments. HTTP servers normalize these, but CDN caching rules or strict URL matchers may not.

**Triple `@font-face` for "Lineto Brown Sub"** — the same family is declared three times with overlapping `src:` lists and conflicting weight assignments. The first declaration has no `font-weight` scoping, causing the browser to use unpredictable cascade order.

**`<title>` does not match subject line:**
- `<title>`: `CB2, FURNITURE AND DECOR FOR THE MODERN HOME`
- Subject: `NEW JUST IN`

Tab/window title shown in some webmail clients will not reflect the campaign.
