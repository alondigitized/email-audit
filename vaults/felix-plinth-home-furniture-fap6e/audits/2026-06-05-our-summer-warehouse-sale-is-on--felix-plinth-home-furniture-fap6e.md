# Our Summer Warehouse Sale is ON
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- Fourteen. I don't say that to be dramatic — I say it because it's the number in my head when this hit the inbox, and it's the number that almost made me archive it on reflex. But "Summer Warehouse Sale is ON" stopped me, because warehouse sales are the one legitimate Pottery Barn email category. I've been circling a sofa for the second house for six weeks and 60% off changes the math, so I opened it.
- What I found was genuinely the most useful Pottery Barn send in this whole two-day deluge. The hero is clean, the category grid is actually functional navigation for someone like me who's shopping Furniture, Rugs, and Outdoor simultaneously, and the "Recommended Just For You" module is the first real signal this brand knows who I am. Bed frames and what looks like a dresser in the thumbnails — things that could actually be on my list. That matters.
- But the email has structural problems that cost it. No end date anywhere. The Affirm block and the app download section are pure bloat sitting between me and the products. And the recommended items are so small I can't read a name or a price without zooming. I'd click — reluctantly, with the specific intent of finding the sofa. I would not forward it. I'd text a link if I found something.

## What's working

- The "Up to 60% off" headline is in the hero, full stop — no hunt required, no percentage buried below the fold like Wayfair tends to do with its secondary discount callouts
- The category grid (Furniture / Outdoor / Sofas & Chairs / Bedding / Rugs...) is real wayfinding for a multi-project shopper — this beats C&B's "NEW MARKDOWNS → " single-CTA approach for navigability
- "Recommended Just For You" with product thumbnails is the first personalization signal I've seen in fourteen emails — whoever seeded those modules with bedroom and case goods should get credit
- "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" is visible and positioned before the footer — as someone who runs rewards math on every purchase, this is information I actually use
- Summer Warehouse Sale is a credible seasonal event with a clear name — not a phantom "savings event" like the earlier Pottery Barn sends this week

## What's weak

- No end date anywhere — Crate & Barrel's "Up to 60% off →" send at least gave me a sense of window; this sale is just... on, indefinitely, somehow
- The Affirm financing block and app download section eat a full viewport of scroll between the category grid and the recommended products — I'm not financing a throw pillow
- "SHOP NOW" is the laziest CTA in the industry; even CB2's "Shop the Sale" is one notch more intentional
- The "Recommended Just For You" thumbnails are the most valuable module in the email and they're rendering at a size where I can't read a product name or a price without zooming — that's a real miss
- Fourteenth email in roughly 48 hours; I'm noting it, and the goodwill this send earns is partially offset by the volume debt the brand has accumulated

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard end date to the hero.** "Up to 60% off — ends June 9" requires zero creative effort and adds real urgency. This is the highest-lift change available to any marketer on this email right now.
- 2. **Enlarge the "Recommended Just For You" cards and show price.** If you're going to personalize, actually personalize — display the product name, the sale price, and the original price at readable scale. That module is doing the most important work in the email and it's being treated as a footnote.
- 3. **Cut the Affirm block and the app download section from this position.** Move them below the fold or to the footer. They belong at checkout, not between the customer and the product content.
- 4. **Rewrite the primary CTA.** "Shop Warehouse Sale" or "Shop the Sale" is minimal effort, meaningfully more specific, and performs better in every test I've ever read.
- 5. **Differentiate the two CTAs visually.** "SHOP NOW" in the hero and "SHOP ALL SALE" in the category section look nearly identical — they compete instead of directing.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Summer Warehouse Sale: up to 60% off furniture & rugs`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Warehouse prices are live — 60% off ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sofas, outdoor, lighting — shop before it ends June 9`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Members earn 10% back. Warehouse Sale runs through the weekend.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CRITICAL] PHP header() calls embedded in `<style>` block**

The following raw server-side code appears verbatim inside a `<style>` tag near the end of `<head>`:

```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```

These are PHP function calls, not CSS. They indicate a template rendering failure — a dynamic image-caching snippet was not executed server-side and its output was written into the HTML as literal text instead. Email clients parse this as invalid CSS and ignore it, but it confirms the template did not compile cleanly.
