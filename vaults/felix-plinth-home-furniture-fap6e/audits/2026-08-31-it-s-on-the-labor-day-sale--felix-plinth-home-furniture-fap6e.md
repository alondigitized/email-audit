## Executive summary

- Okay, "Up to 70% off" in a big burgundy hero banner — that's the most aggressive number I've seen from Pottery Barn all week, and it lands right where my eye goes first. This is a real category grid too: furniture, bedding, sofas, pillows, outdoor, all with their own discount percentage instead of one blanket "everything's on sale" lie. That's honest, and after yesterday's zero-discount lookbook, this is the email I actually wanted from them.
- The "Recommended Just For You" block below is the real hook for me — a Toscana extendable dining table and a Farmhouse platform bed are legitimately on my punch list for one of the two houses. That's the first time this week a brand's algorithm actually pointed at something I'm shopping for instead of just cross-selling candle holders.
- I'd click into Furniture or straight into that dining table. I wouldn't forward this to anyone — it's a sale email, not a "look at this cool thing" email — but I'd mark it as a keeper in my Pinterest-adjacent folder for when I sit down to actually compare quotes across Crate & Barrel, Article, and Wayfair.

## What's working

- The 70% off hero is unmissable and matches the subject line exactly — no bait-and-switch like some of these sends.
- Per-category discount grid (Furniture, Bedding, Sofas & Chairs, Pillows & Decor, Outdoor) lets me skip straight to what I need instead of scrolling a generic "shop the sale" wall.
- "Recommended Just For You" surfaced a dining table and platform bed that are actually relevant to my renovation — better personalization than Wayfair or CB2 have managed lately.
- "Ships Free" badge on the quilt tile is a small but real trust cue for furniture-adjacent categories where shipping cost is always the hidden catch.
- Rewards callout ("Earn 10% Back in Rewards... credit card") speaks directly to my highest-rewards-card habit.

## What's weak

- The product grid images are tiny and low-contrast against each other — on a phone I'd have to squint to tell the Whitaker chair from the Merci candle holders thumbnail.
- "Sofas & Chairs" caps at only 30% off buried in the middle of a grid that's otherwise screaming 60-70% — feels like a bait number up top that doesn't hold for the category I might actually want.
- The Halloween Shop insert mid-email is a jarring context switch — I'm mid-scroll comparing dining tables and suddenly there's a haunted porch. Save that for its own send.
- No fine print or end-date visible near the hero — "Labor Day Sale" implies urgency but there's no countdown or "ends Monday" anywhere I can see, so it doesn't feel as time-bound as Crate & Barrel's "Don't wait" send did.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put an explicit end date/time next to "Up to 70% off" in the hero — Article's Labor Day email at least implied urgency; this one doesn't even try, and Labor Day sales without a deadline just read as "sale."
- 2. Move "Recommended Just For You" up above the generic category grid — that's the section actually earning a click from someone like me, don't bury my dining table five screens down.
- 3. Cut the Halloween Shop insert out of this email entirely; it breaks the sale narrative and belongs in its own themed send.
- 4. Bump the Sofas & Chairs tile discount or drop it from the top-line if it's only 30% — don't let one weak category deflate the 70% headline once someone scrolls to it.

# Technical Audit: Pottery Barn "Labor Day Sale" Email

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a legacy MSO/VML-structured HTML template with heavy tracking instrumentation (57 tracking links, multiple pixels/beacons) but no automated compliance headers detected and near-total absence of image alt text.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 57 tracking/click-redirect links (e.g., `click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=...`) were skipped by automated HTTP probing since they are redirect wrappers — not independently verified as resolving to live destinations.
- Multiple parallel tracking pixels present: `svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=...` (open tracking), `d.turn.com/r/dd/id/...` (third-party pixel, likely Rocket Fuel/Xaxis), `clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/704772d3-...?mi_mid=...` (Movable Ink personalization tracker). Three separate tracking vendors firing on open is worth flagging for load/privacy review, though not a functional defect.
- Two image sources use plain `http://` instead of `https://`:
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
  Mixed-content images can be blocked or flagged by mail clients enforcing HTTPS-only image loading.
- Recipient email address is embedded in cleartext in multiple URLs (e.g., `coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`, `sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`). Standard ESP practice but flagged since it travels over an unencrypted `http://` link in the `svopn.potterybarn.com` case — PII sent in the clear.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- Near-universal missing `alt` attributes across the sample: all `slices_NN.jpg` divider/spacer images, hero images (`slices_01.jpg`, `slices_02.jpg`), product images (farmhouse-platform-bed, dining tables, swivel chair, rug), and footer banners. Screen readers and image-blocked clients (default in Outlook) will render this email with no textual fallback for any visual content.
- Tracking pixels and 1x1 beacons (`d.turn.com`, `svopn.potterybarn.com`, `clickcp.potterybarn.com`) are also flagged as "missing alt text" — expected/non-issue for invisible tracking pixels, no action needed there.
- Template uses legacy table-based layout with MSO conditional comments and `x-apple-data-detectors` overrides — standard for broad client compatibility, no defects observed in the truncated source.
- A stray `<style>` block contains raw PHP-style `header(...)` calls (`header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: no-store...")`) sitting inside a CSS `<style>` tag. This is inert (browsers/mail clients will ignore it as invalid CSS) but indicates leftover/misplaced server-side code accidentally pasted into the template — a template-hygiene defect worth cleanup even though it doesn't break rendering.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
