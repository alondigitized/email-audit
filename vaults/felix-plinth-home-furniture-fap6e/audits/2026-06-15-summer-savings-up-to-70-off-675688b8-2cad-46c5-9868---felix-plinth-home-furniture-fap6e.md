# Summer savings UP TO 70% OFF 🌻
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- Twenty. I'm leading with the count again because I said the same thing at email nineteen and apparently nothing stuck. The June 14 evening send — "Up to 70% off is ON + Lighting to make your space feel magical" — was already nudging me toward the unsubscribe link. This one is functionally the same send with a different lifestyle photo. And that photo is a blue floral bedding spread. I'm not shopping bedding. I am currently trying to source outdoor furniture for a second home and figure out a sofa configuration for the primary. The hero is a complete whiff on where my head is.
- The category grid underneath (FURNITURE, OUTDOOR, SOFAS & CHAIRS, BEDDING, DECOR...) is actually efficient and exactly what I need. But I've already mentally moved on by the time I get there. The "Recommended Just For You" block is the most valuable real estate in this email — browsing-behavior-backed product suggestions — and it's buried behind the Affirm callout and the Design Service block. That's a structural problem that compounds the cadence one.
- I'd mark this read without clicking. The Free Design Service and the 10% Rewards reminder keep it from being a waste — those are two things neither Wayfair nor Article offers — but "UP TO 70% OFF" has run in so many consecutive subject lines it stopped registering as a discount and started registering as background noise.

## What's working

- **"100s of new deals added!"** as first-line body copy (likely the preview text) adds real information the subject doesn't — subject gives the how-much, preview signals inventory movement. That's complements, not repetition.
- **Category navigation grid** (FURNITURE, OUTDOOR, SOFAS & CHAIRS, BEDDING, DECOR, PILLOWS & THROWS, WALL ART, BATH) is clean and scannable — eight pathways, no copy required to self-route.
- **Free Design Service** block is a genuine differentiator. Crate & Barrel doesn't surface this in their promo sends, and Wayfair absolutely doesn't have the service. It earns its real estate.
- **Earn 10% Back in Rewards** footer reminder is always useful — it's the reason I pick Pottery Barn over CB2 on equivalent products, and keeping it visible makes the send worth receiving.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no text overlap, no empty merge fields at the size I'm viewing.

## What's weak

- **Floral bedding hero** is pretty and totally wrong for me — there's nothing in it that signals outdoor furniture, living room renovation, or anything currently in my cart. Demographic mismatch on top of category mismatch.
- **"SHOP ALL SALE" CTA** has now appeared so many consecutive times from this sender that it has ceased to function as a call to action. It's furniture.
- **No deadline anywhere** — Article's "Summer hosting made simple" earned an 8 from me in part because the editorial urgency was baked in. This email treats 70% off as a permanent condition with no end date, which is exactly what makes it feel like the permanent condition.
- **"Recommended Just For You" is buried** — the highest-intent, most personalized section is three folds down. That section should be leading.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rotate the hero to match browse behavior** — if my history shows outdoor seating and sofas, that's the hero. Article ran "Summer hosting made simple" with a single outdoor table shot and I clicked immediately. Pottery Barn has the product; it's choosing the wrong one to lead with.
- 2. **Put a hard deadline on the offer** — "Sale ends Sunday" or "Ends June 22" in the subject and repeated in the hero. CB2's "100+ new items added to Warehouse Sale" got an 8 from me partly because the framing implied scarcity. No deadline kills urgency completely.
- 3. **Pull "Recommended Just For You" up to fold two** — directly after the hero, before the category grid. That's where the intent is highest and the conversion opportunity is real.
- 4. **Retire "SHOP ALL SALE"** — swap it for "Shop Outdoor" or "Shop My Saves" tied to whatever browse session drove the recommendations. Something specific with a destination.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your saved outdoor picks are in the sale — ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New drops hit the sale: furniture, outdoor, bedding`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The sofa and deck furniture you've been watching just dropped.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `100s of new items added — see what's new in your saved categories`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CRITICAL — PHP `header()` calls embedded inside a CSS `<style>` block**
The final `<style>` tag in `<head>` contains:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
This is server-side PHP that was not executed — it was passed through raw into the HTML output. Several email clients (notably Outlook and some Gmail views) will render unknown CSS text as visible content. This is a template build/rendering pipeline defect.

**HIGH — 43 images missing `alt` text**

### 5. Compliance

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
Not captured by AgentMail relay. If genuinely missing from the envelope, this violates RFC 2369 and CAN-SPAM best practices. Gmail and Yahoo enforced this header as a hard requirement for bulk senders (>5k/day) starting February 2024.

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent**
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe. Same Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender mandate. Without this, Gmail shows a manual unsubscribe flow that increases friction and spam complaints.

**WARN — `Authentication-Results` header not found**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status is unknown via the AgentMail relay. If the relay strips these headers, the underlying authentication cannot be confirmed. Sending domain is `e.potterybarn.com` — should be verifiable via direct MX/SPF lookup against that domain.

**INFO — `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />`**
This suppresses HTTP `Referer` headers on all outbound link clicks from webmail clients that render the email in a browser context. Any analytics that rely on referrer (rather than UTM params) will show traffic as direct.
