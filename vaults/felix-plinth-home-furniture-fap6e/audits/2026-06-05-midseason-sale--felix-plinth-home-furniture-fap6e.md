# MIDSEASON SALE
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- Twenty. That's where we are with CB2 since mid-May, and this one lands on the same day as "A well-collected home office" — so I've already processed one CB2 this morning before this one hit. The goodwill they built with the Todd Snyder collab a couple days ago has been systematically drained.
- What I actually see: clean bedroom hero in warm terracotta and ivory neutrals, a prominent MIDSEASON SALE headline, a "SHOP NOW" button. For a split second my two-renovation brain lit up — I genuinely need bedroom furniture for the second property. But then the interior design services block shows up mid-scroll with a "$1,000" threshold and a "BOOK YOUR FREE APPOINTMENT" CTA, and the email completely loses its narrative. Which pitch is this? Sale email or design-services lead gen? Pick a lane.
- The bigger problem is the hero is doing no real work. Pottery Barn's email yesterday led with "up to 60% off" in a warehouse banner at the very top — I knew exactly what I was walking into. CB2 is asking me to infer what "MIDSEASON SALE" means in actual dollars. I'll grudgingly click because I do need pieces right now, but I'm not forwarding this. On a day where I've already had one CB2 in my inbox, this gets a skim-and-close from me more often than not.

## What's working

- **Bedroom hero is category-correct.** Warm neutrals, layered textiles, a bed I'd actually consider — hits the second-home sourcing project at exactly the right moment.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean.** MIDSEASON SALE reads immediately above the hero; my eye doesn't have to hunt for what this email is about.
- **"SHOP NOW" button is unambiguous.** Single verb, single action, no confusion.
- **"MORE FOR YOU" product grid.** Individual pieces are visible — a sofa, benches, side tables — which gives me something to evaluate without committing to the full browse. More brands should do this.
- **App store links and credit-card promo in the footer.** Acknowledges I have a relationship with this brand beyond the one email.

## What's weak

- **No discount percentage in the hero.** "MIDSEASON SALE" with zero numbers is the same as Wayfair screaming "BATH MATS You'll Love!!" — I'm doing the math, which means I might not bother. Article and Pottery Barn both lead with the number. CB2 is making me earn it.
- **Interior design services block kills the momentum.** A "$1,000" spend threshold and a "BOOK YOUR FREE APPOINTMENT" CTA in the middle of a sale email is a completely different conversion objective dropped in mid-stream.
- **ALL CAPS subject line.** "MIDSEASON SALE" in the inbox looks like a subject line from a brand I don't trust. CB2 is better than this — "JUST DROPPED: CB2 x Todd Snyder" landed without screaming and scored higher.
- **Second CB2 email today.** This isn't a visual bug I'm attributing to the email itself, but it's the context I'm actually opening it in. Cadence is a feature of the email experience and twenty sends since mid-May is not a feature.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the discount number in the hero.** "Up to 40% off" or whatever the actual markdown is — one number, in the hero, before the fold. Pottery Barn does this instinctively; CB2 keeps hiding the ball.
- 2. **Cut the design services block or move it to a standalone send.** Free design appointments are actually interesting to someone renovating two homes, but not buried halfway through a sale email. It deserves its own moment — or at minimum its own section at the very bottom after the sale is fully established.
- 3. **Lowercase the subject line.** Consistent with how the Todd Snyder and "new collab" sends landed — those felt premium. This one feels like a clearance rack.
- 4. **Tighten the "MORE FOR YOU" grid with category labels.** If those products are actually personalized to my browsing, say so. "Based on what you've been saving" earns a different kind of attention than a generic product grid.

## Technical Audit — CB2 "MIDSEASON SALE"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional table-based email with responsive media queries. Three infrastructure-level issues confirmed from source: double-slash font URLs causing redirect overhead, a live template placeholder comment, and cross-brand font origin dependency.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Double-slash URLs in `@font-face` declarations** — three font requests will hit a 301 redirect before resolving:
```
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2
```
Each redirect adds ~50–150ms latency on initial render. Single-slash paths in the three preceding `@font-face` blocks (same stylesheet) are correct — this is inconsistent within the same `<style>` block.

**UTM parameters** — HTML is truncated before CTA links appear; UTM coverage could not be verified.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Unremoved template placeholder comment** (production defect):
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
This is a developer scaffolding comment with a typo ("IMPUT" vs "INPUT") that shipped in production. No functional impact, but exposes internal tooling artifacts and indicates a QA gap.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — prevents user-initiated zoom:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Low-vision users who rely on pinch-zoom are affected.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied via universal selector**:
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```
The `none` value (rather than `100%`) suppresses OS-level font scaling. Combined with `max-height: 1000000px` on every element, this is an unusually aggressive reset that risks overriding accessibility accommodations set by the user's device.

**Mobile breakpoint at `max-width: 418px`** — non-standard threshold. Devices with viewport widths of 419–479px (e.g., iPhone SE landscape, some Android mid-range) will not receive mobile-optimized layout styles. Standard breakpoint is 480px.

**Cross-brand font origin dependency** — four `@font-face` declarations load from `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/`:
```css
src: url("https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/1e07d600-…woff2")
```
If Crate & Barrel's CDN or the `/webfonts/` path changes, CB2 emails silently degrade to fallback fonts. This is an infrastructure coupling risk, not a rendering issue today.

**Duplicate `@font-face` family name without weight specifiers** — "Lineto Brown Sub" is declared four times. The first declaration has no `font-weight` or `font-style` descriptor, which means it acts as the default catch-all and may shadow the weighted variants in non-Outlook clients.
