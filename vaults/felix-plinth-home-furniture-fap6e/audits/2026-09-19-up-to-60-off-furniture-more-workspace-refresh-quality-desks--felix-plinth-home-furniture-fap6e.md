# Up to 60% off furniture & more + Workspace refresh: Quality desks
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-19**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one actually lands — "Workspace Refresh" with a real hero shot of that Pacific Mini Writing Desk against a plaster-pink wall, and it goes straight into desk collections. I'm mid-renovation on the second house's office nook right now, so a desk email isn't noise, it's timing. Three collections deep — Pacific, Aubrey, Marina — each with a lifestyle shot and named SKUs, that's the kind of specificity I actually want when I'm comparison-shopping wood tones across two houses.
- Where it loses me is length. This is a genuinely long scroll — three desk collections, then "More Workspace Updates," then a "Recommended Just For You" grid that's rugs and curtains and dining tables, completely off the desk thesis, then New Arrivals, then the Halloween Shop. By the time I hit Halloween decor I've forgotten this was a desk email. Article's "Storage solutions for every room" send stayed tighter and more focused on the actual problem I'm solving; this one out-executes Article on hero quality but then sprawls past where it should've stopped.
- I'd click into the Pacific Collection — that hero image did its job. I wouldn't forward it, and I'd scroll-fatigue out before the Halloween Shop banner.

## What's working

- The "Workspace Refresh" hero with the Pacific Mini Writing Desk is a real, styled lifestyle shot — not a stock render — and it's exactly my current project (second-house office).
- Three named desk collections (Pacific, Aubrey, Marina) each get its own room shot plus individual SKU thumbnails and prices-implied labels — that's the product-detail level I need to compare wood tone and footprint.
- The "Design Tip: Style a beautiful desk with small proportions as an entryway or side table" callout is a smart secondary use-case nudge — shows they're thinking about small-space buyers, not just home offices.
- "Up to 60% off" in the top banner is a real, visible number, not buried.

## What's weak

- The email keeps going long after the desk story is told — rugs, curtains, dining tables, and a Halloween Shop banner all crammed into what should've been a focused desk-refresh send.
- "Recommended Just For You" is generic cross-sell, not personalized to workspace shopping — a curtain rod and a dining table don't belong in a desk email.
- No pricing shown anywhere on the actual desk SKUs — I have to click through to find out if "up to 60% off" applies to the Aubrey Executive or not.
- The rewards/Affirm/app-download stack at the bottom is fine as a footer but it's competing with three other CTAs by the time I get there.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after the Marina Collection — end on "Shop All Desks" and drop the curtain/rug/dining table grid entirely; let the desk story breathe instead of diluting it.
- 2. Put an actual price or % off directly on each desk SKU tile (like Wayfair does, for better or worse) instead of making me click three times to find out if Aubrey is part of the 60% sale.
- 3. Move the Design Tip earlier, right under the hero — it's a good hook and currently gets buried between two desk collections.
- 4. Drop the Halloween Shop banner from this send — cross-seasonal whiplash right after a workspace-focused email undercuts the message.

## Technical Audit

1. Technical Summary
Standard Bronto/EDM-generated Pottery Barn HTML with typical tracking-heavy structure; QA flags missing header-level unsubscribe compliance signals, two `http://` image sources, and near-total absence of `alt` attributes across content and tracking-pixel images.

2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 62 tracking/click-redirect links present (`click.e.potterybarn.com`, `clickcp.potterybarn.com`, `d.turn.com/r/dd/...`), all skipped for HTTP probing per QA — link destinations not verified as reachable.
- Open-tracking pixel served over plaintext HTTP: `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` — recipient email address is exposed in a cleartext URL query string, sent over an unencrypted connection.
- Secondary open-tracking/analytics pixel: `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/3c2741b2-916a-46d3-910c-9327276bf423?mi_cid=...&mi_mid=...`.

3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 0 of the ~50+ `<img>` elements carry `alt` text (QA: 50+ "missing alt text" warnings) — includes hero banners, product slices, and footer social/legal icons. This breaks screen-reader accessibility and leaves the layout content-less when images are blocked.
- Two images load over `http://` instead of `https://`: `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` and `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` — mixed content may be blocked or flagged by mail clients enforcing HTTPS.
- Table-based layout with MSO conditional comments and legacy DOCTYPE (XHTML 1.0 Transitional) — consistent with intentional Outlook/legacy-client targeting, no issues.
- `<style>` block contains stray PHP-style `header(...)` calls (`header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); ...`) embedded as literal CSS text inside a `<style>` tag — these are inert (not executable, just dead text in the CSS), but indicate a template artifact/leak from a PHP asset script that should not be in production email markup.

4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- Recipient email address appears merged in plaintext in multiple tracking URLs (e.g., `sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`, `coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`) rather than a hashed/opaque identifier — increases exposure risk if links are logged by intermediary proxies (evidence given truncated source; full personalization token audit not possible beyond these instances).

5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- `List-Unsubscribe` header not detected (QA WARN) — may be a relay-capture artifact rather than true absence; cannot confirm compliance from available data.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe) not detected (QA WARN) — same caveat.
- `Authentication-Results` header (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) not present in the captured headers — authentication status unverifiable from this data.
- Footer unsubscribe/preference-center link not visible in the truncated HTML sample provided — cannot confirm presence/absence of a body-level unsubscribe mechanism.

6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Sampled destination links use proprietary encrypted query tokens (`?qs=ABB7InYiOjEsImQiOjUwMDR9...`) rather than standard UTM parameters — landing-page attribution depends entirely on the ESP's redirect resolution; cannot verify UTM continuity without following redirects (which QA explicitly skipped).
- One direct (non-redirected) product-page link observed: `https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=...` — no UTM params attached, so this hit won't carry campaign attribution if a subscriber clicks it directly.

7. Recommendations
- Add descriptive (or explicitly empty `alt=""` for purely decorative/tracking images) `alt` attributes to all `<img>` tags, prioritizing product images (`slices_01–20.jpg`, desk roundup images) for accessibility compliance.
- Migrate the two remaining `http://` image assets (`Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, `slices_02.jpg`) to HTTPS.
- Move the open-tracking pixel off plaintext HTTP and stop passing raw recipient email addresses in tracking-pixel query strings; use a hashed/opaque subscriber ID instead.
