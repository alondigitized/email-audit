# “Sense & Sensibility”: See the movie, get the look
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-16**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction: this is the sixth Crate & Barrel email I've logged since yesterday morning, and the first one that's about a mood instead of a product category. The Thanksgiving dining room banner ("up to 30% off") is pinned above a Jane Austen movie tie-in. That's an odd stack. The "Sense & Sensibility" lookbook hero is moody and library-green, and I'm renovating two houses, so I did slow down on it.
- Then it keeps going. It's one very long scroll through living room, bedroom, dining, entertaining, kitchen and home office. Each block is a lifestyle photo with a "Shop [room]" button. The movie premise gets a few paragraphs of setup, and then it's a general catalog dump. Pottery Barn's "Get your living room ready for guests" did the same editorial thing with one room and one idea. This one tries to be the whole house.
- Would I click? Probably one tap into the dining room block, because the 30% banner is the only offer and it's the room I'm actually furnishing. I'd save it to my Pinterest folder, not forward it. I wouldn't mark it read-and-forget either, because the wallcovering and dark-wood dining photos are good reference.

## What's working

- The "up to 30% off" Thanksgiving dining room banner sits at the very top. It's the one concrete offer, and I saw it before I scrolled.
- The dark, moody Sense & Sensibility lookbook hero feels like an actual point of view, and it matches the older-home renovation mindset.
- The room-by-room structure with a "Shop Dining Room" or "Shop Bedroom" button under each block lets me jump straight to the room I'm working on.
- The "Shop Wallcoverings" block is a smart pull for someone mid-renovation. Plaid and floral paper is hard to find well-styled elsewhere.
- The "we love these for you" product row at the bottom has small, clean product tiles, and I could see prices.

## What's weak

- The sale banner and the movie hero fight each other. I can't tell whether this is a dining event email or a film tie-in. It's two emails stacked.
- It's a very long scroll. By the kitchen and home office blocks I'd lost the thread, and the closing sections (home office, Design Desk, app download) read like boilerplate.
- "Get Tickets" is a strange CTA from a furniture retailer. It competes with the shop buttons and sends me off to buy a movie ticket.
- After yesterday's Thanksgiving Dining Room Event and "Designed to get dinner on the table ASAP", I'm seeing the same dining photography again. That's the C&B family snooze I mentioned.
- Some product captions in the room blocks are tiny. I'd have to zoom to read the names, and there's little price info up top.
- There's nothing on rewards math, swatch programs, delivery windows for big furniture, or showroom integration. The Design Desk block at the bottom is the only service cue, and it's buried.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one story. Lead with either the lookbook or the dining event, and put the 30% offer in the hero button copy, not in a strip above the film art.
- 2. Cut the scroll to about three rooms. Lead with dining and living, because those are the highest-ticket rooms for this kind of buyer.
- 3. Drop "Get Tickets" to a text link so the shop CTAs win.
- 4. Add prices and delivery windows on the hero products. A sofa or dining table without a ship date is a half-answer for me.
- 5. Move the Design Desk and swatch-style service up. Someone mid-renovation would care.
- 6. Suppress or consolidate sends. Six emails in two days from one family is too many.

## Technical Audit

# Technical Audit: Crate & Barrel, "Sense & Sensibility": See the movie, get the look

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional template with a 460px mobile breakpoint and `lang="en"`. The automated QA found no hard failures (0 issues, 4 warnings). The main problems are missing alt text on 48 images, unverifiable header-level compliance data, and a recipient-identifying hash sent to a third-party pixel.

The HTML source was truncated, so footer, unsubscribe and link-level checks are limited to the QA data.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Tracking links not probed:** 80 links were skipped as click-redirect domains, so none of their destinations were verified. The report cannot say whether any are broken.
- **Third-party pixels:** Five `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?...&n=1` through `&n=5` requests (LiveRamp) load on every open. Repeated pixel calls add load and an extra third-party dependency.
- **First-party pixels:**
  - `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/.../o.gif` is the open pixel.
  - `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/4afcd7c2-...?mi_cid=...&mi_mid=...` is a second tracker.
- **Recipient identifier shared with a third party:** The `s=` value on the rlcdn pixels (`dbe8da718e2997cce014546f16c5722eaa3c322f28d40f0a8386f8fd1015f5a8`) matches the `mi_u=` prefix used on first-party `mi.crateandbarrel.com` URLs. This looks like a per-recipient hash, probably of the email address, passed to an external ad-data domain. I can't confirm the hashing from the source, but I'd treat it as a privacy risk.
- **Plain-text part is mostly URLs:** 16,230 of 22,612 characters (about 72%) are URLs, over the 70% threshold. This is a spam-filter risk and makes the text version hard to read.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on 48 images.**
  - Hero: `scene7.com/.../2026%5F1013%5FCB%5FSenseSensibility%5F1%5FHeroVide...`. This is the primary content image, so the email is unreadable with images off or in a screen reader.
  - 33 content images under `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/` (and `/m/47/bbf6f9d4-...png`).
  - 7 dynamic product images (`mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png`).
  - 1 image, `971aa10f-...jpg`.
  - 6 tracking pixels (5 rlcdn, 1 `o.gif`) plus the `dv.crateandbarrel.com` tracker. Pixels should still carry `alt=""`.
- **Pinch-zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="..., maximum-scale=1" />` blocks zoom on mobile, which is an accessibility problem.
- **Unescaped ampersand:** `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` should be `&amp;` under the XHTML 1.0 doctype. This is a minor validity issue.
- **Duplicate meta tags:** `format-detection` is declared four times, and `charset` is declared both as `http-equiv` and as `<meta charset>`. This is harmless but redundant.
- **Leftover template placeholder:** The comment `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` (misspelled) was left in the shipped HTML.
- **Conflicting `img` max-width rules:** `@media (min-width:768px) { img {max-width:600px} }` is followed by `@media (min-width:640px) { img {max-width:640px} }`. The later rule wins, so the 768px rule has no effect at desktop widths.
- **Global line-height:** `*{... line-height:100%}` applies to every element and can make text tight in some clients.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tokens (`{{...}}`, `%%...%%`, `[FIRSTNAME]`) are visible in the available source or QA output.
- The only per-recipient values are the `mi_u` and `s=` hashes in tracking URLs.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **No `List-Unsubscribe` header** was found. The QA tool notes it may not be captured by the AgentMail relay, so this may be a capture artifact rather than a sender fault.
- **No `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) header** was found, so one-click unsubscribe is unconfirmed. Gmail and Yahoo require it for bulk senders. The same relay caveat applies.
- **No `Authentication-Results` header** was captured, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The sender domain is `mail.crateandbarrel.com`.
- The in-body unsubscribe link and physical postal address could not be checked because the source was truncated before the footer.
- The recipient-hash sharing described in section 2 is a privacy-disclosure concern.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- All 80 links are tracking redirects that were not resolved, so the final destinations, UTM parameters and landing-page alignment could not be verified. This is a gap in the automated check, not a confirmed defect.
- The visible URLs are all email infrastructure hosts (`image.mail.`, `mi.`, `dv.`, `scene7`). The truncated source shows no direct `crateandbarrel.com` product URLs.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to the hero image and all 33 content images. Add product-name alt text to the 7 dynamic `rp/*.png` images. Add `alt=""` to the pixels.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Confirm at the sending side (not through the relay) that `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` are present and that SPF, DKIM and DMARC pass.
4. Review the LiveRamp pixels: cut the five duplicate calls to one if possible, and check that sharing the recipient hash with `sr.rlcdn.com` is covered by the privacy policy and consent.
5. Shorten the plain-text version's URLs, or add more readable text, to get below the 70% URL ratio.
6. Clean up the template: fix `&` to `&amp;` in the `<title>`, remove the duplicate meta tags and the `IMPUT HERE` placeholder, and fix the conflicting `img` max-width queries.
7. Re-run the audit with the full HTML and with redirect resolution on a sample of the 80 tracked links, to verify destinations, UTMs and the footer unsubscribe and address.
