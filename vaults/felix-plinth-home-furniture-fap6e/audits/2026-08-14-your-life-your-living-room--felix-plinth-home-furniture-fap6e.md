- "Don't settle on style" next to the Sven Sofa Bed in that dark olive is genuinely the kind of thing I'd screenshot for my wife — it doesn't scream "guest room futon."
- The step-by-step structure (style → organization → flow) actually maps to how I think about renovating right now — function first, then aesthetics.
- Neutral, warm palette throughout — beige boucle, oak, olive green — feels consistent with the last few Article sends I've seen, so at least the brand voice isn't whiplashing me.
- Each section has its own clear CTA button ("Shop Sleeper Sofas," "Shop Storage Tables," "Shop the Isla") so I'm not hunting for where to click.

## What's weak

- Zero pricing anywhere in this email. Not even a "starting at $X." Crate & Barrel's sister brand CB2 at least put "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" in the subject on their 7/10 send — here I have no idea if the Marina Sofa Bed is $800 or $2,800 without clicking through.
- It's long. Really long. By the time I hit the third "step" I've forgotten what the first one was about — no anchor nav, no jump links.
- The "Better to bundle" CTA at the bottom feels tacked on and undermines the whole "here's a thoughtful solution to your problem" narrative — suddenly it's just an upsell.
- No swatch or fabric-sample callout, which is odd for a brand pushing boucle vs. linen vs. leather sofa beds — that's exactly the kind of thing I'd want to order a sample of before committing to a sleeper sofa.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price anchor somewhere near the hero or at least near each hero product — "Sleeper sofas from $999" would do more work than any of the lifestyle copy.
- 2. Trim to two "steps" instead of three, or split this into a series — the Isla nesting tables section is the weakest link and dilutes the sleeper sofa pitch that's actually strong.
- 3. Add a small "order a sample" or fabric swatch CTA under the sofa bed section — matches how I actually shop (I will not buy an $1,800 sofa sight-unseen on fabric).
- 4. Cut or reposition the "bundle" CTA — either fold it into step three naturally or drop it; right now it reads like a bolt-on.
