# 🎃 Our frightfully fun Halloween shop is OPEN →
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-09**
## Executive summary

- Fourth Crate & Kids email in my inbox this stretch, and this one's actually the most coherent of the batch — Halloween shop launch, cute banner graphic, felt pumpkin treat bags. Problem is: I don't have kids. None of this is for me. The halloween cat treat bags and ghost throw pillows are genuinely charming as *design objects*, but I'm not the buyer, and by the time I hit "up to 50% off summer sale," "back-to-school gear," and a credit card pitch all crammed into one scroll, it's clear this is just Crate & Kids' full inventory dump with a jack-o'-lantern banner slapped on top.
- The "back-to-school sale: up to 20% off" bar above the Halloween hero is a straight-up hierarchy fumble — I open an email with a pumpkin emoji subject line and the first thing I see is unrelated back-to-school messaging fighting the halloween banner for attention. Compare that to CB2's 8/8 fabric-swatch send, which actually knew what it was about start to finish.
- Not clicking, not forwarding. Marking read and moving on — this is the fourth Crate & Kids/C&B-family email this week and my brain has fully snoozed the sub-brand at this point.

## What's working

- The Halloween banner graphic (felted pumpkin/ghost/cat bunting) is genuinely cute seasonal art direction, best visual in the email.
- "First look: the Halloween shop" is a clean, specific hero headline with a clear "Shop Halloween" CTA button.
- Product tiles (pumpkin treat bag, cat hooded towel, ghost throw pillows) are well-lit and load cleanly, no broken images.
- Nice touch that "Selling Fast!" urgency tags sit right on the product photos instead of buried in copy.

## What's weak

- Zero reason for me to be on this list — no kids, not renovating a nursery, and yet I'm getting the full kids catalog: backpacks, baby dinnerware, crib sale, back-to-school gear.
- The email tries to be four campaigns at once (back-to-school sale bar, Halloween shop, summer furniture sale, back-to-school gear, credit card pitch, design service, app download) — nothing gets to breathe.
- That back-to-school promo bar at the very top directly undercuts the Halloween subject line I clicked into the email for.
- Credit card cross-sell ("new! the card you love just got better") this deep into a Halloween email is pure filler — nobody scrolls this far for a rewards card pitch.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email down to one campaign — Halloween shop only. Kill the back-to-school bar, the summer furniture sale, and the credit card block entirely; let the pumpkin banner and product grid stand on their own.
- 2. If Crate & Kids insists on multi-campaign sends, at least sequence them so the subject-line topic matches what's above the fold — right now the first thing I see contradicts the subject.
- 3. Segment: I'm mid-reno on two adult homes with zero kids in the picture. A "no kids in household" suppression rule would keep me off this entire sub-brand's list.
- 4. Move the credit card and app-download blocks to their own dedicated send — they don't belong bolted onto a seasonal shop launch.
