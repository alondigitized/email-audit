# The chicken coop everyone LOVES. See why →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- I'll be honest — "The chicken coop everyone LOVES" is the first Wayfair subject line in about a week that made me actually pause before deleting. Not because I need a chicken coop. I do not need a chicken coop. I'm renovating a primary in Silver Lake and a second home in the desert and neither of those projects involves poultry. But there's something almost performance-art about a home goods retailer leading with this, and I clicked open purely out of morbid curiosity — which is either a win for Wayfair's subject line team or a sign I've been broken by the volume.
- Then the email opened. The chicken coop is the hero. It has stars. "People love this chicken coop." The 70% OFF banner is plastered across the top like a county fair sale. Below it: a grid of generic picks, Open Box Deals, a rug section, ottomans. The chicken coop was the hook and the pivot to generic home picks is abrupt enough to feel like a bait-and-switch. I'm not mad — I'm just not clicking anything either.
- This lands between yesterday's trash cans email (6/10, pure mismatch) and the rugs send the day before (8/10, at least had a price anchor). I'm marking it read and moving on. I would not forward this. I might text a friend "Wayfair is doing chicken coop marketing now" as a joke, which is a different kind of engagement and not the one their email team wants.

## What's working

- **Subject line broke pattern.** After four consecutive rug emails and a trash-can non-sequitur, a chicken coop is genuinely surprising. That alone got the open.
- **70% OFF banner is visible immediately.** I didn't have to hunt for the offer — it's the first thing below the nav, banner-width. That's correct layout.
- **"People love this chicken coop" + star ratings** is a tight little piece of social proof — compact, visual, doesn't ask me to read a paragraph.
- **Open Box Deals section has actual product variety.** This is the one section where the email tries to be useful for someone outfitting a house. Props for including it.
- **"Small space?" section at the bottom** lands for me — I have a small guest room situation at the second home and ottomans as storage is a real solution I'm thinking about.

## What's weak

- **The chicken coop is a cold pivot.** The subject implies the whole email is about the coop. It isn't. That pivot from "viral farm accessory" to generic picks with no editorial bridge is jarring.
- **"70% OFF" is Wayfair's usual inflated-baseline game.** I've been burned enough times to mentally discount these headlines. Crate & Barrel's Summer Sale email last week at least made the original price visible — this just throws a big number and hopes I don't think too hard.
- **"Let's complete your space ↑" copy is meaningless.** That upward arrow goes nowhere conceptually. Article's "New Arrivals Just Landed" from a few weeks back had cleaner directional CTA copy — at least it told me what I was clicking toward.
- **The product grid in "Your Home, These Picks" is random.** Bedding, mirrors, what looks like outdoor furniture — it's not curated for anyone in particular. Pottery Barn's recent sends have been sale-hammer-heavy but at least the product mix coheres around a category.
- **No size, material, or shipping callout visible anywhere.** I'm buying furniture for two homes. Lead times and dimensions are exactly what I need. Zero of that is present.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Bridge the chicken coop to the rest of the email.** If you're going to use a viral product as the hook, editorialize it — "Our most-talked-about product this week. Here's what else people are loving." One sentence of connective tissue turns a non-sequitur into a brand voice moment.
- 2. **Replace "Your Home, These Picks" with a category-specific grid.** If you know I've been browsing rugs and lighting, serve me that. This generic grid is a missed personalization opportunity and it's exactly what separates Wayfair from Pottery Barn's editorial emails.
- 3. **Make the Open Box Deals section the hero for a returning customer.** That's where the real value-to-me lives. Right now it's buried after the chicken coop.
- 4. **Kill "Let's complete your space ↑" and write a real CTA.** "Shop storage under $150" or "See all open-box furniture" — specific verb + noun, not a slogan.
- 5. **Call out shipping windows or dimensions somewhere.** Even one line: "Ships in 2–4 days · Free on orders over $35." That's what converts me on furniture emails.
