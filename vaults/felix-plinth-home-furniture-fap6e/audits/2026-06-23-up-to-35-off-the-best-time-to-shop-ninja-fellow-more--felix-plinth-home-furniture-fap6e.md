# Up to 35% off: the best time to shop Ninja, Fellow & more!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- Okay — Ninja and Fellow from Crate & Barrel. Not what I expected when I saw the sender name. My C&B folder has been wall-to-wall Warehouse Sale furniture and Crate & Kids drops for the past week (the Finn Collection, new beds and dressers, Justin Theroux's registry — seventeen emails that I've mentally catalogued and mostly ignored), and now suddenly I'm looking at a kitchen electrics promo. It's a genuine category pivot, and honestly? It caught me.
- The thing is, I'm outfitting two kitchens right now. The second house needs everything from the countertop up, and Fellow specifically is a brand I've had open in another tab for three weeks — I'm trying to decide whether to just pull the trigger on the Stagg EKG. So this email landed at a weird moment where it's simultaneously "Crate & Barrel does this?" and "wait, this is actually useful." The 35% off is real money on Fellow, which doesn't discount much anywhere.
- That said, the Ninja pairing undercuts the premium positioning. Fellow is a $200 kettle. Ninja is the air fryer my neighbor bought from Costco. Lumping them together under one sale banner makes both feel cheaper. I'd click through to the Fellow section and probably ignore the Ninja half entirely. I wouldn't forward this — it's too utility-specific to share.

## What's working

- **The Fellow call-out in the subject is doing work.** Fellow is a recognizable signal to the design-conscious home crowd — it filters in immediately as "this is for someone like me."
- **35% off a premium brand is rare and credible.** Fellow and C&B don't discount constantly, so this feels like a real window, not a manufactured sale.
- **Visual hierarchy lands on the appliances first.** The hero goes right to the product imagery — coffee equipment is visible above the fold, no preamble lifestyle shot to scroll past.
- **Clean layout, no render bugs.** The product grid holds up, images load, nothing overlapping. Compared to the mid-render chaos I sometimes get from Pottery Barn's longer promos, this reads fine on my phone.
- **The tableware section further down is genuinely useful.** I need to fill out two dining tables. The fact that it slides from electrics into plates and serveware without feeling like a non sequitur is a quiet win.

## What's weak

- **Ninja drags the brand positioning down.** Fellow sits at one end of the kitchen equipment prestige scale; Ninja is a different audience entirely. The "& more" in the subject already hedged the premium signal — seeing Ninja confirmed in the body makes this feel like a clearance catch-all.
- **Sender alias is "Crate & Barrel SALE" — that's a trust haircut.** I know it's them, but the alias reads promotional rather than brand, which is exactly what I file away without reading.
- **No friction-free path to just the Fellow products.** If I want to skip Ninja entirely and go straight to the Fellow Stagg EKG, I'd have to hunt for it. Article would have led with a single hero product and one CTA. This scatters attention.
- **Time-bounded urgency is absent.** "Best time to shop" is not a deadline. Wayfair's "AREA RUGS." email at least implied inventory scarcity. This could be running through September for all I can tell.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split Ninja and Fellow into separate sends, or at minimum give Fellow its own hero lane.** Fellow is an editorial story. Ninja is a promo. They belong in different emails or at least different sections with a visual break. The premium buyer you're targeting with Fellow will clock the Ninja association and mentally discount.
- 2. **Add a hard end date to the offer.** Even "through Sunday" lifts urgency massively. "Best time to shop" is inert copy.
- 3. **Lead the Fellow section with a single hero product and specific savings.** "$60 off the Stagg EKG Kettle" is a reason to click. "Up to 35% off Fellow" is not.
- 4. **Fix the sender display name back to "Crate & Barrel."** The SALE alias should not be customer-facing. It signals promotional ghetto, not brand store.
- 5. **Bring the tableware closer to the top.** Two homes = two kitchen tables. The plate section buried in the lower third is invisible to anyone who opens, sees Ninja, and bounces.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Alt text: 50 images missing `alt` attributes**

Every product image and all decorative GIFs lack alt text. This breaks the email for screen readers and renders it content-free when images are blocked (default in Outlook, many corporate clients). Affected examples:
- `31ea28b4-2d64-4123-a33c-4511a20ac4c4.jpg` — product image (no alt)
- `ccceec39-1c00-4459-a214-b3aa0bfa6e5d.gif` — animated element (no alt)
- `ae314b10-e7df-42d5-8722-315e643d7c27.gif` — animated element (no alt)
- Tracking pixels (`o.gif`, `448796.gif`, `f3c0d22992a01946.png`) also lack `alt=""` — these should carry empty alt, not omit the attribute entirely.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
`telephone=no` and `address=no` appear in both a combined meta and individual metas — redundant but harmless.

**Template comment left in production**
`<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` is visible in the HTML `<head>`. This is a build/QA process failure — production HTML should not contain unfilled template comments.

**CSS media query conflict**
Two `@media` rules targeting desktop images conflict:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
The 640px breakpoint rule is declared after and overrides the 768px rule on screens 768px+ wide, capping images at 640px rather than 600px. Likely unintentional.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)
