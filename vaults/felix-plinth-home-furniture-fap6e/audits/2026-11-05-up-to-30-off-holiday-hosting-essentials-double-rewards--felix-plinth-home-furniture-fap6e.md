# Up to 30% off holiday hosting essentials + Double Rewards!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-05**
## Executive summary

- This is the fifth Crate & Barrel send today, and I'm done pretending they're separate emails. The Double Rewards banner is the same one from "Starts TODAY! Open to shop Double Rewards →" and "Double Rewards + Up to 30% off drinkware & more!" earlier. The one new thing is the hosting angle: a plate-and-glassware grid under "host & toast." That is a real hook, and I'm doing two kitchens and a Thanksgiving, so it does land with me.
- The email is also very long. It stacks a hero, a six-tile category grid, a Thanksgiving table feature, a recipe block, a second Double Rewards banner, a product row, a credit card pitch, an app pitch and TheDesignHub. By the time I reached the second "last Double Rewards this year!" block I'd already read the offer once. Pottery Barn's Christmas Shop lookbook had one reason to exist and stuck to it. This one keeps repeating itself.
- Would I click? Maybe once, on the dinnerware tile, and only because I'm planning a table. I wouldn't forward it. I'd skim it, then archive it in the Pinterest folder.

## What's working

- The hero is a plated dinnerware setting on a dark, moody background. It's a good photo that looks like my Pinterest board.
- The tile grid gives a clear percentage per category: dinnerware 20%, wine glasses 30%, serveware 30%, drinking glasses 30%, table linens 30%, flatware 20%. I can see the math at a glance.
- "5 styles for your Thanksgiving table" is the most useful block. It gives me a style choice, not just a discount.
- The Double Rewards math is stated: earn 10–20% back in Reward Dollars with the Crate Card, through Nov. 17. That's a credible deadline, and it matters to a rewards-card shopper like me.
- The product row near the bottom shows prices, like the Impressions 15-oz juice glass. It feels like a catalog page I'd save.

## What's weak

- The Double Rewards pitch appears twice in one email, and it was already in four earlier sends today. It's the same banner I saw this morning.
- The card, app, TheDesignHub and recipe blocks pile up. They dilute a decent hosting email into a catch-all.
- The "Shop Now" buttons are small and generic in a long scroll. On a phone these tap targets are tiny, and the type is hard to read without zooming.
- The fine print sits in dense blocks at the bottom. The "up to" and "select styles" qualifiers on the offer, plus the card terms, feel like they're hiding the real exclusions.
- The "we love these for you" row is nine-dollar glasses. They have nothing to do with my renovation, and Wayfair at least pretended to personalize with its "picked just for you" tiles.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut it to one hero, one grid and one rewards banner, and drop the repeated card, app and recipe blocks. Merge the two Double Rewards pitches into one placement.
- 2. Space out the Double Rewards sends. Five in a day trains me to ignore the brand, and CB2's run of eight emails since 10/30 is the same mistake.
- 3. Make the CTA copy specific, like "Shop dinnerware" instead of "Shop Now," and make the buttons bigger.
- 4. Add a swatch or sample cue for tableware and linens, plus a delivery-by-Thanksgiving note. Shipping clarity matters for anything I order for the holiday.
- 5. Put the Thanksgiving-by-date shipping cutoff near the top, where it creates real urgency.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a Movable Ink-built (`mi.crateandbarrel.com`) XHTML template. The 57% QA pass rate comes mostly from about 34 missing-alt warnings and from header checks that the AgentMail relay probably made unverifiable. The HTML source was truncated, so footer, unsubscribe and link-level findings can't be confirmed.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unprobed links:** 68 links were skipped as tracking/click-redirect domains, so none were checked for status codes, redirect chains or destinations.
- **Third-party pixels:** five `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=56aaa278…&n=1` to `n=5` pixels (LiveRamp) fire on open. They carry a per-recipient hash in the `s=` parameter, so they are a tracking and privacy surface beyond the ESP's own tracking.
- **Recipient IDs in URLs:** `mi_u=56aaa2787a6773d0…` appears in `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png` and `/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif`. This is a per-recipient identifier in image URLs.
- **Leftover template placeholder:** `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` is still in the `<head>`. It is harmless but shows the template wasn't cleaned (note the "IMPUT" typo).

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text, content images (20):** these are all on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/{1,47}/…`. Examples:
  - `bbf6f9d4-86cc-437a-aa80-391067b74306.png`
  - `296bfc38-c721-473e-a1f2-854211792a2a.jpg`
  - `971aa10f-db11-4b79-b1d6-d95ad4507a7c.jpg`
  
  If these are the product and promo images, an image-blocked or screen-reader view loses the offer content.
- **Missing alt text, Movable Ink dynamic images (7):** `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/{560d850e7e517ede,789e7381ff44be74,5ec241ce0b852c66,00423cc9007b550c,f5ca909404423879,45e8c84f5a9185ff,18f126218cc222ec}.png`. These are probably dynamic content blocks, so they need meaningful alt text.
- **Missing alt text, tracking pixels (7):** the 5 `rlcdn.com` GIFs, `o.gif` and `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/c0f7d973…`. Use `alt=""` explicitly; the warning is low severity here.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="… maximum-scale=1" />` prevents pinch-zoom, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Duplicate and redundant meta tags:**
  - Two charset declarations (`http-equiv` and `meta charset`).
  - Three `format-detection` tags: one combined, then separate `date=no`, `address=no` and `telephone=no`. The later duplicates can conflict with the first.
- **Unescaped ampersand:** `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` is invalid under the declared XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype and should be `&amp;`.
- **Global CSS rules:**
  - `p{margin:0 auto; text-align:center}` and `*{line-height:100%}` apply to all elements.
  - The two `img {max-width}` media queries overlap. For widths of 768px and up, the later 640px rule overrides the 600px rule, so the 600px rule is dead code.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unrendered merge tokens (`{{…}}`, `%%…%%`, `[FIRST_NAME]`) were visible in the truncated source.
- The only personalization evidence is the `mi_u` per-recipient hash in Movable Ink URLs, which is rendering correctly.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe:** `[WARN]` header not found. The QA note says the AgentMail relay may strip it, so it is unconfirmed rather than a confirmed defect.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** `[WARN]` header not found, so one-click support can't be verified. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require it for large senders.
- **Authentication-Results:** header absent, so SPF/DKIM/DMARC status is unknown. The sender domain `mail.crateandbarrel.com` is a dedicated subdomain, which is a good sign, but nothing is verified.
- **Footer:** the visible in-body unsubscribe link and the physical postal address are in the truncated portion, so they can't be confirmed.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Not assessable. All 68 links are tracking redirects that were not resolved, so UTM parameters and landing pages could not be checked against the "30% off holiday hosting + Double Rewards" offer.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Check the unsubscribe headers (`List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`) and `Authentication-Results` in the raw message at the receiving mailbox. Don't treat the QA warnings as sender defects until you've confirmed the relay isn't stripping them.
2. Add descriptive `alt` text to the 20 `image.mail…` content images and the 7 `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png` dynamic images. Set `alt=""` on the pixels.
3. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
4. Escape the title as `Crate &amp; Barrel`, and remove the duplicate charset and `format-detection` tags and the leftover `IMPUT HERE` comment.
5. Extend the QA tool to follow the 68 redirect links (a `HEAD` or `GET` that follows redirects) so it can check final URLs, UTM parameters and status codes.
6. Re-run the audit on the full, untruncated HTML so footer compliance and merge tokens can be checked.
