# Our newest Texas store JUST opened! Shop a curated list of faves →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-22**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is the second time this exact "our newest Texas store just opened" email has landed in my inbox — I flagged the near-duplicate send with the tracking-code suffix a few days back, same hero shot, same stone fireplace and leather sofa, same "beautifully furnished" opener. That hero image is genuinely good — it looks like a room I'd screenshot for the second-house mood board — but everything under it is just Crate & Barrel's standard weekly module stack: bestselling furniture, decor, KitchenAid grid, tableware, Design Desk, holiday teaser, "we love these for you." The store opening premise gets one line and then evaporates.
- The kitchen brands section (KitchenAid, Staub, Le Creuset) is the one part that'll actually pull my eye since I'm mid-renovation on both houses and I do care about cookware upgrades. But there's zero swatch, fabric, or finish-sample content here for a brand this furniture-heavy, which is exactly the kind of thing Pottery Barn's design-services email nailed with that credenza vignette.
- Would I click? Maybe scroll to the kitchen module. Forward to a friend? No — nothing here is Texas-store-specific enough to be worth sharing, and I already forwarded the near-identical send once. This is a mark-as-read.

## What's working

- The hero shot (stone fireplace, leather sofa, brass lighting) is a legitimate scroll-stopper and matches the reno aesthetic I'm chasing.
- KitchenAid/Staub/Le Creuset grid is relevant — I'm actually shopping cookware upgrades for the second-house kitchen right now.
- Bestseller badges and star ratings on furniture pieces give some social proof without me having to dig.
- TheDesignDesk free-appointment banner is a real utility for someone renovating two houses at once, even if buried mid-scroll.

## What's weak

- Subject promises a Texas store opening, but the body drops that thread after one sentence — it's just the standard weekly module carousel underneath, same as CB2's "just one more day" sale email reused across sends.
- No swatch/fabric/finish-sample program anywhere, which is table stakes for a furniture brand at this price point — Pottery Barn at least shows a materials vignette.
- "First look: holiday 2028" this early feels stale and out of season, not urgent.
- This is a near-duplicate of the send I already reviewed this week — same hero, same layout, just a different tracking suffix. That's the Crate & Barrel/CB2 family snooze creeping back in.
- No loyalty/rewards math shown despite Crate & Barrel having a card program — I put purchases on my highest-rewards card and this gives me no reason to care which card that is.

## Recommendations

- 1. Actually build the email around the Texas store — local product picks, a "shop this room" from the actual store, or a grand-opening-specific offer — instead of bolting a generic module stack under a one-line headline.
- 2. Cut the duplicate-send cadence — I got this same creative twice in one week; consolidate sends or vary the hero so it doesn't read as a resend.
- 3. Add a swatch/sample CTA near the furniture modules — even a "order fabric swatches" link would close the gap with Pottery Barn's design-services angle.
- 4. Surface Crate & Barrel Card rewards math on at least one module — I choose purchases based on which card pays me back most.
- 5. Drop "holiday 2028" teaser this far out — swap for a renovation/fall-timeline hook that matches where I actually am in my project.
