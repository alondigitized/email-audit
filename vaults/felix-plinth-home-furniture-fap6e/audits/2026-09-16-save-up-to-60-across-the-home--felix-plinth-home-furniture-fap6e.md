# Save up to 60% across the home
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-16**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "the warehouse sale — Savings Across the Home" — this is Pottery Barn going full liquidation mode, and honestly it's the most room-by-room organized email I've gotten from them all week. Bedroom, living room, dining room, each with its own "shop the category" breakdown and a clean shoppable grid underneath. Compare that to the September 15 "FREE design services" send, which was smart but abstract — this one's the opposite, it's just inventory, but at least it's inventory sorted the way I actually shop, one room at a time since I've got two houses to furnish.
- The "Recommended Just For You" block with the curtain rod, the Reeve hardwood rug, the Cayman platform bed — that's the section that actually got my eye to slow down, because it looks like real personalization rather than a generic warehouse dump. But then it closes with the Halloween Shop banner, and that's the second time this week Pottery Barn has tried to hand me a plastic skeleton mid-renovation-decision-fatigue — I flagged the "Halloween favorites never stay in stock" email yesterday as a hard no, and stapling it onto the end of a furniture sale email doesn't fix that, it just tells me they're recycling the same seasonal push everywhere.
- Would I click? Yeah, probably into the dining room section — that wood table and rug combo is close to what I'm circling for the second house. Wouldn't forward it to a friend, this is routine catalog noise, not something remarkable enough to share.

## What's working

- Room-by-room structure (bedroom → living room → dining room) matches how I actually plan a reno, one space at a time.
- "Ships Free" badges on bedding, throws, and pillows removes a real friction point for furniture-adjacent purchases.
- "Recommended Just For You" grid feels like actual behavioral personalization, not just "here's everything we sell" like Wayfair's recliner email did.
- Rewards card callout at the bottom (10% back, no annual fee) is relevant since I already run everything through my highest-rewards card — good place for it, not shoved into the hero.
- Clean, consistent product photography — nothing looks like a stock-photo mismatch, unlike some of CB2's parent-brand overlap issues.

## What's weak

- "Up to 60% off" in the hero with no minimum spend or category carve-out visible — feels like the Wayfair problem of a big number that may not actually apply to what I want.
- The Halloween Shop banner tacked onto a furniture sale email is off-brand-context and the second Halloween push from Pottery Barn in two days — wrong moment for it.
- No color-palette or "shop the room" swatch/sample callout anywhere, which is exactly the kind of tool that would help me commit to bedding or rugs across two houses at once.
- Legal fine print block at the bottom is a wall of gray text — not a problem I need to read, but it visually drags the email out longer than it needs to be.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the Halloween Shop module entirely from a furniture sale email — let seasonal decor run its own campaign, don't dilute a warehouse sale with it.
- 2. Put a real number or category next to "up to 60% off" in the hero — even "dining sets up to 60%" would beat the vague ceiling.
- 3. Add a swatch/sample CTA under the bedding and rug sections — "order a swatch" is the single most useful button PB could add for someone juggling two color palettes right now.
- 4. Move "Recommended Just For You" higher, right after the hero — it's the strongest, most relevant block and it's buried past three other room sections.
