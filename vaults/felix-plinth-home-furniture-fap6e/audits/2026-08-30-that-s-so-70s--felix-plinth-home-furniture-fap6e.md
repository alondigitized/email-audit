# That’s so ‘70s
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-30**
## Executive summary

- Fifteenth CB2 email of the month and this is the one where they actually did something interesting: no discount headline up top, just "TIME WILL BE KIND TO THIS / A vintage Italian reissue. Something '70s. That cult favorite. These are the chairs you'll covet forever" over a moody single-chair shot. It's basically an editorial chair drop, and unlike the last few sends, the hook isn't "SALE" — it's provenance. Each chair below is tagged with a year and a designer name (1954 Vintage Italian Reissue, 1986 Vintage Danish Reissue, Art Deco Moment) which is the kind of detail that actually lands with me since I'm mid-renovation on two houses and I'm the guy who reads the designer credit before the price.
- That said, there's zero pricing anywhere near the chairs, and the Double Rewards banner is buried two-thirds down after six chair tiles. I'd scroll through the pretty pictures, maybe screenshot the Meda or Fitz chair for my Pinterest-adjacent folder, but I'm not clicking to "SHOP ACCENT CHAIRS" without a price. This is a save-and-forward-to-my-designer email, not a buy-now email — and CB2 keeps forgetting that accent chairs are a considered purchase, not an impulse click.

## What's working

- The design-history angle ('70s reissue, 1954 Italian, 1986 Danish) is genuinely more compelling to me than another "% OFF" banner — it's the first CB2 send this month that felt like a POV instead of a clearance sale.
- Each chair gets its own full-width hero shot with clean, moody staging — better product photography than the last few CB2 sends and way more editorial than Wayfair's canopy grid-of-tiles approach.
- "DOUBLE REWARDS IS ON / GET 20% BACK IN REWARD DOLLARS" plus the CB2 Visa signature card mention is a real, specific loyalty mechanic — more concrete than Pottery Barn's lookbook email, which had zero discount language at all.
- "MORE FOR YOU" personalized module at the bottom (a bed, a dining chair, a bookshelf, side tables) is a nice touch that at least gestures at cross-category relevance instead of just more chairs.

## What's weak

- No prices anywhere on the six hero chairs — I have to click through just to see if the Meda Chair is $400 or $1,200, which is real friction for a considered furniture purchase.
- The "DESIGN LEGENDS" and "'70s INSPIRED" eyebrow tags are inconsistent in placement and style chair to chair — feels like six loosely related product pushes stitched together rather than one curated capsule.
- Double Rewards, the actual concrete offer, is stuck below six chair images — Crate & Barrel's "Up to 40% off + Double Rewards" send led with the discount and the reward stack together, this buries it.
- This is my fifteenth CB2 email this stretch and even a good creative concept doesn't fix the fact that I've started auto-archiving this sender without reading past the preview line.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price (or "starting at $X") on at least the hero chair in the header block — that's the single biggest lift, since I won't click through six product pages just to price-shop.
- 2. Move the Double Rewards 20%-back banner up near the hero, right under the intro copy, so the loyalty math is visible before I've scrolled through six chairs.
- 3. Pick one framing and commit — either this is a "'70s reissue" capsule story or a generic accent-chair catalog; right now the eyebrow tags wobble between "DESIGN LEGENDS," "'70s INSPIRED," "CULT CLASSIC," and "ART DECO MOMENT" with no throughline.
- 4. Cut send frequency — fifteen emails deep, even a strong concept like this one reads as noise; a biweekly "Design Legends" series would land harder than daily blasts.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Vintage design, modern rewards — see the full capsule`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `From a '54 Milan original to a '86 Copenhagen classic`
