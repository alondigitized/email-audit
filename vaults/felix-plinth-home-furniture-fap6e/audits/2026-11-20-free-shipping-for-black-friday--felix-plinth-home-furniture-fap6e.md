# FREE SHIPPING FOR BLACK FRIDAY
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-20**
## Executive summary

- CB2 again. The sender name is still "CB2 BLACK FRIDAY," and by my count this is the thirteenth send in about five days. The hero is "FREE SHIPPING, orders of $99 or more" over a glass-and-candle shot, with one SHOP NOW button. The "Get it in time for Thanksgiving" cutoff strip above it is the most useful thing in the email. I'm renovating two houses, so shipping dates and shipping cost actually matter to me.
- After that it's a long scroll that recycles the "Pairs well with the Black Friday sale" grid (furniture, lighting, rugs, mirrors), the 60% off banner, and the app and rewards tiles. I've seen all of it in "So many cult favorites on sale" and "Dinner parties await." The "Win a $5,000 Refresh" design-appointment promo is the one new beat. I'd skim it, maybe save the rug and pendant tiles to my Pinterest folder, and mark it read. I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- The Thanksgiving delivery-date strip answers my real question: will this arrive in time? It's more useful than anything Wayfair has sent me.
- "FREE SHIPPING" in huge type is unmissable. The offer lands in about one second.
- The grid has furniture, lighting, rugs and mirrors up front, which is my actual shopping list. The burgundy chair and the globe pendant are the kind of thing that gets saved.
- The photography is moody and consistent, with warm tones that stay on-palette. It looks like CB2 and not a clearance flyer.
- "Earn 10% back in Rewards Dollars" is in the footer. That's the rewards math I care about, even though it's buried.

## What's weak

- The free-shipping threshold is $99. For a sofa or dining table that's meaningless. I want to know about white-glove or freight delivery on big furniture, and the email says nothing about it.
- It's the same Black Friday grid and 60% banner I got on 11/18 and 11/19. Pottery Barn did the same thing with its maroon banner, but CB2 does it more often.
- The delivery-date strip is tiny, and the struck-through 11/17 date is hard to read on a phone. That strip is the best content in the email and it's the smallest.
- Four competing CTAs sit in a row: Shop Now, Shop the Sale, Shop Now for the 15% off in the new year, and Book a Free Design Appointment. There are also footer tiles for the app, the card and texts. The 15%-in-the-new-year offer and the 60% off offer dilute each other.
- The fine print at the bottom is a dense wall. "Orders of $99 or more¹" with a footnote makes the headline offer feel less honest.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the hero a single message: free shipping, with the delivery cutoff dates large and directly under it. Move the grid below that.
- 2. Add a line on large-item and furniture delivery, since the $99 threshold ignores big-ticket buyers.
- 3. Cut the send count. Consolidate to one or two sends a day, or at least don't repeat the same grid.
- 4. Pick one secondary offer. Drop either the new-year 15% or the sale banner from this send.
- 5. Make the Rewards Dollars math more visible, for example "Earn 10% back on this order." It's currently a footer tile.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Order by 11/23 for two-day shipping. Sofas, lighting and rugs included.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional layout with MSO conditional comments and a media-query mobile layer. The main confirmed problems are 12 content images with no alt text, 11 `http://` third-party pixels, a viewport tag that blocks zoom, and a leftover template placeholder comment. Unsubscribe and authentication headers could not be verified. The HTML source was truncated, so footer and link-level checks are incomplete.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unprobed links:** 62 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe. Their destinations, redirect chains and status codes are unverified. This is a coverage gap, not a confirmed defect.
- **Insecure tracking pixels:** 11 third-party pixels load over plain `http://`. Gmail's image proxy and other clients may block them or show mixed-content warnings. Evidence:
  - `http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?dtm_com=2&dtm_cid=2437...`
  - `http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2437/pub1.php` through `pub10.php?d98e199bf5027afea04b790655b3dee2=1`
- **Cookie-sync pixels:** the ten `pub*.php` calls are cookie-redirect syncs to an ad-tech domain, which adds many third-party requests per open. Some clients and privacy tools strip them.
- **Movable Ink pixels:** `https://mi.cb2.com/p/up/09aebc5b21344cf7/o.gif?mi_u=...` and `https://dv.cb2.com/o/6571013d-...?mi_cid=...&mi_mid=...` are HTTPS and first-party-hosted. Both are missing alt text. For pixels this is minor but trivial to fix (`alt=""`).

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt on content images (12):** all are served from `https://image.mail.cb2.com/lib/fe9213727564027a72/m/1/`, for example `ca52acde-6c7c-4b14-916c-a199723498cc.jpg`, `7e302125-...jpg` and `e4289cf9-...jpg`. If the email is image-heavy, it is unreadable with images off and for screen readers.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` prevents pinch-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4).
