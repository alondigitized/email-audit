# Step inside an AD100 designer’s West Hollywood home
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-31**
## Executive summary

- Thirteen. I'm landing on thirteen CB2 emails since mid-May, and this is the one where the pivot away from offers becomes the whole point. After "OUTDOOR IS ON SALE" — which was loud, blunt, and honestly the best CB2 send in this cycle — they've swung to full editorial mode: a West Hollywood home tour with an AD100 designer named Ron Cassidy, a "new collab is coming" teaser, and a "SHOP THE COLLECTION" button that is trying to do two jobs at once (preview hype + immediate conversion) and ends up doing neither particularly well.
- I'll give CB2 credit: the photography is doing real work here. The room shots are warm, layered, textured — the kind of interior that actually makes me pause because I'm mid-renovation on two houses and I'm scanning every image for a dresser or a sideboard I haven't considered. Ron Cassidy's space reads as aspirational but not unattainable, which is the CB2 sweet spot when they hit it right. The issue is the structure: this is an announcement email dressed up as a shop-now email, and neither landing quite works.
- I would not click today. I'd archive it, probably screenshot one of the room photos to a reference folder, and wait for the actual drop email — which, given this brand's cadence, will arrive within 48 hours anyway.

## What's working

- **The photography earns the scroll.** The West Hollywood interior has warmth and specificity — actual styling choices visible, not a sterile showroom render. That's what I'm looking for when I'm decorating.
- **"AD100 designer" is a credibility signal** that lands if you know what it means — which, as someone who follows design publications, I do. It's shorthand for "this person is vetted by editorial taste, not just a paid collaboration."
- **"THE COLLECTORS" framing is smart.** It signals a curated, limited-feeling range rather than a volume play — which matters for CB2 positioning against Crate & Barrel's more mainstream catalogue.
- **The interior design services module with a concrete dollar figure ($1,000)** is the most actionable thing in the email for someone actively renovating. It's buried, but it's there.
- **Clean render, no layout breaks.** After some of the desktop-vs-mobile disasters I've seen lately, a CB2 email that just... works is worth noting.

## What's weak

- **"A NEW COLLAB IS COMING" kills conversion intent.** If it's coming, why is there a "SHOP THE COLLECTION" button? That contradiction makes me distrust both messages.
- **No pricing anywhere on the hero collection.** Pottery Barn's "All bedding ships free" sent the same week gave me a concrete reason to click immediately. This asks me to click purely on aesthetic interest, which is a harder sell when I've already seen a dozen CB2 emails.
- **Thirteen emails in roughly two weeks.** At this cadence, the editorial pivot reads as filler between sale emails, not genuine storytelling. "Your free living room redesign" last week was better positioned to earn the long-form treatment.
- **The "MORE FOR YOU" product recommendations at the bottom are generic.** Article's post-Memorial Day sends did this better — the recs felt curated to the theme. These feel like algorithm exhaust.
- **The $1,000 interior design service offer is undersold.** It's the most relevant thing in the email for someone in active renovation mode and it's in a module I almost missed.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to one job: tease OR sell, not both.** Either make this a pure editorial preview ("The collab drops Friday — save the date") with a waitlist or notification CTA, or move the drop date and make it shoppable. The hybrid is the worst of both.
- 2. **Lead with the interior design services module for the renovation-aware segment.** "$1,000 toward your redesign — meet the AD100 designer behind it" is a far stronger hook than a home tour for someone like me.
- 3. **Surface one hero product with a price.** Even one sideboard or lounge chair from the Cassidy collection with an actual number anchors the editorial and gives me something to click toward.
- 4. **Cut the cadence.** Two CB2 emails a week, not five. Wayfair can get away with volume because they're selling on discount frequency. CB2's brand positioning requires scarcity signals — and daily sends destroy that.
- 5. **Reframe the preheader** to complement rather than echo the subject.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ron Cassidy's West Hollywood picks — new CB2 collab`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Inside an AD100 designer's home (collab drops soon)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `See the pieces he chose — and get $1,000 toward your own redesign`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The Collectors collab: warm, layered, nothing like your last CB2 email`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta — confirmed issue**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents user zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` applied globally — confirmed issue**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applied via universal selector, not just `body`. Overrides user font-size settings on iOS. Should be `100%` on `body` only.

**`line-height: 100%` on universal selector — confirmed issue**
```css
*{... line-height: 100%}
```
This overrides inherited line-height on all elements including headings and buttons. Can cause text clipping in Outlook and tight leading on mobile. Should be scoped to `body` or specific selectors.

**Non-standard mobile breakpoint**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { .mobile { width:414px !important; } }
```
The 418px breakpoint is non-standard (common values are 480px or 375/390px). The `.mobile` class sets `width:414px` inside a `max-width:418px` query — on a 415–418px viewport this forces 414px content into slightly less space.

**Scaffolding comment left in production — confirmed**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
Developer placeholder comment (with typo "IMPUT") is present in the sent HTML. No functional impact but indicates a template production gap.
