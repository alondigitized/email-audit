# New bedding for kids, toddlers & babies →
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- Forty-one. That's where the C&B family now sits in my July inbox after this one lands, and I want to hold that number up before I say anything else, because context is everything. This is Crate & Kids — the same sub-brand that already hit me with "FREE SHIPPING ends tomorrow!" twice today. Twice. Same subject line, same brand, two separate sends inside the same afternoon. And now a third Crate & Kids email before dinner. I don't know how this passed any send-cadence gate, but here we are.
- The email itself is actually clean. Genuinely well-composed — aspirational lifestyle hero, soft neutrals and organic textures, a logical top-down flow through category sections: full/twin bedding, toddler, baby. No broken images, no layout chaos. If this were the first Crate & Kids email I opened this week it would feel like a useful editorial drop. My second home has a guest room I'm debating whether to double as a kid-ready space, and I do care about what goes on those beds.
- But it's not the first email. It's the third today. I skimmed it, appreciated the photography, and immediately marked it read without clicking. I might forward it to my wife if she hasn't already seen it — but even that impulse died when I remembered she's probably drowning in the same inbox.

## What's working

- **Hero photography is genuinely good** — soft sage greens, layered textiles, the kind of well-lit lifestyle shot that earns its placement. It looks like something that belongs in the actual catalog.
- **Category architecture makes sense** — kids → toddler → baby is logical sequencing and easy to skim down the page.
- **No render bugs I can see** — images load, text doesn't overlap, layout holds at whatever mobile width this screenshot represents.
- **The sub-brand targeting is correct** — I am actually on the Crate & Kids list and I do have kids' rooms to furnish, so this isn't a mismatch on paper.
- **CTA buttons are visible and placed at logical intervals** — not buried, not competing with each other at the same visual level.

## What's weak

- **Three sends from Crate & Kids in a single day is indefensible.** Two "FREE SHIPPING ends tomorrow!" blasts followed by a new-arrivals editorial breaks any coherent cadence logic. No adult consumer reads that as strategy.
- **No offer anchored to the new arrivals.** Article recently sent me a zero-discount editorial on entryway furniture that worked because it was confident and informative. This send feels like it wants to be editorial but is surrounded by urgency-play siblings — the dissonance is jarring.
- **Nothing in this email treats me like a known customer.** No name, no "back in stock in your saved sizes," no "completes your wishlist" hook. Pottery Barn's design-services email from this same cycle felt more personalized than this does.
- **The "new" claim is undermined by cadence fatigue.** By the time I'm opening a third email from the same sender in one day, "new" reads as just another word.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the third send today.** Seriously. Suppress any editorial drop on a day when urgency sends already went out from the same sub-brand. One send per sub-brand per day, hard cap.
- 2. **Anchor the editorial to a concrete offer** — "New arrivals + free shipping through midnight" pulls both the discovery email and the urgency email into a single send and collapses the triple-tap problem.
- 3. **Lead with something I haven't seen** — a swatch program callout, a "ships in X days for back-to-school" confidence signal, a "pairs with your C&B wishlist" hook. CB2's recent "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" at least implied occasion-based selection logic.
- 4. **Give me a reason to forward it.** A gift-registry link for a friend's baby shower, a "build a set" bundle prompt — something shareable.

Sender is "Crate & Kids." Gmail web and some webmail clients display the `<title>` value. This surfaces the parent brand rather than the sub-brand.

**Conflicting `img { max-width }` media queries**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
At viewports ≥768px, both rules apply. Source order causes the 768px rule (`max-width: 600px`) to win over the 640px rule (`max-width: 640px`) — correct outcome, but the 640px rule is effectively dead above 767px. At 640–767px only the 640px rule applies. Likely unintentional layering; verify intended image caps per breakpoint.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html

```
Prevents users from pinching to zoom. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Should be removed.

One combined tag plus three individual tags cover the same attributes (telephone, address, date). No rendering harm, but indicates template hygiene issues.

