# Our 4th of July Sale STARTS NOW 💥
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- Forty-two. That's my rough Pottery Barn count, and I'll say this for "Our 4th of July Sale STARTS NOW 💥": it's actually doing something different from the last four sends. The June 25 curtains-and-florals email, the June 25 outdoor-essentials send, the June 24 quilts, the June 24 handcrafted rugs — all of them were orbiting the same "Up to 70% off" umbrella without adding a new layer. This one pulls a new lever: Extra 20% off stacked on top of the existing sale. For someone pricing out sofas and outdoor sectionals for two simultaneous renovation projects, that escalation is an actual signal, not a rebrand of the same promo.
- The structure is clean. The hero communicates the stacked discount at the top without asking me to scroll. Four category tiles — Furniture (Up to 70% off), Bedding (Up to 40% off), Pillows & Decor (Up to 40% off), Outdoors (Up to 40% off) — map almost exactly to my live shopping lists. And the mid-email "Extra 20% off Open Box Outlet Deals" callout is the kind of savings math I actually stop and run. CB2 scored an 8 from me last week on their "FLOOR SAMPLE SALE" because they understand that a furniture buyer responds to genuine discount signals on real inventory, not vibe marketing. Pottery Barn is doing a version of that here with the outlet callout, even if it's buried one scroll down.
- Would I click? Yes — the Open Box Outlet section gets the tap. Would I forward it? No; I'd drop it into my home-projects folder. Mark as read without opening? Not this one. The extra 20% stacked discount earns it.

## What's working

- **"Extra 20% off" hero is a genuine escalation**, not a repackage. After four days of "Up to 70% off" messaging, stacking an additional discount on top reads as a real event. That's a meaningful cadence upgrade.
- **Category tiles hit all four of my live shopping categories** — furniture, bedding, pillows/decor, outdoors. No wasted real estate pushing me toward something irrelevant like table linens or gift wrap.
- **Open Box Outlet Extra 20% section is the sleeper hit.** Outlet pricing plus another 20% off is furniture math that actually pencils. This is the same reason CB2's floor sample email outperforms every other furniture send I get: real inventory + real discount = click.
- **"Recommended Just For You" personalization block is visible and present.** Whether the algorithm is dialed in to my actual browsing I can't tell from here, but signaling personalization earns a point.
- **Seasonal timing is credible.** June 26 drop with a July 4 implicit deadline gives me a genuine, near-term reason to act. This isn't the "Summer Sale Extended Again" non-urgency I've trained myself to ignore.

## What's weak

- **Qualifying fine print under the Extra 20% hero reads small.** I can see there's condition language below the big offer number. If there's a minimum spend threshold and it's buried, I'm going to click through, find out the sofa I want doesn't qualify, and close the tab annoyed. CB2 leads with the constraint so I self-select before clicking.
- **"SEE ALL SALE" is a weak primary CTA.** I have four category tiles right below it that are more useful. The button should say "Shop Furniture Sale" or "Shop Outdoor Sale" — send me somewhere specific, not to the whole sale landing page.
- **No anchor product with a visible price in the hero.** Wayfair's "AREA RUGS." format (which I noted as respectable despite its weirdness) at least tells me what I'm getting. Pottery Barn is selling an event rather than a piece, which adds one unnecessary click to my path.
- **Cadence.** This is the fifth Pottery Barn email in approximately 48 hours. The email itself isn't the problem; the accumulated weight is. The 4th of July sale is a real enough occasion to justify it, but I'm filing this in a mental folder labeled "they're at it again" before I've read the subject.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface the qualifying spend threshold in the hero, large and proud** — "Extra 20% off when you spend $150+" eliminates checkout abandonment and treats me like an adult. I'd rather self-select than discover the catch later.
- 2. **Swap "SEE ALL SALE" for a split CTA bar** — "Shop Furniture" / "Shop Outdoors" side by side. I have two houses; I'm not browsing a monolithic sale.
- 3. **Feature one anchor piece in the hero with a visible price** — "The Comfort Roll Arm Sofa: now from $1,299" gives me a mental calibration point. Pottery Barn consistently hides prices above the fold; that's friction for someone who's actively comparison-shopping against Crate & Barrel and Article.
- 4. **Lead the Open Box section with a saved-dollar figure, not just a percentage** — "Extra 20% off: an $800 chair, now $560" is more visceral than another badge. Wayfair does this with some of their outlet tiles and it works.
- **Subject Alt A:** `4th of July: Extra 20% off starts today — furniture, bedding, outdoors`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Stack it: extra 20% off everything on sale this week`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Open Box Outlet deals get another 20% off too — act before the 4th`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Furniture, bedding, outdoors — all stacked deeper for the holiday`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CRITICAL — PHP header() calls inside `<style>` block**
```html
<style>
  ...
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
</style>
```
These are server-side PHP statements copy-pasted into a CSS block. They will not execute. Depending on the email client's CSS parser, they may render as visible text inside the email body or cause the entire `<style>` block to be discarded.

**HIGH — 3 HTTP image sources (will be blocked)**
| Image | Source |
|---|---|
| `spacer_0.gif` | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` |
| `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` |
| `slices_02.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` |

Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all proxy or block non-HTTPS image loads.

**HIGH — 47 images missing `alt` text**
All content images, all footer slices, all product recommendation images, and two tracking pixels have empty or absent `alt` attributes. This renders the email effectively blank for recipients with images disabled (a common default in corporate email clients).

**MEDIUM — Open-tracking pixel served over HTTP**
`http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
HTTP pixel will be blocked on HTTPS-enforcing clients, breaking open-rate measurement.

**LOW — `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**

Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Accessibility failure for low-vision users.
