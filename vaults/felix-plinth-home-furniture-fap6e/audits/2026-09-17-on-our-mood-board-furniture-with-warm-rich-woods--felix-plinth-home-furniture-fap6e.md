# On our mood board: Furniture with warm, rich woods
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-17**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one I'd actually keep open past the first scroll. "Heritage-inspired furniture in warm, dark woods" is exactly the mood board I've got open in another tab right now — I'm literally trying to nail down a wood tone that works across two houses so it doesn't look like I furnished them from two different decades. The Richmond bed, the Biltmore chairs, the Rowan cabinet — this reads like an actual editorial lookbook, not a discount bin.
- What loses me is the length and the Venus Williams mid-scroll detour. I'm seven sections deep — bedroom, accent chairs, dining, storage, finishing touches — before I hit a random athlete collab that has nothing to do with "warm, dark woods." It's a tonal whiplash after all that quiet, moody styling.
- Would I click? Yeah, probably the Richmond bed or the Biltmore chair — those are genuinely my rooms right now. Would I forward it to my wife? Possibly the storage cabinet, since we're arguing about the office right now. Not marking this as read and moving on — this is one of the better sends I've seen this week.

## What's working

- The wood tones are consistent room to room — bed frame, chairs, dining table, cabinet all read like one coherent palette, which is the thing that actually annoys me when three brands push different finishes the same week.
- Section headers ("in the bedroom: rich details," "in storage: heirloom looks, modern function") give me a scannable table of contents instead of one wall of product tiles.
- The styling photography looks like a real room, not a studio cutout — that's the difference between this and Wayfair's "70% OFF" purple banner spam I flagged this week.
- Named pieces (Richmond bed, Biltmore chairs, Brittany table) make we love these for you feel curated, not algorithmic.

## What's weak

- The Venus Williams collab section completely breaks the "warm, dark woods" narrative I'd been following for six sections — it's a different campaign shoved into this one.
- No price or %-off anywhere in the whole email until the vague "$500" mention buried near the bottom app-download blurb — I have no idea if any of this fits my budget.
- It's a marathon scroll. Crate's own dining-room send yesterday ("3 dining rooms, 3 makeovers") was shorter and hit harder — this one dilutes a strong concept by cramming six sub-categories in.
- No swatch/sample program or finish-comparison callout, which is exactly what I need when I'm trying to match wood tones across two renovations — CB2's "Fall 30% Off" send at least led with a number I could act on.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this to 3 sections max (bed, dining, storage) and split the rest into a follow-up send — right now the strong idea gets buried under its own length.
- 2. Pull the Venus Williams collab into its own dedicated email — it doesn't belong in a wood-tone mood board.
- 3. Add a visible price or "starting at $X" under at least the hero product — right now there's zero anchor for whether this fits my budget.
- 4. Add a "shop the wood tone" swatch strip so I can actually cross-reference finishes between pieces — this is the single highest-value addition for someone furnishing two houses at once.
