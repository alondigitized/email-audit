# STARTS NOW: THE BEST SALE OF THE YEAR
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-18**
## Executive summary

- This is the tenth CB2 email in about four days, and the sender name is now "CB2 BLACK FRIDAY." I'd already filed it as "CB2 again" before I opened it. "STARTS NOW" lands a bit oddly after "HOURS LEFT: EARLY BLACK FRIDAY SALE" yesterday and "FINAL HOURS: DOUBLE REWARDS" this morning. The sale "ends" and "starts" in the same week, and my brain gave up on the timeline.
- Once I'm in, the email itself is clean. The moody hero has a green velvet chair and brass tones, and it says "UP TO 60% OFF" with one outlined "SHOP EVERYTHING ON SALE" button. The free shipping line sits right under it. The category tiles for furniture, lighting, rugs, tabletop and bedding are the best part. It reads like a catalog I'd pin, and my Pinterest-adjacent folder would take the rug and the coupe glasses.
- I'd open it and scroll, and I might click into Rugs or Lighting for the second home. I wouldn't forward it, because there's nothing here I'd send a friend. It's a pretty email I've effectively already seen four times this week.

## What's working

- The hero is clear. "UP TO 60% OFF" is the biggest thing after the logo, and I got the offer in about two seconds.
- "FREE SHIPPING ON ORDERS OF $99+" sits right under the hero. For furniture buyers that's a real trust cue.
- The category tiles map to how I shop. I'm doing two houses, so I jump to Lighting, Rugs and Furniture.
- The photography looks like a catalog, not a clearance flyer. That matters when I'm saving things to a mood-board folder.
- The Thanksgiving delivery-date strip is useful. It shows cutoffs of 11/19, 11/23 and 11/24, with 11/17 struck through.

## What's weak

- Cadence is exhausting. This is the tenth send in four days, and "STARTS NOW" contradicts the "ending soon" emails from yesterday.
- The tiles are just photos with labels. There are no prices, no products and no "up to X% off" per category. Pottery Barn at least repeats a number, and Article's flat "UP TO 40% OFF" is simpler.
- "SAVE 15% OFF in the New Year" is a confusing second offer. Is it on top of 60%? It's a mid-email detour, and the "SHOP NOW" button under it is generic.
- The footer fine print is a wall of tiny type. The "up to 60%" claim has exclusions I can't read on a phone. My rewards-card brain can't do the math with that much buried.
- The CB2 credit card pitch, the app download, the texts offer and the gift registry all crowd the bottom. That's four asks after the sale.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put one anchor product in the hero, such as a lamp or rug at a real price with the sale price shown. Right now I'm shopping a mood, not an item.
- 2. Add the discount depth to each tile, for example "Rugs, up to 50% off." Crate & Barrel Kids' "STARTS NOW: Black Friday deals + free shipping!" at least named the free shipping in the subject.
- 3. Reconcile the timeline. If the sale started days ago, don't say "starts now." Lead with the real news, like new markdowns.
- 4. Fold the 15% New Year offer into one line, or cut it.
- 5. Trim the bottom to two asks, with rewards first and the app second.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 60% off + free shipping $99+`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Rugs, lighting & sofas: Black Friday is on`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping on $99+. Order by 11/24 for Thanksgiving delivery.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Our deepest markdowns on lighting, rugs and sofas, through Cyber Monday.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional template with MSO conditionals and mobile media queries. It has 11 content images with no alt text, and 11 third-party pixels loaded over plain `http://` (also without alt). The 57% QA pass rate comes from warnings only (0 hard failures). The truncated HTML and missing headers leave compliance and link-level checks unverifiable.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Link probing was not possible.** 64 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped, so destination validity, redirect chains and 404s are unverified.
- **11 third-party pixels load over `http://`.** These are Dotomi/Conversant cookie-sync beacons:
  - `http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?dtm_com=2&dtm_cid=2437&...`
  - `http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2437/pub1.php` through `pub10.php?d98e199b...=1`
- Mixed-content or HTTPS-only clients may block these, so the beacons can silently fail. The sheer number of sync pixels (10 `pubN.php` calls) also adds remote requests on open.
- **First-party tracking pixels are present and HTTPS:** `https://mi.cb2.com/p/up/09aebc5b21344cf7/o.gif?mi_u=...` and `https://dv.cb2.com/o/86c8a81b-...?mi_cid=...&mi_mid=...`. Both lack `alt`.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **11 content images have no alt text.** All are on `image.mail.cb2.com/lib/fe9213727564027a72/m/1/`, including `add58a32-...gif`, `6b5bab9f-...jpg`, `e9c8b20f-...jpg`, `2fd45f06-...jpg`, `5d3a3907-...jpg`, `b016c51e-...jpg`, `3572eb2a-...jpg`, `a0ddcbb9-...jpg`, `047124ce-...jpg`, `20185973-...jpg` and `ca52acde-...jpg`. With images blocked or a screen reader, these render as nothing or as filenames.
- **The tracking pixels also lack alt.** Add `alt=""` to them (and to purely decorative images) so assistive tech skips them.
- **Zoom is disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="... maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile (WCAG 1.4.4).
- **Text resizing is suppressed.** The global `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;-ms-text-size-adjust:none}` and `body{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` prevent user text scaling.
- **Template residue is left in the source:** `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->`. It is harmless but shows an unreviewed template.
- **Web fonts are loaded cross-brand.** `HNW05` is served from `www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/...`, and the Lineto and GT Sectra fonts from `www.cb2.com/Styles/...`. Most email clients ignore `@font-face`, so a fallback stack is required. `a { font-family: 'Lineto Brown Light Sub', Helvetica, sans-serif }` has one, but `GT Sectra` and `HNW05` fallbacks can't be confirmed in the truncated source.
