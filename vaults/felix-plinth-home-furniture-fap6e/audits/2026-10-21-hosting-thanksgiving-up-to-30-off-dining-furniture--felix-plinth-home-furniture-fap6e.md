# Hosting Thanksgiving? Up to 30% off dining furniture →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-21**
## Executive summary

- This is the first Crate & Barrel send this week that matches what I'm doing. I'm renovating two houses, and a dining room event is on topic. The hero is a round wood table under a big "up to 30% off" headline, and I got the point in about two seconds. The "Hosting this year?" line under it is a small human touch. After the 10/20 pile of kids' chair, baby bestsellers and Philips sends, this one finally feels like it was written for me.
- It loses steam below the hero. Once I scroll, it becomes a long catalog with two sweepstakes pitches, a card pitch and an app pitch. The "Hosting Guide" banner has a button, but the text on the photo is hard to read. The Design Desk sweepstakes ($5,000) is a second hero fighting the first. I'd click the dining tables tile, save the email to my inspo folder, and skip the rest.
- I wouldn't forward it. I might send the table to my spouse, but nothing here is shareable beyond the product shot.

## What's working

- The hero is clear: a styled table, "the Thanksgiving dining room event, up to 30% off," and a single Shop Now button.
- The four-tile grid (tables, seating, storage, lighting) matches how I actually shop a dining room, one piece at a time.
- The lighting tile calls out "20% off select lighting," which is a useful extra for a renovation.
- The photography is moody and neutral. It's closer to my palette than the Wayfair sends with the blank hero.
- The Design Desk free-appointment offer is genuinely useful for someone with two projects.

## What's weak

- The hero has a $1,499 table price in tiny type, and the fine print is microscopic. "Up to 30%" tells me nothing about the specific table.
- There are too many competing CTAs: Shop Thanksgiving, Shop The Dining Furniture Event, Shop All Dining Furniture, Book an Appointment twice, Learn More, Download Now. It reads as indecisive.
- The Design Desk block is repeated. Its banner appears mid-email and again near the bottom.
- The "we love these for you" block is generic cookware and a sofa. It looks like a different email, and the tiny product text is unreadable on my phone.
- Nothing about furniture shipping, lead times, or whether I'd get it before Thanksgiving. For a table, that's the first question I'd ask.
- It's still the 6th Crate & Barrel family send in two days, so even a good one feels like noise.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut it down to the dining event. Drop the generic recommendations, the app and the card pitch, and keep one Design Desk block.
- 2. Name a real hero product with its sale price and a delivery-by-Thanksgiving promise. Pottery Barn's "top 100 gifts" is cluttered too, but it at least shows a price on the gift. CB2's "up to 60% off" was clearer.
- 3. Make the product tiles tappable with price and size in readable type.
- 4. Add a cutoff date for ordering in time for the holiday. That would make the urgency credible.
- 5. Add swatch or fabric samples and a rewards-math line for cardholders ("earn 10% back on this table").

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible source is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional email with MSO conditionals and `lang="en"`. The confirmed problems are about 26 images with no alt text (including 7 tracking pixels) and a viewport tag that blocks zoom. The header, authentication and link-destination warnings can't be confirmed from the data given.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified destinations:** 59 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are click-redirect domains. Final URLs, redirect chains and 404s are unknown, so this is a coverage gap rather than a defect.
- **Third-party pixels:** five identical `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=dbe8da71…&n=1` to `n=5` beacons are present. This is LiveRamp, a third-party domain. The `s=` value is a 64-hex identifier, which looks like a hashed subscriber ID. Five calls differing only by `n=` is redundant and adds load requests.
- **Subscriber ID in image URLs:** every `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png` URL and `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif` carries `mi_u=dbe8da71…`. Each URL is therefore unique per recipient, which blocks shared image caching. Proxies and forwarding will also expose the identifier.
- **Open pixels:** there are two, `mi.crateandbarrel.com/.../o.gif` and `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/e5ccb949-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…`. Neither has alt text.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (26 images):**
  - 11 content images on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/` and `/m/47/`, for example `bbf6f9d4-…png` and `ef861136-…jpg`.
  - 1 image on `s7d5.scene7.com`, `…ThanksgivingGuide_Tertiary…`.
  - 7 product-recommendation images on `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png`. These are likely product tiles, so screen-reader and images-off users would get no product or price context.
  - 7 tracking pixels (5 `rlcdn`, `o.gif`, `dv…`). These should carry `alt=""`, `width="1"`, `height="1"` and a `display:none` style so they are skipped by assistive tech.
  - The QA check may flag an empty `alt=""` as missing. The truncated source can't confirm which images have the attribute absent and which have it empty.
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` disables pinch-zoom, which fails WCAG 1.4.4.
- **Conflicting image rules:** `@media (min-width:768px){img{max-width:600px}}` and `@media (min-width:640px){img{max-width:640px}}` both match at 768px and above. The later 640px rule wins, so the 600px cap is dead code.

  - A duplicate charset declaration is present (`http-equiv` and `<meta charset>`).
  - `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` has an unescaped `&` under an XHTML doctype.
  - `<meta charset="utf-8">` is not self-closed.
  - A template placeholder comment is left in the source: `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->`.
