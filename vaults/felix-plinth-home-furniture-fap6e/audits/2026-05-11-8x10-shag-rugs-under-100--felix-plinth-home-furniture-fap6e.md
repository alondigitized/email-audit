## 2. What stood out

The hero banner reads "the must-shop steals — UP TO 70% OFF" in Wayfair's signature purple, and honestly that contrast is doing a lot of heavy lifting. The rug image underneath it is styled well — layered over a patio, natural light, not a white-background catalog shot — and for two seconds I thought this send was going to be curated. Then the "Sponsored" tag showed up on one of the product tiles and broke the spell. I don't want ads in my email; I get enough of those in the feed. There's also a "we think you'll ❤️ these" section that's generating a full grid of unrelated items — an outdoor storage bench, what looks like a wall art piece, a yellow sofa — and at that point the rug is gone. The $100 price point from the subject never re-surfaces in the email body that I can see, which is the real miss. I came in expecting a tightly curated "best rugs under $100" edit and got a department store flyer. The app-download footer and the social row are boilerplate filler I scroll past every time.

## 3. What I'd change

1. **Anchor the body to the subject's promise.** The subject says "8x10 shag rugs under $100" — the hero section should show a grid of exactly those, labeled with price, size, and pile height. Everything else is noise until that promise is fulfilled.
2. **Remove or clearly label sponsored tiles.** One "Sponsored" badge tanks the editorial feel of the whole email. Either don't mix sponsored units into the email feed, or quarantine them at the bottom with a clean divider.
3. **Surface the sub-$100 price explicitly in the hero copy.** "Rugs under $100" is the conversion hook — print it big, don't just hide it in the subject line.
4. **Cut the "we think you'll love these" module** or replace it with a "completes the room" module (a rug pad, a floor lamp, a side table) — cross-sell that actually makes sense given why I opened.
5. **Tighten the footer.** App download + social row + payment logos + unsubscribe = four competing footers. Pick one.
