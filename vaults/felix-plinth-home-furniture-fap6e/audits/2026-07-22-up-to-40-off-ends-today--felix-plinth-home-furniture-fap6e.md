# UP TO 40% OFF ENDS TODAY
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-two. That's the CB2 count for July, and I'm starting there because this is the email that breaks the "UP TO 40% OFF ENDS TODAY" seal — which, given my inbox history with this brand, I read as "we've been emailing you every day this month and nothing has worked, so here's the discount." The hero delivers more than the subject promised — the bedroom clearance section is showing up to 60% off, not 40%, and the outdoor collection is calling out 50%. So CB2 actually undersold itself in the subject line, which is an interesting move I don't hate in principle. But the all-caps is still there, and "ENDS TODAY" from a brand that's been ending things today since July 1st is not urgency — it's wallpaper.
- The bedroom imagery in the hero is genuinely relevant to me right now. Canopy bed, warm neutrals, natural textures — I'm literally buying bedroom furniture for the second house and that visual stopped my scroll, if only for two seconds. The "MORE FOR YOU" product grid at the bottom suggests they're doing some personalization, though I can't tell if those are truly tailored picks or just clearance filler. The outdoor collection section with what looks like rattan lighting and seating is right in my wheelhouse for the primary renovation's back patio.
- I won't click, but I won't delete it immediately either. I'll save it to my folder and check later if the offer is real or if "ENDS TODAY" becomes "EXTENDED THREE MORE DAYS" tomorrow. I would not forward this to a friend — there's nothing in here that makes it feel curated enough to share.

## What's working

- **Hero image is legitimately on-brand for my renovation context** — canopy bed, warm neutral palette, natural textures, the kind of room I'm actually trying to build in the second house.
- **Outdoor collection section earns its placement** — the rattan/wicker pendant and seating imagery is exactly what I'm shopping for the back patio; good that it's above the fold on desktop.
- **The actual offer beats the subject line** — hero is showing up to 60% clearance and 50% outdoor, so the email delivers more than it promised. That's the right direction.
- **"SHOP THE SALE" CTA is clean and unambiguous** — no "discover" language, no vague "explore more."
- **Design services callout** — after yesterday's dedicated design-services email scored well, keeping it in the rotation as a secondary block is smart positioning.

## What's weak

- **ALL CAPS subject is on autopilot at this point** — thirty-two emails in, it's a brand tic, not a signal. Article sent me "Create a personalized dining room" in sentence case last week and I gave it more attention.
- **"ENDS TODAY" urgency is completely depleted** — CB2 has trained me that today always ends and tomorrow always brings a new ending. Wayfair does this too and I called it out then; CB2 is even worse about it.
- **The subject says 40% and the hero shows 60%** — fine that the offer is better, but the mismatch reads like the subject was written by one team and the email by another. Pottery Barn aligns these consistently.
- **"MORE FOR YOU" grid feels like clearance draining, not curation** — I can see several product thumbnails but nothing that signals CB2 actually knows what I've been looking at or buying.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the real number in the subject** — if the hero is 60% off clearance, say 60%, not 40%. The undersell costs clicks.
- 2. **Kill ALL CAPS on the subject** — this is not a discount store, it's CB2. Article and Pottery Barn don't need to shout. Sentence case or title case reads as more premium.
- 3. **Retire "ENDS TODAY" until it's been offline for two weeks** — give it a rest, then bring it back with an actual hard deadline (tomorrow's date, a specific time) so it means something again.
- 4. **Make "More For You" work harder** — if I've been browsing canopy beds and outdoor seating, show me those specifically with a "you browsed this" label. That's what would actually make me click.
- 5. **Separate the bedroom clearance from the outdoor sale** — two distinct offers in one email dilutes both. Pick the higher-converting one for the hero and let the other live as a secondary section with its own CTA.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 60% off clearance — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Summer clearance: outdoor + bedroom, up to 60% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Canopy beds, outdoor seating, rattan lighting — all marked down.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The summer edit you've been waiting for — deeper discounts, real deadline.`

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Developer comment left in production** (line in `<head>`):
  ```html
  <!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
  ```
  Typo ("IMPUT") and internal authoring note shipped to end recipients. Should be removed before send.

- **Version tag in production**:
  ```html
  <!--V2-->
  ```
  Internal template versioning artifact exposed in live HTML.

- **Malformed font URLs — double forward slash**:
  ```
  https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2
  https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2
  https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2
  ```
  Three `@font-face` declarations use `//` after the domain. Most servers normalize this, but it is a defect and can fail on strict origin validation or certain proxies.

- **`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom** (WCAG 1.4.4 violation):
  ```html
  <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
  ```
  Prevents low-vision users from zooming. Should be removed or set to `maximum-scale=5`.

- **Global text-size-adjust suppression**:
  ```css
  * { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
  ```
  Applied to every element, not just `body`. This overrides browser accessibility settings globally and conflicts with the `body`-level declaration also present in the stylesheet.

- **Non-standard mobile breakpoint with hard-coded width**:
  ```css
  @media only screen and (max-width: 418px) {
    .mobile { width: 414px !important; }
  }
  ```
  418px breakpoint is non-standard. The inner `width:414px !important` hard-codes a pixel width inside a fluid media query context, which will overflow a 375px-wide viewport (iPhone SE/standard).
