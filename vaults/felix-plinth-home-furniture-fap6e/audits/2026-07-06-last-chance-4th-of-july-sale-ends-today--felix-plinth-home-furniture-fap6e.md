# Last chance: 4th of July Sale ends today
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- Fourth. This is the fourth Article email on this exact sale — I've been tracking since June 26 — and the honest truth is I opened it anyway because I haven't pulled the trigger yet and "ends today" is a real forcing function when you've got two rooms to furnish. The red hero is sharp, the category tiles are exactly right for where my head is (bedroom, living room, dining room — yes, yes, and yes), and Article earns some credit for pacing this arc cleanly instead of the CB2 firehose approach.
- But here's what bothers me: the July 3 send told me "up to 40% off." That number is gone from today's email. I'm now on the last day of the sale, the maximum urgency moment, and Article swapped the concrete offer back to "save big with hundreds of deals." That's worse than the previous email — a step backward at the exact wrong moment. Crate & Barrel kept "up to 50% off" in their hero every single send. Pottery Barn punched "extra 20% off select styles" all the way through to their last-day email. Article drops the number when it matters most.
- I'll probably click — I genuinely need stuff and today's the deadline — but I'm doing it despite the email, not because of it. I wouldn't forward this. I'd forward the July 3 one if anything.

## What's working

- **"ENDS TODAY" above the fold in the hero** — no ambiguity, no scroll required, the urgency reads in under two seconds on mobile.
- **Four category tiles map directly to my actual shopping list** — bedroom, living room, dining room covers everything I'm currently furnishing across both houses. Whoever built this grid understood the consideration set.
- **Red mid-section banner "Last call: Our 4th of July Sale ends today"** reinforces the deadline without feeling desperate — it's a second urgency hit that lands naturally as you scroll.
- **Clean render** — no layout breaks, no overlapping copy, no broken image gaps. After the CB2 stack-of-three-sales mess I audited, a clean email is genuinely worth noting.
- **30-Day Satisfaction Guarantee in sub-copy** — small but meaningful for big-ticket furniture. I care about return windows.

## What's weak

- **The specific discount disappeared.** The July 3 email said "up to 40% off" — that number is the entire reason someone like me does math and clicks. Today's hero just says "SALE." Crate & Barrel runs "up to 50% off" in the headline every send; Article removed its number on the most critical day.
- **No specific product in the hero** — this is the last-chance send and I'm looking at a red box with the word "SALE" in it. Pottery Barn's last-day email led with a specific hero product. A concrete sofa or dining table would pull me in faster than a category grid.
- **"EXPLORE WHAT'S NEW" CTA in the footer** — that button belongs on an editorial send, not a deadline email. It fragments attention at exactly the moment you want me channeled toward checkout.
- **The 4th of July framing is two days stale.** I understand the sale window, but calling it "4th of July Sale" on July 6 creates a tiny friction — it reads like a clearance extension, not a designed arc.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the 40% number back in the hero** — "Up to 40% off — ends today" in the subhead or as a badge on the hero image. The July 3 email earned a 9 partly because the discount was specific; this one regresses by removing it.
- 2. **Lead with one hero product, not just the category grid** — pick the bestselling sofa or the dining table that's been moving and make that the above-fold story. The grid can stay below it.
- 3. **Kill "EXPLORE WHAT'S NEW" from the footer** — replace it with a more urgent variant like "SHOP BEDROOM NOW" that matches the category tiles above.
- 4. **Add the discount to the subject line** — "Last chance" without a number is a weaker pull than "Last chance: 40% off ends tonight."

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — Missing `@media` closing brace (dark mode block breaks in strict parsers):**

```css
/* In <style> block 5 — prefers-color-scheme:dark */
@media(prefers-color-scheme:dark){
  ...
  a .link{color:#fff!important}
/* ← NO closing } before </style> */
```

The `@media` block is never closed. In CSS-strict environments this may invalidate the entire style block, disabling dark mode support entirely.

**Error — Trailing comma in selector:**

```css
h1,h2,p,{color:#fff!important}
```

The trailing comma before `{` is an invalid CSS selector. The rule will be silently dropped in most clients, meaning `h1`, `h2`, and `p` text will not invert to white in dark mode.

**Error — Semicolon before `!important`:**

```css
a{color:#fff;!important}
```

`color:#fff;` ends the declaration; `!important` is then a parse error. The link color override will not apply in dark mode. The correct form is `a{color:#fff !important}`.

**Minor — Empty `<title>` tag:**

```html
<title></title>
```

No preheader/title content. Some clients display the `<title>` as a tooltip or accessibility label; empty value reduces screen-reader context and marginally increases spam-filter risk.
