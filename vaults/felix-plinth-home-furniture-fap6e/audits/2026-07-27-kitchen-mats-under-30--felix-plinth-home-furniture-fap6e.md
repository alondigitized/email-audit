# KITCHEN MATS under $30 ✨
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-27**
## Executive summary

- Thirteen. That's the Wayfair count in my inbox now — one after "Kitchen Mats under $30" apparently wasn't even the header hook for this exact send, because kitchen mats aren't even the hero, they're one tile in a "shop before they're gone" grid buried under yet another "80% OFF BLACK FRIDAY IN JULY ENDS TONIGHT" banner that I have now seen with a straight face in the middle of summer for the better part of two weeks. The subject promised me a specific, useful thing — kitchen mats, cheap, done — and instead I get the full Wayfair firehose: rugs, pool tables, birdbaths, doormats, ceiling fans, shoe cabinets, curtains from $9.99. This is not a kitchen mats email, it's a "everything in the warehouse under one purple banner" email wearing a kitchen mats subject line as a disguise.
- I'm mid-reno on two houses right now, so in theory this is exactly my inbox to be in. But the sheer sprawl is the opposite of what I need — I don't want twenty product categories shotgunned at me, I want the two or three things relevant to whichever room I'm actually staring at spreadsheets for this week. CB2's "Just in. Already trending." send earned a 9 from me because it picked one lane and looked expensive doing it. This is the exact opposite instinct — grab everything, staple a purple label on it, ship it.
- Verdict: not clicking, not forwarding. Maybe I archive it into the Pinterest-adjacent folder if the rug tile catches my eye later, but this is a scroll-and-close on my commute, not a stop-and-look.

## What's working

- The "get 5% rewards on every item, always" module is genuinely useful — I'm a rewards-card guy and flat cashback-style messaging is the one thing in this email that speaks my language directly.
- The "Top-Rated Area Rugs" and "Pool Tables Under $800" tiles are believable reno-adjacent categories I'd actually pause on.
- The doormat hero shot ("the warmest welcome") is the one moment of actual visual craft in the whole email — decent styling, real photography, not just a product-on-white crop.
- "Everything Ships Free" stacked with the 80% off banner is at least a concrete, specific claim up top, even if I don't believe the 80%.

## What's weak

- Subject says kitchen mats, hero banner says Black Friday awnings-energy 80% off everything — the two don't match, and I've now caught Wayfair doing this subject/body bait-and-switch on back-to-back sends (yesterday's wall décor email pulled the same trick).
- "ENDS TONIGHT" on a Black Friday-branded banner in July is the same fake urgency I flagged on the awnings send two days ago — I don't believe a deadline that's been "ending tonight" all month.
- The kitchen mats — the entire premise of the subject line — are one small tile in row one and then never mentioned again. Rugs, birdbaths, bread pans, shoe cabinets, ceiling fans all get equal billing.
- No swatch/sample program, no room-planning tool, nothing that treats me like someone mid-renovation trying to match a palette across two houses — just tile after tile of unrelated categories.
- "picked just for you" sits directly above bedding, pool tables, drying racks, and ceiling fans in the same grid — that's not personalization, that's the whole catalog with a friendly label slapped on.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the email actually be about kitchen mats. If the subject says kitchen mats under $30, open with kitchen mats, show me four or five actual mat options with prices, and let that carry the whole send. Right now the subject is bait for a completely different email.
- 2. Cut the category count by 75%. One purple "shop before they're gone" grid, one "picked for you" grid, and a dozen unrelated home categories in between is catalog-dump behavior, not a campaign.
- 3. Kill or timestamp the "ENDS TONIGHT" urgency — I've now seen it on three sends in four days with no actual expiration. It's training me to ignore it.
- 4. Lead with the 5% rewards module higher up — that's the one piece of this email that's actually about me as a repeat/loyalty shopper, and it's buried past the first big sale banner.
- 5. If this is genuinely a broad catalog send, retitle it as one — don't borrow a specific, useful subject line to get the open and then not deliver on it.

- **Subject Alt B:** `New in the kitchen: mats, racks & more under $30`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `5 mat styles, all under $30, free shipping today`
