# Calling all bakers: you have to see this 🍪
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-18**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is a Levain Bakery x Crate & Barrel collab — bakeware and cookie jars, not a single sofa or console table in sight. I stopped scrolling almost immediately because there's nothing here for a guy mid-renovation on two houses. The cookie-cutter set, the "milk & cookies" jar, the brownie recipe tie-in — this reads like it's aimed at someone furnishing a kitchen for entertaining, not someone trying to figure out what dining table survives a second-home reno.
- The one thing that did catch my eye scrolling down was the "up to 25% off bedroom refresh" banner buried past all the bakeware content — that's actually relevant to me, but it's like the eighth screen deep. By the time I got there I'd already mentally filed this as "not my email."
- Verdict: not clicking, not forwarding. This is a fun lifestyle collab for someone who bakes, and I don't bake. Marking as read and moving on — though I'll admit the cookie jar photography is genuinely nice, just wasted on me.

## What's working

- The hero photography is clean and well-lit — that cookie jar shot in particular looks premium, not stock.
- "Bring the famous bakery home" is a clear, specific hook if you're actually into baking.
- The "5 tips to cookie perfection" content block adds some real value/education, not just product pushing.
- The bedroom refresh banner (25% off) is the one relevant offer buried in here — genuine specific % off with a visible CTA.

## What's weak

- This is a bakeware collab email, full stop — zero overlap with furniture, rugs, or anything reno-related, which is 90% of why I open C&B emails at all.
- It's an enormous scroll — cookie cutters, cookie jars, milk & cookies sets, a brownie recipe, "more from our collab" — before it even gets to something like the bedroom sale.
- The relevant offer (bedroom refresh) is stranded near the bottom, way past where most people would've bailed.
- Compare this to C&B's own "Bestselling sofas made even more beautiful" send I read earlier today (6/10, real product names like the Axis sofa and Basque dining table) — that one at least stayed in my lane. This one completely left it.
- No swatch/sample program, no shipping-timeline info, nothing that speaks to my actual furniture-buying anxiety — because none of that's the point of this email, but it means it's just not for me.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this. If I've never clicked a kitchen/bakeware email and I have clicked furniture/bedroom content, this shouldn't be my version — the bedroom sale should be the hero, not an afterthought at position 8.
- 2. Move any home-adjacent offer (the 25% bedroom refresh) up near the top if it has to coexist with this collab at all.
- 3. Cut the email length by half — cookie cutters, cookie jar, milk & cookies set, "more from our collab," and the recipe is a lot of real estate for a niche collab.
- 4. If this must go to non-baking segments, lead subject/preview with the crossover angle ("new cookware, plus 25% off bedroom") instead of full bakery immersion.
