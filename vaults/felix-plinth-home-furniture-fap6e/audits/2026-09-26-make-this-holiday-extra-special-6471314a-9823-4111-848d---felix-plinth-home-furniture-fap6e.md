# Make this holiday extra special!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-26**
## Executive summary

- Okay, Crate & Barrel Kids again — different sub-brand logo, same C&B house style I've been swimming in all week. I don't have kids, so the entire premise of this email is a swing and a miss the second I see "magic they'll never forget" over a photo of a kid unwrapping presents by a stocking. This isn't a renovation email, it's not a furniture email, it's a full toy-and-stocking catalog for a category I'm not shopping in at all right now.
- It's a long scroll too — wish-list toys, decor, personalized stockings, a cooking set, bassinets, a stroller — basically the same "fire the whole cannon" approach the main C&B account used in "Your no-stress holiday season starts…now!" (6.00/10) and "Welcome to your holiday shopping & decorating HQ" (6.00/10), just repackaged for the kids line. At least visually it's clean and well-shot — C&B's photography is consistently good — but there's zero reason for me personally to open past the first screen.
- Verdict: not clicking, not forwarding. If anything I'd forward it to my sister who has toddlers, not keep it for myself. Marking as read and moving on — this is the fourth C&B-family email in my inbox this week and my thumb is on autopilot at this point.

## What's working

- The hero shot (stocking, gift-wrapped presents, kid in matching PJs) is genuinely cozy and on-brand for holiday — nice photography, consistent warm color palette throughout.
- Section breaks are clean — "wish list-topping toys," "fun & festive decor," "a stocking just for them" — easy to scan even scrolling fast on my phone.
- The personalized stocking section (name embroidery, "Leo") is a real point of difference — Pottery Barn's monogram towel push in "The bath towels everyone loves" (6.00/10) at least proved personalization sells, and C&B Kids is doing the same play here with stockings.
- No visual bugs I can spot — no blank boxes like that Wayfair "Hall trees" email that left me staring at empty white rectangles.

## What's weak

- I'm not the parent-of-young-kids persona — nothing here maps to renovation, furniture, or my actual project list, so the whole email is dead on arrival for me.
- It's an exhausting number of sections to scroll through — toys, decor, stockings, cooking sets, bassinets, stroller, "we love these for you" — CB2's "Italian vintage reissues" email proved a tight, curated send with one point of view (6.00/10) beats a kitchen-sink dump.
- The CTA buttons all say generic things like "Shop Now" repeated over and over — no single hero offer or specific % off anywhere I can see, unlike Wayfair or C&B's own clearance emails that at least lead with a number.
- Cadence fatigue is real — this is the third or fourth Crate & Barrel-family send this week between the main brand and now Kids; my brain has fully entered C&B snooze mode.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment harder — if I've never bought a kids' item from C&B, don't send me the full Kids catalog; send me the main-brand furniture/decor line instead. This is a targeting problem, not a creative one.
- 2. Lead with one hero offer and a real number (% off or a price point) instead of a wall of "Shop Now" buttons with no urgency.
- 3. Cut the email in half — pick toys OR decor OR stockings as the lead story, and let the rest live on a landing page, the way CB2's vintage-reissue email stayed focused.
- 4. If sending to a childless subscriber, at minimum swap the framing to "gift for the kids in your life" rather than assuming I have my own.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Personalized stockings, back before they sell out`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Gifts for the kids on your list (not just your own)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Embroidered stockings, wish-list toys, and cozy PJs — shop before the holidays get busy`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New for Leo's list: toys, stockings, and matching family pajamas`
