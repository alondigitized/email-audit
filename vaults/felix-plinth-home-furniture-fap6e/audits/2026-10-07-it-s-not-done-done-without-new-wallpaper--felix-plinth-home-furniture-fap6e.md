# It’s not done-done without NEW wallpaper!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-07**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Barrel again, and it's the Kids sender for the second day running. The wallcovering run is the only reason this one isn't an instant swipe. I'm renovating two houses, so wallpaper is a live question for me. The email is built around it, with a "first-ever wallcovering collection" intro and a wallpaper-by-wallpaper walk-through. It also offers up to 10 free samples, which is the part I care about most.
- The nursery and kids' bedroom framing is a miss for me. I have no kid to put in those rooms. Still, the patterns are what I'd look at: the plaid, the botanical toile and the pink floral. I'd click through for the wallpaper and ignore the Pottery Barn Kids-style crib content. I wouldn't forward it. I'd probably save it to my inspiration folder and move on.
- The cadence is the real problem. This is the same sender family as "Wow-inducing wallcoverings for their space!" earlier today, and it's the same wallpaper story. That one scored 6 for me. This one is a near-repeat with a slightly better hook. I'm also still carrying Venus Williams, the kitchen heirlooms and the sofa edits from the last 24 hours.

## What's working

- The free-samples module ("Get up to 10 free wallcovering samples to see them in your home before you decide") is exactly how I buy wallpaper. I won't commit to a roll without seeing it on my wall.
- The three room vignettes each have one short caption and one "Shop Now" button. The layout is clean and easy to scan.
- The "Shop All Wallpaper" and "Shop All Bestsellers" buttons are clear.
- The top banner shows a real number, "Semi-Annual Bedroom & Nursery Event, Up to 65% off". It repeats lower down, so I'd find it either way.
- The photography is strong, with moody palettes and good styling. It reads as design content rather than a coupon blast.

## What's weak

- Everything is framed around kids' rooms and nurseries. That's the wrong lens for a 36-year-old renovating his own living spaces.
- The 65% off sale is for bedroom and nursery goods. It isn't clearly tied to wallpaper, so I can't tell whether the discount applies to what the email is selling.
- There's no deadline or end date I can read. That kills urgency, and the Semi-Annual banner is the only time cue.
- Below the wallpaper, it turns into a generic product grid, a card promo, an app download block and a TheDesignDesk pitch. The email loses focus there, and it's long.
- The legal fine print at the bottom is a dense wall of text. It makes me wonder what's excluded from the sale.
- It's the second wallcovering send in a day.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the free-samples offer in the hero as the primary CTA. It's the strongest conversion hook here and it's buried mid-email.
- 2. State clearly whether the 65% off applies to wallpaper, and give an end date.
- 3. Make one version for the general home audience, with wallpaper in living rooms, dining rooms and entries. Keep the nursery framing for the subscribers it fits.
- 4. Cut the module count. Drop the app block and the card promo, which pulled focus from wallpaper.
- 5. Space out the Crate-family sends. CB2 hit me fifteen times in about a week, and Wayfair and Pottery Barn have the same problem. Whoever caps frequency first gets my attention back.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's visible markup is a table-based XHTML template with several accessibility problems: about 40 images lack alt text, and the viewport meta tag blocks zoom. Header-level compliance and authentication checks couldn't be verified because the relay didn't capture the headers. The HTML source was truncated, so footer and unsubscribe markup weren't inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Not verifiable:** 74 links were skipped as tracking/click-redirect domains, so destinations, redirect chains and final HTTP status are unconfirmed.
- Third-party and first-party tracking pixels are present:
  - `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?...&n=1` through `n=5` (LiveRamp). Five pixels fire per open.
  - `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif?mi_u=...` (open pixel).
  - `https://dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/25cf0af4-...?mi_cid=...&mi_mid=...`.
- `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png?mi_u=...` appears six times with identical URLs. These are likely dynamic-content or recommendation placeholders, and the repetition is worth checking.
- The `mi_u` and `s=` parameters carry what looks like a per-recipient hash in image URLs. If that hash is an identifier, it is exposed to anything that proxies or logs the images.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text, confirmed by QA on about 40 images.** Examples:
  - Content-style images on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/`, such as `eeb8adae-...jpg`, `e6e3e776-...jpg`, `79d17caa-...jpg`, and the `.gif` files `1b3e75ad-...`, `40f08802-...`, `e74a31cc-...`. If these carry the content, the email is unreadable with images off or with a screen reader.
  - `https://s7d5.scene7.com/is/image/Crate/25%5FMI%5FBottom%5FSpacer%5F40px%5FWhite` (spacer with no alt).
  - Tracking pixels (`rlcdn`, `o.gif`, `dv.`) should use `alt=""` explicitly, so they are ignored rather than flagged.
- `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` disables pinch-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 concern).
- `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` is set on `html`, `body` and `*`. This blocks user text scaling on iOS.
- `*{... line-height:100%}` sets a global line-height of 100%, and `p{margin:0 auto; text-align:center}` is a global paragraph rule. Both can cause text collisions and unintended centering in any block that doesn't override them.
- `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` has an unescaped `&`, which is invalid under the declared XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype. It should be `&amp;`.
- There is conflicting and redundant head markup:
  - Duplicate `format-detection` metas: one `telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no`, then separate `date=no`, `address=no` and `telephone=no`.
  - Both `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type">` and `<meta charset>`.
  - The `@media (min-width:768px) img{max-width:600px}` rule is overridden by the later `(min-width:640px) img{max-width:640px}` rule, since both match at 768px and above.
- A leftover template placeholder remains in the source: `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->`. It has no functional impact.
- Positive: `<html lang="en">` is set and VML/Office namespaces are declared for Outlook.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found in the visible source. No unrendered tokens (such as `{{...}}` or `%%...%%`) appear in the truncated HTML. The tail of the source wasn't available.

- **WARN (unverified):** `Authentication-Results` is missing, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The `From` domain `mail.crateandbarrel.com` is a dedicated subdomain, which is good practice, but alignment is unconfirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to every content image. Use `alt=""` on spacers and tracking pixels, including the `scene7` spacer, the `rlcdn` pixels and `o.gif`.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta, and drop `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` from `*` and `body`.
3. Escape the title as `Crate &amp; Barrel` and consolidate the duplicate `format-detection` and charset metas.
4. Remove or scope the global `line-height:100%` and `p{text-align:center}` rules.
5. Check the identical `f3c0d22992a01946.png` placeholders to confirm they resolve to distinct content per slot, and consider whether five `rlcdn` pixels are needed.
6. Re-run the audit against the raw `.eml`, or with the relay configured to preserve headers. That would verify `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and the SPF/DKIM/DMARC results. Also run it on the untruncated body to check the footer, the unsubscribe link and the redirect chains.
