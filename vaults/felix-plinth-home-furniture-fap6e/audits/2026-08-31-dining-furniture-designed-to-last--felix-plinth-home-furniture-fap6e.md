# Dining furniture, designed to last
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-31**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is actually the sixth Crate-family email clogging my inbox this week, but this one at least earns its place — it's a dining furniture roundup, and dining furniture is literally what I'm shopping for on the second-home reno right now. The hero "dining furniture that fits your life" with the navy dining set against paneled walls is a legit good room shot, and then it just keeps going — Lakin, Edendale, Dakota, Basque, Terra — table after table after table, which either shows real range or is exactly the kind of scroll-fatigue email I complain about. There's also a "Double Rewards" + "up to 60% off Labor Day" banner buried mid-email that honestly should've been up top given I put purchases on my highest-rewards card specifically for stuff like this.
- I'd click on this one — Dakota's solid oak table caught my eye and the Edendale banquette looked like something that'd actually work in the second-home layout. Wouldn't forward it to a friend, this is a "read it myself, screenshot the Dakota table to my inspo folder" email, not a "hey check this out" email. Compare to CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" from earlier this week — that one felt like a snoozy repeat of the parent brand; this one at least picked a category (dining) and stuck the landing on relevance, even if the execution runs long.

## What's working

- Actually on-category for me — dining furniture, not the Crate & Kids nursery spam I've been drowning in all week (looking at you, "Beatrix bedroom collection" and the Halloween trick-or-treat bags).
- Multiple named collections (Lakin, Edendale, Dakota, Basque, Terra) with distinct wood tones and finishes — good for someone comparing options across a whole room, not just one table.
- The "your dining room refresh starts now" module with rugs/lighting/sideboards/all dining furniture is smart cross-sell logic — I do need a rug for that room too.
- Real oak/wood grain visible in the Dakota and Basque shots — texture and material actually readable, which matters when I'm judging "will this look cheap in person."

## What's weak

- It's exhausting — five-plus product blocks before you even hit the actual sale banner. Article's Labor Day email this week was tighter and got to the discount faster.
- The "Double Rewards + up to 60% off" Labor Day Event is buried more than halfway down. That's the actual conversion lever and it's an afterthought.
- No pricing anywhere near the top tables — I have to click through blind on Lakin, Edendale, Dakota, etc. Wayfair's Labor Day email at least stamps "70% off" right on the hero.
- The Design Desk "enter for a chance to win $500" sweepstakes and "get more done with a home office" section feel like a completely different email got stapled onto the bottom — scope creep.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the "Double Rewards + Up to 60% off Labor Day Event" banner to the top, right under the hero — that's the actual reason to open this instead of just archiving it, and right now it's on page 3.
- 2. Cut the product count in half. Pick your two or three strongest sellers (Dakota and Lakin looked the best) and cross-sell the rest via the "shop all dining furniture" link instead of full modules for each.
- 3. Put a price or "starting at $X" on at least the top two hero tables — I shouldn't have to click five times just to see if Dakota is in budget.
- 4. Drop the sweepstakes and home-office section into a separate send — it dilutes the dining focus and reads like a different campaign got tacked on.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `New oak & extendable tables, now up to 60% off through Labor Day`
