## What's working

- The red "last Double Rewards this year!" band is a clear loyalty hook, and Nov. 17 is a credible deadline.
- The 2x2 grid (furniture, bedding and bath, baby gear, rugs) shows the percentages up front, so I can scan the offers fast.
- "Up to 60% off clearance" is a real, separate deal, not a repeat of the hero.
- The Design Desk block, with a $5,000 giveaway and a free appointment, speaks to my renovation. That's a showroom and designer angle, and neither Wayfair nor Pottery Barn pushes it as hard.
- The "we love these for you" product row with prices gives me something concrete to look at.

## What's weak

- The hero is a crib, and none of my kids' rooms are in play. The sender line is "Kids SALE," so this is on me to ignore.
- The hero text is hard to read. "up to 50% off" sits on a busy dark photo, and the red band above it is cramped.
- Double Rewards shows up three times (the top band, the mid-email banner, the card block). It feels padded, and the repetition makes it read as noise.
- It's very long. The Design Desk, the card pitch, the app download and the store locator all stack up, so the offer gets buried.
- There's no swatch or sample content and no shipping or lead-time info. For furniture I care about both.

- 2. Fix the first screen: one offer, one button, with readable contrast on the hero.
- 3. Cut the Rewards repetition to one placement.
- 4. Show delivery windows and a "try a swatch" link on the furniture tiles.
- 5. Move the Design Desk higher for renovators.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** The QA check flags 30 `<img>` elements without alt text.
  - 17 are content images, for example `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/26171910-0ada-47d9-a83d-8056129f4c2f.jpg`, the two `s7d5.scene7.com` images, and the `.gif` `8c4ce5d9-…`.
  - 13 are tracking pixels (`mi.`, `rlcdn`, `dv.`). These should carry `alt=""`.
  - The check can't tell a missing attribute from an intentional `alt=""`. The 17 content images are the ones that matter if the images are links, because image-blocked or screen-reader users then get no link label.
- **Viewport:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 accessibility concern.
- **Text sizing:** `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` and `html{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` conflict with the earlier `body{-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%}`. They disable iOS text scaling.
- **Conflicting image caps:** `@media (min-width:768px){img{max-width:600px}}` is declared before `@media (min-width:640px){img{max-width:640px}}`. At 768px and wider both match, and the later rule wins, so the 600px cap never applies.
- **Source hygiene:**
  - `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` has an unescaped `&`, which is invalid in XHTML. It should be `&amp;`.
  - The `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` template placeholder comment was left in the source.
  - `meta charset` is declared twice (http-equiv and `charset`).
  - `meta format-detection` is declared three times.
  - These are harmless but sloppy.
- **Language:** `lang="en"` is present, which is good.
