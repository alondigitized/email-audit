# STEALS OF THE WEEK
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-20**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "This Week's Steals" — CB2 rolling out yet another sale-stacking email, and honestly at this point my inbox has become a CB2/Crate & Barrel monthly subscription I never signed up for twice. This is the fifth CB2/C&B-family send I've logged this month, and the déjà vu is real: I literally reviewed "Best Sellers, up to 40% off" on 9/17, and here's that exact same "up to 40% off" best-sellers module leading the email again, just retitled under a "Steals" umbrella. The marble coffee table hero is genuinely nice — I'd actually consider it for the primary's living room — but the rest is a grab bag: floor sample sale, bedding clearance, design services pitch, rugs. Too many messages, no single offer I can act on fast.
- The one thing that does land is the rewards module near the bottom — "Earn 10% Back in Rewards Dollars every day on purchases at CB2 and Crate & Barrel" — that's actually useful info for someone stacking two renovations on a rewards card like me. But it's buried below five separate sales pitches, which is backwards. I'm marking this read, not clicking, not forwarding — Pottery Barn's cozy-throws email last week did one cohesive theme and beat this on focus alone.

## What's working

- The marble coffee table hero shot is genuinely photogenic — clean, neutral, exactly the aspirational "second home" look I'm chasing.
- "Floor Sample Sale — In Your Store" signals real, finite inventory, which is a stronger hook than another designer-collab wordplay push.
- The 10% Rewards Dollars callout across CB2 and Crate & Barrel is relevant to how I actually shop — I use the highest-rewards card and track cross-brand stacking.
- Clean section dividers (Best Sellers / Floor Sample / Clearance / Design Services / Rugs) at least let me scan and bail quickly.

## What's weak

- Five distinct offers in one email with no hierarchy on which is the actual best deal — Clearance says 60% off, Best Sellers says 40% off, so which one is "the steal"?
- This is a near-repeat of the 9/17 "Best Sellers up to 40% off" email — same discount tier, same category framing, just re-skinned as "This Week's Steals."
- The Interior Design Services block and rug block feel like filler padding a sale email that ran out of actual steals.
- No urgency or deadline anywhere — "this week's" steals but no countdown or end date visible, so nothing pushes me to act now versus next week's near-identical email.
- Bedding/rug/furniture crammed together dilutes focus — Pottery Barn's single-theme cozy email (7/10) reads cleaner than this multi-sale patchwork.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick ONE hero offer (the 60% clearance, since it's the biggest number) and lead with it exclusively above the fold — cut the email down to two sections max instead of five.
- 2. Add a real deadline — "ends Sunday" or similar — since "this week's steals" implies time-boundedness that never shows up visually.
- 3. Move the Rewards Dollars stacking module up near the hero — that's the actual differentiator versus Wayfair/Article and worth leading with for repeat furniture buyers.
- 4. Cut the Interior Design Services and rug blocks from a "steals" email entirely — save them for a dedicated send so this one stays focused.
- 5. Vary the creative from the 9/17 best-sellers send — reusing the same 40%-off tier and framing within days reads like the same email twice.
