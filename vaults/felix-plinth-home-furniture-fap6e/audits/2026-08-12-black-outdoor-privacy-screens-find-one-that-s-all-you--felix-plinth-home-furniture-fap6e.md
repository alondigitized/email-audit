# BLACK OUTDOOR PRIVACY SCREENS 🔎 Find one that's all you →
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-12**
## Executive summary

- Another Wayfair grid dump, and this time the subject promised "Black Outdoor Privacy Screens" like it was going to be a focused, useful email — it's not. It's the same everything-and-the-kitchen-sink scroll I got yesterday with the chandelier email and the day before with the gazebo one: privacy screens, pergolas, slipcovers, gazebo deals, appliances, chandeliers, fountains, wall art, sheds, shelving, ovens, jewelry boxes, stock pots. That's not a themed send, that's their entire catalog with a purple banner slapped on top.
- The privacy screen photo up top is actually decent — real product, real deck, looks like something I'd genuinely consider for the back of the primary house where the neighbor's yard is too close for comfort. But it's one tile out of maybe 30, buried the second I scroll past the hero. Compare that to CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" email that scored a 7 with me — that one at least kept some editorial focus. This is just noise with a subject line cherry-picked to bait the open.
- Not clicking, not forwarding. Marking as read and moving on — this is the fourth or fifth near-identical Wayfair grid this week and I'm running on fumes for these.

## What's working

- The lead privacy screen shot is a real, relevant product photo — exactly my project right now (fencing the back deck at the second home).
- The "70% OFF Summer Markdowns" badge up top is at least visible without hunting.
- Star ratings under several tiles (slipcovers, gazebo, shelving) give a little social proof I don't usually get from Wayfair.
- The "Wayfair Rewards" and "Flexible Financing" footer icons are a nice touch if I were already deep in checkout mode — not today though.

## What's weak

- Subject says privacy screens, email delivers 30 unrelated categories — total bait and switch, same trick this brand pulled with the "COVERS & CARRY BAGS" send on 8/10 that also didn't match its own subject.
- No pricing anywhere except the vague "70% off" banner at the top — every single tile below just says "Shop now" with no dollar figure or percentage, so I have zero idea what I'm actually saving on any given item.
- Zero color-palette or material consistency — chandeliers next to shag rugs next to soup pots next to stock pots, it reads like a liquidation sale, not a curated look.
- No fit-for-my-project detail on the privacy screens themselves (height, panel count, weatherproofing) — the one thing that could've hooked me got a single generic photo and nothing else.
- This is the fourth Wayfair grid-format email I've opened this week (the 70%-off one on 8/11 scored a 5, the chandelier one a 6) — same layout, same purple bars, different product soup each time.

## Recommendations

- 1. Actually build the email around the subject line — if you're going to lead with "privacy screens," give me three or four screen options with real specs (height, coverage, material) and a real price, not one photo buried in a 30-tile wall.
- 2. Cut the catalog dump. Nobody needs sheds, soup pots, and jewelry boxes in the same email as fencing — split into category-specific sends like Article does with its collection launches.
- 3. Put an actual number on the hero offer tile, not just "70% off" as a banner — tell me what that means for the screens specifically.
- 4. Add a spec callout under the hero product (height, privacy rating, weather-resistant) since that's the actual decision driver for outdoor screening.
