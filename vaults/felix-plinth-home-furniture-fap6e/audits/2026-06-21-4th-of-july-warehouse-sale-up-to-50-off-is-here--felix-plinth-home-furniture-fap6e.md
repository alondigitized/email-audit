# 🎆 4th of July Warehouse Sale: Up to 50% off is here!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- Ten. That's the C&B family counter in my inbox now, and this one comes from **Crate & Kids** specifically — not Crate & Barrel proper. I've been renovating two homes simultaneously and I don't have a single nursery on the floor plan. So the segmentation logic here — "he's on C&B lists, blast him through the kids sub-brand too" — isn't targeting, it's just list arbitrage. I got a near-identical "It's here! Up to 50% off" from the parent brand that I already scored as the ninth send. This is the kids edition of the same email.
- The visual execution is honestly solid. Coastal hero image, clean percentage in the headline, seasonal framing that actually fits June — if I had a nursery to furnish I'd be all over this. But I don't. And the "new! gear up for summer" block mid-scroll undercuts the warehouse-clearance urgency the hero set up. Blowout or new arrivals? Pick one.
- I won't click. I won't forward it. And if C&B keeps routing me through the kids sub-brand for a home renovation I've been doing for six months, I'm going to start unsubscribing from the sub-brands one at a time until only the parent remains.

## What's working

- **Hero clarity is instant.** Sale event, percentage off, patriotic occasion — I parsed the offer in under two seconds without zooming.
- **Coastal hero image earns the season.** The ocean imagery syncs with the 4th of July framing; it doesn't feel like a re-skinned Christmas email.
- **50% is a real number.** Unlike CB2's "even more markdowns just added" send — which made me do mental math just to guess the discount — this puts the percentage directly in the hero. No mystery required.
- **Footer cross-branding is a quiet save.** Seeing CrateandBarrel and CB2 logos at the bottom at least acknowledges the family — if I want to shop the right sub-brand, the breadcrumb is there.

## What's weak

- **Wrong sub-brand, full stop.** Crate & Kids is cribs, toddler furniture, nursery décor. My purchase and browsing history has zero nursery signal. This is the kind of mismatch that makes me question whether C&B actually knows anything about me after years of transactions.
- **Tenth send in one campaign window.** Pottery Barn hit me twice on the first day of summer and I was already fatigued — this family has sent ten emails across multiple sub-brands for one sale event. Article managed to feel fresh after four outdoor sends by simply pivoting indoors; C&B's playbook is just rotating sub-brand logos on the same template.
- **"New! gear up for summer" section conflicts with the warehouse sale framing.** Clearance energy and new-arrivals energy don't coexist cleanly. One undermines the other.
- **Sign-up offer for an existing subscriber.** The "sign up, save 10%" block that appears mid-email treats me like a stranger. I'm already on your list. Pottery Barn at least occasionally hints at member-exclusive access in its sale sends; this feels like a re-acquisition module dropped into the wrong segment.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress Crate & Kids sends for customers with no children's-category purchase or browse history.** Even a blunt "has not viewed nursery category in 18 months" suppression would fix this for customers like me.
- 2. **Hard cap on C&B family sends per sale event — two max across all sub-brands.** The parent, CB2, and Crate & Kids should not be firing independently for the same Warehouse Sale week. Coordinate the sends or lose the subscriber.
- 3. **Swap the sign-up recruitment block for a member-loyalty message.** Double rewards points, early-access window, something that acknowledges I've been on the list. Wayfair does this badly; C&B has enough brand equity to do it right.
- 4. **Choose one message per send: clearance or new arrivals.** The "gear up for summer" new-collection block doesn't belong in a Warehouse Sale email.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents low-vision users from pinch-zooming. Should be removed or changed to `maximum-scale=5`.

**Conflicting desktop `img` max-width rules**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
At viewports ≥768px both queries match. Because the 640px rule is declared later, it wins via cascade — images will render at 640px wide on desktop, overriding the intended 600px cap.

**Global `line-height: 100%` via wildcard selector**
```css
* { line-height: 100%; }
```
Applied to all elements including headings and paragraphs. Can collapse line spacing in Gmail and other clients that don't inherit this correctly. Should be scoped to specific elements.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags** — `date=no`, `address=no`, and `telephone=no` are declared twice (once combined, once individually). Redundant but harmless; indicates copy-paste template debt.

**Dark mode not supported**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Explicitly opts out of dark mode. Acceptable as a deliberate choice, but Apple Mail and Outlook will force-invert background colors on dark-mode users, potentially breaking the visual layout.
