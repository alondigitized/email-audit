# FLAMELESS CANDLES at a great price.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- Sixty-one. That's where I'm at with Wayfair now, and I want to say upfront that I opened this one expecting to actually *see* flameless candles — an item that is, for the record, genuinely useful to me right now. You can't burn a real candle in a half-finished dining room that still smells like joint compound. So the subject had me. Then I opened it and the hero is a Wayfair credit card pitch. A 5% rewards program. Not a candle in sight above the fold.
- This is the exact pattern from the June 19 cat trees email — subject promises a specific thing, body delivers something else entirely. That one baited with cat trees and led with generic markdowns. This one baits with flameless candles and leads with a loyalty card pitch. The "your home. three picks." section lower down might include candles as one of the three items, but I'm not going to reward an email for burying its own subject line's promise underneath a credit card upsell. Compare this to Article's Cillian Collection email — they led with the product hero, showed it in a room, gave me a reason to care. That email knew what it was selling.
- I'm marking this read without clicking. I'm not forwarding it. If Wayfair had actually opened with a candle shot — real lifestyle photography, a room I can imagine in one of my renovation projects — I'd have clicked through immediately.

## What's working

- The 5% rewards section is clean and the offer is real — if I didn't already know about it, it'd be useful context.
- "Let's complete your space" is an okay section label that at least gestures toward a project mindset.
- The email renders cleanly — no broken images, no layout collapses, no overlapping text.
- Wayfair's grid product layout is recognizable and fast to scan; I didn't have to hunt for prices.

## What's weak

- The hero is a credit card offer, not flameless candles — the subject line made a promise the email doesn't keep above the fold.
- This is the second time in two days Wayfair has done this exact bait pattern (cat trees on June 19); I'm noticing the formula and it's eroding trust faster than any single bad send would.
- Flameless candles in June is off-season for the product category — if this was positioned as "safe for renovation spaces," that would've been a real angle. Instead it reads like a clearance-bin blast.
- The credit card pitch feels out of place in what should be a product-forward email — it's an upsell that interrupts the product story.
- No renovation context anywhere. Pottery Barn at least occasionally nods to the "you're building something" mindset in their copy; Wayfair treats me like a random internet shopper every single time.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the product.** The hero module should be a full-bleed lifestyle shot of flameless candles in a real room — moody lighting, renovation context optional but ideal. Kill the credit card upsell as the hero entirely; move it to the footer.
- 2. **Anchor to the renovation use case.** Even a single line — "no flame, no worry while you're mid-project" — would make this feel like Wayfair actually knows anything about me.
- 3. **Retire the bait-and-switch structure.** If the subject says candles, candles lead the email. Full stop. That's what CB2 does with collabs — the Todd Snyder email opened with the Todd Snyder product, not a credit card.
- 4. **Reduce cadence or add variety.** I've had four Wayfair emails in 48 hours before this one. One truly targeted send would do more work than four generic blasts.
- 5. **Give me a specific offer with a number.** "At a great price" is not a price. 20% off, $29.99, buy 2 get 1 — pick one and commit.
