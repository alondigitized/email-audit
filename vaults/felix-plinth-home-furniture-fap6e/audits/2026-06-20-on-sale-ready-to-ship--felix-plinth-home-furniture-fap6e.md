# On sale & ready-to-ship!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- Thirty. That's the Pottery Barn count in my inbox as of this morning, and I need to say it because this email lands exactly one day after three separate Summer Sale emails on June 19 alone — "It's ON!," the design services push, and the "Happening now!" follow-up. Getting a fourth variation in under 24 hours isn't a cadence, it's a compulsion. I'm starting to think the automations are broken.
- That said: "ready-to-ship" is the only genuinely new idea in this send, and I noticed it. I have two living rooms, two dining rooms, and no patience for 16-week lead times. If Pottery Barn had actually built the email around that promise — hero image of something I could receive before Labor Day, a specific category like "In-stock rugs" or "Ready lighting" — I'd have stopped scrolling. Instead, it's the same Summer Sale wrapper with "ready-to-ship" bolted onto the subject line and then immediately abandoned inside the email body. The "DELIVERED IN 1-6 WEEKS" callout under the hero is the closest they get to living up to the subject, and a six-week range tells me almost nothing.
- The "Recommended Just For You" section actually held my attention for a beat — curtain panels, looks like — which is more than I can say for the category grid, which is identical to every other Summer Sale grid they've sent. I won't click through, but I'll file this in my shopping folder the same way I do every PB send. If Article can pivot its whole email around one specific new collection and CB2 can built a full campaign around the Todd Snyder drop, Pottery Barn should be able to build a "ships this week" email instead of this.

## What's working

- **"Ready-to-ship" subject line is genuinely differentiated** from the three June 19 sends — new angle, short, no spam triggers.
- **"Earn 10% Back in Rewards" visible** in the body — I'm tracking that number across my cards and this is the right data point to surface.
- **Affirm block is present** — two homes, big ticket furniture, I actually want the installment math visible even if I'll end up putting it on the rewards card.
- **"Delivered in 1-6 Weeks" callout** is the right idea even if the range is too wide — it tells me this isn't a 12-week custom order scenario.
- **No render bugs.** Layout is clean, category grid reads fine at email width.

## What's weak

- **The subject promise is abandoned immediately.** "Ready-to-ship" is the hook; the email body delivers a generic category grid. Show me a "ships in 1-2 weeks" filter, an in-stock badge, something.
- **Fourth Summer Sale email in 24 hours.** The June 19 "Happening now! Up to 70% off" email already existed. This adds almost nothing new.
- **Bedroom imagery doesn't land for me.** Soft blue linens and bedding as the visual hero — I'm buying a sofa and a dining table, not a duvet. CB2 reads the room on who they're targeting; this doesn't.
- **"SHOP ALL SALE" is the laziest CTA they could write.** Article linked directly to the Cillian Collection. C&B's warehouse sale email pointed me to a specific category. "Shop All Sale" sends me to 4,000 SKUs.
- **Design services module reappears** — I just got a whole dedicated email about this yesterday. Feels like the templates aren't talking to each other.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Build the email around the "ready-to-ship" promise** — pull an actual in-stock-now product feed, badge items with "Ships in 1 week," create a filtered landing page. The subject is doing work the email isn't.
- 2. **Kill the design services module in the repeat context** — if I got the design email yesterday, suppress this block today. Basic suppression logic.
- 3. **Swap the hero from bedroom linens to outdoor/living room furniture** — the June 18 coastal lighting email already established the summer feel; now convert me on a bigger ticket category where Pottery Barn has real differentiation.
- 4. **Add a deadline** — Wayfair doesn't do this well either but at least Crate & Barrel's warehouse sale email has "through Sunday." "Up to 70% off" without an end date doesn't create urgency.
- 5. **Tighten the CTA copy** — "Shop In-Stock Sofas" or "Browse Ready-to-Ship Rugs" beats "Shop All Sale" every time.
- **Subject Alt A:** `What ships fast: our Summer Sale in-stock picks`
- **Subject Alt B:** `On sale now, at your door before August`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `These 70%-off pieces deliver in 1-2 weeks — not 12.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `No backorder. No wait. Summer Sale pieces ready to go.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed broken links will cause dead clicks and failed image loads for affected recipients. Authentication headers were not captured by the relay, leaving SPF/DKIM status unverifiable; compliance headers (List-Unsubscribe, List-Unsubscribe-Post) are also absent from the captured message.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Broken link (403): Credit card apply banner**
```
https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&...
```
The Key CC "Apply & Buy" banner CTA returns HTTP 403. Any recipient clicking that module hits a dead end. Likely a permissions/routing issue on the `clickcp.potterybarn.com` redirect handler for this specific campaign ID.

**FAIL — Broken image src (403): West Elm spacer**
```
http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif
```
This cross-brand tracking pixel from West Elm returns 403 and is served over HTTP (not HTTPS). It will be blocked by most modern clients regardless of the 403.

**INFO — 25 tracking links skipped:** All `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirect URLs were not probed. No confirmed failures in this set, but the two confirmed 403s above are sufficient to flag the template pipeline.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (3 confirmed):** Mixed-content images will be blocked or flagged in most clients:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` (open-tracking pixel)

The open-tracking pixel being HTTP means opens may go unrecorded in clients that enforce HTTPS.

**Alt text absent on 40+ images:** Virtually every content and footer image has no `alt` attribute, including primary campaign images (`S26_TOP_SummerSale_NoUrgency.jpg`, all `slices_0*.jpg` content tiles, product images). Recipients with images disabled see blank space with no fallback text. This is a systemic template issue, not one-off.

**Malformed CSS in `<style>` block:** PHP-style HTTP headers embedded inside a `<style>` tag in the `<head>`:
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are server-side PHP directives that have no effect in CSS and indicate a template assembly error — a dynamic header-injection block was written into the wrong output location. Browsers/clients parse this as invalid CSS and ignore it, but it signals a template rendering defect.

**Empty `<title>` tag:** `<title></title>` — some clients display the title in the tab or preview; leaving it empty is a minor rendering gap.

### 5. Compliance

**WARN — List-Unsubscribe header missing:** Not present in captured headers. RFC 2369 requires this for commercial email; major mailbox providers (Gmail, Yahoo) use it for the native unsubscribe UI. Absence increases spam complaint rate and can trigger suppression at the MBP level.

**WARN — List-Unsubscribe-Post missing:** RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is absent. Gmail and Yahoo have required this for bulk senders (>5k/day) since February 2024. Non-compliance is an active deliverability risk.

**WARN — Authentication-Results not captured:** SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from the relay-captured headers. This is a relay visibility gap, not a confirmed authentication failure — but it means any auth misconfiguration would go undetected by this pipeline.
