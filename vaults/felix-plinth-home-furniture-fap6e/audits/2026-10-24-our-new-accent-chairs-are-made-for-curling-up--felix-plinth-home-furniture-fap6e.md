# Our new accent chairs are made for curling up →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-24**
## Executive summary

- My first reaction was "finally, a send that picks a lane." After a week of Crate & Barrel Kids pileups (the Venus Williams bedroom tour, the holiday toys, the Halloween costumes), a send that's just accent chairs is on topic for me. I'm furnishing two houses, so a swivel chair or a leather lounge is a real purchase. The render came through very small, so I'm judging structure, headlines and layout, not fine print.
- It's well built. The top strip says Thanksgiving dining room event, up to 30% off. Under it is a moody hero, then a clean stack of chairs: Dunbar swivel, Yates, Apero, Cigar leather, Charleston. Each has its own "Shop [name]" button. The swatch-sample block ("try a swatch first") is the part I'd actually use, because I won't buy fabric blind. There's also a TheDesignDesk sweepstakes up to $5,000, plus an app-download block near the bottom.
- Would I click? Probably, on Dunbar or Cigar, and I'd save it to my Pinterest folder. I wouldn't forward it, because the offer is generic and I'd have to hunt for a deadline. The "Thanksgiving dining room event" strip also sits awkwardly over a lounge-chair story.

## What's working

- One category per send. Pottery Barn's "Grinchy mischief" email still wore the same maroon "Extra 20% off" strip as its last five sends. This one at least changes the story.
- Each chair gets its own button with the product name in it ("Shop Dunbar", "Shop Cigar"). That's a verb and a noun, and it beats a generic "Shop Now".
- The "best-seller" and "new" labels help me triage fast, and the "Full 360° swivel" callout is the kind of spec I want.
- The swatch CTA ("Order Free Swatches") speaks to my color-matching worry across two homes. Wayfair never offers anything like it.
- The photography is warm and editorial. The terracotta-and-leather palette is exactly what goes into my inspiration folder.

## What's weak

- The offer is buried. "Up to 30% off" lives in a thin top strip about dining rooms, not on the chairs. Nothing says which chairs are on sale or when it ends.
- There's no deadline and no shipping or lead-time info. Furniture buyers need to know whether a chair arrives in 2 weeks or 12.
- The sweepstakes and design-appointment blocks (TheDesignDesk, "chance at $5000") are a second pitch in an email that was already doing a lot. The app-download block at the bottom is a third.
- The "we love these for you" product grid near the end is mostly kitchenware and small items. Those feel like a different email.
- This is again the same brand I've been getting every day. CB2's "Now & Future Classics" at least felt like a different sender.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the offer on the chairs. Replace the dining strip with "Accent chairs up to 30% off, ends Sunday" and show the sale price on at least the lead chair.
- 2. Add delivery and lead-time info to the product cards, such as "ships in 2 weeks" or "in stock for the holidays".
- 3. Cut the sweepstakes and app blocks, or move them to a separate send. Keep swatches as the secondary CTA.
- 4. Cap send frequency across the Crate family. Article's "Give guests a cozy stay" had the same terracotta mood without hitting me five times in two days.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Link probing was not possible.** 67 links were skipped as tracking/click-redirect domains, so destinations, redirect chains and 404s are unverified.
- **A subscriber-level identifier is in image URLs.** The same hash appears in first-party and third-party URLs:
  - `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/560d850e7e517ede.png?mi_u=dbe8da718e2997cce014546f16c5722eaa3c322f28d40f0a8386f8fd101…`

  - `rlcdn.com` is LiveRamp's domain, and the `s=` value matches the `mi_u` prefix.
  - Anyone who forwards the email or opens it in a preview exposes this identifier. It is also shared with a third party.
- **Five identical third-party pixels.** `sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif` fires five times (`n=1` to `n=5`). That adds latency and gives clients that block remote content or trackers more to fail on.
- **Open and dynamic-content pixels.** `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif` and `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/8fa7d4e9-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…` are open-tracking or dynamic-content calls. These are expected, but they have no `alt` and depend on image loading.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `alt` text.** About 34 images are flagged:
  - 19 on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/{1,47}/…` (jpg, png, gif). These look like content and hero images, including 3 animated GIFs: `f314c9d2-…gif`, `64cd2fda-…gif`, `f3246943-…gif`.
  - 7 on `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png`, which look like dynamic product recommendations.
  - `971aa10f-…jpg` and the 5 `rlcdn` pixels.
  - The `o.gif` open pixel and the `dv.crateandbarrel.com` pixel.
  - If the content images are linked, a screen reader or an image-blocking client gets no text, and the link may be announced as a bare URL. Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""`.
- **Viewport blocks zoom.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` disables pinch-zoom on some clients, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Unescaped ampersand.** `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` has a raw `&` under an XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype. It should be `&amp;`.
- **Duplicate head tags.** There are two charset declarations (`http-equiv` and `<meta charset>`). `format-detection` is declared four times, with conflicting values: `telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no`, then `date=no`, `address=no` and `telephone=no`. This is harmless but redundant.
- **Text resizing is disabled.** `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;…}` blocks user text scaling in WebKit.
- **Leftover template comment.** `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` has no rendering impact but shows the template wasn't cleaned.
