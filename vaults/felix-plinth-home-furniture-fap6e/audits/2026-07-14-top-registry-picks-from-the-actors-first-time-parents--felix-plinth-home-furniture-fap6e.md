# Top registry picks from the actors & first-time parents →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- Forty-one. I said it in the last review and I have to say it again, because this email lands in the same inbox slot, on the same day, from the same sub-brand. This time it's Crate & Kids doing the celebrity registry pitch — "the actors" as first-time parents, a curated grid of nursery furniture, clean editorial layout, soft cream-and-sage palette. Visually, it's doing everything right. Editorially, it's misfiring at me.
- I'm not expecting a child. I'm not shopping for a registry. I'm mid-renovation on two adult homes and the only reason I know what Crate & Kids is selling this week is that they've been in my inbox every few hours since the first of the month. This email landed roughly four hours after the "New bedding for kids, toddlers & babies" send — also Crate & Kids, also baby content. Two children's sends in one afternoon from the same sub-brand. My frustration isn't with the creative, it's with the targeting.
- The celebrity angle is the biggest structural problem in the subject line: "the actors" — which actors? If this is a recognizable couple I'm supposed to know, name them. Pottery Barn's Design Services email two weeks ago didn't make me guess what the hook was. I'm not clicking into an email to find out who the vague celebrities are. I'll mark this read without opening.

## What's working

- **Clean editorial hierarchy.** Logo → hero lifestyle shot → curated product grid. The eye moves naturally down the page, which is more than I can say for a lot of Wayfair sends that pile CTAs on top of each other.
- **Aspirational lifestyle imagery.** The hero image looks warm, soft, and well-composed — appropriate Crate & Barrel aesthetic, not a product dump.
- **Trusted sender.** I know exactly what Crate & Kids is and the quality tier it represents. Zero brand confusion.
- **No hard-sell clutter.** No "ENDS TONIGHT" banners, no stacked promo blocks. It's clean. CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" email felt busier and less confident.

## What's weak

- **"The actors" — unnamed.** If I don't know which celebrity couple this is, the subject line is just noise. Name them or drop the hook.
- **Zero offer.** No discount, no free shipping, no loyalty reward. It's a pure editorial send with no transactional reason to click. Article can pull that off because the content itself is genuinely useful for my renovation. A celebrity registry grid is not.
- **Wrong cohort for me.** This is baby/nursery content. I'm furnishing adult rooms in two houses. C&B has my purchase history — they know I haven't bought a crib. The segmentation is lazy.
- **Second C&B Kids send today.** The "New bedding for kids" email was already in my inbox from this morning. Back-to-back from the same sub-brand on the same day reads like an automation failure, not a campaign.
- **Female-forward lifestyle imagery.** The hero skews visibly toward a mom-in-nursery aesthetic. I'm a 36-year-old male. I'm not the primary read here and the creative confirms it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name the celebrity.** "Top registry picks from [Name] & [Name]" is a completely different email. The vague "the actors" construction is a missed intrigue. If you can't name them (contractual?), reframe to the content: "The nursery edit we can't stop recommending."
- 2. **Segment out the male-renovator list.** I've bought curtain rods and a sofa from C&B. I've never bought anything from Crate & Kids. That signal is available. Use it.
- 3. **One clear CTA with a reason to click now.** Even "Free shipping on all registry items this week" changes the math. Right now there's no hook beyond celebrity association.
- 4. **Don't double-send from the same sub-brand in one afternoon.** Merge this into tomorrow's editorial slot.
- **Subject Alt A:** `[Actor Name] & [Actor Name]'s full nursery list →`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The nursery picks every first-time parent is saving`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Their glider, their crib, their bedding — all in one place.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Real registry picks, curated down to the last pillow.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Title mismatch**: `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` on an email branded and sent as *Crate & Kids*. Screen readers and some webmail clients surface the `<title>` tag.

- **Viewport zoom lock**: `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` — `maximum-scale=1` prevents user-initiated zoom on mobile, violating WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).

- **Conflicting `img` max-width breakpoints**:
  ```css
  @media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
  @media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
  ```
  Because the 768px block appears *after* the 640px block, it takes precedence for viewports ≥768px, pinning images to 600px rather than the intended 640px. The ordering should be reversed, or merged into a single rule.

- **`-webkit-text-size-adjust` conflict**: `html` sets it to `100%`; the wildcard `*` selector immediately overrides it with `none`, which can suppress WebKit text scaling on mobile.

- **`line-height: 100%` on `*`**: Global wildcard override of `line-height` can collapse multi-line text in elements that do not explicitly re-declare it.

- **`alt` text coverage**: Cannot verify — truncated before `<img>` tags appear in body. All product images must carry descriptive `alt` text; decorative spacers should use `alt=""`.
