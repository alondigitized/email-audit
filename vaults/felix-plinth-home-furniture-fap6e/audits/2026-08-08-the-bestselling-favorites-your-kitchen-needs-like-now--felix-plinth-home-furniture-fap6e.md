# The bestselling favorites your kitchen needs (like, now) →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- Finally — a Crate & Barrel email that's actually about a kitchen. After the week I've had with this brand (backpacks, nursery makeovers, kids' bedding), this one's a relief just for being in my category. "The 5 bestsellers your kitchen needs now" is a numbered list format — espresso machine, cookware, cutlery, bakeware, tools — and it's got real star ratings and review counts stamped on each tile, which is the first trust signal I've seen from C&B all week that isn't a lifestyle photo of someone else's kid.
- That said, it's still a wall. Five full sections stacked vertically, each with its own hero photo, rating block, and CTA button, plus a bakery collab and a "Design Desk" $5000 sweepstakes bolted onto the bottom. By the time I hit the co-brand card pitch at the very end I've mentally checked out. Compare this to Pottery Barn's "Everything for a living room made for gathering" send I read earlier — that one at least had a single clear hero moment up top before it got into the room-by-room breakdown. This one opens strong with the espresso machine but then just keeps going with equal visual weight for all five items, so nothing actually reads as "the" bestseller.
- I'd click into the cookware or cutlery section — those are genuine consideration items for the reno kitchen — but I wouldn't forward this. It's solid, not shareable.

## What's working

- The hero product (dark green drip coffee maker + mug set) is a real, specific item, not a vague "shop now" banner.
- Star ratings and review counts on every single tile — cookware, cutlery, bakeware all show "★★★★★" with counts like "1,241 Reviews" — that's the honest social proof CB2 and Article rarely bother with.
- The numbered 01-05 structure gives it a clear browsing logic, like a mini catalog rather than a random product dump.
- Category is 100% on-target for me — kitchen gear for a renovation is exactly what I'm shopping for, unlike every kids' item this brand's shoved at me this week.

## What's weak

- Five nearly-identical full-width sections in a row is a lot of scrolling before you even hit the bakery collab and the sweepstakes — I lost momentum by section three.
- No visible price or %-off anywhere on any of the five bestseller tiles — just "Shop Bestselling [Category]" buttons. I don't know if I'm looking at a $40 spatula or a $400 mixer until I click.
- The "enter for a chance to win $5000" Design Desk consultation banner near the bottom feels bolted on and unrelated to the bestsellers framing — it's a different offer type crammed into the same scroll.
- Zero urgency or timing cue anywhere — no "this week only," no restock countdown, nothing that tells me why I should act today versus next month.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this from five full sections to a top-3 or a tighter grid — Wayfair's "Clearout: 72 Hours" send proved that compressing choices under one clear timer drives more action than an endless scroll of equal-weight tiles.
- 2. Put a price or discount on at least the hero item — "the 5 bestsellers your kitchen needs now" with zero pricing makes me do the math myself, and I won't.
- 3. Move the Design Desk $5000 sweepstakes into its own send — it's diluting the bestseller narrative and reads like two emails stitched together.
- 4. Add a rewards/card callout near the top if I'm a cardholder — the "get the card" pitch is buried at the very bottom where nobody scrolls.
