# Wall decor that’s back in stock

- Ten. At minimum ten CB2 emails since May 17th, and this one drops the same calendar day as "These colors never go unnoticed" — which I also got this morning. I'm running dual-track fatigue at this point: Crate & Barrel family fatigue AND CB2-specific fatigue, and they're compounding. My thumb was already pre-loaded to swipe.

This isn't a discount play, it's a utility email: here are things you wanted that disappeared. And I'm renovating two houses, so wall mirrors are genuinely on the shopping list. - I'd mark it as read and circle back later, not click right now. I wouldn't forward it — wall mirrors are specific enough that none of my friends would care. But I'd bookmark the Anne Mirror tab in my head. That's more than most of this week's CB2 volume achieved.

## What's working

- **Back-in-stock framing is legitimately useful.** After four straight discount emails, this one carries actual information — a thing I wanted came back. That's different.
- **Mirror-heavy product grid is renovation-relevant.** I'm actively hunting wall mirrors for both houses; seeing five or six specific options with names and photos is exactly how I want to be browsed.
- **"SHOP WALL DECOR" CTA is clean and honest.** Verb + noun, no vague "Explore" nonsense. Pottery Barn does this better than most brands, and CB2 matched it here.
- **No render bugs.** After Wayfair's arrow-emoji subject-line chaos and CB2's own inconsistency, this laid out cleanly — hero, products, CTA, footer, done.
- **SEIZE THE MOMENT hero image is atmospheric.** That dark, moody room with the round mirror anchoring it — it's CB2 at its best aesthetically. Doesn't convert me, but it sets tone.

## What's weak

- **Same-day double send.** "These colors never go unnoticed" already hit my inbox today. CB2 sending two emails on May 29th is the thing that makes subscribers start hunting for the unsubscribe link.
- **No sale, no clear offer — and no acknowledgment of that.** Back-in-stock is a valid reason to email, but there's nothing to sweeten the re-engagement. Article ran a "last chance" finish on May 26th that paired urgency with an actual price signal. CB2 gave me product availability and a flat full-price CTA.
- **SEIZE THE MOMENT hero copy is placeholder-level.** It connects to the back-in-stock narrative only if I work to make the leap. Compare to how Article's brand-builder on May 23rd did something similar with restraint and landed cleaner.
- **No pricing or dimensions visible in the grid.** I care about whether the Anne Mirror is 30" or 48" before I click. This sends me to the PDP cold.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress one of the two May 29th sends** — or at minimum merge the wall decor grid into "These colors never go unnoticed" as a secondary section. Two CB2 emails in a single day is the fastest way to train me to batch-delete.
- 2. **Reframe the hero to make "back in stock" the story.** "You weren't the only one watching this" or "These sold out once. Now they're back." is more compelling than SEIZE THE MOMENT, which floats free of context.
- 3. **Add a dimension or key spec under each product name** — even "32"W × 42"H" under Anne Mirror saves me a click and removes friction on big-ticket wall pieces.
- 4. **Surface at least one "as-low-as" price anchor.** Even full-price, showing $249 vs $649 range tells me if this grid is in my budget window before I commit to clicking.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `font-display: swap` in email context (dead code):**
Three `@font-face` declarations include `font-display: swap`, which has zero support in any email client. It is harmless but adds noise and suggests the CSS was copied from a web stylesheet without adaptation.

```css
/* line ~80 in <head> <style> */

```

**Issue — Duplicate `@font-face` family name collision:**
`"Lineto Brown Sub"` is declared three times: once with no weight, once at `font-weight: 400`, and once at `font-weight: 600`. The unweighted declaration maps to a Light variant (`lineto-brown-light.woff`), which will conflict with the weight-400 declaration on clients that process all three rules. The last matching rule wins, making the unweighted block effectively dead:

```css
/* Declaration 1 — no weight, loads Light variant */
@font-face { font-family: "Lineto Brown Sub"; src: url(".../lineto-brown-regular.eot"); ... }

/* Declaration 2 — weight 400, loads Light variant */  
@font-face { font-family: "Lineto Brown Sub"; src: url(".../lineto-brown-light.woff2"); font-weight: 400; }

/* Declaration 3 — weight 600, loads Regular variant */
@font-face { font-family: "Lineto Brown Sub"; src: url(".../lineto-brown-regular.woff2"); font-weight: 600; }
```
