#  ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ UP TO 70% OFF ⭐ ⭐ ⭐
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- This is Wayfair email number — I've genuinely lost count. After the low-maintenance rugs send yesterday (June 30), the 8x10 outdoor rugs the day before, and the ottomans-plus-bird-baths double-tap on June 29, I'm now looking at a Fourth of July Clearance email flanked by three gold stars on each side of the subject. That's the email equivalent of someone waving both arms at you across a parking lot.
- I opened it because 70% off is actually meaningful when you're furnishing two homes at once. But the email itself is a scroll of tiny product tiles — bedroom sets, outdoor chairs, wall art, what appears to be a set of dumbbells — all crammed into a two-column grid with almost no breathing room. The "picked just for you" label above one section is working very hard for something that doesn't feel remotely targeted.
- I wouldn't click. I'd scroll for about four seconds, decide nothing jumped out, and close it. Not forwarding this to anyone — my partner has enough tabs open.

## What's working

- **70% off is a real number.** Not the floor-padding "up to 40%" most Wayfair sends lean on — the 70% headline in the hero is the reason I opened it at all.
- **"Fourth of July Clearance" gives the sale a timestamp.** Feels like there's an actual end date, which is more honest than a perpetual "sale."
- **Star ratings visible on individual tiles.** I look at stars before I read copy — having them in-grid saves me a click.
- **Free delivery over $35 banner is prominent.** Useful friction-reduction for a basket-builder email where I might be adding two or three items.
- **Wayfair is a brand I actually buy from.** The sender name alone pulled this out of the "delete on preview" pile.

## What's weak

- **The subject is spam-signal dressed up as enthusiasm.** Three stars, ALL CAPS, three more stars — I checked twice that it didn't bounce into my promotions folder first.
- **Dumbbells.** There are weights in a home-decor clearance email. Wayfair sells everything, fine, but mixing fitness equipment into a send where I'm shopping for a coffee table is brand-diluting noise.
- **The grid is exhausting.** Dozens of tiles, two columns, thumbnails too small to evaluate. CB2's "SALE OF THE SUMMER" from a few days ago had one confident hero and two supporting tiles — the opposite philosophy, and far more effective at stopping my scroll.
- **"Picked just for you" isn't picked for me.** The items under that label span outdoor furniture, bedroom sets, and wall art with no apparent thread. Article's 4th of July send at least surfaced product types I'd actually browsed. This reads like a bestsellers grid with a personalization label dropped on top.
- **Four emails in 36 hours.** I'm in "batch and ignore" mode with Wayfair now. The low-maintenance rugs send yesterday already pushed the limit — this one tips into fatigue.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with one hero product, not forty.** Pick the single best clearance deal in a likely category (bed frame, sofa, area rug) and build around it with a real "was / now" price. That's what drives a click; a scatter-shot grid drives a tab close.
- 2. **Drop the triple-star flanking in the subject.** It reads as desperation and risks filter flags. A clean line like "Fourth of July Clearance — up to 70% off" is more credible and more clickable.
- 3. **Consolidate the send volume.** One great email beats four mediocre ones. Yesterday's rugs send was already borderline; this one deepens the fatigue hole.
- 4. **Pull the dumbbells.** Segment fitness equipment entirely out of the home-decor clearance flow.
- 5. **Earn the "picked for you" label.** If I've browsed bed frames, show bed frames. Don't label a generic bestsellers row as curated — subscribers notice.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 4th of July deals: beds, rugs, outdoor — up to 70% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ends July 4: clearance pricing on the pieces you've been watching. Free delivery over $35.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Thousands of items cut to clearance. Free delivery over $35 through the holiday.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Font format gap**: `@font-face` loads only `.woff`. No `.woff2` (25–30% smaller, supported by all modern clients) and no `.eot` (Outlook desktop). Fallback `mso-font-alt: "Arial"` is present, so Outlook degrades gracefully, but modern Apple Mail / iOS Mail clients will download a larger file unnecessarily.

- **Fixed-pixel heights on mobile content containers** (confirmed in CSS):
  - `.ProductRec-name`: `height: 48px !important` with `word-break: break-all` — long product names will be clipped; `break-all` also produces mid-word breaks that damage readability.
  - `.ProductRec-stars`, `.ProductRec-shippingCopy`: `height: 16px` — if star images fail to load, the row collapses to 16px with no visible fallback.
  - `.ProductRec-price`: `height: 40px`, `.ProductRec-salesCopy`: `height: 28px` — dynamic pricing/copy that exceeds these heights will be clipped on mobile.

- **Single responsive breakpoint**: Only `480px` and `320px` media queries are defined. No tablet breakpoint (600–768px range). On iPad/Android tablets the email renders at desktop width, which may produce very small tap targets.

- **No `alt` text visibility** in truncated source — cannot confirm images have `alt` attributes. Required for clients with images-off default (Outlook, corporate Gmail).
