# Don’t miss this: Up to 60% off the Summer Sale!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-nine. By my running count, this is email twenty-nine from the Crate & Barrel family since July 8th. And I'll say this for it: it's furniture. Actual furniture, or at least the promise of discounted furniture — not a nursery chair, not a birthday gift shop for children I don't have, not a soccer sweepstakes. The hero loads clean, "up to 60% off" lands immediately, and the category nav underneath — bed & bath, kitchen, outdoor, rugs, clearance — reads like a two-house renovation checklist. That's something.
- But "up to 60%" is doing exactly what it's built to do: make me think I'm getting the good number while the sofa I actually want is sitting at 20%. And the hero image is a moody coastal rocks shot. Pretty. Zero furniture. Compare that to Pottery Barn's "Get the look: The layered bed" from this week — I knew what I was clicking before the email finished loading. Here I'm clicking blind and hoping the sale section doesn't turn out to be 60% off candlestick holders I already own. The buried TheDesignDesk offer — free room design, no catch — is the single most useful thing in this email for someone actively renovating, and it's sitting below an app download CTA.
- I'd open this, scroll once, check the outdoor dining section, and mark it read. I would not click through to the full sale and I definitely wouldn't forward it. The cadence alone has trained me to assume the stuff I want isn't the stuff that's actually 60% off.

## What's working

- **The 60% off Summer Sale is squarely in my lane** — two houses, multiple rooms still needing furniture, a discount ceiling at a brand I already buy from.
- **Category nav does real work** — bed & bath, kitchen, outdoor, rugs, clearance all in one scannable row covers both properties without me having to go hunting.
- **TheDesignDesk offer is a genuine differentiator** — free design help is something CB2 doesn't have and Wayfair definitely doesn't have; this is a real loyalty perk buried in the wrong place.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds together from hero to footer.
- **Heritage-inspired arrivals section** breaks the pure-discount hammer just enough to feel like a brand with something to say.

## What's weak

- **Twenty-nine emails in five days** — no urgency signal in this send means anything, because I've been primed to ignore every "don't miss this" since send number twelve.
- **The hero shows me rocks, not furniture** — Pottery Barn put product in the hero this week; Crate & Barrel put an ocean. I shouldn't have to click to find out if anything I want is even on sale.
- **"Up to 60% off" is a hedge, not a promise** — the actual discount on any item I'd buy is almost certainly on the low end of that range, and I know it.
- **App download and referral program** take up real estate in the body that could show me specific sale pieces — I've already downloaded the app, and I'm not turning into a referral engine at email twenty-nine.
- **No end date visible anywhere** — "Summer Sale" could mean this week or this month and I have no way to calibrate urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put furniture in the hero image.** The outdoor dining table that's 60% off, with a specific price shown — that's the hero. A coastal rock formation is screensaver content. Article's "New arrivals just landed" send last week had product in frame above the fold; I opened it without hesitation.
- 2. **Add a sale end date to the hero copy.** "Up to 60% off — ends July 18" costs nothing and actually creates the urgency "don't miss this" tries and fails to manufacture.
- 3. **Move TheDesignDesk above the app download.** A free room design service is more useful to a renovation buyer than an app re-download nudge. Flip the order.
- 4. **Personalize the category nav or product grid.** I've browsed outdoor dining on this site. CB2's NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE email didn't know what I wanted either — but C&B has more data on me and should be using it.
- 5. **Kill "Don't miss this" as the opener.** It's the same subject line skeleton Wayfair ran for their 48-hour clearance this week.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The outdoor dining pieces you're watching are in the sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Summer Sale: 60% off ends soon — your saved items may be included`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free design help included — build your room list while prices are low.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Outdoor, kitchen, rugs — the rooms you're working on are all in the sale.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Conflicting `img` max-width media queries**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
The 768px rule is declared after the 640px rule. On screens ≥768px both rules match; the last-declared wins (600px). On screens 640–767px only the 640px rule fires (640px). This is probably the intended behavior, but the ordering is confusing and fragile — a future edit could silently flip the cascade.

**No dark mode support**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Forces light rendering in Apple Mail and iOS Mail. No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` override exists. Background `#e6e7e8` and any hardcoded white content cells will invert unpredictably in clients that ignore this meta (Yahoo, Gmail Android).

**Aggressive universal `line-height: 100%` reset**
```css
* { line-height: 100%; }
```
Applied to every element. This collapses multi-line text and can make body copy illegible in some Outlook versions where `mso-line-height-rule: exactly` is also set on the same selector.

**`<title>` uses bare `&` in XHTML context**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
XHTML 1.0 requires `&amp;`. A strict XML parser will reject the document. Most email clients tolerate it, but it's a spec violation.

**Wrong generic font family for Times New Roman**
```css
.mso-font-fix-times_new_roman { font-family: 'Times New Roman', sans-serif; }
```
The fallback should be `serif`, not `sans-serif`. No functional impact in Outlook (the class exists for VML fallback), but it's a latent correctness bug.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
The combined declaration on line 1 already covers all signals:
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
```
Three separate single-attribute declarations follow redundantly. No client behavior impact, but dead markup.

**Alt text** — cannot assess from truncated source. Must verify all `<img>` tags carry meaningful `alt` attributes.
