# ONE DAY LEFT: Warehouse Sale
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- Eleven. That's my CB2 email count since July 1st. I know because I've been tracking. Yesterday's "UP TO 50% OFF RIGHT NOW" already felt like a retailer screaming into the void, and today drops "ONE DAY LEFT: Warehouse Sale" — except when I open it, it's running *two* separate sales simultaneously: a Warehouse Sale at 60% off and a July 4th Sale at 50% off. The July 4th creative is still live. On July 6th. Two days after the Fourth. I've seen this exact confusion in every CB2 email since July 4th and it hasn't resolved.
- Here's the thing though — "Warehouse Sale" is actually the right reframe. It ages better than "July 4th Sale" and the product photography earns the scroll: that dark velvet lounge chair in the hero, the canopy bed setup, the rattan pendant. These are rooms I'm actively trying to build. The 60% figure is real money on furniture I'd actually buy.
- But I can't give full credit to an email that still has a "JULY 4TH SALE" red-banner section buried below the fold on July 6th. That's a brand that didn't swap its creative and hoped I wouldn't notice. I noticed. Mark as read, maybe return to site directly later.

## What's working

- The countdown timer (01 day, 07 hours) at the very top earns its place — it's a concrete forcing function, not decorative noise, and it aligns with the "ONE DAY LEFT" subject.
- 60% off Warehouse Sale is a harder offer than anything Crate & Barrel led with in their review-led email earlier this week — the ★★★★★ send leaned social proof, never led with a discount this steep.
- The velvet chair in the hero and the canopy bed scene are legitimately aspirational — the product photography is doing work.
- The "MORE FOR YOU" personalized grid at the bottom is the most honest personalization in any CB2 email I've seen this week — specific chairs and benches I might actually recognize from browsing.
- "SHOP THE SALE" is a clean, unambiguous CTA — better than Wayfair's habit of promising tub faucets and delivering a sprawling category page.

## What's weak

- The July 4th Sale section is still live — a red-background "JULY 4TH SALE / UP TO 50% OFF*" block sitting below the warehouse hero on July 6th is a creative mistake that undermines the urgency of both offers.
- Two competing sale percentages (60% and 50%) in the same email without explanation create doubt: which products are which? Is 60% only warehouse clearance? Why is July 4th 50% not 60%?
- Zero shipping callout — I'm buying furniture for two homes. No "free shipping on orders over X" in this email is an actual miss for how I decide to click.
- Pottery Barn's "HOURS LEFT" email yesterday, which I scored higher, at least committed to one offer and one message; CB2 keeps hedging with stacked promotions.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the July 4th Sale creative entirely** — the Warehouse Sale framing is stronger, evergreen, and doesn't expire. Running both dilutes both.
- 2. **Add a shipping threshold in the hero or immediately below the CTA** — even "Free shipping on furniture orders over $500" would convert renovators like me who are calculating landed cost.
- 3. **Separate the 60% and 50% product pools clearly** — if warehouse items are 60% and July 4th items are 50%, say that. One sentence of copy saves the confusion.
- 4. **Consolidate to one email today** — Pottery Barn did a cleaner job by landing HOURS LEFT on a single message rather than a double-down send. If this is truly the last day, one focused email at 8am beats two competing sends.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Warehouse Sale ends tomorrow — up to 60% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `60% off warehouse: chairs, rugs, beds — ends tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping on orders over $500. Shop clearance before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The deep-cut stuff — warehouse prices, one day left.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CONFIRM]** `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
This prevents user zoom on mobile — a WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 violation. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[CONFIRM]** Production template artifact comment left in `<head>`:
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
Typo ("IMPUT" vs "INPUT") and the comment itself confirms this is a dev template placeholder that was never removed before send.

**[CONFIRM]** Double-slash in four font `src` URLs:
```
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2
```
The double-slash (`//`) after the domain is technically a valid path (servers normalize it), but it is malformed and will generate 301/302 redirects on some CDN configurations. The single-slash variants used elsewhere in the same file are correct.

**[CONFIRM]** Mobile breakpoint is `max-width: 418px`, not the standard `600px`:
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { ... }
```
This only triggers responsive styles on very narrow screens (iPhone SE and narrower). Most modern phones (375px–430px CSS width at 2–3× DPR) may or may not hit this depending on device. Should be audited against target device matrix — if intentional, document it.

**[CONFIRM]** `*` selector resets `line-height: 100%` globally:
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; ... line-height: 100%}
```
This will collapse multi-line text in elements that don't explicitly override `line-height`, potentially causing text to overlap in some clients. This is a legacy pattern but carries real rendering risk.
