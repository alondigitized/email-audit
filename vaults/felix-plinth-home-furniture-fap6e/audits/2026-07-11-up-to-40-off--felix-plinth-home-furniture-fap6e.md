# UP TO 40% OFF
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- Nineteen. That's the CB2 count since July 1st, and I want to be specific about what broke me here: I already got "Up to 40% off is on now" two days ago, on July 9. This email has the same offer, three words stripped from the subject, and hit send again. Crate & Barrel literally resent an identical email last week and I called it out in the audit — CB2 just did the quieter version of the same thing.
- Inside, the email is clean. The Summer Edit hero is exactly what CB2 does well — minimal, white space, that dramatic dark round table front and center. The "MORE FOR YOU" personalized grid at the bottom is doing real work: platform bed, folding brass table, dining chair options, things that are genuinely on my list for both renovations. If you're going to blast me nineteen times in eleven days, at least make the personalization section actually personal. This one earns it.
- What kills the momentum is a "LEAVE US A REVIEW, WIN $1000!" module dropped dead in the middle of the scroll, right between the hero product and the personalized grid. I was building toward clicking and hit a conversion speed bump. I'd mark this as read. If I had the email open while browsing anyway, I'd probably click the platform bed link. Forward to a friend? No. This is inbox wallpaper.

## What's working

- **The Summer Edit hero is on-brand and sharp.** Clean type hierarchy, that dark dramatic round table, no visual noise — CB2 didn't break its aesthetic here.
- **"MORE FOR YOU" personalization earns its place.** Six specific products with names and visible pricing — platform bed, folding tables, dining chairs — are pulling from categories I'm actually shopping. This is what Article does on their best sends and what Wayfair's first-apartment checklist got right: editorial restraint applied to real inventory.
- **Loyalty math is visible.** "EARN 10% BACK" in the footer is real information for someone tracking rewards stacking across a Crate & Barrel rewards account. Pottery Barn's recent sends bury this — CB2 calling it out clearly is a win.
- **Free shipping is flagged.** For furniture orders this matters, and it's visible without hunting for it.

## What's weak

- **Same sale, different subject.** "Up to 40% off is on now" was July 9. This is "UP TO 40% OFF." That's the campaign minus three words, two days later, with no new hook. Suppress this for anyone who opened or clicked the July 9 send — they know.
- **Review widget is a scroll-stopper in the wrong direction.** "LEAVE US A REVIEW, WIN $1000!" mid-email, before I've clicked a single product, is asking me to do work during the transaction. Post-purchase trigger, not mid-browse.
- **No deadline anywhere.** No end date in the subject, not in the hero, not in the footer. "Up to 40% off" with no expiry reads as a permanent clearance rack, and it kills urgency the same way the July 9 email killed it — which is why that one only converted me to a skim.
- **Four footer benefit blocks compete.** Free ship, 10% back, let's celebrate, get 10% off — four separate messages below the fold, and none of them win. For a rewards-conscious buyer, "EARN 10% BACK" is the most powerful offer in the email and it's sharing space with three others.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress the send for July 9 openers/clickers.** This is the single highest-ROI change. Anyone who already opened "Up to 40% off is on now" has been told. Re-reaching them with a stripped subject burns relationship capital without adding information.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline to the hero.** "Ends Sunday July 13" or "Last 48 hours" gives me a reason to act today instead of closing the tab. Article's clean pivot after their 4th of July sale worked precisely because they stopped saying "sale" and started saying something new — CB2 needs a signal the calendar moved.
- 3. **Move "MORE FOR YOU" up, above the review widget.** The personalized grid is the differentiated content. Lead with it after the hero — or even test it as the hero itself.
- 4. **Kill the mid-scroll review module.** Move it to the post-purchase confirmation email where it actually belongs.
- 5. **Consolidate footer to one message.** Rewards math is the winner for a returning CB2 account. Give it the whole footer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your saved items are in the sale — ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Platform bed. Dining table. 40% off through the weekend.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The Summer Edit sale runs through July 13 — your saved pieces are in it.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Personalized picks from the 40% off edit. The stuff you've been watching.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** disables pinch-to-zoom on iOS Safari — an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4) and flagged by Apple's Mail Privacy Protection crawler:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Mobile breakpoint is 418 px** (`@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)`). Most email clients trigger mobile layouts at 600 px or device width. A 418 px breakpoint means devices between 419–599 px (many Android phones) receive the desktop layout. Standard practice is `max-width: 600px`.

**Cross-domain font dependency on a sister brand**: `HNW05` is loaded from `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/`. If that domain's CDN has an outage or the fonts are moved, CB2 emails silently fall back without notice. Fonts should be hosted on CB2's own domain or a dedicated CDN.

**Duplicate `@font-face` for "Lineto Brown Sub"**: the family name is declared three times with different `src` URLs. The browser/client resolves via cascade; the last declaration wins, which may not be the intended one. Consolidate into a single block with a `src` list.
