# Enter to win $4,199 in back-to-school goodies!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- Fifteen. That's where I am with the Crate & Barrel family, and this is the second version of the exact same giveaway that's hit my inbox today. "Your chance to win $4,199" was already in here this morning. Now it's "Enter to win $4,199 in back-to-school goodies." Same prize pool, different subject line, same campaign — sent twice before end of business. That's not a marketing strategy, that's a send-calendar fire.
- I don't have kids. I'm not buying backpacks. I'm trying to decide between two pendant lights for my secondary dining room while managing a concurrent gut renovation on the primary. Crate & Kids is a brand I'm technically on the list for because the Crate & Barrel CRM swept me in, but every email from them is a reminder that the umbrella doesn't actually understand what I buy. Pottery Barn doesn't cc me into Pottery Barn Kids campaigns at this frequency. That's the gap.
- The production is clean, I'll give it that. The giveaway hero is clear, the layout doesn't break, and whoever thought to tuck a fall arrivals module in the middle had the right instinct. But I'm not entering a sweepstakes for school supplies and I'm definitely not clicking after this much inbox noise. Mark as read, second time today.

## What's working

- **Specific dollar amount in the hero.** "$4,199 in prizes" is concrete in a way that most sweepstakes copy refuses to be — and it reads as more credible than "win big" vagueness.
- **Sponsor logo grid adds legitimacy.** Six brand logos laid out cleanly gives the giveaway more credibility than a solo-brand sweepstakes typically earns.
- **The fall arrivals hook is structurally smart.** Embedding "300+ new fall arrivals, starting at $20" acknowledges that not everyone is entering — someone in the segment might want to shop. Article runs this pattern well: editorial or promo entry, catalog underneath.
- **Clean render, no layout breaks.** After the CB2 volume I've been processing this month, a clean mobile layout with no overlapping text is legitimately appreciated.
- **Enter CTA is unambiguous.** One button, one action, visible without scrolling. That's more than I can say for CB2's "APRES POOL" email, which needed three scrolls to find the actual CTA.

## What's weak

- **This giveaway already hit me once today.** The brand history shows "Your chance to win $4,199" landed this morning. This is the second send of the same campaign within the same calendar day. That's not a frequency problem — that's a mistake.
- **Wrong audience, wrong brand.** The Crate & Barrel CRM has my purchase history. I buy dining furniture, rugs, lighting. The system knows this. Crate & Kids hitting me with a school supplies sweepstakes is a segmentation failure the parent brand should have caught.
- **TheDesignDesk is buried in the footer.** I'm actively renovating two homes. A design services section should not appear below an app download prompt as an afterthought — that's the one content module that's actually relevant to me and it's invisible.
- **No loyalty hook, no member framing.** CB2 at least implies member access when they run private deals. This email treats me like a cold lead despite years of purchase history in the umbrella.
- **No deadline on the sweepstakes.** "Enter now" with no expiry date feels low-stakes and vague. Wayfair's clearance emails always have a hard deadline — even when it's marketing pressure, it creates a reason to act.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the send calendar first.** Two sends of the same giveaway in one day is the highest-priority fix and it's not a creative problem. One send, deployed once, period.
- 2. **Suppress Crate & Kids from non-parent segments.** If the CRM knows I've never purchased kids items, I should get cross-sell only when the product genuinely bridges (guest room furniture, storage). Not backpacks.
- 3. **Lead with the catalog, use the giveaway as a secondary hook.** Flip the hierarchy: "300+ fall arrivals are here" as the hero, sweepstakes as a reason to engage while browsing. Article runs this structure and it's why their home tour email outperformed three of their sale sends.
- 4. **Give TheDesignDesk its own dedicated email.** For subscribers who have renovation-adjacent purchase history, design services content should not be a footer footnote. Pottery Barn does this with standalone design services sends and it's the email I actually read top to bottom.
- 5. **Add a credible deadline to the sweepstakes.** "Enter by August 1" converts better than "Enter now." It's also more honest about how sweepstakes work.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Win $4,199 in prizes — enter by Aug 1`
- **Subject Alt B:** `300+ fall arrivals are here (+ a $4,199 giveaway)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Six brands, one sweepstakes — and our fall catalog just dropped`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New arrivals from $20. Enter the back-to-school giveaway while you browse.`

Sender is "Crate & Kids" but the document title reads "Crate & Barrel." Some clients (e.g., Apple Mail tab previews) surface this value.

**Stale dev placeholder comment (confirmed)**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
Template directive — including the typo "IMPUT" — was not stripped before send. Visible in raw source.

**Conflicting min-width media query order (confirmed)**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
The 640px rule is declared *after* the 768px rule. At any viewport ≥768px both match, and cascade order means the 640px rule wins — the 768px rule is dead. Images render at 640px max on wide desktop viewports regardless of intent.

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks zoom (confirmed)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom. WCAG 1.4.4 failure; known accessibility barrier for low-vision users.

**Light-only color scheme declared (confirmed)**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block present. Forces light rendering in Apple Mail dark mode, risking background inversions on unstyled elements.
