## Executive summary

- Seventy-six. That's where my Wayfair inbox counter sits now, and this one rolls in June 29 on the heels of the large-area-rugs email from the day before — which was at least in my renovation wheelhouse. Bird baths. They want me to click on bird baths. I've got a mudroom console and three pendant fixtures still on my list, I've been running two renovation projects simultaneously for four months, and Wayfair thinks the play right now is garden ornaments with asterisks around the word MAJOR.
- That said, the email itself isn't a wreck. The loyalty callout — 5% rewards on every item, always — is the most useful sentence in the whole thing, because I genuinely do route Wayfair spend through my highest-rewards card and stack the 5% on top. The "picked just for you" grid actually shows rugs and upholstered seating, which is closer to where I'm shopping. And the layout is clean — no broken images, no lorem ipsum bleeding into the hero, no overlapping type. Wayfair keeps the execution tight. The editorial strategy is just a disaster.
- Mark as read, almost certainly. Compare this to Article's 4th of July sale email — opened with one sofa, one price, one button. This is seventeen categories in one scroll. If I happen to be hunting for a birdbath (I'm not), I might click the garden section. But I'm not forwarding this to anyone and I'm not saving it to the folder I use to track deals.

## What's working

- The 5% rewards member callout is immediately useful — surfaces a real, ongoing benefit that affects how I route spend
- "Picked just for you" grid shows rugs and seating, which lands closer to my actual renovation list than the subject line implies
- 20% OFF banner is visible and clean — no hunt required to find the offer
- Layout holds up: no render bugs, no broken image gaps, product tiles are legible
- Late June timing for outdoor décor is at least seasonally coherent — this is the right window if you're going to push birdbaths

## What's weak

- Bird baths are not in my renovation universe — the subject sends me to the wrong store in my brain entirely
- ALL CAPS on the category plus asterisks around *MAJOR* reads like a going-out-of-business banner at a discount liquidator, not a home brand I'm trusting with $1,200 furniture decisions
- No single hero product or price — "MAJOR markdowns" is a promise with zero receipts visible above the fold
- The "your future forest are here" section copy is the kind of marketing whimsy that makes me feel like no one on this email team has ever actually bought a piece of furniture under deadline
- Five Wayfair emails this week already; the cadence has crossed from "stay top of mind" to "I'm about to unsubscribe"

## Recommendations

- 1. **Ditch the bird-bath lead entirely for behavioral targeting** — if Wayfair has my browse data (they do), put the category I've actually touched in the hero; bird baths should be a section for a subset, not the subject line for everyone
- 2. **Kill the asterisk formatting** — it's a spam signal and it reads like a Hotmail forward from 2003; "MAJOR" does more damage than good
- 3. **Surface a concrete number in the body hero, not just a bottom banner** — "Save up to 40% on outdoor & garden" beats "MAJOR markdowns" by a mile; put the receipt in the hero copy, not buried at the bottom as a 20% badge
- 4. **Elevate "picked just for you" to the top** — that section has more renovation-relevant product than anything the subject promises; give behavioral data the prime real estate
