# Find their favorite new sheets
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- Eighteen emails from the C&B family in July, and now they've rerouted me to the kids sub-brand. I want to be clear about my reaction: I didn't hate this. The creative is clean, the bedding photography looks soft and appealing, and there's a legible back-to-school timing logic to dropping a kids' sheet collection in mid-July. But this is the fifth email from crateandbarrel.com or one of its imprints in the last 48 hours, and the cumulative weight of that is real. My brain sees the "Crate & Kids" wordmark and starts calculating whether I already moved this to a folder.
- The core issue is that this is a pure new-arrivals email with zero offer. No percentage off, no member pricing, no free shipping threshold — just "here are new sheets, go shop." Pottery Barn Kids sends the same format and they usually at least staple a loyalty callout to the bottom. Crate & Kids gives me nothing to grab onto beyond the product itself, and after two days of outdoor sale emails from the parent brand, I'm conditioned to expect a discount that never arrives.
- I wouldn't click. I'd probably skim the hero, notice there's no deal, and archive it. I wouldn't forward it — there's nothing urgent or exclusive here to pass along.

## What's working

- **Visual cleanliness.** The layout is tight — no overlapping text, no broken images. The C&B family's house style holds up here, and after seeing the mess some brands make of their mobile templates, that's not nothing.
- **Seasonal timing logic.** Mid-July for kids' bedding is back-to-school adjacent without screaming it. The implicit "stock up before September" message is there for parents who are thinking ahead.
- **Product variety visible.** The grid of color options and bedroom vignettes does the swatch-shopping work I'd normally have to do on-site. Multiple textures, multiple colorways — I can actually evaluate at a glance.
- **Sub-brand clarity.** "Crate & Kids" is unambiguous. I know this is the kids line, not the main store, and that targeting logic was correctly applied — I presumably opted into this list.

## What's weak

- **No offer, fifth email in 48 hours.** The parent brand ran two outdoor sale emails on July 18, two more on July 19, and now this. CB2 had "MORE ON SALE" and "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" back-to-back — at least those were still in offer territory. This one is pure awareness, which I don't have the bandwidth for right now.
- **"Find their favorite" is soft copy.** "Their" is meant to evoke my kids, but it also reads like the team didn't want to commit to any specific value proposition. Compare to Article, which at least built a whole editorial home-tour angle when they went non-promotional — this subject line just says "go look."
- **No loyalty hook.** I have a C&B rewards account. I check my points balance before clicking through anything from this family. That hook is nowhere visible here. Pottery Barn Kids surfaces member pricing in the header — this doesn't.
- **Male renovation framing is absent.** I'm buying for rooms, not just for "the kids." Even a single line like "Finishing a room? Here's where to start with the bed" would've connected to how I actually shop right now.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Attach an offer, any offer.** Free shipping on orders over $X, double rewards points for the week, 15% off kids' bedding — something. A clean editorial email from a brand I trust could work if you've earned it, but not as email five of five in a two-day window.
- 2. **Lead with the renovation angle.** "Outfitting a kid's room? Start with the bed." is more useful to someone like me than "Find their favorite new sheets." Article does editorial anchoring better than this and they're not even a kids brand.
- 3. **Surface loyalty status in the header.** CB2 doesn't do this either and it's a miss across the whole family. Show me my points, show me a member price.
- 4. **Throttle the send cadence.** This is an operational note, not a creative one — but if I've seen four C&B family emails since yesterday, Crate & Kids should suppress me for 48 hours minimum. Wayfair's perpetual free-shipping reminder is annoying; this is structurally the same problem.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** prevents user zoom on mobile — accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4). Remove this attribute.
  ```html
  <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
  ```

- **Conflicting desktop image max-width rules** — two media queries produce a cascade collision. On screens ≥768px, both match; the later rule wins (`640px`), overriding the intended `600px` cap. The 600px rule is dead code.
  ```css
  @media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }  /* overridden */
  @media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }  /* wins on desktop */
  ```
  Consolidate to a single rule.

- **Mobile breakpoint at 460px is narrow** — `.showmobile` renders at `width:414px`, leaving devices with a 461–599px viewport (e.g., some Android models) without mobile styles. Standard practice is 600px.

- **Dark mode not declared** — `color-scheme: light` and `supported-color-schemes: light` are set, meaning Apple Mail will not apply dark-mode overrides. If background colors are hardcoded white (`#ffffff`), text may be unreadable when iOS forces dark mode anyway. Audit all background/text color pairs in the full source.

- **Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags** — `telephone=no, address=no, email=no` appear in both a combined meta and three individual metas. The duplication is harmless but clutters the `<head>`.

- **`alt` text on images**: Cannot verify from truncated source. Must confirm all `<img>` tags carry descriptive `alt` values (not empty or missing).
