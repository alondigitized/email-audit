# Starts now! Bestselling Aspen dinnerware 15% off
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- Okay, dinnerware. Not furniture, not renovation-adjacent, just plates and mugs — but I'll give Crate & Barrel credit for at least keeping it in the "we love these for you" kitchen lane instead of nursery stuff again. My eye went straight to the Aspen table setting hero shot — clean white plates, red napkin, that's a real styled photo, not a stock render — and 15% off with an actual sale price shown per item ($5.05 vs Reg. $5.95) is the kind of concrete math I actually register. But this is basically a straight repeat of the "up to 60% off Summer Sale" send I already screenshotted — same top banner language, similar layout, just a new hero product swapped in.
- The "in season now: summer produce" block with the corn photo and "Get the Recipes" CTA is a total non-sequitur in the middle of a dinnerware sale email — I'm not clicking a recipe link from a plate promo. And then it just keeps going: cookware, a credit card pitch, an app download pitch, a Design Desk pitch. This is five emails stapled together.
- Would I click? Maybe the "Shop the Sale" button if I actually needed dinnerware right now, which I sort of do since we're setting up two kitchens — but I wouldn't forward this to anyone. It's Crate & Barrel doing what CB2 already called out in my head as "permanent C&B family snooze" territory, and Pottery Barn's entryway email at least stayed on one topic. This one sprawls.

## What's working

- The Aspen hero shot is styled well — real napkin, real fruit, real glassware, not a flat product-on-white render.
- Sale price vs. Reg. price is shown line by line under each product ($5.05 Reg. $5.95, etc.) — that's the honest math I want to see before I click.
- A review quote ("A classic design and offers great quality for the price") sits right under the top product grid — actual social proof, not just marketing copy.
- The "we love these for you" section pulled in cookware (Le Creuset, KitchenAid) that's genuinely adjacent to what I'd buy alongside new dinnerware — decent cross-sell logic.

## What's weak

- The summer produce/recipes block has nothing to do with dinnerware — it breaks the whole email's train of thought and I'd scroll right past it.
- This is at minimum the second Crate & Barrel sale email this week hitting the same "Summer Sale" banner copy — déjà vu, just like the round-mirror-over-the-dresser email I flagged before.
- Way too much stapled on after the actual offer — recipes, cookware, credit card, app download, Design Desk appointment. Pick one, not five.
- Nothing here speaks to me specifically as a renovation-in-progress guy — no "furnish your second home" framing, no bulk/registry angle, just generic dinnerware for anyone.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email down to one topic: dinnerware. Drop the produce/recipes block entirely — it dilutes the offer and adds a scroll for nothing.
- 2. Move the review quote higher, right under the hero — social proof next to the offer converts better than after a full product grid.
- 3. Consolidate the bottom stack (credit card, app, Design Desk) into a single small footer module instead of three full-width CTAs — right now it feels like three separate emails glued together.
- 4. Since this is at least the second "Summer Sale" banner this week, vary the top banner copy so repeat opens don't feel like the same email recycled.
