# THIS WEEK’S STEALS
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-09**
## Executive summary

- "THIS WEEK'S STEALS" — okay, so this is CB2 fully back in warehouse-sale mode, three stacked percentage-off banners deep before I even hit a product I recognize. After 8/8's fabric-swatch send (the one I actually gave a 6 to for having a single clean pitch) and the "Love at First Sit" one, this feels like a regression — 40% off August Sale, then 60% off Summer Clearance, then 50% off Outdoor, back to back to back. It's basically the CB2 version of Pottery Barn's tenth "UP TO 60% OFF" email this week, except CB2 usually has more restraint than that.
- The "MORE FOR YOU" grid at the bottom is honestly the most useful part — Andes bed, Foley Mink velvet chairs and stool, an Espira marbled side table — because it's real SKUs with real prices, not another banner. If I'm being honest with myself, I'd scroll past the top third entirely and only stop at the grid, which tells you something about hierarchy here.
- Would I click? Maybe on the outdoor rattan coffee table shot — genuinely nice styling — but I wouldn't forward this to anyone. It reads like three separate emails got merged into one, and I've got renovation decision fatigue as it is between two houses' worth of furniture picks.

## What's working

- The outdoor rattan/wicker coffee table shot with the lemons is legitimately good styling — feels like a real patio, not a stock photo.
- "MORE FOR YOU" grid gives me actual SKUs and prices (Andes bed $999, Foley Mink chair $399) instead of just vague "shop now" energy.
- The 10% Rewards Dollars + App download block at the bottom is relevant since I already shop on the highest-rewards card logic — CB2 at least surfaces it, even if it's buried.
- Clean black-and-white CB2 branding at top keeps it feeling premium instead of discount-bin, despite three sale banners.

## What's weak

- Three different sale percentages (40%, 60%, 50%) for three different categories in one email is exactly the "which sale is even the real one" confusion I complain about — same problem Wayfair had burying its actual bookcase behind a 72-hour clearout banner.
- No single clear CTA — is this an August Sale email, a Clearance email, or an Outdoor email? I genuinely can't tell what to click first.
- The velvet armchair hero (60% off Summer Clearance) has zero context — no fabric name up front, no room shot, just a chair on a plain backdrop, weaker than the CB2 fabric-swatch send from yesterday.
- "LEAVE US A REVIEW, WIN $1,000" sandwiched between sale banners and the product grid feels like a non-sequitur — I'd skip right past it.
- Fine print asterisks on every single banner (40%†, 60%*, 50%†) without any inline explanation is the "buried exclusions" pattern I hate — Article at least keeps its editorial sends asterisk-free.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick ONE hero offer and lead with it — either the outdoor sale (given it's August) or the clearance, not three stacked banners fighting each other. CB2's own 8/8 fabric send proved a single clean pitch converts better for me.
- 2. Move the "MORE FOR YOU" product grid up near the top — that's the only section with real product/price info and it's currently buried below three banners and a review-contest ask.
- 3. Cut the review-contest block or move it to the very bottom near the footer — it interrupts the shopping flow for no reason.
- 4. Add one line of context to the velvet chair hero — fabric name, or "shown in Mink" — since color/fabric consistency is exactly what I'm tracking across three brands right now.
