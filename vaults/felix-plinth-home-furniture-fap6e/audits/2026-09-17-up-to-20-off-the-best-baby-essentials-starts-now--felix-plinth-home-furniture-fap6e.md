## What's working

- Hero banner is dead simple: "Limited time only! up to 20% off baby essentials" over a clean shot of a stroller/bassinet — no hunting for the offer like Pottery Barn's warehouse-sale fake-out.
- Each product block pairs a real lifestyle photo with a specific discount code callout (20% off Stokke, 15% off Maxi-Cosi) — that's a level of offer clarity CB2's "Fall 30% Off, Ends Today" send nailed and most others haven't.
- The registry section ("nicole brydon bloom & justin theroux's registry faves") is a genuinely clever social-proof hook for anyone actually in baby-gear mode.

## What's weak

- Wrong list, plain and simple — I'm renovating two homes, not nurseries. Nothing about crib furniture, changing tables, or nursery decor even brushes up against my actual project.
- Zero swatch/sample or showroom-integration content, which is the stuff I'd actually want from a home brand right now.
- The "it's all on the app, including your chance to win $500" footer feels tacked-on and generic — doesn't reinforce anything specific to baby gear.
- No rewards-card or loyalty-tier messaging visible anywhere, which is exactly the lever that gets me to click on brand emails.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment me out of this list entirely.** This is Crate & Kids emailing someone with zero baby-related purchase history on file for Crate & Barrel proper — that's a data hygiene problem, not a creative one. No subject line fixes a targeting miss this basic.
- 2. If Crate & Barrel wants to cross-sell me from furniture into Crate & Kids, do it contextually — "just moved into a bigger place? here's the nursery" — not a blind blast.
- 3. Add a loyalty-tier or rewards callout since that's what actually gets me past the subject line on any Crate & Barrel family email.
