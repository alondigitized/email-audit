# A world-class bedroom for your soccer star ⭐
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-three. I went back and did the math — this is the twenty-third email from the Crate & Barrel family since July 8th. And Crate & Kids has decided that the send worth burning on number twenty-three is a soccer-star bedroom for a child I do not have. I renovate homes. Two of them simultaneously. I do not have a soccer star. I have a second bathroom that still needs tile.
- To be fair to the email itself: the hero bedroom shot is genuinely appealing. The styling is clean, there's a coherent black-and-white-and-green palette that I'd actually consider for a kids' room *if I had one*, and the product grid below is organized without feeling chaotic. The email does its job for the audience it was built for. That audience is not me.
- Mark as read. Won't forward — none of my friends at this stage are decorating kids' rooms, and the few who are don't need Crate & Kids recommendations from me.

## What's working

- **The hero bedroom shot is styled well.** The black-and-white soccer ball bedding against the clean white walls reads editorial, not juvenile. It's the kind of image I'd stop on if I had a 9-year-old.
- **Visual hierarchy is tight.** My eye goes hero → headline → product grid with no competition from cluttered sidebars or misaligned copy blocks.
- **Seasonal relevance is real.** Summer, kids out of school, soccer season — the timing logic is sound. This isn't a stale send.
- **The product grid has range.** Storage bins, bedding, furniture — I can see they built a full room story, not just a single SKU push. That's what I'd expect from a Crate & Barrel brand doing it right.

## What's weak

- **I don't have kids.** That's the whole problem. If Crate & Kids knows I'm in their system because I've bought from Crate & Barrel, they should know whether I've ever touched the kids' category. I haven't. This should have been suppressed for me.
- **No offer is visible.** I see a product showcase but no percentage off, no free shipping callout, no member pricing — nothing that makes me feel like I need to act now versus whenever. Compare this to how Pottery Barn ran "UP TO 70% OFF" right in the subject line two weeks ago. That's how you earn a click.
- **Twenty-three emails in four days.** Even if this were perfectly targeted at me — a world-class email about the exact tile I'm debating for my second home — the fatigue tax would kill it. Wayfair did the same thing with its outdoor rug loop a few weeks back and I nearly filtered them.
- **The CTA copy is almost certainly "Shop Now."** I can see the buttons from here and I've seen this brand enough to know. CB2's "When your bedroom is just for you" send at least had some voice in its body copy. This is product catalog energy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress this segment.** If I haven't touched a kids' product in two years, I should be out of this send entirely. The biggest lift isn't creative — it's list hygiene.
- 2. **Add an offer.** Anything. Free shipping on orders over $100. 15% off bedroom bundles. Even a "completes the room" bundle price. Article does this cleanly — their new arrivals email last week at least anchored on specific price points. This one floats.
- 3. **Reduce cadence or package it.** Bundle the kids' content into a weekly digest for subscribers who've opted into it. Don't burn a standalone send on a single-theme kids' push to an unsegmented list.
- 4. **Give the hero copy more specificity.** "Bedroom goals for the ultimate soccer fan" (paraphrasing the visible headline) is fine but generic. Name a product. Name a price. Give me something to grab.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` brand mismatch (confirmed bug)**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
The sending brand is "Crate & Kids"; the document title reads "Crate & Barrel." Affects screen-reader announcement and some email clients that display the title in tab/window UI.

**Conflicting image `max-width` media queries**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
At viewport ≥ 768px both rules match; the 640px query is declared *after* the 768px query, so cascade order gives `max-width: 640px` at desktop — images can overflow a 600px container. The wider breakpoint should be declared last to win the cascade.

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile — WCAG 1.4.4 violation (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
`telephone=no`, `address=no` are declared twice each across two separate `<meta name="format-detection">` tags. Redundant but harmless; clean up for hygiene.

**No dark mode support**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Hard-coded to light only. Apple Mail and Outlook for Mac will force their own dark-mode inversion on images and backgrounds rather than using authored dark styles.
