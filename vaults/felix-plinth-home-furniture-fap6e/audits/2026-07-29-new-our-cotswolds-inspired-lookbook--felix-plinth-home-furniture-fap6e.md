# NEW | Our Cotswolds-inspired lookbook
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-29**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so this is the *other* Cotswolds email — same green-room, heritage-English aesthetic I already gave a 7 to yesterday for "New Fall 2026: Inspired by the English countryside." Crate & Barrel is now running the identical collection through two separate sends inside 24 hours, and my brain immediately clocked the deep charcoal-green library shot again before I even registered this was a different subject line. That's a cadence problem, not a creative problem — the creative itself is still genuinely good.
- What actually kept me scrolling was the bottom half, not the top. The "Lupita Nyong'o inside her L.A. home makeover" content from The Design Desk is a legitimately different beat than the usual room-porn scroll, and a free design appointment plus a $5000 sweepstakes entry is the kind of thing I'd actually consider given I'm mid-renovation on two houses right now. That's more relevant to me than another lookbook.
- Net: I'd skim it, not click it. I already "used up" my Cotswolds click yesterday. If anything, I'd forward the Design Desk section to my contractor, not the lookbook itself.

## What's working

- The Cotswolds hero shot — that moody green library room with the arched windows — is still doing real work aesthetically, even on a repeat viewing.
- Section-by-room structure (living room → bedroom → dining → kitchen) makes it easy to jump straight to what I care about, which right now is kitchen and dining since that's the room under construction.
- The Design Desk / Lupita Nyong'o block is a genuine differentiator — no other home brand in my inbox this week (not Pottery Barn, not CB2, not Article) is running celebrity-home content tied to a bookable in-person design appointment.
- "We love these for you" with actual prices visible on four products respects that I comparison-shop before I buy — I can eyeball cost without a click.
- No render bugs, no broken images, consistent moody photography from top to bottom — feels like one coherent shoot, unlike Wayfair's fourteen-category grab-bag emails.

## What's weak

- This is the second Cotswolds-collection email in as many days — CB2's parent company is now stacking near-identical creative on itself, which is worse fatigue than even the Crate & Kids back-to-school run I've been complaining about.
- "Up to 60% off The Summer Sale" at the very top has nothing to do with "Cotswolds-inspired lookbook" in the subject — it's a mismatched hook, and by the time I hit the fall-heritage content the summer-sale banner already feels like bait.
- Pushing a fall/heritage collection this hard in early August, with an entertaining table-setting shot that reads more autumn than summer, is early even for a lookbook — CB2's "24 hours left" send at least matched urgency to the calendar.
- No loyalty or rewards-card messaging anywhere, which is a miss — I buy on whichever card gives me the most points, and a brand this large should be reminding me what I'm sitting on.
- Zero dimension, material, or lead-time info on any hero product — for someone actually furnishing two houses right now, "estate-worthy living room" is nice copy but I need to know if that sofa ships in six weeks or six months.

## Recommendations

- 1. Don't send the same collection twice in one day (or even one week) under two different subject lines — merge into one strong send, or space them by at least 5-7 days like Pottery Barn tends to.
- 2. Move the Design Desk / Lupita Nyong'o content up near the top — it's the most differentiated thing in the email and it's currently buried below six scroll-stops of room shots.
- 3. Fix the header mismatch — either lead with the Cotswolds hero and drop "60% off Summer Sale," or make the subject line about the sale, not the lookbook.
- 4. Add a line of loyalty/rewards messaging near the top CTA — even one sentence ("Members save an extra X%") would move the needle for a repeat shopper like me.
- 5. Put a real deadline on the sale banner — "ends Sunday" beats a vague "up to 60% off" that reads like every other retailer's ceiling-only claim.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Fall's biggest lookbook: The Cotswolds Collection`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New heritage-inspired pieces for every room, plus a free design appointment`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `See the full collection before the Summer Sale ends`
