# Lupita Nyong’o called in The Design Desk—and we’re obsessed
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-22**
## Executive summary

- Okay, Lupita Nyong'o's L.A. home got me to actually stop scrolling — that's a real celebrity, not some influencer nobody, and the outdoor tablescape shot at the top is genuinely nice. But this is Crate & Barrel proper, not Crate & Kids, and after a week of getting hammered by the Kids sub-brand with nursery gliders and bunk beds, I momentarily forgot the parent company existed. Then I scrolled and remembered why: this is a nine-scene home tour dressed up as a marketing email — outdoor, breakfast nook, living room, "designers can help," 25% off bedroom, a clearance callout buried mid-scroll, a new tall table, a "we love these for you" product grid, and a credit card pitch at the bottom. That's an absurd amount of ground for one send.
- The celebrity hook earns the open and the first two scrolls. After that it's the same C&B kitchen-sink problem I flagged on the CB2 "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS" send — too many categories crammed into one email so nothing gets real estate to close. I'd click through on the outdoor dining set because that's actually live on my second-home reno list right now. I would not forward this — Lupita's cute but this isn't shareable, it's just a long catalog with a name attached.

## What's working

- Lupita Nyong'o in the hero photo is a legit scroll-stopper — way better than Article's "Home Tour" with An Trieu, who I don't recognize at all.
- The outdoor dining vignette at the top is directly relevant to my second-home patio project — string lights, teak table, the whole mood.
- Star ratings under each product grid (5-star icons under "Best-selling," "Woven," etc.) give me quick trust signal without clicking in.
- "Book Your Free Appointment" CTA appears repeatedly and is a real differentiator — none of the Wayfair or CB2 sends I've read lately offer live design help.

## What's weak

- Nine distinct sections in one email is exhausting — outdoor, dining, living room, design services, bedroom sale, mystery clearance banner, tall table, gift grid, credit card. Pick one.
- The "up to 25% off bedroom refresh" and the "new markdowns added, up to 60% off clearance" banners are visually almost identical gray blocks back-to-back — I nearly scrolled past both thinking it was one repeated module.
- Zero mention of swatches, fabric samples, or delivery windows for any of this furniture, and I've got two reno timelines to plan around — that's the one piece of practical info I actually need before I'd click "Shop Now" on a sofa.
- The credit card pitch at the very bottom ("now it's all on the app... chance at $800") feels bolted on and generic after all that design-forward content up top.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this into two emails — one strictly the Lupita home tour (outdoor + breakfast nook + living room + book-an-appointment), one for the bedroom sale/clearance/tall table/product grid. The celebrity story deserves to breathe, not compete with a clearance banner three scrolls later.
- 2. Move any real discount (the 25% bedroom or the 60% clearance) up near the top if it's the actual driver — right now it's sandwiched after five scenic photos most people won't reach on mobile.
- 3. Add one line of shipping/delivery-window info near the furniture CTAs — "in stock, ships in 2 weeks" would do more for me than another lifestyle photo.
- 4. Drop the credit card ad from this email entirely — it's the same mismatched sub-brand vibe Crate & Kids clearance-banner tacked-on ask gave me last week.

# Technical Email Audit — Crate & Barrel "The Design Desk" Campaign

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is built on a standard responsive MI (Marigold/Cheetah Digital) ESP template with MSO/Outlook conditional fallbacks; core rendering markup is sound, but the campaign fails multiple compliance and accessibility checks — most notably missing unsubscribe headers and near-total absence of image alt text.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 74 tracking/click-redirect links were present but skipped by automated HTTP probing (expected behavior for redirect-domain links; not itself a defect, but means destination validity is unverified).
- Multiple beacon/pixel trackers are firing: `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*` (7 instances), `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif` (open pixel), `sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif` (LiveRamp/RampID, fired 5x with incrementing `n=` param), and `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/...?cp_tp=v` (conversion/view pixel). Volume is high but consistent with standard MI multi-vendor tracking stacks — flagging for visibility, not as a defect.
- No broken/malformed href syntax detected in the visible source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **39 images missing `alt` text**, spanning both content images (`image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/...jpg/gif`) and tracking pixels (`mi.crateandbarrel.com`, `sr.rlcdn.com`, `dv.crateandbarrel.com`). Tracking pixels don't strictly need alt text, but the ~26 content images (product/lifestyle photography) do — screen reader users get no fallback content if images fail to load or are blocked.
- Template includes correct MSO conditional blocks (`<!--[if mso]>`, `<!--[if gte mso 9]>`), Outlook font-fix classes, and standard reset CSS (`ReadMsgBody`, `ExternalClass`) — good baseline Outlook/Windows Mail compatibility.
- `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` and `format-detection` meta tags present — mitigates Apple Mail auto-linking/reformatting issues.
- Media queries target 460px (mobile) and 640/768px (desktop image max-width) — standard responsive breakpoints, no issues found.
- One malformed HTML comment: `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` — leftover template placeholder text with a typo ("IMPUT"). Cosmetic/internal only (inside a comment, not rendered), but indicates the template wasn't fully cleaned before send.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tags (e.g. `{{first_name}}`, `%%FIELD%%`) or broken personalization syntax found in the visible/truncated source.
- Tracking URLs carry a consistent `mi_u` / `mi_mid` subscriber identifier (`dbe8da718e2997cce014546f16c5722eaa3c322f28d40f0a8386f8fd101...`, `mi_mid=01a0298e-8080-7000-88c2-7a27d8ba095c`) — personalization/identity resolution appears correctly populated, not left as a literal token.
- No issues found beyond the above.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)

Note QA flags this may be a relay capture gap (AgentMail) rather than a true absence — needs header-level confirmation from the raw SMTP transaction, not just the parsed HTML/header snapshot.
- **[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found (RFC 8058)** — same caveat; needed for true one-click (POST-based) unsubscribe.
- **[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not found** — SPF/DKIM pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from this capture. Cannot verify sender authentication independent of relay limitations.
- In-body unsubscribe link/footer was not visible in the truncated HTML provided, so footer-level CAN-SPAM elements (physical address, unsubscribe link) cannot be confirmed or denied from this excerpt.
- **Action needed:** re-verify all three header findings against the raw MIME headers at the mail relay/MTA level, since the AgentMail capture layer is a suspected point of loss, not necessarily the sender.
