**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- Eight. That's my CB2 inbox count since July 1st — I know because the review I did on yesterday's "JULY 4TH SALE IS ON" started the same way. So when this one lands on July 5th, my first instinct is exhaustion. - What this email does right is the anchor. Leading with a specific category at a specific number is smarter than every prior CB2 send this week that led with "UP TO 50% OFF" plus a sweep shot of a living room. The email knows what it's doing in the first scroll. It forgets by the second.
- Would I click? Yes — but only to the lighting section, and only because I'm in active renovation mode. Would I forward it? Absolutely not. My group chat has already received three CB2 sale links from me this week and I'm not pushing my luck.

- **Countdown timer (02:17:04:32)** — the one element doing actual urgency work rather than just saying "hurry up" in copy; it's specific, live, and gives me a concrete reason to act today

## What's weak

- **Sender display name is "CB2 LONG WKND SALE"** — it's July 5th; the long weekend is over; having that baked into the display name makes this feel like a recycled send that missed its window
- **"July 4th Sale" framing has expired** — the subject says "3 days left" but the holiday it's named for happened yesterday; the deadline logic is fine but the brand name for the event is already stale
- **"MORE FOR YOU" algorithmic grid** — the email earns real focus on lighting and then bleeds out into scatter at the bottom; Article keeps their deadline emails clean and single-threaded, CB2 can't resist piling on
- **No loyalty or member pricing signal anywhere** — Article's July 3rd send showed me my member-exclusive rate above the fold; CB2 assumes I know my rewards status without reminding me, which is a wasted close
- **Three competing CTAs in the scroll** — "SHOP PLUSH SEATING," "SHOP OUTDOOR," "SHOP NOW" for warehouse; the email opens with a strong single idea and then splits its own momentum across three directions

- 1. **Kill the "July 4th" label on July 5th; reframe as "Last Chance Lighting Sale"** — the pendant lamp is strong enough to headline without the expired holiday reference pulling focus
- 2. **Promote the countdown timer to true hero placement** — center it above the fold, make the timer the story; right now it's supporting copy when it should be leading

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** (line: `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />`)
Disables pinch-to-zoom on iOS. Violates WCAG 1.4.4. Remove the `maximum-scale` constraint.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` on `*` selector**
The universal rule `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;...}` suppresses iOS text auto-sizing on all elements, including fine-print. Replace with `body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; }` scoped only to body.

**Narrow mobile breakpoint** (`@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)`)
Targets ≤418px logical width. Devices wider than 418px but still mobile (e.g., Android phones at 412–430px logical width) will render the desktop layout. Standard safe breakpoint is `max-width: 480px` or `max-width: 600px`.

**Triple-declared `@font-face` for `"Lineto Brown Sub"`**
The same font-family name is declared three times — once with eot/woff2/woff sources, then twice more with `font-weight: 400` and `font-weight: 600`. The first unweighted declaration will shadow or conflict with the weighted variants in some clients. Consolidate into a single `@font-face` block per weight.

**`"Lineto Brown Light Sub"` declared with `.woff` only** (no woff2)
```css
src: url("https://www.cb2.com/Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff");
```
All other faces include woff2. Modern clients (Gmail web, Apple Mail) will load woff when woff2 would compress ~30% smaller.
