# Bestsellers made for childhood →
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-26**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids again. That's — let me check — the third one of these in my inbox this cycle, and I don't have kids, so "quality furniture built for childhood" is just scroll-fodder for me the second I see the crib in the hero. I get why they're doing a nursery-to-bunk-bed collection tour — jenny lind, classic, bodie, hampshire — it's genuinely nice-looking furniture, and if I were shopping for a kid's room I could see myself lingering on the bodie 3-drawer dresser. But I'm renovating two adult houses right now, and this is Crate & Barrel's kids sub-brand sending me toddler bunk beds and a "$4,199 in prizes" sweepstakes for a "dream nursery to school room."
- The one thing that almost got me was way down at the bottom — "we love these for you" with a backpack, a nightstand, and a glider recliner. That's actual personalization-shaped content, but it's still parked under five full collection blocks of cribs and dressers I'm never going to buy. Article's living room send worked because it matched exactly what I'm mid-decision on; this is the opposite problem — right brand family, completely wrong life stage.
- Not clicking, not forwarding, marking as read and moving on. If anything I'd forward it to my sister who just had a kid, not keep it for myself.

## What's working

- The jenny lind, bodie, and hampshire collection photography is genuinely well-styled — moody, colorful bedroom sets that read as "quality furniture," not cheap kid stuff.
- Star ratings under a couple of the collection headers (jenny lind, classic) are a real trust cue I don't usually get from Crate & Kids sends.
- The "we love these for you" module near the bottom at least gestures at personalization instead of just blasting the same five collections at everyone.
- Clean single-column layout, no render bugs, images all loaded — better execution than the Wayfair kitchen-mats pile-up I sat through recently.

## What's weak

- I don't have kids. Cribs, bunk beds, nursery dressers, and a "dream nursery to school room" sweepstakes are zero-relevance to me — this is CB2/Crate & Barrel's parent list clearly not filtering out childless subscribers.
- Five separate full-width collection blocks (jenny lind, classic, bodie, hampshire, bunk beds) before any real offer clarity — I'm thumb-scrolling forever to find out this is "up to 20% off."
- The "up to 20% off summer sale" banner is buried two-thirds down the email under all the collection browsing — Article leads with its hook up top, this makes me work for it.
- Generic "Shop Bestsellers" / "Shop the X Collection" CTAs throughout — no size, price, or delivery-window specifics anywhere, which matters way more to me on furniture than on throw pillows.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment me out.** I'm on the Crate & Barrel master list for adult furniture — if Crate & Kids can't tell I've never clicked a kids product in a year of emails, stop sending me the full collection tour and cut me down to the "we love these for you" module only, or skip me entirely.
- 2. **Lead with the offer, not the fifth collection.** "Up to 20% off" is currently buried past four scrolls of crib photography — move it into the hero alongside the sweepstakes hook.
- 3. **Give the furniture-buyer facts up top for the pieces that would actually cross over** — the dressers and bookcases in these collections aren't that different from what Crate & Barrel sells adults; call out wood type, dimensions, ships-in-weeks the way I'd want for my own place.
- 4. **Cut collection count per send.** Five full collections in one email is Wayfair-tier bloat; CB2 gets away with weekly "steals" roundups because it's one format, repeated — pick two collections max and rotate.
