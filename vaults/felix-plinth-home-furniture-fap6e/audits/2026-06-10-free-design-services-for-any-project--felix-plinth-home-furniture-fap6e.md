# FREE design services for any project
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-10**
## Executive summary

- Ten. I'm opening with that number because it's the toll this email has to pass through. The ninth was yesterday's bedding bestsellers send, which I described as "the ninth email since Tuesday morning." So today's free design services pitch arrives as, roughly, the tenth Pottery Barn email I've fielded this week. Nine of those were noise. This one is actually signal — I'm renovating two homes simultaneously and free professional design help is not a promotional offer, it's a real service I would genuinely use. The subject stopped me.
- But then I open it and the Kittle cabin is back. I already sat with that module yesterday in the "Inside George Kittle's off-season retreat" send, gave it a 6, and moved on. Seeing it recycled into today's email tells me the editorial team isn't checking what went out 24 hours earlier. Article did the exact opposite last week — their June 9 outdoor living email deliberately built on their June 5 send. Pottery Barn is copy-pasting modules and hoping I don't notice.
- The core offer is good enough to survive all of that. "BOOK DESIGN APPOINTMENT" is one of the cleaner CTAs Pottery Barn has sent me all week, and I'd click it. I would not forward this to anyone — it's too brand-specific and the cadence story is embarrassing.

## What's working

- **The offer is real value, not a discount.** Free design services during active renovation season isn't a promotional trick — it's something I'd actually consume.
- **"BOOK DESIGN APPOINTMENT" is the best CTA Pottery Barn has sent me this week.** Verb + noun, unambiguous, no "Discover More" hedging.
- **Hero hierarchy puts the offer first.** I didn't have to scroll to understand what was being offered.
- **"RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" product grid signals personalization.** Compared to CB2's warehouse sale blast (scored 9/10 but clearly a mass send), this feels like it knows who I am.
- **Key Rewards in the footer.** I'm a member and rewards math is part of how I decide where to spend on big-ticket furniture.

## What's weak

- **Kittle cabin module recycled from yesterday.** I already saw and dismissed this content on June 9. Running it again in the next send is autopilot, not storytelling.
- **No urgency or scarcity on the design offer.** "RSVP" implies limited availability but nothing in the email confirms that. If appointments are actually limited, say so — "3 slots left this week" would make me move immediately.
- **"Any project" is too vague.** I need to know if this covers second homes, remote consultations, full-room planning. Ambiguity on scope makes me delay booking.
- **Ten emails in three days.** Wayfair sent two outdoor emails in one morning yesterday and I flagged the cadence. Pottery Barn has done five times that volume.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop the Kittle module entirely.** Use that real estate to show a before/after renovation project — the use case I actually have — rather than a celebrity home that ran yesterday.
- 2. **Add real scarcity to the appointment booking.** "Next available slots: this week only" or a visible appointment counter turns a passive RSVP into an action.
- 3. **One-line scope clarification under the CTA.** Something like "Covers full-room planning, product curation, and remote consultations" eliminates the hesitation that "any project" creates.
- 4. **Rest the inbox.** Send this email on a Tuesday after a 48-hour silence and the response rate climbs without changing a single word.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Two homes. One designer. No charge.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Full-room planning, furniture curation, and styling — covered by Pottery Barn.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your dedicated designer is ready. Book before the week fills up.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP headers in CSS block (malformed source):**
The second `<style>` block contains raw PHP `header()` calls:
```html
<style>
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, ...");
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
  ...
</style>
```
These are server-side PHP statements that were never executed — they landed verbatim in the HTML output. They are invalid CSS and will generate parser errors in some clients. This is a template/rendering pipeline bug.

**Missing alt text — 34 images:**
Virtually every content and footer image has `alt=""` or no `alt` attribute. Affected images include all body slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_07.jpg`), all 18 footer slices, product images (bed, rug, sheets), and tracking pixels. Screen readers will skip all content; images-off rendering is completely blank with no fallback text.

**HTTP image sources — 2 images:**
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Mixed content will cause these images to be blocked in Gmail (enforces HTTPS), iOS Mail, and most modern clients.

**`<link>` to Google Fonts:**
`<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat&display=swap">` — Google Fonts is blocked in many corporate email environments and unsupported in Outlook. No fallback stack is defined for Montserrat in the CSS; the font will silently fall back to browser default rather than a declared web-safe alternative.
