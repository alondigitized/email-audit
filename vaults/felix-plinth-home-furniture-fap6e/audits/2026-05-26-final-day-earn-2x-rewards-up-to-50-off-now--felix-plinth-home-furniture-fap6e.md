# FINAL DAY! Earn 2x Rewards & up to 50% off now
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-26**
## Executive summary

- Fifty-one. That's where the C&B family email count has landed since Sunday, and Crate & Kids closes out Memorial Day as the fifth distinct sender in the constellation. I'm a guy renovating two homes — neither of which contains a nursery. So the immediate tension here is real: I'm on the C&B list, I track rewards obsessively, and I do care about the 2x offer. But the moment I scroll past the hero, I'm deep in ball pits and baby milestone photos, which is not where my head is at.
- The subject pulled me in on rewards math alone — I've trained myself to notice "2x Rewards" the same way a points-hacker notices a transfer bonus. But the parent brand (Crate & Barrel) was already blasting 60% off *yesterday*, and here's the sub-brand capping at 50%? I caught that. Any subscriber watching the C&B family closely — and I am — reads that as the kids brand getting a slightly worse deal, which doesn't feel right.
- I'm marking this as read without clicking. Not forwarding. The 2x Rewards hook was the closest thing to a save, but there's nothing here I'm actually buying for either renovation right now.

## What's working

- **2x Rewards in the subject is the strongest hook.** I run everything through my highest-rewards card; a loyalty-stacking moment cuts through the noise in a way raw-percentage offers stopped doing around email #40 this week.
- **Clean top-to-bottom layout.** No render issues, clear blue CTAs, images loaded crisply — compared to the CB2 app-push email from a few days back, this is an execution win.
- **"FINAL DAY" is the first two words.** Urgency framing up front, not buried. Pottery Barn's "LAST DAY 🚨" send did the same thing and scored well for the same reason.
- **Multiple product categories shown.** Bedding, storage, lifestyle — at least the scroll gives me a sense of breadth, even if the demo is off for me personally.

## What's weak

- **50% off when the parent brand was at 60% yesterday.** I did that math in real time. Crate & Barrel proper hit 60% on May 26th; the sub-brand lands at 50% the day after on what's supposed to be the final, biggest push. That's a confusing brand-family message and makes the sub-brand feel like it got the smaller slice.
- **This is a kids brand.** I don't have children. The second scroll sends me into nursery territory — ball pits, crawling babies — and I've stopped shopping. No mechanism to surface the adult items C&B carries on this sub-domain.
- **Volume fatigue is doing real damage.** Fifty-plus emails from the same brand family over six days has eroded the urgency signal almost completely. "FINAL DAY" stopped landing around the third time I heard it.
- **No rewards math shown in the body.** If you're leading with "2x Rewards" in the subject, show me what that actually means in dollars or points. Wayfair at least anchors with concrete savings figures; C&Kids is expecting me to do the conversion in my head.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show the loyalty math in the first 200px.** "Members earn 2x — that's $X back on a $500 crib" is infinitely more actionable than "earn 2x Rewards." The subject put it front and center; the body needs to deliver on it.
- 2. **Reconcile the discount gap with the parent brand.** If C&B proper was at 60% and C&Kids is at 50%, either explain why ("kids furniture is already lower-margin") or match it. The inconsistency erodes trust for subscribers watching both.
- 3. **Add a browsing-history segment or category gate.** If I've never browsed nursery on the C&B site, I probably shouldn't be getting the kids sub-brand blast. Article and Pottery Barn both do better segmentation on their list — this is addressable.
- 4. **Cut the cadence by 30%.** The sixth C&B family email in a week is doing negative brand work. One fewer send with higher signal would have kept the urgency real.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 2x Rewards window closes at midnight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stack your points before the Memorial Day sale disappears.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `One day left to earn double on everything in the sale.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users from zooming on mobile. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1` — no visual impact on normal users.

**[HIGH] Wrong `<title>` for sub-brand**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
This is a Crate & Kids email. The `<title>` tag is used by some clients (notably Gmail web) as the accessible document name and appears in browser tab labels when the email is opened in a browser. Should read `Crate & Kids`.

**[MEDIUM] Dead CSS — `min-width: 768px` image rule is unreachable**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
Both queries match at any desktop width ≥768px. CSS cascade applies the later `640px` rule unconditionally, making the `600px` block dead code. Remove the `768px` block or swap the values if 600px was the intended desktop cap.

**[MEDIUM] `*` selector applies text-adjust and line-height globally**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; max-height: 1000000px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly; line-height: 100% }
```
`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `*` rather than `body` suppresses OS-level accessibility font scaling across all elements. `line-height: 100%` globally can collapse multi-line text to single-line in some clients. Both should be scoped to `body` or specific table/td selectors.

**[LOW] Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags — 4 total**
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<!-- ...later... -->
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
The first combined tag is authoritative and sufficient. The three individual duplicates add ~180 bytes of dead markup per send volume.

**[LOW] Dark mode locked to light-only**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Apple Mail and iOS Mail will not apply dark-mode overrides. This is a defensible choice if dark-mode CSS is absent, but it means the email renders harshly in dark environments rather than gracefully degrading.
