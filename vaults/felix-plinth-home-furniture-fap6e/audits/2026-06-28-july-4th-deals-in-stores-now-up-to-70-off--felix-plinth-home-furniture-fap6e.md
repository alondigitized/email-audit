# July 4th deals in stores now! Up to 70% off
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-28**
## Executive summary

- Email forty-six — or whatever we're at with Pottery Barn this month, I've lost count since June 26. But I'll give this one credit for one thing: someone finally switched the sender to "Pottery Barn Design Services" and pushed the in-store angle instead of just recycling the Extra-20%-off blast I've already seen four times. The Floor Model Sale callout in the middle of the email is legitimately useful when you're outfitting a second home — floor models are often 40-60% off and I can actually take them same-day rather than waiting six weeks for delivery.
- That said, the hero above the fold is basically asking me to come into a store to shop a July 4th sale, which is… a lot to ask on a Saturday. The "Book a Free Appointment" button is smart for high-ticket furniture, but it sits next to "Find Your Store" like they couldn't decide whether they were driving foot traffic or converting a design consult. Pick one. The product grid in the middle — Outdoor, Furniture, Bedding categories — is the same skeleton they've been emailing all week just repackaged around the in-store hook.
- Would I click? Maybe the Floor Model section, because I am genuinely looking for floor lamps and a credenza for the second house and floor models solve my "I need it now" problem. Would I forward it? No. Would I book the design appointment? Probably not from an email — but I might remember it next time I'm already in the store.

## What's working

- **Floor Model Sale banner is a real differentiator.** Up to 70% off furniture lighting and more, in-store only — that's a specific, time-pressured reason to act that I haven't seen in the previous PB blasts this week.
- **"Recommended Just for You" section signals they know me.** Whether the personalization is actually good or not, the label alone tells me they have purchase data on me and are at least trying to use it.
- **"Book a Free Appointment" CTA is credible for high-ticket purchasing.** When I'm buying a sofa, I actually want to sit in it. This is the right ask for a design-services arm.
- **Loyalty rewards visible in footer.** 10% back in rewards — I'm on that program, and seeing it reminded me to check my balance, which is more than most footers do.
- **No render bugs that I can see.** Clean layout, images loaded, no broken hierarchy.

## What's weak

- **Hero is trying to do too many things.** "Book an appointment" and "Find Your Store" side by side dilutes both — CB2's June 28 "Furniture on sale right now" send (9/10) had one clear action and I knew exactly where I was going.
- **In-store pivot creates friction for an email channel.** I'm reading this on my phone at 8am. You're telling me to go to a store. That's not a no, but it's a harder ask than "add to cart."
- **The "Explore 4th of July deals" product grid is the same visual template as last send.** Outdoor / Furniture / Bedding buckets, thumbnail photography, "UP TO X% OFF" badges — Pottery Barn has run this exact creative skeleton every email since June 26. I'm pattern-blind to it now.
- **Cadence killed the credibility of the urgency.** "In stores now" implies scarcity but when you've emailed me every day this week I don't believe it's actually running out.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Floor Model Sale, not the generic hero.** That's the most differentiated thing in this email and it's buried below a product grid. Make the floor model angle the hero — it's in-store exclusive, time-limited, and high-discount. That's three urgency signals stacked.
- 2. **Collapse the dual CTA to one.** "Book a Free Appointment" is the smarter ask for a design-services sender. "Find Your Store" belongs in the footer, not competing with the primary CTA.
- 3. **Give the rug recommendation specific product copy.** "Find the Right Rug" tells me nothing. Tell me it's the Jute Woven, 9x12, in natural, at 60% off. That's what Article did with the Cillian collection send and it made me actually look up the sofa.
- 4. **Suppress or throttle this to people who haven't engaged with the prior four sends.** I opened all of them. I clicked once. Send me the floor model email specifically; skip me on the generic 4th of July grid.
- 5. **Vary the sender name more intentionally.** "Design Services" is good differentiation from the main marketing list — but if you're going to use it, lean into that identity in the copy, not just the from-name.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Floor models: up to 70% off — in store this weekend only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Design Services deal: furniture floor models, 70% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Take it home today — no lead times, no waiting. Book your appointment.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Floor model exclusives don't ship — but you can grab them now.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has two confirmed broken assets, a critical template rendering defect that embeds PHP `header()` calls as literal CSS, and systemic alt-text absence across all image slices. Compliance headers are unverifiable through the relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Two broken assets (HTTP 403)**

| Asset | URL |
|---|---|
| Credit card CTA | `https://www.potterybarn.com/pages/credit-card/?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember` |
| West Elm cross-brand tracking pixel | `https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` |

Note: the credit card link lives inside a commented-out `<!-- -->` block in the current HTML, so it is not rendered to subscribers. The 403 suggests the destination URL itself is also broken, which would be an issue if the comment were removed. The West Elm pixel is active and silently failing.

**WARN — 25 click-redirect links unverifiable**

All CTAs route through `click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=...` with opaque query strings. UTM parameters cannot be confirmed without following redirects.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CRITICAL — PHP `header()` calls injected inside `<style>` block**

```html
<style>
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
  ...
</style>
```

This is server-side PHP code that was not executed — it was emitted literally into the CSS context. It is invalid CSS and indicates a template rendering defect (a PHP snippet intended for HTTP response headers was included in the wrong template partial). No known email client will interpret it as CSS, so visual rendering is likely unaffected, but it signals a broken build pipeline.

**FAIL — Pervasive missing alt text (38+ images)**

Every image slice in the body (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_10.jpg`) and footer (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`) has `alt=""`. Product images (beds, curtains, bedding) and the logo also lack descriptive alt text. With images blocked, the email renders as a blank white column for screen readers and image-off clients.

**WARN — Two images served over HTTP (mixed content)**

```
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg
```

Gmail, Outlook, and most modern clients block HTTP-sourced images. These will not render.

**WARN — Cross-brand asset dependency**

`spacer_0.gif` is served from `edm.westelm.com`, a sibling brand domain. It is currently 403ing. Any West Elm infrastructure change breaks this silently.
