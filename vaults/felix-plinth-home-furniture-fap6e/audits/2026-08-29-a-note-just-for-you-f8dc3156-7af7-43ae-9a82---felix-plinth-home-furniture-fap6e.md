# A note just for you!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-29**
## Executive summary

- Same Crate & Kids "text wall" I flagged an hour ago at 4/10, just with a new subject line slapped on it — "A note just for you!" is somehow even more generic than "Hi friend" was, and yes, it still opens with "Hi friend." Not "Hi Felix," not "Hi Registry," friend. Fifth send from this brand family this week and I'm now just numb to it.
- The actual content underneath is fine — up to 50% off nursery furniture, 60% off bedding, Double Rewards stacking on top — that's a real Labor Day stack if I squint. But it's buried in five straight paragraphs of underlined-link soup before I even get to a button, and that button is grayed out enough that I genuinely can't tell if it's clickable or disabled. Compare that to CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" send I gave 7/10 — that one at least led with a real hero image and one clean CTA. This is the opposite of that.
- I'm marking this read and moving on. Not forwarding it — my wife already gets forwarded the Article and CB2 sends when there's an actual visual to react to; there's nothing to screenshot here.

## What's working

- The offer stack itself is legit and specific — 50% off nursery/kids bedroom, 30% off study/storage, 60% off bedding, 50% off rugs, 60% off decor/lighting, 50% off toys. That's a lot of real numbers, not vague "up to X% off" filler.
- Double Rewards on top of the discount is a genuine incentive for someone like me who's chasing rewards-card math.
- The nursery mobile photo lower in the email is a good demographic touch — matches a parent renovating a kid's room, which is exactly my situation right now.

## What's weak

- "Hi friend" again — this brand has sent me at least five emails this week and still hasn't bothered to use my name or acknowledge I'm a registry/repeat customer.
- The hero section is a pure text wall with underlined phrases standing in for buttons — no visual hierarchy, nothing for my eye to land on first. I have to read five paragraphs before hitting the actual "Shop The Labor Day Event" button, and even that looks disabled/grayed out.
- Way too many competing CTAs stacked below the fold — download the app, book a free TheDesignDesk appointment, apply for the Crate Card, find a store. By the third ask I've mentally checked out.
- Zero product imagery in the actual sale pitch — I don't see a single crib, dresser, or rug until the app-download photo, which isn't even shoppable.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the "Hi friend" open entirely — use my name or at least segment by "since you registered for [category]." This is the single biggest miss across all five sends this week.
- 2. Lead with one hero product image and one clear offer (e.g., "60% off bedding") instead of a five-paragraph bullet list — CB2 and Article both prove a clean hero outperforms this format.
- 3. Fix the primary CTA button so it visually reads as clickable — right now "Shop The Labor Day Event" looks grayed-out/disabled against the beige background.
- 4. Cut the secondary asks (app download, design service, credit card) down to one, or move them to a follow-up send — five competing CTAs in one email kills conversion.
- 5. Vary the creative from the last four Crate & Kids/Crate & Barrel sends this week — reusing "Labor Day Event" language for the fifth time is fatigue, not a campaign.
