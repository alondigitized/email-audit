# 🌼 NEW designs from Monique Lhuillier
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- Sixteen. I'm holding that number in my head before I say anything else, because at this point the Pottery Barn inbox experience is its own endurance sport. The June 13 "Up to 70% off 100s of NEW DEALS" send was #15. This one makes sixteen. So the first thing I'll credit: the Monique Lhuillier name is a genuine pattern break. It got me to look twice. That's more than the previous four sends managed.
- But here's the problem — Monique Lhuillier is a bridal designer. I know exactly who she is. Her aesthetic is unmistakable in the hero: soft lemons, fresh florals, romantic garden table settings, the kind of thing that photographs beautifully at a rehearsal dinner and lives nowhere in either of my two renovation projects. The hero image confirms it completely. This email is aesthetically targeted at someone who is planning a wedding or a garden party, not a 36-year-old guy trying to furnish a mountain house and a primary simultaneously.
- The "Recommended Just For You" section below the fold is actually the most useful part of this email for me — there appear to be actual living room and bedroom pieces there. But it's buried under a bridal tablescape I have to scroll past first. I'm marking this read. I won't click. I'll forward it to my partner, who will genuinely love it.

## What's working

- The designer name in the subject is a real break from sixteen consecutive sale-badge sends — the subject pulled my eye even under inbox fatigue.
- The Summer Sale offer (up to 70% off) is retained in the hero, so there's still a discount hook even for people unmoved by the designer angle.
- The email renders cleanly — lemon-yellow florals, no broken images, no layout collapse, the hero loads crisply and hierarchy is unambiguous.
- The "Recommended Just For You" section is structurally smart — PB is acknowledging that the Monique Lhuillier buyer and the renovation buyer might not be the same person and trying to serve both in one send.

## What's weak

- Monique Lhuillier's aesthetic is bridal/romantic/feminine, full stop. As a 36-year-old male with two active construction projects, this hero is not for me and the product imagery confirms it at a glance.
- "SHOP NOW" is the laziest CTA that exists. Even Wayfair — which I've now clocked at 51 emails and counting — occasionally writes "Shop Outdoor" or "Shop Dining." On a designer collaboration email, "SHOP NOW" is a miss.
- The hero product (tabletop, entertaining, floral linens) maps to nothing on my actual buy list right now. I need a sectional and a side table, not a lemon-yellow table runner.
- Sixteenth email. The "Up to 70% off" language in the hero is identical to emails 7 through 15. The designer name is the only variable that changed, and it's not the variable that unlocks my wallet.
- No ship time, availability, or in-stock signal anywhere visible — when I'm buying for two addresses, timing is often the deciding factor and it's absent here the same way it's absent in every Pottery Barn send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this send by gender/renovation signal.** The Monique Lhuillier collab email sent to a known male homeowner should foreground the furniture and home pieces from the collection, not the bridal tablescape. PB almost certainly has the data to do this. Use it.
- 2. **Promote "Recommended Just For You" above the designer hero for the male/renovation segment.** Lead with what I actually want, then hand me to the collab.
- 3. **Write a real CTA for a designer collaboration.** "Explore the Collection," "See Monique's Picks," or "Shop the Collab" — anything that signals this is an event, not a clearance rack.
- 4. **Add a quick-ship or availability signal.** Article does this without cluttering the visual and it's the one thing that actually moves me to click on furniture. CB2's "ALMOST OVER: Design Legends Sale" moved me to click with four words. Urgency without a deadline is meaningless.
- 5. **Reduce cadence.** One well-targeted email beats sixteen untargeted ones. CB2 at 27 sends since mid-May is still less fatiguing than Pottery Barn at 16 sends in under a week.
