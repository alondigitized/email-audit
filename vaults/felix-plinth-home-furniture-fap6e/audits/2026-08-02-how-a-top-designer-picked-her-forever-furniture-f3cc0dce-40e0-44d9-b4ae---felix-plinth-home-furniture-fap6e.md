# How a top designer picked her forever furniture
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-02**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "designer picked her forever furniture" — that's a strong hook for a headline, and then the email just... doesn't stop. Lisa Pollock's home makeover, then a living room, then a dining room, then "come shopping with Lisa," THEN a second designer (Lupita Nyong'o's sister, apparently, via The Design Desk), then the Aspen collection, then an espresso machine, then a credit card pitch. That's not an email, that's a catalog stapled to a newsletter stapled to a financing pitch.
- I'm renovating two places right now so the "forever furniture" angle should land dead center on me, and the living room / dining room sections genuinely do — oak side tables, boucle chair, that extendable dining table, all stuff I'd actually screenshot for my wife. But by the time I hit the second celebrity-designer block I've mentally checked out, and an espresso machine at the bottom feels like it wandered in from a different email entirely.
- Would I click? Yes, on the dining room section specifically, because I need a real table right now. Would I forward it? No — too scattered to send to a friend without a "sorry for the mess" caveat. This gets marked read, dining room section bookmarked, rest scrolled past.

## What's working

- "Lisa Pollock's home makeover" hero photo is a real lived-in room, not a stock white-box — that visual credibility is the thing Crate & Barrel's been doing consistently, same as their 8/10 "Starting at $1599: Sofas made to last" send from two days ago.
- The dining room section names actual pieces (Basque Extendable Dining Table, Libby Dining Chair, Laird Performance Chair) instead of vague "shop the look" copy — that's the level of specificity I want when I'm actually pricing a table.
- Design-forward framing ("a living room with style that lasts") talks to me like someone renovating, not someone browsing for fun.
- Product grid near the bottom shows real thumbnails with visible price context, not a wall of text.

## What's weak

- Two separate celebrity-designer campaigns (Lisa Pollock AND Lupita Nyong'o's sister via The Design Desk) stacked in one email — pick one, I don't need a second designer pitch four scrolls later.
- The espresso machine and the "we love these for you" plate/rug/table grab-bag at the bottom have nothing to do with "forever furniture" — feels like every leftover promo got dumped into this send because it existed.
- Credit card pitch at the very bottom, after I've already scrolled through two designers and a coffee maker, is where attention goes to die — nobody's still reading by then.
- This is basically CB2's problem too — I gave their "best-seller roundup dressed up as a design showcase" a 7 for the same reason, cramming too many categories under one house-y headline. C&B is doing it here with an entire second brand collab bolted onto a single-designer story.
- No pricing visible anywhere in the living/dining sections, just product names — Wayfair's headboard email at least led with a number even though it buried it under a clearance banner.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into two emails: one is Lisa Pollock's living/dining makeover (kill everything after "come shopping with Lisa"), the other is The Design Desk / Lupita Nyong'o's sister piece as its own send. Trying to do both dilutes each.
- 2. Cut the espresso machine and the generic "we love these for you" grid entirely — they have zero connection to the "forever furniture" story and read as filler.
- 3. Move the credit card CTA out of scroll-fatigue territory — either drop it from this email or put it right after the "shop the sale" hero where eyes are still fresh.
- 4. Add actual prices or "starting at $X" under at least the dining table and living room hero pieces — Pottery Barn's florals email skipped this too and it's the difference between browsing and buying for someone comparison-shopping three catalogs at once.

- **Subject Alt B:** `The dining table she says lasts a lifetime`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Real rooms, real picks — shop her exact pieces`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Up to 60% off the pieces she chose to keep`
