# You'll use these plates all. The. Time.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-12**
## Executive summary

- Okay, now THIS is the Crate & Barrel I actually subscribed for. After yesterday's "★★★★★ style for your home" send and the fall tablescaping guide before that, here's a third dinnerware email in three days, but at least this one has an actual reason to exist — new dishwasher-durable stoneware collections (Aspen Coupe, Marin, Mercer, Aspen Rimmed) with real prices and real swatches. I'm mid-renovation on two kitchens, so plates I could actually picture in either one is exactly my lane.
- That said, four collections deep with near-identical white-bowl-of-salad photography starts to blur together by the time I hit "Mercer" — my eye glazed over around the third "Shop Now" button. The DesignerDesk/TheDesignDesk collab section (with a $5,000 sweepstakes) feels bolted on and honestly more interesting than the plates by that point.
- Verdict: I'd scroll and maybe tap into Aspen Coupe since the price point ($34.95 area) is called out clearly, but I'm not forwarding this to my wife — it's too long and too repetitive for that. Mark as read after a lazy scroll.

## What's working

- Real product photography with visible price points on Aspen Coupe and Mercer — that's more than Wayfair gave me this week with its blank white rectangle hero.
- "dishwasher durable + simply beautiful" hero copy speaks directly to how I actually live, not aspirational nursery fluff like the Crate & Kids sends have been pushing.
- Star ratings visible on a couple products (Aspen Coupe, Mercer) — small trust cue that this isn't just stock photography.
- The "we love these for you" personalized-feeling grid near the bottom is a nice touch, even if I can't tell what it's actually personalized on.

## What's weak

- Four back-to-back dinnerware collections (Aspen Coupe, Marin, Mercer, Aspen Rimmed) with the same white-bowl-salad-shot format is a lot of scroll for one email — CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS" send at least varied its visual rhythm.
- The DesignerDesk $5,000 sweepstakes and app-download pitch feel like two completely different emails stapled to the bottom of a dinnerware catalog.
- Nothing here acknowledges I might already own C&B stuff — no "complete your registry" or loyalty-tier language, just cold-catalog browsing.
- By the time I hit the fourth "Shop Now," the visual hierarchy has flattened — nothing tells me which of these four collections is actually the one to click.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this to one hero collection (Aspen Coupe, since it's cheapest and most versatile) and turn the other three into a compact "also new" strip instead of full sections — that alone would fix the fatigue.
- 2. Split the DesignDesk sweepstakes into its own send — it's a genuinely different offer (a $5,000 giveaway) and it's getting buried at position 7 of 8 sections.
- 3. Add one line of loyalty/rewards math ("earn 5% back with your C&B card") since I shop by highest-rewards card and that's an easy nudge for me specifically.
- 4. Tighten subject and preheader — see below.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New stoneware, dishwasher-tested (from $8.95)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Plates that survive your actual kitchen`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Aspen Coupe, Marin & Mercer — durable stoneware starting under $10`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New tabletop essentials, plus enter to win $5,000`
