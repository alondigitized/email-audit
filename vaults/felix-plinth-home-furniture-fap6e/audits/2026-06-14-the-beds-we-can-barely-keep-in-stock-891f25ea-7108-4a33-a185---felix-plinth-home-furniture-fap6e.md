# The BEDS we can barely keep in stock
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- Fifty-two. I don't have the exact count in front of me but we have to be in that neighborhood. And yet — I'll admit — this subject line actually caught me. Both houses are mid-renovation and beds are on my actual list right now. "Can barely keep in stock" is a scarcity signal I can't fully tune out when I'm genuinely trying to buy bed frames this month.
- Then I open it and get a HOT SUMMER SALE with a blue patio umbrella and outdoor seating sets sharing screen space with the beds. The subject made a specific promise and the body walks it back into a generic catch-all sale. That's a subject-line trick written to generate opens, not conversions. Wayfair did the exact same thing on June 13 with swimming pools — "here's a focused thing" that unpacks into an inventory dump.
- The 5% rewards callout in the middle section is legitimately useful to me — I'm running every major furniture purchase through the highest-rewards card I have, and member pricing matters. But one useful element doesn't save a bait-and-switch. I'll mark it read, not click. No forward.

## What's working

- Loyalty rewards ("earn 5% rewards on every item, always!") is front and center — relevant when you're doing the rewards math on a $2,000 bed frame
- HOT SUMMER SALE in the hero is seasonally correct and above the fold; the hierarchy at least moves the eye downward toward products
- Subject line scarcity ("can barely keep in stock") is a real hook for someone actively shopping beds — it's the one moment this email treats me like a known buyer
- Renders cleanly — no broken images, no placeholder text, no layout collapse; Wayfair is consistent here

## What's weak

- Subject promises beds; product grid delivers patio umbrellas and outdoor furniture with no section break — the premise collapses the moment you scroll
- "Score styles you >" is some of the laziest CTA copy I've seen this month — not a verb-noun pair, slightly off grammatically, and gives me no idea where the link goes
- No specific discount percentage anywhere visible — "HOT SUMMER SALE" is noise when Pottery Barn just ran "up to 70% off" the same week and CB2's warehouse sale always leads with a number
- June 13 alone had two emails (swimming pools, outdoor rugs); this is June 14; the cadence has crossed from aggressive into disrespectful
- No hero product — Article will put one beautiful bed front and center with one link; Wayfair gives me twelve tiles of mixed inventory

## Recommendations

- 1. **Honor the subject line's promise**: If you say beds, every tile above the fold is a bed or bedroom piece. The outdoor furniture gets its own send — which, based on the June 13 outdoor rugs email, they already have a template for.
- 2. **Put a number on the offer**: "Up to 60% off beds" converts better than "HOT SUMMER SALE." CB2's warehouse sale emails always lead with the specific figure. Wayfair keeps burying it or skipping it entirely.
- 3. **Kill "Score styles you >"**: "Shop sale beds" or "See what's left" — anything with a real verb and a noun. The current copy reads like a draft that shipped.
- 4. **Hero one product**: Pick the single most-wanted bed in the sale, give it a full-width shot, link directly to its PDP. Article and Crate & Barrel do this routinely; Wayfair keeps defaulting to the mosaic.
- 5. **Throttle**: Two emails on June 13, one on June 14 — this cadence is how you train people to ignore the sender name.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Beds that keep selling out — here's what's left`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Our most-wanted beds, up to X% off this week`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your 5% member rewards stack on every bed in the sale — frames, platforms, storage.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Limited inventory on the beds everyone's buying — shop before they're gone.`
