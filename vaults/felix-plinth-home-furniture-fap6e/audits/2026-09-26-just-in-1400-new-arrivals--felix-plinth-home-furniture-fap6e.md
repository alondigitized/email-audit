# Just in! 1400+ NEW ARRIVALS
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-26**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "Just In! 1400+ New Arrivals" — my first reaction is that this is a category directory, not an email. Pillows, throws, bedding, lighting, armchairs, furniture, decor, tabletop & bar, each with its own tiny header, stacked one after another like I'm scrolling a full department store instead of getting pitched on anything specific. There's no single hero moment, no "this is the one thing you need to see," just section after section of nicely shot room photography with product-category labels underneath.
- The good news is the styling is genuinely on-brand — warm, layered, autumnal rooms that look like something I'd actually want in the second house. But "1400+ new arrivals" is a number designed to make me feel like there's too much to process, not a reason to click. Compare that to the 2026-09-26 "Most-Loved Towels" send, which picked one boring-but-necessary category and let me act on it in five seconds. This one just dumps the whole catalog and hopes something sticks.
- I'm not clicking anything specific because nothing's telling me where to click first — it's "Shop Now" at the top for the whole ecosystem, then a "Shop All New Arrivals" button buried after eight sections of scrolling. I'd skim, maybe pause on the furniture/leather armchair shot since that's my renovation lane, and archive it. Not forwarding this one — there's no single image or offer worth sending a friend.

## What's working

- The furniture and armchair shots (leather chesterfield, that dark wood sofa table) actually match what I'm shopping for on the second-house renovation right now.
- "SHIPS FREE" badges on pillows and throws are a real, useful signal — I care about shipping cost/clarity on bulkier textile items.
- The "Recommended Just For You" grid at least attempts personalization with named SKUs (Cayman Platform Bed, Livingston Executive Desk) instead of generic filler.
- Loyalty content is present near the bottom — "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" ties to the Pottery Barn credit card, which is relevant since I optimize for the highest-rewards card.

## What's weak

- No single offer anywhere — no %/$ off, no promo code, just "new arrivals." Compare Crate & Barrel's 10%-off subject (even though that one bait-and-switched me with a texting popup) — at least it led with a number.
- Eight stacked category sections with no differentiation in visual weight — my eye doesn't know where to land first, it just scrolls.
- "1400+ New Arrivals" as the come-on is the opposite of curation — CB2's "Restock alert" had the same category-dump structure but at least implied urgency (back in stock, might sell out again).
- The rewards/credit-card/app content is crammed into the footer as an afterthought instead of being woven into the shopping sections where it'd actually influence a purchase decision.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick ONE hero category per send instead of eight. If furniture is trending for fall, lead with that, and move "explore everything else" to a single link at the bottom.
- 2. Put an actual number on it — a %-off, a "free shipping over $X," anything. Right now there is zero price signal anywhere near the top of the email.
- 3. Move the 10%-back rewards card mention up near the hero — that's the single most persuasive lever for someone like me who buys on the highest-rewards card, and it's currently buried past six scrolls of product shots.
- 4. Cut the section count in half. Combine Pillows/Throws and Bedding/Lighting into fewer, denser blocks so the email reads as curated rather than exhaustive.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Fall furniture is in — 10% back with your PB card`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New this week: the pieces we'd grab first`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Leather seating, warm lighting, and the throws we can't stop restocking.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your rewards card gets you 10% back on all of it.`
