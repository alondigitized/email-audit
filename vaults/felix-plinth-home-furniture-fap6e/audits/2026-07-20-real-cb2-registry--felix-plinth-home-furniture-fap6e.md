# REAL CB2 REGISTRY
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- Thirty. That's where CB2 lands in my July inbox, and this is the second CB2 email today — the first being "Add these to your art collection," which I actually gave credit for breaking the all-caps streak. That credit evaporated the moment "REAL CB2 REGISTRY" landed with its full-shout subject line. We had one day off. One.
- The word "REAL" is doing something strange here. Real as opposed to what, exactly? A fraudulent registry? I opened this partly out of brand loyalty and partly because I genuinely wanted to know why they felt the need to qualify it. What I found is actually decent — the hero photography with two well-dressed men at what reads as a formal occasion is aspirational in a way that matches my aesthetic register, and the "SHOP MOST HEARTED" framing is the smartest social proof mechanism I've seen in this inbox cycle. The problem is I'm renovating two homes simultaneously. My gift registry mode is completely dormant. This email is built for someone planning a wedding, not someone cross-referencing nightstands across two renovation timelines the way I am.
- I'd scroll it. I'd tap into "SHOP MOST HEARTED" out of renovation-research habit. I wouldn't build a registry, and I definitely wouldn't forward this — it doesn't scan as a "look what I found" email, it scans as "CB2 thinks I'm getting married."

## What's working

- The hero photography earns its space — two men in suits in a formal setting is aspirational, inclusive, and genuinely on-brand for CB2's aesthetic positioning
- "SHOP MOST HEARTED" is smart framing; it turns the product grid into crowdsourced preference data, which is more interesting than a cold editorial selection
- The glassware/table lifestyle shot mid-scroll has the quiet, moody editorial quality that makes CB2 feel different from Pottery Barn's warmer catalog sensibility — it's a real differentiator
- Sending from "CB2 Registry" rather than the parent "CB2" list signals they're managing segmented sends; more than most brands in this inbox bother to do
- Visual hierarchy holds cleanly — hero leads, lifestyle image bridges, grid follows; the eye knows where to go

## What's weak

- All-caps is back the morning after "Add these to your art collection" proved you don't need it — that regression stings
- "REAL" in the subject line raises a credibility question I didn't have before I opened; it reads as either defensive or weirdly casual, neither of which closes a sale
- No offer anywhere visible — pure brand/registry play with zero conversion incentive; even a "complete your registry for 10% off" line would change my calculus
- The product grid in "SHOP MOST HEARTED" is too compressed to create desire at mobile scale — I can see there are items, I can't evaluate them without tapping through
- As the second CB2 email today and the 30th in July, the cadence is working against every individual send, including this one

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop all-caps permanently.** Yesterday proved title case works. "Real CB2 Registry" reads more confident than "REAL CB2 REGISTRY" reads desperate.
- 2. **Either justify "REAL" or cut it.** If there's a campaign story about authentic taste versus trend-chasing, put it in the preheader. If there isn't one, the word is just noise.
- 3. **Add one registry benefit hook.** Free completion gift. 10% off remaining items post-event. Free shipping on registry fulfillment. One line. Right now there's no financial reason to click that isn't available elsewhere.
- 4. **Enlarge the "SHOP MOST HEARTED" grid or reduce to 3 hero items.** Give me something I can evaluate in a single thumb-scroll, not a compression artifact.
- 5. **Wayfair sent me "NIGHTSTANDS ⚫ BLACK FRIDAY IN JULY" the same week and hit me in my actual renovation need.** This email could do the same — if CB2's registry system knows I've browsed certain categories, lead with the most-hearted picks from those categories, not a generic grid.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The pieces people keep adding to their CB2 registry`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Most hearted right now — your registry shortlist`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Glassware, seating, and the classics. Curated by what real registrants are saving.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `See what's trending on CB2 registries — plus, complete yours for 10% off.`

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users from pinching to zoom on mobile. Accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4 — Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Conflicting image `max-width` breakpoints:**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 100% !important} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 690px) { img {max-width: 690px} }
```
The `690px` rule appears after the `768px` rule in the stylesheet. At viewport widths ≥768px, both fire — the `690px` cap wins due to cascade order, overriding the intended `100%` behavior. Fix by merging or reordering.

**No dark mode support detected** — no `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` rule in visible CSS. The hardcoded `background:#ffffff` and `a { color: #333333 }` will not adapt in dark-mode clients (Apple Mail, Gmail app on iOS). Risk of invisible light-on-light text.
