# ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Shop the Finn Collection, starting at $379
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-14**
## Executive summary

- Fourth Crate & Kids/Crate & Barrel-family email in my inbox this week, and this one's the whole-house tour — nursery, bedroom, study space, "finishing touches," backpack bundles, a $5,000 sweepstakes, and a credit card pitch, all stacked in one endless scroll. The Finn Collection itself is fine — greige/sage furniture, clean lines, not offensive — but I don't have a kid in the nursery stage anymore, so a crib-and-bunk-bed collection starting at $379 just isn't where my two renovations are headed. This reads like it got sent to my whole household list instead of to me specifically.
- I'm not clicking. This is a mark-as-read, and if anything I'd forward it to my sister who's actually furnishing a nursery, not to anyone on my own reno group chat. It's competent kids' furniture merchandising, just aimed at the wrong guy.

## What's working

- The "for their bedroom / nursery / study space" section breaks make the scroll at least navigable, unlike Wayfair's wall-of-grid approach.
- Product tiles show GREENGUARD Gold certified badges and "Shop the Finn Collection" CTAs repeated at each section — consistent and easy to find if I were shopping.
- The $5,000 sweepstakes and credit card offer near the bottom are at least a real incentive, more concrete than the generic "greatest hits" quote email from earlier this week.
- Color palette (sage green, warm wood, soft neutrals) is consistent top to bottom — feels like one cohesive collection, not a mashup.

## What's weak

- This is a nursery/kids furniture collection and I'm renovating two adult homes — zero relevance to my actual project. Crate & Kids keeps mistaking me for a parent shopping for a crib.
- The email is enormous — nursery, bedroom, study, "finishing touches," backpacks, sweepstakes, credit card, app download all crammed into one send. Article's "Good changes" email was tighter and more focused even without a discount.
- No swatch/sample program, no shipping-time clarity for furniture (delivery windows matter a lot for a crib or bunk bed), which is exactly the kind of detail Pottery Barn's design-services email at least gestured toward with real appointment info.
- "Starting at $379" in the subject but I have to scroll deep to find any actual prices on individual pieces — CB2's sale email was more upfront with the % off math.
- Backpack bundle and $5,000 sweepstakes feel bolted on, like three separate campaigns duct-taped into one email.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this — if I've never bought nursery furniture from you, don't lead with a crib collection; lead with whatever category I've actually browsed (adult bedroom, home office).
- 2. Cut the email in half. Pick "Finn Collection" or "sweepstakes" or "backpack bundles," not all three plus a credit card pitch.
- 3. Put actual prices on the hero tiles, not just "starting at $379" in the subject line.
- 4. Add a shipping/lead-time line for furniture — that's the #1 friction point on cribs and bunk beds specifically.

- **Subject Alt B:** `New this week: the Finn Collection for kids' rooms`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `GREENGUARD Gold certified, ships in 2 weeks`
