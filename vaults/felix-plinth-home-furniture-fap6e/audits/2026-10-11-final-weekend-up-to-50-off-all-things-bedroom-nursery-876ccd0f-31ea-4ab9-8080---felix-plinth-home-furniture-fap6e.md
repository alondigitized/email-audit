# Final weekend! Up to 50% off all things bedroom & nursery
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-11**
## Executive summary

- This is the same Crate & Barrel Kids send I got earlier today, and my first reaction was fatigue. The earlier "Final weekend!" and "Ends tomorrow: Free shipping on orders $99+" emails already covered the nursery and bedroom event. I've now had roughly six C&B family emails in two days. I'm renovating two houses, and my lists are sofas, lighting and rugs. A crib and baby bath tile stack isn't where I'm shopping.
- The email itself is clean. It has a free-shipping banner, a clear "Up to 50% off" headline, and a long run of tiles with a button under each. But it's about ten category tiles deep before anything is relevant to me. "Lighting & storage" is the only tile that overlaps with my lists, and it sits near the bottom third.
- I wouldn't click and I wouldn't forward it. I'd leave it in the pile with the others. The one thing that might get a second look is the Design Desk "book a free appointment" block, which is the showroom/design-help content I actually care about. It's buried near the footer.

## What's working

- The free-shipping banner at the very top ("limited time only! free shipping on orders $99+") gives me a concrete threshold before I've scrolled.
- The "Up to 50% off" headline and the "Shop the Sale" button sit right under the logo. The offer is obvious.
- Each tile has its own specific button ("Shop Nursery Seating", "Shop Kids Bedding"). That's a verb plus a noun, not a generic "Discover".
- The lifestyle photography is good, especially the dark green nursery room and the grey bedding stack. It looks like the catalog I know.
- The Design Desk "book a free appointment" block is the right idea for someone doing two renovations.

## What's weak

- The email is a very long stack of tiles with no clear lead product, so it's a lot of scrolling for one sale.
- It's the third send today on the same event, with the same 50% message and the same free-shipping hook. That's the repetition I get annoyed by.
- Almost everything is kids and nursery. Nothing speaks to a 36-year-old furnishing two full homes.
- The "we love these for you" strip is strollers and a crib. It's labelled as personalized, but it clearly isn't personalized to me.
- The legal fine print at the bottom is a dense wall of tiny text. Free shipping and sale exclusions would be easier to trust if they were stated up top.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Don't re-send the same event.** If I opened the first two, suppress this one or swap in a different hero.
- 2. **Cut the tile count.** Lead with two or three hero categories, and use browse and purchase history to decide which ones. Crate & Barrel Kids knows I haven't touched nursery.
- 3. **Move "lighting & storage" and the Design Desk block higher** for non-parent segments.
- 4. **Replace the generic "we love these for you" strip** with items based on what I actually viewed.
- 5. **Put the free-shipping terms in plain text next to the banner** instead of only in the footer.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a standard ESP template with `lang="en"`, an XHTML doctype, MSO conditionals and a mobile breakpoint. The QA run flagged 30 images with no alt text, and the unsubscribe and authentication headers could not be verified. The HTML source was truncated, so the body links and footer were not available to inspect.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Link destinations unverified.** 70 tracking links were skipped because they go through click-redirect domains, so none were probed. Final destinations, redirect health and 404s are unconfirmed.
- **Many third-party and tracking pixels.** All of these are image requests that fail QA for missing alt text:
  - `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?...&n=1` through `&n=5`: five LiveRamp pixels.
  - `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png?mi_u=...`: the same URL appears six times.
  - `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif?mi_u=...`: likely the open-tracking pixel.
  - `https://dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/6506b6bc-...?mi_cid=...&mi_mid=...`: a further tracker.
- **Recipient identifier in pixel URLs.** `mi_u=56aaa2787a67...` is carried in the image URLs. This is expected for tracking, but the LiveRamp pixels (`sr.rlcdn.com`) carry the same hash in `s=`, which passes the identifier to a third party. That is a privacy-disclosure consideration.
- **Remote image dependency.** Creative assets are served from `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com` and `s7d5.scene7.com`, so images depend on Apple Mail Privacy Protection, image blocking and CDN availability.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Alt text missing on 30 images.** The count is 15 from `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/.../m/1/*` (including `.gif` files such as `7a163567-...gif`), 1 from `s7d5.scene7.com/.../2026_0926_CBK_Clearance_SecondaryStory.gif`, 6 from `mi.../f3c0d22992a01946.png`, 1 from `971aa10f-...jpg`, 5 from `sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif`, and 1 each from `o.gif` and `dv.../6506b6bc-...`. If the content images are meaningful, blocked-image clients get no fallback text. Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` explicitly.
- **Pinch-zoom restricted.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks user zoom (WCAG 1.4.4).
- **Text resizing disabled.** `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` is set on `*` and `body`, which prevents text scaling in some clients. The same `body` rule is also declared as `100%` earlier, so the declarations conflict.
- **Invalid markup under the XHTML doctype.**
  - `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` has an unescaped ampersand.
  - `<meta charset>` is duplicated alongside the `http-equiv` Content-Type.
  - `format-detection` is declared three times.
  - A template placeholder comment remains: `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->`.
- **Possible oversized-image rule.** `@media (min-width:768px){img{max-width:600px}}` and `(min-width:640px){img{max-width:640px}}` overlap, so the cascade is order-dependent. I couldn't verify the effect on the truncated body.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No unrendered merge tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`) were visible in the portion of the source provided. The per-recipient `mi_u` and `mi_cid`/`mi_mid` parameters populated correctly. The rest of the body could not be checked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` header not found.** The QA tool notes the AgentMail relay may strip it.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found.** One-click unsubscribe is therefore unconfirmed. Gmail and Yahoo require it for bulk senders.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment are unknown. The From domain `mail.crateandbarrel.com` is a subdomain, so alignment needs a check against the raw headers.
- **Footer not verifiable.** The body was truncated before the footer, so the visible unsubscribe link and physical postal address could not be confirmed.

These are inconclusive rather than confirmed failures, because the relay is a plausible cause.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. All 70 links were skipped as redirect domains and no resolved destinations or UTM parameters were captured. The campaign ID appears only in asset filenames (`2026_0926_CBK_Clearance_...`). That date (Sept 26) differs from this send (Oct 11), which suggests creative reuse but is not itself an error.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to the content images, and `alt=""` to the `rlcdn`, `mi.crateandbarrel.com` and `dv.crateandbarrel.com` pixels.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport tag. Replace `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` with `100%`.
3. Escape the ampersand in `<title>` (`&amp;`). Remove the duplicate meta tags and the `IMPUT HERE` placeholder comment.
4. Check the raw `.eml` headers (not the relay copy) for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` and SPF/DKIM/DMARC results.
5. Make the audit pipeline resolve the redirect chains for a sample of the 70 links (HEAD/GET following redirects) so UTM and landing-page checks can run.
6. Consider whether the five LiveRamp pixels carrying the hashed recipient ID are covered by the privacy policy.
