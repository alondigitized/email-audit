# Want exclusive sneak peeks and sale alerts?
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- Nineteen. I had to go back and recount but yes — this is the nineteenth email from the Crate & Barrel family of brands since July 8th. Two and a half days. And the brand's chosen message for send number nineteen is: *would you like to sign up for more communication from us?*
- I genuinely sat with that for a moment. The audacity. The sheer, breathtaking audacity of a brand that has landed in my inbox eighteen times in sixty hours asking me — on the nineteenth swing — if I want "exclusive sneak peeks." This IS the sneak peek. This IS the alert. I am drowning in them. Signing up for a text list from Crate & Barrel right now would be like agreeing to more turbulence mid-flight.
- Below the text-signup hero the email fractures into six or seven completely separate modules: outdoor dining recipes, personalized product picks, a Crate & Kids cross-sell (I don't have kids), a credit card pitch, an app download play, and TheDesignDesk. If this were a physical catalog it would weigh four pounds. I am not clicking the text signup, I am not forwarding this, and I am very much adding to the growing pile of evidence that C&B's email team is operating without a frequency governor.

## What's working

- **"We love these for you" product grid** is actually doing work — chairs, what looks like a KitchenAid-adjacent appliance, a bench — this is the category I'm shopping and someone's personalization engine got close
- **"Shop Bestsellers" secondary CTA** under the hero is a smart escape valve; anyone who doesn't want to join the text list can still convert
- **The outdoor dining section** is seasonally right — I am actively trying to furnish a second-home deck and "take every meal outside" is a real pitch for a real moment in my life
- **Visual hierarchy of the hero** is clean — phone mockup, two buttons, done; the eye knows exactly where to go before it gets lost in the scroll

## What's weak

- **The core ask is tone-deaf at this send cadence** — asking someone receiving their 19th email in 2.5 days if they want to *opt into more* is a self-own; Pottery Barn's "Free Design Services" send from their recent history (scored 8.00 by me) understood the value of a service offer to break cadence; this understands nothing
- **The kitchen-sink scroll** — text signup + recipes + product picks + Crate & Kids + credit card + app + design desk is six separate email campaigns stapled together; Article just sent a clean new-arrivals email (scored 8.00) with one job, one scroll depth, one CTA family; this is the opposite of that
- **Crate & Kids cross-sell is irrelevant to me** — I've now audited three emails this morning and this sub-brand keeps appearing (send #16, send #17, now #19) and I do not have children; CB2's Todd Snyder collab email I audited recently at least knew its audience
- **"Exclusive" is functionally meaningless here** — I've received 18 prior emails without signing up for texts and they weren't exclusive; that word has been drained of meaning before the ask even lands
- **TheDesignDesk promo is buried** — this is the one module that actually has legs for someone like me mid-renovation, and it's four scrolls deep below a credit card pitch

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with TheDesignDesk** — flip the entire email; the design service offer is the one thing that breaks from "promotion" into "value," put it first, let the text-signup be an appendix
- 2. **Kill the Crate & Kids module** — I've told you by browsing history or lack thereof that I'm not in that cohort; CB2's targeting on their collab sends is sharper than this
- 3. **Cut to three modules maximum** — hero + one product category + one CTA; this email is trying to do more jobs than a utility knife
- 4. **Reframe the text signup** as an access tier, not just "more alerts" — "text subscribers saw this lamp 48 hours before it sold out" is a pitch; "exclusive sneak peeks" is not
- 5. **Suppress this send after 10 emails in a rolling 7-day window** — the frequency is the product problem; no creative fix survives 19 sends in 60 hours
- **Subject Alt A:** `Our text list saw this before it hit the site`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Skip the email flood — text alerts only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `48-hour early access on new drops, sale previews, restocks`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `First look at new arrivals before they sell out — one text at a time`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Conflicting `img` `max-width` media queries (confirmed bug):**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
```
At viewport widths ≥ 768px, the 600px rule overrides the 640px rule, constraining images to 600px on full desktop even if the container supports more. The ordering makes the 600px cap the effective desktop value.

**Zoom prevention via viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom in webview renders (iOS Mail, Gmail app). WCAG 1.4.4 failure for users needing text magnification.

**Global `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied via wildcard:**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applies to all elements, suppressing iOS automatic text-size correction globally. Should be scoped to `body` only, or use `100%` rather than `none`.
