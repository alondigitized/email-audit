# 3 DAYS LEFT! Up to 60% off is going, going... nearly gone!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- The countdown timer stopped me — 02:15:26:24, rendered right in the hero, and my brain did the math before I could resist. Two and a half days. That's a real number. The discount also moved: I watched this inbox scream 50% all through July 4th, and C&B quietly bumped it to 60% today. That's the kind of escalation that makes you feel like the deal is actually dying.
- But here's the thing: I already reviewed an email with this exact subject line this morning. Same "3 DAYS LEFT! Up to 60% off is going, going... nearly gone!" — C&B sent this twice on July 5th. That's not urgency engineering. That's a broken drum machine. Compare that to Article's three-email arc over eight days (June 26 → July 1 → July 3) — their deadline card on July 3rd felt *earned* because they paced themselves. C&B is firing the same round twice before breakfast.
- I scrolled the whole thing because I'm genuinely in-market — two active renovations, C&B on both lists. The product grid covers seating, outdoor, and dining in the same scroll, which maps to my current situation. But the cadence damage is real. I'd click a category link if I spotted something I've been watching. I would not forward this. I'd mark it read and move on.

## What's working

- **Countdown timer is doing real work.** "02 : 15 : 26 : 24" in the hero gives the deadline a physical shape — not "hurry," but *this many hours*. That's the right move.
- **The 60% bump registers.** Yesterday was 50%. Today is 60%. That delta is visible if you've been paying attention, and I have been. It makes the "nearly gone" language feel plausible.
- **Blue/navy art direction is a deliberate palette shift.** After four straight red FINAL WEEKEND banners, the cooler hero actually reads as new information rather than a retransmission.
- **Product grid is renovation-broad.** Seating, outdoor, dining — covers enough of my current brief that I kept scrolling.
- **Renders clean.** No broken images, no overlapping text, type is legible without zooming.

## What's weak

- **Duplicate send, same day, same subject.** This is the most damaging thing in this email program right now. The morning version of this email trained me to ignore the next one.
- **Cadence is completely out of control.** Six-plus C&B family sends in 48 hours. Even Pottery Barn — which has been hitting me with "ONLY 48 HOURS LEFT" all week — managed to feel less suffocating than this.
- **CTAs are generic "SHOP NOW" throughout.** The hero puts in real urgency work with the countdown, then hands off to the most anonymous button copy possible. "Shop Upholstery Sale" or "Shop Outdoor" would actually close the loop.
- **No loyalty or member acknowledgment anywhere.** I'm clearly a known subscriber. One line — "your 60% applies automatically as a C&B Rewards member" — would have changed the register entirely from broadcast to relationship.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the duplicate send.** One per day, maximum, during any sale window. Nothing else on this list matters until the program sends each email once.
- 2. **Segment by browse or purchase history.** If I've been looking at upholstered seating, show me the sofa grid. Article's July 3rd email felt sharper because it narrowed. This scattershots dining, outdoor, seating, and kitchen simultaneously.
- 3. **Personalize the hero.** "Felix — 60% off runs out in 2 days" beats "Up to 60% off is going going nearly gone" for someone who's been on this list long enough to see the discount climb.
- 4. **Anchor one hero SKU.** CB2's July 4th email used a single palm-shadow render as a visual stake in the ground. That gives the click a destination. The countdown here has no companion product to click *toward*.
- 5. **Sharpen button copy.** "Shop Outdoor Sale," "Shop Upholstery" — verb + category, not verb + nothing.
- **Subject Alt A:** `60% off ends in 2 days — here's what's left`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The 60% is almost gone. Sofa or outdoor first?`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Countdown is live. Seating, outdoor, dining — final clearance.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Two days left. These pieces at 60% won't come back at this price.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Conflicting `img` max-width media queries (likely rendering bug):**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
Both queries apply at ≥768px viewports. Because the `640px` query is declared *after* the `768px` query with equal specificity, it wins via cascade — images cap at **640px** on all desktop clients, not the 600px the template apparently targets. For a 600px-wide email container, this permits images to overflow by 40px on wider layouts.

**Mobile breakpoint too narrow:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 460px)
```
Standard modern phones range 390–430px logical width (iPhone 15 Pro Max = 430px). Android flagships commonly reach 480px. Devices in the 461–479px range receive desktop layout. Standard practice is 600px or at least 480px.

**Hardcoded `.showmobile` width inside mobile breakpoint:**
```css
.showmobile { width:414px !important; }
```
This is pixel-locked to the iPhone 12/13/14 width, inside a `max-width: 460px` query that won't fire on many current phones.

**Universal selector `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`:**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```
Disabling text scaling on `*` prevents iOS accessibility users from using browser text resize. Use `-webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%` on `body` only.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags:** Three separate `format-detection` metas appear in addition to the combined one — redundant but harmless.

**Duplicate charset declarations:** Both `http-equiv="Content-Type"` and `<meta charset="utf-8">` are present — redundant, no functional impact.

**Alt text on images:** Cannot verify — body truncated before `<img>` tags.
