# Seating spotlight: Why we love leather
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- Thirteen. We are at thirteen Pottery Barn emails in roughly 48 hours, and I want to be honest about where my head was when this one arrived: I almost didn't open it. I had the Chris Loves Julia send from this morning still half-skimmed in my saves folder, and the subject line "Seating spotlight: Why we love leather" read like the brand wanted to have a conversation I didn't ask for. Editorial framing. No offer number in the subject. Just vibes and craft leather content.
- But here's the thing — I'm literally deciding between leather and performance fabric for the reading nook in the second home right now. So "why we love leather" is, annoyingly, exactly the kind of justification copy I need on hand when I'm arguing with myself at 11pm. The hero image pulled me in. A warm, lived-in brown leather sectional, layered room, rich tones. That's aspirational in a way that actually maps to what I'm trying to accomplish. But then the email goes full catalog on me — four rows of products at thumbnail scale — and loses the editorial thread it promised in the subject. I didn't click. I saved it. Mark as read, folder it, revisit when I'm comparison shopping leather versus fabric. Not a click; not a forward.
- Compared to what C&B sent today — "NEW MARKDOWNS! Up to 60% off" — this email is doing a completely different job and doing it without an offer hook to justify the interruption. C&B gives me a reason to act. PB gives me a mood board.

## What's working

- **Hero image is genuinely aspirational.** Warm brown leather, layered textiles, lived-in room — this is the exact reference image I'd save to my design folder for the second home's main seating area.
- **Subject is specific and editorial.** "Seating spotlight: Why we love leather" is a concrete category play. No mystery. No bait-and-switch. For someone actively choosing seating materials, that scans.
- **Product variety is honest.** The grid covers armchairs, accent chairs (that green velvet piece actually caught my eye as a contrast option), and dining/desk chairs. It doesn't pretend the whole collection is leather when it isn't.
- **"Recommended just for you" section.** Spotted near the bottom. It's small, but the personalization gesture is there — that's more than most Wayfair sends bother with.
- **No broken renders, no lorem ipsum, no layout blowout.** Clean execution. After thirteen emails, the production value is consistent. That's worth noting.

## What's weak

- **No offer.** Zero. This is the killer. C&B just sent 60% off markdowns. CB2 dropped a Todd Snyder collab with real event energy. Article sent kitchen content that at least culminated in a call to shop a specific item. PB sends me brand storytelling with no price relief, no discount, no "members get X." At email thirteen, I need a reason to act, not a reason to admire.
- **The editorial premise evaporates after the hero.** The subject promises a point of view on leather. The body delivers a product grid. That's a broken contract. Article's kitchen email actually maintained its editorial voice through the whole send. This drops it after the first scroll.
- **Product thumbnails are too small to evaluate at mobile scale.** Four-up grids at this scale mean I can't read product names, prices, or even confirm the material. Crate & Barrel consistently runs larger hero product callouts with readable prices. This feels like desktop-first design.
- **No pricing visible anywhere.** Not even a starting-at figure. When I'm making real purchase decisions on two homes, I need to know if we're in the $400-chair category or the $1,200-chair category before I even tap through.
- **Cadence is simply unsustainable.** Thirteen emails in 48 hours from one brand. The Chris Loves Julia send this morning was legitimately good and I already saved it. This one competes with its own sibling from six hours ago.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with a clear offer or a strong editorial commitment — pick one.** Either give me a "leather seating, starting at $X" or "free swatching on leather pieces this week," or commit fully to the editorial format with a real paragraph about leather durability, patina, and cleaning (the objections I actually have). The current hybrid serves neither goal.
- 2. **Cut the four-up product grid and replace with two hero products at full width.** Show me the leather sectional from the hero image with a name and a price. Show me one contrasting option (maybe that green velvet chair, positioned as "if leather's not right for you"). Two products, priced, linked. That's a click.
- 3. **Add a leather swatch/sample CTA.** This is the single most useful thing PB could offer someone in my position — a free leather swatch to hold against my paint chips. Article does this implicitly. PB has the infrastructure. Use it.
- 4. **Reduce cadence.** Batch the content sends. The collab email and this leather editorial could have been one consolidated lifestyle send. Instead I got two nearly simultaneous messages competing for the same mental folder.
- 5. **Price anchor in the hero copy.** Even "leather seating from $X" in the subhead gives me a reason to click instead of save.

### 1. Technical Summary

One confirmed broken link (403) on the credit card acquisition banner, combined with a server-side PHP code leak into the HTML `<style>` block and 3 HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources constitute the most urgent production issues. Accessibility coverage is poor at scale, with 50+ images missing `alt` text.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken link — 403 on credit card apply-and-buy CTA**

The primary banner CTA for the Pottery Barn Key credit card returns HTTP 403:
```
https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682
  ?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB
  &marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY
  &external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner
  &cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember
```
This is a high-value acquisition link. Any recipient clicking the card-application banner hits a dead end. The `clickcp.potterybarn.com` subdomain differs from the standard `click.e.potterybarn.com` click-tracking domain used for all other links — likely a misconfigured or expired route in the credit card partner's click proxy.

**[INFO] 23 tracking links unverifiable** — all routed through `click.e.potterybarn.com`; redirect destinations could not be probed. Final-URL integrity is unconfirmed.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[FAIL] PHP server-side code leaked into rendered `<style>` block**

The following PHP statements appear verbatim inside a `<style>` tag in the `<head>`:
```php
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are PHP `header()` calls that were never executed server-side — they were included as raw text in the template, likely from a copy-paste of a dynamic image script. Email clients will treat this as invalid CSS and ignore it, but it bloats the payload and signals template hygiene problems. Strict parsers or security scanners may flag the email.

**[WARN] 3 HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources — likely blocked in modern clients**

| File | URL |
|---|---|
| `spacer_0.gif` | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` |

Gmail and Outlook proxy image loads over HTTPS; HTTP sources either fail silently or trigger mixed-content warnings. The `spacer_0.gif` is additionally cross-brand — it's served from `edm.westelm.com` inside a Pottery Barn email.

This pixel will be blocked or proxied by Gmail's image proxy, making open-rate data unreliable regardless.

**[WARN] 50+ images missing `alt` text**

Subscribers with images disabled see a blank email. Screen reader users get no content.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not captured**

The header was not present in the relay-intercepted copy. If missing in actual delivery, this violates Gmail's bulk sender requirements (enforced since Feb 2024 for senders >5K/day). Could be a relay artifact — must be verified against raw SMTP headers at the MTA.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found**

One-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policy) relies on `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`. Absence means Gmail's "Unsubscribe" UI button may not work, pushing unsubscribes to the in-email link only.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) not captured via relay**

SPF/DKIM pass status is unconfirmed. Given that the sending domain is `e.potterybarn.com` (an ESP subdomain), DKIM alignment to `potterybarn.com` depends on the ESP's signing configuration. Cannot verify without raw headers.

**[OK]** CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link are present in the footer (visible in HTML structure). `robots: noindex, nofollow` is set. No deceptive subject line issues are within scope of this technical review.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**[WARN] UTM parameters on final destination URLs unverifiable**

All 23 non-broken links pass through `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirect wrappers. The raw encoded query strings (e.g., `qs=ABB7InYiOjEsImQiOjQ4...`) are opaque base64/encoded payloads — UTM decoration on landing pages cannot be confirmed without resolving the full redirect chain.

The one fully-resolved link (the broken 403 credit card URL) uses proprietary tracking params (`cm_ite`, `external_id`, `marketingChannelCode`) rather than standard `utm_*` params. If landing page analytics rely on UTMs, this link would not have attributed correctly even if it resolved.

**[WARN] Plain-text alternative is 75% URLs (12,654 of 16,912 chars)**

The plain-text body is effectively a URL dump with minimal human-readable content. Spam filters (SpamAssassin, Postfix milters) penalize high URL-to-text ratios in plain-text parts. This may increase spam folder placement rate.
