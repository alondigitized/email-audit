# RSVP for FREE design services
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-31**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one actually stopped my thumb — "RSVP for FREE design services" lands different than the usual PB sale blast because I'm mid-renovation on two houses right now and I actually need someone to tell me if the jute rug goes with the walnut dining table. The WHAT/WHEN/WHERE layout up top is doing real work: one-on-one sessions, in-home or virtual, book a slot. That's a genuinely useful offer, not another "70% off" retread like the Halloween-bait email I called out on 7/31.
- Where it loses me is the middle third — three near-identical "BRING THIS LOOK TO LIFE" room blocks that just repeat the same CTA with different throw pillows, followed by a "Recommended Just for You" grid that has nothing to do with design services at all. It's like two different emails got stapled together.
- I'd click the "Book a Free Appointment" button — that's a real use case for me right now. I wouldn't forward it to a friend unless they're also mid-reno; this isn't shareable content, it's a personal to-do I'd bookmark and act on later, probably from my laptop when I'm actually ready to sit down with someone.

## What's working

- The WHAT/WHEN/WHERE format up top answers my exact questions before I have to scroll — better than CB2's straight discount blocks I've been seeing all week.
- "In your home, virtually, or in store" — flexibility that respects I've got two properties and not unlimited time to drive to a showroom.
- Unlike Crate & Kids shoving kid content at me for the fourth time this cycle, Pottery Barn stayed in its lane here — this is squarely aimed at someone renovating, which is literally my situation.
- The 10% back rewards card call-out near the bottom is relevant — I already buy on my highest-rewards card, so seeing the math spelled out is useful, not filler.
- Clean render, no broken images, no overlapping text — basic bar, but Wayfair's firehose sends haven't always cleared it.

## What's weak

- Three back-to-back "BRING THIS LOOK TO LIFE" room blocks with the same CTA is padding — I got the idea after room one, and by room three I was just scrolling to escape.
- "Recommended Just for You" grid (curtain rods, a dining table, towels) has zero connection to the design-services hook — it reads like a generic footer block got pasted in, not a personalized cross-sell.
- No real urgency — "spots go fast" is vague filler, not a deadline. Article's "Ends Sunday" send at least gives me a reason to act today instead of next month.
- The in-store "Mixing Wood Finishes" class is a great fit for my actual renovation questions but it's buried below three redundant room blocks instead of leading.
- No pricing or scope info on what the free consult actually includes (how many sessions, is there a minimum spend) — I'd want that before booking, not after.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut two of the three "BRING THIS LOOK TO LIFE" blocks — keep one strong room shot, then move straight into the CTA. That alone tightens this by half a screen.
- 2. Swap the generic "Recommended Just for You" product grid for room-specific products tied to the design service (e.g., "Popular picks from Design Crew sessions") — right now it undercuts the personalization angle.
- 3. Give the appointment booking a real deadline ("Limited Design Crew slots in August — book by 8/15") instead of vague "spots go fast" copy.
- 4. Move the in-store "Mixing Wood Finishes" class higher — that's a concrete, dated, local event and probably converts better than three repeated lifestyle shots.
- 5. Add one line of scope detail under the CTA — "One free 45-min session per home, no purchase required" — so I know what I'm signing up for before I click.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
Email is built on a legacy XHTML 1.0 Strict / table-based ESP template (Salesforce Marketing Cloud-style, `click.e.potterybarn.com` / `image.e.potterybarn.com`) with a raw PHP code leak embedded in the markup and no unsubscribe/authentication headers captured by the relay.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **PHP code leaked into rendered HTML.** Inside the second `<style>` block, literal PHP `header()` calls appear as plain text in the output:
  `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0"); header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false); header("Pragma: no-cache");`
  This is not valid CSS — it indicates a template include (likely a tracking-pixel PHP script) was inlined/rendered as text instead of executed server-side. Cosmetically harmless in most clients (parsed as invalid CSS and ignored) but signals a broken build step upstream.
- All content links route through `click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=...` opaque redirect tokens (e.g. `ABB7InYiOjEsImQiOjQ5NTR9AAsAAAAAA3PNdrlUU7GH0Af...`) — 46 such tracking/redirect links were skipped by the automated HTTP probe per QA (expected for redirect-obfuscated links; not itself a defect).
- Third-party tracking beacons present: `secure.leadback.advertising.com/adcedge/lb?site=695501&srvc=1&betr=30802=711754[8760]` and `d.turn.com/r/dd/id/L21rdC84NjEvY2lkLzE3NDc3ODk3MzMvdC8y/cat/1` — both rendered as `<img>` pixels, both flagged for missing `alt` (expected/appropriate for tracking pixels, but confirms they are pixel-based, not JS-based, trackers).
- Open-tracking pixel `svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` embeds the recipient's raw email address in a plaintext URL parameter (see §4).

## 7. Recommendations
