# The Harvest Decor Shop is open
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-19**
## Executive summary

- This is the sixth Pottery Barn send I've logged in about three days. It's pumpkins and candle lanterns, and I'm renovating two houses with no Halloween or Thanksgiving mantel to style. The dark, moody hero is gorgeous, and it's the kind of image that goes straight into my Pinterest folder. But a decor shop doesn't match what I'm buying right now. Compare it to yesterday's "Nightstands that add storage & style" (10/19) or the 10/17 hand-knotted rugs email, which both hit actual purchases.
- The email does get useful further down. The "Recommended just for you" block has an extendable dining table, curtain rods and a Cayman platform bed, and that's where my brain woke up. There's also a "More room-defining details" nav with Coffee Tables, Armchairs, Throws and Rugs. I had to scroll past a full screen of gourds to reach any of it.
- My verdict: I'd save it, maybe click the dining table, and not forward it. It's a pretty email that's a little off-target for me.

## What's working

- The "Harvest Decor Shop" hero is moody and well photographed. The dark palette feels premium and styled, more like a catalog than a sale blast.
- "In-Stock Furniture Delivered in 1-4 Weeks" sits at the very top. That's the shipping clarity I need on furniture, and it's more concrete than most.
- "Recommended just for you" puts the Banks Extendable Dining Table and Toscana table in front of me. Those are real renovation buys.
- The "Free Design Services / Book a Free Appointment" block is a showroom-style touch I'd actually use across two houses.
- The Earn 10% Back in Rewards card block gives me rewards math, and I'm a rewards-card person.

## What's weak

- The first screen is all seasonal decor, and none of it matches the bedding, rugs and furniture I'm shopping for.
- There's no price, percent off or deadline anywhere. "Step into fall" gives me no reason to act today.
- I scrolled through three or four rows of identical pumpkin and candle tiles with no product names or prices. They read as mood boards, not shopping.
- The Thanksgiving and Christmas Shop banners stack seasons on top of each other. It feels like a catalog dump. Crate & Barrel's free-shipping blasts at least lead with one clear offer.
- The legal and fine print at the bottom is dense and tiny. The financing and rewards disclosures are hard to read on a phone.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the in-stock furniture promise and a single offer. Put the dining tables and bedroom pieces above the pumpkins, since the personalization block is the strongest part.
- 2. Add product names and prices under the decor tiles, or cut the grid to one row.
- 3. Drop the Christmas banner until the Thanksgiving window closes. Wayfair does the pile-on already, and I don't need Pottery Barn doing it too.
- 4. Add a real deadline or rewards hook, for example "Earn 10% back through Sunday".
- 5. Segment by browsing. I've gotten rug, bedding and nightstand sends this week, so a decor send feels off-pattern for me.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a sliced-image layout in XHTML 1.0 Transitional tables, with all links routed through click-redirect tracking. About 50 images have no `alt` text, 3 resources load over plain `http://`, and the recipient address appears in plaintext in pixel and image URLs. Compliance headers could not be verified (the QA warnings point to relay capture), and the HTML source was truncated.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** 58 links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are click-redirect URLs. One example is `https://click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=ABp7ImQi...` on the logo. Destinations, redirect health, and final status codes are unconfirmed.
- **Non-HTTPS tracking pixel:** `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` loads over HTTP. Clients may block it, which makes open tracking unreliable, and it exposes the address in transit.
- **Third-party pixel:** `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/L21rdC84NjEvY2lkLzE3NDc3ODk3MzMvdC8y/cat/1` is an ad-network pixel with no `alt`. It is a privacy and deliverability consideration.
- **Multiple open pixels:** three trackers are present, `svopn.potterybarn.com`, `clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/7417e2c2-...?mi_cid=...`, and `d.turn.com`. The `coreemail.html` image URL also carries the email address.

- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** both are absent in the captured message. The QA note says the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is unconfirmed rather than a confirmed defect. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require both.
- **Authentication-Results:** not found, so SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment are unknown. The sender is `PotteryBarn@e.potterybarn.com`, and the links and pixels use the matching `click.e.`, `clickcp.`, and `svopn.` subdomains.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)

- **Landing pages:** these could not be assessed because the redirects were not resolved. Product images point to `www.potterybarn.com/pbimgs/...`, which is consistent with the brand domain.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to the content slices, products, and banners, and `alt=""` to spacers and pixels. Add live text for key content, or at least the hero message.
2. Move the 3 `http://` assets (`Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, the 2022 brand banner `slices_02.jpg`, and the `svopn` pixel) to HTTPS.
3. Stop putting the recipient address in plaintext in image and pixel URLs. Use an opaque ID instead.
4. Remove the stray `header(...)` PHP text from the `<style>` block.
5. Fill in `<title>` and add `lang` to `<html>`. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
6. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` plus SPF, DKIM, and DMARC against the raw message at the source, not through the relay. Also confirm the footer unsubscribe link and postal address.
7. Resolve a sample of the tracking links, for example with a redirect-following probe, to check destinations and UTM carry-through.
8. Trim the plain-text part, or add real copy, to bring the URL share well under 70%.
