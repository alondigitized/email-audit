# THE LUXURY OF CUSTOM
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-13**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "THE LUXURY OF CUSTOM" — CB2 is doing the swatch program pitch this time, "This Is Personal... We offer 90+ custom fabrics for every shade, mood and vibe. Not for everyone. Definitely for you," with a free-swatches CTA up top. That's actually a beat I don't think they've hit yet this week, and it's relevant — I'm sitting on fabric decisions for two houses right now and burned once already ordering a sofa sight-unseen off a screen color. Then it just... turns into the same scroll I've gotten from this brand five times in seven days: white sofa, leather sofa, neutral sofa, then a "$5,000 REFRESH" countdown-adjacent banner that says "ENDS TONIGHT," an app download push, and a "MORE FOR YOU" grid of six random SKUs.
- The swatch hook at the top is genuinely good and on-brand for how I actually shop — I want to see the chenille before I commit, not after. But CB2/C&B family fatigue is fully in effect now; this is basically the same "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" send with a new headline pasted on. Compare that to Pottery Barn's dining table email from earlier this week, which stayed on one category the whole way down instead of stacking three different sofas plus a sweepstakes plus an app pitch plus a loyalty pitch in one scroll.
- Verdict: I'd click "START WITH SWATCHES" because that's a real, low-commitment action that matches my actual buying process — but I'm not forwarding this, and I'm rolling my eyes at getting my sixth CB2-family email this week.

## What's working

- The "90+ custom fabrics" swatch hook at the very top speaks directly to how I actually make furniture decisions — I don't buy upholstery off a monitor.
- "START WITH SWATCHES" as a real, distinct CTA next to "SHOP SOFAS & SECTIONALS" gives me two honest paths depending on where I am in the process.
- The leather section ("Like Your Favorite Leather Jacket," "100% top-grain needle leather") has actual material specificity — that's the kind of detail I look for before I click.
- Clean, moody photography throughout — the burgundy accent chair and the leather sofa vignette both look like rooms I'd actually want, not stock catalog beige.

## What's weak

- Five sofa/sectional callouts stacked in one email (hero chair, white sofa, leather sofa, neutral sofa, plus the grid) — this is the same repetition problem I flagged with CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS" send, just resorted.
- The "$5,000 REFRESH" sweepstakes with "ENDS TONIGHT" and a design-appointment booking feels bolted onto a swatch email — it's a different funnel (a giveaway) shoved into the middle of a product-education send.
- App download pitch + loyalty signup pitch + Crate&Kids/CB2/Crate&Barrel logo strip at the bottom is a lot of asks in one email — pick one.
- "MORE FOR YOU" grid is generic cross-sell (a bed, resin side tables, a coffee table) with zero connection to the swatch/fabric story up top — feels like a leftover template block, not personalization.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email down to just the swatch story — hero, one or two fabric-forward product shots, one CTA. This is the first genuinely differentiated angle CB2's sent me all week; don't drown it in five more sofas.
- 2. Pull the "$5,000 REFRESH" sweepstakes into its own send — it's competing with the swatch CTA for my one click.
- 3. Drop either the app push or the loyalty push at the bottom, not both back-to-back.
- 4. Make "MORE FOR YOU" actually reflect the swatch/fabric theme (show fabric-customizable pieces) instead of a random bed and side tables.

- **Subject Alt B:** `90+ fabrics. One swatch box. Free.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Order free swatches before you commit to a color`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `See it, feel it, then decide — swatches ship free`
