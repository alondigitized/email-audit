# Something spooky drops TODAY, only on our app! 🎃
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## What's working

- The Summer Sale "up to 50% off" copy is the one thing I'd actually act on — real number, real discount.
- The "we love these for you" product grid has what look like sideboards and dining furniture, which is in my wheelhouse right now. That module, if it were the hero, would've done serious work.
- Crate & Barrel is a brand I trust and recognize, so the open happened — the email didn't have to earn that.
- No render bugs — the layout held together cleanly even with all the competing modules.

## What's weak

- Halloween in July. I understand "Christmas in July" as a retail tradition. Halloween in July is not a thing, and hiding it behind an app download makes it worse.
- The app-download CTA as the hero gate is a conversion killer for someone like me. I already have 6 home/furniture apps. I'm not downloading a seventh to peek at a "spooky" teaser.
- Three competing CTAs (Download Our App, Shop Now, Learn More) with no visual hierarchy between them — Article ran a cleaner single-CTA email last week and it felt like a brand that knew what it wanted from me.
- The sweepstakes module ("You could win 1 of 3 $200...") is more noise. My July inbox already has this from CB2's "UP TO 40% OFF ENDS TODAY" era — I'm numb to sweepstakes overlays at this point.
- The Halloween hero and Summer Sale coexist in the same scroll without explanation. Pottery Barn's Chris Loves Julia collab kept one message, one campaign, one moment — this email is four campaigns badly stitched.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop the Halloween hero entirely or make it its own email.** If the app-exclusive Halloween drop is real and genuinely valuable, it deserves its own dedicated send — not buried under Summer Sale noise. As-is it undermines both.
- 2. **Lead with the 50% off Summer Sale.** That's the money shot. Big number, real discount, relevant to someone actively furnishing two homes. Make it the hero, make the product recs the scroll.
- 3. **Kill the sweepstakes module.** I've seen this in every C&B email for three weeks. It's become invisible — worse, it actively reads as desperation padding.
- 4. **Collapse to one CTA per send.** Pick a destination. App download, Shop Now, or Learn More — not all three.
- 5. **TheDesignDesk callout should be email-only, not tucked into footer.** That's actually a differentiator for someone mid-renovation — but right now it's lost below the fold next to app store badges.

- **Preheader Alt B:** `Up to 50% off. Plus book a free design session at your nearest store.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional table layout with Mailchimp/MI template scaffolding. The HTML source contains unremoved template artifacts and a conflicting media query pair that will produce inconsistent image sizing on desktop clients. The source was truncated, so link, compliance, and personalization sections are partially assessed.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

**Conflicting desktop image max-width rules** — two `min-width` media queries fire simultaneously on screens ≥ 768px, and the later rule wins due to cascade order:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }  /* fires first */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }  /* overrides above */
```
At 768px+, images resolve to `max-width: 640px` rather than the intended `600px`. If the email container is 600px wide, this is harmless — but if any image is coded wider than 600px expecting the 768px cap, it will overflow on desktop.

**Dark mode not supported** — `color-scheme: light` and `supported-color-schemes: light` explicitly opt out of dark mode adaptation. On Apple Mail and Gmail dark mode, background colors and image transparency may invert unexpectedly. No `prefers-color-scheme: dark` override block is present.

**Redundant `format-detection` meta tags** — The combined declaration on line 1 already covers all formats:
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
```
Three subsequent individual declarations (`telephone=no`, `address=no`, `date=no`) duplicate it. No functional harm, but indicates template hygiene gap.

**Alt text on images** — Cannot assess; source truncated before `<img>` tags. Verify all images carry descriptive `alt` attributes, especially the "spooky drop" hero image.
