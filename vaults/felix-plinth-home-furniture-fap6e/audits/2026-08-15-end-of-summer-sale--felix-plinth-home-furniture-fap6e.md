# End of Summer Sale
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-15**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "End of Summer Sale" from CB2 — again. This is the fourth CB2/Crate & Barrel-family email in my inbox this week if I'm counting right (I audited the Floor Sample Sale and Onyx & Walnut ones just yesterday and the day before), and my brain has fully entered C&B-family snooze mode at this point. The hero shot is genuinely pretty — those jewel-toned glasses catching light, moody and editorial — but there's no percentage, no dollar figure, nothing except "Shop the Sale" in tiny type on a black button. I have to actually click through to find out if this is 10% off or 40% off.
- The category grid underneath (Furniture, Lighting, Rugs, Decor) is clean and I do like that green sectional shot, but it's just stock category tiles, not "here's a specific piece at a specific price." Then it pivots hard into "leave a review, win $1,000" and rewards-card cross-sell, which feels like CB2 padding out the email because they didn't have a real offer to lead with. I'd skim this in about four seconds, not click, and definitely not forward — nothing here is specific enough to send a friend.

## What's working

- The hero image is genuinely well-shot — moody glassware, warm light, feels premium and seasonally appropriate for a summer send.
- Category tiles (Furniture/Lighting/Rugs/Decor) give a quick visual scan of what's on sale without forcing me to guess.
- The green sectional in the Furniture tile caught my eye the same way Crate & Barrel's Gather Sectional hero did — that brand clearly knows its color palette works on me.
- Rewards callout (10% back in Rewards Dollars at CB2 and Crate & Barrel) is at least relevant since I know these programs overlap.

## What's weak

- No actual discount number anywhere above the fold — "END OF SUMMER SALE" with a "SHOP THE SALE" button and zero percentage is a worse offer-clarity move than even CB2's own "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" email, which I gave a 7 partly because it was upfront about markdowns.
- Fine print at the bottom reveals "(UP TO 40% OFF SELECT FURNITURE, DECOR, AND MORE)" in tiny gray text — that's the actual offer, buried below four scroll-lengths of app downloads and review contests. That's a real number, and they hid it.
- This is the same "Onyx & Walnut"/"Floor Sample Sale" cadence problem all over again — three CB2 sends in four days is aggressive, and none of them have differentiated themselves with a hero product I'd actually screenshot for my Pinterest folder the way Crate & Barrel's sectional did.
- The "Leave Us a Review, Win $1,000" block eats prime real estate that could've shown an actual sale product with a price.
- No swatch/sample program mention, no shipping-timeline info for furniture — given I'm mid-renovation on two houses, that's the stuff that actually gets me to click, and it's absent.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the "up to 40% off" number in the hero, not buried in 6-point fine print at the bottom — that's the single biggest lift available here.
- 2. Swap at least one category tile for an actual hero product with a price and a "shop now" link, the way Crate & Barrel's sectional email did.
- 3. Cut the "Leave a Review, Win $1,000" block or move it to the very bottom — it's competing with the sale for attention and it shouldn't.
- 4. Add a shipping-timeline note for furniture ("ships in X weeks") — I'm renovating two houses right now and that's the actual blocker on big-ticket clicks.
- 5. Throttle the cadence — three CB2/C&B-family sends in four days is starting to feel like Wayfair's spam pattern, not a premium brand's.

- **Subject Alt B:** `End of Summer: your last shot at 40% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Furniture, rugs, lighting, decor — all marked down through Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The sectional you've been eyeing just got cheaper.`
