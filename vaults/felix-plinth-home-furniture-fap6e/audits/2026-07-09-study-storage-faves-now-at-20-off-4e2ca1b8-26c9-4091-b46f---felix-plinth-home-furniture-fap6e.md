# Study & storage faves—now at 20% off!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- Nine. I know because I went back through my inbox and counted — this is email number nine from the Crate & Barrel family of brands in approximately 48 hours. I want to be fair to the email itself, because the email is actually doing most things right on the content side. Clear hero, specific percentage, clean "Shop Desks" CTA. If this had arrived as email two or three in the cycle, I'd have clicked through to browse desks for the second-house playroom without much friction.
- But it didn't arrive as email two. It arrived at the tail end of a sprint that opened with adult Crate & Barrel lifestyle content, pivoted to fall arrivals via CB Kids, and has now narrowed to study furniture. The subcategory targeting is getting sharper — that's actually good list strategy — but the send volume has burned through every goodwill credit I had before I even read the subject line. My thumb is already hovering over archive.
- I won't click, and I won't forward. The 20% off is real and the layout is clean, but I'm going to do the same thing I did with emails seven and eight from this family: archive without acting and make a mental note to visit Crate & Kids directly when I'm ready to pull the trigger on a desk, bypassing their email program entirely.

## What's working

- The 20% off desk offer is specific and hero-level — no hunting required, it's the first thing you see.
- "Shop Desks" is an unusually literal CTA for this category; Wayfair keeps giving me "Discover" and "Explore" buttons that lead nowhere useful — this one actually tells me what I'm clicking into.
- The lifestyle hero image — warm wood grain, brick wall texture, considered light — reads "tasteful youth workspace" rather than "kid dungeon," which is the right framing for a parent who cares how the room looks.
- The free Design Desk service callout is a smart trust signal for a furniture decision of this size; Pottery Barn does the same thing with their design consultants and it genuinely moves me.
- Layering "up to 20% off select storage" beneath the desk hero creates a logical upsell without cannibilizing the primary offer.

## What's weak

- Nine emails in 48 hours from this brand family. The goodwill tank is empty before I read word one.
- "Crate & Kids SALE" as the sender display name — the all-caps "SALE" appended to a normally premium sub-brand reads like a clearance rack, not a curated shop.
- The lower half of the email carries an app download promo, a TheDesignGeek content block, and what appeared to be nursery/baby product imagery in the recommendations rail — none of which belong in a study-and-storage send targeting parents of school-age kids.
- No urgency signal beyond "now" — CB2 on their current 40% off send at least tells me the sale has a window; this email gives me nothing to act on before I close the tab and forget.
- "Up to 20% off *select* storage" is doing more work than it should — "select" is a trust killer when I've been burned by exclusion footnotes before.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill eight of the nine sends.** The single highest-impact change isn't inside this email — it's the cadence. One well-timed send would have done more than this entire sprint.
- 2. **Add a real deadline.** "20% off through Sunday" or "ends this weekend" gives me a reason to act instead of archive. CB2 and Article both do this reliably; C&B keeps leaving it on the table.
- 3. **Cut the bottom half.** Drop the app promo and the content block on this send. If the offer is study furniture, the email ends after study furniture.
- 4. **Name the exclusion or remove it.** Either say "20% off ALL storage" or list what's excluded. "Select" is the fine print you can't see.
- 5. **Commit to the sub-brand.** Lead confidently as Crate & Kids with its own personality, or fold this into the main C&B send with kids-room segmentation. The hybrid "SALE" handle is neither.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus free design help from our desk pros — no appointment needed`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Study setups, storage finds, and an actual end date`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — prevents user zoom on mobile, violating WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Line:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `*` selector** — overrides the correct `100%` value set on `html` and `body`. This suppresses iOS auto-sizing for all elements, degrading legibility on small screens.
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Should be `100%` or removed from the universal selector.

**Conflicting `img` max-width media queries** — two desktop breakpoints declare opposing constraints; the later-declared rule wins at all widths ≥640px:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }  /* declared first */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }  /* wins at 768px+ */
```
Net effect: images are capped at 640px on desktop, not 600px. Verify which is correct and remove the dead rule.

**Dark mode excluded** — `color-scheme: light` and `supported-color-schemes: light` suppress OS-level dark mode without providing a `prefers-color-scheme: dark` override. Images with white or transparent backgrounds will invert incorrectly in Apple Mail dark mode.

**`<title>` brand mismatch** — document title reads `Crate & Barrel`, not `Crate & Kids`. Minor but affects screen readers and tab display in webmail clients.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags** — `telephone=no`, `address=no`, and `date=no` are declared both in the combined meta and as separate tags. Redundant; de-duplicate.
