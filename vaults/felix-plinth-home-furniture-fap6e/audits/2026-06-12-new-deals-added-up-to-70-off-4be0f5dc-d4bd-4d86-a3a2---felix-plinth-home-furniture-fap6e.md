# NEW DEALS ADDED! Up to 70% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- Thirteen. That's where I am with Pottery Barn, and I'm holding that number at the top because it changes everything about how this email reads. Twelve was the quilt send yesterday. Eleven was quick-ship furniture. Ten was colorful rugs. And — this is the part that actually got under my skin — email ten on June 10 was a dedicated "FREE Design Services for Any Project" standalone. That same Free Design Services module is sitting right here inside this one, three emails later, as a recycled filler block. They're reusing creative faster than I'm buying furniture, and I'm buying a lot of furniture for two properties right now.
- What genuinely stopped my thumb: the "Recommended Just for You" grid below the hero. Farmhouse Platform Bed, Finn Linen Upholstered Bed with Footboard, Tacoma Extendable Dining Table. That is not a random pull. My browse history is clearly feeding this, and those are legitimately items I'm cross-referencing across both properties. The algorithm knows me better than the send-cadence team does. The hero offer is clean — "Summer Sale, Up to 70% off," single CTA, no clutter — and the category nav above the fold is something I actually use as a section index when I shop Pottery Barn deliberately.
- But I'm not clicking "Shop All Sale." That's a haystack. I'm mentally flagging the Tacoma table in the recommended section, closing this, and coming back on desktop. Mark as read. Not forwarding to anyone.

## What's working

- The "Recommended Just for You" module earned its place — Farmhouse Platform Bed and Tacoma Extendable Dining Table are exactly the categories I'm actively comparing across two properties right now.
- Hero offer is unambiguous: large "Up to 70% off," a single "Shop All Sale" button, nothing competing for visual priority above the fold.
- The category nav grid (Furniture, Sofas & Chairs, Bedding, Outdoor, Rugs, Lighting, etc.) is a real utility feature — I use it as an index, not decoration.
- "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" lands for me specifically — I carry the card, I run the math on high-ticket furniture spend, and a reminder embedded in a sale email is the right place to surface it.
- Summer Sale timing is correct; I'm in active buying mode and a well-placed discount reminder can move me off the fence on items I've been sitting on.

## What's weak

- "NEW DEALS ADDED!" in ALL CAPS in the subject is a spam-signal at email thirteen. Pottery Barn has my full purchase history — that tone is for a cold list, not a known buyer.
- Free Design Services appearing again two emails after the June 10 dedicated send makes it feel like module padding, not a genuine offer. Crate & Barrel at least rotates their secondary promos.
- "Shop All Sale" as the primary hero CTA dumps me on a massive landing page — Wayfair's sale emails, even the chaotic ones, at least route to category-specific pages; "DRESSERS & CHESTS" is overkill but at least it narrows the funnel.
- No deadline anywhere in this email. "Summer Sale" is a season, not urgency. Article's "Summer hosting made simple" send framed an occasion; this is just a discount floating in ambient time.
- Affirm module at the bottom is a brand-tone mismatch. If I'm spending $2,000+ at Pottery Barn, I'm putting it on the PB Visa for the 10% back — not splitting it into installments through a third-party financer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the hero with the personalized product grid, not the generic sale banner.** "Your picks are now on sale" with 2-3 of those recommended items and direct product links would double the conversion relevance. The algorithm is doing the work; let it drive the email.
- 2. **Retire the Free Design Services module or make it contextual.** Three emails in nine days with that block and it's wallpaper. Replace it with a contextual prompt: "Decorating two spaces? Book a multi-room consult."
- 3. **Put a deadline on the sale.** "Ends June 16" or "This week only" creates a decision trigger. Without one, I'll rationalize returning later — and later doesn't happen.
- 4. **Split the hero CTA into category-specific buttons** ("Shop Furniture" / "Shop Bedding") — the category grid right below proves their design team already knows this is how customers navigate.
- 5. **Ease the send cadence.** Thirteen emails in nine days from a single sender is an unsubscribe gradient.
