# Up to 40% off ends Monday
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- Article knows how to read a room — or at least a Memorial Day inbox. I have nineteen Pottery Barn emails since Saturday, twelve from Wayfair since Sunday, and somewhere around twenty-four from the Crate & Barrel family since last week. Article shows up with a clean green banner, a four-tile grid, and a deadline I can read in two seconds. That restraint earns immediate goodwill before I even look at the offer. The "ENDS MONDAY" flag at the very top is doing exactly what a deadline reminder email should do — front-loads the one thing a busy person needs first.
- Where it loses me is the category grid. Four tiles: Outdoor, Dining Room, Bedroom, All Sale. Functionally accurate, emotionally inert. I'm actively renovating two houses at the same time. I have a list. I have rooms that need specific things. Article apparently doesn't know any of that, or isn't using it, because this email treats me exactly like someone who landed on their homepage yesterday. CB2 featured actual named products in their "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" send last week — pieces I could reference — and it made me click something specific. This makes me feel like a browser, not a buyer.
- I'll click. Article's aesthetic is right for what I'm building, 40% off is real furniture money, and Monday is two days away. But I'm clicking into a category page hoping to find something, not because the email sold me on a specific piece. I would not forward it — there's nothing surprising here worth sharing.

## What's working

- **"ENDS MONDAY" anchors the entire layout from first glance** — compared to Pottery Barn's habit of burying the expiry in paragraph three of body copy, this is a masterclass in hierarchy.
- **"Up to 40% off" lands in the body copy without requiring a click** — the discount is visible, no mystery, no "open to find out."
- **Green color palette reads as Article immediately** — after a week of cream and beige from Pottery Barn and C&B, the brand signal is instant.
- **Category grid includes Outdoor and Dining Room** — both directly relevant to someone mid-renovation; they didn't park me in bedding and rugs.
- **"SHOP THE SALE" CTA is clean** — verb plus noun, no "Discover," no "Explore the magic."

## What's weak

- **No featured products anywhere** — four category tiles are navigation, not merchandising. This is a sale email, not a sitemap.
- **Free delivery is buried in the trust bar at the bottom** — Article's free delivery on furniture is a genuine leg up on Pottery Barn white-glove fees and Wayfair's checkout-surprise pricing. Hiding it below social icons is malpractice.
- **"Explore What's New" secondary CTA creates decision paralysis** — this is a deadline urgency email. A "new arrivals" button introduces a competing call-to-action in an email that has one job.
- **Third visit to the same sale offer** — May 19 was already "the deals are in full swing." This deadline reminder is valid send logic, but "up to 40% off hundreds of styles" is verbatim the same offer language. If you're going to hit me a third time, give me something new — a price drop, items added to the sale, a reason to look again.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Feature two specific products in the hero with strike-through pricing.** Pick the sofa. Pick the dining table. Show the original price and the sale price side by side. That's what converts someone actively furnishing rooms — not a 2x2 grid of category names.
- 2. **Move free delivery into the hero block.** Right below the sale headline: "FREE DELIVERY on everything in the sale. Ends Monday." That one line does more conversion work than the entire trust bar.
- 3. **Kill "Explore What's New."** One deadline, one CTA.
- 4. **Give me a reason this email is different from May 19.** "40 new styles just added to the sale" — even if it's not true, the framing makes me feel like opening this wasn't redundant.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

**CSS Error 1 — Trailing comma in dark mode selector (drops entire rule)**
```css
/* Inside @media(prefers-color-scheme:dark) */
h1,h2,p,{color:#fff!important}
```
The dangling comma before `{` is an invalid selector. CSS parsers that follow the spec will silently discard this entire rule, leaving `h1`, `h2`, and `p` text uncolored in dark mode on supporting clients (Apple Mail, iOS Mail, Outlook iOS).

**CSS Error 2 — Semicolon before `!important` (drops the declaration)**
```css
a{color:#fff;!important}
```
The `;` terminates the declaration before `!important` is parsed. The `!important` token is then treated as an unknown at-rule and discarded. Link color override in dark mode will not apply.

**Minor:**
- `<title></title>` is empty. Several clients (Gmail web tab, Outlook preview pane) surface the title. Not a rendering blocker but a missed inbox-preview opportunity.
- Alt text on images unverifiable from truncated source — needs full audit.
