# MORE ADDED TO SALE
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- Fifty. That's where I land with the CB2/Crate family this month, and the second CB2 email I've opened today alone — "Up to 50% off furniture & more" hit my inbox earlier this morning. So "MORE ADDED TO SALE" lands with the energy of someone waving their arms at me in a parking lot I'm already in. I get it, CB2. I'm here. I've been here.
- That said, I'll give it this: the hero works. The curved chaise/sofa in that warm neutral tone is genuinely beautiful, and 50% off furniture is the kind of number that stops my thumb mid-scroll when I'm trying to furnish a second bedroom. The July 4th framing is timely, the category tiles (Outdoor, Furniture, Rugs, Lighting, Decor, Tabletop) are exactly what I want to browse, and the "MORE FOR YOU" product recs at the bottom show at least some indication that CB2 knows what I've been looking at.
- But here's the real problem: the asterisk. "Up to 50% OFF*" with a footnote dumps me immediately into defensive-shopper mode. Is it 50% off one lamp from 2019? What's actually in this sale? Article ran "4th of July Sale starts now" earlier this month and the offer felt cleaner. I'd mark this as read, possibly revisit it on the weekend when I have time to actually click through the furniture tile — but I'm not clicking now. No forward.

## What's working

- **The hero product stopped me.** The curved organic sofa in warm cream is exactly the aesthetic I'm looking to bring into the second home. Someone made a good edit choice here.
- **"50% OFF" is big and unambiguous in the hero.** I don't have to hunt for the offer — it's the first thing that lands.
- **July 4th framing is current and earned.** Unlike some of the recycled "SALE OF THE SUMMER" language, this actually ties to a real calendar moment.
- **Category tiles are scannable.** Outdoor, Furniture, Rugs, Lighting, Decor, Tabletop — that's a clean six-tap navigation that maps exactly to what I'm shopping for in two homes.
- **"MORE FOR YOU" with product images and visible prices.** Appreciated the personalization nod at the bottom; it's the one moment the email treats me like a returning customer rather than a cold list.

## What's weak

- **Second CB2 email today.** That's not a gut feeling — the "Up to 50% off furniture & more" was this morning. Two emails in a single day from the same display name "CB2 SALE" is aggressive, and both live in my inbox simultaneously.
- **The asterisk on 50% OFF*.** Every time I see that I start doing math about what's actually included. It introduces friction right in the hero.
- **"MORE ADDED TO SALE" in ALL CAPS does no work.** It tells me nothing about what was added — category, product type, price tier. Compare that to Pottery Barn Design Services this morning, which at least called out "Up to 70% off" with a stores angle.
- **No rewards/points acknowledgment.** I'm buying via whatever card gives me the most back, and CB2 has a rewards program. At 50% off prices, reminding me that I also earn points would close me faster.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Tell me what's "MORE."** If outdoor furniture just dropped into the sale, say that. "Outdoor + rugs just hit the sale — up to 50% off" converts better than a generic announcement.
- 2. **Kill the asterisk ambiguity in the hero.** "Up to 50% OFF select styles" is honest and doesn't tank confidence the way a bare asterisk does.
- 3. **One email per day, CB2.** This and "Up to 50% off furniture & more" should be one send with a combined message, not two inbox slots fought for in the same morning.
- 4. **Surface the specific hero product as a shoppable link with dimensions.** The curved sofa is doing heavy lifting — let me tap it directly to a PDP with size and lead-time info. That's what seals it for someone furnishing two homes.
- 5. **Add a loyalty line.** "Earn rewards on every purchase — shop the sale." One line, no design change needed, closes faster for rewards-card shoppers like me.

**[MEDIUM] Double-slash paths in font `src` URLs**
```css

url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2")
```

Double slashes appear on three separate `@font-face` declarations. While most servers normalize these, it is a defect that can fail on strict CDN configurations.

**[MEDIUM] `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) failure; also flagged by Apple accessibility guidelines.

**[MEDIUM] Wildcard `line-height` rule**
```css
* { line-height: 100%; }
```
Applied globally via `*` selector. In email clients that do not fully sandbox CSS, this bleeds into elements where relative `line-height` collapses text — particularly buttons and multi-line headings.

**[LOW] Mobile breakpoint hardcoded to 418px**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { .mobile { width: 414px !important; } }
```
Targets legacy iPhone 6/7/8 width exactly. Current standard phones (375–430px viewport) and Android devices above 418px receive desktop layout. Industry standard breakpoint is 600px.

**[LOW] Cross-brand CDN dependency for HNW05 font**
```css

```
Four `HNW05` `@font-face` declarations load from `crateandbarrel.com`. CB2 email rendering silently depends on a sibling brand's CDN uptime and cache headers. Any crateandbarrel.com CDN change or CORS policy shift breaks this font with no CB2-side control.

**[LOW] "Lineto Brown Light Sub" missing woff2 source**
```css

  font-family: "Lineto Brown Light Sub";
```

Only `.woff` declared; `.woff2` (supported by all modern clients) is absent for the Light weight. A separate rule lower in the stylesheet loads Light as a weight variant of "Lineto Brown Sub", which may cause inconsistent font resolution depending on client cascade order.
