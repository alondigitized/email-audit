# Don’t miss it: Up to 60% off the Summer Sale!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- My first thought opening this was *didn't I just do this?* And yes — because I literally reviewed "Shop up to 60% off the Summer Sale →" from this exact sender this morning and scored it an 8. The subject changed, the campaign didn't. C&B sent two Summer Sale emails on the same day, which means one of them is already in my "seen it" folder mentally before I even open it.
- That said, the email itself isn't bad. The 60% off hero is front and center, the layout is clean, and there are product cards mid-scroll that look like real picks. What's genuinely weird is the corn photography. A golden ear of corn in the middle of a furniture sale email — even if it's a lifestyle/entertaining nod — snaps the visual flow and made me stop to figure out what I was looking at. That's not the pause you want.
- The TheDesignDesk section buried near the bottom is actually the most useful thing in here for me — I'm renovating two properties and would legitimately use a design consultation service — but it's positioned like an afterthought below the app promo. I'd mark this as read. Maybe click to the sale page if I remembered I wanted a dining bench. Wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- **60% off is front and center in the hero** — no scrolling required, no decoding required. That's the bar and they cleared it.
- **"New deals added!"** is a smart differentiator from the earlier send; gives me a reason to engage even if I've already seen a nearly identical email today.
- **"We love these for you" product carousel** feels like personalization, even if it's probably just algorithmic. I clocked the bench and the bed frame in that row.
- **Heritage-inspired finds sub-section** is a smart way to surface a separate collection inside a sale email — it creates a second reason to scroll.
- **Layout is clean and functional** — no broken images, no overlapping elements, readable hierarchy from hero down.

## What's weak

- **Same-day duplicate send** is the dominant story here. If I got "Shop up to 60% off" at 9 AM and "Don't miss it: Up to 60% off" at noon, that's not urgency — that's noise. Pottery Barn's coffee tables email this week felt curated; this feels like quota-hitting.
- **The corn image.** I genuinely paused. A full-frame photograph of golden corn in a Crate & Barrel sale email doesn't read as "summer entertaining" without copy context. It reads as "wrong email."
- **No deadline anywhere visible** — "Don't miss it" is a hollow urgency phrase without a date or a time. Wayfair's Black Friday in July email committed to dates. This doesn't.
- **TheDesignDesk is buried** — the one section that's actually relevant to someone mid-renovation is positioned after the app download pitch.
- **App promo with "$500"** needs context I don't have. Is that savings? A credit? The number floats without explanation.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Give the sale an end date in the subject and hero.** "Ends Sunday" or "3 days left" would make "Don't miss it" feel real instead of default.
- 2. **Cut one of today's two sends** — or make this one clearly additive ("New arrivals just joined the sale") so it doesn't feel like a retry of this morning's send.
- 3. **Kill or contextualize the corn.** If it's a summer entertaining lifestyle image, say so with a header. Without copy, it's a visual dead-end.
- 4. **Promote TheDesignDesk to section two**, right after the hero. Someone who clicked into this email is already thinking about their home — that's exactly when to offer the consultation.
- 5. **Sharpen the product CTA copy.** "Shop Furniture" or "Shop Dining" beats "Shop Now" for someone who has a specific room in their head.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New deals just dropped: Up to 60% off Summer Sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Summer Sale: 60% off, ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dining, bedroom, outdoor — new markdowns added today`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Hundreds of pieces added. Sale ends Sunday.`

## Technical Audit — Crate & Barrel Summer Sale Email

### 1. Technical Summary

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Viewport `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[MEDIUM] Conflicting desktop image `max-width` media queries — 768px rule is dead code**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }  /* declared first */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }  /* declared second — overrides at 768px+ */
```
At any viewport ≥768px both queries match, but the later 640px rule wins the cascade, making the 768px/600px rule unreachable. Images render at 640px max instead of 600px on desktop. Remove or reorder.

**[MEDIUM] Aggressive wildcard `line-height: 100%` and `max-height: 1000000px`**
```css
* { line-height: 100%; max-height: 1000000px; }
```
`line-height: 100%` on `*` resolves relative to each element's font-size (not computed line-height), causing text clipping in several clients. Scope to `td, p` at minimum.

**[LOW] Dark mode not supported**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Forces light-only. Apple Mail and some Android clients will still invert or recolor; C&B has no control over the result.

**[LOW] Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
Three redundant declarations following the combined one. No functional harm; indicates a template merge error.
