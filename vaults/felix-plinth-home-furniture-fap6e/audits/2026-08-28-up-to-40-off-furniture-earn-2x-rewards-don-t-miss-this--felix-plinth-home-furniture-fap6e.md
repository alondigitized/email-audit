# Up to 40% off furniture + earn 2x Rewards! Don’t miss this →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-28**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is Crate & Barrel's third proper send to me this week — I logged "Double Rewards + Up to 60% off" from them yesterday, and the day before that. Same "Double Rewards" banner, same navy hero block, same star icon. At this point the subject line changed ("Up to 40% off furniture" this time vs. 60% before) but the actual creative is basically a reskin. If I'm renovating two houses and tracking every brand's Labor Day math, this cadence is exactly the thing that makes me start filtering C&B to a folder instead of reading it live.
- The good news: it's a long, dense scroll of actual furniture — dining sets, bedroom pieces, storage, rugs, kitchenware — and the layout is clean enough that I can skim categories fast. The Design Desk callout ($5000 sweepstakes + free 30-minute consult) is the one thing that stood out as genuinely new versus their last two sends, and it's relevant to a two-house renovation.
- Would I click? Maybe, on "Shop Furniture Sale," just because I'm mid-renovation and the dining/storage shots are decent. Would I forward it? No — nothing here is surprising enough to send to a friend. I'd mark it read and move on, and honestly I'm getting close to just muting this sender for the rest of the week.

## What's working

- The category grid up top (suites/sectionals, bedroom, dining, storage/shelving) lets me jump straight to what I care about without reading paragraphs.
- The Design Desk block is a real value-add for someone renovating two homes at once — free styling consult + a $5000 sweepstakes is more relevant to me than another % off banner.
- Kitchen deals section shows actual strikethrough pricing ($104.99 reg $150, $129.99 reg $185) — that's the kind of concrete number that makes a click feel worth it.
- "we love these for you" personalization block at the bottom at least gestures at recommendation, even if generic.

## What's weak

- This is the third near-identical Crate & Barrel send this week — same navy hero, same "Double Rewards" star badge, just the discount percentage swapped between 40% and 60%. Article at least varies its creative between Labor Day pushes; this is starting to feel like CB2's eleven-emails-in-eleven-days problem.
- Subject says "Up to 40% off furniture" but the hero image says "up to 60% off" — that's a real mismatch I can see on screen, not just a hunch, and it undercuts trust in the number.
- Way too much scroll before anything new happens — it's five sale sections deep before I hit the kitchen deals, which is the first thing that felt distinct from yesterday's email.
- No visible loyalty/rewards balance or member tier — "Double Rewards" is pitched at me generically, not tied to my actual account, so it reads like a stranger's blast, not a known-customer message.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the subject/hero mismatch first** — 40% in the subject vs. 60% in the hero image is a credibility problem before I've even scrolled.
- 2. **Cut send frequency** — three C&B emails in a week with recycled hero art is Crate&Kids-level fatigue (I flagged that account for six sends this week already); consolidate to one strong Labor Day send.
- 3. **Move the Design Desk block up** — it's the most differentiated content in the email and it's buried two-thirds down.
- 4. **Surface my actual Rewards balance/tier** if they have my account data — "Double Rewards is on!" with a real number would beat the generic banner.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Labor Day: up to 60% off + Double Rewards, ends 9/8`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Your Double Rewards + new dining sets are live`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: book a free Design Desk consult before the sale ends`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Kitchen deals ship free — see today's top 5`

Standard ESP-templated (MessageGears/MI) HTML email with heavy mobile-responsive CSS and multiple tracking pixels; automated QA reports a 57% pass rate driven mainly by missing image alt text and absent unsubscribe/authentication headers at the relay level.

No broken pixel URLs found in source.
- No issues found with malformed href syntax in the visible source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 33+ `<img>` tags missing `alt` attributes, including all primary product/lifestyle images (`image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/*.jpg`) and all tracking pixels/gifs. Product imagery lacking alt text fails WCAG 1.1.1 and degrades the experience for image-blocked or screen-reader clients — the email would render as a near-blank shell.
- Template includes standard Outlook/MSO conditional comments and `mso-*` fixes, `-webkit-text-size-adjust` resets, and a `max-width` media query breakpoint mismatch: two separate `@media (min-width: 768px)` and `@media (min-width: 640px)` blocks both set `img{max-width:...}` (640px then 600px... actually reversed: 640px block is declared after 768px block, so on screens ≥768px the 640px rule — being later in source and same specificity — will override the 600px rule at those widths too). This ordering could cause inconsistent image scaling on tablet/desktop-width clients that don't respect media query precedence strictly.
- `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` present; no `alt` fallback text strategy for images-off default rendering.
