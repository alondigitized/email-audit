# Up to 40% off is on now
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- Sixteen. I went back and counted — this is email number sixteen from CB2 since July 1st, nine days ago. At some point inbox familiarity becomes inbox wallpaper, and I'm there. The frustrating part is the email itself isn't bad: the Summer Edit hero is clean, the 40% off is front and center, and the outdoor 50%-off module lands right below. If I'd received this in isolation — maybe the third CB2 email this month instead of the sixteenth — I'd probably click without thinking twice.
- But I didn't. I got this on the heels of "BACK IN STOCK" and "EXPERT DESIGN, ON US" both hitting yesterday, and before that two urgency emails on July 7th. The "NOT SOLD OUT, YET" section inside today's email is essentially yesterday's back-in-stock concept with a fresh headline. And the "LEAVE US A REVIEW: WIN $10,000" module mid-email is an odd interrupt — I opened this to shop a sale and suddenly I'm being asked to do a task for a contest. That breaks momentum.
- I'd mark this as read without clicking. Not forwarding it. If I want to shop the outdoor sale, I'll go directly to the site — but that's despite the email, not because of it.

## What's working

- **The hero is clean and reads in three lines.** "THE SUMMER EDIT / UP TO 40% OFF / SHOP THE SALE" — no ambiguity, no mystery, correct hierarchy. Pottery Barn's indigo email this week did something similar and scored well for the same reason.
- **Dual-discount architecture is the right call.** 40% umbrella, 50% on outdoor specifically — those are two different mental buckets for me right now and surfacing them separately is smart.
- **"NOT SOLD OUT, YET" is better headline copy than yesterday's "BACK IN STOCK."** More voice, more personality. Would've worked harder as the lead story.
- **"More For You" product grid with visible prices.** Personalized (or personalized-ish) product selection with actual price points is exactly what I want to see. Intent is right even if the detail is too small to evaluate on mobile.
- **No render catastrophes.** After eight emails from the Crate & Barrel family in 48 hours, I'm now scanning for layout breakage as a reflex. This one passes.

## What's weak

- **Sixteen emails in nine days is past the limit.** This isn't about this specific email — it's about what CB2 has done to its own equity. Pottery Barn has sent twelve since July 1st and their indigo collection email still landed at 8/10 because it felt like an editorial moment. CB2 is running the same promotional rotation every 12 hours and the goodwill is gone.
- **The review contest breaks the sale.** "LEAVE US A REVIEW: WIN $10,000" isn't a bad idea as a standalone send — $10K is real money — but placed mid-email during an active sale, it interrupts the shopping flow at the worst possible moment.
- **"Up to 40% off is on now" has no urgency.** Compare this to Article's "Last chance: 4th of July Sale ends today" — that had a hard end date, a forcing function, a reason to click today instead of next Tuesday. "Is on now" just means the sale exists. So does every other sale I'm ignoring.
- **Outdoor 50% is the bigger number and it's below the fold.** Fifty beats forty. Lead with the better punch or at least A/B it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the outdoor 50% story.** "50% off our outdoor collection" is more specific and a bigger discount. Use "Summer Edit" as the umbrella framing but let the outdoor module be the hero punch — above the fold, first module.
- 2. **Remove the review contest from this email entirely.** Send it post-purchase, or as a dedicated standalone to people who've bought in the last 90 days. Don't put a task inside a sale email.
- 3. **Add a hard end date.** "Sale ends Sunday" is worth three times what "is on now" delivers. Wayfair ran the same outdoor-rugs subject three consecutive days without urgency and I called it out both times — CB2 is making the same mistake.
- 4. **Throttle the cadence.** This is a product problem, not a creative problem. One email per three days, with actual differentiation between sends.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Outdoor: 50% off — sale ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `40–50% off the summer edit, this weekend only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Back-in-stock bestsellers, outdoor picks, and the things everyone else is already buying`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Outdoor sectionals and more — clearance ends Sunday night`

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional email template with VML/MSO conditional blocks. The truncated source reveals several infrastructure-level issues: a leftover template placeholder comment, double-slash font URL paths, duplicate `@font-face` declarations, and viewport/text-scaling attributes that violate WCAG accessibility requirements.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Font CDN double-slash paths** — three `@font-face` declarations contain malformed URLs with double slashes:
```
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2
```
Most servers tolerate this via redirect, but it adds a round-trip and can fail on strict CDN configs.

**Cross-brand font CDN dependency** — `HNW05` font is loaded from `crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/`, not `cb2.com`. Any CDN change or outage on the parent brand's domain silently breaks CB2 email typography.

Cannot assess click-tracking or UTM coverage — source is truncated before body links appear.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — prevents user zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA) requires content to be resizable up to 200% without loss of functionality. This attribute explicitly blocks that.
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` on the `*` selector** — disables OS-level text scaling for visually impaired users. Combined with the viewport restriction above, this is a double block on accessible text scaling.

**Non-standard mobile breakpoint at 418px** — the media query fires at `max-width: 418px` but sets `.mobile { width: 414px }`. Devices at 419–480px (a large chunk of Android mid-range) get neither the mobile nor the desktop layout. Standard safe breakpoint is 480px or 600px.

**`Lineto Brown Sub` declared three times** — the first `@font-face` block has no weight/style qualifiers; the second and third add `font-weight: 400` and `font-weight: 600` respectively. The unqualified first declaration creates a specificity conflict — behavior is undefined across email clients that partially support web fonts (e.g., Apple Mail, Samsung Mail).

**Conflicting `p` rules** — global styles set `p { margin: 0 auto; text-align: center; }` then client styles override with `p { padding: 0 !important; margin: 0 !important; }`. The `text-align: center` from the first rule survives the override, which may center body-copy paragraphs unintentionally depending on where the client style block is parsed.

**`line-height: 100%` on `*` selector** — percentage line-heights do not inherit correctly in all email clients; unitless values (e.g., `1.5`) are safer. This is a known Outlook/MSO rendering trigger.
