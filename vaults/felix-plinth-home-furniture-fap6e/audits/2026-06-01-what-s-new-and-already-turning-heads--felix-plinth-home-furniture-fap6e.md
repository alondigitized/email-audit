# What’s new—and already turning heads
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-01**
## Executive summary

- Five. Five Pottery Barn emails in roughly thirty-six hours, and this is it — the one that finally made me mutter something impolite at my phone. "What's new—and already turning heads" is not a subject line, it's a press release. It tells me nothing I can act on: no category, no specific product, no offer. It's the kind of copy that sounds good in a brainstorm and means nothing in an inbox where I'm already exhausted from the outdoor furniture sale, the Coastal Washed Quilt, the table linen refresh, and the ships-free bedding play that dropped before this one.
- What I actually see in the email is a new-arrivals showcase — lifestyle photography, multiple room vignettes, a "Recommended Just for You" section, a free design services callout, and a PB Key Rewards mention near the footer. The photography is clean and warm. The layout is coherent. But there is no sale. No percentage off. No urgency. After four emails in a day-and-a-half that were all dangling offers, this one shows up and asks me to just... browse. At five emails deep, that's not how I'm wired.
- I will not click. I will not forward. I will almost certainly archive this and then feel the first flicker of unsubscribe intent I haven't felt toward Pottery Barn in months.

## What's working

- The hero photography is genuinely beautiful — light, airy, natural wood tones that land squarely in the aesthetic I'm building toward in both houses.
- The "Recommended Just for You" section is the right idea; showing me a curated edit signals they know something about my history, even if the personalization is shallow.
- PB Key Rewards is visible near the footer — credit for not burying it entirely.
- The layout is clean and structured; bedroom, living, outdoor vignettes flow logically and nothing is broken or overlapping.
- Five emails in and there are still no render bugs, no broken images. Reliable production quality, at least.

## What's weak

- No offer. Zero. After four consecutive emails teasing or delivering discounts, dropping a pure editorial new-arrivals send is a rhythm break that feels like a bait-and-switch on my attention.
- The subject "What's new—and already turning heads" could apply to literally any brand, any season, any year — it's brandless filler.
- Five emails in thirty-six hours is not a cadence, it's an assault. Wayfair managed twenty-nine emails over a longer stretch before I flagged them; Pottery Barn is burning goodwill at a faster rate.
- The "Recommended Just for You" row — I can see it but can't verify it's actually surfacing anything I've browsed. At this point it reads like a module that exists rather than one that works.
- CTA buttons are present but from a quick scan none of them have specific verb+noun copy that tells me exactly what I'm clicking into.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the fifth send or merge it with something that has an offer.** If new arrivals exist, they belong as a section inside a send that also has a reason to click — free shipping threshold, member-only preview, something. A standalone editorial email is earned, not automatic.
- 2. **Make the subject specific.** "What's new" is the weakest possible hook. Name the collection, the room, the material. Article's Memorial Day stretch did exactly this — their "Up to 40% off ends Monday" subject on the 22nd was a masterclass in specificity compared to this.
- 3. **Surface one hero product.** CB2's "Tell everyone you studied art history" worked because it committed to a single piece and a single joke. Pick the most conversation-worthy new arrival and lead with it — make me feel like I'm getting early access, not a catalog dump.
- 4. **Anchor the Recommended section to something I actually viewed.** If I browsed console tables last week, put a console table in that row. Right now it feels algorithmic in the bad way.
- 5. **Preheader should do work the subject can't.** If the subject stays vague, the preheader is the last chance — use it to name a material, a room, or a new collection.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New: The pieces getting the most attention this month`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Just in — [Collection Name] is here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `From living to outdoor — the new arrivals worth a second look`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Trending in-store and online right now`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard-broken links (403) will silently fail for affected recipients, and a systemic HTTP-vs-HTTPS asset problem affects 17+ images in the new-arrivals section. Authentication header visibility is blocked by the relay layer, leaving SPF/DKIM status unconfirmed.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Credit card banner link returns 403**
`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY...`
Recipients clicking the "Apply & Buy" / The Key credit card banner are hitting a dead endpoint. This is a conversion-critical CTA.

**[FAIL] West Elm spacer GIF returns 403**
`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
Cross-brand asset reference is broken and served over HTTP. This is a stale template artifact — the asset domain is a sibling brand (Williams-Sonoma family), not Pottery Barn. Should be removed or replaced.

**24 tracking links skipped** — click.e.potterybarn.com redirect chain was not probed; cannot confirm final destination health.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (17 confirmed)** — The entire `S26_Seasonal_B1_NewArrivals` slice set (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_12.jpg`) is served over plain HTTP:
```
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2026/US/S26_Seasonal_B1_NewArrivals/slices_*.jpg
```
Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook (with image blocking) will refuse or warn on these. The same domain exists on HTTPS — this is a template generation error, not a CDN gap.

Additional HTTP offenders:
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` (open-pixel tracker)

**Missing alt text — 40+ images** — Nearly all content images carry no `alt` attribute. Confirmed missing on the entire new-arrivals slice set, all footer slices, all product images (beds, towels, rod brackets), and promotional banners (`S26_Bottom_UpTo50OffOutdoor.jpg`, `W26_Bottom_GeneralDCBottomBanner.jpg`). Images-off rendering is completely blank.

**PHP headers embedded in CSS `<style>` block** — The following is injected verbatim into a `<style>` tag in `<head>`:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache...");
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are PHP `header()` calls that were never executed server-side and leaked into the HTML output. They are inert in email but indicate a template rendering defect.

**`<title>` is empty** — `<title></title>` leaves the email with no document title, which affects tab display in webmail clients.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header not found** — The QA relay did not capture a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Required for major ISPs (Gmail, Yahoo) to display the native unsubscribe button. If absent at actual delivery, this will suppress the one-click unsubscribe UI and may trigger spam classification.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found (RFC 8058)** — One-click POST unsubscribe is not confirmed. Gmail and Yahoo have required this since February 2024 for bulk senders (>5K/day).

**Authentication-Results not captured** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown due to AgentMail relay interception. Should be verified directly from a delivered copy's raw headers.

**CAN-SPAM physical address** — Not verifiable from the truncated HTML provided; assume present in footer based on standard Pottery Barn template.

**UTM parameters** — The 24 skipped tracking links all route through `click.e.potterybarn.com`, which is a standard ESP click-redirect. UTM attribution depends on what parameters are encoded in the redirect payload. Without probing the final destination URLs, continuity cannot be confirmed or denied.
