# Worth a look 🚨 Up to 70% off + New curtains ship free! Florals, neutrals & more
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- Forty-one. That's my Pottery Barn count, and this one lands June 25 — two days after the "Summer Sale is UP TO 70% OFF" hero drop, one day after the handcrafted rugs pitch and the quilts send. I know they're working a multi-touchpoint drip. I even respect it on paper. But the subject opening "Worth a look 🚨" with a siren emoji is Pottery Barn admitting they know I've been tuning them out — and that admission doesn't make me feel more excited, it makes me feel like they're panicking.
- That said: curtains. I genuinely need window treatments for both houses right now. The primary needs blackout panels in the master, and the second home needs something airy for the main living area. So the curtains angle is probably the most targeted thing Pottery Barn has sent me all week. Free shipping on a product category that tips into the $200–800 range per panel pair? That math matters. This is also notably better than the CB2 "PRIVATE SANCTUARIES" send from June 24, which styled its way past the actual offer — at least here I know immediately what I'm getting and what it costs.
- I'd open it (I probably already have it filed in my renovation folder), scan the curtain modules, and bail at flatware. Forward to a friend? No. This is a saved-for-later email, not a share-worthy one.

## What's working

- **Curtains are the right category lead.** Window treatments are a real line item on both my renovation lists, and Pottery Barn doesn't bury it — it's in the hero above the fold.
- **Free shipping on the hero category is a meaningful hook.** Furniture-adjacent items always have the hidden shipping sting. Making "ship free" the secondary headline on curtains is more useful than just repeating the 70% badge for the third time this week.
- **Floral + neutral framing is basically my actual mood board.** I'm looking at a light-filtering floral for the primary bedroom and something clean and neutral for the second home's main living space. This email is organized around exactly those two camps.
- **The hero visual is clean and styled in context.** Curtains hung in a real room gives me a sense of scale and drape that a flat product shot can't. No broken images, legible text, clear top-to-bottom hierarchy.
- **The Summer Sale badge still carries weight.** Even on day three of the sale, 70% off is a legitimate anchor — PB actually moves panels on these markdowns and I know it from prior receipts.

## What's weak

- **"Worth a look 🚨" reads like a DM from a pushy sales rep.** It's the most passively apologetic subject opener I've seen from a major home brand this season. Article's "Bedroom storage essentials" from the same week — a plain noun phrase, zero begging — moves me faster. The siren emoji signals Pottery Barn knows I'm fatigued and doesn't know what to do about it.
- **Flatware in a curtain email is a dead stop for me.** "Polished Flatware" appearing mid-scroll in a window treatments send is exactly the spray-and-pray move that breaks my trust in their personalization. CB2 stayed in its lane on the "PRIVATE SANCTUARIES" send; Pottery Barn doesn't.
- **"Recommended just for you" looks like rotated bestsellers.** At this resolution the picks could be for anyone. Wayfair at least shows me items from categories I've actually clicked — this section reads algorithmic in the worst way.
- **Cadence fatigue is compounding.** The quilts email two days ago was also "soft colors and textures in the Summer Sale." I am starting to experience brand blur across my own Pottery Barn folder, which is not a thing I thought could happen.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill "Worth a look 🚨" entirely.** It sounds apologetic. Lead with the category and the concrete offer — no preamble, no siren.
- 2. **Cut the flatware module or spin it into its own send.** If the personalization engine thinks I'm a flatware buyer, earn that send separately. Don't dilute the curtain story.
- 3. **Surface one filterable detail in the hero — panel width or light-filtering vs. blackout.** That's the information that actually moves me from browse to cart. Article lists dimensions in the email body; Pottery Barn makes me click twice to find out. That's two clicks I may not take.
- 4. **Add a hard end date.** "Summer Sale" has been running since at least June 23 in my inbox — a specific deadline ("ends Sunday, June 29") gives me a real reason to act now instead of filing this and circling back in a week when the panels I wanted are sold out.
- 5. **Drop the Discover New Arrivals outdoor module.** It's a fourth category pivot in one scroll. One job, one email.

These are server-side PHP directives that have leaked into the HTML output verbatim. They are no-ops as CSS but indicate a template rendering bug — the PHP was not executed before send. No runtime impact in the email client, but it signals a broken build/render pipeline.
