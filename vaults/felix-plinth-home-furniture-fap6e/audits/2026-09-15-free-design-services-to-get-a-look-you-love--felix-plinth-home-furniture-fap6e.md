# FREE design services to get a look you love
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-15**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "RSVP for Your Free Design Services" — this is actually a smart wedge for Pottery Barn to run right now, because I'm literally mid-reno on two houses and the one thing I don't have is time to make design decisions solo. The "what / when / where" format at the top — styling tips, room refreshes, total makeovers, your home or in-store — is clean and answers my first objection before I even ask it. But then it's a wall of "bring this look to life" room vignettes stacked one after another, and by the third one I'm scrolling on autopilot, not reading.
- This is a service email wearing a catalog's clothes. The free design consult hook is genuinely good and different from the last four Pottery Barn sends I've gotten this week (Halloween lawn ghosts, ready-to-ship outdoor, the Kravet collab, the dining table one) — but then it buries that hook under three full room-scroll sections before getting back to the actual CTA. Compare that to CB2's "The Fall Sale is over tomorrow" send — one hero, one deadline, done. This one makes me work for it.
- I'd click the "Book a Free Appointment" button because the offer is real and I need exactly this service, but I wouldn't forward it — nothing here is share-worthy, it's just a scheduling ask.

## What's working

- The what/when/where structure up top is genuinely useful — I know in ten seconds this is a real service, not a coupon dressed up as help.
- "In your home, virtually, or in-store" removes my biggest objection (do I have to drive somewhere and wait around) before I even raise it.
- The credit card rewards block near the bottom (10% back) is relevant since I'm already buying via my highest-rewards card for this reno — that's the right note to hit even if it's buried.
- The in-store "Layering a Pottery Barn Bed" class with a specific date (Sept 27) gives me something concrete and time-bound, unlike most PB sends this week.

## What's weak

- Three near-identical "bring this look to life" room shots in a row (living room, bedroom, entry/console) is a lot of scroll for one ask — Article's home office tour stopped my scroll because it was one specific room tied to one specific decision I'm making; this just cycles through generic vignettes.
- The primary CTA ("Book a Free Appointment") appears three times before the actual "recommended for you" product grid even shows up — that's overkill, not urgency.
- "Recommended Just for You" is generic curtain rods and rugs with zero personalization signal — nothing here reflects that I'm two houses into a reno, it reads like the default merchandising block bolted onto a service email.
- Rewards math and fine print get shoved to a tiny gray footer — if the credit card pitch is going to be in every PB email regardless, at least make the 10% number visible above the fold instead of buried under three scroll-screens of sofas.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the three "bring this look to life" room blocks down to one strong hero image and move straight to the booking CTA — the design-service pitch doesn't need a full catalog scroll to sell itself.
- 2. Move the in-store design class (Sept 27, layering a bed) up near the top — it's the most concrete, time-bound thing in the email and it's currently stranded two-thirds down.
- 3. Personalize "Recommended Just for You" against actual browse/purchase history — if I've been eyeing dining tables (like last week's email), don't show me curtain rods.
- 4. Surface the 10% rewards card callout higher, since that's a real financial hook for someone paying for two renovations.
