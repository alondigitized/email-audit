# Re: your summer savings
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- The "Re:" prefix stopped me cold for about a half-second — which is exactly what CB2 was going for. Then I realized it's just the brand doing the inbox-trick thing and my interest dropped proportionally. I'll give them credit: in a subject line, it's a clever pattern-interrupt. In practice, I've gotten two CB2 emails today already (the "best investment pieces are custom" one landed this morning), and I'm somewhere north of forty CB2/Crate family emails this month, so I'm immune to novelty at this point.
- What actually opened here is a near-naked text email — no hero image, no product shot, just a paragraph with hyperlinked category words. Loungers. Rugs. Glassware. It reads like someone's assistant forwarded me a note, which was probably the intent. And honestly? For a brand that usually leans on moody furniture photography, the stripped-down format is either a deliberate editorial choice or something stripped by my email client. Either way, I'm left with words and no visual hook, which is a problem because the reason I save CB2 emails to my "want" folder is the imagery, not the copy.
- I'd probably click the Warehouse Sale link — they buried it as a parenthetical ("It's our deepest markdowns ever.") and that actually made me slow down. But I'm not forwarding this. And I'm definitely noting that the Floor Sample Sale email from June 25th was doing the same job better.

## What's working

- The "Re:" subject line is a genuine pattern-interrupt in a packed inbox — I noticed it before I noticed the five other sale emails from Article, Wayfair, and Pottery Barn that came the same week.
- The inline Warehouse Sale mention — even buried as a parenthetical — carries weight because floor/warehouse markdowns are where the real value is, and I already know from the June 25 send that CB2's warehouse stock moves fast.
- Plain-text format actually reads like a personal email from someone who knows me, which is a tone shift from the "BIGGER SALE" screamer CB2 ran on June 26 — the contrast is disarming.
- The loyalty hook (10% back at CB2 and Crate & Barrel together) is visible in the footer and relevant to me since I split purchases across both brands.
- July 4th deadline is named in the CTA — credible urgency tied to a real calendar event, unlike vague "limited time" language.

## What's weak

- No product imagery whatsoever in the hero area — for a brand whose whole equity is visual, this email has less visual punch than an Article sale email or the Pottery Barn "bedding 70% off" send that actually showed me a styled bedroom.
- The primary CTA is an inline text link ("Shop the July 4th Sale before it's over"), not a button — I had to hunt for it in the paragraph.
- Two CB2 emails in a single day (this one + "The best investment pieces are custom") is one too many; the cadence erodes the urgency claim.
- "Your summer savings" in the subject promises something personalized but the body opens with a generic "Hi," — no name, no "based on your recent searches," nothing that signals CB2 knows I've been eyeing the Paramount sectional.
- The Warehouse Sale — genuinely the most interesting offer here — is a parenthetical throwaway. Article put their 4th of July Sale in the subject line; CB2 buried their deepest markdowns in a dependent clause.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Warehouse Sale, not the category list.** Move "deepest markdowns ever" to the hero sentence. That's the offer that would make me tap immediately, and it's currently the last thing mentioned.
- 2. **Add one product image.** Even a single styled shot — a lounger on a terrace, a rug in a living room — would close the gap with how Article and the CB2 June 25 Floor Sample Sale email performed. Text-only works for transactional emails; it undersells CB2's creative.
- 3. **Replace the inline text CTA with a button.** "Shop Warehouse Sale" or "See Warehouse Deals" — something tappable on mobile, not a hyperlink buried in a paragraph.
- 4. **Personalize the opening.** If you're going to fake the "Re:" thread format, commit: "Re: the rug you've been eyeing" or at minimum "Hi Felix." A nameless "Hi," undercuts the personal-email illusion immediately.
- 5. **Don't send two emails in one day.** Hold this until June 28 or pair it with the "best investment pieces" send as a single email.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate `<meta charset="utf-8">`** — declared twice in `<head>`. Redundant; harmless in most clients but signals a template assembly issue.

**Quadruple `<meta name="format-detection">` declarations** — one combined tag (`telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no`) followed by three separate single-attribute tags. Only the last declaration wins in some parsers; the redundancy suggests copy-paste drift and wastes bytes.

**Dark mode forcibly disabled** — `<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">` and `<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">` opt the email out of dark mode entirely. Apple Mail, Outlook 2021+, and Gmail Android all respect these tags. If CB2's imagery contains white backgrounds baked into the image files this is intentional, but if not, recipients in dark-mode clients get un-adapted rendering with no CSS `prefers-color-scheme` fallback visible in this source.

**Duplicate CSS rules** — `img {outline:0}` appears in one `<style>` block; `img {border:0!important}` and `img {outline:0}` appear again later. No functional conflict, but indicates unmerged style blocks from different template versions.

**`th {font-weight:normal!important; padding:0!important;}`** — global `<th>` reset is fine for layout tables but will suppress bold on any semantic `<th>` used for screen-reader headers. Confirm no accessibility-intended `<th>` cells exist in body.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**"Re:" subject line prefix is a CAN-SPAM risk** — Subject: `Re: your summer savings`. Prefixing a commercial email with `Re:` falsely implies it is a reply to prior recipient correspondence. CAN-SPAM §7704(a)(2) prohibits "deceptive subject headings." Several ESPs (Klaviyo, Salesforce MC) explicitly warn against this pattern in their compliance docs. This is the highest-priority finding in this audit.

**Unsubscribe and physical address** — not visible in truncated source. Must confirm:
- One-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) header is present (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender rules as of Feb 2024)
- Physical mailing address block is present in footer
- Unsubscribe processes within 10 business days

**Sending domain** — `cb2@mail.cb2.com`. Confirm `mail.cb2.com` has valid SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment with `cb2.com`. Misalignment on the subdomain is a common deliverability issue for subaddressed senders.
