# First look! Our holiday 2026 kids collection is here →
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-12**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids, again. This is the third nursery/kids email camped in my inbox this week, and I already flagged it yesterday when I was relieved the "★★★★★ style for your home" send turned out to be actual Crate & Barrel and not this. My wife runs point on anything with a glider recliner or a Nuna car seat — I'm not the guy scrolling a $1,350 stroller travel system at 7am.
- The advent calendar hero is genuinely pretty — nice styling, warm tone — but "first look at the 2026 holiday kids collection" in September just doesn't land for me. It's the same overreach I called out with Pottery Barn's Molly Sims email: pretty creative, wrong audience for me specifically.
- Not clicking, not forwarding. I'll let my wife scroll past this one if it ever crosses her screen, but for me it's an instant archive.

## What's working

- The advent calendar hero shot is warm and well-styled — genuinely nice photography, not stock-looking.
- "new finishing touches starting at $29" is a real, specific, low-commitment price point — that's the kind of clarity Wayfair keeps fumbling with its broken hero images.
- The design services banner ("enter for a chance to win $5000") is at least a shared program across the C&B family, so I recognize it from the CB2 "Free Design Services" email I read.
- Clean nav bar up top (bedding / decor / stockings / toys) makes the section obvious even if it's not for me.

## What's weak

- Every single "we love these for you" product is a crib mattress, glider recliner, or Nuna stroller — zero relevance to a 36-year-old guy mid-renovation on two houses.
- Holiday kids collection "first look" in mid-September is the same premature-seasonal push I've seen from other brands lately; feels stale before it's even started.
- This is now three Crate & Kids/Crate & Barrel-family emails in a row bleeding together — same countdown-adjacent cadence CB2 already made me numb to this week.
- The credit card pitch ("new! the card you love just got better") buried two-thirds down is a missed opportunity — nobody scrolls that far on a kids email they don't care about.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this list — if C&B knows I've only ever clicked dinnerware/furniture links, don't put me in the Crate & Kids holiday drop at all. That's the single biggest lift here.
- 2. Move the credit card upgrade higher or into its own send — rewards math is something I actually care about, and it's wasted at position 8 of 10 in a kids email.
- 3. If they must cross-sell me into kids content, lead with something dad-relevant (storage furniture, playroom shelving) instead of car seats and gliders.
- 4. Drop "first look" holiday framing until at least October — it reads like the same premature urgency Article and CB2 have both leaned on this month.
