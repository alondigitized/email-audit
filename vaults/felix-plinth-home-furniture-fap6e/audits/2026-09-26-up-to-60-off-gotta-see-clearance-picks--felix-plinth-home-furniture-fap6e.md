# Up to 60% off gotta-see clearance picks →
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-26**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Barrel Kids again — that's the third or fourth logo variant from this house I've had to sort through this week, and I don't have kids, so this whole email is D.O.A. for me the second I see "clearance" next to a fall-leaves hero and a stroller. The 60% off clearance banner up top is genuinely strong creative — clean type, real urgency — but then it immediately buries that under a Venus Williams kids-collab, a Nuna travel system, and a Crate Card pitch. That's four different offers competing for one scroll.
- Would I click? No. Would I forward it? Only ironically, to my partner, captioned "guess who's getting emailed again." This gets marked read and archived to the Pinterest folder without a second look — it's not relevant enough to earn real estate there.

## What's working

- The "up to 60% off clearance" hero is bold, high-contrast, and impossible to miss — best single beat in the email.
- The category grid (Furniture, Rugs, Kids Bedding, Decor & Lighting) at least tries to route non-parents like me somewhere useful.
- Photography is warm and premium-feeling, consistent with the C&B house style I've seen all week — no render bugs, images all loaded clean.
- The Crate Card callout is a real loyalty hook, similar to how Pottery Barn's towel email at least gave me something concrete to act on.

## What's weak

- Sender is Crate & Barrel Kids but the subject leads with a generic "clearance" hook that reads just like regular Crate & Barrel — CB2's restock email at least kept its category list tight and scannable instead of stacking five unrelated campaigns.
- I have no kids — the Venus Williams collab, Nuna stroller, baby crib mattress, and "Busy Baby Activity Chair" are all dead weight for me specifically.
- Way too many CTAs stacked vertically (Shop Clearance, Shop the New Collab, Shop Now x2, Learn More, Download Now, Book a Free Appointment) — no clear single next action, unlike Article's tight "3 Steps" entryway email that kept me anchored to one theme.
- The "It's all on the app, including your chance at $500" line is vague bait-and-switch phrasing — no explanation of the sweepstakes mechanic before asking me to download.
- Preview/preheader territory ("ending soon! up to 20% off the best baby essentials") contradicts the 60% clearance headline right below it — two different offers, two different percentages, in the first two inches of the email.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this list — send kids-specific content only to households flagged with kids/baby registry activity; a 36-year-old on two home renovations doesn't need Nuna strollers in his inbox.
- 2. Pick one hero offer and cut the rest — either it's a 60%-off clearance email or a Venus Williams collab launch, not both crammed top-to-bottom.
- 3. Fix the top banner contradiction — the 20%-off baby essentials line directly above the 60%-off clearance hero undercuts the bigger number.
- 4. Move the Crate Card / rewards pitch higher if you want repeat-purchase behavior from someone like me — that's the one thing in this email that could actually pull me in.
