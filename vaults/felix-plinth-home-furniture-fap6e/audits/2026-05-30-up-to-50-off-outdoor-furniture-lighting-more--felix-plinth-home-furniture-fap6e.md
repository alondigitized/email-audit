# Up to 50% off outdoor furniture, lighting & more
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-five. I didn't count this time — I just know because I reviewed the near-identical "Outdoor sale ⛱️ UP TO 50% OFF" send from May 28th and gave it a 9. That was two days ago. Today I'm getting the same sale, the same hero palette (cream sectional, throw pillows, neutral outdoor scene), and the same headline arithmetic. Pottery Barn is cycling the outdoor event through my inbox like a playlist on repeat, and even I — someone who is actively looking for outdoor furniture for a second home — felt my thumb slow down before I decided to open it.
- What saves this one is the "Recommended Just for You" section below the fold. That's doing something the May 28 send didn't do as visibly: it's treating me like a known customer, not just someone in the outdoor-sale blast segment. The Free Design Services cross-promotion is also smart given they just sent me that 9/10 consultation email yesterday — it reinforces the message rather than contradicting it.
- I would click. The outdoor event is genuinely relevant to where I am right now — second home has zero patio furniture, primary needs a refresh — and 50% off at Pottery Barn is real money. But I'd be clicking with mild irritation, the way you show up to a sale you've already heard about twice. I would not forward this to anyone; I'd clip the category grid to my inbox folder and move on.

## What's working

- **Category grid is immediately actionable.** Chairs & ottomans, benches, café tables, dining tables — four tiles, four clear links. For someone who needs to shop multiple rooms across two properties, that taxonomy works like a menu.
- **50% is a credible Pottery Barn number.** "Up to" softens it slightly, but this brand running half-off outdoor doesn't feel bait-and-switch the way it might from a lower-tier sender. I trust the math here in a way I don't always trust Wayfair's "up to 80% off" screams.
- **Personalized section ("Recommended Just for You") lands.** Even at small thumbnail size, it signals that Pottery Barn knows something about me, not just that I exist on a list. Article has been doing this well all month; it's good to see PB match it.
- **Free Design Services cross-promotion in the footer.** Yesterday's standalone email on that service already has my attention. Reinforcing it inside a product email without competing with the hero is a smart use of real estate.
- **No render bugs, clean layout.** Single column, neutral warm palette, no broken images. Renders fine at the size I'm reading it.

## What's weak

- **This is the May 28 outdoor email wearing a different subject line.** The hero scene, the offer depth, the event naming — nearly identical. CB2 ran "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" this week and I gave it credit for at least feeling like a different creative direction. This doesn't.
- **No deadline anywhere visible.** Article's Memorial Day push ("Up to 40% off ends Monday") gave me a hard date to act on. I don't see one here, which takes this from "I should do this now" to "I'll look later" — and later usually means never.
- **"& more" is doing a lot of heavy lifting in the subject line.** Lighting is actually interesting to me for the second home — but it gets swallowed by the furniture categories and disappears. If lighting is in the sale, show me lighting.
- **Cadence is now the biggest liability.** This is Pottery Barn's cadence problem, not this single email's problem — but it's showing up here as reduced urgency and reduced credibility. The 28th, the 29th (three times), and now the 30th. I'm becoming immune.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline to the hero.** "Ends June 2" or "Sale ends Sunday" changes the decision from optional to time-sensitive. Article does this every time; it works.
- 2. **Surface lighting as a co-equal category in the grid.** It's in the subject but invisible in the body. A fifth tile for lanterns/outdoor lighting would serve me directly and actually expand the relevance beyond furniture.
- 3. **Differentiate from the May 28 creative.** Even a different hero lifestyle shot — a dining scene instead of the sectional — signals to subscribers that this is a new send worth attention, not an inbox echo.
- 4. **Put the personalized section above the fold.** Or at minimum lead the body copy with it. "We think you'd like these" hits harder before the generic sale grid than after.
- 5. **Suppress or throttle for recent openers of the May 28 send.** If I already clicked the outdoor event two days ago, send me something that builds on that — a "still in stock" or "your saves are on sale" message — instead of the same top-of-funnel blast.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your patio, 50% off — chairs, tables, lanterns`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dining sets, sectionals, and outdoor lighting — see what's still in stock.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The sectional you've been circling is half off this week.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard-broken links (403) will silently fail for all recipients, and PHP header directives are embedded verbatim inside a `<style>` block — a template assembly defect with no runtime effect but a clear indicator of a broken build process. Overall QA pass rate is 33%.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Credit card banner CTA — 403**
`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`
The "Apply and Buy" / Key Rewards card banner is dead for every recipient. The `clickcp` redirect domain is returning 403 rather than the expected redirect.

**[FAIL] West Elm spacer pixel — 403 + cross-brand asset**
`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
A West Elm (sibling brand) asset is embedded in a Pottery Barn email and is currently returning 403. This is a shared-template inheritance artifact — a stale cross-brand include that should have been removed or replaced.

**23 tracking links skipped:** All click-redirects through `click.e.potterybarn.com` were not probed. Link integrity on those destinations is unverified.

**Third-party ad pixel present:**
`https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/L21rdC84NjEvY2lkLzE3NDc3ODk5MzMvdC8y/cat/1`
Turn/Amobee (now Sizmek) retargeting pixel fires on open. Introduces a third-party data dependency with no `async` or failure isolation.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP header directives in `<style>` block — template defect**
The following literal text appears inside a `<style>` tag:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are PHP `header()` calls that were not executed server-side and were emitted as CSS text. They are inert in email clients but confirm a template rendering failure in the build pipeline for this module.

**48 images missing `alt` text**
All 16 `slices_N.jpg` content images, all 16 footer `slices_N.jpg`, plus product images (`H25_TOP_InStockFurniture.jpg`, `cayman-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `hydrocotton-quick-dry-towel-l.jpg`, etc.) have empty or absent `alt` attributes. When images are blocked (Gmail Promotions tab, Outlook default), recipients see a blank email.

**3 HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources — blocking risk**
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` (also 403)
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

The CC banner footer asset (`Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`) and a 2022 brand banner slice are served over plain HTTP. Modern email clients and corporate proxies will block mixed-content image loads. The 2022 asset path suggests this module has not been refreshed in 4 years.

**Open-tracking pixel over HTTP**
`http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true`
The Silverpop/Watson open-tracking beacon is HTTP, not HTTPS. This pixel will be blocked by any client enforcing HTTPS-only image loading, causing open-rate undercounting.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` — no impact on deliverability but causes accessibility tools and some preview panes to display nothing.
