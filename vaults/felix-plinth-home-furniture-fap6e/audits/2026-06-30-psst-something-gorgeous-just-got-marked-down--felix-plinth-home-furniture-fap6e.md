# Psst… something gorgeous just got marked down →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- Forty-something C&B emails into June and this one shows up with a subject that sounds like it has a secret to whisper. "Psst… something gorgeous just got marked down" is genuinely good subject-line writing — it pulls the intrigue lever without screaming SALE in all caps. My thumb paused. For like three seconds. And then I opened it and got... a full warehouse clearance email with a "up to 50% off" hero banner, multiple product grid sections, and what looks like the exact same 4th of July warehouse sale chassis I've seen four times already this month. The whispering subject promised me one irresistible find. The body delivered a sale catalog.
- That gap is the whole problem here. I was primed for a single spotlight — one sofa, one dining table, one lamp that just hit clearance — and instead I got a grid of offerings that made the teaser feel like a trick. Pottery Barn pulled something similar on June 30 with their "Extra 20% off select styles" subject that also undersold what was actually a broad storewide push. At least Pottery Barn was upfront about it being a percentage off. C&B winked at me and then opened a brochure.
- I'm marking this read without clicking. I might open the next one if it actually names the product in the subject. I'd forward it to nobody.

## What's working

- The subject line is the best thing this email has going for it — "Psst…" is a genuine pattern interrupt after weeks of SALE/WAREHOUSE/50%-OFF blasts
- "Up to 50% off clearance" in the hero is clear and readable; I didn't have to hunt for the offer
- The overall layout renders cleanly — no broken images or overlapping text visible at the mobile width
- The multi-section structure is familiar C&B template work; products are organized into digestible rows rather than one infinite scroll of chaos
- The hero imagery (looks like outdoor/living room setup) is aspirational enough to match my current headspace — I'm literally picking patio furniture right now

## What's weak

- The subject promises a singular gorgeous thing that "just got marked down" — the body is a warehouse clearance email; that's a bait-and-switch in tone, not just messaging
- There's zero personalization signal visible — I've spent real money at C&B, they know what I've browsed, and this email could have gone to anyone on the list
- After the berry recipes email earlier the same day — June 30, same sender — the cadence is just too loud; two unrelated-feeling sends in one day from one brand tests patience
- No loyalty or member pricing callout visible, which matters to me because I track rewards math religiously
- The "Psst" intrigue hook doesn't survive on mobile if someone glances at it after already seeing three prior C&B sale subjects this week — it reads as noise by that point

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name the product in the subject.** If something gorgeous actually got marked down, say what it is. "Psst… the [Lounge Chair / Meryl Sofa / Camden Dining Table] just dropped in price" would have made me click immediately and kept the intrigue honest. Article did this better in their June 19 Cillian send — named the piece, made me look it up.
- 2. **Build a single hero around the one marked-down item** instead of pivoting to a broad clearance grid. The gap between subject and body is the biggest conversion killer here.
- 3. **Add a member pricing callout** — even one line like "Member Price: $XXX" on the hero product would give me a reason to click rather than just browse.
- 4. **Delay or suppress the second same-day send.** If I already got the berry recipes email that morning, this one should wait until tomorrow. Two sends in one day from the same display name trains me to batch-delete.
- 5. **Include one product detail** — dimensions, a color swatch, availability — visible without clicking. CB2's "SALE OF THE SUMMER" email on a recent send actually surfaced a specific product with dimensions in the hero, which made it feel less like wallpaper.

- **Subject Alt B:** `One piece. Just dropped in price. Worth a look.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Our most-watched item in clearance — for a limited time.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The one thing on your watchlist that just got cheaper.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Conflicting desktop image cap (confirmed):** Two overlapping media queries set contradictory `max-width` values for images:

```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
```

On screens ≥768px both rules fire; the 768px rule wins due to cascade order, capping images at 600px. Images are allowed to expand to 640px only in the 640–767px range — wider than the likely 600px container at that breakpoint. Effect: potential horizontal overflow on tablets.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:** Prevents user-initiated zoom on mobile — an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4 Resize Text).

**Universal `text-size-adjust: none`:**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applied to all elements rather than scoped to `body`. Disables OS-level font scaling for users who rely on it.

**Dark mode forced off:** `<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">` and `supported-color-schemes: light` explicitly block dark mode rendering. Apple Mail and Outlook dark-mode users will see no adaptation — may invert backgrounds or render with poor contrast depending on client.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags:** The combined tag (`telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no`) is immediately followed by three redundant single-attribute declarations. Harmless but indicates template hygiene issues.
