# All the things to finish their dream room
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-six. That's where the C&B family tally lands for me this cycle, and I have to say the number out loud before anything else because it is the most important context for how this email lands. Three consecutive toddler/kids-room sends in a row — Montessori space, "I can do it myself" picks, and now this — and my thumb is hovering over the unsubscribe button in a way it wasn't a week ago.
- That said: the hero is genuinely nice. Gray painted dresser, soft botanical wallpaper backdrop, styled like a real room rather than a catalog drop. This is the kind of thing I actually save to my Pinterest-adjacent folder because I'm furnishing two properties simultaneously and one of them has a kid's room in the plan. ## What's working

- **Hero product is genuinely aspirational.** The gray dresser against botanical wallpaper is exactly the elevated-but-livable aesthetic I'm shopping toward — this is better visual merchandising than the flat product shots I got from Wayfair's wall art send last week.
- **Subject line is under 50 chars and reads clean on mobile.** No all-caps, no exclamation points — respects my inbox.
- **Multi-product grid below the hero gives me options without overwhelming.** Bedding, wall clock/mirror, a few accent pieces — it reads like a curated room rather than a clearance dump.
- **Layout renders without any visible bugs.** No broken images, no overlapping text. After seeing CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" email come in with a wonky grid last week, this feels polished by comparison.
- **Soft, neutral color palette is consistent with the brand.** Doesn't feel like it was produced by a different team than the last send.

## What's weak

- **Third consecutive toddler/kids-room send.** I called this out for the "I can do it myself" email too — segmentation is broken or cadence is broken, pick one. Article managed to send me two relevant outdoor emails in four days without fatiguing me because they spaced them and varied the content angle. C&B Kids is just hammering the same note.
- **No visible offer or price point in the hero.** I can see styling; I cannot see whether anything is on sale. Pottery Barn's George Kittle retreat email at least committed to a specific design service hook. This is beautiful but asks me to click on faith.
- **No personalization visible.** Treated as a stranger, not a customer who's been buying from the C&B family for years. A "your recently viewed" section or even a member pricing callout would change the calculation.
- **"Their dream room" assumes I have a child.** I might. I do. But the copy treats this as a given rather than earning the premise. CB2 this same week opened with a product category that worked equally well regardless of family configuration.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Break the cadence — skip one send or pivot to a parent's-own-space angle.** The third consecutive kids send is the single biggest drag on this email. A "the kid's room is done — now treat yourself" or "their room is almost there; here's the reading chair for the corner" angle would let C&B Kids speak to me as a buyer, not just a shopper for someone else.
- 2. **Add a visible offer signal in the hero or above the fold.** "Free shipping on orders over $X" or "members save 15% on kids furniture" — any concrete hook that gives me a reason to click beyond editorial appreciation.
- 3. **Surface a specific product with a specific name and price.** The dresser in the hero is the star — name it, price it, link directly to the PDP. Article does this every time and it's why their outdoor send scored a 9 last week.
- 4. **Add a loyalty/member callout early.** If I'm a C&B Rewards member, tell me what I get. Don't make me scroll to the footer to find it.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom (WCAG 1.4.4)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Violates WCAG Success Criterion 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**`<title>` brand mismatch**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
Sender is "Crate & Kids"; title reflects the parent brand. This is visible in some email clients (e.g., browser-based preview tabs) and search-indexed previews. Template was not updated from the parent brand template.

**Dead CSS rule — `min-width: 768px` image cap is overridden**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }  /* declared after */
```
Because the 640px query is declared later in the stylesheet, it overrides the 768px rule at all viewport widths ≥640px. The 600px cap is never applied. Remove or reorder.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<!-- then individually: -->
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
Redundant; `email=no` and `url=no` are only declared in the combined tag. Consolidate to one tag.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` instead of `100%`**
```css
html { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
`none` fully disables font scaling in WebKit; `100%` is the correct safe value that suppresses automatic resizing without disabling user-initiated scaling.

**Unfilled template placeholder (no font import)**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
<!---->
```
Font import slot was never populated (note also: "IMPUT" is a typo for "INPUT"). If the design uses a non-system font, it will fall back to the stack silently. Confirm no custom font is expected; if one is, inject the `@import` or `<link>` here.
