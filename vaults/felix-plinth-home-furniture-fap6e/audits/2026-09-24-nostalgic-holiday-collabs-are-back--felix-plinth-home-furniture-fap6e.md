# Nostalgic holiday collabs are back!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-24**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "Holiday Movie Magic" — National Lampoon's Christmas Vacation, Elf, and Peanuts all licensed onto dinnerware and pillows in one email, and it's September 24th. I'll give Pottery Barn this much: it's more creative than the "Christmas Bestsellers Are Back" stocking-and-wreath dump they sent on the 23rd, and it beats the fake "sign up for texts" bait from the same week. This one at least has real licensed product — Griswold family Christmas dinnerware, an "OMG Santa! I know him!" Elf pillow, a Charlie Brown Christmas dessert set.
- But it's still late September, I haven't carved a pumpkin yet, and my eye is genuinely split — there's a Halloween Shop banner with ghost topiaries halfway down competing with three different Christmas collabs above it. That's a seasonal identity crisis in one scroll. And then it dissolves into a totally unrelated "Recommended For You" grid (curtain rods, a dining table, a jute rug) before pivoting to Halloween, then Design Crew, then the credit card pitch. It's five emails stitched into one.
- Verdict: I wouldn't click. It's a novelty scroll, not a shopping trip — I'd screenshot the Griswold plates for my wife because that's genuinely funny and on-brand for our family, but I'm not buying licensed holiday china in September when I've got two houses of actual renovation work still unfinished. Mark as read, maybe forward the Griswold section as a joke.

## What's working

- The Griswold "Christmas Vacation" dinnerware tablescape is genuinely charming and specific — it's not generic red-and-green, it's a licensed bit with a punchline built in.
- Three distinct, well-known IP collabs (Lampoon, Elf, Peanuts) is more creative than the plain "holiday bestsellers" grid Pottery Barn ran on 9/23 — this at least has a hook.
- The legal fine print under the movie logos (National Lampoon, Warner Bros, New Line, Peanuts Worldwide) is a small trust signal — feels like a real licensed program, not a knockoff.
- The Halloween Shop ghost-topiary photo is a strong, moody hero shot on its own — my problem is where it's placed, not how it's shot.

## What's weak

- It's September 24th and this is stacked with THREE Christmas collabs before a single Halloween product appears — Crate & Barrel's Design Desk email at least stayed in-season and solved a problem I actually have right now (spec'ing custom furniture for two houses).
- No offer anywhere in this email — no %, no free shipping, nothing. Compare that to Wayfair's "Open Box steals" framing (even though that one fell apart on delivery) — at least it led with a price angle. This is pure lifestyle/IP content with zero reason to buy today.
- The "Recommended Just For You" grid (curtain rods, a swivel chair, a dining table, a jute rug) has nothing to do with holiday collabs or Halloween — it reads like a bot stitched two unrelated emails together.
- Zero renovation-relevant content for me — no swatch/sample program, no shipping-time clarity on furniture, no showroom tie-in. CB2's Design Desk beat and Article's entryway email both spoke more directly to "I'm mid-renovation" than this does.
- Five different themes (Christmas collabs → generic recs → Halloween → Design Crew → credit card) in one scroll is a lot of unrelated CTAs competing for one click.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into two sends — one pure Christmas-collab email (keep it novelty/gift-guide framed, add a specific offer or "shop the collab" urgency), and let Halloween own its own email instead of getting buried below three Christmas grids in the same scroll.
- 2. Cut or relocate the "Recommended Just For You" grid — it has zero connection to the holiday collab theme and breaks the whole narrative.
- 3. Add an actual offer somewhere in the top third — even "New arrivals, up to 20% off" would give me a reason to click today instead of just admiring the Griswold plates.
- 4. If renovation/furniture is a real segment for me, surface something showroom- or swatch-related near the top instead of curtain rods buried mid-scroll.

## Technical Audit

1. Technical Summary
Standard MessageInteractive/Bronto-style ESP template (potterybarn.com family) with functional tracking/personalization infrastructure; primary issues are missing image alt text, two legacy HTTP asset/pixel references, and unconfirmed compliance headers at the relay layer.

2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 48 tracking/click-redirect links present (`click.e.potterybarn.com`, `clickcp.potterybarn.com`) — not resolvable via HTTP probe per QA tooling, so destination validity is unconfirmed.
- Open/tracking pixels present and functioning as expected: `d.turn.com/r/dd/...`, `svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` — note the persona/recipient email is embedded in plaintext in the pixel URL query string.
- The `svopn.potterybarn.com` open-tracking pixel loads over `http://`, not `https://` — non-HTTPS may be blocked by mail clients and exposes the query string (including recipient email) in cleartext.

3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 40+ `<img>` elements missing `alt` text across header, hero, product slices, and footer blocks (e.g. `slices_01.jpg`–`slices_18.jpg`, product images like `remmy-swivel-chair-1-l.jpg`, `banks-extendable-dining-table-76-128-1-l.jpg`, `cayman-platform-bed-1-l.jpg`). This affects screen-reader accessibility and degrades the fallback experience when images are blocked.
- Two additional non-HTTPS image sources: `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` and `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` — may be blocked by clients enforcing mixed-content/HTTPS-only image loading.
- Head contains a `<link>` to Google Fonts (`fonts.googleapis.com`) — most email clients strip external stylesheet links and ignore `@font-face`, so this has no effect in rendering and is dead weight.
- Stray, non-functional CSS in the `<style>` block: literal PHP-style `header(...)` calls (`header('Content-Type: image/jpeg')`, cache-control headers) sitting inside a CSS `<style>` tag — inert artifact from template reuse, has no effect but indicates unclean HTML assembly.
- Responsive breakpoints rely on `@media` queries only (700px/650px/480px) with no MSO/Outlook-specific fallback beyond basic `mso` OfficeDocumentSettings — legacy Outlook desktop (non-MSO-aware clients) will render at fixed table widths.
