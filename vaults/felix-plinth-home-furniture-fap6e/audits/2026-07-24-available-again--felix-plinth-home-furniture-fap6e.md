# AVAILABLE AGAIN
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-four. That's the CB2 count in my July inbox, and I'm putting it up front because context is everything here. After a week of "UP TO 40% OFF ENDS TODAY" and "THE FURNITURE EVENT IS ON," CB2 finally sent something structurally different — a back-in-stock email. "AVAILABLE AGAIN" is the first subject in days that made me pause instead of auto-scroll. Back-in-stock is inherently more credible than another countdown; it says *you missed something specific*, not *we're discounting everything again*.
- The problem is the email underneath that subject doesn't fully deliver on the promise. What I'm looking at is effectively another furniture event carousel — BED, SECTIONALS, BOOKCASES — stitched together with a "$5,000 iFRESH" sweepstakes block that completely breaks the purchase mindset. I'm mid-renovation on two properties; I want to know *which specific items* came back in stock, not spin a wheel for a home refresh prize. The "SEIZE the MOMENT" hero copy is fine but generic, and the sweepstakes is a detour.
- The "MADE FOR YOU" product grid at the bottom is the only part that feels like CB2 knows who I am. I'd click there. I would not forward this — there's nothing here that's a gift for a friend. Mostly I'd scan the category tiles, check the personalized grid, and close.

## What's working

- **Subject line finally pivoted.** "AVAILABLE AGAIN" breaks the sale-countdown fatigue loop. After four consecutive urgency emails, this reads as new information.
- **"SEIZE the MOMENT" pairs well with the subject.** The hero copy echoes the back-in-stock premise — first glance, the creative logic holds.
- **Category tile navigation (BED / SECTIONALS / BOOKCASES) is clean.** As someone shopping for both homes simultaneously, I can land on the relevant room fast without hunting.
- **"MADE FOR YOU" personalization grid.** It's at the bottom, but it exists — and it's the closest CB2 comes to treating me like a known customer rather than a blast recipient.
- **Warm neutral palette.** Consistent with CB2's brand and with the aesthetic I'm actually trying to land in both spaces. No jarring color shifts, no seasonal mismatch.

## What's weak

- **The back-in-stock premise evaporates inside the email.** Nothing says *these specific items* are what came back. It's the same furniture event carousel dressed in a new subject line.
- **"$5,000 iFRESH" sweepstakes block.** This kills purchase momentum. I'm here to buy a sectional, not enter a contest. Article's "Tailor your living room to your tastes" send last week had zero sweepstakes distractions and scored an 8 from me for that reason alone.
- **No pricing visible in the hero or category tiles.** After days of "40% off" messaging, I have no idea if the sale is still running or if these back-in-stock items are full price. Pottery Barn at least anchors a price or a % in the tile.
- **Category tiles are broad, not specific.** "SECTIONALS" is a department, not a product. Wayfair's Black Friday in July email was chaotic, but at least it named SKUs. This doesn't.
- **Cadence weight.** Thirty-four emails. Even a good email lands differently when it's the 34th.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name the actual items that are back.** Lead the hero with two or three specific products — "The Cava Sofa is back. So is the Maren Bed." That's the email the subject promises. Ship that.
- 2. **Cut the sweepstakes block entirely or move it to footer.** The "$5,000 iFRESH" interrupts a buyer's path. Put it in the footer if legal requires it; don't give it a full mid-email slot.
- 3. **Carry the sale pricing forward.** One line: "The Furniture Event pricing still applies." Or confirm it's over. Don't make me guess.
- 4. **Move "MADE FOR YOU" above the fold.** If the personalization is real, that's the highest-value module in the email. It's currently buried.
- 5. **Tighten the CTA copy on tiles.** "Shop Sectionals" beats "SECTIONALS" as a dead label.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully audit** — HTML is truncated before `<body>` content; no `<a href>` tags are visible in the provided source. Confirm UTM parameters and click-tracking wrapper on all links before send.

**Font URLs with double-slash paths** — three `@font-face` declarations use `//` in the path:


Double slashes typically trigger a 301 redirect, adding latency for clients that load web fonts (Apple Mail, Gmail web). The earlier `BrownRegular` declaration uses single slashes correctly — the `//` variants appear to be copy-paste errors.

**Cross-domain font dependency** — `HNW05` font (4 variants) loads from `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/`. This creates a hard dependency on a sibling brand's domain. If that domain's CDN or CORS policy changes, fonts silently break in CB2 emails without any CB2-side alert.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Unresolved template placeholder left in production:**

```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```

This is a build-time comment ("IMPUT" is a misspelling of "INPUT") that was not removed before send. Harmless to rendering but indicates the production template was not cleaned.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on the `*` selector:**

```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```

Using `none` (rather than `100%`) on a universal selector disables the iOS/Safari text-size adjustment for every element. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) is violated — users who rely on system font scaling will get no adjustment. The `body` rule correctly uses `100%`; the `*` override undoes it.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile — fails WCAG 1.4.4. This is a common legacy pattern but should be `maximum-scale=5` or removed.

**Redundant `@font-face` for "Lineto Brown Sub"** — declared three times with overlapping weight mappings (`400` appears twice with different `src`). Browsers resolve by last-defined rule; the earlier `.eot`-based declaration for legacy IE is effectively dead code given the later woff2-only redeclarations.

**`alt` attributes** — not visible in truncated source; must be verified. All product images require descriptive `alt` text; decorative spacers should use `alt=""`.
