# Our Memorial Day Sale STARTS NOW 🔔
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- I've had five Pottery Barn emails in three days. I did a quick scroll back to confirm this wasn't a re-send of yesterday's "Classic furniture, now in NEW finishes" before I actually read it. The good news: this one leads with a real offer. Extra 20% off stacked on top of markdowns up to 40% is the most aggressive number PB has thrown at me this week, and the four category tiles — bedding, sofas & chairs, pillows & decor, outdoor — cover almost everything I'm actively pricing for two renovation projects right now. The outdoor tile alone made me stop.
- But Crate & Barrel's "Double Rewards" email earlier this week already played my exact nerve. I run the numbers on every major furniture purchase — card rewards, brand program, what stacks, what doesn't — and "Extra 20% off Select styles" without a clear end date or a rewards-program callout is a vaguer pitch than C&B's double-points framing. Wayfair went harder on outdoor dining specifically, and I actually clicked through. PB's outdoor tile is the better product, but the email around it is less sharp.
- I'd click the outdoor tile. I wouldn't forward this — it's a promotional blast, not a find.

## What's working

- Hero offer is the clearest number PB has put in my inbox this week: Extra 20% off + markdowns up to 40% is substantive and legible above the fold.
- Category tiles with specific discount ranges let me triage fast — I can see "up to 50% off Outdoor" in two seconds and know whether to keep scrolling.
- "SHOP EXTRA 20% OFF" is a clean CTA — verb and noun, no "Discover" fuzz.
- "Recommended For You" with actual product thumbnails is more useful than the botanical wallpaper email from two days ago; it at least signals they're pulling from my browse history.
- Zero render bugs. Layout is tight, hierarchy holds, nothing breaks.

## What's weak

- "Up to 20% off Sofas & Chairs" is the weakest tile — sofas are a $2,000–$4,000 line item and 20% off is where the conversation starts, not ends. Wayfair was doing deeper cuts on seating right now and it showed.
- The Affirm "Buy Now, Pay Later" module sitting mid-email is a misread on me. I'm a rewards-maximizer with a dedicated high-cashback card for home purchases. Financing signals a different customer entirely.
- No end date anywhere in the render. "STARTS NOW" without a close date converts urgency into ambiguity — I have no reason to act today vs. tomorrow.
- Fifth email in three days. I'm not unsubscribing, but I'm pattern-matching the subject lines as noise faster than I used to.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an end date to the hero** — "Ends Monday, May 26" turns "STARTS NOW" into a real countdown. Single highest-lift change in this email.
- 2. **Move the outdoor tile to first position** — it's the strongest hook for a renovation-active customer, and it's currently fourth. Lead with what's most likely to convert.
- 3. **Swap Affirm for a Key Rewards callout** — tell me this stacks with member pricing or point multipliers. That's the framing Crate & Barrel used and it landed harder on me personally.
- 4. **Add a "qualifying styles" transparency link** — "Select styles." as a standalone qualifier is a flag that makes me hesitate before getting invested in the hero.
- 5. **Pull back the send cadence** — 5 emails in 3 days collapses the signal-to-noise ratio for every individual subject line.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP header() code injected into a `<style>` block (template defect)**
The following appears verbatim inside a `<style>` tag in the `<head>`:
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are PHP HTTP response header calls that were never executed server-side — they ended up rendered as raw text inside CSS. Email clients ignore them as invalid CSS, but this confirms a template preprocessing failure. Whichever conditional or partial outputs these cache headers did not fire correctly.

**43 images missing `alt` text** — All content images, product images, footer slices, and both open-tracking pixels have empty or absent `alt` attributes. Images-off rendering (common in Outlook, corporate environments) produces a fully blank email. Screen readers receive no content.

**3 image assets served over HTTP (will be blocked)**
```
http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg
```
Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail proxy or block mixed-content image requests. The CC banner footer image (`Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`) is a visible content block, not a spacer — it will silently drop for a significant share of recipients.

**Open-tracking pixel served over HTTP**
```
http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true
  &sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app
```
HTTP open-tracking pixels are blocked by most modern clients. Open-rate data for this send will be artificially deflated.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 violation for low-vision users.

**Empty `<title>` element** — `<title></title>` is blank. Assistive technologies announce document title on load; screen reader users get no context.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent** — Not captured by the AgentMail relay. CAN-SPAM requires a functional unsubscribe mechanism; Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender guidelines additionally require a `List-Unsubscribe` header and one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) for high-volume senders. Missing both increases spam-folder placement risk.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent** — One-click unsubscribe is not supported. Gmail began enforcing this for senders >5k/day in Feb 2024.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not captured** — SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown for this send. Cannot confirm whether `e.potterybarn.com` is properly aligned. If DMARC is enforced on the root `potterybarn.com` domain, misalignment on the `e.` subdomain would cause failures.

**[WARN] Plain-text body is 70% URLs** (9,742 of 13,864 chars) — Spam filters weight high URL-to-text ratios as a signal. The plain-text part should be a readable summary, not a dump of tracking URLs.
