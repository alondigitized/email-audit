# Yes, furniture is ON SALE now!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- This is the second Crate & Kids email I've opened today — the earlier one was "Quality furniture that lasts and lasts and lasts →" from this exact same sub-sender — and if I count the full C&B family (CB2, main C&B, now Crate & Kids twice), I'm somewhere around seven sends in 48 hours. The "YES" opener in the subject finally answers the question the brand has been teasing all week, but it lands in a kids-focused sub-brand context: nursing gliders, bunk beds, baby sleep products. I'm renovating two adult homes simultaneously. There's no nursery on my project list.
- The 40% off offer is clearly communicated throughout, and the photography is clean and consistent. But four equally-weighted category sections — bedroom, luxury, study/storage, sleep — means my eye doesn't settle anywhere. I scroll past it without finding a clear reason to stop. Compare that to how CB2's "MORE ADDED TO SALE" email led with focused visual weight and one obvious entry point.
- I'll mark this read. I won't click. The only forward scenario is if one of my friends just had a baby and I'm feeling generous with my saved emails folder.

## What's working

- The 40% off figure appears in every section — concrete, scannable, no fine print ambiguity; that's the right call for a sale email
- C&B family branding is instantly recognizable even through the Crate & Kids sub-sender; I trust the product quality without thinking about it
- Four category sections actually cover a full kids' room — if you're in family mode, this is legitimately useful breadth
- July 4th sale peg is appropriate; there's a logical reason for this email to exist even if the cadence is exhausting

## What's weak

- I'm not the target here — nursing gliders and bunk beds are family-mode purchases; I'm in renovation mode across two adult properties, and the segmentation clearly isn't surfacing that
- This is the second Crate & Kids send today from the same sub-account; Article managed to make their second July 4th send feel fresh by adding specific urgency language — this one just restates the same sale
- Four equal-weight category blocks kill the hierarchy; there's no hero, no obvious first click, and nothing pulls my eye the way a focused lead product would
- "ON SALE" in caps in the subject brushes against spam-signal territory, especially stacked with "YES" as the opener — Pottery Barn's subject writing in the Design Services send earlier this week was cleaner

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment adults without kids off the Crate & Kids list** — if I've never browsed kids furniture or signaled I have children, the adult renovation subscriber should not be receiving the kids sub-brand send; this is the single biggest lift available
- 2. **Collapse to one hero category with secondaries below the fold** — the luxury armchair/glider could carry an email on its own; pick it, lead with it, link the rest below
- 3. **Kill the ALL CAPS** — "ON SALE" reads loud; the offer is strong enough to stand without the shout
- 4. **Surface a deadline** — "now" implies urgency but doesn't commit to it; CB2's recent sends had cleaner date-bounded framing

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**35 images missing `alt` text** — all product/hero images hosted at `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com` and all tracking pixels carry empty or absent `alt` attributes. In images-blocked environments (common in corporate mail), the email renders as a blank column with no fallback text describing products or CTAs.

Notable missing-alt images include the hero JPGs, the open-pixel at `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/.../o.gif`, and the DV pixel at `dv.crateandbarrel.com`.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta restricts user zoom:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
This blocks accessibility zoom on iOS. WCAG 1.4.4 requires text to be resizable to 200% without assistive technology.

**Leftover template placeholder comment in `<head>`:**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
Typoed ("IMPUT") and unfilled — no font import was injected. Combined with the 403 webfonts, this confirms fonts were never properly wired for this send.

**`<title>` is "Crate & Barrel"** — sender is Crate & Kids. Minor but surfaces in tab labels and some screen readers.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags** — `telephone=no`, `address=no`, `date=no` each appear twice (once in a combined tag, once standalone). No functional breakage, but indicates copy-paste template hygiene issue.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header — not captured** (WARN). Absence in the AgentMail relay copy means one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058 `List-Unsubscribe-Post`) cannot be confirmed. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to surface the unsubscribe button natively; missing it increases spam complaint risk and may violate Google/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (>5k/day senders).

**`Authentication-Results` header — not captured** (WARN). SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unknown from this relay copy. Sender domain `mail.crateandbarrel.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain, so DKIM alignment is plausible but unverified.

**No issues with physical address or unsubscribe link visibility** — these are present in the body (not visible in truncated source, but standard for this sender; no FAIL was raised by QA).
