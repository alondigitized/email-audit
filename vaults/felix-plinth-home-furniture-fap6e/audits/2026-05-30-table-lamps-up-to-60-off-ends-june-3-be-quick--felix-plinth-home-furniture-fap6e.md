# TABLE LAMPS ⌛ UP TO 60% OFF ends June 3 ⌛ Be quick! 
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-eight. That's the Wayfair count since Sunday — and today's send commits what I'd call category fraud. The subject promises table lamps at 60% off, which, for the record, is exactly what I'm looking for right now because I'm outfitting two homes simultaneously and I have a specific lamp hole in my primary living room and two lamp holes in the second home's bedrooms. I opened it. Then the email served me a generic WAREHOUSE CLEAROUT grid: TV stands and living room tables, kids and bedroom furniture, mattresses and more, fitness and exercise. Not a single lamp in the hero. Not a lamp in the category tiles. The closest thing to a lamp is whatever unnamed product is lurking somewhere in the "Your Home, These Picks" scroll section, which I would have to hunt for like a scavenger hunt nobody asked for.
- This is the exact tactic that makes me trust Wayfair's subject lines less and less each week. Pottery Barn ran outdoor furniture and lighting in the same email at the same 50% off discount two days ago and managed to feature the actual products they promised — you clicked Outdoor Sale, you saw patio sofas and pendant lights, it tracked. Here I click TABLE LAMPS and I land in a warehouse. CB2's "OUTDOOR IS ON SALE" from earlier this week was cleaner than this — every category in that email was actually present above the fold.
- I'm marking this read. I'm not clicking. I'm not forwarding it — I'd actually be embarrassed to send this to my partner who's co-managing the renovation with me because it would look like I was trying to trick her into clicking a lamp link that doesn't go to lamps.

## What's working

- **Subject specificity is real** — "TABLE LAMPS" and "60% OFF" and "June 3" is a tight triangle of product, discount, and deadline; I opened it because of those three words working together.
- **Wayfair Rewards is surfaced at the bottom** — I'm a member and I do the rewards math before I buy, so even a footer mention of it registers as a green flag.
- **"WAREHOUSE CLEAROUT" banner reads as a genuine seasonal moment** — late May / early June clearance feels seasonally honest, not manufactured urgency.
- **Clean render** — the email displays without broken images or layout collapse; the four-tile category grid and the product grid below both loaded and spaced correctly.

## What's weak

- **Bait-and-switch, full stop** — the subject is TABLE LAMPS; the hero is a multi-category warehouse clearout with TV stands and fitness equipment. These are not the same email.
- **"Be quick!" and double hourglass emojis read desperate**, not urgent — this is email #28 in a week, and the panic copy makes it worse, not better.
- **No lamp products visible anywhere in the hero or category tiles** — if you're going to promise a category in the subject, at minimum surface one product image of that category above the fold.
- **"shop all sales" is the most generic CTA Wayfair owns** — after promising table lamps at 60% off, the button should say "Shop lamps" and route there, not to the clearout homepage.
- **The cadence is actively eroding trust** — yesterday's #26 and #27 ran rugs and mattress foundations; today is lamps. My brain is filing all of these as "Wayfair is desperate."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Route the CTA to the actual lamp category, not the global sale page.** The single highest-leverage fix: "Shop Lamps Now →" linking to `/lighting/table-lamps/?sort=sale` would have made this a 7-or-8 email instead of a 5. Article did this correctly during the Memorial Day run — "Up to 40% off ends Monday" linked directly to the sale collection, not the homepage.
- 2. **Put one hero lamp product image in the email body.** Even a single styled shot — a brass arc lamp on a linen nightstand — would justify the subject. Pottery Barn's outdoor email two days ago showed me actual outdoor furniture, not a category tile grid.
- 3. **Strip one hourglass emoji from the subject** and lose "Be quick!" — the deadline is already doing the urgency work. Doubling down reads as spam.
- 4. **Personalize the CTA with my browse history** — if Wayfair knows I've been eyeing lamps (and they should, given my browsing), a "Back to your saved lamps" hook would convert me on the spot.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Custom web font will silently fail in most clients:**
```css

  font-family: "Sofia";
  src: url(https://secure.img.wfrcdn.com/st4/stores/common/fonts/sofia/sofia_reg.woff);
```

Web fonts are blocked in Gmail (all variants), Outlook (Windows), and Yahoo. The `mso-font-alt: "Arial"` fallback only applies to Outlook/MSO rendering — Gmail and other clients will fall back to the system default serif/sans-serif unless a `font-family` stack is declared inline on affected elements. Verify that inline `font-family` stacks include a web-safe fallback.
