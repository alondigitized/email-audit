# Enjoy UP TO 70% OFF + Nightstands that add storage & style
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-three. That's where my Pottery Barn counter sits now, and this one drops the same day as their "Happy first day of SUMMER!" blast — meaning I've already deleted one email from this exact sender today before I even got to this. The subject pitched nightstands, which is honestly the right call for me right now: I'm sourcing bedside tables for two primary bedrooms simultaneously, so the category landed. But the execution is a paint-by-numbers Summer Sale send. Hero says 70% off, grid shows five or six named styles — Toulouse, Caden, Sausalito, Farmhouse — CTA says "Shop All Nightstands." That's the whole play.
- The product grid is actually the cleanest part. Named styles with thumbnail shots give me enough to distinguish silhouettes at a glance — dark stained hardwood versus natural oak finish — which is genuinely useful when I'm mentally sorting "primary bedroom in the city" from "second home casual." And the Affirm callout at the bottom is smart for a $400+ nightstand category. What kills it is everything that surrounds the core offer: the "Recommended Just For You" module dumps in what looks like completely unrelated soft goods, the "Complete Your Room" section isn't sharp enough to feel curated, and the cadence exhaustion walking in means I'm already skeptical before I render a pixel.
- I wouldn't click today. I'd screenshot the Toulouse style for my Pinterest-adjacent folder and revisit when I have time to actually compare it against what Article and CB2 have in the same wood tone. Would not forward — this is pure research-inbox material.

## What's working

- **Category targeting is dead-on for me right now.** Nightstands for two bedrooms — I am literally on this shopping trip in real life. If any email this week deserved a click from me, the category was right.
- **Named styles with distinct thumbnail silhouettes** let me visually sort in three seconds without scrolling — Caden's darker finish versus Farmhouse's lighter tone reads clearly even at mobile size.
- **"Do-It-All Nightstands" hero copy** is more useful than the usual "Shop Summer" fluff; "storage & style" is the actual value prop I care about (I need drawer space, not just a surface).
- **Affirm at the bottom** is correctly placed for a $400–$700 nightstand. It doesn't lead with it, but it's there when I start doing math.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, images loaded, nothing broken.

## What's weak

- **Second email today from Pottery Barn.** The "Happy first day of SUMMER!" send already hit me this morning. The June 20 rugs email was email #31. I'm on autopilot-archive mode before the hero even paints.
- **"Recommended Just For You" module feels completely off-brief.** I'm in nightstand mode and suddenly there's what looks like pillows and outdoor furniture mixed in. CB2's "What's old is new again" send kept tighter editorial focus and felt more curated as a result.
- **No sizing or shipping signals visible.** Furniture is logistics. Article always surfaces "in stock, ships in X days" in the product card — Pottery Barn gives me nothing. That's the #1 thing I'd need before clicking a nightstand CTA.
- **"Shop All Nightstands" CTA is category-level, not product-level.** Crate & Barrel's 4th of July Warehouse send at least drives to a filtered sale page by room. Sending me to all nightstands when you already know which style clusters I browse is a missed shot.
- **No deadline anywhere visible.** "Summer Sale" floats without an anchor. Even a "through Sunday" feels more credible than infinite-discount vibes — which by now, at 33 Pottery Barn emails, have completely eroded for me.

## Recommendations

- 1. **One hero nightstand, not six.** Pick the Toulouse or Sausalito, show it styled in a real primary bedroom, link directly to that PDP. The grid is fine below the fold but the hero needs a singular hero product with a price shown. Article does this every single send and it's why I linger on their emails.
- 2. **Add shipping timeline to each product card.** Even "Est. ships in 3–5 weeks" would get me clicking. Without it, I'm going to Wayfair to check lead time anyway.
- 3. **Kill the "Recommended Just For You" module or make it actually bedside-adjacent** — lamps, bedding, a mirror. Right now it reads as algorithmic noise that undercuts the nightstand editorial focus.
- 4. **Give the Summer Sale a close date.** "Ends July 4th" or "Last 48 hours" — anything. The open-ended sale is discount fatigue accelerant.
- 5. **Don't send me two emails in one day.** Batch the nightstand feature into the summer-sale hero send or hold it for the next day's slot.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Side tables with real drawer space, actually in stock.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP headers in `<style>` block (template defect):**
The following code appears verbatim inside a `<style>` tag in `<head>`:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are PHP `header()` calls that were never executed server-side — they leaked into the rendered HTML as literal text inside CSS. Email clients ignore them silently, but this confirms the template was not processed through its intended rendering pipeline for this send.

**HTTP image sources (3):**
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Gmail and Outlook.com proxy images over HTTPS; Apple Mail blocks HTTP images outright on iOS 17+. These will silently fail or be proxied with potential quality loss.

**Missing alt text: 37 images** including the hero banner (`S26_TOP_SummerSale_NoUrgency.jpg`), all 21 nightstand product slices, footer images, and tracking pixels. Recipients with images disabled see no descriptive fallback across the entire email.

**Empty `<title>` tag:** `<title></title>` — no impact on rendering but degrades accessibility in screen readers that announce the document title.

**Google Fonts external load:** `fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat` is loaded via `<link>`. Blocked by corporate firewalls and some privacy-focused clients; fallback stack is `verdana, arial` (acceptable, but Montserrat will not render in ~15–20% of clients).
