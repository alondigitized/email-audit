#  ⏱️ ❗ Clearout: 72 HOURS ONLY ❗ ⏱️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- This is Wayfair in full clearance-mode panic: a wall of purple tiles, every one of them screaming a percentage. Within the first scroll I counted at least eight separate discount offers — 60%, 55%, 45%, 40% — stacked so densely that my eye gave up trying to land anywhere useful and just kind of... bounced. There's no anchor. No single thing Wayfair actually wants me to buy today. It's a liquidation flyer in email form.
- The "picked just for you" row is the one thing that almost saves this. I can see product tiles with star ratings, which is the closest this email gets to acting like it knows me. But it's bookended by so many generic category tiles — "fitness & exercise," "outdoor fun & games" — that any personalization signal gets drowned out. I'm renovating two houses. I do not need resistance bands right now.
- I would not click. I would not forward. I would mark as read, vaguely remember that Wayfair is running a 72-hour sale, and maybe come back through the browser if I remember later tonight. That's the best case outcome for this send, and Wayfair probably knows it.

## What's working

- **Urgency framing in the hero is immediate.** "72-HR CLEAROUT" hits the top of the email and I understood the structure in two seconds — this is a sale with a clock on it.
- **"Picked just for you" with star ratings is the right instinct.** Those product cards with visible review scores are the only moment this email treats me like a customer with a history, not a cold lead.
- **Furniture + decor at 55% is legitimately relevant to me.** Given that I need a console table, a rug, and about four other things across both homes, a furniture clearance from a brand I actually use deserves at least a skim.
- **0% APR Affirm tile is useful context.** I'm already buying on a rewards card but the financing callout tells me Wayfair expects big-ticket transactions, which signals they have real inventory, not just accessory clutter.

## What's weak

- **The discount grid is completely undifferentiated.** Eight tiles showing eight categories at eight discount levels is not a sale — it's a sitemap. Article's home tour email last week did more to get me to a specific product page than this entire layout.
- **"Fitness & exercise" and "outdoor fun & games" are complete misfires.** Wayfair has my order history. They know I bought a media console and a side table. Dumping workout equipment tiles into my clearout email is the definition of not knowing your customer.
- **No single anchor product.** Pottery Barn's indigo color story from earlier this week gave me one clear visual thesis. CB2's "instant character" sends pick a hero item. This email has no hero. There's nothing I'd screenshot and text to my partner.
- **The subject line reads like a spam filter stress test.** Two timer emojis, two exclamation marks, ALL CAPS — this is the kind of construction that makes me pre-sort before I even open.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the grid to three focused categories.** If Wayfair's data says I'm a furniture + decor + lighting buyer, show me those three tiles at clearout pricing and drop everything else. The "fitness & exercise" tile is actively eroding trust that this email knows who I am.
- 2. **Lead with one hero product from the personalized section.** Pull the top-rated, highest-relevance item from my "picked for you" set and make it the visual anchor above the category grid. CB2 manages to do this with a single rug or a single chair — Wayfair has more data and should be doing it better.
- 3. **Rewrite the subject to survive an inbox scan without triggering every spam heuristic.**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Clearout ends in 72 hrs — furniture + decor up to 60% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `72-hour sale: the furniture finds worth the rush`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Including picks we pulled from your browsing history.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Console tables, sofas, lighting — all clearance priced this weekend.`
- 4. **Collapse the footer app pitch.** The "Get Exclusive Deals on the App" section at the bottom is fine but it competes with the sale CTA in an email that already has too many competing actions. Move it to a one-line mention or drop it entirely from clearout sends.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Web font — WOFF only, no WOFF2:**
```css
@font-face {
  font-family: "Sofia";
  src: url(https://secure.img.wfrcdn.com/st4/stores/common/fonts/sofia/sofia_reg.woff) format("woff");
  mso-font-alt: "Arial"

```
Only `.woff` is declared. WOFF2 is supported by every modern email client that supports web fonts at all and offers ~30% smaller payload. Missing `format("woff2")` source means unnecessary bandwidth on supported clients.

**Outlook fallback is correct** (`mso-font-alt: "Arial"`) — no issue there.

**Subject line emoji density:**
```
⏱️ ❗ Clearout: 72 HOURS ONLY ❗ ⏱️
```
Four emoji characters in the subject. Gmail on Android and some Outlook builds strip or misrender stacked emoji sequences. This is a deliverability/rendering risk, not a code defect, but worth noting as a client-compat flag.

**Gmail hack present (expected):**

Standard workaround for Gmail's CSS scoping bug — correctly implemented.

**`word-break: break-all` on product names:**
