- **Price anchor in the subject is real.** Under $30 for kitchen mats is a concrete offer that cuts through at glance — it's the best thing in this send.
- **Rewards block is present.** The 5% rewards mention is visible and I do care about stacking rewards on large purchases; nice that it's surfaced mid-email.

## What's weak

- **Subject-to-body bait-and-switch.** The subject promises a specific product at a specific price; the email opens on a broad summer sale hero. That's a trust friction point, and I noticed it immediately.
- **Visual hierarchy works against the offer.** My eye went to the BIG SUMMER SALE banner before anything kitchen-mat-specific — if the hook is under $30 kitchen mats, that should be the hero, full stop.
- **"Score styles you love" CTA is vague.** Article's CTA copy from their June 9 outdoor email gave me an action and a category; "score styles you love" is the filler button that every brand uses.
- **Cadence is grinding.** This is number 45. Two emails yesterday, one today. Pottery Barn has sent me 10 this month and I actually read them more carefully because the frequency doesn't exhaust me. Wayfair is in diminishing-returns territory.
- **"Picked for you" feels algorithmic, not curated.** I can see the product tiles and nothing screams "we know you're renovating two properties with overlapping style palettes."

- 1. **Lead the body with the kitchen mats.** If under $30 is the promise, make that the hero module — a tight 3-up product grid of actual mats at that price point, right below the header. Don't make me find the offer you advertised.

- 3. **Tighten the CTA copy.** "Shop Mats Under $30" is a specific verb + noun. "Score styles you love" is throwaway.
- 4. **Consolidate sends.** Two emails yesterday, one today — even loyal subscribers will start batch-deleting. Pottery Barn's cadence is tighter and I open them more. Wayfair needs a calendar review.
- 5. **Show the price prominently on the product tiles.** If the whole thesis is affordability, the $XX price should be on the card itself.
