# Dinnerware, linens & more for a well-styled table
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-25**
## Executive summary

- Forty-three. That's the Pottery Barn count in my July inbox as of this one — same day as "Our new lookbook has arrived," which I scored a 5 a few sends ago. At this point Pottery Barn isn't a brand in my inbox, it's a weather system.
- That said, this one's actually decent for what it is. It's a "how to style your dining table" numbered breakdown — dinnerware, glassware, table linens, botanicals, candles — and I'm mid-decision on dining stuff for the second home, so the topic lands. The blue-and-white tablescape hero photo is genuinely pretty, and the numbered list format (1-2-3-4-5) makes it scannable in a way most PB sends aren't. But "Up to 70% off" sitting right at the top with zero specifics, plus the endless "Recommended Just For You" scroll into curtains and rugs and an Affirm pitch, drags it right back into generic PB territory.
- Would I click? Maybe — if I'm actually shopping for a table setup this week, the numbered breakdown is useful enough to tap "Shop Tabletop." Would I forward it to a friend? No. Marking as read and moving on, same as I do with three-quarters of what this brand sends me.

## What's working

- The "5 Ways to Style Your Dining Table" numbered format actually organizes the categories logically instead of dumping a grid of random products.
- Hero photo is a real styled tablescape (blue florals, brass candlesticks, actual place settings) — not a generic lifestyle shot, and it matches the "well-styled table" subject line.
- Two clear CTAs up top (Shop Tabletop / Shop Decor) instead of one vague button.
- "NEW: Lucia Dinnerware Collection" callout gives the send at least one concrete, nameable product.

## What's weak

- "Up to 70% off" with no category or product attached — same vague top-line offer PB runs constantly. Article and Crate & Barrel at least anchor a number to a specific room or collection.
- The email doesn't stop being about dining — it sprawls into curtain rods, bath towels, an accent chair, a rug, a quilt, and an outdoor sale banner. By the time I hit "Discover New Arrivals" I've forgotten this was a tablescape email.
- Rewards/Affirm/App Store block at the bottom is the exact same footer stack I've seen on the last five PB sends this month — zero new info, pure filler.
- No sizing, dimensions, or seating-count info for the dinnerware sets even though that's the thing I'd actually need to know before clicking through.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the send in half — kill everything after "Shop All Tabletop." A dining-table styling email doesn't need curtains, rugs, and an outdoor sale tacked on; it dilutes the one clear idea this email had going for it.
- 2. Put a real number on the offer in the hero — "Up to 70% off" next to a dining-specific stat ("dinnerware sets from $29") the way Crate & Barrel led with "60% off summer sale" on its dining hacks send.
- 3. Add seating/place-setting counts to the dinnerware and glassware tiles — that's the actual blocker for someone furnishing two houses at once.
- 4. Drop the repeated Rewards/Affirm footer or vary it — it's identical across sends and reads as filler by email #40-something.

# Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Dinnerware, linens & more" Email

## 1. Technical Summary
Standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC/ExactTarget)-templated HTML email with legacy Outlook/webmail compatibility hacks. Automated QA surfaced 4 warning categories: missing header-level unsubscribe/auth data, 44+ images without alt text, several HTTP (non-TLS) asset URLs, and a stray non-CSS code fragment in the `<head>`. No hard failures — 50% pass rate, 0 issues, 4 warnings.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 62 tracking/redirect links skipped from HTTP probing (all routed through `click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=...` opaque SFMC redirect tokens) — destinations cannot be verified from this capture.
- Four separate open-tracking beacons present in one send: `image.e.potterybarn.com/lib/...`, `d.turn.com/r/dd/...`, `svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=...`, `clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/{guid}?mi_mid=...` — redundant beaconing increases render latency and blocklist exposure.
- `svopn.potterybarn.com` open-tracking pixel is served over plain `http://`, not `https://` — mixed-content risk in HTTPS-rendering webmail clients.
- Plain-text alternative is 73% URLs (11,117/15,303 chars) — indicates the multipart/alternative text part is mostly link dump rather than readable fallback content.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 44 `<img>` tags missing `alt` attributes, spanning both content banners (`F26_TOP_SummerSalePartC_NoUrgency.jpg`, product shots like `remmy-swivel-chair-1-l.jpg`) and decorative tiling images (`slices_01–20.jpg`, `spacer_0.gif`) — screen readers and blocked-image fallback render nothing.
- 4 image sources use `http://` instead of `https://`: `edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`, `edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, `edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/.../slices_02.jpg`, and the `svopn.potterybarn.com` pixel — may be blocked as mixed content.
- Note: the West Elm asset domain (`edm.westelm.com`) appearing inside a Pottery Barn send suggests a shared cross-brand template/CDN, not necessarily a defect, but worth confirming intentional.
- `<style>` block in `<head>` contains invalid, non-executing PHP code (`header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: no-store...");`) — dead code artifact, likely copy-pasted from a server script into the template; does not break rendering but indicates a template hygiene bug.
- Otherwise standard MSO conditional comments, `ExternalClass`, and text-size-adjust resets are present and correctly formed — no other rendering issues found.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- Recipient address resolved correctly and consistently as `lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` across links (e.g., `.../coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`, `svopn.potterybarn.com/?...&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`) — no unresolved merge tags found in visible source.
- The full recipient email address is passed in plaintext as a URL query parameter to multiple third-party tracking domains (`svopn.potterybarn.com`, `image.e.potterybarn.com` implicitly via `sv_sveme`) — a PII-in-URL exposure pattern susceptible to referrer leakage at each hop.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication)
- `List-Unsubscribe` header not found in this capture (WARN, not FAIL) — may be stripped by the AgentMail relay rather than omitted by the sender; not confirmable from this data alone.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header (RFC 8058) not found — one-click unsubscribe support unconfirmed.
- `Authentication-Results` header not found — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status unknown from this capture.
- Body-level unsubscribe/customer-service link is present (`potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=...`), satisfying CAN-SPAM's visible-mechanism requirement independent of header-level List-Unsubscribe.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- No standard `utm_source`/`utm_medium`/`utm_campaign` parameters found on any link. Pottery Barn instead uses proprietary SFMC redirect tokens (`click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=ABB7...`) and `clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/{guid}?mi_mid=...&cp_tp=...` — landing-page alignment cannot be verified without resolving the redirect, and these were among the 62 links skipped from HTTP probing.
- Product images link directly to `www.potterybarn.com/pbimgs/...` CDN assets rather than through the tracking redirect layer — consistent, no continuity issue there.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add `alt` text to all 44 flagged images; use `alt=""` for purely decorative spacer/slice images rather than omitting the attribute.
2. Convert the 4 remaining `http://` asset/pixel URLs to `https://` to avoid mixed-content blocking.
3. Remove the orphaned PHP `header()` snippet from the `<head>` `<style>` block — non-functional template artifact.
4. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results` headers are actually present at the raw MTA level (outside the AgentMail relay capture) before treating their absence as a compliance gap.
5. Consolidate the 4 concurrent open-tracking beacons where possible to reduce latency and blocklist surface.
6. Avoid passing raw recipient email addresses as plaintext URL params to third-party tracking domains; use a hashed/tokenized identifier instead.
