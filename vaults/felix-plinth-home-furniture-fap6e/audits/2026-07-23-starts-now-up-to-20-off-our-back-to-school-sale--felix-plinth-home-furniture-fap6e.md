# STARTS NOW: Up to 20% off our Back-to-School Sale!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- Four. That's the C&B family count in my inbox today, and I'm starting there the same way I started the last three reviews — because cadence is the story. "Shop up to 60% off the Summer Sale →" this morning. "Don't miss it: Up to 60% off the Summer Sale!" shortly after. And now Crate & Kids is launching a back-to-school campaign at 20% off. I don't have children. That's not a complaint about the creative — it's a statement about the list. I'm here because I'm in the C&B ecosystem and someone's suppression logic apparently doesn't know that a person renovating two homes simultaneously is not in the market for a backpack this August.
- Setting that aside: the creative is actually pretty solid. "Back-to-school without drama" is a real headline — it earns its space by naming what parents actually feel, not just what they're buying. The offer is clearly staged in the hero. The CTAs are unambiguous. If I had a kid, this probably lands. But I don't. And after the parent brand ran 60% off Summer Sale this morning, 20% off registers as a step backward before I've even scrolled. Wayfair's "Black Friday in July" positioning I reviewed this week was setting expectations at a completely different discount altitude — 20% off feels like it wandered in from a different inbox.
- Marking as read. No click. The only forward scenario is texting a link to a friend who mentioned school supply shopping — and that's not what C&B is paying to make happen.

## What's working

- "Back-to-school without drama" is a headline that actually earns its hero space — emotional and specific, not just a price callout.
- The product grid has genuine breadth: backpacks, lunch bags, storage bins, kids furniture — it reads like a real sale, not a thin one.
- "Shop kids" / "Shop baby" is a clean CTA split that acknowledges the audience isn't monolithic. Thoughtful segmentation within the email even if the list itself missed me.
- Visual hierarchy flows correctly: hero → offer → CTAs → product grid. No hunt required.
- No render bugs I can see — clean layout, readable type, no broken image gaps.

## What's weak

- I'm not a parent. End of story — no creative fix compensates for reaching the wrong subscriber.
- 20% off the same morning C&B proper sent 60% off Summer Sale blasts is an offer narrative that falls apart in the inbox. Even if these are different category pools, the discount math reads as a demotion.
- No end date visible anywhere. CB2's "Hours left for up to 40% off" email — which I reviewed from this same week — creates actual urgency by closing a window. "STARTS NOW" tells me when the sale opens but not when it closes. That's only half the pressure equation.
- The sweepstakes section ("enter for a chance to win $4,000 in prizes") lands mid-scroll and kills the buy flow. It's a retention hook, not a conversion hook — put it after the product grid.
- Yesterday this sender sent me "Something spooky drops TODAY 🎃" — a Halloween-in-July app tease that scored a 4 — and today it's back-to-school. The Crate & Kids brand voice feels unmoored.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the list first.** Suppress anyone without a child-category signal in the last 18 months. Nothing downstream matters if the targeting misses this badly.
- 2. **Add a sale end date to the hero.** Pottery Barn's coffee table email this week anchored a deadline and I noticed. "Through August 3rd" or "while supplies last" gives me a reason to act today instead of parking it.
- 3. **Reconcile offer levels across the brand family.** If C&B Summer Sale is 60% and Crate & Kids back-to-school is 20%, acknowledge they're separate pools. Even "in addition to Summer Sale prices" stops the reader from feeling downgraded.
- 4. **Drop the sweepstakes below the product grid.** Let me see and want the products first. The contest is a retention tool — sequence it after the buy CTA fires.
- 5. **Lead the subject with the proposition, not the launch signal.** "STARTS NOW" is about the brand's clock, not my benefit.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` brand mismatch (confirmed bug)**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
The sending brand is "Crate & Kids" but the `<title>` reads "Crate & Barrel." Screen readers and some email clients (e.g., Apple Mail tab labels) surface the `<title>`. This should be `Crate & Kids`.

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Drop `maximum-scale=1`.

**Conflicting `text-size-adjust` declarations**
```css
body { -webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; }
/* later overridden by: */
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; }
```
The `*` rule wins and disables OS-level font scaling globally, including body text. The `body` declaration is effectively dead. If the intent is to prevent auto-sizing only on specific elements, scope it there — not `*`.

**`line-height: 100%` applied to `*`**
```css
* { line-height: 100%; }
```
Applying `line-height: 100%` to every element (including tables and images) is an aggressive reset that can collapse text on certain email clients and conflicts with the responsive overrides that set explicit `line-height` values in the media queries (e.g., `.f10{line-height: 12px!important}`). The `!important` escalation in media queries is a workaround symptom of this.

**Dark mode locked out**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Only `light` is declared. Apple Mail and many modern clients will attempt forced dark-mode inversion on this email, which can produce unexpected results (e.g., white text on white backgrounds). Either add `dark` support or add explicit `prefers-color-scheme: dark` overrides.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
`format-detection` appears four times with partially overlapping values. This is harmless but indicates template assembly sloppiness:
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
Consolidate into the first declaration.
