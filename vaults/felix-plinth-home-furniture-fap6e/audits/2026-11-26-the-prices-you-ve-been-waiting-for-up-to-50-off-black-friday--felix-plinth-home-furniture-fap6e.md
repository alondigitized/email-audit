# The prices you’ve been waiting for: Up to 50% off Black Friday!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-26**
## Executive summary

- This is the sixth Crate & Barrel family send I've dealt with this week, and the second from the Kids arm. I'm renovating two houses and I don't have a nursery. The hero is a pile of nod-print chairs, and the top line says "Don't miss out: 585,267 people are shopping Black Friday now." The "up to 50% off Black Friday + free shipping" header is clear. But the 11/26 "Black Friday just got even BETTER!" email already told me the same thing this morning.
- The body is kids furniture, baby bedding, toys and stockings. That's a long scroll for something I'm not shopping. The Nod chair and the rugs are the only things I'd look at, and a rug is a rug at any scale. I'd skim it, maybe save it to the Pinterest folder, and mark it read.
- I wouldn't forward it. I'd click once at most, on the rugs tile, because I'm at the stage of renovation where I'm comparing rugs.

## What's working

- The "up to 50% off Black Friday + free shipping" header is the first thing I read, and the "Shop Now" button is obvious.
- The "Ships Free*" tags on the tiles help. Shipping cost on bulky furniture is what I check first.
- The Design Desk "Book a Free Appointment" block is the one thing on the page that matches how I shop for furniture. Pottery Barn doesn't make it that easy.
- The upgraded Crate card block mentions Reward Dollars. I'd do the rewards math on that, since I buy with the highest-rewards card.

## What's weak

- The sender name says "Kids," and so does everything in the body. Nothing here speaks to someone doing a home renovation.
- I got "BLACK FRIDAY" under two sender names today. It's the same red hero and the same 50% claim, just reshuffled. CB2 does the same thing, and the Article "Even more Black Friday deals" send looked like its 11/21 one too.
- The email runs about seven screens. The tile grid is dense with tiny captions like "up to 30% off nod chairs" and "up to 20% off holiday decor & bedding." Those are hard to read on a phone without zooming.
- There are three or four "Shop the Sale" CTAs and no single hero product, so nothing tells me where to start.
- The fine print block at the bottom is a wall of tiny text. It undercuts the "free shipping" promise, because I can't tell what the exclusions are.
- "Up to 50%" with several different "up to" figures (30%, 20%, 50%) doesn't feel honest. I can't tell what actually gets 50% off.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this send. Show a household that browses furniture and rugs the Nod chair and rug tiles, not the nursery and stocking tiles. Even a small hero swap would help.
- 2. Make the offer concrete. One named product with a real price ("Nod chair, now $X") works better than five different "up to" claims.
- 3. Cut the page to the top 4–6 tiles and one CTA.
- 4. Move the Design Desk block up for the people who shop furniture. That's the best differentiator in the email.
- 5. Make the fine print readable, or link to it.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Black Friday ends soon: kids' furniture up to 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping on furniture and rugs. Offer ends Sunday, so grab your pick first.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The sampled source is a standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional ESP template, with `lang="en"`, VML/MSO fallbacks and a mobile breakpoint at 460px. The confirmed problems are widespread missing `alt` text (about 29 images flagged) and a viewport tag that disables zoom. Header-based compliance and authentication checks are inconclusive because the relay didn't capture the headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **70 tracking links were not probed.** They are click-redirect domains, so the QA tool skipped the HTTP check. Their destinations are unverified, so I can't confirm they resolve or that there are no broken links.
- **Five LiveRamp pixels are in the body.** The URLs are `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=dbe8da71…&n=1` through `&n=5`, all with the same hashed identifier `s=`. This is third-party tracking beyond the ESP's own pixels.
- **ESP open and impression pixels** are present at `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif?mi_u=…` and `https://dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/e7de2cc8-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…`. They are first-party hosts, so tracking runs on a brand-owned subdomain.
- No broken or malformed links are confirmed in the truncated source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `alt` on content images.** About 20 images served from `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/…` (`.jpg` and `.gif`) lack alt text. That includes `592f13e9-…jpg`, `2635bc5e-…jpg` and animated `91ea068d-…gif` and `ac698ed7-…gif`. One more is on Scene7, `s7d5.scene7.com/.../2026_1105_CBK_PersonalizedGiftShop_Seconda…`, and one is on `.../m/47/bbf6f9d4-…png`. With images blocked, or for screen readers, these show nothing or a filename. This is the most consequential finding.
- **Tracking pixels lack alt.** The `rlcdn.com`, `o.gif` and `dv.` pixels are reported too. These should carry `alt=""` as a decorative image, which is a lower-severity fix.
- **Zoom is disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` blocks pinch-zoom, a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Unescaped ampersand in the title.** `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` uses a raw `&` under an XHTML doctype. It should be `&amp;`. Parsers tolerate it, so the impact is low.
- **Leftover template placeholder.** The comment `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` shows the template wasn't cleaned up. There is no rendering impact.
- **Mixed or duplicated meta tags.** There are three `format-detection` metas, and both `http-equiv` Content-Type and `<meta charset>`. They are redundant, with no functional impact.
- **Text-size CSS.** `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` and `html{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` conflict with `body{...:100%}`. They prevent user text scaling on iOS, which is a minor accessibility regression.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`, `[FIRST_NAME]`) are visible in the provided excerpt. The source is truncated, so the rest of the body wasn't checked.
