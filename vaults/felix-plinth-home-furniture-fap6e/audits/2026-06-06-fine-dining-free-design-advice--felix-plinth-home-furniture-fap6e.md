# Fine dining. Free design advice.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-one. That's the number I'm carrying into this open, and I want that sitting in the room before anything else. CB2 has been relentless since mid-May, and yesterday alone I got the "MIDSEASON SALE" blast and "A well-collected home office" — two in one day. So when "Fine dining. Free design advice." lands this morning, I'm already operating on fumes.
- That said — and this is a genuine surprise — this might be the most *useful* CB2 send in the entire run. I'm literally pricing out dining chairs for a second home right now. Free interior design services from a brand I already trust isn't filler copy, it's a real offer. The hero dining room is aspirational without being pretentious: round table, warm pendant lighting, layered textiles, the kind of setup I've been pulling reference images for. The "3 WAYS TO MEET" block gives me actual options — in person, presumably virtual, something else — which is how I actually want to engage with a designer when I'm juggling two renovation timelines.
- Would I click? Probably the BOOK NOW for in-person. Would I forward it? No — this is a "save to folder" move, not a forward. The only thing holding this back from a strong score is that CB2 has spent three weeks crying wolf and I'm genuinely desensitized. But the email itself is doing the right things.

## What's working

- **The hero is on-brief.** Dining room, soft neutrals, pendant over the table — I've been building this exact mood board for the second house. Didn't have to work to see myself there.
- **"Free design services" is a real value proposition.** This isn't a 15% off coupon dressed up as a favor. For someone mid-renovation with two dining rooms to configure, this is genuinely worth an appointment.
- **3 WAYS TO MEET is actually useful.** Giving me in-person, virtual, and presumably a remote/text option acknowledges that not everyone can walk into a store on a Tuesday. That's meeting me where I am.
- **Clean visual hierarchy.** Hero → service pitch → booking options → product grid. The eye follows the intended path without fighting the layout.
- **BOOK NOW is unambiguous.** Not "Discover" or "Explore" — it's a verb with a commitment behind it. Article does this well, and CB2 is matching that standard here.

## What's weak

- **The "FINER FOLLIES" product grid dilutes the dining focus.** The bottom section mixes in what looks like bedding and accent chairs alongside dining pieces. I'm here for a dining conversation; don't open a different catalog mid-pitch.
- **No social proof anywhere.** I'm being asked to book a design appointment — even one customer quote or "300+ clients styled this year" would move the needle. Crate & Kids pulls parent testimonials; CB2 is asking me to trust the concept alone.
- **The dining room styling skews slightly feminine.** Soft blush tones, florals on the table — not a dealbreaker, but I didn't feel explicitly targeted as a 36-year-old male. A second hero image or a more masculine/neutral tablescape variant would have helped.
- **No urgency mechanism.** "Free design advice" is great but it feels permanently available. Give me a "Book by June 15" hook and I'm clicking today instead of tabbing it for later.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline to the free design services offer.** "Book your free consult before June 20" turns this from a nice-to-have into a calendar item. The MIDSEASON SALE email had urgency; this softer-touch send needs it more.
- 2. **Tighten the product grid to dining only.** Strip out the bedding and anything that doesn't belong on or near a dining table. This email has a clear thesis — don't let the bottom third contradict it.
- 3. **Add one line of social proof near the BOOK NOW buttons.** "Over 400 complimentary consultations booked in 2025" or a single designer name with a credential would reduce appointment friction.
- 4. **Suppress or throttle for the next 3 days.** I'm at 21 CB2 emails since mid-May. This send deserved a cleaner inbox to land in — internal cadence rules need to protect sends like this from getting buried by volume.
- 5. **Try a male-skewing secondary hero.** Keep the primary room as-is, but a darker wood / moodier palette option would signal that CB2 sees me as the customer, not just someone adjacent to the customer.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

| Severity | Location | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| Medium | `<meta name="viewport" ...>` | `maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-zoom. WCAG 1.4.4 violation for low-vision users. Remove or raise to `maximum-scale=5`. |
| Medium | `* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none }` | Global wildcard selector suppresses OS-level font scaling for all elements. Scope to `body` or `table` only. |
| Low | `* { line-height: 100% }` | Applied globally on `*` — intended to fix Gmail clipping but can cause text truncation in non-standard clients. Should be scoped to `table td` or specific containers. |
| Low | `@font-face` — "Lineto Brown Sub" | Family name declared three times with conflicting `src` stacks. The third declaration (`font-weight: 400`) silently overrides the first catch-all rule. Consolidate into a single `@font-face` block per weight. |
| Info | `@font-face { font-display: swap }` | `font-display` is ignored by all major email clients. Harmless noise but adds byte weight. |
| Info | HNW05 font loaded from `crateandbarrel.com` | Cross-domain font load from a sibling brand. Will 404 if that domain's CORS policy changes. Consider hosting on `mail.CB2.com` or the ESP's CDN. |
