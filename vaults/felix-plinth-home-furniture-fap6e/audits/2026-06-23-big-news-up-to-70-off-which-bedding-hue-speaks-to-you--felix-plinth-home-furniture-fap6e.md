# Big news! Up to 70% off + Which bedding hue speaks to you?
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-five. I've lost count of whether it's thirty-five or thirty-six at this point, but "big news" is doing a lot of heavy lifting for what is, by any honest measure, the same Summer Sale I've been triaging since June 20th. The creative pivot to bedding hues is — fine, actually, as a concept — but it reads less like a product moment and more like a Buzzfeed quiz wrapper bolted onto an email they were going to send regardless. I'm renovating two houses simultaneously and yes, bedding is on my list, but the dusty-rose-and-sage palette in the hero shot is aimed somewhere past me, toward someone with a dedicated Pinterest board titled "bedroom refresh inspo." (I have one of those. Let's move on.)
- What saves this from an immediate delete: the "Recommended Just for You" grid near the bottom shows what looks like upholstered furniture and room vignettes, which is where my actual renovation spend is sitting right now. If I'm clicking anything, it's there — not the "Shop Green" or "Shop Rose" buttons in the color module.
- I'm marking this as read. I might screenshot one piece from the Recommended section and send it to my wife. I'm not forwarding. Wayfair's flat declarative "AREA RUGS." subject hit harder than "Big news!" and Article's Cillian Collection send two weeks ago felt like it was talking to a person, not a demographic bucket. This one's talking at a ZIP code.

## What's working

- The "Layers of Color" swatch module is genuinely useful — seeing dusty rose, sage, and the neutral tones side by side in one scannable block is something Crate & Barrel typically buries behind a "view all colorways" click.
- "Up to 70% off" is in the header banner before any scrolling — correct hierarchy, and PB has been consistent on this across the entire June run.
- The "More Bedding Essentials" category grid (Comforters, Sheets, Pillows, Duvets) is tight and triage-friendly for someone furnishing rooms fast across two properties.
- The "Recommended Just for You" section at the bottom has actual furniture and room shots — the only module in the email that speaks to where I am in the renovation right now.
- No visible render bugs; layout reads clean at this width.

## What's weak

- "Big news!" is the most overused retail opener in the genre and the "big news" turns out to be a sale that's been in my inbox since June 20. CB2 running "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" at least doesn't pretend it has a reveal.
- The hero palette — pinks, sage, lavender-adjacent tones — signals a demographic that isn't a 36-year-old male buying for two renovation projects. I don't describe myself as someone who has "a dusty rose moment."
- The quiz framing ("Which hue speaks to you?") doesn't resolve to anything actionable I can see — no quiz mechanic, no personalized recommendation based on what I've actually bought. It's just lifestyle copy with Shop buttons.
- Dual-topic subject line (sale + bedding quiz) splits focus. Article ran one idea per email. This email has two half-ideas connected by a plus sign.
- Third PB email in three days. The cadence itself is eroding whatever goodwill the creative earns.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill "Big news!" and lead with the product angle.** The bedding color module is actually interesting creative — it doesn't need a false-promise wrapper. Just lead with the concept.
- 2. **Tighten the subject to one idea.** Either the sale or the color editorial. The `+` structure makes it feel like two emails squeezed into one subject line.
- 3. **For renovation-active customers, surface the Recommended furniture grid first.** If PB's personalization engine knows I've been looking at upholstered seating and rugs, that belongs above the bedding hero, not below it.
- 4. **Put a deadline on the sale.** "70% off" has been in my inbox since June 20. Without a stated end date, the urgency is zero. Article consistently runs a day-count; this sale feels like it just... exists.
- 5. **Add at least one neutral colorway to the hero** — slate, charcoal, warm linen. Right now the visible palette speaks to one member of a two-person household decision.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Pick a hue, save up to 70% — bedding on sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sage, dusty rose, linen and more — plus your personalized furniture picks`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh colorways at sale prices — and pieces picked from your saves`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP mixed-content — 3 assets served over `http://`:**

| Asset | URL |
|---|---|
| `spacer_0.gif` | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` |

Gmail and most modern clients block HTTP image loads. The Key CC footer banner (`Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`) will render as a broken image for the majority of recipients.

**Open-pixel also HTTP:** `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` — the open-tracking pixel is non-HTTPS, meaning open-rate data will be undercounted in clients that enforce HTTPS-only (Gmail, Outlook.com, Apple Mail with MPP).

**Missing alt text — 50+ images with no `alt` attribute**, including every content image. Confirmed affected image blocks:
- `S26_TOP_SummerSale_NoUrgency.jpg` (hero)
- All 19 `slices_01.jpg`–`slices_19.jpg` bedding color story images
- All 18 footer `slices_01.jpg`–`slices_18.jpg`
- All product spotlight images (farmhouse bed, curtain rod, towel, dining table, quilt)
- `S26_Bottom_UpTo60OffOutdoor.jpg`, `W26_Bottom_GeneralDCBottomBanner.jpg`

With images off (default in many enterprise Outlook configs), the email renders as near-blank.

**Spurious PHP header code in CSS block:**

```html
<style>
  ...
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, ...");
  header("Pragma: no-cache");

```

PHP server-side header calls appear verbatim inside a `<style>` tag. These are inert in email clients but indicate a template authoring error — a PHP snippet was pasted into the wrong section. No rendering breakage confirmed, but it signals a QA gap in template assembly.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post:** Both headers not captured by the AgentMail relay. RFC 2369 (`List-Unsubscribe`) is required for bulk sender compliance (Google/Yahoo 2024 mandate); RFC 8058 (`List-Unsubscribe-Post`) enables one-click unsubscribe. Absence here is likely a relay capture gap rather than a true omission — **verify against raw SMTP headers** before treating as a sender defect.

**CAN-SPAM physical address:** Not visible in the truncated source but typically present in Pottery Barn footer — unable to confirm from available HTML.

**Authentication-Results (SPF/DKIM/DMARC):** Header not captured by relay. `e.potterybarn.com` is a dedicated sending subdomain; DKIM alignment should be verifiable via raw headers. **Cannot assess from current data.**

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The plain-text body is 72% URLs by character count (10,937 / 15,157 chars), which is abnormally high and may trigger spam filters that penalize URL-dense plain-text parts.

UTM parameter presence cannot be fully audited without resolving the 22 redirect links, but the Key Rewards CTA includes `cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember` and `external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner` — WSI/Coremetrics-style tracking, not GA4 UTMs. If Google Analytics is the analytics destination, UTM params should be confirmed on click-through destinations.
