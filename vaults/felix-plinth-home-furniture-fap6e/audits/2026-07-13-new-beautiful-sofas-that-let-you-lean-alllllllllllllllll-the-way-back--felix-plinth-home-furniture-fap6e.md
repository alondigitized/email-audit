# New! Beautiful sofas that let you lean alllllllllllllllll the way back

## Executive summary

- Thirty-five. That's where I've landed in the Crate & Barrel family's July inbox siege, and this one — finally — is almost exactly what I needed. New sofas. Deep seating. Hero image is a sprawling, low-profile sectional in that warm cream-and-oatmeal palette I've been hunting for the second home's living room. The visual is doing genuine work here: big room, good light, couch that looks like it could swallow you whole. I actually stopped scrolling.
- The problem is the subject line got there in the most obnoxious way possible. Stretching "alllllllllllllll the way back" reads like a brand having a Twitter moment — which might work once, but I've opened thirty-four of these emails in five days and I'm not charmed. Meanwhile, there's no price visible anywhere in the hero, no sale event, no member pricing. This is a pure new-arrivals push. I'm not mad at that — I want to see what's new — but CB2 last week ran "GET IT ON SALE" with clear price anchors and I clicked immediately. This one makes me do the work.
- I'd mark it read, visit the site later when I'm not on my phone, and probably not forward it. It's relevant but it doesn't close.

## What's working

- **Hero image is strong.** The large-format sectional photo has good staging — natural light, clean room — and the sofa itself looks premium. This is the visual I'd save to a folder.
- **Product breadth is visible.** The email shows multiple sofa silhouettes with color swatches below the hero, which is exactly how I shop — I need to see options, not just one hero sku.
- **Category is dead-on for me.** Two homes in active renovation. Sofas are a real, live purchase decision right now. C&B figured that out by sending to someone on their furniture list, at minimum.
- **Layout is clean and renders without bugs.** No broken images, no overlapping text, legible on mobile. After a week of C&B sends, this one holds together visually.

## What's weak

- **No offer.** Wayfair hit me last week with up to 70% off storage the same way C&B hits me with editorial new-arrivals, and the difference is Wayfair made me feel like I was leaving money on the table if I didn't click. This email has nothing equivalent.
- **Subject line is a stunt.** The stretched "alllllllll" thing is clever exactly once and I've been getting these emails daily. It skews young in a way that doesn't match the $3,000+ price point inside.
- **Cadence damage is real.** This is approximately the thirty-fifth C&B family email since July 8th. Even when the content is right — and sofa content IS right — I'm reading it in a state of low-grade exhaustion with the brand.
- **No pricing anchors in the scroll.** I can see swatches and product tiles but I have to click through to find out what any of this costs. Article's new arrivals email last week at least showed starting price points. C&B is making me work.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show a starting price in the hero.** "Starting at $1,899" under the hero headline would do more than any copy tweak. That's the single highest-leverage change — it filters intent and rewards it at the same time.
- 2. **Dial back the subject line gimmick.** The stretched vowels work for a youth brand; C&B sells $4,000 sectionals. Pick the voice that closes furniture sales.
- 3. **Lead with the deep-seat dimension detail.** "90-inch depth" or "36-inch seat depth" is the kind of spec I can't get from a photo. Put it in the email and it earns the click without a discount.
- 4. **If there's a member price, show it.** I'm on their list. If Crate Insider members get early access or a % off new arrivals, that's the preheader — not a visual detail.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[BUG] Conflicting image `max-width` media queries — desktop rule is dead**

```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }  /* fires on desktop */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }  /* fires on desktop too */
```

On any screen ≥ 768 px, both queries match. Because the `640px` block appears later in source order, it wins via cascade — overriding the intended `600px` cap with `640px`. The `768px` rule is effectively dead. Intended max-width (600px) is never applied on desktop.

**[BUG] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta prevents user zoom**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

Blocks zoom for low-vision readers. WCAG 1.4.4 violation. Drop `maximum-scale=1`.

**[MINOR] `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` applied globally**

In the MI styling block:
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
A global wildcard suppresses iOS's automatic text-size adjustment on all elements. Should be scoped to `body` at most.

**[MINOR] `*{line-height: 100%}` applied globally**

Same wildcard block. Collides with any element-specific line-height and frequently causes clipped descenders in multi-line copy.

**[MINOR] Dark mode explicitly disabled**

```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Email will not adapt to dark-mode clients (Apple Mail dark mode, Outlook dark). Intentional lockout must be confirmed as deliberate.

**[MINOR] Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**

Three separate declarations for `telephone=no`, `address=no`, `date=no` after a combined one. Redundant, harmless, but indicates copy-paste template slop.
