# The goop sofa that never goes unnoticed
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-four. That's the CB2 tally in my inbox since mid-May, and I want to be upfront about that because it's the lens through which every one of these lands now. The subject pulled me in — "goop sofa" is a phrase I've never seen in a furniture email, and for a few seconds my cursor actually hovered. Points for that. But then I open it and the first thing above the hero is the Warehouse Sale banner — the same banner I saw on June 17 (twice, actually), and then again in a softer form on June 18. The sculptural pink sofa in the hero is genuinely striking — that rounded, cloud-like silhouette is exactly the kind of thing I'd pin for the second home — but "INDULGENCE" as the headline copy when I'm already in warehouse-deal mode creates a weird register mismatch.
- The "goop" framing is clever-ish but it skews Gwyneth. I'm a 36-year-old guy with a renovation spreadsheet and a credit card points tracker, not a jade roller subscriber. The product itself? Could work. The framing? Slightly off for me.
- I'd mark this as read without clicking. Strong enough to glance at, weak enough to file.

## What's working

- The hero sofa is genuinely a statement piece — sculptural, organic, the kind of shape that actually reads differently from the standard sectional grid I've been drowning in
- Subject line created real curiosity; "goop sofa" is specific and weird enough to earn the open
- Warehouse Sale banner gives me an immediate price-reduction signal — I don't have to hunt for the deal framing
- The product grid below the hero is clean; easy to scan on mobile, thumbnail sizing is right
- "MORE FOR LESS" as a section header is the most honest copy in the email — does exactly what I want a sale email to do

## What's weak

- The Warehouse Sale banner at the top makes "INDULGENCE" in the hero feel like a tonal whiplash — pick a lane: luxury editorial or clearance event
- "Goop" is Gwyneth-coded for my demographic; I know the brand, I know the aesthetic, but it reads feminine-skewed in a way the June 19 Todd Snyder collab did not
- No pricing visible anywhere in the hero section — Article's Cillian Collection email showed price anchors right in the grid and it changed the calculus immediately
- The product grid at the bottom is so small I can't tell what anything is — Pottery Barn's "On sale & ready-to-ship" from June 20 gave me readable product names and prices at thumbnail scale; this doesn't
- This is the fourth straight CB2 email that connects, directly or indirectly, to the Warehouse Sale; the urgency has gone flat

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the Warehouse Sale banner from the top if the hero is doing editorial work.** Either lead with the sofa as the draw and put the sale CTA mid-email, or open with the sale and skip the moody hero. Right now they're fighting each other.
- 2. **Price-anchor the hero sofa.** A line under the image — "Was $2,400. Now $1,490." — turns the scroll-stop moment into a click. Wayfair does this badly (they buried it in the flameless candle email), Pottery Barn does it well.
- 3. **Swap "INDULGENCE" for copy that connects the product name to the sale.** Something like "The sofa everyone asks about. On sale now." ties the intrigue to the action.
- 4. **Make the product grid scannable.** Name + sale price on hover or below each thumbnail. Article does this and it's the reason I've saved three of their emails.
- 5. **Give the email a reason to exist beyond the Sale.** Why this sofa, why today, why me specifically? Even one editorial sentence — "We've been calling it the goop sofa since the day it arrived" — earns more trust than another wave of warehouse copy.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues
**Partial audit only — HTML truncated before body/CTA links.**

- **Double-slash font URLs**: Four `@font-face` src values use `https://www.cb2.com//Styles/...` (double slash). These resolve via 301 redirect in browsers but add a round-trip; email clients may not follow redirects for font assets, causing silent fallback to system fonts.
  - Affected: `lineto-brown-light.woff2`, `lineto-brown-regular.woff2`, `GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2`
- **Cross-domain font dependency**: `HNW05` (Helvetica Neue Web) fonts are loaded from `www.crateandbarrel.com`, not `cb2.com`. If `crateandbarrel.com` is unreachable or changes paths, CB2 email rendering degrades silently.
- CTA link tracking (UTM parameters, click-tracking wrapper domains) cannot be verified from the provided source.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Global text-size-adjust disabled (High)**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
The wildcard selector overrides the `body` rule that sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%` four lines earlier. This disables text scaling for all elements in WebKit/IE clients, breaking WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). The inline comment attributes this to a "font fix for small values" — if needed, scope it to the specific selectors causing the problem rather than `*`.

**Issue 2 — Viewport zoom locked (Medium)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom. iOS Safari 10+ ignores it for user-initiated zoom, but Android WebView and older clients enforce it. Best practice for email accessibility is to omit `maximum-scale` entirely.

**Issue 3 — Image alt text unverifiable**
HTML truncated before `<body>` content. Alt text on product images cannot be confirmed.
