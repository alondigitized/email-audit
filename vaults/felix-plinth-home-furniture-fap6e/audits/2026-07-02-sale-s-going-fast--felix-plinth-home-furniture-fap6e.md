# SALE’S GOING FAST
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- This is the fifth CB2 email I've opened today. Fifth. I'm not counting wrong — "Up to 50% off rugs," "New items added. One week left to shop," plus the two from yesterday, and now this. At this point my thumb is doing the swipe before my brain is even reading. But I actually paused, because the hero stopped me: that deep forest-green velvet chair against the near-black background is genuinely nice. The "IT'S SELLING FAST" headline with "JULY 4TH SALE / UP TO 50% OFF" stacked cleanly above a CTA button — someone did the hierarchy right. First visual impression is probably CB2's best of the day.
- The problem is everything beneath the fold. The category grid tiles (Outdoor, Furniture, Lighting, Decor, Tabletop) feel copy-pasted from every prior send this week, and the Warehouse Sale banner — "UP TO 60% OFF" — is the exact same discount percentage I saw in Crate & Barrel's "Get up to 60% off all your outdoor essentials" this morning. The CB2/Crate family is running parallel 60%-off messaging on the same day. At some point I'm just being offered the same lamp in a different wrapper.
- The "MORE FOR YOU" personalized product section at the bottom is the sleeper move here — I actually looked at it. Specific items, crossed-out prices, sale prices. That's the one section that treated me like a known customer. I'd click there before clicking the hero. I won't forward this; anyone I'd forward it to is already getting it directly.

## What's working

- **Hero visual hierarchy is clean**: the green velvet chair is aspirational without being loud, and the copy stack (IT'S SELLING FAST → JULY 4TH SALE → UP TO 50% OFF → button) is readable in one scan.
- **July 4th deadline is credible**: unlike "SALE'S GOING FAST" alone, the specific holiday peg gives the urgency a real anchor — this is tighter framing than Article's "Limited time deals: Shop 4th of July Sale" from earlier this week, which felt vaguer.
- **Category grid covers my actual departments**: Outdoor, Furniture, Lighting — all three are actively on my purchase list for the second home. Pottery Barn ran similar category tiles this morning but with less visual polish.
- **Warehouse Sale callout with its own CTA**: a second offer tier (60% off) below the main hero gives me a reason to keep scrolling. That's smarter than a one-offer email.
- **"MORE FOR YOU" section with actual pricing**: crossed-out original prices next to sale prices, specific product names — this is the only part of the email where I felt recognized as a returning shopper rather than a cold lead.

## What's weak

- **Fifth email today — the cadence is broken**: even if this is technically the best-designed CB2 send of the day, I've already hit "sale fatigue" by send number two. Crate & Barrel is running the same problem on outdoor furniture simultaneously. The family is cannibalizing its own inbox real estate.
- **ALL CAPS subject "SALE'S GOING FAST" reads like shouting**: it's 18 characters of ambient noise. Wayfair's wall-mounted shelves email had emoji spam; this has caps spam. Both feel un-premium for brands that charge $800 for a side table.
- **Hero links to the sale, not a specific product**: I'm staring at a beautiful velvet chair and there's no "Shop the Mello Chair" or whatever it is. The hero visual does all the work; the CTA squanders it by dumping me on a generic sale landing page.
- **"MORE FOR YOU" items are too small on mobile**: the product cards in that section are four-across in a cramped grid. Names and prices are barely legible. The intent is right, the execution is rushed.
- **Crate & Barrel and CrateKids cross-sell at the footer is irrelevant noise**: no purchase signals suggesting I need a kids' furniture brand. This is the wrong moment for it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Throttle to two CB2 emails per day, max**: one morning, one evening. Five sends before noon destroys the brand's inbox equity faster than any creative issue. This is the single highest-impact change.
- 2. **Name the hero product and link directly to its PDP**: the velvet chair in the hero is the actual selling moment. "The Mello Chair — now 40% off" beats "SHOP THE SALE" every time. Article did this right with their June 26 product-specific send.
- 3. **Swap the hero CTA copy from "SHOP THE SALE" to something product-anchored**: "Shop Seating" or "See What's Left in Furniture" at minimum.
- 4. **Promote "MORE FOR YOU" above the category grid**: the personalized section is the most relevant content in the email; burying it five scrolls down is backwards. Lead with what you know about me.
- 5. **Kill CrateKids cross-sell for non-family shoppers**: or replace with a "Design Services" or "Swatch & Sample" prompt that's actually useful to someone mid-renovation.
- 6. **Subject Alt A:** `"July 4th: 50% off + Warehouse up to 60% off"`
- 7. **Subject Alt B:** `"One day left — furniture, lighting, outdoor"`
- 8. **Preheader Alt A:** `"Shop the velvet chair before it's gone — plus warehouse clearance at 60% off"`
- 9. **Preheader Alt B:** `"Your saved items are in the sale. Free shipping, ends soon."`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

This is an unremoved ESP template placeholder (also misspelled: "IMPUT" vs "INPUT"). It is inert but signals the build process didn't strip dev comments before send.

**Issue 2 — Narrow mobile breakpoint likely misses many devices**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { .mobile { width:414px !important; } }
```
The 418 px ceiling targets iPhone SE (375 px) but will miss common Android viewports (360–412 px wide) that exceed 418 px. The fluid/responsive styles inside this block won't activate on those devices.

**Issue 3 — `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Disabling zoom is a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) violation and affects users with low vision.

**Issue 4 — Double forward slashes in font URLs**
```css
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
```
Three `@font-face` blocks use `//` in the path segment (e.g., `cb2.com//Styles/`). Most servers normalize this, but it is technically malformed and can fail on strict CDN/proxy configs.

**Issue 5 — Cross-domain font loading (cb2.com + crateandbarrel.com)**
The `HNW05` font family loads from `crateandbarrel.com` while branded CB2 fonts load from `cb2.com`. If either domain's CORS policy or CDN config changes, font fallback silently activates.

**Issue 6 — Aggressive global `*` reset**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; max-height:1000000px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly; line-height:100% }
```
Applying `line-height:100%` globally via the `*` selector overrides any element-specific line-height and can collapse multi-line text blocks in clients that don't have more-specific rules set.
