# Up to 40% off KitchenAid, Made In & more top kitchen brands!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-31**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so this is the second Crate & Barrel kitchen-specific email I've seen — different from yesterday's furniture push and the Crate&kids nursery spam that's been clogging my inbox all week. Teal KitchenAid stand mixer up top again, plus a whole wall of cookware, blenders, Vitamix, All-Clad — this is legitimately a kitchen catalog dump, not another crib disguised as a "sale."
- The problem is it just keeps going. I scrolled and scrolled and it never stopped — cookware, then bakeware, then a "Labor Day Event" section with furniture and bedding sneaking back in, then a Ninja Slush Twist toy, then a "we love these for you" grid, then TheDesignDesk pitch at the bottom. By the time I hit the fourth "Shop Now" button I'd already tuned out. None of this is renovation-relevant to me — I'm buying sofas and dining tables for two houses, not a $50-off blender.
- Verdict: I'd skim the top third for two seconds, notice the mixer, and archive. Not clicking, not forwarding — my wife would actually want this one (she's the cook), so if anything I'd forward it to her, not a friend.

## What's working

- Teal KitchenAid mixer as the hero is a strong, recognizable product shot — instant eye-catch.
- "Up to 40% off top kitchen brands" in the header banner is a clear, quantified offer right at the top.
- It's genuinely on-theme with the subject line for once — no bait-and-switch like the Crate&kids emails pretending to be something else.
- "Double Rewards" callout up top ties into the loyalty angle Crate & Barrel has been hammering all week (same beat as their 8/30 furniture sends).

## What's weak

- This email is exhausting — it just doesn't end. Kitchen turns into Labor Day furniture turns into a Ninja slushy machine turns into an app download pitch. Zero focus.
- Nothing here matches my actual project — I'm knee-deep in furniture and fixtures for two home renos, not stand mixers and bakeware.
- The design is visual clutter — dozens of near-identical product tiles with tiny price text, no breathing room, nothing telling my eye where to stop.
- By the time the Labor Day furniture section reappears mid-email, it feels like two different emails stapled together.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the email in half.** Kitchen deal, kitchen deal only — hold the furniture/Labor Day content for its own send. This scattergun approach is exactly why my inbox has five Crate-family emails a week.
- 2. **Lead harder with the discount math.** "Up to 40% off" is vague — give me the KitchenAid mixer's actual before/after price in the hero, the way Pottery Barn's Labor Day email did with its "Up to 70% off" banner that scored 8/10 with me.
- 3. **Kill the app-download and TheDesignDesk sections entirely** — by that point in the scroll I've already made my decision, and it just reads as filler.
- 4. **If you're going to cross-sell furniture in a kitchen email, badge it clearly** as a separate section instead of blending it into the scroll.
- **Subject Alt A:** `KitchenAid mixers up to 40% off — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Stock the kitchen: KitchenAid, Vitamix & All-Clad on sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus Double Rewards on every order through Labor Day.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your stand mixer upgrade just got 40% cheaper.`
