# DUMBBELLS ⚡ BLACK FRIDAY IN JULY ⚡ 24 HR  FLASH DEALS ⚡ 
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-26**
## Executive summary

- Seventeen. That's where the Wayfair counter lands now, because after "Sixteen" landed in my inbox with the exact same "BLACK FRIDAY IN JULY / ENDS TOMORROW" banner, here's another one with a subject line promising dumbbells and a body that delivers... flooring, a grill, a cutting board, a dehumidifier, and eventually a pool table. I opened this because the subject said "DUMBBELLS" and I'm mid-renovation on a home gym corner in the second house — and there is not one single dumbbell product, price, or link anywhere in this email. The closest thing is a guy mid-lunge on a deck in the hero row, which is not a product tile, it's stock photography.
- This is the same trick Wayfair pulled with "TRASH CANS" on 7/25 (no trash cans) and "KITCHEN MATS" on 7/25 (one buried tile). At this point I don't trust the subject line to mean anything — it's just whatever noun tested best in the last A/B round. Crate & Barrel's "dining room hacks" send actually opened on dining rooms. This opens on a bait-and-switch.
- Verdict: not clicking for dumbbells because there's nothing to click on. I might skim the mattress and rug tiles since I'm actually in the market, but this isn't getting forwarded to anyone — it's getting archived into the folder with the other fifteen.

## What's working

- "80% OFF / ENDS TOMORROW / EVERYTHING SHIPS FREE" banner up top is legible and does its job as a hero.
- Was/now pricing and star ratings on every tile — I can price-compare without a second click, which Pottery Barn's "On sale & ready to ship" send didn't bother with.
- The category price-band rail (bedding up to 65%, rugs up to 80%, mattresses up to 60%, curtains up to 70%) is genuinely useful shorthand for someone furnishing two houses at once.
- Wayfair Rewards 30% off first-year membership callout is a clean, specific loyalty hook — CB2 buries its perks program way further down.

## What's weak

- The subject says dumbbells. The email has zero dumbbells. That's the whole review right there.
- The "picked just for you" grid is a total grab-bag — flooring, patio storage, a grill, a cutting board, a dehumidifier — with no thread connecting any of it to me specifically.
- Fourth-plus near-identical "Black Friday in July / up to 80% off" framing this week alone (trash cans, trampolines, kitchen mats, now this). The urgency has zero credibility left.
- "Your Home, These Picks" at the bottom just re-runs categories already shown above it (rugs, kitchen mats, bedding sets) — it's the same email restating itself before the footer.
- No CTA copy anywhere says "Shop Fitness" or "Shop Dumbbells" — the only real button is a generic "shop all sales."

## Recommendations

- 1. Match the subject to the actual hero content, or put an actual dumbbell product tile at the top of the grid if you're going to promise it. This is the single biggest lift available — it's the difference between "relevant" and "clickbait."
- 2. Cut the grab-bag hero grid down to 4-6 tiles in one coherent theme (e.g., "home gym" or "outdoor living") instead of nine unrelated categories fighting for the same scroll.
- 3. Kill the redundant bottom rail or make it genuinely different products from the top grid — right now it's a rerun.
- 4. Give the loyalty banner (30% off Rewards) its own send or move it higher — it's a stronger hook than half the product tiles and it's getting buried mid-scroll.
