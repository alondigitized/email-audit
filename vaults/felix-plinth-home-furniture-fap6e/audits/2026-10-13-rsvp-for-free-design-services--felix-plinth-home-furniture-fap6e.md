# RSVP for FREE design services
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-13**
## Executive summary

- Finally, a Pottery Barn email that isn't the dark-green "Fall Sale: Up to 70% off" strip I've seen since 10/06. This one opens on a moody bedroom shot with "RSVP for Your Free Design Services" and a Design Crew badge. I'm renovating two houses at once, so free design help is the first offer from them in a week that matches my situation. I'd open this one.
- The body holds up. There are three styled room shots (a dark bedroom, a dining room with a chandelier, a cozy living room), each with a "Bring this look to life" link. After that comes a "Home for the Holidays" free design class series with dates, then a "Recommended just for you" product grid. It reads like a catalog. But the offer is vague. I never learn what the appointment is, how long it takes, or whether I leave with a plan or just a sales pitch. I'd click "Book a free appointment" to see, then probably close the tab when it asks for a store location.
- Would I forward it? Maybe to my partner, since we're both furnishing rooms. It's a save-to-Pinterest-folder email, not a buy-now email.

## What's working

- The "what / when / where" block under the headline tells me the format fast. It's virtual or in-store, and I pick the time.
- The room photography is moody and well styled. The dark-green and brown palette looks like a real renovation mood board, and that's what I save these emails for.
- "Book a free appointment" appears twice, in the hero and again mid-email, as a dark button on a light background. I can't miss it.
- The "Home for the Holidays" class series lists specific dates (Nov 8, Nov 15, Nov 22, Dec 6, Dec 13) and topics. That gives me concrete things to schedule.
- No sale banner on top. After the 10/12 trio ("Don't miss The Fall Sale," "Everything you need for Thanksgiving," "Fall deals UP TO 70% OFF"), this is a relief.

## What's weak

- "Choose a time—but spots go fast!" is soft urgency with no deadline and no count. I don't believe it.
- The three "Bring this look to life" links are small underlined text. On a phone those are poor tap targets.
- The "Recommended just for you" grid has product names but no prices and no sale flags. Most of it is extendable dining tables. If I already looked at one, I'd want to know whether it's still in the Fall Sale. It doesn't feel personal.
- The lower half piles on extras: an app download, a 10% rewards card pitch, Affirm, and a long block of legal text. That's four asks after the one I came for. The rewards card is a mismatch. I already buy through my highest-rewards card, and 10% back is decent but not enough to make me switch.
- The design service doesn't mention swatches, samples, or color matching. I'd want to know whether the designer brings fabric and finish samples. Shipping timelines for furniture aren't mentioned either.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put one concrete outcome in the hero, such as "Walk away with a room plan and a shoppable list in 45 minutes." Right now "styling tips, room refreshes, total makeovers, and more" could mean anything.
- 2. Add a real deadline or limited-slot cue, like "Appointments open through Nov 30" or "Only 12 slots left this week."
- 3. Spell out what the service includes: samples, color matching, whether it carries over to my second address, and whether designers will see my cart or saved items.
- 4. Make each "Bring this look to life" a full-width button, or link the products shown in the photos.
- 5. Cut the recommendations grid to a short row of items that actually relate to the rooms shown, with prices.
- 6. Move the rewards card, app and Affirm sections out of this send. They dilute the one ask.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is an image-sliced template (7 body slices and 18 footer slices, each wrapped in an opaque click-tracked link). It has a CSS defect, missing alt text on nearly all images, and two non-HTTPS images. The deliverability and unsubscribe-header checks are inconclusive because the relay didn't capture the headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All 46 tracking links (`https://click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=ABp7Im...`) were skipped by the HTTP probe. Their destinations, redirect chains and status codes are **unverified**.
- Third-party ad pixels are embedded:
  - `https://secure.leadback.advertising.com/adcedge/lb?site=695501&srvc=1&betr=30802=711754[8760]`
  - `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/L21rdC84NjEvY2lkLzE3NDc3ODk3MzMvdC8y/cat/1`
  - Neither could be probed, so I can't confirm they still resolve. The `d.turn.com` pixel looks like a legacy retargeting beacon.
- `https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` is a cross-brand asset dependency. If West Elm retires that path, this image breaks.
- A commented-out header block, including a credit-card link with `external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner`, still ships in the delivered source. It is inert but adds weight and leaks internal parameter names.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Invalid CSS in the second `<style>` block.** PHP-style lines were pasted into it:
  ```
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: no-store, ...");
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
  a[x-apple-data-detectors] { ... }
  ```
  Under CSS error recovery, this junk is likely parsed as part of the next rule's selector. That would drop the `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` rule, which is meant to stop Apple auto-linking from restyling text.
- **Missing alt text.** The QA run flags about 35 images. Examples:
  - Body slices `slices_01`–`slices_07.jpg` in `/1013_TUE_AM2_DC_MoodBoard/`. They use `alt=""` and sit inside `<a>` links, so those links have no accessible name.
  - Product images such as `banks-extendable-dining-table-76-128-1-l.jpg` and `remmy-swivel-chair-1-l.jpg`.
  - Footer `slices_01`–`slices_18.jpg` in `/Email-Footer/`.
  - If the body copy is baked into these slices, the email is unreadable with images blocked or in a screen reader. I can't confirm that from the truncated source.
- **Non-HTTPS images**, which can be blocked or trigger mixed-content warnings:
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- **Tracking pixels without alt or dimensions.** The `svopn.potterybarn.com`, `leadback` and `turn.com` pixels, and the `coreemail.html` `<img>`, have no alt. This is minor, but they should use `alt="" width="1" height="1"`.
- **Responsive rules appear unused.** The media query targets `table[class="wrapper"]` and `img[class="photo"]`. In the visible source the tables are fixed `width="650"` with no `class` attribute, and the images have no `class="photo"`. The 320px fallback will likely never apply, so the layout won't scale on mobile. The source is truncated, so classes later in the file are unchecked.
- **Structure.** There is no `lang` attribute on `<html>` and no `<title>`. Content (the ampScript comments) precedes the DOCTYPE.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tokens are visible. The ampScript slot at the top rendered empty.
- The recipient address `lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` appears in plaintext query strings of image requests:
  - `https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=...`
  - `https://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=...`
  - This exposes PII in third-party and server logs. The `coreemail.html` request is also an HTML page loaded as an image, so it can't render and only works as a beacon.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found. The QA tool notes that the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is **inconclusive**, not a confirmed violation. Verify against the raw message at the source.
- `Authentication-Results` was not found, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment are unknown. The sender is `PotteryBarn@e.potterybarn.com`.
- The visible HTML doesn't show the physical address or the unsubscribe link. They are probably in the footer slices, which have no alt text. If so, they are image-only and inaccessible with images off. This needs a check of the untruncated source.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- No UTM parameters are visible. Every CTA is an opaque `click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=` token, and none were resolved, so landing URLs, UTM tagging and subject-to-landing alignment (an "RSVP for FREE design services" page) are **unverifiable** from this data.
- The only cleartext site URLs are in the dead commented block and the product image paths.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Remove the stray `header(...)` lines from the `<style>` block so the Apple data-detector rule parses.
2. Add descriptive alt text to the hero, product and footer slices. Set `alt=""` plus `width="1" height="1"` on tracking pixels.
3. Change the two `http://edm.potterybarn.com/...` images to `https://`.
4. Add `class="wrapper"` and `class="photo"` to the actual tables and images, or rewrite the media queries to match the real markup.
5. Add `lang`, a `<title>`, and move the DOCTYPE to the top.
6. Remove commented-out code and the cross-brand `edm.westelm.com` spacer.
7. Stop putting the raw recipient address in pixel query strings. Use an opaque ID instead.
8. Re-run the QA against the raw MIME message to confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results`. Resolve the tracking links in a browser-based pass to check UTMs and landing pages.
