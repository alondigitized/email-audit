## Executive summary

- Eight. I think this is eight Wayfair emails in roughly five days, and I say "I think" because I stopped counting after the low-pile rug one and just started archiving. So when "KITCHEN MATS under $30" landed again — the exact same subject line they ran on May 18th, two days ago, word for word — my first reaction wasn't "oh interesting," it was "did this get stuck in a send queue somewhere?" I genuinely wondered if I was looking at a re-delivery of a failed batch.
- I opened it anyway because I'm mid-renovation on two kitchens simultaneously and I have a legitimate reason to care about kitchen mats. What I found inside had essentially nothing to do with kitchen mats. The hero is a Wayfair Rewards pitch. Below that: personalized furniture recs, a round outdoor wicker table (nice, but not what I came for), and a grid of sofas and chairs labeled "picked just for you." - I'm marking this as read and not clicking. I'm also starting to think about the unsubscribe link. Compared to the Crate & Barrel send I reviewed a few days ago — which walked in with a concrete "up to 30% off sofas + 2x rewards" and delivered exactly that inside — this is a different class of email program. That one respected my attention. This one is recycling subject lines and delivering unrelated content.

## What's working

- The **rewards section** ("get $1 rewards... on every item, always!") is cleanly designed and prominent — Wayfair's loyalty program is actually a decent value prop for a repeat furniture buyer like me.
- **"People love this table"** is a smart social-proof frame on a single hero product. If I were in the market for an outdoor table, that round wicker piece is exactly the kind of thing I'd click.
- **No render bugs** — email renders clean, images load, text is legible without zooming. At least the production machine works.
- The **"we think you'd ♥ these" / "picked just for you" sectioning** signals personalization, which I appreciate conceptually even if execution is shaky here.

## What's weak

- **Recycled subject line, two days later.** "KITCHEN MATS under $30 ✨" ran on May 18th. Running it again on May 20th, unchanged, tells me no one is minding the store.
- **Subject-body mismatch is severe.** I came in expecting kitchen mats. The email is outdoor furniture recs and a rewards banner. This is a bait-and-switch in effect, even if unintentional.
- **No kitchen mats visible anywhere in the email body.** Not one. Not even a small callout. If the subject is the hook, the body should at least acknowledge it.
- **Eight emails in five days.** Article did their Memorial Day blast and moved on. Pottery Barn sent fourteen and I nearly unsubscribed. Wayfair is trending Pottery Barn, not Article.
- **"Outdoor style goals" at the bottom** is a generic browse-prompt I've seen in every Wayfair email this week. It's wallpaper at this point.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Stop recycling subject lines within a 48-hour window.** This is a CRM hygiene issue — the send scheduler should flag duplicate subjects before they go out.
- 2. **Make the email body match the subject.** If you say kitchen mats under $30, open with kitchen mats under $30. Put a 3x2 grid of mats above the fold. Then upsell me into the recs carousel below.
- 3. **Collapse the rewards pitch into a footer footnote.** It doesn't need to be the hero. Crate & Barrel surfaces their 2x rewards as a line item *inside* the product offer — that's the right structure.
- 4. **Cut cadence.** I'm at unsubscribe risk. One well-targeted email beats three scatter-shot sends.
- 5. **"People love this table" deserves more screen.** That section — one product, social proof — is the most effective unit in this email. It should be the hero, not buried mid-scroll.
