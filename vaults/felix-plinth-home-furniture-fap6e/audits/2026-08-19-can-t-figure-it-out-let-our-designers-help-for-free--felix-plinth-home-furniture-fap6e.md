# Can’t figure it out? Let our designers help for FREE
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-19**
## Executive summary

- This is a Crate & Kids email — nursery furniture, backpacks, car seats, baby gear — and I don't have kids, so it's basically dead on arrival for me. It's the exact same "TheDesignDesk free interior design service" pitch Crate & Barrel and CB2 have both run at me this month, just re-skinned for cribs and toddler backpacks instead of sofas and consoles.
- The free-design-consultation hook itself isn't a bad idea — Pottery Barn's design service, CB2's Paige DeSorbo soft-pitch, this is clearly a company-wide playbook right now — but burying it under nursery makeovers, JanSport backpack bundles, and a $5,000 sweepstakes means there's nothing here for a guy renovating two houses' worth of adult living spaces. I scrolled the whole thing out of curiosity about the design service and found zero furniture I'd actually buy.
- Not clicking, not forwarding — unless I suddenly have a baby on the way, this is an instant "mark as read, delete."

## What's working

- The hero is clean — clear headline ("any project, any budget, we're here to help — and it's 100% free"), one obvious CTA button, no clutter.
- "3 ways to meet" (at home / online / in-store) answers the logistics question immediately, which is more than most of these design-service emails bother doing.
- The nursery makeover thumbnails with real names (Nicole Boyden's nursery, Zenia Roberts' nursery) read as legit before/after proof, similar to how Crate & Barrel's bedroom-makeover send worked for me earlier this week.
- Real product grid at the bottom with visible prices (backpack $59, dresser $499, car seat $329.99) — at least it's not the ghost-image problem I hit with Wayfair's "New score alert: Bookcases" email.

## What's weak

- Wrong brand entirely for my inbox — I'm not on a Crate & Kids list by choice for baby gear, this is a nursery/kids email, not home renovation.
- Backpacks, car seats, and a "win $5,000 for your kid's new space" sweepstakes have nothing to do with a 36-year-old renovating a primary and second home.
- It's the third or fourth "book a free designer" pitch I've gotten from the Crate & Barrel family this week (C&B's own version, CB2's Paige DeSorbo one) — total fatigue, and this one adds nothing new to that pitch except kid-sized furniture.
- The "new card" teaser near the bottom (Crate & Barrel credit card upgrade) feels like a bolted-on afterthought with zero context on what's actually better about it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this out of my inbox entirely — I've never bought kids' furniture from this brand and nothing in my purchase history should trigger a Crate & Kids send. This is a targeting/list-hygiene fix, not a creative one.
- 2. If Crate & Kids must email general C&B subscribers, lead with something adult-adjacent — nursery-adjacent home office or shared-space content — instead of assuming everyone getting this has a baby.
- 3. Drop or de-emphasize the sweepstakes; it reads like filler competing with the actual design-service CTA for attention.
- 4. Give the design service one unifying story across the whole C&B family of brands instead of repeating the same "book free 30-min call" pitch separately from Crate & Barrel, CB2, and now Crate & Kids in the same week — right now it just reads as noise multiplied by three.
