# Our TOP 4th of July Sale picks (up to 70% off)
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-29**
## Executive summary

- Forty-eight. That's a rough Pottery Barn count now, and this one drops June 29 with a subject that promises something I've been waiting for: editorial curation. "Our top picks" implies someone actually chose something. What I get when I open it is the same Extra-20%-off hero that has lived at the top of every Pottery Barn email since June 26, followed by four category tiles with discount ranges. That's not picks. That's a sitemap with a seasonal banner.
- To be fair, there's one genuinely new module buried in the scroll: Open Box Outlet deals with an extra 20% stack. As someone who runs rewards math before every checkout and would absolutely take an open-box piece if it saves me real money on a renovation, that stopped me. It's the most differentiated content this brand has sent me all week. It's also in the fourth position, behind three things I've already seen.
- Mark as read. I'm not clicking from cold — I've been to the sale page twice this week. If the Open Box callout had been the hero, I'd have gone straight to that landing page.

## What's working

- **Category-specific % breakdowns** — "Up to 40% off Sofas & Chairs" vs "Up to 70% off Pillows & Decor" gives me actual budget intel for two renovations in progress. This is how Article structures their sale emails and it works for me every time.
- **Open Box Outlet module** — first appearance of this offer in the whole June 26–29 drum run. Genuinely differentiated, genuinely relevant to a rewards-maximizer who buys on value.
- **"Recommended Just For You" product grid** — personalization signal acknowledged. CB2's WAREHOUSE SALE email (which I scored a 9) leaned into this same pattern and it's the right instinct; I'd rather see products pulled from my browse history than another generic tile.
- **Key Rewards Mastercard section** — I run the math on every major purchase. Seeing the card reminder in the email is a legitimate nudge.
- **Clean render, no layout breaks** — floor-level execution, but it's the floor.

## What's weak

- **Same hero for the fifth consecutive send** — "Extra 20% off" is a blanket discount, not a pick. The subject promises editorial; the hero delivers a campaign tag I've now seen so many times it reads as wallpaper.
- **"Top picks" promise is unkept** — Crate & Barrel's "Up to 50% off furniture = the BEST time to shop" email actually surfaced specific furniture pieces. This email shows category tiles. Those are not the same thing.
- **Open Box is buried** — the one content block that could earn a click is sitting in the third module. On mobile I'm scrolling past it in two seconds if I don't have a specific reason to slow down.
- **Every CTA says "SHOP NOW"** — not a single button tells me what I'm shopping into. After five nearly identical sends, "SHOP NOW" into a category page is not a strong pull.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Promote Open Box Outlet to the sub-hero or module one.** It's the differentiating offer in this entire email. Give it the position it's earned.
- 2. **Actually deliver on "top picks."** Show 3–4 specific products with before/after pricing — "The Farmhouse Sectional. Was $3,200. Now $1,920 with extra 20%." That's a pick. A tile grid is not.
- 3. **Swap generic CTAs for destination-specific copy** — "Shop Open Box," "Explore Outdoor," "Browse Sofas on Sale." One extra word per button, three times the pull.
- 4. **Retire "Extra 20% off" as a hero for any remaining sends in this sale window.** It has now appeared at the top of every email from this address since June 26. It is invisible.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Open Box clearance: stack an extra 20% on Pottery Barn's sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The 4 sale categories worth knowing — and one worth acting on now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sofas to 40% off, outdoor to 60% — plus Open Box deals with an extra stack`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `July 4th top picks — actual products, not just a tile grid`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

```
https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB...
```
This is the "Apply & Buy" / Key Rewards member banner CTA. Recipients clicking it hit a dead end. The `external_id=PB_header_nonmember` parameter suggests this is a non-member variant — possible that the campaign link was generated for a different segment or the redirect target expired.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP assets (mixed content — 5 instances):**

| Asset | URL |
|---|---|
| Spacer GIF (West Elm) | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` — **also 403** |

| Open pixel (svopn) | `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597...` |

Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook on secure connections will block HTTP image sources. The svopn pixel is also the sender's open-tracking beacon — if blocked, open rates will be undercounted.

**Missing alt text — 46 images** have no `alt` attribute, including all 13 main content slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_13.jpg`). With images off (common in B2B/Outlook environments), the email renders as blank. The product images (curtain rod, sheet set, quilt, dining table, towel, rug) also lack alt text, eliminating fallback product identification.

**CSS artifact in `<style>` block:**
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache...");
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
PHP `header()` calls were accidentally pasted into a `<style>` block. These are ignored by email clients but signal a template assembly error — the wrong code fragment was included.

**`<title>` tag is empty.** Minor, but screen readers and some clients use it.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post headers** not captured by QA relay. This is likely a relay artifact (AgentMail not forwarding these headers) rather than absence at the MTA level — Pottery Barn's ESP (Salesforce Marketing Cloud, inferred from `e.potterybarn.com` envelope) typically emits these. Confirm by inspecting raw headers directly; Gmail's "Show original" is the fastest check. If genuinely absent, one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058 / Google/Yahoo 2024 requirement) fails.

**CAN-SPAM physical address** — not verifiable from the truncated HTML but expected in footer; no flag raised without evidence of absence.
