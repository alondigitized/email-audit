# ON NOW: Up to 60% off decor, bedding & more!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-04**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is actually the real Crate & Barrel — not Crate & Kids — and it shows the second the hero loads: a proper living room, tufted couch, walnut console, no toddler quilt in sight. Good. But this is also, by my count, the eighth Crate & Barrel family email in my inbox today, and this one leads with the exact same "Double Rewards is on / up to 60% off / The Labor Day Event" framing I already saw this morning. The category grid — lighting, bedding, rugs, wall decor, outdoor — is comprehensive to the point of being exhausting, and by the time I hit the third "shop the Labor Day Event" button I've mentally checked out.
- The category grid itself is well-shot — real product photography, consistent styling, nothing looks stock or lorem-ipsum broken. But there's zero personalization: no "welcome back," no reference to what I've browsed or saved, nothing that says they know I'm mid-renovation on two houses. It's a mass blast dressed up as a lifestyle catalog.
- I'm not clicking this one. I already clicked through (mentally) on this morning's send with the same offer, and stacking a near-identical Labor Day banner on top of it just reads as noise. Marking as read, not forwarding — nothing here is surprising enough to send to my wife who's doing the actual sourcing for the lake house.

## What's working

- Real Crate & Barrel branding top to bottom — no Crate&Kids logo bait-and-switch like the last three emails in this thread today.
- Hero shot is an actual furnished room (leather sofa, layered rugs, walnut console) — matches my renovation-brain aesthetic far better than CB2's more minimalist styling or Wayfair's cluttered scroll grids.
- "Double Rewards is on" + "up to 60% off" both visible above the fold — concrete offer, no hunting for it.
- The Design Desk section (free design consultation, $500 sweepstakes) is genuinely relevant to someone renovating two homes at once — that's a beat none of Wayfair or Pottery Barn's emails hit today.
- Category grid photography is clean and consistent — no broken images, no placeholder text.

## What's weak

- Eighth email from this brand family today — I'm fatigued before I even open it, and the offer ("60% off," "Double Rewards") is copy-pasted from this morning's send almost verbatim.
- No personalization anywhere — no name, no "items you viewed," nothing that treats me like a known Rewards member vs. a cold subscriber.
- The category grid (11+ tiles: lighting, bedding, rugs, wall decor, outdoor, furniture sale, kids furniture again buried in there) is a kitchen-sink dump, the same complaint I had about Wayfair's clearance email — everything, so nothing stands out.
- "New! Instant Fall Vibes" section switches tone mid-email and dilutes the Labor Day urgency I was just shown at the top.
- Multiple redundant CTAs ("Shop Decor & Textile Sale," "Shop the Labor Day Event," "Shop Now," "Shop New Decor") — none of them tells me which one actually has the best deal on what I care about (lighting/rugs for the reno).

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress send frequency** — I've gotten eight C&B-family emails today. Collapse Crate & Barrel, Crate & Kids, and CB2 into one daily digest or at minimum cap at 2/day per subscriber. This is the single biggest lever; no copy fix beats fatigue.
- 2. **Lead with one hero category tied to my browsing history**, not a wall of 11 tiles — if I've been looking at lighting and rugs, put those first and cut the rest to a "shop more" link.
- 3. **Move The Design Desk section up**, right under the hero — for a two-house renovator that's more relevant than another rug grid, and no competitor email today (Article, CB2, Wayfair, Pottery Barn) offered a real human design consult.
- 4. **Differentiate this send from this morning's** — same offer, so at minimum vary the subject to signal something new (e.g., a specific category spotlight) instead of repeating "up to 60% off."
