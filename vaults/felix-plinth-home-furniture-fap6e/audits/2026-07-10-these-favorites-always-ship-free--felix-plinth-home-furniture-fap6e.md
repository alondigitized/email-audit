# These favorites ALWAYS ship free →
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- Sixteen. I updated my tally — sixteen emails from the Crate & Barrel family of brands since July 8th. Two and a half days. And this one is the sub-brand curveball: Crate & Kids. I don't have young children. My renovation wishlist contains zero backpacks, zero nursery cribs, zero kids' storage ottomans. So when this landed, there was a genuine moment of "did I accidentally opt into something?" followed by the sinking realization that I'm probably just in a master C&B subscriber list getting the full brand portfolio carpet-bombed in sequence.
- That said — "these favorites ALWAYS ship free" is technically a different message than the promo drumbeat I've been living inside. "Always" means permanent policy, not a 48-hour event window. That's a real distinction worth something. But after fifteen emails about free shipping in rotating permutations, my brain has no processing capacity left to notice the nuance. The signal is dead on arrival. I watched the same arc happen in reverse when Article dropped their "new arrivals" reset after four July 4th sale emails — that pivot landed at an 8 because the cadence break was legible. Here, the cadence break is invisible under the noise.
- I'm marking this read. Not clicking, not forwarding. If Pottery Barn's Design Services email got an 8 from me last week by doing exactly one thing differently — offering something that wasn't a sale — Crate & Kids had the same opportunity and sent me a yellow backpack.

## What's working

- "ALWAYS ships free" is a genuine message differentiation from a promotional event — it's a permanent policy claim, and the subject lands it clearly without screaming.
- The yellow backpack hero is a clean, well-composed shot — strong color, good scale, uncluttered.
- Layout renders without issues — no broken images, no text blowouts, tight grid execution all the way down.
- The Crate & Kids sub-brand is labeled clearly in the sender line, so I know exactly which arm of the house sent this.
- The mid-email percentage-off section adds a second value layer beyond shipping — that's smart stacking even if it's below the fold.

## What's weak

- Complete category miss. I'm renovating two homes; the brand has my purchase history and still sent kids' backpacks and nursery furniture. Wayfair's dining bowl email at least hit me in a category I'm actively furnishing.
- Email sixteen from the same brand family in under three days. This is cadence malpractice, not a nuance problem. CB2 x Todd Snyder made me pause at email seventeen in the CB2 stream partly because the collab felt curated — this feels like the opposite.
- "ALWAYS" in all-caps in the subject reads strained, especially after thirteen recent sends where emphasis was earned by nothing.
- The percentage-off offer mid-email would have led better than free shipping — that's the harder value proposition, and it's buried below a hero I've seen six variations of this week.
- Zero personalization. Treated like a name on a list, not a customer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress Crate & Kids for non-parent segments.** If I don't have kids' purchase signals, this sub-brand email should fold into a monthly digest or skip me entirely. The targeting cost of one wrong send is higher than the revenue upside of blasting the full list.
- 2. **Lead with the %off, not the shipping.** Free shipping is table stakes across this whole brand family now. A concrete discount is the actual hook; it shouldn't be mid-scroll.
- 3. **Kill the cadence before you kill the list.** This is email sixteen in 2.5 days. Article proved a cadence break into new arrivals can reset goodwill after a sale marathon. C&B needs to do the same — one well-timed editorial send is worth more than six promotional hammers.
- 4. **Make "always" do work.** If these really are permanently free-shipping SKUs, surface a curated list of 3-5 specific items with photos — let me see what "these favorites" actually are rather than just the hero product.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `These aren't part of the sale. They've never needed one.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Brand/title mismatch**
`<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` — sender identity is "Crate & Kids." Gmail and some preview panes surface `<title>` as the tab/preview label. This creates a brand inconsistency signal that can depress open rates or trigger spam classifiers.

**[MEDIUM] Conflicting image max-width breakpoints**
Two `min-width` media queries set conflicting `img` constraints:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
The 640px rule fires first; the 768px rule overrides it at ≥768px, clamping images to 600px on desktop while allowing 640px in the 640–767px range. The intent appears reversed — desktop should be the wider constraint, not narrower. Likely causes right-side bleed or unexpected image shrinkage on standard desktop widths.

**[MEDIUM] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 failure. Accessible email best practice requires removing `maximum-scale=1` or setting it to a value ≥ 2.

**[MEDIUM] Global text-size-adjust disabled**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applied to every element. Prevents OS-level font scaling for low-vision users. Should be scoped to `body` only and set to `100%`, not `none`.

**[LOW] Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
Four instances declared:
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
The omnibus declaration on line 1 makes the subsequent three redundant. No functional breakage, but indicates template merge error from a partial and a base layout both contributing `<head>` content.
