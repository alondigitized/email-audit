# DESKS 🤩 UP TO 70% OFF IS YOURS 🫵
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- Forty-seven. That's the Wayfair count as of this morning, and I'm leading with it because this is the **third Wayfair email today**. The HOT SUMMER SALE announcement hit me at #46, I scored it a 7 and flagged the cadence risk. Then came the kitchen mats at #45 — that was a 5. And now I'm looking at this. Same HOT SUMMER SALE banner across the top, same campaign, different subject. Wayfair didn't send me a desks email. They cut a desk-shaped window into the same creative and called it personalization.
- Here's the thing though: the subject line *actually worked on me*. DESKS plus 70% off is specific, and I'm genuinely in the market — two properties, both mid-renovation, home office situations unresolved at both. So I opened. And then the hero is the same HOT SUMMER SALE splash from this morning. Desks are one row in a grid that also includes wall art, glassware, a blue cast iron pot, and what looks like bathroom faucets. The "picked just for you" copy sitting under the 70% off number is doing a lot of heavy lifting for very little payload.
- I won't click. Not through this email. I might pull up Wayfair.com directly and run the desk filter myself, but I'm not giving this send the click credit. Mark as read.

## What's working

- DESKS + 70% off in the subject is real specificity — specific enough to get an open from a guy who's actively shopping for home office furniture for two spaces.
- The 70% off hero is visible above the fold with no scrolling required — the eye goes straight to the discount before anything else.
- Free & easy delivery over $35 bar is always the first thing I check on furniture sends. It's there. Good.
- Wayfair Rewards is visible in the footer — I use the points, and seeing it there is a low-key trust signal.
- Clean render, no visible broken images or layout chaos — which, after looking at a CB2 "SO EARTHY" email that was more vibes than product, I appreciate the functional execution.

## What's weak

- Subject promised desks; body delivered the HOT SUMMER SALE again. The home office furniture section exists but it's buried below glassware and kitchen items. That's bait-and-switch in practice even if not in intent.
- This is the third Wayfair email today, and this banner — the HOT SUMMER SALE header — is visually identical to what I saw in email #46 this morning. I noticed immediately.
- No desk dimensions, no style filter cue, no "fits a 10×12 office" callout anywhere above the fold. Article's June 9 outdoor send linked to a single hero product with real specs. Wayfair gives me a thumbnail grid.
- "IS YOURS 🫵" in the subject is gimmicky. The pointing finger emoji reads aggressive, not personal. Article doesn't do this.
- The product scatter — faucets, glassware, rugs, cookware — makes "picked just for you" feel like a lie the algorithm told the copywriter.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make desks the actual hero.** If the subject promises desks, I should land above the fold on two or three desk picks with a "Shop Desks" button — not the recycled HOT SUMMER SALE banner from this morning's send. Pottery Barn's Quick-Ship furniture email (#11 from me, scored 7) gets this right — subject and hero are the same conversation.
- 2. **Suppress the third same-day send.** Cadence suppression logic should be table stakes at Wayfair's volume. The kitchen mats email was already a stretch on June 11; three emails in a day is a trust tax that compounds across the list.
- 3. **Add one desk spec line.** Even "20+ desks under 48 inches wide — ideal for spare rooms" above the product grid would convert me faster than a thumbnail. This is the context I make decisions on.
- 4. **Drop the 🫵 emoji.** The 🤩 is doing the enthusiasm job fine. The pointing finger is gimmicky and slightly infantilizing for a millennial who's spent three years refinishing hardwood floors on his own.
- 5. **Tie the seasonal banner to the category** instead of blanket-running HOT SUMMER SALE for every subcategory email. "Your Summer Home Office Upgrade — Up to 70% Off" would feel like a different email from this morning's.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

**3a. `word-break: break-all` on `.ProductRec-name` (mobile)**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) {
  .ProductRec-name { word-break: break-all; ... }
}
```
`break-all` splits at any character, including mid-word. This can produce visually broken product names (e.g., "Mid-Centu\nry Desk"). The correct value for English content is `overflow-wrap: break-word` or `word-break: break-word`.

**3b. Fixed pixel heights on mobile product cells**
```css
.ProductRec-name { height: 48px !important }
.ProductRec-price { height: 40px; ... }
.ProductRec-salesCopy { height: 28px; ... }
```
Hard-coded heights clip dynamic content. If product names or prices exceed the allotted box (e.g., long names at 13px, discount copy wrapping), text is hidden with no ellipsis — a silent truncation failure.

**3c. No `alt` attributes visible**
Image tags are outside the visible source window. Cannot confirm alt text presence. Flag for manual check — CAN-SPAM and accessibility best practice require descriptive `alt` on promotional images.

**3d. XHTML 1.0 Transitional DOCTYPE**
```html
<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD XHTML 1.0 Transitional//EN" ...>
```
This is industry-standard for email and expected by Outlook. Not an issue.

**3e. Gmail / Outlook client hacks — correctly applied**
- `u ~ div { min-width: 100vw }` — Gmail app dark mode fix, correct usage.
- `u + .Gmail .u-P--5` — Gmail-specific padding override, correct usage.
- `body[data-outlook-cycle]` — New Outlook stacking fix, correct usage.
