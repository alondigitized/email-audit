# Sale on sale 🎉 EXTRA 20% OFF select styles
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- Fifty. That's my rough Pottery Barn email count this month, and this one lands June 30 — a day after I already reviewed two separate Pottery Barn sends running the same "Extra 20% off" hook. The subject even tells on itself: "Sale on sale." Which is the brand admitting it's marking down items that are already marked down, which — fine, I respect the honesty — but the creative makes zero effort to explain *why* that's exciting. I open it and get the same hero I've seen for three days straight: big "Extra 20% off," category nav grid, some recommended bedding tiles I didn't ask for.
- The one thing that actually catches my eye is buried below the fold: the Open Box Outlet Deals block. "Save big on returned items at unbelievable prices" is a genuinely different value prop from the blanket percentage-off playbook. That's the kind of thing I'd forward to my contractor who's outfitting the second house on a tight budget. But it's positioned like an afterthought — fourth element in, below the category nav, below the "Recommended Just for You" tiles that pushed bedroom linens at me again.
- Would I click? Grudgingly, on the Open Box block. Would I forward it? Only if I stripped everything but that one section. Would I mark it read without acting? Honestly, that's the most likely outcome.

## What's working

- **The "Sale on sale" frame is at least honest** — no hiding that this is a clearance-within-a-clearance; that's more straightforward than Wayfair's vague "LOW-MAINTENANCE RUGS" teaser from June 28.
- **Open Box Outlet Deals is the only differentiated offer in the email** — returned items at outlet prices is a real reason to click that I haven't seen in the prior four Pottery Barn sends this week.
- **Category nav grid is useful once I'm in** — FURNITURE, OUTDOOR, RUGS, LIGHTING all visible without scrolling; easy to route myself if I actually want to shop.
- **Pottery Barn Key rewards visible** — I'm a cardholder, so seeing the 10% back reminder is a real reinforcement even if it's buried.
- **No broken images, no layout collapse** — the render is clean; everything loaded.

## What's weak

- **The hero is copy-pasted from yesterday** — the June 29 "Don't forget your EXTRA 20% OFF" send ran this identical headline. I didn't forget. I got the email 24 hours ago. Repeating the same creative on consecutive days treats me like I have amnesia.
- **"Recommended Just for You" pushed bedding again** — I'm furnishing two entire houses; these tiles keep surfacing duvet covers and pillowcases. I'm not complaining about pillows, but I've been clicking sofas and outdoor furniture. The personalization engine is not listening.
- **No deadline anywhere I can see** — "Sale on sale" implies urgency but there's no date, no "ends Sunday," no countdown. CB2's "SALE OF THE SUMMER" send at least felt like a bounded event; this just floats.
- **Too many sections fighting for priority** — Open Box Outlet, Recommended, New Arrivals, App Download, Affirm, Pottery Barn Key. I'm renovating two homes and I want to make decisions, not scroll a catalog.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Open Box Outlet Deals** — that's the only new news in this email. Make it the hero, not a footnote. "Returned furniture. Unbelievable prices. Yours now." is a more compelling hook for someone furnishing two properties than the fourth consecutive "Extra 20% off."
- 2. **Kill the repeat hero creative** — if you sent the same offer yesterday, you need a different angle today: a specific product story, a room vignette, a category the prior email didn't touch. Article pivoted creative between sends; Pottery Barn needs to learn that trick.
- 3. **Add a hard deadline to the offer** — "Ends July 4" or "72 hours only" would convert the casual browser. Without a deadline, "Extra 20% off" feels like it'll be there next week (because it usually is).
- 4. **Retrain the personalization tiles** — I've clicked sofas and outdoor lighting. Push me furniture, not pillowcases.
- 5. **Collapse the lower half** — App download, Affirm, and loyalty can all live in a slim footer row. Stop giving them full sections.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Sale on Sale / Extra 20% Off" (2026-07-01)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Two links return 403 at send time (one a credit card CTA, one a cross-brand asset leak from West Elm), and the HTML template contains non-functional PHP `header()` calls embedded inside a `<style>` block — evidence of a broken template pipeline. Forty-plus images carry no alt text and three assets are served over HTTP.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**BROKEN — Credit Card CTA (403)**
`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY…`
The Apply & Buy / Key Rewards banner link is returning 403. If this is the credit card acquisition CTA it is completely non-functional at send time.

**BROKEN — Cross-brand spacer asset (403 + HTTP)**
`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
A West Elm CDN asset is embedded in a Pottery Barn email. It is both broken (403) and served over plain HTTP. This indicates a shared template was deployed without substituting brand-specific asset paths.

**Tracking coverage**
24 click links on `click.e.potterybarn.com` were skipped by the probe (expected for redirect-based tracking). No issues confirmed or ruled out on those links.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP code inside `<style>` block (template defect)**
The following lines appear verbatim inside a `<style>` tag in `<head>`:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are PHP function calls. They are syntactically invalid CSS and will be ignored by all clients, but they are a hard signal that a PHP image-serving snippet was copy-pasted directly into the HTML template without sanitization. The template pipeline is broken at the source.

**Three HTTP image sources (mixed-content)**
Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail block or proxy HTTP resources. All three will degrade or silently drop these images:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` (also 403)
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=…` (open pixel — tracking loss on HTTPS-enforcing clients)

**Missing alt text — 44 images**
Every content slice (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_13.jpg` in body, full footer slice set, all product images, both tracking pixels) has no `alt` attribute. Subscribers with images off see a wall of broken image icons with no context. Also a WCAG 2.1 failure for decorative-vs-informative image distinction.

**Empty `<title>`**
`<title></title>` — some clients render this in the tab or preview; empty is a minor but unnecessary gap.

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta**
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Low real-world impact but worth noting for accessibility posture.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header — unconfirmed**
QA probe did not capture a `List-Unsubscribe` header. This may be an AgentMail relay artifact, but if the header is genuinely absent, Gmail and Yahoo will suppress the native unsubscribe UI, increasing spam complaint risk under Gmail's 2024 bulk sender requirements.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) — not found**
One-click unsubscribe is not confirmed. Required for bulk senders sending >5k/day to Gmail. Absence puts this send at risk of inbox demotion.

**Authentication-Results — not captured**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown via the relay. Sending domain is `e.potterybarn.com`; authentication posture cannot be verified from available headers.

**CAN-SPAM physical address**
Not verifiable from the truncated source, but the footer structure is present. Assumed compliant unless full source review shows otherwise.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />`**
This suppresses the HTTP Referer header on all clicks originating from the email. If any destination URLs rely on referrer for attribution (rather than UTM params), they will attribute as direct. UTM params in the click links should override this for GA4, but the meta tag creates unnecessary ambiguity.

**Plain-text body is 70% URLs (9,857 of 14,033 chars)**
The plain-text alternative is essentially a raw URL dump. Spam filters penalize this ratio. Subscribers on plain-text clients (some corporate mail systems) receive an unusable experience. No readable content, offer summary, or prose is present.

**Cross-brand CDN contamination**
The West Elm spacer asset (`edm.westelm.com`) appearing in a Pottery Barn email suggests shared template ancestry. If destination links similarly inherited West Elm parameters, UTM attribution would be misrouted — not confirmed from available source but worth auditing the full link set.
