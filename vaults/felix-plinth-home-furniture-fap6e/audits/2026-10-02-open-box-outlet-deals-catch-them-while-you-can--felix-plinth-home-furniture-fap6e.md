# Open Box Outlet Deals—catch them while you can!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-02**
## Executive summary

- Pottery Barn again. By my count this is the eleventh or twelfth send since 9/29, and my brain has started filing them as wallpaper. The subject promises outlet deals. The hero is a flat red box that says "Open Box Outlet Deals" and "Save big," with no number anywhere. "Save big" isn't an offer. I'm renovating two houses and I'd happily buy a returned sofa at 40% off, but nothing here tells me whether that's on the table.
- The rest of the email is the usual Pottery Barn grab bag. There's a Design Crew banner, a "Recommended Just For You" grid, a Christmas Shop block in October, an app ad, a credit card ad and an Affirm ad. None of the outlet items I was promised show up. The grid is the normal full-price catalog: the Cayman bed, the Remmy chair, a jute rug, a curtain rod, a ghost collection. So the email's own hook never gets fulfilled.
- Would I click? Maybe "Shop Now" on the outlet, out of curiosity, but nothing here pushes me. I wouldn't forward it. It goes in the Pinterest-adjacent folder only because of the Remmy chair and the rug, and I'd rather have those in a cleaner email.

## What's working

- The hero is clean and readable. The red block with white serif type is on brand and I got it in one second.
- "Open Box Outlet" is a real, specific concept. Returned and opened items at a discount is the kind of thing I'd actually shop for furnishing a second home.
- The Design Crew "Free Design Services / Book a Free Appointment" banner has a clear button. It also fits how I shop, since I'm planning two houses and want someone to help with the palette.
- The product grid is tidy and uses real, named products like the Remmy Swivel Chair and Reeva Handwoven Rug. That helps a Pinterest-folder person like me.
- The Christmas Shop block's deep red matches the hero, so the palette feels consistent.

## What's weak

- There's no number anywhere in the hero. No "up to 50% off," no dollar amount, nothing. It's just "Save big." Crate & Barrel's Design Desk emails at least give me a concrete project to react to.
- None of the outlet products appear. The grid below looks like the full-price catalog, so the subject line and the email don't match.
- "Catch them while you can" is urgency with no deadline and no stock count. It's hollow.
- The Christmas Shop in early October, plus the ghost collection, makes the email feel like a pile of unrelated promos. It also repeats the same Holiday Lookbook pitch from the 10/1 send.
- The bottom third is the app, credit card and Affirm ads, then dense fine print. The "10% back in rewards" card pitch is buried and doesn't help the outlet offer.
- The cadence is exhausting. I got "Your next great find is ON SALE" and "Ships FREE: Your new favorite throw" earlier today, so this is the third Pottery Barn email today.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the actual discount in the hero, for example "Open Box Outlet: up to 60% off returned & open-box." Then replace the generic grid with four to six real outlet items showing original price and outlet price.
- 2. Cut the Christmas Shop, the app block and the Affirm block from this send. One offer per email.
- 3. Add a deadline or stock signal, such as "Inventory changes daily" or "Ends Sunday," so the urgency is credible.
- 4. Pull the Design Crew banner into its own send. Cap daily volume. Three a day is why I stop opening.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Open-box furniture, up to 60% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Returned sofas & rugs, marked way down`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Open-box chairs, rugs and beds, with outlet pricing on every one.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Inventory changes daily, and these pieces won't be around long.`

- **Mixed content:** three images load over `http://`, which clients may block or rewrite:
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
  - `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=…`
- **Third-party and legacy pixels:** `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/…/cat/1` (Turn/Amobee ad-tech), `svopn.potterybarn.com` and `clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/f5bed2dc-…` are all in the body. Three separate open or tracking pixels is a deliverability and privacy overhead.

- **Pixels and spacers:** these need `alt=""`. The warnings cover `spacer_0.gif`, `d.turn.com/…/cat/1`, `svopn…` and `clickcp…`.

- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" … maximum-scale=1>` prevents pinch-zoom, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post:** neither was found. The QA notes say this may be an artifact of the AgentMail relay, so it can't be confirmed from this data. If it is real, Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements (RFC 8058) are not met.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)

### 7. Recommendations
1. Remove the stray `header(...)` PHP lines from the `<style>` block.
2. Add descriptive `alt` text to the hero, product, and banner images, and `alt=""` to spacers and tracking pixels. Give the footer slices real alt text, or convert the footer to live text.
3. Switch the three `http://` image URLs, including the `svopn` pixel, to HTTPS.
4. Stop passing the plaintext email in `coreemail.html?email=` and `sv_sveme=`. Use a hashed or opaque ID instead.
5. Fill in `<title>`, add `lang="en"` to `<html>`, and drop `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
6. Add `role="presentation"` to the remaining layout tables.
7. Verify the raw headers (`List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, `Authentication-Results`) from the original message rather than the relay copy.
8. Resolve the redirect chains for a sample of the 44 tracking links to confirm UTM tagging and landing pages.
9. Host the spacer GIF on the Pottery Barn domain instead of `edm.westelm.com`.
