# THINGS OF QUALITY
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-16**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "Things of Quality" — CB2 leading with a designer name-drop again, and honestly, third or fourth time this week they've run this exact playbook: "JUST IN ALREADY TRENDING," then a wall of single-item cards, each with a designer credit under the product name (Meda by Gianfranco Frattini, Bixi by Studio Ananda, Fitz by Yuua, Aldine by David Girelli). It's the same structure as the 9/15 "New from David Girelli, VUUE & more" send I already read — same rust-and-oak moodboard, same "shop now" ghost buttons, even some overlapping product names.
- My gut reaction: this is a nice-looking scroll and nothing more. There's no price visible on a single hero item until you hit the "More For You" grid at the very bottom, and even then it's a jumble of unrelated SKUs (a platform bed, a mirror, a side table) with no organizing logic. I'm mid-renovation on two houses right now and genuinely shopping consoles and dining tables — the Onyx-Topped Table and Rich Oak Console sections should be catnip for me — but there's zero shipping/lead-time info, no swatch program mention, nothing that helps me actually decide. I'd scroll through for the eye candy, maybe screenshot the console for my Pinterest-folder, but I'm not clicking anything and I'm definitely not forwarding this — my wife would ask "which one" and I couldn't tell her a price without leaving the email.
- Compare to Article's "Home tour: A welcoming workspace" from earlier this week — that one stopped my scroll because it showed one specific desk+chair pairing in a real room with a story. This is CB2 doing six sequential hero shots with no throughline except "designer says quality." Handsome, forgettable.

## What's working

- The "Fall Sale Up To 30% Off" banner at the very top at least gives me one concrete number before the design-porn scroll starts.
- Photography is consistently moody and on-brand — warm oak, rust velvet, stone textures — CB2's lane and they're not straying from it.
- Designer collab framing (Frattini, Studio Ananda, David Girelli) is a real differentiator from Wayfair's grid-of-everything approach.
- The "Interior Design Services — Expert design, tailored to you, always complimentary" module is genuinely relevant to someone renovating two houses like me.
- Loyalty math is visible at the bottom: "5% Rewards Dollars back" plus "15% off" for the app — a real, specific number, not vague.

## What's weak

- No prices anywhere on the hero products — Meda chair, Onyx table, Rich Oak console, Denim Blue chair, Aldine sofa — I have to scroll to "More For You" for any dollar figure, and even those are for different, unrelated items.
- Zero shipping/lead-time clarity for furniture — as someone juggling two renovation timelines, delivery windows matter more than the designer's bio.
- No swatch/sample-ordering CTA anywhere, which is table stakes for velvet and leather pieces this expensive.
- "More For You" grid at the bottom is a random assortment — bed, mirror table, side table, coffee table, console, dresser — no room-based or category-based logic tying it together.
- This is the same "single hero item + designer name" template CB2 ran on 9/15 and 9/14 — I'm starting to tune it out as wallpaper.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price under every hero product image, full stop — right now I'd have to click through to even window-shop responsibly.
- 2. Replace the generic "More For You" grid with a room-based module (e.g., "Complete the Dining Room") — Pottery Barn's warehouse sale email did this room-by-room and it read far more useful.
- 3. Add a shipping/lead-time chip near the fall sale banner — furniture buyers mid-reno care about this more than the 30% number itself.
- 4. Add a swatch/sample CTA on the velvet and leather pieces (Fitz chair, Aldine sofa) — I won't buy a $2k+ sofa sight-unseen on fabric.
- 5. Vary the template from the last two sends — same "designer name + ghost-outline Shop Now button" structure three times running is losing me.
