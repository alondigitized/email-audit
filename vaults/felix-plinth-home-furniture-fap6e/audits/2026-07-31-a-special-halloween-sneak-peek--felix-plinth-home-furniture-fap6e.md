# A special Halloween sneak peek 👻
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-31**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so the subject promised me "a special Halloween sneak peek" and then the actual hero banner I open to says "Summer Sale: Up to 70% off." That's a bait-and-switch before I've even scrolled an inch — which brand are we running here, the July clearance or the Halloween preview? It takes until the second panel for the ghost decor and jack-o'-lanterns to actually show up, and by then I've already half-decided this is another generic blast.
- Once I get into it, the Halloween collage itself is genuinely well shot — those ceramic ghosts and the black-cat pillow have some appeal for the second-home mantel — but then it dead-ends right back into "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU," which is just a curtain rod, a farmhouse bed, and a dining table with zero connection to Halloween or, frankly, to me specifically. It reads like three different emails stapled together.
- Would I click? Maybe, on the Toscana dining table card since I've actually got a dining table on my list — but I'm not clicking Halloween shop in July/August when I'm mid-reno on two houses. Not forwarding this one; it's not sharp enough. Marking it read and moving to whatever CB2 sent me that week, because that "Study in Creativity" send at 8/10 is still the bar this one doesn't clear.

## What's working

- The Halloween collage photography is genuinely nice — moody, styled, not cheap-looking clip art.
- "Up to 10% Back in Rewards" across Pottery Barn/Williams Sonoma/West Elm is relevant since I run everything through my highest-rewards card anyway.
- The Toscana Extendable Dining Table card shows an actual dimension range (60"–124.5"), which is the kind of spec I need before I'll click anything furniture-related.
- No broken images, no overlapping text — the render itself is clean, unlike some of the messier Wayfair sends I've gotten.

## What's weak

- Leading with "Summer Sale: Up to 70% off" when the subject sold me a Halloween sneak peek is straight-up mixed messaging — pick a lane.
- The "Recommended Just For You" grid has zero connection to Halloween or to each other — curtain rod, farmhouse bed, dining table, towel, swivel chair, duvet — it's a random six-pack, not a curated pick.
- Four separate "SHOP NOW" buttons across the email, all identical copy — lazy compared to CB2's more specific CTAs in their editorial sends.
- Halloween decor isn't remotely close to my actual renovation list right now — this doesn't feel targeted at someone furnishing two houses.
- This is the same brand that hit me with rugs, entryway styling, duvets, and layered-bed content all in the same week — now Halloween on top. That's a lot of Pottery Barn in one inbox.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the "Summer Sale" banner or move it below the Halloween content — don't contradict the subject line in the first thing I see.
- 2. Make "Recommended Just For You" actually themed — either Halloween accents or reno-relevant furniture, not a random grab bag of six unrelated SKUs.
- 3. Differentiate the CTA copy per section — "Shop the Halloween Edit," "Shop Dining," "Shop Outdoor" instead of four identical "Shop Now" buttons.
- 4. Add dimensions/materials to more of the recommended cards the way the dining table card does — that's the detail that actually gets me to click.
- 5. Ease off the send cadence — this is at least the fifth or sixth Pottery Barn email in my inbox this stretch; even a good idea gets diluted at this frequency.
