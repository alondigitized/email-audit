# ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Bestsellers & new arrivals from Nuna!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- This is the third Crate & Barrel family email to land in my inbox on June 23 — after two Crate & Kids free shipping blasts earlier today ("FREE SHIPPING. We love to see it 👀" and "Everything inside ships for FREE!"). At some point the sender pool just becomes noise, and Nuna car seats and high chairs are arriving into a brain that is actively comparing sofas and arguing with itself about bathroom tile grout colors. Wrong category, wrong moment.
- That said: the email itself is not bad. The Nuna hero is clean, the photography is exactly the premium-neutral brand signature I associate with Nuna, and the product grid is organized. The execution isn't the problem. The problem is that I'm 36, I'm renovating two houses simultaneously, and this send treated me like someone who woke up this morning thinking about convertible car seats. I did not. I was thinking about 6% cashback categories and whether a particular sectional ships assembled.
- I marked it as read. Didn't click, didn't forward. If my kid were literally aging out of their current seat and I'd been browsing Nuna for two weeks, maybe. But today? No.

## What's working

- **Nuna's brand aesthetic comes through.** The hero shot — dark background, lifestyle child-in-chair framing — is exactly what Nuna deploys in their own channels. No generic stock imagery.
- **Sender is unambiguous.** "Crate & Kids" reads instantly; I know it's the C&B sub-brand for baby and kids gear, not the main store.
- **Visual hierarchy is sensible.** Nuna logo → hero product shot → product grid. The eye has a clear path.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no layout blowouts, no overlapping text. This is a polished send.
- **Category shift was at least a beat change.** Two free-shipping banners → a brand spotlight at least signals some editorial intent, even if the timing misfired for me.

## What's weak

- **Five star emojis in the subject line.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ reads like a review-rating shorthand when deployed solo, but stacked five-deep in a subject line it becomes a Black Friday spam tell. Nuna is a $400-car-seat brand. This positioning is beneath both of them.
- **No concrete offer anywhere visible.** "Bestsellers & new arrivals" is a browse prompt, not a conversion trigger. No percentage off, no free shipping call-out, no bundle deal. Compare this to Pottery Barn's recent "FREE design help for warm-weather hosting" — they at least attached a service hook to get the click.
- **Sender fatigue is severe.** June 23 is not the day to be the third C&B family email in my inbox. Pottery Barn sent maybe two emails this entire week; C&B has filed four or five before lunch.
- **Zero segmentation signal.** My C&B and C&K browse history is probably 80% rugs, sofas, and lighting right now. This send treated me like someone whose primary identity is "parent of a Nuna-aged child," not "someone mid-renovation who also has a kid."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Reduce or cut the star emoji stack.** Two at most, or swap for a single product-specific emoji. The five-star treatment cheapens a premium brand partnership.
- 2. **Anchor the hero to a specific offer.** Free shipping on Nuna already exists as a live offer today (given the two free shipping sends) — name it in the Nuna email. Or run a "new colorway now available" angle that creates genuine freshness.
- 3. **Suppress renovation-heavy browsers or cross-sell.** If C&B knows I've been clicking sofas for two months, either suppress me from this send or bridge the gap: "Finishing the nursery while you finish the house?" — something that acknowledges what their data should know about me.
- 4. **Use the preheader to carry specific product names.** "The ZAAZ. The RAVA. And what just dropped." gives my inbox list view a reason to pick this over the four other things around it.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` brand mismatch**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
Sending brand is "Crate & Kids"; title reflects the parent brand. This surfaces in tab titles on webmail clients (Gmail, Outlook.com) and in screen reader announcements.

**Unfilled template placeholder with typo**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
A developer comment with a typo ("IMPUT" → "INPUT") was left in production HTML. If a custom font was intended, it was never injected. Low rendering risk, but confirms the template was sent without a final QA pass on the `<head>`.

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users from zooming on mobile. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Global `text-size-adjust: none`**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applied to every element via the universal selector. Overrides the `body`-level `100%` value set two lines earlier and suppresses browser text scaling globally. Should be scoped to `body` only, and set to `100%`, not `none`.

**Conflicting `img` max-width media queries (ordering bug)**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }  /* declared first */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }  /* declared second */
```
At viewports ≥ 768px both queries match; the later declaration wins, so desktop images are capped at 640px — wider than the 600px email container. The `min-width: 768px` rule is effectively dead. Intended behavior is ambiguous; if container width is 600px, reorder so the 768px rule is last and authoritative.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
`telephone=no`, `address=no`, and `date=no` each appear twice (once in a combined tag, once individually). Not harmful, but indicates template merging without deduplication.

**Dark mode not declared**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Only `light` is declared. Apple Mail will attempt auto-dark-mode inversion on unsupported color schemes. No issue if dark mode was deliberately tested and excluded, but it should be documented.
