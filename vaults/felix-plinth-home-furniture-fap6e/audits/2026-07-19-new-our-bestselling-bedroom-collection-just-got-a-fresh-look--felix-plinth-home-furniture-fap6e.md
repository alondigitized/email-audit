# NEW! Our bestselling bedroom collection just got a fresh look
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- Seventeen. That's where I am with the Crate & Barrel family after this one lands, and I'm going to be honest with myself for a second: I almost routed it to trash on muscle memory alone. Yesterday was an outdoor sale. Twice. Then a back-to-school giveaway. Twice. Now this — and it's *bedroom*. Which is, as it happens, exactly where I am right now with both properties. So I paused.
- The email is long — maybe too long — but the hero is doing real work. Natural wood, warm whites, clean styling that isn't trying too hard. This is the aesthetic I've been pinning for the second home's primary bedroom, and C&B has a shot. The "fresh look for the bestselling collection" angle is the right call creatively; it doesn't scream discount, which is a relief after the sale drum C&B has been beating all week. The problem is that the email then pivots into *another* sweepstakes, which killed whatever product momentum I was building.
- I'd save this to my Pinterest-adjacent folder and probably not click today — but I'd come back to it. That's better than the back-to-school giveaway pair got from me, which was a sub-three-second close. I wouldn't forward it, but I'm noting the collection name.

## What's working

- **The hero is actually relevant to me** — bedroom furniture from a brand I trust, styled in exactly the warm-organic palette I'm working toward on the second home. That's not nothing.
- **No emergency sale language** — after a week of "UP TO 60% OFF" hammering, the "fresh look" framing reads like a palate cleanser. Credit where it's due.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean** — hero leads, product grid follows, I know where to look without having to work for it.
- **TheDesignDesk editorial section at the bottom is a nice signal** — C&B treats me like someone who might care about context, not just conversion. Article does this well; C&B is catching up.
- **Contemporary aesthetic matches my taste** — neutral palette, natural materials, no aggressive trend-chasing. It's not pushing a look that's going to feel dated in three years.

## What's weak

- **Another sweepstakes.** I just got the back-to-school giveaway — twice, on the same day — and now there's a "chance to win" block buried in this email too. It's becoming the C&B family filler pattern and it cheapens the new-collection story.
- **No price anchors anywhere visible** — if this is a new product refresh, is the pricing different? The same? Higher because of the update? Pottery Barn's summer sale email at least told me what I was walking into before I clicked.
- **The email runs very long** — by the time I hit the living-room editorial section and then the second sweepstakes mention, I've lost the thread. CB2's "MORE ON SALE" was tighter and more decisive, and I didn't even like that email.
- **"NEW!" in the subject with an exclamation point** — marginally spammy. The copy inside is more confident than the subject lets on.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Tighten to one story, one CTA.** Pick the bedroom collection *or* the sweepstakes — not both. Right now the email is two pitches fighting each other. The product story is stronger; let it breathe.
- 2. **Show one price.** Even "starting at $X" in the hero or above the fold gives me a reason to click rather than save-for-later. Pottery Barn's summer sale made that math easy. C&B is making me do homework.
- 3. **Cut the living-room module.** It dilutes the bedroom focus. If C&B wants to cross-sell, do it in a separate send. I'm already suspicious when the same piece shows up in three brand emails the same week — don't make me feel that in a single email.
- 4. **Consolidate sweepstakes to one clear placement** — or cut it entirely from this email. Sweepstakes is not why I'm here.

**Conflicting image `max-width` media queries**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
At ≥768px both rules match; cascade order means the 600px rule wins (declared later), making the 640px rule a no-op for any viewport ≥768px. If 640px max-width was intended for the 640–767px band, the 768px block needs to be declared *before* the 640px block, or the 640px rule needs a `max-width: 767px` upper bound.

**Viewport zoom lock**
`<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` — `maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom on iOS, which is an accessibility barrier for low-vision users. WCAG 1.4.4 (AA) requires text to be resizable without assistive technology.

**Universal `line-height: 100%` override**
`*{ ... line-height: 100% }` — applying this to every element can collapse line spacing on elements that inherit unexpected values (e.g., buttons, headings). Scope this to `td, p, span` rather than the universal selector.
