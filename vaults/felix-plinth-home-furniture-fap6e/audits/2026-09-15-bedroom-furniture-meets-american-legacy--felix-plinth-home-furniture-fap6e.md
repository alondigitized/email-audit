# Bedroom furniture meets American legacy
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-15**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "The Montana Collection" from Vaughan-Bassett x Pottery Barn — a heritage furniture maker out of Galax, Virginia, "handcrafting wood furniture for more than a century." I'll admit, that stopped me for a second longer than the usual PB send. I'm not actively bedroom-shopping for either house right now, but that dark wood dresser with the mirror and dried florals on top is genuinely a nice styling shot, and the "turned corner posts, generous scale, rich finish" copy at least tries to speak my design language instead of just "cozy fall vibes."
- That said, this is a browse-and-forget for me. There's no price anywhere on this email — not on the collection, not on a single "Recommended Just For You" item — and I don't shop furniture without knowing if I'm looking at $400 or $2,400. Compare this to CB2's "Fall Sale is over tomorrow" send I flagged as a 7: that one led with 30% off and a deadline. This one leads with brand heritage and hopes I click through to find out what it costs.
- Would I click? Maybe, out of curiosity about the collection since I do like the wood tone. Would I forward it? No — nothing here is decision-ready enough to send to my wife or a designer. Mark as read and move on.

## What's working

- The Montana Collection hero shot is styled well — layered bedding, warm wood tone, looks like something that'd actually fit the primary reno's aesthetic.
- The Vaughan-Bassett heritage callout ("family-founded... for more than a century") gives it a story angle none of the recent PB sends (ghost lawn decor, ready-to-ship outdoor) had — it reads like an actual product launch, not a clearance push.
- "More details for your room" cross-sell grid (bedding, lamps, curtains, rugs) is smart room-completion logic, and the "SHIPS FREE" badges on curtains/rugs are a real, visible signal.
- Free Design Services CTA at the bottom is a repeat beat from PB's 9/15 "RSVP for Free Design Services" send I scored 6 on — consistent, and still relevant since I'm mid-reno on two houses.

## What's weak

- Zero pricing anywhere. Not on the hero collection, not on one single "Recommended Just For You" tile. I don't click furniture blind — Crate & Barrel's KitchenAid send at least named the tech being featured; this hides the one number that actually drives my decision.
- The Halloween Shop banner wedged between bedroom furniture and Design Services is a jarring category whiplash — same complaint I had about PB's "Halloween favorites never stay in stock" send, and here it interrupts what was otherwise a coherent bedroom-refresh narrative.
- Six-plus product grids stacked without any "shop the room" price bundle or room total — by the time I hit "Recommended Just For You" I've scrolled past curtain rods and rugs with no idea what any of it costs, so my brain just tunes out the grid.
- No rewards math tied to this specific purchase — the "10% Back in Rewards" card blurb is generic boilerplate at the footer, not connected to the Montana Collection at all.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a starting price on the Montana Collection hero ("Dresser from $X") — this is the single biggest lever. Right now I have zero basis to judge if this is worth clicking through.
- 2. Cut the Halloween Shop banner out of this email entirely — it's competing with a heritage-furniture story and it's the same tonal mismatch I flagged on 9/15's ghost-decor send. Send Halloween separately.
- 3. Add price or "from $X" tags under each "Recommended Just For You" tile — Wayfair and CB2 both show price/discount directly on grid items; PB's making me work harder for less info.
- 4. Tie the rewards card blurb to this specific collection ("Earn 10% back on Montana Collection pieces") instead of generic footer copy.

- 55 tracking/click-redirect links present (`click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=...`, `clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/...`), consistent with standard ESP click-tracking; not independently probed by QA (expected behavior for redirect domains).
