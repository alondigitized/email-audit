# The elements of a welcoming entryway
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so this is "How to Style Your Entryway" and I'll admit it — this is the first Pottery Barn email in this run that's actually built around a room I'm renovating right now, not just a bed I already own. Numbered list, five styling moves, each one tied to a shoppable product. That's a real content format, not just "here's four duvets in a row" like the 7/29 send I dinged a 5 for.
- That said, I'm now at what, seventh or eighth Pottery Barn email in under two weeks? And the entryway content is good, but by the time I hit "More Ways to Accent the Room" and then a second "Recommended Just For You" grid and THEN a third "Discover New Arrivals" sofa section and THEN a fourth "Up to 60% Off Outdoor" banner, this has stopped being an entryway email and turned into the whole catalog stapled together. It's the Wayfair problem — I gave Wayfair a 5 for stacking fifteen categories before I finished my coffee, and Pottery Barn is doing a slower-motion version of the same thing here, just with nicer photography.
- I'd click through on the entryway console table or the woven vine vase, sure — the styling shot at the top is genuinely nice and on-brand for what I'm doing to my entryway. I would not forward this to a friend, and I'm not scrolling to the bottom. Mark as read, maybe tap through on one entryway item, ignore the rest.

## What's working

- The "Style Your Entryway" hero with the numbered 1-5 breakdown (vases, candles, decorative objects, botanicals, baskets) is a real styling narrative, not just a product wall — closer to Article's editorial room-story sends than Pottery Barn's usual duvet-stack format.
- Entryway is a category I'm actually shopping for on this reno, so the targeting is sharper than the last several PB sends I've reviewed.
- Each numbered tip links to a specific product (Woven Vine Vase, Antique Bronze Horse Head, Faux Cone Hydrangea, Artisan Rustic Utility Basket) — that's a real, specific CTA, not a vague "Shop Now."
- The rewards card callout at the bottom (10% back, pre-approve in seconds) is a decent capstone if I actually get that far, though I rarely do.

## What's weak

- The "up to 70% off" banner at the very top has nothing to do with entryway styling — it's just generic sale noise slapped above the actual content, which undercuts the editorial framing before it even starts.
- This email does not end at the entryway. It keeps going into curtain rods, dining tables, a duvet cover, a desk, then a full living room sofa section, then a separate outdoor sale banner. Four distinct sales pitches stacked into one send — CB2 would never let a themed send sprawl like this.
- No color-palette or swatch/sample callout anywhere, which is exactly what I need for a room I'm actively decorating — PB knows I'm mid-reno and still doesn't offer me swatches.
- Cadence fatigue is real — this is close to my eighth PB email recently, and "Beautifully Crafted Quilts" and "The Layered Bed" already used up my patience for "yet another styling guide" this week.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this email in half. Kill "Discover New Arrivals" and the outdoor sale banner entirely — let the entryway content and the "More Ways to Accent" categories stand alone as a tight, single-topic send. CB2's "ends tonight" email worked because it stayed on one idea.
- 2. Drop the generic "70% off" banner from the top, or replace it with an entryway-specific offer ("20% off entryway storage & decor") so the hero doesn't contradict the styling narrative underneath it.
- 3. Add a swatch/sample CTA next to at least one entryway product — I'm mid-reno on two houses and matching finishes is my actual bottleneck, not inspiration.
- 4. Move the rewards card math higher, or at least tease it near the top — "Earn 10% Back" buried at the very bottom after four sale sections gets ignored.
- **Subject Alt A:** `5 easy ways to style your entryway (with real product picks)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your entryway is missing these 5 things`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Vases, candles, and rustic accents — swipe our entryway formula`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New entryway pieces, styled 5 ways`

# Technical Email Audit — Pottery Barn "The elements of a welcoming entryway"

## 1. Technical Summary

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All click-through links route through tracking/redirect infrastructure (`click.e.potterybarn.com`, `clickcp.potterybarn.com`, `d.turn.com`, `svopn.potterybarn.com`) — 60 tracking links were skipped by the automated HTTP probe because they are redirect endpoints, so final destination health is unverified.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 48 images are missing `alt` text, including all 18 header "slices" images, all 18 footer "slices" images, and 8+ product/banner images (e.g., `farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `toscana-extendable-dining-table-60-1245-l.jpg`, `belgian-flax-linen-duvet-cover-2-l.jpg`). With images disabled (a common default in Outlook/Gmail), the email will render as largely blank content with no accessible fallback text.

- A stray `<style>` block contains raw PHP-style `header()` calls (`header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); ...`) embedded as literal text inside CSS — this is inert (won't execute) but indicates a copy-paste artifact from template code that should be cleaned up; it has no functional rendering impact since it's inside a `<style>` tag and ignored as invalid CSS.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header not found** — not captured in the relayed headers, meaning mailbox-provider-level one-click unsubscribe UI may not be offered.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found (RFC 8058)** — even if List-Unsubscribe is present elsewhere, without the POST variant, Gmail/Yahoo one-click unsubscribe cannot function per current bulk-sender requirements.
- **Authentication-Results header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status could not be verified via the AgentMail relay; this needs to be confirmed directly against the sending MTA rather than assumed.
- An in-body unsubscribe/customer-service link exists (`potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=...`), which addresses CAN-SPAM's requirement for a working opt-out mechanism in the body, independent of header-level unsubscribe.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Cannot verify UTM parameter alignment between email links and landing pages — all outbound links are wrapped in tracking redirects (`click.e.potterybarn.com`, `clickcp.potterybarn.com`) with opaque encoded query strings (`qs=ABB7...`, `mi_mid=019fb089-...`), and the automated probe skipped resolving these redirects.

- Recommend following the redirect chain manually to confirm final URLs carry consistent UTM/campaign parameters and land on live, matching pages.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers to comply with Gmail/Yahoo's one-click unsubscribe requirements (enforced since Feb 2024).
2. Confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment directly at the MTA — the missing `Authentication-Results` header here is likely an AgentMail relay artifact, not proof of a real auth failure, but should not be assumed passing.
3. Add descriptive `alt` text to all 48 flagged images, particularly the CTA/product images (currently blank fallback = image-off users see nothing).
4. Migrate the 3 remaining `http://` image sources to HTTPS to avoid mixed-content blocking.
5. Serve the open-tracking pixel (`svopn.potterybarn.com`) over HTTPS to prevent silent open-tracking loss in HTTPS-enforcing clients.
6. Remove the orphaned PHP `header()` snippet embedded in the `<style>` block — harmless today, but signals a template-generation bug worth fixing at the source.
7. Manually resolve the tracking-redirect links to confirm UTM parameters and destination pages are correct, since automated probing could not follow them.
