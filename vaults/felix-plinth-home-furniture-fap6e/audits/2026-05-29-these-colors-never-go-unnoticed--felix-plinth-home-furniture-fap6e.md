# These colors never go unnoticed
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- Nine. Maybe ten. I've genuinely lost count of how many CB2 emails have landed since May 17th, and that number was already sitting at eight when "All green everything" showed up two days ago. So the first thing I clocked — before the hero image fully rendered — was that this is another one. The pivot away from the Memorial Day hammer ("LAST CALL," "Final hours," up-to-50%-off) and toward a color editorial feel is the right move. Tonally, it reads like CB2 trying to reset to brand after two weeks of screaming. I appreciate that instinct.
- That said, the email itself is pleasant without being urgent. It's a color story — lush hero shot, a few styled vignettes, a grid of pieces that lean into statement hues. Nothing is broken, nothing is offensive. But there's no offer. After training me for two weeks to expect a sale, they've served me a full-price lookbook. The contrast is jarring. I'm in renovation mode on two houses simultaneously — I scan emails for reasons to buy, not reasons to admire. This one didn't give me one.
- Mark as read. I wouldn't click through and I wouldn't forward it. Article pulled off this exact editorial-after-sale reset better when they dropped the brand-builder on May 23rd — they at least gave me a "here's what we stand for" opener before sliding back into product. This feels more like CB2 just moved on and assumed I'd follow.

## What's working

- The visual pivot from shouty sale copy to a calm color editorial is exactly the right gear-shift post-Memorial Day — Pottery Barn has been doing this "discovery" mode better than anyone, but CB2 at least recognized the shift was needed.
- Hero image is rich and confident — green velvet, orchids, warm metallics — it actually shows the brand's POV on color rather than just listing SKUs.
- Clean layout, no render issues, type is legible, hierarchy tracks top-to-bottom without fighting itself.
- The product grid at the bottom is a legitimate browsing surface for someone mid-renovation who might be scanning for a specific color story to anchor a room.

## What's weak

- Zero offer. Literally none. After two weeks of conditioning me to expect a discount, going cold-turkey full-price with no bridge ("Member Early Access," "complimentary swatch kit," anything) is a hard landing.
- "These colors never go unnoticed" is a tagline, not a subject line. It tells me nothing actionable. Compare this to Pottery Barn's "Find a rug in a color you ❤️" — even that warmer, less specific email at least told me the category.
- The email cadence is still broken. Nine-plus sends in thirteen days is a problem that no single well-designed email can fix. My trust account with CB2 is overdrawn.
- I can see what looks like a featured product with a price point mid-email, but it's presented editorially rather than transactionally — there's no clear "this piece, this price, add to cart" signal that matches how I actually decide to buy furniture.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a soft hook for the post-sale subscriber.** Even a one-line bridge — "Sale's over. Here's what we'd put in your space anyway." — acknowledges the calendar shift and lands more honest than pretending the last two weeks didn't happen.
- 2. **Surface one hero product with a direct link and price.** Pick the green velvet chair or the statement lamp and make it the anchor. Article does this well: one product, one price, one CTA — the rest is supporting cast.
- 3. **Rest the list.** Skip Saturday's send. The single highest-ROI change CB2 could make right now is not sending an email.
- 4. **Give me a reason to browse that's more than "colors exist."** A filter shortcut ("Shop by room: second home / primary," "New arrivals only") would convert the browsing grid into a destination rather than wallpaper.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Global text-scaling disabled**

```
Applied via the universal selector — overrides OS-level accessibility settings for users who require larger text. WCAG 1.4.4 violation risk.

**[HIGH] Viewport zoom locked**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Same WCAG 1.4.4 concern; modern browsers partially ignore this but it remains a flag.

**[MEDIUM] Conflicting `<p>` rules across two `<style>` blocks**
- Block 1: `p { margin:0 auto; text-align:center; }`
- Block 2: `p { padding:0 !important; margin:0 !important }`

The `!important` in block 2 wins the margin fight, but `text-align:center` from block 1 is not overridden and will cascade into all `<p>` elements. Any left-aligned body copy inside `<p>` tags may unintentionally render centered.

**[MEDIUM] Overlapping `img` media query breakpoints**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 100% !important } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 690px) { img { max-width: 690px } }
```
Source order matters: the 768px block appears first in the stylesheet, so at ≥768px the 690px rule (declared later) wins due to cascade order — the opposite of apparent intent. Images will be capped at 690px even on wide desktop viewports. Swap the block order or add `!important` to the 768px rule.

**[LOW] Double-slash in multiple `@font-face` src URLs**
```
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2
```
Functionally resolves correctly on most servers but is a hygiene issue indicating template assembly errors.

**[LOW] Cross-domain font loading (CB2 email → crateandbarrel.com)**
```css
@font-face { font-family:"HNW05"; src: url("https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/...") }

This works if both domains share infrastructure, but email clients that block third-party resource loading will silently fall back to system fonts.
