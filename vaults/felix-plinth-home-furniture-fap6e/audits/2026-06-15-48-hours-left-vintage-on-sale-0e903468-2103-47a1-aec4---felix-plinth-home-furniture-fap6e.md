# 48 HOURS LEFT: VINTAGE ON SALE
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-eight. That's the CB2 count since mid-May, and I start every one of these there because it's the only honest framing for anything that arrives from cb2@mail.cb2.com at this point. Two days ago I already sat through "ALMOST OVER: Design Legends Sale" — so "48 HOURS LEFT" landing in my inbox now is CB2 running the same play with a slightly different subject line and a fresh countdown clock. At some point the countdown stops meaning anything.
- Here's what cuts through despite all of that: the vintage angle is actually interesting to me. I'm actively sourcing furniture for two renovations, and "Authentic Vintage Rescues for a New Era — Only at CB2" is genuinely differentiated positioning from the steady stream of Crate & Barrel semiannual emails hitting me in the same week. The Warehouse Sale at 60% off, lower in the scroll, is the real hook — I always check warehouse before committing to full price anywhere.
- The problem is the email lies with its subject line. "Vintage on Sale" opens the door, and then the hero is the same Design Legends Sale at 20% off I already saw on June 13. The vintage callout is a mid-scroll blurb, not the headline event. That's a bait-and-switch in feel even if it isn't technically one. I'd probably mark this as read unless I had a specific piece in mind. Not forwarding — everyone I know who'd care is already on CB2's list, probably getting this same email right now.

## What's working

- The "TWO DAYS LEFT / SEMIANNUAL / DESIGN LEGENDS SALE / UP TO 20% OFF" hero hierarchy is instantly readable — no squinting, no mystery about what's being offered.
- Warehouse Sale at up to 60% off earns its placement lower in the email. That deeper discount changes the math enough to keep me scrolling when 20% wouldn't.
- The "MORE FOR YOU" personalized product grid at the bottom shows actual chair models with thumbnails — more useful browsing value than another lifestyle shot of the same leather sectional.
- "EARN 20% BACK" loyalty callout near the footer is worth the real estate. CB2 does competitive rewards math and it shows up without being buried in fine print.

## What's weak

- "48 HOURS LEFT" reads as urgency theater two days after "ALMOST OVER." If the sale is genuinely ending, one well-timed countdown email does the job — two in 48 hours trains me to ignore the clock entirely.
- The vintage angle evaporates after the hero blurb. If CB2 is leaning on "Authentic Vintage Rescues" as differentiation, I need to see the actual pieces — a curated grid with names and limited-quantity signals. One atmospheric lifestyle image of warm brown chairs is not the story.
- "SHOP NOW" CTAs throughout are too generic. Pottery Barn's June 14 send used category-specific CTAs that linked directly into sale sections; CB2 is pointing me at the same landing page twice before I've seen a single specific product.
- The subject-to-hero mismatch — "VINTAGE ON SALE" vs. "DESIGN LEGENDS SALE" — creates a micro-friction moment on open. I briefly feel tricked. That's a trust tax, even if everything technically resolves.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the email with the vintage product story.** If the differentiated hook is sourced vintage pieces, make that the hero image and hero copy — show the actual finds, suggest limited quantities, let scarcity do the real urgency work instead of a countdown clock.
- 2. **Give the Warehouse Sale equal-billing.** 60% off is the bigger number and the better conversion argument. Let it compete visually with 20% off at the top rather than appearing as an afterthought below the fold.
- 3. **Swap "SHOP NOW" for directional CTAs.** "Shop Vintage Finds" and "Browse the Warehouse" are verb + noun, funnel me into differentiated experiences, and don't make the email feel like one giant homepage button.
- 4. **Kill the redundant countdown send.** The June 13 "ALMOST OVER" email already set the clock. If you're sending again 48 hours later, the subject needs new information — a product drop, a price reduction, a sold-out callout — not just a refreshed timer.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Warehouse: up to 60% off. 2 days left.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Design Legends Sale plus CB2's deepest warehouse discounts — ending Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New vintage rescues. Warehouse deals at 60% off. Ends soon.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Unfilled template placeholder (hard defect)**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
This is a raw production template stub — `IMPUT` is a misspelling of `INPUT`. It indicates the template was sent without completing or removing this placeholder. If a font import was supposed to go here, it was omitted.

**Mobile breakpoint too narrow**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { .mobile { width:414px !important; } }
```
418px only catches devices at exactly iPhone SE width. iPhone 14/15 (390–430px logical width), most Android flagships (360–412px), and many mid-range devices will miss this breakpoint entirely. The standard email mobile threshold is 480px or 600px. Recipients on affected devices get the desktop layout forced into a narrower viewport.

**Double-slash paths in font URLs (broken asset risk)**
Multiple `@font-face` declarations have double forward slashes in the path:
```css
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2")
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2")
```
Most web servers normalize `//` to `/`, but this is not guaranteed on all CDN/origin configs. Should be corrected to single slash.

**Cross-brand font CDN dependency**
```css
@font-face { font-family:"HNW05";
  src: url("https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/1e07d600-3787-4517-bd3d-b140f9e54ec2.woff2")
```
`HNW05` (Helvetica Neue) is loaded from `crateandbarrel.com`, a sibling brand's domain. If that domain's CDN has downtime, blocks cross-origin requests, or the path moves, CB2 email font rendering degrades silently. Should be hosted on CB2's own CDN or a shared brand asset CDN.

**`Lineto Brown Light Sub` missing woff2**
```css
@font-face { font-family: "Lineto Brown Light Sub";
  src: url("https://www.cb2.com/Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff"); }
```
Only `.woff` is declared here — no `woff2` format hint. All modern email clients that support web fonts prefer `woff2`. A later duplicate `@font-face` block *does* add `woff2` for weight 400, but the first declaration without a `format()` hint will win in some parsers.

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents users from pinching to zoom, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). iOS 10+ ignores this for accessibility, but it is still considered a defect for audit purposes.

**Global `text-size-adjust: none`**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; }
```
Applied to `*` (every element) rather than `body` only. Combined with `maximum-scale=1`, this doubly impairs low-vision users who rely on browser/OS font scaling.
