Once I clocked that I'd been baited into a doormat email, the rest of the send felt like noise: a mid-scroll bedroom pivot ("upgrading your bed?") that has nothing to do with what was advertised, a "new faves" content dump, and a 60% OFF banner at the bottom that doesn't say 60% off *what*. This email promised area rugs, delivered welcome mats, and sandwiched in bedroom content for no reason. I'll mark it read and move on.

## What's working

- **Wayfair Rewards module is well-positioned** — the "get 5% rewards on every item, always" break mid-scroll is the only section that feels like it knows I'm a member, not a stranger.
- **"First impressions" headline is actually clean** — if this had been billed as a doormat email, that copy earns its place.
- **60% OFF red banner has strong contrast** — reads fast even at thumbnail size, which is about the only place I got a clear offer signal.
- **No render bugs visible** — layout looks clean throughout, nothing broken or overlapping in the screenshot.

## What's weak

- **The subject says area rugs; the body is doormats** — this is a bait. I came for large rugs under $99 and got welcome mats and entry styling. That's a trust-damaging mismatch.
- **Same subject line as yesterday's #24** — running identical subject copy back-to-back days either signals a broken segmentation system or total disregard for subscriber fatigue. Either reading is bad.
- **"Upgrading your bed?" has no business being here** — this is an entryway send, per the subject. The bedroom section feels like a content widget that got pasted in from a different brief entirely.
- **60% OFF floats without an anchor** — what category is 60% off? CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" at least named the things on sale. This banner is a number in search of a noun.
- **Zero area rug product is shown** — the thing I came for isn't in the email. Not one grid tile, not one swatch, not one price. The subject is a lie.

- 1. **Make the body match the subject** — if the subject says area rugs under $99, open with a rug grid: styles, sizes, prices. Pottery Barn would have led with a room shot and a callout like "9×12 for $89 — limited stock." Do that.
- 2. **Anchor the 60% OFF to a category** — "60% Off Entryway Rugs" or "60% Off New Arrivals" — anything that tells me what I'd actually get if I clicked.
- 3. **Cut the bedroom section entirely** — "upgrading your bed?" belongs in a different send. Kill it here, run it tomorrow (though at 26 emails since Sunday, maybe don't).
- 4. **Retire this subject line** — you ran it yesterday. Running it again today tells me you don't know what you've already sent me, which makes me trust your offer logic less.
- 5. **Fix "heeere"** — one extra e reads quirky; three reads spam filter bait in the inbox preview.
