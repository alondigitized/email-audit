# Make your bed a little more colorful
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-18**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "Comfort Meets Color" with that quilted stack shot — I'll admit it's a nice hero image, good texture, clearly signals "new bedding drop." But then it immediately organizes into "shop greens / shop browns / shop blues / shop whites" and my brain just goes flat. This is the exact same maneuver Crate & Barrel pulled with their rugs email last week ("vintage look, modern performance") — take one category, slice it by color, ship it as four CTAs. It's competent, it's on-brand, but it's not a decision I need to make right now.
- What actually stops my scroll is the "Recommended Just For You" block lower down — a curtain rod, a dining table, a platform bed, a rug — because that's real personalization signal, presumably pulled from what I've been browsing for the second house. That's more interesting to me than the color-sorted duvets. Also the "The Warehouse Sale: Up to 60% off" banner is sitting right at the top and I almost missed it because the hero image completely overshadows it visually.
- Verdict: I'd skim, maybe click the "Recommended Just For You" row since that's actually tailored, but I'm not opening this on my phone standing in line anywhere. Not forwarding it — this isn't inspiration-worthy enough for the Pinterest folder, it's just inventory. Mark as read.

## What's working

- The quilted bedding hero shot has real texture and color depth — better production value than Wayfair's "fall trends" vignettes I looked at last week.
- "ships free" called out directly under the hue-picker section removes a real objection for me.
- "Recommended Just For You" actually mixes categories (rod, table, bed, rug) in a way that feels like it's reading my cart/browse history, not just re-serving bedding.
- The rewards card math is spelled out — "up to 10% back in rewards across Pottery Barn, Williams Sonoma, West Elm, Rejuvenation" — that's useful for someone stacking a high-rewards card strategy like I do.
- Loyalty/financing options (Affirm, credit card) are visible without me having to hunt.

## What's weak

- The 60% off Warehouse Sale banner is buried above the fold visually — it's the biggest number in the email and it's the smallest, least emphasized element.
- Four near-identical "shop [color]" CTAs in a row is decision fatigue, not merchandising — same trick Crate & Barrel just ran on rugs, same trick CB2 ran on their color-story send.
- "Print & Pattern Bedding" appearing right after the solid-color grid contradicts the "pick your hue" framing from thirty seconds earlier — feels like two campaigns stapled together.
- The Halloween Shop insert at the bottom is a jarring context-switch after ten screens of quiet neutral bedding — whiplash, not upsell.
- No delivery-time or in-stock messaging anywhere, which West Elm's "Delivered in 1-4 weeks" pill did well last week and I noticed its absence here immediately.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the 60% off Warehouse Sale offer into the hero itself, as the primary headline, not a thin top banner — that's the actual highest-value CTA in the email and it's currently the least visible thing on the page.
- 2. Cut the four color-CTA grid to one "Shop by Color" link that opens a filtered page — stop making me pick a lane before I've even seen the product.
- 3. Move "Recommended Just For You" up near the top, right under the hero — that's the one section actually earning its personalization claim, don't bury it at screen 8.
- 4. Add delivery/shipping-time messaging near the bedding grid, the way West Elm did with "Delivered in 1-4 weeks" — I'm juggling two house timelines and that's a real decision input.
- 5. Drop the Halloween Shop insert from this email entirely — send it as its own themed send, don't tack it onto a bedding-color email.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your color, your quilt — free shipping`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: picks we pulled just for your space`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the sale before it's gone`

- 57 tracking/click-redirect links detected and skipped for HTTP probing (click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=... redirect wrapper) — expected behavior for ESP click-tracking, not itself a defect, but unverified reachability.
- Two non-HTTPS tracking assets: `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` (open-tracking pixel) and `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`. - Recipient email address (`lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`) is exposed in cleartext in the `sv_sveme` open-tracking query param and in the `coreemail.html?email=` link — minor PII exposure in URLs, though standard for this ESP platform.

- Near-total absence of `alt` attributes: 50+ images flagged, including all content banners (F26_Top_WarehouseSale2.jpg, slices_01–18.jpg across two footer blocks, product images for curtain rods, dining table, bed, towel, rug, chair). Screen readers and images-off clients will render a blank email.
- Legacy MSO/Outlook conditional comments and `mso-table-lspace/rspace` resets present — standard defensive markup, no issues found.
- `<style>` block contains stray literal PHP-style `header(...)` calls (`header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');`, cache-control headers) embedded as plain text inside CSS — inert in this context but indicates leftover/copy-pasted template code; won't render or execute, cosmetic-only code hygiene issue.
