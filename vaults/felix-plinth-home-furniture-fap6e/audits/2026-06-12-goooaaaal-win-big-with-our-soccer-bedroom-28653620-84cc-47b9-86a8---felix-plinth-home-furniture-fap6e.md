# Goooaaaal! Win big with our soccer bedroom 🏆
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- Forty-two. That's where I am now, and I want to be precise: **this is the second soccer bedroom email I've received from the Crate & Barrel family today alone**. The first one was "Football or soccer? ⚽ A champion-inspired bedroom" — I reviewed it this morning. Now Crate & Kids is back with a slightly different spin on the same brief, the same product range, probably the same room. My left thumb is conditioned to swipe this into the archive without looking.
- I don't have kids. Crate & Kids knows this, or should — I'm on the Crate & Barrel adult email list, not the kids one. I renovate living rooms, dining rooms, and guest rooms. I have no use for a soccer-themed youth bedroom unless I'm shopping for a nephew's birthday, and even then I'd go to the site directly. The sweepstakes hook — "Win big" — is the nail in the coffin. I'm a guy who buys with a high-rewards credit card because I *have the budget to buy the thing outright*. I don't need to win a bedroom; I need to spec one out.
- Mark as read. No click. Definitely not forwarding to anyone.

## What's working

- **Brand recognition is instant** — the Crate & Kids logo and clean header made it clear this is a legit send from a brand I trust, not a phishing attempt.
- **Seasonal hook is legitimate** — Euro/Copa America timing for a soccer-themed send is defensible calendar marketing, and the hero room photography looks coherently styled.
- **Layout is clean and unbroken** — no render bugs I could see; the product grid renders correctly and the visual flow from hero to product tiles is logical.
- **Product tiles look shoppable** — from what I can see, the items are clearly photographed with enough separation that a real parent could navigate the grid.

## What's weak

- **Wrong sub-brand, wrong list** — I'm a Crate & Barrel adult-furniture customer. This is Crate & Kids. These should be separate audiences and I should never see this if I don't have kids on file.
- **Cadence is broken** — this is the second soccer bedroom email today, and I'm somewhere around email forty-two from the C&B family this month. Article pulled off four emails in twelve days and I still opened all four. C&B is burning every unit of goodwill it built.
- **"Win big" is a budget-shopper signal** — I run my home purchases through a Sapphire Reserve. Sweepstakes copy tells me the brand thinks I'm motivated by luck, not by quality or value. It's demographic mismatch encoded right into the subject line.
- **No price anchor anywhere visible** — unlike the Pottery Barn send this week that put "up to 70% off" in the subject, this email gives me nothing to evaluate the offer before opening. "Win big" is not a number.
- **Repeating a theme that just ran** — Wayfair would never send me a bedroom send and then the same bedroom send four hours later. Even Wayfair, who's been in my inbox forty-eight times, doesn't duplicate the hero concept intraday.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress this entire list segment from the kids' sub-brand** unless parental purchase history or a child's birthday is on file. Sending kids' bedroom content to adult-furniture buyers is the fastest way to teach someone to unsubscribe from the entire C&B family.
- 2. **Consolidate the soccer moment into one email, not two same-day sends.** If "Football or soccer?" ran this morning, this one should have gone out yesterday or tomorrow. The creative team clearly has two briefs colliding.
- 3. **Swap the sweepstakes frame for a product-first offer.** If there's a real prize, bury the entry and lead with the room. "The soccer bedroom your kid will love — shop the look" converts better with my demographic than "Win big."
- 4. **Put a price signal in the subject.** Even "from $49" would give me a reason to open.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Crate & Kids: Soccer bedroom from $49 — shop the look`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Build the ultimate soccer room (and save while you're at it)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Bedding, wall art, and storage that actually survives a 10-year-old.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New soccer bedroom finds — starting under $50.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` brand mismatch — confirmed issue**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
Email sender is `Crate & Kids`. The title tag displays in some clients (e.g., Gmail web tab, screen readers). Should read `Crate & Kids`.

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom — confirmed issue**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents low-vision users from pinching to zoom. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) violation. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on wildcard selector — confirmed issue**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```
Applying to `*` (not just `body`) suppresses OS-level text scaling across all elements. Use only on `body`.

**Conflicting desktop `img` max-width rules — confirmed issue**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
Both fire on screens ≥768px. The 768 rule appears later in source order but was declared *before* the 640 rule, so specificity resolves to `600px` on wide screens. Likely unintentional — images should probably cap at 640px consistently. Remove the 768 rule or reconcile.

**Light-only color scheme — confirmed, informational**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Explicitly opts out of dark mode. Images and backgrounds will not adapt in Apple Mail or iOS dark mode. Acceptable if intentional; flag if dark mode support is expected.

**Duplicate `format-detection` declarations — confirmed, minor**
`date=no` appears in both the combined meta and a standalone meta. Redundant, harmless, but signals template hygiene issue.
