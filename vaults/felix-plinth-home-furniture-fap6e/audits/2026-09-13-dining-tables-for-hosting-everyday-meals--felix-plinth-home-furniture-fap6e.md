# Dining tables for hosting & everyday meals
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-13**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is actually the email I've been waiting for from Pottery Barn — a straight-up dining table email with zero celebrity tie-in and zero credit card pitch above the fold. "Gather Together" as the hero, seating capacity broken out by 6/8/12, extendable call-outs — that's real furniture-shopping information, not lifestyle fluff. Between this, the Smart Seating send from 2026-09-12, and today's holiday preview, PB is clearly having a good week compared to the Molly Sims and Visa card sends earlier.
- That said, it's still a big scroll of near-identical wood tables that start blending together by the third one, and the credit card banner shows back up at the bottom anyway ("Earn 10% Back in Rewards") — so the "no card pitch" win only holds for the first two-thirds of the email. I'd click through on the "Seats up to Eight" or "Seats up to Twelve" section since that's literally my renovation problem right now (two houses, need to furnish two dining rooms at different scales), but I wouldn't forward this to anyone — it's useful, not exciting.

## What's working

- Organizing by seating capacity (Six / Eight / Twelve) is genuinely useful for someone furnishing two houses at different scales — this is the first PB email that speaks to my actual buying constraint instead of a vibe.
- "Extendable" call-outs on the 8 and 12-seat tables answer the exact question I'd have before clicking — good, that's real furniture spec info, unlike CB2's designer-story sends that skip straight to "Cervino performance chenille" without addressing fit.
- Clean, consistent product photography — every table shot in a styled room, no stock-photo mismatch, no render bugs.
- "A Chair for Every Style" section acknowledges that a table and chairs are separate purchase decisions, which is exactly how I shop — mix-and-match, not a package deal.

## What's weak

- It's five tables in a row that all look like slight variations on the same oak/walnut farmhouse silhouette — after Wayfair's identical-grid problem and CB2's family-snooze issue, I'm now noticing every brand doing this same "just keep scrolling past similar wood" thing.
- The credit card pitch ("Earn 10% Back in Rewards... Affirm... Buy Now, Pay Over Time") shows up anyway near the footer — so PB didn't actually resist the card cross-sell, they just moved it lower.
- "The Halloween Shop" wedged in between dining furniture and the design services block feels like a random seasonal tack-on that breaks the whole dining-table narrative I was following.
- No pricing or sale percentage anywhere in the body — every "Shop Now" is a blind click with no idea if I'm looking at $600 or $3,000 before I commit.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a price range or "starting at $X" under each seating-capacity table — I'm not clicking five different "Shop Now" buttons just to comparison-shop blind across five tables.
- 2. Cut the Halloween Shop insert entirely from this email, or move it to its own send — it breaks the dining-furniture narrative and adds nothing for someone here to solve a seating problem.
- 3. Move the rewards/Affirm financing block up near the top or drop it — burying it doesn't disguise it, it's just less discoverable when I actually want it (financing a $3K table is relevant to me right now).
- 4. Differentiate the five table photos more — different wood tones or room styles per capacity tier so they don't visually merge into one long scroll.
