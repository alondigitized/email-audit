# Designed to get dinner on the table ASAP
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-20**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction is that this is a kitchen-tools email wearing a Thanksgiving-dining-room banner. The top strip says "the Thanksgiving dining room event, up to 30% off" and then the hero is a hand holding a salad spinner. I'm renovating two houses and I care about dining rooms, so the banner got my attention, and the email then pulled me into whisks and dish towels. It's the same bait-and-switch feeling I had with Wayfair's comforter subject line, which turned out to be a Way Day teaser.
- It's also very long, with about seven distinct modules before the footer. They are tool tiles, an ASMR meal-prep block, dish towels, a SMEG toaster, a Design Desk sweepstakes, a six-product "we love these for you" grid, a rewards card pitch, an app download and a second Design Desk ask. I do like that Design Desk is in here, since I'd book a free appointment for a house. But it shows up twice and feels tacked on. This is also roughly the 17th Crate & Barrel send in two days, after "Hours left for free shipping!" yesterday. I'd skim it, maybe save the SMEG toaster image to my Pinterest folder, and mark it read. I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- The dark "Thanksgiving dining room event, up to 30% off" strip is the one concrete offer, and it sits at the very top.
- The hero image is big, warm and well lit. It looks premium, closer to Pottery Barn than a clearance blast.
- The category tiles (utensils, mixing bowls, colanders, cutting boards, kitchen storage) have a clean grid and "starting at" price cues. It's easy to scan.
- The SMEG exclusive color and the room-scene imagery are the kind of thing I'd save for the second house.
- The Design Desk appointment is a real service for someone doing two renovations.

## What's weak

- The banner promises dining room and the body delivers kitchen tools and dish towels. The offer doesn't match the content, and the 30% isn't tied to any specific item.
- It's far too long. By the time I reach the app download I've forgotten the hero offer, and Design Desk appears twice.
- It has about eight or more "Shop Now"/"Learn More" buttons and no single primary action. The generic CTA copy is weak.
- Nothing speaks to my actual projects. There are no furniture shipping details, no swatch or sample mention and no dining room product.
- The product grid and rewards-card block are small and text-heavy. Prices and fine print are hard to read on a phone without zooming.
- The cadence is exhausting. After yesterday's free-shipping sends and the "Up to 50% off furniture" emails, this feels like another near-identical blast.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the hero match the subject. If the event is dining room, lead with a dining table, chairs or lighting and put one clear "Shop dining up to 30% off" button under it. Move the kitchen tools to a separate send.
- 2. Cut the email to the hero, one category row, one product grid and one footer ask. Run Design Desk once and drop the sweepstakes and app blocks.
- 3. Make the offer concrete with a deadline, for example "30% off ends Sunday," and say what is excluded.
- 4. Use verb-plus-noun CTAs like "Shop dining tables" and "Book a design appointment" instead of repeating "Shop Now."
- 5. Segment people who browsed furniture so I don't get a cookware email in the middle of a renovation.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dining room event: up to 30% off tables & chairs`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Set the Thanksgiving table: 30% off dining`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dining tables, chairs and serveware, plus free design help. Ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Kitchen tools your hosts will actually use, and 30% off the room around them.`
