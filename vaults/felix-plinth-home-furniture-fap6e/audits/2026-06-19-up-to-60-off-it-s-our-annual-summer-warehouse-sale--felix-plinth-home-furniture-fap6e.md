# Up to 60% off: It’s our annual summer warehouse sale!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- This is now — and I've counted — the third Warehouse Sale email I've received from the C&B family in what feels like the same 48-hour window, and the second one timestamped June 19. What's different this time: the subject has quietly swapped "4th of July Warehouse Sale" for "annual summer warehouse sale," and the discount has been bumped from 50% (in the earlier June 19 send I already opened and filed) to 60%. That escalation is the most interesting thing about this email, and it's interesting for the wrong reason — it tells me every C&B price is negotiable if I just wait long enough.
- That said: 60% off home goods when I'm actively furnishing two properties is not nothing. The hero is clean, the offer leads, and the product scroll has real depth — I can see outdoor furniture, a dining section, what looks like kitchen, and a mid-scroll block of smaller goods before the Design Shed app promo buries itself at the footer. There's enough here to earn an open. It didn't earn a click.
- Mark as read. I'll revisit the sale page directly when I'm ready to pull the trigger on a specific piece — but this email didn't give me a reason to act tonight.

## What's working

- The 60% figure in the subject line is a real number doing real work — even on the fourth send, that's a concrete offer that earns an eye.
- Visual hierarchy is clean and intentional: logo → hero offer copy → product grid, no detours.
- Product blocks cover categories live in my renovation queue right now — outdoor furniture and dining are actual open decisions.
- No render bugs anywhere visible; email renders crisply top to bottom.
- The "annual summer warehouse sale" framing at least gestures at a reason-to-exist beyond pure urgency spam — weak, but present.

## What's weak

- This is the second June 19 C&B warehouse sale email I've opened. Sending two versions of the same event on the same day, with different discount percentages, is not segmentation — it's a mistake that trains subscribers to wait.
- The 50% → 60% escalation across consecutive sends is exactly the move Article avoids and CB2 avoids. It signals that nothing is real until the last email.
- "Annual summer warehouse sale" in the subject is a hedge — they couldn't decide if this is a 4th of July event or a season-end clearance, so they wrote neither clearly.
- The product grid is unedited. CB2's Todd Snyder collab drop knew exactly what it was selling and to whom. This email is talking to everyone, which means it's talking to no one specifically.
- No personalization anywhere — not my name, not a curated "based on your recent saves" module, not even category-level targeting. Pottery Barn's free design advice email on June 19 felt like a service. This feels like a batch.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick a number and hold it.** Escalating from 50% to 60% across the same sale week trains the subscriber to sit on their hands. Lock 60% and commit to it from send one.
- 2. **Segment the product blocks by room, not by category.** I'm a furniture buyer in mid-renovation. If the first thing I see after the hero is a curated "dining room under $800" or "outdoor sets now 60% off," I'm clicking. A general scroll of everything is not merchandising.
- 3. **Put a hard deadline in the email body.** "4th of July" is specific — but only implied by the subject. An actual "offer ends July 4 at midnight" visible in or just below the hero would create the urgency this email keeps gesturing at without delivering.
- 4. **Cut "annual" from the subject.** It makes the event sound evergreen and removes the one-time urgency the sale actually has.
- 5. **Move the Design Shed app promo to its own email.** Burying an app download at the bottom of a clearance blast is a waste of footer real estate in both directions.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Warehouse sale, final push — 60% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dining, outdoor, living — clearing before the 4th. Shop what's left.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `60% is as high as this goes. Here's what's still in stock.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom in mobile browsers. Should be removed or changed to `maximum-scale=5`.

**Conflicting image `max-width` media queries**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
At ≥768px both rules apply; the 600px rule (declared last) wins, making images *narrower* at 768px than at 640–767px. Intended behavior is unclear but the overlap is unintentional.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` in global `*` selector**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
`none` completely disables font scaling on WebKit. The outer `body` rule correctly sets `100%`; the `*` rule overrides it for all descendants. Should be removed or set to `100%`.

**Dark mode not supported**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Clients that force dark mode (Apple Mail, Gmail Android) will still invert or recolor the email. No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block is present. Risk: white backgrounds on text-heavy areas become black.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
`telephone=no` is declared twice (once combined, once standalone). Harmless but signals sloppy template hygiene.
