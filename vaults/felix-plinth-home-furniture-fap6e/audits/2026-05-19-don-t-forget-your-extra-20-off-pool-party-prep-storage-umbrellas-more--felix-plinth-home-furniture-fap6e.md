# Don't forget your EXTRA 20% OFF + Pool party prep: Storage, umbrellas, & more
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- Fourteen. I've been counting because at this volume it's involuntary — fourteen Pottery Barn emails inside roughly 72 hours, and this one opens with "don't forget" as if I personally misplaced the other thirteen. The subject is doing two jobs simultaneously: reminding me of a discount I already saw and pitching an editorial about pool accessories. It's two separate sends stapled together with a plus sign, and both are weaker for it.
- The email itself is genuinely clean. The pool-hero image communicates Memorial Day outdoor immediately — chaise lounges, striped towels, blue water — and the three-section structure (Keep it Organized, Summer Stripes, Shade Please) reads like a coherent seasonal story. There's a "Recommended Just for You" block buried near the footer that's actually the most relevant content in the email, which is a structural crime.
- I'm not clicking. Not forwarding. The offer is real, the production quality is solid, but Pottery Barn has spent down its credibility reserve at this cadence. Article sent me one tight Memorial Day email this week and I bought from it. This is email fourteen and I'm archiving.

## What's working

- **The pool hero is crisp and instantly seasonal.** Chaise lounges, blue water, towels — zero copy needed to understand what's being sold.
- **Three-section editorial gives the email a through-line.** Organized → Stripes → Shade is a logical outdoor prep narrative.
- **"EXTRA 20% OFF" is in the hero above the fold.** The offer is findable without scrolling — that's table stakes, and they cleared it.
- **"Recommended Just for You" at the bottom is the most interesting element in the email.** Actual personalization signals that the brand knows something about me.
- **The seasonal relevance is legitimate.** Pool party prep the week of Memorial Day is the correct send; the timing isn't the problem.

## What's weak

- **Fourteen emails in 72 hours.** The content could be perfect and I still feel harassed. Crate & Barrel has the same cadence problem this week but at least their "up to 30% off bestselling sofas" email gave me content I could use on a current purchase decision — pool storage isn't where I am right now.
- **"Don't forget" is a guilt-trip, not a value proposition.** It assumes I saw the last email and ignored it. I did. Earn the click back; don't nag me into it.
- **The subject is 77 characters and tries to sell two things.** Article's Memorial Day email landed in one editorial line. This reads like someone merged two send briefs into one because the calendar was full.
- **Section headers ("Keep it Organized," "Summer Stripes," "Shade Please") are editorial, not CTAs.** CB2 has this same problem — beautiful editorial voice, no actual conversion architecture. Crate & Barrel's "Shop Sofas" specificity is miles ahead.
- **"Recommended Just for You" is below the fold and three sections deep.** If you have my browse data, put it second, not fifth.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one email.** Either run the "Extra 20% off" reminder or run the pool party editorial. The combined subject is truncated on mobile before it gets to the interesting part.
- 2. **Move "Recommended Just for You" to section two.** It's the highest-relevance content you have — stop burying it.
- 3. **Replace editorial headers with destination CTAs.** "Shop Outdoor Storage" and "Shop Market Umbrellas" tell me where I land. "Shade, Please" tells me nothing.
- 4. **Add an explicit expiry date.** "Memorial Day Sale ends Monday, May 26" converts vague urgency into real urgency.
- 5. **Throttle the cadence.** This isn't a creative note, it's a business one — a single well-timed email converts better than a daily barrage.
