# SUMMER CLEARANCE
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-16**
## Executive summary

- CB2, "Summer Clearance," up to 60% off — and yeah, that's the fifth CB2/Crate & Barrel-family email this week counting the Onyx & Walnut, Floor Sample Sale, "impeccable energy," and yesterday's own End of Summer Sale. This literally looks like a reskin of yesterday's send: same green-water hero photo treatment, same stacked category buttons, same "MORE FOR YOU" product grid underneath. My brain has fully entered C&B-family snooze mode at this point — I flagged it after Floor Sample Sale, flagged it again after End of Summer Sale, and here we are again.
- The actual clearance mechanics are fine — real percentage off, real products, real prices shown right on the tiles, which none of the last three CB2 sends bothered doing consistently. But there's zero urgency, zero "this ends Sunday," and nothing that differentiates this from the sale I already saw yesterday. Compare that to Wayfair's slipcover email — bad execution, but at least it was trying to hook me on something I was actually shopping for. This is just "stuff is cheaper now, go look."
- I'm not clicking. I'm not forwarding. This gets archived next to the other four CB2 emails in the folder without a second look.

## What's working

- Prices are printed right on every product tile — Enyo marble coffee table at $2299, Andes bed at $1199 — no guessing, no click-through-to-find-out-the-price like some of Article's editorial sends.
- The dining/living pieces (Foley Mink velvet armchair, Aqua Virgo burl wood table) are squarely in my second-home reno lane, unlike the Crate & Kids nursery spam I keep getting.
- 10% Rewards Dollars callout is a real loyalty hook and I'm actually enrolled, so that's relevant to me specifically.
- Clean visual hierarchy — hero offer, then CTAs, then products, no overlapping text or broken images.

## What's weak

- Same green-water/reflection hero aesthetic and stacked-button CTA layout as three other CB2 emails I've opened this week — creative fatigue is real and this brand is the worst offender for it right now.
- No deadline anywhere. "Up to 60% off" with a dagger footnote but no end date makes it feel like clearance is just... always happening, which kills any reason to act today versus next week.
- "SHOP ALL" as the top button is the laziest possible CTA — send me to furniture or outdoor directly, don't make me pick from seven near-identical gray buttons.
- The sweepstakes banner ("Win $1,000") sandwiched between the hero and the products is a distraction — I don't need a review-to-win pitch when I'm mid-scroll toward the actual sale.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the sale cadence or at minimum differentiate the creative — I've seen this exact photo treatment from CB2 multiple times this week, rotate in something else or I stop opening these entirely.
- 2. Add a real end date to the clearance ("ends Sunday 8/17") — right now there's no reason to act now versus whenever.
- 3. Replace "SHOP ALL" as the lead CTA with the category I actually buy in — furniture and decor, based on what's already in the grid below.
- 4. Move the sweepstakes banner below the product grid, not between hero and products — it's breaking the path to the sale.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Clearance ends Sunday: up to 60% off furniture`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your reno list, up to 60% off (this weekend only)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dining chairs, tables, and beds — while supplies last`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The pieces you saved, now on clearance`
