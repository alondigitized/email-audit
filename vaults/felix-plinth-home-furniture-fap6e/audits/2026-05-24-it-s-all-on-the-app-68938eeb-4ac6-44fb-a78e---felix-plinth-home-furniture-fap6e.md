# IT’S ALL ON THE APP
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-24**
## Executive summary

- Fifth CB2 email in eight days, and now they want me to download an app. I'm a guy renovating two houses simultaneously — I do not need another loyalty app on my home screen. I need *fewer* notification sources, not more. The hero is at least visually nice — that dark vignette with the candle and the branches is on-brand for CB2 — but the actual ask ("MEET THE CB2 APP") is the exact wrong thing to put in front of someone who's already getting hammered by the Crate & Barrel family ecosystem (we're at 39+ family sends since Sunday per my own tally).
- The four-quadrant "GIFT EXCLUSIVE / EARLY ACCESS / TRACK REWARDS / PERSONALIZED" grid is fine but undifferentiated — Pottery Barn pitches the same exact bullets, Wayfair pitches the same bullets, every furniture retailer with an app pitches the same bullets. There's no specific signup carrot ($20 off first app order? Free shipping on first app purchase?). Just "DOWNLOAD NOW" and trust us.
- Would I click? No. Would I forward it? Definitely not — a friend renovating their place wants product, not a download prompt. Mark as read and move on.

## What's working

- Hero photography is genuinely good — moody, branded, that vignette aesthetic CB2 owns better than C&B does.
- The two-up "NEW + NOW" / "CB2 X LEMIEUX" tiles below the app pitch at least give me something product-shaped to look at if I scroll.
- Brand identity is unmistakable — the CB2 monogram top-and-bottom, the typography, the spacing. Visually CB2 every inch.
- The "LET'S CELEBRATE / GET 15% OFF" rewards block at the bottom is a clearer value prop than the app pitch above it.

## What's weak

- The app pitch is the lead, and there is zero incentive to download. No "first app order ships free," no "$20 off in-app." Just four generic benefit bullets I've seen on every retail app screen since 2018.
- Cadence is brutal. This is CB2 #5 in eight days on top of the C&B family avalanche. I'm in "permanent snooze" mode for this brand network.
- "GO AHEAD. PLAY FAVORITES." is meaningless copy. Article would have written something with a point of view; CB2 wrote filler.
- No specific product, no specific price, no specific deadline anywhere in the body. The 15% off birthday signup at the bottom is the closest thing to a concrete offer and it's buried.
- Tiny tap targets on mobile — that bottom rewards block has four CTAs crammed into a 2x2 grid I'd have to zoom to thumb correctly.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Bribe me to download.** "Get $25 off your first app order" or "Free shipping on your first in-app purchase" in the hero. Without it, this email has no reason to exist for an existing email subscriber.
- 2. **Pick one job per send.** Either pitch the app *or* pitch the CB2 x LeMieux collab. Don't half-pitch both. Article's brand-building send from last week worked because it committed to one beat.
- 3. **Acknowledge me as a known subscriber.** "You've been with us for a while — here's what's better in the app." Right now this reads like a cold acquisition email sent to a list I've been on for years.
- 4. **Kill the four-quadrant bullets.** Show one specific app-only thing — a screenshot of personalized recs, a rewards balance tile, a real screen — instead of generic icons.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Get $25 off — in the CB2 app`
- **Subject Alt B:** `App-only: track orders, earn rewards, save $25`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Download today and we'll knock $25 off your first app order.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Faster checkout, app-only previews, and a thank-you on us.`
