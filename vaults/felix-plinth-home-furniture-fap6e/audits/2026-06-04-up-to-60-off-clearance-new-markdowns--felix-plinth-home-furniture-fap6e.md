# Up to 60% off clearance + new markdowns!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- Forty-six. That's where the C&B family counter is sitting in my inbox now, and this is the fourth time today alone that some variation of "up to 60% off clearance" has shown up from this brand system. The Summer Clearance Event email dropped this morning. Then "NEW MARKDOWNS!" Then "More at up to 60% off." Now this one, from Crate & Kids specifically, with the same hero percentage, the same season, the same clearance story.
- What makes this one extra friction is the product section. "We love these for you" is a row of cribs and nursery furniture. I am renovating a second home and my primary. I am not, to my knowledge, expecting a child. If Crate & Kids has me pegged as a nursery shopper based on some signal I gave years ago, fine — but sending me four clearance emails in a single business day and having the product grid show me cribs is a fast way to get the unsubscribe hover. The hero offer is legitimately good — 60% off clearance on furniture is real money — but the kids content undercuts the relevance hard.
- I mark this as read. I do not click. I definitely do not forward it. At this point I'm genuinely thinking about whether I unsubscribe from Crate & Kids specifically and keep the Crate & Barrel list, the way I've mentally already ring-fenced CB2 from C&B proper.

## What's working

- **Hero is immediately legible.** "Up to 60% off + new markdowns" is big, bold, above the fold — no mystery about what this email is for.
- **Shop Clearance CTA is unambiguous.** There's a clear button right under the hero. No hunting required.
- **Category nav is useful.** Furniture, Rugs, Bedding, Decor links across the top means if I wanted to shop-by-room I could without scrolling far.
- **App 20% extra is a smart hook.** The app download promo with an incremental 20% off is one of the more differentiated signals in this send — CB2's app collab email earlier this week made me think about that. It's buried, but it's there.
- **No render bugs.** Clean layout, images load, nothing is broken.

## What's weak

- **Fourth clearance email today. Fourth.** Pottery Barn has sent twelve emails in 48 hours and at least varied the creative — Chris Loves Julia, new arrivals, different product stories. This is the same 60% off headline, same clearance message, fourth time today. The cadence is the email equivalent of someone tapping you on the shoulder until you leave the room.
- **"We love these for you" is cribs.** If this is personalization based on my browsing or purchase history, it's badly outdated or wrong. Showing me nursery furniture during a clearance push is a demographic mismatch that feels tone-deaf for a 36-year-old guy mid-renovation.
- **TheDesignDesk appears twice.** Once as a mid-email callout, once again near the footer. Pick one placement. The repetition makes the email feel padded.
- **No deadline anywhere I can see.** "Clearance" implies urgency but there's no "ends Sunday" or "while supplies last" visible. Wayfair's outdoor rugs email at least anchored its sale to a timeframe.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop the send to one clearance email today.** This is the highest-leverage change. Every additional send from the same brand system today is actively destroying the brand's inbox presence. Batch the story — one email, the best version, with all the new markdowns consolidated.
- 2. **Fix "we love these for you."** If my segment is renovation-focused, show me floor lamps, accent chairs, area rugs in that section — not cribs. Article nailed this with their kitchen island feature by keeping the product recommendations tight to the opened-email moment.
- 3. **Surface the app 20% in the subject line.** That's an actual incremental offer differentiator. Right now the app promo is buried below three other sections.
- 4. **Collapse TheDesignDesk to one placement** — the mid-email section is fine, cut the footer repeat.
- 5. **Add a soft deadline.** "New markdowns added through Sunday" creates a real reason to click today vs. filing it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New 60% off markdowns — plus 20% extra on the app`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Clearance just got bigger: see what dropped today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Furniture, bedding, rugs — the big reductions are live now`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `App shoppers get an extra 20% on top of clearance prices`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks user zoom — WCAG 1.4.4 failure. Low-vision users cannot scale content. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[MEDIUM] Wildcard `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; max-height:1000000px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly; line-height: 100%}
```
Applying `line-height: 100%` globally via `*` collapses line spacing on multiline elements. This is a known rendering defect on Gmail Android and Samsung Mail. The `max-height: 1000000px` hack (common Outlook fix) applied globally is unnecessary overhead.

**[MEDIUM] Conflicting desktop image `max-width` breakpoints**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
Because `min-width: 768px` is declared *after* `min-width: 640px`, it takes precedence at ≥768px screens — images cap at 600px, not 640px. At 640–767px screens they cap at 640px. This is likely unintentional and may cause sub-pixel layout gaps on large-screen clients.

**[LOW] Dark mode not supported**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Hard-locked to light mode. Apple Mail and Outlook on iOS will force-invert unsupported emails in dark mode, potentially rendering white text invisible on white backgrounds.

**[LOW] Duplicate and redundant meta tags**
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<!-- then individually: -->
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
Four `format-detection` declarations where one suffices. Also `<meta charset="utf-8">` appears twice (once as `http-equiv` Content-Type, once as bare `charset`). Not harmful but indicates template hygiene issues.

**[LOW] Unencoded `&` in `<title>`**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
Invalid XHTML — `&` must be `&amp;`. Some strict-mode parsers will reject this.
