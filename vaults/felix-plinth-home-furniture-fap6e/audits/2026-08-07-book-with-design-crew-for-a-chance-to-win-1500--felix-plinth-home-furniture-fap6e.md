# Book with Design Crew for a chance to win $1500
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- Okay, seventh Pottery Barn email in my inbox this week and this is the one where I actually paused. Not because of the sweepstakes headline — "chance to win $1500" reads exactly like the fine-print sweepstakes bait it is — but because "Design Crew" free appointments plus a real, dated in-store class ("Mixing Wood Finishes," Aug 9, Sunday 10AM) is the first genuinely useful thing this brand has sent me all week. I'm mid-reno on two houses and the recommended grid underneath — platform bed, curtain rods, rugs, a linen duvet cover, an extendable dining table — is scarily well-matched to an actual shopping list I could write right now.
- That said, "a chance to win" is the kind of subject line I've learned to discount instantly — it's the marketing equivalent of a scratch ticket, and Pottery Barn's own "Warehouse Sale" email from yesterday (8/6, scored an 8 from me) proved they know how to lead with a real number instead. I wouldn't forward this to a friend — nothing here is exciting enough to share — but I'd probably click through to book the free design appointment, mostly because it's free and I need the help, not because of the sweepstakes hook.

## What's working

- The "RSVP for Your Free Design Services" module is clean and specific — What/When/Where laid out like an actual invite, not vague lifestyle copy.
- The "Mixing Wood Finishes" in-store class has a real date and time (Aug 9, Sunday 10AM) — that's the kind of showroom-integration content I never see from Wayfair or CB2.
- "Recommended Just For You" is legitimately relevant to my reno: platform bed, curtain hardware, rugs, duvet cover, dining table — no kids' stuff, no off-category filler like the Crate & Kids send I dinged.
- The 10% Rewards card callout ("up to 10% back... across Pottery Barn, Williams Sonoma, West Elm, Rejuvenation") is real loyalty math, not a vague "join and save" nudge.

## What's weak

- Leading with "a chance to win $1500" instead of the free design service itself buries the actually-useful offer under sweepstakes bait — I trust "up to 60% off" (yesterday's Warehouse Sale) way more than "chance to win."
- This is the second design-services-adjacent send this week layered on top of "Art of Organization" (8/6) and the digital catalog (8/5) — my inbox is starting to feel like Pottery Barn is A/B testing hooks on me rather than sending me something new.
- No pricing or swatch/sample info anywhere near the product grid — Wayfair's "Open Box Steals" at least stamped "% off" on every single tile; here I have to click through blind to find out what a Reeva rug even costs.
- The sweepstakes asterisk and full rules block at the bottom (NO PURCHASE NECESSARY, official rules link, odds language) is the kind of buried-fine-print stuff the rubric flags — it's honest in that it's disclosed, but it's also a signal this is filler content dressed as an event.

## Recommendations

- 1. Flip the hero: lead with "Book a Free Design Crew Appointment" as the headline and demote the sweepstakes to a secondary line under it — the free service is the real offer, the $1500 is a bonus, not the draw.
- 2. Put a price or "starting at $X" on at least the top row of the product grid (bed, curtain rod) — CB2's restock email at least had product context; a blind product grid with no price is a tap-and-bail.
- 3. Consolidate — this is the third Pottery Barn "come talk to us about your space" flavored email in five days (Art of Organization 8/6, this one 8/7); pick one beat per week or I start filtering.
- 4. Give the "Mixing Wood Finishes" class its own subject line send for people near a store — burying a dated, RSVP-able event under a sweepstakes banner wastes a genuinely good local hook.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Get expert styling help for your space — no purchase required to book.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Round pedestal tables, rugs, and rod finishes picked for your current cart.`

# Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Design Crew Sweepstakes" Email

## 1. Technical Summary
Standard SFMC-served HTML email (table-based layout, `click.e.potterybarn.com` link wrapper) with widespread missing `alt` attributes, two legacy `http://` image assets, and a corrupted `<style>` block containing literal orphaned PHP `header()` calls. Compliance headers (List-Unsubscribe, Authentication-Results) were not observed in the captured message, though this may reflect relay behavior rather than sender omission.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All content links route through the SFMC click-tracking domain `click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=...` (e.g., the nav logo link and all three hero `slices_0N.jpg` CTAs) — standard ESP redirect wrapping, confirms Salesforce Marketing Cloud as the sending platform.
- 45 tracking/redirect links were skipped by the automated HTTP probe (expected for opaque `qs=` tokenized SFMC redirects — not itself a defect).
- Third-party ad-tracking pixels are present outside the ESP's own tracking: `secure.leadback.advertising.com/adcedge/lb?site=695501...` and `d.turn.com/r/dd/id/...` — both fire on open, independent of the SFMC open pixel (`svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=...&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=<recipient-email>`).
- The recipient's raw email address is passed in cleartext as a query param on two first-party PB endpoints (`svopn.potterybarn.com` open pixel and `potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=...`) — standard for SFMC open-tracking/preference-center prefill, not flagged as a defect, but worth noting since it's PII in a URL.
- The dynamic AMPscript slot at the top of the document is empty: `<!-- begin dynamic ampScript slot --> <!-- end dynamic ampScript slot -->` with no injected content — see Personalization below.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **34 images missing `alt` text** per QA scan, including the three primary hero banner slices (`0807_FRI_AM2_DC_Invite_SweepsBnr/slices_01.jpg`, `_02.jpg`, `_03.jpg`) and all product images (e.g. `farmhouse-platform-bed-1-l.jpg`, `banks-extendable-dining-table-76-128-1-l.jpg`). Since the hero banners carry the primary CTA/message as image text, blocked-image or screen-reader scenarios leave the email with no visible or announced content in that region.
- **2 images served over `http://`** (non-HTTPS): `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` and `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` — mixed-content, likely to be blocked or flagged by mail clients that enforce HTTPS-only image loading (both appear to be stale 2022 credit-card banner assets left in the footer template).
- **Corrupted `<style>` block**: the second `<style>` tag in `<head>` opens with four literal, unexecuted PHP statements before the actual CSS rules:
  ```
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
  ```
  These are invalid CSS and will simply be ignored by rendering engines (no visible breakage), but their presence indicates a template/build-pipeline defect — likely a server-side snippet that was meant to execute elsewhere and got pasted into the static HTML output instead.
- Standard Outlook/Apple Mail compatibility hooks are correctly present: `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, `.ExternalClass` / `.ReadMsgBody` resets, and attribute-selector media queries (`table[class="wrapper"]`, `img[class="photo"]`) used per the inline comment to work around Outlook's `.className` CSS handling.
- No other structural rendering issues found in the truncated source (table-based layout, explicit widths, `mso-line-height-rule:exactly` present for Outlook line-height control).

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The `<!-- begin dynamic ampScript slot -->...<!-- end dynamic ampScript slot -->` block at the top of the document rendered empty — no AMPscript output was captured/resolved in this HTML snapshot. This could be expected (slot legitimately empty for this send) or could indicate a personalization block that failed to populate; cannot be distinguished from static source alone.
- No merge-token syntax (`%%FieldName%%`, `{{...}}`) is visible in the truncated body — all visible copy is static creative.
- The only personalized element observed is the recipient email address embedded in tracking-pixel query strings (open pixel and `coreemail.html`), which is tracking data rather than in-content personalization.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header**: not found in the captured message (QA WARN). Note the QA tool itself flags this may be a capture artifact of the AgentMail relay rather than a true sender omission — SFMC sends typically include this header. Recommend verifying against raw SMTP headers at the mailbox/MTA level rather than the relayed copy.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058)**: not found — same caveat applies; if genuinely absent, one-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender rules) is not supported.
- **Authentication-Results (SPF/DKIM/DMARC)**: not found in the relayed headers — status unknown, cannot confirm authentication passed. Same relay-capture caveat as above.
- No unsubscribe link or physical-address footer was visible in the truncated HTML source provided, so CAN-SPAM footer compliance cannot be confirmed or denied from this excerpt.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- All outbound content links use the opaque SFMC redirect format `https://click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=ABB7...` — the actual destination URL and any UTM parameters are encoded server-side and not visible in the static source, so UTM/landing-page alignment cannot be verified from this HTML alone.
- One direct (non-wrapped) link is visible: `https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` — this is an open-tracking pixel `<img>` src, not a user-facing CTA, so it's not a navigation/landing-page concern.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Verify List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post / Authentication-Results directly against raw SMTP headers (bypassing the AgentMail relay) before treating these as real compliance gaps — the QA tool flags relay capture as a likely confound.
2. Add `alt` text to the three hero banner images at minimum (`slices_01/02/03.jpg` under `0807_FRI_AM2_DC_Invite_SweepsBnr/`), since they carry the primary message and CTA as image content.
3. Replace the two `http://` footer assets (`Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, legacy `0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`) with HTTPS equivalents or remove if the 2022 credit-card banner is no longer in active use.
4. Strip the orphaned PHP `header()` calls from the second `<style>` block in `<head>` — harmless to rendering but indicates a leaking build/template artifact worth cleaning at the source.
5. Confirm with the ESP/CRM team whether the empty AMPscript slot is expected for this send or a failed personalization block, since static analysis can't distinguish the two.
