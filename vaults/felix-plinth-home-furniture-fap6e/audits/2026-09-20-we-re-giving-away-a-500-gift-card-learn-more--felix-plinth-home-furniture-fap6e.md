# We’re giving away a $500 Gift Card! Learn more →
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-20**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids again — this is now the fourth or fifth kid-focused email I've gotten this month from the Crate & Barrel family, and I don't have kids. This one's a "rate and review for a chance to win a $500 gift card" sweepstakes wrapped around a kids' bedroom set (the Avery Indigo Stripe Upholstered Bed), then it sprawls into a Venus Williams tennis-themed kids collab, a baby-essentials sale, a wall of stroller and nursery furniture, a credit card pitch, and an app download push — all in one scroll. It's trying to be six emails at once.
- My gut: skip and archive. There's nothing here for a guy renovating two adult houses — no sectional, no dining table, no swatches, no shipping timelines for actual furniture I'd buy. The one thing that almost got me was "new! the card you love just got better" with Reward Dollars, since I'm a rewards-card guy by nature, but it's buried two-thirds down under strollers and a crib mattress, so by the time I'd scroll to it I've already checked out. Not forwarding this to anyone — I'd forward the actual "Thanksgiving Hosting Guide" (2026-09-20, 7/10) before I'd forward a kids-bed sweepstakes.

## What's working

- The $500 gift card hook up top is a real, specific incentive — sweepstakes with a hard deadline ("Contest ends Sept 30") at least reads as urgent and honest.
- Clean product grid lower down (Nuna strollers, Babyletto recliner, Naturepedic mattress) is well-shot and prices are visible with sale pricing called out in red — that's actual price transparency, which I appreciate as someone who checks rewards math before buying.
- The Crate Card / Reward Dollars callout is relevant to me specifically as a highest-rewards-card shopper — if it were the whole email instead of a footnote, I'd have read it.

## What's weak

- Zero relevance to my life: this is nursery gliders, cribs, baby car seats, and a kids tennis collab. I'm mid-renovation on two adult houses. Pottery Barn's "Ships free: cozy throws, pillows & more" (7/10) at least stayed in my lane the whole way through.
- Way too many campaigns stitched into one send — sweepstakes, Venus Williams collab, baby sale, product grid, credit card, app download, TheDesignDesk. Compare to CB2's "We perfected the fall bedroom refresh" (5/10), which was at least one coherent idea even if the copy overreached.
- The star rating graphic up top ("★★☆☆☆") is confusing bordering on broken — it reads like an actual low product review score sitting right under the headline, not a "rate this" prompt. That's a real visual miss, not just an irrelevance issue.
- No color-palette or swatch-and-sample content anywhere, no shipping-time clarity for the furniture pieces shown — for a brand under the Crate & Barrel roof I'd expect at least a nod to that.
- The subject line "We're giving away a $500 Gift Card! Learn more →" reads exactly like the kind of generic sweepstakes bait Wayfair and CB2 also lean on when they don't have a real hero offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into two sends: one sweepstakes/review-request email, one product email. Trying to do six things at once buries the one offer (Reward Dollars) I'd actually engage with.
- 2. Fix or remove the two-star graphic at the top — it looks like a bad review score, not a call-to-action. That's actively working against the "rate us" ask.
- 3. Move the Crate Card / Reward Dollars block to the top for anyone flagged as a cardholder or high-value shopper — don't bury the one segment-relevant offer under strollers and cribs.
- 4. Suppress this entire Crate & Kids stream for households without kids in profile data — I've now gotten this same nursery/stroller content four-plus times this month with nothing that speaks to an adult-furniture renovation.
- 5. If keeping the sweepstakes, tie the entry mechanic to a product category I actually shop (living/dining/bedroom) instead of routing everyone through kids gear.
