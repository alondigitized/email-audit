# Book FREE design help
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- Eighteen. I'm fairly confident this is the eighteenth Pottery Barn email since Saturday, and by all rights I should delete it reflexively — same as I did with the "ASAP" one yesterday and the telephone-emoji one the day before. But I didn't, because "Pottery Barn Design Services" is a different sender name than I've been getting, and "Book FREE design help" is the first subject line in a week that made me pause instead of reach for the archive button.
- Here's the thing: I'm renovating two houses simultaneously. A free design consultation with someone who knows Pottery Barn's catalog is genuinely, practically useful right now. I've been building a mood board for the primary home's living room and haven't committed on a sofa because I can't get the scale right. This email is offering to solve that problem for free. When I actually opened it, the hero delivered — "RSVP for Free Design Services" front and center, a clear appointment CTA, no buried asterisk promising it's only available in six stores in Wyoming.
- Then the email did what Pottery Barn always does: piled on. Below the design services hook you've got the entire Memorial Day sale grid, a "Have a Summer Series" in-store event promo, a personalized product grid, an Affirm mention, and a loyalty teaser. There's enough content here for four separate emails. I'd click to book the appointment. I would not forward this — there's too much noise for a clean share. But this is the first Pottery Barn email in four days that I filed instead of deleted.

## What's working

- **Subject line is the standout of the week.** "Book FREE design help" — 20 characters, zero caps-lock screaming, a concrete verb, a real service. After seventeen consecutive sale emails, this reads like a different conversation.
- **The hero delivers on the subject.** "RSVP for Free Design Services" is immediately visible, and "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" is the clearest CTA button I've seen from PB this month. No ambiguity about what clicking does.
- **Sender differentiation actually works.** "Pottery Barn Design Services" as the sender name signals segmentation — I presumably ended up on this list because of browsing or purchase behavior. That's more than Article or Wayfair have done for me this week; their blasts treat me like I opened a random catalog.
- **"RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" section has real personalization.** The product grid at the bottom shows items I actually recognize from my own browsing. Not sure how sophisticated the algorithm is, but it doesn't feel like a random homepage dump the way CB2's "Find your favorite new thing" email did.
- **"Earn 10% Back in Rewards" visible in the footer.** I'm always running the math against my Amex rewards rate, so I register this every time. It's not buried.

## What's weak

- **The email loses its thesis about halfway down.** Once you scroll past the design services section, it turns into the exact same Memorial Day sale grid I've seen in the last four emails — "UP TO 50% OFF FURNITURE & DECOR," "UP TO 50% OFF BEDDING & BATH." If you're going to send a Design Services email, commit to it. The sale content dilutes the segmentation signal and makes this feel like a hybrid template that nobody fully owns.
- **The "Have a Summer Series" block is a third story with no setup.** In-store event? Date of June 5 or so? I don't know what this is or why it's in a design-services email. Cut it or give it its own send.
- **No specificity about what "free design help" actually means.** Is it an in-store appointment? A video call? Does someone come to my house? For someone furnishing two properties simultaneously, that answer changes whether I click immediately or spend five minutes trying to find out before giving up.
- **Affirm in the footer feels tacked on.** For large furniture across two renovations, buy-now-pay-later is theoretically relevant — but it's tucked below the social icons in small type, not connected to any specific product or scenario.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to the design services story for the entire email.** Remove the Memorial Day sale grid from this send. The sale already has sixteen other emails. This one has one job: get someone to book a consultation. Every section that isn't about that dilutes the CTA.
- 2. **Answer the format question in the email body.** Add a three-line explainer: "30-minute video call or in-store visit. One-on-one with a certified designer. No purchase required." That copy removes every friction point I have before clicking.
- 3. **Tie the personalized product grid to the appointment.** Instead of "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" as a floating grid, frame it: "Tell your designer what you're considering." That makes the products feel like conversation starters, not random sale inventory.
- 4. **Cut the Summer Series block entirely** from this template. Reserve it for a standalone events email in early June.
- 5. **Surface the rewards math for the session outcome.** "Earn 10% back on anything you purchase after your consultation" — put that near the CTA, not in the footer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your designer is waiting — free consult, no strings`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Two homes? One free design session changes everything`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `30 minutes with a Pottery Barn designer. Pick a time that works.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Tell us what you're working on — we'll bring the room plan.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has two confirmed broken resources (403 on a credit card promo link, 403 on a West Elm spacer GIF), pervasive missing alt text across ~30+ images, two HTTP-sourced images that will be blocked in modern clients, and PHP header calls embedded raw in a CSS `<style>` block — likely a template artifact that will appear as visible text in some parsers.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — 403 on credit card banner link:**
```
https://www.potterybarn.com/pages/credit-card/?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember
```
This link is inside a commented-out `<!-- -->` block in the current source, so it does not render. However, if the comment is ever re-enabled (it appears to be a toggle), it will 403 immediately.

**FAIL — 403 on cross-brand tracker/spacer:**
```
https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif
```
Used as a 1×1 tracking pixel or layout spacer. Returns 403, meaning the image loads broken and any associated open-tracking tied to this asset will fail silently.

**25 click-tracking links skipped** by QA probe — all route through `click.e.potterybarn.com`. Cannot confirm destination integrity without redirect resolution; no structural issue found in the redirect wrapper itself.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP headers in `<style>` block (critical template bug):**
```html
<style>
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
...
```
These are raw PHP `header()` calls pasted into a CSS `<style>` tag. They are not valid CSS. Depending on the mail client, this text may render visibly or cause the stylesheet to fail parsing entirely. This looks like a copy-paste from a PHP image-serving script that was never stripped before template assembly.

**HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources — 2 confirmed:**
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook all block mixed-content HTTP images by default. These footer images will render as broken.

**Missing alt text — 30+ images including all body slices:**
Every content image (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_08.jpg` in the body, all 18 footer slices, all product images) has `alt=""`. Since the email is almost entirely image-based, recipients with images disabled (a common corporate mail policy) see a blank email with no recoverable text. Screen reader users get nothing actionable.

**Spacer/filler characters in preheader:**
The preheader padding div uses both Unicode Ogham space marks (`͏`) and soft hyphens (`­`) — standard technique, no issue.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header not found** — QA could not confirm presence via AgentMail relay. If truly absent, this violates RFC 2369 and Google/Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender requirements (mandatory for >5k/day senders).

**List-Unsubscribe-Post not found** — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is absent or not captured. Required by Google/Yahoo bulk sender policy; failure results in deliverability penalties.

**Authentication-Results header not found** — SPF/DKIM pass status could not be verified through the relay. Cannot confirm DMARC alignment. If SPF/DKIM are failing, this email is at risk of spam folder placement or rejection.

Sending domain `e.potterybarn.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain — DKIM alignment likely configured at the ESP level, but cannot confirm from available data.

The credit card promo URL uses `cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember` as a campaign parameter — non-standard (not UTM). The body CTA click links all route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirect tracking; UTM parameters on final destination URLs cannot be confirmed without resolving the redirects.

It will return whatever status code that page returns; if the page is ever taken down or redirects, open tracking breaks.
