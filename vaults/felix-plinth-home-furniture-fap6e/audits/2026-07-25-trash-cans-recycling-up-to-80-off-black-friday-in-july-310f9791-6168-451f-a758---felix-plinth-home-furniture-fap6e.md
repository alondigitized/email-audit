None of it is trash cans.
- The offer itself is fine on paper — 80% off, everything ships free, Wayfair Rewards footer for people like me who've got points sitting there. But the subject-to-content bait is what sticks. - The 80% off + free shipping banner up top is bold and unmissable, no scrolling needed.
- Star ratings and strikethrough pricing on the grid items give me something concrete to compare — the $259.99 outdoor heater "was $410" at least reads like real math.
- Wayfair Rewards, Pay Over Time, Wayfair Professional icons at the footer are a nice loyalty touch since I do have points banked.
- Clean render, no broken images or overlapping text — better craft than some of CB2's cluttered restock alerts.

## What's weak

- Zero trash cans or recycling anything. The subject line is the entire pitch and the email doesn't deliver a single item that matches it — that's a bait-and-switch, not a merchandising miss.
- "Black Friday in July" for the fourteenth time this week has completely lost meaning — Pottery Barn's "UP TO 70% OFF" at least didn't lean on a fake calendar event.
- The grid is the same undifferentiated mix I've scrolled through in every other Wayfair send — a playset next to a wine fridge next to a bar cabinet tells me nothing about who I am as a shopper.
- No swatch-and-sample program, no room-based curation like Article's living room/dining room rotation, which is the thing that's actually earned my click lately.

## Recommendations

- 1. Match the subject to the content. If you're going to promise trash cans, show me trash cans — a real category send, or drop the bait subject entirely.
- 2. Cut the "Black Friday in July" banner. It's the fourteenth time this framing has hit my inbox and it now reads as noise, not urgency.
- 3. Segment the grid by project stage or room, the way Article does — I'm mid-reno on two houses, show me "kitchen" or "primary bath," not a random dresser-chair-playset mashup.
- 4. Add a swatch/sample CTA somewhere for the furniture pieces — that's the one thing that would actually move me toward a click on a cabinet or headboard.
