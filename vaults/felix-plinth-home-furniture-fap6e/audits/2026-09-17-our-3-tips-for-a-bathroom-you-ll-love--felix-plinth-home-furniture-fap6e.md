# Our 3 tips for a bathroom you’ll love →
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-17**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is actually the one I've been waiting for. "The Design Desk" breaking down bathroom foundation → essentials → natural elements as three numbered steps is exactly the kind of structured, project-based content that fits where I am right now — mid-reno on both houses, and bathroom is genuinely one of the rooms still up for grabs on the second place. It's a real departure from the "20% off baby essentials" Crate & Kids stuff that's been clogging this inbox all week, and it actually beats Pottery Barn's "Meet Rejuvenation" email from earlier — that one was cross-promo filler, this one is an actual project guide with a Book a Free Appointment CTA that speaks to someone actually renovating.
- Where it loses me is the back half — this is a genuinely enormous email. After the three tips wrap, it just keeps going: towels, a kitchen checklist, "your house, your Sunday," "we love these for you," a rewards teaser, an app download pitch. By the time I hit the kitchen section I've mentally closed the loop on "bathroom email" and I'm scrolling through what feels like four different newsletters stapled together.
- I'd click on tip #1 (the vanity/fixtures shot) or "Book A Free Appointment" — that's a real CTA tied to a real project. I wouldn't forward it to a friend, but I might screenshot the natural-elements section for my own reference since I'm still deciding on fixtures for house two.

## What's working

- The numbered "1. foundation, 2. layer in essentials, 3. natural elements" structure gives me an actual framework, not just a product dump — closest thing to Article's home-tour format that scored well with me before.
- "Book A Free Appointment" as a repeated CTA speaks directly to someone mid-renovation, not just browsing.
- The vanity/mirror hero shot at the top is clean, on-brand, neutral palette — no jarring color clashes.
- Star ratings under products (faucet, shower curtain, sconce) give me quick confidence signals without needing to click through.

## What's weak

- This email never ends — bathroom tips bleed into towels, then a full kitchen checklist, then "your house, your Sunday," then a rewards/app pitch. Pick one subject and stop.
- No pricing or % off anywhere in the bathroom section — just "Shop Now" buttons. Compare to Crate & Kids' "up to 60% off clearance" (which I didn't want, but at least it was a real number).
- The "clean kitchen checklist" section is a total non-sequitur dropped into a bathroom email — it actively dilutes the one good idea this email had.
- Rewards messaging ("it all adds up... chance at $500") is buried near the bottom after I've already mentally checked out.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this email in half — ship the three bathroom tips as their own standalone send, and let towels/kitchen/Sunday-house content live in separate emails. Right now it reads like three newsletters got merged under one subject line.
- 2. Add a real offer to the bathroom section — even 15% off vanities or free shipping on Shop All Bath would give me a reason to act today instead of just "someday."
- 3. Move "Book A Free Appointment" higher and make it the singular CTA for the whole top section — right now it competes with three separate "Shop Now" buttons per tip.
- 4. Drop the kitchen checklist entirely from this send — it breaks the one clear narrative thread this email built.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Foundation, essentials, and the finishing touches — plus a free design appointment`
