# ENDS TODAY: Up to 50% off & 2x Rewards!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-26**
## Executive summary

- This is the Crate & Kids arm of the Memorial Day blitz — the fifth distinct C&B family sender since Sunday, and the one that has absolutely no business in my inbox. I have no children. I'm renovating two adult homes. The sage dresser in the hero is genuinely pretty, the photography is clean, and the offer is stacked correctly. And none of that matters because I'm looking at nursery cribs and baby bedding and I am not the person this email was built for.
- The parent brand already sent me 60% off earlier the same day. Now the kids sub-brand shows up at 50% with a narrower catalog. I noticed the discount gap before I finished reading the hero. That's not a great first impression when the C&B family has already lit up my inbox fifty-some times since Friday.
- I'd mark it as read without clicking. I wouldn't forward it. I'd probably shoot myself a note to clean up my C&B subscription preferences this weekend.

## What's working

- The hero layout is excellent — sage dresser on light blue, "ends today / up to 50% off / The Memorial Day Event" in a clear visual stack, and a Shop the Sale CTA that's impossible to miss.
- The photography is genuinely good. Even as someone who has no use for the product, I recognized the catalog aesthetic immediately as premium.
- Double Rewards callout mid-email is a smart secondary hook for loyalty members — it changes the math on an already discounted purchase.
- Clean render throughout: images loaded, no overlapping text, no broken layout gaps.
- The sender display name ("Crate & Kids SALE") makes the sub-brand immediately legible — I knew what I was opening before I clicked.

## What's weak

- I am not a parent, and every product in this email is for a child's room. The segmentation failure here is complete. Compare to how Pottery Barn sent me "Less than 24 hrs for EXTRA 20% OFF + Explore The Bloom Shop" — also in the Memorial Day window — and the Bloom Shop content was adult, aspirational, and squarely aimed at someone renovating a living space. Completely different intent-signal discipline.
- The parent brand offered 60% off in the same-day inbox. Crate & Kids comes in at 50%. That gap felt like a demotion, not a targeted sub-brand offer.
- The app download promo mid-scroll is dead weight — I'm already in the email, I'm not looking for a second commitment.
- This is email 51-ish in the C&B constellation since Sunday. Even Wayfair — who sent me twenty-two emails, culminating in a "STAIR TREADS." subject line — had more variety. The fifth C&B sender doesn't add; it compounds fatigue.
- TheDesignDesk block at the bottom arrived after so much product grid scrolling that I almost missed it — and it's arguably the most interesting content for someone actively designing a home.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the segmentation before everything else.** A "not shopping for kids?" banner linking me to the main C&B Memorial Day sale would have saved this email completely. One link. One retained customer. Article managed to send me a clean last-chance email that at least showed me adult furniture — this could have done the same.
- 2. Move the Double Rewards callout into the hero — it earns more real estate than a mid-scroll block and it's the strongest loyalty signal in the email.
- 3. Kill the app download promo or move it to the footer. It interrupts a sale email with a separate conversion ask.
- 4. If the parent brand is running 60% the same day, either match it or frame the 50% intentionally ("handpicked kids' picks at 50%"). Let me know it's a different offer by design, not by omission.
- 5. Preheader needs to add information the subject doesn't carry — category-specific or product-type language would do it.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Double your Rewards before tonight — up to 50% off kids`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cribs, dressers, bedding, and more — all in one last day`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta blocks user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users from zooming in on mobile. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Dark mode explicitly disabled with no fallback**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Forces light rendering in Apple Mail and other dark-mode-aware clients. This is an intentional choice, but means the email will not honor system dark mode, potentially rendering poorly for dark-mode users unless all background/text colors are explicitly set throughout.

**Conflicting `img` max-width media queries — 768px rule is dead code**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }  /* declared first */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }  /* declared second — wins at all widths ≥640px */
```
At any screen ≥768px, both queries match. The 640px rule is declared later, so it wins via cascade. The 768px / 600px rule is never applied. Either remove the 768px block or reverse declaration order if 600px cap was intentional for wider screens.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
The tag appears four times: once with a combined value string, then three times individually for `telephone=no`, `address=no`, and `date=no`. Redundant but harmless; minor template hygiene issue.

**Universal `*` rule with `max-height: 1000000px`**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; max-height:1000000px; ... }
```
Applying `max-height` to every element is a known email hack to prevent Gmail clipping, but it can produce unexpected overflow behavior in some clients. Acceptable if intentional, but worth flagging.
