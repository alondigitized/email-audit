# 10 new, tech-packed & beautiful kitchen must-haves
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-15**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one's actually kind of great — no Venus Williams anywhere in sight, and it's a genuine numbered kitchen roundup that lands right in the middle of my actual reno decision-making. KitchenAid, Le Creuset, DeLonghi, Ninja, Caraway, JURA — that's real brand density, not filler, and half of it is tagged "exclusive" or "new," which at least makes me feel like I'm seeing something before the algorithm shows it to everyone else. The numbered "1, 2, 3..." format through a literal top-10 also makes it scannable on my phone in a way most of these emails aren't.
- That said, it's basically a wall of "Ships Free! / new + exclusive! / Shop Now" repeated ten times with almost no differentiation in urgency or price — nothing tells me why today versus next week. I'd scroll the whole thing on my commute, probably tap on the Le Creuset Château or the JURA espresso machine since those are the two I've actually been eyeing for the second house kitchen, and I'd screenshot the roundup into my inbox folder rather than forward it — this is a "save for later" email, not a "hey look at this" email.

## What's working

- The numbered top-10 format is genuinely useful for someone mid-appliance-decision like me — I can scan and pick my two or three without reading paragraphs.
- Multiple "new + exclusive" callouts (Le Creuset Château, DeLonghi Magnifica, Ninja Midnight Mocha, JURA Diamond White) give me a reason to look now instead of waiting for the next sale.
- "Ships Free!" badge on nearly every item removes the one thing I always have to check before buying big kitchen gear.
- Product photography is clean and consistent — moody, warm-toned kitchen scenes that actually look like a kitchen I'd want, not a stock catalog shoot.
- The "It's all on the app, including your chance at $500" teaser with a phone mockup at least tries a loyalty/incentive angle instead of just another discount banner.

## What's weak

- Zero pricing anywhere. Ten products, zero dollar signs — for big-ticket stuff like a JURA espresso machine or a 10-piece Le Creuset set, I want at least a "starting at" number before I tap through.
- No actual sale or percent-off — "Ships Free" is the extent of the offer, which is thinner than what I'm used to from this same inbox lately.
- It's ten near-identical "product + Shop Now" blocks stacked vertically with almost no visual hierarchy — nothing tells my eye where to land first, so the "top 10" framing does more work than the design does.
- The "$500" sweepstakes teaser at the bottom is vague — no odds, no deadline, no clear entry mechanic, just a badge floating near a phone screenshot.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add price points (or "from $X") to at least the top 3-4 hero items — Article's home-office send worked because it paired a specific desk + chair with a real styling story; this email has the products but skips the number that actually gets me to click.
- 2. Break the visual monotony — give the #1 and #2 picks (KitchenAid Luxine, Le Creuset Château) a bigger hero treatment and shrink the rest into a grid, the way CB2's "Fall Sale" send used one dominant image instead of ten equal blocks.
- 3. Turn "Ships Free!" into a real, time-bound offer — a deadline the way CB2 did with "Fall Sale is over tomorrow" would push me from "save for later" to "buy today."
- 4. Clarify the $500 sweepstakes mechanic in one line ("shop the app for a chance to win $500, ends [date]") instead of leaving it as a vague badge.
- 5. Cut the list to a tighter 5-6 "best of" instead of 10 — right now it reads like inventory-clearing, not curation.
