# Take the tour: Venus Williams’ dream kid’s bedroom! 🎾
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-24**
## Executive summary

- This is the fifth Crate & Barrel Kids send I've counted in two days, and my first instinct was to snooze it. The render came through tiny, so I'm judging layout and headlines, not fine print. The Venus Williams bedroom tour is a good editorial idea. The striped rug, the mushroom lamp and the arched mirrors are the kind of thing that goes in my Pinterest-adjacent folder.
- It's a long scroll, though. It starts with a tour, then moves to a Venus Williams collection, then "up to 60% off clearance," a $5,000 sweepstakes, a wallcovering launch, product picks, a credit card pitch, an app pitch and a design desk. There isn't a single offer. I'm renovating two houses, and a kid's bedroom isn't on either list, so the relevance is thin. I'd save the room photos for inspiration, but I wouldn't buy.
- Verdict: I'd open it, skim the photos, and not click. I wouldn't forward it, unless a friend with a kid was redoing a room.

## What's working

- The room photography is lovely. The stripes, the arched mirrors and the dome lamp give me a palette to steal.
- Venus Williams as a named designer is a real hook. It's more interesting than another fake customer quote like last week's "So happy this came back!" send.
- The "Shop the Room" and "Shop the New Collab" buttons are clear, and each sits under its own section.
- "We love these for you" puts real products with prices in the back half, which is where I'd actually shop.
- Clearance up to 60% off is at least a concrete number.

## What's weak

- It's far too long and has too many ideas. Tour, collab, clearance, sweepstakes, wallcovering, credit card, app and design desk all compete, and the eye has no single place to land.
- No offer leads the email. The only discount is buried mid-scroll, and "up to 60%" isn't framed against anything.
- The cadence is exhausting. It's the fifth Kids send in two days, after "Holiday toys for babies, toddlers & kids!" and the Halloween "selling fast" send.
- Pottery Barn does something similar with its maroon strip every send, but at least it's consistent. This one has no consistent through-line.
- The sweepstakes and credit card sections feel like filler. Nothing here speaks to rewards math or shipping on furniture, and I can't see delivery timing on the dressers.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut it to one story: the room tour and its shoppable pieces. Move the clearance, sweepstakes, card and app blocks to a separate send.
- 2. Put one offer or one dated reason to act in the hero strip.
- 3. Add shipping and lead-time info to the furniture picks. I need to know if a dresser lands before the holidays.
- 4. Tag each photo with its product name and price so I can save it and buy later.
- 5. Space out the sends. Two days of near-identical promos reads like noise.

  - `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png?mi_u=…`, repeated 6 times.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (confirmed by QA).** About 35 `<img>` tags are flagged. They fall into two groups:
  - **Content images.** There are about 17 of these on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/…` (and `/m/47/bbf6f9d4-….png`), plus the scene7 GIF. With images blocked, these render as blank space.
  - **Tracking pixels.** The rlcdn, `mi.`, `dv.` and `o.gif` pixels need `alt=""` plus `width="1" height="1"`. This is low severity.
- **Zoom is disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="… maximum-scale=1" />` prevents pinch-zoom, an accessibility failure.
- **Invalid markup under the declared doctype.** `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` has an unescaped `&`, which is invalid XHTML 1.0 Transitional. It should be `&amp;`. The title is also generic and doesn't match the subject line.
- **Redundant or leftover head markup:**
  - `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" …>` and `<meta charset="utf-8">` are both present.
  - `format-detection` is declared three separate times, with duplicate `telephone`, `address` and `date` entries.
  - The unfilled template comment `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` is still in the source.
- **CSS conflicts and breadth:**
  - `@media (min-width:768px){img{max-width:600px}}` and `@media (min-width:640px){img{max-width:640px}}` overlap. At 768px and wider, the later 640px rule wins, so the 600px rule has no effect.
  - The global `*{line-height:100%}` and `p{margin:0 auto; text-align:center}` are broad and can cause unintended inheritance.
  - `.mobile{width:460px!important}` is a fixed width on narrow viewports.
