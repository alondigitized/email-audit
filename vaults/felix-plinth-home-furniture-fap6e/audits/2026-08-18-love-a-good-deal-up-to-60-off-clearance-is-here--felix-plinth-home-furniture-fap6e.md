# Love a good deal? Up to 60% off clearance is here
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-18**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "up to 60% off clearance" — my ears perked up for about two seconds, then this thing just kept scrolling. This is a genuinely massive email: a beach-photo hero, then eight category tiles (furniture, outdoor, kitchen, tabletop, lighting, decor, rugs, bedding), then a clean-kitchen push, then a SMEG toaster in "exclusive Matte Mocha," then a Design Sweeps entry with a $5000 giveaway, then a kitchen checklist PDF-style module, then a "we love these for you" product carousel, then a credit card pitch, then an app download push. It's less an email and more a full site nav dumped into my inbox.
- The clearance categories are the one thing that's actually relevant — I'm furnishing two places right now, so "furniture" and "rugs" tiles genuinely made me pause. But by the time I hit the SMEG toaster and the credit card ad, I'd mentally checked out. This is Crate & Barrel's fourth-plus send to me this week (I flagged the apartment-collection one from 2026-08-17 as the good one, at 7-8/10), and this one feels like the "let's clear inventory + cross-sell everything" grab-bag in contrast. I'd click into the furniture or rugs tile specifically, skim past everything else, and definitely wouldn't forward this — there's nothing screenshot-worthy in here, just a wall of tiles.

## What's working

- The hero is clean — beach photo, "up to 60% off clearance" in bold serif, single "Shop Now" button, no clutter up top.
- Category tiles are genuinely useful for someone furnishing two homes — furniture, rugs, and lighting tiles map directly to what I'm shopping for.
- The rugs and bedding tiles show real texture/pattern close-ups, which is the kind of detail I actually care about when picking finishes across two spaces.
- "While quantities last" on the tile grid at least signals real urgency instead of vague evergreen language.

## What's weak

- This email is exhausting — clearance hero, then SMEG toaster, then Design Sweeps giveaway, then kitchen checklist, then product carousel, then credit card, then app download. Article's "Your life, your living room" send at least stayed focused on one theme; this one has no editorial spine at all.
- The SMEG toaster and "clean kitchen" modules have nothing to do with the clearance offer I opened for — it's a bait-and-switch structure, not a clearance email.
- No actual discount math anywhere below the hero — "up to 60%" but every product tile after that just shows full price with no strike-through, so I can't tell what's actually on sale.
- The credit card pitch buried near the bottom feels like a stranger-treatment moment — no "welcome back" or loyalty-tier callout, just a generic card ad mid-scroll.
- Compare to CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" send (7/10) — that one stayed on-message with the sale theme start to finish. This one wanders into toasters and credit cards halfway through.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this email in half. Kill the SMEG toaster, clean-kitchen checklist, and credit card modules entirely — they dilute a clearance offer that should be the whole story.
- 2. Put actual clearance pricing on the product tiles — strike-through original price + sale price on at least the furniture/rugs items, since "up to 60% off" with no visible math anywhere reads as vague.
- 3. Move the "we love these for you" personalized carousel (bed frames, dining tables) up near the top, right under the hero — that's the most relevant content for me and it's currently buried two-thirds down.
- 4. Drop the Design Sweeps $5000 giveaway or make it its own send — a sweepstakes CTA competing with a clearance CTA splits my attention for no reason.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Clearance just dropped: 60% off, real inventory`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns on furniture, rugs & lighting — shop before it's gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Prices this low don't last. See what's on clearance now`
