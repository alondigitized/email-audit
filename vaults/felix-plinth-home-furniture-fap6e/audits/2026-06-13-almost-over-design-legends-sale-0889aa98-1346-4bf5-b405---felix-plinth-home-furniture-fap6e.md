# ALMOST OVER: Design Legends Sale
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-seven. That's the CB2 count since mid-May, and I want it on the table before anything else, because it is still the loudest thing about any email that comes from cb2@mail.cb2.com at this point. "ALMOST OVER" hits differently when I watched "HOURS LEFT" run through on June 9th, didn't click, then watched "WAREHOUSE SALE IS ON" on June 10th — which actually earned attention because it was the first honest volley of that campaign — then "SO EARTHY" tried to reset the mood on June 11th, then "SOFAS FOR SUMMER" revved everything back up yesterday. I know this playbook. I have it memorized. The urgency language is fully depleted for me right now.
- What I did not expect was the editorial turn inside the email. Gianfranco Frattini. Paul McCobb. Steen Østergaard. Archival portraits, real mid-century designers, named sections. This is CB2 doing what CB2 is actually good at — design credibility, European seriousness, the kind of content that belongs in a physical catalog. It broke through my fatigue the same way the June 10th Warehouse Sale email broke through: it was genuinely different in register. If I'm being honest, the Frattini section made me pause.
- Then the "WIN A NEW LIVING ROOM" sweepstakes block appeared, and the spell broke. The credibility that three mid-century design legends lent the top half of this email evaporated into prize-wheel energy. I'd slot this into my design folder and maybe return to it. I would not forward it. I would not trust that the urgency is real.

## What's working

- The "Design Legends" editorial framing is the strongest thing CB2 has sent me this week — archival designer portraits instead of lifestyle shots is a real differentiator and earns genuine attention
- Per-designer section structure (Frattini → McCobb → Østergaard) creates a browse path I can navigate mentally before clicking anything, which is how I actually shop furniture
- Visual hierarchy is clean and immediate — my eye goes straight to the "DESIGN LEGENDS SALE" hero, then drops naturally into the designer sections, no hunting required
- The percentage-off offer is visible in the hero without scrolling — baseline competence that the June 11th "SO EARTHY" email could not manage
- No visible render bugs: layout holds, images loaded, text is sharp

## What's weak

- "ALMOST OVER" is dead on arrival — I've been watching CB2 urgency cycle for five days straight and the phrase registers as background noise at this point, not a real deadline
- The "WIN A NEW LIVING ROOM" sweepstakes block fractures the editorial tone; the email earns credibility through Frattini and Østergaard and then immediately trades it away for a contest gimmick that smells like list-building
- Article linked me directly to a specific product hero on their "Summer hosting made simple" email; CB2 almost certainly drops me on a filtered sale landing page, which adds friction for the most interested buyer
- Cadence is actively destroying the "current campaign" signal — this is the fourth sale-urgency email in five days from the same sender, and I can no longer tell if this is a new message or the same assets with fresh copy bolted on

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a real deadline in the subject or kill the urgency** — if the sale ends tonight at 11:59 PM, say that. If it ends Sunday, say Sunday. "ALMOST OVER" after a week of fake-urgency emails is costing CB2 trust faster than it's closing sales.
- 2. **Cut the sweepstakes block or move it to a post-footer placement** — the Frattini/McCobb/Østergaard editorial is the best thing about this email and the sweepstakes actively undermines it; they are not the same tone and they should not share a scroll
- 3. **Link each designer section to a curated collection page, not to a generic sale landing** — Wayfair's June 12th "Open Box steals" email earned an 8 from me partly because it gave me a specific destination; "Shop Frattini →" is infinitely more actionable than "Shop the Sale"
- 4. **Subject should name a specific designer or piece** — the "Design Legends" label is doing okay work but won't cut through CB2 fatigue; a real name is more specific and more surprising

### 7. Recommendations

1. **Strip the template comment** (`<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT...]-->`) from the send template immediately; add a pre-send linter rule to flag `<!--[` patterns.

2. **Deduplicate `@font-face` for "Lineto Brown Sub"** — it is declared three times in `<style>` with different `src` URLs. The last declaration wins in CSS cascade, meaning the first two blocks (including the `.eot` Outlook fallback) are silently overridden. Consolidate into one block with proper format hints.

3. **Remove `maximum-scale=1`** from the viewport meta or replace with `user-scalable=yes` to restore pinch-zoom for accessibility compliance.

4. **Scope the wildcard rule** — replace `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` with `body{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` to avoid unintended suppression of system scaling in nested elements.

5. **Audit cross-domain font loading** — `@font-face` pulls from both `cb2.com` and `crateandbarrel.com`. Confirm both origins have CORS headers permitting email client fetches; some clients block cross-origin web fonts without `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`.

6. **Widen mobile breakpoint** from `418px` to `480px` to cover the full standard mobile-client range.
