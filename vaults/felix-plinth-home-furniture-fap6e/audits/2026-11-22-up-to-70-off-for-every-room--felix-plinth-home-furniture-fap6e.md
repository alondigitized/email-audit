# UP TO 70% OFF for every room
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-22**
## Executive summary

- Pottery Barn again. That's the same maroon "Early Black Friday, up to 70% off" banner I've now seen five or six times since 11/16, and it opens this one too. My gut reaction was a shrug, because I've lost count of the sends. This one at least earns its keep with the room-by-room structure. I'm renovating two houses, so "bedroom, living room, dining room" is how I actually think about a project.
- The dining table and the cream linen bedroom are the parts I'd save. The Portola bed and the Toscana extendable table go into my Pinterest-adjacent folder, and I'd click through to the dining table. But the price details are tiny, and nothing here helps with the real question on furniture, which is when it arrives. Yesterday's "delivered in 1-4 weeks" email at least gestured at lead times.
- Verdict: I'd click once, on the dining section, and I wouldn't forward it. The "Recommended Just For You" block is a weak spot. It's stockings and a throw, which isn't me and isn't what I've been browsing.

## What's working

- The room-by-room layout (timeless bedroom, cozy living room, inviting dining room) matches how I plan two renovations at once.
- The photography is warm and styled. The Portola bed and the dining table with the pup in frame made me want to save them.
- The "SHIPS FREE" tags on bedding, throws and pillows are small but clear.
- The "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" card block speaks directly to how I buy. I always use the highest-rewards card.
- The "Book a Free Appointment" Design Crew block is a real showroom and service hook.

## What's weak

- It's the same maroon hero I've seen all week. Nothing signals that this send is different from the thirteen before it.
- The product prices ("Was $X, Now $Y") are microscopic. I'd have to zoom to read them, and on a phone the tap targets for the small tiles are tight.
- There's no delivery or lead-time information on any of the furniture. For a sofa or dining table, that matters more than the percentage off.
- "Recommended Just For You" is Christmas stockings and generic throws. That isn't personalized to someone who's been looking at furniture.
- The footer is a wall of fine print with the exclusions buried at the bottom. "Up to 70%" with legal text that long doesn't feel fully honest.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put one hero product with a real price at the top, such as the Toscana table with its sale price and a delivery window. Drop the generic banner.
- 2. Make the price text at least twice the size, and add a "Delivery in X weeks" line under each furniture item.
- 3. Make the recommendations reflect what I browsed, like the dining table and bed, instead of Christmas decor.
- 4. Move the rewards card block up and show the dollar math, for example "$500 back on a $5,000 order."
- 5. Vary the creative between sends. The same banner for a week trains me to skip it.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your bedroom, living room & dining room, on sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Portola bed, Toscana table and more. Delivery windows inside.`

# Technical Audit: Pottery Barn "UP TO 70% OFF for every room"

## 1. Technical Summary
This is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional email built almost entirely from sliced images. About 46 images have no alt text, three assets load over plain `http://`, and the recipient's email address sits in plaintext in two URLs. Compliance and authentication headers could not be verified. The HTML is truncated, and the headers appear to have been stripped by the AgentMail relay.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **57 tracking links were not probed.** They are on click-redirect domains, e.g. `https://click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=ABp7Im...`, so destinations and redirect health are unverified.
- **Tracking pixels are loaded over plain HTTP.** `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=<recipient email>` is a non-HTTPS open pixel and may be blocked or rewritten by clients.
- **There are two other third-party pixels.** `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/.../cat/1` and `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/742304fa-...?mi_cid=...&mi_mid=...` are present. Both lack alt text, and the `d.turn.com` pixel is a third-party ad-network beacon.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **About 46 images lack alt text.**
  - Body offer slices: `slices_01.jpg` to `slices_12.jpg` under `/pb/2026/US/1122_SUN_AM_EBF_SavingsAcrosstheHome/`.
  - Product images: `classic-velvet-stockings-l.jpg`, `faux-fur-ruched-throw-l.jpg`, `toscana-extendable-dining-table-60-125-l.jpg`, and others.
  - Banners: `H26_Bottom_*.jpg`.
  - Footer: `Email-Footer/slices_01.jpg` to `slices_18.jpg`.
  - Because the offer content is image-sliced, it is unreadable with images blocked or by a screen reader.
- **Three images use `http://`.** They are `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, `0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`, and the `svopn` pixel.
- **The viewport meta blocks zooming.** `maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-zoom.
- **Markup gaps:**
  - `<title></title>` is empty.
  - `<html>` has no `lang` attribute.
  - Several layout tables lack `role="presentation"`, e.g. the spacer `<table cellpadding="0" cellspacing="0">` after the logo.
- **Server-side code leaked into the CSS.** The second `<style>` block contains `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: no-store...` and similar. It is harmless but invalid CSS and a template-hygiene problem.
- **A cross-brand asset is referenced.** `https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` is a spacer served from another brand's CDN, with no alt text.
- **A web font link is unlikely to work.** The Google Fonts `<link>` in `<head>` is stripped by Gmail and many other clients.

## 3a. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **The recipient email is exposed in plaintext URLs.**
  - `https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
  - `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?...&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
  - The second URL is non-HTTPS, so the address is sent unencrypted.
- No unresolved merge tokens are visible in the truncated source.

## 5. Compliance
- **`List-Unsubscribe` was not found.** This is likely a relay-capture artifact, so it is unconfirmed.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) was not found.** One-click support is unverified.
- **`Authentication-Results` was not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The `From` domain `e.potterybarn.com` matches the tracking domain, which is a positive sign for alignment but not proof.
- **The footer could not be checked.** It is an 18-slice image sequence with no alt text, so any unsubscribe or address text inside it would be inaccessible. It is not visible in the truncated HTML, so the unsubscribe link and physical address are unconfirmed.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- **UTM parameters could not be verified.** The `?qs=` tokens are opaque and the links were not resolved.
- Product images come from `www.potterybarn.com/pbimgs/...`, but landing-page alignment is unconfirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive alt text to all offer, product and footer slices, with `alt=""` on spacers and pixels. Put real text in the footer unsubscribe and address.
2. Move the three `http://` assets to HTTPS, and ask the ESP to serve the `svopn` pixel over HTTPS.
3. Stop putting the raw email address in query strings (`coreemail.html?email=`, `sv_sveme=`). Use an opaque subscriber ID.
4. Remove `maximum-scale=1`. Set a `<title>` and `<html lang="en">`. Delete the leaked PHP `header()` lines from the `<style>` block.
5. Re-run QA against raw `.eml` source to confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results`. Resolve a sample of the 57 tracked links to check UTM parameters and landing pages.
