# Beautiful storage that keeps you organized
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- Nine. This is the ninth Pottery Barn email in four days. After the sale onslaught — "LAST DAY," then "HOURS LEFT," then apparently more hours — this one lands and just says *hey, storage.* No countdown clock, no fake expiration, no percent-off. I actually exhaled a little. Storage is exactly where my brain is living right now: I'm trying to figure out built-ins for the second home's mudroom and a media credenza situation for the primary. So the category hit. But the email mostly just pointed at stuff; it didn't close.
- The hero is warm, natural materials — open shelving, wicker, that whole catalog-bungalow look I've had pinned for eighteen months. The breakdown by room type (laundry, kitchen, entryway, bath) is genuinely how I think when I'm kitting out a whole house. That's not nothing. But there's no price, no sale, no specific product that made me feel urgency. And the "Recommended Just for You" section dropped in what looked like upholstered beds — in a storage email. Beds. Fine personalization algorithm you've got there, PB.
- I'd mark it, not click it. Might revisit if I'm browsing Saturday morning. Wouldn't forward.

## What's working

- **Category relevance is real.** Baskets, buffets, bookshelves, shelves — with a secondary row breaking down by room (entryway, laundry, bath) — that's exactly how you think when furnishing two houses at once. This is the right editorial bucket.
- **Palette cleanser after a sale-spam marathon.** The "Free design services" CTA at the bottom — same as the email that ran earlier today — actually registers now. I'm more receptive when I'm not being screamed at about a countdown clock.
- **No render bugs.** Layout is clean, warm hero image renders, category grid is readable, section breaks are logical.
- **Rewards callout is visible.** "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" is down the page but present. Since I'm running every purchase through a points card anyway, stacking rewards is always a signal I'll notice.
- **Subject line is human.** 41 characters, no all-caps, no emoji stack, no exclamation marks. After "🚨 LAST DAY" and "🕰️ HOURS LEFT," this reads like a real person wrote it.

## What's weak

- **No offer means no pull.** Editorial is fine in theory but after I've been trained for a week that every Pottery Barn email contains a discount, I open this expecting a hook and find none. I checked twice.
- **"Recommended Just for You" is beds.** In a storage-focused email. Whatever signal the algorithm read to surface upholstered bedroom furniture in the middle of an organization send, it's wrong. Wayfair's personalization misfires this badly regularly; I expect better from PB.
- **The hero CTA copy is generic.** "SHOP STORAGE" and "SHOP MODULAR FURNITURE" — these go to category pages, not a curated room or a tight product story. Crate & Barrel's editorial sends at least anchor around one hero SKU with dimensions and a price. This feels like a sitemap dressed as an email.
- **Cadence is still a problem.** Doesn't matter how good this individual send is — it's number nine in ninety-six hours. There's attrition happening whether the creative team knows it or not.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the hero to one specific product.** Pick the best-selling storage unit — that open credenza in the hero image looks like the Lorraine or the Elden, whichever it is — name it, show a price, link directly to the PDP. "The Elden Modular Cabinet — starting at $899, ships in 2-3 weeks" beats "SHOP STORAGE" every single time for someone actually shopping.
- 2. **Fix the personalization section or cut it.** "Recommended Just for You" showing bedroom furniture in a storage email is worse than showing nothing. If the algorithm can't surface relevant product, pull the section entirely.
- 3. **Add a room-type hook for renovation shoppers.** "Building a mudroom? Start here" or "Whole-room bundles" would have caught me at the exact pain point I'm in. This email knows I buy storage — it should know *where*.
- 4. **Throttle the send cadence.** This isn't a one-email fix, but if I'm getting nine emails in four days, I start filtering the entire sender into a folder and missing the ones that actually matter.
- 5. **Surface "Free Design Services" earlier.** It's buried below two full content sections and the recommendations module. For a renovation shopper who needs help with built-ins, that CTA is the highest-value thing in this email — it should be above the category grid, not below it.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Room-by-room picks so you stop staring at empty shelves`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Modular, quick-ship, and just your style — shop what fits now`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed broken links will produce dead-end experiences for affected recipients. The email also has systemic accessibility gaps (50+ images missing alt text) and three HTTP image sources that security-conscious email clients will block.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**BROKEN — 403 on credit card apply link:**
```
https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682
  ?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY
  &external_id=PB_header_nonmember&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember
```
The Key Credit Card banner CTA returns HTTP 403. Any recipient clicking "Apply Now" hits a dead end. This is the highest-priority fix.

**BROKEN — 403 on West Elm spacer pixel:**
```
http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif
```
This cross-brand pixel 403s and also uses HTTP (see section 3). Likely a stale asset reference from a shared template.

**24 tracking links skipped** — click-redirect domains (`click.e.potterybarn.com`) were not probed; destination URLs behind those redirects are unverified.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (3) — will be blocked by Gmail, Outlook, and most modern clients:**
| Filename | URL |
|---|---|
| `spacer_0.gif` | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` |
| `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` |
| `slices_02.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` |
| Open pixel | `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` |

All four should be served over HTTPS. The open-tracking pixel on HTTP also means open events may not register in clients that enforce mixed-content blocking.

**50+ images missing `alt` text** — the entire main content slice stack (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_14.jpg`), all footer slices, product thumbnails, and banner images carry no `alt` attribute. Screen readers will produce no useful output for the email body. Examples:
- `F26_Top_NewArrivals.jpg` — top-of-email banner, no alt
- `slices_01` through `slices_14` (main content) — no alt
- All 18 Email-Footer slices — no alt

**Invalid CSS inside `<style>` block:** The following PHP header directives appear literally inside a `<style>` tag and will be rendered as broken CSS by all clients:
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are server-side PHP statements that were accidentally copy-pasted into the stylesheet. They produce CSS parse errors and may cause style rules that follow them to be dropped in strict parsers.

**`<title>` tag is empty** — `<title></title>` with no content. Some clients (notably Apple Mail) surface the title in previews or accessibility trees.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not captured** — the QA relay did not surface this header. If it is absent (not merely uncaptured), the email is non-compliant with CAN-SPAM and will be flagged by Gmail's bulk sender enforcement (required for senders at volume).

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found** — one-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders as of Feb 2024. Absence risks inbox demotion.

**SPF/DKIM authentication status unknown** — `Authentication-Results` header not captured by the relay. Cannot confirm DMARC alignment. Sender domain is `e.potterybarn.com` (ESP subdomain); alignment should be verified at the ESP level.

**`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />`** — this suppresses referrer data on all outbound clicks, which conflicts with UTM tracking (see section 6).

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Referrer suppression kills analytics fallback:** The `no-referrer` meta tag means that if UTM parameters are stripped (e.g., by a redirect), GA4 will record the session as Direct with no email attribution. UTM params must be present on every destination URL and should not rely on referrer as a backup.

UTM parameter presence on destination URLs cannot be verified because the 24 click-redirect links were not resolved. This is a gap — UTM attachment should be confirmed at the ESP/redirect layer.
