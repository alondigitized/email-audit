# Happy first day of SUMMER! Enjoy UP TO 70% OFF
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-two. That's the Pottery Barn count in my inbox now, and this one lands as the **second email from them on the literal first day of summer** — the rugs-plus-70%-off send hit me yesterday (June 20), three emails landed on June 19, and now here we are again with essentially the same hero. The email itself is competently assembled: clean "Summer Sale / Up to 70% off" hero, the category nav strip across the middle (FURNITURE, OUTDOOR, SOFAS & CHAIRS, BEDDING, DÉCOR, RUGS — actually useful when you're shopping two renovation projects simultaneously), and a lifestyle shot warm enough to make me want to sit in it.
- Then I got to the "Recommended Just For You" row and lost the thread. A curtain rod bracket. A platform bed. Blackout curtains. And then what appears to be an ice cooler. I'm mid-renovation on a primary and a second home. Not outfitting a tailgate. The algorithm misfired visibly, which undercuts the entire "personalized" pitch. The Free Design Services block and the 10% back in Pottery Barn rewards callout are both legitimately useful for someone routing five-figure furniture spend through a rewards card — those I noticed. But at this send volume, with the same percentage splashed across every subject line since Thursday, I'm not clicking anything unless it's something I already have tabbed.
- Mark as read. Forward to no one.

## What's working

- **Category nav strip is actually functional for a multi-room shopper** — I can jump straight to OUTDOOR or RUGS without wading through a homepage, which matters when I'm shopping both houses in the same session.
- **Free Design Services block is the sleeper hit** — two active renovation projects means I'm genuinely in the market for free layout consultations; this surfaced at the right moment even if it's buried mid-scroll.
- **10% back in rewards callout** — I route big furniture orders through a rewards card anyway; a reminder that Pottery Barn's own card stacks on top of a sale is a real argument for me.
- **Lifestyle hero is visually warm and summer-correct** — the imagery earns the seasonal angle even if the copy doesn't.
- **"Up to 70% off" is a concrete, scannable number** — unlike Article's recent Cillian Collection send (which scored well on editorial voice but never named a discount), PB at least gives me the math upfront.

## What's weak

- **Same offer, fifth send in three days** — the June 20 rugs email had the same 70% headline; June 19 had it twice. At this point "up to 70% off" reads like wallpaper.
- **"Recommended Just For You" section is visibly misfired** — an ice cooler has no business appearing in a renovation-focused recommendation block; it signals PB has no real read on where I am in my purchase journey.
- **No deadline or end-date anywhere visible** — "Summer Sale" could mean July 4, could mean August; CB2's "Even more markdowns just added" send at least implied inventory urgency. This one has no clock.
- **Affirm block at the bottom feels misplaced** — for a $300 pillow, BNPL is unnecessary friction that cheapens the premium positioning PB is trying to maintain everywhere else.
- **The "first day of summer" hook should be doing more creative work** — it's reduced to a subject line throwaway; the body doesn't connect the seasonal moment to any curated assortment or limited window.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the ice cooler recommendation** — or if it has to be there, gate product recs behind actual browsing/cart behavior before surfacing them. One off-persona rec tanks the entire "just for you" framing.
- 2. **Give the sale an end date, even if it's approximate** — "Ends Sunday" or "Through June 29" immediately changes how urgently I engage versus "Summer Sale" floating in time forever.
- 3. **Merge or suppress the cadence** — one well-curated send on June 21 (the actual seasonal moment) instead of five near-identical ones across three days. The June 20 rugs email scored a 9 for me precisely because it had a specific product focus; this one loses that edge.
- 4. **Lead the "Recommended For You" section with renovation-specific categories** — RUGS, LIGHTING, OUTDOOR FURNITURE are where my money is right now. Curtain hardware is fine; a cooler is not.
- 5. **Move Design Services higher** — for someone juggling two projects simultaneously, that CTA is worth more than SHOP ALL SALE but it's buried after the lifestyle image.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Mixed-content / HTTP image sources (3 instances)** — Will be blocked by default in Gmail, Outlook.com, and most modern clients:

| Image | HTTP Source |
|---|---|
| `spacer_0.gif` | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` |
| `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` |
| `slices_02.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` |

The `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` and `slices_02.jpg` are served from `edm.potterybarn.com` itself — the HTTPS versions of these paths very likely exist; this is a hardcoded `http://` reference that should be a simple find-and-replace fix.

**Alt text missing on 43 images** — Nearly every content image (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`, all product images, footer slices) has no `alt` attribute. With images blocked (the default in many clients on first open), the email renders as a wall of blank boxes with no text fallback.

**Stray PHP header() calls inside a `<style>` block** — The following appears verbatim inside a `<style>` tag in `<head>`:

```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```

These are PHP server-side directives accidentally left in the CSS. They are inert in HTML email but indicate a template assembly error — a dynamic image-serving script's PHP headers were pasted into the wrong context.
