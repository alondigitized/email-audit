# Your new favorite landing spot
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- "Love at First Sit" — okay, that's a decent pun, and this is the most cohesive CB2 send I've seen from them in a while. No banner stacking, no "two sales end tonight" chaos, no marble bit running parallel to something else. It's just four sofa/sectional stories — Sonora, Eastman, Ambroise, Belmond — each with a moody lifestyle shot and a one-line design note. This is basically a designer sitting me down and walking me through a new collection, which I don't hate.
- The problem is there's no offer anywhere. Zero percent-off, zero dollar figure, nothing. I'm mid-renovation on two houses right now and my Pottery Barn folder has actual math in it — "60% off," "up to 40%." This is just pretty pictures of velvet sectionals with nothing pulling me toward "buy this today." It's a lookbook, not a marketing email, and CB2's own "THE AUGUST SALE" from two days ago actually had a number attached. This one doesn't even try.
- Would I click? Maybe, out of idle curiosity on the Belmond since it's the closest to what I actually need for the second-home living room. Would I forward it? No — there's no hook, no deal, nothing screenshot-worthy to send a friend except "look at this couch," which isn't really a forward-to-a-friend moment unless we're actively couch-shopping together.

## What's working

- Clean single-column story format — no competing sales banners or overlapping promos, a relief after CB2's own "marble that elevates everything" mess from three days ago.
- The Sonora/Eastman/Ambroise/Belmond naming + "hand-assembled in North Carolina" and "designed in Barcelona" details give it real showroom-quality texture — feels like a catalog, not a blast.
- "CB2 Interior Design Services... complimentary" is genuinely useful for someone renovating two houses at once — that's a callout I'd actually read twice.
- Loyalty math is present at the bottom: "Earn 10% back in Rewards Dollars" — good, they didn't bury it entirely.
- The furniture itself is squarely in my lane — dark velvet sectionals, walnut coffee tables, exactly the palette I'm chasing for both houses.

## What's weak

- No offer, no urgency, no percent-off anywhere above the fold or below it — compare that to Pottery Barn's "Warehouse Sale, up to 60% off" which I scored an 8 on specifically because the number stopped my scroll.
- Four near-identical sectional stories in a row starts to blur together — by Belmond I've lost track of which one had the amber-toned velvet versus which had the deep lounging seat.
- "Leave us a review, WIN $1000" feels tacked on at the bottom, disconnected from everything above it — no context on odds or how many winners.
- This is the third CB2/Crate & Barrel-family email I've opened this week and none of them have referenced each other or offered any kind of bundle/loyalty tie-in across the family, which Wayfair at least attempts with cross-category "must-shop steals."

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real number somewhere in the hero — even "New arrivals, up to 20% off" would beat pure lookbook mode. This is the single biggest lift available; right now there's zero reason to act today versus next month.
- 2. Cut the four sofa stories to two and give the other two real estate to the Design Services CTA — that's actually differentiated content for someone like me mid-renovation, and it's buried at the bottom right now.
- 3. Add a swatch/fabric-sample CTA next to at least one sectional — velvet and "amber-toned" fabric are exactly the kind of thing I need to see in person before dropping sectional money, and CB2 has zero swatch mention here.
- 4. Move "Earn 10% back in Rewards Dollars" up near the CTA buttons instead of stranding it in the footer strip — that's a real incentive being wasted.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Velvet sectionals, hand-assembled in NC — plus free design consults`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a standard MSO/Outlook-compatible HTML template (VML/conditional comments, table-based layout) served from CB2's ESP (`mail.cb2.com` / Bronto or similar via `image.mail.cb2.com`), with 10+ third-party ad-sync pixels from `dotomi.com` and no observable authentication or unsubscribe headers in the captured transmission.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 67 tracking/click-redirect links were present but skipped by automated HTTP probing (redirect-domain links are not resolved by the QA tool) — link destinations are unverified.
- 10 third-party ad-sync pixels load over plain HTTP from `ads.dotomi.com` (`pub1.php` through `pub10.php`, e.g. `http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2437/pub1.php?d98e199bf5027afea04b790655b3dee2=1`) plus one from `login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController`. Non-HTTPS resources are commonly blocked or stripped by webmail clients enforcing mixed-content policy, meaning these ad-sync calls likely fail silently for a large share of recipients.
- Two additional first-party tracking pixels are present: `https://mi.cb2.com/p/up/09aebc5b21344cf7/o.gif` and `https://dv.cb2.com/o/0a5d06ff-386f-41a2-a610-3e926e3405cc` — both HTTPS, no issue there.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 6 product/hero images are missing `alt` text (IDs: `6fe42451…`, `49228348…`, `d44c30ae…`, `3faaed17…`, `83c6fc9a…`, `a600cd52…`, `4addc93d…`, `b3987305…` under `https://image.mail.cb2.com/lib/fe9213727564027a72/m/1/`).
