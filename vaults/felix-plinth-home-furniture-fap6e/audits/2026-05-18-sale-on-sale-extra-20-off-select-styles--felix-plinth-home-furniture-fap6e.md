# Sale on sale 🎉 EXTRA 20% OFF select styles
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- Eleven. This is the eleventh Pottery Barn email in roughly seventy-two hours, and today alone I've gotten two — this one landing directly on top of "So many NEW Memorial Day deals" from earlier this morning. I had to double-check the timestamps. They're both from today. The cadence has crossed from aggressive into actively counterproductive, and I say that as someone who has the Pottery Barn Key rewards card in my wallet right now.
- That said, if I force myself to judge the email on its own merits: the offer is structurally real. Extra 20% off sale styles stacks legitimately — I already did the math on this morning's send. And the "Recommended Just for You" module is pulling the right inventory for me: that outdoor wicker chaise and the farmhouse platform bed are both on my live consideration list for the second house. The algorithm is not wrong. Which is what makes the saturation so maddening — Pottery Barn had me, and they're blasting me into numbness.
- I'd mark this read without clicking. Not because the offer isn't valid — it is — but because I already saw it this morning. I'll revisit the chaise on my own schedule. Not forwarding this to anyone.

## What's working

- The "Recommended Just for You" module is doing real work: outdoor lounge chair, platform bed, full outdoor seating set — these are live renovation SKUs for me, and Crate & Barrel's tabletop send from earlier this week couldn't have cared less about my actual purchase history.
- "Extra 20% off" is legible above the fold without scrolling. Wayfair's clearance blasts bury the actual discount number under emoji soup; this is cleaner.
- The rewards callout ("Earn 50% Back in Rewards") is the single best reason I prioritize Pottery Barn over Wayfair at similar price points — surfacing it this prominently is smart, and it's the one thing this email does that Wayfair never does.
- Affirm placement is appropriate for the SKU range. Outdoor furniture and bed frames are exactly where buy-now-pay-later reduces cart abandonment.
- The category quick-links grid (Outdoor, Furniture, Bedding, etc.) gives me a direct route without depending on whatever the hero algorithm chose.

## What's weak

- "Sale on sale" is the most synthetic construct in retail copy after "doorbuster." I know what it means, but it reads like they're daring me to find the math. No visibility on what the pre-stack base-sale price was before the extra 20% lands.
- Two emails today. One email was pushing it. Two is a statement of disrespect for my attention. Article's welcome email had more considered pacing; even CB2, which also over-sends, spaces their shots better than this.
- The "Recommended Just for You" products show zero pricing. I can see the chaise I want and still can't tell if the 20% makes it actionable without clicking out. CB2's "Now & Future Classics" send showed comparative prices in the grid — a clear upgrade on this pattern.
- No deadline date anywhere in the hero. "Memorial Day" is doing all the urgency work, but I'm inferring an end-date. Crate & Barrel's tabletop send last week made the expiry explicit; this doesn't.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress the same-day duplicate.** One email per day ceiling during the Memorial Day window, full stop. The marginal revenue from a second same-day send does not survive the unsubscribe erosion I'm experiencing in real time.
- 2. **Surface the stacked math in the hero.** Show me: original price → sale price → extra-20%-off price. Three numbers, right there. Don't make me open a calculator.
- 3. **Add a hard deadline to the hero.** "Ends May 26 at midnight PT" is worth more than three exclamation points.
- 4. **Put prices on the recommended products.** Even a range ("From $299 after extra 20%") would close the gap between "I see this and I want it" and "I'll click now."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 20% off sale — ends May 26`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your chaise. Your bed. Your 20% off.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stack it: sale prices + extra 20% = the math actually works.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Outdoor chairs, beds, and bedding — see what extra 20% does to the price.`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Sale on Sale" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard 403 failures (broken links) are present in the email — one on a credit card apply banner, one on a cross-brand spacer image. The email also carries 3 HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources and 38 images with no alt text, creating both rendering risk and accessibility failures.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Credit card banner link returns 403**
`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618...`
The `KeyCC_Banner_Certs_Amp_Updates_v2` module click target is dead. Users clicking the credit card apply/join CTA receive an error page.

**[FAIL] West Elm spacer image 403**
`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
Cross-brand asset is unreachable and served over HTTP. Any client that blocks mixed content will also fail to render it (double failure).

**[WARN] 24 click-redirect links unverified**
All primary CTAs route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` — destination integrity is unconfirmed by QA probe.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (mixed content — 3 instances):**
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Gmail and Outlook.com strip HTTP image sources by default. These assets will be silently blocked for a significant portion of recipients.

**Alt text absent on 38 images**, including all body content slices (`slices_01a.jpg` through `slices_12.jpg`), all footer slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), and product images. With images disabled, the email renders as a blank white block — zero fallback text conveys the sale offer, discount amount, or any CTAs.

**Injected PHP headers in `<style>` block:**
```html
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
Raw PHP `header()` calls appear literally inside a `<style>` tag. These are inert in HTML email but indicate a template assembly bug — a server-side script fragment was pasted into the wrong context. Browsers/clients will parse them as garbage CSS and may log errors.

**`<title>` is empty.** Minor, but some clients display the title as a tab label or preview string.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** — may be stripped by the AgentMail relay before analysis, but cannot confirm presence. CAN-SPAM and Gmail/Yahoo sender requirements mandate this header.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected** — one-click unsubscribe required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy (>5k/day). Absence risks inbox placement degradation.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header absent** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status unconfirmed through relay. Sending domain is `e.potterybarn.com`; alignment should be verified at the MTA level.

**Preheader references "Memorial Day"** — the subject line does not. Verify offer timing against the send date (2026-05-18, the day before Memorial Day weekend begins — acceptable but borderline).

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

No UTM parameters were detectable in the visible `href` targets — all clicks route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirect URLs with opaque query strings. UTM injection (if any) happens post-redirect at the ESP layer. This is standard for ESP-tracked links but means:

- QA cannot verify UTM values without resolving each redirect chain
- If the ESP redirect is misconfigured, GA4/analytics receives untagged direct traffic

The 403 on the credit card banner click target means that CTA's conversion path is completely severed — no landing page is reached regardless of UTM state.
