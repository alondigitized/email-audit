# 👀 MUST-SEE DORM ROOM DEALS 👀
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- Fifty-seven. That's where my Wayfair count sits, and I want that number in the room before I say anything else, because "MUST-SEE DORM ROOM DEALS" landing in my inbox on June 17 — four days after the June 15 outdoor rugs, the June 15 chaise lounge "last call," and the June 16 summer essentials double-tap — is a genuine segmentation failure. I'm 36. I'm managing renovation budgets on two properties simultaneously. My Wayfair cart right now has a credenza, an area rug, and a platform bed frame. Nothing about that history says "please send me freshman move-in content."
- Inside the email, the bones are actually decent. Deal Week is a solid event structure — same logic CB2 used for their Warehouse Sale, which scored an 8 for me because a named campaign gives you permission to show a lot of SKUs without it feeling like carpet bombing. The discounts are real: 40–50% off is meaningful. Some of those product tiles (chairs, bedding, rugs) could pull me in under a different frame. But "dorm room" in the hero anchor poisons the send the moment it hits my eyeballs. The subject line already lost me before I clicked.
- I would not click. I would not forward. I'd mark it as read and feel vaguely annoyed that Wayfair still can't tell I'm a 36-year-old with two renovation projects in flight rather than a college freshman loading a U-Haul.

## What's working

- **Deal Week event framing** is the right structure — a named, time-bounded sale gives the email a purpose beyond "we're discounting stuff again."
- **Discount stack is aggressive**: 40% off in the hero, 50% across product tiles — those are concrete numbers I can actually evaluate.
- **Star ratings visible on product tiles** give me baseline social proof without requiring a click; that's good in-email utility.
- **Visual hierarchy holds**: offer hero at top, product grid below, single "shop all sales" CTA anchoring the bottom — the layout doesn't fight itself.
- **Wayfair Rewards visible in the footer** — brand equity reminder at a glance, which matters for loyalty-points shoppers like me.

## What's weak

- **"Dorm room" in the hero is the entire problem** — there is no universe where this targets me correctly. Crate & Barrel's Warehouse Sale last week featured the same discount depth framed around adult living and scored an 8; same deal, completely different audience lock-in.
- **ALL CAPS + double 👀 emojis in the subject reads as inbox spam** — this is exactly the visual pattern that trains me to skip without reading.
- **"Picked just for you" is clearly not picked for me** — the product tiles don't connect to anything I'd actually browse; the personalization label is doing more work than the algorithm behind it.
- **Cadence is genuinely broken** — four Wayfair promotional touches in three days means I'm arriving at this email already fatigued. Pottery Barn manages to deliver twice-weekly sends that still feel curated; Wayfair at this volume feels like a content fire hose.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the segmentation first** — "Dorm Room Deals" should never reach an adult account with zero dorm-category browse history. Everything else is downstream of this error.
- 2. **Put the Deal Week time-bound in the subject** — it's the best urgency hook in the email and it's buried in the body; reversing that order alone would lift open rate.
- 3. **Kill the double emoji and ALL CAPS** — one tasteful callout or nothing; the current combo telegraphs "promotional bulk send."
- 4. **Collapse the send cadence**: a single "Deal Week is live" email plus one "final day" reminder is a complete campaign. Four emails in three days is an unsubscribe driver.
- 5. **Resolve the hero to a room type based on browse history** — "bedroom," "kitchen," "living room" instead of "bedding & dorm sets" would make this feel like it knows me.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Deal Week is live: up to 50% off bedroom, kitchen & more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Deal Week picks — 40–50% off, ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Top-rated pieces across every room, all marked down through the weekend.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Bedroom, kitchen, living room — your browse categories are all on sale.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Gmail strips `<head>` styles entirely.**
All responsive rules (every `@media only screen` block) and the `@font-face` declaration live in `<head><style>`. Gmail's CSS inliner discards these, meaning:
- No mobile breakpoints fire in Gmail (web and Android app), serving all subscribers a desktop-width layout.

The `mso-font-alt: "Arial"` fallback is correctly set, so Outlook degrades safely. Gmail gets system-default sans-serif — acceptable but unintentional.

**Fixed-height containers will clip dynamic content.**
At `max-width: 480px`:
```css
.ProductRec-name  { height: 48px !important }
.ProductRec-price { height: 40px; vertical-align: top }
.ProductRec-salesCopy { height: 28px; vertical-align: top }
.ProductRec-stars, .ProductRec-shippingCopy { height: 16px }
```
Any product name or price string longer than the fixed height is silently cropped — no `overflow: hidden` paired with `text-overflow: ellipsis` to signal truncation.

**`word-break: break-all` on `.ProductRec-name`.**
This breaks mid-character, not mid-word. A 12-character product name can render as `Desk Or`/`ganizer` across two lines. `word-break: break-word` or `overflow-wrap: break-word` is the correct cross-client value here.
