# FINAL DAY: Up to 50% off our summer sale→
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-10**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids, again — that's the fourth or fifth Crate & Barrel family email camping in my inbox this week, and I don't even have kids. The top banner says "back-to-school sale: up to 20% off," then two inches below it the hero says "up to 50% off summer sale" — two different numbers before I've even scrolled, which is exactly the kind of sloppy stacking that makes me trust an email less, not more.
- The "we love these for you" personalization block is stroller seats, a crib mattress, and a kids' dresser — nothing that maps to a guy renovating two adult homes. I'm not annoyed exactly, more just clocking that this list clearly wasn't built for me; it's whatever Crate & Kids sends everyone who's ever bought from the C&B family. Compare that to CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" send from earlier this week (7/10) — that one at least stayed in adult furniture lane and felt like it knew who I was.
- Not opening the app, not booking TheDesignDesk appointment, not clicking. Mark as read, move on. Wouldn't forward it — nothing here is worth a friend's two seconds unless they've got a nursery to fill.

## What's working

- The nursery hero shot is genuinely well-styled — clean palette, calming, on-brand for the "tranquil bedroom" aesthetic C&B has been running all week.
- Category grid (furniture, kids bedding, baby bedding, decor, rugs, clearance) is easy to scan and clearly labeled.
- "Ships free" tags on a couple of categories are a real, visible signal, not buried fine print.
- The Crate Card "earn Reward Dollars" callout is a legit loyalty hook if I were already a cardholder.

## What's weak

- Headline math conflict right at the top: "up to 20% off" banner vs. "up to 50% off summer sale" hero — pick one number.
- This is Crate & Kids content in my inbox and I have no kids — the personalized product picks (crib mattress, stroller car seat, baby dresser) have zero relevance to me.
- Email tries to do six things at once — summer sale, fall arrivals, backpacks, the Crate Card, an app download sweepstakes, TheDesignDesk — nothing gets to breathe.
- "500+ new fall arrivals starting at $29" mid-email undercuts the "summer sale, final day" urgency above it — feels like two campaigns duct-taped together.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the number conflict — one headline offer, one number, top of email. Don't make me reconcile 20% vs 50%.
- 2. Cut the personalization block entirely for non-parent subscribers, or actually segment by purchase history — right now it reads like a default feed, not "we love these for you."
- 3. Split fall arrivals and the Crate Card pitch into a separate send — this one's trying to be a clearance email, a new-arrivals teaser, and a credit card pitch simultaneously.
- 4. Move the urgency ("FINAL DAY") into the hero visual itself, not just the subject line — nothing in the body signals a clock is running out.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call — nursery & kids sale ends tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cribs, dressers, bedding — everything ships free through midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your cart's about to lose the discount. Shop before it's gone.`
