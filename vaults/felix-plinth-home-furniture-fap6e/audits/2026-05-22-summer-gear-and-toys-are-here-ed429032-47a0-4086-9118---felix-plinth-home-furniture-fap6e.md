# Summer gear (and toys!) are here…
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-five. I think this is twenty-five from the Crate & Barrel family since Sunday, and this one manages to be both the second Crate & Kids email in a single day and the least furniture-adjacent thing they've sent me all week. The first one today — "All the things to finish their dream room" — at least gestured at room furnishing. This one opens with what looks like a sunhat and a backpack. I'm over here pricing out area rugs and pendant lights for two simultaneous renovations and Crate & Kids wants to sell me a stroller.
- The email is clean enough — no broken images, reasonable grid layout, recognizable brand presentation. But "summer gear and toys" is doing zero work for my use case. The hero doesn't immediately shout a discount, there's no hard deadline in sight, and the ellipsis in the subject line reads like the copywriter trailed off mid-thought. Article sent me one email this week with "Up to 40% off ends Monday" and I went straight to their site. This? I'd scroll to the product grid, see nothing I needed, and mark it read.
- Not clicking. Definitely not forwarding. This is a "good to know they sell summer stuff, I'll come back if I need a kids' backpack" send — which means it's doing nothing for me right now.

## What's working

- **Seasonal timing is right** — summer launch content in late May is exactly when it should land. No argument there.
- **Clean layout** — the grid is readable, images are loading, nothing's broken or overlapping. Compared to some Wayfair sends that look like a fire sale exploded, this is composed.
- **Brand identity is legible** — Crate & Kids logo, consistent type, familiar palette. I knew immediately which sub-brand this was without squinting.
- **Product variety is visible** — from what I can see there's a range (gear, toys, possibly some nursery), so it's not a one-SKU blast.

## What's weak

- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is the second Crate & Kids email today. The first one today was already in "fine but forgettable" territory at 5/10. Sending two in one day to the same list burns goodwill fast.
- **No visible discount or urgency in the hero** — the hero reads like a catalog page, not a sale event. Article ran a deadline-driven offer that pulled me to click; this doesn't give me a reason to act now vs. Saturday.
- **"Summer gear and toys" is not my shopping mode** — I'm in full renovation brain. Backpacks and strollers register as "not for me right now." If there's kids furniture buried in here, it's not leading with it.
- **The subject ellipsis is weak** — "Summer gear (and toys!) are here…" trails off. The "(and toys!)" parenthetical feels tacked on, and the "…" creates no intrigue, just incompleteness.
- **No loyalty or Double Rewards callout visible** — the two highest-scoring C&B family emails this week both prominently featured Double Rewards. That hook is completely absent here, which is a miss given they've been conditioning me to expect it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with a discount or tie to the ongoing sale event** — the C&B family has been running "up to 60% off + Double Rewards" all week. This email either shares that umbrella or it doesn't. If it does, say so in the hero. If it doesn't, the cadence is doing active damage.
- 2. **Merge the two same-day Crate & Kids sends into one** — "All the things to finish their dream room" and "Summer gear and toys" in the same inbox, same day, same sub-brand is redundant. Combine them or stagger by 48 hours.
- 3. **Add a deadline** — even "Shop before Memorial Weekend" does more than nothing. CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" at least implied urgency; this email feels like it could have been sent any Tuesday in June.
- 4. **If furniture or outdoor kids pieces are in the assortment, front-load them** — I'm more likely to buy a kids outdoor chair or a bedroom storage piece than a backpack. Sort the grid for renovation-adjacent customers.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Backpacks, play sets, and the outdoor pieces that actually last.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CONFIRMED] `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[CONFIRMED] Conflicting `img max-width` breakpoints**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
Both rules target desktop, but the 640px query is declared after the 768px query. On screens 640–767px wide, the 640px cap applies correctly; on screens ≥768px, specificity is equal so the last rule (640px) wins — overriding the intended 600px cap. Consolidate into a single breakpoint.

**[CONFIRMED] Wildcard `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applying `text-size-adjust: none` to all elements via `*` can break font scaling on iOS Safari and is more aggressive than the body-scoped declaration already present. The body-level rules (`-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%`) conflict with this.

**[CONFIRMED] Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
`telephone=no` is declared twice (once combined, once standalone); `address=no` appears twice. Redundant but harmless — clean up for template hygiene.

**[CONFIRMED] Dark mode not supported**
```html

```
Explicitly locks to light mode. Apple Mail, Outlook (new), and Gmail dark mode will force-invert images and backgrounds. No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` overrides are present in the visible CSS.
