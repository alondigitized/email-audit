# Nuna, Doona & more great baby gear
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-19**
## Executive summary

- Yeah, this one's a swing and a miss for me — I don't have kids, and nothing about Nuna car seats, Doona strollers, or "must-have high chairs" moves the needle when I'm elbow-deep in swatches for two houses. Crate & Kids is clearly a sub-brand of the same Crate & Barrel family that's already flooded my inbox this week with dining rooms and rugs, and now they're just pivoting the same weekly cadence toward a completely different life stage. The production value is genuinely nice — that olive-green convertible high chair and the neutral-tone stroller lineup look premium — but it's the wrong catalog for the wrong guy.
- There's zero renovation crossover here: no nursery-as-room-refresh angle, no "coordinate with your dining room" tie-in, nothing that bridges to what I'm actually shopping for. It just reads as generic new-parent marketing that landed in the wrong inbox segment.
- Verdict: not opening past the subject line in real life, definitely not clicking, and not forwarding — unless I know someone who just had a baby, in which case maybe. Straight to archived, no regrets.

## What's working

- Clean, consistent product photography — the stroller and car seat grids look genuinely premium, on-brand with C&B's aesthetic.
- Brand logos (Nuna, Doona, Stokke, UPPAbaby) up front signal "curated," which builds trust if you're in-market.
- The "quality brands you love" section is smart social proof for actual baby-gear shoppers.
- Clear sectioning (strollers → car seats → high chairs → on-the-go gear) makes it easy to scan if you're actually a parent.

## What's weak

- Wrong persona entirely — nothing here speaks to home renovation, furniture, or my two-house project.
- No urgency or offer up top — I scrolled through five product categories before hitting anything resembling a deal ("limited time only, 20% off"), buried deep.
- The card widget at the bottom ("get the card, get more") feels like a bolt-on financing pitch that's disconnected from the baby gear content above it.
- Compare to Pottery Barn's "Workspace Refresh" desk email I scored 7/10 — that one had one clear hero product tied to a specific room project. This is nine product categories crammed into one scroll with no single hero moment.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment harder — if I've never bought baby gear from this list, don't send me the Crate & Kids drop at all. That's a targeting failure, not a creative one.
- 2. Lead with the actual offer ("limited time only, up to 20% off baby essentials") instead of burying it two-thirds down — Crate & Barrel's own dining room email at least put the hero shot and value prop in the first screen.
- 3. Cut the product-category kitchen-sink approach — CB2's "Floor Sample Sale" worked better at 5/10 because it had a single concrete hook; this reads like a full catalog dump.
- 4. Drop the credit-card pitch to the very end or a separate send — it dilutes the baby-gear message.
