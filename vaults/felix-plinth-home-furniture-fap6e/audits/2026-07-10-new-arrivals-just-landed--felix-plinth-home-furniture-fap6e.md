# New arrivals just landed
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- This one hit differently than the last four Article emails in my inbox. Four 4th of July Sale emails — the last one on July 6 going full "ends today" — and now, four days later, Article pivots to a new arrivals send. No discount, no countdown. Just product. And honestly? That's the right move. I was getting fatigued by the sale drum, and this palette cleanser actually made me stop scrolling for a second.
- The email is very Article: earthy tones, clean layout, that wry copy voice ("Self-centered." is genuinely clever). The tan leather sofa in the hero is exactly the kind of piece I'd bookmark while scoping my second home's living room. The problem is there's no hook beyond inspiration. No price. No deadline. No "here's what's limited." It's a gorgeous lookbook that stops just short of asking me to buy anything.
- I'll save this to my home folder and use it as a reference next time I'm cross-shopping. I won't click through today. Not forwarding it either — nobody I'd send this to is in a buying moment right now.

## What's working

- **The cadence pivot is smart.** After four consecutive sale emails, landing a new arrivals send on July 10 breaks the rhythm in a good way — I didn't feel like I was being asked for money.
- **Hero product is exactly right for me.** Tan leather sofa, warm wood accents — that's the aesthetic I'm sourcing for the second home right now. Article knows their customer.
- **Copy voice is on.** "That's so you." / "Show off a bit." / "Self-centered." — playful, punchy, no buzzwords. A sharp contrast to Pottery Barn's summer sale emails, which have been running generic "Summer Sale is ON" language for two straight weeks.
- **Section structure works.** Living room → bedroom → dining is a logical path through the house. I stay oriented even on mobile.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds.

## What's weak

- **No offer.** Zero discount, zero free shipping threshold, zero urgency. After the 4th of July sale just ended, I was primed to buy. Article could have kept me warm with even a modest "members get early access" or shipping waiver — instead, this is pure inspiration with no path to purchase that closes tonight.
- **"Just landed" is doing no work as urgency.** Wayfair's outdoor rugs email ("Inside: 8' X 10' OUTDOOR RUGS") at least made the product specific. "New arrivals just landed" could mean one piece or fifty. I have no idea what I'm walking into.
- **The body copy is slightly off.** "If anything brand new possesses it's your home" reads awkward — I had to reparse it. Small thing, but Article's usually tighter than that.
- **No product names or prices in-email.** Crate & Barrel's free shipping email (scored a 6, largely for this same reason) at least told me the threshold. Here I don't even know the price range of what I'm looking at. I know CB2 puts at least a product name under the hero; Article should do the same.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a new-arrivals access hook.** Even "First look — new pieces ship in 2–3 weeks" creates soft urgency without lying. Right now the CTA is "SHOP NEW ARRIVALS" but there's nothing perishable about the offer.
- 2. **Name the hero product.** Put the sofa name and starting price under that hero image. CB2 does this consistently. I shouldn't have to click through just to find out if this is a $800 or a $3,200 piece.
- 3. **Lean into the post-sale timing explicitly.** "The sale's over. Here's what's next." — that's a headline that owns the moment instead of ignoring it.
- 4. **Fix the body copy.** "If anything brand new belongs in your home, it's this." — cleaner, same idea.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Meet the new arrivals — no sale required`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Just in: the pieces we're most excited about`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh furniture that's ready to move in.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sofa, bed, dining — we restocked all three.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS syntax error — invalid selector (confirmed):**
```css
h1,h2,p,{color:#fff!important}
```
The trailing comma before `{` is invalid CSS. Strict parsers (WebKit, Gecko) will drop the entire rule, leaving `h1`, `h2`, and `p` text invisible on dark backgrounds.

**CSS syntax error — malformed `!important` (confirmed):**
```css
a{color:#fff;!important}
```
Semicolon before `!important` is invalid. The declaration will be ignored, meaning anchor color won't override in dark mode.

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Several screen readers and email clients (notably VoiceOver on iOS) announce the `<title>` as the document label. An empty value produces a blank or filename announcement. Should be set to the subject line or a descriptive string.

**Missing closing brace in `prefers-color-scheme` block:**
The `@media(prefers-color-scheme:dark){...}` block in the source does not close with `}` before the next `<style>` block begins. This may be a truncation artifact, but if present in the live send, every subsequent style rule would be absorbed into the dark media query.
