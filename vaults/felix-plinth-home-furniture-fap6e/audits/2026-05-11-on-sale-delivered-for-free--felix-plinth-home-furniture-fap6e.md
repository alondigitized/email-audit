# On sale & delivered for free
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-11**
## Executive summary

- Pottery Barn knows I'm in a renovation cycle — I'm literally on their list — so when "free delivery on furniture" hits my inbox during a month where I'm trying to get beds and dining tables into two properties, the timing lands. I opened this faster than I'd admit. The hero image is clean, the category nav is organized, and the "Recommended Just for You" section actually surfaced a Platform Bed and a dining table instead of just throw pillows. That's the thing: the first half of this email feels like Pottery Barn knows me; the second half (quilts, duvets, table linens, towels) feels like a soft-goods department blast that could've gone to anyone.
- The 60% back in rewards pitch is interesting in theory, but I'm already optimizing through a different card. And the Affirm block at the bottom — I'm not financing a $400 lamp through BNPL, so that's dead space for me. The fine print block at the very bottom is a wall of legalese that makes the "free shipping" promise feel hedged even if I couldn't read it clearly.
- I'd file this in my inbox folder for reference during my next Pottery Barn order, but I wouldn't click today. Definitely not forwarding it.

## What's working

- **The Recommended section earned its real estate.** A Farmhouse Platform Bed and a dining table in the same send as free delivery? That's exactly the combination I'm looking for across both houses right now.
- **"In-Stock Furniture Delivered in 4 Weeks"** — that banner at the top is the most useful line in the whole email. Delivery windows matter enormously when I'm coordinating two concurrent renovations.
- **Visual hierarchy holds up.** Hero → offer → categories → recommendations is a clean read. Nothing fights for attention.
- **60% back in rewards is at least visible.** I'll do my own math, but the signal that Pottery Barn has a rewards program is worth noting even if I'm not switching cards for it.
- **No render bugs.** Clean, fast render. Nothing broken or overlapping.

## What's weak

- **"Save on Easy Updates" is the wrong framing for me.** I'm not touching up an existing room — I'm outfitting empty rooms in two houses. "Easy updates" sounds like swapping throw pillows on an existing couch, not furnishing from scratch. Missed the moment.
- **The category carousel is 80% soft goods.** Pillows, Quilts, Duvets, Table Linens, Sheets, Towels, Curtains — this is a linen-closet refresh email wearing a furniture dress. The hero image (navy pillows on a sofa) reinforces the soft-goods pitch, not the furniture.
- **No specific discount percentage anywhere.** "On sale" tells me nothing about the depth of the deal. Crate & Barrel would've said 20% off or 40% off beds. I don't know if this is a clearance event or a 5%-off blip.
- **Demographic signal is off.** The styling — draped throws, soft color palette, curtain close-ups — skews toward a different shopper. I'm not offended, just aware I'm probably not the primary audience for this exact send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the furniture story, not soft goods.** Move a Platform Bed or dining table image into the hero. "On sale & delivered in 4 weeks" is a furniture headline — the email should look like one.
- 2. **Name a number.** "Up to 30% off select furniture + free delivery" beats "ON SALE & SHIPPING FREE" every day. Give me the depth.
- 3. **Trim the category nav to 4 items.** Eight categories in one row is noise. If the audience is a renovator, surface Furniture, Rugs, Lighting, Bedding — not Quilts and Duvets up top.
- 4. **Kill the Affirm block** for high-value furniture buyers. Replace with the rewards math done for them: "Your 60% back on a $1,200 bed = $720 in rewards." That's compelling; "Buy Now, Pay Over Time" is not.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Furniture on sale — free delivery in 4 weeks`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 30% off beds, rugs & dining — ships free`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `In-stock pieces delivered in 4 weeks — no code needed`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Platform beds, rugs, dining tables — all included`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "On sale & delivered for free"

**1. Technical Summary**

Two hard broken links (403) will cause click failures for users landing on the credit card banner and a cross-brand spacer asset. Mixed-content HTTP image references and 38 images without alt text are the dominant rendering/accessibility issues.

---

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**

**BROKEN (403) — user-impacting:**
- `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618...` — credit card apply/buy CTA (`cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`). Users clicking the Key Rewards banner hit a dead end.
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` — cross-brand West Elm asset embedded in a Pottery Barn send; returns 403 and is HTTP.

**Tracking links (25 skipped by probe):** All route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` — structurally correct, not probed. No issues confirmed.

---

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**

**Mixed-content HTTP image sources (3 confirmed, will be blocked in most clients):**
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

All three also appear as tracking/open-pixel assets:
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true` — open-tracking pixel served over HTTP; will be blocked by Gmail, Apple Mail, and most modern clients, silently under-counting opens.

**Alt text gaps:** 38 images are missing `alt` attributes, including all product slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), all in-stock furniture product images, and footer slices. In image-blocking environments (Outlook default, corporate proxies), the email renders as a blank column of broken icons with no readable fallback.

**CSS artifact in `<style>` block:** PHP-style `header()` calls appear verbatim inside a `<style>` tag:
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
```
These are server-side PHP statements that were never executed — they leaked into the template as literal text. They are invalid CSS, will cause parse errors in strict renderers, and expose an unrendered code artifact to any recipient who views source.

**`<title>` is empty** — assistive technologies read the title on email open; a blank title gives screen readers nothing to announce.
