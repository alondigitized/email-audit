# The backpack they’ll want to carry, starting at $39!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids, again. I've now lost count this week — this is at least the fifth send, and this one's literally a backpack sizing guide with small/medium/large tiers, a school-hallway locker photoshoot, and a "what fits inside" packing diagram for each size. I don't have a kid. I've never had a kid. I'm two contractors deep into gutting a primary and a second home right now, and this brand keeps mailing me kids' bedding, celebrity nurseries, and now backpacks like I've got three grade-schoolers I forgot to mention.
- There's a small flicker of relevance buried at the bottom — "500+ new fall arrivals, starting at $29!" with what looks like an actual living space shot — but it's stacked under four screens of backpack-fit content, so I'd never scroll that far. Crate & Barrel's coffee table guide from earlier this week actually landed because it matched my renovation; this is the opposite of that, and it's from the same parent company, which makes the miss more embarrassing, not less.
- Mark as read, don't click, don't forward — unless it's to complain to whoever owns the Crate & Barrel/Crate & Kids segmentation logic.

## What's working

- The size-guide concept itself is clean — small/medium/large with a literal "what fits inside" list is a genuinely useful format, just for a customer with kids.
- Photography is bright and well-art-directed, on-brand with the rest of the Crate & Barrel family look.
- The "500+ new fall arrivals, starting at $29!" banner near the bottom is the one moment that could pull a non-parent like me in, if I ever scrolled that far.
- Clear, repeated CTAs ("Shop Small/Medium/Large Backpacks") — no ambiguity about what to click, just zero reason for me to.

## What's weak

- Entire email is back-to-school kids' backpacks — zero relevance to a renovation-focused Millennial with no kids on file.
- This is the fifth-ish Crate & Kids send I've gotten this week; my brain has fully tuned this sub-brand out at this point.
- The one relevant thing — new fall home arrivals — is buried after four screens of backpack content instead of leading.
- No shipping/delivery clarity for furniture, no swatch program, no rewards math anywhere — this doesn't even try to speak to a home shopper, which is the whole reason I'm subscribed.
- Contrast with Pottery Barn's living room send this week (8/10) — that one at least stayed in-lane on furniture even while overmailing me; this one abandoned the lane entirely.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment me out of Crate & Kids sends entirely — I've never clicked a kids' product in this account, and Wayfair and Pottery Barn manage to keep me in adult-furniture lane every time.
- 2. Lead with the fall home arrivals and the $500 sweepstakes instead of burying them under backpacks — that's the one section with a shot at my click.
- 3. Cap Crate & Kids frequency — five sends in a week for a category I have zero engagement with is how you train me to archive on sight.
- 4. If backpack content must go out broadly, add a one-line "shopping for a home instead?" module up top routing non-parents to furniture/decor.
