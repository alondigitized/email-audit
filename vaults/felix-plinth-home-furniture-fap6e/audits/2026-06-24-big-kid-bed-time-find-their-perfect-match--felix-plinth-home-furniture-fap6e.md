# Big kid bed time? Find their perfect match!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- Nine emails from the Crate & Barrel family in my inbox over the last 72 hours — and this is the *second* Crate & Kids send today alone. The first one this morning was "It's here: new furniture for kids, baby and playroom spaces!" which I gave an 8 and moved on. Now here's a bed-specific follow-up landing before dinner. I have nothing against big kid beds in theory, but doubling up on the same sub-brand within the same calendar day when the parent brand is running an active sale blitz is a scheduling failure, not a targeting win.
- The email itself is actually competent. The hero — dark navy bunk beds in a moody, editorial-style room — looks good. It doesn't scream "toddler clearance." The grid below fans out by style with specific product options, and nothing is broken or overlapping. If I were actively shopping for a transition bed, this would be a useful shopping tool. The problem is it's purely a product discovery email with no offer attached, which is a strange choice when I have a Crate & Barrel "up to 50% off bestsellers" email sitting two rows above it in the same inbox.
- I'll save it to the folder, maybe come back if I need it. I won't click today, and I definitely won't forward it — there's nothing here that says "this is the one" over and above what I already got this morning.

## What's working

- **Hero image earns its real estate.** The dark-finish bunk bed in a styled, slightly moody room is the right move — it doesn't look like a Fisher-Price catalog, it looks like a room I'd actually design.
- **Product grid is genuinely useful.** Multiple bed styles, multiple finishes, clearly organized — if I were in shopping mode, this is the browse experience I want.
- **Brand rendering is clean.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum. The C&B family always holds the layout line and this is no exception.
- **Style range is real.** I can see light wood, dark finishes, bunk and non-bunk options — they're not showing me one SKU and calling it a collection.

## What's weak

- **Two Crate & Kids emails in one day is one too many.** I already got the "new arrivals" category-wide email this morning. This one narrows to beds — fine conceptually, but the sequencing feels like an accident, not a campaign.
- **No offer anywhere.** This arrives while the parent brand is running "up to 50% off" sale emails. Pottery Barn's current Summer Sale email I audited recently has "up to 70% off" in the subject. A product-discovery-only send from the same family on the same day looks tone-deaf.
- **No urgency or reason to act now.** The question mark in the subject ("Big kid bed time?") is doing all the work, and it's not enough — there's no deadline, no "selling fast," no free shipping threshold.
- **The cadence math is broken.** This is the ninth C&B-family email in roughly 72 hours. CB2, Crate & Barrel, Crate & Kids — they're all firing separately, and I'm getting hit from every direction. Even a brand I genuinely like starts to feel like spam at that velocity.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Bundle same-day sends into one.** The "new furniture for kids + playroom" email and this "big kid beds" email should be one send with a beds anchor — not two triggers in one day. A/B the hero, pick a winner, ship once.
- 2. **Attach an offer.** Even "free shipping on orders over $X" or "members save 10%" gives me a reason to click today vs. next week. CB2 and Pottery Barn both showed me dollar reasons in their recent emails; C&K is leaving that lever untouched.
- 3. **Add a deadline.** "New arrivals go fast" or "Limited quantities" isn't a lie for furniture — lead times are real. Use them.
- 4. **Segment tighter.** If you know I've been clicking Crate & Barrel sale emails, don't fire a full-price discovery email the same day. Hold it.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` brand mismatch (confirmed):**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
Sender is `crateandkids@mail.crateandbarrel.com` and subject references "Crate & Kids." The title tag surfaces in some email clients' tab/window chrome and in screen reader announcements. Should read `Crate & Kids`.

**Conflicting desktop image `max-width` breakpoints (confirmed):**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
The `640px` rule is declared after the `768px` rule. At any desktop width (≥768px) both rules match; CSS cascade gives the win to the last declaration — `max-width: 640px`. The `600px` constraint at the 768px breakpoint is dead code and will never apply. Either collapse to a single rule or reorder so the more-specific breakpoint wins.

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport (confirmed):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks user-initiated zoom. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) failure and is flagged by Apple Mail's accessibility checker.

**Universal `text-size-adjust: none` (confirmed):**
```css
*{ -webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; }
```
Applying this via `*` overrides the `body`-scoped rule set earlier and prevents OS-level text scaling for users with visual impairments. Scope to `body` only.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags (confirmed):**
`telephone=no`, `date=no`, and `address=no` each appear twice — once in a combined tag and once standalone. Redundant; consolidate into one tag.

**Layout tables missing `role="presentation"` (likely):**
Template appears to use full table-based layout. Without `role="presentation"` on structural tables, screen readers may announce them as data tables. Requires full source to confirm count.
