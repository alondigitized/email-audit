# Shop the Summer Sale
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-four. That's the Pottery Barn count in my inbox, and I want that number in the room before I say anything else, because this email lands on the same day as "Get the look: This celeb's cozy cabin" and the quilts email. Three PB emails in one June 17 morning. I'm actively renovating two houses — I have actual budget and actual purchase intent right now — but three from the same brand before noon makes me reach for the unsubscribe link on reflex before I've even processed the offer.
- The sale is real, though. "Up to 70% off" lands differently when you're pricing out outdoor sectionals and accent chairs for two properties simultaneously. I paused at the headline long enough to register it. But then the hero image hit: floral blue-and-white bedding, very cottagecore, very much my sister-in-law's Pinterest board. I'm shopping sofas and lighting and outdoor furniture. The email told me to expect a bedding pitch when what I actually needed was to see what was happening in furniture and outdoor categories.
- Mark as read, revisit maybe at lunch if I'm thinking about the outdoor piece I've been watching. Not clicking the hero. Not forwarding.

## What's working

- "Up to 70% off" in the hero is clear and visible — for someone with two renovation budgets in play, that number earns a second of attention.
- The category nav row (FURNITURE | OUTDOOR | SOFAS & CHAIRS | etc.) is an immediate escape hatch from the bedding content and the actual place I'd click.
- "Recommended Just for You" product thumbnails suggest PB is at least attempting to use my browse history — this feels like an individual send, not a pure mass blast.
- 10% Back in Rewards is surfaced — I run rewards math constantly across cards and store programs, so seeing this confirmed in the email is genuinely useful.
- Affirm financing module at the bottom is a real consideration when you're looking at a $2,800 outdoor sectional; I'm glad it's there even if it's below the fold.

## What's weak

- Third Pottery Barn email of the same calendar day. The cadence is the headline now, not the offer. CB2 ran its warehouse sale across multiple sends but at least spaced them 48 hours apart.
- The hero image is floral blue-and-white bedding. I have zero purchase history with PB bedding. The algorithm knows this. This feels like a templated seasonal hero, not anything personalized to who I am.
- The Free Design Services module appears again — this is the exact same pitch from the June 16 "FREE design services to get a look you love" email, which scored a 10 in my history because it felt differentiated. Re-running it 24 hours later in a sale roundup deflates both sends retroactively.
- "Shop the Summer Sale" is the blandest possible subject for a brand with this much product depth. Wayfair's dorm room email was obnoxious but at least it told me *what* was on sale. This tells me nothing I didn't already know.
- "Up to 70%" — I've been a PB customer long enough to know this means the $18 votive holder is 70% off and the Chesterfield sofa I'm tracking is 20% off if I'm lucky.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the hero to furniture or outdoor, not bedding.** I've bought lighting, outdoor chairs, and accent pieces from PB. The personalization data exists — use it. A hero showing the outdoor or living room categories would've stopped my scroll instead of accelerating it.
- 2. **Kill the Design Services re-pitch.** It ran June 16. Running it again June 17 inside a different email undermines both. Replace with a "Items you've been watching, now on sale" module — highest-intent conversion play available and PB already has the browse data to power it.
- 3. **Put a deadline on the sale.** Wayfair always closes with an end date. No deadline means no urgency. "Ends Sunday" would've pushed me to click today instead of maybe-revisiting.
- 4. **Cap at one email per day during sale events.** Three in a morning is loyalty erosion, not loyalty building. The cadence is the reason my open reflex is becoming an unsubscribe reflex.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your outdoor picks just went on sale — up to 70% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sofas, sectionals, outdoor — 100s of new deals just added`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New sale items added today. Ends Sunday.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (will be blocked in many clients)**
Three images are served over `http://`, not `https://`. Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail proxy or block mixed-content resources:
1. `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` — also broken (403 above)
2. `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` — footer credit card banner image
3. `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` — brand banner slice (2022 asset path)
4. `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` — open-tracking pixel

**Alt text absent on 40+ images**
Every content image slice (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), all product images, both footer banner images, and all tracking pixels are missing `alt` attributes. When images are blocked (common in Outlook, corporate clients), the email renders as a blank white column with no readable content. Minimum fix: add descriptive alt text to content images; use `alt=""` on purely decorative spacers and tracking pixels.

**PHP header directives in `<style>` block**
```html
<style>
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, ...");
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
</style>
```
These are PHP `header()` calls placed inside a CSS `<style>` block — they are no-ops in HTML and indicate a template rendering error where server-side code was not stripped before send. They do not break rendering but signal a broken template pipeline.

**`<title>` element is empty**
`<title></title>` — some email clients display the title in tab or preview UI. Low impact but indicates incomplete template population.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not captured**
The QA relay did not observe a `List-Unsubscribe` header. This is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policies (enforced since Feb 2024 for >5k/day senders). If the header is present but stripped by the AgentMail relay, the raw SMTP headers should be checked directly. If genuinely absent, this is a deliverability risk.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found**
One-click unsubscribe (the `POST` mechanism Gmail uses for the native unsubscribe button) appears absent. Gmail will show a less prominent unsubscribe path or may suppress the email for some users.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` not captured**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown from this capture. `From:` domain is `e.potterybarn.com` (ESP subdomain); DKIM alignment with `potterybarn.com` should be confirmed independently.

**CAN-SPAM physical address**
Not verifiable from the truncated source — should be confirmed present in the footer.
