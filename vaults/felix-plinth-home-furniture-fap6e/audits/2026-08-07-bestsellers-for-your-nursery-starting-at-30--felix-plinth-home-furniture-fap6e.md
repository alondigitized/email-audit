# Bestsellers for your nursery starting at $30!

## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids, again. Third one of these in my inbox this week if I'm counting, and I'm counting because it's annoying. Cribs, a "raya oak wood storage crib" at $1,199, a quilt with a name like a wine varietal, a nursery rug, a "baby activity chair" — I don't have kids. I'm elbow-deep in drywall dust on two houses right now, not registry shopping.
- The one section that made me pause for half a second was "we love these for you" near the bottom — strollers, car seats, a changing pad — because it at least looks like an algorithm tried. But it's the same algorithm that's been wrong all week. This is a hard delete, not a forward. My buddy with the newborn already gets this exact email himself.

## What's working

- Clean, editorial layout — soft sage and blush palette, real lifestyle photography, not a cluttered coupon-code wall like some Wayfair sends I've gotten.
- The "up to 50% off summer sale" banner mid-scroll is at least a real, legible offer with a visible button.
- Product tiles show strikethrough pricing (was $1,499, now $1,199) so the math isn't hidden.
- Star ratings appear under a couple hero products, which is a trust signal I do notice even when the product isn't for me.

## What's weak

- Every single hero product is nursery furniture — crib, crib quilt, nursery rug, baby activity chair. Zero relevance to a 36-year-old renovating two full houses.
- "500+ new fall arrivals, starting at $29!" is the exact same subject-line bait I already saw from this brand today under "NEW for fall! Fresh designs starting at $29" — I gave that a 5, this is a rerun of the rerun.
- The Crate & Barrel cross-promo ("now! we love you can love it better") at the bottom just reminds me the parent company already spammed my inbox twice today with near-identical "hosting essentials" emails.
- No swatch/sample program, no shipping-time clarity for furniture, no showroom/design-appointment content relevant to an adult home — just "The Design Desk" logo dropped in with zero context.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment me out of Crate & Kids entirely — I've never bought a nursery item, and this is the fourth near-identical nursery send this week. Basic purchase-history segmentation would fix this in one move.
- 2. If cross-selling to Crate & Barrel shoppers, lead with the adult-home line (rugs, storage, decor) instead of burying it as a footer logo.
- 3. Don't reuse "500+ new fall arrivals, starting at $29" verbatim across sends — even loyal Crate & Kids parents will start tuning it out.
- 4. Drop the redundant "we love these for you" strip unless it's genuinely dynamic — right now it reads like static baby-gear filler, not personalization.
