# STYLES TAKING OVER EVERY CAMPUS >>>
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- Sixty. That's the Wayfair count in my inbox now, and this one lands two days after the June 17 "MUST-SEE DORM ROOM DEALS" send — which I actually rated well, because the timing felt deliberate and the execution was clean. That grace period is over. We are back in dorm territory with a different subject line and the same creative universe, and my goodwill for this campaign has fully expired.
- The core problem is demographic. I'm 36, mid-renovation on two separate houses, and I'm actively searching Wayfair for things like credenzas, linen curtains, pendant lighting, and bathroom vanity hardware. "Dorm haul done right" is aimed at someone who needs a pouf, a command hook, and somewhere to store their protein powder. Pottery Barn sent me a "Looking for FREE design advice?" email this same week and understood exactly where I am in life. Wayfair sent me this.
- Marking it read. Not clicking, not forwarding. If the 50% OFF badge had landed on something from my actual browsing history — a rug for the primary bedroom, a dining table for the second house — I'd have been in cart in thirty seconds. Instead I'm moving on.

## What's working

- **50% OFF badge is immediately legible in the hero** — no hunting, no guesswork, the offer is above the fold and visually dominant
- **"Your move in list, delivered fast & free" subhead is solid service copy** — bundles product selection and delivery promise into one line, and that's useful regardless of category
- **Email renders cleanly** — no broken images, no lorem ipsum, Wayfair's purple-on-white palette is consistent and familiar throughout
- **Wayfair Rewards section visible in the footer** — at least they acknowledge I have a loyalty relationship, even if it's the very last thing in the scroll
- **Section architecture is browsable** — personality picks, small-space storage, new arrivals, styles to scoop up — the scaffold is logical even when the content is wrong for me

## What's weak

- **Total demographic miss** — I'm renovating two adult homes; "EVERY CAMPUS" is a psychographic universe that doesn't overlap with me at all. Pottery Barn's design advice email this same week felt like it was written for someone in my life stage; this does not
- **Second dorm email in three days is cadence waste** — the June 17 version earned its spot. This is the sequel nobody needed. CB2 running the Todd Snyder collab was a singular, well-timed creative bet; this feels like padding a promotional calendar
- **ALL CAPS + >>> subject is the wrong energy** — "STYLES TAKING OVER EVERY CAMPUS >>>" reads like autocorrect left on in a group text. Article used "Spotlight: the Cillian Collection" and it felt like a considered adult brand; this subject does not
- **Zero personalization after sixty emails** — Wayfair knows I've bought rugs and clicked pendant lights. None of that is reflected anywhere here. I'm being served the same blast as every 19-year-old who signed up for a discount code
- **Five competing sections, no single hero product** — when everything is featured, nothing wins. Article spotlights one collection and owns the whole email around it; this one has five autonomous content blocks fighting for attention

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment dorm out of my send stream** — my purchase and browse history skews heavily toward grown-up furnishings; I shouldn't receive back-to-campus emails. This is a segmentation failure, not a creative failure
- 2. **Cap dorm at one send per campaign window** — June 17 covered it; this one is diminishing returns
- 3. **Kill ALL CAPS and the >>> chain** — both read as spam signals and neither adds real urgency; they subtract trust from a brand I otherwise like
- 4. **Collapse five sections into one strong hero product** — pick the single best item, give it room, link directly to the product page; Article executes this reliably better than Wayfair does
- 5. **Bring Wayfair Rewards math into the hero** — if 50% off stacks with points, that number belongs in the first scroll, not the last
- **Subject Alt A:** `50% off small-space storage — fast & free delivery`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Move-in ready: 50% off this week's picks`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Personality-picked styles, from storage to new arrivals`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your move-in list, starting at 50% off`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Web font — WOFF only, no WOFF2:**
```css
src: url(https://secure.img.wfrcdn.com/st4/stores/common/fonts/sofia/sofia_reg.woff) format("woff");
```
WOFF2 is smaller and supported by every client that can render web fonts in email (Apple Mail, iOS Mail). Missing `format("woff2")` entry is a minor performance miss. Fallback `mso-font-alt: "Arial"` is correctly specified.

`.u-Title`, `.u-Title--primary`, `.u-Title--secondary`, `.u-Title--tertiary`, `.u-Title--white` all collapse to `font-size: 32px !important` at 480px via separate rules. Same pattern repeats for `.u-Header` variants. Not broken, but adds ~800 bytes of dead CSS.
