# Don't miss EARLY BLACK FRIDAY
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-24**
## Executive summary

- Pottery Barn again. It's the same maroon "Early Black Friday, up to 70% off" banner I've had since 11/16, and I've lost count of the sends. Today's has the same hero, the same maroon category grid and the same "Shop Now" button. I already got "Early Black Friday deals SELLING FAST" and "1000s of Early Black Friday deals" yesterday. The subject says "Don't miss," but I've missed nothing, because this is the fifth near-copy.
- The email is clean, though. It's easy to scan. The category tiles (Furniture, Sofas & Chairs, Rugs, Curtains) match what I'm shopping for across two houses. The Whitaker chair at $449, down from $899, got my attention. The Toscana dining table is the other thing I'd actually look at. Still, I've seen both before, and nothing here is new.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I were in the middle of a project. I'd probably save it to my inspiration folder and move on. I wouldn't forward it. It has nothing my friends haven't already seen from Crate & Barrel and CB2.

## What's working

- The "up to 70% off" hero is huge and unmistakable, and the single "Shop Now" button sits right under it.
- The category grid lets me jump straight to Furniture, Sofas & Chairs or Rugs without scrolling.
- Concrete was/now pricing on the Whitaker chair ($899 to $449) and the Boucle pillow ($49.50 to $39.50) makes the discount easy to judge.
- The "Ships Free" tags help, since furniture shipping cost is usually the hidden catch.
- The "In-Store Savings / Find Your Store" block fits the way I shop: I touch fabric in a showroom before I order.
- The 10% back in rewards card block is useful for my points math.

## What's weak

- It's the fifth near-identical Early Black Friday send in about a week. This is the cadence fatigue I keep flagging.
- The "Recommended Just For You" block is stockings, a Christmas tree and a hand-quilted quilt. It doesn't reflect anything I've browsed (furniture, renovation). Wayfair's grid at least pretends to track my behavior.
- The page is very long, and the fine print at the bottom is a wall of tiny text. The sweepstakes and the "Design Crew" gift card feel like clutter.
- The "Christmas Shop, up to 50% off" tile pulls attention away from the 70% offer and muddies which deal is the main one.
- There's no swatch or sample program, no delivery timing for furniture, and no color-matching help. These are what actually decide a big purchase for me.

## Recommendations

- 1. Show the furniture deal on top. Lead with the sofa or dining table I browsed, and show a delivery window, so it isn't another generic hero.
- 2. Cut the cadence. Send one Black Friday email a day at most, or suppress it for people who opened yesterday's.
- 3. Replace "Recommended Just For You" with items based on actual browsing, or remove it if there's no data behind it.
- 4. Add a "Free swatches" or "Order a fabric sample" link next to the sofa and chair offers. Add a delivery-by-Christmas note too.
- 5. Move the sweepstakes and legal text out of the main flow.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Whitaker chair $449 + furniture up to 70% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last day: free shipping on Early Black Friday furniture`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dining tables, sofas and rugs, with delivery dates before the holidays.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Order free fabric swatches first, then lock in 70% off.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based, image-sliced build. It is mostly sound structurally, but about 53 images have no alt text and three are served over `http://`. The relay captured neither the authentication headers nor `List-Unsubscribe`, so compliance can't be fully verified from this capture. The HTML source is truncated, so the footer, body links and later markup were not inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unprobed links:** 64 links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are redirect-tracked. Examples are `https://click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=ABp7Im...` on the logo link, and `clickcp.potterybarn.com`. Destination validity and status codes are unverified.
- **Third-party pixels:** `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/.../cat/1` is an ad-network pixel. `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=...&sv_emopen=true` and `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/4186897d-...` are separate open-tracking pixels. That makes three beacons, and the `svopn` one is plain HTTP.
- **Cross-brand asset:** `https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` is a West Elm CDN asset in a Pottery Barn send. It's harmless unless that path is retired.
- **Stray code in CSS:** the second `<style>` block contains PHP-style text (`header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: ...")`). It is ignored by renderers but is a template defect.
