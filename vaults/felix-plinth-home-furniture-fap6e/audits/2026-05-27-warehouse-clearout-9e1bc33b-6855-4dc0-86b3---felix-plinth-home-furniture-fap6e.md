- The flag emoji framing in the subject is the smartest creative choice in this send — on-theme for Memorial Day without stacking `!!!` or ALL CAPS noise.
- 60% off bedroom furniture is a real number. If this were Wayfair email three of the week, I'd stop scrolling.

## What's weak

- Two emails in one day. The cadence problem is bigger than any creative solution available in the template.
- No visible deadline. Article's "Last chance: Memorial Day Sale ends today" was vague, but at least it named a day. "WAREHOUSE CLEAROUT" could end Sunday or in three weeks — I don't trust it enough to click and find out.
- No single hero product. The 60% off bedroom furniture badge is the same visual weight as the 30% off mattresses tile four rows down — both are little discount chips on little thumbnails. CB2's "LAST CALL FOR MEMORIAL DAY" ran a focused hero and earned a 9. This email's grid approach buries the best offer in the noise.
- The discount descending order (60% → 50% → 40% → 30%) reads like a clearance rack markdown, not a curated event.

- 1. **Lead with the 60% off bedroom furniture story, full width.** One room scene, one specific piece (named, linked), a before/after price. Not a 200px badge on a grid tile.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline.** "Ends Sunday at midnight" converts "CLEAROUT" from brand copy into an actual event. Without it, urgency is assumed and ignored.
- 3. **Cut the personalization grid to four or five picks.** Fifteen tiles isn't curation, it's the whole catalog with discount stickers.
- 4. **Don't send two emails in one day.** This is a policy fix, not a template fix.
