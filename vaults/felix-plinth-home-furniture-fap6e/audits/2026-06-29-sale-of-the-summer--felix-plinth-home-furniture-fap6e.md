# SALE OF THE SUMMER
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-29**
## Executive summary

- Forty-seven. That's where I land with CB2 and the Crate family this month, and this one drops a full day after I already reviewed a "WAREHOUSE SALE" email from the exact same "CB2 SALE" display name. Yesterday's send was already a repeat of what I'd been seeing all week. Today I get "SALE OF THE SUMMER" — all caps, same hero territory, same July 4th angle, same velvet chair energy — and the brand expects me to treat it as new information.
- To be fair: the offer is real. The July 4th timing is legitimate. The products visible in the hero and the mid-email grid — the tufted green chair, the ornate area rug, the striped throw — are exactly the categories I'm actively shopping across two simultaneous renovations. If CB2 had said "up to 40% off rugs and accent chairs" in the subject I would have clicked before finishing my coffee.
- But they didn't. So I'm sitting here cross-referencing against Pottery Barn's June 29 "TOP 4th of July Sale picks (up to 70% off)" send, which used editorial framing to earn the click, and wondering if CB2's marketing team shares a calendar. I'd open this — I did — but I'm not forwarding it to anyone, and I'm getting dangerously close to training my inbox rules to snooze "CB2 SALE" for 48 hours between sends.

## What's working

- The July 4th deadline is a real external anchor — unlike the manufactured urgency CB2 has been running all month, this one has an actual date behind it
- Hero product mix is dialed in for someone like me: a statement chair, a patterned rug, a textural throw — not aspirational-only, actual purchases in my pipeline
- "MAKE IT YOURS BEFORE IT'S GONE" mid-email scarcity copy is better deployed here than in the subject; it lands harder when you've already seen the product
- Visual layout is clean, no render bugs, readable hierarchy from the warehouse sale banner straight to the shop-now CTA to the product grid
- "MORE FOR YOU" section at the bottom suggests algorithmic or behavioral curation — the only personalization signal in the whole email, but credit for including it

## What's weak

- This is the second "sale" email from the same CB2 SALE sender in under 24 hours — the creative differentiation between "WAREHOUSE SALE" (June 28) and "SALE OF THE SUMMER" is functionally zero from my inbox
- ALL CAPS subject is exactly the kind of signal I've trained myself to deprioritize; Article has run five emails to me this month in normal case and earns more of my attention every single time
- "SHOP NOW" is the laziest possible CTA — Pottery Barn's June 29 send used "See picks" which felt curated, while this feels like a template default no one questioned
- No indication of whether this is warehouse clearance, floor samples, or sitewide — that distinction matters a lot when you're coordinating delivery timing across two properties
- The percentage-off figure, which I can see in the email body, never made it into the subject line, which is where it would have actually changed my open behavior

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the number in the subject** — "UP TO 40% OFF" converts harder than "SALE OF THE SUMMER" from the inbox; the superlative costs you the anchor
- 2. **Differentiate the creative from yesterday's send** — if you're hitting the same subscriber twice in 24 hours, the hero image and product assortment need to be clearly different; running the velvet chair two sends in a row reads as a production shortcut
- 3. **Replace "SHOP NOW" with a category-specific CTA** — "Shop rugs & seating" or "Shop the holiday sale" immediately signals to browsers like me whether the click is worth it
- 4. **Add one line of clearance context** — "includes floor samples and warehouse stock" vs. "sitewide" changes the planning calculus for anyone doing a real renovation
- 5. **Kill the ALL CAPS subject or at least A/B test it** — sentence case reads warmer and doesn't pattern-match to the spam folder
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 40% off CB2 — sale ends July 4th`
- **Subject Alt B:** `CB2's best summer prices end this weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Rugs, seating, and accents — shop before the holiday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Warehouse prices through July 4th, free shipping on $49+`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** restricts user-initiated zoom — accessibility failure for low-vision users. Line: `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />`

- **Conflicting `<p>` resets across style blocks:** The global block (no media query) sets `p { text-align: center; }`. The screen-scoped block sets `p { padding: 0 !important; margin: 0 !important; }` but does not override `text-align`. Result: all `<p>` elements are center-aligned universally, including any left-aligned body copy.

- **Inconsistent `font-display`:** Three of the six `@font-face` declarations include `font-display: swap`; the other three (including the first `Lineto Brown Sub` and `Lineto Brown Light Sub` declarations) do not. No practical rendering impact in email clients, but indicates copy-paste inconsistency in the template.

- **Alt text:** Cannot audit — image tags not visible in truncated source. Must verify all `<img>` carry descriptive `alt` attributes; this is both an accessibility requirement and a deliverability signal.
