The snooze reflex kicked in before I opened it.
- Once I did open it, it was easy to read. Cribs, then chairs, then dressers, each with its own hero and a "Shop" button. I'd skim it and not click. If a friend were expecting, I'd forward it. For my own projects it's a mark-as-read.

## What's working

- The three-block structure (cribs, chairs, dressers) matches the subject line. Each block opens with a "new!" banner, a big hero and two product tiles. I always know where I am.
- Each product tile has its own "Shop" button, like "Shop Cribs" and "Shop Nursery Chairs". The CTA copy is a verb plus a noun, and it's better than a generic "Discover".
- The Greenguard Gold Certified labels sit above the product names. That's a real trust cue for a category where parents care about safety.
- The "complete the room" grid (rugs, lighting, bedding, wall decor) is a good cross-sell. It reads like a catalog page.
- No broken images or overlapping text anywhere in the render.

## What's weak

- It's nursery-only, so it's irrelevant to me. Nothing here reflects that I've been opening dining and wallpaper sends.
- The sale items are buried. "Up to 60% off clearance" and "free shipping on toys $99+" sit in the bottom third. Anyone not already scrolling to the end misses them.
- The page is very long, with about ten sections. The credit card promo, the app download and TheDesignDesk pile up after the real content, so it feels like one email doing the job of three.
- Compared with the "Final weekend! Up to 30% off dining tables" send, there's no urgency and no deadline here. It's a new-arrivals pitch with no reason to act this week.
- Even at this size, the "we love these for you" block looks like generic bestsellers, not anything personalized.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the best offer next to the hero. A strip like "Free shipping on cribs & dressers" would lift the whole email, because furniture shipping is the question I always ask first.
- 2. Cut the length. Keep one feature per section, and move the credit card, app and TheDesignDesk blocks into a lighter footer.
- 3. Add a deadline, and show delivery windows and dimensions on the crib and dresser tiles. Pottery Barn Kids at least repeats its urgency strip, and this email has none.
- 4. Segment by browsing history. I've been clicking dining and wallpaper, so this send should have gone to someone with a nursery on their list.
- 5. Show swatches or finish options on the dressers (white, walnut, black), since two of them are visibly different finishes.
