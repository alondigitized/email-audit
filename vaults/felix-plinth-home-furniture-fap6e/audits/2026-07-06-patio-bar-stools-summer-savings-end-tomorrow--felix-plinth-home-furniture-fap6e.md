# PATIO BAR STOOLS 🎉 ☀️ 📅 Summer Savings END TOMORROW 🎉 ☀️ 📅 
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- Seventh or eighth Wayfair in five days — I've lost count — and this one opens with the exact same trap as the July 4th "BAKING SHEETS" send: the subject names one very specific, very relevant thing (patio bar stools, which I genuinely need for the second house's deck), and the body delivers the same endless discount grid I've been staring at since July 2nd. There are no patio bar stools in the hero. I see sofas, entertainment units, bed frames, dining chairs, area rugs. The subject is a lure and the body is a warehouse dump labeled "picked just for you."
- To make it worse: yesterday's send was "(Almost) LAST CALL." Now today it's "Summer Savings END TOMORROW." So the last call wasn't the last call. Article ran a tight three-email arc over eight days — open, nudge, close — and their deadline actually meant something. Wayfair has now blown through at least three fake deadlines in four days. I don't believe the countdown. I don't trust the subject. And the 3-emoji-on-each-side formatting (🎉 ☀️ 📅 on the left, 🎉 ☀️ 📅 on the right) is the visual equivalent of a Times Square ticket scalper.
- Mark as read. Not clicking. Definitely not forwarding.

## What's working

- **Wayfair is a known sender** — I'm subscribed, I've bought from them, the brand recognition is there.
- **Discount depth is visible immediately** — the 70% OFF banner and the stacked discount badges (70%, 60%, 50%, 40%) confirm there's a real sale happening, even if the execution is chaotic.
- **Summer sale timing is correct** — July 6th is still peak outdoor-furniture season, so the concept isn't wrong.
- **"Your Home, These Picks" section at bottom** suggests some intent at personalization, even if the grid above it doesn't live up to that promise.

## What's weak

- **The subject is a lie again.** "PATIO BAR STOOLS" in all-caps but the hero is a generic furniture grid. This is the same move as July 4th's "BAKING SHEETS" — which I specifically called out in my audit. I've been trained not to trust Wayfair subject lines now, which is a catastrophic trust erosion for a brand I actually spend money with.
- **"LAST CALL" was yesterday.** Announcing the end is tomorrow, the day after you already announced the end, destroys any remaining urgency credibility. Pottery Barn at least owned the extension honestly with "The 4th is gone but our sale is STILL ON" — it was transparent about the stretch. Wayfair just pretends the previous deadline didn't happen.
- **Six emojis in the subject line, repeated symmetrically** — this reads as desperate or spammy, not festive. My spam radar is up before I even open.
- **The grid is identical to every prior send.** Same "picked just for you" layout, same badge styling, same proportions. I've seen this template six times in five days. It no longer registers as curated; it registers as a template fill.
- **No patio-specific content visible anywhere.** If I'm buying outdoor bar stools, I need weatherproofing info, material callouts (aluminum vs. wicker vs. teak), and a clear way to filter by stool height. None of that is in the email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put actual patio bar stools in the email.** If you're going to name a product category in 30-point all-caps, the hero unit should be exactly that product. Show me three stool options, a price range, a material note. CB2 does this — when they say "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS," the hero is literally a classic piece. Wayfair says "BAR STOOLS" and shows me a sectional sofa.
- 2. **Kill the fake countdown.** One deadline per sale. Commit to it. Article's arc over eight days worked because June 26 opened it, July 1 nudged it, July 3 closed it — and that was it. Wayfair has manufactured at least three "last chances" in four days. Either run a credible countdown timer to a real end date or drop urgency language entirely.
- 3. **One hero, one CTA.** Article, Pottery Barn, and even CB2 at their best lead with a single product or category and a single click destination. This email has approximately 20 clickable thumbnails above the fold. Nothing is the primary action.
- 4. **Cut the emoji tax.** Three emojis on each side of the subject adds nothing and signals low production quality. Pottery Barn got a 9/10 with clean subject copy and zero gimmicks.
- 5. **Acknowledge the cadence honestly.** If you're going to send eight emails in five days, at least vary the creative radically. A dog-themed send breaks pattern. This does not.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Custom font — single format only**
```css
@font-face {
  font-family: "Sofia";
  src: url(https://secure.img.wfrcdn.com/st4/stores/common/fonts/sofia/sofia_reg.woff) format("woff");
  mso-font-alt: "Arial"
}
```
Only `.woff` is declared. No `.woff2` (preferred by modern clients), `.eot` (Outlook 2007–2016), or `.ttf`. Gmail and Apple Mail will attempt the download; Outlook desktop will fall back to `mso-font-alt: Arial` (correct). Low risk in practice given email client support, but adding `woff2` would reduce load time for supported clients.

**Redundant `@media` block declarations**
Each utility class gets its own separate `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)` block rather than a single consolidated block. This pattern repeats at least 20+ times in the visible excerpt. While not a rendering defect, it inflates `<head>` CSS size. Some older clients (Yahoo Mail app, older Samsung Mail) enforce CSS byte limits; if total `<style>` content is large, rules past the limit are silently dropped.

**`word-break: break-all` on product names**
```css
.ProductRec-name { word-break: break-all; ... }
```
`break-all` is more aggressive than `break-word` and will split mid-word at the column edge even when a soft break point exists. Consider `overflow-wrap: break-word` instead.

**`height` fixed values on star/price/copy cells at mobile**
```css
.ProductRec-stars, .ProductRec-shippingCopy { height: 16px }
.ProductRec-price { height: 40px; ... }
```
Fixed pixel heights will clip content if product data (price, shipping copy) is longer than the allotted container on certain Android clients that apply their own font scaling. Cannot confirm actual content length from truncated source.
