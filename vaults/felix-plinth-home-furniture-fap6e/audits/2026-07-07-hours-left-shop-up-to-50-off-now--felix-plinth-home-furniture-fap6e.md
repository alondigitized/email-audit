# HOURS LEFT! Shop up to 50% off now
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- Eight. That's where I've landed on the Crate & Barrel family email tally for today, July 7th — and this one, number eight, shows up wearing the Crate & Kids logo with a countdown timer ticking through its final hours on a sale I've already seen offered at *sixty* percent off from the parent brand. This is fifty. I've already been offered more, from the same company, today. That math doesn't make me want to click; it makes me want to unsubscribe from the sub-brand entirely.
- Beyond the discount regression, I don't have a single nursery, bunk bed, or toddler-scaled anything in either renovation. Both homes are adults-only. The product grid I'm scrolling through is baby strollers and nursery furniture. The "landmark goals for the baby who is going places" copy is charming — genuinely — but it's aimed at a customer who is not me. Crate & Barrel proper clearly knows I'm renovating; Crate & Kids apparently received zero of that signal.
- Mark as read. No click. Definitely not forwarding this — my friends who have kids already know about the sale because they've been CC'd on the same seven prior emails from the parent brand.

## What's working

- **Countdown timer in the hero** — `00:07:28:34` is doing the same urgency work it did in the "Last call! Up to 60% off ENDS TONIGHT!" email from earlier today. It's a real, live clock and it creates genuine pressure even when I don't care about the category.
- **Clean visual hierarchy** — eyes go: logo → timer → headline → product grid. No confusion about what this email is or what it wants me to do.
- **Crisp layout, no render bugs** — every image is loading, nothing is overlapping, text is readable at mobile size.
- **"we love these for you" personalization section** — the intent is right even if the execution misfired completely on category. At least they tried to surface curated picks rather than a generic grid dump.

## What's weak

- **Fifty percent when I already saw sixty** — The "Last call! Up to 60% off the Warehouse Sale ENDS TONIGHT!" email hit my inbox earlier today from the parent brand. Now Crate & Kids shows up with 50% off from the same warehouse sale. The discount went backwards. That's not urgency; that's confusion.
- **Zero demographic fit** — Baby strollers, nursery lighting, toddler furniture. I'm 36, renovating two adult spaces. Pottery Barn at least sends me living room and kitchen content; this feels like a list-management failure where I got bucketed into the C&K segment by mistake.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — This is email eight-plus from the C&B family on a single day. Even the emails I care about are getting buried. Article managed four emails over a multi-week sale arc and I still opened every one; this brand sent eight in a calendar day and I'm numb.
- **"landmark goals for the baby who is going places"** — cute line, genuinely, but it's the sharpest possible reminder that this email was not built for me.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress Crate & Kids sends to non-parent subscribers.** This is the single highest-leverage fix. If my Crate & Barrel purchase history has zero nursery, zero juvenile furniture, zero gift registry — don't route me into the Kids sub-brand blast. Wayfair at least tries to serve me rugs and bathroom fixtures; this is a targeting miss that wastes a send on a warm subscriber.
- 2. **Resolve the 50% vs 60% message conflict before it hits the inbox.** If the parent brand is at 60% off, the sub-brand should match or exceed it, not undercut it on the same day. The countdown timer means nothing if I saw a bigger number two emails ago.
- 3. **Add one adult crossover product if you're going to send to non-parents.** Even a "shop the sale across all Crate brands" CTA at the bottom would salvage the send for someone like me.
- 4. **Kill the ALL-CAPS subject.** "HOURS LEFT!" in caps plus a countdown timer is redundant urgency stacking. Choose one.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Dev placeholder comment left in production** (`<head>`, line ~22):
  ```html
  <!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
  ```
  Typo ("IMPUT") and scaffolding comment should be stripped before send. Low risk, but signals incomplete template cleanup.

- **`<title>` tag brand mismatch:**
  ```html
  <title>Crate & Barrel</title>
  ```
  Email is sent from `crateandkids@mail.crateandbarrel.com` under the "Crate & Kids" brand. The title renders in some clients' tab/preview bars and should match the sending brand.

- **Conflicting `img` max-width media queries:**
  ```css
  @media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
  @media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
  ```
  At ≥768px both rules match; cascade order makes 640px win (declared last), overriding the intended 600px cap for desktop. Reorder or consolidate.

- **`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile — WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 failure:
  ```html
  <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
  ```

- **Global text-size-adjust disabled:**
  ```css
  *{ -webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; }
  ```
  Suppresses OS-level font scaling for users with accessibility needs. Scope to `body` or remove.

- **Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags:** declared once combined and three times individually (telephone, address, date). Redundant; consolidate into one.

- **No dark mode support declared:** `color-scheme` and `supported-color-schemes` are both set to `light` only. No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block present. Not a bug but a rendering gap for dark-mode email clients (Apple Mail, Outlook 2021+).
