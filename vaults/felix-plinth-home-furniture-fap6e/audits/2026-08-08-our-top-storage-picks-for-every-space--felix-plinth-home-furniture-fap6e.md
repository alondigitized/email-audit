# Our top storage picks for every space
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "our top storage picks" — that's actually a headline I sit up for, because storage is 90% of what a two-house reno turns into once the excitement of tile and paint wears off. I need consoles for the media wall, a cabinet for the entryway, bookcases for the office. So credit where due: this one didn't get an instant scroll-past like the Crate & Kids nursery blasts I've been fielding all week.
- But then it just... keeps going. Raffael, Ventana, Mota, Calypso, Keane, Campagna, Siena — seven collections, each with a room shot, a little italicized customer-quote blurb, a "Shop Now" button, and a row of thumbnails with prices. It's a catalog dump, not an email. Nowhere in this entire scroll is there an actual number — no % off, no "save $200," nothing. Compare that to Pottery Barn's "Warehouse Sale — Up to 60% off" from last week, which I gave an 8 because the headline number stopped my scroll cold. This one has zero numbers except a vague "$500" sweepstakes mention buried near the bottom.
- I'd open it — the subject line earns that much — but I wouldn't click past the hero. I might screenshot the Ventana cabinet for my "maybe" folder since it's genuinely a good-looking piece, but I'm not forwarding this to anyone and I'm not entering a sweepstakes to win furniture money. Mark as read, move on.

## What's working

- The subject actually names my category — "storage" is exactly what I'm hunting for mid-renovation, unlike the last two Crate & Kids nursery sends I have no use for.
- The hero copy ("Find a beautiful home for everything you love with bestselling cabinets, consoles and more") is specific to the product type, not generic lifestyle fluff.
- Real named collections with real prices under each thumbnail — I can actually price-compare Raffael vs. Mota vs. Keane without leaving the email.
- The little italicized customer-quote lines under each collection name read like genuine review snippets, which is more trust signal than most of these catalog scrolls bother with.
- Clean render — no broken images, no overlapping text, seven collections deep and it never glitches.

## What's weak

- No discount anywhere. Not one %, not one $ off. That's a real miss when Pottery Barn, CB2, and even Wayfair (pergola sale) are all leading with hard numbers this same window.
- Seven collections in one email is exhausting — by Campagna and Siena I've stopped reading the taglines, I'm just skimming for a console that isn't $1,800.
- The "enter for a chance to win $500" sweepstagraph at the bottom feels tacked on and vague — no rules, no deadline visible, reads like filler instead of a real incentive.
- "Shop Now" repeated seven-plus times is lazy CTA copy — none of it tells me what I'm shopping for beyond what the collection name already said.
- This is the second Crate & Barrel email hitting my inbox in the same window as the "Buy once, use forever: Hosting essentials" send I gave an 8 to yesterday — that one had a real Le Creuset markdown and a Design Desk booking CTA baked into the body. This one has neither a price cut nor as tight a pitch, and it's starting to feel like C&B is just cycling merchandising emails at me daily.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with an actual offer. If there's a storage sale running, put the number in the subject and hero — Pottery Barn's "60% off" proved that's what stops the scroll. If there isn't a sale, don't pretend this is offer-driven; reframe as editorial ("New Fall Storage Edit") instead of implying a deal that isn't there.
- 2. Cut seven collections to three. Pick the best sellers (Ventana and Keane looked strongest to me) and give them real space instead of diluting across a marathon scroll.
- 3. Move the Design Desk / free consultation CTA up near the hero — that's the one thing that actually converts renovation shoppers like me, and right now it's buried two-thirds down.
- 4. Kill or clarify the $500 sweepstakes — either give it real terms and a deadline, or drop it. Right now it just looks like filler copy.
- 5. Give CTA buttons product-specific copy ("Shop Consoles," "Shop Cabinets") instead of seven identical "Shop Now" buttons.

## Technical Audit: Crate & Barrel — "Our top storage picks for every space"

**1. Technical Summary**
Standard multi-vendor ESP template (Cheetah Mail/Marigold-style `mail.crateandbarrel.com` infrastructure + Scene7 image CDN) with heavy MSO/Outlook conditional markup and mobile media queries. Automated QA returned 0 hard issues and 4 warnings, primarily around missing alt text and unverified compliance headers.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- 82 tracking/click-redirect links were present but skipped by the automated HTTP prober by design (redirect-domain heuristic) — link destination health (404s, broken redirects) is **unverified**, not confirmed-clean.
- Multiple third-party tracking pixels fire beyond the standard open pixel: `sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif` (LiveRamp identity resolution, 5 sequential calls `n=1`–`n=5`), `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/...?cp_tp=v` (view-through conversion pixel), and 7 `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png` engagement pixels plus one `p/up/*/o.gif` open pixel. No functional issue confirmed, but this is a lot of concurrent pixel load — flagging for awareness.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- 45 images are missing `alt` text across both CDNs (`image.mail.crateandbarrel.com` product/lifestyle images and `s7d5.scene7.com/is/image/Crate/...` hero/product shots), e.g. `202504_CB_Storage_Hero_Primary`, `202504_CB_Storage_Ventana_Cabinet`, `202504_CB_Storage_Rule`. With images blocked (default in Outlook/many mobile clients), the email renders with no fallback text.
- Head contains a leftover template placeholder: `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` — appears to be an unfilled/unremoved build artifact (also has a typo, "IMPUT" for "INPUT"), harmless but suggests the build wasn't fully cleaned before send.
- MSO conditional VML/Outlook fixes and responsive `@media` breakpoints (460px, 640px, 768px) are present — standard defensive rendering coverage, no issues found there.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
No unresolved merge tags (e.g. `{{firstname}}`, `%%FIELD%%`) observed in the visible portion of the source. The `mi_u=` hash parameter on tracking pixels is a resolved subscriber token, not a broken merge field. Note: HTML was truncated for this review, so body content beyond the visible portion wasn't inspected.

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
- `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) headers were not detected. **Caveat from the QA tool itself: these may simply not be captured by the AgentMail relay rather than genuinely absent** — this needs verification against the raw SMTP headers at the source, not just the relayed copy, before treating it as a real compliance gap. If genuinely absent, this fails Gmail/Yahoo's 2024 bulk-sender one-click unsubscribe requirement.
- `Authentication-Results` (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) header also not found, same relay caveat applies — SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is **unknown**, not confirmed-failing.

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
Cannot be confirmed — all 82 outbound links route through click-redirect domains and were excluded from the automated probe, so post-redirect UTM structure and landing-page destinations were not captured in this pass.

**7. Recommendations**
1. Verify `List-Unsubscribe`/`List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results` directly against raw sender headers (not the AgentMail-relayed copy) to confirm whether the compliance gap is real or a relay artifact.
2. Add descriptive `alt` text to the 45 flagged images, particularly the Scene7 hero/product images, for accessibility and images-off fallback.
3. Remove the leftover `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT...]-->` placeholder comment before next send.
4. Plain-text version is 70%+ URL characters (15,793/22,520) — reduce link density or shorten redirect URLs, as this ratio is a common spam-filter trigger.
5. Follow up on the 82 skipped tracking links with a manual or allow-listed probe to confirm no broken redirects, since this pass provides no signal either way.
