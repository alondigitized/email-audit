# Here’s your chance to win your dream soccer setup ⚽
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- Forty. I'm calling it forty from the C&B family right now, because this email arrived on the same day as the Sonos Match-Day sweepstakes, two back-to-school sends, and a rug material guide that somehow got sent twice. The volume is the story before the subject line is.
- That said — "soccer setup" is a legitimate hook in June 2026. World Cup is happening in North America, I've got an unfinished outdoor space at the second property, and I am absolutely the person who will justify a $3,400 sectional as "infrastructure for the tournament." So the subject line snagged me. For about four seconds.
- Then I opened it and found a sofa in a living room. The hero is beautiful — genuinely nice C&B lifestyle photography — but it's an indoor couch shot for an email promising a "dream soccer setup." The rest scrolls into a familiar outdoor furniture grid I've seen in their summer catalog, with a sweepstakes entry bolted on top. No price to calculate, no rewards math to run. Yesterday's Sonos email was the smarter execution of this exact cultural moment — a real brand doing a real thing. This one feels like the brief said "sports tie-in" and the template team grabbed the closest summer asset. I'll save it to my folder and forget it's there. No click. No forward.

## What's working

- The World Cup 2026 timing is genuinely good — this is exactly when someone with an unfinished outdoor space starts feeling deadline pressure, and C&B is right to chase it
- "Bring home the winning setup" translates the sports hook into home language cleanly — no jerseys or turf, just the aspirational framing applied to furniture
- The multi-product grid below the fold is efficient for a two-property buyer scanning for gaps; I can move fast through it
- Crate & Barrel logo is trust currency — I never question the sender, which is a real advantage over the CB2 "SO EARTHY" email from this week, where I spent three seconds confirming it wasn't spam
- Layout is clean, no render breaks, images load — a functional baseline the Wayfair "🔍 DRESSERS & CHESTS 🔍" email can't always claim

## What's weak

- The hero image is a living room sofa — beautiful, wrong — the "soccer setup" subject line promises an outdoor, big-game-ready space and the first thing I see is an indoor couch
- This is a sweepstakes, not an offer; I'm a rewards-card transactional shopper and there is no math here for me to run
- Yesterday's Sonos Match-Day email already owned this cultural moment with a stronger brand-partnership story — sending a second, thematically identical sweepstakes inside 24 hours is the definition of diminishing returns
- Soccer feels bolted onto C&B's brand DNA from the outside; Article pulled off outdoor lifestyle extension on June 9 because their entire identity lives there — C&B doing soccer reads like a brief, not a conviction
- Still zero personalization after roughly forty sends — I know my account, my rewards tier, my order history is in their system, and they still greet me like a cold list

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the hero:** An outdoor sectional with a wall-mounted TV, an outdoor Sonos speaker, maybe a fire pit — close the gap between the subject line promise and the first thing the eye lands on; this single change is worth more than anything else on this list
- 2. **Anchor a real offer alongside the sweepstakes:** "Enter to win AND get 15% off the setup as a Rewards member" gives transactional shoppers like me a reason to click now instead of leaving it to lottery odds
- 3. **Sequence the sweepstakes emails:** Sonos on Day 1, outdoor furniture on Day 2, outdoor hosting/kitchen on Day 3 — a logical campaign arc instead of two near-identical sweepstakes entries in a single inbox day
- 4. **Add quick-ship language to the product tiles:** "Ships before the quarterfinals" is a Pottery Barn-level utility move (they did exactly this in the quick-ship furniture email) and it turns a browsing grid into a buying grid
- 5. **Tighten the subject line:** just over 50 chars with the emoji, and "Here's your chance to win" is four words of soft preamble before we get to the noun
- **Subject Alt A:** `Win the ultimate home gameday setup`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your outdoor space, World Cup-ready`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Outdoor furniture that ships before the semifinals — plus enter to win.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the setup. Enter the sweepstakes. Both take under a minute.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Conflicting desktop `max-width` media queries (confirmed rendering defect)**

Two media queries target desktop viewports and both set `img { max-width }`:

```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }  /* declared last */
```

At any viewport ≥768 px, both queries match. The 640 px query appears **later in source order**, so its `max-width: 640px` declaration wins via cascade. The intent was almost certainly `600px` on wide screens. Affected images will render 40 px wider than designed on desktop.
