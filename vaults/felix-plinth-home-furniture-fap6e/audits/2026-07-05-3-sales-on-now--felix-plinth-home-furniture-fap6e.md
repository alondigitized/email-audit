# 3 SALES ON NOW
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- Nine. That's where I'm at with CB2 since July 1st, and this one — dropping July 5th and stacking three sales like a combo meal — is where I honestly feel the exhaustion peak. "3 SALES ON NOW" reads like a retailer who's afraid you'll close the tab before they finish their sentence. The countdown timer running up top is a smart move — I clocked it at around 2 days left, which at least isn't fake urgency — and "The Sunday Sale Edit" framing gets credit for being seasonally honest. But the July 4th sale block is still up there, which feels slightly stale on the 5th. The floor sample sale appeared in my inbox on July 3rd already. The Warehouse Sale at 60% off is the only genuinely new offer.
- I'll save this one. Not because I'm excited — but because the Warehouse Sale is the kind of thing I'd lose track of if I archive it. Two houses mean I need floor samples and clearance items, and 60% off clears the math on stuff I'd otherwise buy at full price. I won't forward it, though. This is a solo-shopping email, not a "look what I found" moment.
- Would I click? On the Warehouse Sale section, yes. The rest I'd scroll past.

## What's working

- **The Warehouse Sale is the hidden gem here** — 60% off gets my attention in a way that the July 4th 50% banner doesn't, because it implies genuine clearance, not manufactured discount optics.
- **Countdown timer with days remaining** gives the deadline actual weight; unlike Pottery Barn's vague "sale is STILL ON" language (their July 5th send), this is concrete.
- **Three distinct CTAs with different mechanics** — shop online, find a store, shop the warehouse — that's actually useful if you're splitting purchases across two properties and two logistical contexts.
- **"The Sunday Sale Edit" framing** is a small win; it acknowledges the day and makes the email feel intentional rather than auto-blasted.
- **Layout is clean** — each sale block has its own visual and hierarchy doesn't collapse between sections.

## What's weak

- **The July 4th block on July 5th feels stale.** Article did "Up to 40% off ends Monday" on July 3rd and at least had the discipline to not call it "4th of July" after the fact. CB2 is still waving the flag a day late.
- **"SHOP NOW" across every CTA is interchangeable** — the Warehouse Sale and the July 4th Sale have identical button copy, which flattens urgency. The floor sample block at least differentiates with "Find a Store."
- **The "More For You" product grid is too small to evaluate** — tiny thumbnails and prices I can't read on mobile. If those are actually personalized recs, they're buried. Crate & Barrel's "Last Call" email on July 5th put brand-name products front and center; this doesn't.
- **No rewards/loyalty callout** — I'm making buying decisions on rewards math, and there's nothing here about CB2 Rewards, financing, or stacking member benefits. Pottery Barn consistently threads this in.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Warehouse Sale, not the July 4th one.** The 60% hook is stronger and feels less expired. Flip the block order: Warehouse → Floor Sample → July 4th Sale Reminder.
- 2. **Retire "JULY 4TH SALE" from the copy** — it's July 5th. Rename it "Summer Sale" or "Long Weekend Sale" so it doesn't immediately feel like day-old bread.
- 3. **Differentiate the CTAs** — "Shop Furniture" / "Shop Lighting" / "Find Nearest Store" — give each block a reason to click distinct from the others.
- 4. **Tie in CB2 Rewards math** — even a one-liner like "Members earn double points through Sunday" would catch my attention and change the decision calculus.
- 5. **Make the "More For You" grid actionable** — larger product tiles, readable prices, one-tap add-to-cart. Right now it's decorative.

- **Subject Alt B:** `3 days, 3 sales — pick your save`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Floor samples, clearance, and summer discounts all running now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Warehouse Sale goes 60% off. Floor samples in-store. Summer sale online.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **`maximum-scale=1` viewport meta** blocks pinch-to-zoom on mobile. This is an accessibility violation for low-vision users and violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
  ```html
  <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
  ```

- **`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied globally** via the `*` selector prevents OS-level text scaling. Should be `100%`, not `none`.
  ```css
  *{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;-ms-text-size-adjust:none;...}
  ```

- **Double-slash font URLs** — three `@font-face` declarations use `//` in the path, which produces a malformed URL on some mail client WebView implementations:
  ```css
  src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
  src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2")
  src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2")
  ```

- **Cross-domain font loading from `crateandbarrel.com`** — four `HNW05` `@font-face` declarations pull from `www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/`. While CB2 and Crate & Barrel share infrastructure, email clients that enforce strict CORS or content-blocking policies may silently drop these, falling back to system fonts with no visual warning.

- **No dark mode support** — no `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block. Hard-coded `background:#ffffff` will flash white in dark-mode clients (Apple Mail, Outlook on iOS) before rendering.

- **Non-standard mobile breakpoint** — `@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)` is unusual; 480px is the standard email breakpoint. The `.mobile` class targets `width:414px`, suggesting the intent is iPhone-specific, but this will miss narrower Android devices.
