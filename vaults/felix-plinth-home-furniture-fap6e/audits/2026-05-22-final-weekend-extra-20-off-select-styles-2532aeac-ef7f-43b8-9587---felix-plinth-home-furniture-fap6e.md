# Final weekend! EXTRA 20% off select styles
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- Twenty. I counted before I even opened this one. Nineteen Pottery Barn emails since Saturday, and email nineteen was already throw pillows — so this morning I walked into my inbox knowing roughly what the twentieth was going to look like. I was right. "Final weekend." "Extra 20% off." "Select styles." This is the same offer I got on Tuesday when it said "ASAP," the same offer I got Wednesday with a magnifying glass emoji, the same offer I got yesterday in the "Book FREE design help" email that somehow led with a concierge service but still wrapped the same 20% discount around it.
- That said — the email itself is clean. No broken images. The hierarchy works. Category nav across the top is genuinely useful when I'm shopping Furniture and Outdoor for two separate properties simultaneously. The "Recommended Just For You" block at the bottom has bed frames and platform beds in it, which are literally on my list right now. And the Affirm mention plus the 50% Back in Rewards line are the two things that would actually influence where I buy a $1,200 piece of furniture.
- Would I click? Reluctantly, into the Recommended block — specifically to see whether that platform bed is the Caymen or the Farmhouse. Would I forward this? No. I'd forward Article's single-offer clean send from earlier this week before I forwarded anything from this campaign. Article said "Up to 40% off ends Monday" once and meant it. Pottery Barn has said "final" four times.

## What's working

- **Category nav strip is actually useful at this moment**: Furniture, Outdoor, Bedding, Rugs, Lighting — with two renovation projects running in parallel, having those tabs in one row saves me a homepage click.
- **Hero offer reads in one glance**: "Extra 20% off" is the first thing the eye lands on, and "Plus new finds up to 60% off" is right beneath it. No treasure hunt.
- **Recommended Just For You section is doing real work**: I can see platform beds in there, which is exactly my current shopping category — this is the one part of the email that feels personalized rather than broadcast.
- **Earn 50% Back in Rewards is surfaced**: For someone who routes every large purchase through the highest-rewards card and tracks loyalty programs across PB, Crate & Barrel, and CB2, this number matters. It's the deciding factor between buying here versus finding the same item on Wayfair.
- **Affirm is placed appropriately**: Big furniture buys make sense on installment. Article buried it; this email puts it where it belongs.

## What's weak

- **"Final weekend" means nothing after nineteen previous sends**: The urgency framing was burned on Tuesday. At this point "Final weekend!" reads as "we have more emails scheduled for Sunday."
- **"SHOP ALL SALE" is where buying intent goes to die**: That dumps me into a 300-item clearance grid. CB2 ran "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" with an actual editorial point of view; this is just a portal to everything.
- **No hero product**: Article's best-performing email this week anchored to one specific sofa at 40% off. This email is a menu. Menus don't create urgency.
- **Family of Brands cross-sell is filler**: I know West Elm exists. I've received their emails this week too. That section is real estate that could have gone to a larger Recommended grid.
- **Recommended product images are too small to read**: The product names under those tiles are essentially invisible at normal viewing size. If that's my reason to click, I need to be able to actually see what I'm clicking toward.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the cadence to three sends maximum**: Preview, peak day, genuine final call — and mean it when you say final. Article is winning this Memorial Day weekend in my inbox by sending fewer, sharper emails. Twenty sends is not urgency, it's noise.
- 2. **Promote the top Recommended product into the hero slot**: Pull the highest-ranked item from my personalization engine, set it as the hero with a product name and price point visible, and make the CTA "Shop [Product Name]" — not "Shop All Sale."
- 3. **Replace "SHOP ALL SALE" with a category-specific CTA**: "Shop Furniture Sale" or "Shop Bedroom Sale" — something that tells me where I'm going before I tap.
- 4. **Drop the Family of Brands block entirely**: This is not a brand awareness email. Give that space to a second row of personalized product tiles at a readable size.
- 5. **Make "Final weekend" mean something**: If you're going to use deadline language, commit to it — include a specific end time ("Sale ends Sunday at midnight") or remove the urgency framing that has now been devalued across the entire campaign.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your platform bed picks: 20% off through Sunday only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `This is the last Memorial Day email. 20% off, ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Furniture, bedding, outdoor — your recommended items at Memorial Day pricing`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `50% Back in Rewards + 20% off. Last call before Monday.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HIGH — PHP code fragment inside `<style>` block**
The following server-side PHP code was not executed and leaked raw into the HTML `<style>` tag:
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
CSS parsers will silently ignore this, so rendering impact is likely nil, but it is a template compilation defect — the image-serving script's PHP headers were pasted into the wrong file or the template partial was not pre-processed before inlining.

Gmail and many clients block non-HTTPS image sources. Affected assets:
