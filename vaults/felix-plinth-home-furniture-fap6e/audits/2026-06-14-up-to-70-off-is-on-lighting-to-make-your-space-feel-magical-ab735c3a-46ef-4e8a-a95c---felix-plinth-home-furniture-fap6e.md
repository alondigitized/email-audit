# Up to 70% off is ON + Lighting to make your space feel magical
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- Nineteen. I'm leading with that number because it does more damage to this email than anything in the creative. The June 14 "100+ new sale styles UP TO 70% OFF" send was #18. Now, same afternoon, here comes #19 with the same sale wrapper and a lighting category tacked onto the subject like someone A/B-tested two different briefs and accidentally shipped the fusion version. "Up to 70% off is ON" is functionally the same subject line I saw in emails #15, #17, and #18. The lighting half is actually interesting — I have two properties that need fixture work right now — but it's drowning under the fifth "up to 70% off" cadence send in 72 hours.
- That said, the creative itself is the best lighting-specific email Pottery Barn has sent me in this sprint. The hero is an evening patio scene with string lights and a fire pit. The content blocks — "Add ambiance to every outdoor," "Create an inviting atmosphere," "Linger longer around the fire" — hold together as an editorial story about outdoor living after dark. That's a real use case. Article did the exact same emotional move with "Summer hosting made simple" last week and I clicked through that one. The difference: Article was email four of twelve days. This is email nineteen of five.
- The "Recommended Just For You" section at the bottom is the most compelling thing in this send if the personalization is real. It's also buried at the bottom, which tells me no one decided this email was actually about personalization — it just lives there. I'd file this, not forward it, and maybe revisit when I'm in a Pottery Barn window and not experiencing brand fatigue I can feel in my spine.

## What's working

- The outdoor/lighting hero is genuinely atmospheric — string lights draped over an evening patio, fire pit glowing, it lands for a June send in a way the pure-sale creative does not
- Three thematic content blocks ("Add ambiance," "inviting atmosphere," "linger around the fire") tell a coherent story; more editorial logic here than the June 13 "Up to 70% off 100s of NEW DEALS" send which was just a sale grid
- 70% off is clearly stated in the hero — I don't have to hunt for the offer
- "Recommended Just For You" module signals that Pottery Barn has browsing data on me, which is the one thing that could make me engage
- Clean render throughout — no broken images, no overlapping elements, layout held up

## What's weak

- Nineteen emails in five days is the loudest signal in this send — no subject line wins that fight
- The subject line is two campaigns stitched with a plus sign: "Up to 70% off is ON" and "Lighting to make your space feel magical" feel like they came from different briefs and no one picked one
- "is ON" is strange phrasing — it reads like someone wrote "SALE IS ON" and softened it without actually reworking the line
- Four competing content modules means the email never commits; it's a mood board, not a shopping prompt, and the same lamp I've seen in Crate & Barrel's inbox twice this week might as well be in this grid too
- No deadline on the 70% off — CB2's "Warehouse Sale" framing at least implies scarcity; "is ON" could mean anything from today to next month

## Recommendations

- 1. **Run the lighting editorial as a standalone send — drop the sale wrapper entirely.** The outdoor lighting story is strong enough on its own and would have felt like a break from the sale cadence. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Put an end date on the sale, in the subject.** "Ends Sunday" or "Last 48 hours" would actually move me. "is ON" is not urgency.
- 3. **Collapse four content blocks to two** — one sale hero, one lighting story — and commit to a primary CTA for each. Right now it's asking me to make four different decisions and I make none.
- 4. **Move "Recommended Just For You" to the second module**, immediately below the hero, not buried at the bottom. If the data is real, that's the highest-intent placement.
- 5. **Show a price on at least one hero product.** Wayfair does this poorly too — "up to 70% off" without anchoring on a specific item feels like a floor wax / dessert topping situation.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three ways to make your backyard feel different after dark.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `We pulled your outdoor lighting picks from the sale.`
