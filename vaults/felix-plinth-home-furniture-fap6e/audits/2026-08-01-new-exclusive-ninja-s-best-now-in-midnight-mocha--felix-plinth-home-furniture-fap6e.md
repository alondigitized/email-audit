# New & exclusive! Ninja’s best, now in Midnight Mocha
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-01**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so Crate & Barrel is doing a full-court press on this "Midnight Mocha" Ninja color story, and I clocked pretty quickly that this is basically the sequel to the "Ninja's top kitchen tech in an exclusive, chic hue ☕" email that also hit my inbox today. Same brand, same day, same dark-bronze appliance lineup, different subject line dressing. That's the exact "same lamp in three brands' emails" annoyance I have, except this time it's the same brand emailing itself twice.
- That said, once I get past the déjà vu, there's actually something here for me — I'm mid-renovation on two kitchens, and a Double Blade multicooker and an espresso setup in a color that could genuinely work with either house's palette isn't nothing. The "we love these for you" grid near the bottom (Dutch oven, cast iron, bakeware) reads like they've actually looked at what I've bought before, which is more than I can say for Wayfair's cat tree bait-and-switch send I got dinged for grading. I'd scroll through this one on my commute, but I'm not clicking "Shop Now" today — I'm still deciding if Midnight Mocha matches my counters, and this email doesn't help me answer that.
- Would I forward it? No. Would I mark it read and maybe circle back when I'm actually shopping appliances for the second house? Yeah, probably.

## What's working

- The "up to 60% off The Summer Sale" banner up top gives me an immediate number to anchor on, which is more than CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" send managed with its vague headline.
- The Midnight Mocha color-story consistency across the toaster oven, kettle, mixer, and espresso machine is genuinely well art-directed — it reads like a cohesive line, not a grab-bag.
- The Le Creuset "$175 off 6.5 Qt Dutch Oven" and the "we love these for you" section further down feel like they know I buy cookware, not just decor.
- The credit card rewards block ("the card you love just got better") is the one section that speaks directly to how I actually shop — I run everything through my highest-rewards card, so a cash-back pitch from a brand I already buy from lands better than another generic banner ad.
- Clean render, no broken images or overlapping text — better execution than some of the messier Crate & Kids sends I've been getting this week.

## What's weak

- This is the second Ninja Midnight Mocha email from Crate & Barrel today — I already opened and reviewed the other one. That's aggressive cadence even for a brand I'm generally fine hearing from.
- The hero collection is "new & exclusive," which usually means full price, but it's sitting right under a "60% off" banner — that's the same kind of promise-then-pivot I called out on Wayfair's cat tree email. Pick one story.
- Nothing here addresses shipping timelines or delivery windows for appliances, which matters more to me than it would for a throw pillow — I've been burned by "in stock" claims before on furniture for the second house.
- The email sprawls into heritage decor, a $5,000 design sweepstakes, dinnerware, and a credit card pitch all in one send — by the time I hit the Aspen collection I've forgotten this started as a Ninja appliance drop.
- No swatch or sample option for the Midnight Mocha finish itself, which is exactly the kind of "will this actually match my kitchen" question I need answered before I click anything appliance-related.

## Recommendations

- 1. Don't send two Midnight Mocha Ninja emails on the same day — pick the stronger creative and hold the other for next week. This is the single biggest issue; it's actively working against itself.
- 2. Fix the "60% off" vs. "new & exclusive" mismatch — either discount the Ninja line or drop the sale banner from that section entirely so it doesn't read like bait.
- 3. Move the credit-card rewards block up near the top — that's the strongest hook for a rewards-card shopper like me and it's currently buried near the footer.
- 4. Trim the email — split the design-services sweepstakes and dinnerware catalog into their own sends rather than tacking them onto a kitchen-appliance drop.
- 5. Add a one-line delivery/lead-time note near the hero appliances — even "ships in 3-5 days" would reduce my hesitation to click.
