# 📞 Last call for EXTRA 20% off select styles + Now on sale: Your next favorite furniture find
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-26**
## Executive summary

- Thirty. I'm at thirty Pottery Barn Memorial Day emails since Sunday, and I already know that before I open this one because I've been counting. The phone emoji in the subject — 📞 — is genuinely puzzling for a furniture brand. It's not a callback service, there's no urgent call-to-action that requires a phone, it just sits there looking like a butt-dial. And "Last call" is doing zero credibility work when the "LAST DAY 🚨" send from this morning — email twenty-nine, which I already filed and reviewed — is still sitting five slots up in my inbox thread.
- Once I'm inside, the execution is honestly fine. The hero reads clearly: LAST DAY, Extra 20% off, SHOP NOW. The category grid — Outdoor, Sofas & Chairs, Lighting, Rugs, Curtains — is exactly how I navigate furniture. I'm sourcing room by room across two homes, and being able to jump to "Lighting" directly is more useful than whatever hero product they picked. The Free Design Service callout near the bottom is legitimately relevant to me; two simultaneous renovation projects is precisely its target use case. The Affirm module and the rewards reminder at the bottom are the kind of loyalty math I run in my head before pulling the trigger.
- But the "Recommended Just for You" section handed me a mattress recommendation. I've been looking at dining chairs and outdoor sectionals. A mattress in a Memorial Day furniture email is an algorithm throwing darts. Crate & Barrel's "HOURS LEFT: Up to 60% off + Double Rewards" that I bookmarked this morning felt more like a single, purposeful send. Article's "Last chance: Memorial Day Sale ends today" actually landed on a clean cadence. Pottery Barn has turned this holiday into a DDoS attack on my inbox and killed the urgency on every send since Thursday. I won't click. I'll mark it read and go check whether my C&B cart is still live.

## What's working

- **Category navigation grid is exactly right** — Outdoor / Sofas & Chairs / Lighting / Rugs is how I actually browse; I can land on the room I'm sourcing without hunting.
- **Hero offer is front-and-center** — "LAST DAY — Extra 20% off" renders immediately without any scroll or decode; the visual hierarchy does its job.
- **Rewards callout earns its space** — "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" activates my highest-rewards-card brain; putting the loyalty math in the email is smart.
- **Free Design Service module** — Two homes in simultaneous renovation is the exact use case for this; the callout is useful even if the cadence burned my goodwill before I got there.
- **Clean layout, no render bugs** — Nothing broken, images loaded, text isn't overlapping, the hierarchy is legible at a glance.

## What's weak

- **The phone emoji makes no sense** — 📞 in a furniture brand subject line is jarring; it reads like the creative team ran out of home-related emojis and grabbed whatever was next in the picker.
- **"Last call" arriving after "LAST DAY"** — They sent a "LAST DAY 🚨" email earlier today and then followed it with "Last call." That sequencing destroys whatever urgency either email had.
- **The "Recommended Just for You" products don't feel targeted** — A mattress tile in a Memorial Day outdoor/furniture sale email tells me the personalization engine isn't connected to my actual browse history.
- **Subject line is 91 characters** — Two subject lines duct-taped together with a "+"; on mobile the entire second clause ("Now on sale: Your next favorite furniture find") gets cut and adds nothing anyway.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the second clause from the subject entirely** — Drop "+ Now on sale: Your next favorite furniture find." The extra 20% off IS the news. Everything after the plus is throat-clearing.
- 2. **Drop the phone emoji** — Nothing in this email requires a phone. Use a chair emoji, a house, or nothing. The 📞 actively confuses the brand signal.
- 3. **Honor the "last day" timeline** — If you called it LAST DAY at 9am, don't send a "Last call" at 2pm. It trains me to ignore urgency language from this sender permanently.
- 4. **Make "Recommended Just for You" earn the real estate** — If I've browsed outdoor seating, surface that sofa. A generic mattress recommendation in a Memorial Day send is a wasted module.
- 5. **Add a specific room hook to the hero copy** — "Last chance on outdoor furniture: extra 20% off through midnight" would have me checking my saved items instead of reaching for archive.

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard-broken links (403) will silently fail for affected users — one is the credit card banner CTA, the other a cross-brand tracking pixel from West Elm. Widespread missing alt text and three HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources are the next highest-priority class of issues.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Credit card apply/buy CTA returns 403**
`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&...`
The Key Rewards credit card banner CTA is dead. Users who click receive an HTTP 403; the offer is unreachable.

**FAIL — West Elm cross-brand spacer pixel returns 403**
`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
This is a layout spacer pulled from a West Elm CDN domain. The 403 may cause a broken layout row in image-on clients, and the HTTP source compounds the issue (see §3).

**WARN — 24 tracking links unverified**
All click-redirect links through `click.e.potterybarn.com` and `clickcp.potterybarn.com` were skipped by the HTTP probe. The one confirmed 403 above came from `clickcp` — additional broken links in that domain cannot be ruled out without manual spot-checks.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources — may be blocked by clients**
Three `src` attributes use `http://` rather than `https://`:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` (also 403)
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` (open-pixel tracker)

Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all proxy or block mixed-content HTTP sources. The footer credit card banner (`Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`) and brand banner slice will likely render as broken images in major clients.

**Missing alt text — 44 images**
Every product image, all footer slices (slices_01–18), the main hero banner (`S26_Top_MemorialDayLastDay.jpg`), and the open-tracking pixel have empty or absent `alt` attributes. With images disabled (common in B2B inboxes), the email renders as a wall of blank space with no text fallback for any product or CTA.

**`<title>` element is empty**
`<title></title>` — some clients surface this as the email tab label; leaving it blank is a minor but avoidable gap.

**PHP header directives in `<style>` block**
```html
<style>
  ...
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, ...");
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
</style>
```
Server-side PHP `header()` calls were erroneously included inside a CSS `<style>` block. They are inert in HTML but indicate a template assembly bug — a server-side snippet was pasted into the wrong template section.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)
