# MORE ITEMS ADDED! Up to 30% off outdoor + Double Rewards!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-five. I've been counting. And this one — "MORE ITEMS ADDED!" — is doing the thing I hate most in a sale email: it signals desperation before I even open it. The all-caps subject reads like a markdown booth at a trade show, and after I've already worked through four days of "up to 60% off" drumbeating from the same brand family, this lands at 30% and asks me to feel excited about it. It's not a different category treated with care; it's the same sale with a narrower net.
- That said, outdoor furniture is actually where my head is right now. I've got a back patio at the primary that needs a sofa sectional and a dining situation, and the second home needs everything from zero. So the hook is legitimately relevant. The hero image — an outdoor lounge setup — caught my eye more than the last several Crate & Kids or clearance blasts. The Double Rewards callout is the one thing that made me slow down, because I'm already calculating reward points on every purchase I make right now.
- I'd open this. I probably wouldn't forward it — Article ran a cleaner, higher-discount outdoor send earlier this week and I'd send *that* one to my partner if I were sharing anything. But I'd click into the sectional category just to see what's been added. Mark as partially read, revisit tonight.

## What's working

- **Outdoor is the right category for me right now** — two homes, zero outdoor furniture purchased yet; this email is accidentally well-timed even if the cadence around it is brutal.
- **The hero image earns its space** — the outdoor seating setup looks aspirational without being unreachably editorial; I can picture it on my patio.
- **Double Rewards is the right lever** — I buy on the rewards card and I track points actively; this is the one line that made me stop scrolling.
- **Seasonal relevance is real** — summer outdoor email in late May is the obvious play and it's executed correctly here; compare that to Wayfair's electric-fireplace email earlier this week, which felt like they raided the wrong product silo.
- **The dining table section** — outdoor dining is a second genuine need for me; the fact that they merchandised both lounge and dining in a single send is smart vertical coverage.

## What's weak

- **ALL CAPS "MORE ITEMS ADDED!" is inbox spam-flag energy** — Article managed a clean "Up to 40% off ends Monday" without shouting at me; this looks amateur by comparison, especially coming from a brand that should know its audience.
- **30% after a week of 60% is a perceptual downgrade** — I know it's a different category (outdoor vs. indoor), but my inbox brain doesn't distinguish that cleanly; the last Crate & Barrel email I gave an 8 was screaming "400+ NEW DEALS ADDED: Up to 60% off," and now 30% feels like a step backward, not a new story.
- **The grill/food lifestyle shot** — I'm here for furniture, not a Weber ad; that section read like padding and broke my product-browsing flow.
- **No price anchoring visible** — CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" email at least showed me specific items with specific before/after prices; here I have to click through to find out what 30% off actually means on any given sectional.
- **This is email 25.** I need that to land. Twenty-five from the C&B family since Sunday. The cadence has completely eroded the urgency signal — when everything is a sale every day, nothing is.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the ALL CAPS and reframe around the outdoor-specific benefit** — "Outdoor furniture. Up to 30% off + Double Rewards this weekend." is cleaner, less desperate, and surfaces the two actual reasons to click.
- 2. **Show a price anchor in the hero** — "From $299" or "Sectionals from $799" converts browsers to shoppers; Pottery Barn's "Final Weekend" email did this and it earned the click.
- 3. **Kill the grill/food shot** — I'm in the furniture mindset; food lifestyle is a different newsletter. One strong theme, not three.
- 4. **Surface one hero SKU** — Article linked directly to their most-loved outdoor sofa, not to "outdoor category." That's the move. One anchor product with a specific price creates a reason to click now instead of eventually.
- 5. **Cadence** — I know this isn't a single-email fix, but sending me fewer emails would make each one hit harder. The Pottery Barn sends this week landed at 8/10 in part because they saved their biggest offer for the final weekend framing; this one has no such architecture.

- **Subject Alt B:** `30% off outdoor + Double Rewards: new items just added`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop patio sofas, dining sets, and more — new drops since the last send`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text: 40+ images** flagged across all content images on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com` and all tracking pixels. This is a blanket omission — no `alt` attribute present on any `<img>` tag per QA scan.

- Functional images (product shots, CTAs rendered as images) must carry descriptive alt text.
- Decorative spacers and tracking pixels must carry `alt=""` (empty string) to suppress screen reader output.
- Images-off rendering (default in many corporate Outlook environments) will produce a content-free email with no text fallback for any image-based CTA.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` — this prevents user zoom on mobile, which violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is blocked by iOS accessibility settings in some configurations.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags:** `format-detection` appears three times (consolidated once in the first meta, then again as separate telephone/address/date tags). Redundant but harmless.

**`<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` comment left in `<head>`:** Unreplaced template placeholder comment is visible in source. No functional impact but indicates incomplete template cleanup.

If the header exists on the originating MTA but is stripped by the AgentMail relay, Gmail and Yahoo Mail will not surface the native one-click unsubscribe UI (required for bulk senders under Google/Yahoo 2024 sender requirements for >5k/day volume).

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058): not captured** — Same cause. Without this header, one-click unsubscribe (POST-based) is non-functional in compliant clients, which can elevate spam complaint rates.
