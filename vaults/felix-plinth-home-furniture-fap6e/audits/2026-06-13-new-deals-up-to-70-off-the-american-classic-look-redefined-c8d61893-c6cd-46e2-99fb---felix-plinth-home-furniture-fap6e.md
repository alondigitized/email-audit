# NEW deals up to 70% off + The American classic look, redefined
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- Seventeen. That's the number I said out loud before I even looked at the creative, because this is — by my count — the seventeenth Pottery Barn email since Tuesday. The sixteenth was the Monique Lhuillier collab drop from earlier the same day. The fifteenth was the "Up to 70% off 100s of NEW DEALS" send that itself came right after the farmhouse sale. At this cadence, Pottery Barn isn't running a sale — they're running a stress test on my unsubscribe finger.
- Now, setting the volume problem aside for a second: the email itself is actually two emails stitched together. There's a "Summer Sale: Up to 70% off" hero on top, and then somewhere below the fold there's the "American classic look, redefined" angle — the leather armchair styling and the classic Americana buffet vibe. Both are real ideas. Neither gets enough room. Article ran a cleaner single-concept email earlier this week ("Summer hosting made simple") and it landed harder because the whole email pulled in one direction. This one is a clearance banner riding shotgun with a brand positioning campaign, and they don't love each other.
- I would not click. I would not forward this to anyone — I already sent the CB2 "Design Legends Sale" to my contractor last week because that one had specific pieces with specific discounts. This is too diffuse. I'll mark it as read and move on.

## What's working

- The leather armchair hero is actually a good creative choice for me — warm caramel tone, classic shape, reads masculine without being a stereotype. I noticed it.
- The "Summer Sale: Up to 70% off" callout lands above the fold on mobile and the CTA button is unambiguous — no digging required.
- The buffet/sideboard section speaks directly to second-home furnishing; I'm actively looking for something in that category right now.
- "Recommended Just for You" section at the bottom is a strong signal that someone crunched my browse history — I appreciate the attempt at personalization even if the execution is buried below a scroll wall.
- Clean render: no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds at mobile width.

## What's weak

- Two narratives in one email — Summer Sale clearance energy clashing with "American classic look, redefined" brand story — dilutes both. Pick one.
- The dining table section with the big floral arrangement reads like it was ported in from a different send; doesn't connect to the leather-and-classic-Americana theme at all.
- Seventeen emails in a week means I'm now pattern-matching "Pottery Barn" to "ignore" in my peripheral vision — the cadence problem is now actively working against the creative.
- "Up to 70% off" without a floor stated anywhere visible — classic Pottery Barn fine-print move where the 70% item is the wicker basket I'd never buy and the sofa I want is 15% off.
- The "Recommended Just for You" module is buried so deep in the scroll that it functions as dead content for anyone opening on mobile.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Send one email with one concept.** Either the Summer Sale or the American Classic look — not both. The sale has urgency; lead with that if inventory needs to clear.
- 2. **Lift the personalized recommendations above the fold** — make them the hero, not the afterthought. CB2's "Design Legends Sale" taught me that curated-feeling sends outperform scatter-shot ones every time.
- 3. **Show one hero product with a real price, not just a percentage.** Something like "The Bradford leather armchair — $899, was $1,299" closes harder than "up to 70% off."
- 4. **Cut the floral dining vignette** or save it for a separate send. It breaks the masculine-classic thread.
- 5. **Enforce a send cap** — five emails in seven days is the absolute ceiling for a brand at this price point. Past that you're training your best customers to tune you out.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `The Bradford chair you bookmarked is in the sale.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP code leaked into `<style>` block**
The following raw PHP appears as literal CSS text — it was never executed server-side:
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
This is invalid CSS. Strict parsers may abort style processing at this point, breaking all subsequent rules. Indicates a template include was mis-rendered or a PHP partial was inlined without execution.

**32 images missing `alt` text**
Every content image in the email — including the hero banner (`S26_TOP_SummerSale_NoUrgency.jpg`), all 18 American Classics trend slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), all 18 footer slices, and several product tiles — has no `alt` attribute or an empty one. Clients with images disabled (common in corporate/enterprise environments) will render a blank email.

**3 images served over HTTP (not HTTPS)**
Mixed-content; modern clients will block these:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Additionally the open-tracking pixel is HTTP:
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=...`

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
No impact on rendering, but shows in tab/preview surfaces and some accessibility tooling.

**External Google Fonts call**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
Blocked by Outlook (all versions) and privacy-hardened clients. No fallback cascade specified that approximates Montserrat — fallback chain drops straight to `verdana, arial`.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
Not captured by the AgentMail relay. If missing at send time, Gmail and Apple Mail will not surface the native unsubscribe UI — increasing spam complaints. CAN-SPAM requires a functional unsubscribe mechanism; header absence alone isn't a violation if a link exists in body, but omission of `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) means one-click unsubscribe via Gmail's UI is not supported.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header absent**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown. Sending domain is `e.potterybarn.com`; if DMARC enforcement is active and alignment fails, deliverability is at risk. Cannot confirm without raw headers from a delivered copy.

**Plain-text part is 71.7% URLs**
10,757 of 14,994 characters in the plain-text alternative are URL strings. This ratio is above spam-filter thresholds (typically flagged above 60–70%). The plain-text part appears to be auto-generated from the HTML without stripping tracking URLs down to human-readable form.
