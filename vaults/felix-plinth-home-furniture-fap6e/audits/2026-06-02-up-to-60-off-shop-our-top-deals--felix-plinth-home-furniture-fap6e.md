# UP TO 60% OFF! Shop our top deals →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-six. I'm counting again because that number keeps climbing, and this is the email that made me actually stop and log it. "UP TO 60% OFF!" in the subject with an exclamation mark is the email equivalent of someone shouting at you in a store parking lot. I almost didn't open it — not because I don't care about clearance pricing, but because after the coastal rugs send, the leather sofa editorial, the washable rugs guide, and the two-kids-one-room beds email, all in the last 48 hours, my C&B reflex is starting to feel Pavlovian in the worst way.
- Inside, the email is actually better than the subject promised. The hero is clean — a warm wooden coastal texture behind "up to 60% off clearance" with a clear Shop Now. The category navigation bar is smart. The "we love these for you" product block is where my eye actually went: a couple of furniture pieces that look credibly in my wheelhouse. And the Design Desk callout at the bottom is genuinely useful — Pottery Barn's free design services email from this week scored a 9 from me, and C&B is quietly running the same card, just buried four screens down.
- I'd scroll it, not really click it. I wouldn't forward it. The discount math on "up to 60% off" typically means the piece I want is 20% off; the 60% items are the things that were 80% off nobody's wishlist.

## What's working

- **Hero clarity is real.** The 60% off callout is front and center, the CTA is labeled, and the coastal wood texture aesthetic actually looks like a C&B shoot — not a stock-photo throwaway.
- **Category nav bar.** Furniture / Lighting / Dining & Barware / Bedding & Bath — giving me four shortcuts to skip the editorial and go straight to my category is exactly what a multi-renovation shopper needs.
- **"We love these for you" product block.** Product-level recommendations in a clearance email signals they have actual data on me; the furniture pieces I could see are aligned with the kind of mid-century/modern mix I'm shopping.
- **The Design Desk.** Free design consultation at the bottom is the buried treasure here — exactly the kind of offer I've been eyeing at Pottery Barn. It just shouldn't be buried.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no empty placeholders — especially notable given the email's density.

## What's weak

- **"UP TO 60% OFF!" is shouting at me.** All-caps plus exclamation mark in a subject line is a spam-trigger and a credibility dip. The coastal rugs email this week didn't need to shout; this one does it for no reason.
- **Preheader restates the subject almost verbatim.** "up to 60% off the summer clearance event" when the subject already says "UP TO 60% OFF!" is zero-value preview real estate. Pottery Barn used that preheader space to add a secondary hook; C&B just doubled down on the same line.
- **The bedding guide breaks the renovation flow.** "Find your dream sheets" is fine in isolation, but I came in here thinking furniture clearance, and a plush-pillow editorial sends me sideways. It reads like a conflated send — two separate campaigns stitched together.
- **The Design Desk is screaming to be higher.** Right now it competes with an app download CTA for screen real estate well below the fold. The Pottery Barn design services email this week led with that offer at hero level. C&B is running the same card and losing the comparison.
- **No deadline anywhere.** "Up to 60% off" without a "ends Sunday" or even "while supplies last" is a floaty discount I can ignore until next week — when another C&B email will remind me again.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move The Design Desk above the bedding guide.** A furniture-heavy clearance sale should funnel renovators toward a design consult, not sheets. Swap the module order: hero offer → personalized furniture picks → Design Desk CTA → bedding as supporting editorial.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject — lose the caps, add a deadline.** "Up to 60% off" is fine; the shout is the problem. Something with a time gate converts a passive scroll into a reason to click today.
- 3. **Fix the preheader.** It should add something the subject doesn't say — a category hint, a product type, a secondary hook.
- 4. **Add a hard end date to the clearance offer.** Even "through Sunday" in the body hero would sharpen the CTA from "maybe later" to "let me check the sofa I've been watching."
- 5. **Decouple the bedding guide into its own send.** C&B's own brand history shows editorial-style single-topic sends (leather, coastal rugs) perform better than multi-topic grab-bags. Split this.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Clearance ends soon: up to 60% off furniture →`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer clearance picks — up to 60% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sofas, rugs, lighting — the good stuff is going fast`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus: free design help at your nearest store`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta (confirmed)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks pinch-to-zoom on iOS/Android. WCAG 1.4.4 failure for users who require zoom. Should be removed or changed to `maximum-scale=5`.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags (confirmed)**
The tag appears four times — once as a combined value, then three times individually:
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
...
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
Redundant; harmless but indicates a template assembly error.

**Duplicate `charset` declaration (confirmed)**
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta charset="utf-8">
```
Both are present. Only one is needed; keep the `<meta charset>` form and drop the `http-equiv` version.

**Conflicting desktop image `max-width` media queries (confirmed)**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
At viewports ≥ 768px, both rules match. The 640px rule is declared last in source order and wins in some clients, capping images at 640px on desktop instead of the intended 600px. Either collapse into one breakpoint or explicitly set 600px in both.

**Overly broad `*` wildcard reset (confirmed)**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; max-height:1000000px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly; line-height: 100%}
```
`line-height: 100%` applied globally can collapse multi-line text in certain Outlook versions. `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` on `*` overrides any component-level size adjustments. This is a known Gmail workaround (`max-height`) but the scope is too broad; apply only to `body` or target tables.

**Dark mode not supported (confirmed)**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Explicitly opts out of dark mode. Apple Mail and Outlook will not apply system dark mode. No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block present. This is a deliberate choice but should be documented as intentional.

**Global `<p>` centering (confirmed)**
```css
p{margin:0 auto; text-align:center;}
```
Forces center alignment on all paragraph elements. Any `<p>` used for left-aligned body copy will be incorrectly centered unless overridden inline. Risk of unintended layout in footer/legal text.
