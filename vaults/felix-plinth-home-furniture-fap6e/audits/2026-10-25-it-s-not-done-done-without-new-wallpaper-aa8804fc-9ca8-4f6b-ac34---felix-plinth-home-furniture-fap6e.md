# It’s not done-done without NEW wallpaper!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-25**
## Executive summary

- This is the first Crate & Barrel send in days that matches my project. The first-ever wallcovering collection is a real new-product beat, and the hero is a soft striped nursery wall with a wreath mirror. I'm renovating two houses, so wallpaper is on my list. The catch is that this came from **Crate & Barrel Kids**, and nearly all of it is nursery and kids' bedroom rooms. I'm a 36-year-old guy doing a primary and a second home. I'd save the pretty rooms to my Pinterest folder, but I wouldn't buy for a nursery.
- The "up to 10 free wallcovering samples" block is the best thing in the email. It's the swatch-and-sample program I look for before any big decorative commitment. The rest of the email loses me. After the wallpaper story it turns into a holiday banner, an "up to 60% off clearance" block, a toy shipping offer, a product grid, a credit card promo, an app download and a design appointment. That's six or seven emails in one long scroll.
- Would I click? Maybe once, on the sample link, and only if I were already planning a kids' room. I wouldn't forward it. This is the same Crate family I've been snoozing all week.

## What's working

- The "first-ever wallcovering collection" headline is a real news hook, not another sale strip.
- The room-led blocks (a kid's bedroom, a bedroom with personality, a nursery) show wallpaper in context. That's how I judge a pattern.
- The free-samples block ("see them in your home before you decide") answers my main worry about wallpaper, which is color accuracy.
- The "choose from curated styles" tiles give me a way into the collection, and each room block has its own Shop Now button.
- The Design Desk "Book a Free Appointment" block at the bottom is useful for a two-house renovation.

## What's weak

- The kids framing is too narrow. Nothing in the first scroll says these papers work in a dining room or a primary bedroom.
- The email is far too long. The wallpaper story ends about halfway down, and the holiday decor, clearance, toys, card and app blocks pile on after it.
- The holiday banner text looks muddy over the busy photo and is hard to read at phone size. I'd miss that "Shop 40–50%" offer.
- There's no price, no per-roll cost and no coverage guidance for wallpaper. I can't tell what a wall costs, whether it's peel-and-stick, or how shipping works for rolls.
- It's the same-week pileup again. The 10/25 dining-table send ("Final weekend! Up to 30% off dining tables, chairs & more →") and the Thanksgiving dining-room strip are still fresh, and now there's a third angle from the same company.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to the wallpaper story plus the samples offer. Move clearance, toys, the card and the app to their own sends.
- 2. Put the sample offer in the hero. "Free samples" is the conversion mechanism, so lead with it.
- 3. Show at least one non-nursery room, such as a dining room, powder room or primary bedroom, so adults renovating whole homes see themselves in it.
- 4. Add the basics: price per roll, peel-and-stick vs. traditional, and how many rolls a standard wall takes.
- 5. Fix the contrast on the holiday and clearance banners, or drop them.
- Compared with other brands I read this week:
- CB2's "THIS JUST IN" stayed on one new-arrivals idea and felt like a catalog. This email tries to be a catalog and a clearance blast at once.
- Pottery Barn's maroon "Extra 20% off" strip has been on every send since 10/22. The Crate family is heading the same way by stacking offers.
- Article's "Give guests a cozy stay" was a single room with no offer. It was a cleaner single idea, although it had no hook like free samples.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML is a conventional table-based layout with MSO/VML fallbacks. The confirmed defects are widespread missing `alt` attributes (33 images) and a few markup hygiene problems. The compliance and authentication checks couldn't be verified, because the headers weren't captured by the relay. The source was truncated and 74 tracking links were not probed, so link, footer and UTM checks are incomplete.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified:** 74 links were skipped as tracking/click-redirect domains, so none were checked for resolution, redirect chain length, or final destination.
- **Third-party pixels:** five LiveRamp sync pixels, `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=…&n=1` through `n=5`.
  - They are hosted off the sender's domain.
  - They can be blocked by privacy-focused clients.
  - They add external requests at open time.
- **Repeated beacon:** `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png?mi_u=…` appears 6 times with the same URL. This looks like a repeated per-module tracking image, which is redundant.
- **Other first-party pixels:** `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif` and `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/d7e77b55-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…` are open-tracking pixels.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text, content images (about 20):**
  - 17 on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/…` (e.g. `bbf6f9d4-….png`, `34a386c7-….jpg`, `1b3e75ad-….gif`).
  - 3 on `s7d5.scene7.com`: `2026%5F1002%5FCBK%5FGiftGuide%5FSecondaryStory.gif`, `2026%5F0926%5FCBK%5FClearance%5FSecondaryStory.gif`, and the `…Rule%5FSpacer%5F40px` spacer.
  - With images blocked, or for screen readers, these will render blank or read out filenames or URLs. Alt text is also missing on GIFs, which may carry content.
- **Missing alt text, tracking pixels (13):** the `mi.`, `rlcdn.` and `dv.` images listed above. This is lower impact, but pixels should carry `alt=""`. Screen readers may otherwise announce the filename.
- **Viewport:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">`. `maximum-scale=1` restricts pinch-zoom, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Global CSS:** `p{margin:0 auto; text-align:center}` and `*{line-height:100%; max-height:1000000px}` apply to everything. They can override inherited alignment and line-height unexpectedly.
- **Markup hygiene:**
  - `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` has an unescaped `&`.
  - `<meta name="format-detection">` is declared three separate times, with conflicting values on the first.
  - `.ReadMsgBody` is defined twice.
  - The placeholder comment `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` shipped in production.
  - `<meta charset>` and `http-equiv` Content-Type are both declared. This is redundant but harmless.
- **Strengths:** `lang="en"`, a color-scheme meta tag, and a mobile breakpoint at 460px.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved tokens (`{{…}}`, `%%…%%`, `[FIRST_NAME]`) appear in the visible source. Only the truncated source was checked.

  - `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) is not found.

1. Add descriptive `alt` text to the ~20 content images, especially the GIFs. Use `alt=""` on spacers and tracking pixels.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Escape the title as `Crate &amp; Barrel` and dedupe the `format-detection` metas and the duplicate `.ReadMsgBody` rule.
4. Strip the leftover template comment from the production build.
