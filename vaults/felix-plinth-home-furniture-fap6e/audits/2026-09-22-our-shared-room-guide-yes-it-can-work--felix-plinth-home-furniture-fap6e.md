# Our shared room guide (yes, it can work!) →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-22**
## Executive summary

- Fourth Crate & Kids email this week, and this one's actually got a wedge into my life for once — bunk beds, shared kids' rooms, "two kids, one room, no fighting over drawers" is a real thing people with multiple kids in a house deal with, and I could see forwarding this if I had two kids sharing a room. Except I don't. I've got a second home renovation going on with actual budget, and this is nursery/kids-bedroom furniture top to bottom — bunk beds, cribs, changing tables at the bottom. So structurally this is well done: clear hero, "Book a Free Appointment" CTA repeated multiple times, a tidy "balance it out" styling module, color-cohesion tips — genuinely more useful copy than the ESG deck from two days ago. But it's still Crate & Kids talking to me like I have kids in bunk beds, which I don't.
- Compare this to Pottery Barn's "Free Design Services" email I looked at — that one used a credenza vignette that could double as my actual second-house mood board, brass lamp and all. This is nursery styling. Article's storage-solutions email at least hit a real pain point I'm chewing on (office nook storage). This is closer to a solid, well-organized miss than a swing-and-a-miss like the ESG email.
- Not clicking, not forwarding. Marking as read and moving on — my inbox has become a Crate & Kids/Crate & Barrel echo chamber this week and I'm tuning out the whole family of brands a little.

## What's working

- "our shared room guide" hero copy is concrete and solves an actual parenting logistics problem, unlike the vague ESG send from two days ago.
- Repeated "Book a Free Appointment" CTA button is unambiguous and consistent — I know exactly what happens if I tap it.
- GREENGUARD Gold certification badges next to specific products (dresser, changing table) are a real trust cue, not just brand fluff.
- The "keep color cohesive" module with actual paint/accent swatches is a legit styling tool, more useful than a straight product wall.
- Clean, uncluttered layout — no overlapping text, no broken images, everything renders crisp on mobile.

## What's weak

- I don't have kids sharing a room, so the entire premise — bunk beds, twin-over-twin, crib-to-bed transitions — is a total swing-and-a-miss for my actual project (second home + primary renovation, adult spaces).
- This is my fourth Crate & Kids/Crate & Barrel family email in a week (cozy fall twice, ESG, now this) — the cadence is genuinely wearing me down and I'm starting to auto-skim the sender name.
- No pricing visible until you scroll deep into the "we love these for you" carousel — the hero section has zero dollar signs, so I can't gut-check "is this in my budget" up top.
- The "new! the card you just got richer" module at the bottom feels bolted on and generic — no specific rewards math, just "up to $500" vaguely floating with no context on how I'd hit it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this — if Crate & Barrel/Crate & Kids knows I've never bought nursery or kids' furniture, stop sending me kids'-room content and redirect that inbox real estate to Crate & Barrel adult-room sends. This is the single biggest lift available.
- 2. Cut the cadence — four emails in a week from one brand family is why I'm zoning out; consolidate cozy-fall, ESG, and this into one weekly digest.
- 3. Put a price anchor in the hero, not buried in the product carousel — "bunk bed sets from $899" up top would let me self-select in or out immediately.
- 4. Make the loyalty card module concrete — show me the actual rewards math ("earn $50 back on this $500 purchase") instead of a vague "up to $500."
