# New high chair that will look beautiful in your dining room
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-14**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is the second Stokke Tripp Trapp olive green email from Crate & Kids in one day — I literally just read this exact pitch a few hours ago ("The high chair they'll use until middle school, really, in our exclusive color"), and now here's the same product again with a slightly different subject. I don't have kids yet, so this whole sub-brand shouldn't even be firing at me, but if I did, back-to-back sends for the same chair in the same colorway would read as either a glitch or desperation, not a launch.
- The email itself, on its own merits, is actually well put together — moody green hero shot, real 100+ year Stokke heritage copy, adjustable-chair explainer, cross-sell into bouncers and sleeping bags, then it sprawls into "500+ new fall arrivals," a $5,000 sweepstakes, a new store credit card, and a Design Desk pitch. That's five different offers stapled onto one high chair email. Article's Ashford dining collection email stayed disciplined — one collection, one story, done. This one can't resist throwing in everything Crate & Barrel corporate wants pushed this week.
- Would I click? No — wrong category for me entirely, and even if I were shopping nursery furniture, the duplicate send same-day would make me suspicious rather than curious. Wouldn't forward it either. Straight to read-and-archive.

## What's working

- The olive green Tripp Trapp hero shot is genuinely striking — moody styling, looks like it belongs in an actual dining room, not a nursery catalog.
- "For over 80 years, Stokke has challenged the ordinary" gives real brand pedigree instead of generic marketing filler.
- The adjustable-chair explainer photo (baby seat insert vs. bare wood chair) answers the actual practical question — does this work as my kid grows — before I'd have to ask it.
- Cross-sell into "even more to love from Stokke" (bouncer, changing table, sleeping bag) is at least on-theme, unlike some of what comes after it.

## What's weak

- I got this exact chair, exact colorway, same day as "The high chair they'll use until middle school" — that's not cadence, that's a duplicate send, and it's the kind of thing that makes me trust a brand less, not more.
- The email sprawls way past the high chair into fall arrivals, a $5,000 sweepstakes, a new co-brand credit card, and a free Design Desk consult — five asks competing for one click.
- Nothing here is remotely dining-room-renovation relevant for me — no lamps, no seating, no rugs, no lighting — this is 100% nursery/baby, so as a Crate & Barrel dining-room shopper I'm dead weight on this list.
- No price shown anywhere on the Tripp Trapp itself — for a chair I know runs well over $200, burying that is the kind of soft-pedaling Pottery Barn's Kravet launch also did, and it reads as avoidance, not confidence.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the duplicate send — pick one Tripp Trapp olive angle per day, not two. Two near-identical emails same-day is the single biggest credibility hit here.
- 2. Cut the email down to the chair plus one adjacent cross-sell. Drop the sweepstakes, the credit card pitch, and the Design Desk ad into separate sends — CB2's disciplined single-offer emails outperform this kind of everything-bagel approach.
- 3. Show the price on the hero chair. If it's premium, own it — "Investment-grade seating that adjusts as they grow" — rather than making me hunt for the number.
- 4. Segment nursery/baby sends away from dining-room/renovation shoppers like me — five Crate & Barrel-family emails already hit my inbox this week and none have been for my actual project.
