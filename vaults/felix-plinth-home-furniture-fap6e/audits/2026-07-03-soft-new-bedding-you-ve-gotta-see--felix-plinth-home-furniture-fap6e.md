# Soft new bedding you’ve gotta see
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- Three emails from Crate & Kids today. *Three.* Two of them were the 🎆 warehouse sale — I scored those earlier, had opinions, moved on. Now this one drops: a full editorial, zero-sale, "here's some gorgeous new bedding" send on the afternoon of July 3rd. The tonal whiplash is jarring. One minute they're screaming FINAL WEEKEND at me, the next they're doing quiet editorial.
- And here's the thing — the photography is actually good. That hero bedroom is the exact vibe I'm chasing for the guest room in the second house: soft neutrals, clean lines, nothing fussy. I scrolled further than I expected. The multi-section grid is well-paced and nothing breaks. If this had landed on a Tuesday in March with even a modest free-shipping hook, I'd be in my cart right now.
- But it's July 3rd. Pottery Barn is running extra 20% off. CB2 has its long-weekend sale. Article just dropped a Monday deadline. The entire home goods internet is in discount mode — and Crate & Kids is showing me new arrivals at full price, on the same day their own warehouse sale email is sitting two spots above it in my inbox. I'll scroll through to pin some inspiration. I am not clicking to buy. Mark as read.

## What's working

- **Hero photography earns its placement.** Adult-styled bedroom, muted palette, soft textiles — this looks like a Crate & Barrel proper email, not a kids catalog. The styling speaks to me.
- **Quiet tonal contrast actually lands.** After two "FINAL WEEKEND / 50% OFF / WAREHOUSE SALE" screams from this same sender, the editorial softness is a genuine palate cleanser. Article pulled this move well on June 26 and it worked — opener, not closer.
- **Subject line is compact and clean.** 33 characters, no emoji, no ALL-CAPS, casual voice. "Soft new bedding you've gotta see" reads fast on mobile and doesn't feel like it's trying to manipulate me.
- **Grid layout scrolls without fatigue.** Each product block has breathing room. Fabric/texture close-ups are visible and useful for an actual bedding decision — I want to know what the material *feels like*.
- **No render breaks.** Floor-level criterion, but on a day I've seen worse, it counts.

## What's weak

- **No offer on the worst possible day for no offers.** July 3rd is a sale blackout zone — Pottery Barn, CB2, Article, everyone's running something. A full-price editorial is asking me to pay a premium while my inbox tab is lit up with discounts from the same category.
- **Three emails, three different messages, one sender domain.** Two warehouse-sale emails in the morning, one editorial in the afternoon — Crate & Kids has lost coherent identity in my inbox today. CB2's "Now & Future Classics" send at least kept a consistent sale + editorial blend across sends this week.
- **No urgency anchor of any kind.** Article's July 3rd email told me the sale closed Monday. I knew exactly when to act. This one gives me nothing — not even a vague "just landed" timestamp or "limited colorways" signal.
- **Segmentation feels sloppy.** I'm on the warehouse-sale list *and* the new-arrivals editorial list, and apparently both lists get hit on the same day. These should be suppressed against each other.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Attach a soft offer.** Free shipping on the new arrivals collection is enough. You don't have to torch the editorial tone — just give me one financial reason to act today instead of waiting for the next sale.
- 2. **Add a seasonal hook to the copy.** "Summer sleep" or "refresh the guest room before the long weekend" takes 30 seconds to write and ties this to July 3rd specifically instead of Generic New Arrivals Email Q3.
- 3. **Suppress this from the warehouse-sale list.** If I received the FINAL WEEKEND email today, hold this send for next week. I can't simultaneously be in "clearance urgency" mode and "explore new full-price arrivals" mode.
- 4. **Make CTA button copy actually readable at scroll speed.** Whatever the buttons say, it needs to survive a glance. "Shop Soft Bedding" beats "Shop Now" by a mile.
- **Subject Alt A:** `5 new bedding arrivals we'd actually put on our beds`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your next duvet just landed. (New arrivals.)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Guest room refresh incoming — just dropped, free ship on $75+`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Buttery-soft. Just arrived. Shop before sizes go.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` brand mismatch (confirmed)**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
Email sender is "Crate & Kids" but the title element says "Crate & Barrel." Screen readers and some webmail clients (Gmail web) surface this title. Should be "Crate & Kids."

**Inverted desktop image-sizing media queries (confirmed)**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
Because the 768px rule appears after the 640px rule in source order, on screens ≥768px the cascade resolves to `max-width: 600px` — a smaller cap than the 640px rule below it. Wider viewports get a tighter constraint than narrower ones. The 768px block likely intends 640px, or the rules are in the wrong order.

**`maximum-scale=1` disables user zoom (confirmed)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users from zooming on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Universal `text-size-adjust: none` (confirmed)**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ... }
```
Applying to the `*` selector rather than `body` suppresses OS-level text scaling for all elements, including any dynamic or injected content. Standard practice is to scope to `body` only.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags (confirmed)**
The combined tag and three individual tags produce four `format-detection` declarations — `telephone`, `address`, and `date` each appear twice. Redundant but not broken; remove the individual duplicate tags for cleanliness.
