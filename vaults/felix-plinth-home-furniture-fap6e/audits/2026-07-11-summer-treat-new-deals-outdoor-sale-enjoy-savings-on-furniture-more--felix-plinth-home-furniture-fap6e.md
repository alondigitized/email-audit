# 🍦 Summer treat: NEW DEALS + Outdoor sale! Enjoy savings on furniture & more
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- Seventeen. That's where we are. I went back and counted after the last one — "New to sale & UP TO 70% OFF + A new sofa made for lived-in lounging" landed yesterday — and now this comes in with a 🍦 emoji in the subject line like we're friends who share a punchline. We are not. We are a brand and a subscriber who has received seventeen emails in eleven days.
- That said, I'm not going to pretend this email is bad. Outdoor furniture on sale in mid-July is exactly what I'm shopping for right now — I'm trying to sort out the back patio at the second home before August. The hero image delivers: white upholstered outdoor lounge chairs against a sun-washed deck. That's the dream. "Up to 70% off" is plastered early enough that I don't have to hunt for the number. And the "Shop Outdoor Furniture on Sale" CTA is not buried. This is a competent send.
- But "competent" is doing a lot of work here. The "Recommended Just for You" module below the fold is serving me curtains. Curtains. I'm mid-patio. The ice cream emoji in the subject reads like someone in content strategy said "it's summer!" and no one pushed back. And it's the seventeenth email. I'd mark this as read without clicking unless I'm actively in browse mode on a Friday night. I would not forward this to anyone.

## What's working

- **Outdoor furniture is exactly the right product right now** — the hero is sharp, the furniture reads premium, and both homes need patio work before fall.
- **"Up to 70% off" is visible near the top** — I don't have to scroll to find the discount anchor; that's table stakes Pottery Barn gets right when they bother.
- **"Shop Outdoor Furniture on Sale" CTA is specific** — verb plus noun, not "Discover" or "Explore." Article did something similar in their July 10th send and it's the right call.
- **The hero image is genuinely good** — those white upholstered outdoor chairs look like something I'd actually pin; the product photography earns its real estate.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, images loaded, nothing broken at the fold.

## What's weak

- **The "Recommended Just for You" section is serving curtains** — I'm in outdoor furniture mode, not window treatment mode. This module undercuts the focused outdoor narrative immediately below the fold.
- **🍦 in the subject line is embarrassing** — ice cream cone emoji plus "Summer treat" for an outdoor furniture sale is the copy equivalent of a Slack reaction when a full sentence was warranted. CB2 ran "When your bedroom is just for you" this week with zero emoji and it felt more premium.
- **Seventeen emails in eleven days** — no single-send fix changes the fact that this one arrives carrying that weight. Crate & Barrel pulled the same move and I nearly filtered them.
- **"Up to 70% off" covers the headline discount, but no floor is set** — when I'm buying an outdoor sofa, "up to 70%" without a starting price or a floor number reads as bait. The July 10th free design services send felt more honest precisely because it wasn't hiding behind a sliding scale.
- **No urgency anchor** — no end date, no "sale ends Sunday," nothing. Outdoor season is literally the urgency; just say it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop the email to one per two days maximum** — nothing in this send repairs the seventeen-in-eleven-days damage. Cadence is the real problem. Pottery Barn's free design services send on July 10th worked specifically because it broke the sale drum pattern; this one falls right back into it.
- 2. **Kill the ice cream emoji and rewrite the subject around a specific outdoor SKU or a concrete deadline** — "Patio chairs: up to 60% off this weekend" beats a food emoji every day of the week.
- 3. **Replace the curtains "Recommended Just for You" module with outdoor accessories that cross-sell within the same purchase decision** — rugs, lanterns, outdoor cushions; keep me in one mental shopping basket.
- 4. **Add a sale-end date in the hero or CTA** — "Sale ends July 13" would double my urgency sense without changing a single creative asset.
- 5. **Anchor the discount range** — "Up to 70% off, starting at $X" is honest and Article has been doing this consistently; it builds trust faster than a sliding ceiling.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Lounge chairs, dining sets, rugs — sale pricing through the weekend.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed broken links (403) block live CTAs, and PHP server-response code was accidentally pasted into a `<style>` block — invalid CSS that signals a copy-paste error during template assembly. Accessibility coverage is critically low with 48 images missing `alt` text and four assets served over plain HTTP.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**BROKEN — Credit card apply/buy CTA (403)**
```
https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682
  ?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY
  &external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember
```
This is a primary header CTA (non-member credit card banner). The `clickcp.potterybarn.com` endpoint returned 403, meaning every recipient who clicks this link hits an error page.

**BROKEN — Cross-brand spacer asset (403)**
```
http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif
```
A West Elm CDN asset is referenced in a Pottery Barn email. The domain returns 403 and the asset is also HTTP-only (see §3). This is a cross-brand asset leak in the template.

**23 tracking links skipped** — all routed through `click.e.potterybarn.com`. HTTP probing was skipped for redirect chains; cannot confirm destination health from QA data alone.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical: PHP code embedded in `<style>` block**

Inside the second `<style>` tag, after valid CSS rules, the following appears:
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are PHP `header()` calls, not CSS. They were copy-pasted from a server-side image handler script into the HTML template. Email clients will silently discard them as invalid CSS, but this is a definite template assembly error that warrants investigation — there may be more template corruption not visible in the truncated source.

**48 images missing `alt` text** — includes all content-bearing images:
- Hero banner: `F26_TOP_SummerSalePartC_NoUrgency.jpg`
- All 16 outdoor sale slice images (`slices_01.jpg` – `slices_16.jpg`, path `0530_SAT_AM_OutdoorSale/`)
- All 18 email footer slice images
- Product images (curtain rod bracket, platform bed, towels, sheet set, curtains)
- Tracking pixels (`coreemail.html`, `turn.com/r/dd/...`, open-tracking pixel)

With images blocked (default in Outlook, Apple Mail), the email renders as a blank column for recipients relying on alt text fallback.

**4 images served over HTTP (not HTTPS)** — will be blocked by many clients with mixed-content protection:

**`<title>` tag is empty** — minor, but used by some accessibility tools and screen readers when navigating the email as a document.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — suppresses user pinch-zoom on mobile, which is an accessibility concern for low-vision users.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** — QA notes this may be a relay-capture gap, but if genuinely missing from the envelope, Google and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements (enforced since Feb 2024) are violated for high-volume senders. Requires header-level verification against the raw SMTP envelope, not just the HTML body.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent** — one-click unsubscribe not supported or not captured. Gmail renders a prominent "Unsubscribe" button in the UI only when this header is present; its absence degrades deliverability signal.

**`Authentication-Results` not captured** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown via the AgentMail relay. Cannot confirm authentication posture from this audit.

**`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />`** is set in `<head>`. This suppresses the HTTP `Referer` header on outbound clicks, which may interfere with server-side analytics that depend on referrer data (separate from UTM params).
