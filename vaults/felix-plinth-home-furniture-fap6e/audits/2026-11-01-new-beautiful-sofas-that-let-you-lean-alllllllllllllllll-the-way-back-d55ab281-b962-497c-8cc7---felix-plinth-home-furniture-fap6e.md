# New! Beautiful sofas that let you lean alllllllllllllllll the way back
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-01**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction was that this is the first Crate & Barrel email this week that's about something I'm actually shopping for. I'm furnishing two houses, and a sofa is the biggest line item on both. The "lean alllllll the way back" subject is a bit cute, but it tells me what's inside, and it beats the Kids sends I keep getting. It's still the sixth Crate & Barrel email since 10/31, and my brain has the family on snooze.
- The body is a long scroll, and I'll be upfront that at phone size I can read the headlines and buttons, not the fine print. The hero ("the look you want, the recline you love") is clear. The sections below it (Dritt, Lounge, "reclines anywhere") are product-led and make recliners look like furniture, not dad-cave gear. After that it drifts into the DesignDesk pitch, a sweepstakes, a Black Friday sign-up, a card promo, an app download and a store locator. I'd click "Shop Power Recliners" or the Dritt button. I wouldn't forward it. I'd save it to my Pinterest-adjacent folder next to the Article dining chair email.

## What's working

- The hero copy names the category in plain terms: "new! the look you want, the recline you love," with two clear black buttons, "Shop Power Recliners" and "Shop All Sofas & Sectionals."
- The Dritt block ("didn't know recliners could look like this") shows two fabric swatches, brown and tan. I care about palette and material, so that's a real point in its favor.
- Each section has its own specific button ("Shop Dritt," "Shop Lounge," "Shop Power Recliners"). The verb and the product are both named, which beats a generic "Discover."
- The lifestyle photography is warm, neutral and consistent. It matches the palette I'm already planning for both houses.
- The top strip says "3 days left! accent chairs, tables & rugs up to 20% off." That's a concrete, time-bound number.

## What's weak

- The only price signal is on accent chairs, tables and rugs, while the whole email is about sofas. Where's the sofa offer? Pottery Barn's maroon "Extra 20% off" strip at least applies to what it's selling.
- The email is very long, with about eight sections competing: DesignDesk twice, the $5000 sweepstakes, Black Friday, the card, the app, the store locator and "we love these for you." The dining chair grid and the Reward Dollars block have nothing to do with the recliner story.
- I can't tell what any of it costs or when it ships. For furniture I want lead time, delivery and a swatch or sample option, and I can't see any of that. Article at least shows product and price.
- The subject is about 70 characters, and "alllllllllllllllll" will truncate on mobile. The preview text isn't visible or provided to me, so the inbox line gets no help from it.
- On the cadence, I've had the 10/31 gift card sweepstakes, the "Today only: 20% off top play furniture" send and two "up to 60% off clearance" sends from Crate & Barrel and Kids. I'm fatigued, and I don't have a kid.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put one sofa-specific offer in the hero strip, such as a sofa discount or free delivery on in-stock sofas. Right now the discount doesn't match the product.
- 2. Cut the email to three modules: the hero, the Dritt and Lounge pair, and one collections grid. Move DesignDesk, the sweepstakes, the card and the app to a separate send.
- 3. Add fabric swatch and sample ordering, delivery lead times and a starting price to each product module. That's what actually moves a sofa buyer.
- 4. Segment by browse behavior. I've been clicking furniture, not Kids, so stop sending me the Kids streams.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New: sofas that recline and still look this good`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Power recliners, minus the dad-cave look`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dritt, Lounge and Tilary in performance fabrics, with swatches to order free.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus 3 days left on up to 20% off accent chairs, tables and rugs.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on 36 images.**
  - The 22 content-sized images are 21 on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/…` (for example `bbf6f9d4-….png`, `638b18e8-….jpg`) and the Scene7 asset `2026%5F0217%5FCB%5FPowerCollections%5FMain8%5FOpti`.
  - Another 7 are `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png`, which look like personalized product images.
  - The last 7 are the tracking pixels listed above.
  - Content images with no alt text give image-blocking and screen-reader users no content.
- **Viewport blocks zoom:** `<meta name="viewport" content="…maximum-scale=1">` disables pinch-zoom, which is an accessibility problem.
- **Text resizing is disabled:** `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` appears in `*{…}`, `html` and `body`, which stops user text scaling.
- **Invalid markup:**
  - `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` has an unescaped ampersand under an XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype.
  - `<meta name="format-detection">` is declared three times with conflicting values.
  - `<meta charset>` is declared twice.
- **Template residue:** `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` is a leftover placeholder comment, with a typo.
- **Conflicting image sizing:** `@media (min-width:768px){img{max-width:600px}}` is followed by `@media (min-width:640px){img{max-width:640px}}`. Both match at 768px and above, and the later rule wins, so the 600px cap is dead code.
- **Possible overflow on narrow phones:** inside `@media (max-width:460px)`, `.mobile{width:460px!important}` and `.showmobile{width:414px!important}` set fixed widths. Phones narrower than those widths (for example 375px) would overflow if these classes are applied. The HTML is truncated, so I can't confirm where they are used.
