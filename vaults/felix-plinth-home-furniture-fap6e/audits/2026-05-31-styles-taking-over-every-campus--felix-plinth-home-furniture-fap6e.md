# STYLES TAKING OVER EVERY CAMPUS >>>
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-31**
## Executive summary

- Thirty. That's the Wayfair count as of this Saturday, and the second email today — the outdoor rugs send landed before noon and was email twenty-nine. I want to lead with that number because what I'm reviewing here isn't a misstep in execution, it's a misstep in targeting. The hero says "design your dorm." Not "design your space," not even "back-to-school style for every room." *Dorm.* Wayfair has sent me enough emails about area rugs, lamps, and mattress foundations to know I'm neck-deep in two home renovations — and they followed that run with a send aimed at an 18-year-old moving into a cinder-block double.
- The frustrating part: the creative has real bones. "Modern prep," "country cottage," "Southern charm" are three of the exact aesthetic poles I'm triangulating between on property number two right now. Strip "dorm" from every touchpoint and reframe this as a home style guide, I'd have saved at least two of those sections to my folder. Instead I'm staring at "dopamine decor" and "party accents" and feeling like I accidentally got added to an RA's mailing list.
- Marking it read without clicking. Not forwarding to anyone. The one person I know who'd find this useful just finished junior year and shops at Target.

## What's working

- The five-style editorial grid is a genuinely useful organizing framework — "country cottage" and "Southern charm" specifically map to real design directions I'm actively pursuing on the second property
- Layout renders cleanly throughout: product thumbnails are legible, section headers are readable at a glance, no broken images anywhere
- Wayfair's template is reliably clean — consistent gutters, no display chaos, which is more than I can say for some of the Crate & Barrel sends I've gotten this month
- The furniture/bedding section buried at the bottom is the only block where I could imagine actually tapping something

## What's weak

- "Design your dorm" as the hero headline is the single worst segmentation miss in thirty Wayfair emails — I own two properties and have bought rugs from this brand; there is no data-driven path to this creative landing in my inbox
- "STYLES TAKING OVER EVERY CAMPUS >>>" reads like a TikTok caption — all caps plus three arrows is exactly the kind of subject line I use to train my spam reflexes
- Zero visible offer anywhere in the email — after yesterday's 60% off table lamp send (which was the most compelling thing Wayfair has done in weeks), coming back with no pricing hook at all is a hard step backward
- "More styles to scoop up" as CTA copy — "scoop up" is not how anyone who spends $400 on a rug describes a purchase decision
- Article ran a style editorial on May 23rd with zero campus framing and clean home-specific copy; the comparison is unkind to this send

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this off the homeowner list entirely.** Any customer who's purchased outdoor rugs or is in an active browse session on area rugs should not receive dorm content. First-order CRM fix, nothing creative required.
- 2. **Reframe the identical creative for home.** Same five aesthetics, same product grid — delete every instance of "dorm," lead with "design your room," and this email becomes genuinely useful.
- 3. **Add a pricing anchor to the hero.** Yesterday's lamp email showed exactly how to do this. Even "starting at $X" in the hero gives me a reason to scroll past the fold.
- 4. **Replace CTA copy.** "Shop the look" or "Find your aesthetic" works for any age. "Scoop up" does not.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `5 design styles — which one fits your space?`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Country cottage, modern prep, Southern charm: shop the look`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop rugs, lighting, and accent pieces curated by style — no dorm required.`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `From dopamine decor to modern prep — the looks taking over every room.`
