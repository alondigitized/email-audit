# Don't miss our SUMMER SALE
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- Thirty. That's where I am with Pottery Barn in July, and this one lands with the single laziest subject line in the entire streak. "Don't miss our SUMMER SALE" is what you write when you've run out of words but the send is already scheduled. I almost didn't open it — and the only reason I did is that I'm literally mid-renovation on two houses and I know their 70% off events are real.
- Inside, the actual email is doing more work than the subject gave me any reason to expect. The hero is clean, the category nav is exactly what I need when I'm shopping across furniture AND outdoor AND bedding at the same time, and the "Recommended Just For You" section has something that looks like personalized product cards, which I appreciate even if the products themselves are too small to evaluate at this size. The "Discover New Arrivals" room scene is the most interesting thing on the page — that warm neutral living room is exactly the direction I'm going in the second home — and the SHOP NOW button under it is the only CTA I'd actually click.
- But the Affirm section? Pointless for me. I'm routing every dollar through my highest-rewards card. And the app section below that is padding I've scrolled past in every one of the last twenty-nine emails. I'd click through to the new arrivals section, but I wouldn't forward this to anyone — there's nothing here that isn't also sitting in the inbox of every other Pottery Barn subscriber.

## What's working

- **70% off is real and the hero makes it legible immediately** — it lands above the fold, no hunting required.
- **The category nav is genuinely useful** — Furniture, Outdoor, Sofas & Chairs, Bedding, all in one row; I can route to whatever room I'm working on without digging through a landing page.
- **"Recommended Just For You" signals they know I'm a buyer** — product cards in that section suggest some segmentation is happening, even if I can't evaluate the items at this size.
- **"Discover New Arrivals" + room scene is the strongest creative in the email** — that neutral warm living room is aspirational without being staged, and it's a better reason to click than the generic sale banner.
- **"Earn 10% Back in Rewards" section** — I already know my rate, but it's the right trust signal for anyone who's on the fence about the price.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is the worst in the entire July streak** — "Don't miss our SUMMER SALE" doesn't even put the discount in the subject. Crate & Barrel's outdoor email ("Up to 60% off outdoor & sunny days ahead") is in my inbox the same week and it's doing exponentially more work with the same character budget.
- **"Discover New Arrivals" and "Summer Sale" are two separate messages crammed into one email** — the hero is about clearance and the mid-section is about new product. Pick one or explain why they coexist.
- **Affirm section is audience-blind** — I'm a rewards-card maximizer. Pottery Barn knows my purchase history. This section should be conditionally suppressed for people with a loyalty card on file.
- **The SHOP NOW button under "Discover New Arrivals" is doing no descriptive lifting** — CB2 had the same problem with "MORE ON SALE" but at least the product shots were large enough to sell themselves. Here I want to know: what is this room? What's the collection?

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the discount in the subject line** — "70% off" or "Up to 70% off" should be in the subject, full stop. Every CB2 and Crate & Barrel send I've opened this month that worked had the number in the subject.
- 2. **Split the "New Arrivals" story into its own send** — or if they must coexist, give the new arrivals section a named collection ("The August Edit") so it doesn't just read as filler padding below the sale.
- 3. **Make the "Recommended Just For You" cards bigger** — if you're going to personalize, commit. At this size they're a gesture, not a reason to click.
- 4. **Suppress the Affirm module for rewards members** — or at minimum swap it for a rewards math reminder: "70% off + your 10% back = effective 73% off" would actually be compelling.
- 5. **SHOP NOW needs a noun** — "Shop New Living Room" or "Explore The Collection" does more than "SHOP NOW" ever will.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 70% off — plus 100s of new styles added`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 10% rewards + 70% off Summer Sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Furniture, outdoor, bedding and more — see what landed in the Summer Sale`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New styles added daily. Shop with your rewards and get up to 73% off effective.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has two broken CTAs returning 403, a critical build-pipeline error (raw PHP code embedded in a CSS block), three HTTP image sources that will be blocked or mixed-content-flagged by modern clients, and pervasive missing alt text across 38 images. Authentication and one-click unsubscribe headers are unconfirmed at the relay layer.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Two links return 403:**

| URL (truncated) | Context |
|---|---|
| `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407…?cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember` | Credit card "Apply & Buy" banner CTA |
| `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` | Cross-brand West Elm spacer image (also HTTP) |

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CRITICAL — PHP code embedded in `<style>` block:**

The final `<style>` tag in `<head>` contains unexecuted server-side PHP directives:

```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```

These are not CSS. They are PHP `header()` calls that were supposed to run server-side (likely for a dynamic image endpoint) but leaked into the rendered HTML as literal text. Email clients will either display this as visible text or silently discard it — either way it is a build pipeline failure, not intentional CSS.

**Three HTTP image sources (will be blocked or flagged):**

- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` (also 403)
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&…` (open-tracking pixel)

Gmail, Apple Mail, and most modern clients proxy or block HTTP image loads. The open-tracking pixel on HTTP means open events will be missed or unreliable on those clients.

**38 images missing `alt` text** — affects screen readers and image-blocked rendering (Outlook default, Gmail images-off). Product images in the link-farm section (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_12.jpg`) and all footer slices have empty or absent `alt` attributes.

**Empty `<title>` tag** — minor; some clients surface this in tab/window labels.
