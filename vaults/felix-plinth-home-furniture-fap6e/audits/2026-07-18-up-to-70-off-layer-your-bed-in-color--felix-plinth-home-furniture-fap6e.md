# 💙 Up to 70% off + Layer your bed in color
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- Thirty. That's where I am after this one — thirty Pottery Barn emails in eighteen days, and this is the second one landing on July 18 alone. The first one this morning was the 70%-off catalog preview, and now here's the bedding color story. I checked my inbox. Two. In one day. There's a word for this cadence and it rhymes with Wayfair.
- That said: the email itself is actually well-executed. "Layers of Color" as a concept makes sense after yesterday's indigo send — they're running a thematic color arc, which is more editorial discipline than most brands show. The hero bedding photography is aspirational without being fussy. The color-segmented CTAs (SHOP BLUE / SHOP GREEN / SHOP TAUPE) are genuinely useful for someone like me who is actively buying bedding for two homes simultaneously and needs to make quick decisions by palette.
- I'd mark it as read but not delete it. Probably revisit it tonight when I'm comparing the blue linen options against what Crate & Barrel showed me in their forecast/outdoor email earlier this week. I wouldn't forward it — this is a solo decision for the second home bedroom, and my partner would just send me fifteen pins in response.

## What's working

- **"Layers of Color" hero concept is smart** — it carries the color arc forward from yesterday's indigo send without repeating the same assets. Shows actual creative sequencing.
- **Color-segmented CTAs are genuinely useful** — SHOP BLUE / SHOP GREEN / SHOP TAUPE solves the filtering problem I'd otherwise have to do manually on-site.
- **Bedding essentials module is well-structured** — DUVETS / COMFORTERS / PILLOWS / SHEETS as a quick-nav grid is the right call for someone working through a full-room setup.
- **"Recommended Just For You" section is present** — even if the personalization is probably algorithmic and shallow, the section label signals they're trying to treat me like a known customer.
- **Clean render, no layout issues** — stacked properly on mobile, images loaded, no obvious visual debt.

## What's weak

- **Two emails in one day is the line** — I'll tolerate a high-cadence sale period, but double-tapping the same date tests the unsubscribe muscle memory. Article sent me seven emails in three weeks and I forgave it because the editorial quality stayed high. Pottery Barn is testing that goodwill.
- **"Recommended Just For You" probably isn't** — this section exists in nearly every Pottery Barn email I've opened. If the recommendations actually shifted based on my recent views, I'd engage. If it's the same rug and the same sofa every time, it's furniture spam wearing a personalization costume.
- **No price anchor on the hero products** — SHOP BLUE is a color filter, not a product. I'd need to click, filter again, then find my size. Crate & Barrel's outdoor email at least surfaced a price point in the hero. That's one fewer click I had to make.
- **No deadline visible** — "Up to 70% off" is now the standing state of every email I'm getting from every brand. Without a hard end date, there's no urgency. The Wayfair WALL ART CLEARANCE email had a more honest urgency signal than this.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill one of the two July 18 sends** — this email and the morning catalog preview are competing against each other. The color story is the stronger creative; the catalog preview should've been the supporting send, or the two should have been combined.
- 2. **Anchor a price in the hero** — "From $49" or "Duvet covers from $129" under the hero image would give me something to evaluate without clicking. Article does this in their editorial sends and it converts better.
- 3. **Make "Recommended" earn its section** — if the product grid shifts based on my actual browsing (I've been looking at duvet inserts, specifically king, white), I'd stop scrolling and engage. If it's generic, cut the section and add a second color story.
- 4. **Add a deadline** — even "Sale ends Sunday" changes the calculation. Without it, I'm treating this like a permanent Pottery Barn reality, not an event.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Restock your bedroom: 70% off ends soon`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Duvets, sheets, and pillows in blue, green, and taupe — full sets starting at $XX.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Coordinate your whole bed for less. Shop the summer color edit.`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Up to 70% off / Layer your bed in color"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard-broken links block live CTAs, a cross-brand West Elm asset has leaked into production, and PHP `header()` directives were accidentally injected into a `<style>` block — indicating a template composition failure. Accessibility posture is poor across the entire content section due to universal missing alt text.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Credit card member-banner CTA returns 403**
- URL: `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember&...`
- This is the primary above-fold acquisition link (Apply & Buy / Key Rewards banner). The destination is unreachable; all clicks on this module produce an error page.

**[FAIL] Cross-brand West Elm spacer GIF returns 403**
- URL: `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- A layout spacer from a sibling Williams-Sonoma brand is referenced directly. It is HTTP-only, returns 403, and exposes internal template reuse practices. Most clients will silently suppress it, but the broken asset can affect layout integrity in strict rendering environments.

**22 tracking links not validated** — `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirect domain was skipped by the HTTP probe. These should be spot-checked manually in a pre-send QA pass.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CRITICAL] PHP `header()` directives embedded in CSS block**

Inside the final `<style>` tag in `<head>`:
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are PHP cache-control directives that were inadvertently pasted into a CSS block, likely during template assembly from a server-side image-serving script fragment. Email clients will ignore them as invalid CSS, but this confirms a template composition bug in the build pipeline.

**[HIGH] 50+ images missing alt text**
Every content image — hero banners, all 19 bedding story slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_19.jpg`), 6 product images, footer slices, and promotional banners — has no `alt` attribute. Recipients with images disabled (Apple Mail default, corporate Outlook) see a fully blank email.

**[HIGH] 4 images served over HTTP**
Clients with HTTPS-only image loading (Gmail, iOS Mail, many corporate proxies) will block these:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=...` ← open-tracking pixel

The open pixel being HTTP means open tracking is unreliable — any client enforcing HTTPS will drop it silently.

**[LOW] Empty `<title>` tag** — Some clients render this in the tab/window bar. A descriptive title aids accessibility tooling.

**[LOW] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — Prevents user zoom on mobile, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post headers not confirmed**
- Neither `List-Unsubscribe` nor `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) was captured by the AgentMail relay.
- One-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058 `POST` method) has been required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders since February 2024. Absence increases spam-folder placement risk.
- Note: headers may be present on the actual SMTP envelope but stripped by the relay — this should be confirmed against raw SMTP headers.

**[WARN] Authentication-Results not captured**
- SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from the relay. Sending domain is `e.potterybarn.com`; authentication posture for this subdomain should be verified directly against DNS records and SMTP logs.

**CAN-SPAM structural requirements** — physical address and unsubscribe mechanism are standard components of PB footer templates and expected to be present in the truncated portion of the HTML. Cannot confirm from the provided source slice.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All destination URLs are wrapped in the `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirect layer, so UTM parameters cannot be directly inspected from the HTML source. The one resolvable destination that was not redirect-wrapped (the broken 403 credit card link at `clickcp.potterybarn.com`) contains `external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember` — these appear to be internal Salesforce Marketing Cloud attribution parameters, not standard UTM params. Whether standard `utm_source/utm_medium/utm_campaign` are present downstream of the redirect layer is unverified.
