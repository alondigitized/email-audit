# The marble that elevates everything
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-05**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "The marble that elevates everything" — and my first thought is CB2 has fully committed to a bit here, because there's a top banner for a Bedroom + Bath Event, then immediately below it a whole separate marble-materials story with its own hero, its own "Shop Now," its own scroll-length product parade. Two campaigns duct-taped into one send. I scrolled through Saga Drink Table, Enye Coffee Table, Block Lamp, Vendome Dining Table, Babylon Bistro Table — five marble products in a row before I even hit the design services block — and by product three I'd stopped reading captions and just started scrolling.
- This is the seventh CB2/Crate & Barrel-family email I've chewed through this week (after "Two sales end tonight," the bed & bath urgency send, the Goop color studies drop, and the Cubs sweepstakes), and the material-story format itself is actually decent — it's closer to what Article does with its "Home Tour" sends than CB2's usual discount-block spam. But there's no price anywhere in this entire email until the "MORE FOR YOU" grid at the bottom. No percentage off, no "starting at," nothing. For a guy renovating two houses on a rewards card, that's a hard pass on urgency.
- Would I click? Maybe on the Vendome Dining Table shot — that's the one image that actually looks like a room I'd want, dark seating in the background sold me more than the marble did. Wouldn't forward it. Marking as read and moving on.

## What's working

- The Vendome Dining Table shot with the styled dark dining room behind it is the one image in this email that reads like actual room inspiration, not just a product photo — that's the Article/Wayfair lane and CB2 rarely plays there.
- Consistent marble/stone visual thread across five products ties the "elevates everything" concept together better than CB2's usual grab-bag promo blocks.
- The Interior Design Services block (in-store + virtual consult, book a free appointment) is a real value-add I hadn't seen CB2 push before — that's the kind of showroom-integration content this persona actually wants mid-reno.
- "Earn 10% Back" rewards callout at the very bottom is relevant to me specifically, since I buy on the highest-rewards card — just buried way too low.

## What's weak

- Zero pricing anywhere until the tiny "MORE FOR YOU" grid at the very bottom — five marble hero products, no dollar figure attached to a single one. Crate & Barrel's sofa-raves email at least anchored on social proof; this one gives me nothing to anchor a buying decision on.
- Two competing campaigns jammed into one email (Bedroom + Bath Event banner up top, then a totally separate marble materials story) with no visual transition — it reads like two emails got merged in production.
- Five near-identical "product on marble slab, shop now" blocks back to back is a lot of scrolling for zero differentiation — by the third table they all blur together.
- "LEAVE US A REVIEW. WIN $1,000" sweepstakes wedged between the design-services block and the product grid feels like a random ask with no connection to marble, materials, or anything else in this email.
- No swatch or sample program mentioned anywhere, which for a stone/material-story email aimed at someone furnishing two houses is a real miss — I'd want to see a finish before committing to a $2k+ dining table.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price or "starting at $X" on the hero marble product immediately below the fold — right now there's nothing to react to financially until I've scrolled past a dozen screens.
- 2. Cut the product parade from five tables/lamps down to two or three and let the Vendome dining room shot lead — that's the strongest image in the send, bury it and you bury the click.
- 3. Split the Bedroom + Bath Event and the marble materials story into two separate sends, or at minimum give them distinct visual sections instead of stacking cold.
- 4. Move "Earn 10% Back" rewards messaging up near the hero, not buried below a sweepstakes and a review-request block — that's the one line in this whole email built for someone like me.
- 5. Add a swatch/sample CTA next to at least one marble product — "order a finish sample" is exactly the kind of low-commitment click that gets a two-house renovator to actually engage.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Solid marble pieces, priced from $XXX — plus 10% back on every order`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Book a free design consult before you commit to a finish`
