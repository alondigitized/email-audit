# Quality favorites at new prices
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-25**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids again. This is the second Crate & Kids send I've gotten this cycle — the "quality collections at prices you'll love" one hit a couple days ago — and it's the same problem it's always been: I don't have kids. There's a checkerboard nursery rug, a "too-cute" plush toy called Crate Cuddles, a kids' desk chair, and a crib giveaway sweepstakes. None of it applies to a guy renovating a primary and a second home for himself.
- The one thing that almost pulls me in is the "imperfect checkerboard rug" — checkerboard pattern rugs have actually been all over my radar for the second-home living room, and if this were framed as a general home rug instead of a nursery rug, I'd have paused. Same with the "exclusive Weston bed" — could pass as adult bedroom furniture at a glance if the copy didn't scream toddler room.
- Not clicking, not forwarding. This is a mark-as-read-and-half-regret-the-subscription email. Crate & Barrel's main list at least stays in my lane; Crate & Kids should just unsubscribe me the same way I eventually will.

## What's working

- Clean, consistent product-tile layout — every section follows the same photo/price/CTA pattern, so it's easy to scan even skimming on my phone during commute.
- The checkerboard rug and the Weston bed photography look like real home-decor shots, not overtly juvenile staging, so for half a second they read as relevant.
- Star rating badge up top ("quality favorites, up to 25% off") gives a clear, honest value signal instead of vague marketing fluff.
- The "we love these for you" grid at the bottom at least attempts personalization, even if the underlying products are still nursery-only.

## What's weak

- Every single hero product is nursery/kids furniture — cuddly toys, kids' quilts, hooded towels, kids' desk — there's zero for a childless adult renovating two houses.
- The $500 crib sweepstakes and "TheDesignDesk" consultation booking are dead weight for someone with no baby room to design.
- Demographic mismatch is total: plush animals and a rainbow-decor kids' room photo tell me immediately this wasn't meant for me.
- Compare this to Article's living-room rotation email, which actually tracks with what I'm shopping for room by room — Crate & Kids doesn't even try to segment by whether you have children.
- CB2's bedroom/bath sale at least stays adult-category even when it's redundant with parent-brand C&B; this is a different product universe entirely.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment the list — if Crate & Barrel knows I've never clicked a single Crate & Kids product, stop sending nursery content entirely. This is a targeting failure, not a creative one.
- 2. If cross-selling to C&B subscribers is unavoidable, lead with the crossover pieces (the checkerboard rug, the Weston bed) reframed as general home decor, not tucked inside a kids-only campaign.
- 3. Kill or gate the sweepstakes/consultation CTAs behind an actual "do you have kids?" preference center instead of blasting everyone.
- 4. Match Wayfair's move of at least sticking a real % or $ discount in the subject — "up to 25% off" is buried on the hero image, not the subject line.
