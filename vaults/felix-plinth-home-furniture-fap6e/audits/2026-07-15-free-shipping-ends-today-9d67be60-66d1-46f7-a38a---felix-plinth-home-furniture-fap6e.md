# FREE SHIPPING ends today!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- Forty-seven. That's where the C&B family sits in my July inbox after this one drops, and I want to be precise about that because it changes how every sentence of this email reads. The last count was forty-six when the Crate & Barrel flagship "FREE SHIPPING ends today!" landed — same subject line, different sub-brand, same afternoon. Opening this one felt less like checking mail and more like a Groundhog Day bit.
- What makes this one stranger than the prior sends is the brand: Crate & **Kids**. I'm a 36-year-old guy simultaneously gutting a primary and a second home. Kids furniture is not zero — I have a guest room that could eventually double as a kid's room — but Crate & Kids isn't where my brain goes when I'm pricing out sofas, dining tables, or lighting. The segmentation here is doing nothing for me. The email sent to the "renovating adult with credit card" inbox slot is carrying the "toddler plush and kids bedding" content. That mismatch is the whole story.
- The email itself is clean — well-structured, no render bugs I can see, and the "500+ new fall arrivals starting at $29" block actually gives me something concrete. But "fall arrivals" in the third week of July? Article dropped a clean editorial send about entryways four days ago and didn't pretend it was September. This is rushing. I'll mark it read, not click, definitely not forward.

## What's working

- **Hero is legible and hierarchy holds.** "Ends today! Free shipping" reads instantly — no hunting for the offer.
- **Nav menu has useful sub-categories** ("Shop Storage," "Shop Lighting") that are actually relevant to home renovation even inside a kids-brand email, so there's a side-door to content I care about.
- **"We love these for you" personalization block** is the right instinct — it signals the brand is trying to use data, not just blast.
- **"Freebie starter kit" call-out** is a secondary offer hook that adds value beyond the shipping banner — that's a real reason to scroll.
- **Clean render on mobile.** No overlapping text, no broken images, tap targets look sized correctly.

## What's weak

- **Wrong sub-brand for this segment.** I shop Crate & Barrel proper and CB2 for home renovation. Crate & Kids landing in my inbox as the 47th C&B family email of the month feels like the org chart's problem becoming my inbox problem.
- **Same subject line as the prior send.** "FREE SHIPPING ends today!" already ran from the flagship C&B account earlier today. Two senders, identical copy — it reads as a coordination failure, not a campaign.
- **"500+ new fall arrivals" in mid-July.** Pottery Barn ran a desk email last week that felt grounded in now. This is asking me to mentally skip two months. Doesn't land.
- **"Ends today" credibility is shot.** This is the third or fourth "ends today" message from the C&B family on July 15 alone. When everything ends today, nothing ends today.
- **Kids-specific hero imagery.** Plush toys and kids bedding in the hero is a hard demographic disconnect for a 36-year-old renovating non-kid spaces.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Separate the sub-brand sends.** Don't fire Crate & Barrel AND Crate & Kids with the same subject line on the same afternoon. If the promotion is truly family-wide, merge it into one email or stagger by 24 hours minimum.
- 2. **Rotate the subject off "FREE SHIPPING ends today!"** — that phrase has appeared verbatim across multiple sends. Even a minor reframe earns back some attention.
- 3. **Lead the hero with a specific product category** relevant to the segment (storage, lighting, lounge — things in the nav that could appeal to a home renovator) instead of a plush toy.
- 4. **Move "freebie starter kit" higher.** It's a differentiated offer and it's buried below the fold. If a free gift is on the table, that leads.
- 5. **Drop "fall arrivals" until August.** If you must run it in July, anchor it to a specific ship-by-date ("arrives before school starts") so it doesn't feel like editorial sloppiness.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Crate & Kids: storage + lighting, shipping free`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `500+ new arrivals — and that free kit we keep forgetting to mention`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The storage pieces worth grabbing before shipping kicks back in`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` brand mismatch**
`<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` — email is branded "Crate & Kids SALE." Some accessibility clients (e.g., screen readers, iOS preview) surface the `<title>`. Should read "Crate & Kids."

**Viewport blocks user zoom**
`<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` — `maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom, violating WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, AA).

**Universal text-size-adjust override**
`*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}` — applied to the wildcard selector. Overrides user browser/OS text-scaling preferences globally; accessibility concern for low-vision users.

**`line-height: 100%` on universal selector**
`*{... line-height: 100%}` — clips descenders in Outlook and older Android clients. Should be set per-element, not globally.

**Conflicting desktop image max-width rules**
Two media queries fire for viewports ≥ 768px:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }  /* declared first */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }  /* declared second — wins */
```
The 640px rule overrides the 768px rule on large screens due to source order, so desktop images cap at 640px, not 600px. Likely unintentional; one rule should be removed.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
`format-detection` is declared four times — once combined, then three individual overrides. Redundant; clean up to a single combined declaration.
