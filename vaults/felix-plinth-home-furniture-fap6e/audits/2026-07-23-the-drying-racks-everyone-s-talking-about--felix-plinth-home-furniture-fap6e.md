# The Drying Racks EVERYONE'S talking about 💬
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- Nine. I'm going to say it up front because it's the context for everything that follows: this is my ninth Wayfair email in the span of roughly two days, and the fifth one that landed today, July 23rd. I've already sat through Black Friday in July, outdoor organization, ornaments at 80% off, and end tables. So I opened this one with some real skepticism — but I'll admit "drying racks" is genuinely interesting to me right now. I'm mid-renovation on the second home's laundry room. The subject actually had my attention.
- Then I opened it.
- The hero is a ceiling fan. "Keep your cool." Scrolling down: a Pay Later section, a product grid of patio cushions, a platform bed frame, outdoor chairs, area rugs, a wine cooler. I found zero visible drying rack content. Not in the hero, not in a callout module, not even as a tile in the grid. This is textbook bait-and-switch subject line. Pottery Barn sent me "Coffee tables for any space" last week and I clicked it immediately because the email *was* coffee tables. Article sent me a dining room focus and I read the whole thing because it *was* the dining room. This email promises one thing and delivers a kitchen-sink general blast dressed up with a BFIJ banner that's been in every single Wayfair send this week.
- Mark as read. No click. Won't forward. Mildly irritated.

## What's working

- **"Score styles you ♡" module is a reasonable personalization signal** — the curated row above the fold at least gestures at "we know what you like," even if the rest of the email ignores it.
- **"Keep your cool" is seasonally solid** — a ceiling fan hero in July is at least logical. If this had been the subject, I'd have more patience with the send.
- **BNPL offer has a real button** — "Get It Now, Pay Later" with a visible "Learn More" CTA is at least a functional and relevant offer for big-ticket home purchases.
- **No visible render bugs** — layout looks clean; the grid tiles load, nothing overlaps or breaks.

## What's weak

- **The subject promised drying racks; the email contains none** — this is the cardinal sin. I opened specifically because I need drying solutions for a laundry room. There's not a drying rack in sight. That's a broken promise.
- **"EVERYONE'S talking about" is manipulative social-proof language with nothing behind it** — no review count, no bestseller badge, no "47K sold" number. CB2's "Furniture Event" at least showed me the sale tier clearly; this is a claim with no receipts.
- **The product grid is exhausting** — patio cushions, a bed frame, wine coolers, outdoor rugs. This is not a product assortment. This is a clearinghouse dump.
- **"Learn More" as the only visible CTA copy** — it's the least specific possible call to action. Compare to CB2's furniture event send, which named the category and the discount in the button itself.
- **Fifth email today from this sender** — I don't care how good the subject is at this volume; cadence fatigue is a real thing. My Wayfair folder has become a blur.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the email be about drying racks** — if you're going to lead with it in the subject, build a hero around it. Show me 3-4 models, a bestseller badge, a "ships in 2 days" signal, and a direct "Shop Drying Racks" CTA. I'm literally in market. This was a missed conversion.
- 2. **Retire the Black Friday in July banner** — it's been in every Wayfair email this week. It no longer creates urgency; it creates wallpaper.
- 3. **Replace "Learn More" with something actionable** — "Shop Drying Racks," "See Fan Deals," anything with a noun.
- 4. **Prune the product grid** — pick a lane. Fans *or* outdoor furniture *or* bedroom. Not all three plus rugs and wine coolers.
- 5. **Cut send cadence in half immediately** — five emails in one day from any brand is a quick path to my unsubscribe.
