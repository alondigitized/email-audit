# Everything you need for Thanksgiving
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-12**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction: Pottery Barn again, with the same dark-green "Fall Sale: Up to 70% off" strip on top. It's been there since 10/06, and it was on the 10/12 "Don't miss The Fall Sale" send earlier today. The Thanksgiving tablescape below it is actually pretty. The table, the plaid napkins and the candlesticks look like what I'd want in a hosting-ready dining room. But it's a seasonal-content email stacked under a clearance banner I've already seen five times.
- The email runs very long, and everything gets equal weight. There are four or five "Shop Now"-style blocks: Thanksgiving, the Caterer's Box, New Arrivals, and a Christmas Shop banner that has already jumped the queue. On a phone, the hierarchy is "scroll until you get tired." Wayfair's "72-HR CLEAROUT, ENDS TONIGHT" email at least committed to one offer. CB2's "2 SALES END SOON" stacked two clear offers and kept them organized. This one tries to be a catalog.
- Would I click? Probably one tap, on the "Dining Table" tile or the extendable tables in "Recommended Just For You," because I'm furnishing two houses and I need a table that seats a crowd. Would I forward it? No. I'd save it to my Pinterest-adjacent folder for the table setting ideas and move on.

## What's working

- The hero photo is warm and moody, with a dark green and gold palette. It sells the hosting fantasy better than a flat sale banner would.
- The tiles ("Table setting must-haves," "Seasonal decor touches," "Quality-crafted furniture") are easy to scan and each has a clear label.
- The recommended-for-you grid shows extendable dining tables with a visible size range (76"–120" and 60"–125"). That's the detail I care about for hosting.
- The 10% back rewards card block is clear. I'm a rewards-card guy, and Pottery Barn is one of the few brands that puts that math on the page.
- The Caterer's Box "serve up to 12 guests" is a specific, useful hook rather than generic "decor."

## What's weak

- The green "Up to 70% off" strip has been the top banner on nearly every send since 10/06, and it's still there today. It's wallpaper to me now.
- There is no deadline and no shipping or delivery-window info. For furniture I need to know whether the table lands before the holiday. "Everything you need for Thanksgiving" never says when to order.
- The Christmas Shop banner at the bottom, plus New Arrivals, dilutes the Thanksgiving message. It's the same "too many things in one send" problem as Wayfair's pergola wall.
- The fine print is a dense block in tiny type. I couldn't read it without zooming, and it makes the "up to 70% off" feel hedged.
- No lifestyle models or persona hooks. Nothing here tells me it knows I'm renovating two homes.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to one story: Thanksgiving hosting. Drop the New Arrivals and Christmas banners, or move them to a separate send. This is the single biggest lift.
- 2. Retire or rotate the green sale strip. If the sale is the offer, make it the hero. If Thanksgiving is the hero, make the sale a thin line.
- 3. Add a credible "order by" date for in-stock dining tables and quick-ship items. That would give me a real reason to click now.
- 4. Lead the recommended grid with the extendable dining tables, with seat counts and price. Right now it opens on curtain rods.
- 5. Make the CTAs specific, like "Shop extendable tables," instead of repeated "Shop Now."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Seat 12 this Thanksgiving: tables in stock`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Set the table: Thanksgiving, up to 70% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Extendable dining tables, tabletop and decor. Order by 11/10 for holiday delivery.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus earn 10% back with the Pottery Barn card on your hosting upgrades.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based, sliced-image layout with a working preheader and a Litmus-style MSO fallback. The HTML source and QA data confirm widespread missing `alt` attributes, several non-HTTPS images, and recipient email addresses in third-party pixel URLs. The compliance header results are inconclusive because the relay may have stripped them.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Click redirects not verified.** 52 tracking links on `click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=...` were skipped by the HTTP probe. Their destinations, status codes and redirect chains are unconfirmed.
- **Non-HTTPS tracking pixel.** `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` loads over plain HTTP. It may be blocked or rewritten, which would undercount opens. It also exposes the recipient address in cleartext.
- **Recipient email in image URLs.** `https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` is loaded as an `<img>`. The same address appears in the `svopn` pixel. Anything that logs image requests will capture the address, including proxies and forwarding. The `svopn` pixel is also plain HTTP, so it is visible on the network.
- **Third-party pixel.** `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/L21rdC84NjEvY2lkLzE3NDc3ODk3MzMvdC8y/cat/1` is an ad-network beacon with no `alt`. It adds a third-party dependency and a privacy surface.
- **Cross-brand asset dependency.** `https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` is hosted on a sister brand's CDN, so it breaks if that path is retired.
- **Stale asset path.** `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` is a 2022 asset reused in a 2026 send, over HTTP.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `alt` on about 45 images.** Examples:
  - Hero and banners: `H26_TOP_FallSale.jpg`, `F26_Bottom_NewArrivals_V1.jpg`, `H26_Bottom_ChristmasShop.jpg`.
  - Slice sets: `0927_SUN_AM_ThanksgivingShop/slices_01–10.jpg` and `Email-Footer/slices_01–18.jpg`.
  - Product images: `banks-extendable-dining-table-76-128-1-l.jpg`, `toscana-extendable-dining-table-60-1245-1-l.jpg`, `cayman-platform-bed-1-l.jpg`, `remmy-swivel-chair-1-l.jpg`, `reeva-handwoven-rug-3-l.jpg`, `standard-curtain-rod-wall-bracket-l.jpg`.
  - With images off, or in a screen reader, the entire email is blank or unnamed. The sliced hero is the main exposure, because its message and calls to action exist only as image pixels.
  - The `<img>` tags for pixels (`coreemail.html`, `d.turn.com`, `svopn`, `clickcp`) also lack `alt`. These should be `alt=""`.
- **HTTP image sources.** `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, `slices_02.jpg` (2022 banner) and the `svopn` pixel use `http://`, which may be blocked or flagged as mixed content.
- **Empty `<title>`.** `<title></title>` is empty.
- **Zoom restriction.** The viewport meta has `maximum-scale=1`, which blocks pinch-zoom on some clients and is an accessibility regression.
- **Stray code in CSS.** The second `<style>` block contains PHP text: `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: no-store...")`. It is invalid CSS and can corrupt the rules that follow it in strict parsers. Because it sits after the `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` and `.yshortcuts` rules, the damage is probably limited, but it should be removed.
- **Layout tables without `role="presentation"`.** The spacer tables, such as `<table cellpadding="0" cellspacing="0"><tr><td height="20">&nbsp;</td>`, lack the attribute. Other tables have it.
- **Google Fonts `<link>`.** `fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat` is stripped by Gmail and Outlook. Confirm a fallback stack is declared. The truncated source doesn't show the `font-family` declarations.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No unresolved merge tokens appeared in the visible source. The recipient address is merged correctly into the pixel URLs (see section 2), but putting PII in query strings is the problem there, not the merge itself.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not captured.** The QA tool itself notes this may be a relay artifact, so it is not a confirmed sender defect. If the headers are really absent, the sender won't meet the one-click requirements that Gmail and Yahoo apply to bulk senders.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status are unknown, and nothing can be concluded. Verify against the raw message at the origin.
- **In-body unsubscribe and postal address.** The truncated HTML does not reach the footer, so the unsubscribe link and physical address could not be checked. The `Email-Footer/slices_01–18` images are likely image-based footer content. If the unsubscribe link or address is only in an image with no `alt`, it is not accessible.
- **Plain-text part.** It is 10,095 of 14,273 characters URLs (about 71%), which can be a spam-filter signal. This is driven by long tracking URLs.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **No UTM parameters visible.** All CTAs go through opaque `click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=...` redirects, so UTM presence, final destinations and landing page match could not be verified.
- **Product image hosts.** Product images load from `www.potterybarn.com/pbimgs/...`, the same domain as the site. This is consistent, and nothing suggests a mismatch.
- **Redirect failures.** Because redirects weren't followed, a dead or mismatched landing page can't be ruled out.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to the hero and sliced content images, and use `alt=""` on spacers and tracking pixels.
2. Move the `http://` images and the `svopn` pixel to HTTPS, and replace the 2022 banner asset.
3. Remove recipient email addresses from the `coreemail.html` and `svopn` pixel URLs, using an opaque ID instead.
4. Delete the stray PHP `header(...)` text from the `<style>` block, and fill in `<title>`.
5. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
6. Self-host the `spacer_0.gif` asset instead of using the West Elm CDN.
7. Verify `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and SPF/DKIM/DMARC against the raw message, since this relay couldn't confirm them.
8. Run the 52 tracked links through a redirect-following probe to check destinations and UTM parameters.
