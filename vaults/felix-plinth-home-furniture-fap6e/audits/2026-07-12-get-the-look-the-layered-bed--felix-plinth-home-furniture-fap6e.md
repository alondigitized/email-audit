# Get the look: The layered bed
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- Eighteen. I know it's eighteen because I started keeping count after the July 10 "FREE design help" send, which was the one that finally broke the sale-hammer pattern. And here we are again with another pivot — this time an editorial "get the look" breakdown of a layered bed. I'll say this: the subject line actually made me pause before hitting archive. After a string of "UP TO 70% OFF" and sofa sale headers, "Get the look: The layered bed" reads like a magazine cover pull. It signals this isn't another discount email, and that alone bought it a fair shake.
- Inside, the editorial structure is clean — hero bed shot, then each layer broken out into its own product moment: the ruffled white pillow set, the whitewashed linen duvet, the stripe fill, the decorative throw situation. It reads like the Pinterest board I actually maintain for the second home's primary bedroom. The "Recommended Just For You" module beneath it and the "Shop New Arrivals" block add commerce weight. No visible discount anywhere in the editorial body though, and the styling — soft ruffles, navy romantic — leans noticeably feminine. The bed in the hero is beautiful. It's not quite *my* bedroom.
- I wouldn't forward it, but I'd save it. It goes in the folder. I might click one of those product callout sections later this week when I'm actually in a shopping mindset. Mark as read? No. Click now? Probably not tonight.

## What's working

- **The editorial break is genuinely earned.** After seventeen sale emails, a "how to layer your bed" content send is what kept this out of archive — same reason the July 10 design services email scored an 8.
- **Hero image is doing real work.** The layered navy-and-white bedroom is aspirational and specific enough to be useful — not generic catalog filler.
- **Per-layer product breakouts are smart.** Ruffled pillow → linen duvet → stripe fill → decorative throw: each has its own module. This is how I actually shop bedding; I build up components, not kits.
- **"Recommended Just For You" module.** It's below the fold, but the personalization signal is there. After CB2 hammering the same 40% off to obviously everyone for the past week, a personalization attempt reads as effort.
- **No render bugs.** Clean layout, images loaded, text readable at mobile size without pinching.

## What's weak

- **Styling skews feminine.** The ruffled white pillow and the overall soft romantic palette feels targeted toward a female buyer. I'm renovating a primary bedroom alongside my partner, so I'm in the conversation, but this email didn't market *to* me.
- **No price anchor anywhere in the editorial body.** Article's new arrivals send earlier this month at least gave me a number to evaluate against. I'm buying via my highest-rewards card and I need a reason to pull the trigger tonight; editorial content alone doesn't do it.
- **Multiple soft CTAs with no dominant one.** Each product section presumably has its own link, but there's no "Shop the Full Look" or "Build Your Bed" button that consolidates intent. I'm left scrolling for the thread.
- **Cadence is the invisible tax.** This is a good email in a vacuum. At send number eighteen, the fatigue discount on my engagement is real even when the content type is different.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a single consolidating CTA above the product modules.** Something like "Build This Bed" or "Shop the Full Layered Look" with a button that links to a curated PLP — not a homepage. The editorial sells the vision; the button needs to close the gap.
- 2. **Anchor one price.** Even a "duvet insert from $89" or "starting at $X" line under the hero would give my brain something to evaluate. Wayfair's rug emails underperform when they strip price anchors too — I need a number.
- 3. **Skew at least one product module neutral or male-forward.** Linen duvet in slate or graphite, grid-stitch throw, something that signals both partners are the intended buyer. I'm the one opening this email right now.
- 4. **Suppress or segment for high-frequency senders.** Eighteen emails in twelve days means I'm discounting every send by 20% before I even open it. One good editorial email a week lands harder than four sale blasts.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Start with the duvet insert. Everything else stacks from there.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Build it by layer — from fill to decorative throw.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP code artifact in `<style>` block (template bug):**
The following appears as literal text inside a `<style>` tag — it is PHP code, not CSS:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
CSS parsers silently discard this, so there is no visual breakage, but it confirms a template composition error where a server-side snippet was injected into the wrong block. It also leaks internal caching implementation detail.

**Empty `<title>` tag:** `<title></title>` — some clients surface this in tab or preview contexts.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile, which is an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4).

**`*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` applied globally** — overrides user/OS font size preferences, compounding the zoom issue above.

**50+ images missing `alt` text** — full list in QA findings. Key examples:
- All 16 `slices_0N.jpg` content images (main bed layout)
- `F26_TOP_SummerSalePartC_NoUrgency.jpg` (hero banner)
- Product images: `farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `hydrocotton-quick-dry-towel-l.jpg`, `sausalito-8-drawer-dresser-66-l.jpg`, etc.

**3 images served over HTTP** (will be blocked by image proxies in Gmail, Apple Mail, Outlook):
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
