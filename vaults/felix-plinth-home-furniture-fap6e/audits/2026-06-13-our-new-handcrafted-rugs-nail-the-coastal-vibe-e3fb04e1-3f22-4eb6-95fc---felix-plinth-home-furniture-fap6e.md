# Our new, handcrafted rugs nail the coastal vibe
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- Forty-nine. That's where the C&B family counter lands after this one, and I want to hold that number in front of everything else I say, because this is the second email from this sender on June 13 alone — the "Want forever furniture" send was already in my inbox this morning and scored a 4. So before I even got to the rug photography, I had one eyebrow raised.
- Inside, it's a different story. The email is genuinely good-looking: warm linen tones, texture close-ups that communicate handcraft without me reading a word, room settings with coastal light done right. The Textilique collection name gives me something to search later. This is the kind of email I screenshot and drop into my home inspo folder — which I literally have. But a screenshot is not a click, and there is zero mechanism here to convert my appreciation into an order. No discount, no size reference, no deadline. It's editorial dressed up as a pitch without the pitch.
- I'd save this one and not click. Forward to a friend? Only if they're in the middle of a rug search the same week I am, and even then they'll just get annoyed that there's no price incentive.

## What's working

- The hero room setting communicates "coastal" immediately — warm natural fibers, ambient light, rug centered in a real living room I can transpose onto my sunroom addition.
- Texture close-ups mid-scroll show handcraft quality visually, no copy required. That's confident art direction.
- "Textilique" as a named collection gives me a search term to use later; it's not just "our rugs" pointing to a category page.
- Seasonal timing is right — June, coastal, summer. Pottery Barn just hit me with the Monique Lhuillier florals and it felt precious; C&B's approach here is more lived-in and easier to picture in a real house.
- Clean render top to bottom — no broken images, no text overflow, layout holds.

## What's weak

- Zero offer. CB2 sent "ALMOST OVER: Design Legends Sale" this same week and scored an 8 from me because there was a real deadline and a real discount. This email is beautiful and does nothing.
- "Nail the coastal vibe" in the subject is marketing-speak. I'm not moving off the couch for vibes.
- Two sends in one calendar day from the same domain is bad inbox math. The cadence is burning credit that the photography is trying to earn.
- No size guidance visible anywhere — I'm actively shopping rugs for two different rooms right now and I need to know if this collection does a 9×12. The decision point is right there, untouched.
- The find-a-store section is present but inert — no event, no free swatch callout, no reason to walk in. C&B showrooms are genuinely better than Wayfair for rug shopping (you need to feel the pile) but this email doesn't earn that trip.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a size guide in the hero.** "Available in 5×8, 8×10, and 9×12" next to the hero image cuts decision fatigue in half for anyone actively shopping a specific room. This is the single biggest reason I abandon rug purchases online.
- 2. **Give me a reason to act today.** Even "free shipping on rug orders this week" would do it. Article's "Summer hosting made simple" baked free delivery into the headline and I clicked through. C&B isn't even attempting urgency.
- 3. **Surface the swatch program front and center.** Free rug swatches are a genuine competitive advantage over Wayfair — if it's in this email, it's buried past where I stopped looking.
- 4. **Kill one of the two June 13 sends.** The furniture durability email from this morning and this rugs email are stepping on each other. Pick a lane.
- 5. **Rewrite the subject to be functional, not aesthetic:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `New Textilique rugs — coastal weaves in 8×10 and 9×12`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Natural fiber rugs just landed — coastal tones, handcrafted`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Woven from natural materials. Free swatches available on every style.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Textures that hold up in real rooms. Shop the Textilique collection.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Many modern email clients ignore this, but it should be removed regardless.

**[MEDIUM] Conflicting `img` max-width media queries — cascade order bug**
```css
/* rule 1 — appears first in source */
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }

/* rule 2 — appears second in source */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
At ≥768px both rules match. Rule 2 (640px) appears later in the cascade and wins, making the 600px cap dead code on wide viewports. If the intent is a 600px-wide template, the rule order must be reversed so the 768px breakpoint overrides the 640px one.

**[LOW] `*` selector sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` while `body` sets `100%`**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; ... }
```
Conflicting values on overlapping selectors. `none` on `*` could suppress auto-scaling on WebKit clients for elements that inherit it before `body` specificity resolves. The `*` value should be removed; `body` is the correct target.

**[LOW] Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
The combined tag already covers all cases:
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
```
Three additional single-attribute `format-detection` tags below it are redundant. No rendering impact, but adds unnecessary payload.
