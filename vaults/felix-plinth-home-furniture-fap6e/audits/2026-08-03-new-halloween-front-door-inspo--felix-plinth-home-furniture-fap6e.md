# NEW 🎃 Halloween front door inspo
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-03**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "Style an Enchanting Front Door" — this is exactly the skeleton-and-ghost-gourd email I was worried about after actually liking the last four Pottery Barn sends. We just did indigo ceramics (8/10, 2026-08-02), heritage design (8/10, 2026-08-02), a Print & Pattern florals-and-plaids drop (6/10, 2026-08-02) — three legit home-decor emails in one day — and now it's fully into porch-Halloween theater with fabric ghosts hanging from a pergola. It's tonally the furthest thing from a renovation email I've gotten from this brand all week.
- That said, it's not offensive — the photography is genuinely gorgeous (that stone-and-lantern front door shot is straight out of a design magazine), and it does eventually get to a "Recommended Just For You" block with an actual dining table and a farmhouse platform bed, which is the only section I'd stop scrolling for. But the whole top two-thirds is seasonal decor I have zero use for mid-reno. I'm not clicking into "The Halloween Shop." I might glance at "Recommended Just For You" if I'm already in the email, but I'm not forwarding this to anyone — my buddy doing his own reno would groan at ghost decor before he'd groan at CB2's Cubs Game contest email.
- Verdict: skim to the bottom for the furniture block, then archive.

## What's working

- The hero shot of the stone entryway with lanterns and the wood double door is legitimately beautiful photography — same production quality as the indigo bedding email.
- "Recommended Just For You" resurfaces real furniture (Toscano extendable dining table, farmhouse platform bed, Reeve hardwoven rug) that's actually in my renovation lane.
- The Design Crew "book a free design service" CTA at the bottom is a smart cross-sell for someone mid-reno like me, even buried as it is.
- Rewards card callout (10% back, Affirm financing) is consistent branding I've now seen across several PB sends — feels like a real loyalty program, not a one-off gimmick.

## What's weak

- Wall-to-wall Halloween porch styling — lit ghosts, jack-o'-lanterns, witch broom — when I'm mid-renovation on two houses, not decorating a seasonal porch tableau.
- It took scrolling past four full decor sections ("Ghostly Greeting," "Every Witch Way," "Warm Harvest Glow," "Halloween Headquarters") before hitting anything relevant to me.
- No color-palette or swatch-and-sample callout anywhere, which is the thing that actually gets me to click on a PB email lately (the indigo send nailed this).
- Compare to Article's "Last chance: Summer Sale ends today" — that email leads with a hard deadline and 40% off before you scroll once. This one has zero discount, zero urgency, just mood-board content.
- This is genuinely the fourth-plus PB/PB-family email this week; my inbox is starting to feel like the CB2/Crate & Barrel "family snooze" I called out a few sends back — except now it's a seasonal detour instead of just repetition.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split the send** — Halloween/seasonal decor should go to people actually shopping seasonal, and furniture-reno folks like me should get a dedicated "new arrivals for your reno" email instead of burying the dining table three screens down.
- 2. Lead with the "Recommended Just For You" block, or at minimum put a design-services/renovation CTA above the fold — that's the one thing in here I'd actually act on.
- 3. Add a real offer. Wayfair's "48-HOUR CLEARANCE UP TO 70% OFF" and Article's "ends today" both give me a reason to act now; this email gives me zero — just vibes and pumpkins.
- 4. If Halloween content stays, at least tag it as skippable up top with a one-line "shopping for your home instead? See new arrivals" link so I don't have to scroll past four sections of ghosts.

## Technical Summary

- The `sv_sveme` open-tracking pixel is served over **plain HTTP**, not HTTPS (see Compliance/Rendering below).

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **43 images missing `alt` text** per QA output (hero banner, all `slices_XX.jpg` product tiles, footer banners, tracking pixels). Screen readers will announce nothing or fall back to raw filenames/URLs for these.
- **4 image sources use `http://` instead of `https://`**: `edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`, `edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, `edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`, and the `svopn.potterybarn.com` open-tracking pixel. Mixed content may be blocked or flagged by mail clients that enforce TLS.
- Invalid markup: the second `<style>` block in `<head>` contains literal PHP code fragments — `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: ...`. This is not valid CSS and appears to be leaked server-side template code that was never stripped before send. It's inert in rendered HTML but is a build/pipeline defect worth flagging to the ESP.
- External stylesheet dependency: `<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">` — most major mail clients (Outlook desktop, Gmail app, Yahoo) strip or block external `<link>` stylesheets, so this font will silently fail to load in a large share of clients; no fallback font-family is declared alongside it in the excerpt shown.
- `<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">` and `content="width=1024"` variants are present but commented out — dead code, not a functional bug.

## 7. Recommendations

2. Add descriptive `alt` text to all content images (currently 43 flagged) and empty `alt=""` to purely decorative spacers/pixels for screen-reader compliance.

5. Re-verify `List-Unsubscribe`/`List-Unsubscribe-Post`/`Authentication-Results` against a direct header capture (not the AgentMail relay) before treating their absence as a compliance gap.
