# Color palettes that just feel “goop”
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-19**
## Executive summary

- Another CB2 email — I think this is the sixth or seventh CB2/Crate & Barrel-family send I've had to deal with this week, and this one is a "Goop collection" tie-in with a color-palette gimmick pinned to each room shot. The "IT JUST FEELS 'GOOP'" hero is trying too hard, and the whole thing reads as a moodboard, not an offer. No price, no discount, no urgency — just a scroll of pretty rooms with swatch chips next to each one.
- The one thing that actually stopped my thumb was the "LEAVE US A REVIEW, WIN $1,000" banner buried two-thirds down — that's a real incentive and it's in the wrong spot. Everything above it is just styled inspiration content, which is fine for a Saturday-morning browse but does nothing for someone like me who's mid-renovation and actually needs to buy things this month.
- Verdict: I'd skim it for 3 seconds on the "More For You" product grid at the bottom, maybe screenshot the green Gwyneth chair for my Pinterest-adjacent folder, then archive. Not clicking, not forwarding — this is CB2 doing a lifestyle piece when what I need is Pottery Barn's "Ready-to-Ship Seating, 1-4 weeks" clarity from earlier this week. That email solved an actual renovation problem; this one is just vibes.

## What's working

- The color-swatch chips next to each room (Nadine Chair, Gwyneth Chair, etc.) are a genuinely useful merchandising idea — it's the kind of "does this fit my palette" signal I actually want when I'm juggling two houses' worth of finishes.
- The green velvet Gwyneth chair and dark walnut coffee table shots are moody and well-lit — screenshot-worthy for my inspo folder, same energy as the "A little midcentury. So now." Belmond sectional shot from a couple days ago.
- The "More For You" product grid at the bottom has real prices visible ($399-$2,199 range) — that's the first concrete, actionable info in the whole email.
- The $1,000 review sweepstakes is a real, specific incentive, unlike most of CB2's recent "up to 60% off" vagueness.

## What's weak

- Zero discount, zero price, zero urgency until you're five scrolls deep — Article's "Clocking in" home office email at least had a clear non-sale story; this one doesn't even commit to being inspiration-only or a sale.
- "Goop collection" as the framing is a miss for me — I don't know or care what Goop's aesthetic point of view is, and leaning on a lifestyle-brand crossover instead of CB2's own voice feels like it's chasing someone else's audience, not mine.
- The review-for-$1,000 banner is the strongest CTA in the email and it's stranded in the middle of the scroll, way below the fold — should be near the top.
- This is at least the sixth CB2-family email this week (I've now flagged the Onyx & Walnut, Floor Sample Sale, End of Summer Sale, Paige DeSorbo apartment tour, and "impeccable energy" sends) — my inbox fatigue with this brand is real and this one doesn't do anything to break the pattern.
- No shipping timeline, no swatch/sample-ordering CTA — for someone mid-renovation on two homes, "can I get a fabric swatch before I commit" is exactly the info I need and it's absent.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the $1,000 review sweepstakes or a real % off — that's the only concrete hook in the email; move it above the fold instead of burying it after six room shots.
- 2. Cut the Goop framing entirely and lean into "shop this room's exact palette" — the swatch chips are the actual differentiator, don't dilute them with a borrowed lifestyle brand name.
- 3. Add a "order a swatch" or "free fabric samples" CTA next to the color chips — turns a browsing email into a decision-making tool for renovation buyers like me.
- 4. Add ship times to at least the "More For You" product grid — Pottery Barn's "1-4 weeks" framing works because it answers my actual anxiety.
- 5. Space this out — five to six CB2-family sends in one week is burning my attention before I even open them anymore.

- 68 tracking/click-redirect links were present but skipped from HTTP probing (redirect domains not resolvable via automated check) — cannot confirm destination validity.
- Multiple `dotomi.com` ad-network calls served over plain HTTP:
  - `http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?dtm_com=2&dtm_cid=2437&dtm_cmagic=654788&dtm_fid=103&dtm_format=6&cli_promo_id`
  - `http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2437/pub1.php` through `pub10.php` (10 sequential endpoints, identical query param `d98e199bf5027afea04b790655b3dee2=1`)
- Non-HTTPS resources risk being blocked by mail clients that enforce mixed-content/TLS policies (e.g., Apple Mail Privacy Protection, Gmail image proxy over HTTPS), potentially breaking these tracking pixels silently.

- Screen readers/text-only clients will render these as blank; low risk for the ad-pixels themselves but a real gap for the 8 content images if any are non-decorative.
- Template uses standard MSO/VML conditional comments and media queries for mobile breakpoints (418px, 690px, 768px) — no structural rendering errors detected in the truncated source.
