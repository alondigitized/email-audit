# Shop July 4th deals in stores: Up to 70% off
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- Fifty-three. That's my rough Pottery Barn email count for the month, and I'm keeping track because they've made it necessary. This one drops July 1st from the Design Services sub-account — note the sub-sender — and the entire hero is pushing me toward a store visit. "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" and "FIND YOUR STORE" are the primary CTAs. That's a mismatch against a subject line that led with "up to 70% off." I renovate two homes simultaneously and I buy online, late at night, with my highest-rewards card loaded. Nobody is booking me a design appointment on a Tuesday.
- That said, the red banner floor model sale actually made me stop. "In stores only — limited quantities — up to 70% off Furniture, Lighting & More." That's real. That's the kind of scarcity that makes me check whether there's a PB near my second property. But they don't tell me anything specific about what's on the floor — no product tease, no inventory hint, just "FIND A STORE." Article's last July 4th send at least showed me something concrete before asking me to click. This asks me to plan a commute to a maybe.
- Mark as read. I'd revisit the floor model banner if I happened to be near a showroom this weekend, but the primary CTA isn't getting a tap tonight.

## What's working

- The deal grid is clean and scannable — four categories, four percentages, easy to parse without zooming on a phone.
- The floor model sale in the red banner differentiates this from the other four PB emails I've already seen this week; it's the only one in that batch that offers something genuinely unavailable online.
- The 50% back in rewards section is directly relevant — I'm actively gaming which card earns most at PB, so the credit card pitch isn't noise.
- "Find the Right Rug" on July 12 is unexpectedly specific — a real event, a real date, a real topic. Crate & Kids sent me a vague "quality furniture that lasts" email this week that couldn't name a single thing; this at least names a date.
- Email renders cleanly — no broken images, no layout collapse, text is legible at inbox scale.

## What's weak

- The hero is a design services pitch, not a deals pitch. "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" leading an email whose subject promised "up to 70% off" is the classic bait-and-switch feeling, even if it isn't intentional.
- Everything interesting in this email requires a store visit: floor model sale is "in stores only," the rug class is in-store, the hero tells me to "find your store." I am not going to a store at 11pm.
- This is the fifth Pottery Barn email in my inbox in roughly 48 hours. The cadence is doing active damage to how I receive even the good stuff.
- The rug design class lists the time as "12AM." That's midnight. If it's noon, say 12PM — this is either a data error or the brand's most hardcore customer demographic.
- No specific product anywhere. CB2's "Up to 50% off furniture & more" send showed me an actual sofa. This shows me category tiles and asks me to imagine the rest.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Flip the hero CTA hierarchy.** Put "SHOP ALL DEALS" first and biggest. Demote "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" to a small secondary link. The subject pulled me in with a discount, not a calendar invite.
- 2. **Give the floor model sale one real product.** Even a single teaser image — "Sectional, was $3,200, now $960, Minneapolis Galleria" — would close the commitment gap between "interesting" and "I'm actually driving there."
- 3. **Fix the 12AM timestamp on the rug class.** One character — this is a credibility dent that's trivially fixable.
- 4. **Cut two of the five modules.** App download, rewards card, Affirm, design class, floor model sale is five different jobs. Pick three. The email is trying to convert five different customer segments simultaneously.
- 5. **Surface an online path for the floor model sale.** Even "call ahead and we'll hold it" would reduce friction. Right now there's no bridge between the banner and an action I can take from my couch.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 70% off — floor models, pillows, bedding, chairs`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Furniture, lighting & rugs — in-store clearance, limited quantities this weekend.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Floor model prices you won't find online. Find your nearest store.`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "July 4th Sale / Design Crew" (2026-07-01)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email sends correctly but contains a confirmed PHP code-injection into a `<style>` block and two mixed-content (HTTP) image sources. Compliance header detection was incomplete at the relay layer, leaving SPF/DKIM status and one-click unsubscribe unverifiable.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**No live broken links.** The 403 flagged by QA (`/pages/credit-card/?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB…`) is inside an HTML comment block and is not rendered or clickable by recipients. The QA probe hit the commented-out URL, which is a false positive for end-user impact — but the dead URL is evidence the credit card banner was removed mid-production without cleanup.

**23 click-redirect links** via `click.e.potterybarn.com` were skipped by the probe (expected; this is the ESP's redirect domain). Final destinations are unverifiable from source alone.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CRITICAL] PHP code in `<style>` block — template contamination:**
```html
<style>
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
...
```
These are PHP `header()` calls that were not executed server-side and leaked verbatim into the email's CSS. Email clients parse this as invalid CSS and discard the block. The `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` reset rules and Outlook line-height fix that follow in the same block may be silently dropped as a result.

**[HIGH] 2 images served over HTTP (mixed content):**
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Clients with enforced HTTPS (iOS Mail, Gmail) will block these images silently. Both are on the same CDN host — HTTPS works there; this is an asset-path error.

**[MEDIUM] 30+ images missing `alt` text** — all body slices (`slices_01` through `slices_10`), all footer slices (`slices_01` through `slices_18`), and multiple tracking pixels. The sole image with alt text is the header logo (`alt="POTTERY BARN"`). Sliced-image emails with no alt text render as a blank white rectangle in image-off environments.

**[LOW] Inconsistent responsive breakpoints:** The outer media query triggers at `max-width: 480px` and snaps the wrapper to 320px, but the second `@media screen and (max-width: 650px)` triggers `.mobile-hide`/`.mobile-show` toggles at 650px. The 480px→320px snap is aggressive for modern phones; 600px is the standard trigger for a 650px desktop layout.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unfired merge tokens or broken substitution syntax visible in the delivered HTML. The subscriber address visible in tracking pixels (`lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`) resolved correctly — personalization fired as expected.

---

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** by the AgentMail relay. This is required for CAN-SPAM and is enforced by Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender rules (>5k/day senders). May be a relay capture gap rather than a true absence — cannot confirm either way from current data.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected.** One-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo since 2024) is unverifiable. Same caveat as above.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header absent** from captured headers — SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status unknown. `PotteryBarn@e.potterybarn.com` sends from `e.potterybarn.com` (likely ExactTarget/Salesforce Marketing Cloud); DKIM alignment to the `potterybarn.com` parent domain should be confirmed independently.

Unsubscribe link is present in the email body (visible in footer slice structure); physical mailing address presence is not verifiable from truncated HTML but is standard for this sender.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

No `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, or `utm_campaign` parameters are visible in any href before hitting the click-redirect domain. UTM attribution may be injected by `click.e.potterybarn.com` on redirect — this is common for ESP-managed sends — but it cannot be confirmed without following a redirect chain. The commented-out credit card link used `cm_ite=` (Coremetrics), not UTM, suggesting the site may use a non-Google analytics stack.

No direct landing-page links are present in the visible source — all CTAs route through the click tracker, so landing-page alignment (sale page actually showing July 4th pricing) is not auditable from source.
