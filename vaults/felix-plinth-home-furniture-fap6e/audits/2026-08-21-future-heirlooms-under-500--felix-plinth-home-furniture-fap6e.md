# Future heirlooms under $500
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-21**
## Executive summary

- Okay, CB2 for the third time in as many days — I did the "Floor Sample Sale" yesterday and "Just added to clearance" the day before that, and now it's "Future Heirlooms Under $500." Credit where it's due: this is actually a decent concept for a designer-forward brand — "heirloom" language plays well against the disposable-Wayfair stuff I keep seeing, and the $500 price ceiling is a real, useful filter I don't usually get from these people.
- But there's no discount here. Zero. This isn't a sale email dressed as a lookbook — it's a full-price catalog with "under $500" doing the work a percentage-off badge usually does. After the last two CB2 sends actually had promo language, this one backslides into pure merchandising, and by the time I hit the fifth product-designer callout ("Designed by Studio ANANSI," "Designed by Studio AMARA") it starts to read like a design-school crit rather than an email trying to get me to buy a candle holder.
- Would I click? Maybe on the dining chair or the marble side table if I'm actually browsing, but I'm not opening my wallet off this one. Wouldn't forward it — nothing here is surprising enough to text a friend about, unlike the "Enoki Table Lamp" style pieces Article nailed better in their "New for home office" send.

## What's working

- The "Future Heirlooms Under $500" hook is a genuinely different angle than the discount-stacking noise from Pottery Barn and Crate & Kids this week — it's aspirational without being a hard sell.
- Product photography is consistently moody and on-brand — the Wesler dining chair and Enoki lamp shots look like something out of a design magazine, not a stock catalog.
- Designer attribution (Studio ANANSI, Studio AMARA, etc.) is a nice trust cue for someone like me who's mid-renovation and cares about provenance, not just price.
- Clear section breaks (Furniture → Decor → Lighting) with a "SHOP" CTA after each makes the scroll feel organized rather than an endless product dump like the Wayfair "FOUND: Bar Stools" disaster.

## What's weak

- No dollar or percent discount anywhere despite the "$500" framing implying value — this reads as full retail dressed up as a deal, which is going to feel like bait to anyone who opened expecting a sale after yesterday's actual clearance email.
- "Win a $5,000 Refresh" sweepstakes banner mid-email is a jarring tonal shift — it drags me out of the heirloom/design narrative and into contest-entry mode, which feels bolted-on.
- The "More For You" candle grid at the bottom is generic algorithmic filler — smoked glass hurricane holders in three near-identical shapes don't add anything after I've already scrolled through a curated lookbook.
- Zero shipping/delivery info for furniture pieces (dining chair, marble side table) — as someone actually furnishing two houses right now, that's the first thing I need and it's just not there.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick a lane: either make this a real sale email with an actual number, or drop the "$500" framing and lean fully into the design-story angle — the hybrid undersells both.
- 2. Cut the sweepstakes banner or move it to its own dedicated send — it doesn't belong wedged between the lighting section and the recommendation grid.
- 3. Add basic delivery/lead-time info near the furniture items — CB2 furniture regularly runs weeks-long lead times and that's a real purchase-decision factor for me.
- 4. Trim the "More For You" grid to 2 items max or drop it — it dilutes the curated feel the rest of the email worked to build.
