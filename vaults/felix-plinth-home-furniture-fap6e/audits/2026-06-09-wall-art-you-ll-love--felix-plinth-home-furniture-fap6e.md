# WALL ART you'll love!!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- Forty-two. I've now opened forty-two Wayfair emails, and the number matters because the brand has been ping-ponging between "genuinely good" and "hitting the alarm clock again" all week. "#kitchenmats" from June 7 was a 9/10 — a single clever subject that stopped me mid-unsubscribe. Two days later I got back-to-back rugs and bath mats emails on the same day. And now: "WALL ART you'll love!!" with the double exclamation points, in all caps for the first two words, like a flyer taped to a telephone pole.
- Here's the thing: I actually need wall art. I have bare walls at both properties and have been meaning to scroll through the Wayfair art catalog for weeks. The hero renders nicely — colorful, energetic abstract prints that feel modern enough for the second home's aesthetic. The Great Summer Sale branding is clean, the $1 rewards callout is genuinely useful to me (I'm already a member, I think about the rewards math every single time), and the "picked just for you" circular-product callout shows someone thought about merchandising. But then the bottom half of the email becomes a general catalog dump — outdoor furniture, accent tables, a grab-bag grid that has nothing to do with wall art. The subject promised me one thing; the email delivered approximately six categories.
- I'd scroll this one, begrudgingly, because the timing is right and wall art is legitimately on my list. I would not click with any confidence, and I'd definitely not forward it. Compare this to CB2's "We found you the perfect sofa" from earlier this week — that email felt like a stylist. This feels like Wayfair remembered it hadn't emailed me in the last four hours.

## What's working

- **Hero imagery is genuinely on-point for wall art.** The colorful abstract/botanical prints at the top are appealing and feel current — not stock-photo generic.
- **Great Summer Sale dates are visible** in small text at the top, which gives the campaign a real shelf life rather than floating in evergreen limbo.
- **$1 rewards callout is well-placed.** I always do the rewards math; seeing it near the top means I'm already calculating before I hit the grid.
- **"Picked just for you" section** with the single circular product actually works as a moment of pause before the grid chaos — it implies Wayfair knows my account.
- **Layout is clean, no render bugs.** Everything loaded, nothing is broken or overlapping.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is a relapse.** After "#kitchenmats" proved that a clean, lowercase, specific subject works better, "WALL ART you'll love!!" with ALL CAPS and double exclamation marks feels like the brand forgot its own data.
- **The email doesn't commit to wall art.** The hero promises a curated art send; the bottom half is outdoor furniture and accent tables. If Pottery Barn ran a wall art email, it would be wall art top to bottom.
- **No sizing or dimension info visible.** I'm hanging art on specific walls with specific dimensions. Showing me a pretty print without any size info is the single biggest miss for the category.
- **"Your future 'loves' are here" is a lazy section header.** This is Wayfair's nth variation of "stuff we think you'll like." After forty-two emails, the language has gone completely invisible to me.
- **Back-to-back sends are eroding trust.** The rugs and bath mats emails hit yesterday; the cadence has me pre-annoyed before the email even loads.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to the category.** End the email at wall art. Remove the outdoor furniture, remove the accent table callout, remove anything that isn't art or art-adjacent (frames, gallery walls, ledges). Article sent a focused outdoor email this week and it held my attention longer because of the editorial focus.
- 2. **Add size callouts to every art product.** "24" x 36"" visible under each thumbnail is the difference between a click and a scroll-past. This is the single biggest conversion lever for the category.
- 3. **Subject line — ditch the caps and exclamations.** "#kitchenmats" was #38 and scored 9 because it felt human. "Wall art to fill your walls" or a subject that references scale/size would feel categorically different from this.
- 4. **Preheader needs to complement, not echo.** If the subject is wall art, the preheader should reference the specific offer or sale deadline — not just restate the category.
- 5. **One hero piece, linked directly.** Pick one specific print, name it, show a room render, link to the PDP. CB2's "We found you the perfect sofa" worked because they committed to *one* thing. This email wants me to feel everything and buy nothing.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Wall art for actual walls (and the sizes to prove it)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Great Summer Sale: art that fits`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop prints, canvases, and gallery walls — sale ends June 9`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sizes from 18" to 60" wide. Free shipping on orders $35+`
