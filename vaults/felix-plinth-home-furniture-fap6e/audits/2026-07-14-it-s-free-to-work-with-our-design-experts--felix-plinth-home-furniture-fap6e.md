# It’s FREE to work with our design experts
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-three. That's my July Pottery Barn count after this one lands, and I already got another one from them earlier today — the "UP TO 70% OFF + coastal cottage" blast that I filed and moved on from. So when "Pottery Barn Design Services" shows up a few hours later with a separate pitch, the sender name does its job: I know this isn't just another sale email before I even open it.
- The July 10 "FREE design help" send is the relevant benchmark here. That email was a genuine pattern break — no sale banner, just a direct offer of something useful when you're managing two simultaneous renovations. It bought Pottery Barn real goodwill with me. This email is that email's sibling, four days later, same core pitch. The hero is clean, "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" is right up front, and they've added a Fall Collection in-store preview event with an actual date and time — Sunday, 11AM, "RSVP TODAY" — which is the closest thing to a new reason to click. The "Recommended for You" block at the bottom surfaces bedroom furniture, which is exactly where my renovation budget is bleeding right now.
- But here's the problem: I already decided whether I was booking a design appointment when July 10 landed. This one doesn't give me a new reason. If I acted, I don't need it. If I didn't, this won't move me either. I'd save it to my folder, maybe let the Fall Preview event do some late work, but I'm not clicking "Book" twice on the same pitch in the same week.

## What's working

- **Subject earns the open.** "FREE" upfront, no burying — I read it at inbox speed and immediately knew this wasn't another percentage-off blast. Concrete in a way that CB2's 14-character all-caps "NEW COLLECTION" absolutely is not.
- **Hero CTA is unambiguous.** "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" is a verb and a noun. That's the whole job. Compare Wayfair's "SHOP ALL KITCHEN MATS" — this one tells me what I'm actually committing to.
- **Fall Preview event adds real temporal structure.** Sunday, 11AM, RSVP TODAY — that's a credible deadline with a specific mechanic. Article's July 10 entryway editorial was cleaner storytelling, but it had zero urgency. This email has both.
- **"Recommended for You" earns its placement.** Beds, nightstands, bedding — that's the right category for where my money is going. Whoever seeded this from browse history got it right.
- **10% rewards callout is present.** I'm always doing the rewards math and the Pottery Barn Key mention gives me something to factor against my card's return.

## What's weak

- **Four days ago.** The July 10 design services email already made this pitch. Serving the same concept within a week signals they don't know I saw the first one — exactly the "stranger" treatment I resent from brands I've been shopping for years.
- **Two emails in one day.** The cadence math is simple: "UP TO 70% OFF" this morning, "FREE design help" this afternoon. Even when the second email is better, the second email in a day always loses something.
- **The app download block is dead weight.** A two-button App Store callout eating a quarter of the real estate below the fold. I know the app exists. Everyone on this list knows the app exists.
- **No new hook beyond the event.** If the Fall Preview is the actual news, it should be the subject. Right now it's buried mid-email as an afterthought to a concept I already processed.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Fall Preview event — it's the actual new information.** The appointment offer is evergreen; the Sunday in-store event is time-bounded and specific. Invert the hierarchy so the event is the hero and the appointment booking is the secondary CTA.
- 2. **Cut the app download block, replace with one line of social proof.** "Over X consultations booked this season" or a brief testimonial from a design session would do more to make the appointment feel like a real, active service than an iOS badge.
- 3. **Batch the sends.** A sale email and a service email going out the same day to the same list is a scheduling failure, not an editorial one. One strong email beats two competing ones.
- 4. **Give the appointment CTA a soft deadline.** Even "Available before Fall Preview weekend" adds friction-reducing urgency without being dishonest.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free preview: Pottery Barn's Fall Collection — this Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your free design consult + Fall Preview access`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `In-store Sunday 11AM — plus book a no-cost design session before the season turns`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New fall furniture, free expert help, and 10% back — three reasons to open`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Design Services" Email (2026-07-14)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has one template assembly defect (PHP server directives embedded in a CSS `<style>` block), two broken assets, two HTTP-only image sources, and pervasive missing alt text across ~30 images. Authentication and one-click unsubscribe headers are unverified at the relay layer.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Broken link — commented-out block (low severity)**
The credit card CTA (`https://www.potterybarn.com/pages/credit-card/?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB...`) returned 403. The link exists inside a commented-out `<!-- ... -->` HTML block and does not render to subscribers. The QA scanner extracted it through the comment. The 403 is worth noting if this block is ever uncommented.

**Broken image asset (real, rendering impact)**
`https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` returned 403. This is a cross-brand West Elm CDN asset embedded in a Pottery Barn send. It will render as a broken image in any client that does not suppress missing images silently.

**25 tracking links skipped**
All click links route through `click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=...`. Final destination URLs cannot be verified from HTML alone; downstream link health depends on ESP redirect tables.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP directives inside `<style>` block — template assembly defect**
The following server-side PHP code appears verbatim inside a `<style type="text/css">` block:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are HTTP response header directives from a dynamic image-serving script that were accidentally included in the email template. Email clients treat them as invalid CSS and ignore them, but their presence confirms a template stitching bug. They have no runtime effect but indicate the template pipeline is pulling in fragments from incompatible sources.

**HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources — may be blocked**
Two footer images use `http://` and will be blocked or flagged by clients enforcing HTTPS (Gmail, Outlook.com):
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

**Pervasive missing alt text**
30 images have no `alt` attribute or an empty one. This includes all primary content slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), all product images (curtain rod, bed, towels, quilt, etc.), the header logo, footer slices, and tracking pixels. Clients with images disabled (a significant share of B2C email opens) will render a blank email with no text fallback.

**Responsive breakpoint coverage**
Two separate `@media` breakpoints are present (`max-width: 480px` and `max-width: 650px`). The 480px block uses the XHTML attribute selector form (`table[class="wrapper"]`) while the 650px block uses standard class selectors. This is intentional for older clients but means the two blocks target different client sets — verify the 650px block renders correctly in Outlook clients that strip `<style>` entirely.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unfired merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `%FIRST_NAME%`) found in the visible HTML. Recipient email address is correctly resolved and embedded in tracking pixels:
- `sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` (open-pixel URL)
- `?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` (core email tracking)

No personalization issues found.

---

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header absent**
The `List-Unsubscribe` mail header was not captured by the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Yahoo have required this header for bulk senders (>5k/day) since February 2024. If absent at send time (not just at relay capture), this will suppress inbox placement and one-click unsubscribe UI in Gmail.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post header absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe per RFC 8058 is not confirmed. Required alongside `List-Unsubscribe` for Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender compliance.

**Authentication-Results absent**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unverifiable from the captured headers. Sending domain is `e.potterybarn.com`; authentication posture should be confirmed via DKIM signature on the raw message, not relay metadata.

**CAN-SPAM**
Physical mailing address and unsubscribe mechanism are expected in the footer (standard for WSI/Williams-Sonoma sends); not visible in the truncated HTML but not flagged as absent by QA. Assumed present.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All body CTAs route through `click.e.potterybarn.com`, which should append UTM parameters server-side at redirect time. UTM values cannot be confirmed from the HTML source. The commented-out credit card link uses `cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember` and `external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner` — these are Williams-Sonoma internal tracking params, not standard UTM. If this block is ever activated, confirm whether GA4 or the site's analytics layer reads `cm_ite` correctly or whether standard `utm_source/utm_medium/utm_campaign` are also needed.

No UTM parameters are hardcoded on any live href in the visible HTML — all tracking passes through the click-redirect layer.
