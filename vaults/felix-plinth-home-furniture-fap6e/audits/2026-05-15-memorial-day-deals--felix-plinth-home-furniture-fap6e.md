# 🔴 ⚪ 🔵 MEMORIAL DAY DEALS 🔴 ⚪ 🔵
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- Wayfair's been in my inbox constantly this week — this is the fifth email in three days, and I counted. Tuesday was the outdoor dining play I actually liked, Wednesday was wall decor, Thursday had deck boxes and serving bowls, and now today it's the full Memorial Day cannon. At this pace, Wayfair is running the same playbook Crate & Kids ran on me last week: two emails in 24 hours and I was already composing the unsubscribe in my head.
- What I see when I open this: red-white-blue emoji bunting on the subject, then inside it's a solid grid of discount callouts stacked top to bottom — 70% off, 55% off, 50% off, 40% off, repeat — with a section labeled "MEMORIAL DAY EXCLUSIVE doorbuster deals" that at least *tries* to feel curated. - I would not click "shop all sales." Mark as read.

## What's working

- The "MEMORIAL DAY EXCLUSIVE doorbuster deals" banner with its patriotic treatment gives me a reason to believe there's a hard deadline, not just another always-on promo.
- "Picked just for you" with visible star ratings is the most human part of this email — Wayfair is pulling from my browse history and surfacing reviewed products, which is exactly the hook.
- The 70% OFF and 50% OFF tiles at the top are big, legible, and render clean — no broken images or overlapping text anywhere visible.
- Wayfair Rewards and Flexible Financing in the footer are genuinely relevant to me as someone making large-ticket renovation purchases across two properties right now.
- Timing is credible — it's May 15, Memorial Day is 10 days out, this isn't a stale offer.

## What's weak

- Six emojis in the subject line is a 2019 open-rate hack that in 2026 reads as spam-signal noise. One flag emoji, tops.
- There is no single hero. The entire email is discount tiles — 70%, 55%, 50%, 40%, doorbuster, more discounts — and my eye has nowhere to land. Pottery Barn's most recent send (the "Memorial Day Sale STARTS NOW" email I scored 9/10) led with a single room scene tied to one offer. This is the opposite of that.
- "Shop all sales" is the laziest CTA in email marketing. Send me to a curated Memorial Day landing page or — better — to the outdoor furniture section filtered to my browse history.
- Fifth email in three days. This is the cadence that makes me start treating a brand like a flyer brand instead of a catalog brand I respect.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with a single hero product or room scene.** Pull whatever my browsing says I'm most likely to convert on — outdoor dining, a sectional, a specific case piece — and open with that room photo and ONE offer. The tile grid lives below the fold.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject line.** The tricolor emoji chain is cute for one scroll and then it's noise. Use one flag emoji maximum and say something specific.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Memorial Day Doorbuster: up to 70% off outdoor dining`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Memorial Day picks — deals end Monday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Exclusive doorbuster pricing ends Memorial Day — your saved items included`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Picked for you: dining, seating, and storage at up to 70% off`
- 3. **Collapse the discount grid to three callouts.** 70% is the headline — own it. Two supporting tiles (50%, 40%) live below. The rest moves to the landing page.
- 4. **Replace "shop all sales" with a category CTA** — "Shop Outdoor Dining" or "See My Picks" — linked to a filtered URL, not the homepage.
- 5. **Throttle the cadence.** Two sends maximum for a Memorial Day week: preview mid-week, reminder Friday before the long weekend. Five sends in three days is the fast lane to the Promotions tab.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Web font — missing `woff2` format:**
```css
@font-face {
  font-family: "Sofia";
  src: url(...sofia_reg.woff) format("woff");  /* woff2 absent */
  mso-font-alt: "Arial"
}
```
Only `woff` is declared. `woff2` (supported by Gmail/Apple Mail/modern clients) offers ~30% better compression. Without it, clients that support `woff2` still fall back to system fonts unnecessarily. Add `woff2` as the first format source.

**Aggressive word-break on product names (mobile):**
```css
.ProductRec-name { word-break: break-all; overflow: hidden; height: 48px !important }
```
`word-break: break-all` splits at any character boundary, including mid-word. Use `overflow-wrap: break-word` instead.

**Fixed heights on mobile product cells — text clipping risk:**
```css
.ProductRec-name      { height: 48px }
.ProductRec-price     { height: 40px }
.ProductRec-salesCopy { height: 28px }
```
Any product name, price, or promotional copy that exceeds these fixed dimensions will be silently clipped with no visual indicator. Replace with `min-height`.

**Subject line emoji — rendering risk:**
`🔴 ⚪ 🔵 MEMORIAL DAY DEALS 🔴 ⚪ 🔵` — 6 emoji codepoints. These render as replacement characters (□) in some enterprise environments (Exchange on older Windows, Lotus Notes) and some Android email clients. Preheader/snippet text impact should also be verified.
