# Free shipping starts today! Hurry, just two days to shop
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-18**
## Executive summary

- This is the sixth Crate & Barrel send in two days, and the first with a real offer. The Dutch ovens in the hero are gorgeous, and "free shipping on orders over $99" is something I can act on. It still reads like a sorry-about-the-nursery email. I'm two renovations deep, and a $99 threshold is easy to hit when I'm ordering for two houses.
- Then it falls apart below the fold. After the hero I get a cranberry mocktail, six glasses and bowls at $5 to $13 each, a credit card pitch, an app download and a design appointment. The free shipping offer has nothing to attach to. I wanted furniture, lighting and rugs, and the only things I could add to a cart are juice glasses.
- Would I click? Maybe "Shop Bedding," since I'm furnishing a second home. Would I forward it? No. I'd save it for the Dutch oven photo and move on.

## What's working

- The hero's stacked enameled pots in slate, sage and cream is the Pinterest-folder shot I like. Crate & Barrel's visual taste is still better than Wayfair's.
- "2 days only! free shipping on orders over $99!" is a clear headline with a real deadline and a low threshold.
- The six-link nav (Tabletop, Kitchen, Decor, Bedding, Halloween, Thanksgiving) lets me skip straight to what I want.
- The "Shop All Free Shipping" button is a distinct filled pill under the hero.
- The 10% back in Reward Dollars card block gives me something to run through my rewards math.

## What's weak

- The fine-print line, "Standard shipping; exclusions apply," sits right under the headline and is small and grey. For furniture buyers, "exclusions" means the sofa probably isn't covered. That undercuts the "honest offer" feel.
- The product grid is $5 to $13 glassware and bowls. No furniture, lighting or rugs. Free shipping on a $7.95 tumbler is not exciting.
- The mocktail block is a lifestyle detour. "Shop Aarke" and "Watch on IG" are two competing CTAs for a carbonator I didn't ask about.
- The bottom half stacks four asks: the credit card, the app, a design appointment and a store locator. It's too much after a promo.
- Halloween and Thanksgiving sit in the nav next to the offer. With Halloween nearly over, it feels like leftover seasonal filler.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put one hero product with real shipping pain in the offer block, such as a sofa, rug or lamp. Show a "ships free" tag so the offer feels like it covers furniture.
- 2. Spell out what's excluded in the headline area. A short "furniture ships free over $99" or "most furniture excluded" is better than a vague "exclusions apply."
- 3. Cut the mocktail block, or give it a single CTA.
- 4. Move the credit card, app and design appointment blocks into one footer module.
- 5. Pick up the "Free shipping starts today" language in the preheader so the email reads as one thought.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free shipping over $99, ends Monday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `2 days: free shipping on bedding, rugs & decor`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dutch ovens, glassware, bedding and more. Exclusions apply, see details.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free shipping on orders $99+ through Monday night.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's HTML source is mostly sound, but it has 27 images flagged for missing alt text, a viewport tag that blocks pinch-zoom, and template leftovers in the `<head>`. The QA run reports 57% pass, 0 errors and 3 warnings. The header warnings look like relay artifacts rather than sender faults. The HTML was truncated, so the body, footer and link `href`s were not available to check.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Not verified:** 60 tracking links were skipped by the QA probe (click-redirect domains), so destination status, redirect chains and expiry are unknown.
- **Third-party pixels:** 5 LiveRamp pixels fire on open, one per `n` value (`https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=56aaa278…&n=1` through `n=5`). Five pixels for one pixel ID is redundant, and each adds a third-party request. They carry the same hashed identifier (`s=`) that appears as `mi_u=` on the `mi.crateandbarrel.com` images.
- **Other tracking images:** `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif?mi_u=…` and `https://dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/a024d6c5-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…` are open pixels.
- **Unconfirmed:** whether the `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png` images (7 of them) are pixels or dynamic product images.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** 27 images have no alt attribute. The QA check flags a missing attribute, so I can't tell from this data whether any have `alt=""`.
  - 12 are content images on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/{1,47}/…` (e.g. `bbf6f9d4-…png`, `fc2adc28-…gif`, `1c9c4d87-…jpg`), plus 1 on scene7 (`…CB_Tertiary_VF_Aarke_Social…`).
  - 7 are `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png`.
  - The other 7 are tracking pixels (5 `rlcdn`, `o.gif`, `dv…`) that should carry `alt=""`.
  - The content images are a real accessibility and blocked-image problem, and any with embedded text carry that text only in the image.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">`. `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-zoom, which fails WCAG 1.4.4.
- **Text resize blocked:** `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` appears on `*{…}` and on `body`. `*{line-height:100%}` also forces tight line spacing.
- **Dead CSS rule:** `@media (min-width:768px){img{max-width:600px}}` is overridden by the later, equal-specificity `@media (min-width:640px){img{max-width:640px}}`, which also matches at 768px and up. The 600px rule never takes effect.
- **Invalid markup:** `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` has an unescaped `&`, which is invalid under the XHTML 1.0 doctype. The title is also generic and doesn't match the subject line.
- **Duplicate meta tags:** two charset declarations and three or more overlapping `format-detection` metas.
- **Template leftover:** the comment `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` was shipped (and misspelled).
- **Positive:** `lang="en"` is set and the VML/Office namespaces and MSO conditionals are present.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens appear in the visible source, and the `mi_u`/`mi_cid`/`mi_mid` params are populated. The truncated body wasn't checked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Unconfirmed:** `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) and `Authentication-Results` were not found. The QA tool itself says AgentMail may strip these, so this is a capture gap, not a confirmed sender fault. The email came from `mail.crateandbarrel.com`, a dedicated sending subdomain, and a raw `.eml` is needed to check SPF/DKIM/DMARC.
- **Not assessable:** the footer (unsubscribe link, physical address) was cut off by truncation.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. All links go through click-tracking wrappers that were skipped, so final URLs, UTM params and landing-page alignment couldn't be checked. No issue is confirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to the 13 content images and the 7 `mi…/rp` images (once confirmed as content). Set `alt=""` on the 7 tracking pixels.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` and the `text-size-adjust:none` rules, or confine them to the Outlook/iOS fixes that need them.
3. Escape the title as `Crate &amp; Barrel` and ideally make it match the subject. Remove the duplicate metas and the template comment, and fix the conflicting `img max-width` queries.
4. Check whether the five `rlcdn` pixels can be reduced to one, and confirm the `mi…/rp` assets are not pixels.
5. Re-run the QA on the raw `.eml` with full headers and untruncated HTML. Then verify the unsubscribe link and header, SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment, the postal address, and each tracked link's destination and UTM params.
