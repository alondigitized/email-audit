# Spaces that make learning fun →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-01**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so this is the third Crate & Kids email in my inbox this week if I'm counting right — I already sat through the nursery bait-and-switch ("Shop furniture you'll love now & forever" turning into cribs and gliders) and the backpack drop at "starting at $39." Now it's back-to-school study nooks, organized by age bracket like a Montessori catalog. I don't have kids, so structurally none of this is for me, but I'll say the "play spaces / book nooks / study spaces" segmentation by age (1-3, 4-6, 6+) is at least a smart way to not waste my scroll if I did have a specific kid in mind.
- The layout is clean — proper hero, then repeating product-shot-plus-CTA blocks, which is more disciplined than the last nursery email that felt like five different emails stapled together. But by the time I hit "we love these for you" with a stroller, a backpack, AND a diaper bag reappearing again, my brain just files this under "more Crate & Kids inventory dump" rather than a distinct campaign. Pottery Barn's "on sale & shipping free" send I gave an 8 this week did the opposite — one offer, one visual promise, done. This one's well-built but exhausting in aggregate.
- Not clicking, not forwarding — I'd mark as read and move on. If I had a kid starting school this fall, sure, maybe I'd skim for the desk. As a childless renovator mid-project on two houses, this is pure noise for me.

## What's working

- The age-segmented layout (1-3, 4-6, 6+) is a genuinely useful organizing principle if you're shopping for a specific kid — better than a flat grid.
- "back-to-school sale up to 30% off" is right in the hero, so the offer isn't buried.
- Product photography is consistent and on-brand — same styled-room aesthetic Crate & Barrel uses in their furniture sends, so the family resemblance is intact.
- The $3,000 Design Desk sweepstakes is a distinct, memorable hook I haven't seen in the last few Crate & Kids sends — that's new creative, not a rerun.

## What's weak

- I don't have kids — zero demographic match, so the entire email is dead weight in my inbox, and that's not a knock on execution, just on list targeting.
- This is my third Crate & Kids touch this week (nursery furniture, backpacks, now study spaces) and the stroller/backpack/diaper bag trio shows up again at the bottom — it's starting to feel like one long email chopped into three sends.
- Zero color-palette or swatch-and-sample content, which is the one thing that would actually pull a renovator like me in — I'm mid-project on two houses and would engage with fabric/finish options way faster than a kids' desk.
- The card offer ("the card you love just got better") and app download block at the very bottom are pure filler by the time I've scrolled this far — nobody's still reading at that point.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this out of my inbox entirely — if Crate & Barrel/Kids knows I've never clicked a kids' item across three sends, suppress me from the Crate & Kids stream and put me back in core Crate & Barrel furniture only. That's the highest-leverage fix, full stop.
- 2. Cut the redundant "we love these for you" cross-sell block — I just saw the stroller and backpack in a dedicated email days ago; repeating them here reads as inventory recycling, not curation.
- 3. Lead with the sweepstakes higher up if it's meant to drive urgency — right now it's buried two-thirds down, under three separate age-segment CTAs, and would get more clicks near the top.
- 4. For subscribers like me who skew renovation/furniture over kids' decor, test surfacing a swatch/finish-matching angle even in the kids' vertical — "match your kid's desk to your home office" is a bridge I'd actually click.
