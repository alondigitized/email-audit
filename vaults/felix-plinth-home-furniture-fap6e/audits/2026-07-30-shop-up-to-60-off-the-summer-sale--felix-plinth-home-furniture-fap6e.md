# Shop up to 60% off the Summer Sale →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so my first reaction was déjà vu — this is the exact same hero shot I already screenshotted this morning: the round mirror hanging over the Campagna Rattan and Natural Oak dresser, "Sale $1,500 Reg. $1,999." Different subject line ("Shop up to 60% off" vs. yesterday's "only for a limited time"), same creative. That's not a fresh send, that's Crate & Barrel testing subject lines on me and hoping I don't notice. I noticed.
- Past the déjà vu, this is a long scroll — furniture sale, outdoor sale, new arrivals banner, DesignDesk sweepstakes, a six-item "we love these for you" grid, a Crate Card pitch, an app download pitch, another DesignDesk pitch, then footer. That's basically four separate emails stapled together. The DesignDesk $5,000 sweepstakes is the one thing that actually made me pause — I've got two houses going at once and a free design consult is genuinely useful to me, not just filler.
- Would I click? Probably, on "Shop Furniture Sale" specifically, since that dresser is my exact price point and material palette. Would I forward it? No — nothing here is a "hey look at this" moment for a friend, it's just Tuesday's sale email. Read, maybe click through on the dresser, archive.

## What's working

- The hero image is legitimately my taste — rattan/oak dresser, round mirror, warm neutral palette. Same one that stopped my scroll yesterday, so at least the creative team knows what works on people like me.
- Actual dollar comparison on the hero item ($1,500 vs. $1,999) instead of just a vague percentage — I can do that math instantly.
- The "we love these for you" grid has real dimensions and wood/finish callouts (e.g., "82"-118" Weathered Light Brown Solid Wood Extendable Dining Table") — that's the product detail I actually shop by.
- DesignDesk sweepstakes + free appointment is a genuinely relevant hook for someone renovating two homes right now, unlike Crate & Kids emails I keep getting that assume I have a toddler.

## What's weak

- Same hero creative as today's earlier send — this reads like a resend with a swapped subject line, not a new deal. If I'm subscribed and paying attention (I am), that's annoying, not clever.
- Headline says "up to 60% off" but the actual hero item is 25% off ($1,500/$1,999) — that's the bait-and-switch math CB2 and Article have both been guilty of this month too. Show me the 60% item if you're leading with it.
- Too many competing CTAs — Shop Now, Shop Furniture Sale, Shop All Sale, Shop New Arrivals, Book Your Free Appointment, Learn More, Download Now, Book a Free Appointment again. That's Wayfair-level CTA sprawl ("fifteen things before I've finished my coffee") in a brand that usually shows more restraint.
- The Crate Card pitch and app download pitch both show up mid-scroll with zero context for whether I'm already a cardholder or app user — feels like a generic template, not personalization.

## Recommendations

- 1. Don't resend the same hero creative same-day under a different subject — if it's the same offer, don't make me feel like I'm getting spammed twice by one brand in one morning.
- 2. Fix the headline math — either lead with a 60%-off item in the hero slot, or change the headline to match the 25% math actually shown.
- 3. Cut the CTA count in half. Pick one primary action (Shop Furniture Sale) and let DesignDesk be the single secondary ask — right now nothing reads as "the" thing to do.
- 4. Move the DesignDesk $5,000 sweepstakes higher — that's the strongest hook for a renovation-stage buyer like me and it's buried past two sale sections.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your Campagna dresser just dropped to $1,500`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New markdowns: furniture, rugs, lighting up to 60% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: enter to win $5,000 toward your reno with The Design Desk`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `1,500+ new heritage-inspired pieces just landed`
