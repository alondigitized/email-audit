- The "American craftsmanship" section with North Carolina sourcing is a real trust signal — more substantive than anything Wayfair's cutting-board blast or Article's storage email offered.
- Coffee table row gives me an actual price anchor ("starting at $500") — that's the kind of concrete number I look for before I click through.
- Venus Williams collab is a legitimate scroll-stopper — distinct from the generic lifestyle shots everywhere else in this email.
- Clean, consistent product photography throughout — no render bugs, no broken images, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- No single hero offer — this is six or seven mini-campaigns stapled together (sofas, chairs, tables, consoles, rugs, lighting/pillows/decor, Venus Williams, rewards, app). Compare to the actual Oceanside sends from 2026-09-21 — those had one sofa, one price, one CTA. This buries it.
- Zero discount or percentage-off anywhere in this entire email — just "shop" CTAs everywhere. CB2's "Now & Future Classics on Sale" at least led with a sale peg.
- By the time I hit the Venus Williams section I'd already scrolled past 5 "Shop X" buttons — CTA fatigue sets in hard.
- Rewards blurb ("5% back in Reward Dollars or 30% off a purchase") is crammed at the very bottom after a dozen product tiles — that's a real member benefit and it's buried where nobody scrolls to it.

- 1. Pick ONE hero — either lead with the craftsmanship/sourcing story or one flagship item, not both plus five sub-categories. The Oceanside sofa emails proved C&B can do a focused send; this reverts to catalog-dump mode.
- 2. Move the Reward Dollars / 30%-off member benefit up near the top — that's the actual incentive that gets me to click, not "shop accent chairs."
- 3. Add a real price or % off to at least the hero module — right now the only number in the whole email is the $500 coffee table starting price.
- 4. Cut the email in half — accent chairs, media consoles, and "these make your space feel finished" (lighting/pillows/decor/mirrors) could be a separate send instead of tacked onto an already-long scroll.
- 5. Move the Venus Williams collab higher — it's the most distinctive content in the email and it's stranded near the bottom.
