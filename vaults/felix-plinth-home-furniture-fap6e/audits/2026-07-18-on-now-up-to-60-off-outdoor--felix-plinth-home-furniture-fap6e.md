# ON NOW! Up to 60% off outdoor!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- Sixteen. That's where I am with the Crate & Barrel family after this one lands, and I already feel the reflexive eye-roll loading before I even register what the email is about. But here's the thing — it snapped me back. Outdoor dining chairs for the second home have been sitting in my browser tabs for six weeks, and 60% off outdoor is a real offer, not a lifestyle content piece or a third iteration of a giveaway I didn't enter the first two times today. The hero shot of that dark outdoor dining set is exactly the aesthetic I've been circling. Editorial, clean, aspirational without being ridiculous.
- But I cannot let the Crate & Barrel family off the hook on cadence. The CB Kids giveaway landed twice this morning — "Your chance to win $4,199" and then "Enter to win $4,199" as if I hadn't seen the first one — and now the mothership is in with a separate "SALE" sub-brand sender name. I see what they're doing. I don't love it. - I'd click — the outdoor category is live, the offer is real, the furniture in the hero is what I'm looking for. But I'm clicking because the product is right, not because this email earned it. Mark as read and shop, not forward to anyone.

## What's working

- **The offer is real and timely** — 60% off outdoor when I have a deck to furnish at the second home is not a coincidence, it's the right email at the right time
- **The hero image is editorial quality** — dark outdoor dining set, staged on a proper deck, immediately communicates aesthetic and product category without confusion
- **"shop now" beats "Discover"** — at least the CTA is an action; CB2's "APRES POOL" email this week made me click through to a homepage, which is worse
- **Seasonal alignment is solid** — outdoor clearance in July makes obvious sense; no stale February promo language in a summer send
- **The "for you" personalized section** acknowledges I have purchase history, which is more than the back-to-school CB Kids giveaway sends did with me today

## What's weak

- **"Crate & Barrel SALE" is a ghost sub-brand** — splitting off a "SALE" sender is a transparent inbox-frequency hack; I know Crate & Barrel can't send more than X emails per week, so they invented a sibling to sidestep it
- **The giveaway module kills momentum** — I don't want to enter a $1,000 backyard grillout contest, I want to see what's 60% off; after two CB Kids giveaway emails today this mechanic reads as filler not feature
- **"ON NOW!" as a subject opener** is the same register as CB2's all-caps "APRES POOL" — one exclamation mark in a subject reads like a shout, and this already starts with a two-word all-caps phrase
- **No material detail anywhere in the hero** — outdoor furniture requires material-level trust (aluminum vs. rust-prone steel, synthetic vs. teak) and neither the image nor the headline gives me anything; Wayfair's wall art clearance email at least called out specific product types
- **No hard close date** — "ON NOW!" implies urgency without delivering it; Pottery Barn's 70% off email had a visible deadline, which actually pushed me toward a click

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop the "SALE" sub-brand sender** — send from "Crate & Barrel" and own the cadence instead of masking it behind a secondary from-name; the split is obvious and makes the brand feel less premium
- 2. **Add a sale end date in the subject and hero** — "ends Sunday" or "through July 20" turns "ON NOW!" from a shout into a credible deadline; that's the single highest-lift change available here
- 3. **Move the giveaway below the fold** — the $1,000 contest is fine as a secondary engagement hook, but it's eating prime real estate above the scroll that should belong to the 60% off products
- 4. **Surface one material callout in the hero** — a one-line badge like "All-weather aluminum · Teak · Sunbrella fabric" addresses the biggest outdoor furniture objection without touching the layout
- 5. **Cut or compress "The Backyard Spot" content module** — editorial content is the Article model, and Article does it better by making it a full editorial moment; here it reads like a newsletter buried inside a sale email and dilutes both

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Conflicting `text-size-adjust` rules:**
- `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` (universal selector, first `<style>` block)
- `body{-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%}` (second `<style>` block, also in `html` rule)
- Universal `none` will win in most parsers due to specificity tie-breaking on cascade order, suppressing user-agent text scaling in iOS Mail.

**Competing desktop image max-width breakpoints:**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
At ≥768px, both rules match. The 768px rule is declared last and wins, capping images at 600px on desktop — contradicting the 640px container expectation set elsewhere. If the email container is 600px, this is harmless; if 640px, images will be clipped.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Disables user zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 violation for subscribers who rely on pinch-to-zoom.

**`line-height: 100%` on `*`:**
Universal `line-height: 100%` applied to all elements. This is a known cause of clipped descenders and tight line spacing in Gmail and Apple Mail, particularly for multi-line text blocks.

**Duplicate CSS rule blocks:**
The following rules are declared in two separate `<style>` blocks, which is redundant and increases payload size:
- `.ReadMsgBody { width: 100%; }` — appears twice
- `.ExternalClass * { line-height: 100%; }` — appears twice
- `body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; margin: 0; }` — appears twice
- `table td { border-collapse: collapse; }` — appears three times

**Missing preheader text:** Not visible in provided source. If absent from the body, inbox preview will render the first visible HTML content (often navigation or legal copy) as the preheader.
