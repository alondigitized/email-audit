# Their room isn’t complete without…
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-16**
## Executive summary

- Third Crate & Kids email in two days, and this one's the worst offender yet — it's not even pretending to be about one thing. It's a kitchen-sink scroll: decor, rugs, lighting, "cozy kids bedding," "soft baby bedding," backpack bundles, a credit card pitch, and a Design Desk appointment plug, all stacked in one endless email. I don't have kids, I don't have a nursery, and I definitely don't need a "soft, new baby bedding" section with a photo of an actual infant. Straight to archived, no click.
- The "finishing touches for their dream room" hero at the top almost had me for half a second because it reads generic enough to apply to a guest room, but then it nosedives into cribs and backpacks by the third scroll. Compare that to Crate & Barrel proper's bedroom bestsellers email I looked at yesterday — Coveteur canopy bed, "up to 25% off," at least a real number attached to a real category I'm shopping. This has no percentage, no dollar figure, nothing. Just an endless carousel of "shop now" buttons.
- Not forwarding this to anyone — unless it's to my sister as a "why am I getting this" joke. Marking as read and moving on.

## What's working

- The opening hero shot (folded throws + stool) is neutral enough it could pass for any age room for a second.
- The dresser and rug sections have decent product photography — clean, well-lit, not cluttered.
- Layout itself renders cleanly, no broken images or overlapping text that I can see.

## What's weak

- Baby and tween content mixed into the same send as "modern lighting" and "soft & durable rugs" — this brand can't decide who it's emailing.
- No offer anywhere. Not one percentage, dollar amount, or "free shipping" line in the entire scroll — compare to C&B's own "25% off" bedroom email from yesterday.
- A literal photo of an infant in a crib halfway down. I don't have kids. This is Crate & Kids sending me content that has nothing to do with my life.
- The credit card pitch and "app + chance at $500" section at the bottom feels bolted-on and desperate after nine unrelated product sections.
- Way too long — by the time I'd hit "backpack bundles" I'd already mentally checked out three sections earlier.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop sending me Crate & Kids at all — segment me out based on zero clicks across three sends this week. If C&B can't do that, at minimum split this into single-topic sends (rugs email, lighting email) instead of nine categories in one scroll.
- 2. Lead with an actual offer. Wayfair's "pieces from $30" cookout email and CB2's "up to 60% off" clearance both gave me a number in the first screen — this gave me nothing.
- 3. Cut the length by 70%. Nobody scrolls past baby bedding to find "we love these for you" gift-guide picks.
- 4. Drop the infant/baby imagery for anyone not already tagged as a parent — it's the single biggest "this isn't for me" signal in the email.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New arrivals for every room, up to 25% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Rugs, lighting, decor — all just landed`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh picks for the room you're working on right now`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `No kids required — just new stuff for your space`
