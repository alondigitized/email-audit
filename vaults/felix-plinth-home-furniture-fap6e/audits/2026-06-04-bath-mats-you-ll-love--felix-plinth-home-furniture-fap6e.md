# BATH MATS You'll Love!!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-five. That's the Wayfair count now, and this one lands on the same day as the outdoor rugs send — two emails before lunch on June 4th alone. My tolerance is running on fumes.
- I opened it. Bath mats are legitimately on my list for both houses, so "BATH MATS You'll Love!!" — despite the ALL CAPS scream and the double exclamation marks — got a click from me. What I found inside is the problem: the hero section is "Talk of the Town," which reads more like a generic lifestyle reel than a curated bath mat edit. Then the "We Love These for You" grid drops me into what looks like a mix of dressers and bed frames. I had to hunt for anything that resembled a bath mat. That's a broken promise.
- I would not click through. I would not forward this. I'd mark it read, note that Wayfair still doesn't know how to send a focused category email, and compare it unfavorably to the Pottery Barn "Chris Loves Julia" send from earlier this week, which showed me a coherent visual world and didn't make me search for the thing the subject line promised.

## What's working

- **Sender recognition is instant** — Wayfair's purple header with the logo and nav (Shop Info, My Account, New Arrivals) loads without a hitch. No broken images, no overlapping text.
- **Star ratings in the product grid** — Social proof is visible on the product tiles. That's the one trust signal I'd expect and they delivered it.
- **"Shop All Our Favorites" button is visible and clear** — Not great copy, but at least there's a primary CTA with a button I can tap.
- **Wayfair Rewards / perks callout at the bottom** — I'm a member, so seeing the rewards ecosystem flagged is mildly useful, even buried.
- **Subject line is short** — 23 characters, renders clean on mobile, no truncation.

## What's weak

- **"Talk of the Town" hero is doing nothing** — The hero image shows a lifestyle scene that reads more outdoor-adjacent than bath-anything. Outdoor rugs hit my inbox two sends ago; this feels like recycled creative with a different subject line.
- **The product grid is category soup** — "We Love These for You" shows what looks like dressers and bed frames alongside anything bath-adjacent. If I came for bath mats, this grid makes me feel tricked.
- **ALL CAPS "BATH MATS" + double exclamation marks** — This is the kind of subject line that triggers my spam radar. CB2 managed to announce the Todd Snyder collab drop with far more restraint and still felt urgent.
- **Zero sizing or spec info visible** — Bath mat sizing is everything. A 17"×24" and a 24"×36" are completely different purchase decisions. Not a single dimension in the grid.
- **No offer** — No percentage off, no free shipping threshold, nothing. Article managed to pull me into a kitchen island email on brand voice alone; this has neither voice nor price hook.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero product and commit.** Show me one bath mat — styled in an actual bathroom, with a rug pulled from a real collection — not a lifestyle deck scene. Pottery Barn's Julia Berolzheimer edit did exactly this: one visual world, one mood, coherent from hero to grid.
- 2. **Purge the off-category products from the grid.** If your subject says bath mats, every tile should be a bath mat variant. A dresser in a bath mat email destroys trust.
- 3. **Add a size filter or spec callout.** A simple "Available in 3 sizes" or "17"×24" | 20"×32" | 24"×36"" line under each product is the kind of friction-reducer that turns a browse into a cart add.
- 4. **Kill the double exclamation marks.** Full stop.
- 5. **Add a price anchor.** Even "from $19" in the subhead gives me a reason to click instead of filing it.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your bathroom's missing this (bath mats, curated)`
