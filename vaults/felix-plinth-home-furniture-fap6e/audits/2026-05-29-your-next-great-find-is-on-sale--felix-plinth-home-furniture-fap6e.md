# Your next great find is ON SALE
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-four. I know because I counted, and this is the third Pottery Barn email today alone — I already reviewed the free design consultation send and the rug color one before lunch. So "Your next great find is ON SALE" has to fight its way through that inbox fog the moment it lands, and the subject alone doesn't do it. It's the marketing equivalent of a shrug.
- That said — I actually opened the email and it's not bad inside. The hero image is doing something right: that cognac leather armchair in a bright, airy room is exactly the palette I've been pulling for the second home. The category grid (Furniture, Tabletop, Bedding, Bath, Outdoor, Pillows & Décor) is clean and scannable, and the "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" section below does make me pause because I want to see if PB's algorithm has actually figured me out. The "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" callout and the Affirm mention near the footer are both visible, which helps for the big-ticket furniture line items I'm sitting on.
- I'd scroll, honestly. I might click into the recommended section. I wouldn't forward this — there's nothing here a friend would find funny or useful. It's a competent sale email from a brand I trust, buried under two of its siblings from the same morning.

## What's working

- **Hero image aesthetics are on point** — that warm terracotta/cognac leather chair reads like something I'd actually spec for the second home; PB's eye for room staging beats Wayfair's cluttered product-dump layouts every time.
- **Category navigation tiles are well-organized** — Furniture, Tabletop, Bedding, Bath, Outdoor, Pillows & Décor in one clean row lets me self-sort without hunting; Article's "Complete your home" email does this too, but PB's visual execution is cleaner.
- **"Recommended Just for You" section** — feels genuinely personalized even if it's just algorithmic; I at least check whether the system knows me.
- **Loyalty and financing both visible** — the 10% Rewards callout and Affirm logo near the footer are relevant signals; I'm running rewards math every time I buy furniture and it's good that both are present.
- **No render bugs** — images load, text is legible, layout holds together; CB2's "These colors never go unnoticed" send from this week had tighter art direction but this is cleaner than Crate & Barrel's product-anniversary email which felt weirdly boxy.

## What's weak

- **No specific percentage off anywhere visible in the hero** — "ON SALE" is doing zero lifting compared to PB's own "Outdoor sale ⛱️ UP TO 50% OFF" from yesterday, which told me exactly what I'd save; this one makes me do work.
- **Third email today from this brand** — the free design services email and the rug email already hit this morning; I'm in inbox fatigue and this generic subject isn't distinguishing itself from the pile.
- **"RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" products aren't readable at this resolution** — the section exists but I can't make out product names, prices, or whether anything in there matches what I actually need; it's a personalization promise the layout doesn't fully cash.
- **Free Design Services reappears** — the callout block mid-email was the entire *point* of the morning send; seeing it again here makes the earlier email feel like a teaser for this one, which wasn't communicated, and now both feel redundant.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the percentage in the hero** — "Up to 40% off furniture, décor & more" in the hero headline, full stop; the outdoor sale email from yesterday showed how much that one number changes the gravity of the send.
- 2. **Kill the Free Design Services block here** — you sent that email this morning; repromoting it in the same-day general sale send dilutes both messages; either sequence them or collapse into one.
- 3. **Make "Recommended Just for You" the primary story** — lead with the personalized picks, not a generic hero; that's the only hook that's differentiated from a Wayfair blast.
- 4. **Subject needs a number** — anything beats the current vagueness.
- 5. **Add a deadline** — even "this week only" would help; Article ran their Memorial Day sale with clear end dates and it made every email feel more decisive.
