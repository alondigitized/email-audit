# Hours left for Double Rewards & up to 60% off!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-09**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is actually Crate & Barrel proper, not another Crate & Kids nursery blast — that alone gets my attention since I've been auto-scrolling past four or five Crate & Kids "Ends Tonight" emails this week and I don't have kids. The swivel accent chair up top ($750, marked down from $999) is a real product with a real price, sitting in a room shot that actually looks like something I'd stage in the second house. That's more than CB2's generic "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS" banner gave me.
- That said, it's still the same countdown-clock playbook the whole C&B family has run at me nonstop this week — 00:07:07:27, "don't miss out," "hours left." I'm numb to the clock at this point; Article and CB2 ran the identical device on me within days of each other. The "we love these for you" grid at the bottom is the best part of the email — a sofa, a dresser, a dining table, all with visible sale prices — but I have to scroll past two redundant "Double Rewards" restates and a full app-download pitch to get there.
- I'd click through to the accent chair or the dresser, but I'm not forwarding this to anyone — it's competent, not exciting, and I've seen this exact layout from this brand family too many times this month.

## What's working

- Real hero product with a visible price drop ($999 → $750) — more concrete than CB2's vague "classics on sale" header.
- The "we love these for you" grid gives me six actual SKUs with prices, not just a category nav wall.
- Reward card math (10-20% back in Reward Dollars) is spelled out with a date range, not buried fine print.
- Room styling (muted, neutral, the swivel chair) matches my actual renovation aesthetic — better demographic fit than Crate & Kids' nursery palette.

## What's weak

- Countdown clock again — this is the fourth or fifth timer I've seen from this brand family this week (Crate & Kids ran three, CB2 ran one). I don't trust the urgency anymore.
- The email repeats the "Double Rewards is on!" block twice (once up top, once in a dark banner mid-email) — redundant, feels like padding.
- Category button wall (furniture sale, sofas & sectionals, kitchen sale, etc.) pushes the actual hero grid further down — Wayfair's dining-chair email had the same problem, burying the specific product behind a nav dump.
- App-download push with a "$500 chance" sweepstakes tease feels like a bolt-on, unrelated to what I opened this for.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut one of the two "Double Rewards is on!" blocks — the repetition dilutes the offer instead of reinforcing it.
- 2. Move the "we love these for you" product grid up, right after the hero chair — that's the strongest content in the email and it's stuck two-thirds down.
- 3. Drop or shrink the countdown clock — I've seen it from this brand five times this week; a specific end-date line ("Ends Sept 9") does the same job without the fatigue.
- 4. Cut the category-button wall to 3-4 links max instead of 8 — most of that space could go to more hero products.

Content images lacking `alt` degrade the experience for screen-reader users and when images are blocked by default (common in Outlook/Gmail); tracking pixels missing `alt` is low-risk but `alt=""` is still best practice to avoid announcing raw filenames. The HTML uses legacy table-based layout with extensive MSO conditional comments (`<!--[if mso]>`, `<!--[if gte mso 9]>`), Bronto/MI vendor boilerplate (`.ExternalClass`, `.ReadMsgBody`), and duplicated `format-detection` meta tags (three redundant declarations for telephone/address/date instead of one combined value) — functional but bloats `<head>`.
