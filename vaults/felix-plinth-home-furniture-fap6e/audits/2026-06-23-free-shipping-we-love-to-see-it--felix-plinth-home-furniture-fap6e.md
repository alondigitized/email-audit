# FREE SHIPPING. We love to see it 👀
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- Second free shipping email from Crate & Kids today. I know this because I just processed "Everything inside ships for FREE!" from the same sender earlier on June 23rd, and here we are again. The cadence fatigue alone is enough to make my thumb hover over the archive. That said — this one is slightly more interesting in execution. The "We love to see it 👀" copy is clearly chasing a younger, more casual register, and at 36 I'm right on the edge of finding it charming versus eye-roll-worthy. For me it lands on the eye-roll side. The eyes emoji reads Gen Z to me, and I'm not a Gen Z.
- The email itself is built around a grid of children's products — a yellow backpack, nursery bedding, a black stroller, assorted nursery furniture. Clean layout, warm palette, no obvious broken images. This is competent C&B family work. But I'm actively spec-ing bathroom tile and dining chairs right now, and I'm staring at a stroller. That's the mismatch. Crate & Kids has been hitting me with the Finn Collection, the free shipping blasts, and now this — and none of it is connecting to either of the two houses I'm actually trying to furnish.
- I'd mark this read and move on. I would not click. I would definitely not forward — I don't have friends looking for strollers right now, and the email doesn't suggest anything I'd send a "hey check this out" to someone mid-renovation.

## What's working

- **Free shipping offer is impossible to miss.** The hero messaging reads clearly without scrolling — it's the first thing the eye lands on, which is the job.
- **No render bugs.** Clean layout, images loaded, grid is intact. After the volume of C&B family emails this week, at minimum this one didn't break.
- **Product grid is visually cohesive.** The warm/neutral palette across the grid — yellow bag, soft nursery textiles, natural wood tones — feels intentional and on-brand. It doesn't fight itself.
- **Short subject line.** Thirty-five characters clears mobile without truncation. I'll take it.

## What's weak

- **This is the second free shipping email from Crate & Kids on the same day.** "Everything inside ships for FREE!" already landed. When Wayfair does this, at least they're shuffling product categories. This feels like a resend with different subject copy.
- **"We love to see it 👀" is not Millennial voice.** This phrase comes from a very specific Gen Z cultural register. For me it creates distance, not warmth. It reads like a brand trying to sound like their intern.
- **Entire product assortment is children's.** Stroller, nursery furniture, kids bag, children's bedding. I'm in the C&B family because I'm buying dining tables and rugs. Nothing in this visual grid connects to what I'm actually shopping for.
- **No deadline on the shipping offer.** Article's "Bedroom storage essentials" email landed recently and didn't waste CTA real estate either, but at least that email had products I'd put in a room. This one doesn't even tell me when the offer expires.
- **App download section near the footer feels buried and off-topic.** The mobile app promo showing up mid-scroll in a free shipping email is a context mismatch. Save it for a dedicated send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Don't send two free shipping emails in one day from the same brand.** One of these should have been suppressed or the second should have been held 24 hours. No single change in the creative fixes this — it's a scheduling and suppression problem.
- 2. **Replace the emoji-driven casualness with a concrete hook.** The "we love to see it" line is doing nothing but signaling voice. Cut it. Lead with what ships free and why that matters — especially if there's a cart-size threshold.
- 3. **Segment the product grid.** If you know I'm buying home goods (not children's products), show me the nursery-adjacent items that still have adult crossover — baby shower gift items, kids furniture that doubles as keepsakes. Or just don't send the kids email to people who haven't purchased in that category.
- 4. **Add an offer deadline.** CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" email had the same problem — no deadline, so no urgency. Even "this weekend only" changes the read behavior.
- 5. **Kill the app promo in this email.** Separate send, dedicated moment, not buried in a free shipping blast.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta (confirmed)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on iOS/Android. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, AA). Should be removed or set to `maximum-scale=5`.

**Global `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` (confirmed)**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```
Applied to every element via universal selector. Suppresses OS-level accessibility font scaling for all text. Should be scoped to `body` only with value `100%`, not `none`.

**`<title>` tag brand mismatch (confirmed)**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
Email is from *Crate & Kids* (`crateandkids@mail.crateandbarrel.com`); title reflects the parent brand. Screenreaders and some email clients surface this.

**Dark mode forced off (confirmed)**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Explicitly opts out of dark mode in Apple Mail and supported clients. No dark-mode `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block present. May render poorly for ~35–40% of Apple Mail users who use dark mode.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags (confirmed)**
The combined tag (`telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no`) plus three separate individual tags for `date`, `address`, and `telephone` are all present — redundant, though not harmful.

**Conflicting `img` max-width media queries (confirmed)**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
Both fire at ≥768px; the 600px rule (lower specificity, later in cascade) wins on desktop — the 640px rule is functionally dead above 767px. Likely a copy-paste artifact; consolidate to a single rule.

**Mobile fixed-width treatment (confirmed)**
```css
.showmobile { width: 414px !important; }
```
Fixed at 414px against a 460px breakpoint. On 375px-viewport devices (iPhone SE, older models) this forces horizontal scroll within that element class.
