# 🚨 LAST DAY for extra 20% off select styles
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn email number seven — seven — in roughly 96 hours. I didn't count that as a rhetorical flourish; I literally counted from the brand history. "The 4th is gone but our sale is STILL ON" landed yesterday with a little heart emoji. Before that, "Ends tomorrow." Before that, "ONLY 48 HOURS LEFT." And today we're on LAST DAY with a siren. The problem isn't the email — the email is actually fine. It's clean, the category tiles are useful, and there's a "Recommended Just for You" section that at least gestures at personalization. The problem is that "Last Day" stopped meaning anything two emails ago, and Pottery Barn has burned through all of their urgency vocabulary before I've even had coffee.
- That said, if I had somehow missed the last six, this is a competent send. The hero leads with the primary offer, the category tiles give me a fast scan of what's discounted (sofa/chairs at 70%, outdoor at 40%), and the Open Box Outlet section buried below the fold is actually the most interesting part for a renovation project — which tells me the layout buried its best card. Article's July 3rd send hit a cleaner urgency arc in three emails over eight days. Crate & Barrel just emailed me the identical "ENDS TOMORROW" message twice in the same inbox session. Pottery Barn is doing the same thing, just louder.
- I'd click — reluctantly, and only to check whether my wishlisted sectional is included in "select styles" — but I would not forward this to anyone. If you forward a siren-emoji email to a friend, you owe them an explanation.

## What's working

- **Category tiles are genuinely useful.** Up to 70% off Sofas & Chairs, up to 40% off Outdoor — that's a fast, scannable breakdown I can mentally cross-reference against my renovation lists before committing to a scroll.
- **"Recommended Just for You" section.** It exists. Barely visible, but it signals Pottery Barn is at least pretending to know me, which is more than Crate & Barrel's duplicate inbox drop managed this week.
- **Open Box Outlet section.** If I'm buying furniture for a second home that guests will sleep in but not scrutinize, open box pricing is actually relevant to me. Smart category inclusion.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum. Layout holds. That's the floor, but it clears it.

## What's weak

- **"Select styles" qualifier in the subject line.** I've been burned enough times to know this means maybe 40% of what I actually want is in the sale. After seven sends building the offer up, that asterisk has an outsized deflation effect.
- **"Last Day" credibility is gone.** They said "Ends Tomorrow" on July 5th. Then sent "The 4th is gone but our sale is STILL ON" the next day. Now it's Last Day again. Article ran one clean countdown arc. Pottery Barn has been running fake sunsets all week.
- **Hero room scene reads feminine.** Warm neutrals, throw-pillow styling, bedroom linens — not wrong, but it doesn't signal that anyone knows I'm a guy renovating two homes who wants to see a sectional or an outdoor dining set in the hero.
- **The primary CTA "Shop Our Sale" is an invitation to browse, not to buy.** After six prior emails, I need specificity — "Shop Sofas Up to 70% Off" would at least point me somewhere.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Retire the urgency language and switch to a categorical hook.** After seven sends in a week, "LAST DAY" means nothing. "70% off the sofa you've been watching" would cut through. Make the hero do the work the subject line has stopped being able to do.
- 2. **Move Open Box Outlet above the fold.** That section is genuinely differentiated — no competitor is pushing distressed inventory directly to people who are mid-renovation and cost-conscious. It's buried below category tiles that I could find on the homepage.
- 3. **Make the "Recommended Just for You" products visible and clickable at a glance.** Right now it's thumbnail-sized. If you have my browse data, use it — Article does this better on desktop.
- 4. **Drop the 🚨 emoji.** It codes as panic-marketing. A 36-year-old guy doing a renovation doesn't respond to sirens; he responds to precision. The urgency is better served by the words.
- 5. **Add a "check if your item is included" link or wishlist CTA.** I'm on a list. Tell me whether what's on my list is in the sale. That's the click I actually want to make.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CRITICAL — PHP header() calls inside `<style>` block:**

```html
<style>
  ...
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
</style>
```

These are server-side PHP function calls that were not executed — they leaked raw into the HTML. In permissive email clients they are silently ignored; in strict parsers they may corrupt the surrounding CSS block, breaking layout. This is a template build/rendering pipeline failure.

**FAIL — 47 images missing `alt` text.** Every product image, banner, and footer slice has no alt. Affects screen reader users and any inbox with images disabled by default (Gmail, Outlook). Key missing instances:
