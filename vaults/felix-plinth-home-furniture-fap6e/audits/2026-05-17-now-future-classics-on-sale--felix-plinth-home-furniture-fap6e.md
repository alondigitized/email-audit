# NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- CB2 lands in my inbox the same week I've already chewed through five emails from its parent company, Crate & Barrel. My brain is in a permanent C&B family snooze. So the first thing I did was check whether this was actually different enough to justify the open — and honestly? It is, barely. The dark espresso velvet chair in the hero is legitimately the kind of piece I'd send to my wife at 11pm with "thoughts?" But the subject line came in all-caps like a clearance bin announcement, which immediately lowered my expectations before I ever scrolled.
- The "WORTH A DOUBLE TAKE" headline is clever — it's doing real positioning work, suggesting these are investment pieces disguised as a sale. That's the right message for someone who shops CB2 deliberately rather than impulse-buying. The grid of NEW LIVING ROOM / NEW LIGHTING / NEW BEDROOM categories works harder than I expected for a scroll-past email.
- I would not forward this to a friend. I'd probably click the velvet chair link and then get annoyed that the sale price isn't anywhere in the hero. Mark as read with a moderate chance I circle back on my laptop.

## What's working

- **The hero product earns its place.** That espresso velvet chair is exactly the kind of statement piece that differentiates CB2 from the Crate & Barrel mothership — and I'm genuinely in the market for seating for the second home.
- **"WORTH A DOUBLE TAKE" is doing real copy work.** It reframes a standard sale email as a curation play, which is how I actually think about CB2 versus Wayfair.
- **The category grid is genuinely useful.** NEW LIGHTING, NEW RUGS, NEW BEDROOM — I can scan and immediately know which vertical to click. That's better than Wayfair's "under $29.99" grid, which is just price-range chaos.
- **The 5% back callout is visible** without having to hunt for it. CB2's rewards structure matters to me — I have the card, I run purchases through it.
- **No render bugs.** It's clean. No broken images, no lorem ipsum, no layout collapse. At least they have the basics right unlike some of those chaotic Wayfair sends.

## What's weak

- **The subject is all-caps and vague.** "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" reads like a banner in a showroom window, not a message to someone who's already a customer. Compare it to Pottery Barn's "Extra 20% off is ON + Find bedding in your favorite color" — same sale energy, but at least PB told me the discount percentage.
- **No specific offer in the hero.** The word "SALE" is the only price signal above the fold. No percentage, no dollar threshold, no time horizon. I'm being asked to click blind. Article's welcome email felt more respectful of my time than this.
- **This is the C&B family's sixth email to me this week.** Even if the design is strong, the cadence has poisoned the well. CB2 and Crate & Barrel share a parent — my brain files them together, and together they're noisy.
- **"Future classics" goes nowhere.** It's an interesting concept in the subject line and the hero, but the category grid below just says "NEW [ROOM TYPE]." If you're going to position these as classics, name the pieces. Show me the specific sofa that earns that label.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a real discount percentage in the hero.** "Up to 30% off" beats the word "SALE" every single time. If it's a range, say "up to." Don't make me click just to find out.
- 2. **Name the hero piece.** The velvet chair has a name. Tell me what it is. "The Lennox Chair. On sale." Done — that's a click.
- 3. **Tie "future classics" through the body.** If you're pitching investment pieces, show me why. A tiny callout — "Style it once. Keep it forever." — under two or three featured SKUs would lock in the concept.
- 4. **Earn the send cadence.** CB2 should coordinate with Crate & Barrel on suppression so I'm not getting six emails in a week from the same family. Or at least make this email's category clearly CB2's lane (modern/contemporary) versus C&B's (classic/transitional) so I can tell them apart in my inbox.
- 5. **Mix the subject case.** All-caps registers as shouting. Title case reads as confidence.
