# DOUBLE REWARDS§ IS ALMOST OVER
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-07**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so this is the credit card pitch, not the furniture sale itself — "DOUBLE REWARDS IS ON, GET 10% 20% BACK IN REWARD DOLLARS" (yeah, that's a typo, "10% 20%" both sitting there like nobody proofread it) tied to the CB2 Visa Signature card. I don't have this card, so the hero offer is a non-starter for me on click one. Then it pivots into the exact same six-category shop grid — Living Room, Lighting, Dining Room, Decor & Mirrors, Bedroom, Rugs — I've now seen from CB2 basically every day this week, capped off with a "LABOR DAY SALE UP TO 50% OFF" banner that feels bolted on after the fact.
- What actually matters to me — rugs, because I'm doing area rugs in both houses right now — is buried as one thumbnail of six, no price, no "% off" on the tile itself. Compare that to CB2's own "Rugs on sale. So worth it" send from 2026-09-05 which at least led with "UP TO 50% OFF RUGS" as the headline; this one demotes rugs to a grid square behind a credit-card come-on. Article's "Last call: up to 40% off ends today" send this week put the number in a giant hero with zero scrolling — that's the bar, and this doesn't clear it.
- Verdict: not opening the card offer, not clicking through the grid for a category I don't need today, marking as read. Wouldn't forward — nothing here is interesting enough to send a friend, and honestly the credit-card angle is a bit of a bait-and-switch given the subject line promised something more exciting than "sign up for a store card."

## What's working

- The countdown timer up top ("02:07:20:26") is at least consistent with the urgency beat CB2's been running all week, so it reads as on-brand rather than random.
- Photography is genuinely nice — that pendant-lamp living room shot and the walnut credenza in Decor & Mirrors both look like rooms I'd actually want, which is more than I can say for some of Wayfair's stock-photo grids.
- The six-category grid is clean and legible, good use of whitespace, easy to scan even if the content itself is stale.
- Loyalty tie-in ("Earn 10% Back in Rewards Dollars every day on purchases at CB2 and Crate & Barrel") is at least relevant since I do buy across both banners.

## What's weak

- The headline offer is a credit card signup, not a furniture discount — for someone like me who's mid-renovation and buying constantly, this reads as "we want your credit line" more than "here's a deal," and it's not what the subject line implied.
- "GET 10% 20% BACK" — that's a visible typo/merge error sitting right in the hero, which kills trust fast.
- Rugs, my actual priority, get zero special treatment — just one square in a six-square grid, same as Decor & Mirrors, which I'm not shopping for.
- This is at least the sixth CB2 email in my inbox this week and the shop grid is identical structure to the 09/05 and 09/06 sends — same categories, same layout, just a different top banner. The fatigue is real.
- The Labor Day "UP TO 50% OFF" banner at the bottom feels like an afterthought slapped under a credit card pitch, when it's honestly the more compelling offer and should've been the hero.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the furniture discount, not the credit card — "UP TO 50% OFF" should be the hero image, credit card offer moves to a secondary module below the fold. Article proved this week that a clean, giant "40% off" hero with no scroll gets the click; CB2 buried its best asset under a card pitch nobody asked for.
- 2. Fix the "10% 20% BACK" typo immediately — that's a credibility hit that costs more than any layout fix could recover.
- 3. Personalize the grid order or headline toward past browsing — if I've clicked rugs before (I have, repeatedly, this week alone per the brand history), put Rugs first with a price callout, not sixth alphabetically.
- 4. Cut send frequency — six sends in a week is Crate & Barrel-family levels of fatigue on both banners; consolidate into fewer, higher-value sends so the "sale ends" urgency actually means something.
- 5. Put a specific price or % off directly on the rug/category tile instead of making me click in blind.

# Technical Audit — CB2 "Double Rewards" Email

**1. Technical Summary**
Standard MI/Bronto-templated HTML email; core rendering markup is sound, but the message carries legacy ad-network beacons/cookie redirectors and lacks visible RFC 8058 unsubscribe support in the captured headers.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- 61 tracking/click-redirect links were present but skipped by the automated HTTP probe (redirect domains) — not independently verified as functional.
- Multiple **non-HTTPS (`http://`) third-party beacon/redirect calls** to `ads.dotomi.com` (`pub1.php` through `pub10.php`) and `login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController` — these are legacy Dotomi cookie-sync/retargeting pixels. Mixed-content `http://` resources are commonly blocked or stripped by mail clients (Gmail, Outlook), meaning this tracking network sync likely fails silently for most recipients.
- Standard first-party tracking pixels present and HTTPS: `mi.cb2.com/.../o.gif` and `dv.cb2.com/o/...` (Bronto/CM engagement tracking) — no issues.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- **9 content images missing `alt` text** (all `image.mail.cb2.com/lib/fe9213727564027a72/...` product/hero images) — screen readers and blocked-image fallback will show nothing meaningful. Evidence: e.g. `f9a45447-5688-4f48-86d5-5223895fbdb3.jpg`.
- The 10 Dotomi `pub*.php` and `UCMController` images also lack `alt`, but these are non-visual tracking beacons, so missing alt is not a user-facing accessibility defect — flagged only under Link & Tracking above.
- Template includes standard Outlook/MSO conditional comments, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, and mobile media queries (`max-width:418px`) — no structural rendering issues found in the visible markup.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
No merge/personalization tokens (e.g. `%%FIRSTNAME%%`, `{{...}}`) visible in the truncated source. No issues found.

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
