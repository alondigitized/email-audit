# Furniture on sale right now
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-28**
## Executive summary

- Forty-five. I stopped counting precisely sometime around the "Everything in stock now" send on June 26, but that's roughly where I am with the CB2 and Crate family this month, and this one drops on a Saturday — a day after two CB2 sends and less than 48 hours after the "Re: your summer savings" inbox-trick. When "Furniture on sale right now" lands with the sender name "CB2 SALE," my first move isn't curiosity, it's recognition that this is the brand running its promotional engine on full blast.
- Open it, though, and the email is actually fine. The hero is clean: 4th of July Sale, 50% off furniture, SHOP NOW. Below that, a three-column category grid — outdoor, rugs, lighting — that I appreciate because it means I can skip the sale homepage entirely and go straight to the rugs section where I'm actually looking. The warehouse sale callout at 60% off adds a stronger discount tier. The "MORE FOR YOU" product cards at the bottom have visible prices and look like they were at least loosely personalized rather than just a dump of bestsellers.
- The problem is what the email can't control: I am immune to it. Article's 4th of July send hit me last week with a clear hero sofa I spent five minutes looking up. Pottery Barn's Extra-20%-off drum from June 28 gave me a dollar figure I could do actual discount math on. CB2 is giving me a percentage and a holiday and a vague gesture toward sale content. I'd click one category link to check the warehouse rugs, then close. Would not forward.

## What's working

- Hero offer is immediately above the fold: "50% OFF FURNITURE" is the first thing my eyes land on, no scroll required
- Category shortcuts (SHOP OUTDOOR, SHOP RUGS, SHOP LIGHTING) are verb-noun CTAs that actually take me somewhere useful — a rare improvement over the generic SHOP NOW for everything
- 60% off warehouse sale as a second tier gives me a reason to scroll past the hero — the escalating discount structure is smart
- "MORE FOR YOU" section with individual product cards and visible prices suggests some degree of personalization rather than a pure hero-and-categories template
- Render is clean — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum; CB2 is consistent on the basics

## What's weak

- Subject line "Furniture on sale right now" is aggressively generic — no discount figure, no product name, no hard deadline. Compare that to Article's July send, which at least led with the sale name, or Wayfair's LARGE AREA RUGS subject that at least gave me a category with price context
- "SHOP NOW" on the hero CTA is doing exactly zero differentiation — if you've already gotten me past the subject and preview, give me "Shop Furniture Sale" or "Shop 50% Off" so I know what I'm clicking into
- Nothing in the email tells me when this ends. "4th of July Sale" implies July 4th, but that's inference on my part — no "sale ends July 4" line in the hero block means I don't feel urgency
- This is the fifth-plus CB2 send in roughly 96 hours. The cadence has trained me to process these like utility notifications
- No product-level detail anywhere — I'm furnishing two houses and I need to know if that credenza ships in three weeks or three months; this email has no answer for that

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the offer in the subject** — "Furniture on sale right now" leaves the 50% in the hero where only people who open will see it. Make the opener do the work. You already have the offer; use it.
- 2. **Add an explicit deadline line inside the hero block** — "Sale ends July 4" next to the SHOP NOW button converts casual browsers into deadline-motivated clickers. The holiday framing is already there; just make it explicit.
- 3. **Replace the hero CTA** — "SHOP NOW" has the energy of a default button state. "Shop Furniture Sale" adds one noun and earns the click.
- 4. **Add a lead-time or stock signal to the product cards** — even "Usually ships in 3–4 weeks" is information that furniture buyers need. Article does this; CB2 doesn't.
- 5. **Throttle the cadence** — if the rest of July looks like June, I unsubscribe. Five sends in four days means I'm training myself to archive this sender on sight.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Global `text-size-adjust: none` override**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```
Applied to every element via the `*` selector. This suppresses iOS auto-scaling globally and can make small text unreadable. Should be scoped to `body` only, and set to `100%` not `none`.

**Inconsistent `font-display` across `@font-face` declarations**
Four `@font-face` blocks for `"Lineto Brown Sub"` are declared; only two include `font-display: swap`. The first two (`.eot`-based and `.woff`) omit it, causing potential FOIT (flash of invisible text) in unsupported clients.

**EOT format still present**
```css
src: url("...lineto-brown-regular.eot");
src: url("...lineto-brown-regular.eot?#iefix") format("embedded-opentype"), ...
```
EOT is an IE6–IE8 format with ~0% modern market share. Dead weight that adds parse cost without benefit.

**Double-slash in font URLs**
```
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2
```
URLs contain `//` after the domain. Most servers normalize this, but it is a defect and could break on strict CDN configurations.

**Mobile breakpoint is atypical**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)
```
418px is an unusual breakpoint. Common safe values are `480px` or `414px` (iPhone Plus/Max). At 418px, iPhone 14 Pro (393px logical width) hits this breakpoint but iPhone 14 Plus (430px) does not, potentially leaving the largest-screen iPhones on the desktop layout.
