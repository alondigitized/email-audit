# Hardworking desks, from $599
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-four. That's where I am with Pottery Barn in July after this one drops, and I almost didn't open it on that basis alone — but "Hardworking desks, from $599" stopped me. I have two homes in active renovation and at least one of them needs a proper home office setup. I've been procrastinating on that exact decision for three weeks. For a moment, this email had a genuine shot.
- The hero delivers. A warm, styled workspace with a statement wood desk, organized shelving, and a proper task chair — that's exactly the visual I'd pin. This is the version of Pottery Barn that earns a folder in my inbox. And then the email pivots halfway down to a "Back to School" section and loses me completely. I don't have school-age kids. I have two partially gutted houses and a rewards card strategy. The moment I hit that section, the email stopped being about me and started being about someone else's September to-do list.
- Mark as read. Maybe saved to the folder. Won't click today.

## What's working

- **Subject line is the best thing in this email.** "Hardworking desks, from $599" is concrete, uses a real adjective that implies function over decoration, and includes a price anchor — that's three things working at once. Closer to what Article does than what Pottery Barn usually sends me.
- **Hero image earns the open.** The workspace setup is aspirational but livable — I can picture it in the second bedroom I'm converting to a study. No abstract lifestyle fluff; just a well-staged desk I might actually buy.
- **The $599 floor is honest.** No fine-print asterisks visible, no "starting from" buried small. The price is in the subject and presumably backed up in the first product tile.
- **Category discipline.** Unlike the July 14 "Summer deals UP TO 70% OFF + New arrivals for a coastal cottage look" blast that threw half the catalog at me, this stays in its lane — desks, home office. I can respect that.

## What's weak

- **The "Back to School" pivot is a deal-breaker.** I'm 36 and renovating two homes. Partway through the email, the content shifts to student desk setups and a different buyer mode entirely — parental, deadline-driven, September-anxious. That's not me. CB2 doesn't pull this split on me; even Crate & Barrel's "Get beautifully organized" send last week stayed coherent even while padding the footer.
- **No sale signal on the desks themselves.** The July 13 email promised "up to 70% off." The July 14 email doubled down on it. I've been trained to expect a markdown from Pottery Barn by now. "From $599" is a floor, not a deal. If desks are on promotion, I can't see it.
- **Cadence drag.** Email number twenty-four from one brand in fifteen days taxes even genuine category interest. The desk category works for me right now; the volume erodes the trust needed to act on it.
- **CTA button text too small at mobile render size.** I skim this stuff in my saved folder on my phone. If I have to zoom to read the button, I've already moved on.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the Back to School section or send it separately.** Home-office shopper and back-to-school parent are genuinely different buying modes. If Pottery Barn has any profile data on me at all, this section shouldn't have survived segmentation.
- 2. **Surface a desk-specific sale if one exists.** The brand ran "up to 70% off" language two days ago across the full catalog. Desk-specific markdown language — "up to 40% off home office through Sunday" — would convert me faster than a price floor.
- 3. **Add a dimension/size filter CTA.** "Shop by desk size" or "Find one for your space" would serve someone working with a specific room footprint far better than landing me on a 400-item category grid. Article does this cleanly.
- 4. **Dial back the frequency.** A well-timed desk email once this week outperforms three in a row. The July 14 design-services email was the best thing in my July inbox from this brand — it opened breathing room. This one doesn't have that same runway because it's arriving the next morning.

### 1. Technical Summary

One confirmed broken CTA link will silently drop credit-card acquisition traffic, and PHP server-side header calls have leaked verbatim into a CSS `<style>` block, indicating a template-assembly defect. The email is otherwise structurally sound but carries significant accessibility debt across its image layer.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken link — 403 on The Key CC apply-and-buy banner**
`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember&...`
The credit-card application CTA returns HTTP 403. Any recipient clicking the loyalty/CC acquisition block gets an error page. This is the highest-priority fix.

**[INFO] 23 tracking links unverified**
All click-through links route via `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirect. Destination URLs and UTM parameter integrity could not be confirmed by HTTP probe.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[FAIL] PHP header() calls leaked into CSS `<style>` block**
The third `<style>` tag in `<head>` ends with raw PHP source:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are invalid CSS and will be silently ignored by email clients, but their presence signals that a server-side image-caching snippet was pasted into the wrong template partial. No functional breakage, but the root template defect should be corrected.

**[FAIL] 57 images missing `alt` text**
Includes all content-bearing images: `F26_TOP_SummerSalePartC_NoUrgency.jpg`, `slices_01.jpg` through `slices_24.jpg` (office furniture grid), product thumbnails (towel, duvet, dining table, curtain, bed), and all footer slices. With images disabled — common in corporate mail clients — the email renders as a blank grid with no readable content.

**[WARN] 3 image assets served over HTTP**
These may be blocked by mail clients enforcing HTTPS:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` — cross-brand West Elm asset
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Additionally, the open-pixel beacon routes over HTTP:
`http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=...`
Open rates will be undercounted wherever HTTP image requests are blocked.

**[WARN] Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` — some mail clients (Samsung, some webmail) surface this in the tab bar. Populate with the subject line or brand name.

**[WARN] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
`<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` prevents user-initiated zoom. Fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text).

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
Not captured by the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to surface the native unsubscribe UI; its absence degrades the unsubscribe UX and increases spam complaints. CAN-SPAM requires a functional unsubscribe mechanism in the body, which appears present (footer link visible), but the header-level mechanism is missing or was stripped in transit.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent**
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is not confirmed. Google and Yahoo's 2024 bulk-sender requirements mandate this for senders over 5k/day.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header absent**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from the captured message. This may be a relay artifact, but should be verified against raw headers in the ESP dashboard.

**[OK]** `<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow" />` and `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />` are both present.
