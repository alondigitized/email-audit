# Inspired by the English countryside + ready for Double Rewards!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-26**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is Crate & Barrel proper, not the Crate & Kids account that's been spamming me all week with nursery gliders. Different subject line, different energy — "an estate-worthy living room," "an heirloom bedroom," "the modern English farmhouse kitchen" — this is actually speaking my renovation language. Two houses in progress means every room-by-room breakdown like this is relevant to me somewhere.
- That said, it's a wall of scroll. Living room, bedroom, dining room, entertaining, kitchen — five full room sections stacked before I even hit the actual sale grid, and I'm mid-thumb-scroll on my phone the whole time. It reads more like a catalog flip-through than an email. Double Rewards is the actual hook buried at the top and then repeated at the bottom, and "up to 60% off The Labor Day Event" is the real offer, but it's competing with a lot of moody lifestyle photography for my attention.
- I'd scroll through this one, honestly — the "modern English farmhouse kitchen" section caught my eye since that's basically what I'm doing at the second house. I wouldn't forward it, but I'd probably screenshot the kitchen section into my inbox folder for reference. Better than the last five Crate & Kids sends by a mile.

## What's working

- The room-by-room structure (living, bedroom, dining, entertaining, kitchen) matches how I actually think about a renovation — one category at a time, not a generic grid.
- "Double Rewards" is called out twice — top and bottom banner — so I know exactly what the loyalty math is before I even get to the sale.
- The English farmhouse kitchen section is genuinely on-trend for what I'm doing right now, unlike CB2's identical "SUITE UPGRADES" sends I've been getting nine days straight.
- Photography has consistent warm, moody tone throughout — doesn't feel like three different photoshoots stitched together, which is more than I can say for some of these emails.
- Clear "Shop [Room]" CTA buttons under each section, so I'm not hunting for where to click.

## What's weak

- It's just really long — five full lifestyle sections before the actual discount grid shows up. Pottery Barn's "Delivered in 1-4 Weeks" email got to the point faster and that's the thing I actually care about mid-renovation.
- "Up to 60% off" appears three separate times (hero, mid-banner, footer grid) with no single clear number — feels like the classic "up to" vagueness where the real average discount is much smaller.
- The sale grid at the bottom crams eight category tiles ("kitchen essentials," "bedding & more," "outdoor furniture," "rugs," etc.) that don't map back to the rooms I just scrolled through — feels like two different emails got taped together.
- No delivery-time or shipping-clarity messaging anywhere, which is exactly what's on my mind splitting two renovations — Pottery Barn beat this email on that specific point.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the lifestyle sections from five rooms to two or three, and move the Labor Day discount grid up near the top — I shouldn't have to scroll past four rooms to see the actual percentages.
- 2. Put one concrete number in the hero instead of "up to 60% off" repeated three times — even "40% off sofas, 60% off rugs" reads more honest than a vague ceiling.
- 3. Add a delivery-time badge like Pottery Barn's "Delivered in 1-4 Weeks" — that's the actual differentiator that would get me to click over a competitor.
- 4. Tighten the footer grid so it echoes the room sections above instead of introducing new categories (rugs, wall decor) I never saw upstream.
