# How designer Lisa Pollock crafted the perfect coastal bedroom
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-22**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is a change of pace — Crate & Barrel finally sent me the design-story email instead of another flash sale, and it's actually decent scroll bait: a Florida designer named Lisa Pollock walking through a "coastal bedroom" refresh with a canopy bed, a rattan dresser, and a slouchy neutral palette. That's a real departure from "Floor Sample Sale" and "Future Heirlooms Under $500," the last two C&B/CB2 sends I chewed through this week.
- The problem is it doesn't stay a design story. By the third scroll it's already pivoting to "up to 25% off your bedroom refresh," then "new markdowns added! up to 60% off clearance," then a Design Desk sweepstakes, then a "we love these for you" product grid with a KitchenAid mixer and dinnerware that has nothing to do with bedrooms. It's five emails stapled together wearing one designer's name as a hat.
- I'd scroll through it on the toilet, maybe screenshot the canopy bed for my wife since we're doing the primary bedroom this fall, but I'm not clicking through five sub-offers in one email. This gets marked read, not forwarded, not clicked.

## What's working

- The Lisa Pollock framing is a real hook — Article did the same "designer home tour" beat with An Trieu ("Don't sleep on storage") and it worked there too; this genre clearly performs when brands use it, and C&B finally borrowed it.
- The canopy bed hero shot is genuinely on-brand for "coastal bedroom" and matches the kind of thing I've got bookmarked for the second-home primary.
- Quote pull-outs ("slowing down influenced every choice") break up the wall of product shots and make it feel less like a catalog dump than usual.
- The mattress cross-sell ("a new mattress for better rest") is at least topically adjacent to bedroom, unlike the KitchenAid mixer buried near the bottom.

## What's weak

- The offer stack is a mess: 25% off bedroom, then 60% off clearance, then a $5000 sweepstakes, then a credit card push, then unrelated kitchen product — by the time I hit "we love these for you" I've forgotten this was supposed to be a design story.
- The KitchenAid mixer and Apple dinnerware grid at the bottom has zero connection to "coastal bedroom" — that's just generic merchandising tacked onto a designer email, and it kills the narrative thread CB2's "Future Heirlooms" send actually managed to hold.
- No fabric/swatch program mentioned anywhere, which is exactly what I'd want on a canopy-bed-and-linens story — I'm not buying a $1,500 bed off a JPEG without seeing the actual weave in person.
- The credit card plug ("new! the card you love just got better") feels bolted on — it's the fourth or fifth distinct CTA competing for my thumb by that point.
- This is C&B's fourth-ish email this week per my own history (garlic gadgets, flatware guide, two Crate & Kids sends) — the cadence is still relentless even when one send is genuinely better than the rest.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email in half. Ship the Lisa Pollock story as its own send with just the 25% bedroom offer, and push clearance/sweepstakes/card/kitchen grid to separate sends — Wayfair's "On sale now: pergolas" worked specifically because the subject matched the whole email's contents; this one promises a design story and delivers a five-headed merchandising blast.
- 2. Add a "shop the room" swatch/sample CTA near the bed and dresser shots — furniture guys like me need fabric-in-hand before we drop four figures on a canopy bed.
- 3. Move the mattress cross-sell higher and drop the KitchenAid/dinnerware grid entirely — it's off-category and dilutes the one thing this email did well.
- 4. Give the sweepstakes and card pitch their own dedicated send instead of tacking them onto a design story that's trying to earn trust.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Rattan, woven textures, and the canopy bed she built the whole room around`
