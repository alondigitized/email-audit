# Up to 50% off top dressers, beds, gliders & more!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-17**
## Executive summary

- This is the second Crate & Barrel Kids send I've gotten today, and it's the same nursery pitch again. The hero is clear: "limited time only! up to 50% off furniture" over a crib-and-cozy-room photo, with a free-shipping banner on top. But I'm furnishing two grown-up houses, so kids' dressers, gliders and toddler beds aren't on my list. The screenshot is a very tall, tiny render, so I'm going on structure and the headlines I can make out.
- The email is well built. It has a clean stack of products, each with a "Shop Now" button and a second "Shop the Sale" link. The sections are easy to tell apart, and there's a "we love these for you" grid near the bottom. It's also long. After the product stack it keeps going with clearance, wallcoverings, baby gear, a registry-style card, an app promo and a design-help block. That much length makes me feel like a stranger on a mass list.
- Would I click? Probably not, unless I needed a dresser. The Redondo 54" natural oak 6-drawer dresser caught my eye, and a dresser is a dresser. I'd screenshot that one into my inspiration folder. I wouldn't forward it. I'd mark it read, which is what I did with the earlier Kids SALE email today.

## What's working

- The "up to 50% off furniture" headline is unmistakable, and the "limited time only!" line gives it a deadline.
- Free shipping on top of the email matters to me, because furniture shipping is where I get burned.
- The product stack is easy to scan. Each item has a photo, a name, a sale price against the original price and a Shop Now button.
- The Redondo oak dresser is a genuinely good-looking piece, and the warm wood tones would fit a grown-up room too.
- The "we love these for you" grid and the design-help block near the bottom feel like useful extras.

## What's weak

- It's aimed at nursery buyers. Cribs, gliders and toddler beds mean nothing to me, so this was never going to convert.
- The email is very long, with six or seven distinct sections. By the app promo I'd stopped reading.
- Every product has two links, a "Shop Now" button and a "Shop the Sale" text link. That's redundant and clutters the layout.
- The "up to 60% off clearance" and "very best baby gear" blocks dilute the 50% hero. It's three offers fighting for one slot.
- The same creative landed hours ago. Crate & Barrel is hitting me with near-duplicate sends, which is the cadence problem I hate most.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this send. Show it to people who've browsed nursery or registry, and send me the dining and bedroom content I actually clicked on before.
- 2. Cut the email to the hero and three or four products, and drop the app promo and design-help blocks from this send.
- 3. Keep one CTA per product. Drop the secondary "Shop the Sale" link.
- 4. Add swatch or sample info, delivery windows and the sale end date next to the offer.
- 5. Add rewards or member pricing. I buy with the highest-rewards card, and I'd like to see the math.

## Technical Audit

**1. Technical Summary**

About 45 images lack alt text, and the QA data can't confirm the unsubscribe headers or SPF/DKIM. The QA run scored a 57% pass rate with 0 errors and 3 warnings.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- 88 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe, so destination validity, redirect chains, and expiry are unverified.
- The HTML source was truncated, so I can't confirm individual `href` values, broken anchors, or UTM presence.
- Tracking pixels are present and are also flagged as missing alt:
  - `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif?mi_u=…` (open pixel)
  - `https://dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/b3993086-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…`

  - 6× `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png?mi_u=…`
- The `rlcdn.com` pixels carry a hashed identifier (`s=dbe8da71…`) to a third-party domain, and five of them fire in one send. This adds third-party requests and a privacy consideration, though it isn't a functional defect.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- **Missing alt text:** about 45 `<img>` elements are flagged. The count is approximate because the QA output may be truncated.
  - Content images on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/*.jpg|gif`, roughly 25 of them. Examples: `6cda691d-b295-47d6-aa7c-8c52d1eec48a.jpg` and `f1db6dd8-071b-4d4b-ab5d-5549f0cf3f58.gif`.

  - If these images are blocked, as is common in Outlook, the content is unreadable and screen readers get no description.
  - Pure tracking pixels and spacers should use `alt=""` rather than no attribute, so screen readers skip them.

- **Viewport:** `maximum-scale=1` in the viewport meta blocks pinch-zoom on some clients, which is an accessibility concern.
