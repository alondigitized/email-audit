# Up to 60% off The Summer Sale! Don’t miss out →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-14**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is Crate & Barrel proper (not Kids), and it's actually got a real number up top — "up to 60% off The Summer Sale" over a beach photo. That's the clearest single offer I've seen from this brand family all week, so credit where it's due. But by the time I've scrolled past the sale grid I'm hit with a Levain bakery collab, a Howell x Harrier AD magazine sweepstakes, a free design service pitch, a "we love these for you" product wall, a new credit card push, an app download nudge, and a second Design Desk plug. That's eight distinct asks in one email.
- My highest-rewards card is already the Chase Sapphire, so the "new Crate Card" pitch is dead on arrival — I'm not opening a store card for Reward Dollars when I'm already stacking travel points on this reno. The sweepstakes and bakery collab feel like filler dressed up as content, and neither has anything to do with why I opened this email, which is the sale.
- Would I click? Maybe, just for the "Shop the Sale" button up top — furniture clearance is genuinely relevant with two houses in progress. Would I forward it? No. Would I scroll all the way through this novel of an email? Also no — I'm bailing after the category grid.

## What's working

- The hero is unambiguous: "up to 60% off The Summer Sale" with a clean "Shop Now" button, no hunting required.
- The category grid (bed & bath, outdoor, kitchen, rug, decor & lighting, furniture clearance, tabletop clearance) maps directly to what I'm shopping for across two renovations.
- "we love these for you" product wall shows real SKUs with prices — a dining table, an upholstered bed, a console, a nightstand — that's the kind of specificity CB2's recent "impeccable energy" email lacked.
- No render bugs — images loaded clean, text is legible, buttons are tappable.

## What's weak

- Way too many competing CTAs stacked in one send — sale, bakery collab, magazine sweepstakes, design service, credit card, app download. Wayfair's "Black Outdoor Privacy Screens" email stayed on one message; this one can't decide what it's selling me.
- The Howell x Harrier AD sweepstakes requires subscribing to a magazine newsletter to enter — that's a real ask buried as a throwaway section, not "an honest offer."
- New credit card push feels tone-deaf for someone who already routes every purchase through a rewards card for maximum points.
- This is the fifth Crate & Barrel-family email in my inbox this week (following the nursery grid, the Finn Collection whole-house tour, and two Stokke Tripp Trapp sends) — the cadence is genuinely exhausting and this one doesn't earn extra attention by being longer.
- "clearance" appears as its own category AND a repeated line item — mildly confusing hierarchy in that grid.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this to two sections: hero sale banner + category grid. Kill the bakery collab, sweepstakes, and credit card push from this specific send — save them for their own dedicated emails so they don't dilute the actual offer.
- 2. Move "we love these for you" product recommendations up higher, right under the hero — that's the strongest content in the email and it's currently buried two-thirds down.
- 3. If The Design Desk free service is worth including, give it one clean placement, not two (it shows up mid-email and again near the footer).
- 4. Drop the new-card pitch entirely from sale emails — it reads as a bait-and-switch after "60% off" gets me in the door.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 60% off furniture, rugs & clearance — ends soon`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your reno list, up to 60% off right now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop bed & bath, outdoor, kitchen, and furniture clearance while it lasts.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The pieces you've been eyeing, marked down across every room.`
