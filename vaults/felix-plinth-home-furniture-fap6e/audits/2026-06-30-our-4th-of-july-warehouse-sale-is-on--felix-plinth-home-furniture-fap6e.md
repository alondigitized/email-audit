# Our 4th of July Warehouse Sale is on! 🤩 🎉
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- The "New items added!" hook in the hero is genuinely the smartest thing this email does. I already watched this warehouse sale announcement roll out on June 29 — the "Selling fast" send that hit a clean 8 — and a second wave needs a reason to exist. "We added more inventory, come back and look" is a valid reason. Respect where it's due.
- But then I scroll to "we love these for you" and the personalization serves me a stroller. I'm 36, I'm mid-renovation on two houses simultaneously, my Crate & Barrel wish list runs to twelve pages of furniture and textiles, and there is not a single nursery in my floor plan. Whoever signed off on that segmentation logic needs to cross-reference the CB main account before pushing baby gear at someone who's been ordering accent chairs and area rugs for eight months.
- Then there's the OpenTable block. A restaurant reservation prompt. In a furniture sale email. I don't even know how to process that as a reaction. I'd click the warehouse sale button to see what's actually been restocked — that "new items" hook is real bait — but the strollers and the OpenTable detour both push me toward "mark as read" instead of "tab to open." Not forwarding this one.

## What's working

- "New items added!" in the hero earns its place — gives the second wave send a reason to exist that the June 29 "Selling fast" version didn't need to justify
- 50% off is competitive: Article's "4th of July Sale starts now" and CB2's "SALE OF THE SUMMER" are both running this week, and 50% matches or beats both
- "Shop the Warehouse Sale" CTA is clean and immediately visible — dark button, unambiguous copy, no mystery about where I'm landing
- Email renders cleanly — no broken images, overlapping text, or layout chaos at any visible section

## What's weak

- "We love these for you" served me strollers — this is the personalization algorithm firing against the wrong segment entirely; it makes the whole block feel untrustworthy going forward
- "Up to 33% off BabyStyle fan faves" is a full section I'd scroll past without blinking — wrong audience, wrong moment
- The OpenTable integration mid-email is a co-marketing deal that made sense in a deck and breaks the experience in practice; I checked twice thinking it was a layout bug
- "Is on!" in the subject tries to sound energetic and lands slightly awkward — it's a phrasing I'd expect from a UK retailer, not American home goods copy
- This is the 40-something C&B family email I've reviewed this month; the cadence fatigue is brand-level, not send-level, but it still taxes every open

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the personalization first — strollers for a home-renovation customer is the kind of miss that trains people to permanently ignore the "we love these for you" block, which kills a high-value module
- 2. Kill the OpenTable section entirely — no half-measures; it doesn't belong in a furniture sale email at any volume
- 3. Make "new items added" earn its weight in the hero — which categories got restocked? Rugs? Seating? The vague framing loses half the click potential
- 4. Suppress the BabyStyle fan faves section for anyone not in the baby-purchase funnel (Crate & Kids has the data; use it)
- 5. Tighten the subject so it doesn't lean on two emojis to carry the energy

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` brand mismatch (confirmed)**
`<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` — sender is "Crate & Kids SALE." Incorrect brand shown in Gmail/Outlook tab labels and screen reader document title announcements.

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom (confirmed)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-zoom on iOS. WCAG 1.4.4 failure (Resize Text, Level AA).

**Conflicting `img max-width` media queries (confirmed)**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
At ≥768px both rules match; CSS cascade resolves to `600px` (later rule wins), meaning images are actually *narrower* at wider viewports than at 640–767px. Likely unintended — images cap at 600px on large desktop screens.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust` self-conflict (confirmed)**
```css
body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; }   /* in reset block */
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }      /* in global * rule */
```
The wildcard `*` overrides the body declaration for all descendant elements, disabling iOS font scaling entirely. Contradicts the intent of the body rule.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags (confirmed)**
`format-detection` is declared four times: once combined, then three separate declarations for `date=no`, `address=no`, and `telephone=no`. Redundant; the combined declaration on line 1 already covers all three.

**Duplicate `charset` declaration (confirmed)**
Both `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">` and `<meta charset="utf-8">` are present. Harmless but noisy.
