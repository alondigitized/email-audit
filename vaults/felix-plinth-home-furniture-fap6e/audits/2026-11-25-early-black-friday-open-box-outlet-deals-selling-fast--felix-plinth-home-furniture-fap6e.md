# EARLY BLACK FRIDAY + Open Box Outlet Deals—selling fast!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-25**
## Executive summary

- Pottery Barn again. It's the same maroon "Early Black Friday, up to 70% off" banner I've had since 11/16, and I lost count of the sends a while ago. The twist today is "Open Box Outlet Deals," a big maroon block with "Selling fast" over it. As a guy furnishing two houses, I do like the idea of open-box and returned pieces. The block gives me no product, though. I get a headline, one line of copy and a SHOP NOW button, and I'm left guessing what "selling fast" even refers to.
- Below that, the email goes back to its usual routine. It has a Design Crew banner, a "Recommended just for you" grid of velvet stockings, a crewel stocking, a faux fur throw, a waffle robe, a curtain rod and a quilt, then an "In Stock & On Sale" tease with a sideboard. I'm renovating, not hanging stockings. The Design Crew free-appointment pitch showed up in the 11/24 "FREE design help in stores" send and appears twice here. Both the banner and the sweepstakes block repeat it.
- Would I click? Maybe the Open Box block, out of curiosity, but I wouldn't expect to find a lamp or a sideboard I'd actually buy. I'd save it to my inbox folder and not forward it. Mostly I'm annoyed that the real offer is hidden.

## What's working

- The "Open Box Outlet Deals" idea is a fresh angle. After a week of "UP TO 70% OFF" it gives me a reason to open this one.
- "Selling fast" is a credible urgency cue for one-off returned inventory.
- The Design Crew free-appointment block fits my two-house renovation. The CTA, BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT, is a clear verb and noun.
- The product grid is clean and photographed well. Each item has a readable label, and the quilt and rod hardware are on-brand for a reno.
- The "Earn 10% back in rewards" card block is the only place that speaks to my rewards-card habit, and it's easy to find.

## What's weak

- The Open Box hero has no product imagery at all. There's nothing to show what's discounted or by how much, and the banner above it still says only "up to 70% off."
- The "Recommended just for you" grid is seasonal filler (stockings, a robe). It feels segmented by calendar, not by my renovation browsing.
- The Design Crew pitch repeats, with a banner mid-email and a sweepstakes version lower down. Nothing in the layout sets the two apart.
- The In Stock & On Sale sideboard is the most relevant item to me, but it's buried near the bottom with a small text link instead of a button.
- The fine print is a long wall of tiny type. For a sale where exclusions apply, I'd want the main caveats near the offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put real open-box products with outlet prices in the hero. Show a lamp, a console and a sofa with "was/now" and condition notes.
- 2. Replace the stocking grid with furniture and lighting, or with an open-box grid.
- 3. Pick one Design Crew placement and cut the second.
- 4. Make the sideboard block a proper product module with a button.
- 5. Add shipping and availability clarity for furniture: how many units are left, and whether returns can ship or must be picked up.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary

The email is a Salesforce Marketing Cloud-style, 650px, table-based send. Its main technical problems are about 40 images with no alt text, four `http://` image sources, and the recipient's email address in plain-text pixel and image URLs. The source is truncated, so the unsubscribe link, physical address and landing-page parameters could not be verified. Of the 3 QA warnings, only the 2 compliance ones are header-level, and they are likely relay artifacts (see section 5).

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **Click tracking:** 48 links go through click-redirect domains, so the HTTP probe skipped them. Example: `https://click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=ABp7ImQi...` (logo link). Destination status, redirect chain and final URLs are **unverified**.
- **Insecure tracking pixel:** `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` loads over plain HTTP. It may be blocked or rewritten, which would undercount opens.
- **Third-party pixel:** `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/L21rdC84NjEvY2lkLzE3NDc3ODk3MzMvdC8y/cat/1` is a Turn/Amobee ad-network beacon. It is a third-party tracker with no alt text.
- **Open pixel:** `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/b88556a6-...?mi_cid=...&mi_mid=...` is the first-party open pixel. It has no alt text, and `alt=""` is the correct value for it.
- **Cross-brand asset dependency:** `https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` is a Pottery Barn email loading a spacer from the sister brand's CDN. If West Elm removes it, the spacing layout breaks.
- **Stale asset paths:** `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` is a 2022 asset, and `.../pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` is an evergreen asset. Both are HTTP.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **Missing alt text:** about 40 `<img>` elements have none. This covers the hero (`H26_Top_EBF_NoUrgency.jpg`), the open-box slices (`slices_01.jpg`), every product image (stockings, throw, robe, curtain bracket, open-box quilt), the bottom banners and the whole footer. The footer is sliced into 18 images (`Email-Footer/slices_01.jpg` to `slices_18.jpg`) with no alt text, so any footer links (including unsubscribe and preferences, if they sit in those slices) have no text equivalent when images are blocked.
- **Blocked-image failure:** with images off, the email shows only the preheader text and blank space, because the hero, offers and footer are all image-only.
- **Mixed content:** four `http://` image sources (`Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, `slices_02.jpg` and the `svopn` pixel, plus the HTTP click-tracking path above). The `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` and `slices_02.jpg` images may be blocked or flagged as insecure content.
- **Empty `<title></title>`.**
- **Viewport:** `maximum-scale=1` disables pinch-zoom, which is an accessibility failure (WCAG 1.4.4).
- **No `lang` attribute** on `<html>`.
- **Stray non-CSS in `<style>`:** the PHP `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: ...")` lines sit inside the second `<style>` block. They are invalid CSS and appear to be a template leak. Parsers skip them, but it is sloppy and can break the rule that follows in strict clients.
- **Mixed `role="presentation"`:** the content tables have it, but the outer wrapper tables and the 20px spacer table (`<td height="20">&nbsp;</td>`) do not. Screen readers may announce them as data tables.
- **Mobile selectors:** `table[class="wrapper"]` and `img[class="photo"]` only match elements whose class is exactly that value. The visible markup uses `class="contenttable"`, so these rules are likely dead.
- **Web font:** `<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat">` is ignored by Gmail and Outlook. This is only a concern if Montserrat is the only font in the stack.
- **Minor:** the preheader contains an unescaped `&` ("outside the box & save BIG").

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

- No unresolved merge tokens (`%%...%%`, `{{...}}`) in the visible source.
- **PII in URLs:** the recipient's address is a plaintext query parameter in two image URLs: `.../customer-service/coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` and `...sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`. These are HTTP and HTTPS fetches that leak the address to the server, to any proxy or logs on the path, and to anyone who forwards the message. One of them (`svopn`) is over HTTP, so the address is also visible in transit. `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` limits Referer leakage but does not cover the request itself.
- `coreemail.html` is served as an image (`<img>`), which is an unusual use for an HTML endpoint.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)

- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) not found.** The QA tool notes these may simply not be captured by the AgentMail relay, so this is **inconclusive**. Gmail and Yahoo require one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders (5,000+ messages a day), so confirm against the raw message headers.
- **Authentication-Results not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment is **unknown** for the same reason. The visible From domain (`e.potterybarn.com`) and the click domain (`click.e.potterybarn.com`) are aligned.
- **Unverifiable from the truncated HTML:** the visible unsubscribe link and the physical postal address. The footer is 18 image slices without alt text, so the unsubscribe link is probably inside an image map or a slice link, which makes it inaccessible with images blocked.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)

- **UTM/campaign parameters cannot be confirmed.** All 48 links are wrapped in opaque `click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=...` redirects, so the final `utm_*` parameters and landing pages are not visible. Campaign IDs appear only in the pixel URLs (`mi_cid`, `sv_cid=0053_00597`).
- **Product image sources** (`www.potterybarn.com/pbimgs/ab/images/dp/wcm/...`) point at product-page images, but the matching click destinations are hidden behind the tracking wrapper. Alignment with the "Early Black Friday / Open Box Outlet" landing pages is unconfirmed.

## 7. Recommendations

1. Add descriptive `alt` text to every content image. That means the hero, the offer slices, the product images and all 18 footer slices. Use `alt=""` for spacers and tracking pixels.
2. Convert the four `http://` image sources to `https://`: `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, `slices_02.jpg` and the `svopn` pixel.
3. Remove the recipient's email address from the image and pixel URLs. Use an opaque subscriber or job ID instead, and don't use an `<img>` that loads an HTML endpoint (`coreemail.html`).
4. Verify the real headers (List-Unsubscribe, List-Unsubscribe-Post, SPF, DKIM, DMARC) on the raw message. Don't rely on the relay-captured copy.
5. Put real text, or at least alt text, on the footer's unsubscribe and preference links so they work with images off.
6. Fix the template: add a non-empty `<title>`, `lang="en"`, `role="presentation"` on all layout tables, drop `maximum-scale=1`, and remove the PHP `header()` lines from the `<style>` block.
7. Self-host the spacer instead of loading `edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`, and retire the 2022 asset paths.
8. To close the continuity gap, follow a sample of the 48 tracked links to check final URLs, status codes and `utm_*` parameters.
