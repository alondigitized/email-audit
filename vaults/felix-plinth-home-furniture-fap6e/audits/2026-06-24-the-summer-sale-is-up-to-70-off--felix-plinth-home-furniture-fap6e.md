# The Summer Sale is UP TO 70% OFF
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-eight. That's where I'm at with Pottery Barn, and this one lands the morning after "Discover bestselling furniture, ready to ship," which landed the afternoon after *two* June 23 sends — "Big news! Up to 70% off" and "FREE design help" — which landed the day after the ice cream emoji one. I am not reading the subject line anymore. I'm just checking the body to see if there's anything new.
- And here's the honest answer: there actually is something slightly different. "100s of new items added!" is a better reason to look than "same sale, new day." The "Recommended Just For You" module is the first personalization I've seen across this entire Summer Sale cadence, and the outdoor living section in "Discover New Arrivals" is relevant — I have a patio at the second house that needs a seating situation now, before August. Those two things almost rescued my attention.
- What didn't: the hero is floral blue bedding. I'm a 36-year-old dude trying to source sofas and dining tables for two properties. Pottery Barn absolutely knows I'm on their list — they've emailed me 38 times — and the best they could do is a Pinterest-soft bedding shot. I'd mark this as read. If I had two minutes I'd scroll to the recommendations module and maybe click there. I would not forward this because everyone I know has already seen this sale.

## What's working

- **"100s of new items added!"** is the only line in this entire Summer Sale run that gives me a reason to look today vs. yesterday — that's the actual news and it's being undersold as subtext
- **"Recommended Just For You"** is exactly what this cadence needs; it's the one module that treats me like a known customer rather than a generic sale recipient
- **Category nav** (Furniture, Outdoor, Sofas & Chairs, Rugs, Lighting) lets me self-route in two seconds; I don't have to hunt through the sale
- **Affirm financing** is the right call for a home furnishings email — I'm making a four-figure sofa decision and payment flexibility genuinely factors into which cart I complete
- **"Discover New Arrivals" outdoor module** is seasonally correct and differentiates from the sale body — that's the patio situation I actually need

## What's weak

- The **floral blue bedding hero** is a demographic miss — nothing in that image says "36-year-old male renovating two houses"; a statement sofa or an outdoor dining set would land in a completely different place for me
- **No deadline.** "Up to 70% off" with zero urgency cue means I snooze this for later that never comes; Crate & Barrel's "Bestsellers" send this week at least felt like it had a sale anchor even without a hard date
- **Fifth Summer Sale email in 72 hours.** The cadence has murdered the urgency the offer is supposed to carry — I've been watching this sale for five days and felt exactly zero pressure to act
- **"SHOP ALL SALE" sends me to everything** — CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" send earlier this week at least tried to land on a curated page; this is basically homepage with a discount filter and I have to do the work

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the hero to outdoor or living room.** Sofas, outdoor seating, dining — something that reads male/renovation. The bedding play belongs to a segmented send, not the blast.
- 2. **Make "100s of new items added!" the headline.** That's what differentiates this email from yesterday's. Lead with the news.
- 3. **Add a sale end date.** Even "ends Sunday" would change my behavior. Without it, "up to 70% off" is just ambient wallpaper.
- 4. **Promote "Recommended Just For You" above the category nav.** That's the one module converting this from a broadcast to a conversation — it should be the first thing I see after the hero.
- 5. **Suppress this send to anyone who already received the June 23 "Discover bestselling furniture" email.** The customers most likely to churn from this list are the most engaged ones being carpet-bombed.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New to the Summer Sale: 100s of items just dropped`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Summer Sale edit — refreshed today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Outdoor seating, sofas, rugs — fresh arrivals at up to 70% off.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New furniture and outdoor just landed in the sale.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed broken links will cause 403 errors for recipients clicking a credit card banner and loading a cross-brand spacer image. Forty-four images across the email carry no alt text, and three image assets are served over plain HTTP — all of which will be blocked by modern email clients enforcing HTTPS.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Credit card apply banner — 403**
`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY...`
The loyalty/credit card CTA returns HTTP 403. Any recipient clicking the "Apply & Buy" or non-member Key Rewards banner hits a dead end. This is the highest-severity broken experience in the send.

**[FAIL] West Elm spacer image — 403**
`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
This cross-brand tracking pixel/spacer 403s and is also served over HTTP (see §3). Stale asset reference from a Williams-Sonoma sibling brand; should be removed or replaced with a self-hosted equivalent.

**25 tracking links skipped** — click-redirect domains (`click.e.potterybarn.com`, `clickcp.potterybarn.com`) were not probed by QA. Destination health of those links is unverified.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image assets (3 instances) — will be blocked by Gmail, Outlook 365, Apple Mail:**
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

**Missing alt text — 44 images**, including all content slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg` in both body and footer sections), all product images (bed, curtains, towels, sheets, quilt), and tracking pixels. Recipients with images disabled see a blank email. Screen readers get no information.

**CSS in `<style>` block contains invalid PHP header() calls:**
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache...");
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are server-side PHP directives copied verbatim into a `<style>` block. They are invalid CSS, ignored by all clients, but indicate a template error that could corrupt rendering in strict parsers.

**`<title>` element is empty** — assistive technologies and some email clients use this for tab/window labeling.

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta** — suppresses pinch-to-zoom on mobile, an accessibility regression.
