# Bedding & more UP TO 70% OFF 📣
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- Forty-five. That's roughly my Pottery Barn count now, and this one lands on June 27 wearing a completely different subject promise — bedding, 70% off — than what actually opens the email. The hero says "Extra 20% off Select Styles," which is *word-for-word* the headline from the June 26 "ON NOW: Extra 20% off select styles" send — the one I actually thought was a strong email. So Pottery Barn took their best recent send, recycled the hero creative, changed the subject line to promise bedding at 70% off, and buried that actual offer three modules down in a nine-tile category grid. That's not a new email. That's a reskin.
- The bones aren't broken. The "Recommended Just For You" section with four product tiles is doing more heavy lifting than anything Wayfair managed in their "UP TO 70% OFF is HEREEE 🔔🔔🔔" send from the same day — where I got a department-store grid and zero personalization signal. And the Affirm integration and $50 Rewards module at the bottom speak to how I actually buy: I'm running two renovation budgets in parallel, so buy-now-pay-later and rewards math genuinely matter.
- I'd mark this as read and keep scrolling unless one of those recommendation tiles happens to catch my eye. Not a forward. Not a click on purpose.

## What's working

- **"Recommended Just For You" tiles** — four specific products surfaced mid-email; that's the most relevant module in here and the only thing that could make me actually stop.
- **Dual offer structure** — "Extra 20% off" stacked above "UP TO 70% OFF" category deals gives two reasons to engage instead of one generic headline.
- **Earn $50 in Rewards** — I'm in the Key program; seeing that called out in the body matters to me and reinforces the loyalty relationship.
- **Open Box Outlet module** — differentiated play; Article and Crate & Barrel almost never break out outlet/returned items as their own in-email section. Credit for the format.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, images loaded, nothing overlapping.

## What's weak

- **Subject/hero mismatch** — Subject promises "Bedding & more UP TO 70% OFF" but the hero is the same "Extra 20% off Select Styles" creative from June 26. That's creative recycling within 24 hours and it reads as bait-and-switch.
- **Category grid overload** — Nine tiles: Sofas & Chairs, Outdoor, Dining, Bedding, Tabletop, Pillows & Throw, Lighting, Bath, Shop All Sale. Pottery Barn just listed their entire store. Crate & Kids managed a focused storage-furniture email this same week without this kind of shotgun approach.
- **Open Box Outlet for a new-renovation shopper** — I'm buying new for two homes. Returned items at "unbelievable prices" isn't my use case right now and undercuts the aspirational tone Pottery Barn usually earns.
- **No deadline anywhere** — Five 4th of July emails from this brand and not one has told me when the sale actually ends. "4th of July Sale" isn't a deadline; July 4 is still four days away. This kills urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the hero match the subject** — If the subject leads with bedding at 70% off, open with a bedroom image and bedding-specific 70% off pricing. Don't make me scroll three modules to find the offer I was promised in my inbox.
- 2. **Move "Recommended For You" above the category grid** — That personal hook is the most compelling thing in here; it shouldn't come after a nine-tile department store listing.
- 3. **Collapse the category grid to four tiles max** — Bedding, Furniture, Lighting, Outdoor. Force a choice, give me focus.
- 4. **Add a hard deadline** — "Ends July 4 at midnight" in the hero line. Every send this week has implied urgency through 4th of July theming without ever stating when it's over. Article did the same lazy thing in their "4th of July Sale starts now" send and it cost them CTR.
- 5. **Retire the "Extra 20% off Select Styles" hero** — That creative did its job on June 26. Running it again 24 hours later with a different subject is a trust eroder.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your picks: new bedding + extra 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop before the holiday sale closes — your recommended styles are waiting.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Up to 70% off bedding + extra 20% off select styles, through July 4 only.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed broken destination links and a PHP code artifact leaked into a `<style>` block are the highest-severity issues. Accessibility is broadly deficient with 47 images missing alt text, and three images (including an open-tracking pixel) use plain HTTP, which modern clients will block or warn on.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Two links return HTTP 403**

- `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?...&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember` — the "Apply and Buy" Key Rewards credit card banner link. The destination is dead; clicking this CTA yields a 403. This is a revenue-impacting broken experience.
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` — a West Elm–domain asset embedded in a Pottery Barn email. It 403s and is also served over HTTP (see §3). This is a cross-brand template contamination: a shared Williams-Sonoma component block was included without updating the asset domain.

**[INFO] 24 click-redirect links were skipped** by the probe (expected for `click.e.potterybarn.com` tracking URLs). No further failures detectable without following redirects.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CRITICAL] PHP `header()` calls inside a `<style>` block**

The following code appears verbatim inside a `<style>` element in `<head>`:

```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```

These are PHP server-side directives that should have executed when serving an image resource — they never ran, and instead landed literally in the HTML. This is a build-pipeline artifact. The CSS parser ignores them, but they indicate a broken template include and may signal that a dynamic image component silently failed during assembly.

**[FAIL] 47 images missing `alt` text**

Every product image slice (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_14.jpg` in two separate sections), all footer images, all product thumbnails, the open-tracking pixel, and cross-brand spacer gif have no `alt` attribute. When images are blocked (the default in many corporate and iOS Mail clients), the email renders as a blank white column with no textual context.

**[WARN] 3 images served over HTTP (not HTTPS)**

| Image | URL |
|---|---|
| `spacer_0.gif` | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` |
| `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` |
| `slices_02.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` |
| Open-tracking pixel | `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` |

Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail proxy or block mixed-content HTTP image loads. The open-tracking pixel being HTTP means open-rate measurement is unreliable in those clients.

**[WARN] Empty `<title>` tag** — `<title></title>`. Some clients (Outlook desktop) display the title tag as a window or tab label. Blank is benign but sloppy.

**[WARN] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** suppresses user-initiated zoom, which is an accessibility concern (WCAG 1.4.4).

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent** — not captured by AgentMail relay. If truly missing from the envelope, Gmail and Yahoo Mail will suppress the native unsubscribe UI and may route to spam. Required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (Feb 2024+).

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe not confirmed. Same enforcement concern as above for high-volume senders.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header absent** — SPF/DKIM pass/fail status unknown due to relay. The sending domain `e.potterybarn.com` (ESP subdomain) should have both; inability to confirm is a monitoring gap, not a confirmed failure.

**[INFO]** `meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer"` strips the `Referer` header on all outbound clicks. This is standard practice for email privacy but means server-side referrer analytics on the landing page will show no email referrer — attribution must rely entirely on UTM params.

CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link: not verifiable from the truncated HTML, but standard for Pottery Barn sends. Assumed present in footer.

The broken `clickcp` link carries `cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember` and `marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY` — UTM attribution for that CTA is lost entirely because the link 403s before any redirect fires.

**[WARN]** The `no-referrer` meta tag (§5) means landing-page analytics cannot use `document.referrer` as a fallback — UTM params must be present on every tracked link or attribution is dark.
