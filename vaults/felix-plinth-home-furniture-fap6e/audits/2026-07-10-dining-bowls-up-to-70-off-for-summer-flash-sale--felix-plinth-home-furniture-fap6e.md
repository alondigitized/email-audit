# DINING BOWLS ✨  UP TO 70% OFF for SUMMER FLASH SALE ✨ 
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- Here's what happened: I saw "DINING BOWLS" and the lizard brain renovation part of me woke up briefly — I have a second kitchen to stock and a dining room at the primary that's getting a full reset. For about a second and a half, this felt like it might be the email I'd save to my folder. Then I opened it and found... area rugs. Entryway furniture. TV stands. Lighting. The four featured categories are perfectly generic Wayfair summer sale content, and dining bowls appear to be nowhere in the body. This is now the second time in five days I can document a Wayfair subject line that doesn't match the email it's attached to — the outdoor rugs send on July 6 was the same bait-and-switch move.
- The "ENDS TONIGHT" urgency on the hero banner would carry weight if I hadn't seen some version of this deadline in at least three of the last four Wayfair emails. At this point "ENDS TONIGHT" from Wayfair reads like "open 24 hours" on a diner sign. I believe it the way I believe a furniture store's "GOING OUT OF BUSINESS" banner that's been up since 2019.
- I'm marking this read and moving on. No click. Definitely not forwarding it — I've already complained to two friends about Wayfair's inbox behavior this week, and sending them this email would make my point better than anything I could write.

## What's working

- **"ENDS TONIGHT" countdown on the hero** — even with my fatigue, a hard deadline in the hero banner does create a micro-moment of attention. The yellow/high-contrast color treatment makes it register fast.
- **Specific category discounts listed** — area rugs 75% off, entryway 40%, TV stands 45%, lighting 45% — these are real numbers, not vague "big savings" language, and I appreciate that Wayfair still commits to specifics.
- **"Picked just for you" framing** — the personalization label is there, and I can see Wayfair is at least attempting to show products based on my history. It doesn't feel random.
- **Wayfair Rewards visible in footer** — I'm a rewards member and I do track the points math. Seeing it there is a quiet reminder that I'm leaving points on the table if I don't buy somewhere. Low-key effective.
- **Free delivery callout over $35** — clean, visible, and it removes a friction point. This one is always worth including.

## What's weak

- **Subject line says dining bowls; email delivers rugs and TV stands.** This is the thing that should be embarrassing to whoever approved this send. Article ran a tight new-arrivals email on July 6th where every visible product matched the subject. This is the opposite of that.
- **"ENDS TONIGHT" has lost all credibility at this cadence.** I've seen it — or a variation of it — across multiple consecutive Wayfair sends this week. It's the countdown clock that cried wolf.
- **Five emails in five days, and this is the fifth.** Pottery Barn sent me 14 emails in roughly ten days and I still gave their Summer Sale send an 8 because the creative earned it. Wayfair is sending at similar volume with a fraction of the precision. It's the wrong tradeoff.
- **No dining bowl or serveware product anywhere I can see.** If this is supposed to be a flash sale on dining bowls, show me a dining bowl. Show me a specific SKU. Crate & Barrel's family brands at least put product hero shots that match what they promised in the subject — this has nothing.
- **"Shop all sales" CTA is a punted opportunity.** If the subject promised me 70% off dining bowls, the CTA should drop me on a dining/serveware sale page. Sending me to the all-sales page is making me do your job for you.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Match the subject to the email content — or match the email content to the subject.** Pick one. If dining bowls are genuinely 70% off, build the hero around one beautiful bowl, show three to five SKUs in the "picked for you" row, and link the CTA to `/dining-tabletop` not `/sale`. If this is just a summer sale blitz, don't put a specific product in the subject line you have no intention of featuring.
- 2. **Replace "ENDS TONIGHT" with an actual deadline string.** "Sale ends July 10 at midnight ET" is more credible and more actionable than a generic countdown I've stopped believing. Pottery Barn's Summer Sale email I reviewed gave real end-date framing and it landed better for it.
- 3. **Cut send frequency by at least 30% this week.** You're past the point of diminishing returns. Article went quiet for four days after their 4th of July sale blitz and came back with a crisp new-arrivals send that felt like a breath of fresh air. That restraint is what made me actually look. Wayfair's current pace is training me to archive on sight.
- 4. **Personalization in the hero, not just in a small-print section label.** If you know I've been browsing dining and kitchen for a second home, put that in the hero copy. "Your second home needs a table setting too" is a sentence that would have made me stop scrolling.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Web font with incomplete fallback stack** — `@font-face` loads `Sofia` from `secure.img.wfrcdn.com`. The only fallback specified is `mso-font-alt: "Arial"` (Outlook-only attribute). Gmail (web + Android), Yahoo, and AOL strip `@font-face` entirely and have no declared `font-family` fallback in the rule. If body text or headings inherit `Sofia` without a system-font stack (e.g., `font-family: "Sofia", Arial, sans-serif`), those clients render the browser/OS default, which may break layout metrics.

- **`.u-Title` font-size collapses to 32px on mobile for all title variants** — all five title classes (`--primary`, `--secondary`, `--tertiary`, `--white`, plain) override to the same 32px. If typographic hierarchy is load-bearing for scannability, this flattens it on mobile. Not a bug but a known limitation to document.

- **`min-width: 100vw` on `u ~ div`** — this is the standard "Blue Gmail" fix and is intentional, but it prevents Gmail Desktop from rendering the email narrower than the viewport. No issue unless the desktop layout has a hard max-width container (verify `#Container` width in full source).

- **`@supports (-webkit-touch-callout: none)` block** — iOS Safari-specific image inline fix is present. Correct usage.

- **No `alt` text visible** in truncated source — cannot confirm. Must verify all `<img>` tags carry descriptive `alt` attributes, especially product images.
