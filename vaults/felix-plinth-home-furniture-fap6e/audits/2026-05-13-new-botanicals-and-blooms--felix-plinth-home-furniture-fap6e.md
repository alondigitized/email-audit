# NEW! Botanicals and blooms 🌿
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- Pottery Barn knows I'm renovating. They've been in my inbox for years and I've bought enough from them that I'd be embarrassed to tally it up. So when I see a spring botanical push from PB, my first instinct is to give it a minute — I might actually need a centerpiece for the dining table in the second house. Editorial format, clean photography, organized into three buckets (centerpieces, artful displays, lush foliage) — this is Pottery Barn doing what Pottery Barn does, which is sell me a lifestyle first and a product second.
- That said, I scrolled to the bottom looking for a reason to *buy today* and came up mostly empty. No "20% off" banner. No spring sale. Just aspirational room shots and a "Recommended Just for You" strip that I've seen in every PB email for the last 18 months. The content is pretty, but it's not a buying trigger — it's a mood board. I'd probably tab this into my folder of "stuff to reference when the living room is ready," which is not the same as clicking.
- Forward to a friend? Honestly, maybe to my partner as a "thoughts on this style?" text screenshot. But I'm not sending a Pottery Barn bloom email to my guy friends. Mark as read and folder it.

## What's working

- **Brand recognition is instant** — Pottery Barn's masthead and photo style are immediately familiar; zero trust friction.
- **Seasonal timing is dead-on** — Mid-May botanicals and blooms is exactly the right moment; I'm thinking about outdoor entertaining and fresh styling right now.
- **Three-category structure is useful** — Breaking it into Centerpieces / Displays / Foliage gives me a mental map without me having to hunt for what I need.
- **"Recommended Just for You" strip** — Even if I'm skeptical of the algorithm, the fact that PB is attempting personalization makes me feel like a known customer rather than a cold blast target.
- **Photography quality** — Lifestyle shots are aspirational but not alienating; the room settings look like places I'm actually trying to build, not a fantasy loft I'll never afford.

## What's weak

- **No offer anywhere** — I buy with my highest-rewards card because I'm always running a numbers game. A purely editorial email with no price hook gives me zero incentive to transact *today*. It's a catalog, not a campaign.
- **Subject line "NEW! Botanicals and blooms"** — The all-caps NEW! feels slightly desperate and the emoji skews the tone a little soft for me. I'd respond better to something with a sharper hook.
- **CTAs are category links, not products** — Clicking "Strong Centerpieces" drops me into a browse grid. I'd rather see one hero product with a dimension or two so I can immediately evaluate fit for my specific tables.
- **No sizing/scale context in the photos** — For someone buying furniture and decor for two homes, I need a sense of *how big is that vase relative to the credenza*. Not one caption gives me a measurement.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with one hero product and a price** — Pick the best-performing centerpiece from the last 30 days, show it at scale in a room shot, and put the price right on it. Editorial mood is fine, but anchor it to something transactable.
- 2. **Add a limited-time framing** — "In for spring, limited quantities" or even a soft membership discount badge would give me a reason to act now instead of folder-and-forget.
- 3. **Include at least one dimension or scale callout** — Even a single "18" H × 10" W" under the hero product would do more for my decision than three more lifestyle shots.
- 4. **Test a more direct subject line** — The emoji and editorial tone are fine for repeat openers but don't drive urgency.
- **Subject Alt A:** `PB's best spring botanicals — shop before they're gone`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Fresh centerpieces for every table in your home`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New arrivals in faux and live botanicals — sized for dining, console, and side tables`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Spring styling is here. Find the right scale for every room.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility


These are server-side PHP statements that were never executed — they were injected raw into the CSS block. Email clients will treat them as invalid CSS and discard them, but their presence indicates a broken template pipeline step.
