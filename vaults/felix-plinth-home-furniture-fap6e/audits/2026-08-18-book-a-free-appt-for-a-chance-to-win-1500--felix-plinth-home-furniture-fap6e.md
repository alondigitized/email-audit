# Book a FREE appt. for a chance to win $1500!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-18**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "Book a FREE appt. for a chance to win $1500" — this is Pottery Barn's ninth email to me this week and it finally did something different: a Design Crew Sweepstakes tied to booking a design consult, with the "for your living room / bedroom / dining room" inspo shots stacked below. I'll give them credit, this is a genuinely different angle than the lamp roundup yesterday or the towel sale the day before — it's not just another product grid with a discount slapped on top.
- But "chance to win" is doing a lot of lifting for a $1500 gift card that requires me to sit through a design consultation to even be entered. I'm mid-renovation on two houses right now, so a free designer session isn't nothing, but this reads more like a lead-gen funnel dressed up as a sweepstakes than an actual offer. The fine print at the bottom ("no purchase necessary," odds language) confirms my gut — this isn't really about the money.
- Would I click? Maybe, only because I'm actually renovating and a free design consult has real utility to me right now regardless of the sweepstakes gimmick. Wouldn't forward it to a friend — this isn't a "look at this deal" email, it's a "PB wants my calendar" email.

## What's working

- The sweepstakes framing is a genuine change of pace after eight straight product/sale emails from this brand this week — at least it's not the ninth "Now on Sale" banner.
- The WHAT/WHEN/WHERE breakdown under the hero is clean and answers my actual questions (what am I committing to, in-store or virtual) before I even have to click.
- "For your living room / for your bedroom / for your dining room" room-by-room inspo shots are relevant to me specifically since I'm renovating two homes and thinking room-by-room right now.
- The Halloween design class insert lower down feels like a genuine loyalty touch, not just another sales push, even if it's off-topic for this email.

## What's weak

- The $1500 prize requires meeting with a designer before Aug 30 — that's a real time commitment being asked in exchange for a sweepstakes entry, and the email undersells how much friction that is.
- No actual discount or specific price anywhere in this email — compare that to PB's own "Bath towels from $24.50" send from two days ago, which at least led with a real number. This one's all vibes and inspo shots.
- The "Recommended Just For You" grid at the bottom (curtain rods, dining table, rugs, bedding) has zero connection to the sweepstakes hook up top — feels bolted on, like two emails smashed into one.
- This is Pottery Barn's ninth send this week. Even a genuinely different creative angle can't fully escape the fatigue — I'm starting to auto-skim their subject lines.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with what the design consult actually costs me in time — "30-min virtual or in-store session" — so I know what I'm signing up for before I decide to click. Right now it's vague on effort.
- 2. Cut the unrelated "Recommended Just For You" product grid entirely from this send — it dilutes the sweepstakes message and this isn't a shopping email, it's a lead-gen email.
- 3. Put a hard number on the design service itself (e.g., "normally a $99 value, free this month") so there's a tangible reason to book beyond the sweepstakes odds.
- 4. Move the Halloween design class to its own dedicated send — Article's recent "Clocking in" home-office email worked because it stayed in one lane; this one splits attention between sweepstakes, room inspo, seasonal class, and product recs.

# Technical Email Audit — Pottery Barn "Book a FREE appt." Campaign

## 1. Technical Summary
This is a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) table-based HTML email with tracked click redirects and a mix of HTTPS/HTTP image assets; automated QA returned a 57% pass rate with 3 warning categories and no hard failures.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All CTA/hero links route through `click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=...` SFMC tracking redirects (49 total tracking links) — QA skipped HTTP probing on these per policy, so live destination resolution is unconfirmed.
- Secondary/third-party tracking pixels present: `secure.leadback.advertising.com/adcedge/lb?site=695501...`, `d.turn.com/r/dd/id/...`, and `svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` — note the recipient's raw email address (`lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`) is passed in plaintext as a URL query parameter on the open-tracking pixel, which is a PII-in-URL exposure (logged in browser history, proxy logs, referrer headers).
- Malformed `<style>` block: raw PHP-style `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');` and `header("Cache-Control: ...")` statements are embedded directly inside a `<style>` tag in the `<head>` (not inside a comment or script) — this is invalid CSS and will be ignored by clients, but indicates a broken/leftover code fragment from a different asset (likely a tracking pixel handler) that was pasted into the template.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- Two images load over plain `http://`, not `https://`: `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` and `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` — these will be blocked or flagged as mixed/insecure content in many clients and browsers.
