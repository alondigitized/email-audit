# Just in! Our new digital catalog
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-one. That's where I am with Pottery Barn in July, and this one lands on a Sunday morning while I'm cross-referencing bedroom furniture specs between my two renovation projects. The subject — "Just in! Our new digital catalog" — is pure utility, no sell, and I'll be honest: the only reason I opened it is because I'm actually in the market. The fall catalog in late July makes total calendar sense to me. Retailers do this. I know they do this. And when you're the one trying to decide between the Stratford bed frame for the primary and something sleeker for the second home, a fresh catalog is legitimately useful.
- What the email delivers is cleaner than I expected at send thirty-one. The fall catalog hero lands first with an inviting room scene, there's a live "Summer Sale Up to 70% off" banner that actually matters to me right now, and the "Recommended Just for You" section in the middle pulls specific products — sofas, seating — that aren't random. That personalization module is the only reason this email clears the threshold of "I might actually click something."
- That said, the competing story arcs (summer clearance + fall launch + new arrivals + in-store design classics + app download + Affirm + rewards) make this feel like five emails stapled together. I won't forward it. I might open the catalog link. Probably on my laptop, not my phone.

## What's working

- **Fall catalog timing is legitimate.** Late July is exactly when I want to be planning fall furniture purchases — I marked the primary bedroom install for September and the second home for October. This landed at the right moment.
- **"Recommended Just for You" module.** Seeing actual product cards with prices mid-scroll is the one thing that could pull a click from me. It's not generic. It's working off purchase history.
- **Summer sale + fall catalog combo.** Running both simultaneously is smart — the sale creates urgency while the catalog creates discovery. Crate & Barrel's "The fall vibe you need? Dark, rich woods" from earlier in the week did the same thing but committed harder to the seasonal narrative; this one hedges, but at least it's honest about the transition.
- **Rewards section is visible.** "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" — I'm a Key Rewards member and the reminder matters. Didn't have to hunt for it.
- **No broken images, clean layout.** At send thirty-one I'm looking for any reason to unsubscribe. Render bugs would've been it. Everything loaded.

## What's weak

- **Five value propositions, zero hierarchy.** Fall catalog, summer sale, new arrivals, in-store design classics, app download, and Affirm financing — I had to actually scan to figure out what this email was *about*. CB2's "$1K COULD BE YOURS TO WIN" email I audited recently had one anchor offer. One. This has six.
- **"GET INSPIRED" is the wrong CTA for me.** I'm not browsing for inspiration at the moment — I'm making purchase decisions. "Shop the Fall Catalog" or "See What's New in Bedroom" would have landed harder for where I am.
- **The app push feels like padding.** I already have the app. The "Our App Makes it Easy" module near the footer reads as filler they couldn't cut. Article never does this in their editorial emails — they trust that you'll download the app if you want it.
- **The outdoor room scene in "Discover New Arrivals" is seasonal whiplash.** You're launching the fall catalog but then I scroll down to a bright outdoor patio scene. Which season are we selling?

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one story and commit.** Lead with the fall catalog OR the summer sale clearance — not both in the same hero block. If the catalog is the news, make it the entire email and let the sale be a banner footnote.
- 2. **Replace "GET INSPIRED" with a category-specific CTA.** Based on my purchase history (they know I've been buying bedroom and dining furniture), this should say "Shop Bedroom" or "Explore the Fall Catalog: Bedroom." That's the click I'd take.
- 3. **Cut the app module entirely.** If it has to stay, move it to the footer. Padding mid-email signals you ran out of content.
- 4. **Reconcile the seasonal imagery.** The outdoor patio vignette needs to go. If you're launching fall, commit to warm interiors, texture, layering. Crate & Barrel's dark woods email from July 18 got this right — every image reinforced the same seasonal mood.
- 5. **Personalized subject line.** "Just in! Our new digital catalog" works, but "Felix, the Fall Catalog is here — plus 70% off ends soon" would have pulled an instant open before I even thought about it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Fall Catalog + 70% off summer ends soon`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Fall Catalog just dropped — shop before sale ends`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New bedroom, dining, and living room arrivals — plus your summer savings window is closing.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your first look at fall — and 70% off is still on through the weekend.`

Two links are confirmed broken (403), 40+ images lack alt text, three image assets load over HTTP, and a block of PHP `header()` calls is erroneously embedded inside a `<style>` tag — a server-side template leak. Authentication and one-click unsubscribe headers were not captured by the relay.

**[FAIL] Two broken links returning 403:**

- **Credit card apply/buy CTA** — `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY…` — renders 403. Any recipient clicking this CTA hits a dead end.
- **West Elm spacer GIF** — `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` — cross-brand asset served from the wrong domain, returning 403. This also leaks a West Elm CDN dependency into a Pottery Barn send.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP `header()` calls inside `<style>` block (template artifact):**

```html
<style>
  ...
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
</style>
```

This is server-side PHP that was not stripped before render. It will not execute in any email client, but it is visible in source, exposes implementation details, and is invalid CSS that some clients may choke on.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected** — one-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy since 2024) may not be supported. Needs ESP-level confirmation.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not captured** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown from this relay. Should be verified against raw headers at delivery.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />` tag is set in the `<head>`. In webmail clients that render meta tags (Gmail web, Yahoo), this strips the HTTP `Referer` header from outbound clicks. Since the email relies on click-redirect tracking via `click.e.potterybarn.com`, UTM parameters are embedded in the redirect URL itself — so GA4/analytics attribution should survive. However, any analytics relying on the referrer header (rather than UTM params) will receive null referrers.

No raw destination URLs are visible in the truncated source to verify UTM parameter consistency across all CTAs.
