# You’ll use these every single day
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-13**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is the fifth Crate & Barrel-family email in my inbox this week and it's the first one that's actually about my kitchen, not somebody's kid's nursery. "The kitchen tools we can't live without" — mixing bowls, cutting boards, colanders, a salad spinner, dish towels, cookware — this is stuff I'm genuinely pricing out for the second house's kitchen right now. It's long, it scrolls forever, and there's a lot of "shop now" repetition, but at least everything in it is in my actual renovation lane.
- The catch is there's no real offer here. I scrolled through utensils, cutting boards, cookware, a fall tablescape, a $5,000 Design Desk sweepstakes, and a "new! the card you love just got better" credit card pitch, and the only concrete price signal anywhere is "crafted-to-last cookware... at a price you'll love" with a "new lower price" callout — vague. Compare that to the Pottery Barn "quick-ship furniture" email that at least told me delivery windows in weeks. This is pure lifestyle browsing, not a deal.
- I wouldn't click anything specific — I'd maybe scroll on my phone during coffee, mentally bookmark the cutting boards and the cookware section, and move on. Not forwarding this to anyone. It's competent catalog content, not a compelling send.

## What's working

- Hero image and copy are squarely in my zone — kitchen tools for a working kitchen, not kids' bedding for once.
- Good product variety with real category groupings (utensils, mixing bowls, cutting boards, cookware, dish towels) that mirrors how I'd actually shop a kitchen refresh.
- The cookware section calls out "new lower price" — that's the closest thing to an actual deal signal in the whole email.
- Photography is consistent, moody, on-brand — feels like the real Crate & Barrel, not the Kids spinoff, so my brand trust is intact here.

## What's weak

- No headline discount, no %/$ off anywhere — just "at a price you'll love," which is marketing-speak for "we're not telling you the number."
- Way too long — kitchen tools, meal prep video bait, dish towels, cookware, fall tabletop, Design Desk sweepstakes, "we love these for you" grid, then a credit card pitch. That's five different campaigns stapled together.
- The fall tablescape and Design Desk sections are a hard left turn from "kitchen tools" — feels like they just crammed every active campaign into one send instead of picking a lane.
- Credit card cross-sell at the bottom ("new! the card you love just got better") is the kind of bolt-on that CB2's better sends (like the "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" one I scored a 7 on) never bother with — they stay focused on one message.
- "meal prep, but make it ASMR" reads like it's chasing a TikTok trend rather than talking to an actual 36-year-old renovating two kitchens.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this to one campaign. Lead with kitchen tools, kill the fall table and Design Desk sweepstakes sections, save the credit card pitch for its own send.
- 2. Put an actual number on the cookware price drop — "new lower price" with no digits is a wasted CTA line.
- 3. Trim the scroll — six-plus product sections plus two sweepstakes plus a credit card pitch is fatigue, not merchandising.
- 4. Drop or clarify the "ASMR" meal-prep bit — cute internally, doesn't tell me what's actually for sale.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `New lower prices on the cookware set you've been eyeing`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Cutting boards, mixing bowls, and colanders — starting at $12.50`
