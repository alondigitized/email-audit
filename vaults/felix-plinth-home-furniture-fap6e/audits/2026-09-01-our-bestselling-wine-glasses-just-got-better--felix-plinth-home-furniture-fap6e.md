# Our bestselling wine glasses just got better →
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-01**
## Executive summary

- Ninth Crate & Barrel-family email this week — I stopped counting which persona folder to drag these into. The subject promised wine glasses got an upgrade, but that's buried somewhere in the middle of a scroll that opens with "the new fall table," dinnerware, mugs, more dinnerware, a Labor Day mega-banner, kitchen deals, and mattresses. Mattresses. From the wine glass people.
- The Hudson Stemware section itself is actually decent — three glasses with liquid in them (red, martini, white) is the clearest single visual in the whole email, and the copy "beat your headroom collection" (or whatever that pun is) at least tries. But it's drowning. This is the same problem Pottery Barn's "70% off" blast had — everything crammed in, nothing prioritized — except C&B does it worse because they've sent me eight other emails this week already doing the same thing.
- I would not click. I might scroll past the stemware section if I'm already deep in it, but from the inbox this reads as "another C&B everything-email" and gets archived unread most days. Not forwarding — nothing here is a "hey check this out" moment, it's just catalog sprawl.

## What's working

- The Hudson Stemware trio shot (red wine, martini, white wine) is genuinely clean and matches the subject line's promise, even if it's not the hero.
- "Double Rewards" + Labor Day banner gives me a real, math-able reason to buy now if I were already in the market.
- Product photography is consistently on-brand — moody, styled, not stock-y — same quality bar as their "Apartment Collection" send I liked last week.

## What's weak

- The subject says wine glasses, the hero says "the new fall table" — that's a bait-and-switch, and I'm already primed to distrust this after CB2's credit-card-disguised-as-furniture email.
- This is trying to be a general catalog blast (dinnerware, mugs, furniture, lighting, mattresses, kitchen tools) instead of the focused stemware send the subject line sold me on.
- Cadence is brutal — ninth C&B-family email this week, and unlike the dining furniture send that earned its place because I'm actually shopping dining, this one has no category I'm actively hunting.
- No sizing/capacity info on the glasses themselves (oz, dishwasher-safe, breakage guarantee) — the stuff I'd actually want before buying stemware.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the wine glasses the actual hero — cut the top "fall table" dinnerware block entirely and lead with the stemware shot within the first screen. If the subject sells glasses, the email should be about glasses.
- 2. Cut the email in half. Split into two sends: one stemware/bar send, one Labor Day sitewide send. Article figured this out — their Labor Day sale finally led with the discount instead of another lifestyle story.
- 3. Add glass specs near the hero image — ounces, dishwasher-safe, breakage replacement program if they have one.
- 4. Move Double Rewards up or make it its own line, not buried mid-scroll under three product grids.
