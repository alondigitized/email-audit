# NEW MARKDOWNS! Shop up to 60% off →
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- Two homes, two shopping lists — and I actually opened this one without regretting it. After a week of Crate & Barrel family sends that had me questioning my life choices (nursery bundles, a Crate & Kids gift card sweepstakes that somehow hit me *twice* on the same Tuesday, a "Love them now & forever" hearts-and-all send from C&K proper), this is the email I actually needed: Summer Clearance, up to 60% off, from the main Crate & Barrel account. The hero lands clean. The offer is real. For someone who needs to fill two living rooms simultaneously and is being very careful about which markup he pays, this is the kind of send I save.
- Then I scroll. There's a full Crate & Kids product grid halfway down — "we love these for you" — and then an app push with an "exclusive coupon" tucked in after the kids furniture. The email had one job. It recruited a toddler's registry to help.
- I'd click, but I'd do it off the hero before I hit the kids section. By the time I got to the app promo I'd already filed this mentally under "catalog I'll revisit on Sunday" and moved on.

## What's working

- **The hero is genuinely clean:** "up to 60% off / The Summer Clearance Event" is unambiguous — I know in two seconds what I'm clicking into and why it matters to me right now.
- **60% is a real ceiling:** After CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" last week — which buried the actual discount range below a lifestyle shot — seeing a specific number in the hero is a small act of respect.
- **"Crate & Barrel SALE" as sender label:** This single segmentation decision probably saved the open. I've been training myself to ignore C&K sends; the sender name told me this was different before I read a word.
- **Summer Clearance timing is right:** It's late May, I'm actively pricing sectionals for the second house, and a clearance event framing signals genuine inventory movement rather than a manufactured promo.
- **Category grid gives me a menu:** Being able to see furniture, outdoor, and at least two other categories without clicking is useful when I'm cross-shopping two properties with different needs.

## What's weak

- **Crate & Kids embed is dead weight for me:** I have no children. The nursery product grid from yesterday's standalone C&K send reappears here, mid-email, right after the main clearance CTA — it's not a cross-sell, it's a momentum killer.
- **"NEW MARKDOWNS!" in ALL CAPS kills the tone:** Article's Memorial Day subject ("Last chance: Memorial Day Sale ends today") and CB2's "All green everything" both feel like they were written by someone. This feels like a bulk-list macro.
- **No deadline anywhere visible:** "Summer Clearance" is vague — is this through Sunday or through July? A single "ends June 2" line in the hero would have turned a nice offer into a legitimate click driver.
- **App push is buried where it does no work:** If the exclusive coupon is real and meaningful, it belongs above the category grid, not after kids furniture. I scrolled past it mentally before I found it.
- **Cadence fatigue is a real cost:** This is the tenth-plus Crate & Barrel family email I've audited recently. Even when the email is good, the brand is burning open-rate equity it doesn't have in reserve.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Strip the Crate & Kids product grid from this send entirely.** It belongs on a segmented C&K list with its own hook. If the goal is family-brand exposure, earn the embed with a dedicated "C&B + C&K: summer sale, all under one roof" framing — don't just inject it mid-clearance.
- 2. **Put a deadline in the hero.** "Through Sunday" or "Ends June 2" immediately below the headline converts a pleasant clearance promo into urgency. Pottery Barn does this on nearly every sale email and it works — I always know exactly how much time I have to drag a partner into the decision.
- 3. **Move the app coupon to sub-hero placement or cut it.** If the discount is real and exclusive, it should be the second thing I see, not the fifth. Something like "New: unlock an extra 10% in-app" right below the clearance header would actually make me download the app. After the kids furniture it's just noise.
- 4. **Lose the ALL CAPS.** "New markdowns: up to 60% off" reads cleaner and still carries the same information.
- 5. **Test a product-led subject line.** Naming a category or anchor piece — rather than the event — gives me a reason to open even mid-cadence fatigue.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sofas, dining, outdoor — all marked down. Ends Sunday.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Zoom disabled via viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents users from pinching to zoom. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[HIGH] Global text-size-adjust disabled**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applied to `*` (all elements), not just `body`. Blocks OS-level font scaling for low-vision users. Scope to `body` only and use `100%`, not `none`.

**[MEDIUM] Conflicting image max-width media queries**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
The `640px` rule is declared *after* the `768px` rule. At viewport ≥768px both apply; CSS cascade gives `max-width: 640px` to the 640px rule (last-wins), overriding the 768px rule's `600px`. This is logically inverted — larger viewports end up with larger max-width than intended. Reorder so the wider breakpoint is last, or consolidate into one rule.

**[LOW] No dark mode support**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Explicitly blocks dark mode rendering. Images and background colors may invert poorly in dark-mode clients (Apple Mail, Gmail app). Consider `content="light dark"` with tested dark-mode overrides.

**[LOW] Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
The tag appears four times with overlapping values — once combined (`telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no`) and then individually for `date`, `address`, and `telephone`. The redundant entries add noise but cause no functional error. Consolidate to one.
