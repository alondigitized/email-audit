# Something special is up to 60% off! Shop clearance →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-26**
## Executive summary

- Okay, forty-six. I say that because this is — I swear — the same "up to 60% off, shop clearance" beach-hero subject line Crate & Barrel already sent me, word for word, banner for banner. Either my inbox glitched or they're just resending the identical clearance push under a fresh timestamp, and neither option makes me feel like a valued customer, it makes me feel like a target on a spreadsheet.
- That said, credit where due: the actual body of this email is better merchandised than most of what lands in my inbox. The category grid — furniture, outdoor, kitchen, tabletop & bar, lighting, decor & pillows, rugs, bedding & bath — is genuinely useful for someone furnishing two houses at once, and the "we love these for you" block at the bottom with real SKUs and real prices (that Bassett dining table, the Anneli bed) is the first personalization signal in the whole email. But then it just keeps going — Lupita Nyong'o's home tour, a $5,000 Design Desk sweepstakes, a new credit card pitch, an app download push — and by the time I hit "love it? see it at your nearest store" I've forgotten what the original offer even was.
- Verdict: I'd scroll it once for the product grid, maybe screenshot the dining table for my Pinterest-adjacent folder, and move on. Not forwarding it — there's nothing here worth sending a friend, and honestly the duplicate-subject thing makes me want to check if I fat-fingered "mark unread."

## What's working

- The beach hero is clean and the offer is right there — "up to 60% off The Summer Sale," no hunting required.
- The clearance category grid is real merchandising, not just a discount slapped on a stock photo — I can see furniture, rugs, lighting, bedding as distinct buckets, which matters when I'm shopping two houses in parallel.
- "We love these for you" actually shows product + price ($1,799 dining table, $2,299 bed) — more concrete than CB2's "bedroom & bath" banner-only push from earlier this week.
- The Design Desk / free appointment CTA is a real differentiator — Article's dining-room editorial worked on me the same way, and this is C&B doing its own version of "talk to an actual designer," not just "buy now."

## What's weak

- Same subject line, same hero, as an email I already opened — that's not cadence, that's a duplicate, and it's worse than Wayfair running "Sixteen" and "Seventeen" back to back with the same Black Friday-in-July banner, because at least Wayfair changes the countdown.
- Way too much bolted on after the clearance section — celebrity home tour, sweepstakes, credit card upsell, app download — four different asks competing with the one offer that got me to open this.
- "While quantities last" is the only urgency cue and it's meaningless — no date, no countdown, nothing like the credible deadlines I've seen work better elsewhere.
- "Shop Now" and "Shop All Clearance" are generic verbs — doesn't tell me if rugs or lighting are actually the deep-discount categories, so I have to click blind.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the email in half.** Clearance grid + one CTA is the whole email. Lupita/Design Desk and the credit card pitch deserve their own sends — bundling them dilutes the one thing that got me to open.
- 2. **Fix the duplicate-send problem.** If this really is a resend of a subject I already saw, that's a segmentation/suppression bug worth flagging internally before it costs opens.
- 3. **Add a real deadline.** "While quantities last" → a date. Even Wayfair's fake-feeling countdowns beat vague scarcity.
- 4. **Make the CTA specific to category**, e.g. "Shop Rugs — 60% Off" instead of one generic "Shop Now" for the whole grid.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Clearance ends Sunday: up to 60% off furniture & rugs`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your reno list, 60% off — final clearance markdowns`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns on rugs, lighting & dining — while they last`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Furniture, bedding, outdoor: this week's deepest cuts`
