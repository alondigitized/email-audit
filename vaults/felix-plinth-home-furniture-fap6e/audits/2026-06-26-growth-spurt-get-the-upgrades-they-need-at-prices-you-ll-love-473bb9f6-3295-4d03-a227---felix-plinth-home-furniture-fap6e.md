# Growth spurt? Get the upgrades they need at prices you’ll love
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids dropped a sale email on me — the twenty-second email from the C&B family this month, and notably the *second* send today with this exact subject line. I already reviewed what appears to be the other variant of this send (different internal ID, same subject), which means this email hit my inbox twice on the same day. I don't have kids. Crate & Kids knows I'm on the C&B list, but "growth spurt" and "they" — the pronoun does all the audience-targeting work here, and it's work I'm watching from the outside.
- To be fair, the email itself is well-built. The hero is a clean, magazine-worthy kids' bedroom. The offers are real — up to 50% off bedroom, 50% off nursery, 30% off study furniture. Layout is crisp with no broken images. But none of that matters when I'm furnishing a second home in a style that has nothing to do with a crib glider.
- The July 4th sale window is here. Article opened clean with a percentage lead. CB2 sent me a floor-sample drop that I actually opened twice. Crate & Barrel's own family sent me "forever faves" and wine glasses today without a single discount. Now I'm getting kids furniture twice. I'm going to archive this in 1.8 seconds. Would not click, would not forward — unless I had a friend who just had a baby and I suddenly felt useful.

## What's working

- **Offer clarity is sharp**: Three distinct discount tiers (50% bedroom, 50% nursery, 30% study) give me a hierarchy at a glance — I know exactly what's deepest without reading fine print.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean**: The hero bedroom image is the largest block, leads the eye, and the sale callout is directly underneath it — no hunt required.
- **Renders without bugs**: No broken images, no overlapping text, no layout collapse. Clean execution in every visible section.
- **Category breadth makes sense internally**: For the *right* audience, bedroom + nursery + study + textiles + baby basics is a coherent kids-home bundle.
- **Subject has a personality**: "Growth spurt?" is a hook. It's not the flattest opener Crate & Barrel has written this week (that trophy goes to "Shop the forever faves edit" on the same day).

## What's weak

- **Wrong list, full stop**: I don't have kids. "They" in the subject does all the targeting — and it immediately signals this isn't for me. Article and Pottery Barn didn't pull this trick during July 4th week.
- **Duplicate send, same day**: This subject hit my inbox twice on June 26. Two different internal IDs, same copy, same day. Whether that's an A/B test or a segmentation error, it reads as careless.
- **No seasonal anchor visible**: It's July 4th sale week. CB2 led with "FLOOR SAMPLE SALE" — that felt urgent and real. This email doesn't acknowledge the holiday or put a clock on the offer, which dilutes the urgency.
- **Fourth C&B family email of the day**: Counting wine glasses, forever faves, and now two growth-spurt sends — the cadence is a trust problem.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this away from non-parent buyers.** If I've never purchased or browsed Crate & Kids, don't send me the Crate & Kids sale. Pottery Barn Kids and Pottery Barn are separated in their CRM — C&B should do the same.
- 2. **Add a deadline**: "Sale ends July 4" or even a simple "this week only" would lift click rates materially. Right now the urgency is entirely in the reader's imagination.
- 3. **Kill the duplicate send**: One email, one audience, one day. Two sends of the same subject on the same day is how you train your list to unsubscribe.
- 4. **Lead with the deepest discount**: "Up to 50% off" should be in the hero headline, not just a caption. Article's July 4th email put the number front and center — that's why I noticed it.
- 5. **Tie to a season or moment**: "Summer bedroom refresh" or "Before school starts" gives a reason to buy now that's stronger than a standing discount.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 50% off bedroom & nursery furniture — sale ends July 4`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Kids furniture, up to half off: bedroom, nursery & study`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Bedroom sets, nursery gliders, study desks — everything's discounted this week`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the biggest kids furniture sale of summer — 50% off select styles`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**WARN — 35 images missing `alt` text**, including product images, decorative GIFs, and tracking pixels. Notable categories:

- **Product/content images** (23 `.jpg` files via `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com`): Missing alt degrades the image-blocking experience in clients like Outlook and Gmail (images-off mode).
- **Tracking/beacon images** (no alt expected but should be `alt=""`):
  - `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png` — open pixel, appears 6 times
  - `sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif` — LiveRamp pixel, appears 5 times
  - `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/.../o.gif` — MI open tracker
  - `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/a92285e1-...` — additional tracking beacon

  Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` (empty string) to suppress broken-image icons in image-blocked clients. Currently they have no `alt` attribute at all.

**HTML source note:** The document uses `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` — a placeholder comment left in production HTML. Not a rendering bug, but indicates the template wasn't fully cleaned before send.

**CSS note:** Conflicting `img { max-width }` rules exist — `768px` breakpoint sets `max-width: 600px` while a `640px` breakpoint sets `max-width: 640px`. Because both media queries can apply simultaneously on screens 768px+, the 600px rule wins due to source order, which may unintentionally constrain images on mid-range displays.
