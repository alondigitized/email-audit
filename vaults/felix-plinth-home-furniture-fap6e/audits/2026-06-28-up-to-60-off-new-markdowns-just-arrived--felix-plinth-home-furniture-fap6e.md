# Up to 60% off?! New markdowns just arrived →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-28**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-three. That's the C&B family email count for June at this point, and I almost didn't notice this one broke the streak — because they're finally not screaming "4th of July Warehouse Sale" at me again. "New markdowns just arrived" is a pivot I actually respect. The email leads with cookware, which is not nothing — I'm outfitting two kitchens right now and I've had a Le Creuset-adjacent dutch oven on my mental wishlist for a month. The blue pot visible in the second block made me slow down.
- The problem is the subject line undercuts the moment. The "?!" combo — on a C&B send — reads like a Wayfair markdown blast, not the brand I go to when I want to feel like I'm making a considered purchase. And then the email does what C&B always does: it just keeps going. Cookware, then a dining section, then furniture, then a loyalty nudge, then the app. By the time I've scrolled past the third product grid, I've lost the thread.
- I'd mark this one as "read and maybe return to later" rather than click immediately. I wouldn't forward it. The blue dutch oven might get a second look tonight, but only because I was already thinking about it — not because this email pulled me there.

## What's working

- **The cookware lead is genuinely relevant.** Cast iron and a statement dutch oven at the top of the email is exactly the kind of purchase I'm circling right now for the second home's kitchen, and it's more specific than the "shop our sale" vagueness that's been drowning my inbox from Pottery Barn this week.
- **"New markdowns" is a fresher hook than anything the C&B family sent in the prior four days.** After three consecutive "4th of July Warehouse Sale" subjects, this at least sounds like something changed.
- **The render is clean.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem-ipsum placeholders — which sounds like a low bar but the C&B family has fumbled this before.
- **The blue pot is doing real work.** A single hero product that reads well at thumbnail size — that's the kind of thing that stops the scroll even when the surrounding email is cluttered.

## What's weak

- **"?!" on a Crate & Barrel email is brand damage.** Article sent me a cleaner, more confident sale announcement on June 26 without a single punctuation trick. C&B is supposed to be the aspirational shelf above Wayfair, not competing with flash-sale energy.
- **Too many product modules with no single clear winner.** The email goes cookware → dinnerware → dining furniture → more furniture → loyalty → app. That's five tonal shifts. Pottery Barn's Extra 20% send this week at least committed to one visual story even if they've sent it five times.
- **No deadline, no urgency that lands.** "Just arrived" is movement, not a clock. CB2's "Re: your summer savings" email at least implied scarcity through the reply-thread trick. This one just trails off.
- **Loyalty section is buried at the bottom.** If I have rewards points, that information belongs near the offer — not as an afterthought after three furniture grids.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the "?!" from every future C&B subject line.** It's a one-character brand deposition. Replace with a dash or just the arrow they already use: `Up to 60% off — new markdowns just arrived →`
- 2. **Pick one hero product and commit to it.** The blue dutch oven is the email. Build the whole send around it: close-up, price, a single CTA. The cookware-to-furniture pivot in the same scroll is the reason I disengage.
- 3. **Add a deadline, even a soft one.** "Through the weekend" or "while quantities last" anchors the offer without feeling fake. Right now "just arrived" implies scarcity but delivers nothing.
- 4. **Bring the rewards math up.** If I have $40 in rewards and this dutch oven is 60% off, tell me that in the hero — not in a collapsed module at pixel 1,200.
- 5. **Surface the actual sale price on the hero product.** Showing "was $X, now $Y" on the blue pot would convert me faster than any CTA button.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Fresh markdowns — up to 60% off starting now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The dutch oven, the cast iron, the dining table you've been watching — they all dropped.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New price drops across cookware, furniture, and more — see what just landed.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CONFIRMED ISSUES:**

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents user-initiated zoom. Fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Conflicting `img` `max-width` rules — potential overflow on 640–767px viewports**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
At viewports 640–767px, the `640px` rule wins (it's declared last and specificity is equal), allowing images up to 640px wide inside a 600px container. On clients that respect both rules, this can cause horizontal scroll. Reorder so the 768px rule is declared last, or use a single rule.

**Dark mode forced off — no fallback**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Combined with no `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block, this suppresses dark mode entirely. Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook 2019+ will force a light-only render. Not a rendering bug per se, but a deliberate capability exclusion worth logging.

**Duplicate `charset` declarations**
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta charset="utf-8">
```
Redundant; harmless in practice but signals template hygiene issues.

**Alt text on images** — cannot verify (body truncated). Must be audited separately.
