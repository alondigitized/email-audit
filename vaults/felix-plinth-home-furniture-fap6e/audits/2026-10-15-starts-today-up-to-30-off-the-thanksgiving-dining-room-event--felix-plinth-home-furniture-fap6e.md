# STARTS TODAY: Up to 30% off The Thanksgiving Dining Room Event!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-15**
## Executive summary

- My first reaction was that this is the seventh or eighth Crate & Barrel send I've logged in two days, after yesterday's sofa countdown and the "5-star wine glass" email. But this one is different because it targets what I'm renovating for. The dining room is the first room I'm furnishing in the second house, so a Thanksgiving dining event with 30% off tables, chairs and storage is on-category. The hero is a real table with a price, **$1,699 against a regular $2,199**, and that's what I came for.
- The problem is that the email keeps going after the dining-room pitch. It goes on through kitchenware, a design-appointment sweepstakes, the Crate card, the app and a second design-appointment ad. It's a catalog, not a campaign. Would I click? Yes, the dining tables tile, probably straight to the Pottery Barn comparison tab. Would I forward it? Only to my partner, to settle the table question.

## What's working

- The hero shows one real table with a sale price and a regular price. That tells me what the discount means in dollars.
- The 2x2 tile grid (dining tables, seating, storage, "shop all dining sale") is how I actually shop a room. I go table, then chairs, then sideboard.
- "Top kitchen deals (they ship free!)" answers the shipping-cost question up front. The Staub cast iron at up to 40% off is the strongest kitchen offer.
- The Crate card block, "10% back in Reward Dollars across all our design forward brands," speaks to how I buy. I'd run the rewards math before checkout.
- The Thanksgiving Hosting Guide gives the discount a reason to exist, which beats a flat "SALE" banner.

## What's weak

- I can't find an end date for the event. "Starts today" creates urgency, but I never see when it stops. Yesterday's sofa email had a hard deadline, and this one should too.
- Below the dining section the email turns into a grab bag. The kitchenware, the $5,000 sweepstakes, the app push and the second TheDesignDesk block each pull me somewhere else.
- TheDesignDesk appears twice with the same "Book a Free Appointment" button. It reads as filler.
- The "we love these for you" row (stainless cookware, espresso machine, wine glass, plates) has nothing to do with dining furniture. It also doesn't feel personal. It looks like the same bestseller module every Crate send uses.
- There are four or five dining CTAs ("Shop Now," "Shop Thanksgiving," "Shop The Dining Furniture Event," "Shop All Dining Furniture"). The hero button is just "Shop Now," which is vague.
- The legal fine print at the bottom is dense and tiny. For "up to" claims on select styles, I'd want exclusions visible earlier.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after the dining section and the Crate card. Make one event with one deadline, and move kitchen, app and sweepstakes to separate sends. This would help most, and it would also ease the cadence fatigue.
- 2. Put the end date in the hero and in the subject. Without a date, the event doesn't feel urgent.
- 3. Replace "Shop Now" with "Shop dining tables" and show a second hero price. A table plus chairs set price would help me budget.
- 4. Add furniture-specific information near the tiles. That means delivery windows, free swatches, and whether the Thanksgiving window is realistic for a table ordered today. That would matter more to me than cookware.
- 5. Merge the two TheDesignDesk blocks into one, below the dining tiles.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dining tables up to 30% off, ends Oct 20`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Thanksgiving table: up to 30% off dining`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Tables, chairs & sideboards on sale now. Order by Oct 20 for holiday delivery.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Extra 10% back in Reward Dollars with your Crate Card on dining furniture.`
- Compared with the others in my inbox, Pottery Barn's "Beautiful dinnerware for every day" was a mood-driven send I'd actually save, and it didn't dump six modules on me. CB2's "FINAL HOURS FOR THE UPHOLSTERY EVENT" had a hard deadline, which this email lacks. Wayfair's pergola emails with the blank hero are worse, so Crate at least renders cleanly and delivers a real offer. Article's "Fall is for full tables" has a similar dining-room theme, but it's a styled story, whereas this is a coupon.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
Crate & Barrel's email is a Marigold/Mapp-style XHTML 1.0 Transitional template with MSO conditionals. It has 66 click-tracked links and about 29 images without alt text. The automated checks found 0 hard failures and 3 warnings (57% pass rate), and all three warnings are header-capture gaps that can't be confirmed from this data. The HTML source was truncated, so findings are limited to what is visible.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** 66 tracking or click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe. Destination validity, redirect chains and dead links are unverified.
- **Repeated third-party pixels:** `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=dbe8da71…&n=1` through `n=5` are five LiveRamp pixels with the same recipient-linked `s=` hash. Only `n` changes, so these are redundant requests that add weight and third-party calls.
- **Other open/tracking pixels:** `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif?mi_u=…` and `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/16dc16c5-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…` are also present. The `mi_u` recipient hash is embedded in the `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png` image URLs too, so those images are per-recipient requests rather than cacheable static assets.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text, content images:** about 20 images lack alt text.
  - 15 are on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/*.jpg` and `…/m/47/bbf6f9d4-….png`.
  - One is on `s7d5.scene7.com/…2026%5F0920%5FCB%5FThanksgivingGuide%5FTertiary…`.
  - The seven `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png` images are probably dynamic content, such as product tiles or countdowns. These images likely carry the offer and product content, so images-off and screen-reader users get no text equivalent.
- **Missing alt text, tracking pixels:** the five `rlcdn.com` GIFs, `o.gif` and the `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/…` pixel also lack alt. If alt is absent rather than `alt=""`, screen readers may announce the filenames or URLs. Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""`.
- **Viewport blocks zoom:** `<meta name="viewport" content="…maximum-scale=1">` prevents pinch-zoom, an accessibility issue on mobile.
- **Markup problems:**
  - `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` has an unescaped `&` in an XHTML doctype. It should be `&amp;`.
  - There is a leftover template comment, `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->`.
  - `format-detection` is declared three times with overlapping values.
  - `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; … line-height:100%}` is a blanket reset with `max-height:1000000px`. It is a common Outlook and Gmail workaround, but it forces line-height on every element.
- **Dark mode:** `color-scheme: light` and `supported-color-schemes: light` explicitly opt out of dark mode. This is intentional, not a defect.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens such as `%%…%%`, `{{…}}` or `[FIRSTNAME]` appear in the visible source. The `mi_u`, `mi_cid` and `mi_mid` parameters are resolved per-recipient values. The remainder of the HTML was truncated, so tokens further down can't be ruled out.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058):** both were not found. The QA tool itself says this may be an AgentMail relay capture artifact, so it is **not a confirmed sender defect**. Since Feb 2024, Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require one-click unsubscribe, so verify against raw headers from a direct-delivery seed inbox.
- **`Authentication-Results`:** not found, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment are unknown. This is again attributed to the relay. The From domain `mail.crateandbarrel.com` is a subdomain of the brand domain, which is the expected pattern.
- **Footer unsubscribe link and postal address:** the footer is beyond the truncated source, so neither could be checked.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not verifiable. All 66 links were skipped as tracking redirects, so there is no resolved destination, UTM set or landing-page check. The `scene7` asset path (`…2026_0920_CB_ThanksgivingGuide…`) suggests creative reused from a 9/20 campaign. That is only an asset naming observation, not a defect.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Re-run the audit with unwrapped redirects.** Follow a sample of the 66 tracking links and check their destinations and UTM parameters. Raw headers from a direct-delivery seed would settle the header warnings (List-Unsubscribe, List-Unsubscribe-Post, Authentication-Results).
2. **Add descriptive `alt` text** to the ~20 content images, especially the offer and product images. Add `alt=""` to all tracking pixels.
3. **Remove `maximum-scale=1`** from the viewport meta tag to restore pinch-zoom.
4. **Consolidate the five `rlcdn.com` pixels** into one, since they share one hash and differ only in `n`.
5. **Clean up the template:** escape the `&` in the `<title>`, delete the `IMPUT HERE` placeholder comment and dedupe the `format-detection` metas.
