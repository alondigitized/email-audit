# Your first look: 1600+ NEW arrivals
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-nine. I say the number out loud because it's the only honest frame for anything Pottery Barn sends me now — this is email thirty-nine in July. And I'll be fair: when I saw "1600+" in the subject line I actually slowed down. I'm in the middle of simultaneously renovating two properties, which means there is always something on my list, and a truly massive new-inventory drop is not nothing.
- But here's the problem Pottery Barn keeps creating for itself: the hero image is genuinely gorgeous — dark, warm, leather-heavy editorial room shot, exactly the vibe I'm trying to pull off in the second-home living room — and then the CTA asks me to "Explore New Arrivals" with nothing anchoring me to a reason to buy today. No discount. No deadline. No category filter. CB2 ran a 40% furniture event this same week and followed up with urgency emails every time I didn't convert. Pottery Barn is handing me a 1,600-item catalog and calling it a first look.
- I'll save this to my inbox folder — I do that with Pottery Barn more than I should, and it's still somehow not useful because the cadence buries every genuinely relevant send. I won't click through. I won't forward it. If I told a friend "Pottery Barn's thirty-ninth July email just dropped," they'd delete me from their contacts.

## What's working

- **The hero image earns its real estate.** Dark, moody, layered — leather chairs, warm ambient lighting, floor-to-ceiling bookshelves. This is aspirational in a way that actually matches my taste. Whoever styled that shot understood the audience better than whoever approved thirty-nine sends in a month.
- **"1600+" is a real subject-line hook.** That number is specific enough to create genuine FOMO — it implies something is in there for me, even if I have to hunt for it.
- **"Recommended just for you" section.** It's algorithmic but the intent is right. The acknowledgment that they know my browsing history kept my eyes moving down when I otherwise would have closed out.
- **Clean render, no breakage.** No overlapping text, no broken images, legible at phone-screen scale. Pottery Barn's production quality is consistent — that's worth noting even when everything else is frustrating.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer.** This is a catalog with a "shop now" button attached. Article sent me a dining room email this week that felt like it was written for where I actually am in my purchase journey. This one asks me to browse on spec.
- **"Explore New Arrivals" is a nothing CTA.** Explore is what you say when you don't know what I want. Compare to Article's specific category CTAs or even Crate & Barrel's "How to nail that eclectic vibe" send from earlier this week — both gave me a landing page promise. "Explore" is a dead end verb.
- **No category segmentation in the hero.** I'm renovating two homes. I have active decisions open in living room, dining, and bedroom simultaneously. The hero shows a living room editorial but the email makes no attempt to route me. Wayfair's "Black Friday in July" was a mess of exclamation points but at least it spoke to categories.
- **The cadence has poisoned the well.** The coffee tables send — #37 in July, which I actually scored an 8 — landed when I was mid-decision and it worked because it was specific and well-timed. But when that's surrounded by thirty-eight other sends, even good emails get buried under exhaustion.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Route me by category in the hero.** Three panels: living room, dining, bedroom — each with a specific CTA. I am a known customer in the middle of a renovation; sending me to a 1,600-item firehose is the same as sending me nothing.
- 2. **Add a soft offer to create any reason to buy today.** Even "free standard shipping on new arrivals through Sunday" changes the math. CB2 has conditioned me to expect 40% off furniture. Pottery Barn is asking me to compete against that with no incentive.
- 3. **Deliver on the "first look" exclusivity the subject promises.** Say "early access ends Sunday" in the hero. One line. The subject created urgency; the body killed it completely.
- 4. **Halve the monthly send cadence, full stop.** The coffee tables email worked. This one doesn't. The difference is not the creative — it's that thirty-eight sends of noise made it impossible for any single send to matter.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop living room, dining, and bedroom — all new arrivals.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free shipping on everything that just dropped — through Sunday.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard-broken links (403) block user navigation and a cross-brand asset leak (West Elm spacer GIF) indicates an asset management error. Accessibility posture is poor with 40+ images missing alt text, and three mixed-content HTTP image loads will be silently blocked in modern clients.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Critical — Broken Links (403)**

- `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?...` — The Pottery Barn Key credit card apply-and-buy CTA returns HTTP 403. This is the primary non-member conversion link (`cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`). Any click lands on an error.
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` — Returns 403 and is also HTTP (mixed content). This is a West Elm asset incorrectly referenced in a Pottery Barn send — likely a copy-paste error from a sibling brand template.

**Tracking domains skipped** — 24 click-redirect URLs through `click.e.potterybarn.com` were not probed. These should be validated in a pre-send environment that resolves redirects end-to-end.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Mixed-content HTTP image loads (will be blocked)**

Three `src` attributes use `http://` — all will be silently dropped in Gmail, Apple Mail, and most modern clients:

| Filename | HTTP src |
|---|---|
| `spacer_0.gif` | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` |
| `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` |
| `slices_02.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` |
| Open-tracker | `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` |

**Alt text** — 40 images have no `alt` attribute set. Critical omissions include all `slices_0*.jpg` content images and both promotional banner images (`F26_TOP_SummerSalePartC_NoUrgency_thintopbanner.jpg`, `F26_Bottom_NewArrivals.jpg`, `S26_Bottom_UpTo60OffOutdoor.jpg`). When images are blocked, the email renders as a blank white rectangle with no text fallback.

**Embedded PHP headers in CSS block** — The `<style>` block near the end of `<head>` contains raw PHP `header()` calls:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are server-side PHP directives embedded in client-side CSS. They have no effect in email but signal a template assembly error — a PHP image-serving snippet was pasted into the wrong context. Harmless to render but indicates broken template tooling.

**`<title>` is empty** — `<title></title>` leaves the tab/preview label blank; some clients use this as a fallback label.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe / RFC 8058** — Neither `List-Unsubscribe` nor `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers were captured. The QA note attributes this to AgentMail relay not forwarding these headers, but the absence should be confirmed against raw SMTP headers before clearing. RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender rules (>5k/day senders); Pottery Barn at this volume almost certainly falls under that threshold.

**SPF/DKIM/DMARC** — `Authentication-Results` header not captured by relay; actual pass/fail status unknown. Cannot confirm deliverability authentication from this data.

**CAN-SPAM** — Physical mailing address and unsubscribe mechanism appear present in the footer (standard for WSI brands), but cannot be fully verified from the truncated source.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The credit card CTA link (`clickcp.potterybarn.com`) is broken (403), so continuity for that funnel is fully broken.

For content CTAs routed through `click.e.potterybarn.com`, UTM parameter structure could not be verified since those links were skipped by the HTTP probe. A manual spot-check on one decoded click URL should confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` are present and match the send.
