# All green everything
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- Eight. This is CB2 email number eight in roughly eleven days, and I clocked that before I even processed the creative. The context meter in my head is already pegged. That said — and I genuinely didn't expect to type this — the actual email is a breath of fresh air compared to the Memorial Day blitz it's following. After back-to-back "LAST CALL" and "Final hours" hammers, they've completely dropped the urgency theater and swapped in a palette-led editorial. The deep forest-green hero with the Salerno sofa is genuinely good-looking. CB2 remembered they're a taste brand.
- But here's my honest read: the creative pivot works, the cadence doesn't. If this had landed as email three or four, I'd be sitting up straight. Landing as email eight-in-eleven-days, I'm treating it the way I treat the Pottery Barn catalog that shows up after I've already ordered — appreciated, but not actionable right now. The "All green everything" angle is interesting enough that I didn't immediately swipe-delete, which is more than I can say for the app-download plea from three days ago. I'll skim. I won't click. I definitely won't forward — my renovation partner would ask why I'm still on the CB2 list at this volume.

## What's working

- **The hero image earns its real estate.** Dark forest green sectional against a warm-toned room — this is exactly the kind of moody-but-livable palette I'm testing in the second home. CB2 at their best.
- **Product naming is specific.** "The Salerno" is called out clearly. That's a name I can search later, which is how I actually shop big furniture — note it, look it up on the weekend, check if it's in the showroom.
- **The editorial pivot is strategically smart.** After seven straight promotional emails, a color-story framing feels like a reset. Pottery Barn did something similar with their pool-and-outdoor send — dropped the urgency, let the product breathe. It works.
- **Layout is clean, no render issues.** The lower product grid reads fine. No overlapping text, no broken images, no lorem ipsum showing through. The visual hierarchy from hero → sub-collections → grid is coherent.
- **Color palette is cohesive throughout.** Greens, warm woods, aged brass — every image in this email is curated into a singular mood. That takes real creative discipline.

## What's weak

- **Cadence fatigue is the elephant in the room.** Eight emails in eleven days is not a relationship — it's a siege. No amount of beautiful creative overcomes the fact that I'm already treating CB2 as background noise this week.
- **Zero urgency or offer clarity in the hero.** The Salerno is beautiful. Is it on sale? Is Memorial Day pricing still running? I genuinely can't tell from the hero, and I'm not scrolling three screens down to find out.
- **"All green everything" as a subject is editorially cute but functionally weak.** Article ran a plain "Up to 40% off ends Monday" last week — I knew exactly what I was opening. This one requires me to decide if I want to browse a mood board, which is a higher-energy ask.
- **No loyalty hook or member-tier signal.** I've bought CB2 furniture. I've been on this list for what is apparently a very long time (see: the bewildering "Welcome to CB2" email from ten days ago). Nothing in this email acknowledges that history.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a clear offer line to the hero.** Even a slim banner — "Sale styles included. Now through Sunday." — would convert the aesthetic browsing into a buying moment without killing the editorial feel. Wayfair buries the offer in emoji chaos; CB2 could thread this needle cleanly.
- 2. **Cool the send frequency for a week.** This is a brand strategy note, not a design note, but it's the highest-leverage change available. Every email from here forward gets penalized by the volume of the last eleven days.
- 3. **Give the Salerno a clickable price or "View in Showroom" CTA.** I buy furniture the way I buy appliances — I want to see it first. A "find this in your store" link would move me further down the funnel than a generic shop-the-collection button.
- 4. **Segment this send to the renovation audience.** "All green everything" in a second-home context is directly relevant to me. A brief personalization hook — "Building a room around a statement piece?" — would make this feel less like a broadcast and more like CB2 actually knows why I'm on the list.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Deep green, done right — shop the collection`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sale pricing still running. The Salerno + 40 green picks inside.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The color story we've been building toward. Shop while sale runs.`
