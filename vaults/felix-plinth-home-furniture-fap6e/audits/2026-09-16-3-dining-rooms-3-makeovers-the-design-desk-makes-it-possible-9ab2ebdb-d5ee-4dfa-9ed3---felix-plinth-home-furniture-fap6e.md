# 3 dining rooms, 3 makeovers. The Design Desk makes it possible →
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-16**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is déjà vu — I already flagged this exact "3 dining rooms, 3 makeovers" send an hour ago at a 5, and looking at it again my read hasn't really changed. The Design Desk framing — "let our pros handle your dining room makeover, no matter the size" — still hits me because I'm genuinely staring down two dining rooms right now with zero plan for either. The "small nook? no problem" / "open, functional" / "showstopping" three-scenario structure is smart because it doesn't force me to guess which one applies to me.
- But this is the second time this exact creative has landed in my inbox today, just with a different variant ID apparently, and that's the kind of thing that makes me start ignoring the sender entirely. Crate & Barrel, unlike Pottery Barn's warehouse sale email which at least organized by room with clear savings, isn't giving me a price anywhere in this whole scroll — just "Book Your Free Appointment" over and over.
- Would I click? Maybe, once — to poke at "Shop Dining Furniture" under scenario 2, since that round table setup is closest to my actual space. Forward to a friend? No. Mark as read and move on, annoyed I got it twice.

## What's working

- The three-scenario dining room structure (small space / open plan / showstopper) actually maps to real decision points, unlike CB2's usual "wall of single-item cards" approach.
- "No matter the size" directly answers the exact anxiety I have about sizing furniture for two different dining rooms.
- Repeated "Shop Dining Furniture" CTAs stay on-category the whole way down — no bait-and-switch into kids' Halloween stuff like the Crate & Kids emails keep doing.
- Product photography is clean and the room-by-room labeling ("1. small nook," "2. an open dining room," "3. showstopping") gives me a scannable structure I can act on.

## What's weak

- This is a repeat send of an email I already reviewed today — same subject, same hero, same structure. That's worse than CB2 running the Crate & Barrel family playbook twice in a week; this is the identical creative twice in one day.
- Zero pricing or discount visible anywhere in this entire scroll — "Book Your Free Appointment" isn't a purchase incentive, it's a services upsell, and I came here to buy furniture, not schedule a consultation.
- The "$500 chance" and "10% off Pottery Barn Card" mentions at the bottom are buried in tiny text after I've already scrolled past everything I care about.
- No swatch/sample program mentioned anywhere, which matters a lot when I'm picking dining chairs and finishes sight-unseen for two houses.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the send logic first — I should not be getting the same "3 dining rooms" email twice in one day. That's a segmentation/dedup bug, not a content problem, and it's the single biggest thing tanking trust here.
- 2. Put an actual number on the Design Desk offer — free appointment is fine, but pair it with something concrete like "save 15% when you book," the way Pottery Barn led with hard percentages in their warehouse sale.
- 3. Move the rewards card math ("10% off," "$500 chance") up near the top — right now it's dead last, after I've already decided whether to engage.
- 4. Add a swatch/sample CTA next to at least one dining set — finish and fabric decisions are exactly where I stall out on two-house renovations.
