# KITCHEN MATS under $30 ✨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- Three. That's how many times Wayfair has sent me "KITCHEN MATS under $30 ✨" in the last three days — July 14, July 15, and now today. I clocked the first one. I grimaced at the second. By the third, I'm not annoyed at the product — I'm annoyed at whoever runs their ESP, because this feels less like a targeted campaign and more like a system stuck in a loop that nobody caught. At some point in the last 72 hours, Wayfair stopped feeling like a brand I shop and started feeling like a brand that's spamming me.
- The email itself, viewed cold, has real bones. The lemon-print mat hero is pleasant and seasonally right for July. The 5% rewards callout is relevant to how I actually buy. The product grid under "picked just for you" has enough variety — flatweave, runner, solid, patterned — that it at least feels curated. But then I scroll up and there's a 0% APR/Affirm banner sitting above the hero mat, which makes zero sense on a $30 purchase and immediately signals that this creative was frankensteined together from other templates.
- I'm marking this read without clicking. I already skimmed this layout twice this week. If I end up needing a mat for the second kitchen, I'll go directly to Wayfair.com — or honestly, I'll check Article first since their July entryway editorial actually made me feel like they knew what I was building.

## What's working

- The lemon-print hero mat is visually appealing and season-appropriate — it looks like July in a good way
- 5% rewards callout is legitimately relevant; I track rewards across cards and this matters to my buying math
- "Picked just for you" product grid has genuine variety — enough styles that someone with taste could actually choose
- Price anchor ($30) is clearly communicated from subject to body with no bait-and-switch
- Renders clean on mobile — no broken images, no overlapping copy, no placeholder text

## What's weak

- Third identical subject line in three consecutive days — at this point it's a suppression logic failure, not a campaign
- The 0% APR / Affirm banner is floating above the mat hero on a sub-$30 email — wildly mismatched and it breaks the opening visual hierarchy
- "Score styles you 💛" as preheader is brand-speak that evaporates — it doesn't add anything the subject didn't already say
- No single clear CTA; the email has at least four distinct clickable zones competing for the same scroll
- Treated me like a first-time visitor — no mention of rewards balance, no "shop for your second home," nothing that suggests Wayfair knows I've been a customer all month

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the suppression rule first.** Don't resend the same subject to someone who opened or didn't click within 48 hours. Pottery Barn managed to send me 25 emails in July without looping on the same one — Wayfair has sent this same send three times to the same inbox.
- 2. **Kill the APR/Affirm banner in this email.** It belongs in a furniture or big-ticket appliance context — not adjacent to a $30 mat. The July 15 Affirm email already did that job.
- 3. **Collapse to one CTA.** Either "Shop kitchen mats" or "See your picks" — not both, plus a rewards reminder, plus a financing banner. Pick a lane.
- 4. **Personalize the product grid header.** I have two active shipping addresses. Something like "Two kitchens, twice the upgrades" or even just surfacing my rewards balance ("You've earned $X back — use it here") would have changed my posture completely.
- 5. **Reframe the price positioning.** CB2 and Pottery Barn both anchor on quality-for-price when they go under a threshold — Wayfair leads with the floor price but doesn't tell me *why* these are worth $30. "Machine washable. Ships free. Under $30." would be a sharper three-word value stack.
