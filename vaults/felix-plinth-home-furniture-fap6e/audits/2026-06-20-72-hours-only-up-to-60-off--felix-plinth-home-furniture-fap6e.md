# ⏰ 72 hours only: UP TO 60% OFF ⏰
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- Sixty-two. That's the Wayfair count in my inbox now, and this one landed the same day as the flameless candles email — the one where I opened expecting to actually *see* flameless candles and got three lines of category tiles instead. So I'm walking in already slightly annoyed, and the subject line is doing the exact thing I've come to expect: clock emojis, "72 hours only," "UP TO 60% OFF." Emphasis on *up to*.
- That said — I will concede this one is cleaner than most of what Wayfair has sent me lately. The "72-HR CLEAROUT" hero reads instantly. The four category discount tiles right below it (50% off living room, 45% off home office) are actually useful to me right now since I'm mid-furnishing two properties simultaneously. And the "picked just for you" section in the middle is the first time I've seen Wayfair even gesture at personalization in the last dozen sends. Products with star ratings, actual photos I can assess at a glance — that part earns some credit.
- But then I scroll and it falls apart into the usual grid of everything at once: outdoor sheds, entryway benches, exercise equipment, storage — the whole warehouse dumped into one email. The Afterpay placement is weirdly prominent for a sale event; if the discount is real, why lead with financing? Compare this to the Pottery Barn "On sale & ready-to-ship" send I reviewed recently — they pinned lead times and shipping status right in the hero, which is exactly what I need when I'm ordering a sofa for a house that needs to be livable by August. Wayfair just gives me percentages and a grid. I'd mark this as read. I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- **"72-HR CLEAROUT" hero lands immediately** — large type, purple brand color, no ambiguity about what this email is selling.
- **Four category tiles with specific discount percentages** — 50% living room, 45% home office, 40% home & exercise, 30% bedroom is actually useful segmentation for someone furnishing multiple rooms.
- **"Picked just for you" is the right instinct** — product thumbnails with visible star ratings are the closest this email gets to making me feel like a known customer rather than a list entry.
- **72-hour deadline feels credible** — it's bounded, it's specific, it doesn't say "ENDS SOON" with no date like CB2 did in the "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" send.
- **Wayfair Rewards visible in footer** — small, but it's there; a member gets a reminder that this sale compounds on points.

## What's weak

- **"Up to 60% off" is doing a lot of hedging work** — the actual category discounts I can see are 30–50%. That 60% figure is the ceiling for one thing buried somewhere in the clearout. Article wouldn't run a hero that said "up to" anything; they'd just show the price.
- **Two emails in one day is too many** — the flameless candles email went out June 20 too. I haven't processed one before the next one arrives. Cadence is broken.
- **Afterpay placement feels off-tone for a clearout** — if you're selling me a genuine 60% off event, why is there a financing callout mid-scroll? It undercuts the "great deal" framing.
- **The lower scroll devolves into everything at once** — outdoor sheds, yoga equipment, entryway benches. Pottery Barn's "On sale & ready-to-ship" kept category focus tight. This loses the thread.
- **No shipping info anywhere visible** — I am buying furniture. I need lead times. The Category Tiles give me a discount; they don't tell me if the sofa ships in 3 days or 8 weeks.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill one of the June 20 sends** — or at minimum make them thematically different. Same-day sends from the same brand with the same "sale" framing train subscribers to batch-ignore.
- 2. **Anchor the hero to one specific category with a real number** — not "up to 60% off everything." More like "Home Office: 45% off this weekend" targeted to what the "picked just for you" data already knows about me.
- 3. **Move "picked just for you" above the category grid** — personalization is your best asset in this email; don't bury it. Article leads with the specific collection or product; Wayfair buries the personal touch in row three.
- 4. **Add a shipping confidence line to the hero** — something like "Most in-stock items ship within 5 days" at the hero level would have me clicking immediately. I'm on a timeline. Pottery Barn figured this out.
- 5. **Drop the Afterpay feature** — it belongs in a financing-focused email, not a clearout event.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `word-break: break-all` on product names (mobile):**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) {
  .ProductRec-name { word-break: break-all; ... }
}
```
`break-all` splits words at arbitrary character positions (e.g., "Chair" → "Cha-ir"). `break-word` or `overflow-wrap: break-word` is the correct value for wrapping long product names without mid-word breaks. This will visually mangle product titles on iOS/Android.

**Issue — Web font with no broad fallback stack:**
```css
@font-face { font-family: "Sofia"; src: url(...sofia_reg.woff); mso-font-alt: "Arial" }
```
`mso-font-alt` handles Outlook only. Gmail (webmail + Android), Samsung Mail, and other clients that strip `<style>` will silently fall back to the user-agent default — not "Arial". The font stack on elements using `font-family: Sofia` should explicitly declare: `"Sofia", Arial, sans-serif` to guarantee a safe fallback everywhere.
