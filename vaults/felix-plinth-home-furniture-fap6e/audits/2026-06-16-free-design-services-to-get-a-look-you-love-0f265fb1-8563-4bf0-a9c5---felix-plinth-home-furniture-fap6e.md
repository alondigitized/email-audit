# FREE design services to get a look you love
**Score:** 10/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-two. I'm not even going to wait — that's where the Pottery Barn counter sits in my inbox as of this morning, and this one lands from a different sender line — "Pottery Barn Design Services" — which is either a clever list segmentation move or just PB covering all their bases before Sunday's sale cutoff. I clocked the same tactic from CB2 Design Services a few days ago and it worked slightly better there because CB2's overall email volume hadn't completely numbed me yet. Here, I know PB so well I can identify the font before I read the words.
- Here's what makes this one actually hard to ignore: I'm renovating two properties simultaneously and free interior design help is exactly the kind of service I'd pay for at a place like Decorist or even at a PB showroom. So the offer lands. But the hero bedroom image — all floral pattern and saturated jewel tones — does not look like either of my rooms or my aesthetic, and it knocked me sideways for a second. The dining and living room shots below it recovered some ground.
- Would I click? Yes — specifically on "Book an Appointment," not "Shop the Style." The patio sale with a Sunday June 21 cutoff is a bonus close I didn't expect. I would not forward this to anyone; I'd file it in my inbox folder and open the appointment booking page while my coffee brews.

## What's working

- **Offer is genuinely useful.** Free design services from a brand I'm already spending serious money with, while I'm mid-renovation on two properties — this is the right email for this moment.
- **Sub-sender creates novelty.** "Pottery Barn Design Services" reads differently from the main PB sender in my inbox; it didn't auto-collapse into the pile of 21 prior PB promos.
- **"Book an Appointment" CTA is unambiguous.** Verb plus noun, no "Discover More" vagueness. CB2 Design Services used "Schedule Your Appointment" — same energy, equally clear.
- **Personalized product rail ("Recommended Just For You")** is at least attempting to show me things tied to my purchase history rather than default bestsellers.
- **Key Rewards loyalty section at the footer** is smart for me specifically — I always cross-reference which card to run the purchase on, and seeing my loyalty tier reminder sharpens that decision.

## What's weak

- **Hero bedroom image skews decidedly feminine.** Floral, saturated, maximalist — that's not how I think about either renovation. Article's home-tour emails use real, slightly editorial lifestyle shots that feel gender-neutral; this opening shot felt like it was pulled from the women's catalog side.
- **Design services section is light on specifics.** What do I actually get? How long is a session? In-store only, virtual, or both? Wayfair's recent "8' X 10' OUTDOOR RUGS" email was annoying for being overly literal, but at least I knew exactly what I was clicking into. Here I'd be booking a mystery appointment.
- **No design-services-specific time urgency.** The Sunday June 21 deadline is pinned to the patio sale, not to the design appointment offer — so the CTA with the clearest deadline is for a secondary offer, not the hero.
- **Floral bedroom as the opening visual is a mismatch** for anyone shopping the more architectural, mixed-material direction I'm going with both properties.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with a project-oriented hero, not a style shot.** Show a before/after or a split renovation scene — something that signals "this is for people rebuilding a room from scratch," not "this is bedding inspiration." Article does this better with their home-tour format.
- 2. **Add a what-you-get summary.** Three bullets: session length, virtual vs. in-store options, what deliverables I walk away with. Remove all ambiguity from the appointment CTA.
- 3. **Attach a deadline to the design appointment offer itself.** "Book before June 21 and get priority scheduling" or similar — leverage the existing Sunday cutoff across both offers, not just the patio sale.
- 4. **Swap the hero image for something neutral/architectural.** The dining room shot lower in the email is actually better for me than the opening bedroom.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Book a free design session — two-home renovators, this is for you`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Your free PB design consult is waiting (here's what to expect)`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Virtual or in-store, our design experts work around your schedule — no fee, no obligation.`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Tell us what you're building. We'll help you get the look — completely free.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[FAIL] PHP `header()` calls injected into `<style>` block**
The second `<style>` block in `<head>` opens with:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are PHP server-side directives that were never executed — they leaked as literal text into the CSS block. No browser or email client interprets them as CSS; they are silently ignored. The presence indicates a template preprocessing failure where a PHP file's header logic was copied raw into the HTML template. This is a code hygiene issue; it poses no rendering risk but signals a broken build/template pipeline.

**[FAIL] Two images served over HTTP (not HTTPS)**
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail proxy/block mixed-content HTTP images. Both are footer assets and will render as broken images in most modern clients. These should be `https://`.

**[WARN] 38 images missing alt text**
Every content slice (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_14.jpg`), all footer slices (14 images), product images (bed, quilt, towel, etc.), and tracking pixels have `alt=""` or no `alt` attribute. Since the email is entirely image-based (sliced JPGs carry all copy and CTAs), subscribers rendering with images off receive a blank email with no actionable text fallback. Slices carrying primary CTAs should have descriptive alt text at minimum.

**[WARN] Inconsistent responsive breakpoints**
Two media queries target different widths:
- `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)` → sets `table[class="wrapper"]` to 320px
- `@media screen and (max-width: 650px)` → toggles `.mobile-hide`/`.mobile-show`

The 480px breakpoint collapses to 320px, which is below the viewport of virtually all modern smartphones (375px minimum). The effective mobile layout will be 320px wide with visible side margins on most phones. This appears to be a stale legacy breakpoint from a 2016-era template.
