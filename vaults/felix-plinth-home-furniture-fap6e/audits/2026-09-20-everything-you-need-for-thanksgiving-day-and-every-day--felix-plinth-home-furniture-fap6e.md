# Everything you need for Thanksgiving day…and every day!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-20**
## Executive summary

- Okay, my first reaction: didn't I already get this exact email? "The Thanksgiving Hosting Guide" hit my inbox on the same day, 2026-09-20, scored a 7 from me, and this is basically the same guide — tonal tableware, cookware, the toaster oven, pie stuff, dining tables, Venus Williams collab at the bottom — just repackaged under a new subject line, "Everything you need for Thanksgiving day…and every day!" That's a little sloppy on Crate & Barrel's part. I don't need two versions of the same seasonal roundup in one day.
- That said, on its own merits it's a well-shot, cleanly organized email — every section does its job (tonal table, glassware, cookware, kitchen appliances, bakeware, tools, dining furniture, recipe, design consult) and nothing looks broken. But there's zero actual offer anywhere in this thing. No percent off, no free shipping banner, nothing. Compare that to Pottery Barn's "Ships Free: Cozy Throws" email, which at least led with a concrete shipping incentive — this is just a lookbook with "Shop Now" buttons.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I scroll to the dining table section since I'm mid-renovation on two houses and could use a real table. Would I forward it? No. Mark as read and move on — I already saw this content once today.

## What's working

- The hero "Thanksgiving hosting guide" framing is clean and immediately tells me what this is — no guessing.
- The tonal tableware section has visible star ratings under the products, which gives me some social proof I don't usually see in these grid emails.
- "A table made for gathering around" section with the actual dining table shot is the one part that's relevant to my current renovation — I need real furniture, not another cheese board.
- No render bugs — every image loaded, layout is tight, nothing overlapping. That alone puts it ahead of Wayfair's shower curtain email where the hero was just blank white space.
- The Venus Williams collab card at the bottom is a nice surprise beat that breaks up the product-grid monotony CB2 and C&B usually fall into.

## What's weak

- This is a near-duplicate of the same-day "Thanksgiving Hosting Guide" send — same content, different subject. That's cadence fatigue I called out with the Crate & Kids "England Collection" running three times this month; now C&B proper is doing it too.
- No offer anywhere. Not even a modest free-shipping line. Pottery Barn's cozy-throws email beat this by leading with a real incentive.
- Every CTA is just "Shop Now" — repeated eight-plus times down the page. Zero differentiation in copy, so nothing tells me which section actually has something worth my click.
- This is entirely tableware/kitchen/hosting content — nothing about the dining table's dimensions, wood finish options, or lead times, which is the actual info I need mid-renovation.
- No loyalty or rewards messaging, even though I'm buying on a rewards card specifically to rack up points — C&B doesn't acknowledge that at all.

## Recommendations

- 1. Don't send two "Thanksgiving guide" emails with overlapping content on the same day — pick one angle (either the hosting guide or the "everything you need" framing) and kill the redundant send.
- 2. Add one real offer — even 15% off dining or free shipping over $75 — somewhere above the fold. Right now there's no reason to act today versus next week.
- 3. Vary the CTA copy per section — "Shop Cookware," "Set the Table," "Browse Dining" — instead of eight identical "Shop Now" buttons.
- 4. Move the dining table section higher — that's the highest-intent content for someone like me who's actually furnishing a home, not just prepping a holiday spread.
- 5. Surface loyalty points or rewards-card messaging near the CTA, since that's literally how I decide which retailer wins my cart.
