#  🔔 PSA 🔔 UP TO 70% OFF is HEREEE 🔔
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- Seventy-four. That's where the Wayfair pile sits now, and this one lands the same day as "STATEMENT HEADBOARDS THIS WAY →" — which I also had to sit through on June 27. Two Wayfair emails in one day, and this is the second. I know what I'm getting before I even open it: a grid of mismatched product tiles, a discount percentage with "up to" buried somewhere, and a subject line so loud it makes me feel like I'm being yelled at by a brand that knows my address but not my name.
- The "PSA" and "HEREEE" thing is doing the most. I get what they're going for — it's trying to feel like a text from a friend who found a deal. But I am a 36-year-old man pricing out countertops for two kitchens simultaneously, not a college sophomore panic-shopping dorm decor at 2am. The triple bell emoji in the subject is genuinely the kind of thing I have to suppress an eye-roll over. Article's "4th of July Sale starts now" from the same week was five words and zero emoji and I still clicked.
- Inside the email, the Fourth of July Clearance framing is legitimate and the discounts are real — 50% and 70% off tiles are visible. But the product grid is scattershot: dining chairs next to a ring-light mirror next to plates next to area rugs. I'm in the middle of furnishing two homes. I need one well-targeted hero product, not a flea market layout. I'd mark this read and go back to the headboard email from this morning if I needed to actually buy something.

## What's working

- **Fourth of July Clearance is a real hook.** Seasonal campaign, legitimate clearance framing — this is actually a reasonable time for me to be in the market for big-ticket pieces.
- **"Picked just for you" section.** The personalization module in the middle gave me actual pause — a ring/accent table and what looked like a storage console. That's the only part of this email where it felt like Wayfair was talking to someone who'd shopped there before.
- **The discount numbers are clear.** 50%, 40%, 70% off — numbers are legible, not hidden. Stars visible on some tiles in the personalized section add a minimal trust signal.
- **Free delivery over $35 callout.** Still the single most useful piece of copy for furniture consideration. It's there. That matters.

## What's weak

- **The subject is a spam filter in human form.** Three bell emoji, "PSA," and "HEREEE" with three E's — this is the subject line equivalent of someone standing outside your door with an air horn. Even if I would have opened it, I'm embarrassed by how it looks in my inbox alongside Pottery Barn's clean sends.
- **Same day as the headboards email.** Two Wayfair emails on June 27 is two too many. Pottery Barn spaced their 4th of July messaging more intelligently, and CB2 at least varied the creative register between sends. Wayfair just fires again.
- **Grid layout has no anchor.** I can't identify the hero product or the hero deal. It's tiles all the way down — dining chairs, a mirror, plates, storage boxes, rugs. Zero editorial curation. Article sends a single hero image and a single CTA and I look up the sofa. Wayfair sends me a department store circular.
- **"Up to 70% off" is doing no work.** The subject promises 70% — the email delivers a field of 40% tiles. "Up to" is the universal signal for "the thing you want is probably not included."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the emoji and the "HEREEE."** The subject reads as desperation from a brand that knows it's emailing me too much. One clean declarative line would land harder with someone who just spent 20 minutes comparing Crate & Barrel sofa legs.
- 2. **One hero product, one hero deal.** Pick the most relevant item for a returning customer in the renovation/furniture segment and lead with it. Not a grid. One product, one price, one CTA.
- 3. **Don't send twice on the same day.** Merge the headboards send and this clearance send into a single email or stagger them by 48 hours. The cadence is eroding whatever trust the brand history built.
- 4. **Make the personalization section the lead.** "Picked just for you" is buried mid-scroll. That's the highest-value module in this email — put it first, right after the clearance banner.
- 5. **Spell out what "clearance" means for the customer.** Ends Sunday? Final quantities? No returns? Give me the actual constraint so the urgency feels credible instead of manufactured.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your clearance picks: 50–70% off, this weekend only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your saved styles are in the sale. Free delivery over $35.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Dining, rugs, storage — clearance prices through Sunday.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — fixed-height product name truncation without ellipsis:**

```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) {
  .ProductRec-name {
    word-break: break-all;
    overflow: hidden;
    display: block;
    height: 48px !important;
  }
}
```

`overflow: hidden` with a fixed `height` clips text silently — no `text-overflow: ellipsis` or `-webkit-line-clamp` to signal truncation. Long product names will be invisibly cut on mobile. `word-break: break-all` also breaks mid-word, which degrades readability.

**Also flagged:**
- All `.u-Title` variants collapse to a single `32px` on mobile regardless of hierarchy — `--primary`, `--secondary`, `--tertiary`, `--white` share identical breakpoint rules with no differentiation.

**No issues found** in the media query structure itself or the mobile layout utility classes (`.u-Block`, `.u-HalfWidthBlock`, etc.) — these follow standard email responsive patterns.
