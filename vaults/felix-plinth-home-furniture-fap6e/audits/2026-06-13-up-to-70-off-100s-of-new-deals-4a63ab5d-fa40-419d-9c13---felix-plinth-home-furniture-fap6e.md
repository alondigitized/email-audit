# Up to 70% off 100s of NEW DEALS 🎉
**Score:** 10/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- Fifteen. That's the number I'm carrying into this email, and it does more damage than anything in the creative. June 13, another "Up to 70% off" Pottery Barn send — the fifteenth since Tuesday, by my count. The June 12 "NEW DEALS ADDED" email already ran essentially this hero: creamy sofa in a staged living room, same percentage, same button copy. I had to scroll back in my folder to confirm this wasn't a re-send. It wasn't. But it's indistinguishable enough that my thumb almost hit delete before the image loaded.
- What saves this email is the "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" section. Six product tiles, apparently seeded from my browse history or purchase behavior, sitting in the middle of an otherwise generic category grid. After fourteen identical-feeling emails, one moment of actual personalization is remarkable — in the way a stranger saying your name is remarkable after an hour of small talk.
- I'm marking this read without clicking. Wayfair's "Open Box steals" from June 12 earned a click from me by doing something structurally different. Article's summer hosting email earned a click because the photography actually rotated. This email earns nothing because it spent its credibility on emails eleven through fourteen.

## What's working

- The category grid — Furniture, Outdoor, Sofas & Chairs, Bedding, Decor, Lighting, Rugs — maps cleanly to what I'm actively buying for two properties. Functions like a shortcut menu rather than a generic homepage redirect.
- "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" section is the only thing in this email that feels like PB looked at my account. Six tiles with specific items breaks the template monotony in a meaningful way.
- "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" is visible and legible at a glance. I run all PB spend through the Key card specifically for the math — reinforcing that in-email is useful.
- "Free Design Service" callout lands for me. I've used a PB design consult before and I'd consider booking again with two rooms in progress simultaneously.
- Affirm placement is appropriate — visible for high-ticket furniture consideration without being the lead.

## What's weak

- This is functionally the same hero as the June 12 "NEW DEALS ADDED" send and the June 12 "UP TO 70% OFF" send. Three emails with the same "70% off + new deals" structure in 24 hours erodes the signal on all three.
- The sofa hero is soft-focus generic — nothing dates it to June 2026 vs. any Tuesday in the catalog era. Compare to Article, whose lifestyle photography actually rotates by collection and reads seasonal. This reads like a template fill.
- "SHOP ALL SALE" isn't a CTA — it's a direction. CB2's "ALMOST OVER: Design Legends Sale" gave me a named collection with a specific endpoint. This gives me a door to a warehouse I've visited fourteen times.
- No deadline anywhere visible. "Up to 70% off" with no end date reads as a permanent condition by now, which ironically kills urgency. Crate & Barrel's outdoor email at least named a seasonal reason to act.
- "NEW DEALS" in partial all-caps plus the 🎉 emoji in the subject has a mild promotional-folder vibe — nothing egregious, but it reads lower-effort against Article's clean no-emoji approach.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Flip the order — lead with "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" above the category grid.** The personalized tiles are the only differentiated content in this email. Burying them below a navigation bar I've seen fifteen times means most subscribers never reach them.
- 2. **Put a hard deadline in the hero and the subject.** "Ends Sunday" or "72 hours left" would change my behavior. The absence of any expiration means this offer has no urgency gradient — same as email #11.
- 3. **Name what's actually new.** "100s of new deals" is a boast, not a signal. "40+ lighting styles just added to sale" or "New: outdoor furniture drops this morning" is a reason to open. Tell me the specific category that triggered this send.
- 4. **Rest one day.** A single skipped day makes email #16 feel like an event. The current cadence has trained me to process PB emails as background noise — the same place Wayfair has been for two months.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your personalized picks are in — plus 70% off across furniture, outdoor, and more.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Lighting, sofas, outdoor seating — here's what dropped in the sale since Thursday.`
