# People coming for dinner? Start here →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-15**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one actually lands closer to my life than the last four C&B emails combined — "the dining room edit: meet our bestsellers" and then wall-to-wall dining tables. We're mid-renovation on two houses and I genuinely do need a dining table for at least one of them, so a subject like "People coming for dinner? Start here" paired with actual product — Lakin, Terra, Roseau, Yukon — is the first thing from this brand all week that isn't background noise. That said, it's still just... a table catalog. Four bestseller tables in a row with matching chairs, star ratings, and swatch chips is competent but not exciting, and by table number three my eyes glazed the way they did with the flatware guide.
- I'd scroll through this one on my phone during coffee, maybe tap into the Roseau table since it's got the nicer wishbone-style chairs, but I'm not forwarding this to anyone — there's no single hero moment that says "look at this," just a lineup. Compare this to CB2's "Onyx & Walnut" send, which had one genuinely arresting hero shot I actually stopped scrolling for — this has four decent shots and no standout. I'd mark it read, maybe bookmark the Roseau table, and move on.

## What's working

- Subject actually matches a real scenario I have — hosting, needing a table — unlike the nursery chair or bunk bed emails clogging the same inbox today.
- Star ratings under Terra and Roseau give a little social proof I can trust at a glance.
- Swatch/finish chips next to each table (walnut, natural oak, black, etc.) are exactly the kind of material info I actually need mid-renovation — better than Pottery Barn's bed email, which didn't show finish options this clearly.
- "Bring the room together" section with bar carts, cabinets, and lighting is a sensible upsell that stays in the same room, not a random category jump.
- The bedroom refresh + $500 gift card banner near the bottom is a real, specific offer, not vague "shop now" language.

## What's weak

- Four nearly identical table-and-chairs blocks in a row is a lot of scrolling for one room category — Article's "editorial mode" sends at least vary the pitch even without a discount.
- No percentage-off or dollar offer on the actual dining furniture — the only real deal (bedroom, $500 gift card) is buried two-thirds down, disconnected from the dining hook up top.
- "Bestseller" is stamped on basically every table, which dilutes the word — if everything's a bestseller, nothing is.
- The "we love these for you" grid at the bottom feels like generic algorithmic filler tacked on after the email already made its point.
- No delivery/lead-time info anywhere, which matters a lot for dining tables specifically — I'm renovating on a timeline and "in stock" vs "8-week lead time" changes whether I click at all.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the four-table lineup to two, and lead with whichever has the best sell-through — Roseau, based on the chair styling — then let "shop all dining" handle the rest. Nobody needs four full product blocks before the fold ends.
- 2. Move the $500 gift card offer up near the top or at minimum tease it in the preheader — right now the strongest offer in the email is the one most people will never scroll to.
- 3. Add a one-line delivery/lead-time note under at least the hero table ("ships in 2 weeks" or similar) — this is the single biggest missing signal for someone actually furnishing a house right now.
- 4. Drop "bestseller" from at least two of the four tables so it means something when it's used.

- **Subject Alt B:** `4 dining tables, ready before your next dinner party`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: a $500 gift card with our credit card`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `In-stock tables, real lead times, no guesswork`
