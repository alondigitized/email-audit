# Tell us what you think for a $1000 gift card
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-04**
## Executive summary

- Okay, I open this and my first thought is: didn't I already see this? Literally an hour ago it was "Your chance to win a $1,000 Gift Card" with a twin bed hero, and now it's "Tell us what you think for a $1000 gift card" with a living room hero — same sweepstakes, same $1,000, same August 31 deadline, different lead image. This is the fifth Crate & Barrel-family email in my inbox today and the campaign fatigue is real.
- That said, buried under the review-sweepstakes ask, the actual content isn't bad — there's a legit "we love these for you" grid with a dining table, king bed, nightstand, and media console, all with real dimensions and prices, which is exactly the kind of reno-relevant stuff I'd screenshot into my Pinterest folder. But it's wedged between a corn-and-tomatoes farmers-market recipe block and a credit card upsell, so the hierarchy is a mess — I don't know if this is a review ask, a produce newsletter, a furniture sale, or a card pitch.
- Verdict: I'm not clicking "Write a Review," I've got two houses of actual furniture decisions to make and this isn't one of them. I might scroll past to the product grid out of habit, but I'm not forwarding this — if anything I'd screenshot the duplicate-subject-line problem and send it to a friend who works in email marketing as a "look at this mess" joke.

## What's working

- The hero shot of the neutral, mid-century-adjacent living room is exactly my aesthetic — matches the vibe I've been pulling for both houses.
- The "we love these for you" grid has real specs (82"-118" extendable, solid wood, weathered finish) and prices up front — no click-to-find-out-price nonsense.
- The Aspen collection tableware block with "15% off" is a clean, specific, single-message section — best-designed block in the whole email.
- Crate Card callout ("now earning Reward Dollars") is at least trying to speak to someone like me who buys on the highest-rewards card.

## What's weak

- This is a near-duplicate of the "Your chance to win a $1,000 Gift Card" send I already opened today — same sweepstakes, same deadline, just a reshuffled hero. That's not a new email, that's a resend with different art.
- Seven different asks stacked in one send (review sweepstakes, recipes, tableware sale, furniture grid, credit card, app download, design service) — nobody's hierarchy needs is that broad, and nothing gets to breathe.
- The corn/tomatoes "in season now" recipe block has zero connection to furniture or renovation — it reads like a different brand's newsletter got pasted in.
- No star ratings or review counts anywhere near the products, which is ironic for an email whose entire hero CTA is "write a review."
- "Write a Review" is the visually dominant CTA, but it's the least relevant action for someone mid-reno who wants to shop, not opine.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop sending the same $1,000 sweepstakes under a new subject line the same day — CB2 already burned me out this week running near-identical "on sale" emails back to back, and now the parent brand is doing it too. Pick one creative, one send, done.
- 2. Cut the recipe block entirely, or move it to a separate seasonal-lifestyle newsletter — it has nothing to do with furniture and just adds scroll fatigue.
- 3. Lead with the Aspen 15% off or the furniture grid, not the review ask — Article and Wayfair both lead with an actual product or design story, and I open those first even when I'm not shopping.
- 4. Add star ratings to the furniture grid products — if you want me to trust the review sweepstakes premise, show me existing reviews are worth something.
- 5. Drop either the credit card block or the app-download block — having both plus a design-service CTA in the same footer stretch is one ask too many.
