# WIN A CUBS GAME
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-09**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "WIN A CUBS GAME" — this is not a subject line I expected out of CB2, and I'll admit it got me to open it faster than the fabric-swatch send from 8/8. I'm not even a huge Cubs guy, but club-level tickets plus a $2,000 travel voucher plus a $1,000 CB2 gift card is a genuinely fun sweepstakes stack, and the hero photo — that big tufted sectional with the marble coffee table — is exactly the kind of room I'd screenshot for my own living room mood board. Problem is, after that hero, the whole thing deflates into the same "NEW & BEST SELLING" cocktail-glass banner and app/rewards footer grid I've seen in every CB2 and Crate & Barrel email this week.
- This isn't a click for me in the sense of buying anything — it's a maybe-enter-the-sweepstakes email, not a shop email, and CB2 knows that, which is honestly refreshing after a week of "STEALS" and "AVAILABLE AGAIN" banners. I'd enter for the tickets more out of curiosity than brand loyalty, and I might actually forward this to my buddy who's a bigger Cubs fan than me — that's a real forward, not a hypothetical one. But I'm marking it read after that, not browsing rugs.

## What's working

- The Cubs tie-in is genuinely different — after CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" and two straight discount-banner sends, a sweepstakes hook is a nice change of cadence.
- The prize stack is spelled out in real numbers ($2,000 travel voucher, $1,000 gift card, club-level tickets, on-field access) instead of vague "enter to win big" copy — that's the kind of specificity Wayfair's slipcorner email got right and most sweepstakes emails skip.
- The hero room shot is on-brand and aspirational — that sectional and coffee table combo is a "yeah I'd buy that" scene, not a stock-photo throwaway.
- One clear CTA button ("ENTER NOW") sits right under the offer with no scrolling needed.

## What's weak

- Zero mention of what furniture is even eligible or featured beyond the sectional in the photo — I can't tell if this ties to a specific collection I could shop, unlike Pottery Barn's "PRINT & PATTERN" story send that at least named a chair.
- The second half — "NEW & BEST SELLING" with a cocktail glass set — has nothing to do with the Cubs hook and reads like a bolted-on afterthought; it kills the momentum the hero built.
- No mention of odds, entry deadline date, or how many entries I get — just a tiny dagger-footnote at the very bottom in gray-on-white I'd need to zoom to read.
- This is the third CB2/C&B-family email in my inbox this week (after 8/8's fabric send and the "Love at First Sit" send), and none of them acknowledge the others — no "last chance," no thread, just another standalone drop.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the "NEW & BEST SELLING" glassware block entirely for this send — it's diluting a genuinely interesting hook with unrelated inventory. Let the sweepstakes be the whole email.
- 2. Put the entry deadline and odds/eligibility summary in actual body copy, not buried footnote text — "Enter by 8/16" builds real urgency Pottery Barn and Wayfair rarely bother with.
- 3. Name the sectional and coffee table in the hero shot with a "Shop this room" link — turns the aspirational photo into a second, tertiary CTA without competing with "ENTER NOW."
- 4. Add a rewards-member callout ("Rewards members get bonus entries") to reward the 10%-back program CB2 already pushes in the footer — right now the sweepstakes and loyalty program don't talk to each other.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Enter to win Cubs tickets + a $1,000 CB2 gift card`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Club-level Cubs seats, $2K travel, your call`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ends 8/16 — no purchase necessary, odds and details inside`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Two tickets, a $2K travel voucher, and $1,000 to spend at CB2`
