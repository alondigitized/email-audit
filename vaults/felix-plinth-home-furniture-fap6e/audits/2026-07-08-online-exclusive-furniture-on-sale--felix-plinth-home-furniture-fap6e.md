# Online exclusive: Furniture ON SALE
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- Eleven. I've now received eleven Pottery Barn emails since roughly July 4th, and this one lands with "Online exclusive: Furniture ON SALE" which, I'll admit, actually cuts through the fatigue because furniture is the exact thing I'm buying right now for two properties simultaneously. The hero delivers — Summer Sale, up to 70% off, organized by collection (Sorrento, Livingston, Farmhouse). That structure is useful. I can immediately map Sorrento to the second home's outdoor-adjacent spaces and Livingston to the primary's living room project.
- But then the email just... keeps going. Sale furniture, then "Recommended Just For You," then a whole "Discover New Arrivals" module, then an app download ask, then a credit card pitch. That's four distinct conversion goals crammed into one scroll. CB2 ran 14 emails in 8 days this month and I started ignoring them around email 10 — Pottery Barn is on the same trajectory for me. The cadence is the real damage here, not the creative.
- I'd click the Sorrento or Livingston collection links because I'm actively in the market. I would not forward this — forwarding implies "you need to see this," and what I'd actually text my wife is the specific product link after I click through, not the whole email.

## What's working

- **Collection-first hierarchy.** Breaking the sale into Sorrento / Livingston / Farmhouse is exactly how I shop — I'm mentally matching room aesthetics, not hunting a single SKU.
- **70% off is a number that earns a scroll.** The hero communicates the ceiling discount clearly; that's a real promise that makes me want to verify it.
- **"Recommended Just For You" section.** I notice this and I appreciate the signal that Pottery Barn is pulling from my browse/purchase history. Wayfair does this well; Pottery Barn executing it here is a trust cue.
- **10% back in rewards is visible.** I run everything through the highest-rewards card, so seeing the loyalty benefit inline reminds me to check whether the PB card is actually the right instrument for a furniture purchase at this discount level.
- **"Online Exclusive" framing.** Smart angle — it implies I can't get this in-store, which for someone who does hybrid (showroom browse → online buy) actually creates a reason to act digitally right now.

## What's weak

- **New Arrivals module inside a sale email is a strategy collision.** If I'm here for the 70% off, a "Discover New Arrivals" section at full price pulls in the opposite direction. Crate & Barrel tends to send clean, single-purpose emails — this one tries to be both a clearance event and a product launch.
- **App download ask in the middle of checkout momentum.** Nobody is pausing a furniture purchase consideration to download an app. That module belongs in a post-purchase confirmation, not here.
- **No visible deadline on the sale.** "Summer Sale" is open-ended. Article ran a "Last chance: ends today" email last week that actually forced my hand — Pottery Barn's version gives me permission to procrastinate.
- **The credit card pitch competes with the primary offer.** I already know about the PB card. Pitching it mid-email is a context switch that stalls the browse-to-click journey.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is the eleventh email in under five days. Even a strong offer is harder to take seriously when the brand has been crying wolf on "LAST DAY" and "HOURS LEFT" for a week straight.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill two of the four conversion modules.** One email, one goal. Keep the sale with the collection structure and the loyalty rewards note. Cut the New Arrivals module, the app download, and the credit card CTA — those belong in separate sends or in the post-purchase flow.
- 2. **Add a sale end date in the hero.** "Ends Sunday" or "Ends July 13" would do more work than anything else in this email. Article showed me how that single edit changes behavior — I bought the dining chairs after "ends today" appeared.
- 3. **Promote one hero product per collection, not just a category label.** The Sorrento, Livingston, and Farmhouse blocks are category buckets. Lead each with one specific piece at a specific discount — "The Benchwright Dining Table, now $899 (was $1,899)" beats "SHOP FARMHOUSE COLLECTION" every time.
- 4. **Reduce cadence or segment harder.** Send me furniture; stop sending me decorative accessories. Wayfair at 11+ emails this week still lands better because their subject lines at least vary the category signal. Pottery Barn is running identical promo language on repeat.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Sorrento & Livingston furniture, up to 70% off — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your saved furniture is on sale (online exclusive)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Summer Sale ends Sunday — collections picked for your spaces`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Up to 70% off the pieces you've been watching`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Online exclusive: Furniture ON SALE"

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard broken experiences (timeout + 403) will silently drop clicks for affected link targets. PHP `header()` calls are embedded verbatim inside a `<style>` block, indicating a template rendering failure that never got caught in QA.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Click-redirect timeout** — The Apply & Buy banner CTA (`clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-...`) timed out during probing. If this reflects production latency, the link is functionally dead for a percentage of recipients. No fallback destination is present.

**[FAIL] 403 on tracking spacer** — `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` returns HTTP 403. This is a West Elm asset served over plain HTTP, cross-domain from a Pottery Barn send — likely a stale template carry-over. The 403 will cause a broken image request on every open.

**[WARN] 25 tracking links unverified** — All click.e.potterybarn.com redirect links were skipped by the probe. These should be spot-checked manually against the final destination URLs before send.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CRITICAL] PHP header directives inside `<style>` block** — The following raw PHP output appears literally in the CSS, never executed:

```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```

This is a template preprocessing failure. The PHP was not interpreted before send; it now lives as invalid CSS text. Some parsers will ignore it; others may partially corrupt the surrounding style block.

**[FAIL] 3 images served over HTTP (mixed content)** — These will be blocked by Gmail, Yahoo, and most modern clients:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` (open-pixel tracker)

**[WARN] 30+ images missing alt text** — The bulk of content images (hero banners, product shots, footer slices) have no `alt` attribute. Clients with images disabled will render a blank email. Screen readers have no fallback text for any visual content.

**[WARN] `<title>` is empty** — `<title></title>` provides no context for screen readers or accessibility tools.

**[WARN] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile, which is an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4).

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not captured** — The AgentMail relay did not surface this header. If it is absent at the MTA level (not just relay-invisible), this violates Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender requirements (enforced Feb 2024). Needs verification at the raw SMTP layer.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not captured** — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is required for bulk senders sending >5k/day to Gmail. Absence here is a compliance gap if the header is genuinely missing (not just relay-stripped).

**[WARN] SPF/DKIM status unknown** — `Authentication-Results` was not captured via the relay. Cannot confirm DKIM signature validity for `e.potterybarn.com`. Unverified authentication is a deliverability risk with major inbox providers.

**CAN-SPAM physical address** — Not verifiable from truncated source; assumed present in footer (standard for PB sends). Should be confirmed.
