# Something festive just landed on our app!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-10**
## Executive summary

- Okay, on September 10th, Crate & Barrel wants me to download their app for a "holiday sneak peek" — in the first week of September, when I haven't even finished my Labor Day furniture orders. I get the whole "beat the rush" logic, but the CTA is literally "Download Our App," not a shoppable offer, so my actual eyeballs skip right past it to the second block, which is the one I actually care about: bedding that "ships free" in fall colors, followed by the Design Desk sweepstakes for a $5000 gift card and free designer consultations. That's the stuff aimed at someone mid-renovation like me.
- This is the fourth or fifth C&B-family send I've caught this week (I flagged the Double Rewards one yesterday and about four Crate & Kids countdown blasts before that), and this is at least a different beat — no urgency clock, no "ends tonight." It's a calmer, more editorial email than CB2's exhausting "00:07:30:24" ticker I keep watching tick down. Still, leading with an app download instead of a real offer is a miss for my persona — I don't need another app, I need a coupon code or a swatch.
- Would I click? Probably scroll straight to the Design Desk section and maybe the "we love these for you" product grid — that Barrett sleeper sofa and the Keane dresser are legitimately my taste. Wouldn't forward this to a friend, wouldn't screenshot it. Marking as read after a skim.

## What's working

- The furniture and product photography is genuinely good — that living room shot with the sectional and the brass pendant looks like something out of my Pinterest-adjacent folder, not stock art.
- The "we love these for you" grid with prices visible (Barrett sofa $1599, Keane dresser $2599, Basque dining table $1799) is exactly the personalized, price-transparent format I want — no clicking through five pages to find out what something costs.
- The Design Desk free-consultation + $5000 sweepstakes is a smart hook for someone renovating two houses at once — that's a real reason to engage, unlike most brands' generic "shop now."
- Bedding section correctly flags "Ships Free" right in the headline — shipping clarity for a big/bulky category is something I always scan for.

## What's weak

- Leading the whole email with a holiday app-download push in early September feels tone-deaf to where I actually am in my renovation calendar — I'm buying sofas and dressers right now, not garland.
- The hero CTA "Download Our App" isn't a purchase action at all — for a guy who buys via his highest-rewards card the second he sees a real deal, this is dead weight at the top of the email.
- No visible loyalty/rewards math up top even though I know from the card block further down that I get 10% back in Reward Dollars — that should be higher up, not buried near the footer, especially compared to how CB2 and Crate & Barrel proper keep hammering "Double Rewards" language front and center in their recent sends.
- Two separate app-download CTAs (top hero and mid-email "It's all on the app" block) is redundant — Wayfair and Pottery Barn don't make me download anything to see a sofa price.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap the hero from "holiday sneak peek / Download Our App" to the bedding or Design Desk offer — something with a real dollar figure or free-shipping claim front and center, not an app install ask.
- 2. Cut one of the two app-download blocks entirely; one mention is plenty, and it frees up space to push the $5000 sweepstakes higher, which is the strongest hook in this email.
- 3. Surface the 10% Reward Dollars card benefit near the top instead of near the footer — that's the kind of loyalty math that actually gets me to click, and right now I have to scroll past four sections to find it.
- 4. Add a swatch/sample CTA next to the bedding "new colors" block — I'm mid-renovation and picking fabrics/colors is exactly where I stall out and need a nudge.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Book a free design session before Sept 13 — win $5000`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New fall bedding colors, ships free`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free 1:1 design consultation + a shot at $5000 toward your renovation`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Softer sheets, bolder colors — plus 10% back with your Crate Card`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
Standard MI (Marketing Innovation) transactional email template with heavy tracking instrumentation; compliance headers (List-Unsubscribe, DKIM/SPF) are unverifiable from the relay capture, and the majority of images lack alt text.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 54 tracking/click-redirect links present, routed through what appears to be an MI (mi.crateandbarrel.com) click-tracking domain — these were skipped by automated HTTP probing (expected behavior for redirect-wrapped links, not itself a defect).
- Multiple pixel trackers detected: `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png` (7 instances), `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif` (open-tracking pixel), `sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif` (LiveRamp/RampID pixel, fired 5x with incrementing `n=` param), and `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/72e70e70-...` (likely DoubleVerify/viewability tag). This is a normal but dense third-party tracking stack for a major retailer ESP send.
- No broken or malformed `href` values found in the visible source; full link destinations were truncated in the provided HTML and could not be verified against expected landing pages.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **15 images missing `alt` text**, spanning both content images (product/hero images served from `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com` and `s7d5.scene7.com`) and tracking pixels (`mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*`, `sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif`). Content images without alt text degrade the experience for screen-reader users and when images are blocked by default (common in Outlook/Gmail); tracking pixels technically don't need alt text but should carry `alt=""` to avoid being flagged by accessibility scanners.
- Template uses legacy XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype and table-based layout with extensive MSO conditional comments — standard for cross-client compatibility (Outlook desktop), not a defect.
- Responsive breakpoints defined at `max-width:460px`, `min-width:640px`, and `min-width:768px` with mobile-specific class overrides (`.mobile`, `.showmobile`, `.f10`–`.f20`) — structure looks intact based on the visible `<style>` block.
- `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` is generic and does not reflect campaign content; low-impact but affects browser tab/webmail preview identification when the email is viewed as a landing page.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
