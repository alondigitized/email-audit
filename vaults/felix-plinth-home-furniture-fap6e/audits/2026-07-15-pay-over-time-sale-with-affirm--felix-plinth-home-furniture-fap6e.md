# 🔔 PAY-OVER-TIME SALE WITH AFFIRM 🔔 
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- I've been on a Wayfair streak this week — kitchen mats, bins and buckets, a chicken coop I definitely did not need, trash cans that turned out to be a general clearance email — and now this. The Affirm pitch lands as the sixth Wayfair notification in four days, which is its own problem before I've even read the first word. The email body is essentially a category dump dressed up with a pay-over-time hook, and the hook is the thing that actually loses me: I buy on the Chase Sapphire. Every. Time. I'm not splitting a $400 area rug into four Affirm installments. That's what points are for.
- That said — two renovations running simultaneously means I genuinely need about 60% of what's on this scroll. Outdoor furniture, major appliances, storage, rugs. The "Picked just for you" section mid-email earns real estate because it shows items with star ratings that don't look randomly pulled. But the surrounding architecture undercuts it: there are eight or nine competing percentage-off banners stacked below the Affirm hero, and by the time I hit the fourth category tile I've lost track of what the email is actually *about*. It's a sale. Yes. On what? Everything. That's not an email, that's a catalog.
- I'd scroll once for the "Picked just for you" section, click nothing, and archive it. I would not forward this. Wayfair needs to decide whether this is an Affirm-financing email or a summer sale email, because trying to be both at once makes it feel like neither.

## What's working

- **The Affirm 0% APR banner sits at the very top** — even though the financing angle doesn't match how I shop, the offer is visible immediately. No hunting.
- **"Picked just for you" actually earned its name** — the mid-email personalized section has star ratings on individual products, which feels like real data, not a random slot-fill.
- **Star ratings visible inline** — I can see review counts on the picked-for-you tiles, and that's the one place in this email where I'd actually pause.
- **Category breadth hits my renovation checklist** — outdoor furniture, major appliances, storage, rugs. My two kitchens and two mudrooms are genuinely represented here.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, nothing overlapping, images load correctly, readable type.

## What's weak

- **The Affirm hook is a miss for a rewards-card shopper** — I am not this audience. I'd rather hit the Sapphire for the points and pay it off in full. The entire email lead is built around a value prop I'll never use.
- **Eight competing percentage-off banners erase each other** — 70% off, 50% off, 40% off, 60% off, 55% off. After the third one my brain just starts reading "off off off off." Compare this to how Article ran a clean editorial send last week — no discount, one category, one visual story. Wayfair looks undisciplined next to it.
- **No specific product hero** — Pottery Barn's entryway-furniture send from earlier this month showed me three specific pieces. This shows me a grid of maybe forty SKUs. Nothing sticks.
- **The email's stated reason to exist is Affirm financing, but most of the email is regular sale content** — the two strategies don't reinforce each other. Is Affirm making specific categories cheaper? Am I getting a better rate on rugs? The connection is never made.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is Wayfair email number six in four days. The bins-and-buckets send on the 13th actually had me interested. By this one, I'm numb.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick a lane: Affirm email OR sale email.** If this is an Affirm email, show me a $1,200 sectional and tell me what four payments look like. If it's a sale email, lead with the product, not the payment mechanism. Right now it's both and neither.
- 2. **Cut the category stack in half.** Six categories max, each with one hero product. Let me land somewhere. The current structure is a Wayfair.com homepage, not a curated send.
- 3. **Elevate the "Picked just for you" block to the hero position.** That's the strongest real estate in the email — it implies Wayfair knows my order history. Lead with it. Make the personalization the story, not the payment plan.
- 4. **Dial back send frequency** — or at minimum vary the format. Bins on Sunday, appliances Tuesday, Affirm Wednesday reads as list spam. Article would not do this.
- 5. **Add one credible deadline** — "Sale ends Sunday" costs nothing and would actually give me a reason to act today rather than archive-and-maybe-later.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Font format: woff only, no woff2**
```css
@font-face {
  src: url(https://secure.img.wfrcdn.com/st4/stores/common/fonts/sofia/sofia_reg.woff) format("woff");
}
```
No `woff2` source declared. Modern clients (Gmail app, Apple Mail, iOS Mail) support woff2 and it's ~30% smaller. Fallback degrades to `mso-font-alt: "Arial"` in Outlook — acceptable, but woff2 should be added.

**Aggressive word-break on product names**
```css
.ProductRec-name { word-break: break-all; }
```
`break-all` breaks mid-word on any character. Should be `word-break: break-word` or `overflow-wrap: break-word` to avoid visually fragmented product names on mobile.

**Duplicate media query blocks**
The `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)` rule is split into at least two separate blocks in `<head>` — one for button classes, one for layout utilities. Some clients (older Android Gmail) parse only the first matching media block. These should be consolidated into a single `@media` block to avoid rule-drop.

**No visible `<meta charset>` or viewport `<meta>` in truncated head**
Standard email practice includes `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />`. Cannot confirm presence without full `<head>` — verify in full source.

**`u ~ div { min-width: 100vw }` Gmail hack present**
This is a known Gmail width fix; its presence is intentional and correct.
