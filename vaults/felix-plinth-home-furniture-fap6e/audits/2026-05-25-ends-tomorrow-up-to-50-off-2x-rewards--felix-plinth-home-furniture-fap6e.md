# ENDS TOMORROW: Up to 50% off & 2x Rewards!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- Forty-seven. I'm holding the running tally of C&B family emails since Sunday and this is number forty-seven — and the third Crate & Kids send on the same calendar day. The other two I reviewed earlier today (the "Beds, cribs & more" and "Top-rated furniture" Double Rewards sends) both earned 9s from me because they did clean work: clear hero products, sharp rewards math, relevant product categories. I came into this one with goodwill. And on paper the offer is real — 50% off kids furniture is genuine money when I'm fitting out two homes.
- What kills the landing is the clock. The subject screamed "ENDS TOMORROW" when it hit inboxes yesterday. I'm opening it today, the day it was supposed to end, and the countdown timer at the top is sitting at something like 00:00:05:04. The body copy has been updated to say "ends today!" which is technically accurate, but now there's a subject-line-to-body mismatch visible the moment you open it. The Pottery Barn "Extra 20% off ENDS TOMORROW" email pulled the same move last week and I called it out there; the C&B family has now baked this credibility problem into every sub-brand's Memorial Day urgency stack.
- If I'd opened this yesterday morning — fresh timer, subject and body in sync — I'd have clicked. Today I'm reading a nearly-expired deal and the impulse is gone. Mark as read.

## What's working

- The sage/teal dresser as hero is genuinely good product photography — one piece, one color, and I immediately know whether I'd put it in a room.
- "50% off + 2x Rewards" is the right rewards-card math to show a member; I think about stacking my highest-rewards card with a C&B bonus and the numbers move.
- The "#1 furniture collection" claim with visible star ratings is something neither the mothership "ends TOMORROW" emails nor the CB2 sends bother to include — actual social proof in the body.
- Visual hierarchy is solid: offer and timer land above the fold, products below — I see the deal before I'm asked to browse.
- Crate & Kids earns the benefit of the doubt as a trusted sub-brand. After the CB2 "IT'S ALL ON THE APP" disaster earlier this week, a brand that leads with product instead of a download prompt feels like a relief.

## What's weak

- Body says "ends today!" — subject said "ENDS TOMORROW." That contradiction is visible the second you open it and makes the urgency feel like a cut-and-paste error.
- Third Crate & Kids send today with the same urgency frame the mothership has been running for three days straight; the C&B family's Memorial Day cadence has hit a saturation point where "ENDS TOMORROW" is functionally background noise.
- The "It's all on the app" section at the bottom is the exact same appendage CB2 pushed at me, which I scored a 4. It doesn't belong in a sale-urgency email — it dilutes the single ask.
- The hero dresser reads slightly ambiguous — is it kids line or mainline? For a sub-brand called Crate & Kids, a clearer "kids room" context cue (a small detail, a room shot) would sharpen the targeting.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the subject-to-body time framing** — either send the "ends today" version the morning of the deadline or update the subject dynamically. Wayfair manages this better with day-of subject lines that match in-body clock state; C&B should too.
- 2. **Kill the app-download footer** — one ask per email. Pottery Barn doesn't embed app-download CTAs in sale closers; it shouldn't be here either.
- 3. **Give the hero a room-scene anchor** — the standalone dresser is beautiful but a tight kids-room crop (even a lifestyle inset) signals "this is where this piece lives" without adding length.
- 4. **Break the "ENDS TOMORROW" template** — the C&B family has sent this framing so many times in five days that it no longer registers. The kids sub-brand should be sharper and more specific.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Kids furniture: 50% off ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Double Rewards on cribs & beds — final hours`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Our #1 collection included — free shipping on $49+`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack your rewards before midnight — beds, dressers & storage`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Conflicting `img` max-width media queries — wrong value wins on desktop**

```css
/* Query 1 */
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
/* Query 2 — declared after, so wins at 768px+ */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```

Both queries match screens ≥ 768px. Because query 2 is declared later in the stylesheet, `max-width: 640px` overrides `max-width: 600px` on all screens ≥ 640px. The 600px cap is unreachable.

**[MEDIUM] `<title>` brand mismatch**

```html

```

Sender identity is "Crate & Kids SALE"; the title reflects the parent brand. Some email clients surface `<title>` in accessibility trees and screen reader announcements.

**[MEDIUM] `*` selector overrides are overly broad**

```css

```

`line-height: 100%` applied globally collapses line height on multi-line text blocks across clients that honor it. `max-height:1000000px` on `*` is a Yahoo Mail hack that can cause overflow side effects in modern webmail.
