# New deals for TODAY ONLY, leftovers unlikely 🍽️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-27**
## Executive summary

- Pottery Barn again. It's the same plaid-bordered maroon "Black Friday, up to 70% off" hero I've had since 11/16, with "NEW DEALS ADDED" on top. The subject promises "TODAY ONLY" deals, but the hero doesn't say so. The real change, "TODAY ONLY DEALS," is a four-product strip about two-thirds of the way down. It's towels, sheets, and a barware gift set. None of that is the living room or dining furniture I'm shopping for across two houses.
- The long scroll isn't bad. The category blocks (sofas, dining, bedroom) at least speak to a renovator. But I got "NEW! TODAY-ONLY DEALS + 20% off all ornaments" earlier today, so this is the same send with a different subject. The cadence has gone past being a sale. Would I click? Maybe once, on the dining table, if I were already shopping. Forward it? No. Mark it read and move on.

## What's working

- The "Savings for an inviting dining room" and living room blocks are in my category. They're the only part that feels like it's for someone furnishing a house.
- "SHIPS FREE" tags on throws and pillows address my shipping question, though I care more about furniture freight.
- The "In-Store Savings / Find a Pottery Barn near you" block nods to showroom integration, which I'd actually use to touch fabric.
- The "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" card block speaks to my rewards-math habit. It's buried near the bottom, though.

## What's weak

- "TODAY ONLY" is the subject's whole hook, and it shows up only as a small block below the fold. The hero is unchanged.
- The today-only items are towels, sheets, and a gift set, which feel like filler next to the furniture sections above.
- The "Recommended just for you" row shows faux fur throws and stockings. That doesn't read as personalized to a guy mid-renovation.
- The email is very long, and the fine print is dense and tiny. I can't read the exclusions without zooming, which hurts the honest-offer feel.
- The Christmas Shop and Williams-Sonoma-family links add more clutter. I already get this same family stream from West Elm and Rejuvenation.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the "TODAY ONLY" deals in the hero, with a deadline and the strongest furniture deal there. Don't recycle the 11/16 banner.
- 2. Lead with the dining table or a sofa. Competitors do this better: CB2's "Hosting favorites up to 60% off" hero at least named the category and said it ships free.
- 3. Show a real deadline ("ends 11:59 PT") and a plain statement of what ships free.
- 4. Cut the stockings and ornament content from a furniture-intent send. Wayfair's wall of 80% off is louder, but it at least stays on category.
- 5. Move the rewards card block up and add a swatch/sample or in-store-pickup line.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based, image-sliced build, with 16+ promo slices and 18 footer slices, sent through Salesforce Marketing Cloud (`click.e.potterybarn.com`). It has a leaked server-side code fragment in its CSS, links that wrap images with no accessible name, the recipient's email address in plaintext in third-party pixel URLs, and mobile CSS that doesn't match the visible markup. The header-based compliance and authentication checks are inconclusive because of the relay.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Destinations unverified.** 69 links on `click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=…` were skipped by the HTTP probe, so landing-page status, redirect chains and 404s are unknown. This is a QA coverage gap, not a confirmed defect.
- **Third-party ad pixels.** The source loads `secure.leadback.advertising.com/adcedge/lb?site=695501…` and `d.turn.com/r/dd/id/…/cat/1`. They add external dependencies and tracking beyond the ESP's own open pixel.
- **Cross-brand asset dependency.** `https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` is used in a Pottery Barn send. It will break if the West Elm asset is moved.
- **Stale asset reuse.** `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` is a 2022 campaign asset in a 2026 send.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Leaked PHP in `<style>`.** The second `<style>` block contains `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: no-store…"); header("Pragma: no-cache");` as literal text. This is invalid CSS and becomes part of the prelude of the next rule, so the `a[x-apple-data-detectors] {…}` rule is likely discarded. That would let iOS auto-link styling (blue underlined dates and addresses) through.
- **Linked images with empty alt.** `slices_01.jpg` and `slices_02.jpg` are wrapped in `<a href="https://click.e.potterybarn.com/…">` and have `alt=""`. The links then have no accessible name, and with images blocked the promo content disappears. The QA flags the other slices too: `slices_03`–`16` of the promo, `H26_InStoreSavingsBanner.jpg`, `H26_Bottom_XmasShop_50off.jpg`, `H26_xBrand_Bottom_Banner_Red/slices_01–09`, and `Email-Footer/slices_01–18`. The QA reports them as missing alt, though the visible source shows `alt=""` for the first two. The product images (`faux-fur-ruched-throw-l.jpg`, `banks-extendable-dining-table-76-128-l.jpg`, etc.) are also flagged.
- **Mobile CSS doesn't match the markup.** The media query targets `table[class="wrapper"]` and `img[class="photo"]`. In the visible source, the body tables are fixed `width="650"` with no `class="wrapper"`, and the slice images have no `class="photo"`. The 480px rules therefore likely never apply, leaving a fixed-width 650px layout on mobile. Only `.mobile-hide` and `.mobile-show` are used. The source is truncated, so later sections may differ.
- **Non-HTTPS images.** `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` and `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` may be blocked or rewritten by some clients.
- **Content before the DOCTYPE.** AMPscript comments (`<!-- begin dynamic ampScript slot -->`) sit before the `<!DOCTYPE>`, which can trigger quirks mode in some renderers.
- **Tracking pixels without `alt=""`.** `coreemail.html`, `lb`, `1` and `svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=…` are flagged. They are pixels, so the impact is minor, but `alt=""` is still missing.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **No unresolved tokens** (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`) are visible in the truncated source.
- **Recipient email in plaintext in image URLs.** Two examples:
  - `https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
  - `https://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`

  The address goes into server logs and referrers, and the `svopn` host is a third-party domain. It's a privacy exposure, and the plaintext address is also what makes the email-open pixels person-identifying.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found, and `Authentication-Results` was not found. The QA itself attributes these to the AgentMail relay stripping headers, so **SPF/DKIM/DMARC and one-click unsubscribe are unverified, not confirmed failures.** Re-test against the raw message headers at the original recipient, or via a seed inbox.
- The footer unsubscribe link and physical postal address are in the truncated portion of the source and could not be checked.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Every CTA is wrapped in the SFMC redirect `click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=…`, which hides the final URLs, so UTM presence and landing-page alignment can't be assessed.
- The commented-out header block shows the site's own tracking uses `cm_ite=` and `marketingChannelCode=` rather than `utm_*`. The Pottery Barn URLs in the source show no `utm_*` parameters.
- Product image URLs point directly to `www.potterybarn.com/pbimgs/…`, which is fine.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Remove the stray `header(...)` PHP lines from the `<style>` block and confirm the `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` rule renders.
2. Give each linked promo slice a descriptive `alt` that includes the offer text, and set `alt=""` explicitly on the tracking pixels.
3. Add `class="wrapper"` and `class="photo"` to the tables and images, or rewrite the media query to match the real markup, so the layout is fluid on mobile.
4. Switch the two `http://` images to `https://` and retire the 2022 banner asset.
5. Hash or tokenize the recipient email in the `coreemail.html` and `svopn` pixel URLs rather than passing it in plaintext.
6. Move the DOCTYPE to the first line, or keep the AMPscript output out of the rendered document.
7. Re-run the compliance and authentication checks on raw headers, and extend the probe to resolve `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirects so landing URLs and UTMs can be audited.
