# OUTDOOR WALL DECOR at a great price.
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- Seventeen. That's where the Wayfair tally sits since Sunday, and the subject line on this one promises "OUTDOOR WALL DECOR at a great price" — which is at least a category I haven't been pitched yet this week. So I open it. And what's the hero? A rug. Then another rug. Then a bench. Then a coffee table. Then, somewhere down in the "we think you'll ♥ these" basement, a single welcome sign that vaguely qualifies as outdoor wall decor. Bait, meet switch.
- The top banner is "75% OFF the much shop ideals" — and yes, I think that's supposed to say "must shop deals" but the rendered text reads like a typo I have to squint at on my phone. That's not a tap target I trust. Meanwhile Pottery Barn at least keeps its subject line and hero in the same zip code, and Article's brand-building email from earlier this week made me actually *like* the brand instead of resenting it.
- Would I click? No. Forward? Absolutely not. Mark as read and keep scrolling — and honestly, I'm one more bait-subject away from hitting unsubscribe on Wayfair entirely.

## What's working

- The product grid is at least clean — square tiles, consistent crops, no overlapping junk.
- The 75% OFF callout is loud enough that I see the headline number immediately.
- The bench-on-porch shot midway down is genuinely nice styling — felt like a Crate & Barrel beat for a second.

## What's weak

- **The subject lies.** "OUTDOOR WALL DECOR" and the hero is rugs. This is the second day in a row Wayfair has pulled this bait-and-switch — the wall-mounted shelves email yesterday did the exact same thing.
- The banner text reads "the much shop ideals" — either it's a typo or the font rendering ate it, but either way I can't tell what I'm looking at.
- Seventeen emails in a week. I am numb. Compare that to Article's *one* well-crafted send and it's not close who I'd shop with.
- No price on the hero tiles — just "75% OFF" as a blanket claim with no anchor product, no MSRP, nothing concrete.
- Zero personalization. I've been pricing benches and rugs for two houses for weeks; Wayfair has *all* that browse data and chose to send me a generic grid.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Match the subject to the hero.** If the email is "outdoor wall decor," the first three tiles below the fold need to be welcome signs, metal art, planters with hooks — not rugs. Rugs got their own email yesterday.
- 2. **Fix the banner copy.** "The much shop ideals" is either a typo or a render bug; either way it's the first thing I read and it kills trust instantly.
- 3. **Throttle the cadence.** Seventeen sends in a week is a self-inflicted unsubscribe pipeline. Cut to three.
- 4. **Show a price on the hero.** "75% OFF" with no anchor product is meaningless. Pick one $29 welcome sign that was $119 and put it front and center.
- 5. **Use my browse history.** I've looked at outdoor furniture all month — segment me into that, not the everyone-blast.
