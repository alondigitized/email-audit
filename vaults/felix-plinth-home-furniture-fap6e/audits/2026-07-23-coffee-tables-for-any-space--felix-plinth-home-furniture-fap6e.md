# Coffee tables for any space ☕️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-seven. That's Pottery Barn in my inbox for July — and I said the same thing at thirty-four, thirty-five, and thirty-six. But I'll be honest: "coffee tables" stopped me. I am literally mid-decision on a coffee table for the second home. The living room has been running an embarrassing placeholder for three months. So I opened it with low resistance and genuine intent.
- The hero delivered. Classic PB lifestyle photography — warm tones, layered textiles, a room that looks like someone spent actual money and thought. The product grid showed meaningful style range: I spotted at least a couple of finishes and silhouettes that I'd consider. And the "Recommended Just For You" module in the middle of the scroll actually registered on me, which is rare — most personalization blocks in these emails feel like they were copy-pasted in from a template nobody updated.
- What kills it is the absence of any offer. No member discount, no shipping threshold, no "Key Rewards early access." Article landed in my inbox four days ago with a tighter editorial layout and at least an implied value story. CB2 ran "Hours left for up to 40% off" the same week. Pottery Barn is asking me to browse a $600–900+ category at full retail with zero urgency. I marked this as read, noted I need a coffee table, and will wait for the sale email. Not clicking today.

## What's working

- **Hero photography is genuinely good** — the room feels aspirational without being unattainable, which is exactly the register I need when I'm in active decision mode on a piece.
- **Category timing was right** — coffee tables are a real gap in my second home; the subject pulled me in without manipulation.
- **Style range in the product grid** — I could see multiple finishes and forms, which signals they're treating this as a real shopping tool, not just a brand billboard.
- **"Recommended Just For You" module landed** — the placement mid-scroll felt intentional, and it actually showed me furniture in other categories I'm researching.
- **Key Rewards loyalty callout exists** — at least they acknowledged I'm a member, even if they didn't do anything with that status.

## What's weak

- **No offer, full stop** — no discount, no shipping promo, no points multiplier. For a purchase this size, I'm not converting without a reason.
- **"Shop Now" is the laziest CTA in email marketing** — CB2's "Hours left for up to 40% off" last week had a verb *and* a number; "Shop Now" is meaningless.
- **App download module breaks the scroll** — I'm in browsing mode for furniture, and a "Download the Pottery Barn App" interrupt mid-scroll is a jarring pivot that costs them momentum.
- **No size/dimension callouts** — I'm trying to fit a specific table in a specific room and the product tiles give me nothing to work with before clicking.
- **Cadence has eroded any residual urgency** — at email thirty-seven in a single month, "new arrivals" from Pottery Barn no longer reads as news.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a member moment** — even "Key Rewards members earn 3× points this week on coffee tables" would give me something to act on today. They have my loyalty data; they're not using it.
- 2. **Replace "Shop Now" with something functional** — "Find Your Coffee Table" or "Shop All Sizes" gives the CTA a job to do.
- 3. **Add room-size guidance to the product tiles** — a callout like "Works in rooms under 12 ft" or a small-space tag would collapse my decision cycle significantly.
- 4. **Kill or relocate the app module** — push it to the footer; it's a retention play, not a conversion play, and it's stopping the scroll at exactly the wrong moment.
- 5. **Drop cadence or segment harder** — thirty-seven emails in a month means every send competes with itself; a weekly curated digest for active renovators would outperform this volume.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Key Rewards members: new coffee tables — earn 3× points this week`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The coffee table your living room is waiting for`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Solid wood, marble, storage shelves — 14 styles, starting at $XXX. Find your size.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `From small-space to statement piece — Key Rewards points on every order this week.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[FAIL] PHP header() calls inside `<style>` block — template rendering bug**
The following literal PHP code appears inside a `<style>` tag in the delivered HTML:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
This is not valid CSS. Some email clients will render this as visible text; all clients will fail to parse the surrounding style rules cleanly. This is a template pipeline defect — PHP was not executed server-side before the HTML was assembled.

**[WARN] 51 images missing `alt` text**
Includes all content slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_17.jpg` across two sections), all footer slices, promotional banners (`F26_TOP_SummerSalePartC_NoUrgency.jpg`, `F26_Bottom_NewArrivals.jpg`), product images, and tracking pixels. When images are blocked (default in Outlook, Gmail with images off), the email is entirely inaccessible to screen readers and image-blocked clients.
