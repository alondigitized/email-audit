# Last chance: Memorial Day Sale ends today
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so Article finally joined the Memorial Day pile-on. After last week's restrained "The furniture you've always wanted" brand-builder and the very competent "Up to 40% off ends Monday" send, *this* is the actual last-chance closer — and it's the weakest of the three. It's clean, it's on-brand green, it's not screaming in red emojis like Wayfair did this morning. But it's also weirdly thin — no actual discount number anywhere on the hero. Just "MEMORIAL DAY SALE" in a big leafy block and a "Shop the Sale" button. Where did the "Up to 40% off" go? That was the whole hook two days ago.
- The four category tiles — Outdoor, Dining Room, Bedroom, All Sale — are at least useful. I'm mid-renovation on a second home, so Outdoor and Dining Room are live tabs for me. But there's no product, no price, no specific piece I can imagine in my space.
- Would I click? Maybe Outdoor, because I'm actually shopping patio right now. Forward to a friend? No. Mark as read? More likely — Article has already earned my trust this week, and I know what the sale is. This one's a brand reminder, not a closer.

## What's working

- Calm, sage-green palette is a genuine relief next to Wayfair's red-dot panic and Crate & Barrel's fortieth countdown clock today.
- The four shoppable tiles give me a way in by room — Outdoor and Dining Room are exactly where my head is.
- "30-Day Satisfaction Guarantee" callout is the kind of trust cue furniture buyers actually care about — beats CB2 yelling about an app.
- "Last call. Our Memorial Day Sale ends today." is a clean, factual closer. No emoji theater.
- Footer is tidy — Help Center, Satisfaction Guarantee, Shipping and Delivery icons are the three things I'd actually want one tap from.

## What's weak

- **No discount number on the hero.** "Up to 40% off" was the whole reason I cared on May 22. Dropping the number on the actual last-chance email is malpractice.
- The "SALE" wordmark looks like a houseplant logo — pretty, but it buries the offer. Pottery Barn would never let the % vanish on closing day.
- Tiles link to categories, not products. I'm a known customer with browse history — show me the patio sectional I looked at, not "Shop Outdoor."
- No rewards/loyalty math, no shipping clarity on the hero, no "delivers by [date]" — all the things furniture buyers actually need to pull the trigger.
- Zero personalization. Article has my email, my zip, presumably some browse data. This could be addressed to anyone.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the number back.** "Up to 40% off — ends tonight" in the hero band. The sale ending is not the offer; the discount is.
- 2. Swap one of the four category tiles for an actual hero product with price + sale price + "delivers in 2 weeks" — give me one thing to click, not four buckets.
- 3. Add a single line on shipping: "Free shipping over $999, ends with the sale." Furniture-buyer brain needs the delivery math.
- 4. Personalize the tile order — if I browsed outdoor, lead with outdoor. Article has the data.
- 5. Time-stamp the urgency: "Ends 11:59 PM PT." Vague "today" is weaker than a real clock.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call — Memorial Day savings end at midnight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Outdoor, dining, bedroom — sale prices end 11:59 PT.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free shipping over $999. Closing tonight.`
