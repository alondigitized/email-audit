- Pottery Barn already hit me with the outdoor sale angle this morning (scored it a 9) and stacked an extra 20% on top — C&B is arriving second with a weaker compound offer and no member callout
- The lower sections of the email seem to mix in what look like indoor upholstered pieces — if the subject says outdoor furniture, committing to that all the way down matters; mixed categories blur the offer scope

- 1. **Add an end date.** "Ends July 6" is two words that create urgency without gimmickry. Right now there's no reason to act today.
- 2. **Commit to outdoor only, all the way down.** Strip the indoor-looking product rows at the bottom or label them clearly. Mixed categories in a focused sale email feel like the brand didn't curate the send.
- 3. **Surface a member benefit.** A single line — "Crate & Barrel Credit Card holders get an extra 10% today" — would acknowledge I exist as a customer. Pottery Barn does this and it's a real differentiator that moves me.
- 4. **Lead with a specific hero product, not just a category.** "The Pacifica Teak Dining Set, now $X — plus 60% off the rest" beats a generic offer header for high-consideration purchases where I'm doing real comparison shopping.
- 5. **Build a same-day suppress rule across C&B brands.** The toddler emails that came before this one burned up the inbox goodwill this email needed. Inter-brand suppression on send days would fix that upstream.
