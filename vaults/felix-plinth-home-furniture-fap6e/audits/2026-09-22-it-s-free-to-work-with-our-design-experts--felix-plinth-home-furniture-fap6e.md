# It's FREE to work with our design experts
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-22**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this actually pulls me in more than I expected — "Free Design Services" as the hero, with that credenza vignette (brass lamp, stack of books, ceramics), is a scene I could screenshot for the second-house mood board right now. Free 1:1 design help, "any time that works for you," in-home/virtually/in-store — that's a real answer to the actual problem I have mid-reno, which is that I don't have time to make twenty small decisions across two houses. This is a step up from the last few PB sends I've read, which were all rugs and duvets and warehouse-sale banners.
- Where it loses me is the length — this thing goes on forever. Curtains, gallery walls, statement lighting, a second CTA block, an in-store class invite, then a whole "Recommended Just For You" product grid, then app download, then a credit card pitch. By the time I hit the rewards card block I've mentally checked out. Compare that to CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" (7/10 in my inbox recently) — tight, one offer, done. This is the opposite: one good idea buried under five more asks.
- Would I click? Yeah, probably — the "Book Your Appointment" CTA at the top is legit relevant to me right now. Would I forward it to a friend? Only the top third, cropped — not this whole scroll.

## What's working

- "Free Design Services" + the what/when/where three-column breakdown answers my objections before I even have them — that's smart, concrete copy.
- The hero vignette (brass lamp, wood credenza, layered ceramics) is exactly the kind of styled shot I'd pin — same instinct that worked in the "Rugs that give any room a fresh POV" send (6/10) with that living room shot.
- "Free" is unambiguous and appears twice (headline + in-store class) — no vague "premium experience" fluff.
- The in-store design class RSVP (Sept 27, Layering a Pottery Barn Bed) is a nice concrete, dated event — feels like an actual local perk, not just email filler.
- Curtains, gallery walls, and statement lighting subsections all pair a real room photo with a specific "Book Now" — better structured than the "Cozy layers" bedding email (6/10) which just dumped a grid.

## What's weak

- It's way too long — five sub-sections before you even get to the second CTA, then a whole separate product grid after that. Nobody's still reading by "Earn 10% Back in Rewards."
- The "Recommended Just For You" grid feels bolted on and generic — curtain rod, swivel chair, two beds, towels, sheets — that's not "for me," that's "for anyone who's ever bought furniture."
- Design services and product recommendations are two different asks crammed into one email; it dilutes the free-consult hook that actually landed.
- The credit card + Affirm pitch at the bottom is a hard left turn from "let a designer help you" — feels like three different teams contributed sections independently, no unified narrative.
- No pricing/timeline honesty on the design service itself — "book now" for what, a 15-minute call or a multi-week engagement? I still don't know what I'm actually signing up for.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this to roughly a third of its length — hero + what/when/where + one CTA + maybe the in-store class. Kill the mid-email product grid and the redundant second "Book a Free Appointment" banner entirely.
- 2. Move the rewards card and app download into their own dedicated send — they have nothing to do with design services and just create scroll fatigue.
- 3. Add one line of process clarity under the CTA: how long is the consult, is it really free with no strings, what happens after I book. Right now the offer is a headline, not a plan.
- 4. If "Recommended Just For You" is meant to be personalized, prove it — pull from my actual browsing/registry, not a generic bed-and-towel grid, or cut it.
- 5. Give the in-store class its own visual weight — a Sunday morning bed-layering class is a genuinely good hook for someone furnishing two houses and deserves more than a small banner near the bottom.
