# Happening now! Up to 70% off + Ready-to-ship outdoor essentials to start summer
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- Forty-one. That's my Pottery Barn count now, and this one lands June 25 — the same calendar day I already got the curtains-and-florals send ("Worth a look — Up to 70% off + New curtains ship free!"). So before I even open it, I'm already pre-fatigued. That said, when I do open, the outdoor hero is genuinely the right call for me right now. I have a second-home back patio that needs seating and a primary-home terrace that needs a dining set, and "ready-to-ship" in the subject line is the one phrase that actually cuts through the noise for someone mid-renovation who has been burned by 16-week lead times.
- What deflates it: the "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" section halfway down the email appears to be showing bedroom or indoor upholstered pieces — injected personalization that breaks the outdoor flow completely. I'd already mentally committed to browsing the outdoor grid and then the algorithm yanked me sideways. CB2's July 4th send this month kept its editorial lane straight all the way through; no rogue recommendation modules. Article's bedroom storage send from earlier in the week did the same — it picked a theme and held it. Pottery Barn pulled the rug.
- I'd mark it read, probably not click through today — but I might star it to come back to the outdoor sectionals later in the week. Would not forward. This is a utility email, not a "you have to see this" email.

## What's working

- **Hero image earns its pixels.** The outdoor living scene — teak-toned furniture, lush green backdrop, cushions in warm neutrals — matches exactly how I imagine the second-home patio. It's aspirational without being unrealistic.
- **"Ready-to-Ship" is load-bearing copy.** For anyone mid-renovation, shipping lead times are the entire decision. That phrase in the subject and presumably echoed in the hero is the single most useful thing this email says.
- **Category navigation chips are smart wayfinding.** The row of category pills — PILLOWS, SECTIONAL, COFFEE TABLES, SOFAS & SECTIONALS — lets me self-select depth without scrolling. That's the right structure for a catalogue this broad.
- **70% off is visible and unambiguous.** No "up to X% on select styles††see fine print" vagueness in the hero visual. The discount is front and center.
- **Seasonal timing is correct.** Late June outdoor push makes obvious sense. This isn't a stale promo — it reads current.

## What's weak

- **Same-day double send kills cadence trust.** On June 25 I got curtains-and-florals and then this. Pottery Barn sent me two emails in one day, both riding the "up to 70% off" umbrella. Wayfair does this and it's why I've rage-unsubscribed from three of their sub-lists. Pottery Barn is flirting with the same fate.
- **"RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" breaks the outdoor brief.** Indoor upholstered pieces injected mid-email mid-outdoor-theme is a jarring context switch. Crate & Barrel's Bear watch-party email kept its editorial tight from hero to footer — this email doesn't.
- **"Happening now!" urgency is empty.** This is at least the third "happening now / right now / today only" variation I've seen in the last ten days. It's lost all credibility as a time signal.
- **No end date anywhere visible.** CB2's July 4th send gave me a hard deadline — nine days out, clear anchor. This email offers no temporal hook to act on, so "I'll look later" wins and I don't come back.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the same-day second send.** One email a day, maximum. If the curtains send went out June 25, this one waits until the 26th. Cadence discipline is the highest-leverage change available.
- 2. **Anchor a deadline in the hero.** "Sale ends July 4" or even "This weekend only" would do more work than three "Happening now!" repetitions across ten days.
- 3. **Strip the "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" module or replace with outdoor-specific personalization.** If you're going to inject an algorithm block, train it to the email's theme. Show me outdoor furniture based on my browse history, not the bedroom set I looked at two weeks ago.
- 4. **Add one ready-to-ship product callout with a ship date.** "Ships in 3–5 days" next to a specific hero sectional would make this email more useful than an entire category grid.
- 5. **Tighten the subject line.** The current subject is 77 characters — it wraps on every mobile client.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your patio, before July 4th`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP header() calls inside `<style>` block — templating error**
The following literal PHP code appears inside a `<style>` tag:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
This is invalid CSS. It will either render as a parse error (silently ignored by most clients) or, in strict parsers, corrupt adjacent style rules. This is server-side code that escaped into the template output unexecuted — likely from a dynamic image-serving module.

**3 images served over HTTP (will be blocked)**
| Image | URL |
|---|---|
| `spacer_0.gif` | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` |
| `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` |

Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail block mixed-content image loads. The open-tracking pixel over HTTP will produce false negatives on open-rate reporting.
