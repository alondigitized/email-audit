# CAT TREES for less. *MAJOR* markdowns ➡
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- Fifty-nine. That's the Wayfair count now, and I want that on the table before I say anything else, because the subject line on this one is doing something I find genuinely irritating: it promises me one very specific thing — cat trees — and then opens on "The must-shop deals," a hero headline so generic it could belong to literally any email in my inbox this week. I have two houses I'm gutting simultaneously. I am not in a cat tree moment. I have never been in a cat tree moment.
- What makes this worse is that I just reviewed four Wayfair sends in the past two days. June 17 was dorm deals and exclusive-brand area rugs. June 18 was outdoor rugs and popular picks. Now June 19 is cat trees — which apparently means "a big grid of whatever's on sale," including what looks like outdoor chairs, a hammock, colorful towels, and a ceramic vase. The body doesn't match the subject, the subject doesn't match my life, and Wayfair has sent me enough emails this week that I'm starting to think the segmentation team took the week off.
- I'm marking this read. I'm not clicking. I'm definitely not forwarding it.

## What's working

- Wayfair's logo and layout are clean and immediately recognizable — no render bugs I can see, the grid is tidy.
- The red/orange promotional banner near the top signals a concrete offer quickly, which is at least the right instinct.
- The outdoor furniture and patio sections toward the bottom are seasonally appropriate for late June — that part of the product mix makes sense.
- The app CTA at the bottom is clean and unobtrusive; it doesn't cannibalize the main offers.

## What's weak

- The hero headline "The must-shop deals" completely ignores the subject line's cat tree promise — that's a bait-and-switch and I noticed it in the first two seconds.
- Cat trees are not a renovation category. I'm on Wayfair's list because I'm furnishing two houses, not because I own a cat. This send reached the wrong segment of my inbox behavior.
- The product mix is a scatter-shot grab bag — rugs, towels, hammock, ceramics, outdoor umbrellas — with no narrative thread. Pottery Barn's coastal lighting email last week had a point of view. This has none.
- Wayfair sent me four emails in the previous two days. This fifth one, especially on a mismatched category, lands with all the charm of a spam folder test.
- No visible pricing specifics in the hero — "major markdowns" is vague. Compare to Crate & Barrel's Bear collab, which at least gave me something concrete to anchor to.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this send away from active renovation customers.** Anyone who's been clicking on living room furniture, lighting, and area rugs for six months is not the target for a cat tree blast. Run a suppression pass on heavy buyers in core home categories before sending a pet accessories email.
- 2. **Make the hero match the subject.** If you're going to lead with cat trees, put a cat tree front-and-center above the fold. Right now the subject line and the email body are two different emails.
- 3. **Give me a specific number, not a vibe.** "Major markdowns" means nothing. "Up to 55% off cat trees — today only" means something. Crate & Barrel and Pottery Barn both do this better.
- 4. **Reduce the send cadence.** Five emails in three days from the same brand is the fastest path to an unsubscribe from someone like me. Batch the outdoor content, the pet content, and the seasonal deals into fewer, denser sends.
- 5. **Give the mid-email "we ♥ you" section a job.** The purple loyalty-ish block in the middle reads like a dead zone right now — if I'm a rewards member, tell me my points balance or what I've unlocked. Make it earn its space.
