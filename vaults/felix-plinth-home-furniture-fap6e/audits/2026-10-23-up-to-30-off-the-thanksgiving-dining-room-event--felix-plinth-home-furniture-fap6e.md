# Up to 30% off The Thanksgiving Dining Room Event!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-23**
## Executive summary

- This is the same Thanksgiving dining room email I audited earlier today, and it's now a repeat. It's still the right email for me. I have two dining rooms in play, and a hero with a walnut table, upholstered chairs and "up to 30% off" tells me what it is in about two seconds. Yesterday's "TODAY ONLY: 20% off lighting!" was main-brand and relevant, so Crate & Barrel is back to being useful after the Kids-sender noise.
- The email then runs out of focus. After the dining hero and the 2x2 tiles, it drifts into accent chairs and rugs, then The Design Desk sweepstakes, then Affirm, then a "we love these for you" grid of stainless pans, a toaster oven and a coffee table, then the credit card, then the app, then The Design Desk again. That's eight or nine pitches for one dining event. The second hero looks like a rug in a living room and doesn't help me pick a dining set.
- Would I click? Yes, on "Shop All Dining Furniture", and I'd probably save it to my Pinterest-adjacent folder as dining inspiration. I wouldn't forward it. The 30% is "up to", and I have no way to compare a sale price to a regular price on a chair I haven't seen.

## What's working

- The hero names the event, the discount and the category in the first screen. I don't have to decode it.
- The 2x2 tile grid (dining tables, dining seating, dining storage, select lighting) maps to how I shop a room. I'm picking a table, chairs and a sideboard separately.
- The dining photography is calm, warm and neutral-toned. It fits the color palette I'm trying to keep consistent across two houses.
- The Design Desk free-appointment offer is the one thing that's useful for a double renovation. It's like what Article did in its free interior design email.
- The Crate Card line, "10% back in Reward Dollars", speaks to how I buy. I'd run it through my highest-rewards card anyway, but I'd still do the math.

## What's weak

- The email is too long. Compared with Wayfair's "DINING SETS" send, which never showed a dining set, this one shows dining but then buries it under cookware and a toaster.
- The rug and accent chair hero is a second "event" with its own "up to 20% off", so I'm looking at two discount tiers in one email.
- The Design Desk appears twice and the sweepstakes copy is small. The "$5000" entry is a distraction from a sale email.
- Three stacked buttons in the middle ("Shop The Dining Furniture Event", "Shop All Dining Furniture", "Shop Thanksgiving") compete with each other. The hosting guide is also a hero image with a button, and the text over it is hard to read.
- Cadence. This is the second C&B send in two days, and the third sale-driven one. I also have Pottery Barn's "extra 20%" sending four times and CB2 stacking on top. The same lamp or chandelier showing up across brands is exactly what irritates me.
- The fine print is small and dense. "Up to" with no clear shipping or delivery timing for furniture means I can't tell whether a table gets here before Thanksgiving.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to dining only. Keep the hero, the tile grid, one "Shop All Dining Furniture" button, and a single bar for Design Desk and Crate Card. Drop the cookware, toaster and rug sections.
- 2. Add a delivery-by line, for example "Order by 11/5 for Thanksgiving delivery", and one best-seller table with a sale price and a struck-through regular price.
- 3. Show a swatch or finish row on the hero table so I can check it against my existing palette.
- 4. Choose a single tier, either 30% or 20%, and make it the one number in the email.
- 5. Consolidate to a single Design Desk module.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dining tables up to 30% off, in time for Thanksgiving`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Thanksgiving table: order by 11/5`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Tables, chairs and sideboards, plus free Design Desk appointments.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sale ends soon. See which finishes ship before the holiday.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional template with MSO conditional comments and mobile media queries. The main confirmed issues are about 31 images flagged for missing alt text and a viewport tag that blocks zoom. The compliance and authentication items couldn't be verified. The HTML source is truncated, and the relay may have stripped headers. The QA run reports 0 errors, 3 warnings and a 57% pass rate.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Click tracking not verifiable:** 64 tracking links were skipped because they are click-redirect domains. Destination health, redirect chains and 404s are untested.
- **Third-party pixels:** five `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=56aaa278…&n=1` through `n=5` calls are LiveRamp-domain pixels. They are third-party beacons, and the QA flagged them for missing alt text.
- **Open and tracking pixels:** `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif?mi_u=…` and `https://dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/7d28d0a6-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…` are first-party tracking images. They are not an issue by themselves, but they should be hidden from screen readers (`alt=""`).
- No broken or malformed hrefs are evidenced in the visible source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (about 31 images):**
  - **Content images:** 14 hosted at `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/{1,47}/…` (for example `bbf6f9d4-…png`, `ef861136-…jpg`, `5c7b78d8-…gif`), plus a Scene7 image, `2026%5F0920%5FCB%5FThanksgivingGuide%5FTertiary%2D…`. If they carry offer or product information, that content is lost when images are blocked.
  - **Dynamic product images:** seven `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png` images (`560d850e7e517ede.png`, `789e7381ff44be74.png`, and others).
  - **Pixels:** the rlcdn, `o.gif` and `dv.` images above.
  - The QA can't tell an absent `alt` from an empty `alt=""`. The pixels should use `alt=""`, and the content images need descriptive text.
- **Viewport:** `<meta name="viewport" content="… maximum-scale=1" />` restricts pinch-zoom, which is an accessibility problem for low-vision users.
- **Duplicate meta tags:** `format-detection` is declared three times (`telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no`, then `date=no`, `address=no` and `telephone=no`). The `charset` is also declared twice, via `http-equiv` and `<meta charset>`. This is redundant but harmless.
- **Template leftover:** the comment `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` is still in the head. It's cosmetic, but it shows an incomplete template.
- **Dead CSS rule:** `@media (min-width:768px){img{max-width:600px}}` comes before `@media (min-width:640px){img{max-width:640px}}`. Both match at 768px and above, and the later one wins, so the 600px cap never applies.
- `lang="en"` is present, which is good.
