# Shop it first: our Venus Williams collab, only on our app!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-14**
## Executive summary

- Yep, called it — this is that Venus Williams email I flagged yesterday, and seeing the full render just confirms it: the hero is still an app-only phone-mockup gate, but scroll past it and the rest of the email is a wall of nursery gliders, Nuna car seats, and a "Design Desk" pitch that has nothing to do with a tennis star collab. I don't have kids. I'm renovating two houses full of adults' furniture, and Crate & Kids keeps sending me baby registries and $1450 stroller systems like I've got a nursery to fill.
- The Venus Williams hook is genuinely the most interesting thing this brand's sent me all week, and they buried the actual product — is it clothing? home goods? a tennis-adjacent furniture line? — behind "download our app to find out." That's the same complaint I had about this exact email yesterday. Nothing's changed.
- Not clicking, not forwarding. If anything I'd forward it to a friend with a toddler as a joke about the "$500 in the app" scroll-bait, not as a real recommendation.

## What's working

- The Venus Williams hero shot is clean and well-composed — no clutter, confident typography, genuinely made me pause for a second.
- "TheDesignDesk" free-consultation block is at least a real, concrete offer (custom mood boards, 3D previews) — that's closer to my renovation reality than anything else in this email.
- Product grid images are crisp, prices are visible up front, no lorem-ipsum or broken renders anywhere in the scroll.

## What's weak

- Fifth Crate & Kids email this week and it's the same nursery-and-stroller inventory I've already scrolled past four times — Nuna car seats, gliders, a "Newborn Essentials Shop" cameo via the dresser and activity chair.
- The one interesting hook — a celebrity collab — has zero product detail. No price, no "shop the line," just "download the app." Compare that to CB2's "Grand entrances" email, which told me exactly what category (console tables, entryway mirrors) I was getting before I clicked anything.
- Two separate app-download CTAs (top hero, bottom "$500 chance") plus two separate "Design Desk" pitches — this email doesn't know what it wants me to do.
- I'm not a Crate & Kids demographic at all, and stacking a credit-card pitch, a design consult, and a sweepstakes into one send just reads like the brand hoping something sticks.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the app-only gate on the Venus Williams collab — show me one hero product with a price and a "Shop the Collection" button. If it's genuinely app-exclusive, say why (early access window, limited drop) instead of just demanding a download.
- 2. Cut this email in half — pick either "Venus Williams drop" or "nursery shopping picks," not both crammed under one subject line.
- 3. Segment me out of Crate & Kids nursery sends entirely — I've now gotten five in a row with zero baby-relevant signal from my purchase history.
- 4. Move the Design Desk (the one section actually relevant to a renovator like me) up near the top instead of sandwiched between strollers and a credit card pitch.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Venus Williams x Crate&Kids: see the full collection`
- **Subject Alt B:** `First look: our tennis-star collab drops today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `No app required — shop the full Venus Williams line right here`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Limited drop, first 48 hours only — here's what's in it`

# Technical Audit: "Shop it first: our Venus Williams collab, only on our app!" (Crate & Kids)

## 1. Technical Summary
Email renders via a standard Bronto/legacy MI (Message Inbox) template stack with heavy MSO/Outlook conditional CSS; the DOCTYPE and rendering scaffolding are sound, but the automated QA pass returned a 57% pass rate driven almost entirely by missing image `alt` attributes and absent email-authentication/compliance headers at the transport layer.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 62 tracking/click-redirect links were present and skipped by the automated HTTP prober (expected behavior for redirect-wrapped links — not itself a defect, but means link-destination validity is unconfirmed by this audit).
- Multiple tracking pixels/beacons detected: `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png` (6 instances), `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif`, `sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif` (5 instances, LiveRamp/RLCDN), and `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/...` (DoubleVerify). No functional issue found, but the volume (12+ separate pixel calls) is notable for load/privacy review outside this audit's scope.
- No broken/malformed href patterns identified in the visible source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **13 images/pixels missing `alt` text**, confirmed by QA output, including primary content images (e.g., `bbf6f9d4-...png`, `b3558db6-...jpg`, `971aa10f-...jpg`) and the Scene7 hero asset (`2026_0914_CBK_VenusWilliams_AppSneakPeek`). Decorative tracking pixels lacking alt is acceptable, but the content/hero images should carry descriptive `alt` for screen-reader users and for when images are blocked (common in Outlook/Gmail default states).
- Template uses `<style>` blocks with responsive `@media` breakpoints (460px, 640px, 768px) and MSO conditional comments (`<!--[if gte mso 9]>`, `<!--[if mso]>`) — standard, well-formed defensive coding for Outlook desktop rendering.
- `img {display:block}` and `border:0` resets applied globally — good practice, no issues found.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No merge tags, personalization placeholders (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `%%field%%`), or unresolved token syntax were visible in the truncated source. No issues found based on available content — recommend re-checking the untruncated body if personalized greeting/product blocks exist further down.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header not found** — QA flagged this may be a relay-capture artifact (AgentMail) rather than a true absence; needs verification directly against the raw MIME headers from the sending MTA, since CAN-SPAM/Gmail & Yahoo bulk sender rules require a functioning unsubscribe mechanism.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found (RFC 8058)** — one-click unsubscribe support cannot be confirmed. If genuinely absent, this is a compliance gap under Gmail/Yahoo's 2024+ bulk sender requirements.
- **Authentication-Results header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown from this capture; cannot confirm authentication posture. Flag for direct verification against the source MTA rather than the relay.
- No unsubscribe link text/footer was visible in the truncated HTML sample provided, so in-body unsubscribe link presence/placement could not be independently confirmed.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable — the 62 tracking/redirect links were skipped by the prober, so destination URLs and their UTM parameters are unknown. Recommend a follow-up pass that resolves the redirect chain (e.g., via the `mi.crateandbarrel.com` or Bronto click-tracking domain) to confirm final landing URLs carry consistent `utm_source`/`utm_medium`/`utm_campaign` values and land on live, matching pages (e.g., the app-exclusive Venus Williams collab page).

## 7. Recommendations
1. Verify List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post / Authentication-Results directly against raw headers from the originating MTA (not the AgentMail relay capture) to rule out a false positive before treating as a compliance defect.
2. Add descriptive `alt` text to the 6 non-pixel content/hero images identified above (empty `alt=""` is acceptable only for the tracking pixels/beacons).
3. Re-run the link prober against resolved (unwrapped) redirect targets to confirm the 62 tracking links resolve successfully and to validate UTM continuity into the landing page(s).
4. Confirm one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) is implemented given Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender enforcement — this is the highest-priority open item since it affects deliverability at scale, not just this single send.
