# Staying grounded in color
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-13**
## Executive summary

- CB2 again, and this is the fifteenth send I've logged in about five days. The sender name, "CB2 Free Design Services," made me look twice. It's the first time they've addressed my actual situation, which is two houses and one palette. The subject doesn't promise a sale, and I appreciated that after "ONLY HOURS LEFT TO SAVE" this morning. But the email opens with a "FINAL HOURS: The Long Weekend Sale, up to 60% off" banner anyway. The design-services story sits underneath it as if it's an afterthought.
- The middle is the best part. There's a styled "Grounded Spaces" hero, a wardrobe-stylist story, and a "Book your free design appointment" button. Below that is a moodboard grid of warm neutrals, then a "2 ways to meet, in store or online" block. That's close to what I'd want from a showroom-integration email. Then the email turns back into a promo pile: Upholstery Event up to 25% off, free shipping on orders of $99 or more, the app, Rewards, and a 15% signup offer. It's a lot to scroll through, and the pile is the same one I've seen all week.
- Would I click? Probably, on the design appointment, because I'm furnishing two places and a free consult has real value. Would I forward it? No. I'd save it to my inspiration folder for the moodboard images and leave the rest.

## What's working

- The "Grounded Spaces" hero and the earth-tone grid match the palette work I'm doing for both houses, and I'd save those images.
- "Book your free design appointment" appears twice, in black buttons with clear copy. That's a real service, not a coupon.
- The "2 ways to meet — in store or online" block ties the showroom and the web together, which I rarely see from these brands.
- The "Living room / Lighting / Dining room / Bedroom / Rugs" tiles let me jump straight to the category I'm shopping.
- The Rewards callout ("earn 10% back in Rewards Dollars, on purchases at CB2 and Crate & Barrel") speaks to my rewards-math habit.

## What's weak

- The sale banner leads the email even though the subject is about color. The mismatch muddies the message.
- Three separate sales stack up: the 60% Long Weekend Sale, the 25% Upholstery Event, and free shipping on $99 or more. Which one is the real offer?
- The design-services section has no swatch, sample, or fabric program, which is exactly what a color-palette email should offer.
- The fine print runs long and is too small to read on a phone without zooming.
- The 25% upholstery offer sits at the very bottom, though I'm picking sofas for two houses.
- CB2 is a Crate & Barrel sibling, and that family has sent me six emails this week. It's the same fatigue as Crate & Barrel's Samsung Frame TV send, where the real offer was in a small banner.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the design-services story and drop the sale banner to a thin strip. The subject says color, so the hero should too.
- 2. Pick one offer. Put the 25% upholstery event and free shipping together, and cut the 60% line unless it applies to what's pictured.
- 3. Add a free swatch or sample CTA next to the booking button. Article's "Fall is for full tables" story email worked because it was one idea, and this should be too.
- 4. Show which products appear in the moodboard grid, with names and prices.
- 5. Cap the cadence. Pottery Barn's strip and Wayfair's pergola wall have taught me to tune this out.

# Technical Audit: "Staying grounded in color" (CB2)

The HTML source was truncated, so anything below the `<style>` block was not inspected. That includes the footer, unsubscribe link, postal address and link hrefs. The QA header checks are likely relay artifacts. I mark what is confirmed and what is unverifiable.

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional layout with MSO conditional comments and `lang="en"`. It has 13 content images with no alt text, and 11 plain `http://` third-party tracking pixels. Compliance and tracking-link checks could not be verified from the data provided.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unprobed links:** The QA run skipped 62 tracking links because they are click-redirect domains. Destination status, redirect chains and UTM mapping are unverified.
- **Insecure pixels (confirmed):** 11 images load over `http://`: `http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?dtm_com=2&dtm_cid=2437...` and `http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2437/pub1.php` through `pub10.php?d98e199b...=1`. Clients that block mixed content or require HTTPS will drop them, which breaks the Dotomi cookie-sync and conversion tracking. Clients that proxy images over HTTPS, such as Gmail, are less affected.
- **Pixel sprawl:** Ten sequential `pubN.php` cookie-redirect pixels plus three first-party beacons (`mi.cb2.com/p/up/.../o.gif`, `dv.cb2.com/o/b1e9befa...`) add load weight and third-party calls on open. `o.gif` and `dv.cb2.com` serve over HTTPS.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text, content images (confirmed):** 13 images on `image.mail.cb2.com/lib/fe9213727564027a72/m/1/` have no `alt`. Examples: `bfcb4908-cb18-459f-aef9-e3e1a1c66de0.jpg`, `a9acf991-bcaf-4541-a82a-780abc47102e.jpg`, `72282bb5-644d-4e88-ae41-1077494521b0.jpg`. With images off, or with a screen reader, the content is blank or reads as a filename.
- **Missing alt text, tracking pixels:** `o.gif`, `dv.cb2.com/o/b1e9befa...` and the Dotomi pixels also lack `alt`. This is low impact, but `alt=""` is the correct value for decorative or tracking images.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` blocks pinch-zoom, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Text scaling disabled:** `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` and `body{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` stop iOS from scaling text. The `.f10` class forces 10px/12px on mobile.
- **Markup nits:**
  - `<meta name="x-apple-disable-message-reformatting">` is not self-closed in an XHTML doctype.
  - The unfilled template comment `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` is still in the source.
  - One font URL has a double slash (`www.cb2.com//Styles/...`). It likely resolves but is inconsistent with the other font URLs.
  - Web fonts come from two domains (`cb2.com` and `crateandbarrel.com`). Fallbacks are declared (`Helvetica, sans-serif`), so impact is minimal.
