# OUTDOOR PRIVACY SCREENS AND PARTITIONS ✨ BLACK FRIDAY IN JULY ✨ UP TO 80% OFF ✨ 
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- Eleven. That's where the Wayfair counter sits in my inbox, and I'm going to say it first because it frames everything — this is the eleventh Wayfair email in roughly two days, and the second one today alone. Yesterday's "WEIGHT BENCHES 🎉 🥳 BLACK FRIDAY IN JULY 🎉 🥳" already used up whatever goodwill I had for all-caps subject lines with strings of emojis. I opened this one purely because outdoor privacy screens are actually on my list for the second home — a screened side yard situation I've been trying to solve since spring. So the subject line did its job, just barely.
- Here's where it falls apart: I open it expecting privacy screens front and center, and instead I get a "picked just for you" product grid full of rugs, storage furniture, some pendant lighting, and what looks like a dehumidifier. The hero shouts 80% off, everything ships free, doorbuster deals — but outdoor privacy screens are nowhere to be seen in the rendered body. The subject teased a specific category and the email body defaulted to their standard batch-and-blast template. That bait-and-switch isn't egregious, but it's enough to kill the click.
- Mark as read. No click, no forward. If Article sent me this — which they wouldn't, because Article actually links to specific products — I might have engaged. This one I scroll through in about four seconds and move on.

## What's working

- **80% off in the hero is immediate and legible** — the red badge lands clean, no ambiguity about whether there's a real offer here.
- **"Everything Ships Free" banner** is in the right place in the hierarchy — it removes a friction point I think about for every outdoor furniture order (dimensions + freight = nightmare math).
- **"Doorbuster deals" section** creates a tiered urgency structure that's smarter than a single banner — at least there's a sense that some items are more time-sensitive than others.
- **"Picked just for you" personalization label** at the top signals they're trying to segment; even if the execution is imperfect, I notice the attempt more than I would from a brand I don't have history with.

## What's weak

- **The subject category doesn't appear in the body.** I opened for outdoor privacy screens; the email body shows rugs and storage. This is the most fixable and most frustrating miss.
- **Eleven emails in two days.** Pottery Barn sent me 39 in July total — bad — but Wayfair has hit ten-plus in 48 hours specifically, which is a different kind of aggression. This cadence is actively training me to stop opening.
- **"Shop all sales" CTA is the laziest possible button copy.** CB2's "THE FURNITURE EVENT IS ON" email last week at least made the CTA feel like an event. This is a filing cabinet labeled "stuff."
- **No specific product linked, no price shown on any tile.** The grid thumbnails are too small at mobile size to identify what anything is, and there's no anchor price — just "XX% off" on things I can't quite make out.
- **The subject line is a carnival barker.** ALL CAPS + three ✨ emojis + "BLACK FRIDAY IN JULY" — it reads as junk mail even when I know it's not, and it's the same structure as yesterday's weight bench send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the body with the actual category from the subject.** If you're going to say "outdoor privacy screens" in the subject, the first scroll on mobile should show me privacy screens — three hero products, prices, dimensions. Then go to the broader sale grid.
- 2. **Show a price, not just a percentage.** "Up to 80% off" on a $30 item is meaningless. Show me a $249 privacy screen marked down from $599 and I'm clicking.
- 3. **Retire the ALL CAPS subject format.** One use of caps reads as urgency; every subject line in caps reads as a car dealership ad. Article and CB2 both manage urgency in sentence case.
- 4. **Kill one email per day minimum from this cadence.** At eleven sends in two days, I am conditioning myself to delete unopened. The 80% open rate this brand clearly doesn't have right now is partly self-inflicted.
- 5. **"Shop all sales" → verb + specific noun.** "Shop outdoor privacy screens" or "Browse doorbuster picks" or literally anything that completes the sentence I started when I opened this email.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three top-rated picks under $300, doorbuster pricing through this week only.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issues found:**

| Issue | Evidence |
|---|---|
| `word-break: break-all` on `.ProductRec-name` | Breaks words at any character boundary, not just word boundaries. Correct value is `overflow-wrap: break-word` (or `word-break: break-word` for legacy WebKit). Can produce mid-word splits on product names. |
| Sofia font only provides WOFF, no WOFF2 | `src: url(...sofia_reg.woff) format("woff")` — WOFF2 delivers ~30% smaller payload; missing format means all clients that support WOFF2 fall back to the `mso-font-alt: "Arial"` Outlook fallback or skip custom font entirely. |
| `height: 48px !important` hard-clamped on `.ProductRec-name` | Fixed pixel heights on text containers clip long product names in most clients without an overflow or ellipsis rule. No `overflow: hidden` + `text-overflow: ellipsis` pairing is visible in the mobile block. |
| `@font-face` in `<style>` only — not inlined | Several major clients (older Gmail Android, some Samsung Mail versions) strip `<style>` blocks in `<head>`. Font will silently fail in those clients; `mso-font-alt` only covers Outlook. |

**No issues found:** Outlook stacking fix (`body[data-outlook-cycle]`), Gmail dark-mode prefix (`u ~ div`), and iOS Safari image-stacking fix (`@supports (-webkit-touch-callout: none)`) are all present and correctly authored.
