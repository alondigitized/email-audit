## What's working

- The "UP TO 60% OFF" headline is massive and legible, so I got the offer in about a second.
- The five category buttons (Furniture, Lighting, Decor, Rugs, Shop All) are full-width, high-contrast white on plum, and easy to thumb on a phone.
- The dahlia hero is moody and on-brand. It's the kind of image I'd save to my Pinterest folder.

## What's weak

- There isn't a single product in the email, no price, and no sale-versus-original comparison. "Up to 60%" could mean one rug.
- This is the same hero as 10/22's Warehouse Sale. It's the third Warehouse Sale email this week, and I'm numb to it.
- "A curated selection of last-chance markdowns" says nothing. Wayfair's pergola emails at least show a $134-off badge on a real product.
- The dining event's "30% OFF" strip sits under the 60% hero and muddies the message. The thin pale text over the photo is also harder to read.
- The footer carries two different fine-print exclusions (60% and 30%), and the "‡" sits right on the headline. I can't tell what's excluded without reading a wall of tiny text.
- A Crate & Barrel logo row is in the footer. After five C&B sends today, that's a reminder I don't need.

- 1. Replace the flower photo and generic copy with 3-4 actual markdown products showing sale and original price. Lead with lighting, since I'd shop it.
- 2. Put the sale end date in the hero. The fine print says October 28, but nothing up top does.
- 3. Drop the dining event, or send it separately. One offer per email.
- 4. Add shipping and availability cues for furniture, like "ships in 2 weeks" or "in stock," and a note on whether sale items can be sampled or returned.
- 5. Don't send the same creative three times in two days. Run a different hero or a "what's left" angle.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's HTML is a conventional XHTML table layout with responsive media queries. The confirmed problems are missing alt text on content images, 11 cleartext `http://` third-party pixel requests, and viewport settings that block zoom. The header-based compliance checks could not be completed because of how the email was captured. The source was truncated, so findings are limited to what was visible.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified tracking links:** 57 links were skipped as tracking/click-redirect domains, so none were HTTP-probed. Destination validity, redirect chains and 404s are unverified, not confirmed healthy.
- **Cleartext pixels:** 11 Dotomi (Epsilon) sync pixels load over `http://`:
  - `http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?dtm_com=2&dtm_cid=2437…`
  - `http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2437/pub1.php` through `pub10.php?d98e199bf5027afea04b790655b3dee2=1`
  - Image proxies and clients may block or rewrite non-HTTPS sources, so these cookie-sync calls will likely fail silently. Where they do load, the request leaves the HTTPS context unencrypted.
- **Ten sync pixels:** Ten separate cookie-redirect pixels in one send is a lot of third-party requests. That is a render-weight concern, and it is also a privacy exposure for recipients.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt on content images (7):** All are on `image.mail.cb2.com/lib/fe9213727564027a72/m/1/`:
  - `99e03f1a-…jpg`
  - `b2c04e8a-…jpg`
  - `2248d9e6-…jpg`
  - `2c006ef6-…jpg`
  - `0042f239-…jpg`
  - `e6d03b59-…jpg`
  - `219caa71-…jpg`
  - With images blocked, these show nothing. Screen readers will read the filenames or skip the images. If any are linked, the link has no accessible name.
- **Missing alt on pixels:** `mi.cb2.com/p/up/…/o.gif`, `dv.cb2.com/o/b7fe354c-…` and the Dotomi pixels have no alt. These should carry `alt=""` and be hidden from assistive tech. Absent `alt` is not equivalent to an empty one.
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` prevents pinch-zoom, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Text resizing disabled:** `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` appears in both the `*{}` rule and `body`. Together with the global `line-height:100%`, this limits user text scaling.
- **Minor:** `<meta name="x-apple-disable-message-reformatting">` is not self-closed in an XHTML 1.0 document. This is harmless in practice.
