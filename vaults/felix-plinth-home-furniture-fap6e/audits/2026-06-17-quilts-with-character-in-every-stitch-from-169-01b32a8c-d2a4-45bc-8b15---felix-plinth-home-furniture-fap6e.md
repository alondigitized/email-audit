# Quilts with character in every stitch, from $169
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-two. I'm keeping the tally going. This is the twenty-second Pottery Barn email I've opened, and it lands the morning after the "Bedding refresh in versatile neutrals" send — meaning PB just hit me with back-to-back bedding emails on consecutive days. That context is inescapable. I'm in the middle of furnishing two bedrooms across two properties, so quilts are technically on my list. But the timing is mid-June, there's a Summer Sale banner screaming 70% off at the top, and the hero product is... a quilt. Quilts and summer sales don't live together in my head. That dissonance is the first thing that hits.
- That said — the email is clean, the product photography is good, and the $169 anchor in the subject actually got me to open. There's a "Recommended Just For You" section that shows some personalization effort, and the Free Design Services module at the bottom is smart cross-sell given I've got two rooms to spec out. The loyalty rewards mention (10% back) is always relevant since I'm routing everything through the highest-rewards card anyway.
- I'd save this to my folder but probably not click right now. The seasonal misalignment is too distracting, and I'm still carrying the psychic residue of yesterday's bedding send. If they'd held this for late August or early September — genuine pre-fall window — I'd have clicked in under ten seconds.

## What's working

- **Price anchor in the subject line did its job.** "From $169" is the reason I opened this over any other mail in my queue. Concrete number, concrete product category, no mystery.
- **Photography is genuinely good.** The hero bedroom shot is styled the way I pin things — layered neutrals, interesting texture, not catalog-generic. Earned a second look.
- **Product grid is tight and shoppable.** Four products across, prices visible, no click-to-see-price friction. This is how PB does it right versus Wayfair, where the exclusive-brand-rug email showed a grid and then failed to deliver on the premise.
- **Free Design Services cross-sell is well-placed.** I noticed this was its own separate email yesterday from a different sender line; embedding it as a module here is the smarter play. I'm building rooms, not buying one quilt.
- **10% back in rewards is visible.** Small but matters. I'm doing the math on every big purchase.

## What's weak

- **Quilts plus Summer Sale banner is a jarring combo.** Crate & Barrel's 4th of July Warehouse Sale email — which landed this same week — at least had seasonal coherence. PB slapped a summer-sale header on a quilt email and hoped I wouldn't notice.
- **This is the second bedding email in two days.** Yesterday's "Bedding refresh in versatile neutrals" and today's quilts send feel like the same campaign split across two sends for no reason I can see. It reads as a coordination failure, not a strategy.
- **No deadline anywhere in the email body.** The Summer Sale is flagged at the top but there's no "ends Sunday" or "through June 22" to create any real urgency. Article ran a tighter time-bounded offer in their summer hosting send.
- **"SHOP ALL BEDDING" goes wide, not deep.** I'd have more interest in a "Shop the Quilt Collection" CTA that keeps me in the specific product family the subject promised. Don't tease specificity and deliver a category browse.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Hold this email until late August.** The quilt story is legitimately good — run it when the air turns and people actually want to think about layering. A Summer Sale hook on this product is fighting itself.
- 2. **Add a sell-by date to the Summer Sale module.** "Sale ends July 4" with a single line is all it takes. CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" had the same problem — the offer felt eternal and therefore skippable.
- 3. **Replace "SHOP ALL BEDDING" with a quilt-specific CTA.** "Shop the Quilt Edit" or "See All Quilts" — stay on the promise from the subject line.
- 4. **Don't run back-to-back bedding emails.** If the machine queued this the day after "Bedding refresh in versatile neutrals," the suppression logic needs a same-category cooldown of at least 5 days.
- 5. **Lean into the renovation angle.** I'm building two bedrooms. A "furnishing a new room?" sub-header or a "as low as $169 for a full bedroom refresh" hook would actually match how I'm thinking about this purchase.

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Quilts with character" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Two CTAs return 403 errors (one a revenue-critical credit card apply link), server-side PHP code leaked into a `<style>` block as a template processing artifact, three image assets served over HTTP, and 34 images lack alt text. Authentication/unsubscribe headers were not captured by the relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[BROKEN — HIGH]** Key credit card "Apply and Buy" CTA returns HTTP 403:
`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY...`
This is an actionable revenue link (header banner), not a tracking pixel. A 403 on the click-capture endpoint means the destination record may have expired or been misconfigured in the ESP.

**[BROKEN — MEDIUM]** West Elm spacer GIF returns 403 and is served over HTTP:
`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
This is a cross-brand asset from a sister property (West Elm) embedded in a Pottery Barn send — likely a copy-paste artifact from a shared template. The domain, brand, and protocol are all wrong.

**[INFO]** 25 click-redirect links via `click.e.potterybarn.com` were not probed — standard ESP click-tracking; no action needed unless destinations are verified separately.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] PHP code leaked into `<style>` block.** The following server-side PHP `header()` calls appear as literal text inside a CSS `<style>` tag in the `<head>`:

```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```

This is unprocessed PHP that was never executed. CSS parsers silently discard invalid rules so rendering impact is low, but it signals a broken template pipeline (a `.php` partial was included without being processed). Any secrets or logic that follows the same path may also leak.

**[HIGH] 3 images served over HTTP** — will be blocked by Gmail, Outlook, and most modern clients with mixed-content enforcement:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` (footer Key CC banner)
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` (open-tracking pixel)

**[MEDIUM] 34 images missing `alt` text.** Confirmed missing on all content images including primary hero (`S26_TOP_SummerSale_NoUrgency.jpg`), product images, and all footer slice images (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`). Affects screen reader users and image-blocked rendering.

**[LOW] Empty `<title>` tag.** `<title></title>` — inconsequential in most email clients but visible in some browser-based preview modes.

**[LOW] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` prevents pinch-to-zoom on iOS, which is an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4).
