# FINAL DAY: Up to 50% off our summer sale→
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-10**
## Executive summary

- Same conflict I flagged on this exact email at the top: the banner says "back-to-school sale: up to 20% off" and two inches below it the hero says "up to 50% off summer sale." Those are two different offers stacked on top of each other with no explanation, and I genuinely don't know which one applies to what in the grid below it. This is Crate & Kids, which means it's also the fourth or fifth Crate & Barrel-family email in my inbox this week — I don't have kids, so a nursery glider and a "Nuna PIPA" car seat aren't doing anything for me regardless of the discount math.
- Buried in the middle is the one section that's actually relevant to my life: "500+ new fall arrivals, starting at $29" with a rug that looks like it belongs in a living room, not a nursery. That's the only thing in this whole email I'd tap. Everything else — backpacks, lunch boxes, car seats, "personalize it" with initials on a diaper bag — is squarely parent-of-a-toddler territory, and I'm renovating two houses of adult furniture.
- Not clicking, not forwarding. Marking as read and moving on, same as the last Crate & Kids send.

## What's working

- The "500+ new fall arrivals, starting at $29" module has a real price anchor and a rug image that could actually fit my renovation, unlike the rest of the email.
- Layout is clean and renders with no broken images or overlapping text — better than the Wayfair gazebo email that was half broken-image placeholders.
- The category grid (furniture, kids bedding, baby bedding, decor, rugs, clearance) is at least organized and scannable, similar to how the mothership C&B "Up to 60% off amazing finds" email structured its grid.

## What's weak

- Two competing offers above the fold — "up to 20% off back-to-school" banner vs. "up to 50% off summer sale" hero — is a worse version of the confusion CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" send had, and CB2 at least kept one consistent number.
- Kids-first: cribs, car seats, backpacks, lunch boxes, nursery gliders. I'm not the customer for 90% of this send.
- The "we love these for you" personalization block is baby gear (car seat, dresser, glider) — there's zero chance this reflects my actual browsing, so it reads like generic Crate & Kids filler, not real personalization.
- "New Crate Card... earning Reward Dollars" pitch at the bottom feels tacked on and untimed against everything above it.
- This is at least the fourth Crate & Barrel-family send this week (Crate & Kids twice, mothership C&B, TheDesignDesk entryway content) — the fatigue Pottery Barn's "eleventh email this week" hit is happening here too, just from a family of brands instead of one.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one offer and one number. Kill either the 20%-off back-to-school banner or the 50%-off summer-sale hero — running both above the fold reads as sloppy, not generous.
- 2. Move the fall-arrivals-from-$29 module up near the top — it's the only section with crossover appeal to a non-parent, don't bury it mid-scroll.
- 3. If this segment (me) has never bought kids' gear, suppress Crate & Kids sends entirely and fold me into mothership Crate & Barrel cadence instead — I'm already drowning in C&B family volume this week.
- 4. Cut or relocate the Crate Card pitch to its own send; it's competing for attention with a sale banner and a fall-arrivals push in the same email.
