# Free shipping on 100s of items. Don’t miss out!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-02**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so this is Crate & Kids again — same sender that already hit my inbox twice today with the identical "School starts SOON! Up to 20% off essentials" subject, and now it's back with "Free shipping on 100s of items." That's the third Crate & Kids/Crate & Barrel-family email I've seen today. My thumb didn't even fully register the subject before I clocked the sender name and went "oh, this again."
- Once I actually open it, the content confirms what I already flagged on the last Crate & Kids send: this is a nursery and baby-gear catalog — car seats, cribs, baby bath robes, a "crate cuddlies" plush section — not home renovation content. I'm mid-reno on two houses. I need dressers, lighting, window treatments, maybe a sectional. A Nuna infant car seat and a "Reclining Baby Car Seat + Stroller Travel System" are not on my list, and no amount of "we love these for you" personalization copy convinces me otherwise, because it very obviously isn't for me.
- Verdict: not clicking, not forwarding. This gets archived into the Pinterest-adjacent folder purely because it's technically Crate & Barrel family and I might want the design-appointment or sofa content buried three brands deep in a Wayfair-vs-Crate-vs-CB2 folder sweep later — but nothing here earns an open click today.

## What's working

- The "when it all ships free (yippee!)" headline is at least a clear, single-minded offer with an unambiguous "Shop All Free Shipping" button — no hunting for the CTA.
- TheDesignDesk section (book a free design appointment, sweepstakes entry) is a real service hook, not just another product tile — that's the one part of this email that could apply to an actual house, not just a nursery.
- The Crate & Barrel Card callout with Reward Dollars is relevant to how I actually shop — I run everything through my highest-rewards card, so a loyalty-card upgrade at least speaks my language even buried this far down.
- Clean render — no broken images, no overlapping text, nothing looks unfinished.

## What's weak

- This is a baby/toddler catalog wearing a "free shipping" subject line — car seats, cribs, baby bath hoodies, diaper-adjacent everything. I don't have kids in the picture; I'm renovating houses, not nurseries.
- Same-day fatigue: this is the third-plus Crate-family send today after two identical "School starts SOON" emails. That's not a campaign, that's spam-adjacent cadence, and it's exactly the "same lamp in three emails" annoyance I already have with Pottery Barn and Wayfair — except here it's the same sender three times.
- Top banner says "back-to-school sale: up to 20% off," then the body pivots to a completely different lead offer (free shipping) — two headline offers competing for the top slot instead of one clean pitch.
- The "we love these for you" personalized grid (backpack, infant car seat, dresser, glider chair, crib, activity chair) reads like a generic new-parent bundle, not anything actually informed by my purchase history. If Crate & Barrel knows my Halloween-wreath-and-sofa history the way their own brand history says they do, this "for you" grid should not be all baby gear.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment me out of Crate & Kids entirely, or at minimum stop sending Crate & Kids and Crate & Barrel same-day — pick one brand voice per day per subscriber. Wayfair's 48-hour rug clearance worked because it was one offer, one category, no competing sibling-brand noise.
- 2. Pick one hero offer and lead with it. Either "up to 20% off back-to-school" or "free shipping on 100s of items" — not both stacked at the top fighting for attention.
- 3. If "we love these for you" is going to claim personalization, actually filter by category history — a Crate & Barrel furniture/decor buyer shouldn't see an infant car seat as pick #2.
- 4. Move TheDesignDesk free-appointment content higher — it's the one section with cross-demographic appeal (renovators want design help too), and right now it's buried under three screens of baby gear.
