# 100+ new sale styles UP TO 70% OFF
**Score:** 10/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- Eighteen. I went back through my folder and this is email number eighteen from Pottery Barn since Tuesday. The last send — the June 13 Monique Lhuillier drop — at least had something new. This one opens with "Summer Sale Up to 70% off" over a tiled category nav I have now memorized: Furniture, Outdoor, Sofas & Chairs, Bedding, Decor, Pillows & Throws, Tabletop, Rugs, Lighting, Bath. Same grid, same percentages, new send date.
- What I'll give them credit for: the "Recommended Just for You" section surfaced a Farmhouse Platform Bed front and center, and I actually have a bedroom to furnish in the second house right now. That single product placement did more work than the entire hero. The Free Design Services "Book Now" block mid-email is also legitimately useful when you're running two renovation projects and can barely remember which house needs what — it's one of the few times that module made me pause. But those two bright spots are floating in the middle of an email I've essentially already read seventeen times this week.
- Not clicking. Not forwarding — everyone I'd forward it to is also getting this same eighteen-email sequence. If I want that bed, I'll search for it directly and skip rewarding this cadence with a click.

## What's working

- "Recommended Just for You" surfaced bedroom furniture — genuinely on my list for house two right now, which is exactly what personalization is supposed to do.
- Category nav is comprehensive and scannable; I can triage in about two seconds without scrolling.
- Free Design Services "Book Now" speaks directly to someone with active renovation decisions open — a rare moment of functional relevance in an otherwise promotional email.
- "Summer Sale" seasonal framing is accurate; it's mid-June, this is not a stale promo.
- The 10% back in rewards section is visible, which matters — I run everything through a rewards card and Pottery Barn knows I'm a member.

## What's weak

- This email's structure is indistinguishable from the June 13 "Up to 70% off 100s of NEW DEALS" send — same hero percentages, same nav grid, same module order. The brand has copy-pasted urgency eighteen times and drained it of meaning.
- "SHOP ALL SALE" as the primary CTA on an email claiming 100+ new styles is lazy. CB2's "100+ new items added to Warehouse Sale" from last week linked straight into the new arrivals — that felt like discovery. This bounces me to a homepage I already know.
- No pricing visible on the "Recommended Just for You" product tiles. Wayfair's beds email last week put price ranges right on the card. I want the number before I click, not after.
- Affirm at the bottom is dead weight. I have never made a Pottery Barn furniture decision because I noticed Affirm in the footer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the send frequency in half, immediately.** One sale email per 72 hours or the 70% off figure stops carrying any weight. Cadence is destroying this brand's inbox equity faster than any creative problem could.
- 2. **Show prices on the personalized product tiles.** Price anchoring plus personalization in one panel would make the "Recommended Just for You" section actually drive add-to-cart decisions instead of thumbnail browsing.
- 3. **Make the hero CTA category-specific.** If the behavioral data shows I've been looking at bedroom furniture, that button should read "Shop Bedroom Sale" — not the entire store. This is table stakes for a brand with Pottery Barn's data.
- 4. **Give Free Design Services its own send.** It's a genuinely differentiating offer buried as module four. For a mid-renovation customer, a standalone design consult email with a booking CTA would convert far better than it does here.
