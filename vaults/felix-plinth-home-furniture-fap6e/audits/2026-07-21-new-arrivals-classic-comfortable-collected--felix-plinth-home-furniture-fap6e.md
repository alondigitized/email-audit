# New arrivals—classic, comfortable, collected
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-three. That's where I am with Pottery Barn in July, and I'll admit this one caught me in a better mood than the three they sent yesterday. Three emails on a single Sunday — a digital catalog, a gift card sweepstakes, a vague "effortlessly styled space" editorial — and now on Monday morning they lead with the one thing that actually earns my attention: new arrivals. The hero room is doing real work. Deep navy blues, warm wood, heritage-collected pieces — that's almost exactly the direction I'm taking the living room at the second home. The instinct to reset after a weekend of catalog and sweepstakes filler is correct.
- But the Summer Sale "Up to 70% off" banner riding above all of it is already wallpaper. I saw that exact offer framing in the July 19 "Happening now" send, which I gave an 8 for hitting the right moment. By now it's just a persistent sticky at the top of every Pottery Barn email, which means I've stopped reading it. What I actually want to know — and what this email never answers — is whether any of these new arrivals are in the sale, or if they're full price. That ambiguity is real friction for someone running purchase decisions through a rewards card calculator.
- I'll save this to my folder for later reference — the room styling alone is worth pinning. I won't click through right now, and I definitely won't forward it. The curtain rod bracket leading my "Recommended Just for You" section tells me the personalization engine is working off a single hardware purchase from six months ago, not anything resembling a room-level understanding of what I'm building.

## What's working

- The hero room image is genuinely on-brief for what I'm speccing — navy and indigo against warm wood is exactly the direction I'm taking the second home's main living space. First time this month Pottery Barn's editorial actually felt aimed at me.
- "New arrivals" as the lead concept after a weekend of sweepstakes and catalog emails is the right reset. It signals fresh inventory exists, which a renovator cares about.
- The outdoor seating vignette lower in the email is well-composed and evocative — rattan, lush greenery, relaxed layering. Summer-appropriate without being lazy.
- "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" is visible and matters. That's the margin math that keeps me buying Pottery Barn at full price instead of waiting for CB2 to run a comparable piece.
- Affirm callout signals high-ticket items are in play — relevant when I'm mid-renovation on two properties and actively spreading purchases across financing options.

## What's weak

- The Summer Sale "Up to 70% off" banner is a re-run from July 19 and has become wallpaper. I've tuned it out.
- "Recommended Just for You" is headlined by a curtain rod bracket. I'm mid-renovation, thinking in rooms, and that widget is pulling from a narrow corner of my purchase history. Article's home tour approach from a few weeks back — an actual room editorial — beats this personalization engine embarrassingly.
- No delineation between which new arrivals are in the sale and which are full price. The banner creates an expectation the body doesn't resolve.
- "Shop Now" as the hero CTA is doing the bare minimum. CB2's recent sends — even with their all-caps problems — at least surface category-level CTAs. "Shop New Living Room" would have landed better here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Separate sale and full-price new arrivals explicitly.** Badge sale-eligible items with a price or percentage inline. Don't let the sale banner float free of the product grid — it creates ambiguity that costs you the click from people who actually do the math.
- 2. **Suppress or demote the Summer Sale header for subscribers who've seen it 4+ times in a week.** If that's not possible at the send level, make it secondary — move it below the editorial hero, not above it.
- 3. **Overhaul the "Recommended Just for You" block.** Cluster by room intent, not single-SKU purchase history. If I've bought a curtain rod bracket, surface the rod, the rings, the curtain panel — not just the bracket again.
- 4. **Give the hero a room-specific CTA the way Article does.** "Shop This Room" or "Get This Look" linked to a shoppable room page beats the generic "Shop All New Arrivals" in click-through every time — and it costs nothing to wire up.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New arrivals: the heritage living room`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Just landed — classic pieces, summer pricing on select styles`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Navy blues, warm wood, and collected pieces that last — see what's new before the sale ends`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The rooms you've been building toward just got new inventory`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP `header()` calls inside `<style>` block — template bug**
The second `<style>` block in `<head>` ends with:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are PHP cache-busting directives that have leaked into the HTML output verbatim. They are invalid CSS and will be silently ignored by email clients, but they indicate template file corruption — a PHP script's `header()` calls were copy-pasted or interpolated into the wrong section. This poses no rendering failure today but is a template integrity defect.

**38 images missing `alt` text**
Virtually every content image — product grid (`slices_02.jpg` through `slices_12.jpg`), hero banner (`F26_TOP_SummerSalePartC_NoUrgency.jpg`), product shots (bed, chair, towel, rug, quilt), footer slices, and tracking pixels — has no `alt` attribute. When images are blocked (default in Outlook, many corporate clients), the email renders as a column of empty boxes with no readable content.

**3 images served over HTTP — will be blocked in secure contexts**
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` — footer Key Rewards image
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` — CC banner slice

Gmail, iOS Mail, and most modern clients will block mixed-content images. The Key Rewards footer image will not render for a significant portion of recipients.

`http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
Not captured by the relay. If missing in the actual envelope, major ISPs (Gmail, Yahoo, Outlook) will not render the native unsubscribe button in the UI — increasing spam complaints and reducing deliverability. Required under Yahoo/Google 2024 bulk sender requirements for >5k/day senders.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent**
One-click unsubscribe (the machine-readable POST variant) is absent. This is now enforced by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders. Without it, senders risk deferral.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not captured**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status is unknown through the AgentMail relay. Cannot confirm authentication chain. If DKIM is failing or misaligned, inbox placement at major providers will be affected.
