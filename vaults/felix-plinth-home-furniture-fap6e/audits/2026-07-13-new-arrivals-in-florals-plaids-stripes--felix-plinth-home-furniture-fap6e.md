# NEW ARRIVALS in florals, plaids & stripes
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- Twenty. I went back and counted because at this point keeping score is the only thing that makes opening another Pottery Barn email feel like an active choice rather than a reflex. Twenty emails since July 1st, and here we land on a new arrivals send — no sale, no urgency — which is actually the move I've been waiting for. The last few Pottery Barn emails were pure sale-hammer, and I'd started treating them like white noise. This one at least gave me something different to look at.
- The florals and plaids in the hero are nice — I could see that plaid wingback working in the second home's sitting room — but the further I scroll, the more the email drifts away from me. The blue-and-white dinnerware spread, the floral duvet in navy paisley, the curtain panels... the styling reads as distinctly traditional, maybe 45+ female. The "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" block halfway down tries to recover but the product mix doesn't obviously connect to someone mid-renovation doing two homes at once.
- I'd mark this as read, not delete — which is a small win for Pottery Barn given where we are in July. Would I click? Maybe the wingback. Would I forward to a friend? Only to my partner for a second opinion on the sitting room chair.

## What's working

- **Break from the sale pattern.** After nineteen consecutive discount-signaling subject lines, "new arrivals" actually reads as a palate cleanser. Article pulled this exact same move on July 10th and it felt fresh; Pottery Barn gets credit for the same instinct here.
- **Hero image is strong.** The wingback chair in that plaid fabric, warm light, layered textures — that's a room I'm trying to build. The visual hierarchy puts me there immediately.
- **"RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" personalization signal.** It's visible and clearly labeled. Crate & Barrel buries personalization or skips it entirely. The attempt is noted even if the execution is wobbly.
- **No render bugs.** Clean column layout, images load, no overlapping text or broken sections. IKEA's transactional OTP email last week was cleaner than some Pottery Barn promos I've seen — this one holds up.
- **Pattern breadth.** Florals, plaids, and stripes in one email means at least one of the three has a shot at landing for any subscriber. The stripes in particular are more seasonless and masculine-skewing.

## What's weak

- **No offer means no urgency.** New arrivals emails need either a discovery hook ("only 12 in stock") or a first-to-know benefit ("exclusive to email subscribers first"). This has neither. Without a concrete reason to click now, I'll tell myself I'll come back and I won't.
- **The dinnerware and floral bedding skew away from me.** Blue-and-white transferware plates and a navy paisley duvet are beautiful objects, but they're not speaking to a 36-year-old guy trying to figure out how to kit out two living rooms simultaneously. Compare CB2's "How a gallerist lives with objects" — at least that editorial angle tried to make the lifestyle feel aspirational to a specific person.
- **App download block kills momentum.** There's a CTA in the lower section pushing the Pottery Barn app. That's fine but it arrives just as I'm getting interested in the product grid. It breaks the shop-flow at the worst possible moment.
- **"RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" doesn't feel targeted enough.** If they know enough to label it, they should know enough to load it with furniture SKUs — not whatever that room shot is. Article's algorithm surfaced stuff I'd actually browsed. This feels like an A/B test on a widget, not real personalization.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a first-mover hook to the hero copy.** Something like "In stores this week — shop before it sells out" or "New this season, available now." New arrivals without a scarcity signal is just a catalog page.
- 2. **Swap the dinnerware hero block for something renovation-adjacent.** I'm on the list because I'm buying furniture and textiles, not because I'm resetting my dining table. Push the wingback or the striped performance fabric upholstery options higher.
- 3. **Pull the app block entirely or push it to the footer.** It is actively interrupting the product scroll at the worst possible time.
- 4. **Let "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" earn its header.** If the personalization engine is pulling furniture and large-format textiles for my account, show me those. If it's not, rename the section or drop it — false personalization is worse than no personalization.
- 5. **Test a stronger subject line with a pattern-specific noun:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `The plaid chair everyone's been waitlisting`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New patterns just landed — florals, plaids, stripes`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `First look at this season's new arrivals — shop before they sell out`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The wingback. The duvet. The plates. All new this week.`
