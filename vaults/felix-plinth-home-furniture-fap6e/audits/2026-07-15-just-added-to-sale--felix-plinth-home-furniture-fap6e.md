# JUST ADDED TO SALE
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-one. That's the CB2 count now, and I'm not tracking it because I enjoy spreadsheets — I'm tracking it because watching the number grow is the only thing that makes opening another one feel like a conscious decision. The subject "JUST ADDED TO SALE" in all caps tells me nothing specific: not what was added, not what category, not what percentage, not when it ends. I open it anyway because furniture is live purchase intent right now and I can't afford to miss a 40% off window on two simultaneous renovation lists.
- Once I'm inside, the email earns some of my time. The "MORE FOR YOU" product grid — a platform bed, a folding table, a rope chandelier, a dining chair, a wall-mount bookshelf — reads like someone actually looked at my browsing history rather than shotgunning the catalog. The outdoor section with 50% off is where my cursor actually goes; my second home needs a patio situation and that's a higher discount on the thing I most need. I'd click "VIEW ON SALE" on the outdoor block.
- But this is landing in the same inbox that already has "GET IT ON SALE" (July 13), "NEW COLLECTION" (July 14), and "Their personal spaces. Our collectors' items." (also July 15) from the same sender. At some point the content doesn't matter — the signal-to-noise ratio kills the brand's own conversion rate. I click the outdoor CTA, mark it read, and feel mildly resentful. I don't forward it. There's nothing here that makes me feel like I found something; it just feels like CB2 found me again.

## What's working

- The hero hierarchy is clean — "THE SUMMER EDIT / UP TO 40% OFF" and the "SHOP THE SALE" button land above the fold before any scrolling, unlike the Wayfair recliners email that buried its primary CTA below a wall of product tiles.
- The outdoor section gets its own module and its own higher number ("up to 50% off") — functionally a second email nested inside, which is smart if you know the recipient is shopping multiple categories.
- "MORE FOR YOU" shows actual before/after pricing on individual products, which is what I need before I commit a click — the Barro Acacia Folding Table at a visible discount is a concrete signal, not vague "deals."
- "EARN 10% BACK" is visible in the loyalty section — I always run the rewards math against my Amex, but I appreciate having the number on screen.
- No render issues anywhere — plant photography is genuinely appealing, layout holds, nothing's broken.

## What's weak

- "JUST ADDED TO SALE" in all caps tells me nothing. Pottery Barn's July 15 subject "New deals up to 70% off + Starting at $899: Sofa collections" gives me a category, a floor price, and a discount rate in the same 57 characters. CB2 just said words.
- No end date visible anywhere. "Up to 40% off" without an expiry is ambient noise; Pottery Barn's same-day email made the time boundary feel real even if the deadline was soft.
- The "LEAVE US A REVIEW, WIN $1000" block sits square in the middle of a purchase funnel. It's a sweepstakes interrupt at exactly the wrong moment — I'm thinking about the folding table and now I'm being asked to pivot to a review form.
- The outdoor collection, which is arguably the highest-conversion item for second-home shoppers, is below the fold and gets smaller product thumbnails than the indoor items. That's the wrong prioritization.
- The preheader appears to show navigation links ("SHOP / FURNITURE / DÉCOR / SALE") rather than intentional copy — wasted real estate in the inbox preview pane.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the outdoor section as the hero** — 50% off outdoor furniture is the higher number and the more specific purchase signal; it should be above the fold, not buried below the Summer Edit banner.
- 2. **Add a hard end date to every offer callout** — "Ends July 20" or "This weekend only" turns ambient sale signaling into actual urgency; Article pulled this off with no discount at all on their July 11 entryway editorial just by implying finitude.
- 3. **Remove the "LEAVE US A REVIEW" block from sale emails** — put it in a post-purchase triggered flow where it belongs; here it's a momentum killer.
- 4. **Drop the all-caps subject line** — it reads spam-adjacent in a crowded inbox and burns the one character budget where CB2 could actually differentiate.
- 5. **Suppress to anyone who opened a CB2 email in the prior 48 hours** unless the new-to-sale items are dramatically different from the last send's feature products (they weren't).

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — `text-size-adjust: none` applied globally:**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
The value `none` is non-standard and disables text scaling entirely on mobile. Spec-compliant value is `100%`. This will cause unreadable text on some Android clients.

**Accessibility violation — `maximum-scale=1` in viewport:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom. Violates WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Should be removed or replaced with `maximum-scale=5`.

**Narrow mobile breakpoint:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)
```
Standard responsive email breakpoint is 480px–600px. At 418px this misses several common Android viewport widths (420px–428px logical width). Likely causes layout breakage on those devices.

**Conflicting `@font-face` declarations for "Lineto Brown Sub":**
Three separate `@font-face` blocks declare the same `font-family: "Lineto Brown Sub"` with overlapping weight and source stacks. The third declaration (`woff2`, weight 400) conflicts with the first (which uses `.eot`+`.woff` with no weight scoping). Last-declared wins in most clients, making the `.eot` stack dead weight.

**"Lineto Brown Light Sub" missing woff2:**
```css
@font-face {
  font-family: "Lineto Brown Light Sub";
  src: url("https://www.cb2.com/Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff");
}
```
Only `.woff` declared — no `woff2` fallback. Modern clients will load unnecessarily large files.
