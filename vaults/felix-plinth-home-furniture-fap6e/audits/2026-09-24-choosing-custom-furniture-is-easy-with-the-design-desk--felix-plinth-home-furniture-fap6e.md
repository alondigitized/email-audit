# Choosing custom furniture is easy with The Design Desk →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-24**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "The Design Desk" — this actually landed at the right time, because I've got two houses going right now and I genuinely don't know how to spec custom pieces for either without paying a decorator. So credit where due: this is a real service pitch, not a lifestyle mood board dressed up as a sale. That said, it's a marathon — I counted like ten sections before I hit the app download pitch at the bottom, and by the "we love these for you" product grid I'd already mentally checked out.
- The core idea — free design appointment, mood board, swatches, "see it in your space" — is genuinely useful for someone renovating two properties simultaneously. But it reads like an internal deck got exported straight into an email. There's no single hero offer, no urgency, just six different CTAs all saying some version of "Book Your Free Appointment." Compare that to how Article's "organized entryway" send worked — one problem, one fix, three steps, done. This is the whole Design Desk pitch deck jammed into one scroll.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I actually sit down and read the whole thing on a Saturday. Would I forward it to a friend? No — it's not punchy enough to share, it's a "bookmark and come back to" email, and those mostly die in my Pinterest-adjacent folder.

## What's working

- The premise is real: free design service + custom furniture is a legit answer to my actual problem right now, not a generic promo.
- "Start with a mood board," "choose what's right for you," "see it in your space" — that's an honest, logical walkthrough of how the service actually works, not just product bait.
- The room photography is consistent — warm neutrals, natural light, the same aesthetic thread runs top to bottom, which is more disciplined than the CB2 app-ad hero I saw this week.
- Bestseller/Exclusive tags on the sofa fabrics and coffee tables give me a little social-proof anchor before I even click through.
- The Caraway coffee maker cross-sell at "$500" tease is a decent impulse add for someone furnishing a whole house, even if it's off-category.

## What's weak

- Way too long — ten-plus sections before the footer. I'm renovating two houses, not writing a thesis; I want the appointment booking CTA above the fold, not buried after five scroll-stops.
- Six nearly identical "Book Your Free Appointment" buttons with zero variation in urgency or framing — by the third one I've stopped reading the button copy entirely.
- No pricing, no timeline, no "how long does a custom order take" — for furniture that's the first thing I actually need to know before I book anything.
- The Caraway coffee maker and the "$500 off" app teaser feel bolted on at the end, like two different emails got stapled together.
- Zero loyalty/rewards-card math anywhere — I put every purchase on my highest-rewards card and this email gives me nothing to calculate.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this to three sections max: what the Design Desk is, how it works (3 steps, like Article did), one CTA. Everything past that belongs on the landing page, not the email.
- 2. Put a real number in the hero — "free 1-on-1 design session, most projects delivered in 8-10 weeks" beats a vague "it's easy" promise.
- 3. Kill five of the six duplicate buttons. One strong CTA at the top, one at the very bottom as a reminder — that's it.
- 4. Move the coffee maker and app-download blocks into a separate, unrelated send — they dilute the custom-furniture pitch and read like filler.
- 5. Add a line about rewards or financing — even "earn 2x points with your Crate&Barrel card" would make this feel built for someone who actually shops the way I do.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your free custom design session — no cost, no catalog guessing`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Skip the guesswork: a real person designs your room, free`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Book a 1-on-1 session, pick fabrics, see it in your space before you buy`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Most custom pieces ship in 8-10 weeks — start with a free consult`
