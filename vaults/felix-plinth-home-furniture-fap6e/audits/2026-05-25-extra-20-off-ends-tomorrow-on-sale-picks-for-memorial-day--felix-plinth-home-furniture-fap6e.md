# Extra 20% off 🕰️ ENDS TOMORROW + On-sale picks for Memorial Day
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-seven. That's where the Pottery Barn tally sits since Sunday, and here's number twenty-seven — clock emoji, "ENDS TOMORROW" in caps, same 20% off offer they've been running since at least Thursday. At this point I have developed genuine immunity. My brain has pattern-matched this creative to "background radiation" and started routing it directly to the same mental folder as white noise and car alarm tests.
- To be fair to the email itself: it's clean. Extra 20% off is in the hero where it belongs. The "SHOP ALL SALE" button is unambiguous. The lifestyle photography is aspirational in the right register for someone juggling two renovations — light-filled rooms, furniture that implies money without screaming it. But "on-sale picks for Memorial Day" in the subject promises me a curated shortlist, and what I got was a category-wide SHOP ALL button and a grid of products ranging from a sectional sofa to a blue-and-white china pattern. Those are not "picks." That's inventory.
- The saving grace — and I mean the only reason I'd linger at all — is the "Recommended Just for You" section at the bottom. That is the single most interesting thing in this email and Pottery Barn buried it like a footnote. I would not click the hero. I might scroll to the personalized grid and see if anything useful shows up for the primary kitchen reno. Would I forward this to a friend? Not a chance — they've already gotten their own twenty-seven.

## What's working

- **Extra 20% off is the first thing I read.** No hierarchy confusion, no buried offer — the discount number is in the hero, large, immediately legible. CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" from earlier this week couldn't even give me a percentage. Pottery Barn gets this right.
- **"ENDS TOMORROW" is credible deadline language.** One specific day. Not "limited time," not "while supplies last." Article's "Last chance: Memorial Day Sale ends today" ran the same play and I respected it there too — a date is better than vague urgency every time.
- **Visual hierarchy holds together.** Logo → offer → lifestyle photo → product grid → CTA. My eye moves through it in order. Nothing fights for attention it doesn't deserve.
- **"Recommended Just for You" section exists.** Even buried, this is the right instinct. At email twenty-seven, the only thing that could convert me is something that signals they know who I am, not just that they have furniture to sell.
- **No render bugs.** The grid is intact, images load, nothing overlaps. Given the volume of sends this week, consistent execution matters.

## What's weak

- **The cadence has destroyed the offer's credibility.** I saw "ENDS SOON" yesterday. I saw "FINAL WEEKEND" the day before. Now it's "ENDS TOMORROW." At some point "ends tomorrow" stops meaning what it says. Crate & Kids sent one Double Rewards email on furniture and it hit differently precisely because they didn't spam me into numbness first — one well-timed message versus twenty-seven fire-and-forget blasts.
- **The subject promises picks; the email delivers a sitewide button.** "On-sale picks for Memorial Day" implied to me that someone at Pottery Barn had identified three or five specific products I should know about. A "SHOP ALL SALE" CTA is the opposite of curation. That's a minor bait-and-switch.
- **The product grid is too wide.** Blue-and-white china alongside a neutral sectional alongside towels — I'm renovating, not hosting a registry party. Wayfair's cabinet-and-drawer-pulls email from this week was annoying in volume but at least it named a specific category I actually need right now. This email tries to be everything to everyone.
- **No price anchors on individual products.** The grid shows items but not their sale prices. If you're selling me on 20% off, show me the math — before and after, right there in the grid. Make the discount feel real on something concrete.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Deliver the "picks" the subject line promised.** Three products, above the fold, with before/after pricing visible. "Barcelona Dining Table: $999 → $799. Sculptural Pendant: $349 → $279." That's what earns the word "picks" and gives this send a reason to exist that the previous twenty-six didn't.
- 2. **Kill the clock emoji.** It's doing desperation-signaling work when "ENDS TOMORROW" already carries the urgency on its own. And after a week of escalating clock/heart/star emoji in subject lines, the emoji is noise, not signal.
- 3. **Surface "Recommended Just for You" above the fold.** That section has the best chance of converting someone who's already tuned out the broadcast cadence. Make it the hero, not the afterthought appended below a category-wide CTA.
- 4. **Drop the china into a separate send.** Segment the furniture buyers from the tableware buyers. I've clicked on sofas and floor lamps. Pottery Barn knows this. Use it.

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn Memorial Day Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard-broken links (403) will silently fail for affected recipients. The email also carries 3 HTTP (non-HTTPS) asset URLs across image sources and a tracking pixel, creating mixed-content blocking risk in modern clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — 403 on credit card banner CTA**
`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&...`
The "Apply & Buy" / Key Rewards credit card banner link returns HTTP 403. Recipients clicking this CTA land on an error. The `external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner` parameter confirms this is the header non-member credit card module.

**FAIL — 403 on West Elm spacer (cross-brand asset)**
`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
A tracking/layout spacer GIF from the West Elm CDN domain returns 403. This is a stale cross-brand asset reference — the file has either been deleted or the domain no longer serves it. It also uses HTTP (see §3).

**INFO — 24 tracking links not probed**
All `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirect links were skipped by the checker. No broken-link confirmation, but no clean bill of health either.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP mixed-content — 3 image sources**

| Asset | URL |
|---|---|
| West Elm spacer | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` |
| Key CC footer banner | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` |
| Brand banner slice | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` |
| Open-pixel tracker | `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` |

Gmail and Outlook.com proxy all images through HTTPS; plain HTTP sources will be re-fetched through Google's/Microsoft's image proxy and may break tracking signal on the open pixel specifically.

**Missing alt text — 40+ images**
Every product slice image (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg` in both the Grid1 and Email-Footer blocks) and all product tile images (`cayman-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `toscana-extendable-dining-table…`, etc.) have empty or absent `alt` attributes. When images are blocked, the email renders as a blank canvas with no legible content.

**PHP-style `header()` calls injected into a `<style>` block**
```html
<style>
  ...
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
</style>
```
Server-side PHP header directives are present verbatim inside a `<style>` tag. These are inert in email clients but indicate a template assembly error — a dynamic image-serving snippet was pasted into the wrong template block. Strict CSS parsers may flag or discard the entire style block.

**`<title>` is empty**
`<title></title>` — some clients (notably iOS Mail) surface the title in notifications or tab labels.

### 5. Compliance

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe` header not captured**
The QA relay did not observe a `List-Unsubscribe` header. If absent from the actual SMTP envelope, this violates Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements (enforced since Feb 2024 for senders >5k/day). Pottery Barn's send volume almost certainly exceeds that threshold.

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found**
One-click unsubscribe is required by the same Gmail/Yahoo policy. Without this header, Gmail will not render the native one-click unsubscribe button, increasing spam complaints.

**WARN — SPF/DKIM authentication status unknown**
`Authentication-Results` header not captured by the relay. Cannot confirm DMARC/SPF/DKIM pass for `e.potterybarn.com`. No finding is raised — but this should be verified against live SMTP headers before the send.

**`<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">`** — correctly present; prevents search indexing of the email web version.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The 24 tracked click URLs all route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirects. UTM parameter presence cannot be confirmed without following the redirects, but the redirect domain pattern is consistent with Salesforce Marketing Cloud link wrapping, which typically injects `cm_ite`, `cm_sp`, or custom params at redirect time.

The 403 on the credit card CTA (`cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`) means that specific conversion path is dead — any attribution tied to that module will not resolve to a landing page.
