# 100+ new items added to Warehouse Sale
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-seven. That number's staying on the table before anything else — this is CB2 email twenty-seven since mid-May, and at this point opening their emails is less discovery, more triage. That said, "100+ new items added to Warehouse Sale" is a subject line that earns a tap from someone mid-renovation on two houses simultaneously. I'm not immune to 60% off on furniture I was already going to buy.
- The email itself is clean — dark editorial hero, offer front and center, category links below it. But then it trips: there's a "LEAVE US A REVIEW, WIN $1,000" sweepstakes block dropped square in the middle of the funnel that kills whatever momentum the hero built. The product grid at the bottom is doing real work — an acacia platform bed at $999, a bleached wood desk, chairs with visible pricing — genuinely useful for what I'm furnishing. It just feels like browsing history rather than a styled edit anyone actually curated.

## What's working

- **"UP TO 60% OFF" as the dominant hero** — the dark lifestyle image, sale copy front and center, the eye lands exactly where CB2 wants it. The hierarchy actually works here.
- **Category list (Furniture / Lighting / Decor / Rugs) is exactly right for an active renovator** — I'm shopping all four of those categories simultaneously. Good signal that this is a sale email, not a brand-mood email.
- **"NOT SOLD OUT, YET" is a smart hook** — back-in-stock framing implies scarcity without being loud about it, and the header has actual personality. This section earns its spot.
- **Visible prices in the product grid** — the Girden Acacia Wood Platform Bed ($999), the Ridge Bleached Wood Desk, the chairs with tags. I can scan and make decisions fast; no "call for pricing" friction.
- **"Earn 10% Back" loyalty callout surfaces in the email** — as a rewards-card optimizer I like doing the math on stacked savings. This rewards section appearing confirms I'm getting something on top of the discount.

## What's weak

- **"LEAVE US A REVIEW, WIN $1,000" mid-email is a conversion killer** — I tapped in to shop a warehouse sale, not to enter a sweepstakes. This block breaks the funnel at exactly the wrong moment.
- **No deadline anywhere** — Pottery Barn's warehouse sale emails always include an end date ("Ends Monday," "Final 48 Hours"). CB2 is floating "UP TO 60% OFF" with zero time anchor; I'm defaulting to "I'll come back" which usually means I don't.
- **"SHOP ALL" is doing all the CTA heavy lifting** — one blunt link to a firehose page. Crate & Barrel's coastal rugs email last week linked directly into specific product families; CB2 is leaving that lift to me.
- **The "More for You" grid reads as algorithmic, not editorial** — six loosely related pieces with no throughline. Article's summer hosting email from earlier this month showed me a tightly styled three-piece table setting with a consistent mood; this is just six things that were probably in my browse history.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill or relocate the $1,000 sweepstakes block** — footer, a separate dedicated email, anywhere but middle of a sale conversion flow. It's pulling attention away from purchase intent at exactly the wrong moment.
- 2. **Add a sale end date to the hero** — even "Ends Sunday" changes my calculus from "I'll come back" to "I need to decide this week." The subject already earns the open; don't let the body lose the close.
- 3. **Make "NOT SOLD OUT, YET" link to individual product pages** — right now the CTA is "SHOP BACK IN STOCK," a category page. The piece in that hero image should go directly to its PDP.
- 4. **Tighten the product grid to three curated pieces with a styling rationale** — give me a capsule, not a catalog. "Furnish a study" or "Complete the dining room" would give this grid an editorial purpose rather than an algorithmic feel.
- 5. **Surface a deadline or inventory count on at least one grid item** — "Only 4 left at this price" on the bed frame would do more work than anything else in the lower half of this email.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users with low vision from zooming. Change to `initial-scale=1` only, or add `user-scalable=yes`.

**Wildcard `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` overrides OS accessibility settings**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;-ms-text-size-adjust:none;...}
```
Applying this via `*` is more aggressive than the `body`-scoped version already present. The body-level rule is correct; the wildcard version should be removed.

**Double-slash typos in three font `src` URLs**
```css
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2")
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2")
```
`//` after the domain works on most servers due to path normalization, but it's non-canonical and can fail on CDN configurations that treat double-slash as invalid. Remove the extra slash.

**Cross-brand font domain in a CB2 email**
Four `@font-face` declarations load from `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/`. This is not a blocking issue if both domains share infra, but it introduces a cross-origin dependency that can cause CORS failures or Content-Security-Policy blocks in certain webmail clients. Verify these fonts are intentionally served from the sibling domain.

**Redundant `@font-face` for "Lineto Brown Sub"**
Three separate `@font-face` blocks declare the same `font-family: "Lineto Brown Sub"` — one without weight/style descriptors (implicit normal/normal) and two with explicit `font-weight: 400` and `font-weight: 600`. The implicit declaration conflicts with the `400` explicit one. Consolidate to the two explicit weight-keyed declarations only.

**"Lineto Brown Light Sub" has no woff2 source**
```css
@font-face{
  font-family: "Lineto Brown Light Sub";
  src: url("https://www.cb2.com/Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff");
}
```
Only `.woff` is declared. All other face declarations include `woff2`. Add a `woff2` source as the first format for modern clients.

**Single mobile breakpoint at 418px is narrow**
The `@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)` breakpoint targets only very narrow screens (iPhone SE-class). No breakpoint exists for 419px–767px (most modern phones). Consider adding a second breakpoint around 600px for the mid-range phone viewport.
