# ☀️Summer Sale starts now
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-27**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so Article just flipped the switch from "here's a curated room story" back into straight-up sale mode, and honestly after four straight editorial sends — dining room, living room, home tour, entryway — I almost respect the change of pace. "40% off" in that big rust-orange type against the yellow gradient is the kind of number that actually stops my thumb mid-scroll, and "STARTS TODAY" as a little badge is a nice touch of urgency that doesn't feel fake.
- But once I'm past the hero, this is just a category grid — Outdoor, Bedroom, Living Room, Dining Room — with zero indication of what's actually on sale or by how much per category. No "up to 40% off sofas," no specific SKU, nothing that tells me if the chairs I've been eyeing since the dining room email are even part of this. And then there's a second banner ("Our Summer Sale starts now") that just repeats the same message I already read at the top, followed by "Explore What's New" and "Shop Best Sellers" buttons that have nothing to do with the sale at all. It feels like three emails got smushed into one.
- I'd click — 40% off is real money on a sectional — but I'm clicking into an unknown, not a specific deal. Not forwarding this one to my wife; the editorial sends (the dining room one, the entryway one) were the ones that felt worth sharing because they matched a decision we're actually mid-way through. This is just "sale exists, go find out."

## What's working

- The number is front and center — 40% off in giant type, no hunting for the offer, unlike CB2's cluttered "Steals of the Week" grids where I have to squint at every tile.
- "STARTS TODAY" gives it real urgency without being obnoxious about it — no fake countdown clock, no "ENDS TONIGHT 📢" spam-caps nonsense like Wayfair's been running.
- The four-category grid (Outdoor, Bedroom, Living Room, Dining Room) at least routes me toward my actual renovation categories instead of a generic homepage dump.
- Clean visual hierarchy top to bottom — yellow hero, then product grid, then footer utility. No overlapping text, no broken images, nothing rendering weird.
- Trust footer strip (Help Center, 30-Day Satisfaction Guarantee, Shipping and Delivery) is reassuring for furniture specifically, since that's the stuff that actually worries me — will this bed frame show up in one piece.

## What's weak

- Zero product specificity anywhere — no hero item, no price, no "the chair from last week's dining room email is 30% off." Crate & Barrel's home-tour-style sends at least show me an actual product I can hover on; this is four stock-photo tiles and a "starts now" repeat.
- That second orange band ("Our Summer Sale starts now") adds nothing — it's the same sentence I already read, just wasting scroll real estate before the "Explore What's New" / "Shop Best Sellers" buttons, which aren't even sale-related. Confusing sequencing.
- No mention of my Article account or order history at all — after four emails in a row building a relationship (dining room, then living room four days later), this one reads completely generic, like they forgot who they were just talking to.
- No indication of what's excluded — "up to 40%" always means the good stuff is capped at 15% and only the stuff nobody wants hits 40%. Pottery Barn's quilt email at least felt honest about its offer; this one's vague on purpose.
- The outdoor tile photo is genuinely great (rattan chairs, warm light) but there's no swatch or fabric-sample callout anywhere in the email, which is the one thing that'd actually move me toward buying furniture sight-unseen.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real product in the hero, not just category tiles — show me the actual dining chair or sectional that's 40% off, with a price. That's the single biggest lift here; right now I have to click blind to find out if anything I want is even included.
- 2. Cut the second "Our Summer Sale starts now" banner entirely — it's dead weight between the hero and the utility buttons, and it repeats a message I've already processed.
- 3. Add per-category discount specificity in the grid captions — "Outdoor, up to 30%" vs. "Bedroom, up to 40%" — so I know where to click first instead of guessing.
- 4. Reference the sequence — a line like "Picked up dining chairs recently? Here's what's on sale" would tie this back to the last two sends and make it feel like they're tracking my actual decision, not blasting a list.
- 5. Swap "Explore What's New" / "Shop Best Sellers" for sale-specific CTAs, or drop them from this send and save them for a non-sale email — right now they dilute the one message this email should be pushing.
