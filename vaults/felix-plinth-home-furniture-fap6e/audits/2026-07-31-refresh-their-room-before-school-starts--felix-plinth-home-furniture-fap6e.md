# Refresh their room before school starts
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-31**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids landing in my inbox is basically the universal signal that whatever list I'm on got cross-pollinated with someone else's segment — I don't have kids, I've never bought a kid's bed frame, and yet here's a full scroll of toddler bedding, "back-to-school" backpacks, and a butterfly costume sandwiched between rugs and lamps. I flagged this exact issue after the last two Crate & Kids nursery blasts, and here we are again, except now it's also trying to be a back-to-school email, a Halloween email, and a 50%-off summer sale email all in the same send. Pick one.
- The one thing that actually caught my eye — genuinely — was the "modern lighting looks starting at $99" block. I'm mid-renovation on two houses and I've complained before about the same lamp showing up in three different brand emails in the same week; this is at least a fresh lighting lineup I haven't seen from Pottery Barn or CB2 yet. But it's buried under four screens of kids' stuff I'm going to scroll straight past.
- Verdict: not opening the CTAs, not forwarding — there's no friend of mine with a kid starting school who I'd send a grab-bag "sale + backpacks + Halloween candy dish" email to. This gets archived, and if it keeps happening I'm one click away from unsubscribing from the whole Crate & Barrel family, which would be a shame because the actual Crate & Barrel sofa email from this same week was one of the best furniture emails I've gotten all month.

## What's working

- The lighting section (pendant, floor lamp, table lamp lineup at $99+) is the one block that's actually adjacent to my renovation list — the rest of the email doesn't do this.
- Clean, consistent Crate & Kids visual branding — no broken images, no overlapping text, easy to skim even scrolling fast.
- The loyalty card callout near the bottom ("the card you love just got better") at least acknowledges existing cardholders instead of only chasing new signups.
- CTA button copy is specific throughout — "Shop New Bedding," "Shop New Rugs," "Shop New Lighting" — not vague "Discover" buttons.

## What's weak

- This is a kids' room email sent to someone with no kids — same complaint I had about the last two Crate & Kids sends. If C&B can't segment better than this after multiple rounds of the same feedback, that's a data problem, not a creative one.
- Four campaigns crammed into one email — back-to-school, general decor, summer sale, and a Halloween-adjacent product ("Miniature Wine Bottle" novelty item, butterfly piece) in the "we love these for you" grid. None of it reads as one coherent message.
- Subject says "before school starts," hero banner says "new finishing touches starting at $20" — not quite Pottery Barn's Halloween-sneak-peek-that's-actually-a-summer-sale bait-and-switch, but it's the same instinct: subject line and hero don't match.
- No specific hero product — it's category tiles (bedding, decor, rugs, lighting) with no single "here's THE thing" moment, unlike the Crate & Barrel recliner email that opened with one clear sofa and one clear hook.
- Zero material/dimension detail on anything, which matters to me for furniture — Wayfair's privacy screen email had the same gap.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment me out of Crate & Kids entirely, or at minimum stop leading with kids' bedding for accounts with no kids-category purchase history.** This is the single biggest lift available — everything else is polish on a targeting miss.
- 2. Pick one campaign per send. Back-to-school OR the summer sale OR seasonal novelty items — not all three stacked in one scroll.
- 3. Give the lighting section (the one relevant block) more real estate — move it up, make it the hero, since it's the only thing here that isn't kid-specific.
- 4. Add one dimension/material line under at least the hero product — even Article, for all its repetitive sale sends, usually shows fabric or wood type.
- 5. Fix the subject/hero mismatch — if the hero is "new finishing touches starting at $20," the subject should say that, not "before school starts."

- **Subject Alt B:** `Lighting, rugs, and decor — all new this week`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Modern lamps from $99, new rugs from $199, and more`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `This week: fresh decor picks across every room`
