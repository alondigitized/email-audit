- **"One week left" in the subject** is the most specific deadline CB2 has given me all month — after a string of soft-launch sale subjects, this one actually tells me something actionable

## What's weak

- **Cadence is completely blown** — three CB2/CB2 SALE sends in one day; Pottery Barn pulls this same volume but at least staggers the creative so each email has a distinct angle; this family just runs the same sale harder
- **Two competing discount numbers in one scroll** — 60% for Warehouse, 50% for July 4th — read as contradictory at a glance; Article committed to a single "4th of July Sale" story and didn't muddy it
- **"SHOP THE SALE" is the laziest possible CTA copy** — Pottery Barn stacked "Extra 20% off" directly onto its button copy; this feels like a template default that nobody changed
- **Lifestyle imagery grid is gorgeous but unfocused** — canopy bed, woven/rattan piece, rug; three different directions with no editorial commitment; Crate & Barrel's kitchen counter email was more effective precisely because it had one category thesis
- **No Insider/member benefits anywhere** — I'm buying on highest-rewards card and I know CB2 has an Insider program, but there's zero callout; missed table stakes for someone like me who is actively optimizing purchase routing

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse to one sale event per send.** The Warehouse Sale (60%) and July 4th Sale (50%) should not share a scroll — they undercut each other. The 60% warehouse story is stronger; own it completely.
- 2. **Promote "MORE FOR YOU" above the fold.** The personalized tiles are the highest-relevance content in the email. Putting them below two full banners and a red interstitial means most people never see them. Lead with behavior-driven content, trail with the broad offer.
- 3. **Put a noun after the verb.** "Shop Warehouse Clearance" or "Shop Furniture Sale" beats "SHOP THE SALE" every time — it sets an expectation before I click.
- 4. **Acknowledge the send cadence.** A one-liner — "We've been busy adding new pieces to the sale; here's what's worth your time" — makes the third email of the day feel intentional rather than automated.
- 5. **Surface Insider rewards.** A single-line callout ("Earn [X]% back as an Insider") closes the loop for customers who are routing purchases through rewards cards.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta** — prevents users from pinching to zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

**Global `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` via `*` selector** — disables iOS Safari's automatic text scaling across the entire email, not just reset elements. Compounds the zoom issue above.
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;-ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```

**Non-standard mobile breakpoint** — `max-width: 418px` is too narrow. Common logical widths for modern iPhones range 390–430px; Android flagships often sit at 412px. A 418px ceiling means devices at exactly 419–430px get neither mobile nor desktop styles. Standard safe value is `480px` or `600px`.
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { ... }
```

**Double-slash font URLs** — three `@font-face` declarations reference paths with `//` in the pathname. Most servers normalize these, but CDN/cache configs may not:
```css
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2")
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2")
```
Single-slash equivalents are safe replacements.

**Mixed font CDNs** — `Lineto Brown` and `GT Sectra` load from `cb2.com`; `HNW05` loads from `crateandbarrel.com`. An outage or cache-busting on the Crate & Barrel CDN silently breaks CB2 email typography.

**`font-display: swap` in email CSS** — this property is not recognized by any email client renderer. It's dead code (minor, but indicates template wasn't stripped for email context).

**Alt text** — cannot verify from truncated source; all `<img>` tags must have `alt` attributes.
