# New! Fall 2026: Inspired by the English countryside
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-29**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one actually stopped my scroll. "The Cotswolds Collection" — moody green living room, that hero shot with the deep charcoal-green library-looking room — that's a genuine aesthetic swing, not another "everything on sale" grab bag. It's giving Restoration Hardware catalog energy but at Crate & Barrel prices, which is exactly the kind of thing that gets saved into my inbox folder for the primary house renovation.
- That said, this thing goes on forever. Living room, bedroom, dining room, "modern cross ration" (whatever that pumpkin/Halloween tablescape section is), kitchen, then it pivots hard into "TheDesignDask" with Lupita Nyong'o and a sweepstakes for $5000 — by that point I've mentally checked out. It's two emails stapled together.
- Verdict: I'd click through on the Cotswolds hero and maybe the bedroom, but I'm not scrolling to the design service pitch or the sweepstakes. Wouldn't forward to my wife unless I trim it down to just the collection shots first — as is, it's too much to paste into a text.

## What's working

- The "Cotswolds Collection" hero with English-countryside styling is a real seasonal story, not a generic "up to 60% off" wall — first Crate & Barrel send in a while with an actual point of view, similar to how their "Designed to get dinner on the table ASAP" send (7/28, 8/10) picked one lane and stuck with it.
- The moody green living room and heirloom bedroom shots have real warmth — dark wood, brass lighting, layered textiles. That's the fall palette I'm actually shopping for right now.
- Individual "Shop the Look" / "Shop Bedroom" / "Shop Dining Room" CTAs under each room section are specific, not generic "Shop Now" buttons repeated five times.
- "1500+ new heritage-inspired finds" gives me a concrete number instead of vague "new arrivals" copy.

## What's weak

- This is way too long — six distinct room sections plus a whole separate TheDesignDask sub-brand pitch with a celebrity tie-in and a sweepstakes. CB2's "24 hours left for the Furniture Event" (7/29, 8/10) proved you can hit urgency and product in a tight scroll; this buries its best asset (the Cotswolds story) under five more scrolls of content.
- The Lupita Nyong'o "L.A. home makeover" section feels bolted on — it's a different brand voice (design-service consultation, not product) shoved into a furniture sale email.
- The "now trending modern cross ration" pumpkin/Halloween tablescape section clashes with "English countryside" — feels like two campaigns merged in one send, the same scattershot problem I dinged Wayfair for with its 14-category grab-bag.
- No visible loyalty/rewards math anywhere — I put big furniture purchases on my highest-rewards card specifically for the points, and nothing here tells me if there's a card bonus or member pricing tied to this sale.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this email in half. Lead with Cotswolds Collection (living room, bedroom, dining room) as its own send, and spin the pumpkin/entertaining section and TheDesignDask pitch into separate emails. Article's recent sends prove editorial-focused single-story emails perform better than kitchen-sink ones.
- 2. Move the "up to 60% off" banner down or make it smaller — right now it competes with "introducing The Cotswolds Collection" for the same visual real estate at the very top.
- 3. Add a rewards/card callout near the top CTA — even a line like "Earn 2x rewards on furniture this week" would matter to how I actually decide to buy.
- 4. Drop or clearly separate the pumpkin tablescape section — it undercuts the English-countryside theme you just spent four sections building.
