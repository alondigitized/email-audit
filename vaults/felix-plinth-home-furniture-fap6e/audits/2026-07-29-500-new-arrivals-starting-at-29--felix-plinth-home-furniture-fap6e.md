# 500+ new arrivals, starting at $29→
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-29**
## Executive summary

- Yep, Crate & Kids again. That's — what, the seventh one of these this stretch, and I still don't have kids, still don't want a nursery. The "500+ new arrivals starting at $29" hero is a fine headline for a parent, meaningless to me, and then it just keeps going — bedroom favorites, nursery loves, playroom picks, a $500 sweepstakes, a 50% off summer sale, a credit card pitch, an app download push. It's basically five emails stapled together.
- The one thing that almost stopped my thumb was that green-and-cream nursery shot with the patterned wallpaper — genuinely nice styling, on-brand with the Cotswolds/English-countryside moodboard C&B's been running all week. But "almost" doesn't cut it when the whole rest of the email is cribs and changing tables.
- Straight to archive. Not forwarding this — unless my sister-in-law's expecting, in which case maybe, but that's not who I am right now.

## What's working

- The nursery vignette (green florals, that warm wood crib) has the same moody-editorial polish as C&B's "Cotswolds Collection" send from yesterday — genuinely good photography, not stock-catalog junk.
- "500+ new arrivals, starting at $29" up top is a real, specific offer with a number and a price anchor, not vague "shop now" filler.
- Clean section breaks (bedroom / nursery / playroom) with consistent "Shop All ___" buttons — easy to scan even if none of it applies to me.
- The $500 sweepstakes banner is a low-friction hook that doesn't require an actual purchase.

## What's weak

- Zero relevance to my life — no kids, no crib shopping, no playroom furnishing. This is the wrong list for me and it's landing anyway, for the sixth-plus time.
- The email tries to do too much: new arrivals, three sub-categories, a sweepstakes, a sale, a credit card, an app download. Article's "Summer Sale starts now" was one clean idea; this is a junk drawer.
- "up to 50% off summer sale" is buried two-thirds down after three unrelated product grids — Wayfair does this same scattershot thing and I dinged them for it too.
- The Crate & Kids co-brand credit card pitch feels like a bolt-on, wedged between product grids with no real segue.
- Nothing here reads as "for someone renovating two houses" — no furniture-grade materials talk, no swatch program, no showroom tie-in, just baby gear.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment me out.** I've gotten enough Crate & Kids sends with zero engagement that this should stop hitting my inbox — send me Crate & Barrel or CB2 (which is actually earning opens from me this week) instead.
- 2. **Pick one offer and lead with it.** Either the $29 arrivals or the 50% sale — not both, and definitely not a credit card pitch wedged in the middle. CB2's "24 hours left" send worked because it had one throughline.
- 3. **Move the sweepstakes up or cut it.** A $500 giveaway is a strong hook buried near the bottom where most people have already bailed.
- 4. **Cut the section count.** Three product categories plus a sale plus a card plus an app is four emails' worth of asks in one send.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New at Crate & Kids: nursery pieces from $29`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 50% off summer sale — plus 500+ new arrivals`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cribs, dressers & playroom favorites, all under $500`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Enter to win $500 toward your nursery — ends soon`

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- No broken or malformed `href` syntax observed in the visible source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **30 of ~34 images are missing `alt` text**, spanning both content images (hero banner, product shots) and tracking pixels. For content images (e.g., `2025_0728_CBK_WholeHomeNewArrivals_HERO`, multiple `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/.../m/1/*.jpg`), missing alt text degrades the experience for screen-reader users and for any client that blocks images by default — no fallback text renders.

- Tracking-pixel `<img>` tags lack `alt=""` (empty alt), which is the correct pattern for decorative/invisible pixels — but since alt is entirely absent rather than empty, screen readers may announce the raw filename/URL for these as well.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No merge tags, personalization placeholders (e.g., `%%FIRSTNAME%%`, `{{...}}`, `[[...]]`), or dynamic-content blocks were observed in the visible HTML. ## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header: not found.** Not captured by the relay, or not sent — cannot confirm one-click unsubscribe support at the header level.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header (RFC 8058): not found.** Without this, mailbox providers (Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender requirements) cannot present true one-click unsubscribe, which is now a compliance risk for high-volume senders as of the 2024 Gmail/Yahoo mandates.
- **Authentication-Results header: not found.** SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status could not be verified via this relay — this is a QA/telemetry gap on the receiving side (AgentMail relay) as much as a sender issue, but it means authentication cannot be confirmed either way from available data.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)

- Because all 81 click-through links were skipped from HTTP probing, landing-page alignment (i.e., whether links resolve to matching live product/category pages on crateandbarrel.com) could not be verified in this audit.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to all content images (hero, product shots); use `alt=""` explicitly for the tracking/pixel images to avoid screen readers announcing raw filenames.
2. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are being sent by the ESP and correctly passed through the AgentMail relay — required for Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender compliance.
