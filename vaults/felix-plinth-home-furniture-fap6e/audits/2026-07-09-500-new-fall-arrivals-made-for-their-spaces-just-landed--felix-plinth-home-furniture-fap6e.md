# 500+ NEW fall arrivals made for their spaces just landed →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- Eight. I know because I went back and counted. This is the eighth email from the Crate & Barrel family of brands in approximately 48 hours, and it's landing on the same day I already received "NEW! 500+ new fall arrivals just landed" — a subject line so close to this one ("500+ NEW fall arrivals made for their spaces just landed") that I had to read it twice to confirm they were different sends. Except they are different: this one is Crate & *Kids*. As in, children. Cribs, canopy beds, nursery art, toddler quilts. I don't have children. I am renovating two adult homes simultaneously.
- I know which catalog is which — that's the whole problem here. I'm on the main Crate & Barrel list *because* I want living room furniture and kitchen shelving, not because I want a convertible crib. Somewhere upstream, someone decided that "Crate & Barrel subscriber" = "also send them the kids catalog" and nobody stopped to ask whether that made sense. It doesn't. The email itself is genuinely well-executed — good photography, clean grid, legible on mobile — but I am categorically not the audience, and the C&B sending cadence has by this point completely desensitized me to the entire family.
- I'm marking this as read. I am not clicking. I would not forward this to a friend unless that friend was expecting a baby.

## What's working

- **Photography is strong.** The children's room shots — the canopy bed, the blush-and-sage palette — look like editorial content, not stock. Crate & Kids does this better than, say, Wayfair, which just shows product on white.
- **Sender identity is crystal clear.** Crate & Kids. I knew exactly which sub-brand before I even opened, which is honestly more than CB2 delivers (I spent the first few seconds of that "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS" send trying to figure out if it was the parent company or the sub).
- **Layout holds up on mobile.** Single-column grid, readable text, no broken images, nothing overlapping. After Wayfair's "8' X 10' OUTDOOR RUGS" debacle — same subject line three days running — clean rendering is worth noting.
- **Seasonal framing is correct.** "Fall arrivals" in early July is right on cadence for furniture with lead times; that part of the editorial calendar is fine.

## What's weak

- **Wrong sub-brand for this subscriber.** I am on the Crate & Barrel list. Not the Crate & Kids list. Cross-pollinating these sends without any segmentation signal is a fundamental data hygiene failure.
- **Subject line is nearly a dupe of a send I already received today.** "NEW! 500+ new fall arrivals just landed" arrived earlier; this one is "500+ NEW fall arrivals made for their spaces just landed." The word swap ("made for their spaces") is the only tell that it's the kids vertical. I had to look twice.
- **Zero offer.** No percentage off, no free shipping threshold, no loyalty pricing, nothing. Pottery Barn's indigo email had cleaner offer clarity at a higher send count — and that still earned an 8 from me. New arrivals alone don't justify inbox real estate at email eight.
- **"Their spaces" is doing all the demographic work.** It's subtle copy for signaling kids content, but it doesn't actually tell a non-parent what he's about to see. If you're going to send the kids catalog to adult subscribers, at least be explicit so I can decide in the inbox whether to open.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress Crate & Kids sends to subscribers with no children's purchase or browse history.** This is the single change that lifts everything else. The email is fine; the list is wrong.
- 2. **If cross-selling kids to adult subscribers, add an explicit signal in the subject.** Something like "Crate & Kids: 500+ fall arrivals →" so I know before opening whether this applies to me.
- 3. **Add a concrete offer.** "Complimentary shipping on orders over $149" or "New members: 10% off your first kids order" would give a non-parent a reason to forward to a sibling who just had a baby. As it stands there's nothing actionable.
- 4. **Deduplicate across the family.** One send per day, maximum, across the combined Crate & Barrel / CB2 / Crate & Kids namespace. I should not receive two "500+ fall arrivals" subjects in the same morning.

- **Subject Alt B:** `New this fall at Crate & Kids — rooms that grow with them`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Canopy beds, cribs, and bedding built to last through every phase.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop 500+ new kids arrivals — free shipping on orders $149+`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Conflicting `img` max-width media queries — wrong declaration order**

Two desktop breakpoint rules are defined in the wrong order:

```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```

At viewport widths ≥768px, both queries match. Because the 640px rule is declared *after* the 768px rule (equal specificity), it wins via cascade — images will render at 640px wide on desktop instead of the intended 600px. The rules need to be reordered or merged.

**[MEDIUM] Global `*` selector sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`**

```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```

The `body` rule immediately below sets it to `100%`, which wins for `body` itself, but all child elements inherit `none` from `*`. This suppresses WebKit auto-sizing on all text nodes on mobile Safari — intended for Outlook hacks but applied too broadly.

**[MEDIUM] Dark mode hard-locked to light**

```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```

Apple Mail and other clients that support dark mode will force the email into light mode. If the design has not been tested in forced-light dark environments, this can produce low-contrast or invisible text for ~35% of iOS Mail users.

**[LOW] Duplicate and redundant meta tags**

`format-detection` is declared four times — once combined, then three times individually. This is not harmful but indicates copy-paste debt and bloats `<head>` size:

```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```

`charset=utf-8` is also declared twice (as `http-equiv` Content-Type and as a standalone `<meta charset>`).
