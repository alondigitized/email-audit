# ON NOW! Up to 40% off top kitchen brands
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-19**
## Executive summary

- This is the fifth or sixth Crate & Barrel send I've logged since 11/17, and the subject just says "ON NOW!" again. Black Friday has already "started now" three times this week. The top banner says "Black Friday is on! Up to 50% off + free shipping," so the "ON NOW" is the same line I've already read. The sender is "Crate & Barrel SALE," which at least tells me it's a promo before I open it.
- The kitchen angle does work for me. I'm renovating two kitchens, so Breville smart ovens, KitchenAid stand mixers and Caraway cookware are things I'd look at. The email is built around brand-by-brand tiles, each with a "Shop Now" button, so I can jump straight to the category I care about. The 40% headline is bigger than the 50% in the banner, which confuses me about which offer actually applies.
- Past the kitchen stretch, it turns into a generic Black Friday grid of furniture, rugs, decor and gifts, plus a TheDesignDesk pitch at the bottom. I'd click once, probably on the KitchenAid tile, and then archive it. I wouldn't forward it. If I did, it would go to my contractor, and only for the Breville oven.

## What's working

- The kitchen-brand tiles (Breville, KitchenAid, Caraway, cookware) are relevant to my renovation and easy to scan.
- Each tile has a specific offer, like "up to $200 off select KitchenAid stand mixers" and "20% off Breville Smart Ovens," so I can see the number without clicking.
- Every module has its own "Shop Now" button, so I can go straight to a category.
- The product photography is clean and well lit, and the hero's light-gray kitchen tools match my aesthetic.
- A "we love these for you" product row near the bottom adds some curation.

## What's weak

- The subject says 40% and the banner says 50% plus free shipping. That's two headline numbers, and I'm left guessing which one applies.
- The "ON NOW!" subject repeats the "starts now" beat from the 11/18 sends, so it's urgency without news.
- The email is very long. After the kitchen section it becomes a generic mix of sofas, rugs and gift tiles, which reads like the same Black Friday email I've already gotten.
- The text under the product images is tiny. On my phone I'd have to zoom to read the sale prices and the fine print.
- The TheDesignDesk and app-download blocks at the bottom feel like an afterthought, and the "$500" offer there is hard to read.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one headline offer and put it in both the subject and the hero, so the 40% and 50% don't compete.
- 2. Cut the generic Black Friday grid in a kitchen-themed send. A segmented kitchen email would say "kitchen" the whole way down.
- 3. Add shipping and delivery timing, and Thanksgiving-arrival cutoffs for appliances.
- 4. Make the sale prices bigger and put them next to the product names.
- 5. Move the "we love these for you" row up, because it's the closest thing to personalization.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ovens, mixers and cookware, with free shipping on orders over $49.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is built on a Marigold/MI template with a 57% QA pass rate (0 errors, 3 warnings). The main confirmed issues are about 45 images with no alt text, a viewport that blocks zoom, and small markup defects. The header warnings can't be confirmed because the QA data comes through a relay.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** 85 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are click-redirect domains. Redirect resolution and destination health are unverified.
- **Third-party pixels:** Five `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=dbe8da71…f5a8&n=1…5` pixels (LiveRamp) are embedded. The `s=` value is a 64-character hex string, which looks like a hashed recipient identifier. Five calls to one endpoint with `n=1..5` is redundant.
- **Per-recipient IDs in image URLs:** `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png?mi_u=dbe8da71…` and `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/a27a3030-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…` carry per-recipient identifiers. They are expected for tracking, but they get logged and cached by image proxies.
- **Open pixel:** `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif` is present, with no alt text.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (about 45 images listed; the report appears truncated):**
  - 30 content images on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/*.jpg` (plus two `m/47` and `m/1` .png/.gif assets). If these are linked product tiles, the links have no accessible name.
  - 7 `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png` images.
  - 5 `sr.rlcdn.com` pixels, the `o.gif` pixel, and the `dv.crateandbarrel.com` image.
  - Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` explicitly. Content images need descriptive alt text.
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` disables pinch-zoom in some clients, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Unescaped ampersand:** `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` is in an XHTML 1.0 Transitional document. It should be `&amp;`. The title also doesn't match the subject line.
- **Conflicting media queries:** `@media (min-width:768px){img{max-width:600px}}` and `@media (min-width:640px){img{max-width:640px}}` both match at 768px and above. The later rule (640px) wins, so the 600px rule is dead.
- **Redundant metas:**
  - `<meta charset>` is duplicated alongside the `http-equiv` Content-Type.
  - `format-detection` is declared three times.
  - A leftover template comment reads `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->`. It is invisible but adds bytes.
- **Caveat:** The HTML source was truncated, so I couldn't check the body markup (role="presentation" on tables, heading semantics, live text vs image-only content).

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`) appear in the visible source. The only personalization is in tracking URLs (`mi_u`, `mi_cid`, `mi_mid`), and those are resolved.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **No confirmed violations.** All three warnings are flagged by the QA tool as likely relay artifacts:
  - `List-Unsubscribe` not found
  - `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found
  - `Authentication-Results` not found, so SPF/DKIM/DMARC status is unknown
- These need raw headers from the original delivery to confirm. They matter because Gmail and Yahoo require one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders.
- The footer unsubscribe link and physical address are not in the truncated source, so I couldn't verify them.
- The sender is `crateandbarrel@mail.crateandbarrel.com` (a subdomain of the brand domain), with images served from `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com`. This is aligned.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not verifiable. The destination URLs are behind the 85 skipped click-redirect links, and the truncated source shows no final landing URLs or UTM parameters. No issues can be confirmed or ruled out.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive alt text to the product and hero images, and `alt=""` to the tracking pixels (`rlcdn.com`, `o.gif`, `dv.`) so screen readers skip them.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
3. Escape the title (`Crate &amp; Barrel`) and consider using the subject line.
4. Fix the conflicting `img max-width` media queries, for example by using non-overlapping ranges.
5. Consolidate the five identical `rlcdn.com` pixels into one, and review whether hashed identifiers in third-party pixels fit the privacy policy.
6. Re-run the compliance and auth checks against raw headers (not the AgentMail relay) to confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and SPF/DKIM/DMARC.
7. Run the audit on the full, untruncated HTML. Follow the redirects to check UTM parameters and landing-page alignment.
