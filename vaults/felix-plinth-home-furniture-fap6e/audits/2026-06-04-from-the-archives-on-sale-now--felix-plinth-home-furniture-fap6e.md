# From the archives. On sale now.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- Eighteen. We're at eighteen CB2 emails since mid-May, and this is the second one today — I already processed the Todd Snyder "JUST DROPPED" collab before lunch. So landing in the same inbox session with "From the archives. On sale now." is a credibility test: can CB2 pivot from hype-drop mode to editorial mode in the span of three hours and make me believe both?
- Honestly? Kind of. The Design Legends Sale framing — Gianfranco Frattini, Paul McCobb, Steen Østergaard — is exactly the kind of provenance play that makes me stop scrolling. I know who Paul McCobb is. I have a Frattini piece on a shortlist for the second home. These aren't lifestyle-shoot nobodies; CB2 actually did the homework. The portrait photos of the designers alongside the furniture are a smart trust signal — this reads like a museum shop had a sale, not like a markdown dump.
- But the fatigue is real and the execution has friction. "SHOP NOW" is doing a lot of lifting for three distinct design lineages. And I'm still irritated that the midseason sale banner just sits there mid-scroll without a clear expiration — same thing that annoyed me in the "MIDSEASON SALE" send from June 3rd. I'd probably save this one to my folder. I'd click one designer section, likely Frattini. I would not forward it — this is a me-thing, not a group-chat share.

## What's working

- **The designer provenance is the differentiator.** Frattini, McCobb, Østergaard with portrait photography feels like a design brief, not a promo blast. This is why I'm subscribed to CB2 and not Wayfair, whose "BATH MATS You'll Love!!" from this morning is still haunting me.
- **Subject line tone shift.** "From the archives. On sale now." is restrained, editorial, no caps, no exclamation points. A genuine palate cleanser from the "MIDSEASON SALE" all-caps flinch from yesterday.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean.** Hero headline lands first, designers cascade in a readable three-beat rhythm, product grid follows. My eye doesn't have to hunt.
- **No render bugs visible.** The warm neutral palette holds throughout; nothing broken, no placeholder text, no layout collapse.

## What's weak

- **The CTA does zero work.** "SHOP NOW" under three separate design legends is one button for three completely different moods. Crate & Barrel's markdown push on June 4th had the same lazy generic button but at least the whole email was one offer. Here, McCobb and Frattini aren't the same customer.
- **No sale deadline visible.** "On sale now" in the subject implies it could end, but the email never says when. Article's kitchen send at least implied a reason to act. This feels like a permanent archive sale, which makes me feel zero urgency.
- **Eighteenth email, second today.** The cadence is the context I can't shake. A genuinely good concept is still getting docked for appearing inside a firehose.
- **The $1,000 mid-email block is confusing at a glance.** I can see a figure in the MIDSEASON SALE section that looks like a spend threshold or reward threshold — but I had to squint. For someone scanning on their phone, that section reads as noise before it reads as benefit.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Give each designer a distinct CTA.** "Shop Frattini," "Shop McCobb," "Shop Østergaard" — three buttons, three clicks, three tracked segments. This is the kind of personalization that turns an editorial email into a data asset for the next send.
- 2. **Put a deadline on the sale.** Even "Ends Sunday" is enough. Pottery Barn's leather seating send from today had no urgency either and it stalled. Don't repeat that.
- 3. **Move the offer clarity into the hero.** The designer credibility earns the click; the discount justifies the cart. Right now the sale lives in a mid-scroll banner. Pull even a simple "up to X% off" into the hero subhead.
- 4. **Reduce cadence or segment this send to design-literate subscribers.** This email should be landing in my inbox solo, not as part of a double-barrel morning alongside the Todd Snyder hype drop.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — text scaling disabled globally:**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
This suppresses OS-level text resizing across all elements. Combined with `maximum-scale=1` on the viewport meta, users with visual impairments cannot enlarge text. Both rules should be scoped narrowly or removed.

**Zoom blocked at viewport level:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom on iOS/Android. WCAG 1.4.4 violation.

**Global paragraph center-alignment:**
```css
p { margin: 0 auto; text-align: center; }
```
Forces center alignment on every `<p>` element. Any left-aligned body copy requires an explicit override on every paragraph — fragile and easily missed in production authoring.

**`line-height: 100%` on `*` selector** — Can clip descenders on certain font/size combinations in Outlook and older Android clients. Safer to scope to specific elements.

**Single-pixel mobile breakpoint at 418px** — No breakpoint covers the 320–417px range or the 430px+ range (iPhone 14/15 Max native width is 430px). Devices at those sizes receive neither the mobile nor desktop layout.

**Duplicate `@font-face` declarations for "Lineto Brown Sub"** — Declared three separate times. The first declaration has no `format()` hints and no `font-display` property; the subsequent two add `font-display: swap` but only cover woff2. The first declaration may cause FOIT in clients that parse it before reaching the corrected declarations.

**First `@font-face` for "Lineto Brown Light Sub"** declares only a `.woff` source with no `format()` hint — no woff2 fallback defined, meaning modern clients load the heavier format.
