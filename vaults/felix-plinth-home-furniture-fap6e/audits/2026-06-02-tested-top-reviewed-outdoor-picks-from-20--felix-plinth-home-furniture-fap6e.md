- "Buy on wayfair.ca" CTA is a hard stop — I'm a US subscriber and a Canadian domain link reads as either a list segmentation error or a sketchy redirect; I'm not clicking it
- The subject promises proof ("tested & top-reviewed") but the reviews section is buried below four product grids and the featured product is unreadable at render size — the subject makes a bet the body doesn't pay off
- Five categories (planters, lighting, coasters, wreaths, outdoor shop) make this feel like an inventory dump, not a curated recommendation; CB2's "new markdowns" email at least commits to a single product hero even when it's lazy
- Wreaths from $20 in June reads as end-of-season clearance, not seasonal relevance — pulls me out of the "summer outdoor refresh" frame immediately
- Coasters from $21 is filler for someone spec'ing a deck renovation; it belongs in a tabletop email, not an outdoor essentials one

- 2. **Lead with the review proof** — if the subject line makes a "tested & top-reviewed" claim, the hero module should show me the top-rated product with a legible star count and review volume up top, not buried in module four
- 3. **Collapse to two categories max** — outdoor seating and lighting is a coherent edit; planters, coasters, wreaths, and lighting is four different shopping missions in one email
- 4. **Add one specific material or spec callout** — "weather-resistant wicker," "UL-listed outdoor string lights, 48 ft" — something that signals these products were actually vetted, not just selected by algorithm; Article's kitchen email earlier this week did this with dimensions and nailed it
- 5. **Drop the wreaths module** — it reads as inventory clearing, not curation, and actively undermines the "tested" credibility of the send
