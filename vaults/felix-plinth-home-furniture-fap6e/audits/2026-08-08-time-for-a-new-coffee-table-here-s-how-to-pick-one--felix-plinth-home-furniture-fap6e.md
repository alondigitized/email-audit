# Time for a new coffee table? Here's how to pick one →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is actually a coffee table buying guide, and I'm mid-renovation on two houses, so this landed on the right desk. C&B did the thing where they break it into "right shape," "right material," "right size" with actual room-scale diagrams showing sightlines and clearance — that's the kind of content that answers a real question I have, not just another "shop now" wall of product tiles. It's a step up from the identical hosting-essentials email I already scored an 8 today, because this one's actually useful rather than just a well-merchandised scroll.
- Where it loses me is length and drift. By the time I hit "big on style, easy on effort" leather chairs, then a whole Cotswolds bedding collection, then a "win $5000" sweepstakes with a Design Desk sales pitch, I've forgotten this was supposed to be about coffee tables. It's four emails stapled together. Article's bedroom-reset send stayed on-topic longer than this one does.
- I'd click through on the coffee table guide itself — genuinely useful for the family room I'm redoing — but I'm not reading past the "shop coffee tables" CTA. Not forwarding it; nothing here is share-worthy, it's just a solid category page dressed up as content.

## What's working

- The shape/material/size breakdown with little room-diagram graphics is genuinely educational — nobody else in my inbox is doing buying-guide content like this, not Wayfair, not Pottery Barn.
- Multiple concrete SKUs with star ratings ("Oakland Brown Oak Square Coffee Table," "Clairemont Natural Oak Round Coffee Table") give me real anchors instead of vague lifestyle shots.
- The "Shop All Coffee Tables" CTA repeats at logical checkpoints without feeling spammy, so wherever I bail out I've got a button.
- Demographic fit is dead-on — natural oak, stone, neutral palette, exactly the "quiet luxury" reno aesthetic I've got moodboarded.

## What's weak

- This email loses the plot after the size section — bedding, a sweepstakes, and a Design Desk pitch have nothing to do with coffee tables and just extend my scroll for no reason.
- The "Design Desk" $5000 sweepstakes CTA buried near the bottom feels like a different marketing team's insert, not something that belongs in a furniture buying guide.
- No pricing anywhere on any of the featured tables — I have to click through blind, and Pottery Barn's Warehouse Sale email at least puts a number (60% off) in front of me before I commit to a click.
- The Cotswolds Collection insert with the sheep-and-hills photo is a jarring tonal shift from clean product photography to lifestyle-brand cosplay.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut it after "find the right size" — end on the coffee table CTA and spin bedding/sweepstakes/Design Desk into their own sends. This email is trying to be three emails.
- 2. Put a price or price range on at least the "bestseller" tables — even "starting at $XXX" would give me a reason to click before I've scrolled past four other things.
- 3. Move the room-diagram sizing tool higher, right under the hero — that's the single best piece of content in here and it's two scrolls deep.
- 4. Drop the Cotswolds/sweepstakes inserts entirely from a guide-format email; save cross-sell for the next promo send.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shape, material, and size — sized to your sofa, not your Pinterest board`
