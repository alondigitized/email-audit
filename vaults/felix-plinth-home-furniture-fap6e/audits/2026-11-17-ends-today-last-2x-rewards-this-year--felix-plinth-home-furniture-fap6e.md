# ENDS TODAY: Last 2x Rewards this year!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-17**
## Executive summary

- This is the third "ends today" Double Rewards send I've had from Crate & Barrel on 11/17. Two come from the Kids arm and one from the main brand. The deadline is real, and I put everything on the highest-rewards card, so 2x back matters to me. But this is the Kids sender again. The rendered email is bunk beds, a nursery, a playroom and a stroller, and I'm furnishing two adult houses.
- The 2x banner at the top does its job in about two seconds. Then I hit a very long scroll of kid-room product grids, with a Crate Card plug and a Design Desk pitch at the end. I'd mark it read. I wouldn't forward it, because nobody in my group chat is shopping for a nursery.
- One caveat on my read: the screenshot is heavily shrunk. I can see the layout and section headers, but I can't read individual prices or product names.

## What's working

- The hero is a bedroom shot with a clear "ends today! last Double Rewards* this year!" headline and a "Shop Now" button. The deadline is obvious.
- Each product row shows an "Earn $X back in Reward Dollars" line. That is rewards math at the item level, which is how I shop.
- The early Black Friday "up to 50% off" block gives me a second, price-based reason to click beyond rewards.
- The layout is clean, with well-spaced two-up product grids. Each section has its own button, so I can skip to the room I care about.
- The Design Desk "Book a Free Appointment" block at the bottom is a showroom-style service touch. It's the kind of thing I'd actually use for a renovation.

## What's weak

- The sender is Crate & Barrel Kids. After the 11/16 run of "Football fans" and "Birthday party" sends, I feel I'm being treated as a parent segment, not a home renovator.
- The email is very long. Bunk beds, nursery, playroom, baby gear and a stroller sit between the offer and anything I'd buy.
- The same "Double Rewards" message is already in two other sends today. Crate & Barrel is repeating itself, like the Pottery Barn "up to 70% off" banner I keep getting.
- The Crate Card block and the app promo with the "$500" chance add more asks after the main offer. It feels like a stack of announcements, not one focused message.
- I see nothing on shipping times or availability for furniture, and no swatch or sample cue. For big-ticket pieces that's what decides whether I click.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment by household type. Send adults renovating homes the main-brand version, with the sofa, dining and rug blocks instead of bunk beds.
- 2. Send one "last call" email per day instead of three. Fold the rewards deadline into a single send, and a rewards-focused person like me wouldn't have a reason to ignore the second and third.
- 3. Cut the lower third to the offer and one service block. Drop either the card promo or the app promo.
- 4. Put a "ships by" or "in stock" line on the featured furniture. CB2 and Article handle this better, with lead-time and quick-ship cues.
- 5. Use the single-offer clarity of Article's "UP TO 40% OFF" with one coral "SHOP NOW" button. This email has the offer but also too many other buttons competing with it.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus up to 50% off early Black Friday, with quick-ship picks for every room.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is built on a standard table-based XHTML Transitional template and has no confirmed critical defects. The QA run passed 57% of checks, with 3 warnings and 0 issues. Most of the findings are missing image alt text, plus compliance and authentication headers the capture pipeline couldn't verify.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified destinations:** 116 links were skipped because they are tracking or click-redirect domains, so none were probed. Final landing URLs, redirect chains and broken links are unconfirmed.
- **Third-party pixels:** five `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=dbe8da71…&n=1..5` calls go to LiveRamp (rlcdn). Their `s=` parameter is a hashed per-recipient identifier. These are pure tracking beacons and add third-party requests.
- **Per-recipient image URLs:** `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png?mi_u=dbe8da71…` appears 6 times, `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif?mi_u=…` is the open pixel, and `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/d6ab52f6-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…` is a dynamic-content image. Each carries a recipient identifier in the URL.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** the QA run flagged dozens of images with no `alt`. Examples:
  - `…/m/47/bbf6f9d4-86cc-437a-aa80-391067b74306.png`
  - `…/m/1/73298d08-6817-4027-9fae-8644d316b283.jpg`
  - `…/m/1/7c91dd26-bf7a-4a16-a52f-0d6f255b792f.gif`
  - roughly 40 more `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/*.jpg` files.

  Most of these appear to be content or product images, so screen readers and image-blocked clients get no content. The pixels (`448796.gif`, `o.gif`, `dv…`) should use `alt=""` explicitly.
- **Viewport blocks zoom:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">`. `maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-zoom, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Invalid markup:**
  - `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` has an unescaped `&` under an XHTML doctype (should be `&amp;`).
  - The `<meta charset>` tag is duplicated alongside the `http-equiv` Content-Type meta.
  - `format-detection` is declared three times.
  - The unreplaced template placeholder comment `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` is still in the source.
- **Generic title:** the `<title>` ("Crate & Barrel") doesn't match the subject line.
- **Global CSS risk:** `*{… max-height:1000000px; line-height:100%}` and `p{margin:0 auto; text-align:center}` are global overrides that can affect inheritance in some clients. This is low severity and not confirmed as a bug.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The truncated source shows no unresolved merge tokens such as `%%…%%`, `{{…}}` or `[FNAME]`. The placeholder comment above is a template note, not a rendered token. The truncation means the full body wasn't checked.

## 5. Compliance
- **List-Unsubscribe not found (WARN):** the QA note says the header may not be captured by the AgentMail relay. It is unconfirmed whether the original message carried it, so treat this as unverified rather than a violation.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) not found (WARN):** this has the same caveat. One-click unsubscribe support is unknown, and Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require it.
- **Authentication-Results not found (WARN):** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown because of the relay. The sending domain is `mail.crateandbarrel.com`, a subdomain aligned with the brand.
- **In-body footer:** the truncated source doesn't show the unsubscribe link or physical address, so these are unverified.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add meaningful `alt` text to every content image, and `alt=""` to tracking pixels and decorative images. Start with the `/m/1/*.jpg` set and `bbf6f9d4…png`.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
3. Fix the `<title>` to use `&amp;`, remove the duplicate meta tags and the leftover `[IMPUT HERE…]` comment, and set a subject-relevant title.
4. Capture raw headers from the source mailbox (not the relay) to confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and SPF/DKIM/DMARC.
5. Resolve the redirect chains for the 116 tracking links through a headless or follow-redirect pass. That would verify destinations and UTM carry-through.
6. Fix the QA tooling gap: store the full HTML and headers, since truncation blocked footer, token and UTM checks.
