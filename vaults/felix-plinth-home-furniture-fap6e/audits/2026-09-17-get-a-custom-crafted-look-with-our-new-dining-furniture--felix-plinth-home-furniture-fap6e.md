# Get a custom-crafted look with our new dining furniture
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-17**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is the first email in a while that actually feels like it was made for one of my two renovations instead of my nonexistent kid or my nonexistent cat. "New dining furniture, just in time for hosting" — I'm mid-decision on dining chairs for the primary right now, so this got a real second of my attention. The "from your saved pins to your dining room" section is a genuinely smart hook for someone who runs a Pinterest-adjacent folder like I do.
- But it sags fast. It's a marathon scroll — arches, sofas, a whole "tailored to your space, custom style under $500" detour, storage, lighting, rugs, a table-setting section, a bag/backpack lifestyle shot, a sofa/dresser grid, then a credit card pitch and an app download at the bottom. By the time I hit "we love these for you" I've forgotten this was supposed to be about dining furniture. Compare that to Article's home-office tour I looked at recently — one room, one story, done. This is five emails stitched into one.
- Would I click? Yeah, probably on "Shop New Dining Furniture" near the top since I'm actually shopping that category right now. Forward to a friend? No — nothing here is share-worthy, it's just a long catalog dump.

## What's working

- "Just in time for hosting" lands with actual seasonal intent, unlike some of Wayfair's random-holiday subject bait I've seen lately.
- "From your saved pins to your dining room" directly speaks to how I actually shop — I do save things to a folder before buying.
- The "custom style, in stock now, tailored to your space, under $500" section addresses real furniture-shopping anxiety (customization vs. wait times) that Pottery Barn's Rejuvenation cross-promo never touched.
- Clean, consistent product photography throughout — no render bugs, no broken images that I can see.

## What's weak

- It's way too long — dining furniture gets buried under sofas, rugs, lighting, tableware, bags, and a credit card pitch before I even get to the bottom.
- Multiple redundant "Shop New Dining Furniture" / "Shop All New Furniture" CTAs scattered without a clear single hero moment — my eye doesn't know where to land first.
- The credit card pitch ("it all adds up to a home you love... chance at $500") feels bolted on, buried mid-scroll instead of tied to a real purchase moment.
- "We love these for you" personalization claim at the bottom is generic sofa/dresser/mirror grid — doesn't feel tailored to me specifically despite the copy implying it is.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this email in half — dining furniture is the subject line's promise, so give me three product shots and one CTA, then stop. Save the sofa/rug/lighting/tableware sprawl for separate sends.
- 2. Move the "from your saved pins" section to the very top, right under the hero — that's the most persona-relevant hook in the whole email and it's currently third scroll-stop deep.
- 3. Cut the credit card pitch entirely from a product email, or move it to its own dedicated send — it doesn't belong wedged between rugs and dinnerware.
- 4. If "we love these for you" is going to claim personalization, actually show it — pull from my saved-pins folder or browse history, not a generic grid.
