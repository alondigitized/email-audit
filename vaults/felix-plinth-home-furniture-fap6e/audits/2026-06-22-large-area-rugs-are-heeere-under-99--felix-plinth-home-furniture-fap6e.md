# LARGE AREA RUGS are heeere! under $99
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- Sixty-four. That's where I am with Wayfair now. And I'm looking at the exact same subject line I reviewed yesterday — "LARGE AREA RUGS are heeere! under $99" — which I scored a 6 on the 21st. Today is the 22nd. Wayfair resent it. Creative unchanged. Nothing to update.
- Here's the frustrating part: rugs are genuinely live on my list. Both houses need floor coverage, I've been measuring rooms for weeks, and I've been cross-shopping Crate & Barrel and Pottery Barn on the same category. I opened this. And what I got was a cafeteria scroll — rugs up top, then bar stools, then storage cubbies, then a clearance pile with no discernible theme — all packed into one email that promised me large area rugs and then wandered away. The 72-HR CLEAROUT badge at the bottom is doing the hardest work in this email and it's buried below three product grids.
- I'm marking this read without clicking. Not because rugs don't matter — they do — but because I saw this yesterday, nothing changed, and I still can't read a rug dimension or a specific price from any of the product tiles. This is the send that finally makes me set up an inbox filter for Wayfair.

## What's working

- The 40% clearout badge is visually distinct and creates real urgency — it's the one element that earns its screen real estate
- The rug-forward product grid in the top section does match the subject line promise, at least initially
- Wayfair Rewards visible in the footer signals I'm shopping as a member, not a cold prospect
- The brand is instantly recognizable; no confusion about who sent this

## What's weak

- This is a direct resend of the 6/21 subject line with zero creative variation — it reads like a system glitch, not an intentional campaign beat; Crate & Barrel ran the same July 4th hero twice but at least swapped the send date by a week
- "score styles you 💜" as hero copy is complete filler — it's not a rug line, not a price line, not even a seasonal hook; Article's Cillian Collection email named the product in the first word and I knew exactly what I was shopping
- The "pace your space" section breaks the rug brief entirely — bar stools and storage cubbies have no business being here when the subject explicitly said large area rugs
- Product thumbnails are too small to read dimensions or pricing without clicking — and for rugs, size is the purchase trigger; Pottery Barn's recent nightstand email kept product specs readable at thumbnail scale
- "heeere!" with the triple-e works in a text from a friend, not in an email I'm using to make a real purchasing decision for two houses

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the resend if the creative is identical.** If the 6/21 send underperformed, change the hero size category (lead with 5x8s instead of 8x10s), update the price anchor, or swap the lead image. Don't hit send on the same asset twice in two days.
- 2. **Lock the body to one category.** Subject says large area rugs — every tile in the scroll should be a rug. Pull bar stools and storage into their own day; Pottery Barn managed to anchor a full email to nightstands while adding supporting items only below the fold.
- 3. **Embed dimensions and price in every product tile.** "8x10 · $79 · Ships free" closes the loop without a click. Right now I have to trust the under-$99 promise blindly.
- 4. **Promote the 72-HR badge above the first product grid.** Urgency should hit before browse fatigue — right now it's buried three scrolls in.
- 5. **Rewrite the hero line to earn the subject.** "Under $99. Oversized. Free shipping." tells me more than the current purple-heart copy ever will.
