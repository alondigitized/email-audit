## Executive summary

- Okay, "THIS WEEK'S STEALS" — so CB2 is back in straight sale mode after that nice editorial detour on 7/30 with the home-office tour I gave an 8. That was a palate cleanser; this is us right back to the discount-block format I've now seen a half dozen times this month. It's a well-shot discount block, I'll give it that — the blue velvet sofa up top is genuinely nice and I paused on it — but four stacked percentage-off banners (40%, 60%, 30%, 50%) with zero mention of which one applies to which product is just visual noise by the third scroll.
- The "MORE FOR YOU" grid at the bottom is actually the most useful real estate in the email for me specifically — a platform bed, a counter stool, nightstands, dining tables — because I'm furnishing two houses right now and that's browsable product, not a banner. But I have to scroll past four nearly-identical hero blocks to get there.
- Would I click? Maybe, straight to the sofa. Forward to a friend? No — this is generic enough that nobody needs my curation on it. Marking as read and maybe circling back for the "more for you" grid later.

## What's working

- The blue velvet sofa hero is a strong opener — good color, good staging, stopped my scroll for a second.
- The "MORE FOR YOU" product grid at the bottom with actual prices (some struck-through/on sale) is the part I'd actually act on — that's real product discovery, not just a banner.
- Outdoor sectional shot with the pool backdrop is well-timed for August — seasonally relevant even mid-renovation chaos.
- Rewards messaging ("Earn 10% Back," "Get 15% Off" for texting) is visible near the bottom, which matters since I'm tracking CB2/Crate & Barrel as a combined loyalty relationship.

## What's weak

- Four different percentage-off tiers (40/60/30/50) with no product-category clarity until you're staring at each banner individually — my eye doesn't know where to land first, unlike the 7/28 "24 Hours Left" send which had one clear number and one clear deadline.
- No actual deadline anywhere. "This week's steals" implies urgency but there's no "ends Sunday" or countdown — compare to 7/29's literal "Ends tonight," which at least forced a decision.
- This is the same discount-block formula I've now seen enough times this month that it's blurring together — Article ran the identical "sale, sale, sale, then finally the tour" pattern through July and I called it out then too.
- Nothing here acknowledges I'm mid-renovation on two houses simultaneously — no "shop by room," no swatch/sample CTA, no shipping-timeline callout for furniture, which is the one thing that would actually move me to click on a sofa I can't sit on first.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick ONE lead offer and put a real deadline on it — the 7/28 and 7/29 sends worked specifically because urgency was concrete. Four stacked percentages with no clock reads as generic, not urgent.
- 2. Move the "MORE FOR YOU" product grid up higher — that's the section doing the actual work for a renovation shopper like me, and it's currently buried after four scrolls of banner.
- 3. Add shipping/lead-time info directly on the sofa and dining table hero blocks — for big furniture that's the actual purchase blocker, not the price.
- 4. Tag categories on each banner explicitly (e.g., "40% off SOFAS," not just "40% off Furniture, Decor and More") so I don't have to guess which discount applies to what I want.
