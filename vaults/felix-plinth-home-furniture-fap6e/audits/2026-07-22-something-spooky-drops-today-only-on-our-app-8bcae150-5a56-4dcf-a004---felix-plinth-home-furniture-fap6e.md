# Something spooky drops TODAY, only on our app! 🎃
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-one. That's where the C&B family count lands for July after this one, and I'm flagging it immediately because I literally reviewed this exact subject line this morning — same "Something spooky drops TODAY" from the same crateandbarrel.com address, sitting in my brand history at a 4.00. Whether this is a resend, an A/B variant, or a technical duplicate, it reads like a mistake. And a mistake from a brand I'm actively shopping for dining tables on doesn't inspire confidence.
- The email itself is a two-headed mess. The hero is a Halloween pumpkin scene pushing me to download the app. Directly below it is a "up to 50% off The Summer Sale" offer with a Shop Now button. Those two messages are fighting each other. One is a season-jumping app-exclusivity tease — in July — and the other is a straight furniture sale that's actually useful to me right now. The product recs in the middle (wood dining tables, benches, what looks like teak or similar) are genuinely relevant to where I am in my renovation, but they're buried under two competing hero sections.
- Mark as read. No click, no forward. If I want the Summer Sale, I'll go directly to the site — I don't need the app.

## What's working

- The Summer Sale 50% off offer is real and visible without hunting for it — that number does its job.
- The "we love these for you" product block shows wood dining pieces that actually match what I'm sourcing for my second home right now.
- No broken images, no overlapping text — the email renders cleanly at this size.
- Crate & Barrel's brand identity is unmistakable; I never confused this for a phishing attempt.
- TheDesignDesk mention at the bottom is the kind of service differentiation I actually value — I've wished CB2 offered something similar.

## What's weak

- Halloween in July. I don't care what's "spooky" until at least mid-September, and even then I want it in a dedicated send, not crammed above a Summer Sale.
- App gate on the hero offer — downloading the C&B app is not something I'm doing to get "first access" to Halloween inventory I didn't know I wanted.
- Two separate campaigns, one email: the hierarchy is broken before I even scroll. This is the same confusion I dinged CB2 for on their "UP TO 40% OFF ENDS TODAY" send, and they at least had a single message.
- This is the second time I've seen this exact subject line hit my inbox today. If it's an intentional resend, that's a trust issue. If it's a bug, it still counts against them.
- The product recommendations (the one part that's relevant to me) are so far below the fold they almost don't exist.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split this into two sends.** Summer Sale gets its own clean email — lead with the furniture, use the product recs as the hero, bury nothing. Halloween teaser goes out in a dedicated send in late August or September, not in July alongside a sale.
- 2. **Kill the app gate on the hero.** Article ran a dining room email this week that linked directly to product pages — no friction, no download prompt. That's why I'm actually considering their chairs. Don't make the first click cost me an app install.
- 3. **Make the product rec block the hero for renovation shoppers.** If your data shows I browse dining furniture, lead with dining furniture. Pottery Barn's Chris Loves Julia send — 8/10 from me — worked because the product match was explicit and above the fold. This buried the relevant stuff.
- 4. **Add a deadline to the Summer Sale CTA.** "Up to 50% off The Summer Sale / Shop Now" is softer than it should be. Wayfair's end-table send hammered "TOMORROW" in the subject and it landed harder than this.
- 5. **Don't resend the same subject.** Full stop.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Summer Sale ends soon: up to 50% off dining & more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your dining table is 50% off. Today only.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Wood tables, outdoor seating, lighting — deep cuts before they're gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The pieces you've been watching just got a lot more affordable.`


`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom. Fails WCAG 2.1 Success Criterion 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[MEDIUM] Global `text-size-adjust: none` override**
```css

```
Applied via the universal selector — overrides browser accessibility settings for all elements, not just the targeted reset on `body`. Scope this to `body` only.
