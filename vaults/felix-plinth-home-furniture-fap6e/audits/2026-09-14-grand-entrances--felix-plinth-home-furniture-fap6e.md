# Grand entrances
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-14**
## Executive summary

- Okay, CB2 again — fourth or fifth time this week in my inbox — and this one's actually smart: "Grand entrances" is entirely about console tables, entryway mirrors, and consoles for the foyer, which happens to be exactly the zone I haven't furnished yet in the primary reno. "FIRST IMPRESSIONS ARE EVERYTHING" as the hero copy, three styled console vignettes (Davison, Rio, Scatola), each with a designer credit — that's a real category-specific send, not another countdown-clock blast.
- That said, my CB2-family fatigue is real at this point. I flagged it with the "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" email and again on the "This sofa could be vintage" send — I've now read five-plus C&B/CB2 emails in a single week, and "FALL SALE — UP TO 30% OFF" is the same top banner I've seen since Monday. The content underneath is genuinely good, styled console-table photography beats what Pottery Barn's Kravet collab or Wayfair's ceiling-fan bait-and-switch are doing this week, but the sale banner reuse makes it blur into the pile.
- Would I click? Probably, yeah — I need a console table for the entry and the Davison one with the dark oak looks right. Wouldn't forward it though, this is a "save to the Pinterest-adjacent folder and revisit when I'm actually pulling the trigger" email, not a "hey check this out" one.

## What's working

- The entryway/console framing is a real sub-category, not generic furniture — matches exactly what I'm shopping for right now.
- Each console (Davison, Rio, Scatola) is named and credited to a designer/studio, which is the kind of detail that makes CB2 feel like a design brand, not a big-box retailer.
- The "MORE FOR YOU" grid at the bottom shows real prices ($1,199, $349, $299, $999) — no hiding numbers, unlike some of Wayfair's vaguer sends.
- "THERE'S MORE" module cleanly segments mirrors / table lamps / decor / wall art so I can jump straight to what I need for the space.
- Interior Design Services banner (in-store or virtual, "complimentary") is a nice trust/loyalty touch I haven't seen CB2 push before.

## What's weak

- "FALL SALE — UP TO 30% OFF" banner is the exact same top-of-email treatment I've now seen from this brand family multiple times this week — it's stopped registering as an offer and started reading as wallpaper.
- The console products themselves don't show a price or a sale % anywhere near the hero shots — I have to scroll all the way to "MORE FOR YOU" to see actual numbers, and even then it's not clear if 30% is applied.
- Card/rewards math is buried in a tiny two-column footer block ("EARN 10% BACK" / "10% OFF SIGN UP") — I put purchases on my highest-rewards card, and this is exactly the kind of math I'd want front and center, not squeezed into fine print at the bottom.
- Three near-identical "SHOP NEW IN ENTRYWAY" CTAs stacked through the email feel repetitive rather than building urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put actual prices under each hero console (Davison, Rio, Scatola) instead of making me scroll to the generic product grid — that's the single biggest lift, since price is what turns browsing into a click for me.
- 2. Cut the CB2 family sale-banner reuse — I've seen "UP TO 30% OFF" in maroon/tan bars from this brand five times this week; a category-specific badge ("Entryway Edit") would stand out more than repeating the storewide banner.
- 3. Move the "10% back" rewards line up near the top CTA — that's a real deciding factor for me and it's currently buried in the footer.
- 4. Drop one of the three "SHOP NEW IN ENTRYWAY" buttons — one after the hero, one at the end is plenty.

- **Preheader Alt B:** `Design-led entryway pieces, plus 10% back on your CB2 card`
