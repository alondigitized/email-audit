# under $29.99
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- This is the second "under $29.99" email I've gotten from Wayfair in about 24 hours. Yesterday's version had the word "All" in front — "All under $29.99" — and landed at a 7. This one dropped the "All" and apparently nothing else changed in the brief. That's not a refinement, that's a typo-level distinction between two sends targeting the same price-cap concept on back-to-back days. I'm at seven Wayfair emails in roughly 72 hours now and my archive finger is getting itchy.
- That said — the email itself is competently assembled. The sectioning is smart: florals, cleaning, metallic modern, big furniture adjacent, outdoor, deals of the day. The 70% off rug in the "deals of the day" block is genuinely the most relevant thing here for someone mid-renovation, but it's buried five sections deep. The $29.99 cap is doing a lot of heavy lifting to make cleaning sprays and flatware feel adjacent to someone who's pricing dining sets and area rugs.
- I would not click. I would not forward. I would open the deals of the day rug link if a friend texted me about it separately, but on its own the email gets archived without ceremony.

## What's working

- The section labels ("metallic modern," "the cleaning aisle," "your florals from $15") create quick visual scannability — I can jump past the stuff I don't want without reading every product
- "Deals of the day" with a 70% OFF badge is the one signal that feels like real money on the table, not a curated-to-a-price-cap dump
- No broken images, no layout wreckage — cleaner than half the Crate & Barrel SALE sends I've gotten this week
- The "big fat furniture" label is the only moment of brand voice that felt like a human wrote it — it's punchy in a way the rest of the copy isn't

## What's weak

- This is functionally the same email concept as yesterday's send, with a subject line that is one word shorter — Pottery Barn ran repeated hero creative multiple times in a week and it felt like laziness; this lands the same way
- The $29.99 cap is fine for florals and flatware but it trains me to not expect anything relevant to my actual renovation — no one buying a dining set or area rugs is getting excited about a $29.99 ceiling
- Five distinct category sections before you hit the one genuinely strong offer (the rug deal) is too much cognitive overhead — Article's welcome email had a single focal point and it was more actionable than this entire send
- "Deals of the day" implies a clock is ticking but there's no expiry visible — just a badge, no timestamp, no "ends tonight"
- The outdoor section showing what appears to be a bike feels randomly appended — Wayfair selling bikes, fine, but it reads like a clearance shelf nobody curated

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the 70% off rug, not the $29.99 price cap** — the rug deal is the only offer here with renovation-budget relevance. Put that in the hero, price-cap becomes a secondary sub-text.
- 2. **Add a genuine deadline to deals of the day** — "Ends tonight at midnight" converts. The badge alone doesn't.
- 3. **Kill one of these two sends** — if you ran an "All under $29.99" yesterday, this version should not exist today. Merge the content, update the subject, or hold it 72 hours. The Article welcome email I got this week hit once and stayed memorable; Wayfair hits seven times and becomes wallpaper.
- 4. **Collapse to three sections max** — florals, decor/modern, deals of day. Cleaning aisle and bikes dilute the home renovation signal that would keep someone like me engaged.
