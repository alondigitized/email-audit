# NEW MARKDOWNS! Up to 60% off clearance→
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- Eight. That's where the Crate & Barrel family count lands in my inbox across two days, and this one is a near-direct sequel to what the prior reviews already called the "new markdowns fire drill" — same concept, one day later, nearly identical message. When I see "NEW MARKDOWNS! Up to 60% off clearance" in my inbox the morning after I've already processed a clearance push from the same sender, the best-case read is that their ESP had a scheduling hiccup. The worst-case read is that someone on the team thinks volume equals conversion.
- Here's the frustrating thing: the email itself is actually solid. The hero delivers 60% off cleanly. The product grid shows clearance breadth — creams, warm woods, muted tones — which happens to line up with where I am on the second-home project right now. The "TheGroupDesk" section is a nice lifestyle anchor... but it appears *twice* in the same scroll, which suggests editorial and clearance got frankensteined together by accident. And there's an inexplicable "Celebrating 30 years of our Texas design hub" block mid-email that reads like someone accidentally copy-pasted from a brand anniversary send.
- I'm not clicking. Not because there's nothing here — there probably is — but because I've seen this movie already and Article just ran "Last chance: Memorial Day Sale ends today" with an actual close date. I know what urgency looks like when it's real. This isn't it. Mark as read.

## What's working

- 60% off is a concrete ceiling, not vague "savings" language — that number stops the scroll even when I'm numb.
- Hero visual hierarchy is clean: clearance offer lands above the fold, muted palette reads well, no competing elements fighting for attention.
- Product grid approach is right for clearance — browsing is serendipitous, showing breadth makes sense.
- No render bugs: layout is intact, images are loaded, nothing is broken or cut off.
- Crate & Barrel has enough trust equity with me that I don't immediately archive — the logo buys a few seconds.

## What's weak

- This is the second "new markdowns/clearance" send in two days. The cadence is not a feature.
- ALL CAPS "NEW MARKDOWNS!" in the subject is a spam-folder aesthetic. CB2 ran "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" last week without shouting and it felt like a brand; this feels like a clearance rack flyer.
- TheGroupDesk section appears twice in one email — either the template has a duplicate block or editorial and clearance content got merged. Either way it breaks purchase intent.
- "Celebrating 30 years of our Texas design hub" mid-scroll editorial makes zero sense inside a clearance email. I'm here to find 60% off floor lamps, not brand history.
- No price anchoring in the product grid — no "was / now" visible anywhere. Pottery Barn's Chris Loves Julia send last week didn't run a sale at all, but it made me stop and look at specific pieces. One "Was $399, Now $159" hero item would outperform a full grid of thumbnails with no context.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Retire the repeat** — this concept ran yesterday; either merge the two sends into one well-timed push or let the second one evolve into a "last call" closer with a hard deadline. Article did exactly this: brand build, then sale, then "ends today" as the genuine final frame. The sequencing earns the click.
- 2. **Add a deadline in the hero** — "Up to 60% off clearance" is permanently open-ended. "Through Sunday" or "while quantities last, ending June 1" changes my calculus. Wayfair ran "LARGE AREA RUGS are heeere! under $99" two days after their first rug push and at least varied the price anchor and urgency framing. C&B just ran the same headline.
- 3. **Price-anchor at least one hero product** — crossed-out original price, now price. One item. It validates the "up to 60%" claim and gives me something to anchor the rest of the grid against.
- 4. **Pull TheGroupDesk into its own standalone send** — it's good content that deserves its own moment, not a clearance-email appendage that shows up twice.
- 5. **Drop the Texas design hub anniversary block** — no one browsing 60% off clearance furniture wants a brand history lesson mid-scroll.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Preventing user zoom on mobile is a Level AA accessibility failure. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Issue — Conflicting `img` max-width breakpoints:**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
These are declared in source order with the 768px rule first. Since 768px > 640px, at viewport ≥768px both rules match — the 640px rule wins due to cascade order (later in source), not the 600px container width. This likely causes images to overflow their 600px table container on wide-screen clients. The 768px rule should come *after* the 640px rule, or consolidate to a single breakpoint.

**Issue — Dark mode not declared:**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Opting out of dark mode entirely forces light rendering in Apple Mail and some Android clients. Acceptable if intentional, but flag to confirm it is a deliberate decision and that light-background images (white product shots, white CTAs) have been validated against forced-dark rendering in clients that ignore this meta.

**Cannot assess — `alt` text on images:** Body is truncated. Verify all `<img>` tags carry non-empty `alt` attributes, particularly product images and CTA buttons.
