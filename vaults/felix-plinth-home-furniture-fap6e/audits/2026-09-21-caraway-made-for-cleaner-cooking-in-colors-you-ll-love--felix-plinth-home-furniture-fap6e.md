# Caraway: Made for cleaner cooking in colors you’ll love
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-21**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one's actually a legit product email, not another sofa/nursery dump — Caraway cookware, ceramic non-stick, five-star review pull-quote right at the top ("I wish I'd made the switch to Caraway sooner!"). That's a real hook, and it's cookware, which is at least adjacent to the reno/kitchen-refresh stuff I'm actually thinking about for the second house's kitchen. The color-and-material story is consistent all the way down — cream, sage, marigold, gunmetal — and it reads like an actual line launch, not a discount dump.
- That said, this thing is enormous. Cookware set, bakeware, food storage, kitchen gadgets, cutting boards, a Venus Williams Crate & Barrel collab that has nothing to do with Caraway, a "we love these for you" cross-sell grid, and then a "TheDesignDesign" in-home design service pitch at the very bottom. By the time I hit Venus Williams I've completely forgotten this was a Caraway email. It's the same structural problem CB2's bedroom email had, and the same one the "everything room" sofa send had on 2026-09-21 — start tight, sprawl into a full catalog.
- Would I click? Yes, on the cookware set specifically, because the hero pull-quote and the "Shop Caraway" button are right there above the fold with no scroll needed. Would I forward it? No — this is a single-guy-buys-a-pan decision, not a "hey check this out" moment with a friend. I'd click through on the pan, then close the tab well before Venus Williams shows up.

## What's working

- The five-star testimonial headline up top is a strong trust cue — better than Wayfair's flag-emoji "LAST CALL" urgency-with-no-product I saw on the cutting boards send.
- Color/material consistency (cream, sage, marigold ceramic) is clean and matches how Caraway actually markets — feels like an intentional palette, not stock photography grabbed at random.
- "Exclusive" tags on the color variants (Blue Lagoon, Cream, Rose Gold hardware) work for me — I like knowing I'm getting something not everywhere.
- Product photography is sharp, no render bugs, no broken images — genuinely cleaner than the Pottery Barn cozy-textiles send, which felt more like a stock catalog scroll.
- "Shop Caraway" appears twice early with real product context around it, so the CTA isn't hiding.

## What's weak

- It just doesn't stop — cookware, bakeware, food storage, gadgets, cutting boards, THEN a Venus Williams tennis collab that's a completely different brand story bolted onto the same email.
- The Venus Williams block especially: no connective tissue to Caraway at all, it reads like two emails stapled together.
- "TheDesignDesk" in-home design service pitch at the bottom feels like a third, unrelated ask crammed into an already-long email — that's the kind of overreach that makes me stop reading before the actual offer.
- No price anywhere, no % off, no member pricing visible — for a "new colors" merch push that's fine, but there's zero urgency or reason to buy *today* versus bookmarking it.
- Zero mention of any rewards program or my card — I'm buying this stuff on my highest-rewards card regardless, but a loyalty nudge would've helped seal a same-day click.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this email in half. Caraway cookware/bakeware/storage is one coherent story — ship that alone, save Venus Williams and the design service pitch for separate sends. This is the single biggest lift available.
- 2. Put an actual price or bundle discount under the hero pull-quote — right now there's no reason to act now versus later.
- 3. Drop the Venus Williams tennis collab entirely from this send — it has zero relevance to cookware shoppers and actively dilutes the hero product.
- 4. Move "we love these for you" cross-sell grid up near the top-performing cookware section, or cut it — by the time it appears, attention is gone.
