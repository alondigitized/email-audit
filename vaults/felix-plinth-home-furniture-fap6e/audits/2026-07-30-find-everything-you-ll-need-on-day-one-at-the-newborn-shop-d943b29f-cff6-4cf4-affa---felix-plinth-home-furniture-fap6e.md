# Find everything you’ll need on day one at The Newborn Shop!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- Third Crate & Kids nursery email in the same week, and I still don't have a kid. My gut reaction scrolling this on my phone: it's a wall of cribs, swaddles, and "keepsake" gift sets stacked eight sections deep, and none of it applies to either of my two gut-renovations. I get that "The Newborn Shop" is a real merchandising concept for someone actually expecting, but for me it's just more of the same brand fatigue I flagged on the Beatrix collection send from the same day.
- The one thing that made me pause was the "up to 50% off summer sale" and back-to-school block near the bottom — that's actual Crate & Barrel-adjacent furniture territory, not nursery gear, and it's buried after a dozen scrolls of onesies and swaddle blankets. If that block led the email instead of closing it, I'd have clicked. As is: mark as read, no forward, no click.

## What's working

- The neutral, warm-toned nursery styling (cream walls, oak crib, rattan pendant light) is genuinely on-brand and would catch my eye if I were shopping this category — it's the same restrained palette that made the Campagna Rattan dresser shot from the Summer Sale emails work.
- "up to 50% off summer sale" + "up to 50% off back to school" near the bottom is a real, concrete offer with a visible button — if I were scanning fast that's the one thing I'd register.
- Clean single-column layout, no overlapping text or broken images — technically well-executed, same production quality as their other sends.
- The "win a chance to win $2000" callout is a decent attention-grabber, more novel than another straight discount ask.

## What's weak

- I have no infant, no nursery, no baby registry — this is the third nursery-focused Crate & Kids email this week (after "NEW! Meet the Beatrix collection" and the Beatrix repeat) and it's the least relevant to me yet since it's not even a specific new collection, just a full-category dump.
- The email is enormous — crib, bedding, swaddles, robes, curtains, rugs, changing pads, "nursery finishing touches" — fifteen-plus product blocks before you even hit anything I'd shop. That's Wayfair-pool-table-email levels of everything-but-the-kitchen-sink, and Wayfair scored a 5 from me for exactly that sin.
- The actually-relevant "50% off summer sale / back-to-school" furniture block is the second-to-last thing in the email. Article and CB2 lead with their hero offer up top; burying mine after a full nursery catalog means I'll never scroll that far on a phone.
- "gifts they'll use from day one" and the hooded-towel keepsake set — sweet if you're the target parent, completely irrelevant noise for me and just adds to the scroll fatigue.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this. If Crate & Kids' data shows a subscriber has never clicked a crib, bassinet, or nursery-bedding link, don't send the full Newborn Shop catalog — lead with the summer sale/back-to-school furniture block instead, since that's the parent-brand crossover content I actually engage with.
- 2. Cut the email in half. Fifteen product sections before the one offer relevant to broader home shoppers is a scroll-fatigue problem, same critique I gave Wayfair's pool-table blitz.
- 3. Move "up to 50% off summer sale" to the top or send it as its own dedicated email — don't let it die at the bottom of a nursery-gear wall.
- 4. If this is genuinely meant for expecting parents only, say so in the subject so non-parents can skip it guilt-free instead of opening and bouncing.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The Newborn Shop: everything for day one (new parents, start here)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 50% off summer + everything for baby's first days`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cribs, bedding, and gear — plus 50% off summer furniture sitewide`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New to nursery shopping? Start with our day-one essentials guide`
