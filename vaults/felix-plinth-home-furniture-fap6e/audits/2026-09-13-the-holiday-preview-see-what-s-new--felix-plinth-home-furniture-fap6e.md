# The holiday preview: See what’s new
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-13**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction: this is a genuinely well-shot email, and it's the first Pottery Barn send this week that isn't leaning on a celebrity name or a credit card pitch to get my attention. "In-Stock Furniture Delivered in 1-4 Weeks" as the top banner is smart — that's a real answer to a real question I have mid-renovation, before I even hit the hero image. The bedroom shot is moody and well-styled, and the grid of holiday product shots underneath actually reads like a lookbook, not a random SKU dump.
- That said, "holiday preview" in September is a stretch for where my head's at. I'm hanging curtain rods and picking dining tables right now — not thinking about garland. The "Recommended Just for You" block redeems it a bit because it's genuinely my stuff: curtain rods, a dining table, a platform bed, linen curtains — that's actually personalized to a guy furnishing two houses. I'd scroll past the holiday stuff fast but stop on that section.
- Would I click? Yes, on "Recommended Just for You," specifically the Toscana Extendable Dining Table or the curtain rod — those are live decisions for me. Wouldn't forward to a friend, wouldn't click the holiday hero. Marking as read after I scan the rec block.

## What's working

- The "In-Stock Furniture Delivered in 1-4 Weeks" banner up top speaks directly to renovation-timeline anxiety, which is exactly my mental state right now.
- "Recommended Just for You" is legitimately relevant — curtain rods, dining table, platform bed, linen curtains all match what I'm actually shopping for across two houses.
- The bedroom hero shot is well-composed and not overly styled/precious the way the Molly Sims send was.
- Clean, obvious CTAs ("Shop Now," "Shop the Holiday Preview") — no ambiguity about what to click.
- Rewards card + Design Crew + app download modules at the bottom are useful context I actually care about as a repeat renovator, not filler.

## What's weak

- "Holiday preview" in mid-September is early even for me, and I'm not in a holiday-shopping headspace — I'm in a "still building the bones of the house" headspace.
- The middle product grid (candles, ornaments, garland-adjacent stuff) is mostly decor dressing, not furniture — low relevance to someone still doing big-ticket items.
- The Halloween Shop banner sandwiched between holiday content and "Recommended for You" is a jarring seasonal whiplash — holiday, then Halloween, then rewards card, all stacked with no throughline.
- This is at least the fourth or fifth Pottery Barn email this week (following the seating email 2026-09-12, the Visa pitch 2026-09-12, and Molly Sims 2026-09-12) — the cadence is genuinely wearing me down, and this one doesn't clear the bar high enough to justify another open.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with "Recommended Just for You" instead of burying it two-thirds down — that's the section with my actual purchase intent, and it's getting crowded out by holiday/Halloween noise.
- 2. Cut the holiday-preview framing entirely for a Sept 13 send, or reframe it as "new arrivals for fall" so it doesn't feel four months premature.
- 3. Pull the Halloween Shop module out — it's whiplash sandwiched between holiday content and personalized recs, and it dilutes an otherwise coherent narrative.
- 4. Add shipping/lead-time specificity to the personalized product tiles themselves (not just the top banner) — if the Toscana table is in stock and ships in 2 weeks, tell me right there, not just in the generic hero.

# Technical Email Audit — Pottery Barn "The holiday preview"

## 1. Technical Summary
Standard ESP-templated (Bronto/Oracle-style, `e.potterybarn.com`) HTML email with MSO/Outlook conditional comments and table-based layout; automated QA passed 57% of checks with 0 hard issues and 3 warning categories, driven mainly by widespread missing `alt` text and two `http://` (non-TLS) image sources.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 46 links route through click-tracking/redirect domains (`click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=...`, `clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/...`, `d.turn.com/r/...`) and were skipped by the automated HTTP probe — destination validity is unconfirmed.
- Open-tracking pixel `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` is served over plain HTTP and embeds the recipient's email address directly in the query string in cleartext.
- No issues found with malformed/broken href syntax in the visible source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 30+ `<img>` elements are missing `alt` attributes (e.g., `H25_TOP_InStockFurniture.jpg`, `slices_01.jpg`–`slices_18.jpg`, `standard-curtain-rod-wall-bracket-l.jpg`, `toscana-extendable-dining-table-60-1245-1-l.jpg`, `cayman-platform-bed-1-l.jpg`) — screen readers and image-blocked clients will render this email with no accessible text.
- Two images load over `http://` instead of `https://`: `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` (`http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`) and `slices_02.jpg` (`http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`) — mixed content on an otherwise HTTPS-served template may be blocked or flagged by mail clients (Gmail/Outlook image proxies typically upgrade, but native http assets can fail silently in stricter clients).
- `<style>` block contains inert PHP-style `header(...)` calls (`header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); ...`) sitting inside a CSS `<style>` tag — these are not executable in this context and are dead code, but indicate the template source was likely copy-pasted from a server-side script; no functional rendering impact.
- Head includes both `<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">` and `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` — appropriate for email, no issue.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- Recipient identity is passed via query-string merge values rather than templating tags visible in-body, e.g. `coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` and `sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`.
- The recipient's full email address is exposed in plaintext in two separate outbound URLs (one over unencrypted HTTP). This is a PII-in-URL exposure risk (leaks via browser history, proxies, referrer headers, server logs) rather than a rendering defect — flagged for compliance/security awareness.
- No unresolved merge tags (e.g. `%%FIRSTNAME%%`, `{{token}}`) found in the truncated source.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- `List-Unsubscribe` header: not found — one-click unsubscribe may not be surfaced in-inbox by clients that support it (Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements).
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058): not found — even if a `List-Unsubscribe` header were added, without this the one-click POST mechanism is unavailable.
- `Authentication-Results` header: not found — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status cannot be verified via the AgentMail relay capture; cannot confirm authentication posture from available data.
- In-body unsubscribe link/postal address (CAN-SPAM requirement) could not be verified — the provided HTML source is truncated before the footer, so presence/absence of a visible unsubscribe link is unconfirmed from this evidence alone.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- All outbound content links are wrapped in ESP click-tracking redirects (`click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=...`, `clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/...`), which were not resolved by the automated probe — actual landing-page URLs and any UTM parameters they carry could not be inspected or validated against `potterybarn.com`.
- Some image assets reference production site paths directly (e.g. `www.potterybarn.com/pbimgs/ab/images/dp/wcm/202634/0007/toscana-extendable-dining-table-60-1245-1-l.jpg`), confirming the mailer and site share the same asset/product ID scheme, but this doesn't confirm click-through destination alignment since those are `<img>` sources, not link targets.
- No issues found regarding domain mismatch between sender (`e.potterybarn.com`) and site (`potterybarn.com`) — subdomain relationship is consistent with standard ESP setup.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to all product/banner images, or at minimum empty `alt=""` for purely decorative slices/spacers, to satisfy accessibility requirements.
2. Migrate the two remaining `http://` image sources (`Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, `slices_02.jpg`) to `https://`.
3. Move the open-tracking pixel (`svopn.potterybarn.com`) to HTTPS and stop passing the raw recipient email address in the query string on both tracking endpoints — use an opaque hashed/tokenized identifier instead.
4. Add `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers to enable one-click unsubscribe and reduce spam-complaint risk with major mailbox providers.
5. Confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC are passing at the actual sending MTA — the missing `Authentication-Results` header here is most likely a relay-capture gap, but should be verified directly against send logs rather than assumed.
6. Since all links are tracking-wrapped, periodically spot-check a sample of resolved redirect destinations to confirm they land on the intended, UTM-tagged pages.
