# An instant classic: our new Beatrix collection ♥️
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-31**
## Executive summary

- Yeah, no — this is Crate & Kids, and I don't have kids. The hero is a dark green "Beatrix bedroom collection" that actually looks like a real adult-adjacent bedroom set (that green upholstered headboard is nice, I'll give it that), but scroll two inches and it's straight into a nursery — crib, changing table, mobile over a bassinet. I don't need any of that. This is the sixth Crate-family email clogging my inbox this week (two Crate & Barrel kitchen sends, two furniture pushes, and now this), and it's the second time Crate & Kids specifically has shown up. My brain has fully written off this sub-brand as noise at this point.
- The one thing that stopped my thumb for half a second was the top banner: "Double Rewards is on! Earn 7X the points" — that's actually a strong loyalty hook if I were in the market. But I'm not, so it's wasted on me. Would I click? No. Would I forward it? Only ironically, to razz a friend who just had a baby. Straight to archived-unread.

## What's working

- The Beatrix bedroom hero shot (dark green headboard, blush pink bedding) is genuinely well-styled — moody, not twee, could pass for a grown-up guest room.
- "Double Rewards is on! Earn 7X the points" at the very top is a real, specific loyalty number — better than the vague "member benefits" language I usually get.
- The swatch/material close-ups under the hero (fabric textures, hardware finishes) is the kind of detail-oriented content I actually want to see from a furniture brand — Article and CB2 don't always bother with that.
- Multiple clear CTAs ("Shop New," "Shop the Collection," "Shop the Sale") so nobody's hunting for a button.

## What's weak

- I'm not the customer. Cribs, bassinets, nursery wallpaper, changing tables — zero relevance to a guy renovating two adult homes.
- This is the sixth Crate-family send this week per my inbox — Crate & Barrel furniture, Crate & Barrel kitchen (twice), and now Crate & Kids twice. Even Pottery Barn's Labor Day blast didn't feel this repetitive, and that one at least led with one big number.
- The "Up to 60% off The Labor Day Event" section buries the actual percentages in a dense grid of tiny product tiles — I have to squint to find which category gets what discount, versus Pottery Barn's single burgundy "Up to 70% off" banner that I could read from across the room.
- The whole email is a scroll-forever wall of tiles — furniture, nursery, "more new fall arrivals," rugs, lighting, a sweepstakes, a credit card pitch, an app pitch. No hierarchy, just fatigue by the time I hit "TheDesignDesk" card offer at the bottom.
- Nothing here acknowledges I've never bought kids' furniture from them — no "since you shop Crate & Barrel" tie-in, just a blind blast.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment me out of Crate & Kids entirely** — if I've never clicked a nursery item, don't send this sub-brand's list. This is the single biggest lever; no subject line fixes wrong-audience targeting.
- 2. Lead the Labor Day section with one big number like Pottery Barn does ("Up to 60% off") in a hero banner, not scattered across eight small captions.
- 3. Cut the email in half — Beatrix collection + Labor Day sale is plenty; drop the sweepstakes, credit card, and app pitch into a separate send.
- 4. If you must send me Crate & Kids content, reframe it as a gift-buying angle ("shopping for a shower gift?") instead of assuming I'm a parent.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `7X Rewards points on Beatrix, plus our biggest nursery sale of the season`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the collection everyone's asking about — Double Rewards ends soon`
