# The high chair they'll use until middle school (really!) in our exclusive color→
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-20**
## Executive summary

- Another one from Crate & Kids, and no, I still don't have kids. But I'll give this one credit for actually having a hook — the Stokke Tripp Trapp in olive green is a genuinely nice-looking piece of furniture, and the "they'll use until middle school (really!)" angle in the subject is a smart move because it reframes a baby product as a long-term furniture investment, which is exactly the kind of thing that would catch my eye if I actually had a kid to buy for.
- The green palette is moody and editorial, closer to what I'd expect from Crate & Barrel proper than the pastel nursery stuff this list usually throws at me. But it's still Crate & Kids, it's still not relevant to my household, and this is now the sixth-ish kid-focused send I've gotten this month — I flagged the exact same fatigue on 2026-09-20's "$500 Gift Card" emails twice. I'm not opening this on my phone, I'm not forwarding it, and I'm archiving without a click.

## What's working

- The olive green Tripp Trapp hero shot is genuinely well-styled — moody, warm-toned wood tones, looks like something CB2 would shoot, not typical "nursery pastel" Crate & Kids.
- "new & exclusive" framing on the color variant gives a real reason this email exists, unlike the recycled gift-card contest sends.
- The "iconic high chair" section with the kid mid-meal is a decent lifestyle shot that sells the product's actual use case.
- Multiple CTAs ("Shop the Tripp Trapp," "Shop Now," "Shop All Baby Gear") keep the path to purchase short if I were in-market.

## What's weak

- I don't have kids — zero demographic match, so no amount of good styling here overcomes irrelevance, same problem I called out on the last two Crate & Kids sends.
- The email is enormous — Tripp Trapp hero, "even more to love," Halloween teaser, 20% off Stokke banner, "we love these for you" grid, credit card pitch, app pitch — it's basically five separate emails stapled together, and by the time I hit the credit card upsell I've lost the plot on what this email was even about.
- The Halloween "first look" section and the Stokke sale banner both compete with the high chair for hero status — there's no single clear offer, which tanks the visual hierarchy this subject line promised.
- "up to 20% off Stokke bestsellers" is buried two-thirds down after I've already scrolled past a Halloween costume tease — that's the actual offer and it's nowhere near the top.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the email in half.** Lead with the Tripp Trapp + the 20% off Stokke offer together at the top, then stop. Save Halloween, the credit card pitch, and the app download for separate sends — this is the same bloat problem Wayfair had with its "ROCKING CHAIRS" email that buried the actual chair.
- 2. **Put the discount in the subject line.** "up to 20% off Stokke bestsellers" is a real, specific offer — burying it as a mid-scroll banner while the subject leads with vague "really!" copy wastes the strongest lever you have.
- 3. **Segment me out.** I've now gotten enough Crate & Kids sends this month that a "do you have young kids?" preference-center nudge would do more for deliverability than another well-shot green chair.
- 4. **Drop the credit card and app pitches from a product-led email** — they read like desperation once I've already scrolled past four other CTAs.

- **Subject Alt B:** `The high chair that grows with your kid (now in olive)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Exclusive color, plus 20% off Stokke bestsellers through Sunday.`
