# Starts at $29.50: Artisanal vases
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-seven. I'm up to twenty-seven Pottery Barn emails now. The design services send came through earlier this morning, the rugs guide landed before that, and now artisanal vases. The cadence is approaching Wayfair kitchen-mat levels — except Pottery Barn keeps sending things I actually need, which is the infuriating part about being mid-renovation on two houses simultaneously.
- The subject line earns the open. "Starts at $29.50" is a real number that does real work — it tells me immediately that this isn't a $400-minimum browse situation. The email delivers on the promise: the hero image is beautiful, the color story (cream, sand, terracotta, soft stone) is exactly the direction I'm going in the second house, and the opening product grid is clean and scannable. CB2's "New chairs. Instant character." had sharper editorial confidence, but this has specific transactional clarity that CB2 often fumbles.
- Where it loses me is below the fold. I came for vases. The Bloom Shop extension makes editorial sense — flowers and vessels pair naturally. But by the time "More Easy Decor Updates" melts into "PERSONALIZED JUST FOR YOU" bedroom content and then a rattan outdoor sofa I didn't ask about, I've checked out. Article sent an entire email about entryway furniture and never broke focus once. This email is four separate campaigns dressed up as one. I'd click through on the vase section. I would not forward it to anyone.

## What's working

- **"Starts at $29.50" in the subject is the sharpest thing in this email.** Most brands hide the floor price; putting it in the subject line treats me like an adult who can do math. It's what got the open.
- **The hero image is genuinely aspirational in the right register.** Cream, terracotta, natural stone — nothing trend-chasing, nothing that'll age badly. It's styled for someone with a real point of view, not a stock photo dump.
- **Product grid executes cleanly.** Individual named vase cards, visible layout, scannable without effort. I can actually see what I'm being sold.
- **"The Bloom Shop" section is a logical extension.** Vessels lead to florals — the transition earns its place and the imagery is strong.
- **"PERSONALIZED JUST FOR YOU" is a visible trust signal.** Small, but I notice when a brand tries to segment rather than blast.

## What's weak

- **The email is trying to do too many jobs.** Vases, florals, decor updates, personalized picks, new arrivals, outdoor furniture — the $29.50 artisanal hook gets buried under its own scroll depth.
- **"Summer Sale Up to 70% Off" in the hero undercuts the subject's honesty.** The subject set a specific floor; the hero immediately reintroduces the vague ceiling. They're working against each other.
- **No deadline anywhere.** "Summer Sale" means nothing without an end date. Even a vague "this weekend only" would be better. Article ran a zero-discount editorial send and felt more trustworthy than this because it didn't wave a fake clock.
- **The outdoor rattan sofa at the bottom is a total tonal break.** I opened for artisanal vases. That's a different purchase, a different decision timeline, and a different email. It looks like a homepage module accidentally included.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the email to two sections max.** Vases as the hero, Bloom Shop as the natural editorial extension, hard stop. The personalized picks and outdoor furniture are separate emails — send them separately.
- 2. **Add a credible deadline.** "Sale ends Sunday" or even "while quantities last" converts browsing intent into cart action. Right now there's no reason to click today vs. next Tuesday.
- 3. **Put one hero product front and center with real specs.** Pick the $29.50 entry vase, name it, call out its dimensions and material. I'm buying for two specific rooms. "Artisanal" is nice but "14" H × 6" W, matte stoneware" is what gets added to cart.
- 4. **Dial back frequency.** This is the third Pottery Barn email today. Wayfair sent me kitchen mats three times in three days and I muted them mentally for a week. Don't earn that reflex.

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Artisanal Vases" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has two confirmed broken destination links (403), a PHP template rendering bug that leaked server-side `header()` calls into a `<style>` block, 15 HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources across the main content module, and missing one-click unsubscribe headers required for Gmail/Apple Mail compliance. Combined, these issues represent functional breakage and deliverability risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[BROKEN — HIGH]** Credit card banner link returns 403:
```
https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY...
```
The "Apply and Buy" / Key Rewards member banner CTA is dead. Any recipient who clicks the credit card upsell gets a 403.

**[BROKEN — MEDIUM]** West Elm spacer image returns 403 and is wrong-brand:
```
http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif
```
A West Elm CDN asset is embedded in a Pottery Barn email. The 403 suggests the asset was removed from the West Elm CDN. This points to a shared template where a brand-scoped asset reference was not updated.

**[INFO]** 23 tracking/redirect links via `click.e.potterybarn.com` and `clickcp.potterybarn.com` were not probed. With 2 of the probed `clickcp` links already returning 403, additional failures in that subdomain cannot be ruled out.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH — Template Bug]** PHP `header()` calls appear verbatim inside a `<style>` block:
```html
<style>
  ...
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
</style>
```
These are server-side PHP statements that were not executed — they were printed as literal text into the CSS context. Email clients will parse them as invalid CSS and discard them, but the presence of bare code in the stylesheet indicates a template rendering failure. The template or partial responsible for this block should be audited.

**[HIGH]** 15 content images in the primary vases module (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_16.jpg`) are served over `http://`:
```
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2026/US/W26_Decor_B8_VasesRoundUp/slices_01.jpg
... (through slices_16.jpg)
```
Outlook on Windows and security-hardened corporate mail gateways block mixed-content HTTP image loads. The entire product grid may render as broken images for a material portion of recipients. All `edm.potterybarn.com` assets should use `https://` — the domain supports it (other assets in the same email use HTTPS successfully).

Additional HTTP sources with the same risk:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` (also 403)
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true...` (open-tracking pixel)

**[MEDIUM]** 45+ images have no `alt` attribute, including all product grid slices, the hero banner (`F26_TOP_SummerSalePartC_NoUrgency.jpg`), footer slices, and the credit card banner. When images are blocked (images-off rendering, screen readers, corporate proxies), recipients see no fallback text for any of the primary content.

**[LOW]** `<title></title>` is empty. Some email clients surface the title as a tab label or preview label in threaded views.

**[LOW]** `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />` suppresses the HTTP Referer header on click-throughs. This conflicts with UTM tracking — if any landing page relies on referrer-based attribution as a fallback, it will be stripped.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**[HIGH]** `List-Unsubscribe` header was not detected. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to surface the native unsubscribe button. Its absence increases spam complaint rates and risks delivery throttling by these providers. The QA note attributes this to AgentMail relay capture limitations — the header should be confirmed directly against the raw SMTP envelope.

**[HIGH]** `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected. Google and Apple require this for one-click unsubscribe compliance for bulk senders (>5k/day). Without it, Gmail's "Unsubscribe" button either disappears or falls back to a two-step flow, increasing complaint probability.

**[MEDIUM]** `Authentication-Results` (SPF/DKIM) header not captured via relay. Sending domain is `e.potterybarn.com`. DKIM alignment and SPF pass status cannot be confirmed from this source. Should be verified directly from a raw header dump.

**[INFO]** Third-party tracking pixel present: `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/...` — a DSP retargeting pixel. No legal issue per se, but it should be disclosed in PB's privacy policy and confirmed against applicable state privacy laws (CCPA, etc.) if not already.

CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link appear to be present in the footer (visible from the HTML structure), but could not be fully confirmed from the truncated source.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM Params, Landing Page Alignment)

The 23 tracking links all route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` or `clickcp.potterybarn.com` redirect handlers. UTM parameter injection is presumably handled server-side by those redirect handlers rather than baked into the HTML `href` values — this is standard for ESP-managed link tracking.

The `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />` tag (noted above) will strip the HTTP referrer on all click-throughs, which could affect any GA4 or Adobe Analytics configurations that use referrer as a fallback channel signal alongside UTMs.

Subject line ("Starts at $29.50: Artisanal vases") implies a price-anchored landing page. If the hero click-through lands on a general vases collection page rather than a pre-filtered view at or below that price point, that's a continuity gap — but this cannot be confirmed without probing the redirect chain.
