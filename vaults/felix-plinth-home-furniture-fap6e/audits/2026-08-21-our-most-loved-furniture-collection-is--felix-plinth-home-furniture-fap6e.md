# 💗Our most loved furniture collection is…
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-21**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids, again — my inbox has basically adopted a permanent nursery-and-toddler-room subscription. This one's "our #1 furniture collection: meet Jenny Lind," a five-star-rated crib-and-bunk-bed lineup that spans bedroom, nursery, and playroom. My kid isn't a baby anymore, so the crib and bunk bed content is dead air, but I'll admit the spindle-wood aesthetic and the "GREENGUARD Gold Certified" badges are the kind of detail that would actually matter to me if I still had a nursery to furnish — that's real substance, not marketing fluff.
- This is the same brand that sent "NEW markdowns! More at up to 60% off" twice this week and scored a 4 and a 5 from me because it was pure nursery-stroller noise with no price anchor. This send is a step up in craft — it's a proper collection story with named SKUs and star ratings — but there's still zero pricing anywhere until you scroll to "we love these for you," and even then it's tiny product-grid pricing, not a hero offer. Compare that to the Crate & Barrel "bedroom refresh" email that hit 8/10 for me because it led with a real canopy bed at $1,899 (reg. $2,299) right up top — that's the difference between a lookbook and an email that gets me to click.
- Verdict: I'm not clicking, I'm not forwarding. This is the kind of email I skim for two seconds, note "no toddler beds relevant to me," and archive.

## What's working

- The five-star "#1 furniture collection" framing up top gives it a credibility hook before I even see product.
- GREENGUARD Gold Certified callouts on the cribs/dressers are exactly the kind of safety/material detail a parent (even one past the nursery stage) recognizes as legit, not marketing filler.
- The room-by-room structure (bedroom → nursery → playroom) is a clean, scannable organization — easier to parse than CB2's grab-bag "future heirlooms" grid.
- "500+ new arrivals, starting at $39" section at least gives me one concrete price anchor lower in the email.
- The back-to-school swatch-wall graphic is a nice visual break from all the brown wood-toned product shots.

## What's weak

- No price shown anywhere near the hero collection — I have to scroll deep before any dollar figure shows up, unlike the C&B bedroom-refresh send that put $1,899 right under the hero image.
- This is nursery/toddler furniture (cribs, bunk beds, changing tables) — completely irrelevant to my "renovating two houses, buying grown-up furniture" reality, same miss as the last two Crate & Kids sends.
- Four "Shop [Collection]" CTAs stacked down the email dilute the one thing I'd actually click — there's no single unambiguous hero CTA.
- The "it's all on the app... chance at $500" teaser at the bottom feels bolted-on and unrelated to the Jenny Lind story above it.
- Emoji in the subject (💗) reads a little juvenile for what's otherwise a fairly premium-looking collection page.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real price under the hero bed image immediately — "Jenny Lind Deep Brown Bunk Bed, $649" type specificity, the way C&B's bedroom email did with the canopy bed. That single change would do the most to make this feel like an offer instead of a catalog page.
- 2. Collapse the four "Shop X" CTAs into one primary "Shop the Jenny Lind Collection" button plus secondary text links for room-by-room — right now nothing reads as the primary action.
- 3. Segment this — if I don't have a nursery-age kid on file, swap this whole send for the bedroom/playroom sections only, or don't send it at all. Two 4-5/10 nursery emails this week plus this one is starting to feel like list fatigue, not targeting.
- 4. Move the $39-starting-price "new arrivals" section up near the top — that's the most actionable, lowest-friction entry point in the whole email and it's buried two-thirds down.
