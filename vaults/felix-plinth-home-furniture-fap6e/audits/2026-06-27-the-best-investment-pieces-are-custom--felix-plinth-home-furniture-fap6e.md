# The best investment pieces are custom
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- Forty-three. That's roughly where I am with the CB2/Crate family this month, and this one drops June 27 — two days after I already got the July 4th Sale, the Floor Sample Sale, "It's back. And now it's on sale," and "Everything in stock now." So when "The best investment pieces are custom" lands with no price in the subject, no urgency, no percentage — I'm reading it as CB2 trying to reset the tone and sell me something aspirational instead of panicking me with a deadline. I respect the attempt.
- The hero earns its place. That warm, tonal living room with the oversized sectional is doing real work — it's the kind of room I'm mentally staging for the second home right now, and the custom angle is genuinely relevant when I'm trying to spec furniture that isn't going to show up in every Wayfair Open Box email. But then the email pivots mid-scroll into Warehouse Sale territory, and suddenly I'm confused about what CB2 actually wants me to do today. Is this a brand moment or a clearance push? It can't fully commit to either.
- I'd save this to the folder I keep for inspiration — same way I treat the Article Cillian Collection send from June 19, which also led with room aspiration before the offer. But I would not click to cart today. There's no deadline, no specific configuration to start, and I already have a Warehouse Sale tab open from the June 25 send.

## What's working

- **Custom as the lead message** is a smart break from the sale drumbeat — it's the first non-discount subject I've seen from CB2 this sprint, and it snaps me out of inbox fatigue.
- **Warm, editorial hero image** — the tonal room with earth tones and an oversized sofa reads like a CB2 lookbook, not a clearance flyer; it earned a pause on scroll.
- **Product grid execution** is clean and dense without feeling cluttered — I can see enough to know whether a piece is worth a click.
- **Dual-message structure (custom + warehouse)** actually mirrors how I shop: I'd start with a custom sofa and then sweep the sale for accent pieces in the same pass.

## What's weak

- **Two campaigns, one email** — custom investment pieces and a Warehouse Sale are arguing with each other. The subject primes one mindset; the lower scroll delivers another. Compare to Article's June 27 "4th of July Sale starts now," which was a single clean message start to finish.
- **No visible deadline or configuration hook** — for a custom piece, the natural CTA is "start your configuration" with a lead-time signal. I see none of that. Without a "ships by X" anchor, custom furniture is easy to defer indefinitely.
- **Cadence is still a problem** — even with a different subject tone, this is email five in roughly 48 hours from the CB2/Crate family. That wears on you regardless of creative quality.
- **Warehouse Sale section at the bottom feels tacked on** — I already have that tab open. Pottery Barn ran their "Extra 20% off select styles" separately from their main editorial sends; CB2 should consider the same split.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit fully to the custom angle** — strip the Warehouse Sale block out of this email entirely and send it as a standalone. Let this be a pure brand/aspiration send; it earns more respect that way and preserves the Warehouse Sale for its own send (or a follow-up).
- 2. **Add a lead-time urgency hook** — something like "Order by July 10 for August delivery" turns "investment piece" into a real deadline. That's the click I need.
- 3. **Surface the configuration entry point** — a single "Start your custom sofa" CTA with a visible starting price does more work than a generic shop button.
- 4. **Drop email cadence to every 3 days** — five emails in 48 hours is killing the goodwill the custom creative is trying to build.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Custom sofas: August delivery if you order this week`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your next sofa, your exact specs — now in the sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Lead times are shorter than you think. Start configuring.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Up to 50% off custom. Built to order, priced for right now.`

**Font URL double-slash (minor):** Three `@font-face` `src` declarations contain double slashes in the path, which most servers normalize but is technically malformed:
```
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2")

```

**Cross-domain font load:** The `HNW05` typeface is loaded from `crateandbarrel.com`, not `cb2.com`. If that domain's CORS policy or CDN changes, the font silently falls back. Not a blocker but a fragile dependency.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Responsive coverage gap (confirmed bug):** The CSS defines two breakpoints with no overlap:
- Mobile: `@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)`
- Desktop images: `@media only screen and (min-width: 690px)`

Viewports between **419px–689px** receive neither mobile layout fixes nor desktop image constraints. This range covers common Android phones (Samsung Galaxy S series: 412px logical width), tablets in portrait, and browser zoom states. Elements will render at their default widths with no responsive adjustment.

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta (accessibility):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
This blocks pinch-to-zoom for low-vision users. WCAG 1.4.4 (Level AA) requires content to be resizable up to 200% without loss of functionality.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `*` selector:**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```
Applied globally, this disables OS-level font scaling on iOS. Combined with `maximum-scale=1`, text is effectively locked in size for users who rely on system accessibility settings.

**`line-height: 100%` global reset:**
```css
* { line-height: 100%; }
```
A line-height of 1.0 is tighter than WCAG's recommended minimum of 1.5 for body text (1.4.8). This override applies to all elements including paragraph copy.
