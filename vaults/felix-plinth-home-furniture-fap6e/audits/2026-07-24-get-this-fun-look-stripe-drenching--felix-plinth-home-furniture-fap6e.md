# Get this fun look: stripe drenching!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- Thirteen. That's the C&B family tally in my inbox across roughly 36 hours, and "stripe drenching" is the one that finally made me say it out loud. After the duplicate backpack sends, the milestone bedding, and the sustainability pivot, here comes an editorial trend concept aimed squarely at… kids' rooms. I'm renovating two adult spaces simultaneously. Nobody signed me up for the Crate & Kids list, and yet here I am.

Stripe drenching — layering stripe-on-stripe across bedding, walls, and accents — is a real trend, and the photography sells it. The hero image stopped my thumb for a second. But there's no offer, no pricing, no urgency. It's a pure editorial send, which requires more trust and more relevance than this brand has bandwidth for right now given the volume.
- I won't click. I definitely won't forward this — to do so I'd need to know someone actively shopping for a kids' room, and the friend I'd forward it to has already seen the same Pottery Barn Kids content with better photography and a cleaner CTA. Mark as read, move on.

- **The editorial concept has real legs.** "Stripe drenching" is specific and ownable — it's the kind of trend hook that actually belongs in a subject line, unlike "bestsellers for every milestone" from two sends ago.

## What's weak

- **Wrong list. Wrong brand.** I'm a 36-year-old guy renovating two adult homes. Crate & Kids has nothing for me right now unless I'm shopping for a guest room with a very whimsical brief. The segmentation engine should know this.
- **No offer, no reason to click today.** Article sent me a room-specific editorial email two sends ago with a direct link into product pages I was already considering. This sends me to a trend concept with no pricing or promotion attached.
- **Thirteen emails in 36 hours.** I said it in the overview and I'll say it here too: the cadence is the product defect. Even a genuinely good creative concept gets buried under that pile.
- **"Stripe drenching" works as a hook, not as a category nav.** Once I'm in the email, the CTA hierarchy doesn't tell me *where* to start. CB2's "AVAILABLE AGAIN" send — which I also audited this week — at least pointed me at a single restock with a direct link. This email gestures at a vibe.
- **No pricing visible anywhere in the scroll.** Not even a "starting at" anchor. Pottery Barn's design services email had pricing context in the first screen. This has none.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Unfilled template placeholder left in production**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
<!---->
```
This comment block (note the typo: `IMPUT` vs `INPUT`) is a developer instruction that was never removed or replaced. It signals a template hygiene gap; if a font import was intended, it was silently dropped.

**[MEDIUM] Media query ordering conflict — 768px rule is dead code**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
At any viewport ≥ 768px, both queries fire at equal specificity. The 640px rule appears last in source and therefore wins, making the 768px rule (`max-width: 600px`) unreachable. If the 600px desktop cap was intentional, the rule order must be reversed.

**[MEDIUM] `maximum-scale=1` blocks zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Common in email templates but worth flagging for accessibility compliance.

**[MEDIUM] Global `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applied to all elements via the `*` selector. This suppresses iOS auto-sizing across the entire email, which can make small text unreadable without zoom (compounded by the `maximum-scale=1` above).

**[LOW] Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
One combined and three individual `format-detection` meta tags appear in `<head>`. Redundant but harmless.

**[LOW] `<title>` brand mismatch**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
Sender is `crateandkids@mail.crateandbarrel.com` / "Crate & Kids". The `<title>` references the parent brand. Irrelevant to most clients but surfaces in browser-based preview and some accessibility trees.

**[LOW] Dark mode explicitly blocked**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Locks rendering to light mode. Expected if no dark mode styles exist, but a notable rendering gap for ~82% of Apple Mail users who have dark mode enabled.

**[INFO] Developer comments in production**
`<!--V2-->` and multiple `<!----- MI styling start/end --->` / `<!----- CLIENT styling start --->` comments are visible in the delivered HTML. Low risk but reveals template infrastructure to anyone inspecting source.
