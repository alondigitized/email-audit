# FLOOR SAMPLE SALE

## Executive summary

- Floor sample sales are one of the few reasons I actually want to open a furniture brand email. I'm actively furnishing two houses and CB2 floor samples can hit 40–70% off on pieces that are already on my shortlist — so the subject alone put me on high alert. That goodwill lasted about two seconds once I opened it.

By the third red section I've forgotten which sale I actually came in for. Then there's the app download pitch, the 15% rewards reminder, the "let's celebrate" gifting module, and a second 15% off block for email signups. That's six distinct asks in one scroll. CB2 sent me a standalone July 4th email earlier this same day — which means I've now seen "up to 50% off" twice before noon on June 25th. The stacking is a loyalty tax on paying attention.
- I'd open this again — the floor sample angle is real and relevant. I probably won't click the floor sample CTA today because there's no store near me that I know has specific inventory, and nothing in this email tells me what's actually on the floor. Mark as read; come back if I happen to be near a CB2.

## What's working

- The floor sample hero is clean — minimal, white sofa, serif headline. Exactly the CB2 aesthetic I subscribed for.
- "In Stores: Find a CB2 Near You" is honest about the in-person requirement; no bait-and-switch into a dead landing page.
- The "MORE FOR YOU" personalized product block actually shows items in my price range with what look like markdown prices ($225.10, $710.10 — the odd cents signal a real discount, not a round-number guess).
- "EARN 15% BACK" is visible — I'm a member and that math always makes me compute the effective price on anything I'm already considering.

## What's weak

- Three stacked promo banners (Floor Sample → July 4th → Warehouse) kill the hierarchy. I'm doing decision work before I've even looked at a product.
- The floor sample hero CTA is "FIND A STORE," which tells me nothing about what's actually on the floor at a store near me. If there's no inventory locator, this button is nearly useless.
- I got a separate July 4th email from CB2 earlier on the same calendar day. Seeing "THE JULY 4TH SALE UP TO 50%OFF!" again in this email feels like an ad repetition tax.
- The asterisked fine print on the percentage-off claims is visible and small — "up to" doing a lot of work in two different banners back-to-back makes both numbers feel softer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **One email, one job.** The floor sample sale is genuinely differentiated — in-store exclusivity, one-of-a-kind pieces, real markdowns. It deserves its own send. Fold July 4th/warehouse into a different email or a quieter footer module.
- 2. **Show me what's on the floor.** Replace the generic sofa hero with 3–4 actual floor sample pieces available at my nearest store (use geolocation or segmentation by zip). "The Tanner Leather Sofa in your area — now $899" will get me in a car. "Find a Store" won't.
- 3. **Add urgency to the floor sample section.** A soft deadline ("Quantities are extremely limited — ends when sold") would push me to act today instead of "coming back to this later" (which means never).
- 4. **Collapse the app/rewards/celebrate/15%-off stack.** Four consecutive module rows below the fold destroy pace. Pick one secondary ask per send.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HIGH — `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). This value should be removed.

**MEDIUM — Global `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` on `*` selector**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Overrides text scaling across all elements, not just layout containers. This is more aggressive than the `body`-scoped version declared earlier and can harm readability on small screens.

**MEDIUM — `line-height: 100%` on `*` selector**
```css
*{ ... line-height: 100%}
```
Applied globally on `*` this can clip descenders and cause text overlap in table cells, particularly in Outlook and older Samsung clients. Should be scoped to layout-only elements.

**LOW — Mobile breakpoint/class width mismatch**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) {
  .mobile { width:414px !important; ... }
}
```
Breakpoint fires at ≤418px but sets content width to 414px, leaving a 4px gap. On exactly 415–418px viewports the layout will have unintended right padding.

**LOW — `font-display: swap` in `@font-face` declarations**
```css
font-display: swap;
```
`font-display` is a browser CSS property with no support in any email client. It is silently ignored — not harmful, but dead code that signals a web-stylesheet was copy-pasted directly.

**LOW — Double slashes in font asset URLs**
```css
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
```
Three `@font-face` declarations use `//` path separators (`https://www.cb2.com//Styles/...`). Most CDN configurations normalize these, but it is a malformed URL and should be corrected.
