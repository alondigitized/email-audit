# New to sale & UP TO 70% OFF + A new sofa made for lived-in lounging
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- Sixteen. I went back and counted because it felt like a lot and yes — sixteen Pottery Barn emails since July 1st. So I opened this one already running a slight fatigue tax on whatever it had to say. Then the subject line did something interesting: "a new sofa made for lived-in lounging." I'm mid-renovation on a secondary home where the actual brief is exactly that — something that survives real use, not a showroom setting — and that phrase stopped me.
- The problem is the email doesn't know what it is. There's a new product launch (the Bellevue Collection) and a sale event both competing for the hero, and they fight each other the entire scroll. The Bellevue sofa gets the top of the email and deserves it — neutral linen-adjacent fabric, wide proportions, genuinely inviting room-in-context shot — but before I can actually consider it, I'm already looking at a "More Bestselling Collections" grid, a "Recommended Just For You" block, a "Brand New Arrivals" section, and a footer that's practically an app store listing. The sofa never closes.
- Compare this to what Article did after four July 4th sale emails — they pivoted hard to new arrivals and let the product breathe across the entire email. That email scored an 8 for me. The Bellevue Collection deserves its own dedicated send; instead it got attached to a sale announcement like a plus-one who showed up to the wrong party. I'd screenshot the hero, save it, and not click today.

## What's working

- "Lived-in lounging" is exactly the right language for someone furnishing a house that will actually get used — it reads like a positioning choice, not filler copy
- The Bellevue hero image is wide, warm, and in-context — you get a sense of scale and how it layers with the rest of a room
- The neutral warm palette holds together all the way through; no jarring color shifts between sections
- Clean render — no broken images, no layout breaks, type hierarchy is readable at a thumb's length
- "New to sale" as a lead is smarter than just screaming 70% off again — it implies something fresh entered the pipeline, which is a meaningful signal after two weeks of the same sale drumbeat

## What's weak

- The subject line is two separate emails stapled together with a plus sign; a new collection launch and a sitewide sale compete for attention and the sofa loses
- Email sixteen in eleven days — Article pivoted intentionally after a run, CB2 drops a Todd Snyder collab as a distinct event; this just reads like Pottery Barn's send scheduler never paused
- The Bellevue Collection gets no product detail that would actually move me forward: no seat depth, no cushion fill spec, no link to swatches — "lived-in lounging" is a feeling, not a buying decision
- The dark interstitial section mid-email breaks the warm editorial flow like a banner ad got dropped into a catalog
- Four distinct content sections below the hero — the email doesn't trust the sofa to carry the story, so it piles on until the launch gets buried

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split the email.** The Bellevue Collection launch is a real event — give it a standalone send with one hero, one story, one CTA. Keep the sale cadence on its own track.
- 2. **Add one functional spec to the hero block.** Seat depth, fill type, or a "request swatches" CTA — something that moves a furniture buyer closer to a decision rather than just inspiring them to pin it.
- 3. **Cut the "Recommended Just For You" section or collapse it to the footer.** It undercuts an editorial launch email with algorithmic product soup.
- 4. **Pull the dark interstitial.** It's visually jarring and I'd scroll past it on instinct.
- 5. **Cadence audit.** Yesterday's "FREE design help" email scored an 8 because it wasn't a sale. That contrast is the point — after a long sale run, a product story email works. But only if it's allowed to be a product story.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CRITICAL] PHP `header()` calls inside `<style>` block**
The final `<style>` tag in `<head>` contains raw PHP function calls:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are server-side PHP statements that were not executed — they leaked as literal text into the rendered CSS. Email clients will parse this as invalid CSS and may reject or mangle the entire `<style>` block, breaking responsive layout rules that follow.

**[FAIL] 34 images missing `alt` text**
Every content image in the email has no `alt` attribute or an empty one. This includes hero banners (`F26_TOP_SummerSalePartC_NoUrgency.jpg`), all 18 Bellevue collection slices, all 18 footer slices, product images, and tracking pixels. Images-off rendering (common in Outlook, Apple Mail default, corporate proxies) produces a completely blank email.

**[WARN] 3 image sources served over HTTP (not HTTPS)**
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` — cross-brand, also 403
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` — footer credit card banner
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` — dated 2022 asset path

Gmail, Apple Mail (iOS 16+), and most modern clients block mixed-content images. These will render as broken.

**[WARN] Open tracking pixel over HTTP**
`http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
Open tracking served over HTTP. Will be blocked by clients that enforce HTTPS for remote content, silently undercounting opens.

**[WARN] 18-slice image layout for Bellevue collection**
The hero section is assembled from 18 individual `slices_01.jpg`–`slices_18.jpg` GIF slices. A single failed image request or a 1px table gap renders the entire composition as broken strips. No fallback text.

**[WARN] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
`<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` — prevents user zoom on iOS, an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4 Resize Text).

**[INFO] Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` — not critical but surfaces as blank in some clients' tab/preview UI.
