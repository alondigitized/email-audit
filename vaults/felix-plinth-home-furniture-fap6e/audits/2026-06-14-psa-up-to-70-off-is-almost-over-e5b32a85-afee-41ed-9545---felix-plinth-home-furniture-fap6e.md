# 📣 PSA: Up to 70% OFF is almost OVER 📣
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- Fifty-two. We crossed that threshold with the June 14 beds email that actually earned an 8 from me — the subject hooked me because I genuinely needed beds. This one does not have that same leverage. "PSA: Up to 70% OFF is almost OVER" — that is exactly what Wayfair sent on June 13 with the swimming pools email, which itself was the second email of that same day. The 70% off framing is now practically their default banner setting.
- What I see when I open this is a wall of blue-and-orange sale blocks stacked vertically — ENDS TOMORROW at the top, then 50% off, then 50% off, then a "Picked just for you" grid, then 24-hour flash deals with more 50% and 40% badges, then a shed, then more of the same. The layout reads less like a curated send and more like someone dragged seven sub-templates into a single column and called it a day. There's no breathing room, no editorial point of view, no reason to stop at any one thing.
- Would I click? I'd probably scroll the "Picked just for you" row to see if the algorithm got lucky with something actually on my list, then close it. Would I forward to a friend? No. This is the Wayfair equivalent of a stack of flyers left on a windshield.

## What's working

- The **"ENDS TOMORROW" urgency lede** is at least positioned above the fold — I see it without scrolling, and paired with 70% off it's a legible first impression.
- The **"Picked just for you" section** is the right instinct — personalization blocks are how Pottery Barn's Summer Sale email (which I scored a 10) earns clicks from me even when I'm fatigued.
- **Delivery callout ("free & easy delivery over $35")** near the bottom is a trust signal I actually care about when buying large items across two renovation projects; it's there, which is better than not.
- The **Wayfair brand** is still recognized and trusted at the base level — I haven't unsubscribed yet, which says something.

## What's weak

- **"Ends tomorrow" is not credible anymore.** Wayfair used near-identical deadline language on June 13 (swimming pools), and I'm confident June 15 will open with another sale. The urgency is structurally broken.
- **Visual hierarchy is a mess.** At least six distinct discount blocks are visible in one scroll — 70%, 50%, 50%, 50%, 40%, 40% — none prioritized, all competing. CB2's "100+ new items added to Warehouse Sale" email, which I scored an 8, at least had a clear editorial hero before going into the grid.
- **The "Picked just for you" section is too small.** That's the only thing that might be personalized and it's sandwiched between generic sale blocks where I have to squint to see what's actually in it.
- **The megaphone emoji bookends in the subject** feel like they belong in a 2019 DTC brand's Instagram story, not a home goods retailer whose customer is knee-deep in two renovation budgets. Article has never done this to me and their click-to-purchase rate from me is probably better for it.
- **No category anchor.** I'm renovating two houses simultaneously — beds, lighting, rugs, outdoor. Nothing in this email tells me where to look first based on what I've browsed or ordered. The June 13 outdoor rugs email at least committed to a category.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the emoji bookends in the subject and commit to one category.** At email fifty-plus, the only subject lines that still open for me are the ones that reference something specific I actually need. The June 14 beds email proved this. Pick one product type this round and own it.
- 2. **Make "Picked just for you" the hero, not a mid-scroll afterthought.** If the algorithm knows what I've looked at, lead with it. Article does this implicitly through lifestyle curation; Pottery Barn does it explicitly in sale emails. Wayfair is sitting on behavioral data and burying it.
- 3. **Collapse the discount block count from 6+ to 2.** One top-level offer (70% off Summer Sale), one time-specific hook (24-hr flash). The current structure makes every offer feel equally unimportant.
- 4. **Retire "ends tomorrow" for at least two sends.** Or mean it. A credible deadline requires follow-through — if the sale quietly extends on June 15, you've burned the urgency lever for the season.
- 5. **Add a product-specific anchor above the fold.** Not a category — an actual product, ideally one I've viewed. CB2's warehouse-sale email linked specific SKUs. That's what pulls the trigger.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Fixed pixel heights on mobile content containers will clip text:**

```css
.ProductRec-name { height: 48px !important }         /* + overflow: hidden */
.ProductRec-stars, .ProductRec-shippingCopy { height: 16px }
.ProductRec-price { height: 40px; vertical-align: top }
.ProductRec-salesCopy { height: 28px; vertical-align: top }
```

Combined with `overflow: hidden` on `.ProductRec-name`, any user with OS-level large-text accessibility settings or a system font larger than the design baseline will have product names, prices, and shipping copy silently clipped. These heights appear to be fixed to align a product grid; consider `min-height` instead.

**Issue — `word-break: break-all` on product names:**

```css
.ProductRec-name { word-break: break-all; overflow: hidden; display: block; height: 48px !important }
```

`break-all` splits at any character boundary, mid-word. Prefer `overflow-wrap: break-word` (`word-wrap: break-word` for legacy clients) which only breaks when a word cannot fit the line.

**Issue — Custom font is WOFF-only, no WOFF2:**

```css
@font-face {
  font-family: "Sofia";
  src: url(https://secure.img.wfrcdn.com/st4/stores/common/fonts/sofia/sofia_reg.woff) format("woff");
  mso-font-alt: "Arial"
}
```

No `format("woff2")` source listed. WOFF2 is ~30% smaller and supported by all modern email clients that render web fonts (Apple Mail, Samsung Mail). The `mso-font-alt: "Arial"` fallback is correct for Outlook. Low severity — most clients ignore `@font-face` entirely — but worth adding a WOFF2 source for clients that do honor it.

**Issue — Duplicate `@media` blocks for `.u-Button` and `.u-Button--primary`:**

Both classes receive identical padding declarations (`padding: 16px 0 !important` on the td, `padding: 0 32px !important` on the link) in separate media query blocks. This is redundant CSS weight but causes no rendering defect.
