## What's working

- The hero chair is editorial-quality — rounded, architecturally interesting, shot against a moody-plant context that makes me want to recreate it, which is exactly how C&B earns its place in my Pinterest-adjacent folder.
- "Guess what? They swivel" is the first subject line this cycle with actual personality; it breaks the volume monotony in a way yesterday's coffee machine email absolutely did not.
- The email stays in its lane — no kitchen tools, no summer travel, just chairs front-to-bottom. After "Everything you need for summer travel →" landing in my inbox from this same address four days ago, the category discipline here registers as a win.
- Multiple silhouettes (barrel, striped accent, sherpa, dining) in one scroll makes this genuinely useful as a reference document, not just a push to one SKU.
- Clean render on mobile — no broken images, no layout gaps, nothing I have to zoom around.

## What's weak

- No price anywhere visible at the hero level. I have to click before I know if I'm in range, and compared to Article's editorial approach (which still anchors pricing in-email) and CB2's explicit percentage-off framing, that's a friction point that costs C&B the impulse.
- The cadence damage is real. One stellar email after four scattered ones doesn't fully reset the relationship — my brain is still primed to dismiss, even when the content is relevant.
- "They swivel" is the whole feature sell, but the email doesn't demonstrate it visually. I need to feel the swivel — a subtle motion GIF or even an overhead angle showing the 360 range would turn a subject line claim into a product reason-to-buy.
- No loyalty hook. If I have Insider status or there's early-access pricing for members, that's the single sentence that converts a save-for-later into an add-to-cart, and it's invisible here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the hero price.** "From $699" below the lead chair removes the biggest friction point. I'm mentally comparing these against Pottery Barn's sale section in the same breath — give me a number so I can make the comparison without clicking away.
- 2. **Demonstrate the swivel.** A looping GIF — even a 30° rotation — earns the subject line promise. Right now "they swivel" is copy. Make it a feature.
- 3. **Surface a loyalty message.** One line: "C&B Insider? You're seeing this first." It costs nothing to write and changes the psychology from "new arrivals" to "early access."
- 4. **Hold the scattered sends.** The coffee machine email from June 9 scored a 6 and did nothing but dilute the list's open appetite. If that had been held and this chairs send had landed clean as the day's single send, the context would've been sharper for both.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom (confirmed):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Locking `maximum-scale` to 1 prevents users from pinching to zoom on mobile. This fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is flagged by major ESP validators. Remove `maximum-scale=1` or replace with `maximum-scale=5`.

**Issue — Conflicting `img max-width` media queries (confirmed):**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
Both queries fire at ≥768px. Because the 640px rule is declared last, it wins via CSS cascade at all widths ≥640px — the 600px rule is effectively dead code. Intended cap is ambiguous; if 600px is the container width, the 640px rule should be removed or reordered.

**Issue — Dark mode not supported (confirmed):**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Explicitly opting out of dark mode. In Apple Mail and iOS Mail (large audience for retail), this forces light rendering but does not prevent OS-level color inversion from producing unreadable results. Acceptable if a deliberate brand choice, but should be flagged as a known rendering risk.

**Issue — Duplicate `format-detection` declarations (confirmed):**
`telephone=no` appears in the combined tag and again as a standalone meta — minor but indicates copy-paste hygiene issues in the template.
