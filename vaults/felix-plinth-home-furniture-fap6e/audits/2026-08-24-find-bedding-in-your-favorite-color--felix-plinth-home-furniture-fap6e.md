# Find bedding in your favorite color
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-24**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "Layers of Color" — sixth Pottery Barn email in my inbox this week and this one's actually doing something coherent: stacked duvets in four colorways up top, then a color-sort grid (blues, pinks, greens, neutrals) below. That's the same premise as yesterday's "Make your bed a little more colorful" send, just with a cleaner hero — a real stacked-linens photo instead of a flat color-grid graphic. "Up to 15% off Belgian Flax Linen Bedding" is a legible, specific offer sitting right under the hero, which is more than most of this week's sends managed.
- Where it falls apart for me is everything after the color grid — it just keeps going. Bedding essentials nav, then "Recommended Just For You" with a curtain rod, a dining table, and a platform bed that have nothing to do with bedding colors, then a Design Crew sweepstakes, then a full Halloween Shop pitch, then app/rewards/Affirm blocks. This is the fourth time this month PB has tacked a Halloween decor pitch onto a completely unrelated email — Article at least keeps its emails single-premise (bed launch, room story) instead of turning every send into a full catalog dump.
- I'd click "shop blues" or "shop neutrals" since I'm actually pricing bedding for the second-home guest room right now, but I'm not scrolling past the sweepstakes and Halloween shop to get to Affirm and rewards. Not forwarding this — it's a legible email, not a memorable one.

## What's working

- The hero photo — four stacked duvets in different colorways — is a real product shot, not a graphic, and it's the strongest visual PB's sent me this week.
- "Up to 15% off Belgian Flax Linen Bedding" is a concrete, specific offer placed right where my eye lands after the hero.
- The color-sort grid (blues, pinks, greens, neutrals) is a genuinely useful nav pattern for someone shopping by room palette, which is literally what I'm doing for the second home.
- "It all ships free!" next to the section header answers the shipping question before I have to dig for it — Wayfair usually buries that in a banner I have to scroll to find.

## What's weak

- "Recommended Just For You" surfaces a curtain rod, a dining table, and a platform bed — none of that is "bedding in my favorite color," it's just generic cross-sell dressed up as personalization.
- The Halloween Shop block bolted onto a color-of-bedding email is the fourth time this month PB has done this to me; Article never mixes seasonal decor into a product-focused send like this.
- Email is enormous — by the time I hit rewards/Affirm/app blocks I've forgotten what the subject line promised.
- No swatch or sample-order CTA anywhere, which is the one thing that would actually move me to buy bedding sight-unseen instead of just browsing.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email at the "Shop Bedding By Color" button — everything after that (essentials nav is fine to keep, but drop Recommended, Design Crew, Halloween, app/rewards) is diluting a genuinely good, focused premise.
- 2. Add a "order a swatch" CTA near the color grid — I'm not committing to a duvet color off a product photo, and PB has swatch programs for exactly this.
- 3. Make "Up to 15% off" a real button, not just a caption line under the hero image — right now the CTA hierarchy makes "SHOP QUILTS" the only clickable-looking element up top.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
Standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (ExactTarget-style) HTML email with tracking pixels and click-redirects; no rendering-breaking code errors found, but several compliance headers are missing and image alt attributes are absent throughout.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All CTAs route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirect wrappers (e.g. `https://click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=ABB7InYiOjEs...`) — standard SFMC click-tracking, not independently verifiable without following redirects (65 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe, so destination validity is unconfirmed).
- Open-tracking pixel uses plaintext HTTP: `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` — the recipient email address is passed in cleartext in a GET parameter over an unencrypted connection.
- Secondary pixel `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` and `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` also load over HTTP, as does `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`. Mixed-content HTTP images risk being blocked by mail clients enforcing HTTPS-only image loading.
- Additional third-party pixel present: `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/L21rdC84NjEvY2lkLzE3NDc3ODk3MzMvdC8y/cat/1` (Amobee/Turn data-tracking pixel) — confirms this send is instrumented for third-party ad-audience tracking, not just internal analytics.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 46 `<img>` elements are missing `alt` text (all `slices_*.jpg` grid/spacer images, product photography, banners, and both tracking pixels). This degrades the experience for image-blocked and screen-reader clients — recipients get no fallback text for any content image.
- Preheader text is correctly hidden via `display:none !important; mso-hide:all` with zero-width-space padding — implemented correctly, no issue.
- MSO/Outlook conditional comments (`<!--[if mso 16]>`, `<!--[if gte mso 9]>`) and `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` are present — standard defensive markup, no issue.
- Stray `header()` PHP-style calls (`header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); ...`) are embedded inside a `<style>` block. These are inert as CSS (rendered as invalid property declarations, ignored by parsers) but indicate a template artifact/leftover from a PHP asset script that was pasted into the wrong context — worth flagging to the ESP/template team even though it has no functional rendering impact.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tags (e.g. `%%member%%`, `{{first_name}}`) found in the truncated source — personalization appears to have resolved correctly.
- Recipient identifier is embedded in tracking/pixel URLs as a literal resolved email (`lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`), confirming merge fields rendered correctly for this send, but this also means the email address is exposed in plaintext GET parameters over HTTP (see §2/§6 security note).

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header: not found.** No RFC 2369 `List-Unsubscribe` header was captured — cannot confirm one-click/mailbox-level unsubscribe support.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header: not found.** RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender rules) cannot be confirmed as supported.

3. Switch the `sv_emopen` open-tracking pixel from HTTP to HTTPS — it currently transmits the recipient's email address in cleartext.
4. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are actually present in the raw SMTP send (this may be a relay-capture artifact rather than a true gap) — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is required by major mailbox providers' bulk sender policies.
5. Verify `Authentication-Results` (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) against the original transaction rather than the relayed copy.
6. Remove the orphaned `header('Content-Type: ...')`/`header('Cache-Control: ...')` PHP snippet embedded in the `<style>` block — harmless today but suggests a broken template-include and should be cleaned from the source.
