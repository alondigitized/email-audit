# Early on our app: Venus Williams for Crate & Barrel!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-14**
## Executive summary

- Third time this week Crate & Barrel has put Venus Williams in front of me, and this is the same phone-mockup hero I already flagged on the other two sends — just with "Early on our app" swapped in as the subject instead of "Shop it first." At this point I could draw that racket-and-tennis-dress shot from memory. The thing is, past the gate this is actually one of the better-organized emails I've gotten from this brand — clean "we love these for you" grid, real prices, a Halloween seasonal banner that doesn't scream discount. But I'm not downloading an app to look at a dress collab, and burying the recommended-for-you furniture below an app wall means I have to scroll past a pitch I've already rejected twice this week to get to the part I'd actually shop.
- Not clicking, not forwarding. I'd mark this read and move on — same as the last two.

## What's working

- The "we love these for you" product grid is legit useful — real SKUs (Barrett sofa, Keane dresser, Basque dining table) with prices visible, no vague "shop now" filler.
- Halloween section ("so scary, it's chic") is genuinely well styled — moody porch shot, pumpkins and lanterns, no "SALE" banner slapped over it. Reminds me of the wreath-wall hero I liked from C&B yesterday.
- The Crate Card 10% Reward Dollars callout is relevant to me — I'm already running purchases through a rewards card, so a branded card with cash-back on "design forward brands" at least speaks my language, even if I'm not biting today.
- Layout is clean, no overlapping text or broken images — a step up from Wayfair's ceiling fan bait-and-switch I flagged this week.

## What's weak

- Same app-gate hero for the third straight send — I already called this out on yesterday's "Shop it first" email, and seeing it a third time with just a retitled subject line is starting to feel lazy, not iterative.
- The Venus Williams collab itself is invisible past the hero — no product shots of what she actually designed, no price, nothing. It's a teaser for an app download, not a product email.
- TheDesignDesk "book a free appointment" and the second app pitch ($500 chance) feel like two more asks stacked on top of an email that already asked me to download something once.
- I don't have the Crate & Barrel app and nothing here gives me a reason to get it over just browsing their site — CB2's "Grand entrances" email this week linked straight to shoppable console tables, no app friction at all.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the app-only gate for the Venus Williams collab — show me actual product shots and prices in the email itself. If it's genuinely app-exclusive, say that in one line and move straight to shoppable furniture, don't make it the entire hero for the third time.
- 2. Lead with the "we love these for you" grid instead of burying it under two promo blocks — that's the section actually doing work.
- 3. Cut it down to one app ask, not two ($500 sweepstakes + collab gate) — stacking asks reads as "your inbox metrics matter to us," not "we know what you want."
- 4. If this cadence is intentional (three Venus Williams sends this week), vary the creative at least — different crop, different copy angle — so it doesn't read as a resend.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop the fall porch refresh — plus new arrivals we picked for your style`
