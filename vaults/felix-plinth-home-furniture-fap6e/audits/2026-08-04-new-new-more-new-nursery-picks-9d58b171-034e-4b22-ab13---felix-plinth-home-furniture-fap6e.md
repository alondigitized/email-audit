# New, new & more new nursery picks →
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-04**
## Executive summary

- Nope, not me. Crate & Kids sending me nursery furniture is a straight-up data problem — I don't have a baby, I have two houses mid-renovation and a growing pile of Pottery Barn, Crate & Barrel, and Wayfair emails all fighting for the same lamp real estate in my inbox. This is Crate & Kids's third distinct pitch to me this week after the school-gear 20%-off send and the two Levain Bakery cookie emails, and each one is aimed at a life stage I'm not in — first backpacks, now cribs and gliders.
- That said, credit where due: the dressers in the "new, timeless dressers" section are genuinely nice-looking pieces, and if I squint I can see myself wanting a scaled-up adult version of that natural oak dresser for the primary bedroom. But that's me doing the brand's targeting work for them.
- Marking as read, not clicking, definitely not forwarding — unless it's to razz a friend who actually just had a kid, in which case sure, I'll forward it as a joke.

## What's working

- The dresser shots (Jenny Lind, Natural Oak, Waterfall Kids) are shot cleanly against neutral backgrounds — same styled-room competence Crate & Barrel's other lines bring.
- "up to 50% off summer sale" banner is a real, legible offer with a visible button, unlike some of the vaguer CTAs I've seen from CB2 lately.
- The credit card teaser at the bottom ("get the card you love just got better") is at least relevant to me as a rewards-card guy — I run everything through my highest-cashback card, so that's the one section that speaks my language.
- Layout hierarchy is clean top to bottom — hero, then category blocks, no overlapping text or broken images.

## What's weak

- Entire email is nursery furniture, gliders, cribs, and "swaddled in style" copy — zero relevance to a childless renovator. Article and Wayfair at least send me styled-room inspiration I can use; this is a hard skip.
- The "we love these for you" grid at the bottom is clearly templated cross-sell, not actual personalization — it's just more crib/dresser SKUs, no attempt to read that I've never clicked a Crate & Kids link.
- Model/lifestyle photography (parent holding infant, nursery glider) is about as far from my demographic signals as this brand gets — no ambiguity there.
- Cadence is rough — three Crate & Kids/Barrel-family sends this week already (school gear, two cookie collabs) and now this. Compare that to Pottery Barn's "Online Sale Exclusive," which at least changes up furniture categories instead of repeating a kids-only theme.
- "up to 50% off summer sale" is buried two-thirds down after five nursery product blocks — CB2's sale banner problem all over again, main offer under a hero I don't care about.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment me out of Crate & Kids entirely.** I've never bought baby furniture from this brand family — suppress nursery/kids sends for accounts with no kids-category click history and route me to Crate & Barrel's adult furniture list instead.
- 2. **Lead with the sale, not the nursery hero.** If Crate & Kids must email non-parents, put "up to 50% off summer sale" in the hero position — that's the one line with universal appeal.
- 3. **Cross-sell the dressers to the main Crate & Barrel list.** That oak dresser and the Jenny Lind piece would test well with an adult-bedroom audience — Crate & Barrel is already sitting on the customer data to know I'm not a nursery shopper.
- 4. **Slow the family-brand cadence.** Three sends in a week across Crate & Kids and the Levain collab is Sephora-numbing at this point — space it out or dedupe against what Crate & Barrel proper already sent me.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `New oak and Jenny Lind dressers, now up to 50% off`
