# These Halloween favorites never stay in stock
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-15**
## Executive summary

- Okay, giant ghost lawn decorations "selling scary fast" — that's a hard no for me right out of the gate. My gut reaction scrolling this is "wrong renovation, wrong Felix." I'm two houses deep in a reno right now, trying to source curtain rods and platform beds, and Pottery Barn opens with a haunted front-porch tableau of sheet ghosts on stakes. That's not my project. It's not even close to my project.
- The thing is, buried about two-thirds of the way down, there's actually a "Recommended Just For You" block with a Standard Curtain Rod, a Cayman Platform Bed, a Farmhouse Platform Bed, a Belgian Flax Linen Curtain — that's the email I want, and Pottery Barn had the data to lead with it and chose ghosts instead. Compare that to Crate & Barrel's "Your whole home organized" send from earlier this week — that one found an actual reno-relevant lane (storage) and led with it. This one makes me scroll past nine Halloween product shots to get to five thumbnails that are actually on-brief for a guy furnishing two houses.
- Would I click? Maybe, but only on the "Recommended Just For You" row, and only after scrolling past a wall of decor I have zero interest in. Wouldn't forward this to a friend — nothing here screams "look what I found," it's just seasonal catalog filler. This gets an open based on brand recognition alone, not a read-through.

## What's working

- The "Recommended Just For You" section is genuinely personalized — curtain rods, platform beds, linen curtains all track with an active furniture buildout, and it's the one part of this email that feels like Pottery Barn knows I'm mid-renovation.
- Clean, on-brand product photography throughout — the styled vignettes (mantel, dining nook, bar cart) are shot consistently and don't look cheap, same visual polish I noted in the Kravet collab email from yesterday.
- "SHOP ALL HALLOWEEN" and "Halloween Headquarters" CTAs are unambiguous buttons, easy to tap on mobile.
- Rewards math is spelled out clearly near the bottom — "Earn 10% Back in Rewards," "Earn 3-36% APR" with Affirm — that's the kind of concrete financing/rewards detail I actually look for before a big furniture buy.

## What's weak

- The entire top two-thirds of this email is Halloween decor — ghosts on the lawn, skeleton candelabras, spooky bar cart cocktails — none of which has anything to do with furnishing a primary and a second home. This is the same demographic mismatch problem CB2's "Colors That Just Make Sense" had, just with worse seasonal timing for me specifically.
- Subject line "These Halloween favorites never stay in stock" sets false urgency for a category I'm not shopping in — feels like Wayfair's "48-HR 70% OFF" countdown energy, manufactured scarcity on candle sticks and skull platters.
- No swatch/sample program content, no shipping-timeline clarity for the furniture pieces that ARE shown (Article's "Patio season" email at least stated a lead time; here the reno-relevant items get zero delivery info).
- The "Design Crew / Free Design Services" block is a nice hook for someone renovating but it's wedged in after the Halloween shop, the app download, and the recommended grid — buried, not led with.
- I'm getting this the same week as "Introducing our NEW Kravet Collection" and "Dining tables for hosting & everyday meals" — three Pottery Barn sends in two days is a lot of cadence, and this is the least relevant of the three.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the "Recommended Just For You" furniture block, not the ghost lawn display — if Pottery Barn has the segmentation data to know I've been browsing curtain rods and platform beds, put that above the fold instead of six screens of Halloween decor.
- 2. Split this into two sends or two clearly separated sections — Halloween shop for the seasonal-decor crowd, reno/furniture picks for people like me — rather than forcing me to scroll past nine product shots of ghosts to reach five that matter.
- 3. Add delivery-window copy to the furniture recommendations (the Farmhouse Platform Bed, the Cayman Platform Bed) — "Ready to ship" messaging worked well in the 9/14 outdoor essentials send, do that here too.
- 4. Move "Free Design Services" up near the recommended grid — that's a real value-add for someone mid-reno, not an afterthought before the app download pitch.
