# Even more markdowns just added
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-five. That's the CB2 count since mid-May, and this one lands on June 20 — the **same day** as the goop sofa send I was already lukewarm on. Two CB2 emails in a single calendar day is a choice, and I'm not convinced it's the right one.
- That said, the warehouse sale content is genuinely useful. I'm mid-renovation on two properties right now and UP TO 60% OFF furniture is the exact category I need. The "NEW ITEMS ADDED" framing is the correct move — it gives me a reason to open something I've already seen — but the execution dilutes the punch by burying it under a floor sample section I've literally already gotten as a standalone email (June 17's "FLOOR SAMPLE SALE" scored a 9 from me; this one recycles it verbatim). It feels like CB2 is padding the send to justify the volume, not giving me something new.
- The "MORE FOR YOU" product grid at the bottom is the most interesting piece here — actual product thumbnails, names, and prices that are in my wheelhouse. But by the time I scroll that far, I've already mentally filed this as the second CB2 of the day and my patience is thin. I'd mark as read, maybe revisit the product grid on desktop. No forward.

## What's working

- **UP TO 60% OFF in the hero** — big, legible, impossible to miss on first glance. The visual hierarchy actually works: NEW ITEMS ADDED → 60% → WAREHOUSE SALE, in that order.
- **Category segmentation buttons** — FURNITURE / LIGHTING / DECOR / RUGS / SHOP ALL is exactly how I shop. I know what I need and I want to click directly in.
- **"MORE FOR YOU" product grid** — six specific products with names and prices visible. This is closer to how Article does it (single-collection spotlight) and it makes the email feel less like a generic blast.
- **EARN 10% BACK** — app rewards callout is small but present. I notice it. CB2 loyalty math matters to me.
- **No render issues** — clean layout, images loaded, no broken blocks.

## What's weak

- **Floor Sample Sale is recycled content** — I got this as a standalone email on June 17, three days ago. Including it here as a secondary section with the same "IN STORES FOR A LIMITED TIME" headline reads as filler, not added value.
- **Second send of the day** — the goop sofa email already hit me this morning. Two from the same sender on the same day erodes goodwill fast, and this brand is already on thin ice for volume.
- **"LEAVE US A REVIEW, WIN $1000"** — odd placement mid-email. I don't want to write a review while I'm trying to shop; this belongs in a post-purchase triggered send, not jammed between the warehouse sale and the product grid.
- **"UP TO" framing with unreadable fine print** — Wayfair did the same thing in their 72-hour blast and I flagged it there too. I know "up to 60% off" means the one item I want is probably 25% off. The fine print under the hero is too small to read, which makes the offer feel less clean.
- **No time deadline visible** — "limited time" is in the floor sample section but nowhere in the main warehouse sale hero. Pottery Barn's recent 70%-off email had an explicit countdown-style framing; this doesn't.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop the Floor Sample Sale section or replace it with something new.** If you want to cross-promote in-store, make it a one-liner CTA, not a full mid-email section that I already received as its own email.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the warehouse sale hero.** Even "ends Sunday" would close more of my tabs. The June 17 Floor Sample Sale email earned its 9/10 partly because it felt time-limited.
- 3. **Move the review solicitation to post-purchase triggered flow.** It has no business living in a promotional email.
- 4. **Let the "MORE FOR YOU" grid breathe** — put it higher, or make it the anchor of the email rather than the afterthought after the win-$1000 row.
- 5. **Don't send two emails the same day.** Not an email-level fix, but the cadence issue is the root problem.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users from zooming text on mobile. Violates WCAG SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Global `text-size-adjust: none` is overly aggressive**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;-ms-text-size-adjust:none;}
```
Applying to `*` suppresses OS-level text scaling for all elements. Should be scoped to `body` at most, and only as a targeted fix — not a blanket rule.

**Conflicting `@font-face` blocks for "Lineto Brown Sub"**
Three separate `@font-face` declarations share the same `font-family: "Lineto Brown Sub"` name:
- First: no `font-weight` descriptor, loads `.eot`/`.woff` (IE legacy)
- Second: `font-weight: 400`, loads `.woff2` only
- Third: `font-weight: 600`, loads `.woff2` only

The unweighted first declaration conflicts with the weight-400 second. Browsers may ignore or unpredictably resolve the duplicate. Consolidate into a single weighted set and drop the legacy EOT block (IE is not a supported email client).

**Double slashes in font asset URLs**
```
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2")
```
`//Styles/` contains a double slash. Servers typically normalize this, but it risks cache key mismatches at CDN layer. Fix to single slash.

**Overlapping responsive image breakpoints**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 100% !important} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 690px) { img {max-width: 690px} }
```
At ≥768px both rules fire; `!important` on the 768px rule always wins. The 690px rule is dead code for viewports above 768px. Collapse into one rule or reorder with correct specificity.

**Mobile breakpoint targets 418px (dated)**
`.mobile { width:414px !important }` maps to iPhone 6/7/8. Current flagship CSS widths are 390px (iPhone 15 Pro) to 430px (15 Pro Max). This doesn't cause breakage but the hardcoded pixel width is fragile.
