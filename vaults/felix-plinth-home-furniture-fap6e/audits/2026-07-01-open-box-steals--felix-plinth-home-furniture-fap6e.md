# Open Box steals
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- This is my fourth or fifth Wayfair drop in roughly 48 hours — I have a mental counter running at this point — and I'll give it this: "open box" is at least a different frame than the rug/sale blitz of the last three sends. The concept is real. If Wayfair's returned inventory has the dining set I've been configuring in my head for the second home, I'd want to know about it, especially when I'm trying to squeeze every dollar of rewards across two renovations simultaneously.
- But then I look at the actual email and it's a seventeen-row category list in lavender with a dutch oven floating in the hero. A **dutch oven**. Not a sofa, not a dining table, not a bed frame — a cooking pot. I'm renovating two properties, not opening a restaurant. Pottery Barn's recent "Extra 20% off" email used actual room photography. Article's 4th of July sale led with a product in a real living room. Wayfair is out here using kitchenware to sell furniture.
- I'd mark this read without clicking. The category breakdown is useful enough that I might screenshot it for later reference — if I'm actively hunting a coffee table deal, I'd come back to this. But as a cold open on July 1st when I've already been through sends from CB2, Pottery Barn, and Article this week? It's not competing.

## What's working

- **"Open box" is legitimately differentiated** — after the "#kitchenmats" email this morning and the star-emoji sale yesterday, this naming convention is a fresh frame that implies real scarcity and actual markdown depth, not another warehouse event.
- **The category-by-category percentage breakdown is smarter than "UP TO 70% OFF EVERYTHING."** Dining Room Sets at 65% off, Coffee Tables at 60%, Lighting at 70%, Bathroom Vanities at 45% — this is granular and credible. I trust category-specific numbers more than a blanket claim.
- **Clean render, no bugs.** The purple/white execution is on-brand, readable on mobile, no broken images or layout breakage.
- **Dining Room Sets, Beds, Coffee Tables, Lighting, Bathroom Vanities are all in my cart universe.** If I had to find my categories in a list of seventeen, they're there.

## What's weak

- **The hero product is a dutch oven.** This is a fundamental creative misfire. Open box furniture sale, and the image is a green cooking pot. I had to look twice.
- **Seventeen category rows with no product photography is a nav menu, not an email.** I need to see the thing to want the thing. This is a directory with a purple background.
- **"SHOP ALL SALES" is the weakest CTA I've seen this month.** CB2's "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" email at least had editorial energy. Article's sale email linked to a specific hero piece. This is a button that says nothing.
- **Open box inventory is by definition limited — and Wayfair never says that.** No "while supplies last," no "one of each," no scarcity hook whatsoever. The entire pitch of open box is lost.
- **This is the fifth Wayfair email in roughly 48 hours.** The "#kitchenmats" send landed earlier today. Cadence fatigue is real, and it's coloring how generously I evaluate this one.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with one actual open-box product at a dramatic price.** "This dining table retails for $899. Open box: $449. One left." That's the email. One product, one price, one reason to act before someone else does.
- 2. **Make the scarcity explicit in the hero.** "Open box inventory is one of a kind. When it's gone, it's restocked by someone else's return — not Wayfair." That's a real reason to click today instead of Tuesday.
- 3. **Replace the dutch oven with furniture photography.** A sofa, a dining set, a bed frame — anything from the seventeen categories I'm about to be asked to browse.
- 4. **Add a deadline or refresh cadence.** "This week's open box inventory" or "Deals refresh Friday" — something with a time boundary that justifies opening the email now.
- 5. **Surface Wayfair Rewards in the body, not the footer.** "Open box prices stack with your Rewards balance" is the sentence that makes me reach for the right card.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Open-box price drops: dining sets, beds, lighting`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Someone returned a $900 dining table. Want it?`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Limited open-box inventory — when it's gone, it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Up to 70% off furniture opened once, priced way down.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Web font: WOFF only, no WOFF2.** The `@font-face` block declares a single `woff` source. Gmail and most Android mail clients ignore web fonts entirely; the CSS provides no explicit CSS fallback stack (only `mso-font-alt: "Arial"` for Outlook). Any client that can load fonts but prefers WOFF2 will also miss it. Impact: Sofia brand font silently drops to system default across a large client share.

- **`u ~ div { min-width: 100vw }` Gmail hack** is present. This is intentional (prevents Gmail clipping), but it can cause horizontal overflow on narrow viewports if any table cell's min-width pushes past the screen width. Confirm with Gmail mobile rendering test.

- **Dual breakpoint strategy (480px and 320px)** — the 320px breakpoint only targets `Footer-link` font-size. If the design ships any other elements that need 320px adjustments, they are unaddressed.

- **`@supports (-webkit-touch-callout: none)` iOS Safari hack** for half-width block images — this is a valid workaround but scoped to iOS only; Android WebView email clients with similar stacking issues would not be covered.

- **Alt text on images** — not visible in the truncated source. Flag for QA team to verify all `<img>` tags carry non-empty `alt` attributes, especially product images which carry price and description context.
