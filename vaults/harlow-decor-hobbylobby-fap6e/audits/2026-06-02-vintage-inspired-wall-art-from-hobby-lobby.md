# Vintage-Inspired Wall Art From Hobby Lobby®
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- This is Hobby Lobby's thirteenth-ish email in as many days, and it finally arrives with a genuinely different subject line — "Vintage-Inspired Wall Art From Hobby Lobby®" is the most targeted, product-specific hook this brand has written in the entire May-June run. The problem is the email body has nothing to do with it. The hero says "Spring Essentials," and what follows is the same cascading department grid — Home Decor 40%, Spring Shop 50%, Fabric 30%, Online Clearance 75% — recycled since at least May 17. There is no vintage wall art section, no curated gallery moment, no editorial reason to have written that subject at all.
- Opening an email expecting a vintage wall art edit and landing on an undifferentiated department dump is bait-and-switch at the subject line level. The creative potential here is real — vintage aesthetics and gallery-wall content are genuine differentiators in the craft/home segment — but it was squandered on a content-less template swap. Compare this to the May 26 "Light Up The Season" send (scored 9/10 on execution alone but dinged for verbatim offer repetition): at least the body delivered on what the subject implied.
- The department-dump template does its structural job — readable percentages, visible CTAs, clean rendering — but that is the floor, not the ceiling. This email promises something better and doesn't come close to delivering.

## What's working

- Subject line is the strongest in this entire sending run — editorial, product-specific, no "savings" or "shop now" filler
- Free shipping threshold ($50+) is clearly anchored in the header on load
- Discount hierarchy is scannable: 75% clearance is the most aggressive offer Hobby Lobby has surfaced in recent memory
- Email renders cleanly — no broken images, no layout collisions, no visible lorem ipsum
- 40%/50% department offers are instantly readable without engaging with any copy

## What's weak

- Subject promises vintage wall art; the hero headline says "Spring Essentials" and no vintage wall art section appears anywhere in the body — a full subject/body disconnect
- Thirteenth near-identical department-dump template in ~13 days; subscriber fatigue is measurable at this cadence regardless of how good any individual subject line is
- 30% All Fabric is the weakest discount in the grid — surrounded by 40-75% offers, it reads as filler and undercuts perceived value
- Zero urgency signal anywhere — no deadline, no "ends Sunday," nothing to prevent the "I'll look later" defer that kills conversion
- No editorial voice — the brand has a distinctive craft/creative identity that never shows up in the body copy

## Recommendations

- 1. **Deliver the subject line promise.** If you write "Vintage-Inspired Wall Art" in the subject, the hero image and the first content section must show vintage wall art. A three-product editorial grid — distressed frames, gallery-wall prints, earthy palette — would close the gap. The assets almost certainly exist. This single fix changes the quality tier of the entire send.
- 2. **Swap the hero.** Replace "Spring Essentials" with an editorial "Vintage Gallery Wall" hero — a styled lifestyle shot, vintage frames, muted tones. The subject earned an audience who wants curation; give them curation.
- 3. **Add a hard deadline.** "Offers valid through Sunday" is one line of copy that would move browsers to buyers. Every recent Hobby Lobby send has been open-ended. Add the close date.
- 4. **Cut two departments, expand the remaining three.** Six categories plus clearance is visual congestion. Home Decor, Spring Shop, and Clearance are the strongest anchors — give them room, larger product imagery, and CTA buttons that name the destination ("Shop Vintage Frames") instead of the generic action.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Vintage Wall Art Is In — 40% Off This Week`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Build Your Gallery Wall: Vintage Frames + Art Inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Curated vintage styles you won't find in the weekly ad — plus 75% off clearance.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `40% off Home Decor + a gallery wall moment worth building. Ends Sunday.`

## 1. Overview

Standard Responsys-platform HTML email with VML and MSO conditional comments for Outlook compatibility. Four QA warnings surfaced: two compliance header gaps, one deliverability signal, and one missing alt attribute — none are blockers, but the header gaps warrant follow-up with the relay configuration.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

No issues found in the truncated source. Links appear to route through `link.hobbylobby.com` (Responsys click-tracking domain), which is expected.

**Deliverability risk — plain-text body:** QA reports 14,735 of 17,083 plain-text characters are URLs (86%). This is abnormally high. Likely cause: auto-generated plain-text is being populated with raw redirect URLs rather than human-readable text. Spam filters weight plain-text URL density; this ratio can suppress inbox placement.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text (confirmed):**
```
<img src="https://link.hobbylobby.com/mo/eORqXTkAb7_837015858_125436_3938941_134217.gif">
```
This is a tracking pixel (`.gif` URL pattern via `link.hobbylobby.com/mo/`). Alt text on tracking pixels is typically `alt=""` (empty string, intentional). The QA flag is technically correct — the attribute is absent rather than explicitly empty — which will cause some screen readers to announce the full URL. Fix: add `alt=""` explicitly.

No other rendering or accessibility issues identified in the available source. MSO/VML conditional blocks are present and structurally valid. Viewport meta tag is correctly set.

**Minor CSS typo (non-breaking):**
```css
.lhp2, .lhp2 font {line-height:1.2; verticl-align:middle;}
```
`verticl-align` is misspelled — should be `vertical-align`. Ignored by all clients; no visual impact.

**Malformed border-radius values:**
```css
.ctacr1 {... -moz-border-radius:px; -webkit-border-radius:px; border-radius:px;}
```
All three radius values are `px` with no numeric value. This is effectively `0` in most clients and will silently fail. If rounded corners are intended on this CTA, the values need a number (e.g., `4px`).

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens visible in the truncated source (no `%%TOKEN%%` or `${placeholder}` literals left unsubstituted). No issues found based on available source.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent (QA confirmed):**
This header is not present as captured through the AgentMail relay. RFC 2369 and Gmail/Yahoo 2024 bulk sender requirements mandate this header for commercial email. Its absence does not mean it was never sent — the relay may be stripping it — but this must be confirmed against the raw SMTP envelope.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (QA confirmed):**
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe header is missing. As of February 2024, Gmail and Yahoo require this for senders of >5,000/day. Hobby Lobby is a major bulk sender; this is a deliverability compliance risk if confirmed missing at the MTA level, not just at the relay capture layer.

**`Authentication-Results` header not found:**
SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unknown because the header was not captured by the relay. The sending domain is `create.hobbylobby.com` (subdomain). Without confirming DKIM alignment on this subdomain, DMARC pass status cannot be verified.

CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe mechanism are expected to be present in the email footer (not visible in truncated source) — cannot confirm or deny from available HTML.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
