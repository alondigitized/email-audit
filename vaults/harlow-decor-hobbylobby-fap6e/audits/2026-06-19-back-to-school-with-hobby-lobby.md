# Back To School With Hobby Lobby®
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- "Back To School With Hobby Lobby®" arrives June 19 — which puts it about six weeks ahead of most back-to-school shopping peaks. The hero executes decently: a girl reading among books and school supplies under a bold "Classroom Decor" header is the right visual anchor for the subject line. That's where the coherence ends.
- Below the hero fold, this email collapses into the same mega-digest format seen in the June 18 "Up To 75% Off Savings Inside!" send — six stacked discount tiles spanning Home Decor, Spring Shop, Summer Toys, July 4th, Hobbies, and Fabric. The Spring Shop tile is the most damaging detail: that promo ran as the headline on the June 17 send (the 8/10 "50% Off The Spring Shop® Crafts"), and it's still here two days later while the subject promises a BTS-focused email. The BTS frame collapses the moment the eye drops below the hero.
- The CTA — "CLICK HERE TO VIEW OUR WEEKLY AD" — sends you to the full circular rather than a curated BTS landing page. This email is a weekly ad dressed in a back-to-school costume. It's not bad, but it's not what the subject promises.

## What's working

- Hero image and category label ("Classroom Decor") visually match the subject line — the top 25% of the email earns the click.
- Free shipping threshold ($59+) is prominently displayed at top — removes a friction point.
- July 4th tile is timely (16 days out on June 19) and breaks up the routine category stack.
- Summer Toys at 50% off is seasonally coherent and a genuine discount depth.
- Layout renders cleanly — no broken images, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- The Spring Shop tile (50% off) is stale carryover from the June 17 hero send — a subscriber who opened that email sees recycled creative two days later.
- Six department tiles with zero thematic connection to "Back To School" undercut the subject line immediately.
- The primary CTA ("CLICK HERE TO VIEW OUR WEEKLY AD") is generic and buries any BTS-specific urgency.
- No deadline anywhere in the email body — no "ends Sunday," no "this week only" — makes all six offers feel low-stakes.
- BTS on June 19 is early; no copy anchors it ("get ahead of the rush," "teachers: shop before the crowds") to justify the timing.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the mega-digest and curate to BTS.** If the subject is "Back To School," every tile below the hero should be BTS: Classroom Decor, Art Supplies, Custom Frames for classrooms, Fabric for school projects. Reserve the weekly-ad dump for the weekly-ad email (which already exists).
- 2. **Replace the CTA with a category-specific link.** "Shop Classroom Decor" beats "View Our Weekly Ad" — it closes the subject-to-click loop.
- 3. **Drop the Spring Shop tile entirely.** It was the hero two days ago. Running it again as a sidebar tile signals "we ran out of ideas."
- 4. **Add a timing peg.** One line of copy — "Teachers, get your room ready before the rush" or "Schools start in August — shop now while shelves are full" — earns the early-season send.
- 5. **Put a deadline on at least the lead offer.** "40% Off Classroom Decor — this week only" gives the click a reason to happen today.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed defects in embedded CSS:**

**a) Malformed `border-radius` in `.ctacr1`:**
```css
.ctacr1 { -moz-border-radius:px; -webkit-border-radius:px; border-radius:px; }
```
Unit `px` with no numeric value is invalid. CTA button using this class will have no border-radius applied — browsers discard the entire property. Fix: supply a value (e.g., `border-radius:4px`) or remove.

**b) Empty `color` value in `.ctacr1`:**
```css
.ctacr1 { color:; }
```
Empty property declaration. No immediate visible break (inherited color applies), but it's a parse error that some strict renderers may flag or use to invalidate the surrounding rule block.

**c) Typo in `.lhp2`:**
```css
.lhp2 { verticl-align:middle; }
```
`verticl-align` is not a valid CSS property. Vertical alignment on elements using this class will not apply. Fix: `vertical-align:middle`.

**d) Viewport meta missing `width=device-width`:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="initial-scale=1, user-scalable=yes">
```
Without `width=device-width`, mobile clients may render at desktop width and scale down. Standard fix: `content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1"`.

**e) Custom font dependency:**
```css
font-family: Museo Sans, Trebuchet MS, Verdana, Tahoma, sans-serif
```
`Museo Sans` is not a web-safe font and will not load in any major email client without embedded font support (only Apple Mail / newer iOS Mail support this). Fallback chain to `Trebuchet MS` is present — confirm designs are validated against the fallback rendering.
