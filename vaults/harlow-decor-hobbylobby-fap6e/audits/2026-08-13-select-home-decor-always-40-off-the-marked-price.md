# Select Home Decor – Always 40% Off The Marked Price
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-13**
## Executive summary

- This is Hobby Lobby doing what Hobby Lobby has done every single day this week: a massive, undifferentiated tile-grid promo dressed up with a subject line that promises focus. "Select Home Decor – Always 40% Off The Marked Price" says Home Decor, and the top two tiles *are* Home Decor and Textiles at 40% — but by the third scroll you're in Christmas Trees, Football Party, Dorm Essentials, Sewing Notions, and Furniture, none of which have anything to do with the subject. This is basically the same sprawling category catalog audited on 8/6 ("Hurry In! Up To 75% Off Savings!") and 8/11 ("Cozy Living With Inviting Textures"), just re-skinned with a different lead tile.
- The subject line is at least honest for the first fold — that's an improvement over 8/9's "50% Off Christmas!" bait — but it collapses almost immediately into the brand's default kitchen-sink layout. There's no hero image with lifestyle photography, no single clear CTA, just repeating "CATEGORY / ALWAYS 40%(or 50%,75%)/THE MARKED PRICE" tiles stacked for what looks like 20+ screens.
- Judgment: functional but lazy. It'll get scanned, not read, and the subject-to-body mismatch after the first two tiles trains subscribers to stop trusting the subject line — a pattern already visible across this brand's last six sends.

## What's working

- The subject's claim is true for the top of the email — Home Decor and Textiles are both prominently at 40% right where you land.
- Discount percentages are large, red, and legible against white space — easy to scan at a glance.
- "Free Shipping On Orders $59 Or More" banner is a real, unambiguous, universally-relevant offer placed above the fold.
- No render bugs — no overlapping text, no broken images, no lorem ipsum across this very long render.

## What's weak

- Subject promises "Home Decor" but the email sprawls into 20+ unrelated categories (Christmas trees, football party, dorm essentials, jewelry making, sewing notions) — a subject/body mismatch, same failure mode flagged on 8/9 and 8/11.
- No single primary CTA — dozens of tiles each with their own micro-offer means the eye has nowhere obvious to land.
- Extreme length turns this into a static catalog scroll rather than a marketing email; nothing signals what to click first.
- Zero seasonal coherence — Christmas trees and football-party gear sit two tiles apart from "Fall Essentials," which itself contradicts the literal August date.
- Product photography is small, generic stock-catalog thumbnails (a mirror clip-art icon, a generic teddy bear) rather than styled lifestyle shots that would sell the "Home Decor" story the subject promises.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the email to the subject's promise.** If the subject says Home Decor, send Home Decor — wall clocks, canvas art, decorative hardware, framed art — and nothing else. Move Christmas, sports, dorm, and craft categories into their own dedicated sends (which this brand clearly already produces).
- 2. **Pick one hero offer and one CTA button.** Lead with a single lifestyle photo (a styled room) and one "Shop Home Decor" button above the fold; push the tile grid below as secondary browsing, not the entire email.
- 3. **Drop stale/contradictory tiles.** Christmas Trees and Fall Essentials next to Football Party in August reads as inventory-dump, not curation — trim to what's actually seasonally relevant.
- 4. **Shorten dramatically.** A subscriber shouldn't need to scroll 20+ screens; cap at 5-6 tiles max or split into a multi-part series.
- **Subject Alt A:** `40% Off All Home Decor — Today Only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Refresh Your Walls: Framed Art, Clocks & More, 40% Off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New arrivals in wall art, mirrors, and clocks — all always 40% off marked price.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus free shipping on orders $59+, no code needed.`
