# Stars, Stripes & Savings 🇺🇸 40% Off July 4th Decor
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- This is Hobby Lobby's best subject-to-hero alignment in the recent audit window, and the timing is exactly right — emailing on June 30 for a July 4th holiday four days away is textbook. The hero delivers: red, white, and blue decor, American flags, woven baskets, and a big "40% Off / July 4th" treatment that earns the alliterative subject line. Unlike the misfired June 28 "Fall Starts Now" circular that felt seasonally confused, this email knows what it is.
- The problem is what comes below the fold. Within two rows, the email reverts to the same six-department mega-circular template Hobby Lobby has sent every week this month — including the Spring Shop 50% tile (running since June 17), Summer Toys, and a "40% Fall" section sitting directly below a July 4th hero. That Fall tile is the most damaging element in the render: it signals this is the standard template with a patriotic banner bolted on top, not a genuinely curated holiday send.
- This is a clear step above the June 28 5/10 circular, and the subject line might be the second-best in the entire audit window behind the June 26 America's 250th Anniversary send. But that June 26 email had a premium video-implied hero and a tighter single-theme narrative. This one fractures at row two.

## What's working

- Subject line "Stars, Stripes & Savings 🇺🇸 40% Off July 4th Decor" is alliterative, specific, and timely — concrete discount plus named category plus holiday name in one clean phrase
- Hero image delivers the promise: patriotic color palette, flags, and seasonal decor items match the subject exactly
- "Free Shipping On Orders $59 Or More!" as the top-of-email banner adds a conversion incentive that the subject doesn't repeat, making it useful as a preheader
- "75% Online Clearance" tile is a legitimately strong secondary offer that rewards scrollers
- Timing is correct — July 4th send on June 30 gives 4 days to act, not 4 days after

## What's weak

- "40% Fall" tile appears two rows below the July 4th hero — a direct calendar contradiction that breaks the holiday immersion
- "50% Items Labeled The Spring Shop®" is still running, unchanged from the June 23 and June 17 sends — the brand failed to retire or rename it for July 4th week
- No visible urgency deadline on the hero — "Ends July 4th" or "4 days only" copy is absent, wasting the implicit time pressure the holiday provides
- Hero CTA button is small and text is illegible at this render size — can't confirm it's verb+noun specific copy
- High send frequency (7 emails in the last 10 days) dulls the holiday-urgency signal even when the individual send is well-timed

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add explicit deadline copy to the hero** — "40% Off · Ends July 4th" directly on or below the hero badge. This is the single highest-lift change; implicit deadlines don't convert as well as named ones.
- 2. **Pull the Fall tile for this week** — "40% Fall" contradicts the July 4th theme and signals template laziness. It belongs in the July 7 send, not July 4th week.
- 3. **Retire or rename the Spring Shop tile** — at minimum swap it to "4th of July Crafts 50% Off" for this week if the products overlap.
- 4. **Make the hero CTA specific and legible** — "Shop July 4th Decor" (verb + noun) at a larger button size with readable text.
- 5. **Add a curated product strip** — 3–4 specific July 4th SKUs (a flag wreath, a tabletop centerpiece, a garland) between the hero and the circular, giving the holiday send a point of view before opening the full catalog.
- **Subject Alt A:** `40% Off July 4th Decor — Ends Saturday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last 4 Days: Patriotic Decor 40% Off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping $59+ · flags, wreaths, tabletop & more`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock up before the 4th — 40% off patriotic decor ends Saturday`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bug — empty `color` declaration:**
```css
.ctacr1 {color:; ...}
```
The `color` property has no value. CSS parsers will drop the entire declaration block in strict clients; in lenient clients the color is undefined, likely inheriting black text on the `#013088` blue CTA background. This may cause the button label to be invisible.

**Confirmed bug — invalid `border-radius` values:**
```css
.ctacr1 {-moz-border-radius:px; -webkit-border-radius:px; border-radius:px;}
```
All three radius values are `px` with no leading integer. These are invalid and will be ignored by all clients, rendering the CTA with sharp corners (inconsistent with the intended rounded style defined elsewhere as `3px`).

**Confirmed bug — CSS property typo:**
```css
.lhp2 {verticl-align:middle;}
```
`verticl-align` is not a valid property. The intended `vertical-align:middle` will never apply, affecting CTA button text vertical centering.

**Minor — mixed DOCTYPE:**
```html
<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN" ...>
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" ...>
```
HTML 4.01 Transitional DOCTYPE with XHTML namespace declarations is technically inconsistent but is standard practice in ESP-generated email HTML and unlikely to cause real rendering issues.

**Custom font load risk:**
`.ff3ng` references `Museo Sans` as the first family. Web fonts are blocked in virtually all email clients; fallback chain (`Trebuchet MS, Verdana, Tahoma, sans-serif`) is present and appropriate.
