# 🇺🇸 Celebrate America’s 250th Anniversary In Style
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- This is the strongest subject line Hobby Lobby has sent in the entire audit window, and the hero earns it — a warm patriotic tablescape with gnomes, festive decor, and a video play button that implies premium content. For the 48 hours before July 4th, tying the send to the 250th anniversary creates genuine inbox distinction against a sea of generic "4th of July Sale!" blasts from competing retailers.
- Then you open it. The moment you scroll past "CLICK HERE TO VIEW OUR WEEKLY AD," the patriotic concept evaporates and you're back to the same stacked-tile mega-circular that ran June 18, 21, 24, and 25. The 50%-off Spring Shop® tiles appear here — again — three days after they headlined the June 23 send. "40% Off Classroom Decor" sits in the fourth row of a July 4th email. The brand found a legitimately good hook and then declined to build anything under it.
- The email is the best-subject, same-body send in this streak. That combination is both the win and the waste.

## What's working

- "🇺🇸 Celebrate America's 250th Anniversary In Style" is the most culturally specific subject Hobby Lobby has used across all eight audited sends — a real differentiator from the sale-headline format.
- Hero image delivers directly on the theme: patriotic tablescape, gnomes, festive decor, all on-brief.
- The video play button overlay on the hero is the first creative experiment in this audit; it creates a reason to click beyond the discount stack.
- "Free Shipping On Orders $59 Or More!" is clearly visible above the fold.
- Discount percentages (50%, 40%, 30%) are bold, consistent, and scannable across every tile.

## What's weak

- The 50%-off Spring Shop® promo reappears here, three days after it led the June 23 send — signals disorganized send calendar and burns subscriber trust.
- "40% Off Classroom Decor" in a July 4th email is a jarring non-sequitur with zero patriotic tie-in.
- No deadline copy anywhere — "sale ends July 4th" would convert the implicit urgency the subject creates into an actual purchase driver.
- "CLICK HERE TO VIEW OUR WEEKLY AD" routes to a generic weekly ad rather than a curated July 4th collection — the worst possible landing page for the best possible subject line.
- Six emails in eight days; even a strong subject arrives in congested airspace of the brand's own making.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Build the July 4th story below the fold.** The top two tile rows should be July 4th-specific product heroes with names and prices (e.g., "Americana Gnome 3-Pack · $12.99"), not generic department discounts. The June 23 Spring Shop send scored 8/10 precisely because the body maintained single-theme discipline. Replicate that here.
- 2. **Kill the Spring Shop tiles.** They ran three days ago. Appearing a second time inside a patriotic send signals an uncoordinated calendar.
- 3. **Reroute the primary CTA.** "Shop 4th of July Decor →" linking to a filtered collection would meaningfully lift click-through compared to "CLICK HERE TO VIEW OUR WEEKLY AD."
- 4. **Add a single deadline line.** One line under the CTA — "Sale prices valid through July 4th" — activates the urgency the subject promises.
- 5. **Cut unrelated departments from the hero section.** Classroom Decor, Wearable Art, and Custom Frames add nothing to a patriotic send. Push them to a text-only secondary block or cut entirely.

## Technical Audit — Hobby Lobby "Celebrate America's 250th Anniversary"

### 1. Technical Summary

ESP-wrapped HTML 4.01 Transitional template with MSO conditional blocks for Outlook, routed through `link.hobbylobby.com`. QA surface: 1 hard failure (broken link), 3 CSS defects in the stylesheet, 2 missing compliance headers, and 2 deliverability signals that warrant attention.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] "My Account" nav link resolves to a relative URL**
The ESP wrapped a bare path `/login` instead of an absolute URL. The redirect resolver cannot follow it, producing `unknown url type: '/login'`.

- Redirect wrapper: `https://link.hobbylobby.com/u/nrd.php?p=eORqXTkAb7_4588525_127545_1_49&...`
- Destination at template authoring time: `/login` (relative — unusable by the ESP)
- Impact: clicking "My Account" results in a dead link for all recipients.

**[WARN] Plain-text part is 84% URLs by character count**
10,452 of 12,482 characters in the plain-text alternative are URL strings. Spam filters (SpamAssassin `URI_ONLY`, Gmail heuristics) penalize plain-text bodies that are overwhelmingly links with no prose. This is a send-time deliverability risk, not a template defect per se, but it is fixable in the ESP's plain-text generation settings.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Tracking pixel missing `alt` attribute**
`eORqXTkAb7_837015858_127545_4588525_136418.gif` has no `alt`. For a 1×1 open-tracking pixel, the correct fix is `alt=""` (empty string), which suppresses screen-reader announcement and satisfies WCAG 1.1.1.

**[DEFECT] CSS typo — `verticl-align` in `.lhp2`**
```css
.lhp2, .lhp2 font { line-height:1.2; verticl-align:middle; }
```
`verticl-align` is not a valid property; the declaration is silently ignored. Any CTA button using `.lhp2` may misalign on clients that would have respected `vertical-align:middle`.

**[DEFECT] Malformed `border-radius` values in `.ctacr1`**
```css
.ctacr1 { ... -moz-border-radius:px; -webkit-border-radius:px; border-radius:px; }
```
All three `border-radius` declarations contain a unit (`px`) with no numeric value. The property is dropped by all parsers. Any element styled `.ctacr1` renders with square corners regardless of intent.

**[DEFECT] Empty `color` value in `.ctacr1`**
```css
.ctacr1 { color:; ... }
```
Empty value is invalid CSS; the color rule is ignored. If `.ctacr1` is the primary CTA class, text color falls through to inherited or default values, which may break contrast on the `background-color:#013088` (dark blue) background.

No unresolved merge tokens visible in the truncated source. The `e-locale="en-US"` and `e-is-multilanguage="false"` attributes are set correctly. Full-source review recommended to confirm no tokens remain in footer or conditional blocks.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
The QA relay did not capture a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Gmail and Yahoo Mail require this header for bulk senders (≥5,000/day) as of February 2024 enforcement. Its absence risks inbox demotion to the Promotions tab or bulk folder.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click machine unsubscribe (required by the same Gmail/Yahoo policy) depends on both `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`. Neither is present. This may reflect header stripping at the AgentMail relay rather than the ESP not sending them — confirm against raw headers at the originating MTA before treating as an ESP configuration issue.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not captured**
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown. If the relay is stripping authentication headers, audit results cannot confirm deliverability posture. Recommend validating directly against `create.hobbylobby.com` MX/SPF records and the DKIM selector published in DNS.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The available source does not show UTM parameters appended to destination URLs after the `link.hobbylobby.com` redirect resolves. This cannot be confirmed from the truncated HTML alone. If the ESP is not injecting `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` at redirect resolution time, all click attribution will appear as direct traffic in analytics. Verify by expanding one redirect URL and inspecting the final destination.
