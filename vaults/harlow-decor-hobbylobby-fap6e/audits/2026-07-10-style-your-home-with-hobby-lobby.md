# Style Your Home With Hobby Lobby®
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- "Style Your Home With Hobby Lobby®" attempts the same editorial-lifestyle move that scored the brand its best recent result — the July 3 "Small Space, Big Style" send at 6/10. The hero earns it: a warm, basket-and-basket-and-frames styled room shot with "Home & Wall Decor" locked over it is the strongest opening Hobby Lobby has run this week. But the email can't sustain the premise. Scroll past the lifestyle moment and you're back in the familiar mega-circular — a two-column tile grid serving up Summer Toys, Classroom Decor, and most damningly, a "50% Off Items Labeled The Spring Shop" block on July 10th. Spring. In July.
- The subject line doesn't help. "Style Your Home With Hobby Lobby®" is a brand tagline, not a subject line. It tells you nothing about what's inside, who it's for, or why today is the day to act. Paired with the six-send-in-nine-days cadence, this lands in an already-fatigued inbox with nothing to distinguish it from the July 7, 8, or 9 sends.
- The edit is simple: the hero is doing real work — lean into it. Drop the circular format, pick one room story and one offer, and let the email be what the hero promises.

## What's working

- **Hero lifestyle image is genuinely appealing.** The styled room shot with warm tones, baskets, and wall art is the best hero frame this brand has shipped this week — it earns attention.
- **"Home & Wall Decor" headline is specific** and gives the email a clear editorial angle, at least above the fold.
- **Free shipping threshold ("$50 or more") is front-and-center** in the header — a useful transactional hook that likely populates inbox preview text.
- **75% off depth is unusual and would stop a scanner** — the most aggressive discount visible in any department tile.

## What's weak

- **"Spring Shop" promoted on July 10** is the single most damaging element in the email — it signals clearance-dump, not curated sale.
- **Subject line "Style Your Home With Hobby Lobby®" is a tagline, not an offer** — identical entropy to "Dine In Style" (July 7) and equally non-committal about what's inside.
- **Hero promise vs. body content mismatch.** "Home & Wall Decor" hero delivers Summer Toys, Dorm Essentials, and Classroom Decor tiles — the circular undermines the editorial setup.
- **"Click Here To See Our Weekly Ad" CTA is a traffic funnel, not a conversion trigger.** It sends you to a generic ad rather than the home decor section the hero just sold you on.
- **No urgency signal anywhere.** No end date, no countdown, no "this week only" — six straight days of promotions with no scarcity.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the subject line earn the hero image.** The room shot is doing the creative work — the subject should close the loop with a specific offer from it, not a brand tagline.
- **Subject Alt A:** `50% Off Frames, Wall Art & More — Shop Home Decor`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Refresh Every Room: Home Decor Up To 75% Off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping on $50+. New arrivals, baskets, gallery frames and more.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the full Home & Wall Decor collection — free ship at $50.`
- 2. **Pull the Spring Shop tile entirely or relabel it "Summer Clearance."** Any seasonal label that contradicts the calendar erodes trust. "Spring Shop" in mid-July reads as leftover inventory being offloaded, not a curated offer.
- 3. **Replace "Click Here To See Our Weekly Ad" with a category-specific CTA.** "Shop Home & Wall Decor →" converts off the hero. The weekly ad link can live in the footer.
- 4. **Pick one department and own it.** Summer Toys and Classroom Decor are noise in a Home & Wall Decor email. Each deserves its own send — or at minimum, a secondary section header that signals a tonal shift.
- 5. **Add urgency.** "Sale ends Sunday" or a specific date does more than any additional %Off tile. No deadline = no reason to act today over tomorrow.

## Technical Audit — Hobby Lobby "Style Your Home" Email

**Reviewed:** 2026-07-10 | **From:** noreply@create.hobbylobby.com

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard ESP-generated table layout with Outlook/VML compatibility shims and responsive breakpoints. Four QA warnings were flagged; none are blocking, but the missing authentication header and plain-text URL density are the most consequential.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text part is URL-heavy**
QA: 14,254 of 16,433 chars (87%) in the plain-text alternative are URLs — well above the 70% threshold. This ratio is a spam signal in Barracuda, SpamAssassin, and some Gmail filters. The plain-text version is almost certainly auto-generated from the HTML with no prose substitution. The ESP should generate a curated plain-text body rather than dumping raw href values.

No broken or malformed `href` values were detectable in the truncated HTML, and all observed links route through `link.hobbylobby.com` (ESP redirect/tracking domain), which is expected.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing `alt` text on tracked image**
QA identified one image with no `alt` attribute:
```
src: https://link.hobbylobby.com/mo/eORqXTkAb7_837015858_129617_1357900_138593.gif
```
The filename pattern (`_129617_1357900_138593.gif`) is consistent with an open-pixel/tracking beacon, so a blank `alt=""` is technically correct — but the attribute itself is absent rather than present-and-empty. Screen readers may announce the full URL. Fix: add `alt=""` explicitly.

**CSS typo — `verticl-align`**
In `.lhp2`:
```css
.lhp2, .lhp2 font {line-height:1.2; verticl-align:middle;}
```
`verticl-align` is a misspelling of `vertical-align` and will be silently ignored by all clients. Any CTA or table cell using `.lhp2` that relies on vertical centering will fall back to default (top) alignment in some clients.

**Incomplete border-radius values in `.ctacr1`**
```css
.ctacr1 {... -moz-border-radius:px; -webkit-border-radius:px; border-radius:px;}
```
All three `border-radius` values are unitless (`px` with no number). This is invalid CSS and will result in sharp corners on the `.ctacr1` CTA button regardless of intent.

**`user-scalable=yes` vs. email convention**
`<meta name="viewport" content="initial-scale=1, user-scalable=yes">` — this is fine and correct (blocking user zoom on email is an accessibility violation). No issue.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (`{{`, `%%`, `[[`) were visible in the truncated source. The `e-locale="en-US"` and `e-is-multilanguage="false"` attributes are ESP rendering directives and appear to have fired correctly (no raw token leakage). No issues confirmed.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not captured**
QA: Header absent from the relayed copy. Under CAN-SPAM this header is not legally required, but Gmail and Yahoo's February 2024 bulk sender mandates require it for senders above 5k/day. If Hobby Lobby qualifies as a bulk sender (almost certainly yes), a missing `List-Unsubscribe` header will push mail toward the Promotions tab and can trigger delivery penalties.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not captured**
One-click unsubscribe is required by the same Gmail/Yahoo mandate for bulk senders. If this header is absent at the MTA level (not just missing from the AgentMail relay capture), it is a compliance gap. This should be verified against raw SMTP headers at the receiving MTA, not just the relayed copy.

**`Authentication-Results` header not found**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status is unknown from this capture. The sending domain `create.hobbylobby.com` should be aligned with a DMARC policy on `hobbylobby.com`. This cannot be confirmed from available data — the AgentMail relay did not forward the authentication results header. No issue can be confirmed, but the gap in visibility is itself a monitoring risk.

**Physical address / unsubscribe footer**
CAN-SPAM requires a visible physical postal address and a functional opt-out mechanism in the email body. These are standard Hobby Lobby footer elements — not visible in the truncated source but assumed present. Cannot confirm or deny from available data.
