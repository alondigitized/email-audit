# Come On In!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a welcome / account-creation email, not a promotional send. Hobby Lobby is using it to drive new account registrations, dangling wishlist management, order tracking, and payment options as the reasons to sign up. The hero is a beautifully styled farmhouse shelf — wicker baskets, white ceramics, fresh white blooms — which fits the brand aesthetic cleanly. The layout is competent and renders without visible bugs.
- The core problem is that "Come On In!" does zero work as a subject line. It is the blankest possible invitation with no offer, no specificity, and no reason to act now. For a welcome email that is asking for a real commitment (account creation), there is no incentive visible — no first-order discount, no exclusive member benefit, no urgency. The email looks nice but gives the recipient no compelling reason to click.
- The call-to-action itself — "Create An Account" — is present and readable, but account creation is friction, not reward. Without a hook (say, "get 10% off your first order"), this email will underperform even against Hobby Lobby's own baseline.

## What's working

- **Hero image is on-brand and high quality** — the farmhouse shelf styling immediately signals Hobby Lobby's aesthetic without any copy.
- **"Create An Account" CTA is unambiguous** — clear verb-noun, visible button, no guessing required.
- **Clean layout with no render bugs** — text, images, and footer elements all render correctly; no broken assets visible.
- **Account benefits are spelled out** — "Edit Your Profile · Start A Wishlist · Check Your Order Status · Set Up Your Payment Options" gives a functional reason to sign up.
- **Footer is fully populated** — app store badges, social icons, and utility links (Gift Cards, Order Status, Contact Us) all visible and properly laid out.

## What's weak

- **Subject line "Come On In!" is pure filler** — no offer, no product, no urgency. It could belong to any retail brand on any day of the year.
- **No offer or incentive for account creation** — asking a recipient to create an account without a reward (first-purchase discount, member-only pricing) ignores the standard welcome-email playbook.
- **No personalization whatsoever** — not even a first name in the hero headline, despite this being a triggered welcome flow where the email address is already known.
- **Hero copy ("Welcome!") wastes the prime real estate** — a generic one-word headline over a beautiful lifestyle image is a missed opportunity to lead with a value proposition.
- **No seasonal urgency or campaign hook** — despite the spring flowers in the photography, no copy ties this to a sale, season, or deadline.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a first-order incentive to the CTA** — "Create an account and get 15% off your first purchase" converts a passive registration ask into a transactional reason to act immediately. This is the single highest-lift change.
- 2. **Rewrite the hero headline** — swap "Welcome!" for something that leads with the benefit: "Your creative home starts here" or "Save more every time you shop."
- 3. **Rewrite the subject line** — "Come On In!" belongs on a door mat, not an inbox.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your Hobby Lobby account is ready — grab 15% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Welcome! Here's what your new account unlocks`
- 4. **Rewrite the preheader to complement, not mirror, the subject.**
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Wishlists, order tracking, and exclusive deals — all in one place.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Create your account and start saving on every in-store or online order.`
- 5. **Personalize the hero with a first name** — "Welcome, [First Name]!" is table stakes for a triggered welcome email and takes one merge tag.
- 6. **Tie the lifestyle image to copy** — the farmhouse shelf is evocative; caption it: "Curate your space with Hobby Lobby's home décor, right from your account."

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Bug — Malformed border-radius on `.ctacr1` (CTA button class):**
```css
.ctacr1 {
  -moz-border-radius:px;
  -webkit-border-radius:px;
  border-radius:px;
}
```
All three declarations are missing the numeric value — `px` alone is invalid. Every client will ignore these rules; the button will render with square corners regardless of intent. **Affects all clients.**

**Bug — CSS typo in `.lhp2`:**
```css
.lhp2, .lhp2 font {line-height:1.2; verticl-align:middle;}
```
`verticl-align` is not a valid property. The intended `vertical-align:middle` is silently dropped.

**Bug — Empty `color` value in `.ctacr1`:**
```css
.ctacr1 {color:; ...}
```
Empty property value. All clients will ignore it. If a foreground text color was intended for this button, it is not applied.

**Viewport meta** — `user-scalable=yes` is fine (it's the default and avoids iOS accessibility violations from `no`). No issue.
