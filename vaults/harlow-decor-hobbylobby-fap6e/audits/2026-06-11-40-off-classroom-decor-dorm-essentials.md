# 40% Off Classroom Decor & Dorm Essentials!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- This is Hobby Lobby's nineteenth-or-so email in roughly as many days, and the subject line — "40% Off Classroom Decor & Dorm Essentials!" — is actually a smart pivot. It names two specific, co-branded categories that make early-June sense: classrooms restock, students prep dorms. The angle is the freshest hook in at least a week, and at 43 characters it fits mobile without truncation.
- The execution, however, is the same cascading department-dump template that has appeared in every prior audit. The email opens with a "Spring Lookouts" banner and a generic Home Decor 40% off tile, not the classroom or dorm content promised in the subject. By the time the Classroom Decor and Dorm Essentials tiles appear, the subscriber is already four or five scroll-lengths deep, sandwiched between Graduation, Party, and Floral tiles that have nothing to do with the subject hook.
- The net effect is a subject line that over-promises and an email that under-delivers. The back-to-school angle had real potential to feel targeted; instead it reads like a label slapped on the standard weekly ad.

## What's working

- **Subject line specificity is the strongest in recent memory** — "Classroom Decor & Dorm Essentials" names two real audience segments, not a vague lifestyle hook.
- **Graduation tile is a logical companion** — pairing graduation with classroom/dorm in the same email gives the academic theme a coherent arc.
- **40% off is a clean, credible number** — consistent across tiles, no asterisk-heavy fine print visible.
- **No visible render bugs** — images load, text doesn't overlap, layout is clean on the render.
- **Cadence differentiation** — at least the category pairing is different from the July 4th decor drums of June 7 and June 9.

## What's weak

- **Hero betrays the subject** — the email opens with "Spring Lookouts" and generic Home Decor, not classroom or dorm content; the subject promise evaporates immediately.
- **Classroom Decor and Dorm Essentials tiles are buried mid-scroll** — both subject-line categories appear well below the fold, after Home Decor, Textiles, Spring Shop, and Graduation tiles.
- **No urgency mechanism visible** — 40% off is asserted but no "ends Sunday" or date anchor appears anywhere on-screen; there is no reason to act today vs. next week.
- **Too many categories dilute the hook** — Floral, Wearable Art, Sewing Notions, Furniture, and Custom Frames all appear, turning a targeted back-to-school angle into another everything-store dump.
- **No single hero product** — not one specific classroom or dorm item is shown; every tile is a category label with a discount badge, which requires the subscriber to do all the imagination work.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the email body with the promised categories.** The Classroom Decor and Dorm Essentials tiles should be the hero block, full-width, above the fold — not buried tile #6 and #7. Move Spring Shop and Floral to the bottom.
- 2. **Add a time anchor.** "Ends Sunday" or "Valid through June 15" immediately gives the subscriber a reason not to defer the click. Without it, 40% off reads as always-on noise.
- 3. **Show one specific product.** A single styled classroom supply flat-lay or dorm room vignette in the hero converts casual openers to clickers far better than a badge on a grey tile.
- 4. **Retire or reframe "Spring Lookouts."** The spring banner is stale language in mid-June; it conflicts with the academic-season framing. Use "Back to Class" or "Dorm Ready" as the email header instead.
- 5. **Collapse the category grid to 6-8 tiles maximum.** Nineteen categories in one send is exhausting. If classroom/dorm is the thesis, every tile should support it or be cut.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dorm & Classroom Haul — 40% Off Starts Now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Back to Class Early: 40% Off Everything You Need`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Grab it before the rush — classroom decor, storage, and dorm must-haves all on sale`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock your classroom or dorm for less — 40% off through Sunday`

## Technical Audit — Hobby Lobby "40% Off Classroom Decor & Dorm Essentials"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Table-based HTML 4.01 Transitional layout with MSO conditional comments and a standard ESP redirect domain (`link.hobbylobby.com`). QA returned 0 hard errors and 4 warnings; the most actionable issues are two CSS property defects in the rendered stylesheet, a missing alt attribute on the tracking GIF, and unresolvable compliance headers at the relay layer.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text body is 89% URLs (12,724 / 14,335 chars)**
This far exceeds the 70% threshold flagged by QA. A plain-text part that is almost entirely link redirect strings — with no prose — looks machine-generated to spam filters and degrades deliverability scoring. The plain-text alternative should mirror the readable content of the HTML version, not expose raw ESP redirect URLs.

**Tracking pixel alt attribute missing**
`https://link.hobbylobby.com/mo/eORqXTkAb7_837015858_127203_1460336_135184.gif` has no `alt` attribute. While intentionally invisible, a missing `alt` on any `<img>` is flagged by accessibility validators and some ESPs' own pre-send checks.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS typo — `verticl-align` (line in `.lhp2` rule)**
```css
.lhp2, .lhp2 font {line-height:1.2; verticl-align:middle;}
```
`verticl-align` is not a valid property; the intended `vertical-align:middle` is silently dropped. Affects CTA button vertical centering in any client that parses embedded styles.

**CSS defect — bare `px` values in `.ctacr1` border-radius**
```css
.ctacr1 {... -moz-border-radius:px; -webkit-border-radius:px; border-radius:px;}
```
All three `border-radius` declarations are missing their numeric value, rendering as invalid. The CTA button rendered via this class will have no border-radius in clients that respect these rules.

**Tracking GIF alt text** — noted above under §2; also an accessibility gap per WCAG 2.1 (all `<img>` elements require `alt`, even decorative/tracking ones — use `alt=""`).

**Doctype** — `HTML 4.01 Transitional` with `xmlns` XHTML attributes is technically inconsistent but is accepted practice in email and poses no rendering risk across major clients.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No issues found. The truncated source contains no unresolved `%%TOKEN%%`, `{{variable}}`, or `[FIRSTNAME]`-style placeholders visible in rendered text regions.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent at relay**
QA could not confirm the header via the AgentMail relay capture. This header is required for Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender compliance (enforced Feb 2024). If the ESP injects it after relay capture, this is not a live defect — but should be verified in a header dump from an actual delivered copy.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent**
One-click unsubscribe (required for Gmail/Yahoo bulk senders sending >5k/day) depends on this header alongside `List-Unsubscribe`. Same caveat applies: confirm via delivered-copy header inspection, not relay capture.

**`Authentication-Results` absent**
SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unknown. The sending domain is `create.hobbylobby.com` (subdomain); confirm that DKIM signing is configured for this subdomain and that the SPF record for `hobbylobby.com` includes the ESP's sending IPs. A missing or failing authentication record will trigger spam folder placement at major providers.

**CAN-SPAM** — physical mailing address and unsubscribe link are present in standard Hobby Lobby footer boilerplate (visible in the truncated source via the `.fs10`/`.fc6` footer block pattern); no CAN-SPAM structural violation detected.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Cannot confirm UTM parameters on destination URLs** — all links route through `link.hobbylobby.com` ESP redirects; destination URLs are not exposed in the HTML source. UTM attribution should be verified by expanding one redirect URL and confirming parameters such as `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` are appended before the handoff to `hobbylobby.com`. Without this, GA4 / site analytics will attribute the traffic as direct.
