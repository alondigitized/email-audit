# Ready For More Savings? See Inside.
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- This is Hobby Lobby's "everyday low price" pitch — a distinct positioning from their usual sale events, trading "50% off" for "YOUR PRICE" sticker branding and flat everyday-best prices on a two-column product grid. It's a meaningful strategic shift in tone, but the execution buries it. The subject line — "Ready For More Savings? See Inside." — is a blind-box teaser that gives away nothing, and the hero headline ("Our Everyday Best Price On Thousands Of Items Storewide") is positioning copy rather than an offer. The result reads like a weekly circular without a clearance hook.
- The product mix is genuinely eclectic: $1.99 charger plates, $4.99 grapevine wreaths, $2.99 candy melts, $3.99 balloon helicopters, $10.99 sparkling drink sticks. Nothing coheres around a theme or recipient need. This is a warehouse dump with a logo on it.
- The July 7 send ("Dine In Style With 50% Off The Spring Shop®") scored 4/10 in part because the subject didn't match the body. This email flips the problem — the subject is so deliberately vague it makes the body mismatch unavoidable regardless of what's inside. That's not an upgrade.

## What's working

- **Free shipping callout is above the fold** — "$59 or More" threshold is immediately visible and creates a concrete incentive to add to cart.
- **"YOUR PRICE" sticker concept is clearly communicated** — the callout box in the hero explains the new pricing language before the grid, which is necessary onboarding for an unfamiliar program.
- **Specific dollar prices throughout** — every product tile has a hard price. No "up to X% off" ambiguity. That's a cleaner promise than a sale event.
- **No render bugs** — the two-column grid is intact, images load, text doesn't overlap.

## What's weak

- **Subject is a blind-box throwaway** — "Ready For More Savings? See Inside." tells the subscriber nothing. It's the most overused subject-line pattern in email marketing and signals low-effort creative.
- **No theme or category anchor** — grapevine wreaths, candy melts, charger plates, and balloon helicopters share no seasonal, lifestyle, or category logic. The eye has nowhere to land after the hero.
- **CTA sends to weekly ad, not to the offer** — "CLICK HERE TO VIEW OUR WEEKLY AD" is a redirect away from the email's stated premise. If the email is about everyday best prices, the CTA should land on an everyday-pricing landing page, not a rotating weekly circular.
- **No urgency signal whatsoever** — everyday pricing by definition has no deadline, but the email doesn't use that permanence as a selling point either. It just sits there with no reason to act now versus tomorrow.
- **Heavy cadence fatigue** — this is the fifth Hobby Lobby send in seven days. The subject's "Ready For More Savings?" phrasing implicitly acknowledges fatigue but doesn't resolve it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to name the value proposition** — "YOUR PRICE" is a brand-new program concept. The subject should introduce it, not tease around it. Something like "Introducing: YOUR PRICE — Our Best Price, Every Day" earns the open by giving subscribers a reason they haven't heard this week.
- 2. **Build the hero around one category, not 20 SKUs** — pick the three most compelling everyday-price examples (the $1.99 charger plate is actually interesting), build a lifestyle hero around them, and let the grid reinforce. Right now the hero and the grid are doing the same job at the same scale.
- 3. **Point the CTA at an everyday-price landing page** — "Shop Everyday Best Prices" beats "View Our Weekly Ad" when the email's premise is permanence over promotion.
- 4. **Anchor one sentence of urgency to the new program** — "These prices don't expire — but your cart does." Lean into the permanence as a feature, not a liability.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your Price. Every Day. No Sale Required.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Best Price Without Waiting For A Sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Look for the YOUR PRICE sticker — everyday best prices on thousands of items, no coupon needed.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Our everyday low prices are locked in — no sale, no expiration, just the best price storewide.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard Responsys/Oracle-generated HTML mail with functional table-based layout and Outlook VML conditionals. The primary technical concerns are missing authentication headers at the relay layer, a broken border-radius CSS value in a CTA style, and a missing `List-Unsubscribe` header — all of which affect deliverability and compliance posture.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text body is URL-saturated**
- QA finding: 13,022 of 15,614 characters (83%) in the plain-text part are URLs.
- This ratio is well above the 70% threshold and is a known spam-filter trigger. The plain-text alternate should contain human-readable content mirroring the HTML, not just a list of tracked redirect URLs.

**Link domain:** All links route through `link.hobbylobby.com` (click-tracking redirect). No broken redirect destinations were detectable from the truncated source, but the URL saturation in the plain-text part suggests the plain-text version was auto-generated from the link list rather than written separately.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text on tracked GIF**
- `eORqXTkAb7_837015858_129613_4798569_138479.gif` has no `alt` attribute.
- Evidence: `src="https://link.hobbylobby.com/mo/eORqXTkAb7_837015858_129613_4798569_138479.gif"`
- Screen readers will skip this element silently; if it is a tracking pixel this is acceptable, but if it carries any visual content (e.g., a promotional badge) the missing alt is an accessibility gap.

**Malformed border-radius on `.ctacr1` CTA**
```css
.ctacr1 {
  -moz-border-radius:px;
  -webkit-border-radius:px;
  border-radius:px;
}
```
- The radius value is empty (`px` with no number). The property will be ignored by all clients. If the design requires a rounded CTA button, this needs a numeric value (e.g., `4px`).

**Vertical-align typo in `.lhp2`**
```css
.lhp2, .lhp2 font { verticl-align:middle; }
```
- `verticl-align` is not a valid CSS property (missing `a`). Ignored by all clients. Unlikely to cause a visible regression since the block is also controlled by table `valign`, but should be corrected.

**Legacy doctype**
- `<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` with an XHTML namespace is a Responsys platform default. No active bug, but it forces quirks-mode parsing in some webmail clients.

**Viewport meta allows pinch-zoom** (`user-scalable=yes`) — correct and accessible.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
- QA finding: header not present as captured by the AgentMail relay.
- Gmail and Yahoo (as of Feb 2024 bulk sender requirements) require `List-Unsubscribe` for any sender exceeding 5,000 messages/day. Hobby Lobby almost certainly crosses this threshold. Absence of the header risks inbox placement demotion.
- This may be a relay-capture gap rather than a true omission — confirm against raw SMTP headers from the MTA.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent**
- One-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy) requires both `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`. If the sending platform (Responsys) is not injecting these, the brand is non-compliant with current inbox provider requirements.

**Physical address** — not verifiable from the truncated source. CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid postal address in every commercial email. Should be confirmed in the footer section.

**Unsubscribe mechanism** — an unsubscribe link is expected in the footer; its presence cannot be confirmed from the truncated source but no `<a>` with "unsubscribe" text is visible in the truncated portion. Needs verification against the full HTML.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All outbound links pass through `link.hobbylobby.com` (Responsys click-tracker). UTM parameter appending is handled at the redirect layer, so parameters on final destination URLs cannot be confirmed from source alone.

**Risk:** If the Responsys redirect configuration does not append `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` before forwarding to `hobbylobby.com`, session attribution in GA4/Adobe Analytics will be lost (sessions landing without UTMs will be bucketed as Direct).

This should be verified by clicking a sample link from a test send and inspecting the final URL.
