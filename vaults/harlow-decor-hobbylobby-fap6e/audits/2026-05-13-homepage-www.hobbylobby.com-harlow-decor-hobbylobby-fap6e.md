# Homepage snapshot · www.hobbylobby.com · 2026-05-13
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- Oh, Hobby Lobby knows their audience. The second this loaded I saw flower pots, a white garden bench, a garden goose — and 50% off the Spring Shop staring me right in the face. This is a brand that's been doing right by me for years, and this homepage is playing to its strengths: seasonal decor, a real discount, and a clean "here's what you need to do" button. Nothing flashy, nothing confusing. Very Hobby Lobby.

## What's working

- **The 50% off Spring Shop headline** — I know Hobby Lobby's rotating category sale like the back of my hand, and seeing it confirmed right here saves me the mental math. I was going to check this weekend anyway.
- **The hero image** — flower pots, a white slatted garden bench, floppy garden gloves, a duck garden statue. That is my Pinterest board. No styling trickery, no model I can't relate to — just the stuff I'd actually buy.
- **The sale end date** — "Sale Valid Online & In-Store through May 16, 2026" is printed right under the button. I don't have to hunt for it. That May 16 date is this Friday. That creates real urgency for me without feeling manipulative.
- **The "Weekly Ad" button** — it's the first tappable thing after the logo. I live by that weekly ad. Putting it front and center shows they understand how I shop.
- **Free shipping threshold** — $59 or more, stated clearly in the top banner. Reachable in one cart for me. Good.

## What's weak

- **No loyalty/rewards visibility** — I accumulate Hobby Lobby points. There's a person-silhouette icon in the header, but zero indication of my rewards balance or a "members save more" nudge. That's a missed re-engagement signal for someone like me.
- **"Shop Now" is generic** — Under all that Spring Shop context it works, but if this button said "Shop Spring Decor" or "Browse The Spring Shop" I'd feel even more pulled in. "Shop Now" is a placeholder.
- **No category quick-links below the hero** — From what I can see above the fold, it's hero image, big offer, one button. If I want Floral, Farmhouse, or Home Decor specifically, I have to either search or dig into the hamburger menu. One horizontal scroll of category chips would help.
- **No social proof** — Not a single review star, "best seller" badge, or "customers love" callout. For a $30–$80 decorative item, that matters.
- **The orange strip at the bottom is cut off** — I can see the top edge of what looks like another promo module, but it's cropped. Whatever's there isn't doing its job if I have to scroll to even see it exists.

## Recommendations

- **Add a rewards/loyalty strip just below the hero** — Even a one-line "You have 240 points" or "Rewards members: show this for an extra 10% off" would make me feel seen as a returning customer, not just a passerby.
- **Swap "Shop Now" for "Shop The Spring Shop"** — Five extra words, zero ambiguity, stronger connection to what I just read.
- **Add a horizontal category chip row below the hero** — Floral | Farmhouse | Garden | Seasonal. One tap to my aisle instead of fighting the hamburger nav.
- **Surface a "Trending in Spring Decor" rail below the fold** — Even three product thumbnails with a price and one review star count would bridge the gap from "interested" to "tapping through."
