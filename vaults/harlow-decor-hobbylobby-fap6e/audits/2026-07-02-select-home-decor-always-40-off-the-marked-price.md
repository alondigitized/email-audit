# Select Home Decor – Always 40% Off The Marked Price
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- This is the Hobby Lobby mega-circular in its standard form — the same stacked-tile layout that ran on June 25 ("Enhance Your Space With Select Home Decor"), June 24, and again on June 18. Twenty-plus department tiles from Home Decor down to Floral, each with a percentage off badge. Nothing about this email is wrong exactly; Hobby Lobby's audience knows this format. The problem is the subject line: "Always 40% Off The Marked Price" is their permanent pricing model, not a sale — using "Always" actively destroys any urgency the send might have had.
- The deeper miss is strategic. July 4th is two days away. The June 30 send ("Stars, Stripes & Savings") earned an 8/10 by going all-in on the holiday. This send buries a July 4th tile in the middle of the circular while leading with a generic home decor header and also, inexplicably, featuring Fall Floral and Classroom/Dorm content. That's three calendar moments competing in one email, which is the same diffusion problem as the June 28 "Fall Starts Now" send — seasonal incoherence erodes trust.
- The execution is technically clean: no broken images, consistent brand type, clear 40% off signals throughout. But sending a generic circular two days before a major holiday the brand just nailed is a cadence fumble. This should have been a second July 4th push, not a reset to the weekly digest.

## What's working

- **Consistent discount signage** — every tile has a clear, legible percentage badge; the offer is never ambiguous once you're inside the email.
- **No render bugs** — layout holds, images load, text doesn't overlap; technically clean execution.
- **Category breadth serves browsing** — for a subscriber who genuinely shops multiple Hobby Lobby departments, this format lets them skip to what's relevant.
- **Logo + brand colors immediately recognizable** — no confusion about who sent this.

## What's weak

- **"Always 40% Off" telegraphs permanent pricing, not a promotion** — subscribers who know Hobby Lobby know this is the standard shelf price; "Always" is the opposite of urgency.
- **July 4th tile is buried mid-scroll** — with the holiday 48 hours out, this is the single most time-sensitive offer in the email and it's not the hero.
- **Fall Floral and Classroom/Dorm content on July 2 creates seasonal whiplash** — three calendar moments (summer, July 4th, back-to-school/fall) in one email dilutes all of them.
- **Same mega-circular format as June 25** — nearly identical creative, nearly identical subject theme ("Select Home Decor"), negligible creative refresh.
- **No single CTA or anchor product** — twenty tiles with no editorial hierarchy means nothing is the hero.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with July 4th as the hero** — it's two days away; the June 30 send proved this creative converts. A second, tighter July 4th push (decor + entertaining + crafts only) would outperform a reset to the generic circular.
- 2. **Retire "Always" from subject lines** — it signals no deal. Replace with a deadline or a specific product category.
- 3. **Cut the Fall Floral and Classroom/Dorm tiles** — save them for the July 7 send; they create confusion here and dilute the holiday moment.
- 4. **Add a single editorial pick** — one featured product with a real price and link, above the tile grid, would drive click intent before the scroll fatigue sets in.
- 5. **Refresh the mega-circular cadence** — this format has now appeared essentially unchanged four times in eight days; subscribers are training themselves to ignore it.

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This is the Hobby Lobby mega-circular in its standard form — the same stacked-tile layout that ran on June 25 ("Enhance Your Space With Select Home Decor"), June 24, and again on June 18. The problem is the subject line: "Always 40% Off The Marked Price" is their permanent pricing model, not a sale — using "Always" actively destroys any urgency the send might have had.

The deeper miss is strategic. July 4th is two days away. The June 30 send ("Stars, Stripes & Savings") earned an 8/10 by going all-in on the holiday. That's three calendar moments competing in one email, which is the same diffusion problem as the June 28 "Fall Starts Now" send — seasonal incoherence erodes trust.

The execution is technically clean: no broken images, consistent brand type, clear 40% off signals throughout. But sending a generic circular two days before a major holiday the brand just nailed is a cadence fumble. This should have been a second July 4th push, not a reset to the weekly digest.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Bug: Malformed `.ctacr1` CSS rule**
```css
.ctacr1 {
  color:;                          /* empty value — invalid */
  border-radius:px;               /* missing numeric value — invalid */
  -moz-border-radius:px;
  -webkit-border-radius:px;
}
```
The `color:` property is empty and `border-radius:px` has no numeric value. Browsers/clients that don't silently discard malformed declarations may inherit unexpected color or fail to apply the border-radius. This affects any CTA button using `.ctacr1`.

**Bug: CSS typo in `.lhp2`**
```css
.lhp2, .lhp2 font {line-height:1.2; verticl-align:middle;}
```
`verticl-align` should be `vertical-align`. The vertical alignment will not apply in any client.

**Unreliable `vw` units in `.rh20`**
```css
.rh20, .rh20 div {height:20px !important; height:5.56vw !important;}
```
`vw` units are unsupported in Outlook, iOS Mail pre-iOS 10, and several webmail clients. The cascade ordering means `5.56vw` overrides `20px` in supporting clients — intended — but the behavior is inconsistent across the email client matrix without a tested fallback.
