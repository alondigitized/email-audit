# Select Home Decor – Always 40% Off The Marked Price
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-03**
## Executive summary

- This is Hobby Lobby reverting hard to type — the exact wall-to-wall category tile grid the brand has run daily since at least 08/23, now stretched to roughly 20 categories in one scroll (Home Decor, Textiles, Mirrors, Fall Decor, Christmas Decor/Trees/Floral/Crafts/Party/Lights, Football Party, Party, Floral, Hobbies, Wearable Art, Crafts, Frames, Paper Crafts, Yarn, Jewelry Making, Sewing Notions, Custom Frames, Furniture). Every prior audit flagged this exact format as generic and undifferentiated, and this send is the least differentiated of the bunch — it doesn't even have a hero image or seasonal styling shot like 08/28's tablescape or 09/01's Kinkeeping launch.
- The subject promise ("Select Home Decor – Always 40% Off") is technically true but buried: Home Decor is one tile among twenty, and the "Always 40%" framing (repeated on nearly every tile at 30/40/50%) reads as an everyday baseline price rather than a real sale, which erodes urgency across the whole email.
- Judgement: this is a low-effort, high-volume "everything store" blast, not a targeted send — it will get skimmed and deleted by anyone not already mid-project on a specific craft category.

## What's working

- Clean product photography on each tile (clocks, throws, quilt sets, Christmas trees, wreaths) — no broken images or render glitches.
- Discount badges (40%, 50%, 25%, 30%) are legible and consistently placed on every tile.
- "Free Shipping On Orders $59 Or More!" banner up top gives one clear, concrete incentive immediately visible.
- Footer social/contact icons and store info render cleanly with no layout breakage.

## What's weak

- No single hero or focal point — with 20 near-equal-weight sections, the eye has nowhere to land, contradicting the subject's implied focus on Home Decor.
- "Always 40% The Marked Price" language, repeated across nearly every category, signals this isn't a real promotion at all — just standing store pricing, which trains subscribers to ignore future "sale" language.
- Extreme length (20 categories in one vertical scroll) guarantees most recipients never scroll past the top 3-4 tiles.
- Christmas and football-party categories mixed into a "Fall Essentials" banner in early September reads as premature/cluttered rather than seasonally coherent.
- Same generic grid format as 08/30, 08/27, and 08/23 sends — zero creative differentiation from a brand that has shown it can do better (08/28 tablescape, 09/01 Kinkeeping).

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this to one hero category (Home Decor, matching the subject) with a real styled photo, 3-4 supporting tiles max, and a single clear CTA — stop trying to cram the entire store catalog into one email.
- 2. Drop "Always X% Off" language entirely or replace with a real time-bound offer; "always" discounts read as fake urgency and undermine trust.
- 3. If cross-selling other categories, move them below the fold as a compact "Shop More" strip, not full-width repeated sections.
- 4. Separate fall/everyday content from Christmas content — bundling Christmas trees and football party supplies under a "Fall Essentials" banner in early September confuses the seasonal signal.
