# Find What You Love With 66% Off The Spring Shop®
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- This email opens better than Hobby Lobby's recent mega-circulars: there's a genuine lifestyle hero image anchoring a specific 66% off headline, which is a meaningful step up from the pure discount-badge-on-white-tile approach that scored 4s on July 7, 9, and 12. The Spring Shop brand is at least consistent with past sends, and the free shipping callout at the very top is a smart conversion lever.
- The problem starts immediately below the hero. The Spring subcategory grid (Furniture, Outdoor, Entertaining, Garden, Table, Statues) fades into a second tier of totally unrelated department tiles — Home Decor 40%, Summer Toys 66%, Fall 40%, Dorm Essentials 40%, All Fabric 30%, Classroom Decor 40% — and ends with a 75% Online Clearance section. That's five distinct campaign themes in one scroll. It's not "find what you love," it's "hunt through everything we have."
- The larger miss is seasonal. It's July 14. Running a Spring Shop hero when summer is fully underway — and when the very same email promotes dorm and classroom decor — signals editorial calendar slippage. Hobby Lobby ran the "X% Off The Spring Shop®" hook on July 7 too, and it scored a 4. The hero here is better, but the skeleton is identical. Call it a 6: real improvement at the top, same structural problems everywhere else.

## What's working

- Lifestyle hero image (florals, pottery, warm spring objects) is an actual photograph, not a discount badge on white — a genuine visual upgrade over the July 9/12 tile dumps.
- 66% is a concrete, high-discount anchor that earns attention in the inbox.
- "SHOP NOW" CTA button is visible and unambiguous in the hero section.
- Free shipping threshold ("Free Shipping On Orders $59 Or More!") is called out at the very top, before the subscriber has to hunt for it.
- Spring subcategory grid (six spring sub-departments) gives the hero section structural depth rather than a dead-end single link.

## What's weak

- Spring Shop is seasonally stale on July 14 — this campaign concept should have wrapped by late June.
- The below-the-fold tile dump (six department tiles + clearance) is the same kitchen-sink format Hobby Lobby has sent at least five times this month; it dilutes the lead offer.
- "Find What You Love" is an empty emotional hook that promises personalization and delivers a department store circular.
- Discounts spanning 30%–75% in one email make none of them feel special.
- No deadline or urgency signal anywhere — nothing to motivate clicking today over ignoring until later.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the Spring Shop hero and pivot to a Summer/Fall Transition anchor** — mid-July is "summer ends soon + back-to-school starts now." Lead with that editorial beat; the tile mix would feel intentional instead of stale.
- 2. **Collapse the below-the-fold tiles from six departments to three** — Summer, Dorm Essentials, and one craft category. Cut the rest.
- 3. **Add an expiry date** — "Ends Sunday" or "This Week Only" at the hero level would dramatically lift urgency without a single other change.
- 4. **Replace "SHOP NOW" with category-specific CTA copy** — "Shop Spring Clearance" or "Shop 66% Off" gives the button actual information.
- 5. **Write a real preheader** — the current screenshot gives no evidence of a preheader; if it's blank or leaking junk, that's a wasted inbox impression.
- **Subject Alt A:** `66% Off Spring — Before Summer Takes Over`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Spring Clearance + Dorm Head Start`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spring clearance, summer finds, and dorm picks — all this week only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `66% off Spring Shop items + dorm essentials arriving now.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard MSO/VML table-based HTML build sent through Hobby Lobby's ESP (attributes suggest Salesforce Marketing Cloud). Four QA warnings require attention: missing compliance headers, an invalid CSS rule on a CTA element, and a plain-text body that is almost entirely URLs — all of which are live deliverability and rendering risks.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

No issues found with click-tracking structure — all links appear to route through `link.hobbylobby.com` (ESP redirect/tracking domain), which is expected.

**One finding:**
- The open-tracking pixel `eORqXTkAb7_837015858_129946_4719632_138650.gif` is also hosted on `link.hobbylobby.com`. No `alt` attribute is present on this element. Minor deliverability risk if image-blocking clients render a broken-image icon instead of nothing.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS bugs (confirmed in source):**

| Selector | Issue | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `.ctacr1` | `color:` has no value — empty property | `color:; font-weight:normal;` |
| `.ctacr1` | `border-radius` missing unit on all three vendor prefixes | `-moz-border-radius:px; -webkit-border-radius:px; border-radius:px` |
| `.lhp2` | Typo: `verticl-align` instead of `vertical-align` | `.lhp2, .lhp2 font {line-height:1.2; verticl-align:middle;}` |

The `.ctacr1` rule is applied to at least one CTA button (`background-color:#013088`). The missing `border-radius` value will cause that rule to be silently dropped in standards-compliant clients — rounded corners will not render. The missing `color` value means the text color on that CTA is inherited rather than explicitly set, which may or may not produce the intended white-on-blue appearance depending on cascade order.

**Font fallback:**
- `.ff3ng` declares `Museo Sans` as the first font family. Museo Sans is not a system font and no `@font-face` or web font `<link>` is present in the truncated `<head>`. All clients except Apple Mail (which supports some Google Fonts via remote CSS) will fall back to `Trebuchet MS`. Not a bug, but worth noting if the design depends on Museo Sans rendering.

**Accessibility:**
- Confirmed missing `alt` text on `eORqXTkAb7_..._.gif` (QA finding). Tracking pixels conventionally use `alt=""` to suppress broken-image display — this element omits the attribute entirely.
- Cannot audit remaining images from the truncated source; full-source check recommended.

**Responsive breakpoint:**
- The only declared `@media` query triggers at `max-width:300px` — an unusually narrow threshold. Most mobile email clients reflow at 375–600px. If there are no additional media queries beyond the truncated point, the email likely does not reflow on standard mobile viewports.
