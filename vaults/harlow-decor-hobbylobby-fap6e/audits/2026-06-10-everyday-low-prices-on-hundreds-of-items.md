# Everyday Low Prices On Hundreds Of Items!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-10**
## Executive summary

- This is Hobby Lobby's "everyday pricing" email — a product dump at regular, non-discounted prices with no promotional hook. After back-to-back July 4th sale emails on June 7 and June 9 (both running "40% Off July 4th Decor"), this send abruptly pivots to a "YOUR PRICE sticker" concept that signals no discount at all. The word "everyday" in the subject is literally the antonym of urgency, which makes this the weakest hook in the brand's recent audit history.
- The visual structure follows Hobby Lobby's now-exhausted template: branded header → "Spring Essentials" category tiles → wide white-background headline → ~20-item product grid. The product mix is also incoherent: candy wafers, AA batteries, a balloon helium tank, foam bricks, and iron-on vinyl share the same grid under a "Spring Essentials" banner that doesn't connect to any of them.
- Verdict: skip-worthy. Coming off two event-driven July 4th promos, this email offers neither seasonal excitement nor category specificity. It lands as filler, and the 18th-ish email in 18 days from this brand makes the cadence fatigue worse.

## What's working

- Free shipping threshold ($59+) is prominently badged at the very top — a concrete, specific benefit front and center.
- Specific dollar prices on every product give subscribers something tangible to evaluate even without a sale framing.
- The "CLICK HERE TO VIEW OUR WEEKLY AD" CTA is high-contrast, large, and unambiguous.
- No render bugs — all product images load, text is legible, two-column grid is intact throughout.
- Brand visual identity (logo, orange palette, typography) is consistent and instantly recognizable.

## What's weak

- "Everyday Low Prices" is explicitly a non-sale; the subject implies a deal that doesn't exist, which erodes trust on open.
- The "YOUR PRICE Sticker" concept is opaque jargon — new subscribers won't know what it means, and no explanation is given in the body.
- Subject says "Hundreds Of Items" but the email renders ~20; that's a visible overpromise within the same send.
- Pivoting away from the July 4th momentum built in the June 7 and June 9 sends — with no bridge copy — wastes the runway those emails created.
- The "Spring Essentials" header is off-cadence for a brand that spent the prior week in summer/Fourth-of-July mode, and none of the 20 products in the grid look like spring items.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add comparative pricing to at least the hero products.** "Reg. $X → YOUR PRICE $Y" on three to four items makes the "everyday best price" claim tangible. Without a from/to or a % callout, this email is functionally a catalog page with no incentive to act now.
- 2. **Bridge back to the July 4th momentum.** The last two emails built event-driven urgency; killing it cold with "everyday prices" is a wasted lead. One sentence — "Stocking up for the Fourth? No coupon needed — these are always our best prices" — connects the thread.
- 3. **Cut the product grid in half and add category CTAs.** Twenty miscellaneous small-format products at identical layout depth flatten the eye. Lead with four to six hero products per section with a "Shop Floral Foam," "Shop Adhesives" button beneath each cluster.
- 4. **Explain "YOUR PRICE" in one sentence.** "These are our permanent low prices — no coupon, no catch" removes the jargon barrier immediately below the badge.
- **Subject Alt A:** `No Coupon Needed — These Are Always Our Best Prices`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$1.99 Phone Chargers, $4.99 Foam Bricks + 18 More`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping on $59+ — shop permanent low prices, no code required`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Glue guns, foam, vinyl, batteries — see what's always priced low`
