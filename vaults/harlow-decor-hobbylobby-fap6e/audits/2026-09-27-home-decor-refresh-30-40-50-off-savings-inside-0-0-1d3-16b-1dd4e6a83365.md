# Home Decor Refresh – 30%, 40%, 50% Off Savings Inside!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-27**
## Executive summary

- This is the exact same category-tile wall Hobby Lobby has been running since late August, now stretched to a genuinely absurd length — roughly 30 stacked blocks running from Home Decor through Furniture, with zero hero image, zero lifestyle photography, and zero visual breathing room. The "Fall Essentials" free-shipping banner at the very top is the only concession to seasonal storytelling; everything below it is a flat grid of product photography and repeated "40%" and "50%" badges. It's functionally a category directory, not a marketing email.
- Compare this to 2026-09-23's "Say Hello To Pumpkin Season" send, which at least opened with a genuine styled hero (scarecrow, pumpkins, sunflowers) before falling into the same tile grid — that email earned a 7 because the first screen did real work. This one skips the hero entirely and goes straight to tiles, which is a step backward even by this brand's own recent standard.
- Judgement: this is a catalog scan dressed as an email, and it's one of the weaker sends in the recent run — it will perform on raw frequency/habit clicks from existing categorical shoppers, not on anything this email itself does to earn attention.

## What's working

- The free-shipping banner ("Free Shipping On Orders $59 Or More!") is legible and sits above the fold.
- Discount badges (40%, 50%, 30%) are large, high-contrast, and easy to scan at a glance.
- Category labels are bolded and consistently placed above each product row, so the grid is at least internally organized.
- No visible render bugs — images load, text doesn't overlap, nothing looks broken.

## What's weak

- No hero image or lifestyle photography anywhere — the email opens directly into a product grid, unlike the 09-22 and 09-23 sends that led with a styled scene.
- The subject promises "Home Decor Refresh" but Home Decor is one category among ~30; Christmas, Football, Jewelry Making, Sewing Notions, and Furniture are all crammed in, diluting the stated focus entirely.
- Extreme length (30+ stacked tiles) means there is no visual hierarchy — nothing tells the eye where to land first, and most readers will bail before reaching Furniture at the bottom.
- Several sections show "Always" repeated with no real urgency mechanic (e.g., "ALWAYS 40% Off The Marked Price"), which reads as static pricing rather than a limited-time promo, undercutting the subject's "Savings Inside!" framing.
- The Wearable Art "$3.42" price paired with "20%" off looks like a rounding artifact of a reference-price display, not a compelling call-out — this kind of oddly-specific price appearing this large is a credibility ding.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with a real hero tied to the subject line.** If the subject says "Home Decor Refresh," open with a styled room shot and a single dominant CTA, the way the 09-22 Christmas send did — then let the category grid follow below for browsers, not as the entire email.
- 2. **Cut the grid to 8-10 categories max, or split into two sends.** A 30-tile scroll has no chance of sustained attention; segment by intent (Home Decor + Textiles + Frames in one send, Christmas/Holiday in another) instead of dumping the whole catalog.
- 3. **Replace "Always 40% Off" language with time-bound framing.** Even if the discount is evergreen, pair it with an end-dated hook ("This week only: bring it home for the holidays") so the offer feels like news, not permanent price.
- 4. **Fix or drop the oddly-specific $3.42 Wearable Art price.** Round it or replace with a percentage-only badge to avoid the reference-pricing look.
- 5. **Subject/preheader rework** to reduce the mismatch between promise and content:
- **Subject Alt A:** `Home Decor Just Got 40% Off — Shop the Refresh`
- **Subject Alt B:** `30-50% Off Fall Decor, Frames & More (This Week)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New wall art, mirrors, clocks and textiles — all marked down.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus: free shipping on orders $59+.`
