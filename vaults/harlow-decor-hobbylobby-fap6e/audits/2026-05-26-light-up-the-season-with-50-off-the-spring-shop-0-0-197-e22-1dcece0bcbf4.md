# Light Up The Season With 50% Off The Spring Shop®
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-26**
## Executive summary

- "Light Up The Season" is Hobby Lobby's eighth email in ten days, and it arrives with a fundamental structural problem: the 50% off Spring Shop offer is a verbatim repeat of the May 19 "Picnic Party" send that scored 8/10. Any subscriber who opened that one already knows — and either bought or moved on. The new subject line creates a specific expectation of lighting-focused content (outdoor lanterns, string lights, summer ambiance), and the hero product — a teal outdoor lantern — actually supports that frame. Then the body dumps the same cascading department grid anyway.
- The email is competently executed. The 50% callout is visually dominant, the department tiles are clean, and the Summer Toys category (50% off) is a new beat that signals the seasonal pivot to summer. The "6,300+ items" figure in the Spring Shop section is useful scale context that almost no Hobby Lobby send has communicated before. Those are genuine wins.
- But the cadence is the story here. Eight sends in ten days, with the lead offer recycled from a week ago. Even a well-executed email bleeds open rate and click rate in that cadence window. Hobby Lobby is burning subscriber goodwill at a pace that will show up in unsubscribe spikes within the next two to three cycles.

## What's working

- **"Light Up The Season" subject is the brand's best copy in this run** — specific, seasonal, and evocative in a way "Your Weekly Ad Is Here!" never is.
- **Hero product (teal outdoor lantern) directly supports the subject line** — this is the rare Hobby Lobby send where the hero image and subject line are in sync.
- **"6,300+ items" in the Spring Shop section** provides rare, useful context for a shopper deciding whether the category is worth browsing.
- **Summer Toys at 50%** is a new seasonal beat that correctly anticipates the early-June mindset — this is timely and hasn't appeared in recent sends.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no empty placeholder fields visible.

## What's weak

- **The 50% Spring Shop offer is a direct replay of the May 19 "Picnic Party" send** — same discount, same named shop, same department grid below it. Seven days is not enough reset time.
- **The "Light Up The Season" framing promises a curated lighting edit; the body delivers the same 8-department dump.** The subject sets a specific expectation the layout doesn't honor.
- **"Free Shipping On Orders $39 Or More!" leaks as the preheader** — instead of complementing the subject's seasonal energy, the inbox preview reverts to a transactional utility message.
- **No deadline or urgency mechanism is visible** — the seasonal frame implies now-or-miss-it but nothing in the body makes that explicit.
- **Eight sends in ten days.** Cadence fatigue is the biggest delivery risk this brand faces right now, and this send does nothing to signal a pause or a gear-shift.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Run a dedicated lighting edit, not a weekly-ad repurpose.** The "Light Up The Season" concept is strong. A send that leads with 3-5 lighting SKUs (lanterns, string lights, Edison bulbs, pathway lights), each with a direct product link, would convert the good subject into actual clicks. The current body is a bait-and-switch at the creative level.
- 2. **Rest the Spring Shop 50% offer for at least 10 more days.** Resending the same offer a week after it already ran degrades urgency credibility for future sends. If inventory goals require another push, at minimum add a hard end-date ("Ends Sunday") so there's a reason to act now rather than next week's email.
- 3. **Fix the preheader.** "Free Shipping On Orders $39 Or More!" is not preheader copy — it's body text leaking into inbox preview. Wire in copy that actually extends the subject line.
- 4. **Reduce frequency or segment.** Eight sends in ten days is a retention risk. A curated "best-of-the-sale" weekly digest would serve engaged subscribers better than daily blasts of the same grid.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Outdoor Lanterns, String Lights & More — 50% Off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Spring Shop Is Ending Soon — 50% Off This Week Only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Outdoor lanterns, patio lights, and 6,300+ spring items — half off`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Everything labeled Spring Shop is 50% off — shop before it's gone`

## Technical Audit — Hobby Lobby "Light Up The Season / Spring Shop® 50% Off"

### 1. Technical Summary

The email renders from a standard ESP-generated HTML 4.01 Transitional template with Outlook VML fallbacks. Two CSS defects (a malformed `border-radius` and a typo in `vertical-align`) will silently break CTA button styling and layout in affected clients. The more critical finding is that the plain-text part is 85% URLs by character count, a known deliverability risk flag.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Tracking pixel — missing alt attribute**
The open-tracking pixel has no `alt` attribute:
```
src: https://link.hobbylobby.com/mo/eORqXTkAb7_837015858_124834_4920166_133429.gif
```
QA confirms: `[WARN] Image missing alt text`. Without `alt=""`, some clients (Apple Mail image-blocking, screen readers) will render a broken-image icon or expose the raw URL. Should be `alt=""`.

**Click tracking domain**
All links wrap through `link.hobbylobby.com`. The truncated HTML prevents full UTM verification (see §6), but the redirect chain itself is standard.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed `border-radius` on CTA button — `.ctacr1`**
```css
.ctacr1 {
  -moz-border-radius:px;
  -webkit-border-radius:px;
  border-radius:px;
}
```
The numeric value is missing — `px` with no number is invalid CSS. All three prefixed declarations will be dropped. The CTA button will render with square corners on non-Outlook clients regardless of design intent.

**CSS typo — `.lhp2`**
```css
.lhp2, .lhp2 font {line-height:1.2; verticl-align:middle;}
```
`verticl-align` is not a valid CSS property (`vertical-align` is). The vertical alignment rule is silently ignored on every element using this class.

**Responsive breakpoint at 300px**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width:300px) { ... }
```
The single fluid breakpoint fires at 300px. The industry standard for email reflow is 480px or 600px. Devices between 301–599px (the majority of mobile viewports) will receive the desktop layout unmodified — no column stacking, no fluid images.

**Web font reference without `@font-face`**
`.ff3ng` includes `Museo Sans` in its stack but no `@font-face` declaration is present in the truncated source. Museo Sans will silently fall back to Trebuchet MS in all clients. Not a bug if intentional, but confirms the font stack is decorative.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No issues found. No unrendered merge tokens (`{{`, `%`, `[%`) are visible in the HTML source.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header — not captured**
QA: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found`. The QA note attributes this to AgentMail relay interception. However, the absence is still a risk: Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policies (enforced since February 2024) require a `List-Unsubscribe` header with a one-click POST endpoint on all commercial senders over 5k/day.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) — absent**
QA: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found`. Without this header, Gmail's "Unsubscribe" button in the UI falls back to loading a URL rather than firing a silent POST — increasing friction and reducing compliance with Google's one-click unsubscribe mandate.

**Authentication-Results — not captured**
QA: `[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found`. SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from this relay capture. If `link.hobbylobby.com` is a separate sending domain from `create.hobbylobby.com`, a DKIM alignment mismatch is possible.

**CAN-SPAM physical address**
Not verifiable from truncated HTML — footer content was cut off. Assumed present given the sender's scale, but should be confirmed in full source review.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters — unverifiable**
The HTML is truncated before destination URLs are exposed through the `link.hobbylobby.com` redirect wrapper. UTM coverage cannot be confirmed. This is a gap in the QA capture, not necessarily a bug in the email.

**Plain-text part — 85% URL content**
QA: `[WARN] Plain-text is >70% URLs: 12682/14933 chars are URLs`. The plain-text alternative is nearly entirely composed of raw redirect URLs, providing no readable content. Spam filters (SpamAssassin `URI_HEX`, Barracuda) penalize plain-text parts dominated by URLs. This is the highest-severity deliverability risk in this send.
