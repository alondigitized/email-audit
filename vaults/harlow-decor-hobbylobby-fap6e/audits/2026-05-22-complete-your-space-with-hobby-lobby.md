# Complete Your Space With Hobby Lobby®
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Hobby Lobby's fifth send in six days, and it follows the exact same weekly-ad-digest skeleton as the May 17, May 19, and May 21 emails — department-level discount tiles stacked below a lifestyle hero. The wrinkle here is the "Bedroom Refresh" hero image, which gives the send a vaguely editorial feel and is the most on-theme creative Hobby Lobby has deployed in this run. But the subject line completely squanders it: "Complete Your Space With Hobby Lobby®" has no offer, no urgency, and no specificity — it reads like a tagline, not a reason to open.
- The discount grid below the hero (40% Floral, 40% Hobbies, 50% Summer Toys, 40% Party, 30% All Fabric, and at least one Spring Shop callout) is the same cadence dump the brand has been running all week. There's no escalation, no new offer, and no reason this email couldn't have been the May 21 send with a different hero image.
- The bedroom lifestyle photo is the strongest single element in this email. The subject line is the single biggest drag. That gap is where this send loses.

## What's working

- **"Bedroom Refresh" hero lands well** — a real lifestyle staging image that grounds the email in a tangible home decor context, a notch above the generic Spring Essentials banners used earlier this week.
- **40%/50% discount callouts are visible** — the department-level tiles communicate value clearly at a glance even if they're not offer-first.
- **Free shipping bar persists at top** — low-friction, trust-building, always a good anchor.
- **Subject line character count is fine** (~38 chars) — fits mobile preview without truncation.

## What's weak

- **Subject has zero offer signal** — "Complete Your Space With Hobby Lobby®" is brand copy, not a reason to open. Compare to the May 19 "Picnic Party! 50% Off The Spring Shop®" which put the number in the subject.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is send five in six days; a subscriber who got the May 17, 19, 20, and 21 emails will recognize the grid layout before they read a word.
- **Hero lifestyle image doesn't anchor to a specific discount** — "Bedroom Refresh" feels like a campaign theme, but nothing in the hero says "40% off everything in this room" — the payoff is buried in the tile grid below.
- **No urgency or deadline visible anywhere** — no "ends Sunday," no "this week only," nothing to create a reason to act now versus waiting.
- **Spring Shop® grid tile reappears again** — this is now the third consecutive send featuring a Spring Shop callout; the repetition degrades perceived scarcity.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the number in the subject line.** The bedroom creative is strong enough to anchor a specific offer. Leading with "40% Off Bedroom Decor" or "Bedroom Refresh — 40% Off" turns a tagline into a reason to open.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Bedroom Refresh — 40% Off Home Decor`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Complete the room: 40% off bedding, frames & more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New spring styles for every room — plus 50% off Summer Toys this week only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the Bedroom Refresh edit → then 40% off Floral, Fabric & more inside.`
- 2. **Add a deadline to at least one hero offer.** Even "Offer valid through Sunday" on the Bedroom Refresh tile would create urgency the current send entirely lacks.
- 3. **Anchor the bedroom hero to a single featured product or room kit.** Pick three to five products visible in the lifestyle shot and link them directly. Right now the hero is decoration — it doesn't drive a click.
- 4. **Thin the department tile grid to five maximum.** Six category tiles at 40% off dilutes each one. Rotate three featured this week; save the rest for next send — which given cadence will arrive in roughly 36 hours anyway.
- 5. **Break the cadence.** After five sends in six days, the sixth should either skip or look distinctly different (a single-SKU spotlight, a customer story, a how-to) to reset subscriber attention before the next weekly ad drop.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text body URL density — HIGH RISK**
QA reports 14,538 of 16,742 plain-text characters are URLs (87%). Spam filters (SpamAssassin, Outlook Exchange) penalize plain-text parts that are overwhelmingly link lists with no prose. This is almost certainly auto-generated from the HTML and never humanized.

**Tracking pixel missing alt**
```
https://link.hobbylobby.com/mo/eORqXTkAb7_837015858_124176_4818631_134164.gif
```
Image has no `alt` attribute. While a 1×1 pixel tracker doesn't require descriptive alt text, a missing `alt=""` (explicit empty) causes some clients to render a broken-image placeholder.

**Redirect domain**

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS bug — empty `color` value on CTA**
```css
.ctacr1 {color:; ...}
```
Empty property value. Browsers/clients will ignore or inherit — CTA text color is undefined and will vary by client.

**CSS bug — malformed `border-radius` on CTA**
```css
.ctacr1 {
  -moz-border-radius:px;
  -webkit-border-radius:px;
  border-radius:px;
}
```
All three declarations are missing the numeric value. The CTA button rendered via `.ctacr1` will have sharp corners in all clients regardless of design intent.

**CSS typo — `vertical-align` misspelled**
```css
.lhp2, .lhp2 font {line-height:1.2; verticl-align:middle;}
```
`verticl-align` is not a valid property. Vertical alignment for `.lhp2` elements will not apply; layout impact depends on which elements use this class.

**Viewport meta — missing `width=device-width`**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="initial-scale=1, user-scalable=yes">
```
Standard responsive email practice includes `width=device-width`. Without it, iOS Mail and some Android clients may default to desktop viewport width before the media queries fire. Low severity but addressable.

**Alt text**
The tracking GIF lacks `alt=""`. All other image alt coverage cannot be assessed from the truncated source.
