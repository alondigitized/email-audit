# Sunny Days Ahead ☀️ 50% Off Spring & Summer
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- "Sunny Days Ahead" is Hobby Lobby's tenth email in roughly ten days, and the pattern is now impossible to ignore: it is the same cascading department-dump skeleton that appeared on May 27, May 26, May 24, May 22, May 21, May 20, and May 17. The subject line does the right thing — "Sunny Days Ahead ☀️ 50% Off Spring & Summer" is fresh, seasonal, and clean — but the moment you open it, the illusion collapses. The Spring Shop® 50% off tile is a word-for-word repeat of the May 26 send, which was itself flagged as a repeat of May 19. The 75% clearance tile buried mid-scroll is the only genuinely new signal in the body, and it's easy to miss in a wall of 40% department labels.
- The structural problem here isn't any single section — it's fatigue. A subscriber who has opened even two of the last ten sends has seen virtually every tile, every percentage, and every department listed in this email. The subject buys goodwill in the inbox; the body immediately spends it.
- Clear judgement: competent execution of a badly overused template, sent one (or six) times too many. The 75% clearance angle deserved its own dedicated send.

## What's working

- Subject line is the strongest in the recent run — "Sunny Days Ahead" is evocative, the ☀️ is restrained (one emoji, not a stack), and "50% Off Spring & Summer" gives the concrete offer upfront.
- FREE SHIPPING on orders over $50 is surfaced near the top — a genuine friction-reducer for the category.
- The 75% off Home Decor clearance tile is a legitimately stronger offer than the usual 40% baseline; it stands out if you reach it.
- The department stacks are consistently labeled and scannable — if you know what you need, the email functions as a lookup table.

## What's weak

- The Spring Shop® 50% tile is a verbatim repeat of the May 26 send (itself a repeat of May 19) — three fires from the same rocket.
- No single hero product or lifestyle image anchors the scroll; the email is 100% text tiles and micro-thumbnails.
- Zero time-bound urgency — no "ends Sunday," no countdown, no expiration — which undercuts any impulse to act now vs. next week.
- Cadence has reached unsustainable density: ten sends in ten days means the subscriber's inbox looks like a Hobby Lobby flyer stack, and unsubscribe pressure will be real.
- The 75% clearance angle is buried mid-scroll rather than leading the email, where it would have commanded the most attention.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the 75% clearance hero, not 40% Home Decor.** 75% off is the sharpest number in this email; it should be the subject hook and the above-fold visual. Right now it's wasted mid-scroll.
- 2. **Add a hard end date.** One line — "Prices valid through Sunday, June 1" — converts a passive browse into a timed decision.
- 3. **Collapse the department list to 4-5 curated categories.** Fifteen categories at 40% off reads as "everything is always on sale" — which kills urgency. Pick the 4 most seasonally relevant and cut the rest.
- 4. **Rotate the Spring Shop® offer off.** It has now run three consecutive times unchanged. Retire it for one cycle or reframe it with a new visual and countdown.
- 5. **Add a preheader that doesn't repeat the subject.** The current render likely leaks navigation junk into inbox preview; a crisp "Clearance hits 75% — plus free shipping over $50" would do real lift.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 75% Off — Biggest Clearance of the Season`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Clearance Alert: 75% Off Home Decor This Week Only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Clearance hits 75% off + free shipping on orders over $50`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Summer's here — save up to 75% before stock runs out`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Hobby Lobby: "Sunny Days Ahead ☀️ 50% Off Spring & Summer"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email renders on a legacy HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype with Outlook/VML conditional blocks and a standard ESP redirect/tracking stack. QA flagged four warnings: missing `List-Unsubscribe` headers, unknown SPF/DKIM authentication status, a GIF missing alt text, and a plain-text part that is >88% URLs — all addressable.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text part is nearly all URLs (88.3%)**
- QA measured 11,787 of 13,351 plain-text characters as URLs.
- Ratio this high is a strong spam-filter trigger (SpamAssassin `RAZOR2_CF_RANGE_51_100`, Barracuda heuristics). Plain-text should be a readable prose version of the email, not a raw URL dump.
- Likely cause: ESP auto-generated the plain-text from the HTML without stripping redirect-wrapped links.

No broken or malformed links were detectable from the truncated source, but all outbound links route through `link.hobbylobby.com` (ESP redirect domain), which is expected.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text on tracked GIF**
- `<img src="https://link.hobbylobby.com/mo/eORqXTkAb7_837015858_125508_549324_133309.gif">` has no `alt` attribute per QA.
- This is almost certainly the open-pixel/tracking beacon. Standard practice is `alt=""` (empty string) to suppress screen-reader noise and suppress broken-image chrome when images are blocked.

**CSS typo: `verticl-align`**
- `.lhp2, .lhp2 font { verticl-align:middle; }` — misspelled property will silently no-op in all clients. Low impact for this property, but confirms the stylesheet has not been linted.

**Doctype: HTML 4.01 Transitional**
- Not an error — this is the standard for broad email client compatibility, including older Outlook. No action needed.

**VML / MSO conditional blocks**
- Present and correctly structured (`<!--[if (gte mso 9)]>`). No issues found.

**Viewport meta**
- `user-scalable=yes` is set correctly (accessibility requirement; `no` would be a violation).

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No exposed or unresolved merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `*|FNAME|*`) are visible in the truncated source. `e-locale="en-US"` and `e-is-multilanguage="false"` are ESP metadata attributes — no issues.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not present**
- QA warning: header not captured by the AgentMail relay. CAN-SPAM does not mandate this header, but Gmail and Yahoo (as of Feb 2024 bulk sender requirements) require it for senders above 5,000/day. If Hobby Lobby qualifies as a bulk sender, absence will cause Gmail to suppress the one-click unsubscribe UI and may affect inbox placement.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click) is also absent — required alongside `List-Unsubscribe` to satisfy the Gmail/Yahoo 2024 mandate.

**Authentication-Results header not found**
- SPF and DKIM pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from the captured headers. The sending domain is `create.hobbylobby.com`; if DKIM is not signed for this subdomain, it will fail DMARC alignment and degrade deliverability at major providers.
- This is a relay capture gap (AgentMail) rather than a confirmed failure, but it should be verified directly against raw headers from a delivered copy.

**Unsubscribe link in body**
- Not visible in the truncated source, but standard for Hobby Lobby emails. Cannot confirm or deny from the provided HTML fragment. If absent, that is a CAN-SPAM violation.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All links route through `link.hobbylobby.com` redirect domain. UTM parameters are not visible in the pre-redirect URLs (they may be appended post-redirect at the ESP layer). This cannot be confirmed as missing without following the redirect chain, but it warrants verification — if UTMs are absent, campaign attribution in GA4/Adobe Analytics will be lost.

No landing page was loaded for this audit; continuity of offer (50% off Spring & Summer) to destination page cannot be confirmed.
