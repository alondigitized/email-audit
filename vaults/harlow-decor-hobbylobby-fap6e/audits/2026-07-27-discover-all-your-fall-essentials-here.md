# Discover All Your Fall Essentials Here!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-27**
## Executive summary

- This is Hobby Lobby's cleanest Fall creative to date — a genuine lookbook format instead of the mega-circular grid they've leaned on all month. The hero ("Fall Essentials / 40% Off Fall" over a styled mantel shot) sets a warm, editorial tone, and every section below it (Kitchen & Dining, Floral, Pumpkins, Wall & Table Decor, Pillows, Outdoor Decor) follows the same visual formula: full-bleed lifestyle photo, category name, one "Shop Now" button. It reads more like a catalog than a coupon blast.
- The problem is the subject line undersells all of that work. "Discover All Your Fall Essentials Here!" is the vaguest possible framing for an email that actually leads with a hard 40%-off number — that number belongs in the subject, not buried in the hero art. It also lands one week after the July 21 "Bring Cozy Vibes – 40% Off Fall!" send with the same 40%-off Fall hook, so a regular subscriber has seen this exact discount pitched twice now.
- Net: strong creative execution, weak subject strategy, and a cadence problem the brand keeps repeating.

## What's working

- Hero is legible and hierarchy-correct — "40% Off Fall" and "Shop Lookbook" are the first thing the eye hits.
- The lookbook structure (repeating photo → category label → single CTA) is far more cohesive than Hobby Lobby's typical stacked-tile circulars.
- Category breadth (kitchen, floral, pumpkins, wall decor, pillows, outdoor) gives every likely shopper a relevant entry point.
- No render bugs — all images load, no overlapping text, buttons are consistent throughout.
- Visual styling (warm woodgrain, plaid, candlelight) is on-brand and consistent scene to scene.

## What's weak

- Subject line doesn't mention the 40% off — the single strongest piece of copy in the whole email is invisible until you open it.
- "Discover" is filler CTA language, not a concrete hook — no product, no urgency, no number.
- No deadline anywhere — "40% Off Fall" with no end date reads like a standing markdown, not a limited promotion (consistent with the brand's habit of open-ended discount language flagged in the July 23 "Always 40% Off" review).
- Every section links to a category page, not a specific product — nothing gives a shopper a reason to click *this* item versus browsing the site directly.
- Fine print at the footer ("Discounts provided every day, marked prices reflect general U.S. market value...") undercuts the "40% Off Fall" headline — it's the same everyday-discount disclaimer language as prior sends.
- This is the second "40% Off Fall" framing in eight days (July 21 → now), which reads as repetition rather than escalation.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the 40% in the subject.** This is the single highest-leverage fix — the offer is the whole email's value prop and it's currently absent from the one line that decides opens.
- 2. Add a credible deadline to the hero ("Ends Sunday") so the discount doesn't read as evergreen/everyday.
- 3. Link at least one or two hero tiles (Pumpkins, Floral) to a specific bestselling SKU instead of a category landing page — give people something concrete to want.
- 4. Vary the Fall hook from the July 21 send — same "40% Off Fall" language two sends running flattens the campaign; rotate to a specific category lead (e.g., "Pumpkins" or "Wall Decor") to reset the angle.
- 5. Move the discount disclaimer out of load-bearing position or soften it — it currently reads as a hedge against the headline claim.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Pumpkins, Pillows & More: 40% Off Fall Starts Now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New arrivals in florals, pillows, and outdoor decor — all 40% off.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your fall mantel, porch, and table — all in one lookbook.`
