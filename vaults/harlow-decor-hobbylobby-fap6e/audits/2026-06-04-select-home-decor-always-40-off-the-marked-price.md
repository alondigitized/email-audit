# Select Home Decor – Always 40% Off The Marked Price
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- This is Hobby Lobby's weekly ad email — probably their fifteenth send in as many days — and it follows the exact same cascading department-dump template that has appeared in virtually every prior audit. The hero is "Home Decor 40% Off" under a "Spring Essentials / Your Weekly Ad (06-08-26)" banner, and then the email just keeps scrolling: Textiles, Mirrors & Lamps, Spring Shop, Graduation, Classroom Decor, Summer Toys, Party, Hobbies, Wearable Art, Books, Frames, Art, Paper Crafts, Jewelry Making, Sewing Notions, Custom Frames — eighteen-plus departments stacked vertically with thumbnail images and percentage callouts.
- The subject line does something self-defeating: "Always 40% Off" signals permanent everyday pricing, which kills urgency. But the email itself says "Your Weekly Ad (06-08-26)" — a specific expiration date. That mismatch is the single biggest waste in this send. The Graduation section is genuinely timely for early June, but it's buried mid-scroll with no visual elevation.
- The template works exactly as well as it always does — dependable but not compelling. Regular Hobby Lobby shoppers know how to use it. Anyone else bounces.

## What's working

- **40% off hero is immediately visible** — the eye lands on Home Decor 40% Off before any scrolling.
- **Graduation section is seasonally on-point** for early June; this is the right moment to feature it.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum visible anywhere.
- **Textiles sub-breakdown is useful** — upholstery, bath towels, curtains, bath rugs, wall clocks are all called out, which helps a shopper decide quickly.
- **Weekly ad date (06-08-26) anchors urgency** for subscribers who know the cadence.

## What's weak

- **"Always 40% Off" in the subject line destroys urgency** — "always" reads as permanent pricing, not a weekly deal worth acting on now.
- **Graduation is a high-intent June category but gets the same visual treatment as every other department** — it should be the secondary hero, not row six.
- **Eighteen departments means zero priority** — the subscriber can't quickly find what's most relevant to them; everything competes equally.
- **No single product hero or link** — every CTA goes to a department category page; there's nothing specific enough to trigger impulse action.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is roughly the fifteenth email in fifteen days; yesterday's "Colorful Florals 50% Off" and June 2's "Vintage-Inspired Wall Art" were structurally identical. A subscriber scanning their inbox has already pattern-matched "Hobby Lobby = more of the same."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the subject line's "Always" problem first** — swap it for time-bounded language that matches the weekly ad expiration. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- **Subject Alt A:** `40% Off Home Decor – Sale Ends June 8`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Weekly Ad: Home Decor, Frames & More 40% Off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 50% off The Spring Shop® and 75% off select home decor — this week only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Graduation gifts, summer toys, custom frames — your full weekly savings inside.`
- 2. **Elevate Graduation to a co-hero** — put it directly below or beside Home Decor with a distinct visual treatment. June is graduation season; it's the most purchase-intent category in this email right now.
- 3. **Cut to eight departments max** — ruthlessly pick the eight strongest categories by margin or seasonal relevance, remove the rest, and give each remaining section a product-level image rather than a category icon thumbnail.
- 4. **Add one specific product link per featured category** — instead of "Shop Home Decor," link to a single trending SKU (e.g., a specific mirror or lamp) alongside the category CTA. Product-level links consistently outperform category links in retail email.
- 5. **Add a short urgency footer** — one line: "Weekly savings valid through June 8, 2026 in-store and online." It's implied by the weekly ad header, but never stated explicitly in the body.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open-tracking pixel missing alt text**
QA confirmed: `<img src="https://link.hobbylobby.com/mo/eORqXTkAb7_837015858_126561_3344412_134213.gif">` has no `alt` attribute. While invisible tracking pixels intentionally render empty, the missing attribute is an accessibility violation and will flag in some inbox renderers.

QA finding: 12,168 of 13,779 plain-text characters are URLs. This ratio is a known spam-filter signal. The plain-text part appears to be auto-generated from link redirect strings rather than human-readable copy, which degrades deliverability scoring on content-based filters (SpamAssassin, Proofpoint).

**Click tracking**
All links appear to route through `link.hobbylobby.com`. Standard ESP redirect — no anomaly, but see UTM section below.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS bug — malformed `border-radius` on `.ctacr1`**
In the embedded `<style>`:
```css
.ctacr1 { border-radius:px; -moz-border-radius:px; -webkit-border-radius:px; }
```
The numeric value is missing — `px` with no leading number is invalid CSS. This means the CTA button declared with `.ctacr1` will render with no border-radius in all clients. The `background-color:#013088` and padding will still apply, so the button is not broken — but the intended rounded-corner shape is lost everywhere.

**CSS typo — `verticl-align` on `.lhp2`**
```css
.lhp2, .lhp2 font { line-height:1.2; verticl-align:middle; }
```
`verticl-align` is not a valid CSS property. The vertical centering on elements using `.lhp2` will silently fail in all clients.

**MSO/Outlook conditional CSS**
The MSO overrides for `.ol650`, `.ol660`, `.tcta4br`, `.lhp2/.cta4b` are present and structurally correct. No issue.

**Viewport**
`initial-scale=1, user-scalable=yes` is correct and not pinched — no issue.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header absent** (QA WARN)
`List-Unsubscribe` was not captured by the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Yahoo require this header for bulk senders (>5k/day) under their 2024 enforcement rules. If the header is present upstream but stripped by the relay, that needs relay-level investigation. If it's genuinely missing, this is a deliverability risk and a Yahoo/Google bulk sender policy violation.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post absent** (QA WARN, RFC 8058)
One-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) is absent. This is now required by Google and Yahoo for bulk senders alongside the base `List-Unsubscribe` header.

**Authentication-Results not captured** (QA WARN)
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown due to relay not forwarding the `Authentication-Results` header. Sending domain is `create.hobbylobby.com`. Cannot confirm alignment without raw headers from a direct receive.
