# Dine In Style With 50% Off The Spring Shop®
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- This is yet another Hobby Lobby mega-circular — and the subject line doesn't remotely match the email body. "Dine In Style" promises a table-setting or entertaining angle, but the hero delivers the same Spring Shop 50% off tile that appeared in the July 1 "Charming Outdoor Decor" send and multiple June sends before it. There is no dining-specific content anywhere in the visible render — no tableware, no entertaining products, no food styling imagery. The subject is bait; the body is the standard circular.
- Below the Spring Shop hero, the email stacks the full department grid: Summer Toys 50% off, Home Decor 40% off, July 4th 66% off, Fall 40% off, Classroom Decor 40% off, Dorm Essentials 40% off, and Online Clearance at 75%. Seven departments, one email, zero focus. The July 4th tile at 66% off is the single standout — that's a genuinely strong discount — but it's buried at position four in the grid, three weeks after the holiday email that earned an 8/10 on June 30.
- The honest read: this email is a recycled layout with a misleading subject. The Spring Shop tile has been sent so many times in recent weeks (July 1, June 23, June 17) that it registers as noise. If you opened expecting dining content, you'd feel deceived. If you ignore the subject and treat it as the weekly circular, it's fine — but "fine" isn't what earns clicks.

## What's working

- **Free shipping bar** is visible at the top ("Free Shipping On Orders $59 Or More") — clear, concrete, no ambiguity.
- **66% off July 4th** tile is the strongest offer in the email and the discount is eye-catching even in the grid.
- **75% Online Clearance** at the bottom is a legitimate anchor discount that could pull deal-seekers.
- **Multiple CTAs** are present for each tile — navigable for a subscriber who just wants to browse departments.

## What's weak

- **Subject-to-body mismatch is severe** — "Dine In Style" implies food/entertaining; there is no dining-specific product visible anywhere in the render.
- **Spring Shop hero is recycled creative** — this same 50% off Spring Shop tile has appeared in at least three recent sends; no subscriber freshness left.
- **July 4th tile is badly timed** — the holiday is today (July 7 send); a 66% off post-holiday clearance angle would make sense, but the creative doesn't say "clearance" or "last chance."
- **Grid overload with no hierarchy** — seven department tiles of nearly identical visual weight give the eye nowhere to land after the hero.
- **No personalization or editorial angle** — the "Dine In Style" hook is completely abandoned after the subject line.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Honor the subject line or change the subject.** If you're going to call it "Dine In Style," the hero needs tableware, placemats, or entertaining product. If that content doesn't exist, use an honest subject. The mismatch will drive unsubscribes.
- 2. **Lead the July 4th clearance angle** — today is July 7; position the 66% off tile as "Post-Holiday Clearance" with urgency copy. That's the one offer that has a credible reason to exist today.
- 3. **Cut the grid from seven tiles to three or four.** Spring Shop, Fall, and Dorm Essentials are all competing for attention. Pick the two most seasonally relevant and give them room.
- 4. **Retire the Spring Shop creative.** Sending the same tile for the fifth time in three weeks trains subscribers to ignore it. Either refresh the imagery or pause the offer until it has news again.
- 5. **Add a deadline to at least one offer.** Not a single tile shows an expiration date, which removes urgency entirely.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Post-July 4th Clearance: 66% Off + 50% Off Spring`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Today Only: 66% Off July 4th Decor — Plus 50% Off Spring`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping on $59+ orders — shop before Summer inventory runs out.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The biggest discounts of the week, ending soon.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Bug 1 — CTA border-radius is missing its value:**
```css

  -moz-border-radius:px;      /* invalid — no number */

The `color:;` on the same rule is also empty — foreground text color on this CTA is undefined and will fall through to inherited or default client color.

**Bug 2 — CSS typo on `.lhp2`:**
