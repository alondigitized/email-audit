# ☀️ Soak Up The Sun & Score Savings
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-31**
## Executive summary

- "Soak Up The Sun & Score Savings" is Hobby Lobby's twelfth (or thereabouts) email in twelve days, and it is structurally identical to every send since at least May 17: a seasonal lifestyle hero, then an unbroken vertical cascade of department tiles, each stamped with a discount percentage. The summer-sun framing is a thin creative layer over an identical skeleton. The May 28 send used the same ☀️ emoji and "Sunny Days Ahead" summer theme; this one is that email with a slightly different headline.
- The actual discounts are real and visible — Home Decor 40%, Spring Shop 50%, a 75% clearance tier, Frames 40%, Fabric 30%, Custom Frames 50% off — so there is genuine value in the email for the right shopper. But the department-dump format asks the reader to do the work of finding what matters to them, rather than making one compelling offer and leading with it. For a subscriber who has received twelve near-identical emails in twelve days, the cognitive load of scanning twenty-plus department tiles to find their category is now the barrier, not the deals.
- Verdict: a competent catalog email that is working against itself through sheer repetition and format fatigue. The summer hook is not differentiated enough from the May 28 send to restart attention. Score it as a serviceable weekly ad, not a standout send.

## What's working

- **Breadth of genuine deals** — multiple percentage tiers (20%, 30%, 40%, 50%, 75%) give different shoppers a real reason to engage, and the discounts are clearly labeled on every tile.
- **Seasonally accurate framing** — "Soak Up The Sun" on May 31 is on-calendar; Summer Toys and Spring Shop placement near the top makes sense.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout collapses visible; the email displays as intended.
- **75% clearance call-out** — the deepest discount tier breaks the monotony of the 40% baseline and could pull click-through from deal-seekers scanning quickly.

## What's weak

- **Template fatigue is now the primary problem** — this is the same cascading department-stack skeleton the brand sent on May 28, May 27, May 26, May 24, May 22, May 21, and May 17. A subscriber can no longer tell one send from the next.
- **No single hero CTA** — there is no primary button or focal offer that resolves the email; the eye falls into a tile grid with no clear next step.
- **Subject line is a near-clone of May 28** — "Sunny Days Ahead ☀️ 50% Off" → "☀️ Soak Up The Sun & Score Savings" is the same emotional beat, same emoji, same summer register, weaker specificity (no % in subject).
- **Twenty-plus department tiles with no editorial curation** — Graduation decor, Wearable Art, Sewing Notions, and Summer Toys share a scroll. There is no point of view.
- **Cadence has reached subscriber-fatigue territory** — twelve emails in twelve days means the marginal open rate on this send is measurably lower than send #3 or #4.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Force a single hero offer and cut the tile count by half.** Pick the three or four categories with the deepest discounts or the tightest seasonal story (e.g., Summer Toys 20% + Outdoor/Party + Custom Frames) and lead with them. Every other category goes to a "See all deals" grid below the fold. A shorter email with a clear choice converts better than a twenty-tile catalog.
- 2. **Put the best discount number in the subject line.** "75% off" is in the email body; it should be in the subject. That is the hook that earns the open.
- 3. **Give the clearance tier its own CTA block.** The 75% off signal deserves a dedicated "Shop Clearance →" button above the fold, not a buried tile.
- 4. **Slow the send cadence or segment.** Every-day sends with near-identical templates train subscribers to ignore the brand. Even going to every other day, or splitting by category interest, would lift per-email engagement.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Clearance prices on hundreds of items, plus 50% off the Spring Shop.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `This week: 40% off Home Decor, 50% off Frames, and 75% off clearance picks.`

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text URL density (WARN)**
QA reports 11,928 of 13,530 plain-text characters are URLs (88%). The plain-text version was auto-generated from HTML and contains raw redirect URLs rather than human-readable copy. This ratio exceeds the 70% threshold that triggers spam-filter scrutiny at several major ISPs (Gmail, Yahoo).

**Redirect domain confirmed:** All tracked links pass through `link.hobbylobby.com`. Consistent — no mixed redirect domains observed in the truncated source.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — tracking GIF (INFO)**
```
src: https://link.hobbylobby.com/mo/eORqXTkAb7_837015858_124809_3249896_134212.gif
```
This appears to be the open-tracking pixel. No `alt=""` attribute is present. Screen readers will announce the raw filename. Should be `alt=""` (empty string) to suppress reader announcement.

**CSS defect — typo in `.lhp2`**
```css
.lhp2, .lhp2 font {line-height:1.2; verticl-align:middle;}
```
`verticl-align` is not a valid CSS property. Vertical alignment will not apply in any client. The Outlook conditional override correctly uses `vertical-align`, so Outlook is unaffected, but modern webmail clients (Gmail, Apple Mail) will silently drop this rule.

**CSS defect — malformed `.ctacr1` rule**
```css
.ctacr1 {color:; ... -moz-border-radius:px; -webkit-border-radius:px; border-radius:px;}
```
Three issues in one declaration: `color:` has no value (empty property), and all three `border-radius` values are `px` with no numeric prefix (invalid length). The CTA button styled with `.ctacr1` will render with the browser default link color and square corners in non-Outlook clients. The Outlook conditional resets `border-radius:0` which masks the issue there.

**Viewport meta tag — incomplete**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="initial-scale=1, user-scalable=yes">
```
Missing `width=device-width`. Without it, some Android webmail clients will not trigger the responsive breakpoint, potentially rendering the desktop layout on mobile.

No unfired or raw merge tokens (e.g., `%%firstName%%`, `{{name}}`) visible in the truncated source. `e-locale="en-US"` and `e-is-multilanguage="false"` are ESP rendering attributes, not subscriber tokens — no issue.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header absent (WARN)**
The `List-Unsubscribe` mail header was not captured by the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Yahoo now require this header for bulk senders (>5k/day). Its absence will suppress Gmail's native unsubscribe UI and risks bulk-sender policy violations.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post absent — no one-click support (WARN)**
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) is missing. As of February 2024, Google and Yahoo require this for bulk senders. This is a compliance gap if volume thresholds are met.

**Authentication-Results header not found (WARN)**
SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unresolvable from the relay capture. Sending domain is `create.hobbylobby.com`. If DKIM alignment is not verified end-to-end, combined with the missing `List-Unsubscribe` issues, inbox placement risk is elevated.

**CAN-SPAM physical address** — cannot confirm presence from truncated source. Must verify the footer contains a valid U.S. postal address.
