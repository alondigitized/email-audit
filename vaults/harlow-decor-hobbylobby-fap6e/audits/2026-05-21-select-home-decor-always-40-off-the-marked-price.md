# Select Home Decor – Always 40% Off The Marked Price
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- This is the same weekly-ad-digest format Hobby Lobby ran on May 17 ("Your Weekly Ad Is Here!") and May 19 ("Picnic Party!")—a cascading department dump covering 15+ categories from Home Decor to Sewing Notions to Custom Frames. The subject line teases "Select Home Decor" specifically, but the email delivers the entire store. That bait-and-switch isn't deceptive, but it is a missed opportunity: recipients who opened expecting a Home Decor focus get a wall of competing offers instead.
- The bigger problem is timing. Yesterday's send landed on a strong Memorial Day hook ("40% Off Florals 💐") with clear seasonal energy. Today's send drops that theme entirely and reverts to evergreen language—"Always 40% Off"—on Memorial Day weekend, one of Hobby Lobby's highest-traffic periods. The creative regression undercuts any goodwill the May 20 send built.
- The email is functional and renders cleanly, but it's a flyer, not an email. If you want clicks, you pick a lane. This one doesn't.

## What's working

- **Home Decor leads the fold.** The subject aligns with the first visible section, so the open-to-body handoff is clean.
- **No render issues.** All product images appear, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum, layout holds.
- **Discount density is high.** 40% appears across nearly every section—subscribers who scan for deals will find something relevant.
- **The "Always 40% Off" framing is honest.** Hobby Lobby's permanent Select Home Decor pricing is a known subscriber expectation; it's not misleading.

## What's weak

- **Subject promises Home Decor; body delivers the whole store.** Fifteen-plus departments compete for attention after the first section—focus evaporates immediately.
- **Memorial Day momentum abandoned.** Yesterday's send had holiday energy; this one uses "Always" and "Spring Essentials" as if May 21 doesn't exist.
- **"Always" kills urgency entirely.** The word is the opposite of a reason to act today.
- **No single hero product or linked category.** Every section is a label + percentage—there's no depth, no product image that earns a click.
- **Fifth email in five days.** Cadence fatigue is real; this send adds volume without adding value.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Tie the subject and email to Memorial Day weekend.** This is peak craft-shopping season—Memorial Day décor, summer entertaining, graduation gifting are all live right now. Use that instead of "Spring Essentials" (which is stale by late May).
- 2. **Pick one hero category and give it a real moment.** The May 19 "Picnic Party" send showed this works—one frame, one offer, one seasonal story. Apply that to a Memorial Day or summer home refresh angle.
- 3. **Replace "Always" with a deadline.** Even "This week only" changes the calculus. "Always" is the enemy of the click.
- 4. **Trim the department list.** Five categories max. Every additional section dilutes the lead.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Hobby Lobby "Select Home Decor – Always 40% Off"

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a table-based HTML build with MSO/VML conditional blocks and a responsive CSS layer. No critical rendering blockers were found, but two compliance gaps (missing RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe headers) and a deliverability risk (authentication headers absent at relay) require attention before the next send.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text body is URL-saturated.**
QA flagged 11,852 of 13,430 characters (88%) in the plain-text alternative as URLs. This is well above the 70% threshold and will trigger spam filters on several ISPs (Yahoo, Outlook) that score plain-text part ratio independently.

**No issues found** with link structure or redirect chains based on available source (tracking domain `link.hobbylobby.com` is consistent across CTAs).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text on tracked GIF.**
`eORqXTkAb7_837015858_125172_2705681_133304.gif` (src: `https://link.hobbylobby.com/mo/eORqXTkAb7_837015858_125172_2705681_133304.gif`) has no `alt` attribute. This is almost certainly an open-tracking pixel, but the missing `alt=""` (even an empty string) is an accessibility violation and causes a lint warning in strict clients.

**CSS typo in `.lhp2`.**
`verticl-align:middle` — misspelled property, silently ignored by all clients. Minor, but worth fixing in the template stylesheet.

**`.ctacr1` border-radius values are empty.**
```css
.ctacr1 { -moz-border-radius:px; -webkit-border-radius:px; border-radius:px; }
```
All three values are `px` with no number — invalid CSS, stripped by clients. If border-radius is intentional on that CTA variant, populate the values.

**`.fc1 color` on `.ctacr1` is also blank** (`color:;`) — will inherit unpredictably depending on client.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `[FNAME]`) visible in the truncated source. No issues found.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected.**
QA reports the header may not be passing through the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to surface the unsubscribe button in the UI; its absence increases spam complaints. Verify at the ESP (Intelitix/Responsys or equivalent) that the header is set on outbound SMTP and survives the relay hop.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not present.**
One-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender rules for >5k/day sends) depends on both `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`. Missing the POST header means Gmail will fall back to a redirect unsubscribe or suppress the button entirely — both outcomes increase complaint rate.

**`Authentication-Results` header absent.**
SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unknown due to AgentMail relay not forwarding the header. Cannot confirm DMARC alignment. This is a monitoring gap, not a confirmed failure, but it should be resolved so each send has a verifiable auth record.

**CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link** — present in the footer based on standard Hobby Lobby template patterns; cannot confirm from truncated source, but no flag was raised by automated checks.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM Params)

All visible CTA hrefs route through `link.hobbylobby.com` redirect domain. UTM parameter presence cannot be confirmed from the truncated source, but the tracking domain itself is consistent. **Recommend verifying** that decoded destination URLs include at minimum `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` before links resolve to `hobbylobby.com`.
