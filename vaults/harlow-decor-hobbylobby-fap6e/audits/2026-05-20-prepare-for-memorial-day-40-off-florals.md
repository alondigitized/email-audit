# Prepare For Memorial Day 💐 40% Off Florals
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- This is Hobby Lobby's Memorial Day push — a 40% off florals hero dropped on top of the same weekly-ad product grid that appeared in the May 17 and May 19 sends. The Memorial Day framing is the right instinct: patriotic red/white/blue flowers in the hero look seasonal and intentional. But the hero section is labeled "Spring Essentials," which directly contradicts the subject line's "Prepare For Memorial Day" hook — a sloppy misalignment that undercuts the urgency the subject tried to build.
- The bigger structural problem is that the florals story evaporates the moment your eye moves past the SHOP NOW button. Below it, the email reverts to the familiar wall of department-by-department price callouts — vinyl, foam board, canvas sets, acrylic paint, bandanas, sweatshirts. This isn't a Memorial Day email; it's the weekly ad with a seasonal hat on.
- One more thing that stings: yesterday's email (May 19, "Picnic Party!") led with 50% off. Today is 40% off. That's a step backward in a subscriber's inbox with no explanation. A returning reader notices.

## What's working

- Patriotic red/white/blue floral arrangement in the hero is visually on-brand for Memorial Day — the creative team made the right seasonal call.
- "40% Florals" callout is large and legible; the discount reads at a glance.
- Free shipping threshold ("$59 Or More!") is placed at the very top — removes a friction point early.
- SHOP NOW button is clearly styled and visible in the hero block.
- Individual product pricing throughout the grid is clean and easy to scan.

## What's weak

- Hero section copy reads "Spring Essentials," not "Memorial Day" — body and subject are misaligned, diluting urgency.
- 40% offer immediately follows a 50% offer (May 19) with no explanation — feels like a downgrade, not an escalation.
- No end date or "through Monday" language anywhere visible — Memorial Day urgency is implied, never stated.
- The weekly-ad product grid (vinyl, foam board, paint, apparel basics) swamps the floral angle — the hero's promise isn't kept by the rest of the email.
- Fourth email in five days; cadence fatigue is a real risk by this point in the week.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the hero headline.** Change "Spring Essentials" to "Memorial Day Florals" or "Your Memorial Day Garden" — this single change aligns subject, hero, and CTA into one coherent message.
- 2. **Add an explicit deadline.** "40% Off Florals — through Monday, May 26" costs nothing and converts urgency from implied to real. Visible deadline, no fine print needed.
- 3. **Lead with context for the lower discount.** If you ran 50% yesterday, either run the same or tell the subscriber why this deal is different (e.g., deeper category, online-only). Silence on the step-down reads as bait-and-switch.
- 4. **Tighten or cut the product grid.** Keep the apparel section (bandanas, tees, sweatshirts read as Memorial Day picnic/party items) but move it under a "Memorial Day Essentials" sub-header and cut the unrelated craft supplies below the fold. One story, not two.
- 5. **Surface a preview/preheader that doesn't echo the subject.** The top visible copy ("Free Shipping On Orders $59 Or More!") appears to be functioning as the preheader — that's a shipping detail, not a reason to open.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Through Monday only — shop arrangements, wreaths & more`

## Technical Audit — Hobby Lobby "Prepare For Memorial Day" (2026-05-20)

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard retail HTML4 table-based mailer using Outlook/VML conditional comments and a custom CSS utility class system. QA surfaced 4 warnings: two compliance gaps (unsubscribe headers), one deliverability risk (plain-text URL density), and one accessibility miss (missing alt text). Two additional code-level defects are present in the stylesheet.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text URL density — high severity**
QA confirms the plain-text body is 82.7% URLs (13,364 of 16,164 chars). A plain-text part that is nearly entirely raw redirect URLs (all routed through `link.hobbylobby.com`) is a strong spam-filter signal. Most ESPs auto-generate plain-text from HTML; if that generation is not stripping/summarizing redirect URLs, the output needs a custom plain-text template.

**Tracking redirect domain**
All links resolve through `link.hobbylobby.com`. No issue with the pattern itself, but SPF/DKIM status is unverified (see §5), so the trust chain for that domain is currently unknown.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — tracking GIF**
```
src: https://link.hobbylobby.com/mo/eORqXTkAb7_837015858_123911_4548749_133303.gif
```
The open-tracking pixel has no `alt=""` attribute. While a blank alt is correct for decorative/tracking images (`alt=""`), the absence of the attribute entirely causes some screen readers to read the filename aloud. Fix: `alt=""`.

**Malformed `border-radius` values in `.ctacr1`**
```css
.ctacr1 {
  -moz-border-radius:px;
  -webkit-border-radius:px;
  border-radius:px;
}
```
All three `border-radius` declarations are missing numeric values (unit without magnitude). The CTA button renders with no radius in any client; the fallback is square corners everywhere. The Outlook override block correctly sets `border-radius:0` for MSO, so this only affects modern clients where a rounded button was presumably intended.

**CSS typo in `.lhp2`**
```css
.lhp2, .lhp2 font {line-height:1.2; verticl-align:middle;}
```
`verticl-align` is misspelled — the property is silently ignored. Low visual impact for this class specifically, but indicates the stylesheet was not linted.

**DOCTYPE is HTML 4.01 Transitional**
Not an error for email, but worth noting: `<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` is the intended choice for broad Outlook compatibility. No action needed unless the ESP enforces HTML5.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header — not detected**
```
[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found
```
Gmail and Yahoo require `List-Unsubscribe` for bulk senders (>5,000/day). The QA note attributes this to AgentMail relay capture limitations, but if the header is genuinely absent at delivery, this violates Google/Yahoo February 2024 bulk sender requirements.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post header — not detected (RFC 8058)**
```
[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click not found
```
Without this header, Gmail cannot render its native one-click unsubscribe button. Users who want to unsubscribe must navigate the in-email flow, increasing the friction that leads to spam reports.

**Authentication-Results — not detected**
```
[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found — SPF/DKIM status unknown
```
Cannot confirm whether SPF, DKIM, or DMARC pass/fail for `noreply@create.hobbylobby.com` via `link.hobbylobby.com`. If the AgentMail relay strips these headers, this gap is in the auditing layer, not delivery — but it should be confirmed. A raw header dump from a delivered copy (e.g., Gmail "Show original") would resolve this.

**CAN-SPAM physical address**
Not evaluable from the truncated source. A physical mailing address must be present in the body. Verify it appears in the footer of the full HTML.
