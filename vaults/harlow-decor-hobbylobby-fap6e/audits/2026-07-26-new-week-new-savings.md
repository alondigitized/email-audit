# New Week, New Savings!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-26**
## Executive summary

- This is the most sprawling mega-circular Hobby Lobby has sent in the recent history reviewed — 25+ stacked category tiles, each with its own percentage-off badge, running from Home Decor down through Furniture with barely any editorial break. The subject line, "New Week, New Savings!", is the vaguest and laziest of the batch: no offer, no category, no urgency, nothing that differentiates it from any other Monday email.
- There's no real hero here — just a thin "Fall Essentials" banner and a free-shipping bar up top before the wall of tiles begins. Compare that to the 07-21 "Bring Cozy Vibes – 40% Off Fall!" send (7/10), which used an actual wreath lifestyle shot and three curated subcategories. This one skips the narrative entirely and goes straight into an undifferentiated scroll, closer in spirit to the 07-19 "Summer Savings" circular (5/10) but even longer and less focused.
- Judgment: functional for an already-loyal, already-shopping-list-in-hand Hobby Lobby regular, but a weak send for driving opens or clicks from anyone browsing their inbox — nothing here earns a glance, let alone a tap.

## What's working

- Render is clean throughout — no overlapping text, broken images, or layout breaks across the entire long scroll.
- Consistent Hobby Lobby brand styling (red/cream palette, serif category headers) holds together even at this length.
- Concrete, specific percentage offers (40%, 50%, 66%, 75%) appear on essentially every tile, so a shopper scanning for their category gets an immediate number.
- Free shipping on $59+ is placed first, before any category content, giving a universal low-friction hook.

## What's weak

- No single hero or focal offer — the email is just a stack of near-identical percentage tiles with no product photography narrative beyond small category thumbnails.
- Subject line carries zero information; it's the weakest subject in the brand history reviewed here (compare to "Bring Cozy Vibes – 40% Off Fall!" or "Hobby Lobby Now Hiring for Ontario, CA," both far more specific).
- "Does not include 'Your Price' items" and similar exclusion disclaimers repeat under nearly every single tile, reading as fine-print overload rather than an honest, clean offer.
- No unambiguous primary CTA button — dozens of category text links compete equally, so the eye has nowhere obvious to land first.
- Extreme length (dozens of stacked sections) risks recipients abandoning the scroll well before reaching Furniture at the bottom.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this down to the 3-4 strongest offers (75% off Select Home Decor, 66% off Spring Shop/Toys, 66% off Furniture, 50% off Mirrors & Lamps) and build a real hero around them, with a "Shop All Deals" link out to a landing page for the rest — the current wall-of-tiles format punishes engagement rather than rewarding it.
- 2. Give the subject an actual number — vague "new week, new savings" language doesn't compete with concrete subjects like "Bring Cozy Vibes – 40% Off Fall!" from the same brand.
- 3. Consolidate the repeated "Does not include 'Your Price' items" disclaimers into a single footer note instead of restating it under nearly every category.
- 4. Add one clear, styled CTA button ("Shop This Week's Deals") near the top hero instead of relying purely on category text links scattered through the body.
- 5. A/B test subject and preheader:
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 75% Off Home Decor Starts Now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Fall Essentials + 40–75% Off Storewide`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping $59+ and deep cuts on decor, fabric, and fall crafts.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `This week only: 75% off select home decor, 66% off furniture.`
