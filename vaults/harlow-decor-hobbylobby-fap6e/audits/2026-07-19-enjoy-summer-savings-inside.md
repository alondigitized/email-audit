# ☀️Enjoy Summer Savings Inside!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- This is the Hobby Lobby mega-circular in its purest, most undiluted form: 20+ category tiles, each stamped with a percentage badge, stacked into a two-column grid with no editorial layer, no hero image, and no narrative thread. It is the identical format that earned a 4/10 on July 12 ("Shop Up To 66% Off Seasonal Savings Inside!") and has run in some variation nearly every other day this month. Today's version adds a fresh seasonal wrinkle: the subject line promises "Summer Savings" while the body openly promotes a **Fall Floral** category — a contradiction any alert subscriber will catch and that signals list-and-shoot execution over intentional curation.
- The 40%-off drumbeat across essentially every category also collapses its own urgency. When Home Decor, Textiles, Crafts, Frames, Floral, Paper Crafts, Party, Dorm Essentials, and Classroom are all 40% off simultaneously, nothing is actually on sale — it's just the shelf price with a badge. The send does pack real breadth, and a subscriber hunting for a specific category will find it. But breadth without hierarchy is a catalog, not an email.
- Verdict: interchangeable promotional filler. It will drive some habitual clicks from project-mode customers but earns nothing in terms of brand equity, subscriber engagement, or differentiation from the last six sends.

## What's working

- Discount badges are large and scannable — 40% and 66% figures are immediately readable at thumbnail size.
- The Dorm Essentials and Classroom tiles show some back-to-school awareness; that's a timely hook buried inside an otherwise unfocused send.
- No render bugs visible — tiles load cleanly, no broken images or overlapping text.
- Category breadth means any subscriber with an active project (yarn, jewelry, sewing, crafts) will find their aisle.

## What's weak

- Subject says "Summer Savings" while the body includes a "Fall Floral" category — a seasonal contradiction that undermines credibility.
- This is effectively the seventh near-identical tile-wall in under three weeks (July 2, 7, 9, 12, 16, 17, now 19); subscriber fatigue at this cadence is a real open-rate drag.
- No hero image or editorial narrative — the eye has nowhere to land first; scanning starts at tile 1 of 20+.
- No single CTA button; every tile competes equally, which fragments attention and removes urgency from all of them.
- "Inside!" in the subject line is a throwaway word — it adds five characters and zero information.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one story and lead with it.** "40% off Dorm Essentials before move-in weekend" is concrete and timely. Put a real hero image at the top, then drop the grid below the fold.
- 2. **Fix the seasonal contradiction.** Either rename this "End-of-Summer + Early Fall Preview" or pull the Fall Floral tile. Calling it "Summer Savings" while showing autumn product is the kind of slop that erodes list trust over time.
- 3. **Add a hard deadline.** "This weekend only" or "Ends Sunday" above the grid gives subscribers a reason to act now rather than bookmark-and-forget.
- 4. **Halve the tile count.** 20+ categories is a browse session. Cut to 8–10 to focus attention and make the send feel curated rather than exhaustive.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `40% Off Dorm Essentials — Move-In Is Coming ☀️`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Back-to-School Deals: 40% Off Dorm, Crafts & More`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Stock up before move-in weekend — plus 66% off home decor.`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Classroom, dorm, and craft savings — this weekend only.`

```css

  -moz-border-radius:px;     /* missing numeric value */
```
