# 40% Off Classroom Decor & Dorm Essentials!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- Hobby Lobby pivots to a back-to-school angle the day after July 4th — and the timing is actually smart. Early July is when back-to-school buying starts in earnest, and the brand carries real classroom and dorm inventory. But the execution immediately undermines the premise: the subject promises "Classroom Decor & Dorm Essentials" yet the hero tile is HOME DECOR 40% off, the same category that led the July 2 circular. The dorm and classroom sections are buried partway down another 15+ tile mega-circular, sandwiched between Fall Floral and Party Accessories tiles.
- This is the fifth Hobby Lobby send in five days — following July 3 (Small Space), July 2 (Home Decor circular), June 30 (July 4th), and June 28 (Fall alert). Cadence fatigue is a real suppressor here. On top of that, the Spring Shop® tile makes another appearance — on July 5th. It was already questionable on July 1st; by now it's a stale creative artifact that should have been retired in June.
- The classroom/dorm angle was the right call for today. Swapping the hero to match the subject promise, dropping the Spring Shop, and running this as a focused back-to-school send instead of a mega-circular would have produced a meaningfully stronger email.

## What's working

- **Back-to-school pivot is well-timed**: July 5th is a logical day to shift from patriotic to school prep — early July is when college move-in supply lists and classroom setup budgets open up.
- **Subject line is more specific than recent sends**: Naming two concrete audiences (teachers, college students/parents) is sharper than the generic "Select Home Decor" or "Enhance Your Space" subjects from earlier this week.
- **40% off is a consistent, credible discount**: The offer appears uniform across tiles — reads as a genuine site-wide sale rather than selective cherry-picking.
- **Clean render**: No broken images, overlapping text, or layout breakage visible.

## What's weak

- **Hero-subject mismatch is the central problem**: Open for dorm essentials, land on home decor — any subscriber who took the subject seriously will feel immediately misled before they see the classroom tiles halfway down.
- **Spring Shop tile is still here**: The Spring Shop® section in a July 5th email is a recycled leftover that contradicts the Fall Decor tiles appearing three rows below it in the same email.
- **July 4th tiles are one day late**: Any July 4th decor sections in this send are post-holiday residue. The June 30 send was the right timing; July 5th is clearance territory at best.
- **Cadence is punishing open rates**: Five sends in five days means even a subscriber who wants the classroom content may autopilot-delete this one.
- **No deadline, no urgency**: The offer has no "through Sunday" or "ends Monday" signal anywhere visible, which removes any reason to click today versus next week.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Flip the hero to match the subject**: Put the Dorm Accessories tile first, the Classroom tile second. Everything else follows. The July 2 send already staked out HOME DECOR as the hero; repeating it on July 5th with a different subject is a trust failure.
- 2. **Kill the Spring Shop tile**: It's July. Replace it with a "Back-to-School" callout tile that earns its place in the email.
- 3. **Add a time boundary**: "Through Sunday" or "This week only" gives the subscriber a reason to click now rather than bookmark and forget.
- 4. **Cut to 8–10 tiles**: A classroom/dorm-focused email with 8 targeted tiles would outperform a 15+ tile dump where the featured section is buried.
- 5. **Frame leftover July 4th inventory as clearance**: If the tiles remain, label them "Clearance" — it's honest and creates urgency.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dorm Room Ready: 40% Off Everything You Need`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Back to School Starts Here — 40% Off Dorm & Classroom`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stock your dorm or classroom before the rush — this week only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Pillows, storage, art supplies, frames — all 40% off through Sunday.`

## Technical Audit — Hobby Lobby "40% Off Classroom Decor & Dorm Essentials"

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on a standard HTML 4.01 Transitional table layout with Outlook/VML support, but contains three invalid CSS declarations in the embedded stylesheet that will silently fail in all clients. Deliverability risk is elevated due to missing authentication headers and an abnormally URL-heavy plain-text part.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**No issues found** with the redirect/tracking domain itself (`link.hobbylobby.com`). All CTA links appear routed through the ESP's click-tracking domain consistently.

**Warning — plain-text URL density:** QA reports 12,480 of 14,087 plain-text characters are URLs (88.6%). This exceeds the 70% threshold and is a spam-filter signal. The plain-text part appears to be auto-generated from the HTML without stripping tracking URLs to human-readable form.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Bug — missing `alt` text on tracking pixel:**
```
src: https://link.hobbylobby.com/mo/eORqXTkAb7_837015858_128244_4357413_137854.gif
```
The open-tracking GIF carries no `alt=""` attribute. Screen readers will attempt to read the filename. Fix: add `alt=""`.

**Bug — invalid `border-radius` values in `.ctacr1`:**
```css
.ctacr1 {
  -moz-border-radius:px;
  -webkit-border-radius:px;
  border-radius:px;
}
```
All three `border-radius` declarations are missing the numeric value (unit with no number). These properties are silently ignored in all clients, so the CTA button will render with no border-radius. If rounded corners are intended, values like `4px` are required.

**Bug — empty `color` value in `.ctacr1`:**
```css
.ctacr1 { color:; ... }
```
Empty property value is invalid CSS. The text color for this CTA variant will fall back to inherited styles unpredictably across clients.

**Bug — CSS typo in `.lhp2`:**
```css
.lhp2 { ... verticl-align:middle; }
```
`verticl-align` is not a valid property (should be `vertical-align`). The vertical alignment rule is silently ignored.

**Warning — mobile breakpoint is 300px, not 600px:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width:300px) { ... }
```
Standard mobile breakpoints for email are 480–600px. At 300px, the responsive layout rules won't trigger on any modern smartphone (iPhone SE viewport: 375px; Android baseline: 360px). The email will render at desktop width on all mobile devices.

### 5. Compliance

**Warning — `List-Unsubscribe` header absent:**
QA confirms neither `List-Unsubscribe` nor `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) headers were captured at the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Apple Mail surface one-click unsubscribe UI from these headers; their absence increases spam complaint rate and risks suppression by major providers. This may be a relay capture gap rather than a true omission — confirm against raw SMTP headers from the ESP.

**Warning — `Authentication-Results` header not found:**
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown. Sending domain is `create.hobbylobby.com` (subdomain). If SPF alignment is against `hobbylobby.com` root and DKIM signing domain doesn't match, DMARC may fail. Needs verification at the ESP level.

**CAN-SPAM:** Physical mailing address and unsubscribe link are presumed present (footer was truncated in source). Cannot confirm from provided HTML.
