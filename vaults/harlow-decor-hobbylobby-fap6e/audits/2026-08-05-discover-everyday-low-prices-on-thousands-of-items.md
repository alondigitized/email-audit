# Discover Everyday Low Prices On Thousands Of Items!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-05**
## Executive summary

- "Discover Everyday Low Prices On Thousands Of Items!" is Hobby Lobby doing something this brand rarely does: telling the truth about what's inside. Unlike the 8/4 rug email that promised rugs and buried them in a mixed grid, or the 8/2 football email that opened with a completely unrelated "Fall Essentials" banner, this send's subject and body are actually aligned — it explains the in-store "Your Price" sticker program, then backs it up with 20 real SKUs and real prices. That's a rare moment of subject-body honesty for this brand.
- The problem is the subject itself gives the reader nothing to grab onto. "Everyday low prices on thousands of items" isn't a hook — it's a mission statement. There's no specific category, no deadline in the subject line, no reason to open *this* email over any other Hobby Lobby send this week. And once opened, the email reverts to the brand's default mode: an intro block that's genuinely well-designed, followed by a sprawling 10-row, 20-item price list with zero curation logic (charger plates next to helium tanks next to candy wafers next to cast epoxy).
- This is an honest, clean-executing email that will underperform on opens because the subject line does no work, even though the body itself is one of the tidier grids this brand has sent recently.

## What's working

- The intro block (Fall Essentials banner → free shipping bar → 3-tile category nav → "Our Everyday Best Price" headline with the annotated canvas/sticker callout) is genuinely clear — it explains the "Your Price" program visually instead of just claiming a discount.
- "Sale Valid Through Saturday, August 8, 2026" gives the whole email a credible, near-term deadline.
- Every grid item shows a real price, product name, size/spec, and a clear "In-Store Or Online" vs. "In-Store Only" distinction — genuinely useful shopping info, no vague "shop now" filler.
- Clean render: no overlapping text, no broken images, consistent brand color/logo treatment throughout.
- The "Free Shipping on Orders $59 or More" banner is a real, unambiguous incentive placed high in the email.

## What's weak

- The subject line carries zero specificity or urgency — no category, no percentage, no deadline — which continues a pattern this brand keeps repeating (compare to 8/2's disconnected subject, or 7/30's sprawling grid).
- The 20-item grid has no organizing theme — chargers, foam bricks, wreaths, candy wafers, a $49.99 helium tank, and glue guns all sit in the same undifferentiated list, which dilutes any single call to action.
- There's no strikethrough or "was $X" comparison anywhere — "Your Price" is presented as just a price, not a savings signal, so the discount story is invisible even though the program likely represents real markdown value.
- No loyalty/member benefit is surfaced despite this being exactly the kind of everyday-value message a rewards program would reinforce.
- Given the brand's near-daily cadence (rugs 8/4, football 8/2, refresh-your-space 7/31, spring/summer 7/30), this is arguably the 5th similar promotional send inside two weeks — fatigue risk is real.

## Recommendations

- 1. Rewrite the subject to lead with the mechanism, not the mission — "Look for the YOUR PRICE Sticker: Storewide Savings" is more concrete and curiosity-driving than "Discover Everyday Low Prices."
- 2. Add a "was $X / Your Price $Y" comparison to at least the top few grid items so the discount is visible, not just asserted.
- 3. Cut the grid from 20 items to 8-10 and group by category (Craft Supplies / Home Decor / Party) instead of one long undifferentiated column — reduces scroll fatigue and gives the eye a place to land.
- 4. Move "Sale Valid Through Saturday, August 8" up near the hero instead of burying it at the bottom — it's the only real urgency element in the email.
- 5. Surface loyalty/rewards messaging if a program exists — this "everyday value" framing is the natural home for it.
