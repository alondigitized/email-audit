# Shop Room Decor Kids Will Love!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- This is Hobby Lobby's kids room decor send — a thin editorial wrapper over the same stacked two-column mega-circular that has now run in one form or another since at least July 2. The subject line ("Shop Room Decor Kids Will Love!") is specific enough to suggest a curated send, but the body immediately abandons that premise. The hero is a lifestyle composite image of a styled kids room, which is legitimately nice — but it leads straight into the identical category-badge-on-tile grid: 66% off Spring Shop, 66% Summer Toys, 40% Home Decor, 40% Fall, 30% All Fabric, 40% Classroom Decor. That last one (Classroom Decor) is the only section that earns its place in a kids-room email. The rest is the standard circular dressed up with a new subject line.
- The brand's best recent send — July 10's "Style Your Home With Hobby Lobby" (8/10) — worked because the hero image and editorial tone held through the whole email. This one has a good hero and then collapses into the same tile stack. July 16's "Renew Your Space With Select Home Decor" had the same failure mode, scoring 6/10 for the same reason.
- Verdict: passable cadence filler. The subject gets opens from parents but the body doesn't deliver a focused kids-room experience — it delivers a weekly circular with a kids-room hat on.

## What's working

- Hero image is warm, aspirational, and genuinely on-theme — styled kids room with layered textures, gallery wall, and plants lands better than a plain product tile.
- "Classroom Decor 40% Off" at the bottom is a seasonally smart inclusion (back-to-school timing) and directly relevant to the kids angle.
- Free shipping callout ("Free Shipping On Orders $59 Or More!") is visible in the header and reduces purchase friction.
- The offer stack is clear — every category has a specific percentage, nothing is vague.

## What's weak

- Hero promise (kids room decor) is immediately abandoned — the first discount category is Spring Shop, which has nothing to do with kids.
- Summer Toys and Classroom Decor are the only categories that belong in this email; the other four (Home Decor, Fall, All Fabric) are circular padding.
- The two-column tile grid is visually indistinguishable from the July 2, 7, 9, 12, and 16 sends — subscriber fatigue is real at this cadence.
- No single hero product or specific item is called out — every CTA links to a department, not a curated kids-room pick.
- No loyalty/member pricing or personalization visible anywhere in the render.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the non-kids categories entirely.** Spring Shop, All Fabric, and generic Home Decor don't belong in a kids room send. A 4-block email (Summer Toys, Kids Room Decor, Classroom Decor, Kids Craft Supplies) would feel curated instead of repackaged.
- 2. **Add one hero product with a specific item + price.** Show a specific wall art set or storage bin with a price point — something a parent could click directly to buy.
- 3. **Tie the Classroom Decor callout to back-to-school urgency.** "40% off Classroom Decor — stock up before the first bell" is a CTA that earns the click.
- 4. **Shorten and sharpen the subject line.**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Kids Room Refresh — 40% Off Decor This Week`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Back to School + Summer Toys: 40-66% Off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Classroom decor, summer toys, and storage — all on sale now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free shipping $59+ · Up to 66% off kids picks inside.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bugs:**

- **Malformed `border-radius` in `.ctacr1`** — all three vendor-prefixed and standard values are missing the numeric unit (`border-radius:px` instead of e.g. `border-radius:3px`). The CTA button styled with this class will render with sharp corners regardless of intent on all clients.

  ```css
  .ctacr1 { -moz-border-radius:px; -webkit-border-radius:px; border-radius:px; }
  ```

- **Empty `color` value in `.ctacr1`** — `color:;` is invalid CSS. CTA link text color will fall through to inherited or client-default, risking unreadable text against the `background-color:#013088` (dark blue) background.

  ```css
  .ctacr1 { color:; background-color:#013088; }
  ```

- **CSS typo in `.lhp2`** — `verticl-align:middle` (missing `a`). Property is silently ignored; vertical alignment on that element will be incorrect in clients that apply it.

- **No `lang` attribute on `<html>`** — the proprietary `e-locale="en-US"` attribute is ESP-internal and not parsed by screen readers or browser engines. This breaks language detection for accessibility tools (WCAG 3.1.1). Fix: add `lang="en"` to the `<html>` tag.

- **`<meta name="viewport" content="initial-scale=1, user-scalable=yes">`** — `initial-scale=1` without `width=device-width` means responsive scaling may not trigger on some mobile clients. Standard pattern is `width=device-width, initial-scale=1`.
