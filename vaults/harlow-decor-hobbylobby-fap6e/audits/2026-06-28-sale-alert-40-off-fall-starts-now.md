# 🚨SALE ALERT: 40% Off Fall Starts Now!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-28**
## Executive summary

- This is Hobby Lobby's mega-circular format in full force — the same stacked-tile layout that appeared on June 24 and June 25, and frankly on many prior sends this month. The headline promise is "Fall Starts Now" on June 28, which is a calendar fiction: the email body simultaneously promotes July 4th furniture, summer toys, back-to-school dorm decor, and early fall floral — four seasons jammed into one scroll. That scattershot structure undercuts the urgency the subject is trying to manufacture.
- The send comes two days after the June 26 patriotic "Celebrate America's 250th Anniversary" email, which was the strongest creative Hobby Lobby has put out in the entire audit window. Going from a tightly themed hero to this multi-department dump in 48 hours is the textbook post-peak fatigue pattern. Whatever goodwill the Anniversary send earned is being spent here.
- The 40% off offer is genuine and visible throughout, but the visual noise of 15+ competing department tiles means the eye lands nowhere specific. This reads as a clearinghouse email, not a campaign.

## What's working

- **40% off is plastered everywhere** — the discount message is impossible to miss, and most department headers reinforce it.
- **HOME DECOR leads as the hero** — putting a full-room lifestyle visual at the top gives the email a purpose before the cascade of tiles begins.
- **Fall Floral section is genuinely seasonally relevant** — the fall floral and fall decor tiles are well-executed for a craft-store audience who shops seasonal decor early.
- **No render bugs** — the email lays out cleanly, sections are readable, and images appear to be loading.

## What's weak

- **"Fall Starts Now" on June 28 is a lie the body immediately contradicts** — July 4th and Summer Toys sections appear alongside fall content, destroying the seasonal premise.
- **🚨 and "SALE ALERT" in the subject trigger every spam-signal detector** — all-caps "ALERT" plus the emergency siren emoji reads as promotional desperation.
- **Fifteen-plus competing sections with no hierarchy** — after the hero, every department tile competes equally; there's no editorial decision about what matters most this week.
- **No deadline anywhere visible** — "Starts Now" implies urgency but nothing says when it ends, making the urgency feel hollow.
- **Cadence damage** — this is effectively the fourth promotional mega-circular in five days, immediately after the strong June 26 send. List fatigue is real here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one season and commit.** If fall is the story, pull the July 4th and summer tiles entirely or move them to a secondary digest. One theme per email, always.
- 2. **Add a sale end date.** "40% off Fall Décor — through July 4th only" gives the urgency a credible anchor the subject line can't provide alone.
- 3. **Retire the 🚨 emoji and "SALE ALERT" framing.** The alarm siren is a spam-filter magnet and looks desperate after the elegant patriotic email two days prior. Replace with category-specific language.
- 4. **Compress to 5 departments maximum.** The June 26 send proved that focused creative performs. The circular format should be the exception, not the weekly default.
- 5. **Respect cadence after a strong thematic send.** The June 26 patriotic email earned a 3–4 day cooldown minimum — returning to the circular format 48 hours later is list abuse.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Bug — broken `border-radius` value:**
```css

  -moz-border-radius:px;
```

The unit `px` is present without a number. This silently invalidates all three `border-radius` declarations on any element using `.ctacr1`. Browsers will ignore the rule; Outlook already overrides via the MSO block, so visual impact may be minor — but it signals a template authoring error.

Gmail and Yahoo Mail use this header to surface the unsubscribe button in the UI — its absence degrades deliverability and user experience in major clients.
