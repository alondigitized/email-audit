# Make It Festive With 50% Off Christmas 🎄
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-23**
## Executive summary

- The subject promises "50% Off Christmas," and the email never shows that offer. The hero is a giant "Trim The Tree" video tile with a play button. Below it is a "Christmas Essentials" banner and six category tiles, all labeled "In-Store Or Online." The only 50% badge in the whole email is for Fall decor, a few screens further down. This is the same subject/creative mismatch as the 2026-10-16 "Deck The Halls With 50% Off Christmas" send, only reversed.
- The rest is the usual weekly circular: Fall 50%, Home Decor 40%, Fabric 30%, Football Party 40%, Party 40%, Art Supplies 40%. A text link grid and a navy "Click here to view our weekly ad" bar fill out the bottom. Nothing is broken, and the layout is clean and on-brand. But the email has no single idea. Someone who opens it for Christmas savings has to scroll past two full screens of lifestyle imagery to find any price. Even then, they find no Christmas discount.
- Verdict: a competent catalog with a misleading subject line. It is the fourth near-identical circular in nine days, so it blends in with the earlier ones.

## What's working

- The "Trim The Tree" hero is seasonal and visually rich, and it matches the Christmas subject theme.
- The six Christmas category tiles (Decor, Trees, Floral, Crafts, Party, Lights) are easy to scan.
- The offer tiles use a consistent big-number format: "50% OFF," "40% OFF," "30% OFF." The eye picks up the discounts quickly.
- The "Always 40% off the marked price" framing and the "Sale Valid Through Saturday, October 24, 2026" footer give some honest, credible urgency.
- The free-shipping bar ($59+) is visible right at the top.

## What's weak

- The subject's "50% Off Christmas" is never shown. The Christmas section has no discount, and the only 50% badge is on Fall.
- The Christmas category tiles all link "In-Store Or Online" with no price or offer, so they give no reason to click now.
- The first screen has no offer. It holds a free-shipping bar, a Kinkeeping collection banner, and Kitchen, Bath and Home tiles, all before the Christmas hero.
- There is no single primary CTA. The "Click here to view our weekly ad" bar is a generic, low-energy label, and about 20 links compete for attention.
- The email is very long, with about 8 offer blocks. The 2026-10-22 and 2026-10-21 sends had the same sprawl.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the hero match the subject. Put a "50% Off Christmas" badge on the "Trim The Tree" image, with one button such as "Shop 50% Off Christmas Decor." If Christmas is not actually 50% off, change the subject to match the real offer.
- 2. Move the free-shipping bar and the Kinkeeping/Kitchen/Bath/Home block below the Christmas section, or cut them.
- 3. Put a price or percentage on each Christmas category tile ("Trees – 50% Off") so the tiles earn a click.
- 4. Cut the email to three or four offer blocks and use a real CTA button, such as "Shop This Week's Deals," in place of "Click here."
- 5. Vary the cadence and creative from the last few sends so the subscriber isn't looking at the same circular every other day.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Trim The Tree For Less – Deals Through 10/24`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Trees, wreaths, lights and more. Sale ends Saturday, October 24.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus 40% off home decor and free shipping on $59+.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based, Outlook-aware template sent through a tracked-link ESP (`link.hobbylobby.com`). The 0 failures and 4 warnings are mostly header-capture gaps from the relay. The HTML source shows a few concrete CSS and accessibility defects. The source is truncated, so footer, compliance and UTM checks could not be completed.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Plain-text bloat:** 12,848 of 14,963 characters (about 86%) of the plain-text part are URLs, above the 70% threshold. This points to long redirect-wrapped tracking URLs, and some clients and spam filters penalize that ratio.
- **Incomplete probing:** 12 material links were probed and 33 were skipped by design, and none were reported broken. That is 27% coverage, so the skipped links are unverified.
- **Tracked image domain:** images and links route through `link.hobbylobby.com/mo/...`. This is a normal ESP pattern, but the final destinations are hidden behind redirects and were not resolved in the data provided.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** `https://link.hobbylobby.com/mo/eORqXTkAb7_837015858_140902_4596647_150220.gif` has no `alt`. If it is decorative or a tracking pixel, it needs `alt=""`. If it is content, it needs descriptive text.
- **No `lang` attribute:** `<html xmlns=… e-locale="en-US">` uses a proprietary `e-locale` attribute but no `lang="en"`. Screen readers can't pick up the language.
- **Invalid CSS in `.ctacr1`:** it has `color:;`, `-moz-border-radius:px;`, `-webkit-border-radius:px;` and `border-radius:px;`. The empty values are dropped, so the intended color and radius are lost.
- **Typo in `.lhp2`:** `verticl-align:middle;` should be `vertical-align`, so it has no effect.
- **Responsive breakpoint:** the only visible media query is `@media only screen and (max-width:300px)`. Phones are typically 320–430px wide, so the mobile overrides (`.fl`, `.fw`, `.image`, `.gf`, `.h1`) would not fire on them. Other queries may exist in the truncated portion, so confirm this against the full source.
- **Doctype mismatch:** it uses an HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype with XHTML/VML namespace attributes. This is low risk, but the two are inconsistent.
- **Generic title:** `<title>Hobby Lobby</title>` is minor.
