# 50% Off The Spring Shop® Crafts
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- This is Hobby Lobby's "Spring Shop 50% off" crafts send, arriving June 17 — four days before summer officially begins. The hero executes cleanly: a bold orange "50%" badge over cheerful spring craft imagery, with a clear scope ("items labeled The Spring Shop"). That's a competent promotional email. The problem is everything below the fold: after the hero, the email degrades into a wall-of-price-tags product dump — Iron-On Vinyl, Transfer Tape, canvas sets, Sharpie markers, Rit Liquid Dye, and batteries — with no editorial thread connecting them. Batteries in a spring crafts email is not a typo; it's a symptom of a list-blast mindset.
- Paired with the brand's near-daily send cadence — this is at least the seventh promotional email in eight days — the emotional register of a 50% sale has completely worn out. June 16 sent "Create Cozy" home décor; June 14 sent Father's Day gifts. A subscriber's brain has learned to skip past these subject lines before they even open.
- The email is technically clean and the offer is real. But "technically clean + real offer" in a fatigued cadence at an off-season moment is a 5/10 outcome trying to pass as a 7/10.

## What's working

- **Hero offer is immediately legible.** The 50% badge in orange is the first thing your eye hits; the scoping copy "Items Labeled The Spring Shop® Crafts" tells you exactly what's included.
- **Free shipping threshold is surfaced early.** The "$59 or more" banner at top is visible before the hero — useful context when buying multiple craft supplies.
- **Specific prices on every product.** Each item in the grid has a clear dollar figure, so the reader can evaluate without clicking.
- **No render bugs.** Images loaded, layout held, no broken tiles or overlapping text visible.

## What's weak

- **Spring Shop in late June is a timing mismatch.** Summer starts June 21 — four days from send. "Spring" feels like clearance, not celebration.
- **The product grid is editorially incoherent.** Vinyl, canvas, dye, Sharpie markers, and batteries share no narrative arc. There's no "build this project" or "start this hobby" through-line.
- **"CLICK HERE TO VIEW OUR WEEKLY AD" is the worst possible CTA.** It signals "I have nothing specific to sell you" and sends the user to a PDF-style flier experience rather than a shoppable landing page.
- **No urgency signal anywhere.** The hero doesn't say when the sale ends. Without a deadline, 50% off reads as a permanent markdown, which kills motivation to act now.
- **Cadence fatigue.** Seven emails in eight days means this 50% sale has the same emotional weight as a Tuesday.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the weekly-ad CTA, replace with a category-specific shop button.** "Shop Spring Crafts 50% Off →" pointing directly to the Spring Shop landing page. The weekly ad is a dead end for impulse buyers.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the hero.** Even "Sale ends Sunday" changes the conversion math entirely. No deadline = no urgency = no click.
- 3. **Curate the product grid around one project.** Pick three to five items that build something (e.g., "Make a summer wreath: floral wire, foam sheet, ribbon, vinyl lettering") instead of dumping 20 unrelated SKUs.
- 4. **Rename or reframe "Spring Shop" for the send timing.** At mid-June, rename the frame: "End-of-Season Crafts Clearance" or "Last Chance Spring Crafts 50% Off" is honest and urgency-inducing.
- 5. **Throttle the send cadence.** One email per three to four days for sale periods; subscribers have been conditioned to ignore daily blasts from this brand.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Spring Craft Clearance — 50% Off This Weekend`

## Technical Audit — Hobby Lobby "50% Off The Spring Shop® Crafts"

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on a standard HTML 4.01 Transitional table layout with VML Outlook conditionals and an ESP redirect/tracking layer at `link.hobbylobby.com`. Four QA warnings were raised; two CSS authoring bugs were also identified in the stylesheet that could silently break CTA rendering in specific clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text body is link-saturated (high spam risk)**
QA confirms 83.4% of plain-text characters are URLs (14,787 / 17,725). This pattern — common when ESPs auto-generate plain-text from HTML without stripping tracking parameters — triggers spam filters on several MTA implementations (SpamAssassin `URI_HEX`, Gmail content scoring). The plain-text version should be hand-authored or generated with link shortening rather than raw redirect URLs.

**Tracking pixel routed through redirect domain**
The animated GIF `eORqXTkAb7_837015858_126869_390500_136618.gif` is served via `https://link.hobbylobby.com/mo/...`. This is consistent with standard open-tracking but confirms all link clicks and opens are logged through the same redirect domain — deliverability impact is contingent on that domain's reputation.

No broken link targets were detectable in the truncated source, but full link-resolution testing against final redirect destinations was not possible from this excerpt.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS typo — `vertical-align` misspelled**
In the `.lhp2` rule:
```css
.lhp2, .lhp2 font {line-height:1.2; verticl-align:middle;}
```
`verticl-align` is not a valid property; the declaration is silently dropped. This affects vertical centering of CTA button text in the primary call-to-action component.

**Empty `color` value on `.ctacr1`**
```css
.ctacr1 {color:; font-weight:normal; ...}
```
An empty `color:` value is invalid CSS. Some parsers will invalidate the entire rule block depending on error-recovery behavior. The fallback color is undefined, risking invisible text if the element inherits a background matching the default text color.

**Missing border-radius unit on `.ctacr1`**
```css
border-radius:px; -moz-border-radius:px; -webkit-border-radius:px;
```
All three `border-radius` declarations specify the unit `px` without a numeric value. This is a syntax error and will be ignored by all compliant parsers; the intended rounding will not render.

**`vw` viewport unit in `.rh20`**
```css
.rh20, .rh20 div {height:20px !important; height:5.56vw !important;}
```
`vw` is not supported in the majority of email clients (Outlook, Samsung Mail, older iOS Mail). The `!important` flag means this override will apply in clients that *do* support `vw` but the fallback `20px` line is also marked `!important` — since both are `!important`, cascade order applies and `5.56vw` wins in supporting clients. Intended behavior, but the `!important` on the `px` fallback is redundant and potentially confusing.

**Missing alt text on tracking GIF**
`eORqXTkAb7_837015858_126869_390500_136618.gif` has no `alt` attribute per QA. Screen readers will announce the raw filename or skip the element depending on client. If this is a spacer or tracking pixel, `alt=""` should be explicit.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent from captured message**
QA warns this header was not present in what the AgentMail relay captured. `List-Unsubscribe` is not a CAN-SPAM hard requirement, but it is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policies (effective February 2024) for senders above 5,000/day. Absent this header, Gmail may suppress the one-click unsubscribe UI and is more likely to route to Spam. This may be a relay-stripping issue rather than an ESP omission — confirm against raw headers at the MTA.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe as defined by RFC 8058 requires both `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`. Without it, Gmail's one-click button will not function even if the header link is present. Same caveat about relay capture applies.

**Authentication-Results not captured**
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown from this capture. Given that the sending domain is `create.hobbylobby.com` with links through `link.hobbylobby.com`, DKIM alignment requires the `d=` signing domain to match. No issues confirmed, but this should be verified at the MTA level.

No missing physical mailing address was identified in the truncated source (CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requirement) — the footer was cut off; verify in full source.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

No UTM parameters were visible in the truncated HTML. All click links pass through `link.hobbylobby.com` redirects, so UTMs may be appended at the redirect layer or injected into destination URLs server-side. This cannot be confirmed from the source alone — verify by resolving a sample redirect to check whether `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` are present on the final destination URL. If UTMs are absent from final destinations, attribution in GA4/analytics will be lost and sessions will appear as direct traffic.
