# Celebrate Dad With Gift Ideas From Hobby Lobby®️
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- This is Hobby Lobby's second Father's Day email in 48 hours — following "Thoughtful Gifts For Him 🎁" on June 12 — and it commits a cardinal sin: the subject line promises Father's Day gift ideas, but the body delivers the standard "Week at a Glance" department-dump grid. Scrolling through the render, there is no dedicated Father's Day section, no gifting curation, no "for Dad" hero. The 20-plus department tiles (Textiles, Mirrors, Spring Shop, Classroom Decor, Sewing Notions, Custom Frames, Furniture…) are the exact same format as every other weekly email this brand sends, just with a Father's Day subject bolted on.
- The June 12 send at least used the occasion to reorganize its grid around gifting. This one doesn't bother. The subscriber who opens expecting Father's Day gift ideas lands inside a discount warehouse with no map.
- The cadence context makes this worse: this is roughly the twenty-second email in twenty-two days. Two Father's Day sends back-to-back, both using slightly different subject lines to wrap the same format, signals list fatigue more than campaign intent.

## What's working

- Multiple concrete discounts are visible immediately (40%, 50%, 75%, 30%) — no mystery about whether there's a sale.
- The "Week at a Glance" format is internally coherent and renders cleanly — no broken images or layout collapse.
- Graduation section is seasonally on-point for mid-June, and July 4th preview is timely.
- Hobby Lobby is a highly recognizable sender; the brand header reads instantly.

## What's weak

- Subject/body mismatch is glaring: "Celebrate Dad With Gift Ideas" appears nowhere in the rendered body — no Father's Day hero, no gift curation, no dedicated section.
- Twenty-plus equal-weight department tiles create zero visual hierarchy — the eye has no anchor point and no CTA feels primary.
- Second Father's Day email in 48 hours with the same underlying template; the June 12 send already spent that goodwill.
- "Spring Shop 50% Off" in a Father's Day email sent June 14 is a stale seasonal signal sitting one tile above a July 4th block — three seasons in one scroll.
- No time-bounded urgency anywhere in the body despite Father's Day being 7 days away — a missed conversion lever.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Build a real Father's Day grid.** Pull 5–8 tiles that map to dad-friendly categories (Frames, Hobbies, Best-Selling Books, Wearable Art, maybe Custom Frames for a framed gift) and lead with a simple "For Dad" hero banner above them. The current send is copy-pasting a subject over a template that hasn't changed at all.
- 2. **Add a countdown.** "Father's Day is June 21 — 7 days to order and pick up in-store" is a free urgency lever that's completely absent.
- 3. **Kill the Spring Shop tile.** It's jarring and undercuts any seasonal coherence.
- 4. **Don't send a second Father's Day email two days after the first** unless it's a meaningful escalation (last-chance, a new offer, or a curated list). Reusing the same vehicle with a different subject is list fatigue, not strategy.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text on tracked GIF**
- `eORqXTkAb7_837015858_126192_3089910_136677.gif` has no `alt` attribute.
- Evidence: `[WARN] Image missing alt text: src: https://link.hobbylobby.com/mo/eORqXTkAb7_...gif`
- This is likely a tracking pixel or open-tracker beacon. An empty `alt=""` should be present to satisfy screen readers and image-off rendering; a missing attribute causes some clients to render a broken-image icon.

**CSS typo in `.lhp2`**
- `.lhp2, .lhp2 font {line-height:1.2; verticl-align:middle;}` — `verticl-align` is misspelled (`vertical-align`). The property silently fails; vertical centering on CTA button text will fall back to default in clients that don't apply MSO overrides.

**`.ctacr1` border-radius values are empty**
- `.ctacr1 { -moz-border-radius:px; -webkit-border-radius:px; border-radius:px; }` — all three radius values are the unit `px` with no number. This is a syntax error; the properties are ignored, so the button renders square in all clients.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not captured**
- QA finding: header absent in the relayed message.
- Gmail and Yahoo (since Feb 2024 bulk-sender enforcement) require `List-Unsubscribe` for commercial mail above volume thresholds. Its absence — even if caused by the AgentMail relay stripping it — means inbox placement and the native "Unsubscribe" button in Gmail may be suppressed.
- Evidence: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found`

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not present**
- One-click unsubscribe (required by Google/Yahoo for bulk senders) depends on `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`. Its absence means users cannot use the one-click flow; they are directed to a landing page instead. This is a policy violation for high-volume senders.
- Evidence: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found (RFC 8058)`
