# 66% Off Spring Shop® & Summer Toys
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- This is Hobby Lobby's biggest mega-circular yet — bigger than the "New Week, New Savings!" grid from 2026-07-26 that I scored 6.00/10 for sprawl. That one had 25+ stacked tiles; this one runs to over 20 distinct category sections (Home Decor, Textiles, Mirrors & Lamps, Spring Shop/Summer Toys, Furniture, Fall Floral, Fall Decor, Classroom Decor, Dorm Essentials, Party, Hobbies, Wearable Art, Books, Frames, Art Sale, Paper Crafts, Fabric, Jewelry Making, Sewing Notions, Custom Frames, Furniture again) with almost no visual rest between them.
- The subject promises "66% Off Spring Shop® & Summer Toys" but that offer doesn't appear until roughly a sixth of the way down — the hero is a generic "Fall Essentials" banner plus a $59 free-shipping bar. That's a real subject/hero mismatch: the thing that got someone to open isn't the thing they see first. Worse, the email is simultaneously clearing spring/summer stock and pushing fall/back-to-school (Classroom Decor, Dorm Essentials) in the same breath, which reads as "everything must go" rather than a curated seasonal moment.
- Compared to the cleaner lookbook format Hobby Lobby ran on 2026-07-27 ("Discover All Your Fall Essentials Here!", 8.00/10), this is a regression back to the undifferentiated grid, and it's the more extreme version of that problem. Judgment: this is a catalog dumped into an email, not a marketing send — functional for a die-hard bargain hunter willing to scroll, but a poor experience for anyone who opened on the strength of the subject line.

## What's working

- The subject's headline offer (66% off Spring Shop/Summer Toys) is a real, specific number — not vague "big savings" language.
- No visible render bugs — images load, text doesn't overlap, template renders cleanly section to section.
- Free shipping threshold ($59+) is called out immediately at the top, which is a low-friction, universally relevant offer.
- A clear sale-end date appears in the footer ("Sale Ends Through Saturday, August 1, 2026"), giving the whole send a credible deadline.

## What's weak

- The subject's 66% off Spring Shop/Summer Toys offer is buried well below the fold — the hero shows "Fall Essentials" instead, so the thing that drove the open isn't what greets the reader.
- Zero visible CTA buttons anywhere in the render — every section is header + subhead + product photos with no "Shop Now" affordance, so it's unclear where to click even when interested.
- Simultaneous fall push and spring/summer clearance sends a mixed seasonal signal — is this a fall catalog or an end-of-season clearance email?
- Near-identical repeated disclaimer language ("Does not include 'Your Price' items") across a dozen+ sections reads as exclusionary fine print stacked on fine print.
- This is the largest, least-differentiated grid in the recent run — even more sprawling than the 2026-07-26 "New Week, New Savings!" send that already scored a 6.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the subject's actual offer. If 66% off Spring Shop/Summer Toys is the hook, put that hero at the very top — not six sections down behind a Fall Essentials banner.
- 2. Cut this to one seasonal story per send. Either run the fall/back-to-school push (like the 2026-07-27 lookbook that scored 8.00) or run a dedicated clearance email — not both stapled together.
- 3. Add real CTA buttons ("Shop Spring Clearance," "Shop Fall Decor") to at least the top 3-4 sections so there's an unambiguous next action.
- 4. Cap the section count. 20+ categories in one email guarantees most get scrolled past unread — pick the top 5-6 offers and link the rest to a "See All Deals" landing page.
- 5. Consolidate the repeated "Does not include 'Your Price' items" disclaimer into a single footnote instead of restating it in nearly every block.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Last Call: Spring Shop & Summer Toys, 66% Off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 40-75% off fall decor, furniture, and craft supplies — sale ends Saturday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Clear out spring, stock up for fall — all in one sale.`
