# Homepage snapshot · www.hobbylobby.com · 2026-06-25
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- First impression: this is Hobby Lobby doing what Hobby Lobby does — a big, blunt sale banner and nothing else above the fold. The 50% off The Spring Shop is exactly the kind of rotating-category deal I already had mentally circled, so the offer lands. But the hero visual — colorful outdoor wind chimes and metallic spinners — doesn't reach me as a farmhouse and faux-floral shopper. It looks like the garden novelty aisle, not where I spend my money.

## What's working

- **The 50% off call-out** is massive and immediate — I know Hobby Lobby's rotating sale system cold, and this is the most important number on the page. They got that right.
- **The sale deadline** ("Sale Valid Online & In-Store through June 27, 2026") creates real urgency — two days left. That's the kind of clock that gets me off the couch.
- **The Weekly Ad button** in the nav is a thoughtful touch for someone like me. That's my first tap when I land on this site.
- **Free shipping at $59** is prominently placed in the top banner — I routinely hit that threshold, so it reads as a genuine benefit, not bait.
- **Clean mobile layout** — nothing is crashing, overlapping, or fighting for space. The page just works.

## What's weak

- **The hero image is mismatched**: colorful metallic wind chimes and garden spinners are not what I associate with The Spring Shop's best categories. I can't immediately tell whether my faux florals or home decor items are included in this 50% deal.
- **"The Spring Shop" framing is seasonally off**: summer started June 21. This feels like they forgot to update the campaign creative.
- **Zero personalization or loyalty acknowledgment**: I visit weekly. There's no "Hi, welcome back" energy, no rewards points callout, nothing that distinguishes me from a first-time visitor.
- **No category navigation or product rail**: the entire page (above the fold) is one offer. I can't see what categories are on sale without tapping through — which adds friction for someone who wants to zero in on faux floral or home decor fast.
- **"\*See Weekly Ad For Details" without the weekly ad**: if the asterisk sends me to a PDF, I'd rather the weekly ad content surface right here. The caveat makes me second-guess whether my category is actually included.

## Recommendations

- **Swap the wind chimes/spinners for a lifestyle hero that shows farmhouse-adjacent product** — a vignette with faux stems in a mason jar, a wreath, or a shiplap-backdrop tablescape. Speak to the home decorating segment that makes up the Spring Shop's actual buyer.
- **Update the campaign name**: "The Summer Shop" or "Summer Refresh — 50% Off" would stop the calendar dissonance immediately and ship in a day.
- **Add a single-line loyalty/rewards strip** below the free-shipping banner — something like "Earn points on every purchase" — to give loyal weekly visitors a reason to feel recognized.
- **Surface two or three category chips** below the CTA (e.g., "Floral | Home Decor | Seasonal") so I know at a glance whether my categories are in-scope for the 50% off before I tap.
