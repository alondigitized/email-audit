# Up To 75% Off Savings Inside!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Hobby Lobby's weekly sale mega-digest — every department in the store crammed into one email, stacked top to bottom like a printed circular. The subject promises "up to 75% off" but the hero opens on Home Decor 40%, and the actual 75% headline is buried mid-scroll. That disconnect is the defining problem: the subject over-indexes on the biggest number while the email delivers no coherent narrative around it.
- Compared to the June 17 "50% Off The Spring Shop" send — which had a focused hero, clear badge, and a single department story — this email is the antithesis. Where that one had editorial focus, this one has everything: Graduation, July 4th, Classroom Decor, Art Sale, Paper Crafts, Jewelry Making, Sewing Notions, Custom Frames, and Furniture all fighting for attention simultaneously. High information density works in print circulars because shoppers browse them; in email, it collapses hierarchy and kills the click.
- This is a functional email but not an effective one. It will drive some clicks via sheer volume of links, but it won't move any single offer.

## What's working

- Multiple concrete, department-level discounts (40%, 50%, 75%) are clearly rendered — no ambiguity about there being real savings.
- Seasonal pegs are present: July 4th and Graduation sections give the email some date relevance.
- Render is clean — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum visible anywhere.
- The top of the email (Home Decor + Textiles + Mirrors) is product-relevant for a craft/home persona and renders without layout issues.

## What's weak

- "Up To 75% Off Savings Inside!" is one of the weakest possible subject lines for this brand — it names no category, no product, and "savings inside" is inbox filler language.
- The email has no visual hierarchy: 15+ competing offer blocks at roughly equal visual weight means the eye has no place to land.
- The 75% headline — the only thing that distinguishes this from any other Hobby Lobby send — is buried below the fold behind four other category blocks.
- No time deadline is visible anywhere in the email, so none of the offers feel urgent despite the large discount numbers.
- This follows a near-daily email cadence (six audited sends in eight days); this format makes the fatigue worse, not better.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the 75% block.** If Home Decor 75% off is real, make it the hero — full-width image, big badge, one CTA. Everything else can cascade below it.
- 2. **Cut at least half the department blocks.** Pick five categories max for one send; save the others for tomorrow's email (the cadence supports it). Fewer blocks = stronger hierarchy = higher click rate on the surviving ones.
- 3. **Add a deadline.** A single line — "This week only, through Saturday" — turns browsing into urgency. Nothing in this email communicates why today is different from any other day.
- 4. **Rewrite the subject to name the category at 75%.** "75% Off Home Decor — This Weekend Only" outperforms a generic "savings inside" every time.
- **Subject Alt A:** `75% Off Home Decor — Weekend Only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Biggest Hobby Lobby Sale: 75% Off Inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 50% off Party, 40% off Fabric & more — ends Saturday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Home Decor at 75% off + 14 more categories on sale now.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Hobby Lobby "Up To 75% Off" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard table-based HTML build targeting Outlook (MSO conditional comments) and mobile (media queries). Four QA warnings require attention: missing compliance headers, degraded plain-text ratio, an unauthenticated relay path, and a missing alt attribute. Two CSS defects in the stylesheet are also present.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text URL saturation — HIGH**
QA reports 12,158 of 13,761 characters in the plain-text alternative are URLs (88%). This far exceeds the 70% threshold that spam filters use as a signal. The plain-text body is almost certainly auto-generated from tracked redirect URLs rather than human-readable prose.

**Tracking domain** — links route through `link.hobbylobby.com`. Domain appears consistent with the sending subdomain `create.hobbylobby.com`. No mismatched redirect domains detected in the truncated source.

**UTM parameters** — cannot be confirmed from the truncated HTML. See §6.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — GIF beacon/image**
```
src: https://link.hobbylobby.com/mo/eORqXTkAb7_837015858_127904_1655161_136678.gif
```
No `alt=""` attribute present. Even for a tracking pixel this should carry `alt=""` to avoid screen readers announcing a raw filename.

**CSS typo — `vertical-align` misspelled**
```css
.lhp2, .lhp2 font {line-height:1.2; verticl-align:middle;}
```
`verticl-align` is not a valid property — silently ignored by all clients. Any element relying on `.lhp2` for vertical centering will not align correctly.

**CSS defect — missing value in `border-radius`**
```css
.ctacr1 {
  -moz-border-radius:px;
  -webkit-border-radius:px;
  border-radius:px;
}
```
All three `border-radius` declarations have no numeric value (`:px` is invalid). CTA buttons using `.ctacr1` will render with browser-default (sharp) corners in all clients instead of the intended rounded style.

**Custom font without confirmed `@font-face`**
```css
.ff3ng {font-family: Museo Sans, Trebuchet MS, Verdana, Tahoma, sans-serif;}
```
`Museo Sans` is referenced but no `@font-face` declaration is visible in the truncated source. Most email clients will silently fall back to `Trebuchet MS`. Not a breakage risk given the fallback stack, but layout shifts are possible if the designer assumed Museo Sans metrics.

**Responsive structure** — MSO conditional styles and `@media (max-width:300px)` blocks are present. Structure appears intact for Outlook and narrow viewports.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (`{{`, `%%`, `*|`) visible in the truncated source. ESP template attributes `e-locale="en-US"` and `e-is-multilanguage="false"` are set and consistent.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
QA confirms neither `List-Unsubscribe` nor `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) headers were captured. Gmail and Apple Mail surface one-click unsubscribe UI from these headers. Their absence:
- Increases unsubscribe friction for users
- Triggers Gmail's "Report spam" path instead of managed unsubscribe
- As of February 2024, Google/Yahoo bulk sender requirements mandate `List-Unsubscribe-Post` for senders exceeding 5,000/day

**Root cause** — QA notes these may not be captured due to the AgentMail relay, not necessarily absent from the original send. Confirm with raw headers at the inbox level before treating as a confirmed violation.

**SPF/DKIM authentication unknown**
`Authentication-Results` header not present in captured data. Cannot confirm DMARC pass for `create.hobbylobby.com`. Requires raw inbox header verification.

**Physical address / CAN-SPAM footer** — not visible in truncated HTML. Cannot confirm or deny presence. Must be verified in full source.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Cannot fully evaluate from truncated HTML — all destination URLs are wrapped in `link.hobbylobby.com` redirects. UTM parameter presence and landing page alignment require:
- Clicking through tracked links to confirm final destination URLs carry `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`
- Spot-checking that sale landing pages reflect the "Up To 75% Off" claim

No structural issues detectable at the HTML level.
