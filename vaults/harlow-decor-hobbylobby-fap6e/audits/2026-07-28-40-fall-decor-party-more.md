# 40% Fall Decor, Party & More
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-28**
## Executive summary

- This is a return to Hobby Lobby's mega-circular format after the cleaner "Fall Essentials" lookbook (2026-07-27, 8.00/10) and the pure-branding "Fall Commercial" send (2026-07-27, 7.00/10) — and it shows. The hero itself is genuinely strong: a warm styled-mantel shot with pumpkins, florals, and framed landscape art, "40% Fall / Decor, Party, Floral & Crafts" set in bold black type, and a single blue "In-Store Or Online" button. That's a clean, on-season hero that would stop a scroll.
- But everything below the hero is the sprawling grid Hobby Lobby leans on too often — seven fall category tiles, then a wall of unrelated clearance categories (Spring Shop, Summer Toys, Classroom Decor, Dorm Essentials, Art Supplies, Online Clearance) stacked with no visual break between "fall" and "everything else in the store." It reads like three emails stitched together rather than one campaign, and it dilutes the sharp "40% Fall" hook the subject line promised.
- Net: strong top, muddled bottom. This lands closer to the 6-range sends (2026-07-26, 2026-07-22) than to the 8.00/10 lookbook from the day before.

## What's working

- The hero image is genuinely well-composed — pumpkins, gallery-wall art, throw pillows — and the "40% Fall" headline is legible and on-season.
- Subject line copy ("40% Fall Decor, Party & More") is directly reflected in the hero, so open-to-hero continuity is strong.
- Single clear CTA button ("In-Store Or Online") sits right under the offer with no ambiguity about what to click.
- Free shipping banner ($59+) is placed high and is easy to spot before scrolling.
- No render bugs — every tile, badge, and button rendered cleanly with no overlap or broken images.

## What's weak

- After the fall category grid, the email pivots into Spring Shop, Summer Toys, Classroom Decor, and Dorm Essentials — all off-season or off-message next to a "40% Fall" hero, and there's no header or divider signaling the shift.
- Eleven separate "X% Off" claims compete for attention by the bottom third; nothing after the hero stands out as the offer.
- "Online Clearance" table (Mirrors & Lamps, Hobbies, Best-Selling Books, etc.) is dense small-print text that reads like a footer, not a merchandising moment.
- Every secondary tile repeats "See Weekly Ad For Details" — a soft disclaimer that undercuts the confidence of the 40%/66%/75% headline numbers.
- Cadence concern: this is the third mega-circular grid in the last week (2026-07-26 "New Week, New Savings!", 2026-07-22 "Unbeatable Prices") after two cleaner sends broke the pattern — subscribers who liked the lookbook format are back to the wall-of-tiles.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email at the fall category grid (Fall Decor, Floral, Kitchen, Party, Candles, Crafts) and spin the Spring/Summer/Classroom/Dorm/Art/Clearance block into a separate "Back to Campus & Clearance" send — this single edit would fix the diluted focus and cadence-repetition problem more than any copy tweak.
- 2. If the lower block must stay, add a visual section header ("Also On Sale This Week") to separate it from the fall campaign so the email doesn't read as three merged newsletters.
- 3. Replace repeated "See Weekly Ad For Details" with the actual expiration date inline (the footer already shows "Sale Valid Through Saturday, August 1, 2026" — surface that near the hero CTA for urgency).
- 4. Simplify the Online Clearance table into 2-3 visual tiles instead of an 8-row text grid; it's the weakest visual moment in the email.
- **Subject Alt A:** `40% Off Fall Decor — This Week Only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Fall Refresh Starts at 40% Off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Pumpkins, florals & mantel decor — plus free shipping on $59+`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New fall arrivals just dropped, all 40% off through Saturday`
