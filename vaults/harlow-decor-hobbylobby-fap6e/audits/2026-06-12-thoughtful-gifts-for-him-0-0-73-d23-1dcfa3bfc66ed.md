# Thoughtful Gifts For Him 🎁
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- This is Hobby Lobby's Father's Day send, arriving June 12 — nine days out from Father's Day (June 21). The subject does something smart: it drops the brand's default "40% off [category]" formula and instead leads with a gifting occasion and a human insight ("thoughtful"). That's legitimately the best subject line this brand has sent in the entire audit window. Respect where it's due.
- But the email body betrays the subject line immediately. The hero section is small and somewhat generic — wooden DAD letters and a football-adjacent visual — and within two scrolls you're back in the same department-dump grid that appeared in every prior send: Home Decor, Spring Shop, Summer Toys, July 4th, Classroom Decor, Paper Crafts. Of those six, exactly one (Summer Toys, 50% off) plausibly maps to Father's Day gifting. The subject says "Thoughtful Gifts For Him"; the body says "here's our weekly ad."
- The other glaring own-goal: the email opens with a **"Spring Essentials"** banner showing kitchen items, bath products, and home staging photography — live, at the top of a Father's Day email. The calendar collision is jarring and signals that the template wasn't refreshed for this campaign.

## What's working

- **Best subject line in the audit** — "Thoughtful Gifts For Him" is warm, occasion-specific, and under 30 characters.
- **Father's Day timing is solid** — nine days of lead time is the sweet spot for gift purchasing.
- **Summer Toys at 50% off** is a genuinely relevant Father's Day hook buried in the grid; it deserved hero treatment.
- **50% off "The Spring Shop" items** is a deeper discount than the standard 40% and is a real signal for deal-seekers.

## What's weak

- **Spring Essentials header actively contradicts the Father's Day premise** — the template wasn't swapped and it reads as two separate emails stapled together.
- **Department grid is the same six-tile dump from every other send** — only one of the six tiles has any plausible gift-for-dad relevance.
- **Hero is undersized** — the DAD visual and the "Gifts For Him" headline compete for space with the weekly-ad banner and are gone within one scroll.
- **No urgency or deadline** — nine days to Father's Day is a natural hook, but neither the subject nor the body mentions a ship-by date or "arrives by Father's Day" framing.
- **"Spring Essentials" imagery of kitchen/bath items reads as female-targeted** right above a "For Him" hero, creating a demographic whiplash.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the Spring Essentials header for a Father's Day banner.** This is the single highest-leverage change — a header image of tools, grilling, outdoor gear, or the actual hero product line would resolve the calendar collision and set tone immediately.
- 2. **Rebuild the grid around him, not the weekly ad.** Pull the six-tile template and replace it with a curated 3-tile "gifts under $X" or "top picks for Dad" grid using actual categories: outdoor/yard, hobby/craft kits for men (woodworking, model kits), home bar/entertaining. Drop Classroom Decor entirely.
- 3. **Promote Summer Toys to a featured banner** with a specific price-point call-out (e.g., "Outdoor Games & Backyard Fun — 50% Off"). This is Hobby Lobby's legitimate Father's Day hook and it's buried in tile 3.
- 4. **Add a shipping urgency line.** "Order by [date] — arrives before Father's Day" is a standard lever; it's absent here and easy to add.
- 5. **Make the July 4th tile Father's Day-adjacent** ("Fire Up the Grill Decor — 40% Off July 4th") since outdoor/patriotic overlaps more with the male persona than the current generic framing.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Gifts Dad Will Actually Use — 40-50% Off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Father's Day Is June 21 — Shop Thoughtful Gifts`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Summer toys, outdoor gear, and hobby kits — all on sale this week.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Order by [date] and it'll arrive before Father's Day.`
