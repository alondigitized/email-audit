# Budget-Friendly Shopping With Everyday Low Prices
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-16**
## Executive summary

- This send is the anti-thesis of the subject line. "Budget-Friendly Shopping With Everyday Low Prices" implies broad savings, but what follows is Hobby Lobby's now-familiar wall-to-wall category tile grid — nine category icons (Crosses, Easels, Floral Arrangements, Paint Sets, Art Canvas, Table Clocks, Table Mirrors, Trees, Wood Blocks) followed by roughly 20 individual SKU tiles with specific prices ($1.99 chargers, $49.99 helium tank, $24.99 epoxy). It's essentially a scrollable price catalog, not a marketing email with a hierarchy.
- There is a genuine hook buried at the top — "Free Shipping On Orders $59 Or More!" in a red banner — but it's sandwiched between a nav bar and a seasonal "Fall Essentials" hero that has nothing to do with the subject's low-price promise. By the time a reader scrolls to the actual price list, the email has already burned three sections of unrelated creative.
- Compared to the 2026-09-13 "Fall Into Savings" send (4.00/10) and 2026-09-10 "Select Home Decor" send (5.00/10), this is the same format problem recurring for the third time in under a week — the brand is defaulting to an undifferentiated tile-grid template regardless of subject line. This one scores similarly low: the individual product tiles are clean and legible, but there's no hero, no single offer, and no visual entry point that matches the subject.

## What's working

- Individual SKU tiles are clean, legible, and each has a clear price, product name, and "In-Store Or Online" tag.
- "Free Shipping On Orders $59 Or More!" is a concrete, real offer visible near the top.
- Top nav (Weekly Ad, Stores, Shop, DIY) gives quick wayfinding for a subscriber who already knows what they want.
- No render bugs — all images, prices, and text blocks display correctly with no overlap or broken assets.

## What's weak

- Subject promises "Everyday Low Prices" but the hero is a Fall/seasonal banner ("Fall Essentials") that has nothing to do with pricing — bait-and-switch between subject and top-of-fold content.
- No single hero offer or dominant visual — the eye has nowhere specific to land; it's an undifferentiated grid of 20+ tiles stacked for multiple screens.
- "NEW LOWER PRICES!" headline sits above a random assortment (crosses, easels, floral, paint sets, wood blocks) with no unifying theme or reason to care.
- This is the third near-identical tile-grid format in six days (09-10, 09-13, now 09-16) — cadence fatigue is real for a subscriber already reviewed those.
- CTA is a plain text link ("CLICK HERE to view our Weekly Ad") on a flat blue bar, not a styled button — weak primary CTA.

## Recommendations

- 1. Rebuild the hero to actually deliver on "Everyday Low Prices" — pick 3-4 of the strongest price drops (the $1.99 chargers, $2.99 candy wafers) and feature them big, above the fold, instead of the unrelated Fall Essentials banner.
- 2. Cut the tile count roughly in half — a 20-SKU scroll reads as a catalog dump, not an email; group into 2 tight sections max.
- 3. Turn the Weekly Ad link into a real button with contrasting color and verb-noun copy ("View This Week's Deals") instead of plain text on a bar.
- 4. Vary the format from the last two sends — the brand has run this same grid three times in a week; try a themed collection (e.g., "Under $5 Crafting Essentials") to break the pattern.
