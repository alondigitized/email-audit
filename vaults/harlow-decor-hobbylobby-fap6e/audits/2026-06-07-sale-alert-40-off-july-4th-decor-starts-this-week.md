# Sale Alert 🚨 40% Off July 4th Decor Starts This Week!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- This is Hobby Lobby's seventeenth-ish send in seventeen-ish days, and the subject line is finally doing something different — "40% Off July 4th Decor Starts This Week!" is the most event-specific, timely hook this brand has used in weeks. The urgency is real: July 4th is less than a month out, and leading with a holiday-specific offer is exactly the kind of differentiation that should break through inbox fatigue.
- The body, however, completely undermines it. Open the email and the hero screams **"Spring Lookbook"** — no July 4th imagery, no red/white/blue, no patriotic framing anywhere near the fold. The brand is sending a holiday-specific subject with a recycled spring template, which is the email equivalent of a bait-and-switch. Subscribers who opened for the 4th of July angle are immediately disoriented.
- Below the hero, it's the same cascading 17-department discount dump that has appeared in every single audit since at least May 17. No July 4th section is carved out near the top. The subject-to-body disconnect is this email's defining failure, and it wastes what is genuinely the best subject line this brand has sent recently.

## What's working

- **Subject line is the strongest in the audit run** — "40% Off July 4th Decor Starts This Week!" is concrete, holiday-specific, urgent, and timely. It earns an open.
- **40% off is a clear, specific discount** — no ambiguity about magnitude.
- **Time-bounded urgency is credible** — "Starts This Week" maps to a real upcoming holiday, unlike vague "limited time" copy.
- **No render bugs** — layout is intact, images load, text doesn't overlap.
- **HOME DECOR leads the grid** — at least the most relevant department comes first in the body.

## What's weak

- **"Spring Lookbook" hero directly contradicts the July 4th subject** — this is the single biggest miss; a subscriber who opened for the holiday angle lands on stale spring creative.
- **No July 4th section visible near the fold** — the holiday offer that sold the open is buried or absent in the body.
- **Same 17-department dump template as every prior send** — identical structure to the May 17, May 29, May 31, June 2, June 3, June 4, and June 5 emails; creative exhaustion is now undeniable.
- **🚨 emoji in subject is borderline spam-signal territory** — red alarm siren is aggressive for a craft store; it edges toward the "!!! stacking" the rubric penalizes.
- **No personalization visible** — name, loyalty status, category preference — nothing to individualize the send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the hero immediately** — replace "Spring Lookbook" with July 4th / Americana-themed creative. This is the single change that would most lift this email. A subscriber opened because of the 4th of July hook; land them on 4th of July imagery.
- 2. **Surface a dedicated July 4th shop section above the department grid** — one curated block (flags, patriotic decor, outdoor entertaining) before the kitchen-sink dump would justify the subject and increase click intent.
- 3. **Cut the department grid from 17 to 6-8** — every audit has flagged this. Fewer departments = clearer hierarchy = more clicks to the items that actually convert.
- 4. **Drop the 🚨 from the subject** — the offer is strong enough without it; the siren reads as desperate.
- 5. **Add a deadline** — "Starts This Week" tells me when it begins but not when it ends; "through June 15" would sharpen urgency.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Red, White & 40% Off: July 4th Decor Is Here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Flags, outdoor entertaining, and Americana picks — all at 40% off through [date].`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop our July 4th collection before it sells out — 40% off starts this week.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Hobby Lobby "Sale Alert 40% Off July 4th Decor"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on a Salesforce Marketing Cloud (ExactTarget) template stack with standard MSO conditional rendering. Three CSS declaration bugs are present in the shipped stylesheet, and QA flagged four warnings spanning compliance headers, authentication visibility, and a text-part composition issue that raises spam-filter risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text part is URL-saturated**
QA finding: 12,384 of 13,994 chars (88.5%) in the plain-text alternative are URLs — far above the 70% warning threshold. This is a strong spam-filter signal and suggests the plain-text part was auto-generated by concatenating raw tracked redirect URLs rather than being written as readable prose.

**Click-tracking domain confirmed:** `link.hobbylobby.com` (visible in the tracking pixel `src` attribute). UTM parameter passthrough cannot be verified from the truncated source but should be confirmed at QA time against final landing page URLs.

**No other link-level issues confirmed** from the available source fragment.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS typo — property will be silently ignored:**
```css
/* .lhp2 rule */
verticl-align: middle;   /* should be: vertical-align: middle; */
```
Affects vertical alignment of CTA button text in non-MSO clients.

**Malformed border-radius values on `.ctacr1`:**
```css
.ctacr1 {
  -moz-border-radius: px;     /* missing number — invalid */
  -webkit-border-radius: px;  /* missing number — invalid */
  border-radius: px;          /* missing number — invalid */
}
```
All three declarations are invalid CSS (unit with no value). The intended radius is absent; browsers will ignore these rules entirely. If `.ctacr1` is used for a rounded CTA button, it will render as square corners everywhere.

**Empty `color` declaration on `.ctacr1`:**
```css
.ctacr1 { color: ; }
```
Vacuous declaration — no effect, but indicates a template variable (`%%CTATextColor%%` or similar) that was never resolved before send. If the intent was a specific foreground color, it is missing.

**Missing alt text on tracking GIF:**
```
eORqXTkAb7_837015858_124811_52029_135183.gif
src: https://link.hobbylobby.com/mo/eORqXTkAb7_...
```
This is a 1×1 open-tracking pixel — `alt=""` is the correct value for decorative/invisible images and should be explicitly set rather than omitted, to satisfy screen-reader and accessibility linters.

**Custom font fallback:** `.ff3ng` declares `font-family: Museo Sans, Trebuchet MS, ...`. Museo Sans is a licensed desktop font not available as a web-safe or system font; it will never render in email clients. The fallback chain (`Trebuchet MS, Verdana, Tahoma, sans-serif`) will be used universally — confirm the design accounts for this.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

The `e-locale="en-US"` and `e-is-multilanguage="false"` attributes on `<html>` are ExactTarget/SFMC rendering directives — these are correct and expected. No unresolved merge tokens (`%%...%%` or `{{...}}`) are visible in the available source fragment. The empty `color:` in `.ctacr1` (noted above) is the one probable unresolved token.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
QA confirmed this header was not present as captured by the relay. As of February 2024, Gmail and Yahoo require bulk senders (≥5k/day) to publish a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Its absence risks inbox demotion at both providers.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click machine unsubscribe (required by the same Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy) is not supported without this header paired with a `List-Unsubscribe: <https://...>` URL. If the ESP is configured to inject these headers, the AgentMail relay may be stripping them — that should be investigated at the relay layer.

**`Authentication-Results` header not captured**
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown. The sending domain is `create.hobbylobby.com`; the click/tracking domain is `link.hobbylobby.com`. DKIM alignment requires the `d=` tag to match or align with the `From:` domain. This cannot be confirmed without the raw headers — escalate to the ESP or relay to expose authentication results.

**CAN-SPAM physical address:** Cannot confirm presence from truncated source. Must appear in footer.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Click links route through `link.hobbylobby.com` (ESP redirect layer). UTM parameter presence on final landing page URLs cannot be confirmed from the truncated source. The sale subject ("40% Off July 4th Decor") should land on a page scoped to that promotion with matching UTM campaign values — verify `utm_campaign` is set and not defaulting to a homepage or generic sale page.

No broken or suspicious redirect targets are visible in the available fragment.
