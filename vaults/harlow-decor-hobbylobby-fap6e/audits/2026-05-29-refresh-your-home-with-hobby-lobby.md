# Refresh Your Home With Hobby Lobby®
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- "Refresh Your Home" is Hobby Lobby's eleventh (or twelfth) email in roughly twelve days, and the template is now a known quantity: lifestyle hero photo → "Refresh Your Home" headline with a video play button → department discount tiles stacked below. The home-decor angle gives this send a tighter through-line than most in the recent run — the hero imagery (warm-toned lamp, armchair, vases, wall art) actually matches the subject line — but the execution collapses immediately into the same five-tile department dump. The Spring Shop at 50% and Hobbies/Floral at 40% are legitimate offers; they're just buried in the same grid Hobby Lobby has shown ten times already.
- The more damaging issue: "Sunny Days" at 50% is a word-for-word repeat of the May 28 hero offer ("Sunny Days Ahead ☀️ 50% Off Spring & Summer"). A real subscriber opened that email yesterday. Seeing the same tile today signals either a scheduling error or a brand that simply doesn't care about repetition.
- **Verdict:** Competent on a standalone basis; brand-fatiguing in the context of the cadence. The home focus is the right instinct — lean into it harder or don't bother differentiating.

## What's working

- **Subject-to-hero coherence.** "Refresh Your Home" is matched by warm, aspirational home-decor lifestyle photography in the hero — rare for this brand's recent run, which typically mismatches subject and hero.
- **Free Shipping banner is front and center.** "$59 or more" threshold is immediately visible — a genuine conversion nudge that answers the friction question before the reader asks.
- **Spring Shop 50% and Hobbies 40% are clearly labeled.** The tile typography is legible and the discount percentages are prominent.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, overlapping text, or layout breakage visible.

## What's weak

- **"Sunny Days" 50% tile is a direct repeat of yesterday's May 28 send.** Subscribers who opened both will notice. This erodes trust in the offer and confirms the brand is on autopilot.
- **Hero has a play button but no offer.** The video element creates a hierarchy dead-end — the eye goes to the lifestyle photo and play button, not to a CTA or discount. There's no reason to click the hero.
- **Five competing department tiles dilute focus.** Spring Shop, Sunny Days, Hobbies, Floral, Paper Crafts — none of them dominate. The home-focus setup in the hero is immediately abandoned.
- **No time boundary visible.** Nothing in the render communicates a deadline, sale end date, or urgency signal.
- **Cadence is working against this email.** This is the eleventh send in twelve days. Even a well-executed email loses lift at this frequency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Own the home angle or cut the unrelated tiles.** If the subject is "Refresh Your Home," the department tiles should be Home Decor, Floral, Frames/Wall Art, Textiles — not Hobbies and Paper Crafts. The subject sets an expectation the email body doesn't fulfill.
- 2. **Pull "Sunny Days" and replace it with a net-new offer.** Running the same 50%-off tile two days in a row burns subscriber trust. Swap in Outdoor Living, Patio, or Summer Entertaining to keep the calendar feeling fresh.
- 3. **Add a deadline to the hero.** "Through Sunday" or "This Week Only" in the hero subhead costs nothing and adds the one urgency signal this email completely lacks.
- 4. **Give the hero a CTA button, not just a play button.** A "Shop Home Decor" button directly below the headline would anchor the email's intent before the tile grid starts.
- 5. **Reduce cadence.** Three to four sends per week would recover open rates and make each individual email feel like an event rather than noise.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Home Edit: Up to 50% Off This Week`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns on furniture, wall art, and more — free shipping over $59`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Spring Shop at 50% off + 40% off Home Decor through Sunday`

## Technical Audit — Hobby Lobby "Refresh Your Home" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype with MSO/VML conditional blocks targeting legacy Outlook. Two confirmed CSS bugs in the embedded stylesheet; four QA warnings across compliance and deliverability — none critical, but the missing unsubscribe headers and unknown auth state are actionable.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text URL density — HIGH RISK**
QA reports 14,477 of 16,693 plain-text chars (87%) are URLs. Many ESPs and spam filters treat this ratio as a strong spam signal. The plain-text part should be human-readable prose with a small number of links, not a dump of every tracked redirect URL.

**Tracking domain:** All links and the open-pixel GIF route through `link.hobbylobby.com`. No issues with the redirect domain itself, but UTM parameter coverage on destination URLs cannot be verified from the truncated source (see §6).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS typo — `verticl-align` (line in `.lhp2` rule)**
```css
.lhp2 {line-height:1.2; verticl-align:middle;}  /* should be vertical-align */
```
Property is silently ignored in all clients. Any element using `.lhp2` will not receive the intended middle alignment.

**Malformed `border-radius` and empty `color` in `.ctacr1`**
```css
.ctacr1 {
  color:;                        /* empty value — parse error */
  -moz-border-radius:px;         /* missing numeric value */
  -webkit-border-radius:px;
  border-radius:px;
}
```
All three declarations are invalid and will be dropped. The CTA rendered by `.ctacr1` will fall back to browser defaults for color and have square corners rather than any intended radius.

**Missing alt text on open-pixel GIF**
```
src: https://link.hobbylobby.com/mo/eORqXTkAb7_837015858_124831_610199_134901.gif
```
Confirmed by QA. Screen readers will announce the raw filename. Convention is `alt=""` (empty string) for tracking pixels so assistive technology skips them entirely.

**`viewport` meta — no `width=device-width`**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="initial-scale=1, user-scalable=yes">
```
Without `width=device-width`, iOS Mail and some Android clients will not trigger the responsive layout breakpoints defined in the media queries below. Standard pattern is `width=device-width, initial-scale=1`.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header — MISSING (QA WARN)**
Neither `List-Unsubscribe` nor `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click) were detected by the AgentMail relay capture. Gmail and Yahoo have required one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) for bulk senders since February 2024. Absence does not mean the headers aren't present at SMTP delivery, but the relay did not surface them — this needs verification at the raw message level.

**Authentication-Results — UNKNOWN (QA WARN)**
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown because the `Authentication-Results` header was not captured. Sending domain is `create.hobbylobby.com`; the envelope/DKIM alignment with that subdomain should be confirmed. DMARC failure on a major retail domain would cause significant inbox placement issues.

**CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe mechanism:** Cannot verify from the truncated source alone. These are typically in the footer — needs full-source confirmation.
