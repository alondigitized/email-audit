# Elevate Your Space With Select Home Decor!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-15**
## Executive summary

- The subject promises "Select Home Decor," but the email opens on Christmas. The first screen is a "Free Shipping On Orders $59 Or More" bar, a "Christmas Essentials" banner and a giant "CHRISTMAS 50% off" block. Home Decor ("Always 40% Off The Marked Price") only appears as the second block. This is the same mismatch as the 10/11 "Spice Up Your Fall With 40% Off" send, which also led with Christmas.
- Underneath, it's the Hobby Lobby weekly-ad tile wall again: more than a dozen category blocks stacked down a very long scroll. Those blocks cover Textiles, Fall Decor, Football Party, Floral, Hobbies, Wearable Art, Crafts, Frames, Paper Crafts, Yarn, Jewelry Making, Sewing and Furniture. There's no single hero and no obvious button. The 40% badges repeat so often that none of them stands out.
- It's on-brand and the deal is real, but it's a catalog, not a campaign. Verdict: a middling send that treats the subject line as an afterthought. I could only read the large headings in the render, not the small print.

## What's working

- The Hobby Lobby header and the "Free Shipping On Orders $59 Or More" bar are instantly recognizable.
- The big percent-off badges (50%, 40%, 30%) make the discounts easy to see at a glance.
- The Home Decor block has clear sub-categories (Framed Art, Canvas Art, Tapestries, Decorative Hardware, Wall Clocks) with product photos.
- A credible deadline sits at the bottom: "Sale Valid Through Saturday, October 17, 2026."
- The seasonal mix (fall, football, Christmas) fits mid-October, and I saw no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- The subject says Home Decor, but Christmas 50% is the hero. The subject and first screen don't match.
- There are 14 or more equal-weight tiles, so nothing leads the eye and the scroll is exhausting.
- No clear CTA button is visible. The tiles are the only click targets, and none says "Shop Home Decor."
- Exclusion fine print is scattered under almost every block ("Does not include Seasonal Department…"), which makes the offers feel conditional.
- The deadline is buried at the very bottom. Nothing near the top creates urgency.
- It's another recycled tile wall. 10/8 "Select Home Decor – Always 40% Off" and 10/13 "Fall Florals – 40% Off" covered the same ground.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make Home Decor the hero, since it's what the subject promises. Put it first, with a lifestyle image, "Always 40% Off," and a single "Shop Home Decor" button. Move Christmas lower or to its own send.
- 2. Cut the tile count to 4–6 categories and link each to a real landing page.
- 3. Put the "Ends Saturday 10/17" deadline in the header area, not the footer.
- 4. Collapse the exclusions into one fine-print line instead of repeating them under every tile.
- 5. Don't reuse the same weekly-ad template three times in a week. Rotate in a single-theme send.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Wall art, clocks & tapestries: 40% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Framed art, wall clocks and more at 40% off the marked price. Ends 10/17.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Refresh a room for less, plus free shipping on $59+.`

## Technical Audit

**1. Technical Summary**
The email has no confirmed hard failures: the QA run reports 0 issues and 4 warnings. The main findings are the missing alt text on one tracking image, an unverified unsubscribe and authentication posture, and a few CSS defects in the visible source. The HTML source was truncated, so anything past the `<style>` block is unverified.

- The tracking-image host is `link.hobbylobby.com/mo/eORqXTkAb7_837015858_140751_331568_149246.gif`. This is the ESP's click and open tracking domain, so it is a first-party subdomain.
- The plain text is 11,500 of 13,042 characters URLs (88%), above the 70% warning threshold. This usually means long tracking-wrapped links and can increase spam-filter scoring. The cause is probably redirect-wrapped URLs, but the truncated source doesn't let me confirm that.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- **Missing alt:** `<img src="https://link.hobbylobby.com/mo/eORqXTkAb7_837015858_140751_331568_149246.gif">` has no `alt`. If it is a 1x1 open-tracking pixel, it needs `alt=""` so screen readers skip it. If it is content, it needs descriptive text.
- **Mobile breakpoint:** The only visible media query is `@media only screen and (max-width:300px)`. Phones are generally 320px wide or more, so the responsive rules (`.fl`, `.fw`, `.image`, `.mh`) likely never fire on real devices. The rest of the stylesheet was truncated, so there may be other breakpoints.
- **Invalid CSS declarations in `.ctacr1`:** `color:;`, `-moz-border-radius:px;`, `-webkit-border-radius:px;` and `border-radius:px;` have empty values. Clients drop them, so the radius and colour fall back to inherited values.
- **Typo:** `.lhp2 { verticl-align:middle; }` should be `vertical-align`, so the property is ignored.
- **Document declarations:**
  - The HTML 4.01 Transitional DOCTYPE is paired with `xmlns` attributes. This is common in email but inconsistent.
  - The non-standard `e-locale` and `e-is-multilanguage` attributes are on `<html>`.
  - There is no `lang` attribute, which hurts screen-reader pronunciation.
- **Fonts:** Several font stacks (`Arial Narrow`, `Museo Sans`) have no generic fallback, for example `.ff2 {font-family: Arial Narrow;}`.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
No issues found in the visible source. No unresolved tokens such as `{{ }}`, `%%`, or `[[ ]]` appear in the truncated portion.
