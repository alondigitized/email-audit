# Stock Up On Style With Everyday Deals
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-02**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuine departure from Hobby Lobby's usual wall-to-wall category-tile grid — instead it's an "Everyday Best Price" catalog of 20 individual SKUs with real photography and real dollar figures, from $1.99 charger plates to a $49.99 helium tank. That's more honest and more scannable than the vague "40% off fall" hero banners this brand has been leaning on, and the "Look For The YOUR PRICE Sticker" framing gives it a plausible in-store tie-in.
- The problem is there's no hierarchy at all. Twenty products of wildly different relevance (glue guns, candy wafers, epoxy, batteries, vinyl) are given equal visual weight in a single undifferentiated two-column list — nothing is a hero, nothing says "start here." For a shopper like Walker, a fall/home persona, there's no seasonal or category framing beyond the small "Fall Essentials" banner up top, and most of these products (industrial adhesive, AA batteries, glue guns) sit far outside typical fall-decor browsing.
- Compared to 08/28's tablescape hero (6.00/10, this brand's best send) or even 08/26's art-supply lead (5.00/10), this trades a strong visual hook for a long undifferentiated price list. It's more truthful than the grid-of-categories emails but it isn't going to earn a click on its own merits — it reads more like a weekly circular than a marketing email.

## What's working

- Real dollar amounts on every item ($1.99, $4.99, $49.99, etc.) is the most concrete pricing this brand has shown in its recent history — no vague "% off" language.
- The "YOUR PRICE" sticker callout gives a tangible, recognizable in-store cue that ties the email to the physical shopping experience.
- Free shipping threshold ($59+) is stated plainly near the top, above the fold.
- Clean, consistent product-tile formatting (image, price, name, size, availability) makes the list easy to scan even though it's long.
- "Fall Essentials" banner and Kitchen/Bath/Home Essentials row give at least a thin seasonal frame at the top.

## What's weak

- No single hero product or offer — 20 nearly-equal tiles with no visual anchor means the eye has nowhere obvious to land.
- Product mix is incoherent for a fall/home persona: industrial adhesive, AA batteries, a heat-press vinyl roll, and a helium tank sit next to wreaths and charger plates with zero grouping logic.
- Subject line "Stock Up On Style With Everyday Deals" oversells — most of what's shown (glue, batteries, epoxy, tape) isn't "style," it's craft-supply restocking.
- The email is extremely long (20 products, requires heavy scrolling) with no jump links or category anchors to shortcut to a specific section.
- Primary CTA is a plain text/banner link ("Click Here To View Our Weekly Ad") buried mid-page rather than a clear button tied to an actual offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick 4-6 of the most photogenic, most seasonally relevant items (wreaths, charger plates, canvas) and lead with those as a hero grid; move batteries/adhesive/glue into a secondary "Craft Essentials" section further down.
- 2. Add a real CTA button (not a thin banner strip) directly under the hero copy — "Shop Everyday Prices" — instead of making shoppers scroll to find the weekly-ad link.
- 3. Segment the list into labeled sections (Fall Decor, Craft & Adhesives, Storage & Organization) so the eye has a map instead of one continuous scroll.
- 4. Tighten the subject to match what's actually inside — this isn't a "style" email, it's a value/price email.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Everyday Low Prices: $1.99 Charger Plates & More`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Price Is Here — Storewide Deals Under $10`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `From glue guns to wreaths — see the "Your Price" sticker savings`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free shipping on $59+ orders, plus 20 everyday low prices inside`
- 5. Trim the total item count or paginate — 20 tiles in one continuous scroll is fatiguing on mobile with no way to skip ahead.
