# Unbeatable Prices On Thousands Of Items!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Hobby Lobby's "new lower prices" variation of their now-familiar mega-circular format — two columns of product tiles, each with a product image and a dollar price, preceded by a red "NEW LOWER PRICES!" hero and a shipping bar. The theme is permanent everyday pricing (not a limited sale), which means there's no anchoring against a crossed-out old price, no percentage savings, and no deadline. The result is a grid of $1.99–$49.99 price tags that feel like a store shelf, not an email worth opening.
- The product mix is eclectic without being intentional: grapevine wreaths sit next to AA batteries, Mod Podge next to a balloon helium tank, candy makers next to iron-on vinyl. There is no editorial through-line, no seasonal hook (despite the grapevine wreaths being obvious Fall adjacency material — especially after the July 21 Fall send scored 7/10 by leaning into that angle), and no reason this email had to arrive today rather than any other Tuesday.
- This is the same format that ran July 19 ("Summer Savings Inside"), July 17 ("Room Decor Kids Will Love"), July 16 ("Renew Your Space"), and July 15 ("Sew Much Savings"). The subject "Unbeatable Prices On Thousands Of Items!" is the weakest subject hook in that entire run — it promises everything and says nothing.

## What's working

- **Free shipping threshold is front and center** — the "$59 Or More" banner is above the fold and immediately addresses purchase friction.
- **Specific dollar prices** — every tile shows an exact price ($1.99, $4.99, $5.99), which is more actionable than a vague "up to X% off."
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds across the full grid.
- **"NEW LOWER PRICES!" hero reads fast** — the red treatment and bold type make the value proposition land in under a second.

## What's weak

- **No was/now anchoring** — "new lower prices" is meaningless without showing what the old price was; every tile just shows a price with nothing to compare it to.
- **Subject line is generic hyperbole** — "Unbeatable Prices On Thousands Of Items!" is the weakest hook in Hobby Lobby's recent run and gives the subscriber zero reason to prioritize this open.
- **No category coherence** — AA batteries, helium tanks, Mod Podge, and grapevine wreaths have no narrative thread; the email reads like clearance, not curation.
- **CTA copy is weak** — "CLICK HERE TO VIEW OUR MORE!" is grammatically awkward and non-specific.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is the fifth consecutive send using the same two-column price grid; a subscriber who opened the July 19 Summer Savings send already knows what this looks like.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add was/now pricing to every tile** — even a single "was $7.99" crossed out next to $4.99 changes the cognitive frame from "a store shelf" to "a real deal." This is the highest-leverage single change.
- 2. **Pick a category thread and own it** — the grapevine wreaths and chargers point toward Fall entertaining; lean into that framing with a headline like "Your Fall Table, Priced Lower Than Ever" instead of the generic hero.
- 3. **Fix the CTA copy** — "Shop All New Lower Prices" or "See Every Price Drop" is a quick fix over the current "VIEW OUR MORE" phrasing.
- 4. **Break the cadence pattern** — the next send using this format should either add a countdown ("prices locked through Sunday") or rotate to a single-category spotlight to give subscribers a reason to treat it differently.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New Lower Prices — Wreaths, Crafts & More`
- **Subject Alt B:** `We Cut Prices. Here's What's Lower Now.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping at $59 — stock up on the items you use every project.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Grapevine wreaths from $4.99, glue from $3.99, and more newly reduced prices inside.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS defect 1 — empty `color` value in `.ctacr1`:**
```css
.ctacr1 {color:; ...}
```
The `color` property has no value. Declaration is invalid and will be dropped by all parsers. If this rule provides the CTA text color, the button text may render as the inherited color (likely black on a dark `#013088` background), making it unreadable.

**CSS defect 2 — unitless `border-radius` in `.ctacr1`:**
```css
-moz-border-radius:px; -webkit-border-radius:px; border-radius:px;
```
All three `border-radius` values are missing the numeric component. Invalid — rendered as `0` or ignored. Visual impact is minor (square corners instead of rounded), but confirms a template variable substitution failure: a merge token like `%%BORDER_RADIUS%%` was not resolved before send.

**CSS defect 3 — typo in `.lhp2`:**
```css
verticl-align:middle;
```
Should be `vertical-align`. Property is silently ignored by all clients; middle-alignment on CTA buttons will not apply.

**Dark mode:** No `<meta name="color-scheme">` or `<meta name="supported-color-schemes">` present. Clients that force dark mode (Apple Mail, Outlook iOS) may invert hardcoded `#ffffff` text or `#000000` backgrounds unpredictably.

**Viewport:** `user-scalable=yes` is set, which is correct — do not suppress zoom in email.
