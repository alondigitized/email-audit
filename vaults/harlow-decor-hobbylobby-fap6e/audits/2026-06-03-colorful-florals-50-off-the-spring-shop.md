# Colorful Florals 🌷 50% Off The Spring Shop®
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- This is Hobby Lobby's fourteenth-ish email in as many days, and it arrives as a repackaged version of an offer subscribers have already seen multiple times. The "Colorful Florals" hook in the subject is the most distinctive thing about this send — and the email doesn't follow through on it. The hero image delivers spring garden flair, but the product grid below is the same cascading craft-supply dump (vinyl, foam board, canvas, paint sets, $0.99 sundries) that has appeared in virtually every prior send. The "Colorful Florals" frame effectively promises one thing and delivers another.
- The structural template is identical to May 26 ("Light Up The Season With 50% Off The Spring Shop®") and May 19 before that. Three sends leading with the same 50% Off Spring Shop® offer means a real subscriber has now mentally filed this as wallpaper. Yesterday's "Vintage-Inspired Wall Art" email (June 2, scored 8/10) at least opened with a specific category angle; this one retreats to the generic Spring Shop umbrella.
- Verdict: fine execution on a stale concept. The send would have had more impact if the product grid had been filtered to actual floral/garden SKUs to back up the subject's premise.

## What's working

- **Subject–hero alignment is decent at the surface level.** The "Colorful Florals" subject pairs with a genuinely bright, flower-forward hero image — pots of purple/blue blooms with garden decor — so the first frame of the open is coherent.
- **50% off is a hard, specific number.** The offer badge is prominent and legible without scrolling on the render.
- **Free shipping threshold banner is persistent.** "$59 or more" at the top header is a sensible anchor for any cart-building behavior.
- **Products include visible prices throughout.** The grid shows real SKU-level prices, which is more useful than vague "up to X%" language.

## What's weak

- **Subject–body mismatch undermines the floral premise.** "Colorful Florals" sets an expectation; the product grid is dominated by vinyl transfer tape, foam boards, blank canvas sets, and liquid dye — not florals. The only floral SKU visible is a $14.39 item buried at the very bottom.
- **This is the third (at minimum) 50% Off Spring Shop® email.** May 26 and May 19 led with the identical offer; sending it again on June 3 signals either list fatigue or an exhausted promotional calendar.
- **CTA copy is broken.** "CLICK HERE TO FIND OUR BRAND AD" is one of the weakest calls-to-action in retail email — "brand ad" is internal language that means nothing to a consumer, and "click here" is the weakest possible verb.
- **No urgency signal anywhere.** No expiry date, no "this week only," no countdown — the 50% off floats without a deadline, which lowers the reason to act now vs. later.
- **Product grid is undifferentiated from prior sends.** Foam boards, canvas sets, paint — these same SKUs have cycled through multiple prior emails, and nothing in the layout signals that this grid is fresh.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Filter the product grid to actual floral/garden SKUs.** If the subject says "Colorful Florals," the grid should show floral wire, silk blooms, garden stakes, seed packets, floral foam — not generic craft supplies. This single change would make the email coherent and differentiated from the last ten sends.
- 2. **Fix the CTA.** Replace "CLICK HERE TO FIND OUR BRAND AD" with something product-action-oriented — "Shop 50% Off Florals," "See This Week's Picks," or even just "Shop Now." The current copy actively reads as a mistake.
- 3. **Add a hard expiry date.** "50% off through Sunday" or "offer ends June 8" would convert fence-sitters who are otherwise habituated to ignoring Hobby Lobby email until they actually need something.
- 4. **Rest the Spring Shop® offer.** A category-specific email (florals, outdoor living, summer crafts) with its own % off would feel new. Running "50% Off Spring Shop®" a fourth time loses all residual urgency.
- **Subject Alt A:** `50% Off Every Floral & Garden Pick This Week`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Favorite Florals Are Half Price 🌷`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `In-store and online — no code needed, ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Garden decor, silk blooms, and more — all 50% off through the weekend.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard ESP-generated HTML blast (HTML 4.01 Transitional, Outlook VML conditionals present) with two confirmed CSS bugs in the stylesheet, one missing alt text, and three header-level gaps — two compliance-critical (List-Unsubscribe) and one deliverability-relevant (auth results). ### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text part — extreme URL density (confirmed by QA)**
- 15,085 of 18,044 characters in the plain-text alternative are URLs (83.6%).
- This is well above the ~30% threshold that triggers spam filters (Barracuda, SpamAssassin `URL_RATIO`).
- Evidence: `[WARN] Plain-text is >70% URLs: 15085/18044 chars are URLs`
- Root cause: ESP likely auto-generates the plain-text by stripping HTML and leaving raw redirect URLs, with no prose fallback.

**Tracking domain**

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS typo — `verticl-align` (line in `.lhp2` rule)**
```css
.lhp2, .lhp2 font {line-height:1.2; verticl-align:middle;}
```
`verticl-align` is not a valid property; the intended `vertical-align:middle` is silently dropped in all clients. Affects CTA button vertical alignment.

**Malformed border-radius values in `.ctacr1`**
```css
.ctacr1 { ... -moz-border-radius:px; -webkit-border-radius:px; border-radius:px; }
```
All three `border-radius` declarations are missing the numeric value (e.g., `4px`). The property resolves to `initial` (0) in all clients — corners will be square regardless of design intent.

**Missing alt text on tracking pixel / GIF**
- `eORqXTkAb7_837015858_125437_533277_134218.gif` has no `alt` attribute.
- Evidence: `[WARN] Image missing alt text: eORqXTkAb7_837015858_125437_533277_134218.gif`
- For a tracking/open-pixel GIF, `alt=""` (empty string) is the correct fix — omitting the attribute entirely causes some screen readers to announce the filename.

**Viewport meta — minor**
- `<meta name="viewport" content="initial-scale=1, user-scalable=yes">` — `user-scalable=yes` is the default and redundant, but causes no harm.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent (CAN-SPAM / GDPR risk)**
- Evidence: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found: Header may not be captured by AgentMail relay`
- Gmail and Yahoo require `List-Unsubscribe` for bulk senders (>5k/day) or they surface the "Report Spam" path instead of a clean unsubscribe UI. This is a deliverability and legal exposure item.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent**
- Evidence: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found`
- Without this, Gmail/Yahoo one-click unsubscribe (enforced since Feb 2024 for bulk senders) does not function. Recipients who try to unsubscribe via the client UI may instead mark as spam.

**Authentication-Results header absent**
- Evidence: `[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found: Expected via AgentMail relay — SPF/DKIM status unknown`
- SPF and DKIM pass/fail status for `create.hobbylobby.com` cannot be confirmed. If either is failing, inbox placement and DMARC compliance are at risk. Needs verification at the relay layer.

**Sending domain**
- From address `noreply@create.hobbylobby.com` with HTML e-locale `en-US`. No physical postal address visible in the truncated source — required by CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5). Cannot confirm its presence without the full footer HTML.
