# Homepage snapshot · www.hobbylobby.com · 2026-05-15
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- This is my store, plain and simple. The second that page loaded I saw duck figurines, terracotta pots, faux blooms on a little painted bench — that is my language. Hobby Lobby is leading with their Spring Shop at 50% off and making a strong push to get me in before tomorrow's deadline, which I appreciate because I already had this week flagged on my calendar.

## What's working

- **The hero vignette** — duck figurine, faux flowers in terracotta pots, painted white bench with a pop of purple blooms. I felt seen. That image could be on my Pinterest board.
- **50% OFF in giant type** — Hobby Lobby trains you to look for the rotating 50% category, and here it is, huge, no squinting required.
- **"Sale Valid Online & In-Store through May 16, 2026"** — That one line does a lot of work for a skeptic like me. It tells me the deal is real in-store (not a digital-only trick) and it gives me a hard deadline.
- **Free Shipping on Orders $59 Or More** — The threshold is in the very first banner. $59 is completely realistic for one spring decor haul, so this actually registers as achievable.
- **Weekly Ad button** — Prominent, dark navy, above the search bar. That's the first place I go every single time. They know their customer.

## What's weak

- **No loyalty/rewards hook whatsoever** — I have a Hobby Lobby account. Nothing on this screen acknowledges me. For a weekly shopper, that missed connection feels like walking into your regular coffee shop and being treated like a stranger.
- **"*See Weekly Ad For Details" asterisk** — I know what this means (category exclusions, single-item rule), but a first-time shopper might feel misled when they get to checkout. Small asterisk, big friction potential.
- **No navigation shortcuts visible** — The hamburger menu is there, but with medium tech comfort I'd rather have one or two category pills (Floral, Home Décor, Seasonal) directly under the logo. One tap to my section, not two.
- **Hero image has no people in it** — I love the product vignette, but a lifestyle shot with a woman my age arranging that exact display in her home would have hit harder emotionally.

## Recommendations

- **Add a thin loyalty strip** between the free-shipping banner and the hero — something like "You have X points · Redeem in-store or online" would make a weekly shopper like me feel recognized before I even scroll.
- **Swap the hamburger-only nav for 3–4 category chips** below the search bar ("Floral," "Home Décor," "Seasonal," "Frames") so medium-tech users can reach their section without hunting through a menu.
- **Put a person in the hero** — Keep the farmhouse vignette, but composite in a woman's hands arranging the flowers. It bridges product-shot and lifestyle-shot without losing the aspirational aesthetic.
- **Make the asterisk clause explicit** — Replace "*See Weekly Ad For Details" with "One item per day. Some exclusions apply." Harlow already knows this rule, but the transparency would keep the offer feeling honest to everyone.
