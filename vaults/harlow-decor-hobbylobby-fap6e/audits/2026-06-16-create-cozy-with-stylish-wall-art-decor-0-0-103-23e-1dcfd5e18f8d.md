# Create Cozy With Stylish Wall Art & Decor
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- This is Hobby Lobby's "Create Cozy" home decor send — the latest in what has become a near-daily email cadence. The subject promises a focused, aesthetic home decor experience; the email delivers that for exactly one hero block before immediately devolving into the familiar scatter of six unrelated promotional tiles (Classroom Decor at 50%, Hobbies at 40%, July 4th, All Fabric, Online Clearance). That whiplash is now the defining structural problem with this brand's emails.
- The hero holds up. A warm, gold-toned lamp vignette with a visible 40% off hook on Home Decor is genuinely on-theme and on-season — "cozy" in summer is a stretch, but the creative execution is clean and the product grid beneath it is coherent. If the email stopped there, this would be a solid 7.
- It doesn't stop there. The lower half repeats the "50% Classroom Decor" block that ran in the June 11 send — a near-identical creative. Seeing that same tile less than a week later confirms what was already suspected: Hobby Lobby is running a rotation, not a strategy. That repetition hurts both trust and click motivation.

## What's working

- **Hero is on-brief.** The warm home decor vignette with a lamp and 40% off Home Decor matches the subject line's promise — rare coherence for this brand.
- **Free shipping callout is above the fold.** "$75 or more" threshold is immediately visible, removing a friction point before the scroll.
- **Product grid category is consistent.** Clocks, frames, vases, wall art — everything in the top grid stays in the same category. No sudden jump to craft supplies.
- **Subject line is mobile-friendly at 43 characters** and avoids the usual punctuation spam.

## What's weak

- **The "Create Cozy" theme collapses halfway down.** The moment the promotional tile stack appears — Classroom Decor, Hobbies, July 4th, Fabric — the email becomes a circular instead of a destination.
- **"50% Classroom Decor" is a direct repeat from June 11.** Subscribers who opened that email will recognize the exact same tile five days later; kills novelty and erodes trust in the offer calendar.
- **No urgency signal anywhere visible.** No end date, no "this week only," nothing that creates a reason to act now rather than later (i.e., never).
- **Subject carries zero offer.** "Create Cozy" is lifestyle copy — pleasant but noncommittal. The 40% discount lives inside the email, not in the hook that gets the open.
- **CTA buttons are small and undifferentiated** in the product grid; no single call-to-action stands out as the primary action to take.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the offer in the subject.** "Create Cozy With Stylish Wall Art & Decor" is a mood board headline, not an inbox hook. Adding the discount converts browsers into clickers before they open.
- **Subject Alt A:** `40% Off Wall Art & Home Decor — Cozy Up Your Space`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Home Decor Sale: 40% Off + Free Shipping`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Clocks, frames, vases and more — all on sale this week.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free shipping on $75+ and 40% off everything home.`
- 2. **Cut the promotional tile stack or make it one max.** The email earns the home decor header; it throws it away with five off-topic tiles below. Pick one cross-sell (clearance is fine) and let the hero breathe.
- 3. **Retire the Classroom Decor tile until it's new again.** Running the same creative in back-to-back weeks signals an empty content calendar. If Classroom Decor needs a second push, give it a dedicated email with fresh copy.
- 4. **Add an end date.** Even a vague "This week only" above the primary CTA would lift urgency without requiring a hard deadline.
- 5. **Elevate one hero product with a direct product link.** The lamp in the hero vignette is doing real work — it's the most compelling asset in the email. Link it directly to the PDP instead of a category page.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text body is 86.2% URLs (14,383 / 16,686 chars).** QA flags this at >70%. ### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Bug 1 — CSS typo silently breaks vertical alignment:**
```css
.lhp2, .lhp2 font {line-height:1.2; verticl-align:middle;}
```
`verticl-align` is not a valid property. The rule is silently ignored by all parsers. Intended: `vertical-align`.

**Bug 2 — CTA border-radius values missing numeric units:**
```css
.ctacr1 {
  -moz-border-radius:px;
  -webkit-border-radius:px;
  border-radius:px;
}
```
All three declarations are malformed (unit with no number). The CTA button renders with no border-radius in Firefox/Gecko clients and wherever these prefixed properties are applied. Intended: a numeric value, e.g. `4px`.

**Bug 3 — CTA color property is empty:**
```css
.ctacr1 {color:; ...}
```
Empty `color:` value. Browsers and email clients ignore this; the button text color falls back to inherited or UA default, which may be black on a dark background (`background-color:#013088`). Intended: `color:#ffffff` or matching contrast value.

**Accessibility — Missing alt text on tracked GIF:**
```
src: https://link.hobbylobby.com/mo/eORqXTkAb7_837015858_126868_466968_135736.gif
```
No `alt` attribute detected. Screen readers will either skip or read the raw filename. If this is a decorative image, `alt=""` is required to mark it as decorative. If it conveys content, a descriptive alt string is required.

**Viewport meta missing `width=device-width`:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="initial-scale=1, user-scalable=yes">
```
Without `width=device-width`, mobile clients that honor this tag may render at desktop width before scaling. Standard: `content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1"`.

**Non-system font in stack:**
`.ff3ng` includes `Museo Sans` as the first font. Museo Sans is not installed on any default OS or email client. Fallback chain (`Trebuchet MS, Verdana, Tahoma, sans-serif`) will be used universally, making the Museo Sans declaration dead weight. No web font loading (`@font-face`, Google Fonts link) is present in the truncated source.
