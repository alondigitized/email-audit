# Prep, Pack & Go With Hobby Lobby®!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- "Prep, Pack & Go" sets up a focused lunch-prep editorial, and the hero delivers: a warm lifestyle shot of food containers and kitchen prep gear under a clean typographic headline. It's the most cohesive hero Hobby Lobby has run all week. Then you scroll three inches and it completely falls apart — the offer grid below is the same rotating 40%-off-home-decor, 40%-off-fall, 66%-off-spring-shop tiles that have appeared in some form in every email since July 19. The subject line and the body are two different emails stapled together.
- The most alarming element is a button in the middle of the email that reads **"CLICK HERE TO VIEW OUR ABOUT US"** — placed exactly where a "Shop Lunch Prep" CTA should live. That is either a broken merge field, a mis-inserted default placeholder, or a genuine copy error. Either way, a subscriber who opened expecting lunchbox deals is instead invited to read the company's origin story. That single failure kills any click momentum the hero built.
- On top of that, one of the prominent offer tiles is **"Items Labeled 'The Spring Shop' — 66% Off"** sent on July 24. Spring clearance in late July is defensible; labeling it "The Spring Shop" when fall inventory has been live since at least July 21 makes the email feel like it was assembled from a stale template.

## What's working

- **Hero image and headline** — "Lunch Prep Essentials" over a clean food-prep lifestyle shot is the sharpest editorial entry point Hobby Lobby has run in this audit window; clear purpose, warm visual.
- **Free shipping banner** — prominent, legible, and anchored at the top before any scroll.
- **Offer tile percentages** — 40%, 66%, 30% badges are large and immediately scannable in the grid.
- **Subject line length** — 34 characters, well within mobile-safe range, no gimmicks.

## What's weak

- **"Click Here To View Our About Us" CTA** — placed dead-center after the hero; this is either a template error or a catastrophic editorial misfire. It is not a shopping CTA.
- **"The Spring Shop" tile in late July** — 66% off Spring clearance is a reasonable deal; calling it "The Spring Shop" while Fall and Summer are already live reads as stale.
- **Hero-to-offer disconnect** — "Lunch Prep Essentials" hero leads into home decor, fall, yarn, floral — not a single lunch container or food storage offer in the grid.
- **"Shop In Store >" as every tile CTA** — if these are in-store-only offers, that's a major barrier for an email-opening subscriber expecting to click through online.
- **Repeat creative fatigue** — 40% Home Decor and 40% Fall have run in every email since July 21; yesterday's send (7/23) led with Home Decor 40% as its only message. This is the third consecutive exposure.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix or remove the "About Us" CTA immediately** — replace it with "Shop Lunch Prep Essentials →" linking to a curated collection or at minimum the kitchen/storage category. This is the single highest-impact fix.
- 2. **Retire or relabel "The Spring Shop" tile** — rename to "End-of-Season Clearance" or fold it into a "Clearance" row so it doesn't broadcast staleness.
- 3. **Add one lunch-prep offer tile** — even a "30% off food storage & containers" tile directly beneath the hero would close the hero-to-body gap and justify the subject line.
- 4. **Rotate out 40% Home Decor** — it has now appeared as a lead or prominent tile in the July 22, 23, and 24 sends. If it must stay, demote it to the footer category row.
- 5. **Clarify online vs. in-store availability** — if these deals are in-store only, say so in the hero copy; if they're also online, rewrite CTAs to "Shop Online →".

- **Subject Alt B:** `Pack a Better Lunch — Hobby Lobby Has the Gear`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Containers, bags & kitchen essentials — plus 40% off fall décor this week`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Everything you need for a great lunch setup — in store & online`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] "My Account" nav link resolves to a relative path `/login` at the destination.**

The click-tracking redirect at `https://link.hobbylobby.com/u/nrd.php?...&d=...NavBar_My_Account...` returns an unknown URL type for `/login`. This is a broken user experience: clicking "My Account" likely 404s or lands on an unintended page. **Plain-text version is URL-saturated:** 10,452 of 12,445 characters (84%) are URLs. This is above the 70% threshold and degrades plain-text readability. Some spam filters also penalize high URL density in the text part.

**Tracking pixel present:** `eORqXTkAb7_837015858_130665_3772817_141129.gif` is loaded via the hobbylobby link relay — no issue with the mechanism itself, but see Accessibility below.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text on tracking/open pixel:**
`src: https://link.hobbylobby.com/mo/eORqXTkAb7_...141129.gif` has no `alt` attribute. Screen readers will announce the raw URL or a generic placeholder. For a 1×1 tracking pixel, the correct fix is `alt=""` (empty string) to mark it as decorative and suppress announcement.

**Doctype:** `HTML 4.01 Transitional` with `xmlns` XHTML attributes — a common legacy ESP pattern. No confirmed rendering failure, but modern clients may handle certain CSS resets differently than intended. The MSO conditional blocks (`<!--[if (gte mso 9)]>`) are correctly structured.

**CSS typo in `.lhp2`:**
```css
.lhp2, .lhp2 font {line-height:1.2; verticl-align:middle;}
```
`verticl-align` is misspelled — should be `vertical-align`. This declaration is silently ignored by all clients, meaning the middle-alignment intent is lost for `.lhp2` elements.

**Incomplete border-radius values in `.ctacr1`:**
```css
-moz-border-radius:px; -webkit-border-radius:px; border-radius:px;
```
All three values are empty (`px` with no number). The CTA button will render with square corners rather than the intended rounded corners in all clients.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not detected.** The QA relay did not capture this header. If the header is present at the MTA layer but stripped by AgentMail, no action needed — verify via raw message source in the receiving mailbox. If genuinely absent, major inbox providers (Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail) will not surface the one-click unsubscribe UI, increasing spam complaints.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) header not detected.** One-click machine-readable unsubscribe (required by Google/Yahoo bulk sender policies since Feb 2024) cannot be confirmed. This is a deliverability risk for high-volume sends. Same caveat about relay stripping applies.
