# Your Weekly Ad Is Here!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- This is Hobby Lobby's weekly ad email — essentially a digitized flyer digest. It leads with a broad "Spring Essentials" umbrella and then stacks department-by-department sale callouts (Home Decor, Hobbies, Spring Shop, Graduation, Summer, Party, Floral, Wearable Art, Best-Selling Books, Frames, Art Sale, Paper Crafts, Fabric, Jewelry Making, Sewing Notions, Custom Frames, Furniture). Every section has a discount — 40% off, 50% off, 30% off — and a product thumbnail grid. There's no personalization, no editorial voice, no anchor story. It reads exactly like a printed circular uploaded to email.
- The saving grace is pure utility: Hobby Lobby shoppers who treat this as a shopping list will get value immediately. Everything they need to know — what's on sale, how much — is scannable. But the email does nothing to differentiate, nothing to build desire, and nothing to prioritize one offer over another. Every section competes equally for attention, which means no section wins.
- Compared to last week's "Come On In!" welcome email, this is the opposite problem: that one had no offer, this one has too many. Neither email has a single clear story. The brand's email program still feels like it's running on autopilot.

## What's working

- **Discount clarity is excellent** — every section shows a clean "X% off" callout, large and legible, with no ambiguity about the offer.
- **Department breadth is a strength for a shopping-list audience** — a crafter who buys across categories will find multiple relevant sections in one scroll.
- **Product photography renders cleanly** — thumbnails are legible, no broken images visible, no layout breakage.
- **The "Spring Essentials" masthead ties the sends** — establishes a seasonal context that at least gestures toward a coherent campaign theme.
- **Sale Art headline at $9.99–$24.99 is the most specific price point visible** — rare specificity in an otherwise percent-off-only email.

## What's weak

- **No hero offer — forty sections fight equally for attention**, making it impossible for the eye to land on anything.
- **Zero personalization** — a sewer, a jewelry maker, and a painter all receive the exact same email with no preference signal.
- **Sewing Notions section shows a 99¢ offer with a thumbnail that's nearly unreadable** — the price point is interesting but buried 15+ sections deep.
- **No urgency signal anywhere** — no "this week only," no sale-end date, no countdown; "weekly ad" implies urgency but the email body never makes it explicit.
- **CTAs are absent** — there are no visible buttons. The entire email relies on the user understanding they should click a section header or image, which is non-obvious.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero offer and build the top fold around it.** The current design treats 40% off Home Decor identically to 30% off Paper Crafts. Choose the most traffic-driving category and give it 40% of the above-the-fold real estate.
- 2. **Add explicit sale-end dating** — "Deals valid through Sunday, May 18" — everywhere. "Weekly ad" is not a deadline.
- 3. **Add at least one named CTA button per section** ("Shop Home Decor," "Shop Fabric") so the clickable action is unambiguous.
- 4. **Suppress irrelevant sections per subscriber** based on past purchase or browse category. A jewelry maker does not need the full floral/furniture/book stack.
- 5. **The 99¢ Sewing Notions lead item is your best hook** — move it closer to the top or feature it in the subject line as a tangible price anchor.
- **Subject Alt A:** `40% off Spring Decor + 99¢ notions — this week only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Hobby Lobby deals expire Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Home decor, fabric, frames, art & more — all on sale through May 18`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `This week: 50% off select hobbies, 40% off spring décor, 99¢ sewing picks`
