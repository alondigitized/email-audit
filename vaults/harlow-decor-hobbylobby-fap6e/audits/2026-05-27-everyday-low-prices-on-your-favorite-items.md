# Everyday Low Prices On Your Favorite Items!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- This is Hobby Lobby's ninth email in about ten days, and it takes a structural detour from the brand's relentless %-off weekly-ad format. Instead of "40% off Home Decor," it leads with "NEW LOWER PRICES!" — an everyday-low-price reset announcement. That's a legitimately different story, and the Spring Essentials hero gives it seasonal cover. Compared to the near-identical sends that have piled up since May 17, this one at least has a distinct premise.
- The structural problem is the CTA: "CLICK HERE TO VIEW OUR WEEKLY AD" directly undermines the "permanent lower price" message. Everyday low prices and a weekly ad are conceptually opposite things — the first is a rollback you keep, the second expires Sunday. A subscriber who reads "NEW LOWER PRICES" and then clicks to the weekly ad gets a confusing mixed signal, and that confusion will bleed into conversions.
- The email also carries zero urgency by design (EDLP is the opposite of a deadline), which is fine if the product selection is magnetic enough to drive impulse. A 20-item grid of $1.99 chargers, foam bricks, and scatter stones is not that pull. The "Picnic Party! 50% Off" send from May 19 had more forward momentum than this.

## What's working

- **"NEW LOWER PRICES!" headline** is the first real break from the %-off template in nine sends — gives a fatigued subscriber a reason to believe something actually changed.
- **Specific price points throughout** ($1.99, $4.99, $8.99, etc.) are credible and fast to scan — feels more like a price rollback than a vague promotional sale.
- **Spring Essentials** hero ties the EDLP angle to a seasonal hook, preventing the email from reading like off-season clearance.
- **Free shipping banner** ($59 threshold) is prominent at the very top and adds a concrete secondary incentive.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping elements, no visible layout failures.

## What's weak

- **CTA copy contradicts the premise**: "CLICK HERE TO VIEW OUR WEEKLY AD" directly conflicts with "everyday low prices" — the weekly ad is time-bounded by definition, making the EDLP claim feel hollow.
- **No urgency signal and no savings anchor**: EDLP messaging needs either a hero product ("Was $12.99, now $8.99") or a dramatic category claim to compensate for the absence of a deadline.
- **Twenty-item grid with no hierarchy**: foam bricks, glue guns, grapevine wreaths, a $49.99 pedestal, and composition books are all weighted identically — there's no featured SKU to anchor the eye or justify the scroll.
- **Subject line is the weakest in the recent run**: compared to "Picnic Party! 50% Off The Spring Shop®" (May 19) and "40% Off Florals" (May 20), "Everyday Low Prices On Your Favorite Items!" has no specificity, no urgency, and no hook.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the CTA copy first** — if these are permanent price reductions, the button must say "SHOP NEW LOWER PRICES" or "SEE ALL PRICE DROPS," not "VIEW OUR WEEKLY AD." This single change closes the messaging contradiction.
- 2. **Lead with one hero SKU** — pick the most dramatic rollback (e.g., grapevine wreaths at $4.99) and make it the visual hero above the grid. Give subscribers a concrete "this is lower than it was" moment before the product dump.
- 3. **Add a savings anchor** — even a light "Was $X → Now $Y" label on two or three grid items turns "lower prices" from a claim into proof.
- 4. **Trim the grid to 8–10 items** — 20 items on mobile is a scroll wall; fewer, larger images drive more clicks per item.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `We Just Dropped Prices on Spring Essentials — Starts at $1.99`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `New Lower Prices on 20+ Craft Staples — No Coupon Needed`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Grapevine wreaths, foam bricks, glue guns — prices rolled back for good.`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Permanent cuts on the supplies you restock every season — see what changed.`
