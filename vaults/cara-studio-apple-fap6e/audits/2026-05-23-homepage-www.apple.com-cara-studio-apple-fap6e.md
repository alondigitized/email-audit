# Homepage snapshot · www.apple.com · 2026-05-23
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- Apple's homepage is doing exactly what Apple does — minimal, confident, zero clutter. The iPhone lineup is front and center with a color showcase that's basically designed for someone like me who refreshes hardware on the color drop. It feels premium and current, but there's no financial hook, no Trade-In mention, no Apple Card 3% callout — all the stuff that actually drives me to configure and buy same day is nowhere above the fold.

## What's working

- **Three-phone color lineup hero** — the side-by-side of navy, white, and pink is doing more selling than the copy. That pink is going to move units. For me personally, seeing the color options immediately is the whole point.
- **Dual CTA structure** — "Learn more" for the curious, "Shop iPhone" for the ready-to-buy. Clean split intent. The blue filled button pulls first, the outline button is right there as a softer option.
- **Tight, confident copy** — "Meet the latest iPhone lineup." Three words and a descriptor. Apple's copy register is exactly my reading speed — no fluff, no forced enthusiasm.
- **MacBook Neo teaser below fold** — the headline "Amazing Mac. Surprising price." is an interesting hook peeking up from the bottom. That "Surprising price" phrasing is doing real work for a brand not known for being affordable.

## What's weak

- **No Trade-In CTA anywhere visible** — I literally buy every cycle because Trade-In makes it feel like a wash. The homepage has zero mention of it. This is leaving conversion on the table from return customers like me.
- **No Apple Card 3% callout** — I check my card rewards before every major purchase. This is a first-party financial product and it's invisible above the fold.
- **No pricing or value anchor** — Not even "from $X" or "as low as $X/mo." For someone who knows the line, fine. But even I want to see the monthly with trade-in before I commit to tapping "Shop."
- **Zero human lifestyle presence** — Product shots only. No one using their iPhone. No creative-at-work scene, no studio setup. For a brand that usually sells aspiration and lifestyle, this is oddly clinical.
- **No urgency or inventory signal** — No "new," no launch date, no "just dropped." If this lineup just launched I'd want to know I'm seeing something fresh, not last cycle's shelf.

## Recommendations

- **Add a Trade-In value estimator or banner** — even a strip that says "Get up to $XXX toward iPhone 17 with eligible trade-in" would capture returning customers like me who are mid-cycle contemplating their upgrade window.
- **Drop a lifestyle shot alongside the color grid** — a quick 50/50 split of product vs. a creative-at-work environment using the phone. The M-chip MacBook crowd responds to environment, not just hardware.
- **Surface Apple Card 3% on the "Shop iPhone" flow entry** — even a small badge or footnote near the CTA: "3% Daily Cash with Apple Card." That's a first-party trust signal that genuinely accelerates my decision.
- **Add a "What's new in this lineup" anchor** — one line under the sub-headline calling out the one feature that changed (camera, chip, color). I watch the keynote live but most people didn't — give them the hook.
