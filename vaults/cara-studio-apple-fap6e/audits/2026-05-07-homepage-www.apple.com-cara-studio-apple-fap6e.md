# Homepage snapshot · www.apple.com · 2026-05-07
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-07**
## Executive summary

- This is Apple doing what Apple does best — beautiful, minimal, and on-calendar. The Mother's Day hero is charming and I get it immediately, but it's a gift-guide play, not a "here's what's new for you" moment. Apple feels less like a retailer right now and more like a curated boutique that trusts you already know what you want.

## What's working

- **The illustrated Mother's Day Apple logo** — that floral gift-bow treatment is genuinely delightful. It stopped my scroll because it's unexpected from a brand that usually leads with product photography.
- **"It's not too late to find the perfect gift."** — this copy is doing real work. Low-pressure, but it activates the procrastinator guilt in a warm way. Apple understands tone.
- **The "Shop" pill button** — high contrast blue on the white/gray background, impossible to miss. Single action, no decision paralysis.
- **iPhone teaser below the fold** — "Meet the latest iPhone lineup." peeking through is a nice breadcrumb that tells me there's actual product content if I scroll.
- **Clean mobile nav** — search, bag, and hamburger are exactly where I expect them. Zero friction.

## What's weak

- **No personalization whatsoever** — I've bought multiple iPhones, a MacBook, and AirPods through Apple.com. Not a single "Welcome back" or "Your next upgrade" signal. For an ecosystem brand, this is a miss.
- **"Shop" is doing too much lifting** — where does it go? Mother's Day gift guide? iPhone? AirPods? The CTA is a black box and I have no idea what I'm buying into.
- **Apple Card 3% back is completely invisible** — for a Mother's Day purchase, that cashback reminder would be a genuine conversion push. I literally use the Apple Card every cycle and they're not leveraging it at all.
- **No trade-in hook** — Mother's Day + "upgrade mom's phone + trade in her old one" is a layup. Not even hinted at.
- **Illustrated logo is beautiful but product-free** — there's no actual device visible above the fold. I don't know if I'm being sold AirPods, a Watch, or a gift card.

## Recommendations

- **Replace the generic "Shop" CTA with category splits** — "Shop AirPods," "Shop Apple Watch," "Shop iPhone" as stacked or carousel pills. Mother's Day gifts have obvious SKU clusters; surface them immediately.
- **Add an Apple Card badge to the hero** — one line: "3% Daily Cash back with Apple Card." That's free money I'm already earning — reminding me of it at the gift-decision moment is a no-brainer conversion nudge.
- **Swap the illustrated Apple logo for a lifestyle hero with an actual product** — keep the warm Mother's Day palette but show an AirPods Pro or Apple Watch in that floral environment. Brand *and* product, not brand *instead of* product.
- **Surface a trade-in entry point** — a single sub-headline like "Trade in her old iPhone. Gift her a new one." would unlock the upgrade-gifting mindset and differentiate Apple from any other gift retailer.
