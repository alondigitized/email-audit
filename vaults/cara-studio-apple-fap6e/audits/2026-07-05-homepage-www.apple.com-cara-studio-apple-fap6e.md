# Homepage snapshot · www.apple.com · 2026-07-05
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- This is Apple doing Apple — effortlessly minimal, completely self-assured. The M5 MacBook Air is front and center, and honestly, as someone who watches every keynote and plans her next upgrade around the new color drop, this lands. That said, the homepage is essentially a product poster: it tells me *what* is new but gives me zero reason to act right now versus the next time I feel like it.

## What's working

- **"MacBook Air / Now supercharged by M5."** — Punchy, confident, three words that make me immediately calculate my trade-in timing. This is the copy register I expect from Apple.
- **Dual CTA layout ("Learn more" + "Buy")** — Gives me the option to research OR commit right now. I've already seen the keynote so "Buy" is where I'm going.
- **Product colorway** — That sky-blue finish on the MacBook Air is visible and gorgeous. Color is literally how I decide my upgrade cycle. This is doing work.
- **Minimalist nav bar** — Search, bag, hamburger. No clutter, no pop-ups, no distractions. Respects my time as a high-tech-comfort user who knows what she's looking for.
- **"iPhone / Meet the latest iPhone lineup."** peeking below the fold tells me this is a full product ecosystem page, not a one-product microsite.

## What's weak

- **Zero Apple Card callout** — I get 3% back on every Apple purchase with my Apple Card, and Apple knows this because it's their card. Not surfacing "3% Daily Cash with Apple Card" above the fold for a $1,100+ product is a real miss.
- **No trade-in hook** — My current MacBook is going to fund this purchase. A "Get $X–$Y for your MacBook" banner would have me tapping "Buy" in 10 seconds flat. Nothing here.
- **No color variant preview** — The hero shows one colorway. I chose my last MacBook Air based on the Starlight vs. Midnight decision. Showing the palette up front would immediately deepen engagement for buyers like me.
- **No monthly pricing callout** — Apple Financing and Apple Card installments exist but aren't referenced. This is a $1,000+ device — showing "From $41.62/mo" would lower the psychological barrier to tap "Buy."
- **Lifestyle signal is zero** — It's a product float on a gradient. Beautiful, but no creative pro in her studio, no "built for what you make" moment. For my persona, a creator context shot would land harder than a product render.

## Recommendations

- **Add an Apple Card 3% + monthly installment strip** directly under the CTA row — "From $41.62/mo at 0% APR with Apple Card." This is information I need before I tap "Buy" and it exists; just surface it.
- **Swap the lone product float for a color-spread hero** — show the MacBook Air in all four colorways side by side and let me tap to configure. Color is the emotional purchase trigger for Cara-type buyers and it drives upgrade decisions.
- **Inject a trade-in value teaser** — a one-line banner: "Your 13-inch MacBook Air could be worth up to $XXX. See your estimate →" This closes the loop on upgrade intent immediately.
- **Add one lifestyle anchor** — a single background image or secondary module of someone (young, creative-coded) actually using the Air in a studio or café context. The product render is stunning but it doesn't sell the life, just the object.
