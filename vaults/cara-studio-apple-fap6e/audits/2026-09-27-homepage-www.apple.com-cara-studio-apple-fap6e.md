# Homepage snapshot · www.apple.com · 2026-09-27
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-09-27**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is peak Apple — minimal, moody, all confidence. One black screen, one product, zero clutter. They're clearly pushing the iPhone 18 Pro launch hard, with the Apple One freebie as a quiet loyalty sweetener up top. It's gorgeous and on-brand, but it's also very "look at the phone" and very light on anything that gets me from admiring to configuring.

## What's working

- **The "iPhone 18 Pro" hero, full stop** — big, bold, no ambiguity about what they want me looking at.
- **The chrome "PRO" typographic art** with the phone slotted into the letterforms — genuinely eye-catching, feels like a keynote still, exactly my aesthetic register.
- **Two-tier CTA (Learn more / Buy)** — respects that some of us want to browse and some of us are ready to check out same-day.
- **Apple One banner up top** — three months free is a real, legible perk I'd actually use across Music/iCloud/TV.

## What's weak

- **No price anywhere.** For a $1,000+ purchase I'd expect at least a "from $999" tease before I commit to tapping in.
- **No Trade-In or Apple Card callout above the fold** — both are literally why I buy every cycle, and neither shows up until I presumably scroll or tap through.
- **No "sign in" / account recognition** — I've bought from Apple a dozen times, nothing here acknowledges that.
- **Zero human presence in the hero** — it's all product art, which is very on-brand but does nothing to signal "this is for someone like you."

## Recommendations

- Add a small price anchor under "Pro further." — even "From $999" builds momentum toward the Buy tap.
- Surface Trade-In value estimate as a secondary line near the CTAs ("Trade in iPhone 15 Pro, save up to $X") — this is my exact behavior loop.
- Add a subtle "Welcome back" or sign-in chip near the bag icon for returning Apple ID users.
- Once past this first screen, make sure a color/finish carousel for the 18 Pro is one scroll away, not buried — that's the first thing I'd want to fiddle with.
