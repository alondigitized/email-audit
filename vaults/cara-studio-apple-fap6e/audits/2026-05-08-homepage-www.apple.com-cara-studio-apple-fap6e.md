# Homepage snapshot · www.apple.com · 2026-05-08
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-08**
## Executive summary

- Okay, Apple is doing the most with Mother's Day right now — that illustrated gift-wrap logo is genuinely cute — but this feels like a gift-store campaign more than a tech homepage. I'm an Apple lifer who just came here to check the latest lineup or maybe browse trade-in options, and the first thing I see is flowers. I'm not mad at it, it's on-brand in that Apple way, but it's not speaking to me as a buyer.

## What's working

- **Mother's Day campaign timing** — May 8 is exactly right, and Apple leaning into a gifting moment is smart. I will probably be buying something for my mom.
- **The illustrated Apple logo** — that gift-wrapped, floral logo is actually gorgeous. Very Apple, very now. It caught my eye immediately.
- **Clean visual hierarchy** — one headline, two lines of subtext, one button. Nothing fighting for attention. Classic Apple discipline.
- **"It's not too late"** — that framing creates soft urgency without being pushy, which I appreciate.

## What's weak

- **"Shop" as a CTA is doing nothing for me** — Shop what? For who? Apple has dozens of product lines. One tap to a curated Mother's Day gift guide would close the gap immediately.
- **Zero personalization signal above fold** — I have an Apple ID, an Apple Card, a trade-in cycle. None of that is acknowledged. This page looks identical to what a non-Apple-user sees.
- **No product is visible** — I see a decorated logo and a holiday headline. No iPhone, no AirPods, no Watch, no hint of what I'm actually buying.
- **Navigation is hidden behind a hamburger** — Three icons top-right: search, bag, menu. No persistent product categories. I can't get to iPhone or iPad in one tap.

## Recommendations

- **Surface a curated gift guide CTA** — swap "Shop" for something like "Find the perfect gift" linking to a curated Mother's Day page organized by product category and price point. One extra tap of specificity removes a lot of friction.
- **Add an Apple Card/financing nudge** — even a one-line banner: "3% Daily Cash with Apple Card. 0% APR on all products." For existing cardholders this is a conversion trigger hiding in plain sight.
- **Show a product** — put an iPhone, Watch, or AirPods in the frame alongside the illustrated logo. Right now this reads like a brand ad, not a shopping prompt.
- **Drop a price anchor in the subtext** — "Gifts starting from $29" underneath the headline would give me permission to start browsing without committing to the full store.
