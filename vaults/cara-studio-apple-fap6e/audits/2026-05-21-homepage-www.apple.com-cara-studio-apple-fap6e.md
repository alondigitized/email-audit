# Homepage snapshot · www.apple.com · 2026-05-21
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- This is peak Apple — clean enough to hang in a gallery, confidence bordering on indifference. The hero is running an Accessibility campaign, which is a beautiful brand moment but does absolutely nothing for me standing here ready to spend money on the new hardware cycle. The iPhone module is starting to peek below the fold but there's not a single price, trade-in hook, or Apple Card reference in sight.

## What's working

- **"Accessibility / Innovation that's accessible by design" hero** — the photography is genuinely stunning, full-bleed, and the typography is authoritative. Apple's design language is its own trust signal and this executes it perfectly.
- **Blue "Learn more" pill button** — high contrast against the dark hero image, impossible to miss, thumb-sized. Tactically correct.
- **iPhone module immediately below** — "Meet the latest iPhone lineup" loads in the next scroll unit. Good sequencing: brand story first, product second.
- **Minimal nav chrome** — the black bar with search, bag, and hamburger keeps me in the hero without distraction. Right call for mobile.

## What's weak

- **No trade-in hook above the fold** — I come to apple.com every upgrade cycle and the first thing I want to see is "your iPhone 15 Pro is worth $X." That context sets the whole purchase in motion.
- **No Apple Card callout** — 3% Daily Cash is literally my reason for buying through Apple directly rather than a carrier. Nothing here reminds me of that.
- **"Learn more" CTA goes nowhere I want to go** — it's not a product page, it's an editorial. For someone in shopping mode, this is a speed bump.
- **No product-specific color / finish imagery** — new cycle means new colors. The hero is an editorial lifestyle shot, not the new titanium or desert sand finish that would make me tap immediately.
- **Nav is fully hidden** — the hamburger forces an extra tap to get to my category. One persistent product strip (iPhone / Mac / iPad / Watch) would cut friction.

## Recommendations

- **Add a trade-in value strip below the nav bar** — "iPhone 16 Pro from $X/mo after trade-in" or "Your iPhone 15 Pro could be worth $X" with an Apple Card badge. This is the hook that converts returning buyers and costs one line of UI.
- **Swap the solo hero for a paired layout** — keep Accessibility as a brand module but lead with the new iPhone or MacBook color story. Cara-types open the homepage to configure, not to feel inspired about disability access.
- **Surface the Apple Card 3% row** — a one-line persistent ribbon ("3% Daily Cash with Apple Card — always") right above the footer turns a loyalty program into a purchase accelerant. It's already a feature, just make it visible.
- **Add a persistent mini-nav strip** — five icons (iPhone, Mac, iPad, Watch, AirPods) pinned below the top bar eliminates the hamburger tap and gets power shoppers into config mode in one gesture.
