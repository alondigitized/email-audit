# Homepage snapshot · www.apple.com · 2026-05-17
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- Apple is doing what Apple does — leading with the hardware family, clean layout, no clutter. The iPhone hero is front-and-center with three colorway options fanned out, and the MacBook Air M5 is already teasing below the fold. As someone who literally watches keynotes live and books Genius Bar pickup the day of a new color drop, this feels like a warm welcome — but it's missing the hooks that would make me feel like a known customer versus a stranger walking in off the street.

## What's working

- **The three-phone colorway spread** — showing navy, white, and pink side-by-side is a subtle but effective "which one are you?" moment. That's the visual hook that makes me think about my current phone's color.
- **Dual CTA structure** — "Learn more" + "Shop iPhone" is smart. It respects that I might be research-mode or buy-mode on any given day. No forcing.
- **MacBook Air M5 peek below the fold** — as a MacBook user, catching that headline already makes me want to scroll. The two-product scroll tells me this homepage is going to cover my whole ecosystem, not just push one thing.
- **Apple nav bar** — search, bag, and menu are where my thumb expects them. Frictionless.

## What's weak

- **No trade-in hook** — the single biggest conversion lever for someone like me (Trade-In every cycle) is completely absent above the fold. "Get up to $X for your current iPhone" would change my calculus immediately.
- **No Apple Card / financing mention** — I get 3% back on Apple purchases. That's real money and it's invisible here. A "3% Daily Cash with Apple Card" badge would be cost-zero for them and high-value for me.
- **No human presence** — the hero is product-on-white, no people, no lifestyle, no one who looks like they work at a studio or edit on a commute. Apple can pull off product-only, but it makes the page feel showroom, not story.
- **Collapsed hamburger nav** — on mobile, every category requires an extra tap through a menu. For a site I know well, fine — but if I'm browsing accessories or AirPods, I can't glance-navigate.
- **Zero urgency or timeliness** — the copy "Meet the latest iPhone lineup" works fine but it's evergreen. Nothing signals why I should act today versus next week.

## Recommendations

- **Add a trade-in value teaser to the hero** — even a single line under "Meet the latest iPhone lineup" like "Trade in your iPhone 15 for up to $X credit" turns a browsing moment into a consideration moment. Ship it in a week with personalization later.
- **Surface Apple Card 3% Daily Cash** — put a small badge or line item on the "Shop iPhone" CTA area. "3% Daily Cash with Apple Card." No redesign needed, just one line of copy.
- **Swap at least one phone from product-on-white to a lifestyle context shot** — someone holding the pink one at a studio desk, AirPods in, sketching on an iPad next to it. The colorway story lands harder when it's attached to a moment.
- **Add a scrolling chip bar under the nav** for quick category access (iPhone / Mac / iPad / Watch / AirPods) so power users like me don't have to open the hamburger just to jump to AirPods.
