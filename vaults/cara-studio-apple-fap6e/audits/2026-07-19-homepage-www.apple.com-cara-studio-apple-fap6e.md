# Homepage snapshot · www.apple.com · 2026-07-19
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- Apple is doing its back-to-school push hard right now — the whole above-the-fold viewport is "College, sorted." with a Mac/iPad gift card deal. The visual language is classic Apple: clean, confident, minimal chrome, fun lifestyle collage. As a 29-year-old who's already deep in the ecosystem and watches every keynote, I feel like the targeting is slightly off — I graduated college a while ago — but the aesthetic still speaks my language.

## What's working

- **"College, sorted." headline** — punchy, confident, zero filler words. That's the Apple voice I know and trust.
- **Gift card offer with specific dollar range** — "$100 to $150" is concrete and stacks on top of the education price break, which makes it feel like real value, not a squishy promo.
- **Collage-sticker lifestyle image** — the two models holding baskets stuffed with a MacBook Air (looks like the yellow colorway) and purple iPad surrounded by plants, accessories, and illustrated stickers is visually playful without being loud. It catches the eye.
- **Blue "Shop" pill button** — stands out cleanly against the white background, unmissable at a glance.
- **iPhone peek below fold** — "iPhone / Meet the latest iPhone lineup" starting to appear gives me a reason to scroll even if the hero campaign isn't for me.

## What's weak

- **"Shop" CTA copy is too vague** — where am I going? Education store? Mac page? iPad page? Apple can do better than a one-word verb.
- **No Apple Card 3% cashback callout** — I always think about this when I buy. It's a real differentiator for returning buyers and it's completely absent.
- **No trade-in hook** — as someone who upgrades every two years on a new color drop, a "trade in your old device" nudge above the fold would move me immediately.
- **Hamburger-menu-only nav** — I can't see my category in one tap. Search, bag, and a hamburger is all I get. That adds friction for someone who knows exactly what she wants.
- **Education offer is exclusionary for me** — I'm a working professional, not a student. Unless the fine print (that asterisk) stretches the definition of "education savings," this deal isn't mine to take.

## Recommendations

- **Swap "Shop" for "Get the gift card"** — tell me the action I'm taking, not a generic verb. Specificity drives taps.
- **Add a thin sticky bar above the hero**: "Apple Card holders get 3% Daily Cash on every order." One line, doesn't disrupt the campaign, makes returning buyers feel seen.
- **Add a trade-in callout** — a small module directly under the hero like "Already have a Mac or iPad? Trade it in for credit." This is how Apple gets me to upgrade on cycle.
- **Show at least two category pills** (iPhone / Mac / iPad) between the hero and the iPhone module — lets me skip the hamburger and get to my product in one tap.
