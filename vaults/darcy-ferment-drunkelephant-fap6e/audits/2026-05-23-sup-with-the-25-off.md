# Sup with the 25% off?
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- "Sup with the 25% off?" is Drunk Elephant flexing its brand voice muscle on email #5 of a three-day Friends & Family blitz — and by this point in the sequence, the creative team is working harder to earn the open than to earn the click. The subject line is the most brand-idiosyncratic of the entire run: casual, almost confrontational, implicitly asking *why haven't you bought yet?* That's a reasonable pivot after four polished "here's your discount" emails. The banner copy — "WHY ARE YOU STILL READING THIS. GO SHOP!" — is genuinely funny and self-aware.
- The problem is the email doesn't close the argument it opens. For subscriber #5-in-3-days, the most compelling new information would be an end date. There isn't one visible. The product grid is solid (specific SKUs, prices, multiple CTAs), and the two GWP tiers add genuine value — but those details are below the fold and easy to miss.
- Net judgement: a clever subject line doing heavy lifting for a body that doesn't add much to what yesterday's emails already said. Still a serviceable send, but the wear-out risk is real and an expiry date would have made this the clear standout of the campaign.

## What's working

- The banner "WHY ARE YOU STILL READING THIS. GO SHOP!" is funny, brand-accurate, and actually reads like a friend texting you — perfect for a Friends & Family email.
- "This way to the friend zone." is a sharp, self-aware headline that earns its pun.
- Two tiered GWP offers ($100+ spend = free Hydra + Bright Kit; any purchase = free F. Balm) stack real incremental value on top of the 25% discount.
- The product grid ("TIME TO STOCK UP") names specific hero SKUs with discounted prices — Protini, Babyfacial, Lala Balm, Virgin Marula — giving fence-sitters a concrete landing spot.
- Layout is clean: no broken images, no overlapping text, visual hierarchy directs the eye to the hero offer immediately.

## What's weak

- No end date anywhere visible — email #5 in 3 days desperately needs a deadline to convert the holdouts it's explicitly targeting.
- This is the second send today (the earlier "Drunk classics are 25% off!" already scored 9/10 this morning); the cadence is genuinely punishing at this point.
- The "FUTURE'S LOOKING BRIGHT" and "MASK ME ANYTHING" GWP sections are below the fold and low contrast — easy to miss on a skim.
- "SHOP NOW" CTAs are generic across every section; none direct to a specific product or collection page.
- No social proof, no reviews, no star ratings visible in the product grid despite four named SKUs.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an expiry date to the hero** — "Sale ends Sunday" in the banner would turn "WHY ARE YOU STILL READING THIS" from funny to urgent-funny. The single biggest lift available here.
- 2. **Make one CTA product-specific** — "Shop Babyfacial (25% off)" converts better than a fifth generic "SHOP NOW" to the sitewide sale.
- 3. **Surface the GWP tier above the fold** — the free F. Balm offer is a conversion driver; it's buried in the third content block.
- 4. **Rest the list tomorrow** — from the brand history: this campaign has run 5 emails in 3 days. The next send should either be a hard end-date finale or a post-sale "thanks for shopping" beat, not another discount reminder.

### 1. Technical Summary

Three confirmed structural defects in the `<head>` alone: redundant font loading, broken CSS comment syntax inside media queries, and a missing `lang` attribute. Full link and compliance audit is limited by source truncation.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully confirm from truncated source.** No UTM-decorated links are visible in the provided excerpt. Font asset URLs reference `drunkelephant.com` without UTM params, which is expected for assets — but CTA links must be verified against the full source.

**Third-party font CDN risk:** All `BrownBold`, `BrownLight`, and `BrownThin` weights are loaded from `db.onlinewebfonts.com`. This is a licensing-gray-area font hosting service. If that CDN goes down or rate-limits, fallback rendering kicks in mid-send with no retry. Not a link tracking issue per se, but a load-time and legal exposure point.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — broken CSS inside media queries:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 600px) {
  <!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
  <!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
HTML comment syntax (`<!-- -->`) inside a `<style>` block is invalid. These two rules are dead — they will never apply. Any layout behavior that depended on `.contenttable` or `.mobile` resizing on mobile is silently broken.

**Triple font loading — redundant and slow:**
The same three Brown font variants are loaded three ways simultaneously:
1. `<link>` tags (top of `<head>`)
2. `@import` inside `<style>` (same URLs)
3. `@font-face` blocks (different source URLs for two of the three)

This fires up to 3× the font requests per weight. Most email clients will block external fonts regardless; this multiplies wasted load attempts.

**Universal overrides with side effects:**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; max-height:1000000px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly}
*{line-height: 100%}
```
`line-height: 100%` applied via the universal selector will collapse line spacing on any element that does not explicitly override it — including multi-line alt text, legal footer copy, and unsubscribe text. This is a known legibility trap in Outlook and older Android clients.

**Missing `lang` attribute:**
`<html>` element has no `lang` attribute. Screen readers cannot infer language; some clients will mis-hyphenate or mis-render text.

**No `role="presentation"` confirmation:** Cannot confirm from truncated source whether layout tables carry `role="presentation"` — required for VoiceOver/NVDA to skip them.
