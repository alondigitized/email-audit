# 15% off!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-11**
## Executive summary

- This is a welcome email from Drunk Elephant — the discount code near the top and the "We're delighted you could join us" copy make that unmistakable. The brand's signature playfulness is all over it: the bubbly "hello there!" headline, the Suspicious 6 ingredient wheel, the pastel palette. For a new subscriber trigger, it does the core job: surface the 15% welcome offer fast, then use the rest of the email to build brand conviction before the convert.
- The problem is the subject. "15% off!" is eight characters of generic. It could be from a pizza chain or a car wash. A subscriber who signed up in a moment of impulse and then forgot about it has no context to know this is a premium skincare brand they actually want to open. The email itself is strong; the subject undersells it hard.
- The bottom birthday section feels tacked on — a second, unrelated ask crammed into a welcome flow that already has a clear job to do. This is a clean, well-rendered email doing too many things at once, but the welcome offer mechanics work.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject.** "15% off!" is indistinguishable from every promotional email in the inbox. Name the brand or the benefit.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your Drunk Elephant welcome gift: 15% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Healthy skin starts here — 15% off inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use code [CODE] at checkout. No catches, just clean ingredients.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Meet the Suspicious 6 we leave out — and why your skin will thank you.`
- 2. **Cut the birthday module.** Move the birthday data-capture to a second lifecycle email (Day 3 or Day 7 post-welcome). The welcome email has one job: convert the new subscriber on their first purchase. The birthday ask competes with that.
- 3. **Sharpen the CTA copy.** "HAPPY SHOPPING" is cheerful but vague. Change it to `SHOP WITH 15% OFF` or `CLAIM YOUR DISCOUNT` — a verb + a clear payoff.
- 4. **Add a deadline to the offer.** The 15% code is shown with no expiry visible. Even "good for 7 days" would meaningfully lift click urgency.
- 5. **Anchor the kit.** The "Start with a Littles" starter kit module is the best click target in the email — it's concrete and discounted — but it sits below the fold. Consider pulling it up to sit directly below the code, before Philosophy 101.

## Full review
## 1. Take

This is a welcome email from Drunk Elephant — the discount code near the top and the "We're delighted you could join us" copy make that unmistakable. The brand's signature playfulness is all over it: the bubbly "hello there!" headline, the Suspicious 6 ingredient wheel, the pastel palette. For a new subscriber trigger, it does the core job: surface the 15% welcome offer fast, then use the rest of the email to build brand conviction before the convert.

The problem is the subject. "15% off!" is eight characters of generic. It could be from a pizza chain or a car wash. A subscriber who signed up in a moment of impulse and then forgot about it has no context to know this is a premium skincare brand they actually want to open. The email itself is strong; the subject undersells it hard.

The bottom birthday section feels tacked on — a second, unrelated ask crammed into a welcome flow that already has a clear job to do. This is a clean, well-rendered email doing too many things at once, but the welcome offer mechanics work.

## 2. What stood out

The hero section lands well: "hello there!" in a large, warm typeface with product imagery immediately to the right creates energy without clutter. The 15% code is prominently displayed as a bolded, clearly formatted promo code beneath a short welcome paragraph, and the "HAPPY SHOPPING" button in yellow-gold is easy to find. That's the right visual sequence for a welcome email. The Philosophy 101 section is surprisingly effective brand storytelling — the Suspicious 6 ingredient wheel (drying alcohols, essential oils, silicones, fragrances, chemical sunscreens, SLS) gives a first-time reader a reason to trust the brand before they've bought anything. That's a legitimate win. The "Start with a Littles of everything" kit module with the 15% badge reinforces that the discount applies to a real entry point, not just anything on the site. Where it stumbles: the birthday capture module at the bottom ("YOU SAY IT'S YOUR BIRTHDAY!") is a completely separate lifecycle ask — asking a brand-new subscriber for their birthday in the same email as their welcome offer creates cognitive friction and dilutes both CTAs.

## 3. What I'd change

1. **Rewrite the subject.** "15% off!" is indistinguishable from every promotional email in the inbox. Name the brand or the benefit.
   - **Subject Alt A:** `Your Drunk Elephant welcome gift: 15% off`
   - **Subject Alt B:** `Healthy skin starts here — 15% off inside`
   - **Preheader Alt A:** `Use code [CODE] at checkout. No catches, just clean ingredients.`
   - **Preheader Alt B:** `Meet the Suspicious 6 we leave out — and why your skin will thank you.`

2. **Cut the birthday module.** Move the birthday data-capture to a second lifecycle email (Day 3 or Day 7 post-welcome). The welcome email has one job: convert the new subscriber on their first purchase. The birthday ask competes with that.

3. **Sharpen the CTA copy.** "HAPPY SHOPPING" is cheerful but vague. Change it to `SHOP WITH 15% OFF` or `CLAIM YOUR DISCOUNT` — a verb + a clear payoff.

4. **Add a deadline to the offer.** The 15% code is shown with no expiry visible. Even "good for 7 days" would meaningfully lift click urgency.

5. **Anchor the kit.** The "Start with a Littles" starter kit module is the best click target in the email — it's concrete and discounted — but it sits below the fold. Consider pulling it up to sit directly below the code, before Philosophy 101.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-zoom (accessibility)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents users from zooming on iOS/Android. WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 requires text be resizable to 200% without loss of content. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Issue — conflicting dark mode meta directives**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light dark">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light dark only">
```
The `only` keyword on the second tag (Apple Mail-specific) signals that no other scheme is permitted. Combined with the first tag's `light dark` (no `only`), behavior diverges across clients — Apple Mail honors `only` and locks rendering; other clients see the first meta and may apply system overrides. Reconcile to a single consistent declaration, e.g., `content="light dark"` on both or drop the second meta entirely.

**Issue — custom fonts served from main-site demandware CDN**
All six `@font-face` declarations load from:
```
https://www.drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/Sites-drunkelephant_us-Site/-/en_US/v1719565389232/fonts/
```
Email clients don't share browser cache, so these are always cold fetches. If the main site rotates the version token (`v1719565389232` = 2024-06-28 Unix timestamp), the font URLs in live emails break immediately with no cache fallback. **Host email fonts on a CDN path that is version-pinned and email-specific, or inline as base64 for critical weights.**

EOT format is still declared for each weight (IE6–8 compatibility); effectively dead weight but not harmful.
