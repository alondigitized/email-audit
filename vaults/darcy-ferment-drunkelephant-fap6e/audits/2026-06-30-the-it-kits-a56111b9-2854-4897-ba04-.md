# The It kits
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- Drunk Elephant is running the summer-kit playbook for the third time in eleven days — "Summer to go" on June 21 (9/10), a trio offer on June 24, and now this. The creative is polished and the three-kit structure (TRUST THE PROS / CATCH THE LIGHT / TEST THE WATER) gives the email multiple entry points, which is smart. The 35% off offer is real money. The problem is that none of this matters if subscribers never open it, and the subject line "The It Kits" is doing the heavy lifting with no hands. It names nothing, promises nothing, and asks the reader to already know what an "It Kit" is before committing a click.
- Inside the email, the story is better. The kit-level product descriptions are specific enough to act on, the free shipping banner leads cleanly, and the punchy section headlines hold DE's brand voice without strain. The "SKIN GETS THIRSTY, TOO." lifestyle section at the bottom muddies the water — it re-pitches B-Hydra in a looser format after the email already dedicated a section to the B-HYDRA + LALA RETRO kit, which creates redundancy and dilutes the three-kit structure.
- **Judgment:** A capable email let down by a weak subject line and cadence saturation on the travel-kit theme. The 35% off offer would convert if more people opened it.

## What's working

- Free shipping banner is the first thing visible — a low-friction sweetener before a single product image loads.
- Three distinct skin-goal stories give subscribers a hook that matches where they are: glow, bronze, or hydrate.
- Product names in each section description (D-LUMA, PROTINI, D-BRONZI, LALA RETRO) are specific enough that a returning customer knows exactly what they're buying.
- "35% LESS (IS ALWAYS A GOOD THING.)" reinforces the discount with personality mid-scroll — the parenthetical is good DE voice.
- Layout is clean with no render bugs, no broken images, and a consistent visual cadence from section to section.

## What's weak

- Subject "The It Kits" has no offer signal, no urgency, and no category specificity — it needs prior DE knowledge just to parse.
- No deadline anywhere in the email body; 35% off with no expiry date creates zero urgency.
- Third travel-kit email in eleven days for subscribers who also received "Summer to go" (June 21) — the identical 35% + grab-and-go framing will feel like a resend.
- Zero social proof — especially conspicuous given that "Oh, my stars" (June 19) showed DE can drive clicks on reviews alone.
- The bottom "SKIN GETS THIRSTY, TOO." section re-pitches B-Hydra after it already had its kit moment, creating structural confusion in what should be a clean three-chapter story.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the subject with the discount.** "The It Kits" is an inside reference; 35% off is the universal hook. Put the number in the subject.
- **Subject Alt A:** `3 grab-and-go kits. 35% off. Pick yours.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer kit is 35% off right now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping, no minimum — D-LUMA, D-BRONZI, or B-HYDRA. Which one's yours?`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Glowing skin on the fly, at 35% off. Three kits. One decision.`
- 2. **Add a hard deadline to the hero.** "Ends July 4" or "Weekend only" gives subscribers a reason to act now instead of saving the email and forgetting it.
- 3. **Move the 35% callout into the hero headline.** The discount badge is small and appears on the product image rather than as standalone copy. "35% off your grab-and-go kit" as a sub-headline under "GLOWING SKIN ON THE FLY" would make the offer land before the first scroll.
- 4. **Cut the "SKIN GETS THIRSTY, TOO." section or make it its own email.** B-Hydra gets a full kit section above. The lifestyle hydration riff at the bottom is off-brief for a structured kit send and creates a fourth narrative thread the email doesn't need.
- 5. **Swap the hero image.** "Summer to go" (June 21) was the same kit concept with the same product-on-pastel aesthetic. Subscribers who open both will see near-identical emails nine days apart — a visual difference in the hero would signal "new news" rather than "I already read this."

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CONFIRMED DEFECT — Dead CSS rules via HTML comment delimiters inside `<style>` block:**

```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 600px) {
  *[class=hide] { display: none !important;}
  <!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
  <!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

`<!-- ... -->` is not valid CSS. These two rules are silently ignored by all CSS parsers. If `.contenttable` or `.mobile` elements exist in the body, they will not reflow correctly on mobile viewports ≤600px. This is a layout regression risk on small screens.

**CONFIRMED DEFECT — `text-size-adjust` set to `none` instead of `100%`:**

```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```

`none` fully disables user text scaling on iOS/WebKit. The correct value is `100%` (prevents automatic font inflation while preserving user zoom). Using `none` is a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) violation.

**Minor — Duplicate font loading:**
The same three Brown variants (`BrownBold`, `BrownLight`, `BrownThin`) are loaded via both `<link rel="stylesheet">` tags and `@font-face` declarations pointing to the same `db.onlinewebfonts.com` CDN. This fires redundant HTTP requests on load.

**Minor — Third-party font CDN dependency:**
Six of seven `@font-face` sources resolve through `db.onlinewebfonts.com`, a third-party CDN outside Drunk Elephant's control. Font failure here degrades to system fallback silently. Only `BrownRegular` is self-hosted on `drunkelephant.com`.
