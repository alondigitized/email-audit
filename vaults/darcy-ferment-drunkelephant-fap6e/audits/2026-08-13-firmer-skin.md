# Firmer skin?
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-13**
## Executive summary

- This is the same Kamo Drops product as the 2026-08-11 "Introducing Kamo Drops!" send, now recut around the "firmer skin" clinical claim instead of the launch-hype framing. It's a smart pivot — the earlier email sold novelty ("something new"), this one sells a benefit ("it clinically firms skin"), and pairing that with a dense but well-organized "Kamo is:" spec sheet (What you make it / Ready for action / Clinically proven to) gives the reader real reasons to believe. The three-face hero image is striking and does double duty as diversity-of-shade proof.
- Where it stumbles: the top banner is a second, competing offer (free deluxe sample with subscription $50+) that has nothing to do with "firmer skin" or Kamo, and the bottom "Try This At Home" module introduces yet a third offer (free sample card + travel bag at $110+) with different terms. Three separate asks in one email dilutes the single clear CTA this brand does well when it stays disciplined (see 8/3 "Something big..." at 9/10 for contrast).
- Solid, on-brand, slightly overstuffed. This lands around a 6 — good creative and message hierarchy dragged down by competing offers and repeat-fatigue against the 8/11 Kamo launch.

## What's working

- Clear top-of-fold hero: bold "WE'VE GOT YOU COVERED" headline + benefit copy + "SHOP KAMO" button, all visible without much scrolling.
- The "Kamo is:" info block is genuinely useful — it answers what/how/proof in scannable groups instead of one long paragraph.
- Product swatches shown against real skin tones under the info block reinforce the shade-range and texture claim.
- "SHOP KAMO" CTA repeats twice (hero + info block), consistent green button styling, easy to spot.
- No visible render bugs — clean layout, no overlapping text or broken images.

## What's weak

- Top banner promotes a subscription free-gift offer that's unrelated to the "firmer skin" / Kamo story — it competes with the hero for attention before the reader even gets to the actual subject of the email.
- "Try This At Home" section adds a third distinct offer (sample card + free travel bag at $110+) with its own threshold and dates, further splitting focus.
- Three back-to-back fine-print/legal blocks at the bottom (two asterisked offer terms) signal a cluttered, over-promoted email rather than one clean idea.
- This is the second Kamo-centric send in three days (after 8/11's "Introducing Kamo Drops!") — risks cadence fatigue if the subscriber isn't ready to buy yet.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the top banner entirely (or move the subscription free-sample offer to its own dedicated send) so "firmer skin" / Kamo owns the whole email — this is the single highest-impact fix, since right now the strongest asset (hero + benefit claim) is competing with an unrelated banner for the first thing people see.
- 2. Fold "Try This At Home" down into a lighter secondary CTA ("Find your shade") without introducing a third offer/threshold — or cut it if this is meant to be a focused benefit-education email.
- 3. Add a firmness-specific proof point (before/after photo, % improvement stat, "92% saw firmer skin in 4 weeks") near the hero — the clinical claim is stated but never visually substantiated.
- 4. Since this follows the 8/11 launch email closely, consider referencing it lightly ("Already tried Kamo? Here's why it works") to make the repeat send feel like a follow-up, not a duplicate.
