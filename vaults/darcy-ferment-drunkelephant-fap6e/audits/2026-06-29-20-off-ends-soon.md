# 20% off ends soon!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-29**
## Executive summary

- This is Drunk Elephant's SheerID professional-verification play — the third time they've run it in three weeks (June 8, June 17, and now June 29). The creative is functionally identical to the June 17 send that scored 5/10: same "IT SERVES YOU RIGHT." hero, same model, same five category buttons. The only meaningful update is a small copy twist — the discount is framed as "up from 15% for a limited time only," which positions the 20% as a temporary boost rather than the standing offer. That's a real upgrade in messaging, but it barely justifies another deployment of the same asset.
- For anyone who doesn't identify as a teacher, first responder, military member, healthcare professional, or student, this email is effectively zero-value. The offer is gated behind a SheerID identity verification step — meaning the click doesn't even get you closer to a product; it gets you closer to a bureaucratic process. The CTA "GET VERIFIED" is fine for a first send; for a third send to the same list, it's background noise.
- The execution is clean, the brand aesthetic holds, and the "ends soon!" subject drives open curiosity — but there's no new angle, no new creative, and no reason a returning subscriber should find this more compelling than the nearly-identical email they ignored two weeks ago.

## What's working

- **Concrete % lift framing** — "up from 15% for a limited time only" is the one new idea here. Framing the 20% as a temporary upgrade over the standard 15% adds urgency without fabricating it.
- **Visual hierarchy is tight** — hero headline lands first, offer copy is readable, five segment buttons scan instantly without crowding.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no layout issues, no font collisions. The email displays correctly.
- **Subject brevity** — "20% off ends soon!" at 18 characters reads well on mobile, clear, no spam signals.

## What's weak

- **Third outing of identical creative** — the "IT SERVES YOU RIGHT." headline, model, and five-button layout are copied verbatim from June 17. Subscribers who saw that send are seeing nothing new.
- **No product hook** — there's no skincare product visible anywhere. This email could be for any brand offering a pro discount. The Drunk Elephant product catalog is completely absent.
- **"Ends soon!" without a date** — vague urgency. Subscribers who've seen this twice already know the deadline isn't binding.
- **Gated audience, non-gated send** — if this goes to the full list (not just profession-verified segments), the majority of recipients have no path to the offer and no reason to click.
- **CTA is a process, not a reward** — "GET VERIFIED" asks subscribers to do work before they get anything. No product browsing, no discovery — just paperwork.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a product tie-in** — show one or two flagship SKUs the verified discount applies to. Let subscribers visualize what they're actually getting 20% off. This is the most impactful single change.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline with a specific date** — "Ends July 3" beats "ends soon" every time, especially on a third send.
- 3. **Suppress or re-segment the list** — anyone who clicked on the June 17 send and didn't convert should get a different message; anyone already verified should skip this entirely and go to a product-first email.
- 4. **Refresh the hero creative** — even a different crop, headline variant, or color-way would signal "new send" vs. "oops, same email again."
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% off for teachers, nurses & first responders — ends July 3`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 20% discount just got bigger (limited time)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Up from 15% — verify once, save every month on the products you love.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Show us what you do and we'll show you 20% off — for as long as you need it.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] HTML comment syntax breaks mobile CSS rules**
Inside the `@media only screen and (max-width: 600px)` block, two rules are wrapped in HTML comment delimiters:
```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
`<!-- -->` is invalid inside a `<style>` tag. CSS parsers discard these lines entirely, so `.contenttable` and `.mobile` width overrides never fire on narrow viewports. This is a live layout defect on mobile.

**[MEDIUM] Triple font loading — redundant HTTP requests**
Brown Bold, Light, and Thin are loaded three ways simultaneously:
1. `<link href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...">` in `<head>`
2. `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...)` in `<style>`
3. `@font-face` src blocks pointing to the same CDN

The `<link>` and `@import` are redundant for the same URL and add unnecessary round-trips. Most ESP environments and email clients (Gmail, Outlook) will block the external requests regardless.

**[MEDIUM] Third-party font CDN dependency**
`BrownBold`, `BrownLight`, and `BrownThin` are served from `db.onlinewebfonts.com`, a font aggregator. `BrownRegular` is served from first-party Demandware CDN (`drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/`). The CDN split is inconsistent and `db.onlinewebfonts.com` has no SLA guarantee. If the CDN is unavailable or rate-limits the request, all three weight variants fall back to system fonts, while Regular does not.

**[LOW] `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied globally**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```
Setting this to `none` (vs. `100%`) on the `*` selector suppresses iOS's automatic text scaling. This is an accessibility concern — users with low vision who rely on browser/OS zoom will have text scaling actively disabled.

**[LOW] Apple Mail link affordance removed**
```css
.appleLinks a { color: #000000 !important; text-decoration: none !important; }
```
Any link wrapped in `.appleLinks` renders identically to surrounding body text in Apple Mail — no color, no underline. If CTA buttons use this class, they will be visually indistinguishable from non-interactive text on macOS/iOS Mail.
