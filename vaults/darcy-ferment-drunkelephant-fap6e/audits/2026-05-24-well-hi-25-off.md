# Well, hi, 25% off!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-24**
## Executive summary

- "Well, hi, 25% off!" is email #7 in Drunk Elephant's Friends & Family blitz — and at this point the brand is just rotating greetings around an identical hero. We've now seen the "25% OFF SITEWIDE" wordmark in essentially this exact form on May 21, 22, 22, 23, 23, 24, and now again on 24. The creative does try to earn its send with two threshold callouts ("Future's Looking Bright" Bright Eyes set + free $100 gift, "Mask Me Anything" full mask gift at $250) and a "Best of Friends" grid of four hero SKUs with starred member pricing — but the open carries the weight of seven near-identical sends in four days.
- The execution itself is clean: no render bugs, clear hierarchy, product shots are crisp, CTAs are everywhere. But the subject "Well, hi, 25% off!" is the weakest of the week — it's a greeting wrapped around the same number, with zero new information. After the cleverness of "Sup with the 25% off?" yesterday, this lands like a tired hello.
- Honest read: solid template, fatigued cadence, lazy subject. A loyal subscriber has either bought by now or filtered the thread.

## What's working

- Two clear tiered incentives visible without scrolling far: $100-set bonus and full-size mask gift at $250 — actual reasons to spend more.
- "Best of Friends" grid surfaces four specific hero SKUs (Protini, B-Hydra, T.L.C., Polypeptide) with both retail and member price displayed — easier conversion than a homepage punt.
- Clean visual hierarchy: brand wordmark → 25% OFF SITEWIDE → tiered bundles → product grid → reassurance copy.
- Member pricing called out with the asterisk and footnote — the loyalty angle is real, not a tease.
- Demographic/category fit is neutral skincare-positive — no off-tone modeling.

## What's weak

- Subject line is the weakest of the week — "Well, hi, 25% off!" reads like a placeholder, no new hook, no urgency.
- This is the 7th send in 4 days hammering the same 25% SITEWIDE wordmark. Creative fatigue is undeniable.
- Tiny preheader "Pssst! This way to the friend zone." is cute but doesn't carry any real information.
- No deadline visible on the hero — "future you will love you" is soft urgency for a sale that's already been running four days.
- Four product CTAs all say generic "SHOP NOW" — no variation, no specificity per product card.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with a deadline.** This is email #7 — the only thing that will move a fence-sitter now is "ends Monday." Put it in the subject and above the hero.
- 2. **Differentiate the subject from yesterday's "Sup with the 25% off?"** — voice already covered. Switch to urgency or a category angle.
- 3. **Vary the four CTAs** in the grid — "Shop Protini," "Shop B-Hydra" — they're already on a product card, the verb+noun is free real estate.
- 4. **Surface the threshold math more aggressively** — $250 = full-size mask gift is the real lever after four days of seeing 25%. Make that the hero, not the wordmark.
- 5. **Cap or pause the sequence** — by send #8 the unsubscribes start eating the LTV the promo was supposed to build.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Third-party font CDN dependency (reliability risk):** Six font references point to `db.onlinewebfonts.com` (e.g., `https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/4150d76a852e1c349e0b6fe15f29d9ca?family=Brown+Bold`). Most email clients strip `<link rel="stylesheet">` and `@import`, and this domain is not Drunk Elephant–controlled — a TLS/availability outage would silently degrade typography. The `<link>` tags also appear *before* `<meta charset>`, violating HTML head ordering.
- **Broken/commented media-query rules:** Inside `@media only screen and (max-width: 600px)` the block contains HTML comments used to disable CSS rules: `<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->` and `<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->`. HTML comments are not valid CSS — Outlook/Gmail will either ignore the surrounding block or mis-parse subsequent rules. Use `/* ... */` instead.
- **Mobile sizing bug in `.vouchertext4` / `.fsname`:** `max-width:15px !important; max-height:15px !important; width:17px !important;` on a text element clamps the box to 15px while font-size is forced to 15–17px, guaranteeing clipped or overflowing glyphs on screens ≤418px.
- **`max-height:1000000px` universal selector:** Applied via `*{...max-height:1000000px;...}`. This is a known Gmail-clipping workaround but is applied with `*` which is heavier than necessary and can interfere with image sizing.
- **Accessibility:** Cannot verify `alt` attributes, semantic structure, or role attributes — image markup is past the truncation point.
- **Doctype:** XHTML 1.0 Transitional is acceptable for email but `<!DOCTYPE>` is appropriate; no issue.
