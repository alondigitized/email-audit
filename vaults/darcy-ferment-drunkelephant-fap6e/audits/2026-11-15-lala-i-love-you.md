# Lala, I love you
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-15**
## Executive summary

- This is the second send in a row built around the Made-to-Measure Moisture refill kit. It follows 11/13's "Winter is coming", and it is a better execution of the same story. The lavender hero with the Lala jar and refill is clean, and "Fa la la, LALA" is a charming pun. The subject "Lala, I love you" is warm and on-brand, though it never says what the offer is.
- The offer stack is crowded. The top banner promises up to 6 free sample duos at $65+, $95+ and $125+. The hero sells the refill kit. A "$83 VALUE: $123" badge sits on the second image. A Kamo bundle at 20% off closes the email. The sample banner is the real hook, but it sits in a dark gray strip that reads like a header, not an offer. After four Singles Day sends with a similar tiered sample structure, a returning subscriber will see this as another gift-with-purchase email.
- Verdict: pretty and on-brand, but the offer hierarchy is muddy and the kit's price is missing. It is a solid middle-of-the-road send.

## What's working

- The hero image is cohesive and premium. The lavender and yellow palette is consistent with the brand's recent sends.
- The "$83 VALUE: $123" badge gives a concrete savings figure ($40 off) that is easy to read.
- The copy is short and benefit-led: "ceramide-rich, barrier-restorative" and "A daily go-to for any age".
- The two "SHOP NOW" buttons are clear and white-on-color, so they stand out.
- The Kamo bundle block adds a second, unrelated 20% offer for shoppers the kit doesn't suit.

## What's weak

- The sample-duo banner, which is the strongest incentive, is small, all-caps and gray. It is easy to skim past.
- The kit's price is only implied by the "$83" badge. It appears in the second image, not the hero, and it isn't tied to a CTA.
- Both "SHOP NOW" buttons are generic and don't say what you'll shop.
- The offers compete: samples, a kit and a bundle. The Kamo bundle feels tacked on.
- The legal text is dense and sits at the bottom. The "*" qualifier on the sample offer is visible in the banner, but its terms are far below.
- There is no social proof or reviews anywhere.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead the hero with one offer: "$83 kit (a $123 value) + free samples." Put the price and savings in the hero image, not in the second block.
- 2. Make the sample tiers a visual element, such as a three-step bar for $65, $95 and $125, instead of a gray text strip.
- 3. Replace "SHOP NOW" with specific CTAs: "Get the Lala kit" and "Bundle Kamo + save 20%".
- 4. Add a review line or star rating under the second product block.
- 5. Move the Kamo bundle to a separate send, or label it clearly as a second offer.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Lala never runs out + free samples`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Limited-edition refill kit, plus up to 6 free sample duos with your order.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML source is legacy XHTML 1.0 Transitional with third-party font loading. The QA run found 0 hard failures and 3 warnings, plus several flagged items: 3 `http://` images, 2 images without alt text, and 13 click-tracking links that were never probed. ## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **Recipient email in a URL:** the Everest tracking URL carries the recipient address as a query parameter:

  Plaintext PII in a URL can leak through proxy logs, referrers, and forwarded or archived copies.
- **Tracking pixel:** `https://mi.drunkelephant.com/p/up/80acaf1c74f9e002/o.gif?mi_u=98161986&mi_ecmp=us_de_broadcast_promotion_hollalarefill_p…` is an open-tracking pixel. It carries a user ID (`mi_u`) and a campaign ID.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Invalid `@import` placement:** the `<style>` block opens with `body {…}` and only then has three `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/…)` rules. Per the CSS spec, `@import` must come before all other rules, so these imports are ignored. The same fonts are also loaded by `<link>` tags in `<head>`, so the `@import` lines are redundant.
- **Third-party font dependencies:**
  - `db.onlinewebfonts.com` serves BrownBold, BrownLight and BrownThin.
  - `BrownRegular` loads from a versioned Demandware path (`…/v1719565389232/fonts/BrownRegular.eot`) that will break when the storefront redeploys.
  - Most email clients ignore web fonts, so fallbacks matter.
- **Missing alt text (flagged by QA):**
  - `o.gif` (mi.drunkelephant.com pixel).
  - The `everestengagement.com/ea/dKhpn7WOgU/` image.
  - Both are likely tracking pixels and should carry `alt=""` explicitly.
- **Insecure images:** three social icons use `http://`: Facebook (`cc02d07d-…png`), Instagram (`9b452915-…png`) and TikTok (`29f975e1-…png`), all from `http://image.cheers.drunkelephant.com/lib/fe3211717064057c751c78/m/1/`. These can be blocked or flagged as mixed content.
- **Fragile media-query selectors:** the rules use exact-match attribute selectors such as `*[class=vouchertext4]`, `*[class=mobile1]` and `*[class=blocktable]`. These stop matching if an element gets a second class. The `*{line-height:100%}` and `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` universal rules are also heavy-handed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` on the raw message at the origin MTA, not through the relay. Add them if they are missing.
2. Capture the `Authentication-Results` header from the original delivery to confirm SPF, DKIM and DMARC.

4. Add `alt=""` to the tracking pixels (`o.gif` and the Everest image).
5. Remove the recipient email from the Everest URL query string (`?e=…`). Use an opaque token instead.
6. Move `@import` rules to the top of the `<style>` block, or delete them since the `<link>` tags already load the fonts. Ensure web-safe fallback stacks exist.
7. Resolve the 13 skipped tracking links with a redirect-following probe. Confirm final landing pages return 200 and that UTM parameters survive.
