# Final hours for 25% off!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourteenth email in Drunk Elephant's Friends & Family blitz, landing on May 28 — one day after "We extended the sale…but this is it!" announced what everyone understood to be the closing act. The subject "Final hours for 25% off!" is indistinguishable from the urgency language used in sends eight through thirteen, and a subscriber who has been reading along has every reason to assume it's another false alarm. The brand has cried wolf too many times this week.
- What saves this send from being purely cynical is a live countdown timer showing 06 hours, 51 minutes — not the frozen 00:00:00:00 that made the May 26 "Last day" email embarrassing. The creative team also makes a quiet self-aware pivot: "No FOMO here!" and "ORDER NOW, so we don't have to say we told you so" read as the brand winking at its own reputation for extended "final" sales. That copy line is the single best piece of writing in the entire fourteen-email sequence.
- The secondary MASK ME ANYTHING GWP module — free full-size F-Balm with $75+ — adds a second reason to convert beyond the discount, which none of the prior sends bothered with. That's a meaningful structural improvement. But it doesn't undo the fatigue of being the fourteenth identical-feeling email in five days.

## What's working

- **Live countdown timer** (06:51:14 remaining) gives the "Final hours" claim actual evidence, unlike the frozen-at-zero timer on May 26.
- **"ORDER NOW, so we don't have to say we told you so"** — dry, brand-voice-consistent, meta-aware; the best line in the whole series.
- **MASK ME ANYTHING GWP module** adds a spend incentive layered on top of the sitewide discount, giving fence-sitters a second reason to hit the threshold.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean** — 25% OFF dominates above the fold, product stack fills the mid-section, secondary CTA is properly de-emphasized below.

## What's weak

- **"Final hours" lands as noise** after "Adios," "Last day," "25% off is going...," "Ending soon," "We extended the sale…but this is it!" — credibility is spent before the email opens.
- **"No FOMO here!" is cute but confusing** — it signals to already-fatigued subscribers that the brand knows you're ignoring them, but doesn't give them a new reason to re-engage.
- **No new angle or product spotlight** — this is functionally a resend of every prior email in the sequence, just with a live timer and a GWP appended.
- **The GWP threshold ($75+) is buried in the secondary module** and doesn't make it into the hero or subject, wasting the strongest incremental offer in the email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the GWP the headline hook** — "Free F-Balm + 25% off: 6 hours left" is a stronger open signal than "Final hours for 25% off!" because it adds a concrete *new* reason to act that wasn't in the previous thirteen emails. The GWP is doing all the lifting to differentiate this send; put it up front.
- 2. **Move "so we don't have to say we told you so" into the subject line** — it's the one piece of copy that acknowledges the fatigue and turns it into humor. That tone is what makes a fatigued subscriber actually open.
- 3. **Drop "No FOMO here!"** — it's too clever-cute and ambiguous. The email is *all* FOMO. The line contradicts itself and wastes the hero's most prominent text position before the offer.
- 4. **Add a final tally** — "6 hours. This is the one we meant." A single line that directly addresses the credibility hole would convert better than pretending it doesn't exist.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free F-Balm + 25% off. 6 hours. For real.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `We said "this is it" and we meant it this time`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free full-size F-Balm when you spend $75+ — ends tonight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Last 6 hours: 25% off + a free full-size F-Balm on us`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical: HTML comments embedded inside a CSS `<style>` block**
Two rules inside the `@media only screen and (max-width: 600px)` block are wrapped in HTML comment syntax:
```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
HTML comments (`<!-- -->`) are invalid inside `<style>` blocks. These rules are silently dropped by all conforming CSS parsers, meaning `contenttable` and `mobile` elements will **not** resize on mobile. This is a functional rendering bug.

**Triple font loading — same fonts requested three times each:**
Brown Bold, Brown-Light, and Brown-Thin are each loaded via:
1. A `<link>` tag in `<head>` pointing to `db.onlinewebfonts.com`
2. An `@import` inside `<style>` pointing to the same URLs
3. An `@font-face` block inside `<style>` pointing to the same URLs

This generates redundant HTTP requests on every open, inflating load time. `<link>` and `@import` for the same resource is doubly wasteful; combining both with a third `@font-face` block is an authoring error, not intentional fallback.

**Third-party font CDN (`db.onlinewebfonts.com`):**
Three of the four font families are served from `db.onlinewebfonts.com`, a third-party CDN not operated by Drunk Elephant. `BrownRegular` is correctly self-hosted at `drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/`. The three externally-hosted fonts are subject to CDN outages, privacy proxy blocking, and corporate firewall filtering — all of which will silently fall back to the stack's web-safe fallback. Acceptable risk in email if fallbacks are defined, but the inconsistency is an authoring gap.

**`.sm { display:none; }` — no corresponding show rule visible:**
A `.sm` class hides elements by default. No `display:block !important` counterpart is visible in the provided media queries for `.sm`. If used on mobile-only content, those elements may be permanently hidden. Requires full-source verification.

**Aggressive global CSS reset:**
```css
* { line-height: 100%; }
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; max-height: 1000000px; }
```
The `max-height: 1000000px` hack on `*` is a Gmail clipping workaround, implying the rendered email is at or near Gmail's 102KB clip threshold. Should trigger a file-size audit of the full HTML.
