# Making Kamo even better
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-15**
## Executive summary

- This is a founder-style "how I use it" email wrapped around a gift-with-purchase that is almost hidden. The hero reads "KAMO HER WAY": a dark green block, a big smiling portrait of Karen, the director of e-commerce, and a small outlined "SHOP KAMO" button. The two-step routine (splash water, then Kamo + Protini) is easy to follow. The close-up of her applying it plus the pull quote makes it feel like a real recommendation.
- The weakness is that the money is at the bottom. "It's Pick Your Own Pouch Day!" sits below two Shop Kamo buttons, and its terms are in tiny grey type. They read: buy Kamo plus a qualifying moisturizer, get a pouch and a B-Hydra sample, valid through 10/17/26. A subscriber has to scroll about 2,500px to learn there's a reason to buy today. The subject, "Making Kamo even better," also promises a product improvement that the email never delivers. It's the third Kamo-adjacent education send in a week, after "Kamo plus one" (10/11) and "All the moisturizers" (10/13), and the first to actually put the gift in front of people.
- Verdict: a pleasant, on-brand email that doesn't lead with its strongest asset. I'd call it a 6 to 7 for execution and a 5 for results, because the offer is invisible in the inbox and above the fold.

## What's working

- The hero is warm and human. A real person and a named "Karen" feel more credible than a bottle shot.
- The two-step layout is skimmable, with alternating image sides and short copy.
- The Kamo + Protini product shot pairs the hero product with the qualifying moisturizer.
- The testimonial pull quote ("one drop mixed with Protini") is the strongest copy in the email.
- Nothing is visually broken, and the green and white palette matches the brand.

## What's weak

- The gift offer and the 10/17 deadline are at the very bottom, and the terms are in tiny low-contrast fine print.
- The "SHOP KAMO" buttons are small, thin-outlined and grey-on-white, so they are easy to miss. The first one sits on a busy image edge.
- The subject, "Making Kamo even better," doesn't match the content. There's no product news, and the offer isn't hinted at.
- This is the third Kamo-centric send since 10/11. The offer started 10/10, so by now it's an old promotion with no new angle.
- The hero never shows a price, a percentage or a gift. The only quantified value is in the fine print.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the pouch offer to the hero. Use a banner such as "Free pouch + B-Hydra sample with Kamo + a moisturizer," with a solid, filled green button. Keep Karen's routine as the supporting content below it.
- 2. Make the buttons bigger and filled, and tie the copy to the offer: "Get my free pouch."
- 3. Add urgency in the visible copy: "Ends Oct 16, 11:59 PM ET." The fine print says 2:59 AM ET on 10/17, so match that in plain language.
- 4. Spell out the qualifying moisturizers near the offer instead of only in the legal text.
- 5. Make the subject say what the email says, so it's honest and clicked for the right reason.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free pouch with Kamo, ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `How our e-commerce director wears Kamo`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Buy Kamo + a moisturizer, pick your free pouch and get a B-Hydra sample.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Karen's 2-step routine, plus a gift that's gone after Oct 16.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's HTML has no blocking defects. The QA run found 0 errors and 3 warning groups, for a 57% pass rate. The main gaps are insecure `http://` image sources, unverified compliance and authentication headers, and a recipient email address sitting in plaintext in tracking URLs.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Click links not probed.** 12 tracking links were skipped because they are click-redirect domains, so none of their destinations were verified.
- **Recipient email in the tracking URL (PII).** The Everest pixel/link is `https://drunkelephantus.everestengagement.com/ea/dKhpn7WOgU/?e=darcy-ferment-drunkelephant-fap6e@etell.app&c=us_de_broad…`. The address is unencoded in the query string, so it can leak through logs, referrers and forwarded mail.
- **Second tracker with a user ID.** The tracking image `https://mi.drunkelephant.com/p/up/80acaf1c74f9e002/o.gif?mi_u=98161986&mi_ecmp=us_de_broadcast_promotion_kamokarenfeatur…` carries a numeric user ID (`mi_u`). The source shows two separate tracking systems (mi.* and everestengagement.com).

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Insecure images.** Three social icons load over `http://` from `image.cheers.drunkelephant.com/lib/fe3211717064057c751c78/m/1/`. They are Facebook (`cc02d07d-…png`), Instagram (`9b452915-…png`) and TikTok (`29f975e1-…png`). Clients that proxy images over HTTPS, or block mixed content, may drop them.
- **Missing alt text.** `o.gif` and the `everestengagement.com/ea/dKhpn7WOgU/` image have no alt attribute. Both look like tracking pixels, so the impact is low, but they should carry `alt=""`.
- **Ignored font imports.** The `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/...)` rules come after the `body{...}` rule in the `<style>` block. CSS requires `@import` to come first, so they are ignored. The three `<link rel="stylesheet">` tags load the same fonts, so the imports are redundant.
- **Invalid markup.** `<!-- *[class=contenttable] … -->` comments sit inside the `@media (max-width:600px)` block. HTML comment markers there are not valid CSS and can break rules in some parsers.
- **Third-party font dependency.** Brown Bold, Light and Thin come from `db.onlinewebfonts.com` and Brown Regular from `drunkelephant.com` (Demandware static path). Most clients ignore web fonts, and these add external requests and a dependency on third-party uptime.
- **Zoom disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom in clients that honor it.
- **No document language.** `<html>` has no `lang` attribute, which hurts screen readers.
- **Generic title.** `<title>Drunk Elephant</title>` is the same on every send.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens appear in the visible source. The recipient email is populated in the tracking URL (see section 2). The source is truncated, so the rest of the body could not be checked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **No `List-Unsubscribe` header** was captured, and **no `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header** (RFC 8058 one-click) either. QA notes the AgentMail relay may strip these headers, so this is unconfirmed rather than a confirmed failure.
- **No `Authentication-Results` header.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown for the `info@cheers.drunkelephant.com` sender. This is also likely a relay artifact.
- **Footer not verified.** The truncated source doesn't show the physical postal address or the in-body unsubscribe link, so CAN-SPAM footer compliance couldn't be checked.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **No UTM parameters visible.** Campaign attribution appears to run through the redirect wrappers (`mi_ecmp=us_de_broadcast_promotion_kamokarenfeatur…`, `c=us_de_broad…`).
- **Landing pages unverified.** All 12 click links were skipped, and the destinations are hidden behind redirects, so landing-page alignment and UTM pass-through couldn't be checked.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Switch the three social icon URLs on `image.cheers.drunkelephant.com` to `https://`.
2. Stop putting the raw email address in the Everest `e=` parameter. Use an opaque hashed or tokenized ID instead.
3. Add `alt=""` to both tracking pixels.
4. Move `@import` rules to the top of the `<style>` block or remove them, since the `<link>` tags already load the fonts. Remove the HTML comments inside `@media`.
5. Add `lang="en"` to `<html>` and drop `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
6. Capture raw headers directly, not through the relay, to confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment.
7. Resolve the redirect chains with a follow-redirects probe to verify the landing URLs and UTM pass-through.
