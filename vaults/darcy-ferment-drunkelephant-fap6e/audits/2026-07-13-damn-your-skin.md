# Damn, your skin!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- This is a vitamin C editorial send — "Bye-Bye, Bleh!" is the hero headline, and the email walks through three C-series products (C-Firma Fresh Day Serum, C-Luna Hydrabright Serum, C-Tango Multivitamin Eye Cream) in a tight vertical stack. The angle is clear: vitamin C for everyone, positioned as a brightening/anti-aging routine-builder. The banner at the top reprises the Babyfacial leave-on mask offer from the July 10 "Time to come clean" send, which suggests this is a double-dip loyalty welcome still running — a fine tactic but it dilutes the vitamin C focus.
- The email is visually clean and legible. Product images are sharp, copy is concise per card, and the orange palette ties naturally to the vitamin C ingredient story. That said, there is no concrete offer in the body — no discount, no GWP, no bundle. It's pure editorial, which is a weaker conversion vehicle unless the reader is already in-market for vitamin C.
- **Judgement:** A competent ingredient-education send that should have been the vehicle for a real offer. Without a price hook, this is a catalog, not a campaign.

## What's working

- **Ingredient coherence** — "BYE-BYE, BLEH!" + vitamin C copy throughout creates a single-minded editorial thread.
- **Banner CTA surfaces the active offer** — the top-of-email Babyfacial GWP strip ensures there's at least one promotional hook visible immediately, even if it belongs to last week's send.
- **Product cards are scannable** — each card leads with the product name, a one-sentence value prop, and a "SHOP NOW" button; low cognitive load.
- **Photography is on-brand** — clean white/orange product shots, no clutter, consistent with the DE aesthetic.
- **"For more bright ideas" footer CTA** — the closing guide link is a reasonable retention play for readers who didn't convert on any individual product.

## What's weak

- **No offer in the body** — three product cards, zero discounts, no urgency. The reader has no reason to buy today vs. tomorrow.
- **Banner offer is a carryover** — the Babyfacial GWP strip at the top is the same offer from the July 10 send; subscribers who've already seen it will register staleness.
- **Subject line doesn't match the email** — "Damn, your skin!" implies a reaction to *your* skin (personalized compliment) but opens into a generic ingredient editorial. The payoff doesn't match the promise.
- **Three "SHOP NOW" buttons for different products with no hierarchy** — the CTAs are identical in weight; there's no recommended hero or editorial point of view on which product to start with.
- **Fine print is dense and tiny at the bottom** — standard but the block is notably long, occupying a meaningful portion of the scroll depth.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a concrete offer to the body** — even a "free deluxe C-Firma sample with any C-series purchase" or "15% off your first vitamin C pick" would convert this from browsing content to a buy trigger. Without it, the email educates but doesn't sell.
- 2. **Reconcile the subject with the content** — "Damn, your skin!" signals a personal moment; the body should open with a mirror-back ("Here's what's making it glow") rather than a generic "There's a vitamin C for everyone."
- 3. **Declare a hero product** — pick C-Firma (the strongest seller by brand history) as the lead recommendation and visually elevate it; the two supporting products become secondary. Editorial POV converts better than a flat menu.
- 4. **Retire or refresh the banner** — if the Babyfacial GWP is still live, add an expiry date so it reads as urgent; if it's expired, remove it.
- 5. **Differentiate the CTA buttons** — "SHOP C-FIRMA," "TRY C-LUNA," "SHOP EYE CREAM" over three identical "SHOP NOW" calls; specificity lifts CTR.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Triple font loading (confirmed bug).** The same three Brown variant fonts are loaded three separate ways in the same `<head>`:
- Via `<link rel="stylesheet">` (lines 1–3 of the source)
- Via `@import url(...)` inside the `<style>` block
- Via `@font-face` declarations in the same `<style>` block

This generates 6–9 redundant HTTP requests per open and increases render-blocking time. Only one loading mechanism should be used.

**HTML comments inside `<style>` block (confirmed bug).** Two rules are wrapped in HTML comment syntax inside the CSS:

```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

These are invalid CSS. Gmail, Apple Mail, and most modern clients will silently drop both rules. The `contenttable` and `mobile` responsive widths are therefore non-functional, likely causing layout breakage on small screens.

**Contradictory `text-size-adjust` (confirmed bug).** The `<body>` sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%` (correct), but a subsequent wildcard rule overrides it:

```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}
```

The wildcard wins on specificity in most clients. Setting this to `none` prevents iOS Mail from scaling small text up to readable size — a WCAG 1.4.4 failure and a usability regression for mobile readers.

**Third-party font CDN dependency.** `BrownBold`, `BrownLight`, and `BrownThin` are all served from `db.onlinewebfonts.com`, which is not a brand-controlled or contractually guaranteed CDN. `BrownRegular` is served from `drunkelephant.com` directly. A CDN outage on the third-party domain would cause all four weight variants to fall back to system fonts with no brand control. Recommend migrating all weights to the brand's own CDN.
