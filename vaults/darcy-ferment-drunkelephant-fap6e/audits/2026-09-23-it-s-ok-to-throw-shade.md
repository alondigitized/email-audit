# It’s OK to throw shade.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-23**
## Executive summary

- This is a shade-finder tool push for the new Kamo Triple-Peptide Cover Drops, wrapped around a genuine, well-executed diversity-forward hero image — five forearms across the skin-tone spectrum with actual shade codes stamped on each. The top banner does the real selling ("20% off a Kamo + full-size moisturizer bundle") while the body is pure product education: shade matching, a sample "MEDIUM 14W" result, an application close-up, and a bundled sample-card/travel-bag gift with a $120+ order. It's consistent with the brand's recent run of product-education sends (2026-09-19's Kamo drops send, 2026-09-17's "Hydrated skin") rather than the countdown-timer "SECRET SALE" machine from earlier in the cycle.
- The subject "It's OK to throw shade" is a decent pun but undersells what's inside — there's a real 20% bundle offer buried in a gray banner most inboxes will clip before the reader ever scrolls to the tool. The email is well-built and on-brand, but it's asking the reader to do work (take a shade quiz) rather than handing them a reason to click right now.

## What's working

- The five-forearm hero image is a strong, clear diversity signal with visible shade codes (9C, 8N, 13W, 16N, 21C, etc.) tying directly to the shade-finder pitch.
- Two clear "FIND YOUR SHADE" CTA buttons, well-placed after the hero and after the demo shot.
- The top banner surfaces a concrete offer (20% off Kamo + full-size moisturizer bundle) with a clear domain call-to-action.
- The "MEDIUM 14W" sample result plus the eye-application close-up make the product's actual use case concrete, not abstract.
- Fine print is present but not predatory — dates, exclusions, and per-order limits are disclosed rather than hidden entirely.

## What's weak

- No overlapping text or broken images, but the offer banner is small, gray, and easy to skim past — it doesn't visually compete with the green shade-finder headline below it.
- Primary CTA is "FIND YOUR SHADE," not a direct purchase or "SHOP NOW" for the 20% bundle — the actual discount lives one more click away from any button.
- The subject line pun doesn't reference the offer, the product name, or a concrete benefit, so it undersells a real discount that's live only through 9/29.
- The gift-with-purchase ($120+ threshold) is only spelled out in body copy near the bottom, easy to miss if reader doesn't scroll that far.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the top banner do double duty — the 20% off deserves its own CTA button, not just bolded text, since it's the only naked-percentage offer in the whole email.
- 2. Rename the primary CTA closer to "Shop Kamo Drops" or "Find My Shade & Save 20%" so clicking feels tied to the discount, not just a quiz.
- 3. Pull the free gift-with-$120+-order detail up near the hero — it's a genuine incentive currently buried in paragraph copy above the footer.
- 4. Tighten the subject to reference the concrete offer or new product, since the pun alone won't differentiate this from prior Kamo sends.

- **Subject Alt B:** `25 shades, 1 you. Plus 20% off your bundle.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Take our 2-minute shade quiz, then bundle & save through 9/29.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Meet Kamo Triple-Peptide Cover Drops — matched to your exact undertone.`
