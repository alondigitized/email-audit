# Sleep your way...
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- "Sleep your way..." leans hard on intrigue — the truncated subject teases without revealing, and the email delivers with a polished four-step nighttime routine editorial (Marula cleanser → B-Hydra → Bora Barrier → F-Balm). The creative is clean, the hierarchy reads well on desktop, and the free shipping banner adds genuine transactional value up top.
- But the email tries to do too much: a routine walkthrough, a "35% LESS" kit promo with broken body copy, and a secondary B-Hydra hydration block all competing for attention below the fold. The weakest moment is that "35% LESS IS ALWAYS A GOOD THING" section — the copy reads "Glow we're talking signpost or summer days. But when it comes to glowing skin, you're welcome." — which is garbled draft-level copy burying what should be the email's strongest conversion hook.
- The bigger structural problem is creative déjà vu. DE ran an almost identical concept on June 24 ("The ultimate hack?" — multi-product overnight routine, 35% off trio, B-Hydra as a key step). B-Hydra now appears in this inbox for the fourth time in two weeks. Subscribers are going to feel like they've read this email before they finish the hero.

- **Subject line curiosity.** "Sleep your way..." is DE at its most playful — the truncated tease works without spam signals.
- **Free shipping banner is prominent and clear** — no-minimum threshold is up front and unambiguous.
- **Four-step routine structure** gives the email editorial backbone; product image + short copy + "SHOP" link per step is easy to scan.
- **F-Balm step copy stands out** — "You'll wake up plump and hydrated like you can't believe" is DE's confident voice at its best.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean** — hero lands first, step grid is easy to parse, no render bugs visible.

## What's weak

- **"35% LESS" body copy is broken** — "Glow we're talking signpost or summer days" is garbled draft copy that buries a real discount.
- **No urgency anywhere** — no deadline on free shipping, no expiration on the 35% deal; browsers won't convert without a clock.
- **B-Hydra fatigue** — appears in Step 2 AND the "SKIN GETS THIRSTY, TOO." closing section, on top of June 24 and June 19 sends. The product is losing novelty.
- **No single hero product** — four routine steps dilute click intent; subscribers have to decide what to click rather than being pulled to one destination.
- **Seasonal disconnect** — it's July 2 (the week of July 4th) and the email has zero holiday or summer hook. It reads as evergreen.

- 1. **Fix the "35% LESS" copy immediately** — the only price discount in the email is buried under broken copy. Rewrite: "Save 35% when you bundle the full nighttime routine — sleep better, spend less." Add a real CTA button to the kit.
- 2. **Add a deadline** — "Free shipping ends July 4th" or "Kit savings through Sunday" gives subscribers a reason to click now rather than closing the tab.
- 3. **Make F-Balm the single hero product** — the strongest copy in the email is about F-Balm. A one-product hero ("The overnight mask that makes you wake up different") with the 4-step routine as supporting context below would unify the send.
- 4. **Rest B-Hydra** — four sends in two weeks is oversaturation; give Bora Barrier or F-Balm the "SKIN GETS THIRSTY" slot.
- 5. **Tie to the July 4th moment** — even one line in the hero ("All weekend, free shipping") makes this feel timely rather than pulled from the evergreen queue.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue A — HTML comment syntax inside CSS `<style>` block (invalid):**
```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
`<!-- -->` inside a `<style>` block is not valid CSS. These are dead rules — the intended responsive widths for `.contenttable` and `.mobile` will never apply. The `@media (max-width: 600px)` block silently loses two layout rules in every client.

**Issue B — Triple font loading for BrownBold, BrownLight, BrownThin:**

Each of these three variants is loaded three ways simultaneously:
1. `<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...">` in `<head>`
2. `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...)` inside `<style>`
3. `@font-face { src: url("https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/t/...") }` also inside `<style>`

This fires 9 redundant HTTP requests (3 fonts × 3 load paths). Strip the `<link>` tags and `@import` lines — the `@font-face` declarations alone are sufficient and are already present.

**Issue C — `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Suppresses pinch-to-zoom on iOS. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Issue D — `*{line-height: 100%}` universal rule:**

Applied to every element globally. Outlook interprets unitless percentage line-height as exact, collapsing multi-line text. Scope this rule to `td` or remove it entirely and set line-height per element.

**Issue E — Third-party font CDN (`db.onlinewebfonts.com`):**

BrownBold/Light/Thin are served from `db.onlinewebfonts.com`, a public font redistribution site with unclear licensing. BrownRegular is self-hosted on `drunkelephant.com`. The inconsistency creates a single-point-of-failure dependency on an uncontrolled CDN. Self-host all four variants from `drunkelephant.com` to match the BrownRegular pattern.
