# A flood of hydration
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-04**
## Executive summary

- This is the cleanest execution of the holiday-kit push so far. The hero carries a real price anchor: a "$90 VALUE: $143" badge on the Haute Hydration kit. The "$158 for $99?" send on 9/29 promised a price and never showed one. This one shows it, and the "In Haute Water" headline pays off the "flood of hydration" subject.
- Below the hero, the email becomes a three-product walkthrough: Mello Marula cleanser, Lala Retro cream and Amino Rain serum. It reads like editorial education, with no further offer. The kit appears to be the product, but the body never says the three items are the kit contents, and nothing links the hero to the sections below. This is the third holiday-kit send in about a week, after 9/27 and 9/29, so the creative is getting familiar.
- The weak spot is the closing "THE BAG ALONE...!" block. It offers a free bag at $100+, but the kit is $90. A reader who buys the kit alone doesn't qualify. The deadline (10/8) and exclusions sit in gray fine print at the bottom. The email is solid and on-brand, but it gets pulled in two directions.

## What's working

- The "$90 / VALUE: $143" badge is visible in the hero and gives a concrete savings frame.
- The yellow-and-houndstooth hero is bold and on-season, and the eye lands on the kit and headline first.
- "In Haute Water" echoes the subject's water metaphor, so the opening is coherent.
- The product sections are clean and scannable: one product, one short benefit line, a clear hierarchy.
- Nothing is broken. Images load, text doesn't overlap and spacing is consistent.

## What's weak

- The free bag needs $100+ and the kit is $90, so the two offers conflict without saying so.
- Both main CTAs read "SHOP NOW", which is generic. The second one sits under individual products and doesn't say where it goes.
- The only deadline (10/8, 2:59 a.m. ET) is in gray footnote text. Nothing in the hero creates urgency.
- There are no reviews, ratings or social proof anywhere, even though the copy sells texture and glow.
- The body never ties the three featured products back to the kit, so it's unclear whether the walkthrough is the kit contents.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the offer logic. Either say "Add the kit + one more item for the free bag" in the bag block, or lower the threshold to $90. Put the bag offer and its deadline in the hero, not the footer.
- 2. Label the walkthrough "What's inside the kit" and show the kit price next to each product, so the education leads straight to the offer.
- 3. Replace the generic CTAs with "Shop the $90 kit" in the hero and "Get the free bag" in the lower block.
- 4. Add one proof point near the serum, such as a star rating or "best-seller" line.
- 5. Break the repetition. If the 9/27 and 9/29 sends already featured this kit, lead with the price and the deadline here instead of repeating the same creative.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cleanser, cream and serum for $90, plus a free bag when you spend $100.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary

The visible source has no hard failures (0 QA issues, 3 warnings). The confirmed defects are non-HTTPS social icons, an invalid `@import` placement, a viewport that blocks zoom, and the recipient email address in a tracking-pixel URL. The unsubscribe and authentication headers could not be assessed because the relay didn't capture them. The HTML is also truncated, so the footer and most links were not visible.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **Unverified links:** 13 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe, so their destinations and redirect chains are unverified. This is a coverage gap, not a confirmed defect.
- **Recipient address in a pixel URL:** `https://drunkelephantus.everestengagement.com/ea/dKhpn7WOgU/?e=darcy-ferment-drunkelephant-fap6e@etell.app&c=us_de_broad…` puts the recipient's email in the query string in cleartext. It can end up in proxy, CDN and server logs.
- **Second open pixel:** `https://mi.drunkelephant.com/p/up/80acaf1c74f9e002/o.gif?mi_u=98161986&mi_ecmp=us_de_broadcast_promotion_holhautehydrati…` carries a user ID (`mi_u`). The email therefore runs two parallel tracking systems, Everest and `mi.drunkelephant.com`.
- **Insecure images:** the Facebook, Instagram and TikTok icons load from `http://image.cheers.drunkelephant.com/lib/fe3211717064057c751c78/m/1/…png`. This risks mixed-content blocking and proxy rewriting, and Gmail's image proxy may break or warn on them.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **Missing alt text:** `o.gif` and the Everest image have no `alt`. Both are tracking pixels, so the impact is low, but an empty `alt=""` is still the correct markup.
- **Invalid `@import`:** the `<style>` block has `body {…}` before the three `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/…)` rules. `@import` must come first, so these are ignored by compliant parsers. The same fonts are also loaded by `<link>` tags and `@font-face`, so they are declared three times.
- **Third-party font hosts:** fonts load from `db.onlinewebfonts.com` and from `drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/…/BrownRegular.eot`. Most major clients (Gmail, Outlook) ignore web fonts anyway, so the fallback stack matters. It was cut off in the truncated source, so I couldn't check it.
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="…maximum-scale=1">` prevents pinch-zoom, a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Global line-height:** `*{line-height:100%}` plus `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` disables text-size adjustment and compresses line spacing, which hurts readability for low-vision users.
- **Stray comments in CSS:** the `@media` block contains `<!-- … -->` markers. They are invalid inside CSS, and some clients may drop the `contenttable` and `mobile` rules they wrap.
- **Fixed mobile widths:** `.blocktable{width:414px !important}` and the `vouchertext4` rules (`max-width:15px`) can clip content on narrow viewports. This is plausible but not confirmed without a render.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`) are visible in the supplied source. The recipient address is merged correctly into the Everest URL, which is the privacy issue noted in section 2. Body copy was truncated, so token coverage there is unconfirmed.

## 5. Compliance

- **`List-Unsubscribe`:** not found. The QA tool says the AgentMail relay may strip it, so it is unconfirmed whether the sender sent it.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058):** not found, same caveat. This matters for Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender requirements.
- **`Authentication-Results`:** not found, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment are unknown. The From domain `cheers.drunkelephant.com` and the image host `image.cheers.drunkelephant.com` are consistent.
- **Footer:** the in-body unsubscribe link and physical postal address could not be checked because the HTML is truncated.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

- **Campaign tag:** `mi_ecmp=us_de_broadcast_promotion_holhautehydrati…` is a campaign identifier, but it is cut off.
- **UTM parameters:** none are visible in the supplied source. Because 13 links were skipped, I can't confirm whether `utm_*` parameters survive the Everest redirect to drunkelephant.com.
- **Landing alignment:** no landing-page URL could be checked, so none is assessed.

## 7. Recommendations

1. Serve the three social icons over HTTPS (`image.cheers.drunkelephant.com` should support it).
2. Replace the cleartext email in the Everest pixel with an opaque token or hashed ID.
3. Move the `@import` rules to the top of the `<style>` block or drop them. Remove the `<!-- -->` markers from the CSS and the duplicate font declarations.
4. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
5. Add `alt=""` to both tracking pixels.
6. Re-run QA against the raw `.eml` to check `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results`. Resolve the 13 redirects with a follow-redirects probe to check UTM persistence and landing destinations.
7. Re-run the audit on the full, untruncated HTML to check the footer (unsubscribe link, postal address) and any merge tokens.
