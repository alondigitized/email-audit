# SMS SOS
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-25**
## Executive summary

- This isn't really a product email — it's an SMS list-building unit wearing a product email's clothes. The hero real estate goes entirely to "STAY CLOSE" and a phone mockup showing a text-signup confirmation, with the actual product grid ("you're gonna like these guys") pushed below the fold as an afterthought. Compare that to "Hot takes" (7/17, 10/10) or "Golden hour every hour" (7/19, 9/10), both of which built the entire send around one clear product premise — this one splits its attention between two competing offers (the top banner's deluxe-sample-with-$75+ order, and the hero's 15%-off-for-texting) before it ever gets to product.
- The execution itself is clean — no render bugs, on-brand minimalist styling, real star ratings and social-proof counters on the product tiles. But the subject line "SMS SOS" is the weak link: it's a pun that doesn't communicate what's inside, doesn't reference skincare at all, and reads more like an internal campaign codename than a subscriber-facing hook.
- Net: solid production values undercut by a confused priority order and a subject line that won't earn the open. This lands as a mid-tier send — worse than the brand's editorial-led emails, better than a generic blast.

## What's working

- Clean, on-brand visual execution — no overlapping text, no broken images, consistent Drunk Elephant minimalist aesthetic throughout.
- The phone-mockup device (hand holding iPhone with the SMS confirmation bubble) is a smart, concrete way to show subscribers exactly what they're signing up for.
- Product grid has real social proof — star ratings plus "X people are checking this out" counters on all four tiles (Protini, B-Hydra, D-Bronzi, C-Firma).
- The 15% off is called out in bold pink against the gray hero background, so it's the one thing that pops visually.

## What's weak

- Subject line "SMS SOS" gives zero indication of the offer, the category, or why a subscriber should care — it's a pun, not a hook.
- Two competing offers stacked at the top (free deluxe sample on $75+/$50+ orders vs. 15% off for texting) dilute each other instead of one clear ask.
- The actual product showcase — the part most likely to drive a purchase click — is buried below an SMS-signup CTA that most existing customers will just skip past.
- No visible time-bound urgency in the primary creative; the dated windows (7/19–7/26, expires 8/21) are relegated to dense fine print at the bottom.
- Secondary "SHOP NOW" links are generic and undifferentiated across all four products — no hero product gets a distinct call to action.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the subject line first — "SMS SOS" is the single biggest drag on this send's performance since it won't get opened. Lead with the concrete offer instead.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Text us, get 15% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your phone just got a glow-up`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Exclusive drops, early sale access, and 15% off — straight to your texts.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free deluxe samples on orders $75+, plus 15% off for joining our texts.`
- 2. Pick one hero offer, not two — either lead with the deluxe-sample-on-$75+ promo or the SMS 15%-off, not both stacked in the first screen.
- 3. Move the product grid above or immediately adjacent to the SMS block so the send still functions as a merchandising email, not just a list-growth ask.
- 4. Surface the offer expiration date (7/26) near the hero copy, not just in the footer fine print — real deadlines drive urgency and this one is currently invisible.
- 5. Differentiate the four "SHOP NOW" CTAs — e.g., "Shop Protini," "Shop B-Hydra" — so each tile reads as its own decision point rather than a repeated button.
