# 10% off looks good on you
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-01**
## Executive summary

- "10% off looks good on you" is a Subscribe & Save push wearing an autumn-of-summer bonus-gift promo as a second act. The top two-thirds is entirely dedicated to getting people onto a subscription — "RUNNING ON EMPTY? NOT SO MUCH," a five-step how-to, and two identical "SUBSCRIBE TO SAVE" buttons — while a "SUMMER ENDING? NOT ON OUR WATCH" free-gift-with-purchase panel gets bolted on below with almost no visual transition between the two offers.
- Compared to the brand's stronger recent sends — "Lippe lock" (9/10) and "Golden hour every hour" (9/10) — this one lacks a single unifying premise. Those emails built around one clear hook and two hero SKUs; this one stacks three overlapping offers (10% subscription discount, free deluxe sample at $50+, free 5-piece set at $110+) each with its own threshold and date window, which reads as an internal-marketing-calendar dump rather than a crafted send.
- It's clean, on-brand, and functionally clear about what button to press, but it's not targeted or urgent enough to pull a strong open, and the offer stack undercuts the click. Middle-of-the-pack — competent execution, unfocused strategy.

## What's working

- The five-step "How to save" section is genuinely useful UX — it demystifies subscriptions in a way most DTC brands skip.
- Two identical "SUBSCRIBE TO SAVE" CTAs (one above the fold, one after the how-to) give the reader two low-friction chances to convert without re-reading.
- The teal-and-black product grouping shot is well-lit and on-brand, consistent with prior sends like "Golden hour every hour."
- Playful, consistent brand voice ("Your skin barrier would like to thank you now," "There is no step 5!") keeps it from feeling like a generic subscription-nag email.
- Render is clean — no overlapping text, no broken images, no lorem ipsum.

## What's weak

- Two distinct offers (subscription discount vs. free gift with purchase) are crammed into one email with no header separating them — it reads as two emails stapled together.
- The subject line promises "10% off" but the hero headline doesn't reinforce it until deep in the how-to steps (Step 4) — a scanning reader could miss the actual number entirely.
- No single hero product or bestseller is called out — both product shots are group lineups, so there's nothing specific to click toward.
- The fine print at the bottom is dense and covers two unrelated promotions with different dates and thresholds ($50+ vs. $110+), which risks confusing rather than reassuring.
- This is the brand's 7th-ish send in under two weeks per the history log — cadence is heavy, and this one is the least differentiated of the run.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into two emails or pick one offer to lead with — the subscription push and the free-gift threshold promo are competing CTAs and diluting each other. Subscription-focused emails should stand alone.
- 2. Move the "10%" number into the hero headline instead of burying it in Step 4 — right now the subject's promise and the hero copy don't visually connect.
- 3. Swap the group product shots for one hero SKU with a clear "starting at" price or before/after visual — give the eye something specific to land on and click.
- 4. Add urgency to the subject/hero if the gift-with-purchase deadline (8/3) is real — right now the strongest lever in the email (a ticking clock) is buried in 9pt fine print.
- 5. Trim or bullet the disclaimer block — two offers, two date ranges, and a "cannot be combined" clause in dense paragraph form reads as legalese, not benefit.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Free gift ends 8/3 — here's how to save`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Subscribe once, save every time — plus a free 5-piece set at $110+`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `10% off, free shipping, and a $21 gift — ends soon`
