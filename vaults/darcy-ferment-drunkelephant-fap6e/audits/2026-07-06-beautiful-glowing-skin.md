# Beautiful, glowing skin
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- This is a routine-builder editorial — "YOUR BEST SKIN IN TWO STEPS" pairs two cleansers against four moisturizers in a clean two-column grid. The concept is solid and visually well-executed, but it's functionally the same editorial playbook DE ran on July 2 (nighttime routine) and June 24 (Reboot Camp). Three "choose your routine" sends in two weeks is pushing subscriber fatigue hard. More critically: there's no discount in the hero. The June 24 and July 4 sends both led with 35% off; this one buries a gift-with-purchase offer at the very bottom of the scroll where most subscribers won't reach it.
- The subject "Beautiful, glowing skin" is the weakest DE has sent in this entire recent run — 23 characters of pure aspiration with no offer signal, no curiosity hook, no product specificity. After "Sleep your way...", "The It kits", and "The ultimate hack?", this subject line doesn't earn its open.
- The email is polished enough to drive clicks once someone's inside, but getting subscribers to open it in the first place is the real problem here.

## What's working

- Two-step cleanser → moisturizer framing is a fresh enough angle to differentiate from the nighttime-routine and overnight-reset sends
- Individual SHOP links beneath each product reduce friction at the item level
- Hero image with the model is strong — clean, confident, on-brand
- Brief product callouts ("lightweight, firming gel cream packed with peptides" for Protini) give just enough detail to differentiate without cluttering the grid
- "SKIN GETS THIRSTY, TOO." bottom section introduces a GWP promotional layer, even if buried

## What's weak

- "Beautiful, glowing skin" is the blandest subject in DE's recent run — zero offer signal, zero curiosity, no reason to prioritize it in a crowded inbox
- No offer in the hero — the GWP requires a purchase threshold and is past the fold; DE's higher-performing sends (June 24, July 4) front-loaded the 35% discount above the fold
- Seventh email in roughly two weeks; weak subject on a high-cadence brand is a compounding problem
- "SHOP NOW" and bare "SHOP" CTAs are the lowest-specificity copy possible — no verb+noun, no reason-why, no tie to the two-step concept
- The "SKIN GETS THIRSTY, TOO." GWP section feels bolted on rather than integrated — it breaks the two-step narrative instead of closing it

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pull the GWP into the hero or at minimum directly after Step 2.** If the free B-Hydra with $50+ spend is the actual conversion hook, it should appear in the first screenful — not buried below six product tiles. Restructure: hero → Step 1 → Step 2 → "Complete your routine, get a free B-Hydra."
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to use the two-step angle or the GWP as the hook** — both are stronger than aspirational filler.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your routine in 2 steps (+ a free serum)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The 2-step skin formula you've been sleeping on`
- 3. **Replace "SHOP NOW" with a concept-specific CTA** — "Build My Routine" or "Shop the Two-Step" ties the button back to the editorial frame and is meaningfully more specific.
- 4. **Add urgency or a deadline to the GWP.** "While supplies last" or an explicit end date gives subscribers a reason to act today rather than tab back later and forget.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free B-Hydra with your $50+ order — while supplies last`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Step 1: cleanse. Step 2: moisturize. Step 3: free serum.`
- 5. **Thin the cadence or force differentiation.** Seven sends in two weeks with overlapping editorial formats trains the list to ignore. Either increase the gap or make the editorial angle clearly distinct (a launch, a collab, a limited-window deal) — not another routine-builder.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[BUG — Dead CSS rules]** Two mobile width overrides inside the `@media (max-width: 600px)` block are wrapped in HTML comment syntax, making them inert:

```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

Inside a `<style>` block, `<!-- -->` is treated as a CSS comment. Elements using `.contenttable` and `.mobile` will not receive the 414px mobile override. This likely causes layout breakage on narrow viewports.

**[BUG — Triple font loading]** The Brown font family loads three ways in the same document, generating redundant HTTP requests:
1. `<link>` tags in `<head>` pointing to `db.onlinewebfonts.com`
2. `@import` rules inside the `<style>` block (same URLs)
3. `@font-face` declarations in the same `<style>` block

All three fire on parse. Remove the `<link>` tags and `@import` rules; keep only `@font-face`.

**[ACCESSIBILITY — zoom disabled]** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` — `maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).

**[ACCESSIBILITY — text-size-adjust disabled]** `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;-ms-text-size-adjust:none;}` applied globally. This overrides the OS-level text scaling that low-vision users rely on.

**[RENDERING — global line-height]** `*{line-height: 100%}` applied to every element. This collapses descenders on multi-line body copy and can clip text in certain email clients (notably Outlook 2016+).

**[RELIABILITY — third-party font CDN]** BrownBold, BrownLight, and BrownThin are served from `db.onlinewebfonts.com`, a CDN not operated by Drunk Elephant. Corporate email proxies and some ISPs block third-party font CDN domains. The self-hosted `BrownRegular` at `drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/...` is the correct pattern — extend it to the other weights.
