# Hi again, 25% off!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- This is the twelfth email in Drunk Elephant's Friends & Family blitz, and it's the one that makes the fake-deadline story official. Yesterday's "Adios, 25% off..." said goodbye; today's "Hi again, 25% off!" says hello again — and the hero subhead literally reads "IT'S BACK!" which is the brand explicitly confirming that its own urgency was theater. A live countdown timer showing ~12 hours, 57 minutes is genuine evidence the sale has been extended, but every subscriber who opened the "Adios" farewell yesterday now knows that DE's "last day" copy means nothing.
- The email itself is clean and reasonably well-structured. The GWP mechanic ("MASK ME ANYTHING" — spend $70+, get a full-size T.Slam) is the one genuinely new hook this send brings that earlier emails didn't. The product tile grid at the bottom gives motivated buyers specific paths to purchase. Neither saves the send from the trust damage built into its own creative.
- Verdict: A technically competent email that rewards every subscriber who decided to ignore yesterday's goodbye. The creative team executed; the campaign strategy burned the credibility they spent three days building.

## What's working

- Countdown timer is live (~12 hours remaining) — actual time-bounded urgency, not the frozen 00:00:00:00 from the May 26 "Last day" send.
- "MASK ME ANYTHING" GWP (full-size T.Slam with $70+ spend) is a new secondary incentive not present in any prior send in this blitz.
- Product tile grid shows four specific SKUs with names and prices — clearer purchase path than the abstract hero-only sends mid-week.
- Clean render: no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout bugs visible.
- Subscribe to Save mention in the preheader adds a loyalty angle differentiated from the discount-only subject.

## What's weak

- "IT'S BACK!" in the hero subhead is the brand broadcasting its own bait-and-switch in plain text — it confirms the "Adios" deadline was fabricated.
- "Hi again" in the subject reads as a self-aware shrug rather than a real explanation; it doesn't earn back the trust it acknowledges losing.
- All CTAs read "SHOP NOW" — no differentiation between browsing the sitewide sale, claiming the GWP bundle, or buying one of the four featured SKUs.
- This is the twelfth send in seven days, with two sends on both May 25 and May 26; list fatigue is past the point of diminishing returns.
- The GWP mechanic is the email's best hook but it lives below the fold, subordinate to the same hero creative subscribers have seen in every send since May 21.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name the extension honestly in the hero.** "We extended it — one real final day" is more trustworthy than "IT'S BACK!" because it acknowledges the behavior rather than papering over it with enthusiasm.
- 2. **Lead with the GWP.** The T.Slam gift-with-purchase is the only new reason to open this email versus the previous eleven. Swap it above the fold with the countdown timer; let the sitewide discount support it rather than the other way around.
- 3. **Differentiate CTAs by module.** "Shop Masks," "Shop the Sale," "Add to Routine" — any verb-noun pair that tells the buyer where they're going is better than three identical "SHOP NOW" buttons.
- 4. **Impose a real cutoff and honor it.** The cadence (two May 25 sends, two May 26 sends, now May 27) has reached the point where extending the sale a third time after multiple "final" emails would cause permanent open-rate damage.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

**a. Duplicate font loading — triple-request pattern for BrownBold/BrownLight/BrownThin**

Each of the three fonts is loaded via three separate mechanisms:
1. `<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/{hash}?family=Brown+Bold">` in `<head>`
2. `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/{hash}?family=Brown+Bold)` inside `<style>`
3. An explicit `@font-face` block inside `<style>` pointing to the same font files at `db.onlinewebfonts.com/t/{hash}.woff2`

The `<link>` and `@import` reference the same CSS stylesheet URL — that alone is a duplicate request. The redundant inline `@font-face` adds a third load path. This wastes round-trips and can cause render delays in clients that do honor web fonts (Apple Mail, Outlook iOS).

**b. HTML comment syntax inside `<style>` block**

```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

`<!-- -->` is HTML comment syntax, not CSS. These appear to be dead/commented-out responsive rules. In strict CSS parsers these are silently ignored; in some email preprocessors they can leak into rendered output or cause parse errors. Dead code should be removed, not commented out.

**c. `maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

Disables user pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Accessibility violation — WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) requires content to be resizable up to 200% without loss of function.

**d. Global `line-height: 100%` reset**

```css
*{line-height: 100%}
```

Applied to all elements. A 100% line-height computes to 1× the element's own font-size, which is tighter than the WCAG 1.4.12 minimum of 1.5× for body text. This overrides any web font's natural metrics and can cause descenders to clip.

**e. `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied globally**

```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}
```

Suppresses the user agent's automatic text scaling on mobile. Acceptable as a narrow reset on the `body` element (standard email practice); applying it via the `*` selector to all elements is over-broad.

**f. Third-party font CDN dependency**

`BrownBold`, `BrownLight`, and `BrownThin` are served from `db.onlinewebfonts.com` — not controlled by Drunk Elephant. `BrownRegular` is correctly self-hosted at `drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/...`. The inconsistency means three of four font weights have an external single point of failure.
