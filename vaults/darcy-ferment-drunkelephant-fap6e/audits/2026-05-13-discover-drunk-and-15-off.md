# Discover Drunk (and 15% off!)
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- Drunk Elephant is doing what premium skincare brands do well: leaning hard into the product family narrative. The "JOIN THE ULTIMATE cream family" hero is clean, on-brand, and gives you three distinct products with distinct skin promises — a logical send for someone who's new to the lineup or in consideration mode. The 15% promo code is prominent at the top banner, so the value prop lands immediately.
- The structural problem: this email is trying to do three jobs at once — introduce the cream trio, cross-sell the broader catalog ("we saw you coming"), and collect birthday data at the bottom. None of those jobs undermine the others, but the cumulative weight means the email keeps asking the reader to do something new every scroll. The birthday CTA at the bottom especially lands as a surprise guest rather than a designed outcome.
- Net judgement: a competent, attractive email that would convert mildly interested Drunk Elephant shoppers but won't create urgency in the fence-sitters. The 15% discount is real but the lack of a deadline defangs it.

## What's working

- **Promo code is front-loaded** — the code appears in the very first visible band of the email, removing any friction around "where do I apply this?"
- **Three-product feature format** is clear and easy to scan — Berry Barrier, Lala Retro, and Protini each get a benefit line + imagery, so readers self-select by concern.
- **"we saw you coming"** is a clever, voice-consistent section title for the product grid — it feels like Drunk Elephant's playful tone, not a generic recommendation block.
- **Birthday data collection** is a smart CRM play surfaced organically at the bottom rather than shoehorned into the hero.
- **Render is clean** — consistent yellow/green palette, no broken image gaps, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **No deadline on the 15% off** — the code is visible but there's nothing pushing urgency ("ends Sunday," "48 hours only"); the discount sits passively.
- **"SHOP" CTA is too generic** — a button that just says "SHOP" after three specific product callouts is a missed opportunity; each product deserved its own link/button.
- **Preview text repeats the 15% angle** — the preheader opens with "SETS, WALLET...WHAT ARE YOU FORGETTING? YES! Your 15% off coupon!" — it restates what the subject already says rather than complementing it.
- **Split-purpose bottom section** — the birthday ask competes with the product grid for the reader's final action; neither wins clearly.
- **No social proof** — for a brand whose loyalists post obsessively, there's no review snippet, UGC image, or star rating in sight.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline to the 15% off** — even "Offer ends Sunday" in the top banner would convert fence-sitters without any redesign.
- 2. **Replace the single "SHOP" button with per-product CTAs** — "Shop Berry Barrier," "Shop Lala Retro," "Shop Protini" — the three-panel format is already built for this.
- 3. **Rewrite the preheader to extend the subject**, not echo it — the subject already mentions 15% off; the preheader should tease the product angle.
- 4. **Give the birthday ask its own standalone email** — separating it from a promo send removes the cognitive friction of two competing actions.
- 5. **Add one review snippet** under the hero (e.g., "The Lala Retro changed my skin in two weeks — @username") — Drunk Elephant has enormous UGC equity; use it.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Mobile zoom blocked:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents user zoom on mobile. This violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1` or replace with `maximum-scale=5`.

**Issue — `font-display:swap` is a no-op in email:**
All six `@font-face` declarations include `font-display:swap`. No major email client (Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail) honors this property. It adds dead weight but causes no rendering breakage. Remove for hygiene.

**No issue — Dark mode meta tags:**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light dark">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light dark only">
```
Correctly implemented. Verify the `<style>` block (not visible in truncated source) also includes `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` rules — meta tags alone don't apply dark styles.

**No issue — Outlook VML namespaces:** Properly declared.
