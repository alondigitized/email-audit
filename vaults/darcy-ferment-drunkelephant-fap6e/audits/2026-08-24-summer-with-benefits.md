# Summer with benefits
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-24**
## Executive summary

- This is a shift away from Kamo Drops as sole hero — D-Bronzi (a bronzing skincare product) takes the top spot, with Kamo Drops relegated to a small footer teaser. It's a genuine product-diversity move after five straight Kamo-centric sends, and the "sun-kissed" seasonal framing lands as timely for late August. The ingredient-education module (Matrixyl 3000, Cocoa Extract, Chronocyclin) is a strong, on-brand execution that this list clearly favors.
- But the email buries its actual offer. The banner promises a free deluxe sample with $50+ subscription orders, yet the hero CTA ("SHOP D-BRONZI") doesn't connect to that offer directly, and there's a second, different offer (deluxe sample + Protini packette at $75+) further down for a different product line. Three separate asks — D-Bronzi, cleansers, Kamo Drops — dilute a single send. Solid ingredient storytelling, muddled offer architecture.

## What's working

- Clear hero image and headline ("SKINCARE FIRST. JUST MAKE IT SUN-KISSED.") ties a seasonal moment to a specific product.
- The ingredient-callout module (Matrixyl 3000, Cocoa Extract, Chronocyclin) is genuinely educational and visually clean — dotted-line annotations on macro ingredient photography.
- Repeats the "SHOP D-BRONZI" CTA twice, reinforcing the primary product without being pushy.
- No render bugs — images, buttons, and copy all display cleanly.

## What's weak

- Two competing, differently-worded offers (free sample at $50+ in the header banner vs. deluxe sample + Protini at $75+ in the cleanser block) creates confusion about which deal actually applies to which purchase.
- Three distinct product pushes (D-Bronzi, cleansers, Kamo Drops) in one email with no clear priority — the eye doesn't know where to land after the hero.
- Kamo Drops, the brand's dominant recent campaign, is now shrunk to a low-priority footer card with a plain "SHOP KAMO DROPS" link, a jarring cadence shift with no explanation.
- Legal fine print for the offer is dense and pushed to the very bottom, disconnected from the CTA that supposedly triggers it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one hero offer and put its terms directly under the CTA button, not in a banner disconnected from the button copy — e.g., "SHOP D-BRONZI — Free deluxe sample with $50+ subscription."
- 2. Cut this to two blocks max: D-Bronzi hero + ingredient education, then one secondary CTA (either cleansers or Kamo, not both) — three asks split attention.
- 3. If Kamo Drops inventory urgency ("before it sells out") is real, give it a proper module with a product image and full CTA button, not a shrunk footer card — this brand's own history shows Kamo sends outperform (7/10 launch) when given real visual weight.
- 4. Consolidate the two-tier fine print into a single, shorter offer summary near the top.
- **Subject Alt A:** `D-Bronzi: your skin's favorite fake tan`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Sun-kissed skin, zero sun required`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: a free deluxe sample when you subscribe`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New peptide blend, buildable color, no SPF tradeoffs`
