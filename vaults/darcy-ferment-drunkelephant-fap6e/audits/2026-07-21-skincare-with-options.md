# Skincare with options
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- "Skincare with options" is the weakest subject line Drunk Elephant has sent in the last two weeks — compare it to "20-minute hack," "Hot takes," or "Golden hour every hour," all of which arrived with personality and a hook. This one gives nothing away. The body, however, has a genuinely interesting premise: mix your DE products in your palm instead of layering, save time, save money, no order required. It's a differentiating message that plays to DE's formulation story. The execution splits the email into two disconnected halves: a banner-level offer (free deluxe samples of D-Bronzi and Lala Retro with $95+ orders) that gets about 6pt of real estate, and a hero built around the mix-in-palm concept that runs the full body. These two messages never properly unite.
- The brand voice shows up ("glooom," the three-beat save-time/save-money/save-brainpower structure), and the purple Lala Retro product shot is clean. But the CTA is a generic "SHOP NOW" and the offer is buried in the top banner rather than anchored in the hero where it would actually land. The result is a pleasant, low-urgency editorial email that will convert loyal DE fans and skip everyone else.
- The mix-in-palm concept is smart enough that it deserved its own send with a subject to match. Instead it got "Skincare with options," which describes the product category, not the idea.

## What's working

- The three-beat body rhythm ("Save time… save money… save brainpower") is clean, quick to read, and on-brand.
- Lala Retro product shot is sharp and the purple-neutral palette pairs well with the skin-tone hand image.
- The mix-in-palm concept is genuinely differentiating — DE's formulations support it and it addresses a real friction point (layering fatigue).
- Banner offer (D-Bronzi + Lala Retro samples with $95+) references two marquee products that fans actually want.

## What's weak

- Subject "Skincare with options" is placeholder-level copy — no curiosity, no specificity, nothing a subscriber would recognize as DE.
- The banner offer and the hero creative (mix-in-palm routine shortcut) are two separate ideas that never resolve into a single proposition.
- "SHOP NOW" is the weakest possible CTA for an email built around a technique — it wastes the setup.
- No urgency signal anywhere: no deadline on the samples offer, no "while supplies last," nothing.
- Fine print block at the bottom is long and dense enough to undercut trust at the scroll end.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to sell the technique, not the category.** The mix-in-palm concept is the hook — lead with it.
- 2. **Merge the offer into the hero.** Something like "Mix D-Bronzi + Lala Retro in your palm — try both free with $95+" makes the banner and the hero one idea instead of two.
- 3. **Replace "SHOP NOW" with a technique-specific CTA** — "Try the Mix," "Build My Routine," or "Get Both Free."
- 4. **Add a soft deadline to the samples offer** — even "this week only" would activate latent intent without feeling manufactured.
- 5. **Cut or collapse the fine print block** — surface the one real condition ($95 minimum) inline in the offer, not in 6pt footer text.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Dead CSS rules (silent breakage):**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 600px) {
  <!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
  <!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
HTML comment syntax (`<!-- -->`) inside a `<style>` block is not valid CSS. Both `contenttable` and `mobile` responsive rules are completely ignored by all email clients. Any elements using these classes will not resize on mobile.

**Triple-redundant font loading:**
Brown Bold, Brown-Light, and Brown-Thin are each loaded three ways simultaneously — `<link>` tag, `@import`, and `@font-face`. This triples HTTP requests for fonts that most email clients (Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail with images off) will block regardless. Effective fallback is system sans-serif; the triple load adds overhead with no upside.

**Dual `@font-face` sources for BrownBold:** The font is declared first via first-party CDN (`drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/...`) then again via third-party (`db.onlinewebfonts.com`). The second declaration shadows the first.

**`maximum-scale=1` viewport:** Prevents user-initiated zoom on mobile — an accessibility regression for low-vision users. WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text) flags this.

**`.appleLinks a { color: #000000 !important; text-decoration: none !important; }`** — Forces all Apple Mail link color to pure black with no underline. If any link sits on a dark or image background, it becomes invisible. Verify contrast ratio across all image/background contexts.

**`*{ -webkit-text-size-adjust: none }` on all elements** — Suppresses browser text-size accessibility settings globally, not just on the `body`. Overly broad; should be scoped to `body` only.

**Duplicate `line-height: 100%` declaration:**
```css
.ExternalClass p, ... { line-height: 100% }
/* ... */
* { line-height: 100% }
```
The `*` rule makes the `.ExternalClass` rule redundant. Not a breakage, but a maintenance noise flag.
