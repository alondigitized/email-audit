# Do I hear 15% off?
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-21**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, single-idea reminder of the Subscribe & Save event that Drunk Elephant launched on 2026-10-19 with "15% off, you say? Our Subscribe to Save Event starts now!". The hero is the same grey backdrop with the same product lineup: Protini, C-Firma, B-Hydra and T.L.C. It reads well. The headline is large, the tiered offer (15% / 20% / 10%) is stated in plain copy, and the pastel-and-neon bottles on white plinths are on brand.
- The problem is novelty. This is the second near-identical send in three days, and it comes a day after the 10/20 verification-gated send. "Do I hear 15% off?" is a cute auction riff, but it promises less than the body delivers, since the real hook is a lasting subscription discount. The headline, "AND YOU'LL NEVER RUN OUT AGAIN", starts mid-thought. It looks like the continuation of a message the subscriber never saw.
- Verdict: it is competent and tidy but redundant. It works as a reminder for people who ignored the first send. The deadline is only in the fine print, and the offer gets no extra reason to click. It is a 6.

## What's working

- The offer ladder (15% off the first delivery, 20% off the second, 10% after) is bolded in the body and easy to scan.
- The product lineup is bright and clearly shows the range, and the colors pop against the grey.
- The layout is a single column with generous whitespace and no render bugs.
- The "SHOP NOW" button sits above the product image, so it is visible early.
- The fine print covers dates, free shipping and cancel-anytime. The Oct 27 end date is stated clearly.

## What's weak

- The headline "AND YOU'LL NEVER RUN OUT AGAIN" has no setup. It reads as a continuation of a missing earlier line.
- The CTA is a thin, low-contrast outlined box with generic "SHOP NOW" copy. It looks weaker than the headline and the bold product art.
- The deadline (Oct 27) and "free shipping" appear only in small grey fine print, not in the hero.
- The creative is nearly identical to the 10/19 send, which signals list fatigue and weak differentiation.
- Nothing is linked to a specific product, price or review. There is no social proof, and nobody learns which product to start with.

## Recommendations

- 1. Differentiate from the 10/19 send. Lead with a deadline ("Ends Oct 27") and a specific hero product, such as Protini with the actual subscription price. Otherwise skip the reminder or send it only to non-openers.
- 2. Fix the headline so it stands alone. Use "Subscribe once. Save 15%, then 20%, then 10% forever."
- 3. Make the CTA a filled, high-contrast button with specific copy, such as "Start my subscription".
- 4. Move the end date and "free shipping" up into the hero body copy.
- 5. Add one line of proof, such as a star rating or a "most subscribed" tag on a product.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last week: 15% off your first subscription`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Ends Oct 27: Protini, on repeat`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `15% off delivery one, 20% off delivery two, free shipping. Cancel anytime.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email has no hard QA failures (0 issues, 3 warnings, 57% pass rate). The confirmed problems are mixed-content image sources, a recipient email address in a tracking-image URL, and `@import` rules that are likely ignored. Unsubscribe and authentication status couldn't be verified from the captured data.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** 10 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they use click-redirect domains, so none could be checked for destination, status or redirect chain.
- **Recipient email in a URL:** the image `https://drunkelephantus.everestengagement.com/ea/dKhpn7WOgU/?e=darcy-ferment-drunkelephant-fap6e@etell.app&c=us_de_broad…` carries the recipient address in plain text in the query string. It can end up in proxy logs, referrer data and third-party server logs.
- **Tracking pixel:** `https://mi.drunkelephant.com/p/up/80acaf1c74f9e002/o.gif?mi_u=98161986&mi_ecmp=us_de_broadcast_promotion_subweekresend_p…` is a normal open pixel. Its `mi_u` parameter is a per-recipient ID.
- **Campaign name:** `mi_ecmp` contains `subweekresend`, which suggests this is a resend. That's inferred from the name, not confirmed.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Mixed content:** three social icons use `http://`, which may be blocked or flagged by clients that enforce HTTPS:
  - `http://image.cheers.drunkelephant.com/lib/fe3211717064057c751c78/m/1/cc02d07d-c9bb-4057-b5d7-62f3bec2c521.png` (Facebook)
  - `…/9b452915-64bf-4b29-b08b-c4a42802457a.png` (Instagram)
  - `…/29f975e1-6f94-429c-ae2b-8ee6f90786cb.png` (TikTok)
- **Missing alt text:** two images have none, `o.gif` and the `everestengagement.com/ea/dKhpn7WOgU/` image. Both look like tracking pixels, so the impact is low. If they're 1×1, `alt=""` is the correct form.
- **`@import` placement:** the three `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/...)` rules come after the `body{…}` rule in the `<style>` block. CSS ignores `@import` unless it comes first, so those imports are likely dead. The matching `<link>` tags in `<head>` mean the fonts may still load in clients that support `<link>`. Gmail and many other clients strip both `<link>` and `@import`.
- **Third-party font host:** the `@font-face` sources point to `db.onlinewebfonts.com`, which adds a third-party dependency. Clients that don't support web fonts fall back to the stack, and the visible portion shows no fallback defined for BrownBold, BrownLight or BrownThin.
- **Invalid markup:** `<!-- … -->` comments sit inside the `@media (max-width: 600px)` block in `<style>`. Some parsers may mishandle them, and they're unnecessary.
- **Truncated source:** the HTML was cut off, so I couldn't check the body markup, such as `role="presentation"`, `lang` or button fallbacks.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No unresolved merge tokens (such as `{{…}}`, `%%…%%` or `[FIRST_NAME]`) are visible in the truncated source. The only personalization seen is the recipient email in the Everest URL and the `mi_u` ID in the pixel URL. The body wasn't fully captured, so this isn't a complete check.
