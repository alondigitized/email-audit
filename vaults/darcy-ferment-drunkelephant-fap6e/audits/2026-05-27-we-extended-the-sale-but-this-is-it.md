# We extended the sale…but this is it!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- This is email thirteen in Drunk Elephant's Friends & Family blitz, and it earns one distinction no prior send in the series did: it admits the extension right in the subject line. After "Last day for 25% off!" (May 26), "Adios, 25% off..." (May 26), and this morning's "Hi again, 25% off!" — all implying the sale had closed — "We extended the sale…but this is it!" at least stops pretending. That's a lowered bar, but it clears it.
- The creative choice here is a full 180: no imagery, no countdown timer, no branded hero — just a plain-text letter signed by Anna, head of marketing. Against a week of glossy graphic sends, the format creates real contrast. The content, for the first time in the sequence, is actually specific: spend $50 for a sample, spend $100 for the sample plus the Hydra and Bright kit, spend $175 for all of that plus a free full-size F-Balm mask. That tiered incentive is the best content the blitz has produced.
- The problem is execution. There is no CTA button anywhere in the email. The only clickable elements are bare text links embedded in prose. The tiered reward structure — the email's entire reason for existing — is buried in a bullet list inside a wall of grey text with no visual hierarchy to guide the eye. Strong content, zero mechanics to convert it.

## What's working

- **Honest subject** — admitting the extension upfront is more credible than the "Adios → Hi again" whiplash from the May 26 sends.
- **Tiered spend rewards are the series' best offer** — specific product names (Bouncy Brightfacial sample, Hydra and Bright kit, F-Balm mask) at concrete spend thresholds ($50/$100/$175) give a real reason to increase cart size.
- **Plain-text format creates pattern interrupt** — after a week of graphic-heavy sends, the personal-letter aesthetic stands out and may register as more urgent or human to fatigued subscribers.
- **Multiple category links** — "essentials," "moisturizers," "serums," "new" give active shoppers several entry points to relevant product pages.
- **Compact and scannable structure** — the bullet list at least makes the offer readable, even without visual weight.

## What's weak

- **No CTA button** — the only clickable paths are inline text hyperlinks; no button means no clear conversion anchor and no accessibility for skim readers.
- **Zero visual hierarchy** — a wall of body-weight grey text from top to bottom; the eye has nowhere to land, nothing draws attention to the offer or the deadline.
- **"ENDS TONIGHT" carries no credibility** — after five days of fake final deadlines, subscribers who have been tracking this series will not believe it. The extension admission in the subject actually reinforces that skepticism.
- **"Hello there!" as effective preheader** — without a dedicated preheader tag, the inbox preview is almost certainly "Hello there!" — twelve characters of wasted prime real estate that tells a subscriber nothing.
- **Five dense footnote paragraphs in italics** with no visual separation from the body copy create a wall of fine print at scroll end.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a single CTA button** — even in plain-text style, one styled button ("Shop before midnight →") doubles as the visual anchor the entire email is missing. Put it immediately after the tiered-rewards bullets.
- 2. **Lead with the tiered rewards, not the apology** — the F-Balm GWP at $175 is the most compelling hook this blitz has had. Open with it, not with two paragraphs of preamble.
- 3. **Specify the deadline exactly** — "ENDS TONIGHT at midnight ET" is meaningfully harder to dismiss than "ENDS TONIGHT." Specificity at least signals operational honesty even when trust is low.
- 4. **Bold the key numbers** — "$50 / $100 / $175" and "25% off" should visually pop; at body-text weight they disappear into the block.
- 5. **Write a real preheader** — the F-Balm is free and full-size; that belongs in the preview line, not buried in a bullet.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Sale extended one last time — free F-Balm ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Spend $175, get a free full-size F-Balm — midnight deadline`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free full-size F-Balm at $175. Friends & Family closes at midnight ET.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `25% off + tiered freebies. This one's actually the last time.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — HTML comment syntax inside a `<style>` block (confirmed bug):**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 600px) {
  <!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
  <!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
`<!-- -->` is HTML comment syntax. Inside a `<style>` block it is not valid CSS. Most modern parsers ignore it silently, but strict CSS parsers (e.g., some Outlook and Samsung Mail versions) may parse the surrounding rules incorrectly. The commented-out rules serve no purpose and should be removed entirely.

**Issue 2 — Triple font loading for the same typefaces:**
- `<link href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...">` in `<head>` (3 tags)
- `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...)` in `<style>` (3 duplicates)
- `@font-face` blocks pointing to the same `db.onlinewebfonts.com/t/` files

Result: up to 9 external font HTTP requests for 3 font weights. Clients that respect web fonts (Apple Mail, iOS Mail) will fire all of them. This adds latency with no rendering benefit.

**Issue 3 — Aggressive global `*` reset:**
```css
*{line-height: 100%}
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; max-height:1000000px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly}
```
`line-height: 100%` applied to `*` overrides all descendant line-height values unless individually re-declared. This is a known cause of clipped or collapsed text in table cells across Yahoo Mail and older Outlook builds. The `max-height:1000000px` is a recognized Gmail clip-prevention hack but should be scoped rather than applied globally.

**Issue 4 — Third-party font CDN (`db.onlinewebfonts.com`):**
This is not a Drunk Elephant–controlled domain. If the CDN is unavailable or rate-limits the send volume, fonts silently fail across all rendering environments. Self-hosted fonts on `drunkelephant.com` or a controlled CDN are more reliable.
