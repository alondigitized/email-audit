# Summer to go
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- This is DE's second summer travel-kit email in 11 days — the first was "Easiest glow ever" (June 10, 6/10), which leaned on OTG routine storytelling. This send leads harder on product and price: the hero kit shot with a visible "$109" badge is cleaner and more persuasive than that lifestyle-first approach. The five-product breakdown panel names D-Bronzi, Lippe Balm, Beste No. 9, Lala Retro, and B-Hydra without requiring any scroll — that does real work.
- The biggest drag is the subject line. "Summer to go" is ambient and soft where the email body actually delivers something concrete — a $109 kit and a 35% off offer. Neither surfaces in the inbox. The second miss is "SHOP SHOP SHOP" as a CTA, which reads as a running joke attached to a real discount.
- Overall this is a competent kit pitch with clean visual hierarchy and a hero that earns the click — but the inbox hook and mid-email CTA writing leave the offer's value stranded.

## What's working

- **Hero product shot lands immediately**: the kit is well-staged, the "$109" badge is clearly readable, and the "GLOW FORTH" button sits clean beneath it.
- **Five-product breakdown panel**: naming every SKU in the kit gives it legitimacy and gives B-Hydra loyalists (DE's most-emailed product this arc) a direct anchor.
- **35% off surfaces mid-scroll, not buried**: the discount section appears in the email body rather than the footer — a structural win.
- **Seasonal fit is tight**: "Summer Better Than Others" plus a travel bag image in late June reads on-calendar, not stale.
- **Clean render throughout**: no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout issues visible.

## What's weak

- **Subject squanders the offer**: "$109 kit" and "35% off" both go unmentioned in "Summer to go" — every inbox signal that drives opens is left on the table.
- **"SHOP SHOP SHOP" is a misfire**: a playful triple kills the momentum of the 35% discount it's attached to; a discount deserves a directive.
- **"Making It Rain" section is cryptic at scroll depth**: from this render it's impossible to tell if this is a GWP, a samples play, or a separate product — the headline alone doesn't land.
- **No urgency anywhere**: the 35% offer shows no deadline; without one, there's no reason to click now versus tomorrow.
- **Social proof absent**: after three straight B-Hydra emails culminating in the June 19 "Oh, my stars" review-dump, this email resets to zero — the star-ratings content that email built up is directly applicable to the kit and wasn't ported over.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to surface the offer.** "Summer to go" gives nothing concrete; the email has a real price and a real discount — put one in the inbox.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The summer kit is $109 — 5 bestsellers, one bag`
- **Subject Alt B:** `35% off summer kits — our fastest packing list`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `D-Bronzi, B-Hydra, Lala Retro + more — all in one bag`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Grab the kit before the discount ends`
- 2. **Fix the 35% CTA copy.** Replace "SHOP SHOP SHOP" with a single clear directive — "SHOP KITS" or "SEE ALL KITS." One verb, one noun, done.
- 3. **Add a deadline to the 35% offer.** Even "ends Sunday" creates urgency without dishonesty. Without it, the section reads like a permanent shelf price.
- 4. **Clarify "Making It Rain."** If it's a GWP, lead with "Spend $X, get this." If it's a standalone product, give it a name. A vague section at scroll-depth bleeds attention without converting it.
- 5. **Pull one B-Hydra review into the kit pitch.** "Oh, my stars" (June 19) was built entirely on 5-star social proof for B-Hydra — a single pulled quote validating the kit's anchor SKU would connect the arc and close the loop.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — broken mobile CSS rules (HTML comments inside `<style>`):**

```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 600px) {
  <!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
  <!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

`<!--` and `-->` are HTML comment delimiters, not CSS syntax. Every compliant CSS parser discards these two rules entirely. Elements with `class="contenttable"` and `class="mobile"` will not receive their responsive widths on screens ≤600px, likely breaking the mobile layout.

**Accessibility — pinch-zoom disabled:**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

`maximum-scale=1` prevents users from zooming, violating WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove the attribute or set `maximum-scale=5`.

**Accessibility — global text-size-adjust suppression:**

```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}
```

Applied to every element via the `*` selector. This blocks iOS's automatic text legibility adjustments across the entire email, not just the intended targets.

**Triple font loading — same weights loaded three ways:**

- `<link href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...?family=Brown+Bold">` (HTTP request at parse time)
- `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...?family=Brown+Bold)` inside `<style>` (second request, render-blocking)
- `@font-face { font-family: "BrownBold"; src: url("https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/t/...") }` (third declaration)

Brown Bold, Brown-Light, and Brown-Thin are all loaded this way. BrownRegular is the exception — hosted on `drunkelephant.com` via `@font-face` only (correct approach). The three duplicated weights generate 6 unnecessary HTTP round-trips per render.

**Third-party font CDN dependency:**

Three of four font weights depend exclusively on `db.onlinewebfonts.com`. If that CDN is unavailable, Bold, Light, and Thin fall back to the system stack. Regular is self-hosted and unaffected.
