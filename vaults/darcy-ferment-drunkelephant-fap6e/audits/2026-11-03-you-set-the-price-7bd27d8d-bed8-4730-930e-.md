# You set the price!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-03**
## Executive summary

- This is a holiday gift guide built around price tiers: under $30, $50 and $100. The yellow-and-lavender hero is bold and on brand, and "Gifts under $30, $50, and $100" says what the email is in a few words. The budget framing is the right idea. The recipient-based copy is the best part: "For the teacher's aides. The UPS guy." and "Your sister (the nice one)." are funny and sound like Drunk Elephant.
- The subject, "You set the price!", is clever but gives away nothing about the contents. It sits in a run of gifting sends that began 10/28 ("All the gifts.") and 10/30 ("The powerhouses"), so this is the third gift-themed email in a week. The body is a long scroll of 9 kits. The only discount is a conditional fine-print offer at the very bottom, and it is easy to miss.
- Overall it is a solid, well-organized gift guide that is brand-safe and useful. It has no price-cutting or urgency hook, and the bottom CTA buries the one real offer. That puts it in the mid-to-high range for a promo email.

## What's working

- The three-tier structure ($30 / $50 / $100) lets a shopper self-select by budget.
- The price badges on every product (for example $29, $49, $89) put the answer to "how much?" next to each image.
- The "$49 / $81 VALUE" and "$89 / $120 VALUE" badges on the kits show savings without a percent-off claim.
- The recipient-based copy is warm and funny, and it keeps the brand voice consistent.
- Each tier has its own "Shop gifts under $X" button, so every section ends in a clear next step.
- The alternating left/right product layout keeps a long scroll from feeling monotonous.

## What's weak

- The hero CTA, "Shop now", is generic and links to a broad collection rather than a specific product.
- The only discount is the "Kamo + D-Bronzi" gift-with-purchase at the very bottom. It is a small, hard-to-read block with a spend-$75 condition and a "Shop now" button.
- The fine print is tiny, so the offer's conditions are easy to miss.
- Nine product blocks make the email very long, and the value-badge text is too small to read comfortably on mobile.
- No reviews or social proof appear anywhere. The 11/1 "Kamo in the headlines!" send had some, and the contrast is noticeable.
- There is no urgency. The only deadline sits in the fine print at the bottom ("through 11/5/2026"), which is the strongest hook and the one placed least visibly.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the gift-with-purchase offer into the hero.** Put "Free D-Bronzi sample with $75+" above the fold, with the 11/5 deadline in plain sight. That is the single biggest lift.
- 2. Cut to 2–3 products per tier, and show the best-seller or the biggest value-badge kit first.
- 3. Replace "Shop now" with a specific CTA such as "Shop gifts under $50".
- 4. Add one social-proof line (a rating or press quote) near the top kit.
- 5. Enlarge the value badges, since they are the closest thing this email has to a price-cutting message.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Gifts for every budget (from $24)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Free D-Bronzi sample + gifts under $100`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Teacher, sister, bestie: kits from $24, plus a free sample with $75+ through 11/5.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your holiday cheat sheet, sorted by price. Free sample ends 11/5.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's markup is mostly standard XHTML table layout, but it has a few confirmed code-level issues: plain-HTTP images, unlabeled tracking pixels with the recipient's address in the query string, and invalid CSS ordering. Compliance and authentication could not be verified because the relay did not expose the headers. Click destinations were never probed.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **14 tracking links were not probed.** The QA check skipped them as click-redirect domains, so destinations, redirect chains and final status codes are unverified.
- **Recipient email in a URL.** The pixel `https://drunkelephantus.everestengagement.com/ea/dKhpn7WOgU/?e=darcy-ferment-drunkelephant-fap6e@etell.app&c=us_de_broad…` carries the address in plain text. Anything that logs the URL (proxies, CDN logs, referrers) will store it.
- **Visible campaign ID.** The pixel `https://mi.drunkelephant.com/p/up/80acaf1c74f9e002/o.gif?mi_u=98161986&mi_ecmp=us_de_broadcast_promotion_giftsbyprice_pr…` carries a numeric user ID (`mi_u`) and a campaign slug (`mi_ecmp`). I can't tell whether this is a problem, because the URL is truncated.
- **Two tracking pixels from different vendors.** Both `mi.drunkelephant.com` and `everestengagement.com` fire on open. That is not a defect, but it is redundant and adds third-party requests.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on both tracking pixels** (`o.gif` and the `everestengagement.com/ea/dKhpn7WOgU/` image). Impact is low because they are 1×1 beacons. They should still have `alt=""`, since a missing attribute makes screen readers announce the filename or URL.
- **Three images served over `http://`**, which clients may block or flag as mixed content:
  - `http://image.cheers.drunkelephant.com/lib/fe3211717064057c751c78/m/1/cc02d07d-c9bb-4057-b5d7-62f3bec2c521.png` (Facebook)
  - `…/9b452915-64bf-4b29-b08b-c4a42802457a.png` (Instagram)
  - `…/29f975e1-6f94-429c-ae2b-8ee6f90786cb.png` (TikTok)
- **No `lang` attribute.** The `<html xmlns=… xmlns:v=… xmlns:o=… xmlns:w=…>` tag has none, which hurts screen-reader pronunciation.
- **`<meta name="viewport" … maximum-scale=1>`** blocks pinch-zoom on supporting clients, which is an accessibility problem.
- **Invalid CSS ordering.** The `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/…)` rules come after other rules in the `<style>` block. Per spec they are ignored. Fonts load only through the `<link>` tags, which most email clients strip. Gmail and Outlook will fall back to system fonts.
- **HTML comments inside `<style>`.** The `<!-- *[class=contenttable] … -->` and `<!-- *[class=mobile] … -->` blocks sit inside the media query. Some parsers drop the rule or the rest of the block.
- **External font hosts.** Fonts load from `db.onlinewebfonts.com` and `www.drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/…`. The Demandware path includes a version hash (`v1719565389232`) that can go stale.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No unresolved merge tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`) are visible in the truncated source. The one rendered personalization value is the recipient address in the `e=` parameter noted above.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) were not found.** The QA tool itself says the relay may not capture them, so this is unconfirmed. If they really are absent, Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules are not met.
- **Authentication-Results was not captured.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment are unknown. The From domain is `cheers.drunkelephant.com`, and the image host is the same subdomain.
- **The footer unsubscribe link and physical address are not visible in the truncated HTML.** I can't confirm or refute them.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Landing-page UTM parameters could not be checked, because all 14 click-tracking links were skipped and the source is truncated.
- The only campaign identifier visible is `mi_ecmp=us_de_broadcast_promotion_giftsbyprice_pr…`. It is a vendor tracking parameter, not UTM.
- Whether the destination matches the "You set the price!" / gifts-by-price offer is unverified.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Change the three social icons to `https://`. The host `image.cheers.drunkelephant.com` should already serve TLS.
2. Add `alt=""` to both tracking pixels.
3. Stop putting the raw email address in the pixel URL. Use an opaque or hashed subscriber ID.
4. Add `lang="en"` to `<html>`. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
5. Move the `@import` rules to the top of the `<style>` block or drop them. Remove the HTML comments from inside the CSS. Make sure fallback fonts are specified.
6. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` against the raw message headers, not the relay copy. Check SPF, DKIM and DMARC the same way.
7. Resolve the tracking redirects with a real follow-through to check final URLs, status codes and UTM presence.
