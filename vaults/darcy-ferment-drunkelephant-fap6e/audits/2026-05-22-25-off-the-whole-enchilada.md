# 25% off the whole enchilada
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Drunk Elephant's third Friends & Family email in as many sends — and notably the second email on May 22 alone, following "Stock up at 25% off!" which already logged an 8/10 earlier today. The creative team earns partial credit for layering the pitch differently: rather than just resending the 25% off call-to-action, this email wraps three distinct offer tiers (sitewide 25%, a $100+ threshold GWP of the Hydra + Bright Kit, and a free full-size mask offer) before landing on a curated product grid. The "whole enchilada" brand voice continues the playful streak cleanly.
- The trouble is fatigue math. Subscribers who opened "Stock up at 25% off!" within the last few hours are now being told — again — not to miss the sale. The new offer layers are real differentiation, but they require a subscriber to re-engage with a message that reads structurally similar to the last one. The urgency line ("don't wait!") is soft and deadline-free, which is the single biggest missed opportunity on a day-three sale.
- The email executes Drunk Elephant's house style competently and the product grid with named SKUs and sizes gives people genuine reasons to click. But the cadence is doing real damage to what would otherwise be a solid send.

## What's working

- **Layered offers make this send feel distinct**: 25% sitewide base + $100 threshold GWP + full-size mask offer is a real progression, not a copy-paste of the prior email.
- **Brand voice is consistently on**: "why are you still reading this, go shop!" in the top banner and "the whole enchilada" in the subject line are unmistakably DE — playful without being annoying.
- **Product grid with named SKUs** (T.Tronopolsol Vitamin C, B-Hydra, T.L.C. Framboos, A-Passioni Retinol Cream) gives subscribers specific anchors to click, not just a generic brand shop.
- **Hero hierarchy is clean**: "25% OFF SITEWIDE" in large type dominates the above-fold space; the eye doesn't get confused about what the email is for.

## What's weak

- **No deadline anywhere visible**: "don't wait!" is the sole urgency signal, with no sale end date. On a day-three send, "ends tonight" or "ends Sunday" would dramatically lift click intent.
- **Second email of the same day**: subscribers already received "Stock up at 25% off!" on May 22 — sending a near-identical offer block twice in one day risks unsubscribes more than it drives incremental revenue.
- **Three-section content structure before the product grid** (sitewide → threshold GWP → mask offer) creates decision paralysis; the strongest GWP hook (free full-size mask?) lands below the fold as an afterthought.
- **CTAs are all text-based "SHOP NOW" links**, not branded buttons — this is a pattern across recent DE sends and it underserves mobile tap targets.
- **Dense fine print at the bottom**: multiple full paragraphs of exclusion/condition language; at this size it reads as hedging rather than honest disclosure.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline to both the subject line and the hero** — "Sale ends Sunday" changes the entire calculus for on-the-fence subscribers and is the single biggest lever available right now.
- 2. **Lead with the mask GWP**, not the threshold kit — "free full-size [Mask name]" is more emotionally compelling than "spend $100+" framing; put it in the first section and name the product explicitly.
- 3. **Convert "SHOP NOW" text links to real CTA buttons** — brand-colored, full-width on mobile, with specific copy ("Shop Serums," "Claim Your 25%") rather than the same label repeated five times.
- 4. **Trim or consolidate the three offer blocks** into one tight section: "25% off everything. Spend $100 and get [X]. Spend $150 and get [Y]." Ladder logic is compelling; three disconnected blocks are not.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last chance: 25% off + free full-size gift`
- **Subject Alt B:** `25% off ends Sunday — whole enchilada`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stack your 25% off + unlock a free full-size mask at $100.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The Hydra Kit, the mask, the savings — one more day to grab it all.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Invalid CSS inside `<style>` block — HTML comment syntax in media query**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 600px) {
  <!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
  <!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
HTML comment delimiters (`<!-- -->`) are not valid CSS. These rules are silently dropped by all modern CSS parsers. Any layout rules inside those comments will not apply on mobile.

**[HIGH] `.appleLinks a` suppresses link affordance in Apple Mail**
```css
.appleLinks a {color: #000000 !important; text-decoration: none !important;}
```
This forces all links to black with no underline in Apple Mail — making CTAs visually indistinguishable from body text. Apple Mail is typically the #1 or #2 client by share in beauty verticals.

**[MEDIUM] Duplicate font loading — 6 extra HTTP requests**
Each of the 3 `db.onlinewebfonts.com` fonts is loaded twice: once via `<link>` in `<head>` and again via `@import` inside `<style>`. This doubles round-trips for fonts that most email clients will ignore anyway. Remove the `<link>` tags; keep only `@font-face` declarations.

**[MEDIUM] Universal `line-height: 100%` override**
```css
*{line-height: 100%}
```
Applied after `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; ...}`, this cascades into every element including `<td>`, `<p>`, and `<span>`, potentially collapsing multi-line text. Any per-element `line-height` set later via inline styles will still win (inline beats class), but element-level stylesheet rules will not.

**[MEDIUM] Split font CDN origins**
`BrownRegular` is served from `www.drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/...` (versioned, controlled). `BrownBold`, `BrownLight`, and `BrownThin` are served from `db.onlinewebfonts.com` (third-party, unversioned). A CDN outage or rate-limit on `db.onlinewebfonts.com` silently degrades 3 of 4 weights to system fallback with no error.

**[LOW] `.sm { display:none; }` — no corresponding show rule visible**
This class hides elements globally with no visible media query re-showing them. If `.sm` is used for mobile-only content, it will be permanently hidden unless a `display:block !important` rule exists later in the stylesheet (not visible in truncated source).

**[LOW] `max-height:1000000px` on all elements**
```css
*{max-height:1000000px;}
```
This is a known Gmail clipping workaround (Gmail clips HTML emails exceeding ~102KB). Its presence suggests the email is at or near Gmail's clip threshold — clipped emails show a "View entire message" link and suppress the pixel/open tracking for that portion.
