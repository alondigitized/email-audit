# Lippe lock

## Executive summary

- "Lippe lock" is a tight, single-purpose email: prep-and-repair lip care for National Lipstick Day, built around two SKUs (Lippe Balm and Plump-C Tripeptide Lippe Mask) bracketed by "Pre-Game" and "After-Party" labels. It's the most focused, best-art-directed send in recent memory from this brand — no five-tile grid, no eight-SKU wall like 7/27's "Our top shelf," just one macro beauty shot and two clean product still lifes.
- The top banner is doing double duty as a gift-with-purchase pitch (free 5-piece set at $110+, deluxe sample on $50+ subscriptions) stacked above a completely separate lip-care narrative, and that's the one structural tension in an otherwise clean layout — the offer banner and the "Plump Start" story don't really talk to each other. Compared to 7/25's "SMS SOS" (a list-building unit dressed as a product email) and 7/23's "Feeling sticky?" (a looser three-serum roundup), this is closer in craft to 7/17's "Hot takes" — a real premise, a real before/after arc, consistent visual language.
- Where it loses points is entirely in the inbox moment: "Lippe lock" is a clever pun but tells a recipient nothing concrete, and against a brand sending every 2 days this week, subject-line fatigue is a real risk. Strong body, weak door.

## What's working

- The "Pre-Game" / "After-Party" framing gives the two-product lineup a clear, memorable structure instead of a flat list.
- Hero image (finger on lower lip, teal background) is a genuinely strong, on-brand visual that earns the scroll.
- Each product gets its own full-bleed still life, a name, a one-line benefit, and its own "Shop Now" link — no ambiguity about what to click.
- Top banner surfaces two concrete, dollar-threshold offers (free 5-piece gift at $110+, deluxe sample at $50+ subscription) right at the open.
- Clean render — no overlapping text, no broken images, no lorem ipsum.

## What's weak

- Subject line "Lippe lock" carries zero information — no product, no offer, no urgency — and reads as internal wordplay rather than a reason to open.
- No preview/preheader is visible in this render, so the subject gets no assist from a second line of copy.
- The gift-with-purchase banner and the lip-care story are two different campaigns stapled together; the banner never mentions lips or lipstick day, so it reads as a bolt-on.
- CTA copy is the same generic "Shop Now" three times — nothing ties the button text to what's actually being sold in each section.
- No social proof (ratings, review counts) anywhere, despite this being a repurchase-driven category where "does this actually work" matters.

## Recommendations

- 1. Rewrite the subject to lead with the occasion or benefit instead of the pun alone — pair concept with concrete payoff. The current subject undersells a genuinely strong creative concept.
- 2. Add a preheader that names the free-gift threshold — right now that offer is buried in a banner graphic with no text-based reinforcement in the inbox.
- 3. Either drop the top banner or reframe it around lips specifically (e.g., tie the free gift to the lip routine) so the email reads as one campaign, not two.
- 4. Swap "Shop Now" for product-specific CTA copy ("Shop Lippe Balm," "Shop the Mask") to strengthen scannability and personalization.
- 5. Add a one-line proof point under at least one product (rating stars or "as seen in" / "cult favorite" language) — DE has this kind of equity and isn't using it here.
