# Time to come clean
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- "Time to come clean" arrives as a dual-purpose send: a loyalty club welcome (15% off code + free Babyfacial leave-on mask with $75+ orders) welded onto a full-cleanser lineup editorial. That's a meaningful offer stack — the Babyfacial is Drunk Elephant's hero SKU and the July 8 "20-minute hack" send already proved their audience responds to it. The problem is the offer is compressed into a small welcome box at the top while the body spends all its energy on six products with one-liner descriptions that give the reader zero help choosing between them.
- The hero copy lands well — "WE'RE HERE TO DO YOUR DIRTY WORK" is punchy and the summer grime angle is seasonally correct. But beneath that, the editorial collapses into a rote grid: six cleansers, six "SHOP NOW" text links, and descriptions so thin ("Our gentle cream cleanser," "No rinse to rinse") they read like placeholder copy. No skin-type routing, no key ingredients, no reason to pick Beste No. 9 over Mello Marulo.
- The subject is the biggest miss. This is a welcome-with-offers email — arguably the most valuable send in the lifecycle — and it leads with a pun instead of a benefit. A subscriber staring at their inbox doesn't know there's a 15% off code and a free Babyfacial waiting inside. They just see a riddle.

## What's working

- The welcome box immediately signals something exclusive — club membership + a code + a GWP creates a real reason to scroll
- "WE'RE HERE TO DO YOUR DIRTY WORK" is a genuinely strong hero line; it's specific to the category and seasonally relevant without being stale
- Product lineup is tightly focused — all six items are cleansers, no category drift muddying the editorial concept
- The free Babyfacial GWP is a smart tie-in; it echoes the July 8 "20-minute hack" send and will resonate with engaged subscribers who've seen that email
- Clean, uncluttered visual presentation with consistent product photography throughout

## What's weak

- Subject "Time to come clean" undersells the actual offer payload — a welcome email with a 15% code and a free hero-SKU GWP should lead with those benefits, not a wordplay hook
- The 15% off code (`CRME_CM-P223-2N3-52`) is rendered in small, visually cluttered text inside the welcome box — hard to read and easy to overlook
- Six product descriptions are essentially non-functional: "Our innovative jelly cleanser" tells a buyer nothing about fit, skin type, or use case; "No rinse to rinse" reads like a typo
- No urgency anywhere — no code expiry, no "offer ends" language, nothing to interrupt the scroll-and-forget reflex
- Fifth email in eight days; the July 6 "Beautiful, glowing skin" send already featured cleansers in its two-column grid, making this feel like a repeat

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to lead with the offers** — this is the highest-leverage change. A welcome send with a code and a GWP earns an explicit benefit subject.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Welcome to 15% off — plus a free Babyfacial`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 15% off code + a gift inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Find your cleanser match, then grab your code at checkout`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free Babyfacial leave-on mask with $75+ — while supplies last`
- 2. **Promote the free Babyfacial GWP into the hero** — it's buried in the welcome box as a secondary line; it should have its own visual callout or a dedicated hero module since it's the most emotionally compelling element in the entire email
- 3. **Add skin-type routing to the product grid** — even a single label per product ("Best for: sensitive skin," "Best for: full-face makeup removal") would cut choice paralysis and drive clicks with intent rather than confusion
- 4. **Add code expiry copy** — "Code valid for 7 days" or "Offer ends July 17" creates urgency without pressure; right now the code feels permanent and therefore ignorable
- 5. **Tighten the product descriptions** — rewrite each to lead with the one differentiated benefit ("Removes waterproof SPF without stripping," "The bar for oily skin that never feels tight") rather than the product form factor

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — HTML comments break responsive CSS:**

Inside the `@media only screen and (max-width: 600px)` block, two rules are wrapped in HTML comment syntax:

```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

HTML comments (`<!-- -->`) are not valid CSS. Every email client that processes the stylesheet will silently discard these two rules. The `contenttable` and `mobile` classes will receive **no mobile width overrides**, causing layout breakage at narrow viewports.

**Triple font loading — unnecessary requests:**

BrownBold, BrownLight, and BrownThin are each requested three independent times:
1. `<link href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...">` in `<head>`
2. `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...)` inside `<style>`
3. `@font-face { src: url("https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/t/...") }` in `<style>`

This produces 9 redundant font HTTP requests per render. BrownRegular is self-hosted from `drunkelephant.com` while the other three weights depend on the third-party CDN `db.onlinewebfonts.com` — an availability dependency outside brand control.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

Prevents user-initiated zoom on mobile. This violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA) and is flagged by iOS accessibility audits.

**Universal `line-height: 100%` rule:**

```css
*{line-height: 100%}
```

The `*` selector applies to every element. On multi-line text nodes, `line-height: 100%` resolves to exactly 1× the font-size, producing zero leading between lines. This typically renders body copy as visually collapsed or overlapping in clients that honor `line-height` strictly (Outlook, Apple Mail).

**`.sm { display:none; }` without visible show rule:**

A hide-on-desktop class is declared but no corresponding `display:block !important` override appears in either media query block in the visible source. If `.sm` elements are meant to surface on mobile, the show rule may be missing or further down the truncated source.
