# Save on smoothies (15% off!)
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- This is a split-purpose send that can't decide what it wants to be. The top banner leads with a 15% off coupon — a real, actionable offer — but then hands off to a brand-story block ("mixing is the new") about smoothie-inspired skincare philosophy, and then pivots again to a birthday data-capture ask. Three different emails in one, and none of them fully land.
- The smoothie/mixing metaphor is on-brand for Drunk Elephant and the visual is clean, but the email never connects the discount to any specific product. There's no hero product shot, no "here's what to mix, here's what it costs, here's what you save." The 15% off is left floating with a promo code and zero product context below it.
- The birthday section at the bottom feels bolted on — a loyalty mechanic buried below the fold after an already unfocused body. Net result: a recipient who came in for the 15% discount leaves without knowing what to buy.

## What's working

- **Promo code is front and center** — `CRMD_EW-P2Z3-Z3L5-S2` is displayed in the banner, easy to copy, no hunting required.
- **Clean layout, no render bugs** — text is legible, no overlapping elements, images load correctly.
- **Brand voice is consistent** — the smoothie analogy fits Drunk Elephant's established "biocompatible, mixable" positioning.
- **Color palette is immediately recognizable** — yellow, pink, and teal match Drunk Elephant's signature look; brand recognition is instant.

## What's weak

- **No product appears anywhere** — 15% off is a strong offer, but the body never shows what you'd actually buy; the CTA "MIX IT UP" goes to the homepage, not a curated collection.
- **The birthday section is below the fold and off-topic** — asking for birth date after pitching a discount dilutes both messages and makes the email feel disjointed.
- **"Mixing is the new" headline is incomplete** — the phrase trails off ("mixing is the new [blank]"), which reads like an editorial slide, not an email headline. Confusing at a glance.
- **No urgency on the discount** — fine print says expires in 30 days, but nothing in the hero reinforces that deadline to drive action now.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one hero product (or curated set) between the code and the CTA.** Show the Beste No. 9, the Marula Oil, or a "starter smoothie" kit — something to buy. The offer has no object right now.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA from "MIX IT UP" to something specific** — "Shop the Smoothie Kit," "Start Mixing — 15% Off," anything with a noun.
- 3. **Cut the birthday section or give it its own send.** It earns nothing here and makes the email feel like two campaigns stapled together.
- 4. **Bring the expiration date into the hero** — "Offer ends June 12" in the banner converts wanderers into buyers.
- 5. **Rewrite the section headline** — "Mixing is the new" is not a complete thought at a glance. Try: "Mix like a pro — here's where to start."
