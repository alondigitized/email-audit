# 15% off looks good on you
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-21**
## Executive summary

- This is a Subscribe & Save push dressed up as a discount email — the 15% headline is real, but it's contingent on committing to two-or-more recurring subscriptions, not a straightforward "15% off your order" like the subject implies. The banner at top does disclose the mechanic, but the hero copy ("Running on empty? Not so much") and the 5-step how-to-save walkthrough are doing a lot of work to sell a commitment product as a discount. It's a clear escalation from the brand's countdown-timer "SECRET SALE" pattern (2026-09-08, 2026-08-31) into a retention/LTV play, which tracks with 2026-09-17's "Hydrated skin" and 2026-09-15's product-education tone — Drunk Elephant seems to be cycling away from urgency-discount sends toward habit-building and cross-sell (the Kamo Drops insert, the "you're gonna like these guys" grid).
- The layout is clean, on-brand, and legible — dark banner, clear hero, clear CTA button repeated twice, then a tidy step-by-step, then a secondary product push, then a social-proof grid. No render bugs. But it's a busy email trying to do three jobs (subscription pitch, Kamo cross-sell, best-seller grid) and the actual "15% off" offer gets diluted by the second half of the email pivoting entirely off-topic to Kamo.
- Judgement: solid execution, muddled offer. The subject overpromises simplicity the email doesn't deliver, and the back half undercuts its own subscription CTA by dragging attention to an unrelated product launch.

## What's working

- Clean visual hierarchy: dark disclosure banner → bold headline → single CTA button, easy to scan on open.
- CTA button ("SUBSCRIBE TO SAVE") is repeated twice, unambiguous copy, high contrast.
- The 5-step "How to save" section demystifies subscription mechanics simply, including a light joke at step 5 that fits the brand voice.
- Social proof grid at the bottom (star ratings + "X people are checking this out") adds credibility and gives multiple product entry points.
- Fine print/terms are present and specific (dates, exclusions) rather than buried or vague.

## What's weak

- Subject says "15% off looks good on you" — a blanket-sounding discount — but the actual offer requires subscribing to 2+ products, a much bigger ask than the subject implies (bait-and-switch risk).
- The email splits focus: it pivots hard into a Kamo Drops cross-sell ("HAVE YOU KAMO'D YET?") that has nothing to do with the subscription offer, diluting the primary CTA.
- No visible product being discounted — the hero image shows five different SKUs, so there's no single hero product to anchor the click.
- "10% off and free shipping on every order" appears in step 4 while the headline banner says 15% — the two numbers sit close together and could read as inconsistent at a glance (10% is the standing subscription rate, 15% is the promo, but the juxtaposition invites confusion).

## Recommendations

- 1. Align subject and offer: either lead with "Subscribe to 2+, save 15%" so expectations match the ask, or restructure the email so a flat 15% single-order sale is actually available — right now there's a mismatch between promise and mechanism.
- 2. Cut the Kamo Drops section entirely or move it to a separate send — it competes with the subscription CTA instead of reinforcing it.
- 3. Add a single hero SKU (e.g., Protini, the top grid performer) instead of a five-product lineup shot, so there's a clear "click this" target.
- 4. Clarify the 10%-vs-15% figures — bold or restate "15%" in step 4 instead of letting "10%" sit unexplained next to the headline claim.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your skin barrier called. It wants a subscription.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Two or more subscriptions = 15% off your first delivery.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `No countdown, no code — just set it and save.`
