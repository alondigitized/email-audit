# Introducing Kamo Drops!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-11**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuine new-product-launch email, and it's the strongest kind of send this brand can do — new SKU, clear before-you-scroll hero, one unambiguous "SHOP KAMO" CTA repeated three times. Kamo Drops is positioned as a four-in-one (concealer/foundation/tinted moisturizer/firming) product, and the copy does real work explaining *how* to use it rather than just naming it, which is more useful than the ingredient-hero-metaphor treatment from "The secret weapon" (2026-08-09).
- The top banner is doing double duty as a distraction, though — "Free Deluxe Sample with new subscription orders of $50+" competes with the hero for attention in the first 100px, and it's not even about the product being introduced. It's a good offer, just poorly sequenced ahead of the launch reveal.
- Compared to "Something big..." (2026-08-03, 9/10), which built anticipation for an unnamed launch, this is the actual payoff — but it undersells itself by not repeating shade range (25 shades) or the "undetectable 12-hour" claim anywhere past the hero. A solid, on-brand send; not a top-tier one because the offer stacking (sample + travel bag + sample card, three different thresholds) muddies an otherwise clean launch story.

## What's working

- Single, unmistakable hero: "INTRODUCING KAMO DROPS!" with product shot and immediate benefit copy (25 shades, 12-hour coverage, firms skin).
- "SHOP KAMO" CTA repeats three times with consistent styling — no ambiguity about what to do next.
- The "dab/apply/mix" explainer section does real product education, which past sends (like the retinol roundup) sometimes skipped in favor of metaphor.
- Clean close-up model shot demonstrating concealer application under the eye — concrete, not just packaging on a pedestal.
- "Try This At Home" sample-card CTA lowers the risk of trying a new format (25 shades is a lot to commit to blind).

## What's weak

- The top gray banner (free deluxe sample, $50+ subscription) has nothing to do with Kamo Drops and steals the first visual beat from the actual launch.
- Three different offer thresholds are live at once ($50+ subscription sample, $110+ sample card + bag) — no single clear "the offer" for this email.
- No price is shown anywhere for Kamo Drops itself, so there's no way to gauge cost before clicking.
- No social proof, reviews, or "bestseller"/"new" badge treatment despite this being a launch — nothing signals early buzz or credibility for a totally new format.
- Legal fine print block at the bottom is dense and covers two unrelated promos, which reads as offer-stacking rather than one clean deal.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut or shrink the top banner, or move it below the Kamo Drops CTAs — let the launch own the first screen entirely.
- 2. Pick one offer to feature (the sample card + travel bag ties directly to Kamo Drops; the subscription sample doesn't) and drop or footnote the other.
- 3. Add a price or "starting at $X" near the hero CTA — asking someone to commit to a new 4-in-1 format without a price is friction.
- 4. Add a shade-range visual (swatch strip of the 25 shades) — "25 shades" as text alone doesn't convey the breadth that's actually the product's biggest differentiator.
- 5. Consider a "New" or "Just Launched" badge on the hero to signal novelty/urgency beyond the headline text.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Kamo Drops: one product, 25 shades, 4 jobs`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New: the concealer that's also your foundation`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Meet the drop that firms, covers, and blends into anything.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `25 shades. 12-hour wear. One bottle.`
