# The secret weapon
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-09**
## Executive summary

- "The secret weapon" is an ingredient-education email dressed up as a product roundup — retinol as "superhero," three SKUs as "fighters" you get to "choose." It's a clean, well-photographed send with real texture shots (the golden A-Gloei swirl is genuinely nice), but the actual news — 20% off via SheerID for teachers/students/military/first responders — is buried in a small gray banner at the very top and never mentioned again. This is the same SheerID mechanic as the 8/5 "Well, hi, 20% off" send, just recycled two days later with a retinol-education wrapper instead of a general discount pitch.
- The core problem: this reads as a 0.5%/1.0%/0.1% retinol-concentration explainer for people who already know their routine, not a compelling reason to open or click for someone browsing on their commute. It's competent but low-urgency, and the exclusionary SheerID gate caps who it's even relevant to.

## What's working

- Clear three-tier "choose your fighter" structure makes concentration differences (1.0% / 0.5% / 0.1%) scannable at a glance.
- Product photography is strong — the gold marula-oil swirl and cream texture shots add visual interest without clutter.
- "Pro tip" about sunlight/SPF pairing adds real educational value, not just sales copy.
- Clean render, no layout bugs, no broken images.

## What's weak

- The 20% off SheerID offer is the only concrete discount in the email and it's crammed into one dense gray banner up top, never reinforced near a CTA.
- Subject "The secret weapon" is vague and gives zero indication this is about retinol or a discount — pure curiosity-bait with no payoff hint.
- No visible product pricing, and the "SHOP NOW" / "SHOP" CTAs are generic verbs with no product name attached.
- Same discount mechanic (SheerID, 20% up from 15%) as the 8/5 send just four days ago — repetitive offer structure for anyone who opened both.
- Retinol strength framing ("Our strongest," "gentler option") assumes the reader already knows what they need — no guidance for someone new to retinol who doesn't know which "fighter" to pick.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the offer, not the metaphor — move "20% off for teachers/students/military/healthcare" into the hero headline instead of a small banner, since it's the only hard discount in the email.
- 2. Rename subject to hint at the actual content (retinol) and the eligibility hook, since "secret weapon" tells the reader nothing.
- 3. Add a one-line "not sure which one? start here" decision guide above the three products for retinol newcomers.
- 4. Repeat the SheerID discount as a small reminder line under the final "SHOP RETINOL" button so it isn't only visible once at the top.
- 5. Add price points next to each SKU — a discount-driven email with no visible prices adds friction to the click decision.
