# Hey! Grab your Littles 8.0!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- After fourteen consecutive Friends & Family sale emails, Drunk Elephant breaks the blitz with a clean product launch for the Littles 8.0 kit. The tonal shift is genuinely welcome — countdown timers and fake-deadline urgency are gone, replaced by a lifestyle headline, a $65 price anchor, and a full AM/PM routine walkthrough. For a subscriber who survived all fourteen prior sends, this reads like a reset.
- The AM/PM narrative structure is the email's best idea. It turns a product listing into a "here's how your day works" story — a rare piece of genuine utility in a brand known for promo cadence chaos. The 15% Off Club code visible in the green-bordered banner at the top adds a loyalty layer without overwhelming the product message.
- The miss: this landed **one day** after "Final hours for 25% off!" There's no air between the sale blitz and the product launch, so the implicit message is "we just wring you for sale urgency and immediately pivoted to full-price product." The subject line also does nothing — it names the product but gives zero reason to open.

## What's working

- **Tonal reset is real.** "Go anywhere, do everything." is a lifestyle pitch, not a sale pitch — exactly the right gear-shift after 14 promotional emails.
- **Price is anchored clearly.** The "$65" tag adjacent to "That's the Littles 8.0." removes all ambiguity about what this costs.
- **AM/PM structure gives the email utility.** The labeled routine (B-HYDRA to hydrate, to brighten, to moisturize and firm) teaches the subscriber how to use the kit — that's editorial, not just commerce.
- **15% Off Club code in the top banner** is a personalized loyalty touch that rewards subscribers without dominating the creative.
- **"SUMMER SKIN, SORTED"** seasonal hook at the bottom is timely (May 30) and provides a coherent narrative close.

## What's weak

- **Subject line names the product, offers nothing else.** "Hey! Grab your Littles 8.0!" gives a returning subscriber no reason to open that they don't already have.
- **Zero urgency or scarcity anywhere in the body.** For a product launch email there's no "new this season," "limited quantities," or even a "just launched" timestamp.
- **"Spend $100+" section is buried.** That bottom banner with the promotional threshold reads like fine print — it's almost unreadable at thumbnail size and disconnected from the hero offer.
- **No social proof for a new product.** A launch email is exactly when a star rating or a brief review pull-quote would do real work — there's none here.
- **One-day gap after the sale blitz.** Sending a full-price product email the morning after "Final hours for 25% off!" compresses subscriber goodwill with no recovery room.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to sell the benefit, not the SKU name.** The product name means nothing to someone who doesn't already own Littles. Lead with the outcome.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your whole summer routine, in one kit`
- **Subject Alt B:** `8 products. $65. Done.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Meet Littles 8.0 — AM + PM covered.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The only kit your face needs all summer.`
- 2. **Add a launch signal.** Even a one-line "Just dropped — Littles 8.0 is here" callout in the hero gives the email a news hook and justifies the send after the sale blitz.
- 3. **Surface the Spend $100+ promotion as a real visible section** with a clear threshold, not buried in the bottom fine-print area. If it's a meaningful perk, treat it like one.
- 4. **Add one social proof element.** A 4.8★ rating or a single customer pull-quote ("I haven't needed anything else all summer") inside the routine section would close the credibility loop for new buyers.
- 5. **Wait 2–3 days before sending.** Let the sale blitz breathe. The Littles launch lands harder when it isn't fighting the memory of 14 consecutive discount urgency emails.

## Technical Audit — Drunk Elephant "Grab your Littles 8.0!"

### 1. Technical Summary

XHTML 1.0 Transitional with VML/Outlook compatibility markup. Three confirmed structural defects: HTML comment syntax embedded inside `<style>` CSS blocks (dead rules), triple-redundant font loading, and a zoom-blocking viewport meta tag.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**No UTM parameters observed** on any href in the available source. All CTA links appear to point to bare `drunkelephant.com` URLs with no `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, or `utm_campaign` parameters — unconfirmable in full without the complete source, but none are present in the truncated portion.

**Sending domain mismatch**: `From` header uses `cheers.drunkelephant.com`, while body links resolve to `www.drunkelephant.com`. No issues if SPF/DKIM cover the subdomain, but continuity should be verified (see §5).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — Dead CSS rules (invalid HTML comments inside `<style>`):**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 600px) {
  <!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
  <!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
}
```
HTML comment syntax (`<!-- -->`) inside a `<style>` block is not valid CSS. These two responsive-width rules will never apply. Affected classes `contenttable` and `mobile` will not resize on 600px-or-narrower viewports.

**Accessibility — Zoom disabled:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents user-initiated zoom. This violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA) and is blocked by default in iOS 16+.

**Accessibility — Text size adjust suppressed:**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}
```
Value should be `100%`, not `none`. `none` disables the OS-level font scaling that low-vision users rely on, and `*{}` applies it globally.

**Performance — Triple font loading:**
The Brown font family is loaded three ways simultaneously:
1. `<link href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/...">` (3 separate link tags)
2. `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/...)` inside `<style>`
3. `@font-face` blocks also inside `<style>`

This produces 6+ redundant HTTP requests per render. Consolidate to `@font-face` only (most compatible in email) and drop the `<link>` and `@import` declarations.

**Third-party font CDN risk:** `BrownBold`, `BrownLight`, and `BrownThin` are sourced from `db.onlinewebfonts.com`, a third-party service with no SLA. `BrownRegular` is correctly self-hosted on `drunkelephant.com`. All variants should be self-hosted.
