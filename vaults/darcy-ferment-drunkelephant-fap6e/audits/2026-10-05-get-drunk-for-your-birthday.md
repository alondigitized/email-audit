# Get Drunk for your birthday!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-05**
## Executive summary

- This is a data-capture email, not a sales email. Drunk Elephant wants a birthday so it can send a gift later, and the hero says so: "Give us your birthday digits." The creative is strong. The yellow "birthday" lettering, the orange-to-aqua split and the stacked product tower are on-brand and easy to scan. The single yellow "Share Your Birthday" button is unmistakable.
- The weakness is that the email gives nothing concrete in return. "A special surprise to celebrate you when the time comes" has no percentage, dollar amount, sample or free-shipping hook. The recipient is asked for personal data in exchange for a mystery. It also lands the day after "A flood of hydration" (10/4, which had a real "$90 VALUE: $143" anchor) and a run of kit pushes, so it reads as a pivot with no payoff.
- Verdict: a polished lifecycle ask that will earn modest clicks from loyalists. It underperforms because the value exchange is vague, and the subject line's wordplay on the brand name carries most of the interest.

## What's working

- The hero is a clear stack: "Give us your birthday digits" → product tower → one button. The eye reaches the CTA without detours.
- The "Share Your Birthday" button is high-contrast yellow on aqua, with a verb-plus-noun label that says exactly what happens next.
- The multi-product tower shows the range in the brand's colors, and no product is crowded out.
- The layout is clean, with no broken images or overlapping text, and the footer is tidy and standard.
- The "drunk" wordplay is consistent with the brand's voice, which makes the email recognizable at a glance.

## What's weak

- There is no concrete offer. "A special surprise" is the whole incentive, which makes it a weak reason to hand over personal data.
- The CTA sits below the full product image, so on mobile the button is a long scroll away from the headline.
- No product in the tower is named or linked, so there is no shopping path for someone who isn't ready to share a birthday.
- The email never says when the surprise arrives or what form it takes ("when the time comes").
- The yellow outlined "birthday" and the white highlight bar behind "drunk for your birthday" look slightly rough, but they read as intentional styling, not bugs.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name the gift.** Say "Get a free deluxe mini + 15% off during your birthday month." This single change lifts both clicks and data capture.
- 2. Move the button above the product image, or add a second CTA there, so it appears in the first mobile screen.
- 3. Add one line of reassurance on how the birthday is used ("Only month and day, never your year") to lower the friction of sharing.
- 4. Tag two or three products in the hero image to give browsers a path.
- 5. Tie the send to the holiday kit push from 10/4 and 9/27 so the cadence feels like a sequence, not a pivot.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your birthday gift is waiting`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Tell us your birthday, get a free gift`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Share your month and day and we'll send a surprise worth unwrapping.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `A free deluxe mini plus birthday-month savings, on us.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a Salesforce-style triggered birthday send (`US_DE_Trigger_BirthdayAcquisition`) with several confirmed code-level defects: insecure `http://` images, a `viewport` tag that blocks zoom, a missing `lang` attribute, and the recipient's email address exposed in a tracking-pixel URL. The unsubscribe and authentication checks could not be verified because of relay and capture limits, not because they were shown to be absent.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Tracking links not verified:** 10 links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are click-redirect domains. Final destinations, redirect chains and 404s are unconfirmed.
- **Insecure image sources (3):** the social icons load over `http://` from `image.cheers.drunkelephant.com/lib/fe3211717064057c751c78/m/1/`:
  - Facebook: `cc02d07d-…png`
  - Instagram: `9b452915-…png`
  - Tiktok: `29f975e1-…png`
  - Clients that block mixed content, or proxy images over HTTPS, may drop them. This is a real defect, and the fix is changing the scheme to `https://`.
- **PII in tracking URL:** the pixel at `https://drunkelephantus.everestengagement.com/ea/dKhpn7WOgU/?e=darcy-ferment-drunkelephant-fap6e@etell.app&c=US_DE_Trigg…` puts the recipient's email address in plaintext in the query string. It will appear in third-party server logs, and in proxy and Referer logs.
- **Second tracking layer:** `mi.drunkelephant.com/p/up/80acaf1c74f9e002/o.gif?mi_u=98161986&mi_ecmp=US_DE_Trigger_BirthdayAcquisition_Custom_…` is an open pixel with a numeric user ID (`mi_u`). Together with the Everest pixel, the email carries two or more separate tracking beacons, which adds third-party calls and load points.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />`. `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-zoom, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **Missing `lang`:** the root `<html xmlns=… >` has no `lang` attribute, which screen readers use to pick pronunciation.
- **Missing alt text (2):** `o.gif` and the Everest pixel have no `alt`. Both are tracking pixels, so this is low severity. Add `alt=""` and `width="1" height="1"` so they are treated as decorative.
- **Invalid `@import` placement:** the `<style>` block puts `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/…)` after the `body{…}` rule. Per the CSS spec, `@import` must come first, so these three imports are ignored. The `<link>` tags to the same fonts are also stripped by Gmail and by most clients that remove `<head>` links.
- **Fonts fail over silently:** the `@font-face` rules point to the third-party `db.onlinewebfonts.com` and a version-pinned Demandware path (`…/v1719565389232/fonts/BrownRegular.*`). If either goes down or the version path changes, the email falls back to system fonts. I can't confirm from the truncated source that fallback font stacks are declared.
- **Odd mobile CSS:** `.vouchertext4` and `.fsname` set `max-width:15px` and `max-height:12px–15px` with `!important` at ≤600px. Combined with `width:17px !important`, this is likely to clip text on mobile. Confirm with a render test.
- **HTML comments inside `<style>`:** `<!-- *[class=contenttable] … -->` blocks sit inside the style block. They are harmless in most clients, but some sanitizers choke on them.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No unresolved merge tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`) appear in the visible HTML. The recipient email was resolved correctly in the Everest pixel URL (see PII note in Section 2). The remainder of the HTML was truncated and was not reviewed.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` not found:** QA flagged this, but notes the header may not be captured by the AgentMail relay. Treat it as unconfirmed, not as a violation.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` not found:** RFC 8058 one-click support is also unconfirmed, for the same reason. This matters for Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender requirements.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found:** SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment are unknown because the relay doesn't expose the header. The sender is `info@cheers.drunkelephant.com`, a subdomain aligned with the brand's image host `image.cheers.drunkelephant.com`, which is consistent with proper setup. That is circumstantial, not proof.
- **Not verifiable:** the in-body unsubscribe link and physical postal address are not in the truncated source, so they could not be assessed.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **UTM parameters:** none could be confirmed. All 10 click links were skipped by the probe, and the truncated source shows no CTA `href`s.
- **Campaign tagging in pixels:** `mi_ecmp=US_DE_Trigger_BirthdayAcquisition_Custom_…` and `c=US_DE_Trigg…` show a campaign identifier on the tracking pixels, but the values are cut off. The full strings and any `utm_*` on the CTAs can't be compared.
- **Landing-page alignment:** unverified because redirects weren't followed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Change the three social icon URLs on `image.cheers.drunkelephant.com` from `http://` to `https://`.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Add `lang="en"` to `<html>`.
4. Stop passing the plaintext recipient email (`e=…`) in the Everest pixel URL. Use a hashed or opaque ID instead.
5. Add `alt=""`, `width="1"` and `height="1"` to the `o.gif` and Everest tracking pixels.
6. Move the `@import` rules to the top of the `<style>` block, or drop them, and declare web-safe fallback stacks.
7. Re-run the audit against the full raw MIME message to confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results`.
8. Resolve the 10 tracking links with a redirect-following check to verify final destinations and `utm_*` continuity.
9. Test mobile rendering of `.vouchertext4` and `.fsname` for clipping.
