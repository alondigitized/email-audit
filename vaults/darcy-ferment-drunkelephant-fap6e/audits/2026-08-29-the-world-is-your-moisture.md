# The world is your moisture
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-29**
## Executive summary

- This is Drunk Elephant doing what it does best: a clean, catalog-style "cheat sheet" that walks through four moisturizers with product shots, a one-line skin-type callout, and clear hierarchy. Compared to the brand's recent Kamo Drops barrage (five sends in three weeks per the history), this is a genuine palate cleanser — no clinical-claims theater, no repeated hype cycle, just "here's what moisturizer is right for you," which is a legitimately useful email format.
- The free deluxe sample with $50+ subscription orders is a real, specific offer sitting right at the top in a banner, and the product breakdown (Protini for fine lines, Lala Retro for all skin types, Bora Barrier for dry/compromised skin, Marula Oil for glow) does actual skin-type segmentation work that most catalog blasts skip. The footer still can't resist wedging in a Kamo Drops pitch, but it's appropriately small this time, consistent with the 08-24 "Summer with benefits" pivot away from Kamo-as-hero.
- This lands as a solid, above-average execution for the brand — informative, well-organized, on-brand — but it's not a strong open/click driver for someone not already actively shopping moisturizers, since the subject and preview undersell the concrete offer sitting in the banner.

## What's working

- Clear visual hierarchy: hero banner offer → "Moisturizer Cheat Sheet" headline → four-product hero shot → CTA button, in that order.
- Each product gets a distinct, skin-type-specific callout (fine lines/peptides, all skin types/ceramide, dry/compromised barrier, glow/antioxidants) — genuinely useful differentiation, not generic copy.
- Two "Shop Moisturizers" CTA buttons (top and after the product breakdown) give multiple exit points without being pushy.
- No render bugs — images are crisp, text is legible, alternating layout reads cleanly on scroll.
- Fine print at the bottom is specific and dated (subscription window, Kamo code terms) rather than vague legalese.

## What's weak

- The subject line "The world is your moisture" is a pun that doesn't hint at the actual offer (free deluxe sample) or the actual content (a 4-product moisturizer guide) — it's mood copy, not information.
- The hero banner offer (free deluxe sample, $50+ subscription) is only referenced in tiny header text — the big pink/black headline space is spent on "Moisturizer Cheat Sheet," not the discount.
- The Kamo Drops footer plug feels bolted on and slightly incoherent — a tinted moisturizer pitch dropped into what was otherwise a pure-moisturizer educational email.
- No visible loyalty/member pricing or social proof (reviews, ratings) anywhere in the body despite four products being pitched.

## Recommendations

- 1. Rewrite the subject/preview to lead with the concrete offer ("free deluxe sample") since that's the strongest, most specific hook in the email — puns don't out-convert specificity for this list.
- 2. Pull the "$50+ subscription = free deluxe sample" offer into the hero visual itself (currently it's small banner text above the big pun headline) so it's the first thing the eye catches, not the second.
- 3. Add a one-line "which one is right for me?" quiz-style CTA near the top, since the email is already structured as a skin-type decision tree — make that structure explicit and clickable.
- 4. Move or cut the Kamo Drops footer block; it dilutes an otherwise focused moisturizer email and contradicts the "here's your cheat sheet" framing.
