# Grab 20% Off Before It’s Gone!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-08**
## Executive summary

- This is a SheerID professional verification play — Drunk Elephant is offering 20% off (bumped from their standard 15%) for teachers, first responders, military members, healthcare professionals, and students. The creative is clean and on-brand: the headline "IT SERVES YOU RIGHT." does genuine double-duty, nodding to service orientation while promising the product will deliver. The timing is notable: this lands 11 days after a 14-email Friends & Family blitz that peaked at 25% off. Any subscriber who lived through that run will immediately clock that 20% is a lesser discount.
- The structural problem is the subject line. "Grab 20% Off Before It's Gone!" reads as a general promotional offer, but the body gates the discount behind professional verification that the majority of subscribers will not qualify for. That's a broken promise at the inbox level — the subject overpromises, the body narrows. A subscriber without an eligible job title will feel misled after opening.
- The email itself, for a qualifying recipient, is well-executed. The three-step verification flow is clear, the category buttons are easy to identify, and there are no render issues. But broadcasting this to a general list without segmentation undermines what could be a genuinely valued loyalty perk for those who qualify.

## What's working

- **Headline copywriting**: "IT SERVES YOU RIGHT." is a smart double-entendre — serves the professional (service orientation) and serves the skin. Distinctly on-brand wit.
- **Visual layout**: Clean hero with model + headline, then a numbered three-step process, then segmented category buttons. The eye moves naturally top to bottom without confusion.
- **Concrete benefit framing**: The "bumped from 15% to 20%" framing makes the offer feel elevated and time-sensitive, not evergreen.
- **Category button UX**: Five discrete CTAs (Teacher, Student, Medical Professional, Military, First Responder) remove ambiguity entirely — you know exactly which to click.
- **Clean render**: No broken images, overlapping text, or layout issues visible.

## What's weak

- **Subject-to-body mismatch**: "Grab 20% Off Before It's Gone!" implies a general sale. The body immediately reveals it's gated behind professional verification. Non-qualifying subscribers feel baited.
- **F&F comparison problem**: 20% off against the fresh memory of 25% Friends & Family pricing — concluded just 10 days ago per brand history — makes this feel like a downgrade, not a benefit.
- **Verification friction**: The SheerID flow inserts a proof step before any shopping can happen. For a casually interested subscriber, that extra barrier is a meaningful drop-off point.
- **No product shown**: Unlike recent editorial sends ("Hydrated skin," "Pre-bedtime plan"), there's no hero product to anchor the skincare story or give a verified subscriber a reason to shop immediately after clicking.
- **The best benefit is buried**: The monthly recurring discount code is the most compelling part of this program — it's more valuable than a one-time deal — but it's tucked into step 3 of a bulleted list.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the subject line** — narrow it to the actual audience. A misleading general-sale subject erodes trust and attracts opens from people who will immediately bounce; lower open rate with better intent is a cleaner outcome.
- 2. **Lead with the monthly recurring benefit** — "get a new discount code every month" belongs in the hero, not step 3. That's the structural advantage over a temporary sale.
- 3. **Add a product anchor below the verification steps** — a 3-up product grid gives verified subscribers something to shop immediately instead of ending the email after process explanation.
- 4. **Segment the send** — this should only hit subscribers who have flagged a qualifying profession or have engaged with similar segmented content. Broad sends for gated offers train the list to distrust subject lines.
- 5. **Address the F&F hangover explicitly** — give qualifying subscribers a reason why ongoing 20% beats a one-time 25%: it's theirs every month, indefinitely.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Do you serve? Here's 20% off — every month.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Teachers, nurses, military: your ongoing 20% is waiting`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Verified through SheerID — a new discount code lands each month, for as long as you qualify.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `We bumped it from 15% to 20% for a limited time. Yours to keep, every month.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] HTML comment tokens inside `<style>` block**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 600px) {
  <!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
  <!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
`<!-- -->` delimiters inside a CSS block are invalid. Modern parsers treat this as raw CSS text, not a comment. These two rules will never apply on mobile, silently breaking `contenttable` and `mobile` class width overrides for subscribers under 600px.

**[HIGH] Duplicate font load — `<link>` + `@import` for same URLs**
Three `<link>` tags and three `@import` statements in `<style>` reference identical `db.onlinewebfonts.com` URLs for Brown Bold, Brown-Light, and Brown-Thin. This doubles HTTP requests for the same assets on every render. Example:
- `<link href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/4150d76a852e1c349e0b6fe15f29d9ca?family=Brown+Bold">`
- `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/4150d76a852e1c349e0b6fe15f29d9ca?family=Brown+Bold);`

**[MEDIUM] Third-party font CDN dependency (`db.onlinewebfonts.com`)**
All Brown typeface variants load from `db.onlinewebfonts.com`, a public font redistribution service. Corporate email gateways and proxy-heavy environments routinely block requests to unlisted CDNs. The "Brown" typeface is a commercially licensed font (Lineto Fonts); hosting it via this CDN is likely a licensing violation. First-party `drunkelephant.com/demandware.static` sources exist in the `@font-face` block for BrownRegular only — other weights should follow the same pattern.

**[MEDIUM] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users from pinch-zooming on mobile. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, AA). This is particularly relevant for an audience reading product ingredient details.

**[MEDIUM] `.appleLinks` suppresses all link visibility in Apple Mail**
```css
.appleLinks a {color: #000000 !important; text-decoration: none !important;}
```
Any element wrapped in `.appleLinks` will render its links as black, undecorated text in Apple Mail — visually indistinguishable from body copy. If CTAs or promo code links fall inside this wrapper, they will be invisible to Apple Mail subscribers (a significant share of mobile opens).

**[LOW] Global `line-height: 100%` reset**
```css
* { line-height: 100% }
```
Applying `line-height: 100%` to `*` overrides proper leading on all text elements. In Outlook, `100%` line-height on `<td>` elements can cause text overlap at larger font sizes.

**[LOW] Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>` tag**
No `lang="en"` visible on the `<html>` element. Affects screen reader language detection and client-side spell-checking.
