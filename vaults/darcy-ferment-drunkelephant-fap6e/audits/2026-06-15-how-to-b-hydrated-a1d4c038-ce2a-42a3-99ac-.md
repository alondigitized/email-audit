# How to B hydrated
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- "How to B hydrated" is a B-Hydra editorial — hydration benefits upfront, product statistics in the middle, application instructions at the close. The structure is logical and the Drunk Elephant aesthetic is clean throughout. Two SHOP NOW buttons provide clear off-ramps. The email does its job as a serum explainer.
- The problem is context. Drunk Elephant sent a B-Hydra editorial just two days ago ("Smooth, hydrated, and radiant," June 13, scored 8/10). That email leaned into ingredient storytelling; this one leans into clinical stats and how-to framing. The differentiation is real, but a subscriber who just read the June 13 B-Hydra send is going to feel the repetition before they feel the value.
- The secondary problem is the preheader. The first readable text in the render is the "BABY NEWS" BabyFacial cross-promotion — a completely different product with its own purchase requirement. Inbox clients will pull that into preview, so the subject says "hydration" and the preview says "updated BabyFacial formula." The seam is jarring and wastes prime inbox real estate.

## What's working

- **Hero hierarchy is immediate.** "HYDRA POWER" lands first; the benefit copy under it is sharp and short; SHOP NOW follows naturally.
- **Social proof is specific.** 97% / 93% / 93% stats with an explicit "29 people after 8 weeks" disclosure are more credible than vague "dermatologist-tested" language.
- **Application instructions close the loop.** "Mix with any Drunk Elephant serum, oil, or cream" is actionable and subtly cross-sells the broader line without a hard push.
- **No render bugs.** Layout, imagery, and text are all clean across the full render.

## What's weak

- **Second B-Hydra email in three days.** The June 13 send ("Smooth, hydrated, and radiant") already covered this product editorially. The cadence makes this feel like filler, not a new reason to buy.
- **Preheader misfire.** The BabyFacial BABY NEWS banner becomes the inbox preview text, putting an unrelated GWP cross-sell ahead of the hydration story.
- **No price, no offer.** The email drives to "SHOP NOW" without revealing what B-Hydra costs or any incentive to click today over tomorrow.
- **"Clinically proven" headline vs. "consumer perception study" body copy.** The email opens with a clinical efficacy claim, then walks it back to a 29-person perception study. The disclosure is there, but the gap creates a subtle trust dent.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader / BABY NEWS placement.** Move the BabyFacial cross-sell to a footer or mid-email module — don't let it colonize the inbox preview. Write a real preheader that extends the subject's hydration promise.
- 2. **Retire the B-Hydra editorial slot for at least a week.** Two editorial sends on the same product in three days dilutes both. If the send must go out, hook it to a new peg — a limited bundle, the GWP deadline, or a comparison angle.
- 3. **Add a price or incentive.** Even "starting at $XX" or "subscribe & save 10%" turns a content email into a conversion email. The June 11 Subscribe to Save announcement already set that anchor — use it.
- 4. **Reconcile "clinically proven" language.** Either use only the perception-study framing consistently, or cite a peer-reviewed study that earns the clinical claim. Pick one and commit.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**A. Triple font-loading (performance/reliability)**

Each of the three Brown font variants is loaded via three separate mechanisms simultaneously:

1. `<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...">` in `<head>`
2. `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...)` inside `<style>`
3. `@font-face { src: url("https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/t/...") }` also inside `<style>`

This is 9 redundant network requests per render. `db.onlinewebfonts.com` is an unlicensed third-party font mirror — it has documented uptime issues and serves fonts without verified licensing. The self-hosted copies at `drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/...` are authoritative; the `db.onlinewebfonts.com` references should be removed entirely.

**B. CSS rules silently disabled by HTML comment syntax**

Inside the `@media only screen and (max-width: 600px)` block:

```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

HTML comment delimiters (`<!--` / `-->`) are not valid CSS syntax. These rules are parsed as malformed and **never applied** in any client. If `contenttable` and `mobile` classes exist in the HTML body, their mobile widths are silently broken.

**C. Viewport blocks user zoom — accessibility failure**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom on iOS/Android. This violates WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is flagged as an accessibility defect by Apple's own guidelines for email rendering in Mail.app.

**D. Aggressive text-size override**

```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;}
```

The value `none` (not `100%`) completely disables iOS automatic text adjustment. Users who have increased system font size for accessibility will see no benefit. Should be `100%`.

**E. Global `line-height: 100%` on `*`**

```css
*{line-height: 100%}
```

Applying `line-height: 100%` to every element overrides inline styles and can collapse multiline text in clients that respect it (notably Outlook Web). Scope this only to the elements that need it.
