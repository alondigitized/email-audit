# 25% off is going....
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- This is Drunk Elephant's absolute final-hour send of the Friends & Family blitz — the countdown reads 00 days, 01 hour, 07 minutes, 55 seconds. As the ninth email in five days (and the second email on May 25 alone, following yesterday's "Ending soon: 25% off" which logged 8/10 at 3 hours left), it earns its send by doing something earlier emails in the sequence didn't: it adds genuinely new creative below the hero. There's a "FUTURE'S LOOKING BRIGHT" bundle section with a Save $100+ callout and a "MASK ME ANYTHING" section dangling a free full-size P. Baume with $100+ spend — both of which give a fence-sitter a fresh reason to click that the straight 25%-off message no longer provides.
- The standout moment is the banner line "25% OFF SITEWIDE! 30% OFF $275! WHY ARE YOU STILL READING THIS AD SHOP!" — it's peak Drunk Elephant: self-aware, irreverent, and direct. It openly acknowledges subscriber fatigue and converts it into a laugh-and-click moment. That's the creative win this email needed to justify its existence at the end of a nine-send cadence.
- The subject line is the liability. "25% off is going...." with trailing dots is the weakest closing concept in the sequence — compare yesterday's cleaner "Ending soon: 25% off." The ellipsis suggests something is ending but doesn't land the specificity that the actual countdown inside the email earns. At this stage, the open rate lives or dies on brand recognition, not subject craft.

## What's working

- **Sub-90-minute countdown is unimpeachable** — 00 days / 01 hour / 07 min / 55 sec is physically unfakeable urgency; it converts skeptics.
- **"WHY ARE YOU STILL READING THIS AD SHOP!"** is brilliant self-aware humor that names the subscriber's fatigue and turns it into a laugh.
- **Three distinct content sections** (hero offer, bundles, mask/GWP) give the email visual momentum and multiple click paths — earlier blitz emails leaned on the single hero wordmark.
- **Free gift mechanic is new information** — full-size P. Baume with $100+ spend is a spend-incentive angle that hasn't appeared in the audited sequence history, giving fence-sitters a genuine incremental reason to act.
- **Clean, bug-free render** — products are legible, no broken images, layout holds.

## What's weak

- **Subject "25% off is going...."** is soft — the ellipsis creates vagueness rather than urgency; "Ending soon: 25% off" from yesterday's closing email was more direct and still scored better on specificity.
- **CTA button copy is unspecific** — "GET YOU GO" (or similar playful label) is on-brand but doesn't tell a skimmer whether clicking goes to sitewide, bundles, or a specific category.
- **Free GWP is buried two sections down** — the P. Baume with-purchase offer is the freshest hook in this email; it belongs in the hero or banner, not below the fold.
- **Wall of fine print** at the bottom conflicts with the "stop reading, just shop" energy; the dense asterisk block reads as conditions they'd rather you skip.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to match the countdown urgency inside.** "25% off is going...." doesn't clock-match the 1-hour-left reality. A time-specific subject would close this gap immediately.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Final hour: 25% off ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `~60 min left. Then it's full price.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free full-size P. Baume when you spend $100+. Go.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `1 hour left. After that, full price. We warned you.`
- 2. **Move the free GWP to the hero or banner.** "Free full-size [product] with $100+ spend" is the strongest new offer in this email. It should appear in the first 200px, not section three.
- 3. **Make the section CTAs specific.** "FUTURE'S LOOKING BRIGHT" deserves a button reading "Shop Bundles & Save $100+" — not a generic SHOP NOW — so the subscriber knows exactly what the click delivers.
- 4. **Reformat the fine print.** If the conditions are material, a slightly larger font with a clear divider reads as honest rather than buried. The rushed "SHOP NOW" tone clashes with a wall of tiny disclaimers.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] HTML comments inside CSS media query block**
Lines inside the `@media only screen and (max-width: 600px)` block:
```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
`<!-- ... -->` is not valid CSS. These two rules will be silently ignored by all conformant CSS parsers, meaning `contenttable` and `mobile` columns will not resize on mobile. This is a broken responsive layout bug.

**[MEDIUM] Duplicate font loading — double HTTP requests**
The same three `db.onlinewebfonts.com` font families are loaded via both `<link rel="stylesheet">` in `<head>` AND `@import` inside the `<style>` block. This fires six redundant external requests per open.

**[MEDIUM] `maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks user-initiated zoom. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Apple's iOS 10+ ignores this for accessibility, but it remains a compliance flag.

**[LOW] Global `line-height: 100%` reset**
```css
*{line-height: 100%}
```
This overrides all inherited line heights across every element. For multi-line text blocks it will produce cramped, potentially unreadable copy in clients that respect it.

**[LOW] Missing whitespace before `format()` in `@font-face`**
```css
url("...BrownRegular.woff2")format("woff2")
```
The CSS spec requires a space before `format()`. Most parsers are tolerant, but older WebKit-based clients may skip the format hint and fall back to an incorrect file type.

**[LOW] Third-party font CDN dependency**
`BrownBold`, `BrownLight`, and `BrownThin` are served from `db.onlinewebfonts.com`. That service hosts redistributed fonts and has no guaranteed SLA. If it goes down or blocks the request, all three weights fall back to the client's default sans-serif. The same files are available from the brand's own Demandware CDN (`drunkelephant.com`) — self-hosting all weights from that origin would remove the dependency.
