# Kamo FOMO?
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-19**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuine break from the discount-code drumbeat — no "SECRET SALE," no countdown timer, no 20%-off banner as the hero. Instead it's a full product-education send for Kamo Triple-Peptide Cover Drops, a multi-use concealer/foundation/tinted-moisturizer/firming-drops hybrid, echoing the same educational playbook as 2026-09-17's "Hydrated skin" and 2026-09-15's "Problem, meet solution." sends.
- The creative itself is clean and well-branded — consistent sage-green palette, clear product photography, repeated "SHOP KAMO" CTAs at every scroll stop. But the top banner is doing double duty and undercutting the hero: it's pushing a 15%-off subscription offer that has nothing to do with Kamo, competing for attention with "TRIED KAMO YET?" directly below it. The subject line "Kamo FOMO?" is vague and product-name-dependent — if you don't already know what Kamo is, it reads as gibberish, and there's a real cadence risk here since this is now the third product-education send in under two weeks without a clear differentiator in subject lines.
- Net: solid execution, muddled targeting priority (subscription offer vs. new product), and a subject line that only works for existing Kamo fans.

## What's working

- Clear, consistent visual hierarchy — product hero image, headline, and single CTA repeated four times down the page.
- Product benefit is explained in plain language ("Dab it under your eyes... Apply it all over... Mix it with your moisturizer") which does real educational work for a genuinely novel format.
- Second CTA block uses a strong before/after-style close-up model shot showing actual product application on skin — this builds credibility for a "does it all" claim.
- "Fall Skin, Sorted" spend-$100 GWP section at the bottom adds incremental value without cluttering the primary message.
- Clean render — no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout bugs anywhere in the scroll.

## What's weak

- The top banner promotes a 15%-off subscription offer that's completely unrelated to Kamo, splitting attention right at the point of entry instead of reinforcing the hero.
- Subject line "Kamo FOMO?" assumes brand recall of "Kamo" that a non-loyalist won't have — it reads as a fragment, not a hook.
- No price shown anywhere for Kamo itself, and no urgency/deadline tied to the product message (the only urgency in the email is for the unrelated subscription offer).
- Four repeated identical "SHOP KAMO" buttons with no variation in copy across four different sections is a missed chance to reinforce different benefits (shade range, coverage, firming) per click.
- This is the third education-style send in two weeks (following 09-17 and 09-15) — without a subject-line hook that names the win (undetectable coverage, 25 shades), it risks blurring together with the others in a crowded inbox.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut or move the subscription banner — either drop it from this send entirely or move it below the fold so it doesn't compete with the Kamo hero for the first 3 seconds of attention.
- 2. Rewrite the subject line to lead with the concrete hook (25 shades, 12-hour wear, 4-in-1) rather than assuming "Kamo" recognition.
- 3. Add a price or "shop now from $X" near the top CTA — right now there's zero pricing signal anywhere in the email.
- 4. Vary the four CTA button labels by section (e.g., "Find Your Shade," "See The Formula," "Shop The Drop") to make each block do more work.
- **Subject Alt A:** `25 shades, one drop, 12-hour coverage`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Meet the drop that's 4 products in 1`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Concealer, foundation, tinted moisturizer, firming serum — pick your mix.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Undetectable coverage that's also clinically proven to firm skin.`
