# Adios, 25% off...
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-26**
## Executive summary

- "Adios, 25% off..." is the second email on May 26 and roughly the eleventh send in a five-day Friends & Family blitz — and it actually earns its existence more than the previous send did. The subject line is the best Drunk Elephant has written in this entire sequence: punchy, brand-voice, under 20 characters, clear farewell signal. The email also introduces two genuinely new hooks — a 35% off kits section and a GWP tier (spend $100+, get free Hydra and Bright Girl) — that give subscribers who've been ignoring the sitewide 25% a real new reason to act.
- The critical failure is the same one that plagued yesterday's "Last day for 25% off!" send: the countdown timer at the very top of the email is frozen at **00:00:00:00**. For the second email in a row, the first thing a subscriber sees signals that the sale is already over. Whatever lift the "Adios" subject line earns at open, a dead timer immediately murders conversion intent.
- Net verdict: the best creative execution in the late sequence, dragged down by the exact same production bug as the email that preceded it by hours.

## What's working

- **"Adios, 25% off..."** is the sharpest subject in the entire sequence — Spanish farewell, brand personality, < 20 chars, implies urgency without screaming it.
- **35% off kits** is a genuinely deeper discount than the sitewide 25% — gives fence-sitters a last-day escalation reason to click.
- **GWP tier ("Future's Looking Bright" — spend $100+ get free products)** adds a value layer that hasn't appeared before in this blitz.
- Visual hierarchy holds: hero 25% OFF wordmark leads the eye, then GWP, then mask offer, then kits grid.
- The preheader pivots to Subscribe to Save acquisition rather than repeating the sale-end story — differentiated from the subject.

## What's weak

- **Countdown timer is 00:00:00:00 — again.** Same bug as the "Last day for 25% off!" send from hours earlier. Two consecutive emails in a row communicate that the sale is already expired.
- "SHOP NOW" CTAs are generic across every section — kits especially deserved something like "Shop Kits — 35% Off" to make the escalation legible.
- The GWP ($100+ spend threshold) and mask free-full-size offer are visually underpowered — competing with the hero wordmark rather than being called out as separate value moments.
- Kits grid product names and prices are too small to read without zooming — detail that could be a conversion signal is effectively invisible on mobile.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the timer — or remove it entirely.** A frozen 00:00:00:00 is worse than no timer at all. Either show a live countdown or replace with "Sale ends tonight at midnight" plain text.
- 2. **Rename the kit CTAs to make the 35% explicit.** "Shop Kits — 35% Off" outperforms "SHOP NOW" for last-day urgency on a deeper discount.
- 3. **Give the GWP tier a stronger headline.** "Spend $100, get free Hydra + Bright Girl" as a standalone callout above the fold would punch harder than its current buried placement.
- 4. **Cut one section.** Three value propositions (25% sitewide, GWP, mask, kits) compete with each other. Lead with 35% kits as the hero, relegate sitewide to secondary.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Adios to 25% AND free gifts`
- **Subject Alt B:** `35% off kits. Last hours.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Kits are 35% off + spend $100 and snag two free products`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Deeper discounts on kits + a GWP if you hit $100 — ends tonight`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical: HTML comment syntax inside CSS `@media` block**
In the `@media only screen and (max-width: 600px)` block, two rules are wrapped in HTML comment delimiters:
```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
CSS parsers do not strip HTML comments — they are treated as parse errors. These two rules will silently fail in all standards-compliant clients (Gmail, Apple Mail, Outlook iOS/Android). If `.contenttable` or `.mobile` layout behavior is depended on at narrow viewports, the mobile layout will break.

**Triple font loading for the same families**
Brown Bold, Brown-Light, and Brown-Thin are each loaded three times:
1. `<link href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...">` in `<head>`
2. `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...)` in `<style>`
3. `@font-face { src: url("https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/t/...") }` in `<style>`

This generates 6–9 redundant HTTP requests per render. Only `@font-face` survives in most email clients anyway; the `<link>` and `@import` declarations are stripped by webmail clients (Gmail, Outlook.com) at parse time and add latency without benefit in native clients.

**Third-party font CDN single point of failure**
All custom font variants (BrownBold, BrownLight, BrownThin) are served from `db.onlinewebfonts.com`. BrownRegular uses `drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/...`. If `db.onlinewebfonts.com` is unavailable at open time, three of the four declared font weights silently fall back to the system stack. No explicit `font-family` fallback stack is declared in the `@font-face` rules — fallback behavior depends on `body` or element-level CSS not visible in this excerpt.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` disables user pinch-to-zoom on iOS. This is an accessibility issue (WCAG 1.4.4 — Resize Text) and is flagged by automated accessibility scanners.

**Global `line-height: 100%` override**
```css
*{line-height: 100%}
```
The `*` selector overrides all element-level line-height, including any that are set inline on `<td>` or `<p>` elements. This is a known cause of text clipping in taller glyphs and can produce visual defects in Outlook (which applies `mso-line-height-rule:exactly` separately).

**`max-height:1000000px` on `*`**
```css
*{max-height:1000000px}
```
This is a known workaround to prevent iOS Mail from collapsing large emails, but applying it globally via `*` can interfere with `display:none` hiding of elements on desktop — some clients override hidden element dimensions when `max-height` is explicitly set.
