# Something big...
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-03**
## Executive summary

- "Something big..." is a launch teaser, not a shopping email — every asset here is built to get people to click "GET ON THE LIST" for a still-unnamed color/skin product dropping 8/11/26, not to sell what's already on shelves. The two swatch images (a lipstick-tube-style applicator laid into a "clinical color" formula in two shades) are clearly meant to spark guessing-game engagement, and the mystery works better than most brand teasers because the imagery itself is genuinely arresting — glossy pigment smears against green packaging read premium, not gimmicky.
- The problem is the banner up top. "FREE DELUXE SAMPLES OF B-HYDRA AND LALA RETRO WITH ORDERS $85+, AND FREE GROUND SHIPPING" is a completely unrelated evergreen promo bolted onto a launch-hype email, and it actively competes with the teaser for attention in the first 3 seconds. This brand has run this same $85+ gift-with-purchase mechanic before (see 2026-08-01's "10% off looks good on you," which also buried a real message under subscription/GWP mechanics) — reusing it here dilutes what should be a clean, curiosity-driven single-purpose send like "Lippe lock" was on 7/29.
- Cadence is the other flag: this is the second send in three days (8/1 → 8/3) and the seventh in two weeks per the brand history. The creative execution is strong, but stacked against that frequency and a subject line with zero information content, this reads as a mid-tier send that will underperform on opens even if it looks great once opened.

## What's working

- The hero swatch photography (pigment smears over green Drunk Elephant packaging) is visually distinctive and stops the scroll.
- "Here's a hint in two words: Clinical color. Any guesses?" builds a coherent guessing-game mechanic across both product shots — the two shade circles with the lipstick-style applicator pay off the hint.
- Clear launch date ("8/11/26. Mark your calendar.") gives the teaser a concrete anchor instead of vague "coming soon" language.
- Two distinct, unambiguous CTA buttons ("GET ON THE LIST" and "FIND OUT MORE") in on-brand green, both clearly clickable.
- Fine print for the GWP offer is fully disclosed (dates, minimum, exclusions) with no hidden gotchas.

## What's weak

- The top banner promo (free samples/free shipping) has nothing to do with the launch teaser below it — it's a generic, reused mechanic competing for the same eyeball real estate as the actual message of this email.
- Subject line "Something big..." carries zero information — no category, no benefit, no urgency — which is a real risk given this brand already leans on vague subjects ("Skincare with options" scored weakest in recent history for exactly this reason).
- No product name, shade range, or category is ever stated — even color cosmetics vs. skincare is ambiguous from the render alone (the swatches could read as concealer, color corrector, or foundation).
- Two competing CTAs ("GET ON THE LIST" vs. "FIND OUT MORE") for what should be one single waitlist action — mild indecision-inducing friction.
- No preview/preheader text was supplied with this send, which wastes the second-highest-visibility real estate in the inbox.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the GWP banner entirely from this send.** A teaser email needs one job. Let the $85+ gift-with-purchase run in its own campaign; here it just cannibalizes attention from the launch hook.
- 2. **Give the subject line a category anchor even while staying mysterious** — vague curiosity subjects are this brand's recurring weak spot per history.
- 3. **Add real preheader copy** that reinforces the date/urgency without repeating the subject.
- 4. **Collapse to one CTA.** Keep "GET ON THE LIST"; drop or merge "FIND OUT MORE" into the same waitlist flow so there's no split intent.
- 5. **Add one line of category context** ("color meets skincare" or similar) so recipients who don't want to solve a riddle still know what's launching.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The wait is almost over.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Color + skincare in one step. 8/11.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Two shades. One clinical formula. Mark your calendar for 8/11.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `We're not telling you what it is yet. But you'll want to be first.`

# Technical Audit: Drunk Elephant — "Something big..."

## 1. Technical Summary
The email uses a standard MSO/XHTML transitional table-based layout with custom web fonts, but ships with unencrypted image assets, missing unsubscribe/authentication headers, and duplicate font-loading requests.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 12 tracking/redirect links (`mi.drunkelephant.com`, `everestengagement.com` domains) were present but skipped by automated HTTP probing since they are click-tracking/redirect endpoints — link destinations were not independently verified as live.
- Open-tracking pixel confirmed: `https://mi.drunkelephant.com/p/up/80acaf1c74f9e002/o.gif?mi_u=98161986&mi_ecmp=us_de_broadcast_promotion_kamoteaser_prod`.
- No broken/dead links identified in the reachable (non-tracking) portion of the source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Duplicate font loading**: the three custom fonts (Brown Bold, Brown-Light, Brown-Thin, IDs `4150d76a...`, `6145ed70...`, `68d0f984...`) are each loaded twice — once via `<link rel="stylesheet">` in `<head>` and again via `@import` inside the `<style>` block for the same font IDs. This doubles font-fetch requests on clients that render both.
- **Missing `alt` text** on two images:
  - `o.gif` (open-tracking pixel) — acceptable for a 1x1 pixel, but flagged by QA.
  - `https://drunkelephantus.everestengagement.com/ea/dKhpn7WOgU/` image (this one is a rendered `<img>`, not a pixel — missing alt is a real accessibility gap for screen readers).
- **Non-HTTPS image sources** (4 social icons): Facebook, Twitter, Instagram, TikTok icons all load via `http://image.cheers.drunkelephant.com/...`. Mixed-content/non-HTTPS assets are commonly blocked or flagged by mail clients (Gmail, Outlook), risking broken social icons for recipients.
- Doctype is XHTML 1.0 Transitional with `xmlns:v`/`xmlns:o`/`xmlns:w` MSO namespaces present, indicating intentional Outlook/Word conditional support — no issue there.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- One tracking URL embeds the recipient's email address in plaintext query string: `...everestengagement.com/ea/dKhpn7WOgU/?e=darcy-ferment-drunkelephant-fap6e@etell.app&c=us_de_broad...`. Passing raw PII (email address) as a URL parameter is a technical exposure risk — it can leak via `Referer` headers to third-party domains (social icon links, embedded fonts) if the recipient clicks through, and persists in browser history/link-scanning logs.
- No other merge-tag placeholders (e.g., unresolved `{{first_name}}`-style tokens) were observed in the visible source.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header**: not found in the captured headers (may be a relay artifact rather than sender omission — flagged as WARN, not confirmed absent at origin).
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header (RFC 8058)**: not found — one-click unsubscribe support cannot be confirmed.
- **Authentication-Results header** (SPF/DKIM/DMARC): not found — authentication status of this message is unverified via the captured headers.
- Unsubscribe link presence/placement in the visible HTML body could not be confirmed from the truncated source provided.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Campaign tracking uses ESP-native parameters (`mi_u`, `mi_ecmp=us_de_broadcast_promotion_kamoteaser_prod`) rather than standard `utm_source`/`utm_medium`/`utm_campaign` query params — no standard UTM tagging was observed on the reachable tracking pixel.
- Destination/landing-page URLs are wrapped behind `everestengagement.com` click-redirects, so final landing page and UTM alignment could not be verified (redirects were skipped per the QA probe).

## 7. Recommendations
1. Remove the redundant `@import` font declarations in `<style>` since the same fonts are already loaded via `<link rel="stylesheet">` — cuts 3 duplicate network requests.
2. Add descriptive `alt` text to the `everestengagement.com` rendered image (non-pixel).
3. Serve the four social icons (`image.cheers.drunkelephant.com`) over `https://` instead of `http://`.
4. Avoid placing raw recipient email addresses in outbound tracking-link query strings; use an opaque token/ID instead to prevent PII leakage via referrer headers.
5. Confirm at the ESP/relay level that `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are actually being sent (RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe), and that SPF/DKIM/DMARC results are present — current data only shows they weren't captured by the relay, not that they're absent at origin.
