# Hot takes
**Score:** 10/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- "Hot takes" arrives as a summer skin recovery editorial built on a solid, correct premise: sun exposure drains moisture, and these five products address specific post-sun concerns. The hero is crisp, the teal-to-beach visual is seasonally right, and the problem-solution product grid — organized by skin concern rather than product category — is genuinely useful shopping architecture. Drunk Elephant's wry, self-aware copy voice holds throughout.
- The central problem is that the subject line doesn't pay off. "Hot takes" promises bold product opinions or contrarian skincare advice; what follows is a friendly, unremarkable "here are some summer products" editorial. That's a promise-delivery gap that will hurt open rates and slowly trains subscribers to discount future DE subject lines. Compare it to the July 8 "20-minute hack" send — which at least pointed toward a specific product (the Babyfacial) even if the payoff was delayed. "Hot takes" doesn't get even that far.
- The only concrete commercial incentive — 10% off via Subscribe & Save — is buried below five product rows. Most subscribers won't reach it. With seven emails sent in thirteen days, Drunk Elephant is spending goodwill faster than a non-offer editorial like this can replenish it.

## What's working

- **Problem-solution grid architecture** — five product tiles organized by skin concern ("For chapped lips or sunburned skin," "For sun spots and hyperpigmentation," etc.) helps subscribers self-identify immediately rather than scanning a generic product carousel.
- **Hero copy voice** — "GIVE IT A REST. If it's summer (which it is)..." is genuinely in-brand: self-aware, slightly arch, conversational without trying too hard.
- **Seasonal precision** — midsummer skin recovery framing is correctly timed; this email belongs in the third week of July and would feel forced in May or stale in late August.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no layout failures, consistent product image framing and lighting throughout.

## What's weak

- **"Hot takes" subject doesn't match body content** — the email is a helpful summer editorial, not bold product opinions; the mismatch trains subscribers to discount future DE subject lines.
- **10% Subscribe & Save offer is buried** — the only discount sits below five product rows, functioning as a reward for completion rather than an incentive to engage at all.
- **All CTAs read "SHOP NOW"** — five identical generic buttons across five distinct skin concerns; concern-specific copy ("Heal it," "Fade spots") would meaningfully outperform uniform verbs.
- **No urgency signal** — nothing in this email creates a reason to click today versus tomorrow, which matters when cadence is high and subscribers are skimming.
- **Cadence fatigue** — seventh send in thirteen days; even solid editorial accumulates fatigue at this frequency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the subject to match what's inside** — "Hot takes" earns the open on a false premise. Replace with something that names the summer skin recovery angle.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Sun damage, meet your match`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your post-sun skin SOS (5 targeted fixes)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Chapped lips, dark spots, dryness — here's exactly what to use for each.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Summer is rough on skin. These 5 products do the recovery work. Plus 10% off when you subscribe.`
- 2. **Surface the Subscribe & Save offer in the hero or as an early banner** — add "Subscribe & Save 10% on everything inside →" as a sub-headline directly beneath the hero CTA. The offer should pull subscribers into the product grid, not reward them for finishing it.
- 3. **Differentiate CTA copy per tile** — swap all five "SHOP NOW" for concern-anchored verbs: "Heal it" (Wondermelt), "Firm up" (A-Passioni), "Fade spots" (Brightfacial), "Drench it" (F-Balm), "Boost it" (Marula). One word per tile; meaningfully higher click intent.
- 4. **Add one soft urgency line** — a sub-hero aside like "Summer skin damage compounds — the repair window is now" reframes browsing as timely without inventing a false sale deadline.
- 5. **Audit the send cadence** — consider whether this editorial needed its own slot or could have extended the July 15 refillables send. At seven sends in thirteen days, each non-offer email is a debit against future opens.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — HTML comments inside `<style>` block break two mobile rules:**
```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
`<!-- -->` syntax is invalid inside a CSS `<style>` element. Modern parsers ignore these declarations entirely. The `.contenttable` and `.mobile` width overrides do not apply on screens ≤600px. This is a layout regression on mobile.

**Duplicate font loading — 6 extra HTTP requests:**
The same three fonts (`Brown Bold`, `Brown-Light`, `Brown-Thin`) are loaded via both `<link rel="stylesheet">` tags and `@import url()` in the same `<style>` block. Each font is fetched twice before rendering begins. Remove the `<link>` tags or the `@import` blocks — not both.

**Mixed font origins — third-party CDN dependency:**
- `BrownRegular`: self-hosted at `drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/...` (Salesforce Commerce Cloud)
- `BrownBold`, `BrownLight`, `BrownThin`: served from `db.onlinewebfonts.com` (third-party)

`db.onlinewebfonts.com` is not a controlled asset. If it rate-limits or goes offline, three of four font weights fall back silently. No explicit `font-family` fallback stack is declared in the `@font-face` rules.

**`.sm { display:none; }` has no recovery rule:**
The class is globally hidden. There is no `@media` block in the visible source that re-shows `.sm` elements. If this class is applied to mobile-only content, that content is hidden on all viewports.

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Disables pinch-to-zoom. This is an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4) and affects users with low vision.

**`*{line-height: 100%}` universal override:**
Applied to every element. Causes text clipping on multi-line blocks in several clients (notably Samsung Mail and older Android WebView). Should be scoped to specific selectors.
