# Health Education Week (and 15% off) doesn’t last forever
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-23**
## Executive summary

- The email is a last-day push on the verification-gated discount. The countdown banner reads "00 days, 06 hours, 54 minutes". Below it, a lilac hero shows the Amino Rain mirror and the headline "National Health Education Week for All!", with "plus 15% off" in magenta. The urgency is believable and the hierarchy is clean. This is the fifth send in five days on the same Subscribe & Save and Health Education theme.
- The offer is the weak part. The free mirror needs a $100+ order. The 15% needs SheerID verification. The footer says the offer ends today at 4:00 p.m. EST, "after reverts to 15% off," which contradicts the hero's "plus 15% off". Compared with the 10/22 send ("15% off! Er, wait. Is it 20%?") and the 10/21 send ("Do I hear 15% off?"), this one adds a real countdown and a real deadline. It still asks the reader to do homework before saving anything.
- The lower half is muddy. There are five equal-weight outlined buttons on a grey swirl: teacher, student, medical professional, military, first responder. They are functional but dull, and nothing shows a product. Judgement: solid and honest-looking, but fatigued. It will mostly reach people who already qualify and were already going to click.

## What's working

- The countdown timer with "HURRY, OFFER ENDS IN" gives a visible, believable deadline right under the logo.
- The hero hierarchy is clear. The headline, the magenta "plus 15% off" and the "GET VERIFIED" button read in that order.
- The free mirror is a tangible, photographed gift that gives the offer some personality.
- The three-step "get verified, get code, keep saving monthly" list explains the mechanic in plain language.
- Nothing renders badly. There are no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- The fine print undercuts the hero. "After reverts to 15% off" in the footer clashes with "plus 15% off" above it, and the wording is garbled.
- The lower half is five same-weight outlined buttons on a grey swirl. The grey image is dead space and adds nothing.
- The hero offer stacks two conditions (a $100+ order and verification) before any saving, and the mirror threshold is only in the copy.
- The email has no products, no reviews and no social proof. There is no reason to shop beyond the discount.
- The subject is 56 characters and will truncate on mobile. It also sells the holiday rather than the offer.
- This is the fifth near-identical send since 10/19, so urgency is diluted.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the benefit and clear the conditions. Put "Free Amino Rain mirror on $100+ plus 15% off" in the hero. Fix the footer to say plainly what happens at 4 p.m. today.
- 2. Replace the grey swirl and the five stacked buttons with a compact two-by-three grid of verification buttons. Add three hero products with prices underneath so the click has something to land on.
- 3. Add one line of social proof, such as a rating or a "most-gifted" tag on the mirror.
- 4. Rework the subject so the deadline and the gift come first, in under 45 characters.
- 5. Suppress or spread the near-daily Subscribe & Save cadence for people who already opened or clicked earlier in the week.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional layout with tracking-link and image warnings, mostly low severity. The notable finding is that the recipient's email address appears in plaintext in a tracking-pixel URL. The HTML is truncated and 13 links were not probed, so the body, footer and landing pages were not fully verified.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** 13 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are click-redirect domains. Destination resolution and redirect health are unconfirmed.
- **PII in pixel URL:** `https://drunkelephantus.everestengagement.com/ea/dKhpn7WOgU/?e=darcy-ferment-drunkelephant-fap6e@etell.app&c=us_de_broad…` puts the raw recipient email in the query string. It is exposed in logs, proxies and image caches. This is typical of Everest (Cheetah) but still a privacy exposure.
- **Second pixel:** `https://mi.drunkelephant.com/p/up/80acaf1c74f9e002/o.gif?mi_u=98161986&mi_ecmp=us_de_broadcast_promotion_nathealthweeksh` is an open-tracking beacon with a user ID (`mi_u`). Both pixels are tracking-only.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** both tracking pixels (`o.gif` and the `everestengagement.com/ea/dKhpn7WOgU/` image) have no `alt` attribute. They are decorative, so they should have `alt=""`. Without it some screen readers announce the filename or URL.
- **Insecure image sources:** the Facebook, Instagram and TikTok icons load over `http://image.cheers.drunkelephant.com/lib/fe3211717064057c751c78/m/1/…png` (`cc02d07d-…`, `9b452915-…`, `29f975e1-…`). Clients that block mixed content may drop them, leaving the social links with only alt text ("Facebook", "Instagram", "TikTok").
- **Invalid `@import`:** the `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/…)` rules come after the `body{…}` rule in the `<style>` block. `@import` must come first, so these are ignored. The same fonts are also loaded through `<link rel="stylesheet">` in `<head>`, so they are loaded redundantly.
- **Stray comment markers in CSS:** `<!-- *[class=contenttable] {…} -->` and `<!-- *[class=mobile] {…} -->` sit inside the `@media (max-width:600px)` block. Parsers will treat them as invalid selectors and may drop adjacent rules, such as the `*[class=vouchertext4]` rule that follows `-->`.
- **No `lang` attribute:** `<html xmlns=…>` has no `lang`.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="… maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on clients that honor it.
- **Fonts:** four custom font families are loaded from `db.onlinewebfonts.com` and `drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/…/v1719565389232/fonts/`. Many clients ignore web fonts, and the fallback stack was not visible in the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unrendered merge tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`, `[[…]]`) appear in the visible source. The `e=` parameter resolved to an address.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** both are absent from the captured headers. The QA tool itself says the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is unconfirmed and not a verified sender defect. Verify against the raw message at the source ESP.
- **Authentication-Results:** not captured, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status are unknown. The sender domain `cheers.drunkelephant.com` is a dedicated subdomain, which is good practice, but alignment can't be confirmed.
- **Footer unsubscribe link and postal address:** the truncated source doesn't show them, so they can't be assessed.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **Not verifiable:** the 13 click-tracked links were not resolved, so destination URLs and UTM parameters on the landing pages are unknown.
- **Campaign ID:** the only campaign identifier visible is `mi_ecmp=us_de_broadcast_promotion_nathealthweeksh`, which is consistent with the subject line's Health Education Week theme.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Remove the plaintext email from the Everest pixel URL (`?e=…`). Use an opaque recipient ID instead.
2. Switch the three social icons from `http://image.cheers.drunkelephant.com/…` to `https://`.
3. Add `alt=""` to both tracking pixels.
4. Move the `@import` rules to the top of the `<style>` block, or drop them since the `<link>` tags already load the same fonts. Remove the `<!-- -->` fragments from inside `@media`.
5. Add `lang="en"` to `<html>` and remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
6. Re-check List-Unsubscribe, List-Unsubscribe-Post and Authentication-Results on a direct-delivery copy, outside the relay, before treating them as defects.
7. Resolve the 13 redirected links, using a browser or `curl -L` in an allowed environment, to confirm destinations and UTM parameters.
