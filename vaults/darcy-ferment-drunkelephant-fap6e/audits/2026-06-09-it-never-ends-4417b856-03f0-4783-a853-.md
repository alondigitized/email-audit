# It never ends.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- Drunk Elephant is doing something genuinely smart here — reframing their core moisturizer lineup as a refills play that ties eco-friendliness to price savings. It's a positioning move that could convert existing loyalists who already love the Lala, Bora, and Protini and now have a reason to re-buy at a lower unit cost. The creative is clean, on-brand, and the "Your Forever Moisturizers" subheader is the best copy line in the email.
- The problem is the subject line. "It never ends." opens with an existential shrug that tells the reader absolutely nothing. After "Grab 20% Off Before It's Gone!" on June 8 and the SheerID verification push, subscribers needed a clear signal of what's on offer — and this subject gives them nothing to grab onto. The open rate is going to suffer.
- Once you're inside, the email delivers: structured product grid, visible sale pricing with strikethroughs, and a coherent brand voice. But there's no urgency, no social proof, and "SHOP NOW" is doing heavy lifting that more specific CTA copy could do better.

## What's working

- **"Your Forever Moisturizers" is strong editorial copy** — it earns the refill positioning by implying these are worth coming back to, not just commodities.
- **Crossed-out pricing is immediately legible** — Lala at $57 (was $68), Bora and Protini at $65 (was $72) make the savings concrete without needing a promo code.
- **Three-product grid is clean** — each refill has a name, price, and "SHOP" link; no clutter, no confusion.
- **Hero photography is signature DE** — stacked pastel jars, soft lighting, no lifestyle models needed. The brand does this better than almost anyone.
- **No render bugs** — the email renders cleanly with no broken images or overlapping elements.

## What's weak

- **Subject line "It never ends." is too vague** — it could mean anything from a sale extension to a subscription pitch; it earns no click intent on its own.
- **No urgency mechanism** — the refill savings read as permanent pricing, not a time-bounded offer; there's nothing to act on today versus tomorrow.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is generic** — for an email specifically about refills, "Shop Refills" or "Get Your Refill" would be sharper.
- **No social proof** — even a single star rating or "bestseller" badge on the Lala (their most recognized product) would add conversion weight.
- **Eco angle is mentioned once and dropped** — "Save money (and Mother Earth)" is in the subhead but never reinforced. If sustainability is a real hook for this audience, it deserves one more touchpoint.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject** — the product/offer needs to be telegraphed. The eco-savings angle is the real hook; lean into it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your moisturizer refills are here (save + do good)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Refill your favorites. Same formula, lower price.`
- 2. **Rewrite the preheader to complete the subject's thought**, since the current one isn't visible in the render and may be leaking junk.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Lala, Bora, Protini — now in refill format so you never run out`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Eco-friendly refills for your forever moisturizers — starting at $57`
- 3. **Replace "SHOP NOW" with "Shop Refills"** — one word change, meaningfully more specific.
- 4. **Add a deadline or stock-scarcity signal** — even "Limited refill stock" under the hero would justify acting today. Without it, this email does its best work for people already in-market, but converts no one who was on the fence.
- 5. **Pin a star rating under the Lala Retro** — it's the most recognizable product here and a 4.7-star badge would anchor trust for anyone unfamiliar with the refill line.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — broken responsive rules (HTML comments inside CSS block):**

Inside the `@media only screen and (max-width: 600px)` block, two rules are wrapped in HTML comment syntax:

```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

HTML `<!-- -->` comments are not valid inside a `<style>` block (they were historically stripped by old browsers, not parsed as CSS). Inside a `@media` query they are invalid tokens. The CSS parser will discard or misparse these rules. Both `.contenttable` and `.mobile` responsive width overrides **will not apply** on screens ≤600px. This is a layout-breaking bug on mobile.

**Font loading redundancy:**

The same three `db.onlinewebfonts.com` fonts are loaded twice each — once via `<link>` in `<head>` and again via `@import` inside the `<style>` block. This doubles HTTP requests for fonts that most email clients will block anyway:

```
<link href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/4150d76a...?family=Brown+Bold">
@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/4150d76a...?family=Brown+Bold);
```

Remove one or the other; `<link>` is preferred over `@import` in email.

**Third-party font host — `db.onlinewebfonts.com`:**

This is a font redistribution site of questionable legal standing. `BrownBold`, `BrownLight`, and `BrownThin` are sourced from it, while `BrownRegular` is correctly sourced from `drunkelephant.com`'s own Demandware CDN (`/on/demandware.static/...`). The other three variants should be migrated to the same first-party CDN path for reliability and licensing hygiene.

**Global `line-height: 100%` override:**

```css
*{line-height: 100%}
```

Applying `line-height: 100%` to every element is aggressive and can compress multi-line text unreadably. It should be scoped to specific selectors.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`:**

```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}
```

`none` (vs `100%`) disables the user's ability to zoom text on iOS. Prefer `100%` to preserve accessibility and comply with WCAG 1.4.4.

**Alt text:** Cannot confirm — source is truncated before `<img>` tags. Must verify all product images carry descriptive `alt` values; images-off rendering is common in deliverability-challenged sends.
