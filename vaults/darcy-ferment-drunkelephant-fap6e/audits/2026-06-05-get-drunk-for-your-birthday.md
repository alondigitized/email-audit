# Get Drunk for your birthday!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- This is a birthday data-capture play, not a product send — Drunk Elephant wants your birth date in exchange for a vague "special surprise." The creative is clean and exactly on-brand: signature stacked product photography, the DE color palette popping on an orange background, and the brand's best recurring pun deployed in the headline. All of that is in service of an ask with no stated value on the other side of it.
- "Special surprise" is dead weight. Birthday club emails work when the reward is explicit — "20% off your birth month," "a gift on us," anything concrete. Asking a subscriber to hand over personal data for an unnamed treat is a conversion killer, even for a brand as trusted as DE. The mechanics here are fine; the offer architecture is broken.
- One more friction point: this is the second Drunk Elephant email landing on June 5 — the editorial "A pre-bedtime plan" came first today. Even loyal subscribers notice the double-tap.

## What's working

- The "Get Drunk for your birthday" pun is DE's sharpest version of their signature line — natural and not forced.
- Product photography is excellent: the stacked colorful bottles on the warm orange field are instantly recognizable and eye-catching.
- Single-minded layout — one message, one CTA, nothing competing for attention.
- Clean render across the full email; no layout bugs, no broken images, no junk in the footer.

## What's weak

- "Special surprise" is functionally meaningless — no subscriber knows what they're agreeing to, and vague promises convert poorly even from trusted senders.
- Two sends on the same day (this + "A pre-bedtime plan") risks unsubscribe pressure from subscribers who are already in the content-reset recovery window after the F&F blitz.
- Zero social proof or incentive framing — no member count, no "your gift arrives automatically," nothing to reduce signup friction.
- "SHARE YOUR BIRTHDAY" as CTA copy reads clinical against the playful brand voice established everywhere else in the email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name the reward.** "Get 20% off your birthday month" or "a birthday gift, on us" would double conversions. The vague surprise is the single most impactful fix available.
- 2. **Add a proof point or trust signal.** Something like "Join 500K birthday members" or a short line about what past recipients got removes the leap-of-faith.
- 3. **Detach from same-day editorial sends.** Birthday club capture belongs on a standalone send day, not doubled up with content emails.
- 4. **Soften the CTA to match brand voice.** "Tell us your birthday" or "Celebrate with us" lands warmer than the corporate "SHARE YOUR BIRTHDAY."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Tell us your birthday — there's a treat waiting`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your birthday reward is out here. Claim it.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `We throw a mean birthday surprise — but only if we know when.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Join the birthday club. Good things happen in your birth month.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Invalid HTML comment syntax inside `<style>` block (confirmed).**
Two rules inside the `@media only screen and (max-width: 600px)` block are wrapped in HTML comment delimiters:

```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

HTML comments are not valid CSS. These rules will be silently dropped in all conformant CSS parsers. The `.contenttable` and `.mobile` mobile-width overrides are effectively dead code, meaning those elements will not reflow correctly at narrow viewports.

**Issue 2 — Triple-redundant font loading.**
Fonts are declared three ways simultaneously for the same families:
- `<link href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/...">` tags (stripped by Gmail, Outlook.com, Apple Mail iOS)
- `@import url(...)` inside the `<style>` block (also stripped by Gmail)
- `@font-face` declarations (the only mechanism with any email client support)

The `<link>` and `@import` entries add HTTP latency and will never render in the majority of inboxes. They should be removed.

**Issue 3 — Third-party CDN for licensed font assets.**
`BrownBold`, `BrownLight`, and `BrownThin` are served from `db.onlinewebfonts.com`, a public aggregator with no email infrastructure SLA. `BrownRegular` is correctly self-hosted at `www.drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/`. The CDN-hosted variants should be migrated to the same self-hosted path for reliability and licensing compliance.

**Issue 4 — `line-height: 100%` on `*` selector.**
The rule `*{line-height: 100%}` applies globally after the `ExternalClass` normalization block. This will collapse line-height on multi-line text cells in several Outlook versions where `mso-line-height-rule: exactly` compounds the effect.
