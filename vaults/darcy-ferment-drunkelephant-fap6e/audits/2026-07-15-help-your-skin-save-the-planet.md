# Help your skin. Save the planet.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- This email makes a genuine structural bet: lead with product differentiation (refillable moisturizers) over a headline discount. "REPLENISH, REPAIR, REFILL" is clean, alliterative, and on-brand. The three-product grid — Lala Retro, Bora Barrier, Protini — gives the email real specificity, and the eco angle ("Save money and Mother Earth") is a meaningful differentiator in a category drowning in percent-off promos.
- The execution, however, undercuts the concept. "Save money" is a claim with no math behind it. The refill prices ($57, $65, $63) appear in the grid alongside labels that look like value comparisons, but the text is too small to read cleanly — a subscriber scanning on mobile can't quickly assess the savings. The only hard number in the email is the 10% subscribe-to-save section, which is buried below the fold and gated behind a subscription commitment.
- The bigger misfire is at the top: the countdown timer ("THE OFFER ENDS IN 01:15:55:24") is compelling on its own, but the banner text beneath it appears to reference the Babyfacial promotion from earlier in the week — not the refills that dominate the rest of the email. That two-theme collision at the top dilutes both messages. This is Drunk Elephant's seventh email in thirteen days; after the strong "20-minute hack" (9/10) and "Time to come clean" (8/10) sends earlier this month, this one lands as the weakest conversion play in the streak.

## What's working

- "REPLENISH, REPAIR, REFILL" is tight, on-brand, memorable hero copy that earns its size
- Three named products with individual SHOP buttons give shoppers a direct path to what they want
- "Your Forever Moisturizers" subheader creates an effective emotional anchor for refill repurchase behavior
- Clean layout with no render bugs, no overlapping text, no broken images
- Eco positioning is genuinely differentiated; leading with values instead of a discount is a courageous move that builds brand equity

## What's weak

- Savings are unsubstantiated — value-comparison labels next to refill prices are too small to read on render; "save money" reads as claim, not proof
- Countdown timer at the top is promoting what appears to be the Babyfacial/leave-on mask deal, creating a confusing dual-theme collision in the first screen
- 10% subscribe-to-save is below the fold and requires a subscription commitment, making it feel like fine print rather than a lead offer
- "SHOP NOW" is a generic CTA that doesn't reinforce the refills or the savings proposition
- Seventh email in thirteen days — the heaviest send week yet, and this is the lightest conversion offer in that stretch

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show the savings math in the product grid.** Add original-price strikethroughs next to refill prices — "Full size $76 → Refill $57" — so "save money" becomes provable at a glance. This is the single change that would lift this email most.
- 2. **Align the countdown with this email's offer.** Either run a genuine limited-time refill promotion so the countdown has something to count down to, or remove it. Right now it creates urgency for a different email.
- 3. **Retitle the CTA.** "Shop Refills" or "Save with Refills" is more specific than "SHOP NOW" and reinforces the email's core message.
- 4. **Surface the 10% earlier.** Move subscribe-to-save into the hero or mid-email — not as a footer section. Or offer a one-time 10% on the refills purchase without the subscription gate to reduce commitment friction.
- 5. **Add one concrete eco-credential.** "X% less packaging per refill" or "saves Y oz of plastic per year" gives the "Save the planet" claim teeth and makes the value proposition two-dimensional.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Lala Retro, Bora Barrier & Protini — now in refill format`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CRITICAL] HTML comments inside `<style>` block** — the `@media (max-width: 600px)` rule contains raw HTML comment syntax:
```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
HTML comments are invalid CSS. Gmail's CSS parser and several Outlook versions will drop the surrounding rule block entirely on encountering `<!--`, breaking the mobile layout.

**[HIGH] `text-size-adjust: none` instead of `100%`**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}
```
`none` fully disables user font scaling. The correct value is `100%`. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).

**[HIGH] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom in mobile email clients. Second WCAG 1.4.4 violation.

**[MEDIUM] Apple Mail link color suppression**
```css
.appleLinks a {color: #000000 !important; text-decoration: none !important;}
```
Makes auto-detected Apple Mail links (phone numbers, addresses, dates) visually identical to body text — black, no underline. Users cannot distinguish them as interactive.

**[LOW] Triple-redundant font loading** — BrownBold, BrownLight, and BrownThin are each loaded three ways:
1. `<link href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/...">` in `<head>`
2. `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/...)` in CSS
3. `@font-face { src: url("https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/...") }` in CSS

Gmail strips `<link>` tags and `@import` statements; only `@font-face` survives. The first two mechanisms generate HTTP requests with zero rendering benefit and add load time. Additionally, BrownRegular is served from the first-party domain `www.drunkelephant.com`, while the other three weights are served from the third-party CDN `db.onlinewebfonts.com` — an inconsistency that creates an external dependency risk if that CDN is rate-limited or blocked by corporate proxies.
