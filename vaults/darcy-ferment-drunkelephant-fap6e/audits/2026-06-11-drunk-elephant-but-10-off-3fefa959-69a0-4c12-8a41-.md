# Drunk Elephant but 10% off?!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- This email is a program launch for Drunk Elephant's new "Subscribe to Save" auto-replenishment service, using 10% off plus free shipping as the enrollment incentive. The subject line ("Drunk Elephant but 10% off?!") sets an informal, curiosity-baiting tone that half-delivers — the "but" and "?!" imply a surprise discount when the actual offer is more nuanced: you have to commit to a recurring delivery cadence to unlock it. That's a meaningful distinction, and the email never really closes that gap.
- The body is structurally sound — a clean five-step onboarding flow, a strong product hero shot, and dual CTAs — but the framing is all instructions and no conviction. Compare this to the June 9 refills email (the one that tied eco-messaging to savings): that send gave you a *reason to believe* in a new behavior. This one gives you a task list. The Subscribe to Save concept is actually a solid retention play; the email just doesn't sell the upside hard enough before asking for the commitment.
- The "SUPERSIZE YOUR ORDER" section at the bottom also creates a message collision — it's pitching an upsell to someone who hasn't yet decided to subscribe at all.

## What's working

- Product hero photography is clean, on-brand, and prominently placed — DE's stacked-bottle aesthetic earns the email's visual real estate.
- The five-step flow is genuinely clear; it demystifies a subscription sign-up in a way that reduces cognitive friction for a high-commitment ask.
- Two CTAs (above the fold and below the steps) handle scroll-depth variance well.
- Subject line is short at 28 characters — mobile inbox-friendly.
- The "SUPERSIZE YOUR ORDER" panel adds value for someone already considering upsizing, even if it's poorly placed for the primary message.

## What's weak

- Step 3 ("Log in or create an account") is buried mid-flow — for someone on the fence, that's an objection, not a step.
- Zero urgency: the 10% offer reads as permanent, giving no reason to act today over next week.
- No social proof anywhere — subscribing to recurring delivery is a trust ask, and even a single line of "joined by X loyalists" would help.
- "SUPERSIZE YOUR ORDER" competes directly with the subscription CTA and splits the send's purpose.
- Coming one day after the June 10 travel-kit editorial and three days after the June 9 refills email, the cadence is starting to pile up — inbox fatigue risk is real at this frequency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the benefit, not the steps.** Reframe the hero copy as "Autopilot your routine. Always 10% off. Ships exactly when you need it." — then put the five steps below that. Benefits first, mechanics second.
- 2. **Create urgency with "founding subscriber" framing.** "New program — founding subscribers lock in 10% for life" or a limited-time enrollment window turns a permanent-feeling offer into something worth acting on now.
- 3. **Remove "SUPERSIZE YOUR ORDER" from this send.** Trigger it as a standalone email once someone has enrolled — it's an upsell to a relationship that doesn't exist yet.
- 4. **Address the account objection directly.** Change Step 3 to: "Already a member? One tap. New here? 30 seconds." Reframe the friction as trivial rather than letting the word "create" do damage.
- 5. **Add one proof line above the final CTA.** Even "thousands of skincare loyalists have already subscribed" anchors the decision socially.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] HTML comment syntax inside `<style>` block — confirmed bug**

```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 600px) {
  <!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
  <!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

`<!--` / `-->` are HTML comment delimiters, not CSS. Inside a `<style>` tag they are treated as parse errors in standards-compliant clients (Gmail, Apple Mail, modern Outlook). Both `.contenttable` and `.mobile` responsive width overrides will be silently dropped, likely breaking mobile layout.

**[MEDIUM] Triple font loading — same assets requested three times**

The same Brown font variants are loaded via:
1. `<link href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...">` (3 `<link>` tags in `<head>`)
2. `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...)` inside `<style>` (3 `@import` rules)
3. `@font-face { src: url("https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/t/...") }` (3 `@font-face` blocks)

Each open event triggers up to 9 font requests to `db.onlinewebfonts.com` instead of 3. Most email clients block external CSS/fonts anyway, making all 9 no-ops — but for clients that do load them (Apple Mail), this is unnecessary latency.

**[MEDIUM] `.sm { display:none; }` is unconditional**

```css
.sm{ display:none; }
```

This rule is declared outside any `@media` query, meaning `.sm` elements are hidden on all screen sizes, not just desktop. If `.sm` is the mobile-reveal class (common pattern), this inverts the intent. Verify against actual usage in body markup.

**[LOW] Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`**

`<html xmlns="...">` has no `lang="en"`. Screen readers will default to the OS language, which may cause incorrect pronunciation for international subscribers.

**[LOW] `max-height:1000000px` applied globally**

```css
* { max-height:1000000px; }
```

This Gmail clipping hack applied to `*` can interfere with elements that intentionally have `overflow:hidden` or constrained heights. Scope it to `body` or a wrapper `td` rather than the universal selector.
