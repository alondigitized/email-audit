# Last day for 25% off!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-26**
## Executive summary

- This is email ten in Drunk Elephant's Friends & Family blitz — and the single most visible element above the fold is a countdown timer frozen at **00:00:00:00**. That's not urgency; that's a signal the sale is over. Whether the dynamic component broke, the screenshot was captured post-deadline, or the timer simply wasn't updated, every subscriber who opens this sees an expired clock while the copy says "LAST DAY FOR OUR SALE!" The dissonance is impossible to miss.
- Below that defect, the email is actually the strongest creative in the late sequence. The two GWP tier callouts — free full-size F-Balm at $75+, free Hydra + Bright Set at $100+ — are a genuine content addition that didn't appear prominently in emails 4–8. The product grid with real sale prices ($38.25, $47.25) gives subscribers something to click toward beyond the abstract 25% wordmark that has dominated the series since May 21.
- Judgement: operationally broken at the top, legitimately useful at the bottom. The dead timer is the thing a subscriber remembers.

## What's working

- GWP tier callouts ($75+ and $100+ spend thresholds) introduce concrete, spend-ladder incentives not featured in the prior five sends — this is the freshest creative in the late sequence.
- Product grid with named bestsellers and visible sale prices ($38.25, $47.25, etc.) gives the email specificity that the hero entirely lacks.
- "Friends till the end" headline pairs well with "LAST DAY" framing — the only send in the series that uses the sale's end as an emotional beat rather than just a deadline.
- Brand voice ("WHY ARE YOU STILL READING THIS AD SHOP!") stays consistent with DE's playful DNA; it doesn't read as desperate despite being send ten.
- Visual hierarchy is solid from the hero downward — 25% offer, then spend tiers, then specific products.

## What's weak

- **All-zero countdown timer (00:00:00:00) is above the fold and visible immediately** — implies the sale is over, directly contradicting the "LAST DAY" copy.
- The hero creative is functionally identical to emails 5–9 in the sequence; the 25% OFF SITEWIDE wordmark has appeared in essentially every send since May 21.
- Every CTA across all five sections reads "SHOP NOW" — no category differentiation, no specificity, no connection to the spend-tier offers.
- No social proof or product ratings anywhere in the email; the product grid is pure editorial with no third-party signal.
- "Did we mention full size?" appears twice verbatim, back-to-back in adjacent sections — the repetition reads like a copy paste oversight.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill or fix the countdown timer.** An accurate "ends in 4 hours" would create genuine urgency; an all-zero timer actively destroys it. Remove it entirely if the dynamic component can't be trusted.
- 2. **Lead with the GWP, not the sitewide % again.** "Free full-size F-Balm ($32 value) with any $75 order — today only" is more compelling than a 25% headline the subscriber has seen nine times.
- 3. **Make CTAs category-specific.** Replace all "SHOP NOW" instances: "Claim Free Gift" on the GWP tiers, "Shop Serums," "Shop Moisturizers" on the product grid.
- 4. **Add star ratings or "best-seller" labels to the product grid** — any social signal that gives first-timers a decision heuristic.
- 5. **Shorten "Did we mention full size?"** — it's a good punchy line, but using it identically in consecutive sections kills the effect. Use it once, drop it or vary the second.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last day: Free full-size F-Balm with $75+`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Ends tonight — 25% off + free gift with $75`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus free Hydra + Bright Set when you spend $100. Tonight only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Real prices: C-Firma from $38, Framboos from $47. Last chance.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — invalid HTML comment syntax inside `<style>` block:**

```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

HTML `<!-- -->` comments are not valid inside `<style>` tags. CSS parsers in Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook will either silently drop these rules or (in older WebKit/Blink clients) parse the selector text as literal CSS tokens. The intended mobile-width overrides for `.contenttable` and `.mobile` **will not apply reliably**. These must be converted to CSS comments (`/* ... */`) or removed.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

Prevents pinch-to-zoom on iOS. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied globally:**

```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```

Using `none` (rather than `100%`) on the `*` selector disables user-initiated text scaling on all iOS WebKit clients. Replace `none` with `100%` on both properties.

**Duplicate font loading — three separate mechanisms for the same assets:**

1. `<link rel="stylesheet">` tags in `<head>` (three tags → three HTTP requests)
2. `@import url(...)` inside `<style>` (same three URLs — redundant)
3. `@font-face` blocks also referencing `db.onlinewebfonts.com`

This means up to 9 external requests for fonts that most email clients will block anyway. Collapse to a single `@font-face` declaration per weight and remove the `<link>` + `@import` duplication.

**Missing `alt` text confirmation:** The source is truncated before `<img>` tags are visible. Cannot confirm alt attributes — flagged for manual verification.
