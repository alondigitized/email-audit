# Kamo makin’ friends
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-27**
## Executive summary

- This is Kamo Drops send number five in under three weeks, and it's finally moved past hype and clinical-claims territory into genuinely useful territory: a "how to use it" breakdown with three concrete application methods (solo, mixed with Protini, as concealer). That's a smart pivot for a product that's been out for a while now — customers who bought it during launch hype may still not know how to use it, and this email answers that directly.
- The execution is clean — clear green-on-cream brand palette, good product photography, readable three-panel layout — but the email leans entirely on styling tips with zero urgency or price hook until a buried footer PS about a free deluxe sample GWP tied to a $50+ subscription order. That offer deserves way more prominence than a two-line footnote after three "how to" panels.
- Given the cadence (this is the fifth Kamo send since 2026-08-11's "Introducing Kamo Drops!" and the fourth in two weeks per the 2026-08-21 audit), the "educational" angle is the right move to avoid fatigue, but the email undersells its own best asset — the subscription GWP offer — by tucking it at the very bottom.

## What's working

- Clear, teachable structure — three distinct use cases (full coverage, mixed with Protini, undereye concealer) each paired with a demonstrative photo.
- Consistent brand voice — the "say that three times fast" line and casual tone match Drunk Elephant's established playful copy style.
- Two "Shop Kamo" CTAs (top and mid-email) keep the path to purchase visible without over-scrolling.
- No render bugs — images, text, and layout all display cleanly with no overlaps or broken assets.
- Diverse skin tones represented across the three model photos, which reads authentic for a "your skin, your way" positioning.

## What's weak

- The actual offer (free deluxe sample on $50+ subscription orders) is buried in a gray header bar at the very top with no dollar/percent hook, then repeated only in dense fine print at the bottom — most readers will miss it entirely.
- No urgency signal anywhere in the main body — the free-sample deadline (9/1/26) never appears in visible copy, only in 8pt legal text.
- Subject line "Kamo makin' friends" is vague and gives zero indication this is a how-to-use email, so open intent has to come from brand loyalty alone, not content curiosity.
- This is the fifth Kamo-focused send in under three weeks; even with the educational pivot, subscribers who aren't already Kamo owners have no reason to open yet another Kamo-titled email.
- The final "You've Gotta Try This" panel crams a second, different offer (buy Kamo + free Protini/Lala deluxe sample) into the same footer block as the subscription GWP — two distinct offers compressed into one confusing paragraph.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pull the subscription GWP (free deluxe sample, $50+ orders) out of the gray header and footer fine print and give it its own visible banner with the actual deadline ("through 9/1") — this is the strongest concrete incentive in the email and it's currently invisible.
- 2. Rewrite the subject to signal the how-to angle so it stands apart from prior Kamo sends — this email's real value is instructional, not promotional.
- 3. Separate the two footer offers (subscription GWP vs. buy-Kamo-get-deluxe-sample) into distinct, clearly labeled blocks so shoppers don't have to parse overlapping terms.
- 4. Add a light urgency line near the top CTA (e.g., "free sample ends 9/1") rather than leaving all dates in the legal footnote.
- 5. Given five Kamo sends in three weeks, next send should pivot to a different hero product or a Kamo + Protini bundle push to reset the cadence.
