# Choose your own adventure when using your miles
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth AAdvantage email in a six-day post-signup onboarding sequence — welcome (5/15), temp password (5/15), travel benefits education (5/17), and now this. Where the 5/17 send taught the miles/loyalty distinction before asking for anything, this one pivots to activation: here are four ways to redeem, and here's a personalized car-rental bonus. The strategy is correct. The execution has a sequencing problem: the most actionable element in the entire email — a "Just for you" 1,500 bonus miles offer — is buried below the hero and a four-tile redemption-options grid. By the time you reach the offer, the email has already asked you to evaluate flight bookings, car rentals, hotels, and upgrades.
- The brand design is clean, no render bugs, and the "Just for you" framing is a credible attempt at personalization. But this email doesn't have a single job — it's simultaneously a redemption education piece, a lifestyle aspirational piece, and a targeted acquisition offer. Those three things dilute each other. The one change that would lift this the most: make the 1,500-mile car bonus the hero, not the fourth item down.

## What's working

- "Just for you" framing on the bonus miles offer creates a sense of targeting, even if the offer is undersurfaced
- "Book a car" CTA button is clearly labeled and visually prominent once you reach that section
- Four-tile redemption grid (flight, car, hotel, upgrade) efficiently orients a new member on where miles can go
- No render bugs — images load, layout is clean, type is legible throughout
- Consistent AA brand voice and visual treatment across this entire onboarding sequence

## What's weak

- The 1,500 bonus miles offer is the most valuable thing in this email and it lives below three rows of content — well below the fold on mobile
- "Choose your own adventure" is a gamified metaphor that signals nothing concrete; a new member skimming the inbox gets no hint there's an actionable bonus offer inside
- No deadline or expiry on the bonus miles offer makes it easy to defer indefinitely
- The hero image (mountain landscape) is aspirational but disconnected from any of the four redemption tiles below it
- Four equal-weight tiles mean no single redemption path is prioritized — the email trains the reader to scroll without committing

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface the 1,500 bonus miles offer as the hero** — replace "How will you reward yourself?" with a headline that names the offer. The four redemption tiles can stay, but below a lead that gives the reader a reason to act now.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the bonus offer** — even a soft "offer valid through June 15" creates urgency without being pushy.
- 3. **Rewrite the subject to lead with the specific offer** — there's a real incentive inside; the subject buries it.
- 4. **Cut to one primary CTA** — the four tiles each imply a click; the car-bonus button is a fifth. Pick one and commit.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Earn 1,500 bonus miles on your first car rental`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your miles are ready — start with a car rental bonus`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Book a car through AAdvantage and earn an extra 1,500 miles. Offer expires June 15.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Four ways to redeem, one bonus to kick things off — here's your first move.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue R1 — Duplicate Google Fonts load (confirmed)**

Both a `<link>` tag and an `@import` in `<style>` request the identical Roboto URL:
```
https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto:ital,wght@0,400;0,700;1,500&display=swap
```
Clients that process both (e.g., Apple Mail) issue two HTTP requests. Gmail strips the `<link>` tag but honors `@import` in embedded `<style>`, so the `@import` alone is sufficient — the `<link>` is dead weight everywhere and redundant cost in Gmail-supporting clients. **Remove the `<link>` tag; keep only the `@import`.**

**Issue R2 — Duplicate MSO conditional style block (confirmed)**

The `<!--[if gte mso 9]>` block containing `font-family: Helvetica Neue` fallbacks appears **twice** in the `<head>`. The second copy is byte-for-byte identical to the first. This inflates email size and is a template merge defect.

**Issue R3 — Font-stack typo (confirmed)**

```css
font-family: Roboto, ariel, helvetica, sans-serif;
```
`ariel` is not a registered font name. The intended fallback is `Arial`. Outlook and clients where Roboto fails will skip to `helvetica`, bypassing the intended Windows system font. **Fix to `Arial`.**

**Issue R4 — `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom (confirmed)**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Locking `maximum-scale=1` prevents users from pinching to zoom on mobile — a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) failure. **Remove `maximum-scale=1`.**

**Issue R5 — Global text-size-adjust suppression (confirmed)**

```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applying `text-size-adjust: none` to `*` overrides user accessibility preferences and OS-level font scaling system-wide within the email. The intended fix (preventing Outlook's aggressive 120% scaling) should be scoped to `body` or specific table cells, not the universal selector. **Scope this to `body, table, td`.**
