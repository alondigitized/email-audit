# How to earn travel rewards before you reach AAdvantage® status
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- This is the sixth American Airlines email in twelve days to a new AAdvantage member, and the creative is starting to feel like a remix. The tier-progression visual is genuinely well-executed — a clean 15K → 40K → 60K → 75K loyalty ladder with specific benefits at each rung — but the May 24 send ("Choose how you'll earn AAdvantage® miles") already covered adjacent territory. The personalized greeting ("Hi Curt") and clear benefit specificity (free bags, upgrades) show that AA knows what useful onboarding looks like. The problem is this email tries to serve two masters simultaneously: educate about tiers AND sell a Citi/AAdvantage credit card. Neither objective gets full commitment.
- The card upsell — "Earn 3,000 bonus miles when you take 4 flights" — is embedded mid-scroll with a deadline, which is structurally sound for a secondary CTA. But parking "Benefit details" as the primary button above it is a soft close that dilutes everything below. A new member who just got five emails hasn't earned enough trust with the brand to be upsold a credit card; they've barely flown yet. The email does its job as an educational send, but doesn't do it convincingly enough to justify another spot in the inbox.
- The cadence risk is real here. Six emails in twelve days is at the edge of fatigue territory, and this one arrives with the lowest urgency and clearest overlap to prior sends in the sequence so far.

## What's working

- Tier ladder (15K / 40K / 60K / 75K) is clean and immediately scannable — the layout earns attention
- Benefit specificity within tiers ("1 free checked bag," "Complimentary upgrades") is the kind of concrete detail that converts curious new members
- Personalized "Hi Curt" sets a direct tone above the hero
- The card bonus offer has a stated deadline — time-bounded and verifiable
- No render bugs; AA's branding (deep blue, consistent type) is clean throughout

## What's weak

- "Benefit details" as the primary CTA is a dead end — vague, no urgency, no verb+noun specificity
- Two CTAs (tier benefits + card registration) split attention without a clear hierarchy of priority
- Subject line runs 62 characters — over mobile-optimal length and framed educationally rather than as an offer
- Creative overlap with the May 24 send is significant; this doesn't feel like new information to someone who's been reading the sequence
- The "Stay tuned" footer section adds no value and signals that AA is padding the email to fill space

## Recommendations

- **1. Pick one objective per email.** If the business priority is card acquisition, lead with the 3,000 miles offer — use the tier chart as supporting rationale for why the miles matter. If the priority is engagement/education, drop the card upsell entirely and give the tier content room to breathe.
- **2. Replace "Benefit details" with a status-forward CTA.** Something like "See your path to Gold" or "Unlock your first perk" creates forward motion. "Benefit details" reads like a footnote.
- **3. Shorten and sharpen the subject line:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Earn perks before you hit status — here's how`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Free bags and upgrades start at 15,000 points`
- **4. Add a preheader that complements the subject:**
- **Preheader Alt A:** `You're closer to travel perks than you think.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus: a bonus miles offer with an expiration date.`
- **5. Consider a cadence pause.** Six emails in twelve days before the member has booked a single flight risks training them to ignore the sender. One well-timed email at the 3-week mark — tied to a real behavior signal (first search, first booking) — would outperform two reminder emails on reward education.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue A — Duplicate Google Fonts request (double HTTP round-trip)**

Both methods load the same stylesheet simultaneously:

```html
<!-- Line ~9: <link> tag -->
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto:ital,wght@0,400;0,700;1,500&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">

<!-- Line ~13: @import inside <style> -->
@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto:ital,wght@0,400;0,700;1,500&display=swap');
```

`@import` blocks render-critical parsing and fires a second request for the same resource. Remove one — keep the `<link>` tag, drop the `@import`.

---

**Issue B — Font family typo: `ariel` instead of `Arial`**

In the base `.scrollmenu a` selector:

```css
font-family: Roboto, ariel, helvetica, sans-serif;
```

`ariel` is not a font; the intended fallback is `Arial`. The mobile media query version of the same rule correctly uses `Arial`. On clients that block web fonts (many corporate Outlook configs), the fallback chain silently skips `ariel` and falls to `helvetica`, which is fine on Mac but will use the OS default on Windows. Fix: change `ariel` → `Arial`.

---

**Issue C — `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

`maximum-scale=1` prevents users from pinching to zoom on mobile. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) violation and is specifically flagged by accessibility auditors and Apple's Human Interface Guidelines. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

---

**Issue D — Overly broad dark mode color override**

```css
@media (prefers-color-scheme:dark) {
  span, a, td { color: #ffffff !important; }
}
```

This forces every `<span>`, `<a>`, and `<td>` to white with `!important`. Any element with a white or near-white background that doesn't have an explicit dark-mode background override will render as white-on-white text. The targeted classes (`.dark-text`, `.blue-text`, etc.) are the correct pattern; the bare `span, a, td` rule is a catch-all that will cause invisible text in any cell missing a dark background class. Scope this to a wrapper class or remove it and rely only on the targeted selectors.

---

**Issue E — Duplicate MSO conditional CSS block**

The following block appears verbatim twice in `<head>`:

```html
<!--[if gte mso 9]>
<style type="text/css">body,table,td{font-family: Helvetica Neue, Arial, sans-serif!important} ...</style>
<![endif]-->
```

Duplicate CSS in Outlook conditional comments increases parse overhead and can cause specificity conflicts if the blocks ever diverge on edit. Remove the second copy.
