# Limited time: Stand up to cancer as an AAdvantage® member
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-14**
## Executive summary

- This is American Airlines pivoting entirely out of flight/travel-deal territory into a cause-marketing send: donate to Stand Up To Cancer, earn 25 AAdvantage bonus miles per dollar. It's a genuine departure from the recent streak of destination deals (Aug 4), buy-miles pushes (July 16/21), and even the loyalty-roadmap email from Aug 7 — this is the first pure philanthropy play in the recent history. The "100 American" anniversary branding tying into SU2C gives it a legitimate campaign hook rather than feeling like a bolt-on.
- The problem is this isn't a deal for a traveler — it's a donation ask dressed in AAdvantage clothing. For a subscriber conditioned to open these emails for flight discounts or mile-buying offers, "give money, get miles" is a much softer, more optional action, and the email doesn't do much to make the mile-earning math feel compelling (25 miles/dollar is a low redemption rate framed vaguely against a "$25 minimum"). Solid execution, weak commercial pull.

## What's working

- Clear, single CTA — "Donate here today" button is unambiguous and impossible to miss.
- The hero image (crew + SU2C branding + "100 American" mark) is clean, on-brand, and renders without bugs.
- The offer mechanic (25 bonus miles per $1, $25 minimum, thru Sept 30) is stated plainly in bold text near the top.
- Personalized greeting ("Hi, Curt") and account snippet (AAdvantage Member 6V8****) at top ground it as a real member email.
- Legal/terms are present but tucked below the fold, not cluttering the primary message.

## What's weak

- No dollar or mile value is translated into something tangible — "25 miles per dollar" doesn't tell a reader what that's actually worth (e.g., a domestic flight needs 10,000-25,000+ miles, so a $25 donation nets 625 miles — a fraction most readers won't do the math on).
- This is a donation ask, not a deal — it doesn't reduce a price or offer something the recipient keeps, which is a much higher-friction action than "book this flight for $699."
- Fine print buried below undercuts the offer: bonus miles don't count toward status/tier qualification, and donations aren't refundable — that's a real dampener not surfaced near the CTA.
- Visual hierarchy dead-ends after the first screen — the second full-width plane/livery image adds nothing and just pushes footer content further down.
- No urgency treatment (no countdown, no "last chance") despite having a real deadline (Sept 30) — a missed opportunity compared to the July 21 "Last chance" remix that scored well.

## Recommendations

- 1. Translate the offer into a concrete example ("Donate $25, earn 625 AAdvantage miles") right under the bold offer line — vague per-dollar math is the single biggest drag on click-through here.
- 2. Move the "doesn't count toward status" and "non-refundable" caveats (or at least a one-line honest flag) closer to the CTA instead of only in the footer legal block — this is a cause-marketing email; trust matters more here, not less.
- 3. Cut the second full-bleed plane/livery image — it adds scroll with no new information and delays the footer/social proof.
- 4. Add a light urgency element (a "Now through Sept 30" badge or small countdown) — the brand has used countdown timers effectively before (July 21 send).
- 5. Consider adding a "why this matters" trust signal — a stat on funds raised to date or research funded — to justify the ask beyond "we partnered with SU2C."
