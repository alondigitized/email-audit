# Hi Curt, discover exclusive experiences as an AAdvantage® member
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-02**
## Executive summary

- This is the "AAdvantage Events" redemption catalog email — a straightforward tile grid selling eight different ways to burn miles on tickets and experiences (concerts, football, WWE, symphony, off-Broadway musical, Dallas Cowboys packages). It's a clean, well-organized layout with consistent card treatment, but there's no offer at all here — this is a "spend your miles" catalog, not a promotion, so it competes for attention with zero urgency or price hook. Compared to the Aug 7 "See what's coming for AAdvantage members" roadmap email (7/10) or the Sep 1 European destinations send (5/10), this one sits closer to the weaker end — it's a browse-and-redeem menu, not a compelling single-topic hero.
- Execution-wise it's clean: no render bugs, good visual hierarchy, sensible section grouping (Events tickets vs. Exclusive experiences). But eight nearly identical tiles with the same "Find tickets / Use miles today" button repeated verbatim ten times reads as generic inventory, not curated experience marketing — there's nothing here that says "we know Curt specifically."

## What's working

- Clean two-column tile grid renders without any layout breakage or overlapping text.
- Section headers ("AAdvantage Events tickets" vs. "Exclusive AAdvantage experiences") give the email a clear structural logic.
- Diverse experience categories (sports, music, theater) broaden appeal beyond a single interest.
- "You are why we fly" footer banner with app download CTAs is a nice consistent brand touch.
- AAdvantage member number is visible in the header, a small personalization signal.

## What's weak

- No concrete offer anywhere — no miles amount, no discount, no price reference, just generic "Use AAdvantage miles" language repeated across every tile.
- Eight tiles with near-identical CTA copy ("Find tickets" x4, "Use miles today" x4) creates visual monotony and no clear single hero to click.
- No urgency or time-bound framing on any of these — concerts, football, WWE, symphony all feel evergreen/stale, not tied to this specific week.
- No personalization beyond the member number — none of the eight experiences appear tailored to Curt's home city, past bookings, or mile balance.
- The subject line promises "exclusive experiences" but the body is a mile-redemption catalog, not anything genuinely exclusive or limited.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one hero experience and lead with it — a single high-quality image + specific event/date + specific miles cost, instead of eight competing tiles. This is the single biggest lift available.
- 2. Add real numbers: "25,000 miles for two Cowboys tickets" beats "Use miles today" every time — specificity drives clicks per the rubric.
- 3. Localize to the member — if American has Curt's home airport, lead with events near him instead of a generic national grid.
- 4. Vary the CTA copy per tile (e.g., "See Cowboys dates," "Hear Yo-Yo Ma," "Get WWE seats") instead of repeating "Find tickets"/"Use miles today" verbatim.
- 5. Add a deadline or inventory signal ("Cowboys packages selling fast") to create real urgency where none currently exists.

- **Subject Alt B:** `25,000 miles = ringside WWE seats, Curt`
