# Last chance: Get more from every mile you buy
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- This is a "last chance" urgency remix of the July 16 buy-miles email — same campaign, same mechanic, same creative skeleton, just with a countdown timer added and the subject line swapped from "Limited-time offer:" to "Last chance:". That structural self-awareness is actually fine; reminder emails work. The countdown (2 days, ~2.5 hours remaining) is the most effective new element and does meaningful work here.
- What undercuts the email is what it still doesn't tell Curt: the price per mile. The entire offer is a purchase decision — you're buying something — and the email gives you a tiered bonus table without ever showing how much the underlying miles cost. You can see what you'll *get* but not what you'll *pay*. That's a friction point that will bleed clicks.
- The brand cadence is also starting to strain. The July 16 email was the first outright money ask in a sequence that had been building goodwill through flights, partners, and experiences. Now, five days later, AA sends the same ask again. This is the sequence getting impatient.

## What's working

- **Countdown timer is genuinely effective.** The 2-day, 2-hour clock at the top of the email is the single biggest upgrade over the July 16 send — it turns abstract "limited time" into a live deadline.
- **Personalization is right.** "Hi Curt," plus the AAdvantage® Member badge both read immediately; this doesn't feel like a mass blast.
- **Tiered bonus table is transparent.** Showing the full bonus structure from 1,000 miles to 500,000+ lets Curt self-qualify without hunting through copy — a smart UX choice for a loyalty audience.
- **CTA is unambiguous.** "Buy miles" as the button copy is a clean verb + noun; no ambiguity about what happens next.

## What's weak

- **No price shown anywhere.** You can see how many bonus miles you'll receive, but not what miles cost. Without that, the table is incomplete for a purchase decision.
- **Five-day replay with a "last chance" veneer.** This email is structurally identical to July 16. The subject swap and countdown are real improvements but the body creative is recycled wholesale. Curt already saw this offer.
- **"You are why we fly" banner is dead weight.** The lifestyle brand strip at the bottom has no connection to the buy-miles offer — it reads like a footer template that never got swapped for campaign-specific creative.
- **No social proof or endorsement.** Buying miles is a discretionary spend. A short stat ("Members who bought miles redeemed for X destinations") would do real work here; there's nothing like that.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show the price.** The single highest-impact change: add a price-per-mile (or a sample calculation like "10,000 miles = $X, plus 5,000 bonus") next to or inside the bonus table. Without it, Curt has to click through just to find out if the deal is worth it.
- 2. **Differentiate the body creative.** The July 16 send already told Curt the full offer. For the reminder, lead with what he'd do with these miles — a specific redemption example ("enough for a Dallas–Cancún award") to remind him *why* he wants them.
- 3. **Cut or replace the "You are why we fly" strip.** Swap it for a mini-urgency callout tied to the timer: "Only 2 days left — miles expire from bonus eligibility July 22."
- 4. **Add a social proof line.** One sentence — "Thousands of AAdvantage members bought miles in the last 48 hours" — converts the urgency from manufactured to socially validated.
- 5. **Tighten subject specificity.** The subject names the mechanic but not the value. "Last chance: Buy miles, get up to 100% bonus — ends July 22" would outperform on specificity.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last chance: Up to 100% bonus miles — offer ends July 22`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Curt, your bonus miles window closes in 2 days`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Buy 10,000 miles, get 5,000 free. Deadline: 11:59 PM ET July 22.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The more you buy, the bigger your bonus — but not for long.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — unclosed `@media (prefers-color-scheme:dark)` block:**
```css
@media (prefers-color-scheme:dark) {
  ...
  [data-ogsc] span, [data-ogsc] a, [data-ogsc] td { color: #ffffff !important; }
/* ← missing closing } before </style> */
</style>
```
The closing `}` for the media query is absent. This is a CSS syntax error that invalidates the entire dark mode block in strict-parsing clients (Apple Mail, iOS Mail, Samsung Mail). All dark mode overrides — background swaps, text color inversions, CTA background — will silently fail.

**Google Fonts loaded twice:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto...">  <!-- in <head> -->
<style>@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto...');</style>  <!-- also in <head> -->
```
The `<link>` tag is the correct approach; the duplicate `@import` adds an extra network round-trip and is stripped by some clients (Gmail). Remove the `@import`.

**Duplicate MSO conditional comment blocks:**
The identical `<!--[if gte mso 9]>...<![endif]-->` style block appears twice verbatim in `<head>`. No functional harm, but doubles the MSO CSS payload unnecessarily.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks pinch-to-zoom on mobile. This is an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4 — Resize Text) and is flagged by accessibility checkers. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**`.appleLinks a { color: #000000 !important; }` — dark mode conflict:**
This hardcoded black will render as invisible text if the link sits on a dark background in Apple Mail dark mode, since the dark mode rule sets `span, a, td { color: #ffffff !important; }` which the `!important` on `.appleLinks` will override due to selector specificity.

**Global `* { line-height: 100% }` with `mso-line-height-rule: exactly`:**
Applying `line-height: 100%` universally with exact MSO enforcement risks text clipping on tall glyphs or multi-line spans. Prefer scoped selectors on `td` and `p` elements only.
