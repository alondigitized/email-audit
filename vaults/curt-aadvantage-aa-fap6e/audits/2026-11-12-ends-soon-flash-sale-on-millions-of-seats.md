# Ends soon: Flash sale on millions of seats
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-12**
## Executive summary

- This is the "ends soon" reminder for the Nov. 10 "Flash sale on millions of seats" send. It has the same miles-redemption offer, now with a red countdown timer at about 4 hours left. The countdown is a real urgency device and the hero is simple. The subject's "Ends soon" prefix gives a subscriber a reason to open a second email on the same promo.
- The body is a stack of six destination tiles, each with a different button label ("Book now," "Book today," "Find flights," "Search flights," "Find fares"). That dilutes the one clear message. The account card at the bottom shows 0 award miles and 0 Loyalty Points. This recipient can't redeem the headline offer, so the email reads as a taunt. The fine print runs about a third of the screen, and the promo window (Nov. 9–11) is buried at the very bottom.
- Judgment: good urgency mechanics, but it's a repeat send with weak relevance for a zero-balance member. It's a middling reminder.

## What's working

- The countdown banner is high-contrast red and unmissable, and it sits at the top.
- "More seats for less" is a short, clear headline.
- The destination tiles give concrete mileage prices ("U.S. from 8,000* miles," "Europe from 22,500* miles").
- Each tile follows one consistent layout: photo, price, one line of copy, one button.
- The "Ends soon" subject prefix signals a deadline.

## What's weak

- The account card shows 0 award miles, so the miles-only offer doesn't apply to this member.
- Five different button labels across six tiles add noise and give no sense of a primary action.
- This is the second send of the same offer in two days (Nov. 10 "Flash sale on millions of seats"), with no new creative.
- The actual sale deadline (11:59 PM CT, Nov. 11) appears only in the fine print. The body says "next 72 hours," which conflicts with the timer showing 4 hours.
- The legal block is very long, and the footer has an empty box beside the app download.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment by balance. For zero-balance members, swap the offer for "Earn miles toward these fares" or a miles-purchase or credit-card CTA. Redemption copy shouldn't go to people with nothing to redeem.
- 2. Cut the six tiles to two or three, and use one consistent verb on every button, such as "See flights."
- 3. State the exact deadline next to the timer: "Ends tonight, 11:59 PM CT."
- 4. Fix the "72 hours" copy so it matches the timer.
- 5. Add one social proof or availability line, such as "Seats left on NYC routes."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Tonight only: flights from 8,000 miles`
- **Subject Alt B:** `4 hours left: miles fares to Europe and Asia`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sale ends 11:59 PM CT. See where your miles can take you.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fares from 8,000 miles one-way. Last call.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's markup is mostly sound: XHTML doctype, `lang="en"`, a preheader, and MSO fallbacks. Two tracked links contain a literal space in the `x=` query parameter, which made the QA probe fail, and one image still loads over `http://`. The missing header results (List-Unsubscribe, Authentication-Results) are most likely relay artifacts, not confirmed defects.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **[FAIL] Two links broke the probe.** Both are on `https://l.loyalty.ms.aa.com/rts/go2.aspx`:
  - "Download our mobile app", `h=6690333`
  - "American Airlines", `h=6690337`
- **Likely cause:** both `x=` values contain an unencoded space, `...%7cAA Brand-Airline%7cERINC...`. Every other delimiter in that value is `%7c`-encoded. Python's HTTP client rejects a space (and any control character) in the request target, which matches "URL can't contain control characters". I can't see the raw `href` in the truncated source, so the exact bytes are unconfirmed.
- **Impact:**
  - Browsers usually percent-encode spaces, so real users probably reach the destination.
  - Strict clients and security scanners may reject or mangle these links.
  - Both landing pages are unverified.
- **Probe coverage:** 12 links were probed and 22 skipped. The skipped links, including footer and social ones, are untested.
- **Tracking:** links use the Responsys redirect pattern (`go2.aspx?h=…&tp=…&pi=…&hp2=…`). The click domain `l.loyalty.ms.aa.com` matches the sender subdomain `loyalty.ms.aa.com`. No other tracking issues found.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Relative background URL.** In the mobile media query, `#swapbg { background-image: url("../20230508_HTML-32134_M_bg.png") !important; }` is a relative path. It resolves against nothing in an email client, so the mobile background will 404 or silently fail.
- **Insecure image.** The "oneworld logo" `<img>` loads `http://i.loyalty.ms.aa.com/wpm/1021/ContentUploads/american_airlines/CTAs/oneworld_logo_special_online_80x80.gif`. Clients that block mixed content may drop it. The same host is likely reachable over HTTPS.
- **Dark-mode color override.** `@media (prefers-color-scheme:dark) { span, a, td { color:#ffffff !important; } }` forces white text on every `td`, `a` and `span`. Any cell that keeps a light background in dark mode (one not covered by `.lightblue-bg` or `.cta-bg`) would show white text on white. I can't confirm this from the truncated source.
- **Zoom restriction.** `<meta name="viewport" content="…maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom where it is honored, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern. The `* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none }` rule adds to it.
- **Redundant code.** The `<!--[if gte mso 9]>` style block is duplicated verbatim, and Roboto is loaded twice (a `<link>` plus `@import`). This is harmless but adds weight, and Gmail strips both.
- **Not assessable.** Image `alt` text and heading structure fall in the truncated portion of the source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The visible source contains no unresolved tokens such as `{{`, `%%` or `${`.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **[WARN] List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) not found.** The QA tool itself says the AgentMail relay may strip these headers. This can't be confirmed as a sender defect. The sender should verify against a direct-delivery copy of the message.
- **[WARN] Authentication-Results not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown, and this is a relay limitation, not evidence of failure.
- **Footer unsubscribe and postal address.** The QA summary says an unsubscribe link is always probed, but no unsubscribe result is listed. Footer content is outside the visible source, so I can't confirm either.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- **No UTM parameters found.** Attribution relies on Responsys's own `x=` campaign string (`EML|SOLO|20261111|MKT|USPFMOC|AA Brand-Airline|ERINC|US_ROUTE_TRAN_MAXMILES`). This is a design choice, not a defect, but site analytics would depend on the redirect passing it through.
- **Destinations not verified for the two failed links.** Because the probe failed, I can't confirm where they land or whether the app-download link resolves to the right store page.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Encode the space** in the Responsys `x=` value (`AA%20Brand-Airline`, or use a hyphen or underscore in the campaign name). Then re-probe the two failing links.
2. **Change the oneworld logo `src` to `https://`.**
3. **Replace the relative `url("../20230508_HTML-32134_M_bg.png")`** in `#swapbg` with an absolute HTTPS URL.
4. **Scope the dark-mode rule.** Replace the blanket `span, a, td { color:#ffffff !important }` with class-based overrides.
5. **Remove `maximum-scale=1`** from the viewport meta tag.
6. **Check the headers on a direct-delivery copy.** Confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment, since the relay may have hidden them.
7. **Remove the duplicated MSO style block** and the duplicate Roboto load.
