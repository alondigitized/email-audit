# 3,000 bonus miles could be yours – register today
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- This is AA's seventh email to a new AAdvantage member in fifteen days, and for the first time, the brand is making a direct activation ask rather than educating. That pivot is strategically smart: the prior six sends established the miles framework (especially the 5/27 tier-progression send), and this email cashes that equity with a time-bounded 3,000-mile bonus tied to 4 eligible flights registered by 9/14/2026. The offer is clear, the mechanic is visualized simply, and the "Hi Carl" greeting grounds it in a real membership relationship.
- Two structural choices undercut it, though. The "You are why we fly" brand block at the bottom adds scroll with zero conversion value — this is an activation send, not a brand campaign. And the mid-email navigation bar (Book / Buy & Co-ops / Shop / Plan) hands the reader escape routes right after the CTA. Both belong in the trash for this send.
- The offer is right. The execution is a competent template, not a precision-crafted activation. For the seventh email in a crowded inbox, the bar should be higher.

## What's working

- **Concrete number in the headline and subject**: "3,000 bonus miles" appears in both places — zero ambiguity about the reward.
- **"How it works" visual mechanic**: The two-step icon flow (Book & fly 4 flights → 3,000 miles) strips the offer down to what matters without fine-print-first framing.
- **Single dominant CTA**: "Register today" blue button appears below the mechanic with no competing links nearby — strong click target.
- **Name personalization**: "Hi Carl" in the body is a small signal that this isn't a cold blast, reinforcing the member relationship established at signup.
- **Right timing in the sequence**: After six educational emails, pivoting to a concrete registration ask with a deadline is correct sequencing — this email should arrive around here.

## What's weak

- **"Could be yours" hedges a qualified offer**: The subject treats the reader as a prospect, not an enrolled member. If Carl qualifies, say so — the conditional framing creates false uncertainty.
- **Dense fine print block undermines trust**: A wall of terms text at the bottom makes the offer feel more asterisked than it needs to be for a 3,000-mile standard-enrollment promotion.
- **"You are why we fly" is dead weight**: A brand-sentiment image block with no CTA adds length without adding conversion pressure — it exists to feel nice, not to drive registration.
- **Mid-email navigation bar is an escape hatch**: The "Book / Buy & Co-ops / Shop / Plan" band interrupts the read flow between body copy and footer at exactly the wrong moment.
- **4-flight bar has no context for a new member**: Carl just joined. "Book and fly 4 eligible flights" sounds like a lot without a quick frame — "most members do this in a few months of regular travel" would defuse the anxiety.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to treat Carl as already-qualified** — drop "could be yours" and go declarative or imperative:
- **Subject Alt A:** `Register now: 3,000 bonus miles are waiting for you`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Fly 4 flights, earn 3,000 miles — register by Sep 14`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Just book and fly 4 eligible flights — miles post automatically.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your first big miles boost starts with a single click to register.`
- 2. **Cut the "You are why we fly" block entirely** — this email has one job. Every element below the CTA that doesn't re-enforce the registration ask is friction.
- 3. **Remove or push the mid-email navigation bar to the footer** — nav links belong after conversion, not between offer and close.
- 4. **Contextualize the 4-flight requirement** — add one sentence near the "How it works" step: "Most members complete this with normal travel over a few months." This converts the barrier from an obstacle into a reassurance.
- 5. **Compress the fine print** — reduce to 2-3 sentences + a linked "Full terms" for the rest. The legal text as-rendered signals complexity that the actual offer doesn't deserve.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 3.1 — Duplicate MSO conditional block (copy-paste artifact)**
The `<!--[if gte mso 9]>` style block appears **verbatim twice** in `<head>`. Outlook processes both; second instance adds dead weight (~330 bytes) and increases maintenance risk if one copy is later edited out of sync.

```html
<!--[if gte mso 9]>
  <style>body,table,td{font-family: Helvetica Neue...}</style>
<![endif]-->
<!-- identical block immediately follows -->
<!--[if gte mso 9]>
  <style>body,table,td{font-family: Helvetica Neue...}</style>
<![endif]-->
```

**Issue 3.2 — Double Google Fonts load**
Font is loaded via both a `<link>` tag and an `@import` inside `<style>`. The `@import` is redundant, adds a serial network round-trip in clients that do fetch external CSS, and doubles the payload in those that don't block external resources.

```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto...">  <!-- first load -->
<style>
  @import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto...');  <!-- redundant -->
</style>
```

**Issue 3.3 — Font name typo in scrollmenu fallback stack**
Desktop CSS has `font-family: Roboto, ariel, helvetica, sans-serif`. `ariel` is not a valid font name — should be `Arial`. Clients that can't render Roboto will skip straight to `helvetica` instead of using the intended fallback.

**Issue 3.4 — `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom (WCAG 1.4.4)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Preventing zoom fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Change to `maximum-scale=5` or remove the constraint.

**Issue 3.5 — Universal `* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none }` conflicts with body declaration**
The body rule sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%` (correct), but the wildcard `*` rule immediately overrides it with `none` on all elements, which can suppress responsive text scaling on iOS WebKit renders.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**Issue 5.1 — Unsubscribe and physical address not visible in truncated source**
CAN-SPAM requires a functioning opt-out mechanism and valid physical postal address in every commercial email. These are typically in the footer — not yet visible. Flag for confirmation in full source review.

**Issue 5.2 — DMARC alignment risk (ESP subdomain)**
`loyalty.ms.aa.com` as the envelope sender means DMARC alignment depends on SPF passing for `loyalty.ms.aa.com` OR a valid DKIM signature with `d=aa.com`. Confirm `aa.com` publishes `p=reject` or `p=quarantine` and that the signing domain aligns. A misconfigured relay subdomain is the most common cause of bulk mail landing in spam for major airline programs.
