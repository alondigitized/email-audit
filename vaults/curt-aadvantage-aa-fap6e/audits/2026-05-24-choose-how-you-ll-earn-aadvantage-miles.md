# Choose how you’ll earn AAdvantage® miles
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-24**
## Executive summary

- This is the fifth AAdvantage onboarding email in roughly nine days, and the sequence is starting to feel repetitive. The hero — "The more you earn, the better the travel" — is a soft, philosophical lead that doesn't promise anything concrete. Below it sits a four-tile grid of earning paths (American flights, partner airlines, dining, hotel/car), then a co-brand card pitch, and finally a "Just for you: Earn 1,000 bonus miles" register-now block.
- The buried lede is the 1,000-mile bonus offer — that should have been the hero. Instead it's parked below the credit-card pitch where most readers won't scroll. The earning-paths grid is educational and on-brand, but it's the same teaching beat we got in the 5/17 "Everything you need to know to earn travel benefits" send, just reframed with photography.
- Judgement: a 5/10 send. Pretty, coherent, brand-consistent — but the actual conversion lever (the 1,000-mile bonus) is hidden, and the email is the fourth educational/onboarding beat in a row when this subscriber is ready for something to actually do.

## What's working

- Clean four-tile earning grid is visually scannable and reinforces the "many paths to miles" idea.
- Hero photography (couple at scenic overlook) is warm and aspirational, on-brand for AA travel.
- 1,000-mile bonus offer is concrete and credible — a real incentive worth registering for.
- "You are why we fly" footer line is a nice brand sign-off that humanizes the email.
- Visual hierarchy within each tile is consistent — image, label, short copy.

## What's weak

- The 1,000-mile bonus is buried below the credit-card pitch instead of leading.
- Hero headline is abstract poetry ("The more you earn, the better the travel") with no offer.
- This is the 5th AAdvantage email in 9 days — fatigue is real and the content overlaps with the 5/17 send.
- Primary CTA "Learn more about earning" is generic and education-flavored, not action-flavored.
- Co-brand credit-card tile is mixed into the earning grid, blurring the line between earning paths and a financial product pitch.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the 1,000-mile bonus.** Make it the hero. Subject, preheader, and top-of-fold should all be about the registration offer.
- 2. **Move the credit-card pitch below the bonus offer.** Right now it's competing with the conversion moment.
- 3. **Replace "Learn more about earning" with a specific verb-noun CTA** — "Register for 1,000 bonus miles" or "Pick your earning path."
- 4. **Slow the cadence.** Five emails in nine days to a new member is too much; this content could have waited a week.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Register now: earn 1,000 bonus AAdvantage® miles`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 1,000-mile head start is waiting`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Two ways to earn — pick yours and we'll add 1,000 miles to your account.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Activate your bonus by registering — takes under a minute.`

# Technical Audit — American Airlines AAdvantage Email

## 1. Technical Summary
HTML source shows a standard responsive email template with XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype and MSO conditional fallbacks. Truncation prevents full audit of body links, unsubscribe footer, and tracking pixels — findings below are limited to what is visible in the provided source.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- `preconnect` links point to ESP tracking domain `l.loyalty.ms.aa.com/rts/go2.aspx` with opaque `h=`, `tp=`, and `pi=` parameters. This is consistent with ExactTarget/Salesforce Marketing Cloud-style click tracking, but the use of `preconnect` for tracking redirect URLs is unusual — `preconnect` is typically for asset hosts (fonts, CDNs), not redirect endpoints. Browsers/email clients that honor `preconnect` will open a TCP+TLS handshake to the tracking host on render, which may inadvertently fire open-equivalent signals.
- Two `preconnect` entries to the same host (`h=5711406` and `h=5711407`) — one with `crossorigin`, one without. Likely redundant; only the `crossorigin` variant is needed for CORS-fetched resources.
- No `https://fonts.gstatic.com` preconnect despite loading Google Fonts via `fonts.googleapis.com`. The gstatic origin serves the actual font files and would benefit more from preconnect than the tracking host.
- Body anchor `href` values not visible in truncated source — cannot verify UTM tagging, redirect chains, or broken links.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- Doctype `XHTML 1.0 Transitional` is standard for email and renders correctly across major clients.
- `<meta name="viewport" content="...maximum-scale=1">` — `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-zoom on iOS, an accessibility regression (WCAG 1.4.4 Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.
- `<meta name="x-apple-disable-message-reformatting">` is missing a self-closing `/` — minor XHTML validity issue, no rendering impact.
- Google Fonts loaded via both `<link>` and `@import` — duplicate fetch. Email clients largely ignore web fonts anyway (Gmail strips, Outlook ignores); fallback stack `Helvetica Neue, Arial, sans-serif` is correctly set in MSO conditional.
- Duplicate `<!--[if gte mso 9]>` style blocks back-to-back — harmless but redundant.
- `* { max-height: 1000000px; }` universal selector is a known Outlook.com hack but applies to all elements; acceptable for email.
- `mso-line-height-rule: exactly` on `*` combined with `line-height: 100%` may clip descenders in Outlook. Verify in Litmus/Email on Acid.
- No `role`, `lang` on body, or image `alt` attributes visible in truncated portion — cannot verify a11y fully.
