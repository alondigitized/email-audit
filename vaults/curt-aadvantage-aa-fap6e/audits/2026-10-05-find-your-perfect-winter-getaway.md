# Find your perfect winter getaway
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-05**
## Executive summary

- This is a winter-travel catalog with a generic hero and five near-identical image tiles. It never commits to one offer. "Winter travel starts here" and a blue "Book now" button sit above a stack of Japan, U.S. ski, Alps, beach and vacation-package tiles. Only two tiles carry a concrete number: Japan from $949* and up to $200** off an American Airlines Vacations package.
- Compared with the Sept 21 send ("Europe sale ends soon: Book round-trip fares from $622*"), this is a step backward. That email had one fare, one destination and a deadline. This one has no deadline in the body and no clear lead tile. The account module also repeats the Sept 25 summary's problem: it shows 0 award miles and 0 loyalty points. That is a missed chance to say how many miles a trip would earn.
- The email renders cleanly, with no broken images or overlaps. The fine print is enormous, though. It runs as long as the marketing content, with blackout dates and two promo-code blocks (SKI2026, SEA2026) that the body never mentions. Overall it is a competent but diffuse send, a 5 or 6.

## What's working

- The render is clean. Images load, text is readable, and the blue buttons are consistent and easy to tap.
- The Japan tile ("from $949*") gives a real price anchor and a clear verb on the button ("Find flights").
- The imagery is on-season and matches the copy: snow, Alps, beach.
- The brand voice and layout match American's earlier sends, so it looks trusted.
- The account module does not overclaim. It shows the true zero balances.

## What's weak

- There is no single hero offer. The hero has a generic "Book now" and no price, so the eye has nowhere to land.
- Five tiles carry equal weight, so Japan's $949 fare gets no priority over the rest.
- CTA copy rotates between "Book now," "Book today," "Search flights" and "Find flights" with no logic. Three of the five are vague.
- No deadline is visible in the body. The booking window (to Oct 18) is buried in the footer.
- The promo-code details are only in the legal text, so a reader has to hunt for what "up to $200**" requires. That includes the $3,500 minimum spend and the 3-night stay. This undercuts the honesty of the offer.
- The account block shows 0 miles and 0 points with no nudge, so a zero-balance member gets nothing from it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the Japan $949 fare as the hero. Put the price in the headline and add a visible deadline, such as "Book by Oct 18."
- 2. Cut the tiles to three at most, and use one CTA verb pattern like "See Japan fares" or "See ski deals."
- 3. Put the $200 vacation-package offer and its promo code in the body, with the minimum spend stated plainly.
- 4. Replace the zero-balance account module with an earn message, such as "Earn miles on this trip."
- 5. Shorten the legal block, or link to it instead of printing it in full.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Tokyo from $949 — book by Oct 18`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Ski trips: save up to $200 on packages`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Japan fares from $949*, plus $200 off ski and beach packages.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Pick your winter escape: snow, sun or Japan. Fares end Oct 18.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email has a sound structure (`lang="en"`, `<title>`, a hidden preheader, MSO fallbacks, a dark-mode block). Two automated link checks failed, and the source has several defects: a relative-path background image, an HTTP image, and viewport and dark-mode rules that cause accessibility risk. Header-based compliance and authentication checks could not be verified.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **403 on a tracked CTA.** "Save from slopes to shore" returns 403 at `https://l.loyalty.ms.aa.com/rts/go2.aspx?h=6623704&tp=i-1NGB-GT-1PaM-…&pi=dEYAxGXLaTOcab4a7…`. This is a Responsys-style redirector (`/rts/go2.aspx`). A 403 on a click-tracker often means the redirector is blocking the QA probe's user agent or IP, not that the link is dead for recipients. The failure is real in the QA data, but it does not prove recipients are affected. Retest with a browser-like UA.
- **Control character in the "American Airlines" link.** The probe reported `URL can't contain control characters`. The URL shown in the QA output is cut off at `…dEYAxGXLaTOcab4a7`. The same `pi=` value appears intact in the other link, so the `href` likely contains an embedded newline or tab, probably from line-wrapping in the template. Browsers strip these per the WHATWG URL spec. Strict parsers and some security scanners may reject the link. The truncated HTML excerpt doesn't let me confirm the exact character.
- **Unencoded space in the tracking value.** The `x=` parameter contains `AA Brand-Airline` with a literal space: `x=c%7cEML%7cSOLO%7c20261005%7cMKT%7cUSPFMOC%7cAA Brand-Airline%7cERINC%7c…`. The pipes are encoded but the space isn't, which is inconsistent and may break strict parsers.
- **Probe coverage.** Only 12 of 31 links were probed, and 19 were skipped as footer or utility links. Unprobed links are unverified, not confirmed good.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Relative background path in the mobile CSS.** `#swapbg { background-image: url("../20230508_HTML-32134_M_bg.png") !important; … }` is a relative path from an old 2023 build. It resolves against the mail client's origin and will 404, so the mobile background swap fails.
- **Zoom blocked.** `<meta name="viewport" content="… maximum-scale=1">` and `* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }` block pinch-zoom and text scaling, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 risk.
- **Dark-mode rule forces white text.** `@media (prefers-color-scheme:dark) { span, a, td { color: #ffffff !important; } }` applies to every span, link and cell. Any element on a light background without a `.lightblue-bg` or `.cta-bg` class will render white-on-light and be unreadable. The excerpt is truncated, so I can't confirm which elements are affected.
- **HTTP image.** The "oneworld logo" uses `src="http://i.loyalty.ms.aa.com/wpm/1021/ContentUploads/american_airlines/CTAs/oneworld_logo_special_online_80x80.gif"`. Non-HTTPS images can be blocked or proxied with a warning.
- **Duplicated block.** The identical `<!--[if gte mso 9]>` style block appears twice.
- **Redundant font loading.** Roboto loads through both `<link>` and `@import`, and `@import` is ignored or stripped by several clients.
- **Image alt coverage.** I can't assess it beyond the oneworld logo, which has alt text, because the source is truncated.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The visible source shows no unresolved merge tokens such as `%%…%%`, `{{…}}` or `[FIRSTNAME]`. The `hp2=` parameter appears to be a per-recipient hash. The source is truncated, so the body and footer were not reviewed.

## 5. Compliance
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found.** The QA tool says the AgentMail relay may not capture these headers, so this is **unverified, not a confirmed defect**. Large senders must support one-click unsubscribe (Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender rules), so check the raw message headers.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown for the same relay reason. The sender domain is `loyalty.ms.aa.com`, and alignment can't be checked from the data provided.
- **Footer not reviewed.** The in-body unsubscribe link and postal address are not visible in the truncated source. The QA note says an unsubscribe link is always probed, but no result for it appears in the output.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- **No standard UTM parameters.** Campaign attribution is carried in a custom pipe-delimited `x=` value: `EML|SOLO|20261005|MKT|USPFMOC|AA Brand-Airline|ERINC|US_ROUTE_TRAN_WINTER_TRAVEL`. The date `20261005` matches the send date and the campaign label matches the winter-travel theme. Downstream analytics must parse the `x=` value, so the lack of `utm_*` is a limitation, not an error.
- **Landing alignment unverified for the 403 link.** The probe never resolved the final destination, so the "Save from slopes to shore" CTA's landing page could not be checked against the creative.
- **All links go through the redirector.** The final destination is not visible in the source, so redirect behavior can't be audited without following the redirects.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Retest the 403 CTA (`h=6623704`) with a browser UA. If it still fails, report it to the ESP, because a real 403 means a dead primary CTA.
2. Find and remove the embedded newline or tab in the "American Airlines" `href` (`h=6623687`), and percent-encode the space in `AA Brand-Airline` as `%20`.
3. Change the oneworld logo `src` to `https://`.
4. Replace the relative `url("../20230508_HTML-32134_M_bg.png")` with an absolute HTTPS URL, or remove `#swapbg` if it's unused.
5. Remove `maximum-scale=1` and `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` to restore zoom and text scaling.
6. Scope the dark-mode `span, a, td { color:#fff !important }` rule to elements that have dark backgrounds.
7. Remove the duplicate MSO style block and the redundant Roboto `@import`.
8. Verify `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, SPF, DKIM and DMARC from the raw `.eml`, not the relay copy.
