# Limited-time offer: Get more bonus miles from every mile you buy
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a "buy miles" email — the first outright request for money in AA's recent string of sends to Curt. Every prior email in this sequence (flights, partners, experiences, sweepstakes) earned goodwill by giving Curt reasons to engage with the program; this one asks for cash outlay in return for bonus miles. That's a harder pitch, and the email does a decent job of it: a live countdown timer, a clear tiered-bonus table, and a direct "Buy miles" CTA above the fold carry the sell. The personalization (Hi Curt) and member badge reinforce that this is an account-holder offer, not a cold blast.
- The weakness is the economics. The lower tiers — where most casual members will operate — deliver barely 10% bonus (500 miles on a 5k–9k purchase). The headline implies universally generous bonuses, but the table quietly reveals that the value only gets compelling at 131k+ miles. That gap between the promise in the subject and the reality in the table creates a credibility dent for anyone who reads far enough.
- One flag from the prior send history: the July 8 email used the identical subject-line opener "Limited-time offer:" and the July 8 CTA was to earn 5X miles, not spend money. AA is recycling urgency framing while escalating the ask — members who open enough emails will start to feel the formula.

## What's working

- **Countdown timer is real and prominent.** 7 days, 02 hours, 40 minutes visible in the hero — deadline urgency is baked into the render, not just the copy.
- **"Buy miles" CTA is immediate.** Blue button appears above the fold before the table, capturing any member who doesn't need to read the fine print.
- **Tier table is clean and scannable.** Two-column layout, no clutter — the mechanics are easy to parse even at small text sizes.
- **Personalized greeting + AAdvantage member badge.** Both reinforce that this is targeted to Curt's existing account status, not a generic prospect send.
- **"You are why we fly" brand block.** Good tonal balance after a transactional ask — ends the email on warmth rather than a second hard sell.

## What's weak

- **The lower tiers (5k–9k miles) deliver only ~10% bonus.** The headline over-promises for most members who won't buy 131k+ miles. This is visible in the table.
- **Subject line reuses the "Limited-time offer:" prefix** — this is the second consecutive send (after July 8) with that exact opener. It erodes urgency by familiarity.
- **No social proof anywhere.** Zero reviews, no "X members already purchased," no counter of miles sold. A transactional ask would be reinforced by any form of validation.
- **The table doesn't show percentage bonus**, only absolute miles. Casual members won't do the math and will misjudge the deal's generosity without a "% bonus" column.
- **App download widget in footer is competing for attention.** An additional CTA pathway below a transactional email creates split focus at the exact moment you want a single decision.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a "% bonus" column to the table.** Making the value legible (e.g., "+100% at 500k miles") would help members self-select into the right tier instead of defaulting to the smallest purchase and feeling underwhelmed.
- 2. **Break the "Limited-time offer:" subject prefix pattern.** It's appeared twice in eight days. Rotate to something that leads with the hook, not the urgency signal.
- 3. **Move the deadline date into the hero copy.** "Offer ends July 22" in bold text near the countdown would reinforce the timer's urgency for members who ignore animated countdowns.
- 4. **Add a social-proof line.** Even "Join thousands of AAdvantage members boosting their balance" costs nothing and adds credibility to a pay-to-play ask.
- 5. **Suppress the app download widget on this template.** One decision point per transactional email.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Curt, your miles go further — buy now through July 22`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Double your miles: buy before July 22 and get up to 100% bonus`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The more you buy, the more you earn — top tiers unlock up to 500,000 bonus miles.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Seven days left: your AAdvantage balance can grow faster than flying ever could.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CONFIRMED BUG] Unclosed `@media (prefers-color-scheme:dark)` block**

```css
/* inside <style type="text/css"> */
@media (prefers-color-scheme:dark) {
    ...
    [data-ogsc] span, [data-ogsc] a, [data-ogsc] td { color: #ffffff !important; }
/* ← closing } for @media is missing before </style> */
```
The dark-mode `@media` block is never closed. In permissive parsers this is silently recovered, but in stricter clients (some Samsung Mail, older Android webviews) the entire second `<style>` block may be discarded, breaking both dark mode and Outlook-Genesis overrides.

**[CONFIRMED] Duplicate MSO conditional style block**

The `<!--[if gte mso 9]>` block with font fallback rules appears **twice** in `<head>`. Harmless in practice but adds ~500 bytes and signals a template assembly error.

```html
<!--[if gte mso 9]><style ...>body,table,td{font-family:...}</style><![endif]-->
<!--[if gte mso 9]><style ...>body,table,td{font-family:...}</style><![endif]-->  ← duplicate
```

**[CONFIRMED] Double Roboto font request**

The same Google Fonts URL is loaded twice — once via `<link>` and again via `@import` inside a `<style>` block:

```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto:ital,wght@0,400;0,700;1,500&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
...
<style>
  @import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto:ital,wght@0,400;0,700;1,500&display=swap');
</style>
```
One should be removed. Prefer the `<link>` form; `@import` inside `<style>` blocks is blocked entirely by several clients (notably Yahoo Mail).

**[CONFIRMED] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Disables user pinch-to-zoom on mobile, violating WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[CONFIRMED] Wildcard `text-size-adjust: none`**

```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```
Applying `text-size-adjust: none` to all elements (not just `body`) suppresses browser readability adjustments globally, which can create accessibility failures on low-vision displays. Scope this to `body` only.
