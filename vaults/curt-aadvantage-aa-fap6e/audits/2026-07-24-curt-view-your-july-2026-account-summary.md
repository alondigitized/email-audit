# Curt, view your July 2026 account summary
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- This is American Airlines' monthly AAdvantage account summary — a transactional-flavored send that breaks a six-email streak of pure promotionals (destination deals, buy-miles, partner offers). That cadence reset alone earns it goodwill: a subscriber who just saw the July 21 "Last chance: buy miles" pitch is far more receptive to checking their own account status than they would be to a seventh promo in a row. The personalization with Curt's first name in the subject and the account summary widget at the top create an immediate "this is about me" hook that no generic blast can match.
- The weakness is in the mid-email transition. After the account widget, the email pivots to partner "exclusive offers" but renders them at a zoom and image-card size that obscures any concrete pricing. Below that, a nighttime cityscape section promoting what appears to be a program year or card acquisition offer competes for attention without a clear through-line. The app download section at bottom further dilutes focus. The email tries to do three distinct jobs (account report, partner deals, app acquisition) and none of the latter two land with the conviction of the first.
- Still, an account summary with this level of personalization, clean rendering, and well-timed cadence placement is among the stronger sends in this inbox sequence. It's not a sales home run, but it's a relationship-building email that earns the next click.

## What's working

- **Personalization is front-loaded.** "Curt" in the subject + the account summary widget at the top makes the email feel individual from first glance — not just a mail-merge name drop.
- **Cadence break earns attention.** After back-to-back promotional sends (July 16/21 buy-miles, July 22 Australia deals), a transactional account summary reads as high-signal rather than noise.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping elements, no lorem ipsum — the email displays correctly top to bottom.
- **Account summary is placed as the hero.** The flight/points data widget at the top is the right call; it validates the subject line instantly and confirms the email is genuinely about Curt's account.
- **App section has both badges.** App Store and Google Play are both present with a phone mockup, which is a minor but complete execution detail.

## What's weak

- **"CA for flier with exclusive offers"** is oddly worded and feels like a geo-segmentation label that was never dressed up into real copy. It reads as an internal targeting tag, not a headline.
- **Partner offer tiles lack visible pricing.** The three mid-email offer cards show lifestyle imagery but no specific discount, dollar amount, or bonus miles figure — making them decorative rather than actionable.
- **The cityscape/program-year section is opaque.** The bottom large-image block with a nighttime skyline doesn't communicate its offer at a glance — the value proposition (card acquisition? loyalty year rollover?) is buried in text that requires careful reading.
- **Three competing CTAs.** Account summary → View all offers → App download is too many directions for a single send, especially when none of the offers below the fold have specific pricing.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rename the offer section to real copy.** Replace "CA for flier with exclusive offers" with something like "Your exclusive offers this month" or "July member deals, just for you." This removes the leaked-segment label feeling immediately.
- 2. **Put at least one specific offer figure on the cards.** Even "Earn 3x miles at IHG" or "Up to 20% off rental cars" turns the visual tiles from decoration into reasons to click. This is the single highest-lift change.
- 3. **Collapse the cityscape section or make the offer explicit.** Either cut it or replace the headline with a concrete benefit ("Start your next AAdvantage year — free through [date]"). A large image with no clear offer just stalls scroll momentum.
- 4. **Pick one CTA to anchor the email.** The account summary should link to the full statement; drop or downgrade the app section to a footer text link rather than a full image module.
- 5. **Add a forward-looking hook.** This is the July summary on July 24 — six days left in the month. A line like "You have 6 days to earn miles that count toward your July total" gives the account summary real urgency rather than retrospective-only framing.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Curt, your July miles summary is ready`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your July AAdvantage activity — 6 days left to earn`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `See what you earned this month and grab exclusive deals before July ends.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Points earned, exclusive offers waiting, and a few days left to boost your balance.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta** — prevents users from zooming on mobile. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Evidence:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Remove `maximum-scale=1`; AA controls layout via responsive CSS, not zoom locking.

**`.show { display: none; }` in base styles** — a class named `show` defaults to hidden. This is either a misnaming bug or the class is toggled open by media query later. The mobile media query defines `*[class=showmobile]` etc., but `show` itself has no override in the visible CSS. If any element carries only `class="show"`, it will be invisible on desktop. Evidence:
```css
.show { display: none; }
```

**`input { display: none; }` declared twice** — appears in both the carousel 1 and carousel 2 blocks. Globally hiding all `<input>` elements is intentional for the CSS-only carousel trick but will also suppress any real form inputs (e.g., hidden tracking pixels via `<input type="hidden">`). The double declaration is harmless but signals copy-paste without cleanup.

**Duplicate media query rules** — the `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)` block declares `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=hide]`, `*[class=showmobile]`, `*[class=showmobile2]`, `*[class=showmobile3]` twice (once in the main block, again under the "CSS for Yahoo no spaces" comment). The second set is identical and redundant; it inflates payload without effect.

**`@font-face` declares `.woff` only** — no `.woff2` source, no `.ttf` fallback. `.woff` support is sufficient for email clients that honor web fonts (Apple Mail, Outlook iOS), but `.woff2` would reduce font payload ~30%. Low priority.

**No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` styles** — not a hard bug, but AA brand colors (navy/red on white) risk poor contrast in forced dark mode. No issues confirmed without seeing rendered content, but worth flagging as a gap.
