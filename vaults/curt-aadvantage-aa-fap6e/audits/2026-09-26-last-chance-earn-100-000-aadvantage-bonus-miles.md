# Last chance: Earn 100,000 AAdvantage® bonus miles
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-26**
## Executive summary

- This is a bait-and-switch email dressed up in American Airlines branding. The subject line promises "100,000 AAdvantage bonus miles" and the sender is the AAdvantage Program, but the second you land in the body it's an AT&T wireless-switching pitch — "Turn family plans into travel plans," a family-at-a-resort hero image, then two full iPhone 18 Pro / AT&T device ad blocks. There is no travel content, no destination, no flight offer anywhere in this render. It reads like American sold ad space in its loyalty newsletter and the mileage hook is just the lure to get partner-offer clicks.
- Compared to American's own history — the clean $622 Europe fare (Sept 9, 8/10) or even the messier MaximizeMiles partner catalog (Sept 17, 6/10) — this is the most disconnected-from-travel send in the set. It's worse than the Sept 17 partner-offer catalog because that one at least had a vacation package up top; this one is 100% AT&T, top to bottom, with American reduced to a logo and a "You are why we fly" banner at the very end.
- Judgement: technically well-produced (dark mode, clean spacing, no broken images) but the subject/content mismatch is a real trust problem. A subscriber who opens this for miles gets a phone carrier ad.

## What's working

- Clean dark-mode rendering with no layout bugs, overlapping text, or broken images.
- Clear, high-contrast CTA buttons ("Switch & earn today," "Shop now," "Learn more") that are easy to tap.
- Concrete numeric offer stated up top: 100,000 bonus miles + $800 per line, with a hard deadline (Sept 30, 9 p.m. PT).
- Product shot of the iPhone 18 Pro is sharp and recognizable, giving the AT&T block real product appeal.
- Brand identity (American Airlines globe logo, "You are why we fly" banner) is present, keeping it tethered to the AAdvantage program visually.

## What's weak

- Massive subject/content mismatch: subject promises miles, entire body sells AT&T phone plans — no flight, fare, or destination content at all.
- The mileage offer's real mechanics (switch 4 lines, $35 activation, 24-month install plan trap, 60-day qualification window) are only spelled out in dense fine print, not in the hero.
- Three near-identical "shop AT&T" CTAs stacked in a row (Switch & earn, Shop now, Learn more) dilute the primary action instead of driving one clear next step.
- No demographic/travel imagery relevant to a flyer — the hero photo is a family at what looks like a resort, but the deal being pushed is a phone contract, not a trip.
- Fine print block is enormous (roughly a third of the email) with layered conditions (redemption required, tiered by line count, exclusions), which reads as classic buried-terms behavior the rubric flags.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the subject/body mismatch first — either lead the body with an actual travel offer and treat the AT&T tie-in as a secondary module, or rewrite the subject to be honest about what's being offered ("Switch to AT&T, earn 100K miles") so there's no bait-and-switch feeling.
- 2. Cut to one CTA. Three blue buttons pushing the same underlying AT&T switch action is redundant — pick "Switch & earn today" as the sole hero CTA and drop or demote the iPhone/Learn-more blocks below a fold.
- 3. Surface the two or three terms that actually matter (need 4 lines, $35 activation fee, 60-day qualification window) directly under the hero offer instead of only in the small-print wall at the bottom.
- 4. Swap the family-at-a-resort hero photo for something that visually ties the phone-switch action to travel payoff (e.g., a redemption screen showing 100K miles = a real flight), so the visual and the CTA finally agree.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Switch to AT&T, earn 100,000 AAdvantage miles — ends Sept 30`
- **Subject Alt B:** `100K miles. $800 per line. AT&T switch offer ends soon.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Switch 4 lines to AT&T and earn 100,000 bonus miles plus up to $800 per line.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New AT&T customer? This mile + device deal ends Sept 30 at 9pm PT.`

## Technical Audit

1. Technical Summary
Email fails automated QA at a 30% pass rate: all four probed CTA/tracking links contain raw literal spaces in redirect parameters, causing control-character URL errors, alongside missing unsubscribe/authentication headers and one non-HTTPS image reference.

2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 4/4 probed material links fail with "URL can't contain control characters" due to an unencoded space in the `x=` query parameter value `AT&T Solo3` (should be `AT%26T%20Solo3` or similar). Affected links: "American Airlines" (h=6615342), "Redeem miles" (h=6615357), "You are why we fly" (h=6615353), "oneworld logo" (h=6615361).
- The `x=` parameter also contains a literal unencoded `&` (in "AT&T"), which additionally breaks query-string parsing since `&` is the parameter delimiter — the value should be percent-encoded as `AT%26T` before insertion into the URL.
- These are the only 4 of 12 material links actually probed; 11 lower-priority (footer/social) links were deprioritized and not verified — their encoding status is unknown.

3. Rendering & Accessibility
- No issues found in the truncated source (image alt text present per QA link-text extraction, e.g., "oneworld logo"; MSO/Outlook conditional comments and dark-mode media queries are present and structured correctly).

4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No merge token placeholders (e.g., `{{first_name}}`) observed in the truncated source; nothing to flag.

5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- `List-Unsubscribe` header not detected — may be a relay-capture limitation (AgentMail) rather than a sender-side omission; cannot confirm CAN-SPAM one-click compliance from this data alone.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) header not detected — one-click unsubscribe support unconfirmed.
- `Authentication-Results` header (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) not detected via the relay — sender authentication status unknown, not necessarily failing.

6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Tracking parameter `x=c|EML|SOLO|20260925|AADV|MKT|Partner||AT&T Solo3` (and variants with `20260926`) uses pipe-delimited campaign tagging rather than standard UTM params — consistent internal scheme, but the unencoded `&` and space in the trailing segment breaks the parameter for any downstream parser, which would also corrupt campaign attribution on landing.
- Cannot verify landing page alignment since redirect targets (`l.loyalty.ms.aa.com/rts/go2.aspx`) are tracking redirectors, not final destination URLs.

7. Recommendations
- URL-encode the `x=` campaign tag value at generation time (`&` → `%26`, spaces → `%20`) before concatenation into `go2.aspx` links — this is the root cause of all 4 link failures and likely affects the unprobed footer/social links using the same tag.
- Change the oneworld logo image `src` from `http://i.loyalty.ms.aa.com/...oneworld_logo_special_online_80x80.gif` to `https://` to avoid mixed-content blocking in HTTPS mail clients.
- Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are present at the raw SMTP level (check with the sending ESP directly) since relay-side non-capture cannot rule out a genuine compliance gap.
- Verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status directly with the ESP or a raw-header capture outside the AgentMail relay.
