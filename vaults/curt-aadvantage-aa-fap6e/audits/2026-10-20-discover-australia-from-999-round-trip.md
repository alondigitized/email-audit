# Discover Australia from $999* round trip
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-20**
## Executive summary

- This is a clear, single-theme send: a "Limited-time Australia sale" with a specific fare ("from $999*"), a hard deadline (Oct. 25, 2026), and one big blue "Book now" button. It's the most concrete offer in the recent run of AAdvantage sends. The Oct. 7 and Oct. 5 emails were catalogs that never committed to a deal. This one does.
- The email then spreads out. Below the hero are five tiles, each with its own button: Brisbane, Sydney, Flagship Business, Qantas miles and "connecting countries." Only the Brisbane tile repeats the $999 hook. The Flagship $5,499 tile pulls attention toward a very different buyer. The email also never says where the $999 fare departs from. That detail sits in the fine print as "contiguous 48 U.S. states to select destinations," with blackout dates out of Dallas-Fort Worth. A reader has to scroll through 21 numbered terms to learn whether the fare applies to them.
- Judgment: a good headline offer weakened by a catalog-style body and a hidden departure city. The Oct. 19 "Set sail with early Black Friday savings" email promised a different offer than its subject did. This one is consistent between subject and hero, which is better. It also lands only a day later with a different theme, so the cadence feels busy.

## What's working

- The subject and hero match. "Discover Australia from $999*" is followed by "Limited-time Australia sale" and "fares from $999*."
- The deadline of Oct. 25, 2026 is stated in the hero copy, so the urgency is credible.
- The "Book now" button is large, high-contrast blue and sits right under the hero paragraph.
- The Brisbane tile repeats "$999* round-trip" with a specific destination and an "Explore deals" button.
- The Qantas partnership gives the sale a reason to exist, and the co-branded footer banner reinforces it.
- The layout is clean, with no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- The origin city is missing. The email never says where the $999 fare departs from, and the fine print only says "select" cities, so the reader can't tell if the deal applies to them.
- Five tiles with five different buttons ("Explore deals," "Book flights," "Fly premium," "Learn more," "Explore flights") dilute the single offer.
- The Flagship Business tile at $5,499 sits next to a $999 deal and works against it.
- The "Your AAdvantage account" block shows 0 award miles and 0 loyalty points. That reads as a hollow member benefit, and nothing ties the sale to member pricing.
- The hero is plain text with no image. The photos only start below the fold, so the email has little emotional pull for a "Discover Australia" pitch.
- The terms run to 21 numbered items, including blackout dates and a "Basic Economy" caveat, which makes the offer feel less honest.

## Recommendations

- 1. Name the origin and the date range in the hero, for example "Dallas–Brisbane from $999* round trip, travel Oct 26–Jul 25."
- 2. Put a strong Sydney or Brisbane photo in the hero, with the price overlaid on it.
- 3. Cut the body to two or three tiles that support the sale. Drop the $5,499 Flagship tile or move it below the account block.
- 4. Use a single, specific button label such as "See Australia fares," and make every tile's button a variation of it.
- 5. If the member has no miles, replace the 0/0 account block with a "Earn miles on this trip" line.
- 6. Move the key restrictions (Basic Economy, nonrefundable, blackout dates) into one short visible line under the button.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Australia from $999* round trip — book by Oct. 25`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Brisbane or Sydney? Fares from $999* end Oct. 25`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Nonstop to Brisbane, plus new Las Vegas–Sydney service starting Dec. 29.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fly with American and Qantas. Five days left to lock in your fare.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **Dark-mode text color:** inside `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)`, `span, a, td { color: #ffffff !important; }` applies to every cell.
