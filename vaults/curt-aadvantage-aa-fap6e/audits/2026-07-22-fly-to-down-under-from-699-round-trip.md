# Fly to Down Under from $699* round trip
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- This is American Airlines returning to destination-deal mode after two consecutive buy-miles emails (July 16 and 21). The format is nearly identical to the July 14 "South America from $899* round trip" send — hero headline, stacked destination cards, "Fly Now" CTAs, dense footer fine print — just with a new geography. The creative rotation is sensible: cash in the goodwill rebuilt during the partners-and-experiences run, then pivot back to aspirational flight deals. The Australia/New Zealand subject line is one of the strongest in this AA sequence — specific price, specific destination, clean length.
- That said, the email body is doing less work than the subject promises. The hero says "Limited-time Australia and New Zealand sale" without surfacing the $699 price point in the first visible block, and the Qantas co-branding buried in the app-download section is the most interesting piece of strategic information in this email — yet it's hidden at the bottom. The multi-destination card stack is the same playbook as South America, and at this cadence it's starting to feel templated rather than curated.
- This is a competent, serviceable deals email that opens well on the strength of its subject line but loses steam in the body. Good, not great.

- Subject line is the standout: "Fly to Down Under from $699* round trip" is under 40 chars, specific, and memorable — easily the best in this AA sequence since the IHG partners email.
- "Book Now" hero CTA is unambiguous and above the fold.
- Stacked destination card format gives the email clear scannable structure — each block has an image, a destination snippet, and a "Fly Now" button.
- Returning to aspirational flight deals after two consecutive buy-miles emails is the right cadence reset.
- Qantas co-branding (logos together in the app section) hints at a genuine partnership story that adds credibility to the route.

## What's weak

- The $699 price from the subject line is not in the hero section where the eye lands — the opening visual block has "Limited-time Australia and New Zealand sale" but doesn't repeat the hook that earned the open.
- Qantas partnership is the most interesting angle in this email and it's buried in the app-download footer; many subscribers will never see it.
- The four destination card images are generic stock-feeling travel photography — no Australia/NZ specificity visible (a koala, Sydney Opera House, Milford Sound would do more work than sheep on a hillside).
- Dense legal fine print at the bottom is longer and more imposing than the July 14 South America send, making the "from $699*" feel more asterisk-heavy than trustworthy.
- No deadline or end-date visible for the "limited-time" sale — the July 21 send had a countdown timer; this email reverts to vague urgency.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Duplicate MSO conditional block (confirmed bug)**
The `<!--[if gte mso 9]>` CSS block appears **twice** with identical content:
```
<!--[if gte mso 9]>
  <style>body,table,td{font-family:...}.lhfix{...}.supFix{...}</style>
<![endif]-->
<!--[if gte mso 9]>
  <style>body,table,td{font-family:...}.lhfix{...}.supFix{...}</style>  ← duplicate
<![endif]-->
```
No rendering impact (identical rules cascade to the same result), but it's dead weight — adds ~600 bytes and indicates a template assembly error.

**Issue 2 — Unclosed dark-mode `@media` block**
The `@media (prefers-color-scheme:dark)` rule block ends without a closing `}` before `</style>`:
```css
[data-ogsc] a, [data-ogsc] td { color: #ffffff !important; }
/* ← missing closing } for @media block */
</style>
```
This causes the entire dark-mode rule set to be treated as invalid CSS by conformant parsers. Dark-mode overrides will **silently fail** in Apple Mail, iOS Mail, and any client that enforces strict CSS parsing.

**Issue 3 — Broken relative image URL in mobile CSS**
```css
#swapbg {
  background-image: url("../20230508_HTML-32134_M_bg.png") !important;
}
```
Relative paths are non-functional in email; clients have no base path to resolve against. This background image **will not render on mobile**. The filename also contains a 2023 datestamp (`20230508`), suggesting a stale template reference. Must be an absolute CDN URL.

**Issue 4 — Google Fonts double-loaded**
Roboto is requested twice:
- Via `<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto:...">` 
- Via `@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto:...')` inside a `<style>` block

This fires two separate HTTP requests for the same resource. Most email clients block external fonts anyway, so the MSO fallback stack (`Helvetica Neue, Arial, sans-serif`) is what the majority of recipients actually see. The double-load adds latency on clients that do support web fonts (Apple Mail).

**Issue 5 — Aggressive `*` selector**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
    max-height: 1000000px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly; line-height: 100% }
```
`line-height: 100%` applied globally can collapse multi-line button labels and override component-level line heights. Low risk given the MSO override, but worth scoping to `body, p, td` instead.
