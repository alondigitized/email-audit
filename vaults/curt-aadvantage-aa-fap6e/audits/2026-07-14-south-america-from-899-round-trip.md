# South America from $899* round trip
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- This is a paid-flight deals email — dollar pricing, not miles — which makes it a meaningful creative shift from the AAdvantage sequence that has leaned heavily on miles redemptions and partner accrual. The format is a destination grid: hero landscape photo, paragraph intro, then five or six tiled destination rows each with an image, city + price, and a blue "Book Now" CTA. It is clean, functional, and renders without visible bugs. The 100th anniversary AA logo anchors the footer, giving the email an institutional weight the deals content doesn't quite earn on its own.
- The core problem is depth: the destination tiles feel like a generic "deals catalog" rather than a curated South America story. There's no urgency signal in the body, no deadline visible in the offer area, and no miles-equivalent callout to tell an AAdvantage member why they should pay cash instead of redeeming. That gap matters because this audience has been trained by twelve emails to think in miles. Flipping to dollar pricing without a bridge rationale ("cash + miles combo," "earn 3X miles on this booking") leaves the offer feeling disconnected from the loyalty program they just joined.
- Compared to the July 6 replay of the 9,000-miles deal — which landed an 8 — this is the same tier of execution. The destination imagery is attractive, the layout is scannable, but it's a catalog, not a campaign.

## What's working

- **Concrete dollar price in the subject line** — "from $899* round trip" is specific and scannable; this outperforms every vague "great deals" subject in the sequence.
- **Clean destination-tile layout** — image + price + button per row, consistent throughout; easy to scroll and compare.
- **"Book your getaway now" hero copy** — active, direct, no jargon; it matches travel-intent mindset.
- **Destination imagery is aspirational** — the hero mountain/landscape and the in-tile photos all evoke the trip; strong visual motivation to keep reading.
- **No render bugs** — email loads clean, buttons are visible, no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **No urgency signal anywhere in the body** — no "offer ends," no "seats limited," no deadline. The asterisk on $899 implies conditions but never resolves them inside the email.
- **No miles bridge for a loyalty audience** — these are AAdvantage members; no mention of miles earned on the cash purchase, no hybrid option, no reason to book through AA vs. any other OTA.
- **Destination grid reads generic** — the five-plus-destination layout is identical to any carrier's deals newsletter; nothing personalizes it to South America as a region or to this member's travel history.
- **Cadence is getting crowded** — six emails in 12 days (Jul 2–14). This one doesn't feel stale on its own, but the cumulative density increases the chance of a mute or unsubscribe.
- **"Book your getaway now" hero copy does no selling** — it's the same register as placeholder text; the paragraph beneath it (too small to read at thumbnail scale) is carrying explanation weight that a sub-headline should handle.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a miles-earn callout to every tile** — "Book now + earn 3,000 miles" next to the price bridges the cash offer back to the loyalty program. This single change makes the email feel coherent with the sequence.
- 2. **Set a visible deadline in the body** — Even "Book by July 18" under the hero CTA would activate urgency the subject line implies but the body never delivers.
- 3. **Collapse from 5+ destinations to 3 hero picks** — Pick the three strongest routes (likely the cheapest or the most aspirational), give them more real estate and a specific flight detail (nonstop vs. connection, departure city). Catalog emails perform worse than curated ones for loyalty audiences.
- 4. **Replace "Book your getaway now" hero with an offer-forward sub-headline** — Something like "South America from $899 — nonstops included" does more work than the current placeholder-register headline.
- 5. **Tighten the footer / legal block** — The footer appears to have substantial fine-print; condensing it reduces visual weight and pushes the CTA grid higher on the page.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Nonstop options included — earn miles on every cash booking`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — broken dark mode CSS (missing closing brace):**
The `@media (prefers-color-scheme:dark)` block is never closed before `</style>`. The closing `}` for the media query itself is absent, making the entire dark mode ruleset invalid CSS. This will cause dark mode rendering to fail entirely in Apple Mail, iOS Mail, and any client that honors `prefers-color-scheme`.

```css
/* From source — block ends without closing } */
[data-ogsc] span, [data-ogsc] a, [data-ogsc] td { color: #ffffff !important; }
</style>  ← media query never closed
```

**Confirmed issue — broken relative image path:**
The mobile swap background uses a relative URL that will not resolve in any email client:
```css
#swapbg {
  background-image: url("../20230508_HTML-32134_M_bg.png") !important;
```
Email clients strip relative paths. This image will silently fail on mobile. Must be an absolute CDN URL.

**Confirmed issue — duplicate MSO conditional block:**
The `<!--[if gte mso 9]>` style block appears twice with identical content. The second copy is dead weight and increases parse time, though not a rendering failure.

**Confirmed issue — double Google Fonts loading:**
Fonts are loaded via both `<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/...">` in `<head>` AND `@import url(...)` inside a `<style>` block. This fires two separate HTTP requests for the same resource. Most email clients block external fonts anyway, making both redundant; the duplication compounds the wasted request.

**Confirmed issue — `maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on iOS. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) violation and will cause accessibility audit failures.

**Confirmed issue — global `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`:**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applied to every element, this overrides user-configured font scaling. Prefer `-webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%` (used correctly on `body`) rather than `none` on `*`.
