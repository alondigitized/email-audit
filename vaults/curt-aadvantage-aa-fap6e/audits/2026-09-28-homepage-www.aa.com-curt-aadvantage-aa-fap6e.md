# Homepage snapshot · www.aa.com · 2026-09-28
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-09-28**
## Executive summary

- Same AA.com I've had bookmarked for fifteen years — booking widget front and center, which is really all I need. But the hero's pushing a Citi card sign-up bonus with a pool photo, not a flight deal or status perk, which tells me marketing's chasing new-account bodies this week, not road warriors like me. Functional, a little cluttered with that login popup jammed over the art, but I can find the search box in two seconds flat, so I'm not mad.

## What's working

- **The booking widget is right where it should be** — Book/Manage trips/Flight status tabs, no scrolling required. That's the only thing I actually use this site for.
- **"BUR" already sitting in the From field** — somebody's cookie/session logic is doing its job, or it's a smart default. Either way, one less thing I have to type on my phone.
- **oneworld logo top-left** — small thing, but it's a status signal I clock instantly. Tells me alliance partners are still front-and-center.
- **"Redeem miles" checkbox right on the search form** — that's the fastest path to an award search, which is 80% of what I do here.

## What's weak

- **The hero is a credit-card ad, not a flight or status message** — "Earn up to 125,000 bonus miles" is a Citi co-brand pitch. I've got the Executive Platinum card already; this isn't for me, it's for someone building a mileage balance from zero.
- **That "Have an AAdvantage account? Log in here" popup is sitting half on top of the pool photo and half on the Join button** — looks like a layout collision, not intentional design. Distracting, not dangerous.
- **Pool-lounging leisure imagery** — doesn't read "business traveler," reads "spring break." Fine for the masses, does nothing for someone chasing 4 million lifetime miles.
- **No visible mileage balance or status tier once you're presumably logged in territory** — I know this is the logged-out view, but there's nothing here hinting "we know who you are" beyond the BUR autofill.

## Recommendations

- Swap the poolside hero for a business-class cabin shot or a road-warrior-with-a-rollaboard lifestyle image — same card offer, different visual, targets the flyer who's actually going to convert on a $695 annual fee.
- Fix the popup collision — either dock it cleanly below the nav bar or make it a slide-in that doesn't overlap the hero art.
- Give frequent flyers something above the fold besides the card pitch — a "Web Saver fares" or upgrade-availability teaser would land harder with me than a sign-up bonus I don't need.
- Put a real date or countdown on "LIMITED-TIME OFFER" — right now it's just a red badge with no deadline, which reads as permanent marketing copy, not urgency.
