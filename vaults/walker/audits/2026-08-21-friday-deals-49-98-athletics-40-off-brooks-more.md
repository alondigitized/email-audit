## What's working

- Top banner with $20 off $99.98 / $10 off $74.98 and an actual promo code (SAVEMORE) — clear, usable, no guessing.
- Brooks Ghost 17 & Revel 8 called out by name — those are shoes I recognize and might be shopping for.
- Skechers and Crocs price points are big, bold, and easy to read without squinting.
- Four-brand grid layout (Athletics, Brooks, Crocs, Skechers) makes it easy to skip straight to what I care about.

## What's weak

- The whole "Friday Roll Call" chalkboard/backpack section is aimed at parents shopping for kids, not me — same theme this brand's been running all week.
- "Family Athletics" panel leads with Jordan/Converse/adidas for "Adults' starting at $49.98" but the visual is still school-themed with the sitting-on-the-floor pose.
- No arch support, cushioning, or comfort-fit language anywhere — just price points, which is fine for a flash sale but doesn't tell me if these are the slip-on comfort shoes I actually want.
- Boys & Girls Clubs round-up ask at the bottom is nice but has nothing to do with me buying shoes, just adds scroll.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the Brooks/Skechers/Crocs comfort-brand grid up top, right under the coupon banner — that's the strongest content in the email and it's buried two panels down.
- 2. Drop or shrink the "Friday Roll Call" school section — it's the same beat as every email this week, and it pushes the actual deals further down.
- 3. Add one line under the Brooks and Skechers panels noting cushioning or arch support if it applies — turns a price flyer into something that speaks to comfort shoppers like me.
- 4. Test a subject that leads with a comfort brand instead of "athletics" as the generic term.
