# Early Black Friday 👢 30% OFF BOOTS!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-14**
## Executive summary

- DSW again. I counted, and this is the sixth boots email since Wednesday. The top banner, "30% OFF BOOTS*" in big white letters on green with the code BOOTFRIDAY, is easy to read on my phone without my glasses. That part works, and it's the same banner I've seen all week. But then the email wanders off into a "Holiday To-Do(s) List" with a young woman posing in a trench coat and sneakers, a sneaker mule, and flats. I came for boots and got fashion.
- Nothing in here speaks to a 62-year-old guy who wants something cushioned and easy to slip on. I see a Reebok-style sneaker and a Mary Jane, and the "Perfect for Every Event" row has a cozy flat, one tall women's boot and a red flat. The only men's link is a small "Shop Men" under the banner. I wouldn't forward this, and I'd probably skim it and mark it read. If I did click, it would be the men's link, and only because a deal is a deal.

## What's working

- The "30% OFF BOOTS*" banner is huge and readable, and the code BOOTFRIDAY is right under it.
- The green "Shop Boots" button is big and easy to tap with my thumb.
- The "Shop by Size" grid, with half sizes and "Shop Wide Width," is something I'd use. Wide width matters to me.
- The "Free shipping for VIPs / In-store pickup" strip is simple and tells me what I need.

## What's weak

- Almost every picture is women's. The one boot is a tall women's boot, so I can't tell if this is for me.
- "Shop Men" is a tiny underlined link in the corner, and the men's version of the offer is buried.
- After the banner the email drifts to sneakers, mules and flats. The boots promise isn't kept.
- The asterisk and the wall of tiny fine print at the bottom are hard to read. I can't tell what's excluded, and "Hunter Boots" at the very top suggests some brands might be.
- No mention of cushioning, arch support or comfort anywhere, and no prices on any shoe.
- It's the same boots offer I've now seen five or six times this week. I'm tired of it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Show men's boots, with a real comfort product like a cushioned walking boot or a slip-on, and give "Shop Men" its own button next to "Shop Women."
- 2. Cut the lifestyle section and put in three or four boots with prices, "was/now" math, and comfort notes like "memory foam" or "arch support."
- 3. Space out the sends. One strong boots email beats six.
- 4. Put the fine print in plain type and say which brands are excluded.
- **Subject Alt A:** `30% off boots, men's styles too`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Boots for all-day comfort, 30% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use code BOOTFRIDAY. Men's, women's, wide widths.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Cushioned, easy-on boots. Ends Sunday.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email uses a standard Braze/SendGrid-wrapped template. One high-click CTA ("Opt-In to Texts Get 15% Off") returned a 400 in the automated probe, and a few small markup and deliverability issues showed up. The HTML source was truncated, so footer, body and link-level checks are limited. The QA run covered 12 of 68 links, which is a 62% pass rate.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **[FAIL] Broken CTA (HTTP 400):** "Opt-In to Texts Get 15% Off" points to `https://ablink.dsw.com/f/a/FG5NKGGHFIELeqolLlNsXA~~/AAQRxRA~/GpT1cuMQ1d8L...`.
  - The QA output shows an empty resolved destination (`Broken link (400):  `), so the redirect did not resolve.
  - This could be an expired or invalid tracking token, or a destination such as an SMS-opt-in page that rejects the probe's request. The data can't distinguish these, so it should be re-tested manually in a real client.
  - The link is a high-priority CTA offering 15% off, so a real failure costs conversions.
- **Link wrapping:** every probed link goes through `ablink.dsw.com`, a redirect domain. Final destinations can't be verified from the source or the QA data.
- **Coverage gap:** 56 of 68 links were not probed (footer, social and utility links were deprioritized). Their status is unknown.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on two images:**
  - `https://ablink.dsw.com/q/xl0Cy4EBJHUY5ynKVJIEbw~~/AAQRxRA~/anXxMjVW...` has no alt text.
  - `https://mi.dsw.com/p/up/e6f23fb024fdcb2cc855b9ed672710d4/o.gif?...` has no alt text.
  - Both look like tracking pixels. If so, there is no user-facing impact, but they should carry `alt=""` so screen readers skip them and clients don't show alt text when images are blocked.
- **Viewport meta:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` sets `maximum-scale=1`. This blocks pinch-zoom on some mobile clients, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Malformed `<meta>` tags:**
  - `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` has no `http-equiv="Content-Type"`, so the charset declaration has no effect.
  - `<meta content="IE=Edge" />` has no `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`.
  - The Content-Type header probably covers the charset in practice, but both tags are inert as written.
- **Plain-text part:** 21,913 of 26,240 characters (about 84%) are URLs, which is above the 70% warning threshold. The long wrapped tracking URLs (`ablink.dsw.com/f/a/...`) inflate the text part. This raises the spam-score risk and makes the text version hard to read.
