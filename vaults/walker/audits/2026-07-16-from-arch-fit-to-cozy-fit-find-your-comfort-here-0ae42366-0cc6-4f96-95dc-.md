# From Arch Fit to Cozy Fit, Find Your Comfort Here
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- Now this is the email I've been waiting for from Skechers. "Arch Fit to Cozy Fit" — that subject line landed right on my needs before I even opened it. I'm sixty-two years old with feet that have opinions, and Skechers just built me a cheat sheet. The "Four Ways to Comfort" layout breaks down exactly what I care about — Cozy Fit, Arch Fit, Glide-Step, Hands Free Slip-ins — all in one scroll. The 40% off banner at the top means I'm not just window shopping.
- But I have to say it again: that preview text. I have now seen that wall of raw computer JSON garbage — the `@context`, `@type`, `Organization` nonsense — in what I count as nine or ten straight Skechers emails. It was embarrassing in July. It's still embarrassing today. Whoever is responsible for fixing that has my full attention, and not the good kind.
- Still — I'd click this one. The Hands Free Slip-ins section alone is worth a tap. And I'd probably forward it to my buddy Dave who complains about his plantar fasciitis every time we go for our morning walk.

## What's working

- **Subject line finally speaks my language** — "Arch Fit to Cozy Fit" names the two things I actually shop by; this felt like it was written for me, not a generic blast.
- **"Four Ways to Comfort" framing is smart** — breaking the email into four distinct comfort technologies helps me quickly find my lane (Arch Fit and Slip-ins, in my case).
- **Hands Free Slip-ins section is a genuine win** — that section with "stop those fumbling-for-your-shoes blues" copy is exactly the kind of feature-first language that makes me trust a brand knows its customer.
- **40% OFF is front and center** — no hunting for the offer, it's the first thing I see, and the 20% SUMMER code adds a second layer of value deeper in the email.
- **Clean render, no broken images** — everything loaded, every section was readable without zooming, CTAs are clear buttons I can tap without squinting.

## What's weak

- **That broken JSON preview text — again.** Nine emails in a row now. If I weren't already a Skechers buyer I'd have marked this as spam weeks ago. It makes the brand look technically incompetent.
- **The hero couple skews younger** — the man in the hero image looks like he's in his late 30s or early 40s. I don't need to see myself exactly, but a little grayer, a little more my speed wouldn't hurt.
- **No sale deadline visible** — 40% off is great but I don't see a "valid thru" date anywhere. The July 12th email had a "Valid thru 7/13" right there; this one left me wondering if there's urgency or if I can think about it.
- **20% OFF code isn't clearly member-exclusive** — the SUMMER code is buried lower in the email and doesn't tell me if I'm getting a special deal as a Plus member or if anyone off the street gets the same price.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader. Today. Not next campaign cycle.** This is the single highest-impact change available. Real copy in that preview slot — even "40% off Arch Fit, Cozy Fit, and Slip-ins this summer" — would lift opens materially.
- 2. **Add a sale end date under the 40% OFF banner** — something like "Valid thru 7/20" so there's a credible reason to act now rather than bookmark it and forget.
- 3. **Label the 20% code as member-exclusive** — "Your Skechers Plus member code: SUMMER" makes me feel treated like a known customer. Right now it reads like a generic code anyone has.
- 4. **Age up the hero image slightly** — the comfort technology messaging is perfectly calibrated for my demographic; the photography doesn't match. A man who looks 55–65 in quality walking shoes would close that gap.
- 5. **Slim the navigation links at the bottom** — Women / Men / Kids / Clothing / New Arrivals / Sale is a lot of small tap targets stacked at the end. For me on a phone, that section is more friction than help.

### 1. Technical Summary

All 26 CTA links route through a tracking redirect domain (unverifiable by probe), and all hosted images are served over HTTP rather than HTTPS — both are systemic infrastructure issues that will suppress open and click signal in modern clients. One third-party pixel has a missing protocol prefix and will silently fail.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Return Path / Validity inbox-monitoring pixel — broken URL (confirmed bug)**
```html
<img src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1dbf0dcef79cbad82c12fe25342701e4499" ...>
```
The `src` has no scheme. Email clients treat this as a relative path, so the pixel never fires. Fix: `https://pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=...`

**26 click-redirect links — unverified**
All CTA hrefs route through `click.emails.skechers.com`. Final destination URLs and UTM parameter presence cannot be confirmed without resolving the redirects. This is the expected pattern for ESP click tracking but leaves link integrity unaudited.

**Third-party audience-sync pixels present**

```
https://beacon.krxd.net/1x1_usermatch.gif?partner=LprH63a7_sfmc_100007688_sha25&partner_uid=9153f33fe5cdf59baf088bb835d3b39ef90f188bd8540a4faba92899227ced00
```

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources — systemic (14 warnings confirmed)**

All images hosted on `image.emails.skechers.com` use `http://`. Gmail proxies images over its own HTTPS servers so these may still load in Gmail, but Outlook.com, Apple Mail (recent versions), and corporate mail gateways may block or fail to render them. Affects the Skechers logo, all footer icons, app store badges, and social icons. Fix: update CDN base URL to `https://`.

The ink1000.com open-tracking pixel is also HTTP-only:
```
src="http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif?..."
```

**Missing alt text — 2 images**
- `o.gif` (ink1000 pixel): decorative, `alt=""` is acceptable but should be explicit.
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` at path `/m/11/` — path pattern suggests a content image (not a pixel); missing alt text is an accessibility failure.

**Missing `http-equiv` on two `<meta>` tags**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />      <!-- missing http-equiv="Content-Type" -->
<meta content="IE=Edge" />                        <!-- missing http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" -->
```
Neither meta tag has effect without `http-equiv`. Outlook 2016–2019 ignores the charset declaration and may misrender multi-byte characters.

**Duplicate and conflicting media queries**
Two separate `<style>` blocks define overlapping breakpoints: `375px`, `480px`, `640px`, `680px`, `768px`. The `@media (max-width: 480px)` block is defined twice (in two separate `<style>` tags) with different rule sets (`.show` + `.mobile-hidden` vs. `.show` alone). This creates specificity ambiguity; the last declaration wins but this is fragile.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents user zoom on mobile — a WCAG 1.4.4 (Level AA) failure. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `*`**
Disables iOS text scaling globally, which compounds the no-zoom issue for low-vision users.
