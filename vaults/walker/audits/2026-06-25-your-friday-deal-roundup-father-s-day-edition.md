# Your Friday Deal Roundup: Father's Day Edition
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- Opening a "Friday Deal Roundup: Father's Day Edition" on June 25th is like getting a birthday card that shows up four days late — the thought was there, but the moment has passed. Father's Day was June 21. Shoe Carnival already sent me a dead-on pitch on June 14 with Skechers, Nike, and Jordan front and center at the right moment. This one feels like someone forgot to turn off the campaign.
- That said, once I'm actually in the email, there's real substance. Skechers at 30% off, New Balance at 20% off, Crocs at 30% off, Men's Sandals from $19.98 — those are exactly the brands and categories I cross-shop. The Shoe Perks $10 reward reminder at the bottom is useful. The Toy Story licensed section is completely baffling for a 62-year-old man. And the $14.98 Blowout row has shown up in so many Shoe Carnival emails this month that it no longer reads as a deal — it reads as filler they can't move.
- I'd mark this read and probably move on. I might tap the Men's Sandals $19.98 tile if I happened to need sandals today, but the stale Father's Day framing makes the whole email feel like a leftover.

## What's working

- Skechers, New Balance, and Crocs are all present in the brand grid — those are my actual cross-shop brands, and seeing them together in one send makes comparison easy.
- The $20 off $99.98+ / $10 off $74.98+ hero with promo code FORDAD gives me everything I need upfront — no hunting for the discount.
- Men's Sandals starting at $19.98 is a concrete men's-specific category offer with a real price anchor, not just a vague "up to X% off."
- Shoe Perks FREE $10 Reward at the bottom is a solid loyalty nudge — I noticed it even on a long scroll.
- The email renders clean — no broken images, no layout collapse, text is readable at phone size.

## What's weak

- Father's Day was June 21. A "Father's Day Edition" email landing in my inbox on June 25 is stale by four full days — the holiday decision is over.
- The preview text is a raw tracking URL. "Shoe Carnival https://click.email.shoecarnival.com/?qs=ABB7..." looks like a phishing link, not a promotional email. That's the first thing I see in my inbox before I even open it.
- The $14.98 Blowout section has appeared in multiple consecutive Shoe Carnival sends — I noted it again on June 13. Showing it a third or fourth time this month makes it feel like clearance they can't move, not a deal.
- The Toy Story licensed product section is a miss for anyone in my demographic — that's a kids' shoe play, not a Boomer comfort-shoe email.
- Not one word about arch support, cushioning, memory foam, or slip-on ease anywhere in the Skechers or Crocs tiles — which is the only reason I buy those brands.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the Father's Day framing on June 22.** Flip to "Summer Sandals" or "Weekend Comfort Deals" the Monday after the holiday. Stale promo language actively undermines trust.
- 2. **Fix the preheader immediately — this is urgent.** A raw tracking URL reads as either spam or a phishing attempt before the email is even opened. The Shoe Perks reward copy, the promo code, or the $19.98 price point would all be better than a URL.
- 3. **Segment out the Toy Story tile** for any audience over 40. It belongs in a kids'/families send, not a Father's Day men's comfort email.
- 4. **Add one comfort attribute to the Skechers and Crocs tiles** — "Memory foam insole," "Arch fit technology," "Easy slip-on" — whichever is true for the featured style. That's the reason I click on those brands.
- 5. **Retire or refresh the $14.98 Blowout row.** It's appeared too many times this month to feel urgent.

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**`[UNIQUE]` placeholder may be unresolved**
The open-tracking pixel URL contains a literal `[UNIQUE]` string:
```
https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]
```
If this token is substituted at send time by the ESP, it is expected behavior. If the HTML was captured post-send with `[UNIQUE]` still present, open tracking is broken for all recipients — every open registers as the same `d` value, collapsing unique-open counts.

**26 click-tracking links skipped by QA**

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text on two content images (confirmed)**
```
https://image.email.shoecarnival.com/lib/fe3311737164047d701077/m/1/951dfca3-078c-47e4-a9c6-b5630a845bf2.png
https://image.email.shoecarnival.com/lib/fe3311737164047d701077/m/1/05239c62-9b67-4426-b112-0b55431bed66.png
```
Images without `alt` attributes render as broken icons in image-off environments (Outlook default, corporate clients) and fail WCAG 2.1 Success Criterion 1.1.1. These appear to be product/promotional images that should carry descriptive alt text.

**Tracking pixels missing `alt=""`**
Both tracking pixels (`ieezzy5m`, `0di86k1v`) are flagged for missing alt. Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` (empty string, not absent attribute) to satisfy validators and avoid screen-reader noise.

**Malformed IE compatibility meta tag**
```html
<meta content="IE=edge" />
```
Missing the required `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"` attribute. Without it, this tag is a no-op — Outlook 2007/2010 ignores it. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
```

**No dark mode support declared**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light" />
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light" />
```
Light-only declaration. Apple Mail and other dark-mode-aware clients will attempt their own color inversion. If the design has not been tested in dark mode, this is a rendering risk.

**Duplicate CSS property (minor)**
In the global anchor rule:
```css
color:inherit !important; color:inherit;
```
The second `color:inherit` (without `!important`) is unreachable and dead code.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM Params, Landing Page Alignment)

If the send date was later, the campaign tag is misaligned and will produce inaccurate attribution grouping in analytics.
