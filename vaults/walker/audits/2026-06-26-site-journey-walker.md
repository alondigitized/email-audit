# Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Date:** June 26, 2026 | **vs. Yesterday's Score:** 4/10

## 1. Executive Summary

Today's walk through skechers.com had some bright spots in the navigation drawer and product detail page, but two critical failures drag the score down hard: the cart page rendered completely blank — a white nothing — and a "comfort shoes" search surfaced Women's products for a logged-in male shopper. I couldn't complete a purchase even if I wanted to, and the site still doesn't know who I am after I log in. Same score as yesterday. No improvement where it counts.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

No change from yesterday. The navigation got a bit more comfortable to use, but a broken cart is a broken sale. Until that white screen gets fixed and search learns that I'm a man, Skechers is leaving my money on the table.

## 3. What's Working

**BOGO 50% OFF is impossible to miss.** That sticky blue-and-gold top banner is the first thing I see on every page. As someone who does the price math in his head at the register, that kind of persistent deal visibility earns trust.

**The Men's navigation drawer is genuinely good.** Large bold text, full-width tap targets with plenty of vertical spacing — I can hit "Shoes," "Comfort Technologies," or "Hands Free Slip-ins" without squinting or fat-fingering. The "Back" and X buttons are clearly placed. This is how a mobile menu should work for someone my age.

**"Hands Free Slip-ins" is right there in the shortcut rail AND the nav.** The horizontal quick-nav on the homepage (Slip-ins, Sandals, Arch Fit, BOBs) puts my most relevant category one tap away. That's smart.

**The Men's Hands Free landing page leads with fit.** Three pill buttons — Wide Fit, Extra Wide Fit, Medium Fit — are the first thing after the heading. 317 results with fit filtering front and center is the right call. Comfort shoppers think in fit first.

**Product detail page is clean and readable.** The product name is big, the $88 price is prominent, the BOGO members deal is called out in red, and the star rating (5 stars, 20 reviews) is right under the title. Nothing requires zooming.

**ADD TO CART button is excellent.** Full-width, bright blue, bold all-caps text. A 62-year-old can't miss it.

---

## 4. What's Weak

**The cart page is a blank white screen.** This is the single most damaging thing I saw today. After all the work of finding a shoe, checking the deal, selecting a size — I tap View Cart and get nothing. A white page. No product, no price, no checkout button. Dead end. This isn't friction, it's a wall. If this happened to me in real life I'd close the app and go to DSW.

**Search "comfort shoes" serves me Women's shoes.** I'm logged in. The site knows I came through Men's. I search "comfort shoes" and get 1,425 results — the first two cards I see are both labeled Women's. "Relaxed Fit: Glide-Step Comfort — Women's" and "Skechers Slip-ins: Glide-Step Comfort — Soft Power — Women's." I shouldn't have to filter my gender on a search that basic. This is a personalization failure that would make me question whether this site is even for me.

**Login produced no visible personalization.** The Step 3 screenshot is identical to the homepage — same Britto hero, same generic banners, no "Welcome back, Walker" moment, no "you have X points" or "your saved sizes" surfaced anywhere in the journey. Logging in felt pointless.

**Homepage hero is aimed at someone else.** A young woman dancing in paint-splattered fashion sneakers for the Britto collab. I understand it's a campaign, but as a 62-year-old man looking for cushioned walking shoes, there's nothing in that image that speaks to me. The quick-nav below saves it, but the above-the-fold moment is wasted on my demographic.

**"Please select a size" is a warning, not a guide.** The size picker on the Add to Cart step shows just 7.5 through 9.0 in the viewport — I don't know if more sizes exist below. The warning triangle in red is good, but I'd want to see the full size range without scrolling, and a clear "tap your size to add" prompt rather than a post-error alert.

**No comfort specs in the product viewport.** I'm on the product page for a shoe called "Contour Foam — Cozy Fit" and all I see above the fold is the name, stars, price, and deal. I can't see the arch support details, cushioning specs, or any comfort language in the visible area. That's the one thing I actually need before I commit $88.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** This is P0. A blank cart kills every conversion. Whatever JavaScript failure is causing the white screen needs a same-day fix and regression test.

2. **Filter search results by logged-in gender.** A male account holder searching "comfort shoes" should see Men's first. This is a one-line personalization rule that would materially improve relevance.

3. **Add a post-login hook.** After I sign in, surface something — my saved size, my points balance, a "back to where you left off" module, anything. Login should feel like being recognized, not like hitting refresh.

4. **Surface comfort specs in the first product viewport.** At least one bullet — "Air-Cooled Memory Foam," "Arch Fit," "Podiatrist Certified" — should be visible without scrolling on a product detail page. I'm paying $88 for comfort. Sell me on it above the fold.

5. **Expand the size grid in the viewport.** Show at least 8–10 sizes at once on the size selector. I don't want to scroll to find my size or wonder if mine exists.

6. **Swap the homepage hero for Member Month.** The $250 Gift Card Giveaway and BOGO deal are what a 62-year-old shopper responds to. Lead with value, not a fashion collab aimed at 30-year-olds.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Eight-step mobile journey through skechers.com (homepage → login → men's category → shoes → PDP → add-to-cart → cart → search). Primary issues: Kasada bot-protection rate-limiting the cart page in production, a CORS misconfiguration breaking the Stylitics widget, the cart page rendering without document-level metadata, and a 12x TTFB spike on authenticated pages. Third-party script sprawl is severe. LCP and CLS were not captured — the most impactful Core Web Vitals are missing from this dataset.

## Accessibility

**17 violations across 3 pages.**

**View Cart — critical structural gaps (likely SPA shell issue):**
- `document-title` (serious): `<title>` element is absent. Likely caused by client-side cart rendering that replaces DOM content without updating `document.title`.
- `html-has-lang` (serious): `<html>` has no `lang` attribute on the cart page. All other pages correctly declare `lang="en"`. Confirms the cart is served from a different render path (modal takeover or separate shell) that drops document-level metadata.
- `landmark-one-main` (moderate): No `<main>` landmark present.
- `page-has-heading-one` (moderate): No `<h1>`.

**Homepage — component-level violations:**
- `aria-required-parent` (critical): ARIA role used on a child element whose required parent role is absent — likely a list or grid widget with missing container.
- `button-name` (critical): One or more `<button>` elements have no accessible name (no text, no `aria-label`, no `title`). Common in icon-only buttons (hamburger menu, search icon).
- `heading-order` (moderate): Heading hierarchy skips levels (e.g., `<h1>` → `<h3>`).
- `landmark-main-is-top-level` / `landmark-no-duplicate-main` / `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` / `landmark-unique` / `region` (moderate): Multiple landmark and region containment violations, consistent with heavy third-party injection fragmenting the page's landmark structure.
- `aria-allowed-role` (minor): Invalid `role` value on an element that doesn't permit it.

**Search page:** Repeats the landmark/region violations from homepage; likely the same shared layout component.

## Mobile Optimization

- **`--vh` CSS custom property** (`style="--vh: 6.640000000000001px"`): Standard workaround for iOS Safari's dynamic viewport height behavior. Implemented correctly as an inline CSS variable updated via JS. The fractional precision (`6.640000000000001`) suggests a raw `window.innerHeight / 100` calculation without rounding — functionally harmless but could be `parseFloat((window.innerHeight / 100).toFixed(3))`.
- **`vice-managed-src`**: Consent management platform (appears to be OneTrust or a custom CMP) holds third-party scripts behind consent. Correct pattern for GDPR/CCPA compliance.
- **Kasada KPSDK** bot protection is active. See Console & Network Errors for the mobile-impacting issue.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 rate limiting on Kasada fingerprint endpoint (6 occurrences):**
```
https://www.skechers.com/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.513
```
This is Kasada's client-side fingerprinting (`/fp`) being rate-limited. The GUID path segments are the Kasada integration identifiers. During an automated test run, the fingerprint collector retries and hits its own rate limit. The more serious finding is:

**429 on `/cart/`:** The cart page itself returned 429. Kasada is intercepting the cart request and blocking it as bot traffic. If this pattern occurs for real users (e.g., VPN users, unusual device fingerprints, or users who trigger Kasada's threshold), they see a blocked cart — a direct conversion loss event.

**CORS block on Stylitics stylesheet:**
```
Access to CSS at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?base=hotspots&theme=skechers&version=f80ade22a8'
blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header present
```
Stylitics is an outfit-inspiration / "complete the look" widget. Their CDN is not sending the required CORS header for stylesheet loads from `www.skechers.com`. This is a Stylitics-side configuration issue — Skechers needs to file a ticket with Stylitics to add `skechers.com` to their CORS allowlist for this asset. The widget will render broken or silently fail on any page where it's embedded.

**`ERR_FAILED`:** One generic network failure, likely the cascading failure from the blocked Stylitics CSS load.

## Recommendations

**Priority 1 — Revenue impact:**
1. **Kasada cart 429**: Audit Kasada's bot score thresholds for the `/cart/` route. Add a challenge-rather-than-block policy for borderline scores. The fingerprint endpoint 429s suggest the SDK's retry loop is triggering its own rate limiter — review Kasada's retry configuration or increase the `/fp` endpoint's rate limit for first-party script calls.
2. **TTFB 423ms on authenticated pages**: Profile the SFCC session resolution and Monetate personalization pipeline for logged-in users. Consider moving personalization to client-side rendering or caching the session token lookup to reduce server-to-first-byte latency.

**Priority 2 — Accessibility/legal exposure:**
3. **View Cart missing `<title>` and `lang`**: The cart render path is producing a document without these required attributes. If cart is rendered as a full-page navigation (not a modal), ensure the SFCC template includes `<title>` and `<html lang="en">`. If it's a client-side overlay, update `document.title` and confirm the `<html>` element's `lang` attribute is not being stripped.
4. **Button-name violations**: Audit icon-only buttons (likely hamburger menu, search, close) and add `aria-label` attributes.

**Priority 3 — Performance hygiene:**
5. **Deduplicate scripts**: Remove the duplicate `creativecdn` load on Men category and the duplicate `ytc.js` load on all pages. Review GTM tag firing rules for these.
6. **Capture LCP and CLS**: Instrument the test harness to record `largest-contentful-paint` and `layout-shift` PerformanceObserver entries. Without these, the performance audit is incomplete for Core Web Vitals.

**Priority 4 — Third-party:**
7. **Stylitics CORS**: File a support ticket with Stylitics to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` to `web-assets.stylitics.com` stylesheet responses.
8. **WebFont Loader**: `webfontloader.js` 1.6.28 is a 2017 library loaded from cdnjs. Consider migrating to `@next/font` or CSS `font-display: swap` with a `<link rel="preload">` for Mulish, eliminating the JS font loading overhead entirely.
