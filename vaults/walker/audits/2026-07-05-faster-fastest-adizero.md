# Faster. Fastest. ADIZERO.
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- Here we go again with adidas. Second Adizero email I've seen this month — last time it was the "Feel fast" one on June 22nd, and I said right there that I'm 62 and I want to feel comfortable, not fast. Nothing changed. "Faster. Fastest." — that subject line was never written with me in mind.
- I open it and I get three young, elite-looking athletes in coral performance gear. Then a headline about shattering marathon records. Then, I scroll a little and realize the main product push isn't even the shoe — it's a limited-edition black graphic tee. A commemorative *t-shirt*. For a marathon record. I didn't sign up to shop for collector's merchandise.
- I'd mark this read and move on. I wouldn't click, I wouldn't forward. If a buddy asked me "did adidas send you anything good lately?" I'd tell him they're stuck in performance-athlete land and haven't figured out that half their email list is guys like me who just want their feet to feel good at the end of a long day.

## What's working

- **Brand recognition is solid** — I know adidas, I trust the name, and the email looks clean and professional.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean** — hero image leads, big headline follows, body copy under that. My eye knew where to go without hunting.
- **The marathon credibility angle is interesting** — Sebastian Sawe, world records — that's real, verifiable stuff. Not made-up marketing fluff.
- **No clutter or visual chaos** — layout is simple and the text is readable at normal phone size without zooming.

## What's weak

- **The preheader text looks broken** — "The wouldn't have won." reads like a merge tag that didn't fire. That's a quality-control miss that makes me trust the sender a little less before I even open.
- **Young athletes, zero demographic match** — three elite runners in their prime; I'm 62. Not a single signal that says "this shoe is for someone your age."
- **No price, no offer, no discount** — adidas sent me a marketing story, not a reason to buy. I need a number.
- **The hero product is a t-shirt** — I opened hoping to learn about a shoe (even a performance one). A commemorative graphic tee for a marathon record is not why I'm on this email list.
- **"Feel fast" was just two weeks ago** — this is the second Adizero pitch in a row. I haven't changed my mind; sending it again doesn't help.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with comfort crossover copy, not raw speed** — even if Adizero is a racing shoe, cushioning and marathon-grade support translate to all-day wearability. That's my door in. "Built for 26.2 miles — built for everything else, too" lands differently than "Faster. Fastest."
- 2. **Drop the t-shirt section or make it secondary** — if the shoe is the hero, don't undercut it with collector's merch halfway down. If the tee is the real push, lead with the tee.
- 3. **Fix the preheader** — "The wouldn't have won" is a broken sentence. Test merge tags before every send.
- 4. **Add a concrete offer** — even a member price or a free-shipping nudge gives me a reason to click *today* instead of closing the tab.
- 5. **Show the shoe on feet that look like mine** — one image with an older male, walking on pavement, would do more for my click rate than three elite marathoners at the finish line.

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed failures: a broken deep-link URL and a missing plain-text fallback. Several HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources present mixed-content risk, and duplicate `@font-face` declarations indicate a templating/assembly issue.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken deep-link URL**
The QA prober flagged an unknown URL type on this destination:
```
/g/52b718f9-a55a-4586-8f0d-970512002e93?txn=9a156f00-78ac-11f1-b8f9-d2520cded5b8
```
The redirect target uses a `/g/` path scheme the checker cannot resolve. The click-tracking wrapper (`dv.adidas.com/o/...`) encodes a `cp_tp` payload — if the final destination resolves correctly in a browser this may be a QA prober limitation, but the link should be manually verified end-to-end before send.

**[WARN] 23 tracking links skipped**
All click links route through `click.comms.adidas.com` or `dv.adidas.com` redirect chains. These were not probed. Manual spot-check of 3–5 CTAs (especially the primary ADIZERO CTA) is required to confirm destinations are live.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] HTTP image sources (mixed content)**
The same arrow image is referenced 4× via HTTP, not HTTPS:
```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all enforce HTTPS for remote images. This image will be blocked or shown as broken in those clients. Flip to `https://`.

The tracking pixel is also served over HTTP:
```
http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019f33e0ae29-.../UXcWNTZbJdyRATzT_...
```
Same mixed-content risk; open-tracking accuracy will be degraded.

**[WARN] Missing alt text**
Two images have no `alt` attribute:
- `52b718f9-a55a-4586-8f0d-970512002e93` (likely a hero/product image via `dv.adidas.com`)
- `UXcWNTZbJdyRATzT_Ot61kgt1C0_iSwBj9PqoE9Bwjw=452` (tracking pixel — `alt=""` is acceptable here, but must be explicit)

Images-off rendering will show broken icons instead of blank space for the product image.

**[WARN] Duplicate `@font-face` declarations**
`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are declared twice in separate `<style>` blocks with slightly different `src` fallback lists (one includes `Calibri`, one does not). This is benign in most clients but signals a template assembly error — a `<style>` block was likely appended without deduplication.

**[NOTE] HTML 4.01 Transitional DOCTYPE**
`<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` is intentional for broad Outlook compatibility. No issue, but noted as intentional legacy pattern.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] Plain-text fallback absent**
```
Text version is 0 chars
```
CAN-SPAM does not explicitly mandate a plain-text part, but its absence causes deliverability score penalties with major ISPs (Gmail, Outlook) and fails RFC 2822 multipart/alternative best practice. A minimal plain-text mirror of the primary CTAs and unsubscribe link should be present.

**[NOTE] `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">`**
Suppresses the HTTP Referer header on outbound clicks. This is intentional to prevent leaking the ESP redirect URL in server logs of destination sites, but it will also suppress referrer data in any analytics not relying on UTM params. No compliance issue, but relevant to attribution.

Authentication headers (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) are not visible in the HTML source — these must be verified at the envelope level in a delivered sample. Sender domain `us-news.comms.adidas.com` is a subdomain; confirm DKIM signing key covers this subdomain specifically.
