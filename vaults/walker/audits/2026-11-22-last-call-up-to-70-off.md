# LAST CALL: Up to 70% OFF
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-22**
## Executive summary

- Shoe Carnival again. I've lost count, and now they're yelling "LAST CALL" at me for a sale they've been running since the 13th. The same $10 / $20 / $30 coupon I've been getting all week is back, and the top banner says "Black Friday Deals Are On." That doesn't feel like a last call. It feels like Tuesday. The numbers are easy to read on my phone without my glasses, I'll give them that.
- Then I scroll for something for me and find boots for women and kids, a dad with a toddler, Nike and Jordan, and Nike Air. The only shoe I actually wear is Skechers, and it gets one little red banner near the bottom. Nothing says cushioned, slip-on or arch support. There's no men's walking or comfort section at all.
- Would I click? Probably not. Maybe the Skechers 30% banner if I were already shopping. Would I forward it? No. I'd read it and move on.

## What's working

- The "Up to 70% OFF" banner and the $10 / $20 / $30 coupon ladder are big and readable without my glasses.
- The coupon code SAVEMORE is shown plainly, and there are barcodes for the store, which is handy if I'm out shopping.
- The Skechers 30% off banner is the one thing aimed at me, and it's easy to find once I scroll down.
- The sender and the look match the other Shoe Carnival emails, so I know who it's from.

## What's weak

- "LAST CALL" doesn't match a sale that's been running all week. I don't trust the deadline.
- The tiers only kick in at $59.98, $99.98 and $149.98, so that "$30 off" takes a big purchase. It's the same coupon I've seen since the 13th.
- The pictures are women's and kids' boots, a young dad with a toddler, and Nike and Jordan. That's not a 62-year-old's shopping trip.
- Skechers, my brand, is pushed to the bottom. There's no cushioning, slip-on or arch support content, and no men's walking shoes.
- The Nike section repeats itself three times (Nike & Jordan, the three-shoe row, Nike Air), so the email is long and it's hard to know where to tap.
- The fine print at the bottom is tiny and I can't read it on a phone.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with what's ending and when. If it really is the last call, give an hour and a date, and put it at the top.
- 2. Put a men's comfort and walking block up high, with Skechers slip-ins and Hands Free styles, and name the brand and the fit benefit.
- 3. Cut the Nike repeats down to one block and use the space for comfort brands.
- 4. Put the minimum spend right next to each coupon amount in large type, so I'm not hunting for it.
- 5. Use a few different models, including older men, instead of only young people and kids.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Skechers 30% off ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Men's comfort shoes: save up to $30 today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Code SAVEMORE: $10, $20 or $30 off, plus 30% off Skechers through midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Cushioned walkers and slip-ons for men, today only.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The source is a table-based ESP template (emltrk.com click and open tracking) with sound basics (`lang="en"`, viewport meta, `color-scheme`). It has one unresolved merge token in a tracking pixel, several malformed meta tags, and a mobile font size below the usual minimum. Most link, compliance and continuity checks can't be confirmed because the HTML is truncated and 29 links were not probed.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unresolved token in a pixel URL:** `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]` still contains a literal `[UNIQUE]`. If the ESP didn't substitute it, this open or beacon pixel can't attribute the send to a recipient. It may also be a vendor macro that is only rendered at send time, so verify against the raw send.
- **Malformed pixel query string:** `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20261122_nike_jordan_skechers_AM - 20261120_105735`
  - The `utm_campaign` value is nested inside the `t=` parameter, so a standard parser won't read it as a UTM parameter.
  - The value has unencoded spaces (`AM - 2026…`), which strict clients or proxies may truncate or reject.
- **Unverified links:** 29 links were skipped because they are click-redirect domains. Destination status, redirect chain and final URL are untested.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Malformed meta tags:**
  - `<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />` has no `http-equiv`, so the content-type declaration is invalid.
  - `<meta content="IE=edge" />` has no `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`, so it does nothing.
  - The `charset="utf-8"` attribute still works, so impact is low.
- **Missing alt text:** both tracking pixels (`ieezzy5m`, `0di86k1v`) have no `alt`. They should use `alt=""`, ideally with explicit `width="1" height="1"`. This is minor if they are 1×1 pixels, and image dimensions can't be confirmed from the truncated source.
- **Small mobile text:** `.responsive-text` is `12px !important` at `max-width: 600px` and `14px !important` at `min-width: 601px`. Mobile text is smaller than desktop and below the usual 14px minimum.
- **Link affordance:** the global rule `a, a:link, … { text-decoration:none !important; color:inherit !important; }` removes underlines and link colour from all anchors. Text links then rely on surrounding styling to be identifiable, and the `!important` makes this hard to override per link.
- **Doctype:** the HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype is mixed with an `xmlns` attribute on `<html>`. This is harmless in practice, but inconsistent.
- **Not verifiable:** Gmail clipping (102KB), `<img>` dimensions, and alt text on content images, because the HTML is truncated.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The `[UNIQUE]` placeholder in the `0di86k1v` pixel URL is the only token issue visible. See section 2.
- No other tokens are visible in the truncated source.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Authentication-Results header not found.** The QA check attributes this to the AgentMail relay, so it is a test-harness limitation and not evidence of an SPF/DKIM failure. Authentication should be checked against the original delivery (raw headers from a direct-to-inbox send).
- The unsubscribe link, physical postal address and `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers aren't in the provided data. This is not a confirmed problem, but it is unverified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- UTMs can't be validated, because every CTA goes through `emltrk.com` redirects and none were resolved.
- The only visible campaign identifier is the malformed `utm_campaign=SCA_20261122_nike_jordan_skechers_AM - 20261120_105735`. It is embedded in `t=` (see section 2), so it may not reach analytics as `utm_campaign`.
- Landing page alignment with the "Up to 70% OFF" claim is untested.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Confirm whether `[UNIQUE]` in `0di86k1v…?d=[UNIQUE]` is substituted at send time. If it is not, fix the ESP merge syntax.
2. URL-encode the `ieezzy5m` pixel value (spaces → `%20` or `_`). Move `utm_campaign` out of `t=` into its own query parameter if it is meant for analytics.
3. Add `alt=""`, `width="1"` and `height="1"` to both tracking pixels.
4. Fix the meta tags: `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">` and `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge">`.
5. Raise `.responsive-text` on mobile to at least 14px, and don't make it smaller than the desktop size.
6. Restore underline or colour on body-text links, for example by scoping the `text-decoration:none !important` rule to button-style anchors.
7. Rerun the QA with raw delivery headers (Authentication-Results, List-Unsubscribe) and a resolved-redirect crawl of the 29 skipped links, so the UTMs and landing pages can be verified.
