# Hot days. Cool tones.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- Four days ago HOKA sent me "New blues" — a color-drop email built entirely around icy tones and aesthetic vibes. I'm looking at today's send and I swear they just reshuffled the same deck. "Cool it down" hero image, four shoes laid out in a grid, "Shop New Colors" button. If I hadn't kept my inbox organized I'd think I accidentally opened the July 1st email again.
- The shoe in the hero is pale blue and light — pretty, sure, but there's not one word about cushioning, arch support, or how my feet feel at the end of a long day on them. The Skyward Lacelass in the grid actually caught my eye because "lacelass" sounds like it might be easy to slip on, which is exactly what my knees want in the morning. But the email does absolutely nothing with that detail. No "slip-on ease." No "step right in." Just a thumbnail sitting next to a women's Arahi.
- I'd mark this as read. I wouldn't forward it. And if HOKA shows up in my inbox tomorrow with another color story, I'm probably skimming the subject and scrolling past without opening.

## What's working

- **Shoe Finder Quiz.** "Find Your Best Fit — Take the Shoe Finder Quiz to find the best fit for your foot and your needs." That's the one line in this whole email that actually speaks to me. Fit matters more than color any day of the week.
- **Men's options are present.** Challenger 8 and Skyward Lacelass are both labeled Men's, so I'm not scrolling past an all-women's product grid.
- **Clean layout, no broken images.** Everything loaded fine on my phone. Text is readable, no overlap, no mystery white boxes.
- **Free expedited shipping is prominent.** The banner at the top is the first thing I see. $250+ is a high bar, but at least it's clearly stated.
- **HOKA is a name I trust now.** After that "Get the right fit" email from July 3rd — where they finally talked wide sizes and fit — I opened today's. Brand equity carried the open.

## What's weak

- **This is the July 1st email wearing a slightly different hat.** "New blues" was cool tones and color drops. "Hot days. Cool tones." is... cool tones and color drops. Four days apart. I noticed.
- **No price, no deal, no discount.** The only financial perk is free shipping at $250+ — that's two pairs of HOKAs. That's not an offer, that's a minimum.
- **Half the product grid is women's.** Speedgoat 7 Women, Arahi 8 Women. I'm not cross-shopping women's shoes. Makes the email feel like it wasn't built for me specifically.
- **The Skyward Lacelass is never actually described.** If "lacelass" means slip-on, that's a hands-free comfort story I'd actually read — but there's nothing. Just the name and a small photo.
- **Zero comfort language.** No cushioning, no arch support, no "your feet will thank you." The entire copy is about how the shoes look in summer colors.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Skyward Lacelass as a comfort story, not a color story.** "No laces. No bending over. Just slip in and go." That's a hook I stop scrolling for. The color can still be the secondary visual — but comfort needs to be the reason I click.
- 2. **Put a real offer in the hero.** Even 15% off the new colorways, or "free shipping on your first color drop order." $250+ free shipping buried in the header ribbon isn't motivation.
- 3. **Cut the women's SKUs or give them a separate section.** Two of four product tiles being women's dilutes the sense that this was sent specifically to me.
- 4. **Differentiate from the July 1st send.** The creative team clearly has more to say — find an angle ("cool-down recovery," "summer walks in the city," "beat the heat on the trail") that doesn't just repeat last week's colorway drop.
- 5. **Call out the quiz higher.** "Find Your Best Fit" is buried below the fold. That's the most personally relevant content in the email; it should be closer to the hero.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — broken mobile CSS (two declarations):**

```css
/* Line in @media (max-width:480px) */
.nav_padding { padding-top:px !important; padding-bottom:px !important; }
.nav_border  { border-top:px solid ; }
```

Both rules have missing numeric values before `px` and `.nav_border` is also missing its color value. These will be silently ignored by CSS parsers, meaning any element using `.nav_padding` or `.nav_border` on mobile will not render its intended spacing or border.

**`<title>` is empty:**

```html
<title></title>
```

Some email clients (Outlook desktop, Apple Mail) surface the `<title>` as the preview label in certain views. An empty title also fails basic accessibility checks (WCAG 2.4.2).

**Malformed charset meta tag:**

```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```

Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Correct form:

```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```

Without `http-equiv`, this tag has no normative effect in HTML4/XHTML; charset declaration falls back to HTTP headers or email MIME encoding only.

**Duplicate viewport meta tag:**

Two `<meta name="viewport">` tags are present with slightly different `content` values (second adds `user-scalable=yes`). The second declaration overrides the first. Redundant; remove the first.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `*` selector:**

```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }
```

Applied globally rather than to `body`, this can suppress font scaling in mobile Safari for nested elements. Standard practice is `body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; }` — which is correctly present on the `body` rule — but the `*` rule takes precedence due to specificity reset and will override it on all child elements.

**@font-face — limited client support:**

Five `@font-face` declarations load `TheFutureHOKA` variants from `https://www.hoka.com/on/demandware.static/...`. Custom web fonts render only in Apple Mail, iOS Mail, and Samsung Mail. Gmail (web/Android), Outlook (Windows), and Yahoo strip them. The fallback stack is not visible in the truncated source — confirm a web-safe fallback (e.g., `Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif`) is declared on all font-family rules in the body.
