# Your feet just know.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- "Your feet just know." Okay, I'll bite — that tagline is doing some work. It's short, it's a little cheeky, and it made me curious for a half-second what the body would say. But then I open it and the hero is a young guy in shorts holding a tote bag next to the Fresh Foam Roav — which, fine, the Roav is a good walking shoe and I've had my eye on it. The problem is the email immediately pivots away from that shoe and lands on its real pitch: **40% off apparel**. I don't want New Balance track pants. I want a comfortable pair of shoes that won't murder my arches by the third hole at the putting green. The BOGO 50% off footwear is buried underneath the apparel headline like an afterthought.
- Then I hit the CTA: "Visit a store." I'm sitting on my couch reading this on my phone. You want me to drive to a factory outlet? For apparel? That's not how I shop. That's not how most guys my age shop anymore. New Balance has been flooding my inbox — yesterday it was "almost over: up to 25% off," the day before that was "top styles on sale." Now today they're selling me shirts. I'm getting fatigued.
- I'd mark this as read and move on. I would not click, and I definitely would not forward it.

## What's working

- **"Fresh Foam Roav" in the hero** — the shoe is right up front, above the fold, and it's one I actually care about; that got my attention immediately.
- **The tagline is different** — "Your feet just know" is the first New Balance line in weeks that doesn't feel like a template. It earned a second glance.
- **BOGO 50% off footwear is a real offer** — once I read past the apparel headline, the footwear deal is genuinely strong.
- **Clean layout** — no broken images, text is readable, nothing is fighting for space.

## What's weak

- **Apparel leads, not shoes** — the headline offer is "40% off apparel," which is irrelevant to me. I'm here for footwear.
- **"Visit a store" CTA on a mobile send** — I'm on my phone. Sending me to a physical factory outlet as the primary action is friction I won't accept.
- **Model skews 30 years too young** — nothing in the visual says "comfortable shoes for a guy who's been on his feet for six decades." I don't see myself anywhere in this email.
- **No comfort details on the Roav** — if you're going to hero a specific shoe, tell me something: Fresh Foam cushioning, wide widths, slip-resistant sole. Just one line. The shoe is there but it's mute.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is the sixth email in ten days. Yesterday's "almost over: up to 25% off select shoes" already had me checking out. Today's subject pivot to apparel doesn't help.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the footwear deal, not apparel** — swap the headline order. "BOGO 50% off footwear + accessories" is the hook for me. Apparel can be a secondary line.
- 2. **Add one comfort callout on the Roav** — "Fresh Foam cushioning. Wide widths available." That's it. That's all I need to feel like this shoe was picked for me.
- 3. **Change the CTA to "Shop online"** — or at minimum add a parallel "Shop online" button alongside "Visit a store." Don't make me drive somewhere.
- 4. **Age up the model or add a secondary lifestyle shot** — show a guy my age on a walk or at a casual weekend activity. The current model speaks to his kids, not to me.
- 5. **Pull the cadence back** — six emails in ten days is eroding trust. A well-timed weekly email with a clear offer beats daily noise.

- `.bgwhite` CSS background image is declared over HTTP: `background-image: url('http://image.XXXXX.png')`. Even as a placeholder, if this pattern is used in production it will trigger mixed-content warnings and be blocked in HTTPS-loaded webmail clients (Gmail, Outlook.com). ---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@font-face` declarations are malformed — all `src:` descriptors are missing.**
Every `@font-face` block in the first `<style>` tag defines `font-family`, `font-weight`, and `font-style` but omits the required `src:` descriptor. A `@font-face` without `src` is syntactically invalid and is ignored by all CSS processors. Actual font loading is delegated entirely to:
```
@import url("https://use.typekit.net/geb2nor.css");
@import url("https://use.typekit.net/wbe3did.css");
```
However, `@import` inside `<style>` blocks is stripped by Gmail (web and Android), Apple Mail on some versions, and many B2B clients. In those clients, all custom typography silently falls back to Arial/Times New Roman. The `@font-face` skeleton blocks provide no fallback benefit.

**`.bg1` is defined twice with conflicting values:**
```css
/* definition 1 */
.bg1 {background-color: #ffffff;}
/* definition 2 — wins via cascade */
.bg1 {background:#58546d;}
```
Any element carrying `class="bg1"` renders with a dark purple (`#58546d`) background, not white. This is almost certainly unintended given the naming convention. One of these must be renamed.

**Duplicate `font-family` property in bold-italic `@font-face` block:**
```css
@font-face {
  font-family: proxima-nova,sans-serif;
  font-weight: 700;
  font-style: italic;
  font-family: proxima-nova,sans-serif; /* duplicate */
}
```
Authoring error; harmless in effect but indicates a copy-paste defect in the template.

**`<meta content="IE=edge" />` is missing `http-equiv` attribute.** Without `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"` this tag does nothing. Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
```
