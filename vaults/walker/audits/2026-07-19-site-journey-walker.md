# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 4/10 (prev 4/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-19**
## Full review
---

## 1. Executive Summary

Today I went through Skechers.com on my phone as a guy who's been wearing Slip-ins for two years and knows exactly what he wants — cushioned, arch-supported, hands-free. The site gets me to the right neighborhood (Hands Free Slip-ins, Arch Fit) but then fumbles at the finish line in ways that would make me put my phone down and call it a day. The homepage is pitching me golf sneakers in candy colors. The login step never showed me a login screen. The cart page was a complete blank. And when I searched "comfort shoes," the first two results were women's shoes. A 62-year-old with arthritic knees doesn't have patience for this kind of runaround.

**Score vs. Yesterday: No improvement — 4/10 (held, with a new critical regression on the cart page).**

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

The core purchase funnel breaks at checkout. That's not a nuisance bug — that's a lost sale, every time. Everything upstream worked well enough to get me interested, then the cart page served up a blank white screen. That alone tanks the score.

---

## 3. What's Working

**Men's navigation menu is solid.** When I tapped MENU and drilled into Men, the drawer was clean and readable — big text, generous row spacing, no squinting required. "Shoes," "Comfort Technologies," "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Extra Wide Fit" were all right there. That's exactly the language I'm looking for, and I could hit those rows without having to zoom or re-tap.

**Landing on Hands Free Slip-ins felt right.** 312 results, a big clear page title, fit-width filter pills (Wide / Extra Wide / Medium) at the top — useful for me since I run wide. The breadcrumb (Men > Shoes) let me know where I was.

**The Arch Fit 3.0 product page was the highlight.** Name was large and clear. Price ($115.00) was prominent. The "Members Get 20% OFF! Use Code: SUMMER" callout in red caught my eye immediately — that's relevant to me since I'm a loyalty member. One review with a 5-star rating, wishlist button, image carousel arrows. Clean.

**Size picker is tappable.** The size grid (7.0–14.0) had cells big enough to tap confidently without misfiring. The "Please select a size" warning was visible and clear. The sticky full-width "ADD TO CART" blue button at the bottom is exactly right — hard to miss, easy to press with a thumb.

**Discount codes were visible throughout.** The SUMMER code appeared on the top announcement bar, on the product page, and in the search results. Consistent messaging I didn't have to hunt for.

---

## 4. What's Weak

**The cart page was completely blank — a full white screen.** This is the single worst thing on the site today. I went through the whole process — found the shoe, liked it, got to the size screen — and when I navigated to the cart, there was nothing there. Not a spinner, not an error message, not even "your cart is empty." Just white. That's a broken funnel. If I were a real customer, I'd assume something went wrong and leave.

**The login step never showed a login screen.** All three of the first screenshots (Homepage, Dismiss Popups, Log In) looked essentially identical — the same "GAME ON!" hero, the same banners. I never saw a sign-in form, a "Welcome back, Walker" greeting, or any indication the site recognized me. The cart showed "0" throughout. The logged-in experience is either not triggering or not visible in this viewport. Either way, I feel like a stranger on a site I've shopped before.

**The homepage has nothing to do with me.** "GAME ON!" with bold red and green sneakers reads as young, athletic, trend-forward — the opposite of where I'm shopping. No comfort messaging above the fold. No "Slip-ins" or "Arch Fit" hero. The quick-category pills (Slip-ins, Aero Burst, Arch Fit, BOBs) are there but small, tucked below two promotional banners that push apparel discounts. I should see cushioning and comfort within 3 seconds of landing — I don't.

**Search for "comfort shoes" led with women's products.** 1,458 results sounds generous until the first two cards are both labeled "Women's." "Contour Foam – Cozy Fit Pure Comfort" (Women's, $75) and "Relaxed Fit: Glide-Step Comfort" (Women's, $48.99–$65.00). I'm a logged-in man — the site should know to filter or at least sequence results by gender. This makes me feel like the search doesn't know who I am, even if the rest of the site theoretically does.

**Product page had only 1 review on a flagship shoe.** The Arch Fit 3.0 at $115 is a premium item. One review is thin social proof, especially for a 62-year-old who leans on reviews to validate a buy. Hoka and Brooks show hundreds. That matters when I'm cross-shopping.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** A blank cart is a complete purchase blocker. Before anything else, this needs to render correctly or show a clear error state. Priority one.

2. **Make the logged-in state visible.** After login, show "Hi Walker" or at minimum personalize the homepage hero toward men's comfort shoes. The investment in a loyalty program means nothing if the site treats me like a first-time visitor.

3. **Pin gender to search results.** When a logged-in male account searches "comfort shoes," men's results should lead. Filter by account gender by default, with an easy toggle to "see all" — not the reverse.

4. **Swap the homepage hero for comfort shoppers.** "GAME ON!" is not landing with me. A headline like "Walk all day, no bending required" over an Arch Fit or Slip-in image would be far more compelling for my demographic. The golf sneakers in bold colors are for someone 30 years younger.

5. **Add width context to the Slip-ins landing page.** I'm wide-footed and that matters for Slip-ins fit. The Wide/Extra Wide filter pills are there, but the product cards in the scroll don't surface width information. Showing "Available in Wide" on the card thumbnail saves me a click per shoe.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

**4/10 — same score as yesterday, no improvement, with a new regression.**

The navigation structure is sound and the product pages are clean. But a blank cart is a broken sale, and the site still doesn't feel like it knows who I am after logging in. For me to trust my $115 here instead of walking into a DSW or ordering from Hoka's website, Skechers needs to actually use the data they have on me. Right now they're collecting loyalty points and showing me women's shoes in search. That's not a relationship — that's a directory.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Eight-step mobile journey audited across skechers.com (SFCC/Demandware stack). Primary failure: the Kasada bot-protection SDK is rate-limiting itself on legitimate sessions, causing a 429 on `/cart/` that renders the View Cart page as a bare error shell (missing `<title>` and `lang`). Secondary issues: Stylitics widget CORS breakage, a 17× TTFB spike at login/category, and 18 axe violations spanning critical ARIA and missing semantic structure.

---

## Accessibility

**18 violations across 3 page types.**

| Severity | Rule | Page | Root Cause |
|----------|------|------|------------|
| Critical | `button-name` | Homepage | Icon-only buttons (likely carousel/hamburger) have no `aria-label` or inner text |
| Critical | `aria-required-parent` | Homepage | ARIA list/grid items rendered outside their required parent container |
| Serious | `link-name` | Homepage | Anchor tags with no text content and no `aria-label` (likely image links) |
| Serious | `document-title` | View Cart | `<title>` is empty or absent — confirmed caused by 429 error page being served |
| Serious | `html-has-lang` | View Cart | Same root cause: Kasada 429 returns a minimal error shell without `lang="en"` |
| Moderate | `heading-order` | Homepage | H-level skipped (e.g., H1 → H3) in page structure |
| Moderate | `landmark-no-duplicate-main` | Homepage | Multiple `<main>` or `role="main"` elements present simultaneously |
| Moderate | `landmark-main-is-top-level` | Homepage | `<main>` nested inside another landmark element |
| Moderate | `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` | Homepage, Search | `<aside>` or `role="complementary"` nested rather than top-level |
| Moderate | `landmark-unique` | Homepage, Search | Multiple instances of the same landmark role with no distinguishing label |
| Moderate | `region` | Homepage, Search | Content outside any landmark region |
| Moderate | `page-has-heading-one` | View Cart | No `<h1>` in error page shell |
| Minor | `aria-allowed-role` | Homepage, Search | `role` value set on an element that does not permit overriding its implicit role |

The View Cart cluster (4 violations) is entirely caused by the 429 — fix the bot-detection issue and those disappear. The Homepage cluster is structural and requires template-level fixes.

## SEO

- **View Cart missing `<title>`**: Googlebot indexing the cart URL (even if `noindex`) will log a title-missing error in GSC. Fix: ensure Kasada error pages and any fallback render include the full `<head>`.
- **Duplicate Monetate entry scripts**: `se.monetate.net/js/2/` and `se.monetate.net/js/3/` both load on the same page. If the v2 script fires GTM events, those events may double-fire and pollute analytics/attribution.
- **Protocol-relative URL**: `//se.monetate.net/js/2/entry.js` (no scheme). On HTTP it loads over plain HTTP. Should be `https://`.
- No structural SEO issues detected in available HTML (canonical, hreflang, structured data not visible in truncated source).

## Mobile Optimization

- **`--vh` CSS custom property** is set inline on `<html>` (`--vh: 6.64px`). This is a standard workaround for the iOS Safari 100vh bug. Confirms mobile-aware implementation, but indicates they are not using `dvh`/`svh` units (CSS `dynamic-viewport-height`, which is now baseline across browsers). Leaving the JS polyfill in place is harmless but unnecessary overhead on modern browsers.
- No mobile-specific structural issues visible in truncated source. Viewport meta tag not captured in truncated HTML but `lang` + `--vh` pattern is consistent with a mobile-first SFCC template.
- The Stylitics widget (outfit recommendations/styling feature) is **completely broken on mobile** due to the CORS failure — see Console Errors.

## Console & Network Errors

**Kasada rate-limit cascade (6× HTTP 429):**
```
/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39.../fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.522
```
This is the Kasada bot-protection fingerprinting endpoint. The SDK calls `/fp` on page load to establish a trusted session token. Five consecutive 429s on the same endpoint indicate the SDK is retry-looping after being rate-limited by its own backend. **This is a Kasada configuration or capacity issue, not a Skechers code issue** — but it produces visible console noise and causes downstream failures.

**Cart page 429:**
```
429 https://www.skechers.com/cart/
```
The cart route itself is returning 429. This means Kasada is classifying the session as bot-like (possibly because the fingerprinting loop above failed to establish a valid token) and blocking the page render. **This is the root cause of all four View Cart accessibility violations.** A user whose session gets flagged sees no cart — they get a bare error shell.

**Stylitics CORS failure:**
```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?base=grid&theme=skechers&version=696916bb3b'
has been blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```
The Stylitics CDN is not returning CORS headers for cross-origin stylesheet requests. This is a Stylitics-side configuration issue. The `ERR_FAILED` that follows is the cascade of the blocked CSS. The widget either renders unstyled or not at all.

## Recommendations

**P0 — Cart blocked by bot protection (revenue impact)**
Coordinate with Kasada to investigate why legitimate browser sessions are being classified as bots and receiving 429 on `/cart/`. Reproduce with a clean browser profile + no extensions. The fingerprinting loop suggests the `/fp` token isn't establishing before downstream requests fire — may need to increase the initial retry window or fix a race condition in the SDK initialization order.

**P0 — Stylitics CORS**
File a ticket with Stylitics support: their CDN at `web-assets.stylitics.com` is missing `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` (or wildcard) on stylesheet responses. The widget is silently broken for all users.

**P1 — Capture LCP and CLS**
Web Vitals data is incomplete. LCP and CLS are the two primary Google ranking signals. Add `web-vitals` instrumentation or verify that Quantum Metric (already loaded) is capturing these and confirm the data pipeline is working.

**P1 — Fix Homepage ARIA violations**
- Add `aria-label` to all icon-only buttons (hamburger, carousel controls, wishlist)
- Add `aria-label` to image-only links
- Audit and fix `aria-required-parent` violations — likely a component rendering list children without the parent list wrapper
- Consolidate to a single `<main>` element; ensure `<aside>` is not nested inside `<main>`

**P1 — Investigate Login/Men TTFB spike**
428ms vs 25ms baseline is a significant regression on the two highest-traffic authenticated paths. Profile the SFCC request pipeline for these routes — session init, Monetate server-side, and any middleware that runs only on authenticated requests.

**P2 — Remove duplicate third-party scripts**
- `tags.creativecdn.com` fires twice on Men category — remove the duplicate tag from the GTM container or the template
- Audit whether both Monetate v2 and v3 scripts are intentional; they are likely loading the same functionality twice
- Duplicate Yahoo `ytc.js` entries visible in all pages

**P2 — Protocol-relative URL**
Change `//se.monetate.net/js/2/entry.js` to `https://se.monetate.net/js/2/entry.js`.

**P3 — Reduce Web Font weights**
Mulish is loaded in 6 weights (300–800). Audit CSS usage and drop any unused weights. `font-display: swap` should be confirmed to avoid invisible text during load.
