# This is perfect for you!
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-21**
## Executive summary

- Well, "This is perfect for you" and then the hero item is a women's Arch Fit sandal — that's not perfect for me, that's not even for me. First thing I see is "STILL THINKING ABOUT IT? YOU'VE GOT GREAT TASTE!" which is another one of these fake cart-tracking lines, and this time they didn't even get the shoe right for whoever's cart they're pretending to know. Once I get down to the grid of "more options," there's a decent men's slip-on and even that Max Cushioning Arch Fit sneaker I'd actually look twice at — but I had to scroll past a women's sandal to find it.
- No price, no percent off, nothing about arch support or cushioning called out in words anywhere, just pictures. Compared to yesterday's "Wide Fit, High Style" email that actually spoke to something real about my feet, this one's a step backward — it's the same recycled "you were browsing" gimmick from earlier in the week, just with a different shoe stapled to the top.
- Not clicking this one. Mislabeled hero product, no offer, nothing urgent — straight to read and forget.

## What's working

- The men's Max Cushioning Arch Fit sneaker and the Arch Fit Arcade slip-ons in the grid are genuinely my kind of shoe, if only they'd led with those.
- Clean, uncluttered layout — big product photos, easy to tap, no eye strain reading it on my phone.
- "TAKE ANOTHER LOOK" button is clear and easy to hit with a thumb.

## What's weak

- Hero product is a women's sandal — a mismatch as bad as the pink D'Lites they kept pushing me all week, just a different flavor of wrong.
- "Still thinking about it?" — I wasn't. No cart, no browsing history that I recall, same fiction as five other emails this week.
- Zero mention of price, discount, or any reason to act today — nothing time-bound, nothing that separates this from a browse-the-catalog afterthought.
- Subject line "This is perfect for you!" promises personalization the email doesn't deliver — feels like it was written before anyone picked the product image.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the targeting bug first — don't show a man a women's sandal and call it "perfect for you." Lead with the Arch Fit Arcade or Max Cushioning sneaker instead; those actually fit my browsing.
- 2. Put a real number in the hero — a % off or dollar amount. Right now there's no reason to click over just looking next time I'm in a store.
- 3. Drop the fake "still thinking about it" framing when there's no real cart data behind it — it's burned trust after this many identical sends this week.
- 4. Swap in actual comfort/fit language — "arch support," "all-day cushion" — since that's the one thing that'd make me stop scrolling.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Cushioned Slip-Ins, No Laces Required`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Arch support built in. Zero break-in time.`
