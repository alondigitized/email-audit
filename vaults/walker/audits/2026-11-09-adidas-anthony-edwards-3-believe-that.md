# adidas Anthony Edwards 3: Believe That
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-09**
## Executive summary

- Another adidas email, and I've lost count since the 15th. This one at least has a shoe up top, an orange basketball shoe floating on black. But it's a basketball shoe for a 20-something NBA guard, and I walk for comfort. Nothing in the first screen tells me it's meant for me.
- The one line I cared about is buried in the body: "Hyperboost… high cushioning and high energy return." Cushioning is my word. Then the email wanders off into football rookies and a giveaway for adiClub members. There's no price and no percent off. Compared with the 11-05 "Up to 50% off" email, which at least had a number, this one gives me nothing to weigh.
- Would I click? Probably not. I'd skim it and mark it read. I wouldn't forward it, since nobody I know is shopping for a signature basketball shoe.

## What's working

- The hero shoe is big and clear, and it's the first thing I see.
- The "high cushioning" line speaks to what I care about, even though the shoe isn't for me.
- The buttons are black with white text and large enough to tap with my thumb.
- "Find Store Now" is handy. I like trying shoes on before I buy.
- The headline type is large and readable on my phone.

## What's weak

- There's no price, no discount and no member pricing, so there's nothing to compare.
- The shoe is a low-cut basketball model built for "speed and movement." That's the opposite of what I look for.
- The "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" banner at the top is generic and pulls attention from the hero.
- The second half is a football rookie giveaway for adiClub members. It has nothing to do with the first half, and it makes the email feel scattered.
- Two stacked buttons, "Shop Now" and "Find Store Now," give me two choices where one would do. The "Participate" and "Learn More" pair repeats the problem.
- The tiny preheader link, "Shop the latest from Anthony Edwards 3," looks like plain underlined text.
- The subject says "Believe That" while the headline says "With Love." That's a mismatch, and neither tells me what the shoe does for my feet.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one story per email. Keep the shoe, or the giveaway, but not both.
- 2. Put the comfort benefit in the headline. Something like "Hyperboost cushioning, now on the court" gets a walker's attention. Better still, send the comfort-focused Hyperboost line to people who buy comfort shoes.
- 3. Show a price and one button.
- 4. Cut the generic best-sellers banner.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Ant's new shoe: cushioning built in`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `First Hyperboost basketball shoe. See it in store near you.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Lightweight, springy and available at your local adidas store.`
