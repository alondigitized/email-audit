# (!!) Freebie at your local DSW
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-17**
## Executive summary

- So the subject says "Freebie at your local DSW" and I'm thinking, okay, maybe a free pair of insoles or a coupon just for walking in — instead I open it and it's the exact same "UP TO 60% OFF CLEARANCE" banner I've been staring at all week, and the "freebie" turns out to be a free tote bag if I spend $99 and become a VIP. That's not a freebie, that's a loyalty upsell with a bait-and-switch subject line, and by now I've caught on to the trick.
- Below the fold it's the same parade — size grid 5 through 11, boots, sneakers, heels, dress shoes — nothing says men's, nothing says comfort, nothing says arch support. I did spot the Jack Rogers "sandal" thumbnail down near the bottom which at least looks like it could be unisex, but there's zero copy telling me that.
- This is a mark-as-read, maybe an unsubscribe-consideration. I'm not clicking and I'm sure not forwarding this to anybody — there's nothing here for a guy like me.

## What's working

- The red "SHOP BY SIZE" grid is at least a clean, tappable layout if I were shoe-hunting.
- No render bugs — everything loaded, text is crisp, nothing overlapping.
- The tote bag graphic is visually clear even if the offer itself is misleading relative to the subject.

## What's weak

- Subject promised a "freebie" and it's actually a $99 spend threshold plus a VIP signup — that's false advertising by inbox standards.
- Same "up to 60% off clearance" hero I've now seen five-plus times this week — DSW's own history shows this exact banner repeating daily.
- Size grid is 5–11, all women's sizing, right up top — tells me instantly this isn't for me.
- Every single category — boots, sneakers, heels, dress shoes, casual shoes — is women's product, no men's section anywhere I can see.
- Nothing about cushioning, arch support, slip-on comfort, or anything close to what actually gets me to buy shoes.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the subject-to-content mismatch first — either lead with an honest "up to 60% off clearance" subject or actually put a no-strings freebie in the email. Don't call a $99-spend tote a "freebie."
- 2. Segment men out of the women's-only sends — if DSW has zero men's comfort inventory to show me, stop emailing me sneaker/boot/heel drops entirely.
- 3. Retire this exact clearance banner — I've now seen it enough times it's white noise; even the number should rotate or feel fresher.
- 4. If DSW wants my click, show me a men's walking or slip-on shoe with a real price and mention "cushioned" or "arch support" in the copy.

- **Subject Alt B:** `New arch-support sneakers just landed`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping over $35 — shop men's walking styles`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Cushioned comfort, clearance prices`
