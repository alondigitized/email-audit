# Get our favorite styles up to 40% off
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-09**
## Executive summary

- I'm a Skechers guy, not really an adidas man, so right away this email is fighting an uphill battle with me. It landed in my inbox, I recognized the brand well enough, and the "up to 40% off" in the subject got my attention for about two seconds — but when I opened it, I got a dark hero image, big letters saying "ADIDAS FAVORITES," and almost nothing else. No men's shoes front and center, no comfort story, no specific products to look at. It's like they put up a billboard that says "store open, come browse." That's not how I shop. I need to see the shoe, know it fits my foot, and know what I'm saving. This email gives me none of that.

## What's working

- The "up to 40% off" language in the subject line got me to open. That's real money and it registered fast. Once inside, the email loads cleanly — no broken images, no garbled text, nothing confusing. The hero is big and bold, and the offer copy below it is easy to read, which matters when I'm on my phone and not squinting. The footer is tidy and the unsubscribe link is right there where it should be — I appreciate that. Brand feels legitimate.

## What's weak

- Almost everything below the hero is missing. I see "ADIDAS FAVORITES — UP TO 40% OFF" and then a couple of lines of body copy, and then... basically nothing. No product images. No men's shoes. No comfort technology. No slip-ons, no walking shoes, nothing that says "Walker, this is for you." The SHOP NOW button sends me to who-knows-where on the site and I'd have to dig around to find anything relevant. At my age, I don't dig around. If I don't see it, I'm gone.
- The hero image itself is dark and abstract — I can barely make out what I'm looking at. Looks like fabric or maybe a shoe, but I genuinely can't tell. That's a missed opportunity. Show me the actual product on a real person — ideally a guy who looks like he's out for a walk, not a fashion shoot.
- There's no deadline. "While supplies last" means nothing to me — I've seen that on every sale since 1987. Give me a date. "Sale ends Sunday" gets my attention. "While supplies last" gets a shrug.
- And frankly: I'm a Skechers Plus member. Adidas doesn't know that, obviously, but they also don't know anything about me from this email. No personalization, no mention of what category I might care about, nothing. I'm being treated like a stranger off the street.

## Recommendations

- **Put a real shoe in the email.** Show me two or three men's styles with photos, names, and the discounted price next to the original. I want to see what I'm getting.
- **Add a deadline.** "Sale ends Sunday, May 11" beats "while supplies last" every time for getting me off the fence.
- **Add a men's-specific section or CTA.** Even a "Shop Men's Sale" button would help me get to what's relevant faster.
- **Lighten up that hero image.** I can barely read it on my phone screen. A cleaner, brighter image of a wearable shoe would do more selling.
- **Segment the send.** If I had browsed men's running shoes or casual shoes previously, show me those. Blanket sale emails feel lazy.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — adidas "Get our favorite styles up to 40% off"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed failures: a broken redirect URL and a missing plain-text fallback. Six arrow images are served over HTTP and will be blocked by most modern email clients. No other structural anomalies in the HTML source.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect — relative URL in href**
The QA checker caught a link resolving to a relative path:
```
unknown url type: '/g/8f6e2148-82b1-4d5f-9544-54bdd35ab0e5?txn=ee2dfc80-4b64-11f1-9901-d25be01a53a3'
```
The full click-tracking URL wraps to `https://dv.adidas.com/o/8f6e2148-...`. The `/g/` path returned by the redirect chain is a relative URL, meaning the click-tracker is issuing a redirect to a path rather than an absolute URL. This will 404 or behave inconsistently across clients.

**[WARN] 29 tracking links unverified**
All click-wrapped links route through `dv.adidas.com` and `click.comms.adidas.com`. The QA prober skipped these. The broken link above was only caught because the redirect target leaked. Manual spot-checking of 3–5 destination URLs is recommended.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[FAIL] Six HTTP image sources — will be blocked**
The same arrow asset is referenced 6× via HTTP:
```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
Gmail, Outlook 365, and Apple Mail all proxy or block mixed-content images. All six will render as broken images. Additionally, one pixel/tracking image from `click.comms.adidas.com` is also HTTP:
```
http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019e0b1fedba-5a1cbad1-2349-42be-a75a-98235c6a88f3-000000/...
```

**[WARN] Two images missing `alt` text**
- `8f6e2148-82b1-4d5f-9544-54bdd35ab0e5` (linked hero/product image via `dv.adidas.com`)
- `NSt8xo5jJB_gvo0u8BD9ynbqBo3yX_vfZEnlpoz7I_s=452` (the HTTP tracking pixel above)

Screen readers and image-off rendering modes will have no fallback text for the first one. The second is likely a tracking pixel; `alt=""` (empty, not absent) is correct for decorative/invisible images and should be set explicitly.

**[WARN] Malformed charset meta tag**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, this tag has no effect. Most clients tolerate this, but it is technically non-compliant.

**[WARN] Duplicate `@font-face` declarations**
`AdihausDIN` (400, 700) and `AdineuePRO` (400, 600) are declared twice across two separate `<style>` blocks. Both blocks point to identical local-font fallbacks (Arial, Helvetica). The duplication adds ~1 KB of parse overhead with no benefit.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `*|FNAME|*`) are visible in the provided HTML. No conditional blocks appear broken.

---

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text fallback is 0 characters**
The MIME multipart `text/plain` part is empty. CAN-SPAM does not explicitly require plaintext, but many spam filters (SpamAssassin, Postmark's inbound scorer) penalize missing or empty plaintext parts. Some corporate mail gateways reject messages with no plaintext alternative entirely.

**[WARN] `no-referrer` meta suppresses analytics referrer**
```html
<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />
```
This strips the `Referer` header on all link clicks. Email clients that render in a WebView (some Android clients, Samsung Mail) will not pass referrer data to adidas's analytics stack. This is intentional for privacy hygiene but means server-side referrer-based attribution is unavailable as a fallback.

**Sending domain:** `us-news.comms.adidas.com` — SPF/DKIM/DMARC coverage for this subdomain cannot be assessed from HTML alone. No authentication header data was included in the QA findings.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**[WARN] Tracking uses proprietary parameters, not standard UTM**
All outbound links use `cp_tp`, `cp_cid`, `mi_cid`, `mi_mid` parameters — adidas's internal attribution schema, not standard `utm_source/utm_medium/utm_campaign`. This is not an error, but it means Google Analytics / GA4 will not attribute sessions to this email without a corresponding UTM layer or server-side event mapping. If GA4 is in use for cross-channel reporting, UTM params should be appended downstream of the click-tracker redirect.

**[FAIL] Broken redirect destination (see §2)**
The `/g/` relative redirect means at least one CTA lands on a broken URL, severing the email-to-site flow entirely for that link.
