# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-11-09**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I walked through Skechers.com on my phone looking for a comfortable, slip-on, arch-supported men's shoe. The first half went fine. The homepage was readable and the Men menu was clear. The Men's Shoes page had a "Hands Free Slip-ins" tile right at the top, which is what I came for. The second half fell apart. I never got a shoe into my cart. The Add to Cart screen only showed "Please select a size", and the cart page was completely blank white. The "comfort shoes" search showed women's shoes first. The login step looked identical to the logged-out page, so nothing felt like it knew me.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10**, the same as yesterday.

The browsing is decent, but a shopper who can't get a shoe into the cart or see the cart doesn't buy. A blank cart is the worst thing a page can do to someone my age, because I assume the site is broken and go to Hoka or Brooks.

## 3. What's Working

- **The header is clear.** The logo, search, account, cart and MENU are all visible and labeled. The word "MENU" under the icon helps someone who doesn't know what three lines mean.
- **The Men menu is easy to follow.** The text is large and the rows are tall, so a thumb can hit them. The sections are Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections, Collaborations and Clothing & Accessories. "Comfort Technologies" speaks to me.
- **The category tabs match what I want.** Slip-ins, Wide Fit and Arch Fit sit right under the header on the homepage.
- **The Men's Shoes page has a "Hands Free Slip-ins" tile first,** then Walking Shoes. That is my shopping list.
- **The product page has what I look at first.** It shows the price ($165), a 4.5-star rating with 186 reviews, and the Klarna/Afterpay payment options.
- **The size selector is good.** The buttons are big and easy to tap. Sold-out sizes are crossed out and have a little bell for restock alerts.
- **The Add to Cart button is big, dark blue and obvious.**

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart page was blank white.** There was no cart, no message and no error. I would assume the site had crashed.
- **Add to Cart didn't add anything.** The screen showed a red "Please select a size" and the cart badge still said 0. The error is fair, but I had to scroll down to find the size buttons. The site didn't scroll me to them or tell me at the top of the page.
- **Login didn't feel like anything happened.** The page looked the same as before. There was no "Welcome back, Walker", no name and no change to the account icon. I can't tell whether I'm signed in.
- **The popup step added clutter.** The promo strip cut off mid-sentence ("Limited Time Only! 25% OFF Work & Safety - Appli..."), and a stray "Details" link sat off to the side.
- **The promos are confusing.** The top banner says "20% OFF Regular Priced Items", but the product page says "Excluded from promotions" in small orange text. A $165 shoe I can't get a discount on makes me distrust the banner. It didn't match what I saw scrolling, and that matters to me.
- **The search for "comfort shoes" put women's shoes first** (Relaxed Fit: Parallel Lite, women's). I'm a man, and it hadn't picked up on that from my browsing. There were 1,653 results, which is a lot to wade through.
- **The product photo sits below the fold.** On my phone I saw the title, price and payment plans before any picture of the shoe. I want to see the shoe first.
- **Some text is small.** The "Excluded from promotions" line, the gray "809 results" and the "Applied at Cart" lines are hard to read without zooming. The category tabs also cut off at the edge ("Max Cus...").
- **Nothing helps me compare or do the price-vs-quality math.** I didn't see a comparison to Hoka, Brooks or New Balance, and nothing about cushioning or arch support on the product page I landed on. The shoe I landed on was glow-in-the-dark, which isn't what I'd pick.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page first.** A blank page is a lost sale. At minimum, show an empty-cart message with a "Keep shopping" button. If it was a loading failure, add a retry.
2. **Make Add to Cart forgiving.** If no size is picked, scroll to the size buttons and highlight them. Keep the error message right next to the button. Better still, preselect my usual size when I'm logged in.
3. **Show I'm logged in.** Put my name or a "Hi, Walker" in the header. Remember my size and width and my past comfort preferences.
4. **Match the promo to the product.** If a shoe is excluded, say so next to the banner or on the product tile before I click. Don't let the banner promise 20% and the product page take it back.
5. **Show the shoe first on the product page,** with the price and reviews below it. Add a short line for cushioning, arch support and slip-in ease near the top.
6. **Respect that I'm shopping the Men's section.** Search results and recommendations should lean men's.
7. **Increase text size** on the small gray and orange lines to at least 14–16px, and let the category tabs wrap or scroll with a visible arrow.
8. **Add a simple "How it compares" block** for comfort and price. For example: "Same cushioning as shoes at $X more."

## 6. Bottom Line

The Skechers front door is welcoming, and the menu and filters speak to someone who wants comfort and slip-ins. But I couldn't finish the one thing that matters, which is putting a shoe in my cart and seeing it there. A blank cart page after a failed Add to Cart sends me to another brand. ## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

- **Data gaps:** LCP and CLS were not captured for any of the 8 steps (all `?`). Real-user Core Web Vitals can't be assessed from this run. Of the 21 console errors, only 10 were listed.
- **Cart step failed:** `GET /cart/` returned **429**. The View Cart page has no `<title>`, no `lang`, no `<main>` and no `<h1>`, which is consistent with a bare rate-limit or error response, not the real cart template.
- **URL did not change:** the HTML samples for Homepage, Log In and Men category all report `https://www.skechers.com/`. Either those steps are in-page overlays or SPA transitions, or the journey didn't actually navigate. Per-step results may not reflect distinct pages.
- **Accessibility:** 18 axe violations in total, 2 critical, 3 serious.
- **Staging GTM in production:** the GTM container loads with `gtm_preview=env-1&gtm_auth=...`.

- **Duplicate and nested `<main>`:** the Homepage has more than one `<main>` and a `<main>` that isn't top-level. This suggests nested or duplicated templates.
- **Complementary landmark:** an `<aside>` or `role=complementary` is nested inside another landmark on both Homepage and Search.
- **Unnamed controls:** icon-only buttons and links have no accessible name. On a mobile header these are typically the menu, cart, search and logo controls.
- **View Cart:** the missing `lang`, `<title>` and `<main>` look like the error response, not a template bug. Confirm by re-running once the 429 is resolved. If it is the 429 page, rate-limit responses should still ship `lang` and `<title>`.
- **Recurring issues:** `landmark-unique`, `region` and `aria-allowed-role` appear on both Homepage and Search, so they are likely in shared header/footer templates.

## SEO

- The Homepage has `<html lang="en">`, which is fine.
- View Cart is missing `<title>` and `lang`. If that is a real template (not a 429 body), it is an SEO and i18n defect. Cart pages should usually be `noindex` anyway.
- Page-level SEO (canonical, meta description, structured data, `<h1>`) could not be verified because the HTML samples are truncated.
- The URL stays at `/` for Log In and Men category, so those steps cannot be evaluated for crawlable, distinct URLs.

## Performance

- **LCP and CLS:** not captured, so no pass/fail can be given for either.
- **TTFB:** all measured values are under 500ms, which is acceptable. Slowest: Log In and Men category (465ms), Homepage (356ms).
- **Very low TTFB:** the 27ms values on Men > Shoes, Product detail and Add to Cart are cache-hit or non-document-navigation values (SPA, bfcache or prefetch). They shouldn't be read as server performance, and they strengthen the doubt about whether these steps were true navigations.
- **Third-party weight:** the Homepage `<head>` injects 20+ third-party tags: GTM, GA4/gtag, Monetate, Quantum Metric, TikTok, Bing, Taboola, Reddit, Snap, Nextdoor, Pinterest, Yahoo, Impact, Rokt-style tune.js, Pulse Insights, Bloomreach and RTB House. Men category adds more (Taboola, Snapchat, Reddit, Nextdoor, Facebook). Cumulative main-thread and network cost is likely significant on mobile.
- **Webfont loader:** webfont loading goes through `cdnjs.cloudflare.com/.../webfontloader.js` (async). This adds a third-party dependency on the critical text-rendering path and risks FOUT and layout shifts.
- **Script rewriting:** `vice-managed-src` attributes show a consent or script-management layer rewriting tag sources at runtime.

## Mobile Optimization

- Viewport and `--vh` handling is present (`--vh: 6.64px`, i.e. a ~664px viewport height), which is a JS-driven viewport-height workaround.
- No layout, tap-target or viewport meta data was provided, so there is no evidence of defects. The unlabelled buttons and links under Accessibility also affect mobile and assistive-tech users.

## Console & Network Errors

**Console (21 reported, 10 shown):**
- `Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document` (6 of the 10 shown). A script, probably a third-party tag, registers an `unload` handler that the document's Permissions-Policy blocks. `unload` is deprecated and also blocks bfcache eligibility.
- `Unsupported Summarizer API languages were specified...` (4 of the 10 shown). This is Chrome's built-in Summarizer API being called with unsupported language options. The caller is likely a third-party script or an injected extension. Identify it from the stack trace.

**Network (1):**
- `429 https://www.skechers.com/cart/`: the cart page was rate-limited. It was probably triggered by the automated journey, but a 429 on a core checkout path means bot or rate-limit rules can block legitimate shoppers (shared mobile-carrier IPs, retries).

**Staging config:** GTM is requested with `gtm_preview=env-1` and `gtm_auth=7lRL-...`. A preview-environment GTM container is being served in production, which could load unpublished tags.

## Recommendations

1. **Fix accessibility defects:** add accessible names to icon-only buttons and links (`button-name`, `link-name`). Correct ARIA parent/child roles (`aria-required-parent`). Collapse to a single top-level `<main>`. Move `<aside>` out of nested landmarks. Give duplicate landmarks unique `aria-label`s. Fix heading order.
2. **Cart rate-limiting:** review the WAF/CDN rules on `/cart/`. Return a proper HTML error page (with `lang`, `<title>` and `Retry-After`) when it returns 429. Re-run the journey with realistic pacing to see the real cart template.
3. **Remove the preview GTM parameters** (`gtm_preview`, `gtm_auth`, `gtm_cookies_win`) from production, and verify only published container versions are served.
4. **Remove or replace `unload` handlers:** find the offending tag and swap in `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`. This restores bfcache eligibility and clears the Permissions-Policy errors.
5. **Find the Summarizer API caller** and constrain it to supported languages, or remove it.
6. **Audit third-party tags:** consolidate tags through GTM or server-side tagging, defer non-essential pixels until after interaction or consent, and self-host webfontloader.
7. **Fix the measurement harness:** capture LCP and CLS (`PerformanceObserver` with `buffered: true`, finalized after load or on page hide). Record the actual URL per step so that same-URL steps can be verified as real navigations.
