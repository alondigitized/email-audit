# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-30**
## Executive summary

- **From:** SKECHERS | **Subject:** Did you forget something?
- This is a cart abandonment email, and I'll give Skechers credit — they remembered what I was looking at. There's a Slip-ins right there at the top, which is my thing. The "READY TO CHECK OUT?" headline is clear enough that even on my phone I get the idea fast. But after that one product, the email kind of falls apart into a pile of category links and a barely-there 20% off offer that I nearly missed. The email had me at hello, then lost me in the scroll.
- Gets the product in front of me, misses the landing on urgency and personalization.

## What's working

- **The hero product is right.** A men's Slip-ins — Arch Fit Glide-Step Pro — is sitting front and center. That's what I looked at, that's what I came here to see. Good.
- **Headline is readable.** "READY TO CHECK OUT?" on a big gold banner is legible on my phone without squinting. No tiny serif font, no design-over-function nonsense.
- **"COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" CTA is clear.** Two of them, actually — once in the banner, once below the product. Hard to miss.
- **20% OFF is a real reason to come back.** I'm a Plus member and price-conscious. A single-pair discount gets my attention, even if the banner is a bit small.
- **The app download section and Store Finder at the bottom** — I appreciate the curbside pickup callout. Sometimes I just want to go try the shoe on.

## What's weak

- **The 20% OFF offer is buried.** After the hero and the product grid, there's a dark banner that says "20% OFF A SINGLE PAIR." I almost scrolled past it. That's the hook that should've been *above* the product, not below a wall of recommended styles.
- **The "Shop Additional Styles" section is lazy.** WOMEN, MEN, KIDS, CLOTHING, NEW ARRIVALS, SALE — that's just the website menu dumped into an email. I don't need a directory, I need someone to point me at the right shoe.
- **Four small product thumbnails in the grid are women's shoes.** I can see Women's Slip-ins labeled right there. I'm a 62-year-old guy. Why am I being shown pink and floral styles? That's clutter that makes me feel like this wasn't really made for me.
- **No price shown on the hero product.** I want to know if that Glide-Step Pro is $89 or $139 before I decide if the 20% is worth clicking for.
- **The Mother's Day Sale fine print at the bottom** is tiny gray text that I can't really read without glasses. If there's a sale going on right now, tell me upfront — don't hide it in a footnote.
- **Too long.** By the time I hit the social icons I've forgotten why I opened this.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the 20% OFF offer to the top**, right under the hero product. Lead with the rescue offer: "Here's 20% off to finish what you started."
- 2. **Cut the women's products from the grid.** I'm a male shopper. Show me men's colors and men's styles only.
- 3. **Show the product price** on the hero card. Price + discount = decision. Right now I have to click just to find out if it's worth clicking.
- 4. **Replace the "Shop Additional Styles" directory** with 2-3 curated men's Slip-ins picks: "You might also like these." Something with a photo, not just a text link.
- 5. **Surface the Mother's Day sale** if it's relevant — or drop it entirely from this email. An abandoned cart email should have one job.

### 1. Technical Summary

Abandoned cart email delivered via Attentive (ESP: `msgs.skechers.com`, click tracking: `skechers.attentivemail.com`). HTML is table-based and structurally standard, but several infrastructure-level issues are present in the visible source.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**HTTP tracking URLs (not HTTPS)**
All click-tracked links use `http://` (not `https://`) for the Attentive redirect domain:
```
http://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=u001...
```
This sends the subscriber through an unencrypted redirect before the eventual HTTPS destination. Most modern email clients will flag or strip these. The tracking domain should be configured to force HTTPS.

**UPN-encoded destination — UTMs unverifiable from source**
Destination URLs and any UTM parameters are base64/URL-encoded inside the `upn=` parameter. Cannot verify UTM presence or accuracy from the HTML alone (see §6).

**"Web version" link present**
Attentive-tracked link present in row-1; no issues beyond the HTTP scheme above.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<head><title></title>...
```
Screen readers announce the `<title>` as the document label in some clients (Outlook web, older Android). Should be populated with the subject line or a descriptive label.

**Global link style override strips decoration**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This rule is applied unconditionally and will flatten the visual treatment of the unsubscribe link if it lives inside `#MessageViewBody`, making it indistinguishable from body text. Should be scoped to content links, not the full container.

**Mixed preheader spacer characters**
The preheader padding uses two different Unicode spacer strategies in the same string:
- `͏` (U+034F, Combining Grapheme Joiner) × ~150
- `­` (U+00AD, Soft Hyphen) × ~200

Soft hyphens render as visible hyphens in some clients (notably older Samsung Mail). Standard practice is CGJ or zero-width non-joiner only — soft hyphens should be removed.

**Image alt text — not verifiable**
HTML is truncated before product image blocks. Alt text on cart product images is critical for accessibility and for clients with images-off defaults. Cannot confirm from provided source.

**`lang="en"` present** — No issue.
**Viewport meta present** — No issue.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

The preheader reads "You left something in your cart" but no `{{product_name}}`, `{{cart_item}}`, or equivalent merge token is visible in the truncated source. For an abandoned cart trigger, cart item name/image/price tokens are expected and their absence (or failure to render) would produce a generic, low-converting email.

**No first-name token visible**
Subject line and visible body copy contain no personalization tokens. Cannot confirm whether they exist below the truncation point, but this is worth verifying in the full source.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**Physical mailing address — not visible in truncated HTML**
CAN-SPAM §5 requires a valid physical postal address. Not present in the visible portion. Must exist somewhere in the email footer — cannot confirm from provided source.

**Unsubscribe link — not visible in truncated HTML**
Required by CAN-SPAM §5(a)(3) and CASL. Combined with the `#MessageViewBody a { text-decoration: none; color: inherit }` rule noted above, if the unsubscribe link is inside that container and lacks explicit inline styles, it will be visually invisible.

**Authentication headers**
DKIM, SPF, and DMARC cannot be assessed from HTML source. `msgs.skechers.com` is the sending domain — the ESP (Attentive) should have DKIM signing configured for that subdomain. Verify via raw email headers.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters unverifiable**
All links pass through `skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=...`. The decoded destination URLs are not inspectable from HTML source. Must verify by decoding a redirect or checking Attentive campaign configuration to confirm:
- `utm_source=email` (or `attentive`)
- `utm_medium=email`
- `utm_campaign=abandoned_cart` (or equivalent)
- `utm_content` differentiating CTA buttons (logo vs. product vs. checkout CTA)

Without confirmed UTMs, attribution in GA4/analytics will be broken — sessions will appear as direct traffic.

**Cart session continuity**
For abandoned cart emails, the destination URL should deep-link to the pre-populated cart (typically via a session token or cart ID parameter). Cannot verify this from the encoded UPN values.
