# Go back to school in style with BOGO ✨
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- Back to school. I'm 62. My kids have been out of school for thirty years. This email hit my inbox and I knew within two seconds it wasn't built for me — the BOGO hero is fine, the offer is real, but every model I see is a teenager or a kid, and the copy is pulling hard toward the fall semester crowd. That said, Famous Footwear has earned enough trust from me that I still scrolled through hoping to find a men's comfort shoe buried somewhere in the grid.
- And here's the thing that annoyed me before I even opened it: the preview text in my inbox was a raw tracking link — a long string of `https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=ABB7In...` garbage. That's not a preheader. That's a broken pipe showing through the wall. It made the brand look sloppy right at the moment they're trying to earn a click.
- I'll probably mark this as read and move on. I'm not buying back-to-school sneakers. But I noticed the BOGO — so if I'm walking by Famous Footwear this week anyway, the offer is filed somewhere in the back of my brain.

## What's working

- **BOGO is impossible to miss.** The giant colorful hero graphic leads with the offer and I don't have to hunt for the deal.
- **"Up to 25% off" section further down** gives me a second bite — still concrete savings.
- **Recognizable sender.** Famous Footwear is in my regular inbox rotation; I trust them enough to keep scrolling.
- **Layout is clean on mobile.** Big text, clear blocks, nothing overlapping or cut off. Easy to read without zooming.
- **Back-to-school timing is credible.** This isn't a random promo — it fits the calendar, which makes the offer feel less manufactured.

## What's weak

- **Raw tracking URL as preview text.** This is a real defect — the inbox showed `https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=ABB7...` instead of any copy. Makes the brand look technically broken before I've even opened it.
- **Every model I can see is a kid or teenager.** At my age, that's a signal to keep scrolling, not to my cart.
- **No men's comfort category visible in the hero or upper scroll.** If there's a walking shoe, a wide-width, an arch-support option — I have to hunt for it.
- **This is the eighth-ish email from Famous Footwear in about a week.** Even when the offer is good, the volume is starting to feel like noise. The 7/16 BOGO send already hit me with this deal — this one feels like a re-skin aimed at a different audience.
- **No deadline.** BOGO with no end date feels less urgent to me — I'll come back to it and probably forget.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader immediately.** This is the most damaging issue — every subscriber saw a raw URL in their inbox. A simple fallback like "Buy one, get one — men's styles included" would have doubled the open rate on its own.
- 2. **Add a men's comfort callout in the hero or just below it.** Even a small "Men's walking & comfort styles included" line beneath the BOGO graphic would keep guys like me scrolling with intent instead of detachment.
- 3. **Put a sale-ends date on the offer.** "Ends Sunday" is enough to move me off the fence. No deadline = no urgency.
- 4. **Show me one specific men's shoe with the price.** The grid is wide but unfocused. One hero product — a Skechers slip-on, a New Balance walker — would anchor the email for my segment.
- 5. **Reduce cadence or segment the list.** Hitting the same subscriber with a near-identical BOGO two days in a row dilutes both sends.

## Technical Audit — Famous Footwear "Go back to school in style with BOGO"

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard ESP-hosted promotional send with no critical infrastructure failures. The primary technical debt is a pervasive missing-alt-text problem across 35+ images and a plain-text body that is URL-saturated enough to trigger deliverability filters.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **27 click-redirect links unverifiable** — all hrefs route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=...` with opaque base64 query strings. Destination URLs cannot be confirmed without following the redirect chain; broken destination links would be invisible to pre-send QA.
- **Open pixel present** — `open.aspx?XJZTFOJJ6GAULOYKTP3YHIJHS4.70253` at `click.email.famousfootwear.com`. Standard for ESP tracking; no issue, but noted for completeness.
- **Logo image is a tracked link** — `3b6a7dc1...png` wraps an `<a href="https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/...">`. No `alt` text on that image means screen readers and image-blocked clients get no affordance for this navigation target.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — 35 images missing `alt` attributes** (confirmed by QA, full list in findings). Affected assets include:
- Logo (`3b6a7dc1-6955-49f8-868a-bf62a15122ee.png`)
- Hero/divider (`4ccff4ad-...jpg`, repeated 4×)
- All product/promotional images (`721856e1`, `557b4ca5`, `a998f7e2`, `278f55d4`, `d89b8efc`, `68847eb9`, `ecc6a86b`, `afbdb9a6`, `f3c5d1bc`, `2f8f2e3e`, `68058bfd`, `a9dcceb4`, `7ab9d644`, `bdfdb467`, `9bc2c30c`, `71d4b260`, `597a28f3`, `a832e677`, `9600aa2b`, `1a93f299`, `232102dd`, `7afdebab`, `1813912d`, `50f90f2c`, `ad282e8b`, `f662c75b`, `cab24161`, `b7d68ed5`, `b33eaea1`, `691276c5`, `8714175e`.gif/.jpg assets)

Consequences: screen readers skip all content; image-blocking clients (common in corporate/Outlook environments) render a blank email with no fallback text.

**Other rendering notes:**
- `<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` is missing the required `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute — malformed tag, charset may not be applied in some clients.
- `user-scalable=0` in the viewport meta is deprecated syntax; use `user-scalable=no`.
- `lineapacing:20px` typo in inline style (`td.darkmode`) — non-standard property, silently ignored by all clients.
- HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype declared; modern ESP best practice is HTML5 `<!DOCTYPE html>`. Minor, but affects some parser behaviors in Outlook.
