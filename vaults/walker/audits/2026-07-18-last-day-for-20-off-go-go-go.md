# Last day for 20% off—go go go!!!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- I've lost count. At some point this week the DSW emails stopped feeling like a store I like and started feeling like a neighbor who won't stop knocking. This one shows up with "go go go!!!" in the subject — three exclamation points — like whoever wrote it had three cups of coffee and nothing left to lose. And the preview? "TFW when the promo code takes down your total." I'm 62. I had to think for a second about what TFW even means. That's not a great start.
- Inside, the 20% off with code PEACHY is laid out big and clean — I'll give them that. But I scroll past the hero and land on a grid of women's heels and strappy sandals. There's a "SHOP MEN" link up in the nav and a "SHOP WIDE WIDTH" button buried in the size section, but the actual email is selling party shoes to someone who is not me. The one useful breadcrumb — wide-width sizing — is stuck at the bottom where I almost missed it entirely.
- I'll mark this read. I might click SHOP MEN out of habit, but I'm not forwarding this to anyone, and the "SOMETHING BIG IS COMING TOMORROW" teaser just makes me tired.

## What's working

- **20% off is big and visible right away** — I don't have to hunt for the number, and the code PEACHY is right there. That part I appreciate.
- **"SHOP WIDE WIDTH" button in the size section** is the most useful thing in this email for a guy like me — finding wide-width shoes is always a mission, and seeing that call-out lands.
- **Visual hierarchy on the discount** is solid — the giant "20%" is the first thing your eye catches, which is how it should be.
- **VIP rewards mention at the bottom** — 3% back and free shipping is a benefit I care about, and it's there even if it's easy to miss.
- **No broken images or layout wreckage** — at least the thing renders cleanly on my phone.

## What's weak

- **Women's fashion heels dominate the product grid** — "SO NEW, SO YOU" shows strappy sandals and kitten heels. That section is not for me, and it's the biggest real estate in the email.
- **"go go go!!!" and "TFW" in the same send** — the subject and preview are trying way too hard to sound young. I'm not the target of that energy, and it makes DSW feel less like a store that knows me.
- **"SOMETHING BIG IS COMING TOMORROW..." is pure noise** — cryptic yellow teaser for an unannounced deal I have no context for. Don't tease me; just sell me something.
- **Code PEACHY** — I'm fine with promo codes but "regular price styles only" in small text below the hero is the kind of thing that has tripped me up at checkout before. The asterisk earned a squint.
- **Inbox fatigue is real** — this is the latest in what feels like a fifteen-email streak from DSW. The urgency clock has cried wolf enough times that "last day" barely registers anymore.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put men's comfort shoes in the product grid** — if DSW has my purchase history, show me walking shoes, slip-ons, or wide-width cushioned styles. One row of men's hero products would double my click rate on this email.
- 2. **Kill the slang** — drop "TFW" from the preheader and "go go go!!!" from the subject. Write like you're talking to a loyal customer, not a TikTok comment section.
- 3. **Surface the wide-width option higher** — that SHOP WIDE WIDTH button buried in size tiles should be up near the hero for a customer segment that actively searches for it.
- 4. **Cut the teaser block** — "SOMETHING BIG IS COMING TOMORROW" wastes space and goodwill. Either tell me what it is or save it for a dedicated send.
- 5. **Throttle the send cadence** — one more "last day" email in a week of "last day" emails reads as desperation, not urgency.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] `Src:` (capital S) in all 10 `@font-face` declarations**
```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/appboy/...original.woff") format('woff');
```
`src` is case-sensitive in CSS. All major email clients will silently ignore these declarations and fall back to system fonts. Futura PT and HelveticaNow will not render. Fix: lowercase `src:` on all 10 blocks.

**[HIGH] `text-size-adjust: none` + `maximum-scale=1` — WCAG 1.4.4 failure**

CSS reset applies:
```css
*{ -ms-text-size-adjust: none; text-size-adjust: none }
```
Combined with:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
This prevents both pinch-to-zoom and OS-level text resizing on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, AA) violation. Fix: remove `text-size-adjust: none` (use `100%`), and drop `maximum-scale=1`.

**[MEDIUM] `-ms-text-size-adjust` declared twice, second wins as `none`**
```css
*{ -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%; ... -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
The `100%` value is immediately overridden. Effective value on IE/Outlook is `none`.

**[LOW] HelveticaNow served as `.ttf`, Futura PT as `.woff`**
TTF has no native compression; WOFF averages ~40% smaller for the same typeface. Six HelveticaNow `.ttf` files will increase load time on clients that do fetch web fonts (Apple Mail, Samsung Mail). Consider converting to WOFF2.

**[LOW] `<meta content="IE=Edge" />` missing `http-equiv` attribute**
```html
<meta content="IE=Edge" />
```
Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=Edge" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, Outlook/IE will not apply Edge rendering mode.

**[LOW] `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` missing `http-equiv`**
Same issue — `http-equiv="Content-Type"` is absent.
