# LAST CALL 📢 Extra 25% off Clearance ends tonight online
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-25**
## Executive summary

- **Famous Footwear — "LAST CALL Extra 25% off Clearance ends tonight online"**
- **Reviewed:** April 25, 2026
- Famous Footwear hit my inbox with a clearance deadline push, and honestly, first reaction was — *who is this for?* I'm a Skechers man. I've got my Plus card. This email is from somebody else's store, and it shows. The hero deal is real enough — 25% off clearance is money — but the email quickly falls apart for a guy like me. Women's sandals, Nike athletic drops, a loyalty points pitch for a program I'm not even sure I'm in. By the time I scrolled through all of it, I'd lost the thread. The urgency in the subject got me to look. The content didn't give me a reason to stay.

## What's working

- **The hero offer is clear.** "Take an extra 25% off Clearance" comes through instantly. Big text, visible shoes, I understand the deal in two seconds. That's good.
- **Urgency is present.** "Ends tonight" in the subject line made me look. That's the one line doing real work.
- **Brooks callout at $119.99.** That's a legitimate men's comfort shoe brand. If I'm scrolling and I spot a Brooks at that price, I might stop. It's the one product moment that feels aimed at someone my age.
- **Nike section has a clean price drop format.** Big price, simple layout. Even if Nike isn't my brand, I can read it fast.

## What's weak

- **Women's sandals section — wrong lane.** "30% off women's sandals" gets a full content block. I'm a 62-year-old man. I'm not shopping sandals for myself, and I'm not a personal shopper. This wastes my scroll and signals that the email was blasted generically.
- **I'm not a Famous Footwear rewards member — or am I?** The "8X POINTS" exclusive member deal feels disconnected. I'm a Skechers Plus member. Famous Footwear's points program means nothing to me unless I'm enrolled, and this email doesn't acknowledge either scenario. Feels like I was added to a list.
- **That preview text is a raw tracking URL.** Before I even opened this, my inbox showed me a blob of `https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=ABB7InYi...` — that's the preview text. That's not a message. That's a technical accident. I nearly deleted it on that alone.
- **Too many offers stacked on top of each other.** Clearance deal. Women's sandals. DSX brand section. Nike drop. Points promotion. Brooks. This reads like a circular ad, not a message to me.
- **Shoe photography is small.** On my phone, the product images in the grid sections are tiny. At my age, if I can't see what the shoe looks like, I'm not clicking to find out.
- **No Skechers, no Slip-ins, no comfort technology.** Zero. The thing I actually care about isn't anywhere in this email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress the women's module for men.** If Famous Footwear has gender data on me, use it. That whole sandals section should never have rendered for me.
- 2. **Fix the preview text immediately.** A tracking URL as the preheader is unprofessional. Something like "Clearance sale ends at midnight — shop men's styles now" costs nothing and does real work.
- 3. **Lead with a men's comfort angle.** Even one headline — "Men's clearance: comfort styles up to 25% off" — would make this feel relevant.
- 4. **Simplify.** Pick two offers max. Clearance + one featured product. Everything else dilutes the message.
- 5. **Make the Brooks (or similar) the hero for my segment.** Brooks at $119.99 is a legitimate hook for someone my age who cares about fit and support. Bury it at the bottom and you lose the one moment that could have gotten a click.
- 6. **Acknowledge me as a customer, not a prospect.** Even generic loyalty language like "Shop your member deal" would feel warmer than a cold blast.

## Technical Audit — Famous Footwear "Last Call Extra 25% off Clearance"

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a heavily image-dependent HTML 4.01 build with 34 images lacking alt attributes, one active mixed-content (HTTP) resource, and an unverifiable authentication chain due to missing Authentication-Results headers. Deliverability risk is elevated; accessibility is near-total failure.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **34 tracking links unverifiable.** All CTAs route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com` redirect domain. QA probes were skipped for all 34, so broken-link detection produced no signal — destination URLs and HTTP status codes are unknown.
- **Open pixel present** at `https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/open.aspx?RONVOYFYZQOEPIMYO2SXILP764.70247` — standard, no issue.
- **Plain-text part is 79% URLs** (11,589 / 14,576 chars). Spam filters weight the plain-text version; this ratio can trigger content-based filtering. Plain text should be human-readable prose with links, not a URL dump.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **34 content images have no alt text.** Every product, promotional, and UI image is empty-alt or alt-absent. Confirmed by QA across all `image.email.famousfootwear.com` assets. Screen readers produce silence; images-off rendering produces a blank email.
- **Logo image is alt-empty inside a link** (`3b6a7dc1-6955-49f8-868a-bf62a15122ee.png`, alt=""). The wrapping `<a>` has no text content, making the link purpose unannounced to assistive technology. WCAG 2.1 SC 1.1.1 failure.
- **Mixed-content HTTP image:** `http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=...` (element ID `920255575040`). Non-HTTPS source is blocked by default in Gmail, Apple Mail, and most modern clients. This image will not load for most recipients.
- **`user-scalable=0` and `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta.** Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile — accessibility violation and blocked by iOS 10+ (Apple ignores it, but it signals poor hygiene).
- **`<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />`** is missing the required `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute. Correct form: `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />`. Without it, charset declaration is non-functional in some clients.
- **CSS typo:** `lineapacing:20px` in the rewards section `<td>` — not a valid property, silently ignored. Intended value (`letter-spacing` or `line-height`) is not applied.
- **HTML 4.01 Transitional DOCTYPE** — industry-standard for legacy email clients, not a defect in isolation.
