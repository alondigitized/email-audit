# Get a $5 reward for sharing your thoughts
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-28**
## Executive summary

- Well, this is different at least — no shoe in this one, no cart nagging me about that pink floral thing for the fifth time. It's just Skechers asking me to fill out a survey for five bucks. My gut reaction: fine, but why would I do that? I've got 1,000 points sitting there already good for a $5 reward I could redeem right now, no survey required — that little banner at the top actually caught my eye more than the giant "Got a couple of minutes?" banner underneath it.
- I'm not a survey guy. Never have been. Five bucks for filling out a form about "Skechers Plus" — the loyalty program itself, not even shoes — doesn't move me. If it were "tell us about your last pair of Slip-ins" I might bite, but this feels like corporate homework with a coupon attached.
- Wouldn't click, wouldn't forward. I'd probably just hit that REDEEM link for my existing points and ignore the survey pitch entirely. Mark as read and move on.

## What's working

- The "Hi, Walker" and points balance up top feels like they actually know I'm a member, which I appreciate.
- No render bugs, no broken images — clean, simple layout that's easy to read on my phone without zooming.
- The "first come, first rewarded" urgency with a specific number (3,500 members) is a clear, honest mechanic — no fine print tricks.
- They tell me what happens if I miss it ("keep an eye out for the next survey") — no dead end, no guilt trip.

## What's weak

- Nothing here is about shoes. No comfort, no cushioning, no arch support, no mention of a single product — this is the first Skechers email in this whole batch that's 100% disconnected from what I actually shop for.
- My existing $5 reward sitting right there competes with the $5 survey reward — kind of undercuts the offer before I even scroll down.
- "Shop additional styles" section below is just plain text links (WOMEN, MEN, KIDS, WIDE) with zero product photos — feels like a filler afterthought, not a real merchandising push.
- Survey emails in general don't interest a guy my age unless there's a real reason to bother — this reads like a data-collection exercise dressed up as a reward.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with something shoe-related even in a survey ask — "Tell us about your last pair" gives me a reason to care, not just "share your thoughts on Skechers Plus."
- 2. Reconcile the two $5 offers — having my existing reward and the survey reward both dangling $5 in the same email is confusing, not motivating.
- 3. Put real product images in the "Shop additional styles" section instead of bare text links — give me something to look at.
- 4. Shorten the ask — "couple of minutes" is honest but soft; tell me exactly how many questions (e.g., "5 quick questions") so I know what I'm signing up for.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your feedback is worth $5, Walker`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Takes 3 minutes. First 3,500 members get rewarded.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Tell us what you think — reward lands in 2 weeks.`
