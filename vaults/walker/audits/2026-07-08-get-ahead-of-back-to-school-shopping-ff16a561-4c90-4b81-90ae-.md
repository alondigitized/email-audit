# Get Ahead of Back to School Shopping
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- Back to School. I'm sixty-two years old. My youngest kid graduated college during the Obama administration. Skechers knows my account — they send me my Plus monthly summary, they know what I've bought — and they lead with a banner aimed squarely at parents hauling ten-year-olds through the shoe department in August. That's not me.
- Now, the BOGO 50% off sitting right there in big bold letters — that got my attention, same as it would any day of the week. A buy-one-get-one beats a percentage-off any time because I actually understand what it means. And valid through July 13 is a real deadline. I can work with that. But I'm scrolling past Women's, then Men's (okay, there's my section), then Kids', then a bunch of young folks in basketball jerseys hawking apparel I wouldn't wear to pick up the newspaper. The Back to School frame means this email wasn't built with me in mind. It was built for a household with school-age children, and somebody forgot to filter their list.
- I'd click "Shop Men's" on the back of that BOGO deal alone — not because this email earned it, but because fifty percent off a second pair is money in my pocket. I wouldn't forward it to anybody. If a buddy my age asked me, "Skechers any good right now?" I'd probably say "yeah, they've got a BOGO going" and leave it at that.

## What's working

- **BOGO 50% off is the hero and it earns that spot** — the eye goes straight to it, the type is large, and I understand exactly what it means without reading fine print.
- **Skechers Plus badge visible at the top** — I'm a member, and acknowledging that immediately makes it feel like a member email, not a cold blast.
- **Deadline is stated clearly** — "VALID THROUGH 7/13" is right there under the hero. No hunting for the expiration date.
- **Men's section exists with a dedicated "Shop Men's" button** — it's not buried; I can find my category without having to wade through women's and kids' pages.
- **Email renders clean** — no broken images, no jumbled text, no obvious layout disasters. After months of Skechers emails I appreciate when the body itself holds together.

## What's weak

- **Back to School is the wrong frame for me** — I'm not shopping for a child going back to class, and nothing in this email acknowledges that I'm a 62-year-old guy shopping for himself.
- **That JSON code in the preview slot — again** — I've lost count of how many Skechers emails in a row have dumped raw computer gibberish where the teaser copy should be. Yesterday's email had it. The one before that. The July 4th email. Every single one. At this point I just assume whoever runs their email program doesn't know it's happening, or doesn't care.
- **Women's section leads above Men's** — in a BOGO email with no personalization, they put Women's first. As a male subscriber that's a small friction every time I open.
- **No comfort language anywhere** — cushioning, arch support, slip-resistant, wide width — nothing. For a brand built on comfort, that's the sell I respond to and it's completely absent.
- **Apparel section with young models doesn't land** — two people in basketball jerseys aren't speaking to me. That 25% off apparel block felt like a different email stapled onto the bottom.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader — this is urgent and embarrassing.** "BOGO 50% off ends July 13 — your Plus member deal is waiting" would take thirty seconds to write and would immediately stop the bleeding. This has been broken across at least seven consecutive sends.
- 2. **Segment the Back to School send by customer profile.** Customers without recent kids' shoe purchases shouldn't be getting a school-shopping frame. Send me "Stock Up While It Lasts — BOGO 50% Off" and keep the school imagery for the family segment.
- 3. **Lead with Men's, not Women's, for male subscribers.** Personalize the module order by the account's dominant purchase history. That's a basic segmentation move.
- 4. **Add one comfort-specific callout near the Men's section.** Even one line — "Memory foam insoles, wide widths available" — would remind me this brand knows what I shop for.
- 5. **Cut the apparel block or move it below the fold.** I came for shoes. The jersey-and-shorts section breaks the flow and the models don't look like me.

## Technical Audit — Skechers "Get Ahead of Back to School Shopping"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has a critical broken pixel (missing URL protocol), a pervasive HTTP mixed-content problem across 11+ image assets, and a third-party subscriber ID exposure in DMP sync beacons. Core ESP infrastructure (click-wrapping, open tracking) is intact.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Critical — Return Path pixel has a malformed URL (no protocol prefix):**
```html
<img src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1dbf0dcef79cbad82c12fe25342701e4499" />
```
No `https://` or `//` prefix means this resolves as a relative path — effectively `https://emails.skechers.com/pixel.app.returnpath.net/...`. The deliverability monitoring pixel fires nowhere.

**Moderate — Raw subscriber ID exposed in Krux DMP sync beacon:**
```
beacon.krxd.net/1x1_usermatch.gif?...&partner_uid=121173391
```
Numeric subscriber ID transmitted to a third-party domain in plaintext. The SHA-256 hashed variant on the same domain is the correct pattern; the raw numeric ID is not.

**Info — 26 click links not probed** (click.emails.skechers.com redirect domain; UTM destination values unverifiable without resolving the redirects).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**High — 11 images served over HTTP, not HTTPS:**

All `image.emails.skechers.com` assets and the ink1000 tracking pixel use `http://`. Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail proxy or block insecure image sources. Affected assets include the Skechers logo, app store badges, social icons, and the ink1000 open-pixel:
```
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/1/dde00662-...png  (logo)
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/*.png  (10 more)
http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif  (open pixel)
```

**Moderate — Content image missing alt text:**
```
src: http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png
```
This is not a tracking pixel (not 1×1) — it has no `alt` attribute, so image-off renders are blank with no fallback text.

**Low — `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied globally:**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Disables user-initiated text scaling on iOS — accessibility concern for visually impaired recipients.

**Low — Duplicate responsive breakpoint blocks:** `max-width: 375px` and `max-width: 640px` declare identical rule sets in two separate `<style>` blocks. No functional impact but adds ~1KB dead CSS.

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM:** Physical address and unsubscribe link not present in the visible HTML fragment (source is truncated). Cannot confirm or deny — flag for footer review against full source.

**Privacy/CCPA — DMP audience sync beacons fire on open:**
```
beacon.krxd.net/1x1_usermatch.gif  (×2)
beacon.krxd.net/1x1_ad_impression.gif
```
These sync subscriber identity to Salesforce/Krux on email open, before any user interaction. If CCPA opt-out signals are not being respected at the ESP level before send, this constitutes a passive third-party data share. Verify suppression list hygiene covers CCPA opt-outs.

**Authentication:** DKIM/SPF/DMARC headers not derivable from HTML source — must be verified from raw email headers.

Campaign ID in pixels (`PRO_US_NA_U_SITEBUNDLE_FULL_EN_07082026`) should match UTM `campaign` values on landing pages; this cannot be confirmed without resolving the click redirects.
