# New season. New arrivals. 20% off for members
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-02**
## Executive summary

- Okay, my name's up top, my points balance is right there, 1000 points, $5 reward ready to redeem — that part they've got dialed in at this point, same as the Labor Day email. But then it's a woman in a blazer strutting through some warehouse with butterflies and flowers painted around her for "September New Arrivals," and I'm scrolling a good while before I hit anything that looks like it's for me. There's a men's running shoe eventually, tucked under "New for Him" below the women's section, and a guy in a Skechers sweatshirt for the apparel push — that's fine, nothing wrong with it, just not the first thing my eye lands on.
- The 20% off member deal up top is clear and I like that it's stated plainly with a real deadline (thru 9/7), no games. But this whole email reads like a general "new season" catalog blast rather than something built around what I actually buy, which is slip-on comfort shoes with arch support.
- I'd scroll past the top half, maybe pause on the "Hands Free Slip-ins" strip since that's exactly my category, then probably close it. Wouldn't forward it — nothing here is worth sharing with a buddy, it's just a seasonal circular.

## What's working

- My name and points balance are right up top again — feels like they know I'm a member, consistent with the Labor Day and Monthly Summary emails.
- 20% off member offer is stated clean with a real end date (9/7), no fine-print run-around.
- The "Shop Hands Free Slip-ins" strip mid-email is right in my wheelhouse — that's the one line that actually speaks to how I shop.
- Men's running shoe and men's apparel sections do eventually show up, so it's not a total miss on gender targeting, just buried.

## What's weak

- The hero image is a woman modeling a jacket-and-trouser outfit — first thing I see in a "new season" email and it's not my category at all.
- "New for Her" comes before "New for Him" — small thing, but it tells me who this email was really built for.
- No mention anywhere of comfort, cushioning, or arch support — the stuff I actually care about — until that one slip-ins line.
- It's a long scroll of a dozen different sections (women's, men's, apparel, kids nav links, texting, app, curbside) before I get anywhere close to a decision — feels like a general storewide flyer, not something aimed at a guy who just wants his usual walking shoe restocked.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead the hero with the men's comfort shoe or the Hands-Free Slip-ins, not a fashion shot of a woman — if I'm the recipient, put my category first.
- 2. Move "New for Him" ahead of "New for Her," or better, build a men's-only version of this send for guys with a purchase history skewing men's comfort shoes.
- 3. Add one line of comfort-specific copy near the top — cushioning, arch support, slip-on ease — since that's the actual purchase driver for me, not "September new arrivals."
- 4. Trim the email — I don't need kids, clothing, curbside pickup, and app download all in one send when the goal is footwear.
