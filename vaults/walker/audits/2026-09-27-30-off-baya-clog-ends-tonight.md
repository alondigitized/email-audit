# 30% Off Baya Clog - Ends Tonight!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-27**
## Executive summary

- Well, this is the same darn email I already reviewed yesterday — same 30% off Baya Clog, same "Ends Tonight," just now it's actually tonight instead of two days out. Nothing wrong with the shoe itself: plain clog, one style, four colors, clear percentage off. That's the kind of straightforward deal that actually gets my attention instead of a Halloween cartoon or a leopard flat.
- But here's the thing — I already saw this. I flagged the flash sale yesterday and now I'm getting the rerun with the deadline moved up. That's fine for the person who forgot to buy, I guess, but it doesn't feel like anything new was worked for. I'd glance at it, maybe click through out of curiosity on the gray one since it looks like something I'd actually wear around the yard, but I'm not forwarding this to anybody — there's no story here, just "buy now, sale's over."
- Would I click? Probably, low effort. Would I open next time they send the same email a third time this week? That's where I start tuning out.

## What's working

- One shoe, one deal, no clutter — I can see the offer in two seconds without hunting for it.
- The four color swatches (white, pink, gray, tan) give me a real look at what I'd be buying, not some abstract lifestyle shot.
- "Shop the Sale" button is big, black, and impossible to miss — no guessing what to tap.
- Free shipping banner up top is a nice add-on I actually care about.

## What's weak

- I've already seen this exact creative — same headline, same colors, same layout as the "Flash Sale: 30% Off Baya Clogs" email from yesterday, just retimed. Feels lazy, not urgent.
- "Exclusions Apply" with zero explanation is exactly the kind of fine-print dodge that makes me trust the deal less — tell me what's excluded or don't bring it up.
- No price shown anywhere — I have to do the 30%-off math myself or click through to find out if this is actually a good deal.
- It's a women's/unisex clog lineup with no men's sizing or fit note called out — I have to assume it comes in my size.

## Recommendations

- 1. Don't resend the identical creative with just the countdown timer moved up — give me a fresh reason (new color drop, bundle deal, or at least a "last chance" callout that's visually distinct from the first email).
- 2. Show the actual before/after price on at least the hero clog — "$44.99 → now $31.49" does more work than a bare percentage.
- 3. Spell out the exclusions in one short line instead of the vague catch-all — even "excludes Kids' sizes" builds more trust than nothing.
- 4. Add a men's fit note or size range near the product shots since I don't know at a glance if these run true to size for me.
