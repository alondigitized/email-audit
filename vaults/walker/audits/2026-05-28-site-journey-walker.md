# Skechers Site Review — Walker Miles
**Date:** May 28, 2026 | **Previous Score:** 4/10

---

## 1. Executive Summary

There's genuine improvement in the navigation and product pages — Skechers finally gives me a clean, large-text Men's menu with "Hands Free Slip-ins" right in the quick links, and the product detail page for the Max Cushioning Arch Fit 2.0 is readable and hits my comfort keywords. But two critical failures drag the experience down hard: the cart page rendered completely blank (I can't buy what I can't see), and a search for "comfort shoes" returned women's shoes in the top two results despite me supposedly being logged in as a male customer. Personalization is invisible throughout. The site still doesn't know me.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 5/10

**Up from 4/10 yesterday.** Navigation and product pages are meaningfully better. But a blank cart page is a conversion killer — if I got that far as a real shopper, I'm closing the tab and calling DSW.

---

## 3. What's Working

**Men's navigation menu (Step 4)** is the standout win. Big, clean text. Full-width tap targets on every row. "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Extra Wide Fit" all surface as quick links without me having to dig. That's exactly what a guy my age needs — no hunting.

**Hands Free Slip-ins category page (Step 5)** loads cleanly with a clear header, width-filter chips right at the top (Wide Fit, Extra Wide Fit), and 291 results. The breadcrumb "Men > Shoes" orients me. Filter and Sort are easy to find.

**Product detail page (Step 6)** is strong. "Max Cushioning Arch Fit 2.0" — that name sells itself to me. Price is visible ($135), the BOGO deal is called out in red, and the star rating with review count is right under the title. The "Hands Free Slip-Ins" badge gives me confidence. Text is large enough to read without zooming.

**Add to Cart button (Step 7)** — full-width, bold blue, stickied to the bottom of the screen. I can't miss it. Size grid boxes are a reasonable tap size. "Don't See Your Size?" is a thoughtful link.

**BOGO deal persistence** — "Buy 1, Get 1 50% OFF Applied at Cart" stays in the banner and repeats in red on the product page. No confusion about whether the deal applies.

---

## 4. What's Weak

**Cart page is blank. (Step 8)** Completely white. Nothing. No items, no total, no checkout button, no error message. This is a full stop. If I'd spent 10 minutes finding this shoe and picking my size and width, I just hit a white wall. That's my order gone.

**Search returns women's shoes first. (Step 9)** I typed "comfort shoes" and the first two results are both labeled "Women's." I'm logged in. The site should know I'm a man. 1,438 results with no pre-filter to Men's is already overwhelming, but leading with women's product is disorienting. I'm going to wonder if I'm on the wrong page.

**Login produces no visible change. (Step 3)** The page after login looks identical to the homepage before login. No "Welcome back" greeting, no personalized recommendations, no "Your size 10.5 Wide favorites" — nothing. Logging in felt pointless.

**Homepage hero doesn't speak to me. (Steps 1–2)** Two models who look 25–30 years old walking in a city. No older active adult, no comfort story, no "these shoes will get you through the day on your feet." The soccer prize giveaway in the blue box is pure noise — I don't care about a soccer fan prize package.

**Width selection friction (Step 7).** When I land on the size selector, it only shows MEDIUM and EXTRA WIDE — no standard Wide (2E). The product listing page showed a "Wide Fit" filter, so why doesn't the PDP offer it? That's confusing. And the validation warnings ("Please select a width," "Please select a size") appear before I've had a chance to make any selections — they're pre-fire alerts, not helpful guidance.

**No personalization anywhere.** At no point does the site say "based on your past purchases" or "you've bought Arch Fit before." No loyalty points balance, no "You have X Skechers Elite points." I opened my account — show me something for it.

**Popup dismissal is invisible (Step 2).** The banner rotated from "Spring Sale" to "Get Dad a Gift He'll Love" but no obvious modal was dismissed. If there was a popup, it was handled silently. Minor — but I can't tell what happened.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** A blank cart is a purchase-blocking bug. This is the single highest-priority fix. No loyalty email converts if checkout is broken.

2. **Search must filter by gender context when logged in.** If I'm in a male account, "comfort shoes" should default to Men's results. At minimum, show gender filter chips at the top of search results before the product grid.

3. **Add a logged-in welcome state.** Even a simple "Hi, Walker" in the header with a points balance visible from the account icon would signal that my login mattered. Right now it's ghost mode.

4. **Resolve the missing Wide (2E) width on the PDP.** If the category page filters by Wide Fit, the product detail must offer it. Inconsistency here creates doubt — am I on the right product? Does it really come in Wide?

5. **Move validation warnings to post-interaction.** Don't show "Please select a width" on page load. Show it only after I tap ADD TO CART without having made a selection.

6. **Swap or diversify homepage hero imagery.** One panel showing an older active adult — walking a neighborhood, comfortable and upright — would do more to retain a shopper like me than two twenty-somethings.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

The site is improving in the places I browse — the men's nav is the best it's felt, and the product page for the Arch Fit 2.0 would have me reaching for my card. But the blank cart means I literally cannot complete a purchase, and search keeps treating me like I'm shopping for someone else. Yesterday was a 4 because the whole journey felt rough. Today I'd give it a **5** — better in the middle, still broken at the end. Fix the cart and gender-aware search and this jumps to a 7 fast.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Platform is Salesforce Commerce Cloud (evident from `/on/demandware.static/Sites-USSkechers-Site/` paths). The journey loads 15+ third-party vendor scripts. Three distinct failure clusters: (1) Kasada bot-protection generating self-inflicted 429s, (2) the cart endpoint itself returning 429 — blocking the primary conversion step, and (3) two third-party CORS failures silently killing feature widgets. LCP and CLS data was not captured; the performance picture is incomplete.

---

## Accessibility

**Critical (1)**
- `aria-required-parent` (Homepage): ARIA roles present without their required container roles. Screen readers will misinterpret or skip these elements entirely.

**Serious (2)**
- `document-title` + `html-has-lang` (View Cart): The cart page is missing both `<title>` and `lang="..."` on `<html>`. This strongly suggests the cart renders inside an iframe or via a JS-injected document that bypasses the SFCC page shell. Fundamental AT failure — screen readers have no document context.

**Moderate (10)**
- Homepage landmark structure is malformed: duplicate `<main>`, `<aside>` not top-level, multiple landmark uniqueness violations, and content outside any landmark region. Consistent with a CMS-assembled page where includes do not coordinate landmark hierarchy.
- Heading order violation on Homepage — likely an H3/H4 inside a marketing module that precedes an H2 in the natural DOM order.
- Search results page repeats the `complementary`/`landmark-unique`/`region` pattern seen on Homepage, suggesting shared component-level defects.

**Minor (1)**
- `aria-allowed-role` (Homepage): a role value applied to an element where that role is not permitted by the ARIA spec.

---

## SEO

- **View Cart missing `<title>` and `html[lang]`**: If Googlebot ever indexes `/cart/`, these omissions are a ranking signal penalty. More practically, the 429 on `/cart/` (see below) would prevent crawl anyway — but the root iframe/shell issue needs fixing regardless.

- No canonical or hreflang anomalies visible in the truncated source.

## Mobile Optimization

- `--vh: 6.640000000000001px` set as an inline CSS custom property on `<html>` — this is the standard workaround for iOS Safari's dynamic viewport height bug. Implementation appears correct in intent, but the floating-point precision (`6.640000000000001`) is a symptom of direct `window.innerHeight / 100` without rounding; minor but worth normalizing to avoid sub-pixel rendering artifacts.
- `vice-managed-src` attribute pattern is the TrustArc consent management mechanism for deferring third-party script loads. Correct pattern for GDPR/CCPA compliance gating.
- No viewport meta tag issues visible in the truncated source (lang and charset are present on fully-rendered pages).

## Console & Network Errors

**429 — Kasada KPSDK (`/149e9513-.../fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.424`) × 5**
The UUID path prefix is Kasada's bot-protection fingerprinting endpoint. Five 429s against their own endpoint indicate the test/automation environment was flagged and Kasada began rate-limiting the session's fingerprint calls. In a real user session this would not occur unless the user's IP is under heavy shared-IP load. No user-facing breakage expected under normal conditions, but the fingerprinting loop retrying 5 times generates noisy JS errors and wastes bandwidth.

**429 — `/cart/` × 1**
The cart page itself returned HTTP 429. This is distinct from the Kasada fingerprint 429s — this is the SFCC application or CDN layer rate-limiting the cart endpoint. If reproducible, this directly blocks purchase completion. Needs investigation against SFCC rate-limit config or WAF rules.

**CORS — `reporting.cdndex.io/error`**
cdndex.io is a third-party RUM/error reporting vendor. Their `/error` endpoint does not return `Access-Control-Allow-Origin`, so XHR error reports from the browser are silently dropped. The vendor's CDN edge configuration is missing CORS headers for `https://www.skechers.com` as an allowed origin.

**CORS — `web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css`**
Stylitics is the outfit/complete-the-look widget vendor. Their CSS file at `web-assets.stylitics.com` is not returning CORS headers permitting load from `skechers.com`. This means the Stylitics styling widget is **visually broken** (unstyled or not rendered) for all users. This is a vendor-side configuration issue but affects Skechers' product pages.

---

## Recommendations

**P0 — Cart 429 (conversion blocker)**
Audit SFCC rate-limit rules and CDN/WAF configuration for the `/cart/` path. A 429 on cart breaks purchase for affected sessions. Determine whether this is a bot-protection misconfiguration, a session/cookie issue, or a genuine rate-limit.

**P1 — View Cart missing `<title>` and `html[lang]`**
Identify whether the cart renders in an iframe (common in SFCC integrations). If so, the inner document needs its own `<title>` and `lang` attributes. If it's a JS-rendered partial, the parent document's title must be updated dynamically on cart navigation.

**P1 — Fix Stylitics CORS**
Contact Stylitics support to add `https://www.skechers.com` (and `*.skechers.com`) to their CDN's `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` response headers for `web-assets.stylitics.com`. Feature is currently broken for all users.

**P2 — Remove duplicate script loads**
Audit GTM and the TrustArc tag configuration to eliminate the Monetate, Yahoo pixel, and creativecdn duplicates. Each is a double-spend on network and parse time, and Monetate's `//`-protocol load is a mixed-content risk.

**P2 — Fix ARIA landmark structure on Homepage**
The duplicate `<main>`, nested `<aside>`, and content-outside-landmark violations are likely in shared SFCC content includes. Refactor the page shell template to use a single `<main>`, move `<aside>` to document root level, and ensure all content blocks are wrapped in landmark regions.

**P3 — Capture LCP and CLS**
The audit is missing the two most impactful Core Web Vitals. Instrument the test runner to capture `PerformanceObserver` entries for `largest-contentful-paint` and `layout-shift`. Without these, pass/fail against Google's thresholds cannot be determined.

**P3 — Fix cdndex.io CORS**
Contact cdndex.io to add the correct CORS headers to their `/error` reporting endpoint. Until fixed, all RUM error data from the browser is being silently discarded, degrading observability.
