# Friday Labor Day Deals Inside

- Okay, now we're cooking — "Up to 40% Off Our Best Athletic Brands" and right there in the logo lineup, sandwiched between Nike and Jordan, is Skechers. Took less scrolling than most of the recent sends from this brand. Then a few blocks down there's a whole Skechers Family Sale banner with an actual shoe on it, up to 25% off. That's two Skechers mentions in one email, which is more than I usually get.
- But it's still a logo soup at the top — Nike, Adidas, Jordan, New Balance, Converse, Skechers "and more" — so I can't tell what's actually on sale for a guy like me until I click through and start hunting. And then there's a $20-off coupon code buried near the bottom that applies site-wide, no shoe shown at all.
- I'd probably tap on the Skechers Family Sale banner since it's got an actual shoe I can look at, but this isn't a forward-to-a-friend email — it's a "maybe I'll poke around later" email.

## What's working

- Skechers logo makes the top banner, and there's a dedicated Skechers Family Sale block with an actual white sneaker product shot lower down — that's the strongest visual anchor for me in the whole email.
- "LOWEST PRICE GUARANTEED" and "40% OFF" up top is a real, concrete number, not vague filler.
- The $20-off / $10-off coupon codes are specific and usable in-store or online, which matters to me since I like to try shoes on before buying.
- Layout is mostly one column, big text, easy to read without zooming on my phone.

## What's weak

- The hero banner is a brand parade — Nike, Adidas, Jordan, New Balance, Converse, Skechers all crammed into one shelf — so my brand is competing for a sliver of real estate instead of getting its own moment up top.
- No actual product photo, sizing, or comfort/cushioning language anywhere except that one Skechers shoe box shot — nothing about arch support or slip-on comfort, which is what actually gets me to buy.
- The Sandal Blowout and "20% off next purchase in-store" banners are generic filler that don't tell me what shoe or what brand, so my thumb just keeps scrolling past them.
- Bottom third is a wall of fine print and a barcode I can't do anything with from my phone — dead space I have to scroll past to get to unsubscribe.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the Skechers Family Sale block instead of burying it as the fourth banner — that shoe photo and the 25% off number is the only thing in this email that made me stop scrolling, put it first.
- 2. Cut the six-logo brand shelf down to one line of text or drop it — a wall of competing logos dilutes the offer instead of clarifying it.
- 3. Add one line of comfort copy under the Skechers banner — "Hands-Free Slip-Ins" or "Arch Fit cushioning" would do more for me than "up to 25% off" alone.
- 4. Move the $20/$10 coupon code up near the Skechers block so it reads as "here's your shoe, here's your discount" instead of a disconnected offer at the bottom.
