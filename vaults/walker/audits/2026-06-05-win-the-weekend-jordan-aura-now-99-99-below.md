# Win the weekend🔥 Jordan Aura now $99.99 & below!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- I've been getting Famous Footwear emails pretty regularly this week, and I opened this one because I know the brand and I know they sometimes run real deals. The subject said Jordan Aura at $99.99 — specific, concrete, a real price — so I bit. But when I got inside, the hero shoe is a pink-and-white Jordan sneaker. That's a young person's shoe. That's not me. I'm 62, I need something that supports my arches and maybe slips on without a shoehorn. Jordan Aura is not that conversation.
- The saving grace is the $20 off a $100 purchase banner sitting right below the hero — that caught my eye and made me scroll. I noticed Brooks at $109.99 further down, which is actually in my wheelhouse, but by then I'd already mentally checked out of the lead offer. The grid below the banner had some darker, more subdued shoes that might be my style, but small thumbnails on a phone screen don't tell me much about cushioning or fit.
- And yet again — for what feels like the fourth or fifth time in a row — the preview text in my inbox was a raw tracking URL. Same problem I called out on the May 16th email. Same mess on the June 4th send. Famous Footwear clearly hasn't fixed this and it makes me feel like I'm getting bulk marketing, not a curated message. I'd mark this one as read and maybe go back for the Brooks if I'm already buying something else.

- The $20 off $100 banner is bold and reads instantly — that's a real offer and the eye lands on it before I even start scrolling
- $99.99 in the subject line is a concrete anchor — I know immediately what I'm looking at without guessing

- The "Limited time" framing, while vague, at least signals I shouldn't sit on this

## What's weak

- Hero shoe is a pink Jordan Aura — that's aimed at a younger, fashion-forward crowd, not a 62-year-old guy looking for arch support
- Preview text is, once again, a raw tracking URL dumped straight into my inbox — I flagged this June 4th, May 16th, multiple times, and it's still broken
- No comfort cues anywhere near the hero — no mention of cushioning, support, or walkability in the part that's supposed to hook me
- The Brooks section is buried below the fold; the one product that matches my priorities is an afterthought
- "Win the weekend" as a hook means nothing to me — I'm not competing, I'm buying shoes that don't hurt my feet

- 2. **Fix the preview text.** This is the most embarrassing recurring failure in this email program. A real preheader line would lift opens measurably. Just write something.
- 3. **Bring Brooks (or any walking/comfort style) up** to at least secondary hero position — there are clearly multiple audiences in this list and Boomer males with loyalty history deserve one product in their lane above the fold

## Technical Audit — Famous Footwear "Win the Weekend" (Jordan Aura $99.99)

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — HTTP mixed-content image:**
```
src="http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=ABB7InYiOjEsImQiOjQ4OTh9..."
```
Non-HTTPS asset. Gmail, Outlook 365, and Apple Mail block HTTP images by default; this block will render broken for most recipients.

**27 images missing `alt` text** (confirmed by QA), including:
- Logo: `3b6a7dc1-6955-49f8-868a-bf62a15122ee.png`
- 8 personalized recommendation images from `7222883.recs.igodigital.com`
- 16 product/content images from `image.email.famousfootwear.com`

With images blocked (default in Outlook desktop), the email renders as an unlabeled sequence of broken image boxes.

**Malformed charset meta tag:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. In HTML 4.01 this tag has no effect; charset is undeclared. Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```

**Zoom blocked on mobile:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
`user-scalable=0` and `maximum-scale=1` prevent pinch-to-zoom. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is a known accessibility barrier for low-vision users.

**Invalid CSS — `max-height: auto`:**
```css
img { max-width:100% !important; height:auto !important; max-height:auto !important; }
```
`auto` is not a valid value for `max-height`. The correct value is `none`. Some rendering engines will ignore this declaration, potentially breaking image scaling on mobile.

**CSS typo — `lineapacing`:**
```html
style="...lineapacing:20px;..."
```
Property name is misspelled (`lineapacing` → should be `line-height` or `letter-spacing`). Declaration is silently ignored by all clients.

The session ID `990267198170` is present in all eight URLs. These carry no alt text, so fallback presentation when recommendations fail to resolve is a blank/broken image — no product name is exposed to the client.

No unresolved merge token syntax (`%%`, `{{`, `[%`) was detected in the available source.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**Plain-text body is 78% URLs** (8,919 / 11,443 chars):
> `[WARN] Plain-text is >70% URLs: 8919/11443 chars`

This is a deliverability signal; spam filters (SpamAssassin `URI_*` rules, Gmail bulk sender scoring) penalize plain-text versions that are predominantly redirect URLs with no readable content. CAN-SPAM requires a "clear and conspicuous" opt-out — a plain-text version consisting almost entirely of tracking URLs is not readable and may not satisfy that requirement for clients that strip HTML.

**Sender domain:** `famousfootwear@email.famousfootwear.com` — sending subdomain (`email.famousfootwear.com`) is consistent with ESP alignment. SPF/DKIM/DMARC authentication cannot be confirmed from HTML alone; headers were not provided for inspection.

Unsubscribe link and physical mailing address were not present in the truncated source provided. Assumed present in footer; should be confirmed against full source.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 28 CTA and product links route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com` click-tracking redirects. Because QA skipped HTTP probing on all 28, UTM parameter presence on landing pages (`famousfootwear.com`) is **unverified**. Without confirmed `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` values resolving through the redirect chain, attribution for this campaign in analytics is at risk.
