# 🔥HOT SELLER: The Nike V5 Runner
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-06**
## Executive summary

- The V5 Runner up top actually got my attention for two seconds — that's a running shoe lineup, not a lifestyle sneaker, and I can tell just from the silhouette it's got some cushion to it. But "HOT SELLER" in that circle doesn't tell me a blessed thing about arch support or whether it's slip-on friendly, and by the time I scroll past it I'm back in the same grab-bag this brand always sends — Family Sale, Skechers sale, a mystery black smudge box, a coupon. It's the same email I've been getting for three weeks with a different shoe pasted on top.
- I wouldn't click past the hero. Nothing here says "comfort" or "fit" to me, it just says "buy shoes, any shoes, today." And that black panel with the barely-visible "IN STORE ONLY" text — that's the second time this week I've seen this brand run a banner that's damn near unreadable. Not forwarding this one, not clicking, probably scroll past like I did with the "25% off athletics" email yesterday.

## What's working

- The V5 Runner product shot is a real running shoe, multiple colorways, decent size on screen — I can actually see what I'd be buying.
- "Shop Nike" buttons are clear and easy to tap, no fussing to find them.
- The $20/$10 off coupon at the bottom is a real, usable offer with a code I could punch in — SAVEMORE, easy enough to remember.
- Skechers callout with a price anchor ($59.98) at least gives me a number to react to.

## What's weak

- Nobody in this email looks like me — no man my age, no lifestyle or walking shoe, it's all performance running silhouettes and "Family Sale" messaging aimed at parents buying for kids.
- That black panel is nearly illegible — I had to squint to find "IN STORE ONLY" buried in the smudge, same problem I flagged on yesterday's email.
- Not one word about comfort, cushioning, arch support, or slip-on ease anywhere in this whole email — for a running shoe that's usually the whole pitch.
- This is my fourth or fifth Shoe Carnival email this week and it's the same format again: hero, perks banner, family sale, second brand sale, mystery banner, coupon. I could recite it.
- "HOT SELLER" is a shrug of a headline — it's not urgent, not personal, doesn't tell me why I should care today versus next week.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a comfort callout right under the hero — arch support, cushioned ride, whatever the V5 actually offers — because that's the one thing that'd make me stop and read instead of scroll.
- 2. Cut the black mystery banner entirely or fix the contrast — I still can't read it and neither could my wife, probably.
- 3. Swap one of those two Nike product tiles for something in a men's casual or walking category instead of two more performance shoes — give me a reason to think this brand carries my kind of shoe.
- 4. Lead with the $20/$10 coupon higher up — that's the strongest, most concrete offer in the whole email and it's buried at the bottom under two other sales.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
This is a legacy multi-client HTML email (Outlook/Hotmail/Yahoo/Apple/Samsung hacks, 3-tier responsive breakpoints) served via `email.shoecarnival.com` with Cheetah Digital/Marigold-style `emltrk.com` tracking. QA passed overall, but source inspection surfaces an unresolved merge token in a tracking pixel and a malformed meta tag.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 26 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped from HTTP probing (QA: `[WARN] 26 tracking link(s) skipped: Tracking/click-redirect domains`). These wrap destination URLs behind `emltrk.com` redirects, so actual landing-page validity cannot be confirmed from this audit — recommend manual click-through QA on all CTAs before send.
- Tracking pixel `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260806_nike_skechers_AM` has a malformed query string: the `t=` parameter's value is itself `utm_campaign=SCA_20260806_nike_skechers_AM` rather than `utm_campaign` being its own key/value pair. Confirm this is intentional platform encoding and not a broken parameter concatenation.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 4 images missing `alt` text (QA):
  - `9521013e-7444-4804-9257-f83e6db7b8bc.jpg` and `a97db5d3-cfac-4b76-923a-ca38510bf3a6.jpg` — appear to be product images; missing alt text degrades screen-reader experience and shows nothing if images are blocked.
  - Two `emltrk.com` open-tracking pixels — should carry `alt=""` (not omitted) to be explicitly marked decorative for assistive tech, rather than left unspecified.
- `<meta content="IE=edge" />` is missing the required `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"` attribute, so this tag has no effect in any client — the intended legacy-IE rendering-mode override is not actually applied.
- `<meta name="color-scheme" content="light" />` / `<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light" />` declare light-only support with no dark variant — verify this is deliberate, since clients that auto-dark-mode unstyled elements can produce contrast issues on any inline colors not covered by the `.appleLinksWhite/Gray/Black` helper classes.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- Tracking pixel `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]` contains a literal, unresolved `[UNIQUE]` merge token (QA: image src listed verbatim with the bracketed placeholder). This indicates the personalization/merge step did not populate this parameter — should be fixed before send, as it will send an unpersonalized (or broken) tracking call for every recipient.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- Unsubscribe link and physical mailing address are not visible in the truncated HTML provided — cannot confirm CAN-SPAM footer compliance from this excerpt. Recommend verifying the full document contains both.
- Authentication headers (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) were not included in the provided data — cannot assess from this audit.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Only one UTM value is visible in the provided source, embedded in the malformed tracking-pixel query noted in §2 (`utm_campaign=SCA_20260806_nike_skechers_AM`). Because the 26 actual clickthrough links are wrapped redirects that were not probed, UTM parameters and landing-page alignment on the real CTAs cannot be verified in this audit.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Fix the merge-token population so `d=[UNIQUE]` resolves to an actual value before send.
2. Correct the `t=` query parameter on the `ieezzy5m.emltrk.com` pixel so `utm_campaign` is a proper standalone key.
3. Add descriptive `alt` text to the two product images; set `alt=""` explicitly on the two tracking pixels.
4. Add `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"` to the `IE=edge` meta tag or remove it if no longer needed.
5. Manually click-test a sample of the 26 wrapped tracking links to confirm redirect targets and UTM continuity to the site.
