# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-10-15**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I'm a 62-year-old shopper on my phone, and I could not buy a shoe today. The homepage and the Men's menu looked fine, and the Men's Shoes page pointed me at hands-free slip-ins and walking shoes. Then I picked a shoe, tapped Add to Cart, and got "Please select a size". My cart still showed 0. The next screen, the cart page, was completely blank white. I couldn't tell whether it was broken or still loading. The search for "comfort shoes" worked, but the first results I saw were women's styles.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** That matches yesterday's 3/10, so I see no improvement and no clear regression. I only have yesterday's score, not its notes, so I can't compare specific issues. The browsing is decent. The cart is where it falls apart, and a blank cart page ends the sale.

## 3. What's Working

- **Header and readability:** The logo, search, account, cart and MENU are all in one tidy row. Text is a comfortable size without zooming. The MENU icon and label are easy to find.
- **Comfort shortcuts on the homepage:** "Slip-ins", "Wide Fit", "Arch Fit" and "Max Cushioning" sit right under the header. They are exactly my words, and I saw them before scrolling.
- **Men's menu:** It opens as a clean full-width drawer with big, well-spaced rows. It has a clear Back arrow and an X. "Comfort Technologies" is its own entry. The rows are easy to hit with a thumb.
- **Men's Shoes page:** The picture tiles "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" come first, ahead of the 781 results. Filter, Sort, Size and Width buttons are right there. Width matters to me.
- **Pricing and offers:** "2 Pairs for $100" and "20% OFF Sitewide" are clear. Klarna and Afterpay are shown on the product page.
- **Size picker:** The size buttons are big, and the red "Please select a size" message is plain.
- **Search:** It returned 1,592 results for "comfort shoes". The listings show price, a "Contour Foam – Cozy Fit" name and color swatches.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Blank cart page (step 8):** The screenshot is a blank white screen. I would assume the site is broken and go to Hoka or Brooks.
- **Add to Cart did not add anything:** The cart badge still shows 0. The "Please select a size" error is only a warning on this phone view. It sits below the fold, and the product page doesn't scroll me to it. A 62-year-old pressing the big blue button gets no clear next step.
- **Login step shows nothing (step 3):** The screen is the same homepage as before, with no login form or signed-in state. I never felt logged in, and nothing was personalized to me. The "Dismiss Popups" step also looks identical to the homepage, so I can't say whether anything was dismissed.
- **Mixed messages on offers:** The banner says "20% OFF Sitewide" and "2 Pairs for $100", but the product page says "Excluded from promotions". Search results say "20% OFF for Members!" The shoe I landed on was $100 with a promo exclusion. I can't tell which price I'd actually pay.
- **Wrong product for me:** The detail page was a Day-of-the-Dead painted sneaker ("UNO - Alma De Guerrero") with no reviews ("No Reviews") and no mention of comfort, cushioning or arch support. Nothing there tells me why I'd pick it.
- **Search favors women's styles:** The first two results were both "Women's". The site doesn't seem to know I'm a man, even though I had just browsed Men's.
- **No comparison content:** I saw nothing comparing Skechers to Hoka, Brooks or New Balance. I saw no price-versus-quality or "what's the difference between Arch Fit and Max Cushioning" explanation, and no hands-free or arch-support explainer above the fold.
- **Shipping nudge is buried:** "Sign in for free shipping & returns!" appears only after the size picker, in small text.
- **Small touch targets:** The wishlist heart and the "Size Chart" link are small. The tiny alarm bell on the crossed-out 6.5 is nearly invisible.
- **Banner clutter:** Two promo bars stack above the content and push the actual shoes down. On a phone, I scroll a long way before seeing a shoe.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page first.** Never show a blank screen. Show the cart contents, a loading state, or at least a "Your cart is empty" message with a link back to shopping.
2. **Make "select a size" impossible to miss.** Scroll to the sizes and highlight them on a failed Add to Cart. Or pre-select a common size. Show the cart count update and a "Added to cart" confirmation.
3. **Check the journey itself.** Make sure the login and popup steps are really landing on the login form and the popup. If this is a test-script issue, fix it, because I can't judge login personalization otherwise.
4. **Match the promo messaging.** If a shoe is excluded from promotions, say so before the headline banner promises 20% off. Show one clear final price.
5. **Lead with comfort on product pages.** Put cushioning, arch support, slip-in or not, width options and a star rating near the top. "No Reviews" is a trust killer, so hide it or de-emphasize it when there are none.
6. **Remember the shopper's section.** After browsing Men's, show men's results first in search. Add men's, hands-free and wide-width filters as one-tap chips on the search page.
7. **Add a comfort comparison module.** Something like "Skechers Arch Fit vs. Max Cushioning vs. Slip-ins" with price notes would help a shopper who cross-shops Hoka, Brooks and New Balance.
8. **Enlarge small tap targets.** The wishlist heart, Size Chart link and stock-alert bell should be bigger.

## 6. Bottom Line

I like what Skechers is selling. The Arch Fit, Wide Fit and slip-in shortcuts and the Men's menu feel written for me. But I got stuck on a size prompt, then hit a blank cart, and from there I'm done. The shoe that fits wins, and I couldn't even get one into a cart. Another brand will take my money while this one fixes its cart.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The journey was throttled by bot protection, so some of this data can't be trusted.

- **Kasada challenge (`x-kpsdk`) is blocking requests.** The fingerprint endpoint returned HTTP 429 five times. `/cart/` itself also returned 429.
- **The View Cart audit covers an error response, not a cart.** The missing `<title>`, `lang`, `<main>` and `<h1>` match a bare 429 body. They are probably not real cart-template defects.
- **LCP and CLS were not captured on any step.** All eight show `?`. TTFB is the only usable metric.
- **Several steps may not have navigated.** The HTML samples for Log In and Men category both report `https://www.skechers.com/`, and the first scripts in all three samples are identical. Axe results for those steps are probably homepage results.
- **Axe returned results for only three steps.** Homepage, View Cart and Search have violations listed. Log In, Men category, Men > Shoes, Product detail and Add to Cart have none listed. I can't tell whether they were scanned and passed or never scanned.
- **Only 10 of 27 console errors are shown.**

The one clear code-level problem is the homepage accessibility result. It has 2 critical, 1 serious and 5 moderate violations, including duplicate and nested `<main>` landmarks.

## Accessibility

18 violations in total, concentrated on the Homepage and repeated on Search.

**Homepage (10):**
- **Critical, `aria-required-parent`.** Elements with roles such as `listitem` or `tab` sit outside their required parent role.
- **Critical, `button-name`.** Buttons have no accessible name, typically icon-only buttons with no `aria-label`.
- **Serious, `link-name`.** Links have no discernible text, typically image-only links with no `alt` or `aria-label`.
- **Moderate, `landmark-main-is-top-level`.** `<main>` is nested inside another landmark.
- **Moderate, `landmark-no-duplicate-main`.** More than one `<main>` exists on the page.
- **Moderate, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`.** An `<aside>` or complementary landmark is nested.
- **Moderate, `landmark-unique`.** Landmarks are not distinguishable.
- **Moderate, `heading-order`.** Heading levels are skipped.
- **Moderate, `region`.** Content sits outside landmarks.
- **Minor, `aria-allowed-role`.** A role is invalid for its element.

**Search (4):** `aria-allowed-role`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region`. These are the same landmark and role defects as the Homepage, so they probably come from shared header, footer and template markup.

**View Cart (4):**
- **Serious, `document-title`.** No `<title>` element.
- **Serious, `html-has-lang`.** No `lang` attribute.
- **Moderate, `landmark-one-main`.** No `<main>` landmark.
- **Moderate, `page-has-heading-one`.** No `<h1>`.

These are consistent with the 429 response, so treat them as unconfirmed for a real cart page.

The Homepage sets `<html lang="en">` correctly.

## SEO

- **Not assessable from this data.** The HTML samples are truncated before any `<title>`, meta description, canonical, robots or JSON-LD tags.
- **Image-only links (`link-name`, Homepage).** They carry no anchor text, so crawlers get no link context.
- **Landmark and heading structure.** Duplicate `<main>` and skipped heading levels weaken the page's semantic outline.
- **429s on `/cart/`.** If Googlebot gets the same bot-challenge responses, it may see errors or empty documents. The cart should be `noindex` anyway, but the challenge on public category and PDP URLs needs checking.
- **Log In and Men category.** The sampled URLs are both `/`, so nothing about these pages can be assessed.

## Performance

- **TTFB.** The first three navigations took 481–494ms, which is acceptable. The later ones took 27–129ms (Men > Shoes 32ms, Product detail 35ms, Add to Cart 35ms, Search 27ms), which points to cache hits, prefetch or client-side navigation rather than true origin latency. View Cart took 129ms for what was a 429 response.
- **LCP and CLS.** Not captured on any step, so there is no rendering-performance data.
- **Third-party load.** More than 25 third-party scripts are injected before first interaction:
  - **Tag management and testing:** GTM, Monetate (`entry.js` and `custom.js`).
  - **Analytics and session replay:** Quantum Metric, Bloomreach (`brsrvr`), Pulse Insights, Tune.
  - **Ad pixels:** TikTok, Pinterest, Facebook, Snapchat, Reddit, Taboola, Nextdoor, Bing, Yahoo, Impact, creativecdn.
  - **Consent:** TrustArc.

  Many are `async` and consent-managed (`vice-managed-src`), but they still compete for main-thread time. Only the Pulse Insights script carries SRI.
- **Fonts via JS loader.** Web fonts load through `webfontloader.js` from cdnjs (the `wf-mulish-*-active` classes). That puts a third-party CDN request and a script on the font critical path, which risks FOIT or FOUT and layout shift.
- **Resource hints.** Only `dns-prefetch` for monetate.net is visible. No `preconnect` or `preload` appears in the sampled head.
- **GTM environment.** The GTM URL includes `gtm_preview=env-1&gtm_auth=…`, so the site is pinned to a GTM environment snapshot. Confirm this is intended for production.

## Mobile Optimization

- **Pulse Insights reports `device_type=desktop`.** All three requests did, on a journey that was supposed to be mobile. Either the device detection ignores the mobile viewport, or the harness is not presenting as mobile.
- **`--vh` set by JavaScript.** The `html` element carries `--vh: 6.64px`, a JS viewport-height workaround that implies a viewport about 664px tall. `100dvh`/`svh` would remove the dependency on script.
- **Interaction before hydration.** An inline handler calls `preventDefault()` on any `.js-ignore-click` element until `app:JsLoaded` fires. Taps on those links do nothing until app JS loads, and that delay grows on slow mobile networks with this much third-party script.
- **Not measurable from this data:** viewport meta, tap-target sizes and responsive images.

## Console & Network Errors

Only 10 of the 27 console errors were provided.

**Network (9):**

| Count | Status | Endpoint | Likely cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 429 | `/…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.903` | Kasada fingerprinting endpoint rate-limiting the automated session |
| 1 | 429 | `/cart/` | Bot-protection block on the cart document |
| 3 | 403 | `survey.pulseinsights.com/serve` | Survey service rejecting requests (`visit_count=69`, `device_type=desktop`); non-critical to the shopper |

**Console:**
- **429 (×3).** Matches the Kasada fingerprint failures.
- **"Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed" (×4).** A script registers an `unload` handler under a policy that blocks it. Likely a third-party tag, since the first-party inline code only uses `click` and `app:JsLoaded`. `unload` handlers are deprecated and hurt bfcache eligibility.
- **"Unsupported Summarizer API languages…" (×2).** A caller of Chrome's built-in Summarizer API passes unsupported language options. The source is not identifiable from this data. It may be a page script, an extension or the harness.

## Recommendations

1. **Fix the audit before relying on it.**
   - Whitelist or slow the test client for Kasada, or add retry and backoff.
   - Re-run View Cart and only record it when the document is a real 200 response.
   - Record the final URL after each navigation, and fix the step URL capture so Log In and Men category are verified.
   - Capture LCP and CLS with `PerformanceObserver` registered before navigation, with `buffered: true`.
   - Run axe on every step and report which steps were scanned.
2. **Fix the Homepage ARIA and landmark errors.**
   - Remove the nested and duplicate `<main>`.
   - Add `aria-label` or `alt` to icon-only buttons and links.
   - Correct the role parent/child relationships and the heading hierarchy.
   - Label duplicate landmarks (`nav`, `aside`) uniquely.
3. **Trim the third-party script load.** Defer ad pixels until after consent and idle, remove unused ones, and audit what the GTM environment loads. Add SRI where vendors allow it.
4. **Self-host fonts.** Use `@font-face` with `font-display: swap` and `preload` for the critical weights instead of `webfontloader` from cdnjs.
5. **Find and remove the `unload` listener.** Switch to `pagehide` or `visibilitychange` to restore bfcache eligibility.
6. **Check mobile device detection.** Make sure Pulse Insights and similar scripts detect the mobile viewport, and consider replacing the JS `--vh` hack with `dvh` units.
7. **Confirm Kasada isn't challenging search crawlers or real shoppers on `/cart/` and the fingerprint endpoint.** Check the 429 thresholds and crawler allowlisting.
