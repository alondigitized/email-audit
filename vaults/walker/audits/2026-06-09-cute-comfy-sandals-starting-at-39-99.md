# Cute, comfy sandals starting at $39.99
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- Another DSW in the inbox — at this rate I could set my watch by them. "Comfy" in the subject line is my word, and $39.99 is a number I'd seriously consider. So yes, my thumb moved. But the second the email loaded, the headline stopped me cold: "TWO WORDS: CUTE AND COMFY." That word *cute*, in letters about three inches tall, set the tone for everything that followed. Women's slip-ons. Women's flip-flops with floral details. Shop-by-size grid running 5 through 8. This is a women's email that landed in a 62-year-old man's inbox, and it's the second one in two days — yesterday's "Turn heads for only $44.99" was stilettos and the same story.
- DSW has my purchase history. They know I buy men's comfort shoes. Thirteen-plus emails in a week and they still can't route me to the right content. That's not a design problem, that's a list problem.
- Mark as read, move on. No click. Wouldn't forward this to anyone except maybe my wife.

## What's working

- DSW's name in the sender field gets my thumb moving on autopilot — that brand trust is real and earned
- The $39.99 price is right in the hero where I can see it without hunting, and it's specific enough to land
- "SHOP ALL SANDALS" is clean and unambiguous — I know exactly where that button goes
- Email renders perfectly on my phone: no broken images, no overlapping text, no weird gaps
- Summer sandals is the right season — timing is smart even if the targeting isn't

## What's weak

- "CUTE" in the hero in giant letters is a four-letter word for the wrong audience — this is explicitly women's copy
- Every product shown is women's sandals: floral details, raffia weaves, minimalist thongs — zero men's options
- Shop-by-size grid shows 5 through 8; my size is a 10 wide, so that grid is decorative to me
- "Wide Calf" CTA is a women's fit feature — nothing here is sizing me up as a men's customer
- No discount, no sale, no urgency — just a starting-price anchor on an email I have no use for

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the segmentation first** — men's subscribers should never see this email. If DSW has my shoe purchase history, use it. This is the most important fix by a mile.
- 2. **Build a parallel men's sandal send** — same price anchor, different hero. Skechers GoWalk, Vionic, Birkenstock men's, Clarks. I'd actually shop that.
- 3. **Swap "cute" for something neutral** — even "cool and comfy" or "ready for summer" would work for both genders if they insist on a combined send.
- 4. **Surface the rewards/spending threshold prominently** — the top banner looks like it mentions some kind of spend-to-earn offer, but it's tiny and I can barely read it. That's the thing that would keep me in the email.
- 5. **Add a men's navigation shortcut** — at minimum a "Shop Men's" link near the top for people like me who land here accidentally.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta — confirmed issue**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Disables user pinch-to-zoom. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Should be removed or replaced with `maximum-scale=5`.

**Two malformed `<meta>` tags — confirmed issues**
```html
<!-- Missing http-equiv attribute -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta content="IE=Edge" />
```
Both are missing required `http-equiv` attributes. Correct forms:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=Edge" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, these tags are inert. The charset declaration in particular may cause rendering issues in older Outlook/Yahoo clients.

**Capitalized `Src:` in all 11 `@font-face` blocks — confirmed issue**
```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/appboy/...") format('woff');
```
CSS property names are technically case-insensitive per spec, but this will fail W3C CSS validation and may be rejected by strict parsers. All 11 declarations are affected (4× Futura PT, 7× HelveticaNow). Should be lowercase `src:`.

**Inconsistent font file formats**
Futura PT fonts load `.woff`; HelveticaNow fonts load `.ttf`. WOFF is ~30% smaller than TTF. The HelveticaNow fonts also carry cache-busting query strings (`?1755182939`) while Futura PT fonts do not — inconsistent asset management.

**Alt text on images** — cannot verify from truncated source. Must confirm all `<img>` tags carry descriptive `alt` attributes.
