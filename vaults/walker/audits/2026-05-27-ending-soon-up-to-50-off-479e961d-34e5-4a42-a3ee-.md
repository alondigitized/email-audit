# Ending soon! Up to 50% off!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- This is — I've lost count at this point — probably the seventh or eighth Crocs email in my inbox this week. I almost swiped delete by reflex. But when I actually open it, two classic clogs are staring back at me: one red, one navy. No platform wedges. No studded charm slides aimed at teenagers. That alone puts this above about half the Crocs sends I've seen this month.
- The "ending tonight" urgency feels real — not a rolling "don't miss out" that's been running for two weeks. And the secondary "Buy 2, Take 30% Off" stacking deal is a decent hook if I'm already buying. But I scrolled the whole thing and couldn't find an actual shop button — just "shop now" text buried in the body copy, which doesn't tap well on my phone. And the preview text was invisible junk characters. After this much email volume from one brand, I'm running on fumes of goodwill.
- I'd probably click through this one — barely — because the shoe is right and the price math is real. But I wouldn't forward it to anyone, and I'm one or two more of these before I hit unsubscribe.

## What's working

- Classic clogs in red and navy are front and center — these are actual shoes I'd consider buying, not women's wedges or charm-covered slides
- "Ending tonight" gives the sale a credible, specific deadline rather than the usual vague "don't miss out" language
- Email renders clean — no broken images, no overlapping text, nothing screwy on my screen
- The "Buy 2, Take 30% Off" secondary offer is a nice stacking deal and clearly called out
- Offer math is simple — 50% off is something I can do in my head

## What's weak

- No visible CTA button anywhere — "shop now" lives in the body copy as plain text, and that's a lousy tap target on my phone
- Preview text is complete garbage — invisible characters showed up in my inbox instead of any actual copy; I had zero reason from the preview line to open this
- Email fatigue is real at this cadence — this is the same subject line and the same offer I saw earlier this week; the "ending soon" urgency loses its punch when I've seen "50% off" from Crocs three times in seven days
- The Jibbitz charms section is dead space for me — I don't decorate shoes, and it just pushes the sale content further down the page
- No mention of comfort, cushioning, fit, or anything practical — just discount percentage stacked on discount percentage

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a real CTA button under the hero image** — "SHOP THE SALE" in a contrasting color, big enough to tap without zooming. This is the single highest-impact fix.
- 2. **Fix the preheader** — "ending tonight — classic clogs up to 50% off" is five seconds of work and would have meaningfully changed whether I opened this
- 3. **Name what's actually on sale** — "Select Styles" always makes me nervous I'll click through and find nothing I want; "Classic Clogs up to 50% off" is more honest and more compelling
- 4. **Pull back on frequency or rotate the offer** — I've now seen "Up to 50% off" from Crocs multiple times this week; the urgency is fake when the sale never actually ends
- 5. **Drop or demote the Jibbitz section** — segment it out for the charm crowd; I don't need it diluting the main offer

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Two confirmed issues.**

**Issue A — Mixed content: 7 images over HTTP**

All footer social/app icons are served from `http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/`. Affected images:

| Alt text | Filename |
|---|---|

| Instagram | `e18949ff-2d29-4a62-9176-a67132186a90.png` |

| TikTok | `51d8144e-818a-4a00-a812-5cf0429b2429.png` |
| YouTube | `1187ef58-07fe-4985-8349-93e338d86694.png` |

Gmail (Chrome-based) and Apple Mail both block HTTP image loads in HTTPS contexts. These icons will render as broken images or be silently dropped on those clients.

**Issue B — Missing `http-equiv` on charset meta**

```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```

The `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute is absent. Without it, this is not a valid content-type declaration. Some older Outlook and Yahoo clients may fall back to a default charset. The correct form:

```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```

**Issue C — Tracking pixel missing alt text**

```
src: https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&c_emailopened=Yes&...
```

The pixel `<img>` has no `alt` attribute. Screen readers will announce the raw URL. Since the image is `display:none`, real-world impact is low, but it's a markup correctness failure.
