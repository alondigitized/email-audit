# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 2/10 (prev 2/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-08-28**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I got about as far as I did last time before things fell apart. The homepage looks sharp — decent-sized nav tabs for Slip-ins, Arch Fit, Max Cushioning right up top, which is exactly what I'm shopping for. But two things that matter most to me — logging in and checking my cart — simply don't work. Tapping "Log In" did nothing at all; the screen never changed. And when I tapped through to view my cart, I got a blank white screen. I don't even know if my shoe made it into the cart. Search actually worked fine and pulled up relevant comfort shoes. But a site that can't log me in or show me my own cart isn't one I'd trust with my credit card.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**2/10** — unchanged from yesterday. The homepage, category browsing, and search are all solid, but the two conversion-critical steps (login, cart) are both broken. A shopper who can't see their cart abandons right there — that's lost revenue, not a minor annoyance.

## 3. What's Working

- Homepage nav bar surfaces exactly what I care about: Slip-ins, Arch Fit, Max Cushioning — big, tappable text, not buried in a menu.
- The Men > Shoes category page leads with "Hands Free Slip-Ins," "Walking Shoes," "Athletic Sneakers" — that's smart, it's speaking my language before I even search.
- Product photos are clean, the price is big and legible ($90.00), size grid buttons are large and easy to tap with a thumb.
- Search for "comfort shoes" returned genuinely relevant results (New Cool slip-on, Max Cushioning Endeavour) with prices, sale pricing, and wide-width callouts — that's useful for me.
- The size-required validation at least tells me what I did wrong ("Please select a size") instead of failing silently.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Login is a dead end.** Tapping "Log In" produced zero change — the screenshot is pixel-identical to the homepage. Either the tap didn't register or there's no login screen behind it. I can't tell which, and neither could my thumb.
- **Cart is completely blank.** After Add to Cart, viewing the cart loaded a totally white page — no header, no items, no error message. That's the moment I'd give up and close the app.
- **Popup dismissal did nothing visible** — but there also wasn't a popup blocking anything, so that step is a wash rather than a true problem.
- The cart icon shows "0" all the way through the Add to Cart step, so I have no confidence the shoe was ever actually added — likely because I hadn't picked a size yet, but there's no visual nudge pointing me back up to the size grid when I hit "Add to Cart" prematurely.
- Nothing on the product or category pages does any price-vs-quality comparison, and obviously no cross-brand comparison against Hoka or New Balance — I wouldn't expect Skechers to do that, but it means I still have to leave the site to comparison shop.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the Log In tap target immediately.** Whether it's a broken link, a modal that fails to render, or a JS error, this blocks every personalized feature (order history, saved sizes, reward points) for a returning shopper like me.
2. **Fix the blank cart page.** This is the single highest-priority bug — a shopper who adds an item and sees nothing will assume the site is broken and leave.
3. Add a clear scroll/highlight cue back to the size selector when "Add to Cart" is tapped with no size chosen, so the error isn't just a small red line easy to miss.
4. Once cart and login work, surface my saved size and past purchases so the "20% off" and "$5 reward" banners feel earned rather than generic.

## 6. Bottom Line

The bones are good — clean layout, relevant nav labels, decent search — but I can't log in and I can't see my cart. Those are the two steps that turn browsing into buying, and both are broken. Until that's fixed, this is a browsing site, not a shopping site, for someone like me.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The audited journey traverses skechers.com (Salesforce Commerce Cloud / SFRA storefront, `demandware.static` assets) through 8 mobile steps. Two systemic issues stand out: (1) the site's bot-detection layer (Kasada, `x-kpsdk-v` fingerprint endpoint) returned repeated `429` responses during the session, including one `429` on `/cart/` itself, and (2) the "View Cart" step's DOM is missing baseline document metadata (`<title>`, `lang`, landmarks, H1) that is present on every other sampled page — consistent with the cart request having been rate-limited and served a degraded/interstitial response rather than the full SFRA cart template. This should be confirmed against raw response bodies/status codes for that step.

## Accessibility

18 axe violations across 3 of 8 steps (Homepage, View Cart, Search results). By severity:
- **Critical (2):** `aria-required-parent` and `button-name` on Homepage — icon/utility buttons without accessible names and ARIA roles used outside required parent structures will not be announced correctly by screen readers.
- **Serious (3):** `link-name` (Homepage, likely image/icon links with no text alternative); `document-title` and `html-has-lang` (View Cart) — both are baseline WCAG 2.1 A requirements and their absence on a transactional page (cart) is more severe than a marketing page.
- **Moderate (11):** repeated landmark issues — `landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-unique`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` (Homepage, Search), `landmark-one-main`/`page-has-heading-one` (View Cart), `heading-order` (Homepage), `region` (Homepage, Search) — indicating duplicated/nested landmark regions, likely from third-party widget markup (Stylitics, Pulse Insights, Monetate) injecting competing `<main>`/`<aside>` elements into the DOM.
- **Minor (2):** `aria-allowed-role` (Homepage, Search).

The View Cart failures (missing title/lang/H1/main) are consistent with the `429` observed on `/cart/` in the network log — worth re-testing in isolation to confirm whether this is a genuine template defect or an artifact of a blocked/retried request.

## SEO

- `View Cart` is missing `<title>` and `lang` attribute — both are indexation/accessibility-relevant; if this is the actual rendered response (not a fallback from the `429`), it needs a real fix in the cart template.
- Homepage/Login/Men category HTML shows `<html lang="en" class="g-local-en_US ...">` correctly on the pages that rendered normally.
- "Log In" and "Men category" steps both resolved to the base URL `https://www.skechers.com/` in the captured HTML rather than distinct paths (e.g., a login modal URL fragment or `/mens/`). If login is a modal (no URL change), that's expected; if the Men category click did not produce a distinct crawlable URL, that's worth confirming — canonical, distinct URLs per category are required for category-page SEO and this data is inconclusive on its own.
- No canonical/meta description content was included in the truncated samples to evaluate; recommend a follow-up pass with untruncated `<head>` capture.

## Performance

TTFB by step: Homepage 464ms, Log In 383ms, Men category 383ms, Men > Shoes 35ms, Product detail 26ms, Add to Cart 26ms, View Cart 125ms, Search 39ms.

- Homepage TTFB (464ms) is notably higher than every subsequent step and is the only value approaching a threshold worth monitoring (Good TTFB is generally <800ms, but the gap vs. sub-page TTFBs of 26–125ms suggests the homepage does more server-side work — personalization/A-B test evaluation via Monetate is a likely contributor given the number of Monetate scripts loaded).
- Sub-second TTFBs elsewhere (26–125ms) indicate these responses are likely served from CDN/edge cache rather than fresh origin renders — good baseline, but worth confirming they're not cached error/fallback pages given the `429`s observed on the same paths.
- **LCP and CLS were not captured for any step** — this is a data gap, not a "no issues found" result. Given the very high count of synchronous/async third-party scripts on every page (30+ trackers: TikTok, Snapchat, Reddit, Facebook, Bing, GTM ×2, Monetate ×2, Pulse Insights, Impact, CreativeCDN, Taboola, Nextdoor, Quantum Metric, go2sdk/Tune, WebFontLoader), LCP and TBT are likely materially affected. Re-run with LCP/CLS instrumentation before drawing conclusions.

## Mobile Optimization

- No mobile-specific viewport/meta or responsive-layout defects were identifiable from the truncated HTML samples provided.
- The third-party script payload (30+ distinct vendor tags per page, several using `vice-managed-src` indicating a tag-management wrapper) is unusually heavy for a mobile session and is a primary risk to mobile TTI/INP even though it doesn't show up in TTFB.
- No issues found regarding mobile-specific markup (viewport meta, touch targets) — not evaluated in the provided data; recommend explicit mobile Lighthouse/PageSpeed pass.

## Console & Network Errors

- **Bot-detection rate limiting:** 5× `429` on `https://www.skechers.com/149e9513.../fp?x-kpsdk-v=...` (Kasada client-side fingerprinting endpoint) plus 1× `429` on `/cart/`. Repeated fingerprint-endpoint throttling mid-session can degrade bot-check reliability and, if it's throttling real user sessions, may explain the incomplete View Cart render noted above.
- **CORS failure:** Stylitics stylesheet (`web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?...`) blocked by CORS policy (missing `Access-Control-Allow-Origin`) — this stylesheet fails to load entirely, which may cause unstyled/broken outfit-recommendation widgets.
- **`net::ERR_FAILED`:** one unresolved network failure, likely tied to the blocked Stylitics request.
- **Browser API warning (not a site defect):** repeated "Unsupported Summarizer API languages" messages come from the Chrome built-in Summarizer API being invoked with unsupported languages — this is generated client-side by the browser/extension environment running the audit, not by skechers.com code; exclude from site remediation but flag if it recurs across unrelated audits (possible test-harness config issue).

## Recommendations

1. Investigate the `429`s on the Kasada `/fp` endpoint and `/cart/` — determine whether the audit's request pattern is triggering bot-protection throttling, and if so whether real mobile users could hit the same limit during checkout.
2. Re-test the View Cart step in isolation with full response capture (status code + headers + body) to confirm whether the missing `<title>`/`lang`/landmarks are a genuine template regression or a fallback page served during the `429`.
3. Fix the Stylitics CORS misconfiguration (`Access-Control-Allow-Origin` missing on `web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css`) so the stylesheet loads.
4. Address the two critical accessibility violations first (`button-name`, `aria-required-parent` on Homepage) — these block screen-reader users from basic navigation.
5. Consolidate/audit landmark regions (`main`, `aside`) injected by third-party widgets (Stylitics, Pulse Insights, Monetate) causing duplicate/non-top-level landmarks on Homepage and Search.
6. Re-run the audit with LCP and CLS instrumentation enabled — current data cannot support any performance conclusion beyond TTFB.
7. Audit third-party script count/load strategy (30+ tags per page) for mobile TTI/INP impact, particularly on Homepage where TTFB is already the highest in the funnel.
