# 50% off Fashion Sneakers!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- After getting hammered with Shoe Carnival's Father's Day parade for two straight weeks, I figured they'd at least give me a couple of days off. They didn't. But I'll say this — "50% off" caught my eye in the inbox even if "Fashion Sneakers" is not exactly how I think about my feet. I'm a comfort guy, not a fashion guy. Still, I opened it, and I'm glad I did — there's a Men's Sandals section at $19.98 and, more importantly, the "trending for you" block threw me the Brooks Revel 6. Brooks is on my short list right now. That's what actually got my thumb moving.
- What almost stopped me before any of that was the preview text. On my phone, right next to the subject line, I see a wall of gobbledygook — a raw tracking URL instead of real copy. That looks like spam or a phishing link. The only reason I opened it is because I recognize the Shoe Carnival sender name.
- Inside, the countdown clock ("FINAL DAY DEALS — 12:59:59") is the right kind of urgency, and the $10 off code with a real dollar threshold is honest money, not a shell game. I'd click "Shop Men's" for the sandals and probably tap through to the Brooks, but I'd scroll right past the fashion sneaker hero section. Not forwarding this one, but I wouldn't delete it either — I'd click and move on.

## What's working

- **The countdown timer creates real pressure.** "FINAL DAY DEALS — 12:59:59" above the fold on mobile made me feel like I was about to miss something.
- **The $10 off code is specific and honest.** "ONLINE CODE: SUMMER 10 / $74.98+" is a clear threshold — no vague percentage hedging, no buried exclusions visible.
- **Men's sandals at $19.98** — that price point in the men's section is legitimately interesting to me. Right category, right number.
- **The Brooks Revel 6 callout in "trending for you"** is the closest thing to a personalized signal in this whole email. Brooks is a brand I actually cross-shop.
- **Clean layout, no render bugs.** Reads top-to-bottom on mobile without me having to zoom or fight broken images.

## What's weak

- **"Fashion Sneakers" is someone else's headline.** I am not shopping for fashion — I'm shopping for my feet. The hero image is a young woman in casual summer wear. That's not who I am, and it's not who I'm buying for.
- **The preview text is a raw tracking URL.** In my inbox, below the subject, it reads "Shoe Carnival https://click.email.shoecarnival.com/..." — that looks like a broken send or phishing. First impression is bad before I even open it.
- **The offer escalation feels arbitrary.** June 22 was 30% off sneakers; today it's 50% off fashion sneakers. No narrative connecting the jump — just another discount.
- **Afterpay takes up real estate I don't need.** A full barcode block for buy-now-pay-later is not where my eyes want to go. That space could show me more men's comfort options.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader — this is the most urgent fix.** The raw URL leaking into the preview is the single biggest turnoff before the open. Replace it with something like "FINAL DAY: Men's sandals from $19.98 + $10 off with code."
- 2. **Lead with men's comfort, not fashion.** The hero image and headline push me away on first scroll. Swap to a men's sandal or running shoe shot with comfort language — cushion, support, slip-on — and you've got a different email for someone like me.
- 3. **Put the Brooks callout above the fold.** If your algorithm knows I've been looking at Brooks, that's a click waiting to happen — don't bury it after the Afterpay block. Move it to the second module.
- 4. **Give the offer escalation a story.** "We went bigger for the long weekend" earns the 50% headline. A random jump from 30% to 50% with no explanation reads like a desperate markdown, not a treat.
- **Subject Alt A:** `FINAL DAY: Men's Sandals from $19.98 + $10 Off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `50% off ends tonight — grab it before it's gone`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Men's sandals, Brooks, New Balance — final hours. Code SUMMER10 saves you $10.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `FINAL DAY deals: men's sandals at $19.98, fashion sneakers up to 50% off.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Shoe Carnival "50% off Fashion Sneakers!" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound and renders with standard multi-client defensive CSS. Two issues require action before send (or post-send diagnosis): an unresolved merge token in the open-tracking pixel URL and six content images missing alt attributes.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Unresolved merge token in open-tracking pixel**
`https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]`
The `[UNIQUE]` parameter appears literally in the source. If present at send time, every open maps to the same token — per-subscriber open tracking is broken. This should resolve to a subscriber-unique identifier (e.g., an encoded recipient ID or GUID) at ESP render time. Verify whether this was an artifact of QA capturing the pre-personalization template or a genuine send-time failure.

**26 click-tracking links unverified**
All outbound links are wrapped in a click-redirect domain and could not be probed by the QA scanner. Destination URLs, UTM parameter presence, and redirect chain health are unconfirmed.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Six images missing `alt` text**

| Image | URL |
|---|---|
| Product — `176849_388805_1.jpg` | `shoecarnival.com/on/demandware.static/…` |
| Product — `178810_419884_1.jpg` | `shoecarnival.com/on/demandware.static/…` |
| Product — `180673_411292_1.jpg` | `shoecarnival.com/on/demandware.static/…` |
| Content image | `image.email.shoecarnival.com/…465aca37….png` |
| Open-pixel `ieezzy5m` | `ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/…` |
| Open-pixel `0di86k1v` | `0di86k1v.emltrk.com/…` |

Product images rendered in image-off environments (Outlook default, Gmail data-saver) will show blank boxes. Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` explicitly to suppress broken-image indicators.

**Malformed meta tags**
Two `<meta>` tags in `<head>` are missing the `http-equiv` attribute:
- `<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />` — should be `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />`
- `<meta content="IE=edge" />` — should be `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />`

Both are benign in practice (major clients ignore them), but they produce invalid markup.

**Tel/SMS link interaction disabled globally**
```css
a[href^="tel"], a[href^="sms"] { pointer-events: none; cursor: default !important; }
```
This suppresses tap-to-call on mobile. Intentional if no phone numbers appear in the email body; if any do exist in the truncated content, this silently breaks them.

SPF/DKIM/DMARC authentication headers are not present in the provided source (header-level data was not included in the audit input) — these must be verified at the MTA level separately.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Campaign date in UTM tag predates send**
Visible UTM parameter from the tracking pixel:
`utm_campaign=SCA_20260623_sandals_clogs_sneakers_AM`

The campaign code embeds `20260623` (June 23) while the email was sent June 25. This is either a naming convention (campaign created on the 23rd) or a recycled parameter from an earlier deployment. Confirm this is intentional so analytics attribution is accurate.

**Subject/UTM mismatch**
Subject line: *"50% off Fashion Sneakers!"*
UTM campaign tag: `sandals_clogs_sneakers_AM`

The campaign tag includes sandals and clogs alongside sneakers. If the landing page promotes a mixed category, this is fine. If the landing page is sneakers-only, the UTM tag will skew category attribution in analytics.
