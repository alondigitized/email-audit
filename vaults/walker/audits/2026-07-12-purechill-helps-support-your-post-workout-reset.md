# Purechill helps support your post-workout reset
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- I'll say this much — "post-workout reset" is the first subject line adidas has sent me in weeks that isn't about Formula 1 racing or skateboard culture. So it at least didn't go straight to archive. I walk my dog, I stand in line at Costco, I'm on my feet more than people my age expect to be — and the idea of a shoe that helps my feet recover from that sounds like something I'd pay attention to.
- Inside the email, things are clean: big white shoe, bold headline, short copy. Nothing screaming at me. But adidas does that thing again where they describe the technology — "channelweight foam," "durable outsole" — and somehow I come away knowing less than I did before. No price. No wide sizing callout. No button to tap. Just a text line with an asterisk sending me to fine print at the bottom. I'm 62 years old. An asterisk means "catch," and "catch" means I'm reaching for the back button.
- I'd close this without clicking. Maybe tell my buddy Dave about the shoe if the conversation came up, but that's about the extent of it.

## What's working

- The shoe itself looks like my kind of thing — white, low-profile, cushioned sole, no loud colorways trying to impress a 22-year-old.
- "Recharging becomes part of your everyday routine" is actually good copy for me. Everyday comfort is the sell, not race day.
- Layout is clean and phone-friendly. Big image, short paragraphs, nothing to zoom into.
- No athlete collab, no F1 driver, no skateboarder. Just the shoe. That's an improvement over the last several sends.
- "Revitalize" in the hero headline is warmer language than "Faster. Fastest." — at least pointing in the right direction.

## What's weak

- "Post-workout reset" assumes I just left the gym. I didn't. Walk-all-day comfort would land harder for someone with my lifestyle.
- No price anywhere. Not a "starting at," not a "members save X." I can't make a decision on foam descriptions alone.
- The CTA is a plain text line with an asterisk — not a button. Hard to tap confidently on a phone, and the asterisk signals there's a catch.
- No personalization whatsoever. After all these months of adidas emails, I'm still getting treated like a stranger.
- "Channelweight foam" sounds made up. Just tell me my feet won't ache after three hours on them.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price anchor or promotional hook** — even "Shop from $80" gives me a reason to tap and sets the expectation before I land on a product page.
- 2. **Replace the text CTA with a real button** — "Shop Purechill" or "Find My Size" in a tappable button, full stop.
- 3. **Reframe from gym recovery to everyday walking** — "Comfortable from your first step to your last" is a stronger headline for a 62-year-old than "post-workout reset."
- 4. **Show the shoe on a foot, on a real surface** — a lifestyle shot of someone my age on a park path does more than a floating product on a dark background.
- 5. **Kill the asterisk or bury it further** — anything that says "conditions apply" above the fold is friction for someone who didn't come here to read fine print.

## Technical Audit — adidas "Purechill post-workout reset"

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**

**[FAIL] Broken redirect — unknown URL scheme**
The Dynamics click-tracking wrapper at `dv.adidas.com/o/4ef4ffd4...` resolves to a destination with scheme `/g/...` (a relative-looking path, not a valid absolute URL):
```
unknown url type: '/g/4ef4ffd4-e1cd-441c-b5eb-020d17f6db60?txn=7623c300-...'
```
This is a misconfigured redirect target inside the encoded `cp_tp` payload. Recipients who click will hit an error page. The `txn` parameter suggests this is a transactional-style triggered send, meaning it may affect a targeted segment rather than all recipients.

**[WARN] 22 tracking links not probed**

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**

**[WARN] 4× HTTP image sources — same asset, repeated**
The arrow icon (`b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg`) is referenced four times via `http://`:
```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all block mixed-content images by default — the arrow will render as a broken image placeholder in these clients. Fix: change scheme to `https://`.

**[WARN] Additional HTTP image**

**5. Compliance**
