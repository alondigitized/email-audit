# Free Sandals for YOU
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-22**
## Executive summary

- I opened this thinking Shoe Carnival had a free sandal deal for me personally. What I got was a wall of women's and girls' sandals I have zero interest in. The subject says "Free Sandals for YOU" — but the YOU turns out to be someone else entirely. I'm a 62-year-old guy looking for comfortable men's slip-ons, and this email doesn't have a single product aimed at me. The only thing I can use is the $10 off coupon near the bottom, but I almost didn't scroll that far. This felt like a mass blast that landed in the wrong inbox.
- Almost nothing in this email earns a click from me. The hero is irrelevant to my gender. The one applicable offer (the $10 coupon) is buried so deep I'd have given up before reaching it on my phone.

## What's working

- **The $10 off coupon is legible.** The `EXTRA10` code is printed clearly, the barcode is there, and the threshold ($74.98) is spelled out. If I scroll down far enough, I know exactly what to do.
- **Shoe Perks reward mention is appreciated.** I like knowing I can earn toward a reward. That $10 Perks reward block is the one moment the email acknowledges I'm a member, not just a stranger.
- **BOGO mechanic is clear.** If I had a wife or granddaughter shopping, the BOGO Free on sandals would be easy to act on. The offer structure isn't confusing.

## What's weak

- **Entirely wrong audience for me.** Eight sandal product photos — every single one is Women's or Girls'. Not one men's shoe anywhere. I felt invisible looking at this.
- **Subject line overpromises.** "Free Sandals for YOU" reads like I'm getting free sandals. The actual offer is BOGO on Women's & Kids' styles. That bait-and-switch sours the whole experience before I've even scrolled.
- **Preview text is broken garbage.** What showed in my inbox next to the subject was a raw tracking URL — a long string starting with "Shoe Carnival https://click.email.shoecarnival.com/..." That's embarrassing and looked like a phishing attempt to me. Almost didn't open it.
- **Hero image is small and cluttered on mobile.** BOGO FREE in big letters, sure, but the product grid below is tiny. Eight pairs of women's sandals rendered as thumbnail images — on my phone screen those are barely distinguishable from each other.
- **The 50% off Pink Sticker Clearance is in-store only.** I don't make trips to the mall just to browse clearance. If it's not available to buy online, I'm not going.
- **No men's content anywhere.** I'm a registered Shoe Carnival customer. They know I'm male. Why am I looking at platform sandals?

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment by gender before you hit send.** If I'm in the database as male, send me men's deals. Or at minimum include one men's module. Even a "Men's Clearance" footer strip would have made me feel seen.
- 2. **Fix the preview text immediately.** That raw URL is the first thing I see in my inbox. It looks like spam and almost cost them the open. The preheader should say something like "BOGO Free on sandals — and $10 off your next order."
- 3. **Either make the in-store-only offer online-eligible or cut it from email.** In-store only deals in an email I'm reading on my phone create friction and disappointment. Move that budget to an online incentive.
- 4. **Move the coupon up.** The $10 off code is the most actionable offer for someone like me, and it's buried near the barcode at the bottom. Put it above the fold or at least in the middle.
- 5. **Deliver on the subject line.** If "Free Sandals for YOU" is the hook, show me a free-with-purchase sandal offer for men, or clarify immediately at the top that this is Women's & Kids'. Don't make me scroll through 8 women's products to figure out the offer isn't for me.
