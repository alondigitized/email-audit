# adidas! Up to 40% off!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-19**
## Executive summary

- Alright, this one's actually not bad — at least at first glance. Adidas logo's right up top under the "40% OFF" banner, big and bold, no hunting required. Then it drops into "Shoes Under $50 / $75 / $100" grids, which is a format I can actually use — I know my budget before I even click. Problem is, once I look at what's actually in those grids, it's heels, strappy sandals, a couple crocs, and maybe two or three sneakers that could pass as men's. This is DSW's fourth-plus "adidas sale" email in a few days and I'm still waiting for one that leads with a walking shoe instead of a stiletto.
- The "shoes under $X" price-tier idea is smart merchandising, I'll give them that — better than yesterday's leopard bootie parade. But it's still not sorted by gender or category, so I'm scrolling past a lot of product photos that have nothing to do with me before I hit the one New Balance or adidas trainer that might.
- Would I click? Maybe, just to see if the "Shoes Under $75" adidas sneakers are actually in my size. Forward to a friend? No. Mark as read and move on — DSW's sent me enough near-identical adidas promos this week that I've stopped opening most of them on sight.

## What's working

- Adidas logo is front and center right under the headline, no scrolling needed like some of their other sends this week.
- "Shoes Under $50/$75/$100" price tiers speak my language — I shop on value, not brand loyalty.
- I spotted a couple of actual sneakers (adidas and New Balance trainers) in the "Under $75" and "Under $100" rows that could work for me.
- Shop By Size grid near the bottom is handy — half-sizes and wide options are useful once I've found something I like.
- DSW VIP rewards call-out (5% back, free shipping) at the bottom is a decent trust cue for existing members.

## What's weak

- Most of the products shown are women's heels, sandals, and dressy flats — buried among them, not built around men's comfort shoes.
- No mention of cushioning, arch support, or slip-on ease anywhere — nothing that speaks to how I actually pick a shoe.
- I've gotten this same "adidas up to 40% off" pitch four or five times this week now — the offer's stopped feeling special.
- "The Price Is Right" tagline and pastel color grids feel more mall-catalog-for-her than tailored to a 62-year-old guy.
- Fine print at the bottom is dense — "Extra 10% off clearance," exclusions, code BESTTRICK — a lot to parse for a deal that's already fuzzy on which shoes actually qualify.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with a men's comfort category — put a men's adidas trainer or walking shoe in the very first product row, not buried in row six.
- 2. Split the grids by gender or let me filter — "Shoes Under $75" mixing stilettos and sneakers wastes my scroll.
- 3. Add one line of comfort language — "cushioned," "all-day wear," "arch support" — near the hero, since that's what actually gets me to click.
- 4. Cut the repeat cadence — I've seen this same adidas discount messaging daily; space it out or vary the hook so it doesn't blur together.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cushioned styles for every budget, men's sizes in stock`
