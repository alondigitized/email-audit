# New LoveShackFancy styles 🎀
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-10**
## Executive summary

- Another Crocs email. I've been watching these stack up since May, and after the June 13th "Extra 25% off" send — which at least had a real discount to look at — I had some hope they were tightening things up. Then this one landed and I didn't even know what I was looking at. LoveShackFancy. I had to read that twice. My first thought was that my wife got into my phone and signed me up for something.
- I opened it anyway because Crocs is on my list. But the second that hero image loaded — pink bows, ballet flats, women posing on what looks like a fairy-tale beach set — I knew this wasn't for me. Not even close. Every product in this email is a women's fashion collab. There's a Jibbitz charms section featuring tiny golden accessories. The section headers say "More is More" and "Bow Down." This email could not be less targeted at a 62-year-old man looking for a comfortable slip-on with arch support.
- I would not click anything here. I would not forward it to a friend unless it was to my daughter. I'd mark it read and move on, slightly more annoyed with Crocs than I was yesterday.

## What's working

- **Crocs logo is immediately recognizable** at the top — at least I knew who sent it before I even read a word.
- **Four clean CTA buttons** at the bottom ("Shop Clogs," "Shop Jibbitz Charms," "Shop Sale," "Shop Sandals") — readable, tappable, clear labels even on a phone screen.
- **No render bugs** — the email displays cleanly; nothing broken, overlapping, or missing.
- **"Shop Sale" button exists** — the only reason my thumb hovered for even a second. A sale is a sale.

## What's weak

- **LoveShackFancy means nothing to me** — I've never heard of this brand and the subject line gave me zero reason to care. If this is a collab, say something that means something to me.
- **Every product is aimed at women** — ballet flats, pink floral clogs, bow-covered Jibbitz. Not a men's shoe in sight anywhere.
- **No price or offer** — the June 13th email at least said "Extra 25% off." This one has zero discount language. I'm not clicking through on vibes alone.
- **"Bow Down" and "More is More" as section headers** — this copy is for a 24-year-old on TikTok, not a guy who wants to know if his feet will hurt less after nine holes.
- **Preview text is literally blank** — my inbox showed nothing next to the subject line. That's a waste of the one line I read before deciding whether to bother.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the list before you send.** If I'm a guy who's bought men's Classic Clogs or been clicking men's styles, don't send me a women's fashion collab. Or at minimum, lead with a men's product and mention the collab as a secondary story.
- 2. **Include a price signal.** "New LoveShackFancy styles" with no price or discount is not enough to move me. If there's a sale tab at the bottom, promote that upfront: "LoveShackFancy collab — styles from $XX."
- 3. **Fix the preview text.** The blank inbox preview is a wasted opportunity. A real line here — even just "Crocs × LoveShackFancy is here — plus styles on sale" — would at least tell me something.
- 4. **Write subject copy that means something without brand recognition.** I don't know LoveShackFancy. Say what it is — "bold floral styles," "a new collab," something that gives a stranger context.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP images (7 instances)** — all in the footer, served from `http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/`:
- `9c36c0ef...png` — App Store badge
- `13246762...png` — Google Play badge

- `80f68cd8...png` — Facebook
- `51d8144e...png` — TikTok
- `1187ef58...png` — YouTube

Gmail and some corporate mail proxies will block mixed-content HTTP `src` attributes, rendering these images as broken. All other content images use HTTPS.

**Missing alt on tracking pixel:**
```html
<img style="visibility:hidden;display:none" height="1" width="1"
  src="https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?...">
```
No `alt` attribute. Should be `alt=""` to pass accessibility validators and avoid screen-reader noise.

**Malformed meta tags:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />   <!-- missing http-equiv="Content-Type" -->
<meta content="IE=edge" />                     <!-- missing http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" -->
```
Neither tag will be interpreted as intended without the `http-equiv` attribute. Charset should also appear as `<meta charset="utf-8">` for HTML5 compliance.

**Redundant preheader implementation** — two hidden preheader blocks exist: a `.preheader` div suppressed via stylesheet and a second `display:none` div filled with zero-width spacers. Not harmful, but the spacer block's length (~300+ characters of invisible characters) could push the preheader preview text on clients that sum visible+invisible character counts differently.
