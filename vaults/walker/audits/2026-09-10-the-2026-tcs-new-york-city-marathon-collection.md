# The 2026 TCS New York City Marathon Collection
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-10**
## Executive summary

- Marathon gear — that's for the 26.2-mile crowd, not a guy like me who's shopping for something comfortable to wear to the grocery store. Two young, fit runners in racing jackets on that hero image, then a wall of performance shoes with carbon-plate-looking soles (the SuperComp Elite v6 is literally a race shoe, I know that much from my grandson). Nothing here says cushioning for all-day wear or arch support for a bad knee — it says "race pace."
- This is New Balance's third straight email to me built around athletic performance rather than comfort (the running new-arrivals one on the 6th, the Lookbook one on the 5th), and now a marathon collection. I'm starting to think their list just doesn't have a "comfort casual" segment, or I'm on the wrong list entirely. I'd scroll past this in about two seconds and mark it read. No click, no forward.

## What's working

- Clean, uncluttered layout — one hero, one headline, one button, then product tiles. Easy to follow even at a glance.
- "Shop now" button is clear and easy to find, good size for a thumb tap.
- The free shipping banner at $75+ is legible and up top, that's useful info regardless of what I'm buying.
- No render bugs I can see — images loaded, text is straight, nothing overlapping.

## What's weak

- Every model is young, lean, and dressed for actually running a marathon — nothing that reads "comfort walker" to me.
- Every shoe shown is a performance racing shoe (Ellipse, SuperComp Elite) — no mention of cushioning, arch support, or all-day wear anywhere.
- No offer or discount at all — just full-price product with a "Shop now" button, so there's zero incentive to click today.
- Copy about "New York's toughest commute" is cute if you're running the marathon, meaningless if you're not.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop sending me race-specific product drops — if New Balance has a comfort/walking line (Fresh Foam or similar), that's the shoe that'd actually get my click. A marathon collection email should go to their runners' list, not a general list that includes guys like me.
- 2. Add a real offer — a % off or free shipping callout tied to the collection would at least give me a reason to open the button.
- 3. If I am going to get lifestyle-brand emails, show me a model closer to my age and situation, and lead with comfort language (cushioned, arch support, slip-on ease) instead of race jargon.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New: comfort styles built for everyday miles`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Cushioned shoes, no race required`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `All-day comfort, zero laces to fuss with`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free shipping on orders $75+`
