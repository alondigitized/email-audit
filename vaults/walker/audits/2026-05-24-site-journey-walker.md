## Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Date:** May 24, 2026 | **Yesterday's Score:** 4/10

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Same-day Memorial Day, and I went looking for a comfortable slip-on I could wear all summer. The navigation held together better than I expected for a guy my age — big text, clear labels, Hands Free Slip-ins front and center. But the wheels came off hard at checkout: the cart page loaded completely blank. Nothing. Empty white screen. I would have left and gone to DSW's app without a second thought. Search also handed me women's shoes when I'm logged in as a man. The middle of the funnel is decent. Both ends — login confirmation and cart — are broken or invisible.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

No change from yesterday. Navigation improvements are real but the blank cart is a critical revenue blocker that offsets any progress.

---

## 3. What's Working

**Men's navigation menu is genuinely good.** When I opened the MENU and tapped MEN, I got a clean full-screen list with large, finger-friendly text: Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections — all with generous tap targets. A 62-year-old doesn't have to squint or mis-tap. "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Extra Wide Fit" were right there at the bottom of that drawer — that's exactly what I'm looking for, no hunting required.

**Product page communicates the deal clearly.** $110.00, 4.5 stars from 215 reviews, "Buy 1 Get 1 50% OFF Applied at Cart," and the Arch Fit Glide-Step Pro name all landed on one screen. The "Hands-Free Slip-ins" badge was prominent. I knew what I was getting and what it would cost.

**Size selection UI is well-built.** The size grid with individual boxes is readable, the width choice (MEDIUM / WIDE) is front and center, and the red "Please select a width / Please select a size" validation messages are clear. The ADD TO CART button at the bottom is full-width, blue, and impossible to miss — best-designed single element on the whole site.

**BOGO deal is persistent and visible.** The blue top banner followed me through every page. That kind of deal consistency builds trust with me — the email said BOGO and the website says BOGO.

---

## 4. What's Weak

**The cart is a blank white page.** Step 8 rendered nothing — completely empty. This isn't a minor glitch. If I tapped "Add to Cart" and then checked my cart and saw nothing, I would assume the item didn't save, panic about being charged, and close the browser. Cart counter still showed 0 on the add-to-cart screen too, suggesting the add may not have registered at all.

**Login experience is invisible.** Steps 1, 2, and 3 all look essentially identical — same hero image, same nav. There was no login form, no confirmation, no "Welcome back, Walker." I can't tell if I ever successfully logged in. If I'm a returning loyalty member and the site can't even show me I'm recognized, the loyalty program is dead to me.

**Search ignores my gender.** I searched "comfort shoes" and got 1,450 results — but the two visible products were both labeled Women's. I'm logged in (presumably). The site should know I'm a man. Serving me women's shoes first makes me feel like the site doesn't know me at all.

**Homepage feels like it's not for me.** The hero shows a young man and a young woman in a patriotic lifestyle shoot. Fine for brand identity, but there's no signal that this is the site for a 62-year-old who needs arch support and doesn't want to bend over to put shoes on. No "Comfort for Every Day" or "Arch Fit" callout above the fold.

**The horizontal nav bar on the homepage cuts off.** "Slip-ins, Sandals, Arch Fit, BOBs, G..." — that G is cut off. I'd have to swipe to see it, and I might not realize it's scrollable. Information hiding on mobile is a problem for older users who don't automatically swipe horizontally.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** A blank cart is a P0 bug. Whatever is breaking the render — script error, hydration failure, auth state mismatch — needs same-day diagnosis. This alone could be costing meaningful daily revenue.

2. **Add a logged-in confirmation.** Show "Hi Walker" or "My Account" with my name after login. Even a small personalization signal at the top builds trust.

3. **Apply gender filter to "comfort shoes" search when user is logged in.** If you know I'm a man, default to Men's results. Let me remove that filter if I want — don't make me add it.

4. **Add a hero callout for comfort-seekers above the fold.** Something like "Arch Fit + Hands Free Slip-ins for Men" in the top scroll. The product exists. Surface it where I land.

5. **Make the horizontal nav bar scrollability obvious.** Add a faded gradient on the right edge so I know more items are hiding. Or convert to a full-width stacked layout.

But I can't buy anything if the cart is blank, and I don't trust I'm logged in because the site never told me. Until both ends of the funnel work, the navigation polish doesn't matter. **4/10 — flat versus yesterday.** Fix the cart and this gets to a 6 overnight.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

8-step mobile journey audited across skechers.com (Salesforce Commerce Cloud / SFCC). Key findings: the View Cart page renders without a `<title>` or `lang` attribute — likely a rendering failure rather than a template omission. Bot-protection fingerprinting (Kasada KPSDK) is self-rate-limiting (429s on its own `/fp` endpoint), which will degrade bot-detection accuracy. CORS misconfiguration blocks a third-party styling dependency. LCP and CLS data is absent across all steps, leaving the most regression-prone Web Vitals unmeasured.

## SEO

- **View Cart — missing `<title>`**: While `/cart/` is typically `noindex`, a missing title is an indicator of a broken render that Googlebot may log and could affect crawl budget scoring.
- **Duplicate `<main>` on Homepage**: Google's structured content parser may produce inconsistent page segmentation.
- **Protocol-relative URL**: `//se.monetate.net/js/2/...` loads without explicit `https://`, which is fragile in mixed-content enforcement contexts and will generate warnings in strict CSP environments.
- LCP/CLS values were not captured — these are the Core Web Vitals that directly affect the Google Page Experience ranking signal. Instrumentation gap should be closed.

- **View Cart TTFB spike (121ms)**: The 429 on `https://www.skechers.com/cart/` is the likely cause — the server is returning a throttle response, which may force a retry or fallback path before serving HTML.
- **Third-party script payload**: The homepage `<head>` loads 20+ third-party scripts (GTM, TikTok Pixel ×2, Pinterest, Bing, Yahoo ×2, Reddit, Quantum Metric, Impact, CreativeCDN, Bloomreach, Monetate ×2, Snapchat, Facebook, NextDoor, Taboola, PulseInsights, Tune/go2sdk, WebFontLoader). Even with `async`, this saturates the HTTP/2 connection and delays the parser.
- **Duplicate script**: `https://tags.creativecdn.com/dhGobFICKSkk6J9nvQPw.js` is injected twice on the Men category page — once in the static template, once by GTM or Stape (`stapecdn.com/dtag`). This results in duplicate pixel fires.
- **WebFontLoader via cdnjs**: `cdnjs.cloudflare.com/ajax/libs/webfont/1.6.28/webfontloader.js` — v1.6.28 is the last release (2017). Loading it from an external CDN adds a DNS + TLS round-trip. Should be self-hosted or replaced with `<link rel="preload">` + `font-display: swap`.
- **LCP/CLS not instrumented**: Neither metric was captured for any step. Without these, Core Web Vitals compliance cannot be verified.

## Mobile Optimization

- **`--vh` CSS variable**: `style="--vh: 6.640000000000001px"` is set inline by JavaScript as a Safari 100vh workaround. The floating-point value (`6.640000000000001`) suggests this was captured at a very narrow viewport. The pattern itself is standard but the precision artifact (`...0000001`) indicates it's computed as `window.innerHeight / 100` without rounding — `Math.round()` or `toFixed(2)` should be applied to avoid style invalidation noise on resize.
- **`vice-managed-src`**: Several scripts use this attribute instead of `src`, indicating a consent management platform (likely TrustArc, visible in the injected CSS) is controlling load. This is correct behavior for GDPR/CCPA compliance, but the attribute is non-standard and will be ignored by browsers if the CMP script fails to load — leaving those pixels unfired silently.
- **Viewport-relative layout**: No `<meta name="viewport">` visible in the truncated HTML samples, though it may appear further in the `<head>`. Worth confirming it's present and set to `width=device-width, initial-scale=1`.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 — Kasada KPSDK fingerprinting (`/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.416`):**
The UUID path (`149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3`) is the Kasada bot-protection SDK's fingerprint submission endpoint. Receiving 429 on its own bot-detection endpoint means the protection layer is self-throttling the SDK, which degrades bot signal quality. This typically occurs when the SDK version (`j-1.2.416`) is outdated and the server-side expects a newer challenge format. Kasada should be notified to resync the client SDK version.

**429 — `/cart/`:**
The cart endpoint itself is rate-limited, which explains the View Cart TTFB spike and the structural accessibility failures (page rendered before cart data loaded). This is not a synthetic test artifact — it will affect real mobile users under concurrent load.

**CORS — `stylitics.com/style.css`:**
```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?...' from origin 
'https://www.skechers.com' has been blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```
Stylitics (outfit/styling widget vendor) is serving a stylesheet without `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com`. The CSS fails to load, breaking whatever Stylitics widget depends on it. This is a vendor-side misconfiguration; Stylitics must add the CORS header for `skechers.com` on that asset.
