# Celebrate 250 Years with 2.50X Points
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- Well, here we go again — same red, white, and blue parade from Shoe Carnival, two days after the MEGA SALE email landed. They've gotten creative, I'll grant that: 2.50X Points for "250 Years." That's a cute play on numbers, and it got me to look twice. But the first thing I actually saw before I even tapped was that same broken mess of a URL in the preview line — I've seen that exact same junk from Shoe Carnival on June 24th. They still haven't fixed it. At this point it feels careless.
- Once I'm inside the email, the first big section I see is Women's & Girls' styles — 50% and 60% off, with a young woman front and center. I'm a 62-year-old man who came here looking for something comfortable to put on my feet. I had to scroll past that before I found any men's athletic prices. The in-store-only restriction on the Mega Sale doesn't help me either; I'm browsing on my phone from my recliner.
- I'd mark this as read and maybe circle back next time I'm near a Shoe Carnival. The $10 online code is sitting there quietly at the top, but it doesn't tell me what shoes it applies to. For me, that's not enough to pull the trigger from the couch. Not clicking, not forwarding.

## What's working

- **The 2.50X Points hook is clever** — tying the loyalty multiplier to the "250 years" milestone is the kind of thing I notice, even if it takes a beat to land.
- **$10 off with a visible code (SUMMER10)** is front-and-center above the fold — no hunting for it.
- **Layout is clean and bold** on mobile — text is big enough that I don't have to pinch and zoom, which matters more than most email marketers realize.
- **The 250x Points section at the bottom has a barcode**, which tells me it's an in-store loyalty play — at least it's self-contained and easy to use if I'm at the register.
- **Men's athletic pricing ($39.98 / $59.98)** does appear, with a male model — I see something for me, even if it's buried.

## What's weak

- **Preview text is a raw tracking URL — again.** Shoe Carnival had this exact same problem on June 24th. Seeing `https://click.email.shoecarnival.com/?qs=ABB7...` in my inbox makes me wonder what else they're not paying attention to.
- **Women's & Girls' styles lead the body.** That section eats the most real estate in the email. I'm not the target of that offer, and it took me two scrolls to find anything for me.
- **In-Store Only for the Mega Sale** is a wall for anyone shopping on their phone. If I can't tap and buy, that 50%/60% offer is effectively not for me today.
- **Zero comfort language.** No cushioning, no arch support, no "fits wide feet" — nothing I actually look for when I'm deciding to try a new shoe.
- **This is the 7th or 8th Shoe Carnival email in about eight days.** Patriotic theme fatigue is real. I liked the July 1st MEGA SALE; by today it's starting to feel like they're just dressing up the same party in a new outfit.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text.** This is a repeat failure from June 24th. A raw tracking URL in the inbox preview makes the brand look sloppy and costs opens every single time it goes out. This is the single highest-impact fix.
- 2. **Lead with men's offers OR segment the send.** If you know I'm a 62-year-old male, put the men's athletic section first. The women's hero is appropriate for a women's send — not for me.
- 3. **Clarify online vs. in-store upfront.** A small "Online: $10 off | In-Store: Mega Sale + 250x Points" split above the hero would help me immediately understand which offer applies to how I shop right now.
- 4. **Add one comfort signal.** "Extra cushioning" or "arch support styles included" in the athletic section would stop my scroll fast.
- 5. **Give the 250x Points a deadline.** "Earn 250x points through July 4th" creates urgency that the current copy lacks.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$10 off + 250x Points — July 4th weekend only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 250th birthday gift: 2.50X Points in-store`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use SUMMER10 for $10 off online. Earn 250x points in-store through July 4th.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop men's & women's styles — extra points at the register this weekend only.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed `<meta>` for charset:**
```html
<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />
```
The `content` value is truncated (missing `; charset=utf-8`). Correct HTML 4.01 form: `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />`. The `charset` attribute is HTML5-only and conflicts with the declared HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype.

**Malformed IE compatibility meta:**
```html
<meta content="IE=edge" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`. Should be `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />`. Without `http-equiv`, this tag is inert.

**Four separate `<style>` blocks in `<head>`:**
Gmail (web), Yahoo Mail, and some older Outlook builds strip all but the first or merge inconsistently. All media queries and base styles should be consolidated into a single `<style>` block.

**No `<title>` element visible** in the `<head>`. Some clients display the title as a preview label in certain views; its absence is a minor gap.

**Dark mode explicitly disabled:**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light" />
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light" />
```
This is a deliberate choice, but it means the email renders with a white background in dark-mode clients (Outlook, Apple Mail) rather than adapting. If any images have white backgrounds assumed to be transparent, this will be visible as white boxes against the client's dark UI chrome.

**Redundant CSS declaration:**
```css
a, a:link, ... { color:inherit !important; color:inherit; }
```
`color:inherit` is declared twice in the same rule. The second declaration (without `!important`) is always overridden by the first. Minor but signals copy-paste artifact.

**Tel/SMS links disabled:**
```css
a[href^="tel"], a[href^="sms"] { pointer-events:none; cursor:default !important; }
```
This intentionally breaks tap-to-call on mobile. Confirm this is deliberate (e.g., no customer service number should be tappable).
