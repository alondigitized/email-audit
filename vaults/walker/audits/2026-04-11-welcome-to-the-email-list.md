# Welcome to the (email) list 🎉
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-11**
## Executive summary

- A clean, functional welcome email that delivers the promised 10% discount immediately and efficiently. The hero is clear, the VIP upsell is well-placed, and the email closes with practical utility. It doesn't overstay its welcome. The main weaknesses are a cramped footer that buries important content and a product discovery section that feels like a placeholder rather than a genuine recommendation engine.
- Solid execution of a high-value moment. The discount CTA is prominent, VIP cross-sell is well-timed, and the structure is logical. Held back by weak product imagery and a footer that undermines trust.

## What's working

- **Hero clarity.** "10% Off" lands immediately — large type, clear discount code callout, and a prominent "Shop Now" CTA. No ambiguity about what was promised or how to redeem it.
- **VIP upsell timing.** The "Being Shoe Obsessed Has Its Perks" module arrives right after the primary offer, before the email loses momentum. The benefits (5% back, free shipping, free returns) are legible and specific.
- **Category nav.** Shop Women / Men / Kids / All Brands gives the reader a frictionless escape to browse if the 10% CTA doesn't land.
- **Shipping reassurance.** The "Shopping Made Easy" icon row (free shipping, in-store pickup, easy returns, app) closes the email on a practical, trust-building note.

## What's weak

- **Hero imagery.** The product shot is small and stylistically diffuse — a sandal at bottom left, a lifestyle figure mid-frame, neither commanding. For a shoe brand, this is a missed chance to make product desire the first emotion.
- **"Just Dropped" module.** The two tiles labeled "New Arrivals" and "Clearance" are generic category links dressed up as a discovery feature. No imagery, no product specificity, no reason to click beyond the label.
- **Footer.** Dense, small-text block covering legal, preferences, and unsubscribe. The unsubscribe link is not visually distinct — it's buried in a wall of gray text, which can feel evasive and damages trust with new subscribers.
- **No personalization signal.** Nothing in this email acknowledges the subscriber as new. No "glad you're here" framing beyond the subject line, no guidance on what DSW is for someone who just signed up.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Upgrade the hero image.** Use a tight, high-contrast hero shot of one or two standout shoes — make the product the hero, not a lifestyle collage.
- 2. **Make "Just Dropped" earn its slot.** Replace the two generic tiles with actual product thumbnails (3–4 items), price, and a direct "Shop Now" link per item. If that's not possible, cut the module.
- 3. **Add a one-line welcome statement.** A single sentence above the offer ("You're in — and here's what that means") orients the new subscriber and reduces the cold, transactional feel.
- 4. **Surface the unsubscribe link.** Make it visually legible in the footer — a small but readable standalone link builds more trust than burying it. New subscribers who feel trapped unsubscribe faster.
- 5. **VIP CTA hierarchy.** "Join for Free" and "Learn More" side by side creates choice paralysis. Pick one primary action and demote the other.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<meta>` charset missing `http-equiv`**
```html
<!-- Current (broken) -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<!-- Correct -->
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv="Content-Type"`, Outlook and older clients may not honor the charset declaration.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta — WCAG 1.4.4 violation**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents user-initiated zoom. Remove `maximum-scale=1` or set to `5`.

**`text-size-adjust: none` in reset**
```css
* { -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%; -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%;
    -ms-text-size-adjust: none; text-size-adjust: none }
```
`-ms-text-size-adjust` is declared twice — first `100%`, then overridden with `none`. `text-size-adjust: none` disables browser text scaling, which is an accessibility regression. Both should be `100%`.

**Capitalized `Src:` in `@font-face` blocks**
```css
@font-face {
  font-family: 'Futura_PT_Regular';
  Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');
}
```
CSS property names are technically case-insensitive per spec, but `Src:` (capital S) is non-standard and may be skipped by strict parsers in some webmail clients. Should be lowercase `src:`.

**WOFF-only font format — no WOFF2**
All four `@font-face` declarations reference `.woff` only. WOFF2 offers ~30% better compression and is supported by every modern client that supports web fonts in email. Missing WOFF2 means larger payloads where fonts do load.
