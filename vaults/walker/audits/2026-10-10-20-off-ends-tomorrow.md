# 20% off ends tomorrow 🚨
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-10**
## Executive summary

- DSW again, and it's the seventh send on the same HAYRIDE 20% code since "20% OFF?! You better be-leaf it" on the 8th. The siren in "ends tomorrow" is the first real deadline I've seen from them, so I'll give them that. But I've had "20% off" from them all week, and I stopped believing the clock a few emails ago.
- The green banner is easy on my eyes, and "CLAIM 20% OFF" is a button I can hit with my thumb. Then I scroll into 15 shoes, and almost every one is a women's bootie, mule or heeled clog. There are two sneakers that might pass for men's. Nothing says cushioned, slip-on or arch support. Like the booties email earlier today, this one isn't talking to my feet.
- Would I click? Probably not. I'd skim it and mark it read. I'd only tap "Shop Men" if I were already looking for shoes. I wouldn't forward it. My wife might like the booties, but I wouldn't bother her with the seventh copy of the same email.

## What's working

- The green 20% OFF banner is huge, and I can read it without zooming on my phone.
- "CLAIM 20% OFF" is a clear black button, with the HAYRIDE code right in the copy.
- "Ends tomorrow" matches the fine print (Oct 8–11), so the deadline is honest.
- "Shop Wide Width" is a real help for a guy whose feet have widened over the years.
- The Shop by Size block is big and tappable, with large numbers.
- The fine print says Skechers is among the qualifying brands, and that's one of mine.

## What's weak

- The product grid is almost all women's styles, so nothing in it is for me.
- Nothing is about comfort, cushioning, arch support or slip-ons. It's all "brand new... and so you," which is style talk.
- The sixth or seventh email with the same code in a week. The urgency feels manufactured.
- Skechers qualifying is buried in tiny gray fine print, where it could have been a selling point.
- The Adidas "up to 30% off" block has no shoe shown, only the logo. "Select styles" tells me nothing.
- The "Shop Men" link is a small text link under the banner, much smaller than the buttons around it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment by gender. Send me a men's comfort and walking grid (Skechers, Hoka, Brooks, New Balance) and not the women's booties.
- 2. Cut the cadence. One reminder on the last day is plenty, and this one would hit harder if it came after a quiet stretch.
- 3. Put "Shop Men" in a real button the same size as "Shop Women," so I'm not hunting for it.
- 4. Name the brands that qualify (Skechers, New Balance, Clarks) in the body instead of the fine print.
- 5. Show an actual Adidas shoe with a price, plus a line like "was $89, now $62." I like doing that math.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
One probed CTA link ("Opt-In to Texts Get 15% Off") returns HTTP 400, and the plain-text part is about 86% tracking URLs. The HTML source was truncated, so findings are limited to the visible `<head>` and the QA output.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Broken CTA (confirmed by QA):** "Opt-In to Texts Get 15% Off" returns **400** at `https://ablink.dsw.com/f/a/2eNpeodCyuUn6FQ7lZSAuQ~~/AAQRxRA~/Dkagl2X8Eh6m3if4X72NzftN1UAa21sw…`.
  - The QA output reports an empty link text for the failing entry (`Broken link (400):  (link text: ...)`).
  - A 400 from a click-tracking redirector can mean a malformed or truncated payload, or it can be the redirector rejecting the probe's request.
  - The QA data can't distinguish the two, so re-test it with a real browser User-Agent before treating it as a send-blocking bug.
- **Tracking URL length:** the `ablink.dsw.com` URLs run to roughly 600+ characters of opaque payload each.
  - Plain-text is 30,426 of 35,302 characters URLs (86%, against a 70% threshold).
  - This is a deliverability risk (spam-filter heuristics, plain-text readability) and is the likely source of the QA warning.
- **Probe coverage:** only 12 of 78 links were probed (66 skipped). The remaining links are unverified.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `lang`:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` attribute, so screen readers can't pick a language.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4).
- **Ineffective meta tags:** `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` and `<meta content="IE=Edge" />` have no `http-equiv` attribute, so neither does anything. Charset then depends on the MIME `Content-Type` header.
- **Missing alt text (QA-confirmed, 2 images):**
  - A tracked-wrapper image at `https://ablink.dsw.com/q/E9_KSuNBLk0aFFeA9lC1VQ~~/…`. If this is a content image, it needs descriptive alt text.
  - The `o.gif` open pixel at `https://mi.dsw.com/p/up/…/o.gif`. This one should carry `alt=""`.
- **Fonts:** custom web fonts are loaded from `braze-images.com` (`.woff` and `.ttf`) with no visible fallback stack in the truncated source. The CSS shown includes `Helvetica, Arial` only for `sup`. Fallbacks can't be confirmed for the rest.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (`{{ }}`, `%%`, `[FIRST_NAME]`) appear in the visible source or QA output. The `mi_u=225090073` parameter on the pixel URL is a populated per-recipient ID, not a leaked token.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
No issues found in the data provided.
- QA always probes the unsubscribe link, and it did not appear in the failure list.
- `List-Unsubscribe`, SPF, DKIM and DMARC results were not included, so they are unverified rather than passing.
- The footer and physical address are beyond the truncation point.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
UTM parameters can't be verified. Every probed link is wrapped in an `ablink.dsw.com` redirector, and the final destination URLs weren't captured. Landing-page alignment for the "20% off" offer, and the SMS opt-in's "15% off" destination, can't be confirmed from this data.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Re-test the SMS opt-in link outside the probe, and confirm it resolves to a working landing page. Fix the tracking payload if it is truncated.
2. Add `lang="en"` to `<html>`.
3. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
4. Add `http-equiv="Content-Type"` and `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"` to the two meta tags, or remove them.
5. Add `alt=""` to the tracking pixel and meaningful alt text to the wrapped content image.
6. Shorten the plain-text part (fewer links, or shorter tracking domains/paths) to get below the URL-ratio threshold.
7. Extend QA to capture the post-redirect final URLs (to check UTMs) and the raw headers (`List-Unsubscribe`, `Authentication-Results`).
