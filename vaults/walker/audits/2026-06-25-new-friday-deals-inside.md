# NEW Friday Deals Inside
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- First thing I notice scrolling past the hero is that the Inflation Relief banner is gone. After weeks of that red-and-yellow block greeting me every single time I opened a Shoe Carnival email, its absence is actually the thing that caught my attention. The "Friday Deal Roundup" frame is a nice clean change of pace.
- But then I spot it: "GREAT FOR DAD!" stamped on the Brooks section, the Nike section, the athletic section. Father's Day was four days ago. That badge is stale and it shows — it's the kind of slip that makes me feel like I'm getting a recycled batch email, not something put together for this week. The Brooks Ghost 17 deal is genuinely interesting to me — that's a real walking shoe at a real price — but by the time I get past the three "Dad" sections I'm already scrolling through a Women's Sandal Blowout and a Kids' Sandal Sale that have nothing to do with me.
- The $10/$15 off coupon at the bottom with the SAVEMORE code is legitimately useful. Clear, stackable-feeling, usable in-store. But I had to scroll through a lot of women's and kids' content to find it. I'd mark this as read, not forward it, and maybe click the Brooks link if my feet were talking to me that day.

## What's working

- **Brooks Ghost 17 & Revel 8 at $30 off** is the right lead for someone like me — specific shoe names, specific dollar savings, none of the vague "up to X%" hedging.
- **"Friday Deal Roundup" framing** gives the email a believable time peg. Friday feels like a real event, not an invented sale.
- **Coupon block is clean and readable** — $10 off $74.98+, $15 off $92.98+, online code SAVEMORE. Simple math I can do in my head. Works in-store too.
- **No Inflation Relief banner.** After a solid month of that block, its absence actually refreshed the email for me.
- **SHOE PERKS callout at the very top** — I noticed it. Good placement for a loyalty reminder.

## What's weak

- **"GREAT FOR DAD!" badges on June 25 are stale.** Father's Day was June 21. Leaving those tags on is sloppy and makes me feel like I'm reading last week's email.
- **Bottom half of the email is not for me at all.** Women's Sandal Blowout, Women's BOGO, Kids' Sale — three sections in a row I have to scroll past. That's a lot of irrelevant content between me and the coupon.
- **Preview text is a raw tracking URL.** Whatever should have been there didn't load — just a long `click.email.shoecarnival.com` string. That's the first thing I see in my inbox after the subject line, and it looks broken.
- **Subject "NEW Friday Deals Inside" is generic.** No brand, no offer amount, no category. I'd open this because I know Shoe Carnival by now, not because the subject gave me a reason.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pull those "GREAT FOR DAD!" badges immediately.** Swap in something timely — "Summer Sale," "Weekend Only," nothing, even. Stale seasonal copy erodes trust faster than any layout problem.
- 2. **Fix the preview text.** Whatever is falling through to the tracking URL needs to be replaced with a real line. Even "Brooks Ghost 17: $30 off this Friday only" would do the job.
- 3. **Move the coupon block higher.** If I'm the target, the $10/$15 off code is more compelling than the kids' sandal section. Either cut the women's/kids' sections for this send or let gender segmentation do the work and trim it to men's content only.
- 4. **Add one fit or comfort callout to the Brooks block.** Even "wide widths available" or "extra cushioning" gives me a reason to click beyond price alone.

### 1. Technical Summary

Two critical infrastructure defects were confirmed: a zero-width space character (U+200B) is embedded in all five primary tracking URLs, corrupting every click and open event, and an unfilled merge token (`[UNIQUE]`) was sent verbatim in a second tracking domain's redirect URL. The remainder are accessibility gaps.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**CRITICAL — Zero-width space in tracking URLs (5 links)**

All five `ieezzy5m.emltrk.com` URLs contain a U+200B character appended after the campaign value:

```
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?rd&i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260612_friday_roundup_AM​
                                                                                                    ^
                                                                                                 U+200B here
```

This appears at positions 76–78 depending on the URL variant (`rd`, `rl`, `p`, `f`, and the open-pixel). The ASCII codec failure in QA confirms these URLs cannot be percent-encoded or resolved as-is. Likely cause: copy-paste from a rich-text source (Google Docs, Notion, etc.) that silently inserted a ZWSP. All click-tracking and open-tracking for this send is compromised.

**CRITICAL — Unfilled merge token sent to subscribers**

```
https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?rd&d=[UNIQUE]
```

`[UNIQUE]` is a literal string — the ESP substitution did not fire. This URL also timed out during probing, meaning the redirect endpoint is either down or refusing requests with the literal bracket token. Any clicks routed through this domain are dead.
