# Adistar XLG 2.0 changes the game
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- Well, I'll say this much — adidas finally sent me a shoe I might actually wear. The Adistar XLG 2.0 looks like a walking-capable sneaker, not a basketball high-top or a soccer jersey, which already puts it ahead of the last three emails I've gotten from these folks. The word "cushioning" is in the copy, and that word catches my eye every time.
- But then I read the rest of it. "'00s running aesthetic." "Enlarged molded cage." "Adds a modern edge to your everyday look." That's not how I shop for shoes. I don't care about a molded cage or whether my sneakers have a modern edge. I care whether my feet hurt at the end of a long day at the hardware store or a walk around the neighborhood. This email is dressed up for a 28-year-old who pins streetwear fits on Pinterest — not for me.
- The Strava partnership section below the shoe is the final straw. I don't use Strava. I don't track my workouts with an app. I'm not trying to "reach my goals" via a fitness subscription. That entire second block is dead real estate. I'd read this, note the shoe exists, and move on without clicking. Would not forward to a friend — nothing actionable in here for the guys I know.

## What's working

- The Adiprene cushioning callout is the one line of body copy I actually care about — cushioning is why I keep reading comfort shoe emails
- Adidas logo is immediately recognizable; I know who sent this without having to think
- The hero image fills the frame cleanly — no broken images, no overlapping text, loads fast
- Layout is simple and large enough to read on my phone without pinching the screen
- "Explore the latest adidas best sellers / Shop Now" banner at top is a clean, visible entry point if I want to browse

## What's weak

- Not a single price anywhere — I can't do any price-vs-quality math without clicking, and that friction is enough for me to not bother
- The model in the hero reads as a younger woman in streetwear; nothing in this image says "this shoe was built for someone your age, with your feet"
- "Changes the game" in the subject is empty sports hype — tells me nothing about why I'd want this shoe
- The Strava X AdiClub block takes up roughly a third of the email and means absolutely nothing to me
- No fit details — width options, arch support language, removable insole? The one thing that would seal the deal for me isn't there

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the comfort story, not the fashion story.** Rewrite the hero copy to put cushioning and support first: "All-day cushioning in a shoe you'll actually want to wear" beats "adds a modern edge to your everyday look" for someone like me.
- 2. **Put a price in the email.** Even the MSRP. I need to know if this is a $90 shoe or a $160 shoe before I'll click anything.
- 3. **Kill or replace the Strava block.** For older subscribers who aren't Strava users, swap it for a fit/sizing confidence message — "Available in wide widths," "Lightweight at X oz," something I care about.
- 4. **Match the model to the audience.** A guy my age walking a trail or a parking lot in these shoes would do more work for me than a fashion-forward streetwear shot.
- 5. **Add a product-level CTA button** in the body, not just the banner — "Shop the Adistar XLG 2.0" right below the product copy.

## Technical Audit — Adidas "Adistar XLG 2.0" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard failures: a broken redirect URL and a zero-character plain-text fallback. Supporting issues include 8 HTTP image sources and 2 images with missing alt text that will degrade rendering across security-conscious clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect — unknown URL type**
The QA probe resolved the click-tracking URL to an internal path that the redirect server doesn't handle:

- Click URL: `https://dv.adidas.com/o/509b071a-2b35-4f7d-b2e4-c659f885556f?cp_tp=v3.eJzjYuII...`
- Resolved path: `/g/509b071a-2b35-4f7d-b2e4-c659f885556f?txn=ebd3c680-...`
- Error: `unknown url type: '/g/...'`

The `/g/` path prefix is not a registered route on `dv.adidas.com`. Any recipient clicking this link will hit a dead end. The corresponding campaign or product URL was likely misconfigured in the ESP or the destination was unpublished.

**[WARN] 36 tracking links not verified**
All click-redirect links through `click.comms.adidas.com` and `dv.adidas.com` were skipped during probing. Destination URLs and UTM integrity cannot be confirmed without resolving them.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] HTTP image sources — 8 occurrences of the same asset**
The arrow icon is loaded 8 times over plain HTTP:
```
src: http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all block mixed-content HTTP images by default. This asset will render as a broken image on all major clients. The same image is referenced 8 times — a single CDN URL fix resolves all instances.

**[WARN] Tracking pixel served over HTTP**
```
src: http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019ea0467911-53faf430-83b0-4500-bb6b-808503de65c9-000000/...
```
The open-tracking pixel is HTTP. Open tracking will fail silently on clients that block mixed content; this will undercount opens.

**[WARN] Two images missing `alt` text**

| Image | src fragment |
|---|---|
| Inline image (product?) | `https://dv.adidas.com/o/509b071a-2b35-4f7d-b2e4-c659f885556f?cp_tp=v3...` |
| Tracking pixel | `http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019ea0467911-53faf430...` |

Missing `alt` on the first image fails WCAG 1.1.1 (non-text content) and renders as a broken image icon when images are off. The tracking pixel should carry `alt=""` (explicit empty string) to signal decorative intent to screen readers.

**[NOTE] Malformed charset meta tag**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Most clients tolerate this but it is invalid and can cause encoding fallback in older Outlook versions.

**[NOTE] Duplicate `@font-face` blocks**
`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are declared twice across two separate `<style>` blocks with slightly different fallback lists (`Calibri, Sans Serif` in the first block; omitted in the second). Not a rendering failure but adds unnecessary weight and creates a maintenance inconsistency.
