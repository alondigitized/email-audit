# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-08-15**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I came in wanting a slip-on, cushioned walking shoe I could trust without a lot of fuss. The homepage looks fine at a glance — big shoe photo, a slip-ins callout — but almost nothing else worked the way I'd expect. The account/login step never actually showed me anything different, the shopping cart page came back as a **blank white screen** after I tried to add the Contour Foam Slip-ins to my cart, and when I searched "comfort shoes" it handed me women's shoes first even though I'd just come from browsing men's. For a 62-year-old who wants to get in, find a shoe that fits, and buy it, this journey stalls out right at the moment that matters most — checkout.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10** — unchanged from yesterday. The homepage and product page are readable and not bad for a phone, but a blank cart page is a hard stop for a purchase, and that's not a "nice to have" fix, that's the whole transaction.

## 3. What's Working

- Homepage hero image is big, the shoe is clearly visible, and "Up to 40% OFF Summer Styles" and "25% off Apparel & Bags" banners are easy to read without zooming.
- The hamburger MEN menu opens cleanly with big, tappable rows (Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections) — good touch target size, no fumbling.
- Men's Shoes category page leads with useful groupings right up top: "Hands Free Slip-Ins," "Athletic Sneakers," "Slip-On" — that's exactly the kind of comfort-first sorting I want.
- The product page for the Slip-ins Contour Foam is clean: price, star rating (97 reviews), and a large product photo. "Hands Free Slip-ins" branding badge is right there, which speaks to me directly.
- Size and width selector on the PDP has decently sized tap targets, and it's smart that it separates Medium/Wide up front — my feet need the wide option and I don't want to hunt for it.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart page is a blank white screen.** I tapped Add to Cart and went to View Cart, and got nothing — no items, no "your cart is empty" message, nothing. That's a dead end. If this happened on my actual phone I'd assume the site was broken and I'd leave.
- **Add to Cart didn't actually complete** — the size and width fields showed red "Please select a width" / "Please select a size" warnings, meaning the shoe never went in the cart in the first place. There's no visual nudge earlier on the page telling me I need to pick these before the button will work — I'd tap Add to Cart, get an error, and not know why until I scroll back up.
- **Login did nothing I could see.** The screenshots for Homepage, Dismiss Popups, and Log In are all identical. No popup ever appeared to dismiss, and no login form or account screen showed up. If I'm a returning customer, I have no way to tell if I'm signed in.
- **No personalization anywhere.** Cart badge stayed at 0 the whole time, account icon never changed from the generic silhouette. Nothing suggested "welcome back" or showed me anything tailored to me.
- **Search results skew wrong gender.** I searched "comfort shoes" right after browsing Men's Shoes, and both top results were labeled "Women's." That's a basic relevance miss and makes the search feel disconnected from where I just was on the site.
- No visible price-vs-quality comparison content anywhere in this path — no "compare to Hoka/Brooks" messaging, no cushioning/arch-support explainer beyond a small badge graphic.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the blank cart page immediately** — this is the single biggest leak in the funnel. Even an empty-cart state with a "your cart is empty, continue shopping" message would beat a blank white screen.
2. **Surface the width/size requirement before the Add to Cart tap**, not just as a red error after the fact — e.g., disable the button and grey-tag "Select width & size" so I know why nothing happened.
3. **Make login state visible** — swap the account icon or add a "Hi, Walker" once signed in, so returning shoppers get confirmation and some personalization (order history, saved size).
4. **Fix search relevance to respect gender context** — if I'm browsing Men's and then search, weight men's results higher, or at minimum let me filter by gender right at the top of results.
5. **Add a simple comfort-brand comparison or "why Skechers" module** near the PDP — cushioning, arch support, and how it stacks up to Hoka/Brooks/New Balance, since that's exactly the mental math I'm doing before I buy.

## 6. Bottom Line

The site looks the part on the surface — big photos, readable text, a decent menu — but it falls apart exactly where it counts: I couldn't reliably get a shoe into a cart I could see, I never felt recognized as a logged-in shopper, and search sent me to the wrong department. Score holds flat at 3/10 versus yesterday; nothing here has meaningfully improved and the blank cart page is a serious, purchase-blocking regression risk that needs to be top of the list.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary
Mobile journey through skechers.com (Homepage → Log In → Men → Men>Shoes → PDP → Add to Cart → View Cart → Search) surfaced 16 axe violations, 25 console errors, and 7 network-level failures (six 429s, one 403). The site loads a large third-party tag surface (20+ async scripts per page: TikTok, Snapchat, Reddit, Meta, Bing, two Google Ads destinations, GTM, two Monetate loaders, Quantum Metric, Impact, CreativeCDN, Taboola, PulseInsights, a stape.io server-side tag, WebFontLoader) on every page view, including the cart. TTFB is healthy on PDP/cart/search endpoints (23–129ms) but elevated on Homepage/Log In/Men (374ms) — notably, all three share the identical URL `https://www.skechers.com/` in the captured HTML, suggesting client-side state changes were captured rather than distinct server navigations; this should be verified before treating 374ms as a true document TTFB regression.

## Accessibility
- **Critical:** `button-name` — buttons without discernible text on Homepage. Screen-reader/switch users cannot identify these controls.
- **Critical:** `aria-required-parent` — ARIA roles used without required parent context on Homepage, likely from a component (menu/tab widget) missing its container role.
- **Serious:** `document-title` and `html-has-lang` both missing on **View Cart** — the cart document has no `<title>` and no `lang` attribute, meaning assistive tech cannot announce the page or the language. This is a stronger signal than the other findings since it implies the cart may render as a fragment/partial document rather than a full page (worth checking whether cart is served via an XHR-injected template that skips `<head>` population).
- **Moderate (repeated across Homepage, View Cart, Search):** `landmark-one-main` / `landmark-main-is-top-level` / `landmark-no-duplicate-main` / `landmark-unique` / `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` / `region` — duplicated/misplaced `main` and `complementary` landmarks and unlandmarked content. The repetition across three unrelated templates points to a shared layout component (likely the header/aside include) rather than three independent bugs.
- **Minor:** `aria-allowed-role` on Homepage and Search — role attribute used on an element that doesn't permit it.
- **Moderate:** `page-has-heading-one` on View Cart — no `<h1>`, consistent with the missing-title finding above (cart template likely omits standard document scaffolding).

## SEO
- `html-has-lang` missing on View Cart is an SEO signal in addition to an a11y one (language targeting), though cart pages are typically `noindex` so impact is likely low.
- `document-title` missing on View Cart would matter if this route is ever crawled/shared (social/link previews use `<title>` as fallback).
- No canonical, meta-robots, or structured-data issues can be assessed from the truncated `<head>` samples provided — flagging as a data gap, not a clean bill of health.

## Performance
- TTFB: 374ms (Homepage/Log In/Men — same captured URL, see caveat above), 23ms (Men>Shoes), 32ms (PDP, Add to Cart), 129ms (View Cart), 24ms (Search). No values indicate a server-side bottleneck on their own.
- LCP and CLS were **not captured** (`?`) for any of the 8 steps — this is an instrumentation gap in the audit run itself, not a site finding. Re-run with a Performance/Web Vitals observer attached before drawing conclusions on paint or layout stability.
- Third-party script load is heavy and largely render-blocking-adjacent: each page fires 15–20+ async trackers/pixels before or alongside the main DemandWare bundle (`us-skechers-prod.js`). This doesn't show up in TTFB but will affect LCP/TTI/INP, which is exactly the data currently missing above.

## Mobile Optimization
- The homepage sets `style="--vh: 6.64px"` via JS on `<html>` — a manual `100vh`-fix pattern (JS listener recalculating viewport height). This is a known workaround for mobile browser chrome resizing, but it's brittle vs. native `dvh`/`svh` CSS units and can cause a flash of incorrect layout before JS executes.
- Viewport meta tag and responsive breakpoints aren't visible in the truncated `<head>` samples provided — cannot confirm configuration from this data; would need the full, un-truncated head to verify.

## Console & Network Errors
- **429 (Too Many Requests) ×4** on `.../fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.661` — this is a Kasada bot-mitigation fingerprint/challenge endpoint. Repeated 429s mean the client is being rate-limited/throttled by the bot-detection layer itself, which can degrade or block legitimate automated QA traffic and, if triggered broadly, real users on flaky networks retrying requests.
- **429** on `/cart/` — the cart route itself was rate-limited during this run, which is more concerning than the fingerprint 429s since it's user-facing functionality, not telemetry.
- **403** on `BazaarvoiceProductData-GetProduct?pid=test` — the literal query value `pid=test` returned a 403. A hardcoded/leftover test product ID reaching a production ratings-and-reviews endpoint suggests a debug value wasn't parameterized correctly, or a component reverts to a placeholder ID when the real product ID isn't resolved yet (race condition on page load).
- **Permissions policy violation: "unload is not allowed"** — recurring (3+ instances), consistent with a third-party script still attaching `unload`/`beforeunload` listeners, which Chrome now restricts under its Permissions Policy. Likely from one of the many analytics/tag-manager scripts loaded (GTM, Monetate, or an ad pixel).
- **"Unsupported Summarizer API languages"** — recurring (2+ instances), from the browser's built-in `Summarizer` (window.ai) API being invoked with an unsupported locale. This is calling an experimental Chrome AI API — confirm whether this is fired by site code (unexpected on an e-commerce PDP flow) or by a browser extension/flag in the test environment before treating it as a site bug.

## Recommendations
1. Fix the `403` on Bazaarvoice `GetProduct?pid=test` — locate where the placeholder/test product ID leaks into the live request and ensure it's gated behind the real PDP's resolved SKU.
2. Investigate the `429`s on `/cart/` and the Kasada fingerprint endpoint — determine if this run's traffic pattern is being misclassified as bot traffic (headless/automation fingerprint) versus a genuine rate-limiting bug affecting real mobile users.
3. Add `<title>` and `lang` attributes and restore a single `<h1>`/`main` landmark to the View Cart template — this is the most user-impacting a11y/SEO gap found (a core conversion page with no accessible document structure).
4. Fix `button-name` and `aria-required-parent` on Homepage — both are critical-severity and block screen-reader users from core navigation.
5. Consolidate the duplicated `main`/`complementary` landmarks shared across Homepage, View Cart, and Search — trace to the common layout/header include rather than fixing each page independently.
6. Audit and remove stale `unload`/`beforeunload` listeners in third-party tags to clear the recurring Permissions Policy violations.
7. Re-run this audit with LCP/CLS capture enabled — performance conclusions can't be drawn until that instrumentation gap is closed, especially given the heavy third-party script count observed in the HTML.
8. Confirm whether the `Summarizer API` calls originate from site code; if so, remove/guard them, since they're erroring on every run.
