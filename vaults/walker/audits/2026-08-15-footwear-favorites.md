# Footwear favorites
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-15**
## Executive summary

- Another New Balance email, and I gotta say, this one's at least easier on the eyes than most of what they've been sending me. It's a "lifestyle bestsellers" roundup — six shoe styles in a clean grid, up to 30% off with a code. No running-shoe pitch this time, no kids' section, just casual sneakers. Problem is, every single model shown is a young woman — cropped shot of legs in leggings up top, then a twentysomething gal in a hoodie further down. There's nothing here signaling "this fits a 62-year-old guy's feet too," even though a couple of these styles (the 530, the 9060) are genuinely unisex and I could see myself in them.
- I'd scroll through it, maybe pause on the 530 since I've heard good things about that one for all-day comfort, but I wouldn't click. And I definitely wouldn't forward it to a friend — it reads as women's/back-to-school content that happens to include some men's-compatible shoes, not something built for me.

## What's working

- The six-shoe grid is clean and easy to scan — no clutter, clear labels (530, 9060, 204L, 574 Core, 327, BB950).
- "Up to 30% off select styles" with a real code (BTS26) is a concrete, honest offer, not vague fluff.
- No render bugs — everything loaded, nothing overlapping or broken.
- A couple of the featured styles (530, 9060) are shoes I'd actually consider for cushioning and everyday wear.

## What's weak

- Every model in this email is a young woman — the hero shot, the "complete the look" model — there's not one visual cue this applies to a guy my age.
- Zero mention of cushioning, arch support, or comfort — the copy is all "lifestyle bestsellers" and "effortless style," nothing about how these actually feel on your feet.
- That folding-chair furniture image before the 30% off callout is just confusing — what's a wooden chair got to do with sneakers? Same issue I flagged in their "up to 30% off select styles" email from earlier today.
- Nav bar up top only lists Men, Women, Kids, Sale — fine, but the whole email content skews so heavily female it makes that "Men" tab feel decorative.
- "Back to school" framing (BTS26 code, "styles for the new school year") tells me instantly this wasn't built with retirees in mind.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap in at least one shot of a man wearing the 530 or 9060 — those are genuinely cross-gender styles, and right now nothing in the visuals tells a guy like me to look twice.
- 2. Add one line of actual product substance — "cushioned midsole," "all-day support" — instead of just "customer favorites you don't want to miss."
- 3. Cut the random folding-chair image. It breaks the flow and adds nothing.
- 4. If they're going to send me New Balance emails constantly, at least segment me out of "back to school" campaigns — I don't have kids in school, and the BTS26 code framing makes the whole email feel like it's for someone else.
