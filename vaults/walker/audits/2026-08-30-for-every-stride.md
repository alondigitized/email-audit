# For every stride
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-30**
## Executive summary

- Well, this landed in my inbox again and it's kids' shoes — a little girl in a blue New Balance jersey kicking her leg up, then eight product tiles of toddler and kid sneakers, then a photo of four kids sitting on chairs in a classroom-picture setup. I don't have young kids at home. Even the preview text gives it away before I open anything: "Reliable styles shoes for active kids." Nothing here is for me, so I'm not opening this one twice.
- This is the second kids' email from New Balance this month — the "Bestselling styles for kids" one landed August 27th — and I'm starting to wonder if their list segmentation is just broken. I'm a 62-year-old guy who buys walking shoes for himself; there's no reasonable read of my purchase history that says "send him toddler sneakers."
- Straight to archived, no click, definitely not forwarding it — unless it's to my daughter, and that's more a "did you get this too" text than anything New Balance would want credit for.

## What's working

- The layout itself is clean — no broken images, no overlapping text, easy enough to scroll through even if it's not for me.
- Free shipping banner up top is legible and clear, that's a real offer if I did want something.
- The category shortcuts (babies & toddlers, little kids, big kids) are honest about who this is for, so at least I'm not confused about the targeting — just annoyed by it.

## What's weak

- This is a kids' shoe catalog, full stop — nothing about arch support, cushioning, or men's walking styles anywhere in it.
- The hero model is a little girl, the closing photo is four kids on chairs — zero demographic overlap with a 62-year-old man.
- No offer beyond generic free shipping over $75 — nothing that would pull me in even if I were shopping for grandkids.
- Eight tiny product tiles with no context on comfort or fit, just names like "Fresh Foam Arishi v4 Bungee Lace" — means nothing to me.
- Same pattern as the August 27th "Bestselling styles for kids" send — this list clearly isn't being filtered by who actually buys what.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the segmentation — if I've only ever bought men's walking or comfort shoes from New Balance, I should never see a kids'-only send. This is the most damaging issue and it's not a copy problem, it's a targeting problem.
- 2. If New Balance wants grandparents to engage with kids' content, frame it that way explicitly ("shopping for the grandkids?") rather than assuming I have young kids myself.
- 3. Lead with an actual offer or discount, not just free shipping over $75 — nothing here gives me a reason to click even if I were in-market.
- 4. Vary creative — I've now seen too many near-identical grid-of-shoes layouts from this brand in a two-week span (Aug 24, 25, 26, 27, and now this one).
