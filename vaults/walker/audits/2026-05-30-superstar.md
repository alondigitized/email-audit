# Superstar
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- I've gotten more adidas emails this spring than I've had hot breakfasts, and this one might be the most confused of the bunch. The subject line is one word — *Superstar* — which, sure, I remember those shell-toe shoes from back in the day. Run-DMC wore them. That's actually the one hook that might've gotten me to open. But once I'm inside? I'm looking at a young woman folded nearly in half doing some kind of yoga pretzel move, a heritage copy block about how this shoe "stands the test of time," and then — completely out of nowhere — a green prize box for an adiClub-meets-Amazon giveaway tied to the FIFA World Cup. That's two entirely different emails stitched together with some duct tape.
- There's no price. No discount. No "here's where to buy it" button for the Superstar itself. And the preheader at the very top reads "There's a chance it's still the Olympic Shop sale" — which has nothing to do with anything I see below it. I don't know if I'm buying a shoe, entering a sweepstakes, or just watching an adidas brand film. Not what I call a productive use of my Friday inbox.
- I won't click, I won't forward, and I'm one email closer to unsubscribing. Mark as read, move on.

## What's working

- The Superstar name itself carries genuine nostalgia for a guy my age — that shell toe is iconic from the '80s, and the one-word subject did make me pause for a half second.
- The email renders cleanly — no broken images, no overlapping text, everything loads.
- The adiClub giveaway does surface member benefits, so at least they acknowledge I'm in their loyalty program.
- The fine print on the giveaway is actually readable — not buried in 6pt gray text.

## What's weak

- Wrong model for wrong person — a young woman doing contortion stretches tells me nothing about how this shoe feels on a 62-year-old foot that needs arch support.
- No price, no CTA to buy the Superstar — the hero product has no path to purchase that I can see.
- The preheader says "There's a chance it's still the Olympic Shop sale" — I have no idea what that means in the context of a Superstar email; it reads like a copy-paste error from a completely different campaign.
- Two completely separate campaigns (Superstar heritage + FIFA giveaway) crammed into one email with no transition — my eye didn't know where to land.
- Zero comfort messaging — no cushioning, no fit talk, nothing that tells me this shoe is right for walking, standing, or everyday errands.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one story and tell it.** Either this is a Superstar nostalgia email or it's a FIFA giveaway email. Two campaigns in one send splits focus and dilutes both messages. I'd cut the giveaway to its own dedicated email.
- 2. **Add a buy button under the hero.** "Shop Superstar" with a visible price is the most obvious thing missing. Right now I admire the shoe and go nowhere.
- 3. **Lean into the nostalgia angle** — this is actually a winning play for Boomers. Show someone my age in the shoe, reference the era, make it feel like a reunion not a runway show.
- 4. **Fix the preheader** — "There's a chance it's still the Olympic Shop sale" has to be a mistake. It actively undermines trust in the send.
- 5. **Add comfort language** — even one line about cushioning or all-day wear would help bridge the fashion positioning to my priorities.

## Technical Audit — adidas "Superstar" (us-news.comms.adidas.com)

**1. Technical Summary**

Two confirmed failures block clean delivery: a malformed dynamic-content URL that will 404 on click, and a completely absent plain-text body. Eight HTTP (non-HTTPS) image references introduce a secondary rendering risk in security-strict clients.

---

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**

| Severity | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| FAIL | Dynamic content URL uses relative path `/g/` — will resolve as a broken relative link rather than hitting the dv.adidas.com domain | QA: `unknown url type: '/g/2c2d6fd9-27db-43e6-ad09-bae249debde3?txn=332dd780-5bd5-11f1-b649-8e0ec7ba23ea'` |
| WARN | Tracking pixel served over HTTP | `src: http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019e76db072b-...` — unencrypted, may be stripped by Gmail/Outlook |
| INFO | 36 click-redirect links untestable | All route through `click.comms.adidas.com`; UTM param integrity unverifiable from HTML alone |

The `/g/` prefix on the dynamic content block is a template rendering failure — the absolute domain (`https://dv.adidas.com`) was not prepended before send. Any subscriber clicking that block lands on a 404.

**5. Compliance**

| Severity | Finding |
|---|---|
| FAIL | **Plain-text body is 0 characters.** CAN-SPAM §7704 requires a valid physical address and a functional opt-out mechanism to be present; while the law does not mandate a text part, a zero-length text part means any downstream spam filter that scores on text/HTML ratio will treat this as image-only spam. More critically, the unsubscribe link in the HTML footer has no text-part equivalent. |
| INFO | `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />` suppresses HTTP Referer headers on link clicks — intentional for privacy but means analytics platforms relying on referrer (not UTM) will see direct traffic instead of email attribution. |

Physical address and unsubscribe link were not visible in the truncated HTML provided; assumed present in footer based on standard adidas template — verify independently.

---

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity**

- The `/g/` broken URL is a direct continuity break: the dynamic content block click goes nowhere.
- All 36 navigational links route through `click.comms.adidas.com` redirects — UTM parameters cannot be confirmed from the HTML source alone. If any redirect target is missing `utm_medium=email`, attribution will be lost for that click path.
- `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />` (noted above) means referrer-based attribution fallback is disabled globally for this send.

---

**7. Recommendations**

1. **[P0 — Fix before resend]** Resolve the `/g/` dynamic content URL. The template renderer did not prepend `https://dv.adidas.com` to the dynamic block href. Audit the DV block configuration and re-render before any resend.

2. **[P0 — Deliverability]** Add a plain-text MIME part. Minimum viable: physical address, a one-line content summary, and the unsubscribe URL in plaintext. Zero-length text parts degrade spam scoring.

3. **[P1 — Rendering]** Migrate the arrow image (`b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg`) to HTTPS. It appears 8 times; a single CDN URL change in the template covers all instances.

4. **[P1 — Rendering]** Migrate the tracking pixel (`http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...`) to HTTPS to prevent stripping by Gmail and iOS Mail.

5. **[P2 — Accessibility]** Add descriptive `alt` text to the three flagged images. The DV block image in particular is likely a hero product image — blank alt means screen reader users get no context.

6. **[P3 — Hygiene]** Deduplicate the `@font-face` declarations. The two identical `<style>` blocks add parse overhead and ~1.5 KB with no benefit.
