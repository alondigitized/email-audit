# Your favorite sale? 30% OFF NIKE
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- Eighteen days. Eighteen straight mornings with Shoe Carnival in my inbox, and today they're leading with Nike at 30% off for back to school. Here's the thing — I actually stopped on this one for a second, because Nike is a name I know and 30% is real money. But then I looked at the hero image and it's basketball shoes on a green court with a young guy lounging in athletic gear. That's not me. That hasn't been me for about thirty-five years.
- What almost got me was buried below the fold: Brooks. "Price Break. 4 Days Only. $40 Off." Now THAT is speaking my language. Brooks makes the kind of walking shoe I actually wear, and $40 off with a ticking clock is exactly the kind of offer that would get me off the couch and onto the website. But I had to scroll past a college athletics spread to even find it.
- The preheader is a disaster — I opened this email in my inbox and before I even got to the subject, the preview showed a raw tracking URL. That looked like spam. I almost deleted it without opening. Would I click? Maybe — but only for the Brooks section, and only if I can find it again after all the back-to-school noise. I would not forward this to a friend.

## What's working

- **30% off Nike is a legit number** — I notice real discounts and that one lands, even if the shoe style isn't my priority.
- **Brooks section hit home** — "$40 Off, 4 Days Only" with the Brooks logo is the most comfort-relevant offer in the whole email. That's the brand I actually walk in.
- **Multiple price points stacked** — $20 off, $10 off coupons with a visible code (SAVE20MORE) means I don't have to hunt for a deal; it's right there.
- **Loyalty reward visible** — The free $10 reward reminder at the bottom tells me they know I'm a member, which feels like they see me as a returning customer.
- **Email renders clean** — No broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum. It looked like a real email on my phone.

## What's weak

- **Preheader is a raw tracking URL** — In my inbox, the preview line was a long URL starting with "https://click.email.shoecarnival.com" — that reads as broken or spammy. Almost made me delete without opening.
- **Back to school hero has nothing to do with me** — I'm sixty-two. My kids graduated twenty years ago. The "Family Sale" framing and the young athlete model tell me this email wasn't built for Walker Miles.
- **Brooks is buried below two other sections** — The one offer I'd actually act on is third in the scroll order, after Nike and a Puma/New Balance athletics spread.
- **Day eighteen** — Seventeen days in a row I let slide, but I'm starting to feel like this sender doesn't know how to pace themselves. The "your favorite sale?" subject line feels like flattery to compensate for the fatigue.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move Brooks to the hero for comfort-walking segments** — Any subscriber who's clicked Brooks, Hoka, or New Balance walking styles before should get a version where $40 off Brooks is the first thing they see, not the third. A simple reorder.
- 2. **Fix the preheader — any copy is better than a raw URL** — Even "New arrivals for every budget" beats a tracking link. This should be caught before send.
- 3. **Swap the model for back-to-school adjacent** — The solo young athlete image telegraphs "this email is for someone twenty years younger than me." A father walking with a kid, or even just shoes on a trail, would read more inclusively.
- 4. **Add a deadline to the Nike offer** — The Brooks section has "4 Days Only" and that urgency works. The Nike hero just says "Shop Now" with no clock on it — put a date on it.
- 5. **Give the coupon section a hierarchy** — "$20 off" and "$10 off" are stacked at the same visual weight. Tell me which one applies to me and when it expires.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Brooks $40 Off — 4 Days Only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `30% off Nike + $40 off Brooks ends soon`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus $20 off your next order — code inside`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Brooks, Nike, New Balance — comfort prices this week only`

## Technical Audit — Shoe Carnival: "30% OFF NIKE" (2026-07-18)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Two click-tracking URLs are broken by an injected Unicode zero-width space (`U+200B`), making any links that resolve through those redirect entries non-functional. Additionally, the open-tracking pixel contains an unfilled `[UNIQUE]` merge token, indicating open-rate measurement may be disabled for this send.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**CRITICAL — Zero-width space corrupts two redirect URLs**

The QA checker caught a codec error at position 74 in both of these URLs:

```
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?rd&i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260718_nike_brooks_AM​
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?rl&i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260718_nike_brooks_AM​
```

The invisible `​` appended after `_AM` is `U+200B` (zero-width space), likely introduced by a copy-paste from a rich-text source into the ESP template editor. Email clients will either strip it silently (link may work) or pass it URL-encoded as `%E2%80%8B` (link will 404 at the tracker). This needs to be confirmed against the ESP's raw link table; any destination URLs routed through those two redirect entries should be audited.

**INFO — 27 click-tracking links not probed**

All remaining links route through `emltrk.com` click-redirect domains and were skipped by the HTTP prober. No further action unless destination URL mapping can be pulled from the ESP.

**UTM parameter encoding in redirect `t=` param**

The `t=` query parameter on the tracking links carries `utm_campaign=SCA_20260718_nike_brooks_AM` directly (not URL-encoded as a destination query string). Verify this is the ESP's expected format for passing UTM values through to the destination — if the redirect handler doesn't re-append these as destination query params, UTM attribution will be lost.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

| Unique-open pixel | `0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]` |

Product and content images without `alt` text render as broken icons in image-blocked clients (Outlook desktop defaults to images off) and fail WCAG 2.1 SC 1.1.1. Tracking pixels conventionally get `alt=""` to suppress icon display.

**`<meta content="IE=edge" />` missing `http-equiv`**

The tag is malformed — `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"` is absent. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
```
Benign in modern clients but technically invalid.

**Dark mode not supported**

`color-scheme: light` and `supported-color-schemes: light` explicitly opt out of dark mode. No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block is present. Outlook on Windows and Apple Mail will force-invert colors on affected elements for dark-mode users, potentially making white-on-white text invisible.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**CRITICAL — `[UNIQUE]` literal token unfilled in open-tracking pixel**

```
https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]
```

`[UNIQUE]` is an unfilled merge token — it should resolve to a per-subscriber identifier at send time. If this literal string was delivered to recipients, the open-tracking pixel fires with `d=[UNIQUE]` for every subscriber, collapsing all open events into a single opaque bucket. Open rate data for this send is unreliable. Confirm whether the ESP substituted this at delivery or if the raw token was sent.

No other unfilled merge tokens detected in the truncated source.
