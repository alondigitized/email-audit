## Executive summary

- Well, here's another adidas email, and I've honestly lost count. The top of it is a dark plaid photo with "ADIDAS FAVORITES" in thin, widely spaced letters. It looks like a fashion magazine, not a shoe store. I had to scroll past it to find the real news, which is "Up to 50% off for fall, no code needed." That part I like, because I don't want to hunt for a coupon.
- Below that are three black buttons for Men, Women and Kids. Then there's a photo of a cream Handball Spezial sneaker, a retro court shoe with a gum sole. It's a nice-looking shoe, but it's a gifting pitch and it isn't what I'd pick for walking. There's no price, no cushioning talk, and nothing about arch support. "Premium comfort" gets one passing mention.
- Would I click? Maybe "Shop Men" if I'm already curious about a sale, but I wouldn't forward it. It's better than the Pokémon and Real Madrid sends from earlier this week, because it has a real discount. It still isn't talking to a guy who wants a cushioned slip-on.

## What's working

- "Up to 50% off, no code needed" is plain and honest, and I don't have to dig for a promo code.
- The Men / Women / Kids buttons are big, black and easy to tap with my thumb.
- The text is large and readable on my phone without zooming.
- It's a fall sale in October, so the timing makes sense.
- The Spezial photo is clean and shows the shoe clearly.

## What's weak

- The hero is a moody plaid photo with fancy lettering and no offer, so the discount sits below the first screen.
- "Up to 50%" doesn't say which items. I can't tell whether the shoes I'd wear are 10% off or 50% off.
- Nothing about comfort, cushioning, arch support or slip-on ease. Skechers or Hoka would have led with that.
- The Spezial section is a gift pitch for a retro court shoe, and its button doesn't say which shoe or what it costs.
- There's no deadline, so I have no reason to act today. I also see no adiClub points mention, even though I'm a member.
- Another adidas email on top of the six from the past week feels like too many.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the offer in the hero: "Up to 50% off fall favorites" over a real shoe, not a fabric texture.
- 2. Show two or three actual shoes with sale prices, like "Was $100, now $55", so I can do the price-versus-quality math.
- 3. Add a comfort angle for older walkers, such as a cushioned, slip-on or walking pick, with a line about support.
- 4. Add a real end date and a line for adiClub members.
- 5. Make the Men button more specific, like "Shop Men's Shoes on Sale", and cut how often these go out.

# Technical Audit: adidas, "Get 3-Stripes fall faves for up to 50% off"

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML is a standard table-based, responsive template. It has one confirmed deliverability failure (no plain-text part) and several asset and accessibility defects: insecure `http://` images, including the tracking pixel, and missing alt text. The HTML was truncated, so footer, unsubscribe and link-level checks are only partly verifiable.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **26 tracking links were not probed.** QA skipped them as click-redirect domains (`click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/…`). Destinations, redirect chains and final-URL health are unverified.
- **The click and open-tracking domain serves over plain HTTP.** One image has the src `http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/010201a13faeb510-e1fb0c5c-…/kggfHQKeac1sZLP5hojp307E9eq5doaQpF6EcJ5rQGQ=452`. It has no alt text and a hashed filename, which looks like the open-tracking pixel. Over `http://`, clients that block mixed content will not load it, so opens are undercounted. It is also an unencrypted request.
- **Eight `<img>` tags load over HTTP.** The "arrow" image is `http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg`. Some clients block or rewrite non-HTTPS images, and the same asset appears eight times.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** `https://dv.adidas.com/o/d37d33cd-93d5-4665-960e-f84b44b602ec?cp_tp=…` has no `alt`. Screen readers will announce the filename or URL. The tracking-pixel `<img>` above is also missing `alt`, which is harmless if it is a pixel but still worth an empty `alt=""`.
- **Ineffective charset declaration:** `<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` has no `http-equiv="Content-Type"`, so it does nothing. Rendering relies on the HTTP/MIME `Content-Type` header.
- **Incomplete viewport meta:** `<meta name="viewport" content="initial-scale=1.0" />` omits `width=device-width`. Mobile behavior depends on the client's defaults.
- **Doctype mismatch:** The HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype is paired with XHTML-style self-closing tags (`<meta … />`). This is non-blocking and common in ESP output.
- `<html lang="en">` is present.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found in the visible HTML. There are no unresolved merge tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`, `[FIRSTNAME]`) in the truncated source.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Plain-text part is missing: `[FAIL]`, 0 chars.** There is no text/plain alternative. This hurts spam scoring and accessibility, and text-only clients get nothing.
- **Authentication-Results header not found: `[WARN]`.** QA attributes this to the AgentMail relay, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC are unknown. This is a limit of the capture, not a confirmed sender defect. Verify the headers on the original message.
- **Unverified:** The footer is cut off, so I can't confirm the unsubscribe link, physical address or `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers. No violation is claimed.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. Every probed destination is wrapped in the `click.comms.adidas.com` redirector, and QA skipped the probes. UTM parameters and landing-page alignment can't be confirmed from the source or QA data.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Add a text/plain alternative** (multipart/alternative). This is the only hard QA failure.
2. **Move all image hosts to HTTPS.** That covers the `image.link.adidas.com` arrow asset and the `click.comms.adidas.com` pixel. Check that both domains have valid certificates.
3. **Add `alt` text** to the `dv.adidas.com/o/d37d33cd-…` image, and `alt=""` to the tracking pixel.
4. **Fix the head tags.** Use `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8">` and `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0">`.
5. **Re-run QA on the full, untruncated HTML and raw headers.** That would let you check Authentication-Results, `List-Unsubscribe`, the footer address, redirect resolution and UTM parameters on the landing pages.
