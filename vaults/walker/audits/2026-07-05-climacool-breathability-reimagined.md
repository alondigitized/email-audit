## Executive summary

- I'll be honest — "Climacool" sounds like something I should recognize, and "breathability reimagined" tells me the technology is real. But the second I opened this thing, I was staring at a young woman in a sleek black outfit with fire-red shoes that look more like they belong on a runway than on my feet at the grocery store. Nothing about this says 62-year-old male looking for something comfortable he can slip on in the morning.
- The 30% off for the season is buried below the fold. That's the one thing I actually care about, and they made me scroll past a fashion editorial to find it. The Climacool tech — breathability, ventilation — fine, that matters to me in July when my feet are sweating, but they never connected those dots. They just threw a word at me and called it marketing.

- **"Summer Savings" framing makes sense** — it's July, it's hot, seasonal messaging lands as timely.

- **adidas is a brand I know** — I'm subscribed, I've bought from them before, so the sender name gets me to at least open.

## What's weak

- **The model is a young woman in fashion athletic wear** — I'm a 62-year-old guy. That image tells me nothing about whether this shoe would hold up my arches all day.
- **"Breathability reimagined" is tech-speak that floats** — doesn't tell me if this shoe is cushioned, if it's easy to get on, if it runs wide. Words without function don't move me.
- **The offer is below the fold** — on a phone, I'm already looking for a reason to close the email before I ever hit "30% off."

- 2. **Show a men's walking shoe in the hero** — one shot of a wide, cushioned shoe on a real-looking guy my age and I'm already thinking about whether it comes in my size.
- 3. **Connect breathability to real life** — "Your feet stay cool on a full day out" beats "Breathability reimagined" every time. Tell me what it means for me, not what the engineers called it.

## Technical Audit — adidas Climacool (2026-07-04)

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed failures: a broken destination link caused by a malformed relative URL in a click-redirect, and a completely absent plain-text MIME part. Six repeated arrow images load over HTTP and will be blocked by most modern clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken link — relative URL in href**
The QA probe flagged `unknown url type: '/g/05a27910-cfba-4abf-a4b6-5de3da645671?txn=ce822000-7805-11f1-a6f4-d2520cded5b8'`. A relative path (`/g/...`) was passed as the click-redirect destination rather than an absolute `https://` URL. The wrapping redirect (`dv.adidas.com/o/...`) will attempt to resolve it relative to the redirect domain, producing a 404 or error page for any recipient who clicks.

**[WARN] 29 tracking links not validated**
All `click.comms.adidas.com` hrefs were skipped by the HTTP probe. No evidence of breakage, but coverage is incomplete for this send.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] HTTP image repeated 6×**
`http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg` (alt="arrow") is referenced six times over plain HTTP. Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all proxy or block mixed-content images; the arrow asset will silently fail to render on most clients.

**[WARN] HTTP tracking pixel**
`http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019f2f9ad069-596d6d38-234b-414f-a455-a113bee2c3a3-000000/...` is served over HTTP. Same blocking risk as above; also affects open-tracking accuracy if the pixel is used for that purpose.

**[WARN] Missing alt text on 2 images**
- `dv.adidas.com/o/05a27910-cfba-4abf-a4b6-5de3da645671?...` — no `alt` attribute
- `click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019f...` — no `alt` attribute

Both will render as blank broken images in image-off clients with no fallback label.

**[INFO] Malformed charset meta tag**
`<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` is missing the required `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute. Correct form: `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />`. Without it, some clients may not honor the charset declaration.

**[INFO] Duplicate @font-face declarations**
`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are each declared twice across two separate `<style>` blocks with identical `src` values. No functional breakage, but it adds ~600 bytes of dead CSS.

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text MIME part is empty**
The automated check reports the text version as 0 characters. CAN-SPAM does not mandate a plain-text part, but its complete absence is a deliverability signal — SpamAssassin and many ISP filters penalize HTML-only messages with no multipart/alternative fallback. It also degrades accessibility for screen-reader-only or text-mode clients.

**[OK]** `<meta name="ROBOTS" content="NOINDEX, NOFOLLOW" />` and `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />` are both present. Unsubscribe link presence cannot be confirmed from the truncated HTML, but the footer structure references privacy/transactional CSS classes consistent with a footer block.

UTM parameter coverage on the 29 click-redirect URLs cannot be audited because the probe skipped them — verify manually or via a test send that UTM tags (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content`) survive the `dv.adidas.com` redirect chain intact.
