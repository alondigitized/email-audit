# Step into Hyperboost
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-10**
## Executive summary

- Well, here's another adidas email, the seventh one I've seen this week. At least this one has a real shoe in it. The pink and white Hyperboost Edge is pictured big and clear, and the copy mentions "seamless heel-to-toe cushioning." That's the first thing in weeks to make me look twice. Then I saw it's a "super trainer" for "runners chasing progression" with "racing tech," and I'm 62 and walk for comfort. I'm not chasing a pace.
- There's no price, no sale and no member perk anywhere, so I have nothing to weigh against my Hoka or Brooks. The Samba section at the bottom is a flat lace-up, same as the gold Samba from the 10/08 "Step up your gifting game" email. I'd skim it and probably not click. I wouldn't forward it, but I'd leave it unread for a day before deleting.

## What's working

- The big Hyperboost shoe photo is easy to see on my phone, and the headline is large and readable.
- The Runner's World award badge gives me a third-party reason to believe the shoe is good, not just adidas saying so.
- "Seamless heel-to-toe cushioning" is the one line that speaks to what I care about.
- The black buttons are big, with plain "SHOP HYPERBOOST" wording. They'd be easy to tap with my thumb.
- Nothing looked broken. The images loaded and the text lines up.

## What's weak

- There's no price, discount or free-shipping line anywhere. I can't do any price-vs-quality math.
- The shoe is pitched at racers and marathoners, with young athletic models. Nobody in it looks like me or walks like me.
- The black "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" banner at the top competes with the real message. That's three "shop" buttons before I've read a thing.
- There's no sizing, width, arch support or slip-on information. "Super trainer" and "progression" are jargon that doesn't help me.
- The Samba gift block repeats the same flat lace-up sneaker from earlier this week. It has nothing for comfort.

- 3. Cut the top banner and the "Shop Marathon" button. Keep one button per product.
- 4. Swap the repeated Samba block for a comfort or slip-on style, or drop it.

## Technical Audit

1. **Technical Summary**
The email is a Salesforce Marketing Cloud send (`click.comms.adidas.com`, `image.link.adidas.com`) with sound responsive scaffolding. It has no plain-text part (QA FAIL, 0 chars), some `http://` image sources, and missing alt text on at least two images.

2. **Link & Tracking Issues**
- 22 tracking links were skipped from HTTP probing because they are click-redirect domains. I can't confirm they resolve, so this is unverified, not a defect.
- Mixed content: the "arrow" image uses `http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg`. QA flagged it 6 times, so it is repeated in the template. Clients that block non-HTTPS images will drop it.
- The open-tracking pixel is also `http://`: `http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/010201a124338cc5-.../aB-W7a0jMwUlg4UcSw56hIpyvI_0myF5lfnnwjpgGj0=452`. Opens may be undercounted where insecure images are blocked.
- `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` is present. It is harmless for the redirect-based tracking, but it strips referrer data on any direct links.

3. **Rendering & Accessibility**
- Missing alt text on `dv.adidas.com/o/be61c591-46b8-4fb8-a754-f95d6021c2a4?cp_tp=...`. If this is a content image, screen readers and image-blocked clients get nothing.
- The tracking pixel `aB-W7a0jMwUlg4UcSw56hIpyvI_0myF5lfnnwjpgGj0=452` also has no alt. This is low impact because it is a pixel, but `alt=""` is the correct markup.
- The "arrow" image has alt text, but the repeated `http://` source makes it likely to fail.
- The `<!DOCTYPE>` is HTML 4.01 Transitional and `<html lang="en">` is set. This is acceptable for email.
- The custom `@font-face` rules for AdihausDIN and AdineuePRO only map to `local('Arial')` and similar. That is a deliberate fallback, not a defect.
- The meta tags include `<meta name="viewport">` and `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`. The `<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` has no `http-equiv`, so it won't declare the charset in some clients. The MIME header probably covers this.
- The source was truncated, so I can't check for role attributes, table semantics, or the remaining CSS.

4. **Personalization & Merge Tokens**
No issues found. The truncated source shows no unrendered tokens such as `%%...%%`, `{{...}}`, or `${...}`. I can't confirm the rest of the body.

5. **Compliance**
- The plain-text alternative is missing. QA reports 0 chars, and this is the only FAIL. A missing text part raises spam-filter risk and fails for text-only clients.
- I can't verify the unsubscribe link, the physical postal address, or `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers. The truncated HTML doesn't reach the footer and no headers were supplied. I'm not flagging these as missing.
- SPF, DKIM, and DMARC results were not supplied. The sender is `adidas@us-news.comms.adidas.com`, a branded subdomain, which is consistent with proper alignment.

6. **Email-to-Site Continuity**
- The 22 tracking links are wrapped in `click.comms.adidas.com` redirects, so the destination URLs and any UTM parameters are hidden. I can't assess UTM completeness or landing-page alignment with the "Step into Hyperboost" subject.
- Do not infer a problem from the missing UTMs. They may be encoded in the redirect payload.

7. **Recommendations**
1. Add a text/plain MIME part. This is the only confirmed QA failure.
2. Serve all images over HTTPS. Change `image.link.adidas.com` and `click.comms.adidas.com` references to `https://`, especially the repeated "arrow" image and the open pixel.
3. Add `alt` text to `dv.adidas.com/o/be61c591-...`, and use `alt=""` on the tracking pixel.
4. Add `http-equiv="Content-Type"` to the charset meta tag, if the MIME header doesn't already declare it.
5. Re-run the audit with the full HTML and raw headers. That would let us verify the unsubscribe link, the `List-Unsubscribe` headers, the postal address, the authentication results, and the resolved destination URLs with UTMs.
