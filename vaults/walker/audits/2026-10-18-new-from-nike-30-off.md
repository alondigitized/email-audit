# NEW! from Nike + 30% OFF 🔥
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-18**
## Executive summary

- Nike and 30% off. That's what the subject says, and the top of the email delivers it. The picture is a young woman in a pink Nike sneaker, and the "UP TO 30% OFF" is big enough that I can read it without my glasses. But the "Men's" tile is a small orange box on the right, and the hero shoe is a pink women's runner. I'm 62, and none of this was built with me in mind.
- It's also the same orange "BUY MORE, SAVE MORE" block I've been looking at since 10/11. This time it's $10 off $74.98 or $25 off $149.98 with code SAVEMORE. That's three offers stacked: 30% off Nike, 30% off Family Athletics, and the coupon. So which one do I use? The fine print says the coupon can't be combined with other discounts, and that's buried at the bottom. After the 10/14 and 10/17 emails I don't trust that block anymore.
- Would I click? Maybe the "Men's" tile, if I'm already thinking about sneakers. I wouldn't forward it. I'd skim it and move on.

## What's working

- The "UP TO 30% OFF" is huge and readable. I didn't need my glasses.
- Shoe Carnival is a store I know, and the Nike name is a brand I trust for walking shoes.
- The Men's tile is bright and easy to tap, and the layout is simple enough for my phone.
- The wallet coupon and barcodes are handy if I'm standing in a store.
- The Shoe Perks $10 reward line at the bottom gives members something to look forward to.

## What's weak

- The hero is a young woman in pink. It isn't aimed at a 62-year-old man, and Men's gets the smallest slice of the top.
- Two 30% banners and a coupon block compete with each other. I can't tell what the real deal is.
- "Up to" 30% and the fine print about exclusions make me doubt the offer. The shoes shown are mostly women's and kids' styles. Only one men's shoe, a training shoe, is in the grid.
- Nothing mentions cushioning, arch support or slip-on comfort, which is what I shop for. I also see no Hoka, Brooks or Skechers, and no reviews.
- The "Buy More, Save More" block is old news. It's a rerun from this week.
- The fine print at the bottom is tiny. I'd have to zoom in to read it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with a men's comfort shoe and a men's walking Nike, not the pink women's runner. Gender-split the send if you can.
- 2. Pick one offer. Make it either 30% off Nike or the $10/$25 coupon, and say plainly whether they stack.
- 3. Add comfort details like "cushioned" and "all-day walking" to the shoe captions, plus star ratings and prices.
- 4. Move the coupon block down, or retire it. It has been in nearly every send this week.
- 5. Make the exclusions legible in plain sentences near the offer, not in tiny print at the bottom.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cushioned Nike walkers and trainers for men, up to 30% off.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Pick the shoe that feels right. Shop men's Nike sale styles.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a standard ESP-built template (lang, viewport, and mobile and client-fix CSS are all present). Its main defects are two malformed tracking-pixel URLs, one with an unresolved `[UNIQUE]` placeholder, and a very URL-heavy plain-text part. SPF/DKIM could not be verified. The HTML was truncated, so footer and link checks are limited.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unresolved placeholder:** `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]` still contains the literal `[UNIQUE]` token. If this is the delivered markup, the open-tracking ID was never substituted, so opens for this pixel can't be attributed to a recipient.
- **Malformed query string:** `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20261018_nike_athletics_AM​`
  - The `utm_campaign=...` is nested inside a `t=` value, so it is not a real query parameter.
  - The URL ends in an invisible zero-width character (U+200B) after `AM`. That can corrupt the campaign value on the tracking side.
- **Unverified links:** 42 `emltrk.com` click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe, so their destinations and status codes are unconfirmed.
- **Plain-text part:** 8,292 of 11,013 characters (75%) are URLs. That ratio makes the text part hard to read and is a minor spam-filter signal. It comes from long tracking URLs.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** the two flagged images are the tracking pixels above (`ieezzy5m`, `0di86k1v`). They are decorative, so they need `alt=""` explicitly. Low severity, and no content images were flagged.
- **Meta tags:** `<meta content="IE=edge" />` has no `http-equiv`, so it does nothing. `<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />` also lacks `http-equiv`, but the `charset` attribute still works.
- **Doctype mismatch:** the doctype is HTML 4.01 Transitional while `<html>` declares `xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml"`. This is harmless in practice but inconsistent.
- **Link affordance:** the global rule `a, a:link, ... { text-decoration:none !important; color:inherit !important; }` removes underline and color from every link. Unless links are styled inline elsewhere (not visible in the truncated source), they won't be distinguishable from body text.
- **Small text:** `.responsive-text` is set to `font-size:12px !important` at ≤600px, which is below the usual 14px minimum for mobile readability.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- `[UNIQUE]` in the `0di86k1v` pixel URL is an unresolved token (see section 2).
- No other tokens were visible in the truncated source or the QA output.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Authentication-Results:** not found. This is expected from the AgentMail relay, so SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment for `email.shoecarnival.com` is unverified. It is not a confirmed failure.
- **Unsubscribe and address:** the footer, unsubscribe link, physical address and `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers were not in the provided data. I can't confirm or flag them either way.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- The only UTM value visible is `utm_campaign=SCA_20261018_nike_athletics_AM`, and it sits in a malformed pixel URL (see section 2), not on a destination link.
- Landing-page URLs and their UTMs can't be assessed. The 42 links are redirect-wrapped and weren't probed, so alignment between the Nike/30% off messaging and the landing pages is unverified.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Fix the ESP substitution for `?d=[UNIQUE]` and confirm that real sends resolve it.
2. Correct the `ieezzy5m` pixel to `&utm_campaign=...` (or whatever the intended parameter is) and strip the trailing U+200B from the campaign string.
3. Add `alt=""` to both tracking pixels.
4. Replace `<meta content="IE=edge">` with `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge">` or remove it.
5. Keep link styling visible (underline or color) for body-text links, and consider a 14px minimum on mobile.
6. Shorten or suppress tracked URLs in the plain-text part to cut the URL ratio.
7. Re-run the audit with the full HTML and raw headers (`List-Unsubscribe`, `Authentication-Results`) to close the compliance and UTM gaps. Also resolve the `emltrk.com` redirects for landing-page checks.
