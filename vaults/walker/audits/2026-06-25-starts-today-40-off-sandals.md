# Starts today: 40% off sandals 😎
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- I've been opening DSW every single day for two-plus weeks running — so when their name hit my sender column this morning, my thumb moved on autopilot. "Starts today: 40% off sandals." Alright, I'll bite — 40% off is real money, even if it's not the BOGO deal they've been running all week. The email loads fast, the hierarchy is clean, and that big bold offer number is the first thing my eyes land on.
- Then I read the asterisk. *"Regular price women's styles only."* And I look at the nine product images in that grid — strappy sandals, wedge flats, flip-flops in rose gold. Every single one of them is women's footwear. There's a little "SHOP MEN" text link buried in the copy above the grid, but you'd need to go hunting for it. I'm a 62-year-old man with aching arches and I just spent twenty seconds in an email that wasn't built for me.
- The offer shift from BOGO 50% to 40% off also feels like a quiet downgrade — yesterday the math was "buy two, save big," today it's a single-pair discount that only applies to women's styles. I'd mark this read and maybe glance at the clearance link on my way out. I wouldn't forward it. I'd probably open tomorrow's email anyway out of habit, but DSW is burning goodwill here.

## What's working

- **40% off lands instantly** — the hero type is large, high-contrast, and readable without zooming on a phone screen. No squinting required at my age.
- **DSW is a trusted name in my inbox** — muscle memory kept me from deleting this on sight.
- **Semi-annual clearance section adds a second reason to scroll** — "40% off clearance" is a separate value prop and gives me something to look at beyond the sandal grid.
- **VIP loyalty section is visible** — as someone who tracks reward points, I appreciate that it's not buried at the bottom footer.
- **No layout breaks or render bugs** — email rendered cleanly, no missing images or overlapping text blocks.

## What's weak

- **The asterisk does real damage** — "Regular price women's styles only" means the headline offer doesn't apply to me at all. That's a bait-and-switch feeling even if it's technically disclosed.
- **Nine product images, zero men's shoes** — the entire visual real estate is women's sandals. "SHOP MEN" is a text link, not a section, not a product image.
- **The offer is a step back from BOGO** — after ten days of "BOGO 50% OFF," a single-pair 40% discount feels weaker. My brain does the math and it doesn't pencil out as well.
- **Preview text is wasted** — "This is way too good to pass up" tells me nothing I didn't already know from the subject line, and the spacer junk trailing behind it looks sloppy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this email or add a men's module.** If I'm in their men's buyer segment, the hero shouldn't be nine pairs of women's flats. Even one row of men's sandals/slides would justify my opening.
- 2. **Lead the asterisk disclosure earlier.** Right now it reads like a headline that doesn't apply to me. Put "Women's styles — 40% off" in the hero and spare me the fine print hunt.
- 3. **Tie the new offer back to the BOGO context.** "BOGO ended — now take 40% off any single pair" frames the change as a next step, not a downgrade.
- 4. **Replace the preheader with something that earns the open.** "Men's slides included — shop your size before they're gone" would have pulled me in harder than generic hype copy.
- 5. **Make the size shop grid mean something.** Sizes 5–11 look like women's sizes in context. If there's men's inventory, say "Men's 8–14" somewhere visible.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Two meta tags are missing `http-equiv` and are no-ops as written:**

```html
<!-- BROKEN — no http-equiv, charset declaration does nothing -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />

<!-- BROKEN — no http-equiv, IE compatibility hint is never applied -->
<meta content="IE=Edge" />
```

Correct forms:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=Edge" />
```

**[MEDIUM] No `lang` attribute on `<html>` element:**

```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">
```

Screen readers cannot infer language; WCAG 3.1.1 failure. Should be `<html lang="en" ...>`.

**[LOW] HelveticaNow fonts served as `.ttf` — not `.woff2`:**

```
https://braze-images.com/.../original.ttf?1755182939
```

TTF files are 30–50% larger than WOFF2. Six `.ttf` files are loaded, all from Braze CDN. Clients that support web fonts (Apple Mail, some webmail) will download heavier payloads unnecessarily. Futura fonts are correctly served as `.woff`.

**[LOW] Contradictory `text-size-adjust` declarations in `*` selector:**

```css
* { -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%; ... -ms-text-size-adjust: none; text-size-adjust: none; }
```

`-ms-text-size-adjust: 100%` is immediately overridden by `none` later in the same rule. The `100%` declaration is dead code; the `none` value takes effect. Not a functional bug, but indicates copy/paste accumulation in the reset block.

**[LOW] Non-standard `Src:` capitalization in `@font-face` rules:**

All ten `@font-face` blocks use `Src:` (capital S). CSS property names are case-insensitive per spec, but some stripped-down email client CSS parsers may reject non-lowercase declarations. Should be `src:`.
