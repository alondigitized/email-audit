# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-10-04**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I'm a 62-year-old looking for a comfortable slip-on or walking shoe, and I couldn't finish a purchase here. The homepage was clear and the menu was easy to follow. I found a shoe with "Arch Fit" in the name, but when I tried to add it to the cart, all I got was "Please select a size." The cart page was then a blank white screen. The search for "comfort shoes" did work: it returned 1,571 results. The first two products shown were women's shoes, though.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** That matches yesterday's 3/10, so I see no change. I only have yesterday's score, not its details, so I can't say which specific problems got better or worse. Today the shoe-finding steps work and the checkout steps don't. A customer who can't get a shoe into the cart doesn't buy.

## 3. What's Working

- **The Men menu** is clean and easy to read. It has big, well-spaced rows (Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections…) with arrows and a Back button. It's about as simple as navigation gets on a phone.
- **Comfort shortcuts up top.** The homepage nav has "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Arch Fit" and "Max Cus…", which are the words I shop by.
- **Men's Shoes page** leads with "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Walking Shoes" and "Boots." That covers what I want. The 753-result count is clear, and Filter and Sort are easy to find.
- **Size buttons** on the product page are big, roomy and easy to tap.
- **Product page basics** are clear: name, $70.00, star rating and the BOGO 50% offer in red. It's readable without zooming.
- **Search** returned 1,571 results for "comfort shoes," with prices (like $59.99–$88.00), color swatches and the BOGO note on each.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Add to Cart didn't work.** The size error appeared and the cart badge stayed at 0. The page jumped to the size list, but I couldn't tell what had happened. Nothing ever showed a shoe going into the cart.
- **The cart page was completely blank.** I can't tell whether it failed to load or was just empty. Either way, I'd have left.
- **Steps 1–3 looked identical.** The popup step and the log-in step show the same homepage. I never saw a log-in screen or any sign I was logged in, so I didn't see any personalization.
- **Search led with women's shoes.** The first two results were labeled "Women's." Nothing suggested the results were tailored to a man.
- **Most sizes were crossed out.** The size grid had many sold-out sizes (M 4.5, 5.5, 6.5, 7.5, 8.5, 9.5, 10.5, 14 and 15). That's a lot of crossed-out sizes for a basic slide.
- **The shoe I landed on is a unisex slide.** The product page shows only 2 reviews. I'd want more proof before spending $70 on a shoe.
- **Small text and tight spacing.**
  - The blue and gray promo bars at the top use small type.
  - The top nav chips are cut off at the screen edge ("Max Cus…").
  - The header icons are small for my fingers.
- **Hero image is all hype.** It's a purple "Aero Elite" racing shoe. There's nothing about cushioning, arch support or all-day comfort, which is the reason I came.
- **No comparison or price-vs-quality help.** I'm cross-shopping Hoka, Brooks and New Balance. I saw no "why this over others" content anywhere.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart first.** Add to Cart should work, or clearly tell me what's wrong. Select a default size or highlight the size section in a way I can see. The cart page should never be blank. Show an empty-cart message or an error if something breaks.
2. **Make the log-in step actually work.** Show a real sign-in screen, then show a "Welcome back" message afterward. Use my past browsing to show men's comfort shoes first.
3. **Respect the shopper in search.** For a men's shopper, lead with men's results, or give a "Men / Women" toggle at the top.
4. **Put comfort first on the homepage.** Replace some racing-shoe hype with "Arch Fit," "Hands Free Slip-ins" and "Cushioned Walking," with short plain-language benefits.
5. **Make text and buttons bigger.** Use larger promo text, don't clip the nav chips, and make the header icons bigger.
6. **Show sold-out sizes more clearly.** If most sizes are gone, say so up front, or suggest similar shoes that are in stock.
7. **Add simple comparison content.** For example, "How our arch support compares" or "cost per year of wear."

## 6. Bottom Line

Skechers has the right product language for me ("Arch Fit," "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit"), and the menu and search are decent. But I couldn't get a shoe into the cart, and the cart page showed nothing. The shoe that fits wins, and I couldn't even try to buy one. Another store will get my money. The score stays at **3/10**: the shopping steps are better than the checkout steps, and nothing improved overall since yesterday.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The data is limited and probably doesn't reflect a complete journey.

- **LCP and CLS are missing for all 8 steps** (`?`), so there is no Core Web Vitals evidence.
- **The captured URL is `https://www.skechers.com/` for the Log In and Men category steps.** The HTML samples for the Homepage, Log In and Men category steps carry the same `<html>` attributes and the same `--vh: 6.64px` value. These steps may not have navigated, or the capture may be reading the same document.
- **Kasada bot protection is rate-limiting the session.** `/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.825` returned 429 five times, and `GET /cart/` returned 429. The View Cart step was therefore probably evaluated on an error document, not the real cart.

## Accessibility

17 axe violations in total.

- **Homepage (9):**
  - `button-name` (critical): at least one button has no accessible name.
  - `link-name` (serious): at least one link has no accessible name.
  - `heading-order`, `region`: the heading hierarchy skips levels, and some content sits outside landmarks.
  - `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique`: there are multiple or nested `main` landmarks, a nested complementary landmark, and landmarks that aren't uniquely labelled. This points to a structural template problem.
  - `aria-allowed-role` (minor): a `role` attribute is invalid for its element.
- **Search "comfort shoes" (4):** `aria-allowed-role`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region`. These match the homepage violations, so the cause is probably in the shared header or footer template.
- **View Cart (4):** `document-title` (serious), `html-has-lang` (serious), `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one`. A page missing `<title>`, `lang`, `<main>` and `<h1>` all at once is almost certainly the 429 error response, not the cart template. It should be re-tested once the cart loads. The homepage does set `lang="en"`.
- **Not tested:** no axe results are reported for Log In, Men category, Men > Shoes, Product detail or Add to Cart. That means either no violations or no scan, and the data doesn't say which.

## SEO

- **Homepage:** `lang="en"` is present. The `<title>`, meta description, canonical URL and structured data are not in the truncated source, so they can't be assessed.
- **Landmarks and headings:** the duplicate `main` landmark and the heading-order violation on the homepage and search results also weaken document structure for crawlers.
- **View Cart:** it has no `<title>` and no `<h1>`. This is low impact, since a cart page is normally `noindex`. It matters only if bots or crawlers receive this 429 document for other URLs.
- **Rate limiting:** if the same Kasada challenge or 429 responses reach legitimate search crawlers, crawl coverage could suffer. Allowlist verified crawlers.

## Performance

- **TTFB:**
  - 429ms on the homepage, 392ms on Log In and Men category, and 119ms on View Cart.
  - 26–30ms on Men > Shoes, Product detail, Add to Cart and Search.
  - The 26–30ms values are too low for cross-origin server-rendered navigations. They suggest cache or bfcache hits, or client-side transitions, so don't treat them as real server response times.
- **LCP and CLS:** not captured, so there are no findings.
- **Third-party load:** the homepage `<head>` injects more than 25 async tag scripts. The Men category capture adds Snapchat, Reddit, Taboola, Nextdoor, Facebook and Stape. The scripts include:
  - **GTM and Google:** GTM, GA4 (`G-XZTV9LQ9DQ`), Google Ads and Floodlight.
  - **Ad and social pixels:** TikTok, Pinterest, Bing, Yahoo, RTB House (`creativecdn`), Impact and Tune (`go2sdk`).
  - **Personalization and analytics:** Monetate, Quantum Metric, Pulse Insights and Bloomreach (`brsrvr`).
  - **Consent management:** TrustArc, with `vice-managed-src` placeholders for consent-gated scripts.
  - These compete for main-thread and network time. Measure their effect on TBT and INP.
- **Web fonts:** Mulish loads through `webfontloader` from the cdnjs CDN (`wf-*-active` classes). This adds a third-party dependency and a render-time font swap. Self-hosting with `font-display` and a preload would remove it.
- **GTM environment:** the GTM URL carries `gtm_preview=env-1&gtm_auth=…`, which loads a non-default GTM environment. Confirm this is intended for production traffic.

## Mobile Optimization

- The viewport JS sets a `--vh` custom property inline on `<html>` (6.64px in this capture). That is an unusually small value for a mobile viewport. If it is derived from `window.innerHeight`, layouts that rely on it (full-height sections and overlays) could collapse. Verify the value is recalculated after load and on resize.
- No viewport meta, touch-target or responsive-image data is available in the truncated source, so nothing else can be assessed.

## Console & Network Errors

24 console errors and 6 network errors. The repeating messages are:

- **429 on `/<uuid>/<uuid>/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.825` (5 requests):**
  - Kasada's fingerprint endpoint is being throttled. The automation session is probably being flagged as a bot, which is an environment issue and not a site defect.
  - If real users hit this, it would break the challenge flow, so check the 429 rate for real traffic.
- **429 on `https://www.skechers.com/cart/`:** the View Cart page didn't load. This explains the View Cart accessibility violations.
- **"Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document" (4 times):** a script registers an `unload` handler that the Permissions-Policy disallows. It is probably a third-party tag. Unload handlers also block bfcache, so replace them with `pagehide`.
- **"Unsupported Summarizer API languages were specified" (3 times):** this comes from Chrome's built-in Summarizer API being called with an unsupported language. The source is unknown. It could be a first-party script, a third-party tag or a browser extension, so identify it from the stack trace.

## Recommendations

1. **Rerun the audit with the checks that failed.**
   - Confirm navigation actually occurs at each step (the URL stays at `/` for Log In and Men category).
   - Wait for LCP and CLS to be reported, for example with the `web-vitals` library or a `PerformanceObserver` with `buffered: true`.
   - Add a delay or retry, or an allowlisted IP, to avoid the Kasada 429s on `/cart/`.
2. **Fix the homepage and search landmark structure.** Use one top-level `<main>`, move `<aside>` elements out of nested landmarks, and add unique `aria-label`s to repeated `nav` and `region` landmarks.
3. **Add accessible names.** Give every icon-only button and link an `aria-label` or visible text, and correct the invalid `role` values.
4. **Check the cart template once it loads.** It needs `<title>`, `lang`, `<main>` and an `<h1>`. If the 429 error page is what lacks them, serve a proper error document.
5. **Replace `unload` handlers with `pagehide`.** Find the offending third-party tag and restore bfcache eligibility.
6. **Trim third-party tags.** Audit the 25+ pixels, defer the non-essential ones until after interaction or consent, and confirm that `gtm_preview` is meant to ship in production.
7. **Self-host the Mulish fonts** with a preload and `font-display: swap`, instead of loading `webfontloader` from cdnjs.
8. **Check the `--vh` calculation** on mobile viewports.
