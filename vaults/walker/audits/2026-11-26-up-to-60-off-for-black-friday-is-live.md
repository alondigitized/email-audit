# Up to 60% off for Black Friday is live
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-26**
## Executive summary

- Another adidas email, and I've lost count since the 14th. This is the same "up to 60% off" Black Friday message as the 21st and 22nd, with a countdown timer on top. The big white "UP TO 60% OFF" is easy to read, and I'll give them that. But there's no shoe in it. Two young people are lying in front of a brick wall in track jackets. I'm 62 and I want cushioning and arch support, and I see none of that.
- The copy mentions "sneakers" once, tucked in after "jerseys, fleece." Then I get three black buttons: Men, Women, Kids. That's a department directory, not an offer. "Up to 60%" tells me nothing about whether any walking shoe is actually marked down.
- Would I click? Probably not. It's cleanly laid out, but I've seen it three times this week. I'd mark it read and go see what Hoka or Skechers has. I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- The "UP TO 60% OFF" headline is huge and readable on my phone without squinting.
- The countdown timer gives a clear deadline. It says three days, which is about right for Black Friday.
- The Shop Men button is big and black, with plenty of room to tap with a thumb.
- The layout is simple and uncluttered, with one column and no hunting around.
- The body text is a decent size and easy to read.

## What's weak

- No shoe is shown anywhere. The picture is apparel on two young models, and nothing there says comfort or walking.
- It's the third near-identical "up to 60%" send, after "Save up to 60% during Black Friday" on the 21st and "Get up to 60% off gifts for Black Friday" on the 22nd. It feels like spam by now.
- "Up to 60%" is vague. I can't tell if any shoe I'd buy gets that discount, and I know the best deals are usually on the stuff nobody wants.
- The buttons only point to Men, Women and Kids. Nothing sends me to walking, slip-on or cushioned shoes.
- The hero image seems to sit left-aligned with white space on the right, so the layout looks a little off-center.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real product in the hero, like a cushioned walking shoe or slip-on with its sale price. Put "$XX, was $XX" on it instead of a vague "up to."
- 2. Cut the duplicate sends. One strong Black Friday email beats three that look the same.
- 3. Replace the Men/Women/Kids buttons with category buttons such as "Shop walking shoes" or "Shop slip-ons under $80."
- 4. Add a line about comfort, like cushioning or all-day wear, plus a few star ratings.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Walking shoes from $50 off — Black Friday ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Cushioned sneakers up to 60% off, 3 days left`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Comfort sneakers, slip-ons and walking shoes, all marked down through Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Pick your size now before the best deals sell out.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's HTML is mostly sound, but it ships with no plain-text part (QA FAIL). It also loads several images over `http://`, has two images with no alt text, and has a malformed charset meta tag. The source was truncated and 23 tracking links were not probed, so footer, compliance and UTM checks are incomplete.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Skipped probes:** 23 links were skipped as click-redirect domains. Their destinations and status are unverified, so nothing can be said about broken links.
- **Plain-HTTP tracking asset:** An `<img>` is served from the click-tracking domain over HTTP: `http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/010201a21643861a-f97fa454-4443-4591-b559-168deedea489-000000/ioR0IFaIDm0O7UsuTTH1oYEB5LNRWuemZdA--2JS7Z8=452`. It looks like an open-tracking pixel. Clients that block mixed or non-HTTPS content may drop it, which undercounts opens.
- **Per-send token:** The `/CI0/…` path embeds a per-send token. Any tracking link rewritten through `http://` is also exposed to downgrade or interception.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Non-HTTPS images:** The `arrow` image, `http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg`, appears 6 times over `http://`. It may be blocked or show a mixed-content warning. The same asset is probably available over HTTPS.
- **Missing alt text:**
  - `https://dv.adidas.com/o/85cef444-492a-4e05-8b20-ad057e63a8ad?cp_tp=…` has no `alt`. If it is content or linked, it is unreadable to screen readers and blank when images are off.
  - The click-domain tracking image (`…=452`) also has no `alt`. A tracking pixel should carry `alt=""`.
- **Charset declaration:** `<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` has no `http-equiv="Content-Type"`, so it does not declare the charset. This is a risk for non-ASCII characters if the MIME header charset is lost.
- **Duplicated CSS:** The `<style>` blocks repeat the same `@font-face` and media-query rules (AdihausDIN, AdineuePRO, `.net-footer-logo-v2`). This adds avoidable weight, which matters for Gmail's ~102KB clipping threshold.
- **Positives:** `lang="en"` and a viewport meta are present. Web fonts fall back to Arial/Helvetica through `local()` sources and `mso-font-alt`.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found in the visible source. No unresolved tokens such as `%%…%%`, `{{…}}` or `[FIRSTNAME]` appear in the excerpt.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Plain-text part:** QA FAIL. The text version is 0 chars, so the message is multipart-less or HTML-only. That hurts deliverability scoring and accessibility.
- **Authentication:** `Authentication-Results` was not captured. The AgentMail relay strips or omits it, so SPF/DKIM/DMARC status is unknown. This is a limitation of the test pipeline, not a confirmed sender fault. The From domain `us-news.comms.adidas.com` is a dedicated subdomain, which is good practice.
- **Not verifiable:** The unsubscribe link, postal address and `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are not in the truncated source or QA output.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. The visible source shows no destination URLs, and all 23 links were skipped because they are redirect-wrapped. UTM parameters are not visible, and the landing pages are unverified.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add a `text/plain` alternative. The QA check currently fails with 0 chars.
2. Serve every image over HTTPS, specifically `image.link.adidas.com/.../b0c011a2-…jpg` (6 instances) and the `click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/…` pixel.
3. Add `alt` text to the `dv.adidas.com/o/85cef444-…` image, and `alt=""` on the tracking pixel.
4. Change the charset tag to `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />`.
5. Consolidate the duplicated `<style>` blocks to reduce size and the clipping risk.
6. Re-run the audit with the full HTML and raw headers. Resolve the redirect chains to confirm landing URLs and UTMs. Capture `Authentication-Results`, `List-Unsubscribe` and the footer, or check SPF/DKIM/DMARC against the original sending domain.
