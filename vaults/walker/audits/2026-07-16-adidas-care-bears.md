# adidas | Care Bears
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- I looked at this thing for about four seconds and counted two things that have nothing to do with me. Care Bears — the little colorful cartoon characters from when I was already in my twenties — dressed up as sneakers. And then, below that, a "$60 and under school sneakers" section aimed at parents shopping for kids heading back to class. I am not a parent of a first-grader. I am not a nostalgic cartoon collector. I am a man whose feet hurt by 3 p.m. and who wants to know if a shoe has arch support before he thinks about what's printed on the side of it.
- The visual execution is clean enough — no broken images, the Care Bears hero pops with bright pastel colors and the shoe is front and center. But clean execution of the wrong product is still the wrong product. Adidas has now sent me soccer jerseys, F1 car collabs, race day shoes, and kids school sneakers — and I keep waiting for the email that says "hey, here's a comfortable walking shoe that won't destroy your feet." It hasn't come.
- I'd mark this as read without clicking. Wouldn't forward it either, unless I had a grandkid who liked cartoons — and even then I'd just text a link, not forward a marketing email. Adidas continues to treat my inbox like a spray-and-pray catalog.

## What's working

- **Clean layout, no clutter.** One hero image, one headline, one supporting section — the email doesn't try to do ten things at once visually, just two.
- **$60 and under is a real, legible number.** I can see it immediately without squinting. Price is always the first thing I look for, so putting it in a bold header earns a point.
- **The Care Bears shoe image is eye-catching.** The pastel colors and cartoon clouds are bright enough that I noticed it before I realized it had nothing to do with me.
- **No screaming subject line nonsense.** No !!! or ALL CAPS. Just the brand and collab name. Calm.

## What's weak

- **Care Bears is not a 62-year-old man's purchase driver.** This IP is aimed squarely at millennial nostalgia or gift-buying for young kids — neither is me.
- **"$60 and under school sneakers" is parenting content, not my life.** Adidas used half this email on back-to-school kids shoes. I haven't bought a kid school shoes in thirty years.
- **Not a single word about comfort, cushioning, or arch support.** The only thing that would make me consider an adidas shoe is knowing whether my feet will survive a three-mile walk. Silence on that front.
- **No CTA specific to the Care Bears product.** The top banner says "Shop Now" for best sellers generically, but the actual Care Bears hero has no button. I'd have to know to tap the image itself.
- **Fifth email from adidas in nine days that misses me entirely.** Yesterday was Club América soccer. Two days before that was the July newsletter. They sent me a racing shoe email, an F1 collab, and now Care Bears. At some point I start thinking about unsubscribing.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment me out of Care Bears entirely.** If adidas knows I open emails about cushioned walking shoes and am a 62-year-old male, there is no universe where I'm the right recipient for this send. Save this creative for their younger, style-forward list.
- 2. **If you must send me this, lead with the product benefit, not the IP.** "Comfort meets Care Bears — supportive sneakers starting at $60" gives me a hook. The current version leads with nostalgia I don't have.
- 3. **Add a CTA button directly under the Care Bears hero.** Don't make me guess I can tap the image.
- 4. **Drop the school sneakers section or make it its own send.** Two completely unrelated campaigns in one email — fashion collab AND back-to-school kids — dilutes both messages.
- 5. **Give back-to-school a dollar figure on the hero, not buried below the fold.** "$60 and under" deserves to be in the section header image, not just the text.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Care Bears x adidas — styles from $60`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New: adidas Care Bears collab just dropped`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Pastel colorways, classic silhouettes — shop the full collection now`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Care-A-Lot called. Your next sneaker is ready.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed failures: a broken click-tracking URL with an unresolved relative path, and a missing plain-text MIME part. Four HTTP (non-HTTPS) image URLs and two missing alt attributes compound the risk across rendering and deliverability dimensions.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Unresolved relative URL in click tracker**

The QA probe found an `unknown url type` at:
```
/g/2a46004c-0077-41b2-ab89-1f59d95ef9b9?txn=9b774f00-816a-11f1-aa3d-9e016bacd681
```
This relative `/g/` path was not expanded to an absolute URL before send. The associated tracking redirect (`https://dv.adidas.com/o/2a46004c...`) exists, but the inner destination URL is unresolvable — any recipient clicking this link will land on an error or loop. Affects at least one CTA in the email.

**[WARN] 23 tracking links not probed**

All outbound links route through `click.comms.adidas.com`. Destination URLs cannot be verified without probing the redirect chain. Broken destination URLs (404s, stale product pages) are invisible to this audit.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] HTTP image sources — 5 instances**

The same arrow asset is served over HTTP on 4 occurrences, and a tracking pixel on 1 additional:
```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg  (×4)
http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019f6d2a4c4e-...  (×1)
```
Apple Mail, Gmail, and Outlook 365 block mixed-content HTTP images by default. The arrow asset will be invisible to a significant share of recipients. All image URLs should use HTTPS.

**[WARN] Missing alt text on 2 images**

- `https://dv.adidas.com/o/2a46004c-0077-41b2-ab89-1f59d95ef9b9?...` — no alt attribute
- `http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/.../pUMxRv0LOwPo0Dhf...` — no alt attribute

The first appears to be a content image (likely a hero or product shot given the tracking path). Images-off rendering will show blank boxes with no context.

**[INFO] Duplicate `@font-face` declarations**

`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are declared twice across two `<style>` blocks with differing fallback lists (`Calibri`/`Sans Serif` in block 1, omitted in block 2). The second block overrides the first. No functional breakage, but dead CSS weight.

**[INFO] `<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` missing `http-equiv`**

The charset meta tag is missing the required `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute. Some legacy clients (Lotus Notes, older Outlook) may not parse the encoding declaration correctly, risking mojibake on special characters (e.g., `®`, `™`).

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text MIME part is empty (0 chars)**

The QA check confirms the plain-text alternative is absent or zero-length. This creates two problems:
- **CAN-SPAM / CASL**: Strictly requires multipart/alternative with a readable plain-text part. A 0-char body does not satisfy this.
- **Spam scoring**: SpamAssassin and similar engines penalize HTML-only messages. Missing plain text increases spam folder placement risk.

**[INFO] `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />`**

This tag instructs clients to strip the `Referer` header on outbound navigation. While benign in most email clients (which ignore it), any webview-based client or preview pane that honors it will suppress referrer data on landing pages, potentially breaking session attribution in analytics tools that fall back to referrer before UTM.

Unsubscribe link and physical sender address cannot be confirmed from the truncated HTML — these should be verified in the full source.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**[WARN] Non-standard tracking parameters**

The one resolvable destination URL uses proprietary parameters (`cp_tp`, `cp_cid`, `mi_cid`, `mi_mid`) rather than standard UTM params. If the landing page analytics stack expects `utm_source` / `utm_medium` / `utm_campaign`, these clicks will be bucketed as direct/unattributed traffic in GA4 or similar tools. Verify the landing page tag plan accounts for these param names.

**[WARN] Destination URL health unverifiable**

The 23 click-redirect links route through `click.comms.adidas.com` which the QA probe skipped. At least one CTA link is confirmed broken (see §2). Remaining CTAs should be manually click-tested in a seed inbox before the send window closes.
