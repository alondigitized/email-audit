# The 1080, in new colors
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-01**
## Executive summary

- Well, finally, an email that's about one shoe and doesn't make me hunt for it. The 1080 is the cushioned walker I'd actually look at, and the big photo at the top shows it right away. Four colors laid out in a grid, so I can see what's what without scrolling through a pile of lifestyle photos. Compared to the young folks in cream polos from the 9/30 "Understated neutrals" send, this is a big improvement.
- But there isn't one word about why I'd buy it. No price, no "cushioned," no "arch support," no men's or women's label. "Only at New Balance" is the whole pitch, and that's the brand talking about itself. The only dollar figure is free shipping at $75+, which is up in a gray strip I'd skim right past. "Pykrete" is a funny name too. I don't know what it means.
- Would I click? Probably, because I'm already curious about the 1080 and the shoe photos are clear. Would I forward it to a friend? No. Nothing in here is worth passing along. I'd tap "Shop now" and see what it costs.

## What's working

- The hero shoe photo is big and easy to see on my phone, and I knew right away it was a sneaker email.
- The four-color grid lets me compare the shades quickly. The black pair looks like something I'd wear.
- This is the same shoe from top to bottom, so there's no confusion about what they want me to look at.
- "Shop now" is a clear black button, big enough for my thumb. "Find a store" next to it is handy too.
- The "Running / Walking / Lifestyle" links at the bottom are a nice touch, since walking is what I care about.

## What's weak

- There's no price anywhere on the shoe. I do the price-versus-quality math on every pair, and this gives me nothing to work with.
- The email says nothing about cushioning, arch support, or fit. Those are the reasons I'd buy a 1080 over a Hoka or a Brooks.
- It doesn't say who the shoe is for. Men's, women's, unisex? The colors lean women's (that mauve one), and I can't tell.
- Free shipping at $75+ is in a gray strip at the top, so I almost missed it. The $75 threshold isn't a deal on a shoe this pricey anyway.
- The text under the headline is small and light gray. I'd have to squint at it.
- Nothing in here tells me it's for a loyalty member. It treats me like a stranger.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price and a comfort line under the headline, like "The 1080 v14: plush cushioning for all-day walking, from $164." That one change would help most.
- 2. Tell me who it's for with a "Men's" label or a "Shop men's" button, so I'm not guessing.
- 3. Add a short line about fit, such as "Available in wide widths." That's a real reason for me to tap.
- 4. Make the free-shipping banner bigger, or pair it with something I can actually use.
- 5. Explain what "Pykrete" means, or drop it. A plain color name works better.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Probe coverage:** 12 material links were probed and 20 were skipped. No broken links were reported among the probed set, and the unsubscribe link is always probed. The skipped links (footer, utility, social) are unverified.
- **Placeholder asset URL:** `.bgwhite` contains `background-image: url('http://image.XXXXX.png')`. It is an unresolved placeholder, and it is plain `http://`, which clients can flag as mixed content. Any element using `.bgwhite` requests a dead URL.
- **Tracking pixel:** `https://link.email.newbalance.com/mo/egR1f966kc_818512772_12315932_1384054_23248453.gif` is the ESP open-tracking pixel. It has no alt text, which is harmless functionally. It should carry `alt=""`, `width="1"`, `height="1"`.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (QA-confirmed):**
  - `ShippingIcons_06.png`
  - `ShippingBanner_DarkMode_03.png`
  - the tracking GIF above
- **Template leftovers:** The two shipping assets live under `/custloads/818512772/NBUS/myNB_Welcome/UpdatedImages/`. That is a welcome-series path, which suggests a reused template for a "The 1080, in new colors" product email.
- **Generic `<title>`:** `<title>New Balance Template</title>` is a template name, not the subject.
- **`@font-face` rules have no `src`:** For example, `@font-face { font-family: proxima-nova,sans-serif; font-weight: 700; font-style: normal; }`. Without a `src`, the web fonts never load and everything falls back to Arial or Times New Roman. One rule also repeats `font-family` inside the same block (`proxima-nova` 700 italic). Quoting the family names (e.g. `'proxima-nova'`) would also be safer than the comma-separated values used here.
- **`<meta content="IE=edge" />` has no `http-equiv`:** It does nothing.
- **Conflicting `.bg1` rules:** `.bg1 {background-color: #ffffff;}` is later overridden by `.bg1 {background:#58546d;}`. Elements using `.bg1` render purple-grey (#58546d), not white, in light mode.
- **Doctype and namespace mismatch:** The HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype is paired with an `xmlns` attribute and self-closed `<meta ... />` tags. Browsers and email clients tolerate it, but it is inconsistent.
- **Dark-mode link contrast:** `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark) { a {color:#cf0a2c !important;} }` on a `#151415` background gives about 3.3:1 by my calculation. That is below the 4.5:1 WCAG AA threshold for normal-size text, and the rule applies to every link.
