# $59.98 Skechers? Yes, Please
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-16**
## Executive summary

- A deal-first Skechers promotional email that leads with a strong price anchor ($59.98) and tries to pack in four separate value propositions — clearance, an in-store game, savings codes, and loyalty points. The hero is clear and the price is prominent, but the email quickly devolves into a cluttered coupon sheet that dilutes attention and makes it hard to know what action to take. Useful to deal-seekers; forgettable to everyone else.
- Strong on price value. Weak on focus, visual hierarchy, and click intent clarity.

## What's working

- **Price anchor in the hero.** "$59.98" in the subject line and immediately visible in the email creates instant relevance. The "SMOOTH MOVES" headline and the Skechers lifestyle imagery give it decent aspirational framing.
- **Multi-product grid.** The Skechers product tiles above the fold show variety and reinforce that the deal applies to a real selection, not a single SKU.
- **50% Off Clearance module.** The bold yellow block is visually loud and should drive foot traffic for bargain shoppers. Easy to scan.
- **Dual coupon block.** The $10/$25 off tiered incentive is a proven mechanic. The barcode is present and visible, suggesting it's functional.

## What's weak

- **Too many competing CTAs.** The email contains at least four distinct offers: Skechers at $59.98, New Balance/Adidas Athletic Sale, 50% off clearance (in-store only), and $10/$25 off codes. None is clearly primary — they all fight for attention.
- **Brand collision.** New Balance and Adidas appear in the athletic sale module. Introducing competing brands right below the Skechers hero weakens the Skechers message and makes the email feel like a circular, not a brand campaign.
- **"Play. Shoot. Win." module is vague.** There's a contest/game mechanic visible but zero context is given in the scannable area — no prize callout, no compelling hook. Most readers will scroll past it.
- **In-store only caveat.** The 50% off clearance is in-store only, which is a significant friction point given that most email recipients are in a digital mindset. It's disclosed, but feels like a bait-and-switch moment.
- **Bottom third collapses into small print.** The barcode, code block, and legal text compress into a low-contrast, hard-to-read section. The barcodes are tiny on any mobile screen.
- **Preview text is broken.** The preview shows a raw URL instead of copy — a significant deliverability and engagement miss right at the inbox.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the preview URL.** Replace the broken preview text with something like "Grab Skechers styles starting at $59.98 — plus 50% off clearance in stores." This alone will lift open rates.
- 2. **Pick one primary CTA per email.** Lead the whole email with Skechers. If New Balance/Adidas need to be featured, make a separate send.
- 3. **Give "Play. Shoot. Win." a prize hook.** Even "Win a $100 gift card" in the module header would make it worth clicking.
- 4. **Increase barcode size.** Especially if this email is expected to be shown at a register — the current barcodes are too small to scan reliably from a phone screen.
- 5. **Add an online coupon code callout earlier.** The $10/$25 off appears very low in the email. Move the "SAVEME" code up, above the fold or adjacent to the hero product.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

```html

```

No dark-mode CSS is provided. Apple Mail and Outlook for Mac will force-invert colors on images and backgrounds — this requires explicit `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` overrides or the `color-scheme` values should be removed if not supported.

**Malformed meta tag — missing `http-equiv`:**

```html

```

Should be:

```html

```

The attribute `content` without `http-equiv` or `name` is invalid; the IE-edge directive will not be applied.

Minor, but indicates copy-paste template drift.
