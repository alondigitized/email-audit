# Drenched in color
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- I open this thing and the first two words I see — "Motion Party," in a blinking yellow banner — are the same two words HOKA used on me back in early June. At least that send told me it was Global Running Day, so there was a hook. This one just... repeats the party. Doesn't explain why the party matters. Doesn't tell me what shoe I should care about or why I should care today. It's colorful, I'll give it that. But a wall of neon pink, orange, blue, and green shoes doesn't tell a 62-year-old guy with sore arches anything useful.
- What really puts me off is the bottom half: a Strava Challenge. I've heard of Strava. I don't use Strava. I'm not training for anything. I'm just trying to find shoes that get me through a day of walking without my feet screaming at me by 3 PM. Asking me to join a running challenge is like handing a coupon for rock climbing to someone who just wants a comfortable chair.
- I mark this as read. I don't click. I don't forward it. Maybe I'd show it to a grandkid and say "look, HOKA makes these in really wild colors now."

## What's working

- **HOKA brand recognition is instant** — the name and logo are clear up top; I know this brand, I trust it, and I've bought from them before.
- **"The Party Starts Now" CTA section** is readable at a glance — the 2x2 product grid is clean and not cluttered.
- **Shoe names are labeled** under each product (Bondi 9, Speedgoat 7, Skyward X 2, Elevon 6) — I can actually read them without zooming.
- **Layout is clean** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum garbage; the email rendered correctly.
- **The Bondi 9 is in here** — that's actually a shoe I know has serious cushioning, and that's my category.

## What's weak

- **"Motion Party" again** — HOKA ran this exact theme in the June 3 email and it confused me then. Running it back three weeks later with "Drenched in color" as the subject line doesn't clarify it; it just makes me feel like their marketing team is stuck in a loop.
- **Every shoe shown is in a loud, neon-bright colorway** — the Bondi 9 is pink, the Speedgoat is orange, the Skyward X is purple-blue. I want a Bondi in navy or gray, not a shoe that looks like a highlighter.
- **The Strava Challenge section is wasted real estate on me** — if you're segmenting at all, I am not the Strava runner. This reads like the email team sent the same creative to every list.
- **Zero mention of comfort, cushioning, or fit** — the entire email is about color. Nothing about what these shoes feel like underfoot, which is the only reason I'm looking at HOKA in the first place.
- **No offer, no incentive** — no free shipping, no member price, no percentage off. Just "shop this colorway." That's not enough to get me off the couch.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Reframe the hero around one shoe, not four** — the Bondi 9 is the right anchor for my segment. Lead with it: "The Bondi 9 you already know — now in summer color." That bridges the colorway angle to the reason I'm actually here (cushion, all-day comfort).
- 2. **Cut the Strava Challenge for non-runner segments** — either segment this out of sends to casual/comfort shoppers, or swap that block for a product feature callout (arch support, rocker sole, whatever the Bondi 9's story is this season).
- 3. **Add one concrete incentive** — free shipping on summer colorways, member pricing, anything. Right now I have no reason to click vs. just browsing on my own time.
- 4. **Show a men's colorway prominently** — at minimum, give men's shoes their own row or callout. The pink Bondi front and center signals this send wasn't built with me in mind.
- 5. **Write a subject line that earns the open** — "Drenched in color" tells me nothing actionable. I need a reason to tap.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Bondi 9 just got a summer refresh`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New HOKA colorways — men's picks inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Same cushion you love, now in 4 bold new colors`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Motion Party Pack is here — shop men's styles now`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Duplicate `<meta name="viewport">` tags with conflicting values.** The first omits `user-scalable` (browser default is `no`); the second explicitly sets `user-scalable=yes`. The last-declared tag wins in most parsers, making the first one dead markup and the intent ambiguous. One tag should be removed.

  ```html
  <!-- tag 1 — no user-scalable -->
  <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
  <!-- tag 2 — conflicts -->
  <meta content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=yes" name="viewport" />
  ```

- **Malformed CSS — `.nav_padding` (inside `@media max-width:480px`).** Missing numeric value before `px`; the rule silently fails on mobile.

  ```css
  .nav_padding { padding-top:px !important; padding-bottom:px !important; }
  ```

- **Malformed CSS — `.nav_border`.** Missing both the numeric length and the color value; the border will not render.

  ```css
  .nav_border { border-top:px solid ; }
  ```

- **Empty `<title>` tag.** Affects screen reader accessibility and some email client preview rendering. Should contain the subject line or a descriptive string.

  ```html
  <title></title>
  ```

- **`<meta>` charset tag missing `http-equiv` attribute.** Without `http-equiv="Content-Type"` the tag is non-standard for HTML email and may be ignored by legacy clients.

  ```html
  <!-- current (non-standard) -->
  <meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
  <!-- correct -->
  <meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
  ```

- **Custom `@font-face` fonts will not render in Gmail, Outlook (Windows/Mac app), or most webmail clients.** `TheFutureHOKA` and `TheFutureHOKAMono` are served from `hoka.com/on/demandware.static/…`. This is expected behavior in email, but confirm fallback stack is acceptable where the font is blocked (Apple Mail and some mobile clients will render the custom font; all others will fall back).
