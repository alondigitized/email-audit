# Discover the Paris Tennis Collection
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- Adidas is back in my inbox — seventh time in recent memory — and once again I'm looking at something that has nothing to do with why I'd ever buy a shoe. A Paris Tennis Collection. Young athletic models in black tennis gear holding rackets. "A fusion of sporting codes, Parisian style, and exceptional performance." Not a single word about cushioning, walking comfort, or anything my feet care about.
- I'll be straight: I recognized adidas in the sender line, so I opened it. But the email lost me in about three seconds, same as the Spezial send last week and the Superstar nostalgia trip the week before. The hierarchy is clean, the images render fine, the logo is where I expect it — the execution isn't the problem. The targeting is. They keep mailing me like I'm a 28-year-old heading to Roland Garros.
- Mark as read. Don't click. Definitely not forwarding this to anyone I know.

## What's working

- **adidas name recognition** — I open their emails because I know the brand. That trust is real even when the content misses.
- **Clean, uncluttered layout** — The hero image renders crisply, the headline is large, and nothing is broken or overlapping. Easy to read at arm's length on a phone.
- **"Shop Now" button is visible** — It's in the top banner, clearly labeled. At least I'd know where to tap if I wanted to.
- **Topical campaign** — Paris Tennis Collection with the French Open season approaching is timely and coherent brand positioning. Just not for me.

## What's weak

- **Zero comfort language** — No mention of cushioning, arch support, fit, or anything a 62-year-old with tired feet cares about. The whole email is performance-sport and Parisian fashion.
- **Wrong demographic in the imagery** — Two young, lean athletes with rackets. I don't see one model who looks anything like me. Adidas knows I'm in this list — they've sent me seven emails — and still every send looks like a college campus photo shoot.
- **No offer, no price, no hook** — There's no sale, no percentage off, no member deal. Just "here's a collection." After seven sends, I'm still waiting for the first one to give me a reason to buy today.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — Seven adidas emails in six days. I'm starting to tune them out. This one doesn't break the pattern in any way that would wake me up.
- **Tennis is a dead end for me** — I don't play tennis. I walk. Comfort sneakers, slip-ons, maybe a light trail shoe. Tennis court performance gear is as far from my list as soccer cleats.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment me out of the tennis send, or add a comfort-walking block.** If adidas has walking/comfort shoes (Cloudfoam, Grand Court, anything cushioned), put one at the bottom. One product. One price. One tap. Right now I'm invisible in this email.
- 2. **Add a concrete offer.** Even a modest "Members get free shipping on orders over $60" gives me a reason to act today. A collection reveal alone asks me to browse with no incentive.
- 3. **Use a real human-scale subject line.** "Discover the Paris Tennis Collection" is category copy — it sounds like an ad banner, not a reason to open an email.
- 4. **Break the cadence.** Seven sends in six days with zero comfort relevance is training me to ignore adidas in my inbox. One well-targeted email a week would outperform this.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Comfortable enough for all day — new at adidas`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cushioned styles built for comfort, not just the court`

## Technical Audit — adidas "Discover the Paris Tennis Collection"

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has two confirmed infrastructure failures: a malformed redirect URL that produces a broken experience for at least one link, and a missing plain-text alternative. Several HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources and missing alt attributes are additional delivery and accessibility risks.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect URL**
The click-tracking wrapper contains a relative-style path `/g/edcc5028...` where an absolute URL is required:
```
unknown url type: '/g/edcc5028-0fb1-4ffb-98be-2fa4c060474c?txn=cbb54b00-...'
```
The decoded destination resolves to `https://dv.adidas.com/o/edcc5028-...`, but the tracking layer is emitting a malformed `/g/` path instead of the full HTTPS URL. Any recipient clicking this link gets a broken experience. The `dv.adidas.com` domain appears to be a dynamic/personalized content server — the `/g/` vs `/o/` path mismatch suggests a template substitution error in the redirect builder.

**[WARN] 17 tracking links skipped (not verified)**
All 17 click-redirect URLs route through `dv.adidas.com` or similar tracking domains. None were HTTP-probed, so additional broken links cannot be ruled out.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Two HTTP image sources (mixed content)**
`http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg` appears twice (same asset, duplicate `<img>` tags). Modern email clients that enforce HTTPS will block these — the "arrow" asset will silently disappear for a non-trivial share of recipients.

**[WARN] HTTP pixel/beacon**
`http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...` is also HTTP. This is likely an open/click tracking beacon; HTTPS downgrade here affects measurement accuracy.

**[WARN] Missing `alt` text on two images**
- Tracking pixel at `click.comms.adidas.com` — alt="" acceptable for a pixel, but not present at all.
- Hero/product image at `dv.adidas.com/o/edcc5028-...` — a non-decorative product image with no alt text fails WCAG 2.1 AA (criterion 1.1.1) and renders blank to screen readers.

**Font declarations duplicated**
`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` `@font-face` blocks appear in two separate `<style>` tags with near-identical `src` values (minor difference: second block omits `Calibri` and `Sans Serif` fallbacks). The duplication is harmless but indicates a template merge issue — the canonical fallback stack is inconsistent across the two declarations.

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text alternative is 0 characters**
The MIME multipart envelope contains no usable plain-text part. CAN-SPAM does not strictly mandate a text/plain part, but its absence:
- Triggers higher spam scores in SpamAssassin and similar filters (missing `text/plain` is a known signal)
- Breaks accessibility for plain-text mail clients and some corporate mail gateways that strip HTML

No other CAN-SPAM issues are identifiable from the available HTML (physical address and unsubscribe mechanism appear to be present in the footer based on the CSS class names `.mobile-footer-padding-block`, `.mobile-privacy-transactional`). Cannot confirm unsubscribe link target without full HTML.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The broken `/g/` redirect (item 2 above) is the primary continuity risk — recipients who click that link do not reach the intended landing page. No UTM parameters are visible in the decoded destination URL (`https://dv.adidas.com/o/edcc5028-...`); UTM attribution appears to be handled entirely by the `cp_tp` / `cp_cid` / `mi_cid` parameters in the tracking wrapper rather than appended to the final destination URL. This is an ESP-specific attribution pattern and is not inherently broken, but if the redirect itself fails, attribution is lost entirely.
