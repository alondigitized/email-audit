# Summer Wardrobe 🤝 Up to 50% off!
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-07**
## Executive summary

- Crocs sent me a "Up to 50% off + Extra 25% off sale items" email with a big headline and a quad-shot photo of their classic clogs. That's it. No button. No link. No "Shop Now." No products shown that a 62-year-old guy would actually want. The offer sounds decent on paper but the email basically dead-ends right after the hero image. I'm a Skechers guy, not a Crocs guy, so even the brand is a miss for me — but even *Crocs people* would struggle to act on this email because there's nowhere to go.

## What's working

- The headline is big and legible — I can read "Up to 50% Off" without squinting on my phone, no problem.
- The offer stacks nicely: 50% off AND an extra 25% on sale items. That combination got my attention for a second.
- The clog image is clean and uncluttered. I can see the product clearly.
- Fine print is present at the bottom, so the offer at least has visible terms.

## What's weak

- **No CTA button anywhere.** This is a big problem. After I read "Up to 50% Off," I'm supposed to do... what exactly? There's no "Shop Now," no "See Men's Sale," nothing. The email just ends with app store badges and social icons. I wouldn't know where to tap.
- **Not my brand.** I'm a Skechers guy. Crocs didn't earn a spot in my rotation. Even if I was curious, this isn't targeted at me.
- **Product imagery says nothing about men's or comfort.** I see a classic clog in four colorways — olive, white, cream, gray. No men's-specific framing, no comfort tech callouts, nothing that speaks to a 62-year-old guy's priorities.
- **The preview text is garbage.** All I got in my inbox was invisible Unicode spacer characters — that's the preheader stuffing trick. My inbox showed nothing meaningful next to the subject line.
- **"Exclusions Apply." with a period just floating there** with no link to explain what's excluded. That's the kind of fine print that makes me suspicious before I've even scrolled down.
- **App store badges as the call-to-action.** They put the App Store and Google Play badges where a shop button should be. I'm 62 — I'm not downloading a new app to browse a one-day sale.
- **No loyalty or member callout.** No personalization, no "Hey Walker" — they treated me like a cold stranger.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a CTA button above the fold.** "Shop Men's Sale" in a big green button, right under the hero headline. This is the single most important fix.
- 2. **Show me one or two specific products.** A men's clog or sandal at a specific price — "$29.99 was $59.99" — does more work than an abstract percentage.
- 3. **Fix the preheader.** "Extra 25% off sale styles — ends May 11" would actually make me more likely to open next time.
- 4. **Drop the app store badges.** Not the right ask for a discount email. Save that for an onboarding flow.
- 5. **Add a deadline more prominently.** The fine print says May 11 at 11:59pm MT — that's real urgency but it's buried in small gray legalese. Put it in the body near the offer.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Malformed charset meta tag (high priority)**

```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```

Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. The correct form is:

```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```

Without `http-equiv`, some older clients (Outlook 2007–2016) may ignore the charset declaration entirely, risking mojibake on special characters (em dashes, curly quotes, the `🤝` in the subject line).

**Issue 2 — 7 footer images served over HTTP (medium priority)**

All social/app-store/store-finder icons use `http://image.crocs-email.com/...`:

| Alt text | Filename |
|---|---|
| Download on the App Store | `9c36c0ef-333c-4e6c-bec8-9e0ac799d475.png` |
| Get It On Google Play | `13246762-1111-4174-bca8-2f84a6bf62a5.png` |
| Find Your Store | `c4df71ec-309f-44f4-b10a-27bb864eeb78.png` |
| Instagram | `e18949ff-2d29-4a62-9176-a67132186a90.png` |

| YouTube | `1187ef58-07fe-4985-8349-93e338d86694.png` |

Modern webmail clients (Gmail, Outlook.com) enforce HTTPS-only image loading and will block or show broken images for these assets. The body/hero images appear to use HTTPS — the HTTP issue is isolated to the footer template.

**Issue 3 — Tracking pixel missing alt attribute**

```html
<img style="visibility:hidden;display:none" height="1" width="1"
  src="https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?...">
```

No `alt=""` attribute. While the image is visually hidden, screen readers may still announce it as an unlabeled image depending on client. Standard practice is `alt=""` on all decorative/tracking images.
