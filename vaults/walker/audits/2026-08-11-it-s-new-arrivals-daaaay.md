# IT'S NEW ARRIVALS DAAAAY 📣
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-11**
## Executive summary

- Well, "New Arrivals Day" got my thumb moving but the second the page loaded I knew this one wasn't for me. Every single shoe on this page — heels, strappy sandals, wedges, ballet flats — is a woman's shoe. I scrolled through the whole thing looking for a sneaker, a slip-on, anything with a rubber sole and some cushion, and came up empty except for one red athletic shoe buried in the second row that turned out to be a women's style too.
- The "up to 40% off select Nike" banner at the top is the same trick DSW's been running on me all week — same headline I saw in Thursday's email, still just a swoosh logo with no actual shoe pictured. I've learned by now not to trust that banner means anything for me specifically.
- This one's an easy skip. Not clicking, not forwarding — my wife might actually like some of these heels, but that's her inbox, not mine.

## What's working

- The "UP TO 40% OFF SELECT STYLES FROM NIKE" banner up top is bold and easy to read on a phone screen.
- "Shop by Size" grid near the bottom is a nice, clear layout if I were shopping — big tap targets, easy to hit with a thumb.
- The $10 shoe donation VIP reward program is a genuinely interesting angle I haven't seen from them before.
- No broken images or layout glitches that I could see — everything rendered clean.

## What's weak

- Not one men's shoe anywhere in this entire email — it's wall-to-wall heels, sandals, and wedges.
- The Nike banner is the same bait-and-switch I flagged in Thursday's "40% off Nike" send — no actual sneaker shown, just a logo.
- "HEY, WHAT'S NEW?" and "THAT'S NOT ALL, FOLKS" — cutesy copy that doesn't land with me, feels aimed at someone younger.
- "SHOP WOMEN" and "SHOP ALL WOMEN" buttons repeated twice — there's no "SHOP MEN" button anywhere on the page.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment men from women — if DSW has my browsing history, send me a men's comfort/walking shoe grid, not the women's heel wall. This is the single biggest fix.
- 2. Show an actual Nike sneaker in that top banner instead of just the logo — a picture of the shoe on sale is what earns the click.
- 3. Drop the "HEY, WHAT'S NEW?" / "THAT'S NOT ALL, FOLKS" tone — a plain product-forward layout reads more trustworthy to me.
- 4. If there's genuinely nothing new for men this cycle, at least don't send it to me — or lead with something universal, like the shoe donation VIP program.
