# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-10-13**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I got as far as picking a shoe and then hit a wall. The menu and search are good for a comfort shopper. But Add to Cart didn't go through because no size was selected, and the cart page came up as a blank white screen. The logged-in experience never showed up: steps 2 and 3 look identical to the homepage, and the page still said "Sign in for free shipping & returns!" Yesterday was 3/10, and today's funnel is just as broken.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10, unchanged from yesterday.** Browsing and search work well, but I never completed an add-to-cart and the cart was empty and blank. A buyer can't buy, so the score stays near the floor. I can't see yesterday's screenshots, so I can't say whether any single step regressed. The one clear improvement is search, which returned relevant comfort results. The cart and login problems are still there.

## 3. What's Working

- **Menu drawer (step 4):** Large, well-spaced rows (Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections, Collaborations, Clothing & Accessories). It has a clear Back button and X, and I can tap it without squinting. It's the best step for a 62-year-old.
- **Comfort language up front:** The top bar has "Slip-ins, Wide Fit, Arch Fit, Max Cushioning." The Men's Shoes page opens with "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" tiles. That's what I shop for.
- **Search "comfort shoes" (step 9):** It returned 1,601 results. The first items are Slip-ins Contour Foam and a Relaxed Fit wedge, with prices and color swatches. Filter and Sort are clear.
- **Product page:** The title is bold, the price is $88.00, and there's a 4.5-star rating with 18 reviews. The Hands Free Slip-ins badge is easy to spot. The Add to Cart button is big, full-width and high-contrast.
- **Pricing honesty:** "Members get 25% OFF! Applied at Cart" is shown on every product.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart is blank (step 8).** The screenshot is a plain white page with no header, items or message. I can't tell if it was still loading, crashed or was empty.
- **Add to Cart didn't add anything (step 7).** The page showed "Please select a size" in red and the cart badge stayed at 0. The error is fair, but it appeared only after I tapped. The size grid should have been in view, or the page should have scrolled to it.
- **Sizes are mostly sold out.** 7.0 through 10.5 and 11.5 are crossed out with tiny bell icons. Only 6.5, 11, 12, 13 and 14 look available. For an average 62-year-old man, the common sizes are gone. The "notify me" bell is too small to find or tap.
- **Size buttons are small and tight.** They're about 55×38 px with little space between them. That's tolerable but not comfortable for older thumbs.
- **No visible login or personalization.** Steps 2 and 3 are the homepage again, so I never saw a popup dismissed or a login form. Nothing greeted me by name, and the page still told me to sign in.
- **The homepage hero is a lifestyle photo.** It has no headline or CTA in the first screen, and it pushes the actual products below the fold. The promo bars use small text.
- **No cross-brand comparison or price-vs-quality math.** Nothing tells me why $88 is worth it against Hoka, Brooks or New Balance. There's no cost-per-wear, no arch-support explanation and no returns comparison.
- **Small gray text** on the breadcrumbs and the red "Applied at Cart" lines is hard to read without zooming.
- **The nav chips are cut off.** "Max Cus…" runs off the screen, and there's no hint that it scrolls.
- **Step 4 never showed a Men landing page.** I got the menu drawer instead, and the screenshot has a dark overlay.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart first.** Make /cart/ always render a header, the item list or an empty-cart message, and a checkout button. Alert on blank renders.
2. **Show the size picker above Add to Cart, or auto-scroll to it.** Pre-select a size when only one is in stock, and give an inline error right at the size grid.
3. **Make the sold-out sizes clear.** Say "Sold out in these sizes," enlarge the notify-me action, and suggest similar shoes that are in stock in the sizes people buy most.
4. **Make size buttons bigger.** Use at least 44×44 px with more spacing, and a larger base font (16 px or bigger) for prices, promos and breadcrumbs.
5. **Put a comfort-first message at the top of the homepage:** "Hands-free. Arch-supported. Cushioned." with a visible shop button, and make the nav chips obviously scrollable.
6. **Add a short comfort-comparison module** on product pages: arch support, cushioning, weight, and what you pay per year of daily walking compared with other brands.
7. **Make login visible.** Confirm the sign-in flow works on mobile, and show a greeting, saved size and order history once signed in.
8. **Keep search as is,** and add "Hands Free" and "Arch Fit" quick filters to the results page.

## 6. Bottom Line

I'd like these shoes. The slip-ins pitch, the menu and the search all speak to a guy who wants comfort, and I found a shoe I'd consider. But I couldn't buy it. I got a size error, then a blank cart, and I never saw a logged-in experience. If this happened at the shop I'd go to Hoka or Famous Footwear instead. The comfort story is good, so the cart and size flow are the first things to fix.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

- **Journey integrity:** The run was only partly valid. Skechers' bot-protection layer (Kasada, `x-kpsdk`) returned HTTP 429 on its fingerprint endpoint (5 of the 6 network errors). `/cart/` also returned 429. The View Cart findings below describe a blocked response, not the real cart page.
- **Navigation:** The HTML samples for Log In and Men category both report URL `https://www.skechers.com/`, and their `<head>` content is identical to the homepage. Either those steps did not navigate, or the URL was captured before navigation finished. Findings for those steps are unreliable.
- **Metrics:** LCP and CLS were not captured for any step (`?`). Only TTFB is available.
- **Axe:** 18 violations were reported. Homepage has 10, including 2 critical and 1 serious. View Cart has 4. Search has 4. No axe results are listed for Log In, Men, Men > Shoes, Product detail or Add to Cart. The data does not say whether those steps were clean or not scanned.

## Accessibility

**Homepage (10 violations):**
- `button-name` [critical]: one or more buttons have no accessible name.
- `aria-required-parent` [critical]: ARIA child roles are missing their required parent role.
- `link-name` [serious]: one or more links have no discernible text.
- `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-main-is-top-level` [moderate]: more than one `main` landmark, and at least one is nested. This suggests templates are composed on top of each other.
- `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique`, `region` [moderate]: landmark structure is incorrect, and content sits outside landmarks.
- `heading-order` [moderate]: heading levels skip.
- `aria-allowed-role` [minor]: an invalid role is used on an element.

**Search "comfort shoes" (4 violations):** `aria-allowed-role`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region`. These are the same landmark and role issues as on the homepage, so they likely come from a shared template.

**View Cart (4 violations):** `document-title` [serious], `html-has-lang` [serious], `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one`. A page with no title, `lang`, `main` or `h1` is almost certainly the bare 429 error response, not a cart template defect. The cart's real accessibility is untested.

## SEO

- **Cart:** the 429 response has no `<title>` and no `<h1>` (`document-title`, `page-has-heading-one`). It is a transient bot-block, not an indexable page, but confirm that a rate-limit response never carries a 200 status or is cacheable at the CDN.
- **Homepage:** `<html lang="en">` is present. The truncated `<head>` shows no title, meta description or canonical, so those are unverified.
- **Other steps:** the Log In and Men category samples are the homepage, so category SEO could not be evaluated.

## Performance

- **TTFB:** Homepage 427ms, Log In 401ms, Men 401ms, Men > Shoes 62ms, Product detail 55ms, Add to Cart 55ms, View Cart 130ms, Search 57ms. All are within the "good" range (under 800ms).
- **First hits:** The homepage, Log In and Men figures (about 400ms) look like full document loads. The 55–62ms figures look like cache hits or client-side transitions. They may not be comparable measurements.
- **LCP and CLS:** Not captured, so there is no Core Web Vitals verdict.
- **Third-party load:** The `<head>` injects more than 30 third-party scripts. All load async, but the main-thread and network cost is unmeasured.
  - Tag managers and personalization: GTM, Monetate, Quantum Metric, Pulse Insights, Bloomreach (`brsrvr`).
  - Ad and social pixels: TikTok, Snapchat, Reddit, Pinterest, Meta, Taboola, Nextdoor, Yahoo, Bing, Impact, creativecdn (RTB House).
- **Duplicates:** `s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` appears twice, and `tags.creativecdn.com/...js` appears twice on the Men step. Duplicate tag injection wastes requests.
- **Font loading:** `webfontloader.js` comes from cdnjs, an extra third-party dependency in the font-loading path.

## Mobile Optimization

- **Viewport:** The page sets a `--vh` custom property (6.64px per 1vh, which is a 664px-tall viewport). This is the usual workaround for mobile browser-chrome viewport units and works as intended.
- **`<head>` content:** It was truncated, so I can't confirm the `meta viewport` tag.
- **Touch targets:** Axe doesn't flag any target-size violations, but those rules may not have run.

## Console & Network Errors

- **429 on `/<uuid>/<uuid>/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.864` (5 times):** This is the Kasada bot-detection fingerprint call, and the repeated 429s mean the automated session was rate-limited. Two follow-on effects:
  - Subsequent requests such as `/cart/` were blocked.
  - Measurements after the first block are partly unreliable.
- **429 on `/cart/`:** The cart document was blocked outright. This is the likely source of the View Cart axe findings.
- **"Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document" (4 times):** A script is registering an `unload` handler, which is deprecated. Chrome now blocks it by default under Permissions-Policy. The source is probably third-party. The handler also blocks bfcache eligibility, so it is worth locating.
- **"Unsupported Summarizer API languages…" (3 times):** This is a Chrome built-in AI API warning. A script, or possibly the test browser, calls the Summarizer API without a supported language. It is low severity but should be traced to its source.
- **GTM preview environment:** The GTM URL contains `gtm_auth=…&gtm_preview=env-1&gtm_cookies_win=x`, so a non-default GTM environment is loading on `www.skechers.com`. This could be intentional, but it is unusual for production and may serve unreleased tag configurations.

## Recommendations

1. **Re-run the audit** with an allowlisted IP or a Kasada bypass for the test agent, and with throttled step pacing. Until then, the cart, Log In and category results can't be trusted.
2. **Add navigation assertions** so each step verifies the URL or page title changed. Today Log In and Men both report the homepage URL.
3. **Capture LCP and CLS** with a `PerformanceObserver` that has `buffered: true` and waits for `visibilitychange` or a fixed idle window. Both are currently `?`.
4. **Fix critical accessibility issues on the homepage:** give every button an accessible name (`aria-label` or text) and correct the ARIA role and parent structure. Fix `link-name` for icon-only links.
5. **Fix the landmark structure:** make a single top-level `<main>`, add unique `aria-label`s to repeated landmarks, move `<aside>` out of nested containers, and wrap stray content in landmarks.
6. **Check the GTM `gtm_preview=env-1` parameter** in the production container snippet.
7. **Audit third-party tags:** remove the duplicate Yahoo and creativecdn injections and find which script registers the `unload` handler. Moving the pixels behind a consent-gated tag manager would reduce main-thread cost.
