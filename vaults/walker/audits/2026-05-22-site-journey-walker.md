# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 4/10 (prev 4/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-22**
## Full review
---

## 1. Executive Summary

I'm Walker Miles, 62, and I just spent time on skechers.com on my phone trying to find a pair of slip-ins I could actually buy. The navigation is cleaner than most and "Hands Free Slip-ins" is easy to find — that part was genuinely good. But by the time I got to the cart, the whole thing fell apart. Literally. Blank page. Nothing. And when I searched "comfort shoes," the first results it showed me were women's shoes. I'm a 62-year-old man. The site doesn't seem to know anything about me, even after I logged in.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: **4/10**

Same score as yesterday. The blank cart page is a critical checkout-killer. No movement on personalization, login confirmation, or search relevance. The men's nav earned some points back, but the back half of the funnel is broken.

---

## 3. What's Working

**Men's navigation menu is genuinely good.** The drawer is clean, big text, full-width tap targets — I could read every option without squinting. "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Extra Wide Fit" are right there as quick links at the bottom. Someone thought about who's shopping in this category. That's me.

**Hands Free Slip-ins category page is well-organized.** "286 Results," filter chips for Wide Fit / Extra Wide Fit / Medium Fit up top, Filter and Sort buttons clearly visible. Easy to orient myself.

**Product detail page is solid.** Big product name, 4.5 stars / 215 reviews, price ($110) front and center, BOGO 50% OFF deal in red below the price, and the Hands Free Slip-ins badge is prominent. That's all the information I need to make a decision, laid out where I can find it. The "Arch Fit" name is right in the title — that matters to me.

**Size/width selection is usable.** MEDIUM and WIDE are two big buttons. The size grid has decent tap targets. "ADD TO CART" is a full-width blue button pinned to the bottom — hard to miss.

**BOGO deal is prominent throughout.** The banner follows me across every page. I notice it, I understand it. That's good marketing.

---

## 4. What's Weak

**Cart page is a blank white screen.** This is the single biggest problem on the entire site. After going through the whole journey — finding the shoe, picking a size, hitting Add to Cart — I land on a completely empty page. Nothing. No items, no total, no checkout button. That's not a UX issue, that's a broken pipe. No purchase happens here.

**I have no idea if I'm logged in.** Step 3 was supposed to be Login. What I see is the exact same homepage with the same cart badge showing "0." No name, no "Hi Walker," no loyalty points displayed, no account state whatsoever. For a Members Only offer that's plastered on every page, the logged-in state is invisible.

**Search ignores my gender.** I searched "comfort shoes" and got 1,443 results — the first two products on screen are both labeled "Women's." I've been navigating the men's section, I'm logged into an account — and the search doesn't know I'm a man shopping for men's shoes. That's a waste of my time and the first thing that would make me close the tab.

**Homepage hero is not for me.** The "Red, White & Blue Collection" features a guy in his 30s in skinny jeans and a woman in a tennis dress. Not a single person who looks like they share my foot problems. I'm not saying I need a gray-haired model, but nothing on that homepage signals "this is for someone who needs arch support."

**Popup dismiss (Step 2) is unclear.** The banner text changes but there's no visible overlay or modal to dismiss. It's possible the popup didn't load, or auto-dismissed, but I didn't feel like anything was resolved — I was just left looking at the same page.

**No personalization anywhere.** Zero. No "welcome back," no "based on your browsing," no "you might also like" pitched at comfort walkers. If the site knows who I am, it's hiding it.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** This is a P0 bug. A blank cart means zero conversion from anyone who went through the funnel. Check cart rendering on mobile — the session/cookie state may not be persisting through to the cart URL.

2. **Confirm login visually.** Show my name or a "Welcome back" message in the header after login. The loyalty prompt ("Earn a $5 Reward") is on every page but means nothing if I don't know I'm logged in.

3. **Gender-filter search results by account context.** If I'm logged in as a male account and have been browsing men's shoes, search should default to men's results. At minimum, add a gender filter chip at the top of the results page.

4. **Move "Hands Free Slip-ins" to the horizontal scroll nav on the homepage.** It's currently buried under MENU > MEN. The horizontal nav bar has room — put it right after "Slip-ins." That's my first click every time.

5. **Show loyalty balance on the logged-in homepage.** The Members Only banner is doing work, but it's pushing me to log in when I might already be logged in. Surface my points balance or reward status instead.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

The middle of this journey — Men's nav to product detail — is the best I've seen on this site. Clean, readable, comfort-first messaging. But the cart is broken and the search thinks I'm shopping for women's shoes. Those two failures mean no sale today. If I were comparison-shopping against Hoka or Brooks, I'd have left after the blank cart and not come back. Score holds at **4/10** — no regression from yesterday but no progress either. Fix the cart and the gender-search issue and this could jump to a 6 or 7 fast.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Audit covers 8 steps of the mobile purchase funnel: Homepage → Login → Men category → Men > Shoes → Product Detail → Add to Cart → View Cart → Search. Key findings: a bot-detection system (Kasada) is rate-limiting automated requests, the View Cart page is missing critical HTML document metadata, and the site loads an unusually high number of third-party tracking scripts on every page load.

---

## Accessibility

**15 violations across 3 pages. 2 are critical/serious.**

**Homepage (8 violations):**
- `aria-required-parent` [critical] — An ARIA role is used outside its required parent container. Likely a widget (e.g., listbox/option, grid/gridcell) rendered without its wrapping role present in the DOM.
- `aria-allowed-role` [minor] — A native HTML element has an ARIA role that contradicts its implicit semantics.
- `heading-order` [moderate] — Heading levels are skipped (e.g., `<h1>` → `<h3>`), breaking screen reader document outline.
- `landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-unique`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` [moderate] — Multiple `<main>` landmarks detected, and landmark elements are nested inside other landmarks. This is a structural DOM issue, likely caused by injected content (Monetate personalization or GTM snippets adding markup inside existing landmarks).
- `region` [moderate] — Content exists outside any landmark region.

**View Cart (4 violations — most severe):**
- `document-title` [serious] — `<title>` element is absent or empty. The cart page is likely rendered client-side (SPA transition) and the title is not updated after navigation.
- `html-has-lang` [serious] — `<html>` element lacks a `lang` attribute. This is inconsistent with other pages (which correctly set `lang="en"`), suggesting the cart loads in a separate iframe or via a partial render path.
- `landmark-one-main` [moderate] — No `<main>` landmark present.
- `page-has-heading-one` [moderate] — No `<h1>` present.

**Search results (3 violations):**
- `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique`, `region` [moderate] — Same landmark nesting pattern as Homepage; likely the same root cause.

---

## SEO

- **View Cart missing `<title>`** — Cart pages are typically `noindex`, but a missing title still affects crawl quality signals and is a Lighthouse failure.
- **`--vh` CSS custom property** set inline on `<html>` via JavaScript (`style="--vh: 6.640000000000001px"`). This is a mobile viewport height hack applied synchronously; if the script errors, layout breaks. Not crawl-blocking but indicates viewport handling is JS-dependent rather than using `dvh` units.
- **Duplicate `vice-managed-src` scripts** — Yahoo `ytc.js` is loaded twice on Homepage and Men category (two identical `<script>` tags). No direct SEO impact but signals tag manager configuration drift.
- **Third-party script volume** — 20+ async third-party scripts in `<head>` (GTM, TikTok, Pinterest, Reddit, Bing, Snapchat, Taboola, Nextdoor, Impact, Tune, Bloomreach, Quantum Metric, Monetate x2, Stylitics, Pulse Insights, CreativeCDN). Each is a crawl/render dependency risk.

| Step | TTFB | Notes |

| Login | 42ms | Good |
| Men category | 42ms | Good |
| Men > Shoes | 29ms | Excellent |
| Product detail | 34ms | Good |
| Add to Cart | 34ms | Good |
| **View Cart** | **167ms** | ~5× higher than average |
| Search | 31ms | Good |

- **View Cart TTFB spike (167ms):** The 429 response logged against `https://www.skechers.com/cart/` directly explains this. The cart endpoint is being rate-limited by Kasada (bot protection), causing a redirect/challenge cycle before the page can be served. In production for real users this would manifest as a delayed or failed cart load.
- **Script blocking risk:** WebFontLoader (`webfontloader.js` from cdnjs, `async`) loads Mulish in 6 weights. The `wf-*-active` classes on `<html>` suggest FOUT (flash of unstyled text) is being managed via JS class injection, which is a CLS risk if font swap timing is misaligned.
- **Monetate loads two separate entry points** (`se.monetate.net/js/3/...` and `//se.monetate.net/js/2/...`) on every page. Double-loading the personalization engine wastes bandwidth and adds render-blocking risk.

## Mobile Optimization

- **`--vh` custom property** (`6.640000000000001px` = `664px / 100`) is a standard workaround for `100vh` misbehaving on iOS Safari. The floating-point precision in the value (`6.640000000000001`) suggests no rounding is applied — acceptable but worth normalizing to avoid subpixel rendering inconsistencies.
- **No CSP issues detected** in HTML source for core pages, but Stylitics stylesheet is blocked by CORS (see Console Errors). This means Stylitics outfit/hotspot widgets silently fail on mobile — no error state is surfaced to the user.
- **Script loading strategy:** All tracking pixels use `async`, which is correct. No `defer` abuse detected. However, Monetate's `entry.js` is loaded via `//` (protocol-relative URL), which is a legacy pattern — it will follow the page protocol but is less explicit than `https://`.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 Rate Limiting (Kasada bot protection) — 6 requests:**

All 429s target the same Kasada fingerprinting endpoint:
```
/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.416
```
Plus one against `/cart/`.

This is Kasada SDK v1.2.416 failing its device fingerprint challenge — consistent with automated/headless browser detection. The fp endpoint is being rate-limited because the session signature doesn't match expected browser signals. **This is expected behavior for automated test environments** but confirms the audit was conducted from a flagged session, which means the View Cart 167ms TTFB and cart 429 are artifacts of bot detection, not representative of real user performance.

**CORS block — Stylitics stylesheet:**
```
https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?base=hotspots&theme=skechers&version=b243d07f50
```
The Stylitics CDN is not returning `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com`. This is a Stylitics-side misconfiguration or the `version=b243d07f50` asset has expired/been rotated. The widget loads but renders without its stylesheet — broken visual state for outfit/hotspot components on affected pages.

## Recommendations

**Priority 1 — Fix (correctness/compliance):**
1. **View Cart `<title>` and `lang` attribute** — If cart is rendered in a frame or SPA route, ensure `document.title` is set on navigation and the root `<html>` element carries `lang="en"`. These are WCAG 2.1 Level A failures (Success Criteria 3.1.1 and 2.4.2).
2. **Fix `aria-required-parent` on Homepage** — Identify which component is rendering orphaned ARIA roles (likely a carousel or listbox). This is a critical axe violation and breaks screen reader interaction entirely for that widget.
3. **Stylitics CORS** — File a ticket with Stylitics to re-add the `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` header for the `skechers` theme endpoint, or pin to a version that serves valid CORS headers.

**Priority 2 — Reduce risk:**
4. **Deduplicate Monetate entry points** — Remove either `js/2/` or `js/3/` Monetate loader. Serving both is wasteful and may cause double-execution of personalization logic.
5. **Deduplicate Yahoo ytc.js** — One `<script>` tag should be removed from the tag manager container.
6. **Landmark structure** — Audit GTM and Monetate injected HTML to ensure third-party content isn't being inserted inside `<main>` or creating duplicate landmark roles.

**Priority 3 — Test infrastructure:**
7. **Kasada bypass for synthetic monitoring** — Work with Kasada to allowlist monitoring IPs/user-agent strings, or use a session token provisioned for testing. Current test results for `/cart/` and fingerprinting endpoints are not representative of real user experience due to bot detection interference.
