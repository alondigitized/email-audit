# Sale: $50 gift card for $40
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-25**
## Executive summary

- Well, at least this one's got a real number attached to it — $50 gift card for $40, that's a straight 20% off, I can do that math in my head standing in line at the DSW register. But then I get down two more scrolls and it's a soccer player in a red jersey standing in front of a hot pink backdrop, holding a ball, pitching me a signed World Cup ball sweepstakes. I don't play soccer, I don't collect signed balls, and I already told these folks that last week with the Real Madrid jersey email.
- The gift card idea itself isn't dumb — I'd actually use $40 toward a pair of Sambas or whatever they've got in a wide width, if adidas made shoes that fit my feet the way Hoka or New Balance does. But nothing in this email tells me the card can go toward comfort shoes for a guy like me. It's just "3-Stripes for less" and then straight into soccer-fandom territory again.
- Would I click? Maybe, just for the gift card math — buy $40, get $50 to spend, hard to argue with that if I was already going to buy something. Would I forward it? No. Mark as read and move on is about right here.

## What's working

- The "$50 gift card for $40" banner is dead simple and specific — no guessing what the deal is.
- There's an actual expiration window buried in the fine print (Aug 14–28) — a real deadline, even if it's not shown up top.
- Top black banner with "SHOP NOW" is a clean, unambiguous button I can tap without squinting.

## What's weak

- The hero image is soccer jersey fabric, not a shoe — same visual crutch this brand always leans on.
- Second half of the email is a sweepstakes for a signed World Cup ball — zero relevance to a guy shopping for comfortable walking shoes.
- Nothing on the page shows what I could actually buy with the gift card — no shoe, no price, no "here's what $50 gets you."
- "Own a piece of history" model is a young soccer player, not exactly speaking to me at 62.
- I've seen this same soccer-jersey-hero move from adidas three sends running now (Real Madrid kit on 8/23, MLS Archive on 8/18) — feels like the same three people run this account.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap the hero image for an actual comfort shoe — a Samba or a cushioned walking sneaker — and show what the $40 buys, since the card offer is the strongest thing in this email and it's wasted on jersey fabric.
- 2. Cut the World Cup ball sweepstakes entirely from this send — it drags a solid, simple offer down into "not for me" territory. Split it into its own campaign for the soccer crowd.
- 3. Add a men's/comfort category link right under the gift card banner — "Shop men's comfort styles" — so the offer has somewhere relevant to land.
- 4. Move the expiration date (Aug 14–28) up near the hero, not buried in six paragraphs of legal fine print at the bottom — that's the urgency that should be doing work for you.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Buy $50, pay $40 — gift card sale ends 8/28`
- **Subject Alt B:** `20% off adidas gift cards, this week only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stock up before the price on your next pair goes back up.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Limited-time gift card deal — shop shoes, gear, and more.`
