# In case you missed it
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- Yet another New Balance email — this is starting to feel like they've got me on speed dial. This one's an apparel push: hoodies, joggers, track jackets, modeled by young folks in their twenties. "Trackside Apparel" — sounds like something for my grandkids, not me. I scroll and scroll through hoodies and sweatpants before I finally hit actual shoes way down at "Complete the look," and even those are buried under a wall of clothing I have zero interest in.
- There is a real offer here — up to 30% off with code BTS26 — but it's stuck at the very top in tiny red text and then again at the very bottom, and I have to wade through five screens of joggers to get back to it. If I hadn't scrolled all the way down I'd have never seen the shoe grid at all.
- I'm not clicking this one. Nothing here is speaking to a 62-year-old guy who wants a pair of comfortable walking shoes — it's all "back to school" athleisure for kids. Marking as read and moving on, same as most of these lately.

## What's working

- The 30% off code (BTS26) is a real, specific offer, even if it's buried.
- I recognize the sender and the New Balance logo immediately, so at least I know who's talking to me.
- The shoe grid at the bottom does show real product — 9060, 530, 574 Core, 740 — models I've actually seen before.
- Clean layout, no broken images or overlapping text that I can see.

## What's weak

- The whole email is apparel-first — hoodies, joggers, track jackets modeled by twentysomethings. I didn't come here for a sweatsuit.
- "Trackside Apparel" as the hero doesn't tell me a thing about comfort, cushioning, or fit — it's just a name.
- I have to scroll past what feels like a dozen clothing shots before any shoes show up.
- No mention of arch support, cushioning, or sizing anywhere — the stuff that actually gets me to click.
- Subject line "In case you missed it" is vague filler; doesn't hint there's a discount or shoes involved at all.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the shoe grid and the discount code, not five screens of hoodies — if the offer's the reason to open, put it up top where I can see it without scrolling.
- 2. Put the 30% off code in the subject line or right under the hero image, not just in tiny red text at the top and bottom.
- 3. Swap the hero model/copy for something that speaks to comfort — one line about cushioning or all-day wear would go a long way.
- 4. Cut the apparel down to a couple images max and give the shoe section room to breathe — that's the section I actually care about.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 30% off shoes — code BTS26`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your walking shoes just got 30% cheaper`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns on 530, 574 Core, and 740 — through Aug 20`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Save 30% on comfort styles, no membership required`
