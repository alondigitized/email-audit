# More savings? Say less.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-27**
## Executive summary

- Crocs again, and I'm losing count. This morning's "2 Pairs for $50" email was clean, and now I get a send that says the 20% code "cannot be combined with the 2 for $50 promotion." So the two offers fight each other. By the time I've read all that, I'm doing math, which is what I said I liked about this brand's last email not making me do.
- The big "60% OFF" and COZY20 are easy to read on my phone. But then I get a $30 clog banner, a Grinch clog at 45% off, Minnie Mouse at 50% off, and an Afterpay block. That's five or six offers in one email. The three star-rated quotes ("I came for one pair," "I deserve a treat") are cute, but that's not what I shop on.
- Would I click? Probably not. I'd skim it, see another clog sale, and mark it read. I wouldn't forward it. My buddy who wears Crocs to the garden center already gets these.

## What's working

- The "60% OFF" headline and COZY20 code are big, black-on-white and readable on my phone without zooming.
- "Classic clogs starting at $30" is a clear price. I can judge that against Hoka or Brooks without a calculator.
- The "Shop Black Friday" and "Shop All Doorbusters" buttons are large, dark and easy to tap.
- The footer states the deadline, Sunday, November 29th at 11:59pm MT, which is a real end date.
- The "Free shipping on qualified orders" strip at the top is a trust cue.

## What's weak

- The stacking rule conflicts with the 2 for $50 deal I got earlier today. I can't tell which deal is better.
- Every product shown is a clog, with no walking shoe, no cushioned slip-on and nothing about arch support. The Grinch and Minnie Mouse clogs feel aimed at somebody else.
- The three review bubbles take up a huge chunk of the top and push the actual products down. The shoe looks like a fuzzy-lined clog, and no price is attached to it.
- "Exclusions Apply" and "(almost) everything" feel like fine print hiding in plain sight. That makes me trust the 60% less.
- The Afterpay section is a big block I don't need. I pay for my shoes.
- It's the fifteenth Crocs email in about two weeks, and the same "extra 20%" pitch I got on 11/25 and 11/26.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one offer per email. If the 20% code can't be combined with 2 for $50, send those on separate days or tell me up front which is better for me.
- 2. Put a comfort product in the hero, such as a lined clog or a slip-on, with a price and a line on cushioning or support. Skip the character collabs for guys like me.
- 3. Cut the Afterpay block and shrink the review bubbles.
- 4. Cut the cadence. Fewer, better-aimed emails would get me to open more.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use code COZY20 for an extra 20% off. Ends Sunday 11:59pm MT.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible markup is mostly sound: it declares `lang="en"`, has a viewport meta tag, uses a Gmail hack, a preheader and a mobile breakpoint at 639px. Issues are limited to malformed `<meta>` tags, one tracking pixel without alt text, and checks that couldn't be completed because the source is truncated or the headers are missing.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** QA skipped 21 tracking links (click-redirect domain `click.crocs-email.com`) without an HTTP probe, so destination status and redirect chains are unconfirmed.
- **Third-party pixel:** `https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&c_emailopened=Yes&d_dpuid=9153f33f…` is an Adobe Audience Manager open-event beacon. It has no alt attribute (QA WARN). Add `alt=""` and `width="1" height="1"` so screen readers and image-blocked clients skip it.
- **Open pixel:** `click.crocs-email.com/open.aspx?qs=…` is correctly sized (1x1, `alt=""`) and wrapped in a `height:1px;overflow:hidden` div. No issue.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Malformed meta tags:** `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` has no `http-equiv="Content-Type"` (or use `<meta charset="utf-8">`). `<meta content="IE=edge" />` has no `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`. Both are inert as written. Charset is usually covered by the MIME `Content-Type` header, but the in-document declaration is ineffective.
- **Generic title:** `<title>Crocs</title>` is the same for every send, which gives poor context in "view in browser" and screen-reader contexts. This is minor.
- **Missing alt text:** the `demdex.net` pixel is the only image QA flagged. Alt on content images couldn't be checked because the HTML was truncated.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unrendered tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`, `[FIRST_NAME]`) appear in the visible source. The remainder of the body is truncated, so this is not a full check.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Authentication-Results:** the header was not found (QA WARN). QA attributes this to the AgentMail relay, so SPF/DKIM/DMARC status is unknown rather than failed. Verify against the raw original message headers.
- **Unsubscribe and address:** the unsubscribe link, the physical postal address and the `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are not in the visible source or QA data. They are unconfirmed, not missing.
- **Sender alignment:** the From domain `crocs-email.com` matches the click-tracking domain `click.crocs-email.com`, which is good for alignment.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
UTM parameters and landing-page URLs couldn't be assessed. Every tracked link is wrapped in an opaque `click.crocs-email.com` redirect, and QA skipped probing all 21 of them. No landing page destination or UTM values were observed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Fix the meta tags: `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">` and `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge">`.
2. Add `alt=""` to the `demdex.net` beacon.
3. Re-run QA against the raw message, with full HTML and headers, to confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC and `List-Unsubscribe`.
4. Resolve the 21 click-tracking redirects with a follow-redirect probe to confirm final URLs, status codes and UTM presence.
5. Set a per-campaign `<title>`, such as the subject line.
