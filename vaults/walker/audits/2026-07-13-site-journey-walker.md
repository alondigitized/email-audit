# Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Date:** July 13, 2026 | **Yesterday's Score:** 4/10

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Top to bottom, this site still doesn't feel like it was built for me. The homepage is screaming at young athletes, the cart page loaded as a blank white screen — an outright broken step — and when I searched "comfort shoes," the first two results were women's shoes. Login left no visible trace that I was recognized. The nav panel and the product size grid were the only bright spots. Compared to yesterday, there's no meaningful improvement and at least one possible regression: the cart page is dead on arrival.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

No change from yesterday. The cart failure alone is a conversion killer. The search gender-mismatch and the absent logged-in personalization mean I'm not being served as a known customer. Skechers is leaving real money on the table with a buyer who came in with intent.

---

## 3. What's Working

- **Promo banners are clear.** "20% OFF Regular Priced Items — Code: SUMMER" is right at the top, legible, not tiny. I noticed it immediately and it stuck with me through the whole journey.
- **Men's nav panel is organized.** The slide-out menu showed "Shoes," "Comfort Technologies," "Collections," and — right at the bottom — "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Extra Wide Fit." Those last three are exactly what I'm looking for. Large text, good tap spacing. Easy to use.
- **Hands Free Slip-ins category page is well-structured.** The page title is big and clear. The fit filter buttons — Wide Fit, Extra Wide Fit, Medium Fit — are large blue pill buttons that are easy to tap. 313 results tells me the inventory is there.
- **Product name and price are easy to read.** "Skechers Slip-ins: Arch Fit D'Lites 330" at $130 loaded cleanly, big font, no squinting.
- **Members discount is prominent on the PDP.** "Members Get 20% OFF! Use Code: SUMMER" in red right under the price — that's $26 off, I'd notice that.
- **Size grid on Add to Cart is solid.** Thirteen size options, reasonably spaced boxes, easy to tap. The "Please select a size" error message is clear and shows up before the button, not after a failed tap.
- **ADD TO CART button is full-width, high contrast, big.** Best touch target on the whole site.
- **No aggressive popup.** The "popup dismiss" step just cycled the promotional banner — no intrusive modal blocking the screen. That's a relief.

---

## 4. What's Weak

- **Cart page is completely blank.** Step 8 was a white screen. Nothing. No products, no total, no checkout button. If I actually added something and hit "View Cart," I'd think I broke something and leave. This is the single biggest failure in the journey.
- **Login is invisible.** After the login step, the site looks identical to before I logged in. No "Welcome back, Walker." No name in the header. No points balance. No indication that the site knows who I am. For a 62-year-old who clicked through a loyalty email, that's disorienting — did it work?
- **Homepage hero is wrong audience.** "GAME ON!" with bright red, green, and blue retro athletic shoes. That's not me. That's a 22-year-old. Nothing on the visible homepage speaks to walking comfort, arch support, or slip-ins. I have to scroll or hunt for what matters to me.
- **Search "comfort shoes" returned women's results first.** 1,445 results sounds great until you see the first two are women's shoes — "Cozy Fit Pure Comfort" and "Relaxed Fit: Glide-Step Comfort," both labeled Women's. I'm logged in (supposedly) as a man who just browsed the Men's section. This is a failure to use any session context.
- **Only 7 reviews, 3 stars on the featured product.** The Arch Fit D'Lites 330 at $130 has 3 out of 5 stars and only 7 reviews. At that price point, I'm hesitant. I'd want 50+ reviews and 4+ stars before committing. Skechers isn't giving me confidence here.
- **No comfort credentials on the PDP above the fold.** I can see the Slip-ins badge logo, but there's no quick callout for arch support level, cushioning type, or "great for all-day walking." I have to scroll or trust the name alone.
- **No personalization anywhere.** No "Based on your browsing" suggestions, no loyalty points display, no saved size. The site treats me like a stranger at every step.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** A blank cart is a broken checkout. Whatever JavaScript or API call is failing there needs to be the top engineering priority. Every failed cart visit is a lost sale.

2. **Show logged-in state visibly.** Put my first name or a "Hi, Walker — 320 points" line in the header after login. Tiny change, massive trust signal.

3. **Filter search results by active session gender.** If I've just navigated Men's > Shoes and I'm logged in as a male account, default the "comfort shoes" search to Men's. Women's results appearing first is a basic personalization failure.

4. **Surface comfort credentials on the PDP above the fold.** A two-line callout — "Arch Fit insole | Air-Cooled Memory Foam | Hands-Free entry" — before the size grid would answer my biggest questions without scrolling.

5. **Swap the homepage hero seasonally for comfort shoppers.** "GAME ON!" is right for some buyers. But a returning comfort shopper deserves to land on something that says "built for your feet, not just your look." At minimum, the horizontal shortcut nav (Slip-ins, Arch Fit) needs to be more prominent — right now it's easy to miss.

6. **Add more reviews or surface Arch Fit category reviews.** Seven reviews on a $130 shoe is thin. Even pulling in aggregate ratings from the Arch Fit line would help close hesitant buyers like me.

The bones are there — the nav knows I care about Slip-ins and Wide Fit, the promo is clear, the size grid works. But the cart is broken, search ignores who I am, login doesn't acknowledge me, and the homepage speaks to someone 40 years younger. I came in with real intent to buy an Arch Fit slip-on. The site almost got me to the size selector, then fell apart. Holding at **4/10** — no regression on the parts that worked yesterday, but the blank cart is either new or newly confirmed, and it's a hard ceiling on any higher score.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Site runs on Salesforce Commerce Cloud (SFCC/Demandware), evidenced by `/on/demandware.static/Sites-USSkechers-Site/` asset paths and TTFB signatures. Primary technical risks are: Kasada bot-protection 429s breaking the cart flow, a View Cart page that drops its `<title>` and `lang` attribute (indicating a broken SPA render path), a CORS-blocked Stylitics widget, and ~20+ third-party scripts loaded per page with at least two loaded in duplicate.

---

## Accessibility

**17 violations. 2 critical/serious issues on View Cart are the most urgent.**

| Severity | Rule | Page | Root Cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical | `aria-required-parent` | Homepage | ARIA role used on an element outside its required container (e.g. `role="option"` outside `role="listbox"`) |
| Serious | `link-name` | Homepage | One or more `<a>` tags with no text content and no `aria-label` |
| Serious | `document-title` | View Cart | `<title>` element is empty or missing entirely |
| Serious | `html-has-lang` | View Cart | `<html>` tag lacks a `lang` attribute on the cart render path |
| Moderate (×3) | Landmark structure | Homepage, Search | Nested `<aside>` inside `<main>`, duplicate `<main>`, content outside landmarks |
| Moderate | `heading-order` | Homepage | Heading levels skip (e.g. h1 → h3) |
| Moderate | `landmark-one-main`, `page-has-heading-one` | View Cart | Cart page has no `<main>` and no `<h1>` |

The View Cart `html-has-lang` + `document-title` pair is the most concerning: the Homepage source shows `<html lang="en">` and presumably a title, so these attributes are being dropped on client-side navigation to the cart — a regression in the SPA routing layer, not a static-page issue.

---

## SEO

- **View Cart missing `<title>`**: If Googlebot crawls `/cart/` in this state, the page indexes with no title. Low crawl priority for cart, but still a signal of broken templating.
- **`<html lang>` missing on cart**: Hreflang inference breaks; multilingual ranking signals are lost for that URL.
- **`heading-order` violation on Homepage**: Non-sequential headings fragment the content hierarchy for crawlers.
- **20+ third-party `<script>` tags in `<head>`**: Even with `async`, these create DNS lookup overhead and compete with LCP resource discovery. No `<link rel="preconnect">` observed for high-priority third-party origins (Monetate, Bloomreach, Quantum Metric).
- LCP and CLS values were not captured — without these, Core Web Vitals scores for CrUX field data cannot be assessed.

Monetate loads both `v2` and `v3` entry scripts simultaneously on the Men category page — this is a configuration error that doubles the personalization overhead.

**Script bloat (from HTML source):**

Identified on Homepage and Men category `<head>`:
- Google Tag Manager + 3 `gtag.js` instances
- Monetate v2 (`//se.monetate.net/js/2/...`) **and** v3 (`https://se.monetate.net/js/3/...`) loaded concurrently
- TikTok pixel (3 separate scripts)
- `tags.creativecdn.com` loaded **twice** on Men category
- `s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` loaded **twice** on Homepage and Login
- Bloomreach (`brsrvr.com`), Quantum Metric, Snapchat, Reddit, Facebook, NextDoor, Taboola, Impact, Tune, Pulse Insights, WebFontLoader

**Duplicate scripts are a definite defect**, not by design. The `ytc.js` double-load and `creativecdn` double-load indicate a tag manager misconfiguration.

**WebFont loading**: Using `webfontloader.js` (v1.6.28, from CDN, 2019-era library) instead of `font-display: swap` on the `@font-face` declarations. This can cause FOIT/FOUT and inflate LCP.

## Mobile Optimization

- `--vh: 6.640000000000001px` set as an inline CSS custom property on `<html>` — this is a JS-computed viewport-height workaround for mobile browser chrome. The imprecise float value (`6.640000000000001`) indicates the script is using raw `window.innerHeight / 100` without rounding, which is a minor code quality signal.
- No `<link rel="preconnect">` observed for dominant third-party origins — on mobile, DNS + TLS negotiation for 15+ origins is measurably harmful to Time to Interactive.
- LCP and CLS not captured, so image lazy-loading and layout stability on mobile cannot be assessed from this data.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 — Kasada bot protection rate-limiting itself (5 of 8 console errors, 5 of 6 network errors):**

```
https://www.skechers.com/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/
  2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.522
```

This URL pattern (`x-kpsdk-v`) is the Kasada KapSolver fingerprinting endpoint. Getting 429s from your own bot-protection SDK is a symptom of either: (a) the SDK retrying too aggressively and hitting its own rate limit, or (b) the bot-protection system misclassifying the session (automated audit tool, headless browser) and throttling all its own challenge requests. Either way, this is generating noise in production error monitoring and indicates the Kasada integration is not gracefully handling the rate-limit case.

**429 — `/cart/` (1 network error):**

The cart page itself returned 429. This means Kasada classified the audit session as a bot and blocked the cart load entirely. Real users on slow connections or with certain browser configurations may hit this too. This is the highest-severity runtime issue in the data.

**CORS — Stylitics stylesheet blocked:**

```
Access to CSS at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?base=grid&theme=skechers&version=48eb0a00f5'
blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```

Stylitics (outfit/complete-the-look widget) is serving its stylesheet without the required CORS header for cross-origin fetch. This breaks the widget's styling. The fix belongs on the Stylitics CDN configuration, but the team should file a ticket with Stylitics and verify whether the widget is rendering broken or falling back silently.

## Recommendations

**P0 — Fix immediately:**

1. **View Cart title/lang regression**: Audit the SPA routing layer (or SFCC template) for `/cart/`. The `<html lang="en">` and `<title>` present on Homepage are being dropped on cart renders. Likely a missing server-side template merge or a client-side `document.title` reset that never fires.

2. **Cart 429 from Kasada**: The bot-protection challenge is blocking cart access for automated sessions. Work with Kasada to tune challenge thresholds or add an exclusion for session flows that have already demonstrated human behavior (product view → add to cart). Real users with VPNs, privacy browsers, or unusual network paths may be getting cart-blocked.

**P1 — Fix in next sprint:**

3. **Remove duplicate scripts**: `ytc.js` ×2, `tags.creativecdn.com` ×2, Monetate v2 + v3 simultaneously — all definite tag manager misconfigurations. Audit GTM/TrustArc container for firing rules that trigger the same tag twice.

4. **Resolve Stylitics CORS**: File with Stylitics support to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` on `web-assets.stylitics.com`. Until fixed, the widget CSS is not loading.

5. **Fix critical ARIA violation** (`aria-required-parent`): Inspect the element with the orphaned ARIA role on Homepage and either wrap it in the correct parent or remove the role.

6. **Add `aria-label` to empty links** (`link-name`): Icon-only or image-only `<a>` tags on Homepage need descriptive labels.

**P2 — Performance hygiene:**

7. **Add `<link rel="preconnect">` for top 3–4 third-party origins** (Monetate, Quantum Metric, Bloomreach, GTM) to eliminate DNS/TLS round-trips on mobile.

8. **Replace `webfontloader.js`** with `font-display: swap` on `@font-face` declarations to remove a script dependency and eliminate FOIT.

9. **Instrument LCP and CLS** — all 8 steps are missing these values. Without them, Core Web Vitals field performance is unauditable. Add RUM (Real User Monitoring) reporting for LCP/CLS per-route if not already wired.
