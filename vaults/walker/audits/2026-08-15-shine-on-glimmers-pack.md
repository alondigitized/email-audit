# Shine on: Glimmers Pack
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-15**
## Executive summary

- Well, HOKA's back in the inbox again — that's seven emails from these folks in about two weeks now, and this one's just a colorway push. "Get the Glimmers" with a tan Bondi 9 shining under some studio light — fine, it's a nice-looking shoe, but there's no substance here. No mention of cushioning, arch support, fit, price, nothing. Just "shimmering new colorways," which tells me exactly zero about whether this shoe would feel any different on my feet than the last six I've seen from them.
- Six shoe photos stacked up, split men's and women's, and then it's straight into runners sprinting down a road and a "Road Running Guide." I don't run roads, I walk. This whole email reads like it's built for someone training for a 5K, not a guy looking for something comfortable to wear to the hardware store.
- Wouldn't click, wouldn't forward. Mark as read and move on — same as the last several from this brand.

## What's working

- The tan Bondi 9 hero shot up top is clean and easy to see — no clutter around it.
- "Shop the Collection" button stands out fine against the black bar.
- Product grid is organized clearly, men's and women's side by side, easy to scan on my phone without pinching.
- Free Expedited Shipping offer at the bottom is at least a concrete, real perk, even if it's buried way down.

## What's weak

- Nothing here mentions comfort, cushioning, or arch support — just "shimmering" colors, which means nothing to me.
- Every model in the running shots is young and mid-stride sprinting — that's not who's reading this at 62 looking for a walking shoe.
- The "Road Running Guide" section has zero relevance to me; I'm not chasing a faster split time.
- No price, no percent off, no real offer until you scroll all the way to a shipping perk that requires signing up for texts.
- This is the seventh HOKA email in two weeks and it's yet another "new colors, same shoes" send — nothing new to say.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with a use-case, not a color story — tell me if the Bondi 9 or Clifton 11 is better for all-day standing or walking, not "shimmering."
- 2. Cut the road-running guide entirely for segments like mine, or swap it for something like a "comfort finder" quiz.
- 3. Put an actual price or discount in the hero, not just a shipping perk buried at the bottom.
- 4. Use an older, everyday model in the lifestyle shot instead of sprinters — show someone walking a dog or strolling downtown.
- 5. Ease up on frequency — seven sends in two weeks is starting to feel like noise, not news.
