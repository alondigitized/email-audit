# Last chance to save 30% off your purchase
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-11**
## Executive summary

- A plain brand email for once, and I'm glad. There's no pink flower sneaker. After the cart nags yesterday ("Your Items Are Waiting!", "Your cart expires soon!"), a clean 30% off banner feels like a breather. It says "Hi, Walker," shows my 1000 points, and puts the deal in big white numbers on a plaid background. I get the offer in two seconds.
- My problem is that it's a shotgun blast. Women, Men, Kids, three shoes each, and nothing that says "this one's for you." The men's row has a white Arch Fit-looking runner, a brown slip-on and a black hiking boot, but there are no names, prices or arch-support callouts. The "Last Chance" banner is also confusing. The fine print says valid through 10/11, which is today, but the banner says "thru 10/15." I'd click, because 30% off plus my points is a real incentive. Then I'd have to hunt for the comfort shoes myself. I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- "Hi, Walker" with 1000 points and a $5 reward link tells me they know me. It doesn't push me, which I like.
- 30% off is huge and I can read it without zooming on my phone.
- The orange SHOP NOW and SHOP MEN'S buttons are wide and easy to tap with my thumb.
- The men's row has a slip-on style in it, and slip-ons are what I wear.
- The footer is clean, and the "Shop Additional Styles" list includes WIDE, which matters for my feet.

## What's weak

- The "Valid thru 10/15" line in the banner doesn't match the footer, which says 10/11. I don't trust a deadline that contradicts itself, and the footer fine print is tiny.
- The shoes are small and have no names, prices or features. There's no mention of Slip-ins, Arch Fit or cushioning.
- Women, Men and Kids all get equal space, so the Men's section starts halfway down the email.
- "Last chance" on a sale with a shifting end date feels like pressure, and I've seen enough nagging this week.
- The exclusions ("select accessories, prior purchases, sale styles") sit in the fine print. I'm wary of a deal with that many carve-outs.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one comfort hero for men, such as a Slip-ins shoe, with its name, the sale price and "Hands-free, no bending."
- 2. Make one deadline consistent across the banner and fine print, and show it as a real date and time.
- 3. Put short feature tags under each shoe: Arch Fit, Cushioned, Wide Available.
- 4. Use my history to put Men first and shrink the Women and Kids blocks.
- 5. Move the main exclusions up near the button.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Walker, 30% off ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `30% off Slip-ins and Arch Fit, today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your 1000 points are still good for $5 off. Hands-free comfort, 30% off through tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Men's cushioned slip-ons and wide sizes included. Discount applies automatically at checkout.`

## Technical Audit

**1. Technical Summary**
The email has a confirmed broken personalization slot ("Hi ,") and several tracking-pixel defects: one malformed protocol-less URL and two non-HTTPS images. The HTML source is truncated, so findings below are limited to the visible head and the QA data.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- Malformed pixel URL: `<img src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1db…" width="1" height="1" />` has no scheme. Mail clients resolve it as a relative path against the message or CDN base, so it 404s or is never requested. The Return Path/Validity seed-tracking pixel therefore doesn't fire. It also has no `alt` attribute.
- Non-HTTPS tracker: `http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif?mi_u=8107766165&mi_ecmp=PRO_US_NONPUR_U_SITE_NONPURCH_EN_10112026_B`. It is an insecure third-party pixel, and its query string carries a numeric user identifier (`mi_u`). Gmail's image proxy and strict clients may block or rewrite it.
- Third-party data-sharing pixels: three `beacon.krxd.net/1x1_usermatch.gif` and `1x1_ad_impression.gif` calls (Salesforce DMP/Krux). Two pass a SHA-256 hash (`partner_uid=9153f33f…ced00`) and a subscriber ID (`partner_uid=121173391`). These are identity-sync calls, not just open tracking.
- The primary open pixel is `click.emails.skechers.com/open.aspx?…` (SFMC), which is fine.
- 41 tracking links were skipped by the QA probe, so click-through destinations are unverified.
- The plain-text part is 4198/5993 characters URLs (70%), which is a spam-filter risk. The long tracked URLs are bloating the text alternative.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- `o.gif` (ink1000.com) has no `alt` attribute (QA WARN).
- `dde00662-169f-447d-b0e2-fc65f6c2290c.png` at `http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/1/…` has no `alt` and loads over `http://`. It is a content image on the email's own CDN, so it may be blocked or trigger mixed-content warnings. Its missing alt means nothing shows when images are off.
- Meta tags are malformed: `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` and `<meta content="IE=Edge" />` lack `http-equiv`, so they do nothing. The charset declaration is ineffective, though the HTTP/MIME header probably covers it.
- The preheader is `display:none !important; font-size:1px`, with no padding characters (`&zwnj;&nbsp;`). Body text may leak into the inbox preview after "Shop best-selling comfort before the sale ends".
- `* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none }` blocks text-size adjustment, which can hurt accessibility for users who scale text.
- The `viewport` meta has `maximum-scale=1`, which blocks pinch-zoom on some clients.
- The root layout is a fixed-width `table width="640"` with no `role="presentation"`, so screen readers may announce it as a data table.
- Several tracking pixels are `display:none` or 0×0 with no alt text (minor).

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
- **FAIL: empty greeting slot.** The rendered greeting reads "Hi ," because the first-name field is empty and no fallback is set. This is a broken-experience defect. The SFMC AMPscript or personalization string needs a default, for example `%%=IIF(Empty(@firstName),"there",@firstName)=%%`.
- No unresolved raw tokens such as `%%...%%` or `{{...}}` appear in the visible source. The rest is truncated.

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
- The unsubscribe link, physical address and List-Unsubscribe headers are not in the truncated source or QA output, so I can't confirm them.
- The From address is `no-reply@emails.skechers.com`. A no-reply sender weakens reply-based engagement signals, and CAN-SPAM requires a functioning reply or opt-out mechanism, so the opt-out must be verified elsewhere.
- SPF, DKIM and DMARC results were not provided, so authentication is unverified.
- Privacy: the Krux/ink1000 pixels pass hashed email and subscriber IDs to third parties. This should be covered in the privacy policy and honor opt-outs (CCPA/GDPR).

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
- All 41 click-tracking links go through SFMC redirects and were not probed, so UTM parameters and destinations are unverified.
- The only campaign identifier visible is `PRO_US_NONPUR_U_SITE_NONPURCH_EN_10112026_B`, passed to Krux and ink1000 as `campaignid`/`mi_ecmp`. It is not confirmed on landing URLs.
- The subject says "30% off", and the offer and landing page can't be checked from the truncated source.

**7. Recommendations**
1. Fix the empty greeting by adding an AMPscript fallback ("Hi there,"), or drop the name when it's empty. This is the highest priority.
2. Correct the Return Path pixel to `https://pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=…`, or remove it if it's no longer used.
3. Move `ink1000.com/o.gif` and the `image.emails.skechers.com` PNG to HTTPS, and add `alt=""` to the pixels and descriptive alt text to the PNG.
4. Fix the meta tags (`http-equiv="Content-Type"`, `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`), remove `maximum-scale=1`, and add `role="presentation"` to layout tables.
5. Shorten the tracked URLs or shift weight toward text in the plain-text part to bring URL density below 70%.
6. Audit the third-party pixels (Krux, ink1000) for privacy compliance and whether they're needed.
7. Re-run the audit on the full HTML to verify the unsubscribe link, the address, List-Unsubscribe headers, authentication results and UTM parameters on the 41 skipped links.
