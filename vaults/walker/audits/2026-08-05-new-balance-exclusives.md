- The four sneakers underneath — 9060, ABZORB 2000, 740, FuelCell Rebel — are all chunky retro-runner styles shot on young models' feet and legs. Nothing here says "casual walking shoe for a 62-year-old" and nothing tells me what these actually feel like on. I scroll, I see sneakers, I move on.

- Free shipping banner up top ($75+) is at least a real, visible offer.
- Clean layout, no overlapping text or broken images that I can see.
- "Shop now" and "Find a store" buttons are both clear and tappable.
- Four products laid out simply in a grid — easy enough to scan even with my eyes.

## What's weak

- Model is a young woman lounging on the floor — nothing about this says "for you, Walker."
- "Night Lights" pack name means nothing to me and isn't explained — no colorway story, no functional reason to care.
- Zero mention of comfort, cushioning, or arch support anywhere in the copy — the stuff that actually sells me a shoe.
- No price, no percent-off, no member discount on any of these four sneakers — just "Exclusives," which is marketing-speak for nothing.
- These look like fashion sneakers (chunky retro dad-shoe aesthetic) — no walking/comfort framing, no men's-specific push despite a "Men" nav tab up top.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap the hero model for someone closer to my life stage, or at minimum add a men's shot alongside — right now this reads as a young-women's fashion drop.
- 2. Put an actual price or discount on the product tiles. "Exclusives" with no number attached gives me nothing to act on.
- 3. Add one line of comfort copy under each shoe — cushioning type, arch support, "all-day wear" — something I can compare against my Hokas and Brooks.
- 4. Lead with the men's or unisex angle if this pack comes in my size, not just women's styling shots.
