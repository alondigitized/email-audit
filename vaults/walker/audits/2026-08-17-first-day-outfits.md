# First day outfits
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-17**
## Executive summary

- "First day outfits" — well, that tells me right off the bat this one's not for me. No grandkids in my inbox, this is a "back to school" email, and every model here is a young woman in crop tops and bike shorts. I'm 62, I'm not shopping for anyone's first day of anything except maybe a walk around the block, so I scrolled right past the outfit section.
- Buried at the bottom of the "pair it with" grid there's actually some shoes I recognize — the 530, the 740, a couple of AC Runners — those are real New Balance styles I've looked at before. But there's zero mention of cushioning, arch support, or comfort, just names and prices missing. And then, same as the August 14th email, there's those wooden folding chairs again for no reason I can figure. Furniture, in a shoe email, twice in one week.
- Wouldn't click, wouldn't forward. Mark as read and move on — this is background noise from a brand that's sent me five near-identical or off-target emails in the last week.

## What's working

- The sale banner up top is clear — "up to 30% off select styles" with a straightforward "shop now" link
- I did recognize a few sneaker models by name (530, 740, 9060, 204L) toward the bottom
- Clean layout, no overlapping text or broken images that I could see
- Simple, uncluttered white space between sections — easy enough to scroll on my phone

## What's weak

- Whole hero and "First day outfits" section is aimed at back-to-school shoppers, not a 62-year-old guy
- Every model is a young woman — no demographic match for me at all
- Shoe tiles show product names but no price, no "comfort" or "arch support" callout, nothing that speaks to why I'd buy
- Those wooden chairs show up again, just like the August 14th email — makes me think somebody's mixing up campaigns
- No loyalty or member pricing shown even though I've bought from them before

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this list — send the school/lifestyle stuff to parents and give guys like me a "comfort essentials" version built around the walking and lifestyle shoes, not crop tops
- 2. Drop the wooden chair image entirely, or if it's a real collab, explain what it's doing in a shoe email
- 3. Put prices and a comfort/arch-support callout right under each shoe tile — that's the deciding factor for me, not the outfit around it
- 4. Lead with the shoes I'd actually consider (530, 740, AC Runner) instead of burying them under three outfit sections
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `New markdowns on walking shoes — up to 30% off`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Comfort styles just dropped to 30% off`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Cushioned everyday sneakers, now on sale`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `The 530 and 740 are marked down through 8/20`
