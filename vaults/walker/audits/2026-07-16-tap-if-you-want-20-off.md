# Tap if you want 20% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- After what felt like ten straight days of DSW sandal emails — some landing twice in the same afternoon — I was almost relieved when yesterday's "20% off starts NOW" finally pivoted away from beachwear. This one continues that same pitch, and I'll give DSW credit where it's due: the orange Summer Savings banner is big and visible, the discount is front and center, and the size shop and wide-width buttons are right there without hunting.
- But then I get past the hero and hit six shoes in a two-row product grid — every single one of them women's. Heeled booties, pointy-toe pumps, mary janes. Not one thing I'd put on my foot. I'm a 62-year-old man trying to find something cushioned that doesn't argue with my arch, and DSW clearly knows there's a Men's tab in the nav — they just didn't bother building this email around it.
- I'd mark it read and move on. Maybe tap the Men's nav link directly if I remember, but I'm not clicking any CTA in this body.

## What's working

- **Clean hero.** "SUMMER SAVINGS" banner is big, orange, and the first thing my eye lands on — the 20% off message doesn't require squinting or scrolling.
- **Wide width option.** "SHOP WIDE WIDTH" button sitting right under the size grid is exactly the kind of signal I look for. Comfort shoppers live and die on width, and DSW put it where I can see it.
- **Size navigation.** Tap a number, go straight to filtered results — that's how I actually shop on my phone.
- **VIP perks section.** Seeing the rewards callout near the bottom reminds me why I've kept this subscription. Free shipping and the birthday gift still mean something.

## What's weak

- **Every product in the grid is women's footwear.** Heels, booties, mary janes — I counted all six. As a man, nothing in that grid speaks to me. That's the whole ballgame right there.
- **"Hard YES" in the preheader.** Had to ask my daughter what that meant. That's Gen-Z slang dropped into an email presumably going to people who've had DSW reward cards since the Clinton administration.
- **Zero comfort language anywhere.** "Cushion," "arch support," "walking comfort" — none of it appears. This is pure fashion merchandising aimed at someone other than me.
- **Fine print wall at the bottom.** Dense legal text plus a barcode takes up a significant portion of the email. On a phone, that's a lot of tiny-font scrolling I'm not doing without my reading glasses.
- **Same offer, different subject.** This is the identical 20% off pitch from yesterday. When a promotion shows up two days running with barely a different wrapper, I start wondering if it ever actually ends.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment men into a men's version.** If DSW knows I'm a male VIP, showing me six women's heel styles is a wasted send. Swap in walking shoes, comfort sneakers, anything from the men's aisle. One segmentation switch flips this from irrelevant to genuinely useful.
- 2. **Replace "Hard YES" with something that sells the offer.** The preheader slot is valuable; spend it on what's in the sale, not slang aimed at a college student.
- 3. **Add one comfort-specific hook in the hero.** Even a single line under the discount — "cushioning you can feel" or "walking shoes included" — tells comfort shoppers we're welcome at this sale.
- 4. **Trim or collapse the fine print block.** A "Terms" link handles the legal requirement; the current wall of text makes the email feel longer than it is on mobile.
- 5. **Freshen the 20% framing.** If this discount is running another day, give it a new angle — a product category, a deadline, anything — so it doesn't read as the same promotion on a loop.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Walker, your 20% off works on men's comfort styles too`
- **Subject Alt B:** `20% off — wide widths and walking shoes included`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New arrivals in men's comfort. Your size is in stock.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Men's picks on sale now — see what fits`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility
**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). iOS Mail and some Android clients honor this.

**`text-size-adjust: none` conflicts with prefixed declarations:**
```css
* {
  -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%;
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%;
  text-size-adjust: none;   /* ← overrides the 100% values above */
}
```
The unprefixed `none` is more aggressive than `100%` — it suppresses user-initiated text scaling rather than just automatic OS adjustment. On supported clients this can break accessibility for low-vision users.

**Missing `http-equiv` on charset meta:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Should be `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />`. Without `http-equiv`, Outlook and some legacy clients may not pick up the charset declaration from this tag.

**Font format mismatch:** Futura PT is served as `.woff`; HelveticaNow is served as `.ttf`. TTF files are typically 2–3× larger than WOFF. No `format('woff2')` variant is offered for either family. No system-font fallback is declared inside the `@font-face` blocks themselves (fallback depends on the element-level `font-family` stack, which isn't visible in the truncated source).
