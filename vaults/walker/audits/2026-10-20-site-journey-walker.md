# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-10-20**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I got through the front of the store fine. The homepage loaded, the menu was easy, the men's shoe list had a "Hands Free Slip-Ins" tile, and the shoe page showed price and reviews. Then it fell apart. My Add to Cart tap was stopped by a red "Please select a size" error, the cart icon still said 0, and the cart page was a completely blank white screen. Search for "comfort shoes" worked, but the first two results were women's shoes. I never got a shoe into the cart. The login step also showed me nothing that told me I was signed in.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** That's the same as yesterday's 3/10, so no net change.

- I can't see yesterday's screenshots, so I can't point to specific regressions or improvements.
- The path to a purchase is still broken at the last two steps. A blank cart is a lost sale.
- The menu and the "Slip-Ins / Wide Fit / Arch Fit" bar are good, but they don't make up for the cart.

## 3. What's Working

- **Comfort labels up top.** The row under the logo says "Slip-ins, Wide Fit, Arch Fit, Max Cushioning". That's what I shop for. Whoever puts it first gets my attention.
- **The Men menu is easy to use.** The drawer has large, bold, well-spaced rows (Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, and so on), a clear Back button and a clear X. I could tap those without trouble.
- **Men's Shoes has tiles that fit me.** "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" are the first two. Filter, Sort, Size and Width buttons are also right there.
- **The shoe page shows the basics.** It shows $165.00, 4.5 stars with 181 reviews, and a Slip-ins logo. Klarna and Afterpay installment options appear below the price, which helps with price-versus-quality thinking.
- **The size buttons are big enough.** The size boxes are roomy and easy to tap, and sold-out sizes (12.5 and 14) are struck through.
- **The Add to Cart button is large and easy to find.** It's wide, dark blue and clearly labeled.
- **The error message explains itself.** It says "Please select a size" in red.
- **Search had plenty of results.** It returned 1,622 results, and the first two Slip-ins styles (Contour Foam, Relaxed Fit) at least sound comfort-related.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart page is blank white.** There's no header, no items and no message. I'd assume the site is broken and go buy from Hoka or Brooks.
- **Add to Cart didn't work as I'd expect.** The shoe page didn't have a size pre-selected, so I got the error and the cart stayed at 0. The error sits above the button, so it is easy to miss. On a phone I had scrolled down to the button and could barely see the problem.
- **The shoe page puts "Excluded from promotions" in orange right under the price.** Meanwhile the banner at the top says "20% OFF Sitewide" and "2 Pairs for $100". That is confusing, and it makes me doubt whether the email or banner deal applies to this shoe.
- **Nothing shows I'm logged in.** The Log In step looks identical to the homepage. There's no "Hi, Walker", no points balance and no personalized shoe suggestions.
- **No popup was visible.** Step 2 is the same as step 1, which is fine for me, but I can't tell whether a popup was handled.
- **Search results didn't match me.** The first two results are women's shoes, even though I'd been browsing Men's. The results header also says "Best Matches" with nothing about Men's or comfort.
- **No comparison or math content anywhere.** I saw no "vs Hoka" or "vs Brooks" content, no cost-per-wear, no explanation of arch support. The Men's Shoes page opens with a performance running shoe ("Exclusive"), not a comfortable walking shoe.
- **The text is small in places.** The "Details" links, the "Applied at Cart" lines, and the Klarna/Afterpay text are small for a 62-year-old without zooming. The top promo bar and the gray bar under it also take up a lot of the screen before any shoe shows.
- **The homepage doesn't give a clear next step.** The hero is a lifestyle photo of young people. There's no headline and no "shop comfort" button visible above the fold, and the category row cuts off ("Max Cus…").

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page now.** Render a visible empty-cart or loading state, and test that cart pages never come back blank on mobile.
2. **Make size selection obvious.** Either pre-select the most popular size, or scroll to and highlight the size row when someone taps Add to Cart without one. Don't leave the shopper looking at a button with the error out of view.
3. **Show a cart confirmation.** After a successful add, show a mini-cart or toast, and update the badge count.
4. **Clear up the promo conflict.** If a shoe is "Excluded from promotions," say so next to the banner, or hide the sitewide banner on that page. An explained exclusion is fine. A surprise at checkout isn't.
5. **Show the logged-in state.** Put a greeting and a "Recently viewed" or "Your size" row on the homepage so I can tell it knows me.
6. **Respect the gender context in search.** If I browsed Men's, show men's results first, or add a visible Men/Women filter chip.
7. **Add comfort content to the shoe pages.** A short "Arch support: high / Cushion: plush / Slip-in: yes" summary, plus an honest "How it compares" block, would do more for me than the lifestyle photo.
8. **Bump up the small text.** Make the promo, price-per-month and review links larger, and shrink the stacked promo bars so a shoe appears sooner.

## 6. Bottom Line

The browsing part feels like a store I'd consider. The menu, the Slip-ins tiles and the big buttons are friendly to someone my age. But I couldn't finish a purchase. The size error stopped me, and then the cart page showed nothing. If I'd been spending real money, I'd have left for another brand. The score stays at **3/10** until the cart works and tells me what's going on.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

- 8-step mobile journey. Of 17 axe violations, 2 are critical and 3 are serious. Console shows 24 errors (10 listed) and the network log shows 6 failures, all HTTP 429.
- The 429s come from the Kasada bot-protection fingerprint endpoint (`/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.917`, 5 times) and from `GET /cart/`. The cart request was rate-limited or blocked, so the View Cart step did not render a real cart. That makes the View Cart axe results unreliable (see Accessibility).
- LCP and CLS were not captured for any step (`?`). There is no Core Web Vitals verdict from this run.
- The HTML samples for "Log In" and "Men category" report the URL `https://www.skechers.com/`, the same as the homepage. Those steps may not have navigated, so their results may duplicate the homepage.
- The samples are serialized live DOM, not server responses. They show runtime state such as `wf-active` classes, a JS-set `--vh`, and injected async scripts. They can't be used to judge server-rendered markup.

## Accessibility

Homepage (9 violations):
- **critical** `button-name`: one or more buttons have no accessible name.
- **critical** `aria-required-parent`: elements with child-only ARIA roles sit outside their required parent.
- **serious** `link-name`: links have no discernible text.
- **moderate** `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region`. Together these point to a nested or duplicated `<main>` and landmarks that aren't top-level. Injected widgets or templates may be the cause.
- **minor** `aria-allowed-role`.

Search "comfort shoes" (4 violations):
- `aria-allowed-role`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region`. These repeat the homepage landmark problems, which suggests a shared header, footer or layout component.

View Cart (4 violations, likely artifacts):
- `document-title`, `html-has-lang`, `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one`.
- `/cart/` returned 429. These findings most likely describe the block or error response, not the cart template. Don't treat them as confirmed cart defects. Re-test once the cart loads.

Coverage:
- No violations were reported for Log In, Men, Men > Shoes, Product detail or Add to Cart. Given the URL issue above, don't read that as a clean result. Confirm axe ran on the correct DOM for those steps.

## SEO

- The homepage has `<html lang="en">`, which is correct.
- The truncated samples don't show `<title>`, meta description, canonical, hreflang, robots or JSON-LD. These can't be assessed from this data.
- The 429 on `/cart/` and the bot-protection fingerprint calls don't affect indexing here, since the cart isn't indexable. Confirm that crawler user agents (Googlebot, Bingbot) are not challenged or rate-limited by the Kasada layer on indexable pages.
- If the Log In and Men steps stayed on `/`, category-page SEO (titles, canonicals, pagination, product schema) was not tested.

## Performance

- TTFB: Homepage 433 ms, Log In 405 ms, Men 405 ms. These are well under the 800 ms "good" threshold.
- TTFB on later steps was 26–30 ms (Men > Shoes, Product detail, Add to Cart, Search) and 141 ms (View Cart, a 429 response). Values that low suggest cache hits, soft navigation or bfcache, not full document loads. They are not comparable with the first three.
- LCP and CLS are missing for all steps. Likely causes are an observer attached after navigation (no `buffered: true`) or soft navigations that don't emit LCP. Fix the instrumentation before drawing conclusions.
- Third-party load is heavy. The homepage sample injects tags from at least 15 vendors: GTM/gtag (4 requests), TikTok (3), Monetate (2), Yahoo, Pinterest, Quantum Metric, Impact, RTB House, Bloomreach, Bing, Pulse Insights, Tune/go2sdk and cdnjs WebFont. The Men sample adds Taboola (3), Snapchat (2), Reddit, Facebook, Nextdoor and Stape.
- Duplicate tag loads:
  - `s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` appears twice on every sampled page.
  - `tags.creativecdn.com/dhGobFICKSkk6J9nvQPw.js` appears twice in the Men sample.
- Fonts load through the JS WebFont Loader from cdnjs. Six Mulish weights are active (`wf-mulish-n3` to `n8`), which delays font use until JS runs and adds a third-party dependency. Native `@font-face` with `font-display: swap` and fewer weights would avoid both.

## Mobile Optimization

- An inline script calls `preventDefault()` on any click on `.js-ignore-click` until `app:JsLoaded` fires. Those links are inert until the app bundle finishes loading. Heavy third-party JS on mobile makes that window longer.
- The viewport height uses a JS-set `--vh` variable (`6.64px`, so a 664 px viewport). Native `dvh`/`svh` units remove the script and the resize-related layout shifts.
- Touch-target size, viewport meta and tap delay can't be assessed from the truncated samples. No mobile-specific rendering failures appear in the data.

## Console & Network Errors

- **Network (6):** 429 on `/{guid}/{guid}/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.917` five times, and 429 on `https://www.skechers.com/cart/` once.
  - Five fingerprint calls in one session suggests the SDK retried after being rejected.
  - The cart 429 means the Add to Cart → View Cart flow did not complete. Whether Add to Cart succeeded is unverified.
  - This is likely a consequence of automated traffic. Shared-IP or VPN users could hit the same limit.
- **Console, `Failed to load resource: 429`:** this is the same set of failures as the network 429s.
- **Console, `Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document` (5 of 10 listed):** some script registers an `unload` handler. Chrome is phasing this out, and it blocks bfcache. The source file isn't in the data, but third-party tags are the likely origin.
- **Console, `Unsupported Summarizer API languages…` (2 of 10 listed):** this is a Chrome built-in AI API call made without supported language options. The origin is unattributed. It could be a page or third-party script, or the browser or automation environment.
- Only 10 of 24 console errors were provided. The remaining 14 are unreviewed.

## Recommendations

1. **Re-run the cart step.** Use a throttled or allowlisted session so `/cart/` and `/fp` don't return 429. Then re-run axe on the real cart. Check for a retry loop in the Kasada SDK.
2. **Check journey navigation.** Confirm Log In and Men category actually navigate, since both report the homepage URL. Re-run axe and the vitals on the correct pages.
3. **Fix instrumentation.** Register the LCP, CLS and INP `PerformanceObserver`s with `buffered: true` on each hard navigation, and report soft-navigation vitals separately.
4. **Fix the critical and serious a11y issues.** Add accessible names to the unlabeled buttons and links (`aria-label` or visible text). Restore valid parent/child ARIA role structure. Reduce the page to a single top-level `<main>`, and give repeated landmarks unique labels.
5. **Remove `unload` listeners.** Find the registrant (DevTools bfcache test or a code search) and replace it with `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`.
6. **Deduplicate and audit third-party tags.** Remove the double loads of `ytc.js` and `creativecdn`, defer non-essential pixels until after interaction or consent, and review the tag inventory.
7. **Replace the JS font loader.** Use native `@font-face` with `font-display: swap` and fewer Mulish weights.
8. **Remove the click-suppression gap.** Server-render real navigation for links, or shorten the window before `app:JsLoaded`.
9. **Replace `--vh` JS with `dvh`/`svh`.**
10. **Complete the SEO check.** Capture full `<head>` output (title, canonical, hreflang, JSON-LD) for the category, product and search pages, and verify that crawlers aren't served 429s.
11. **Identify the Summarizer API caller.** Find who calls it and add supported `outputLanguage` and `expectedInputLanguages` options.
