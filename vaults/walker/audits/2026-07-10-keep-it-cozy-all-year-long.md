# Keep it Cozy All Year Long
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- The broken JSON schema preview is back. By my count that's eight Skechers emails in a row with that raw code garbage sitting in the preview slot — I've stopped being surprised and started being annoyed. I tapped anyway because Skechers has my loyalty points and my foot history, and "Cozy Fit" at least points in the right direction.
- Inside, the email does more right than the subject line deserves credit for. The BOGO — buy one, get one fifty percent off, or twenty-five percent off a single pair — is in a banner right at the top where I can see it. And then they break down the product by technology: Cozy Fit x Slip-ins, Cozy Fit x Glide-Step, Cozy Fit x Arch Fit. Those last two hit my exact checklist. Slip-ins means I don't bend over to tie anything. Arch Fit means my feet don't ache by noon. Having both in the same email, with dedicated shop buttons, is exactly how Skechers should be talking to me.
- What drags it back down: the hero is a woman sitting in a velvet chair surrounded by pastel pink shoes. That's not me. I had to look past a "SHOP WOMEN'S COZY FIT®" button before I found "SHOP MEN'S COZY FIT®." And this email landed two days after the Back to School send — which, as I noted that day, I am sixty-two years old and my kids are grown — and one week after the Final Call summer sale wrapped up. "Cozy All Year Long" in the first week of July just doesn't have me reaching for my wallet today. I'll probably click on the Arch Fit section eventually, but not urgently. Wouldn't forward it to anyone.

## What's working

- BOGO offer is in a banner above the hero — I see the deal before I even see the shoes.
- Breaking out Slip-ins, Glide-Step, and Arch Fit as separate sections with distinct shop buttons lets me go directly to what I want.
- "Cozy Fit x Slip-ins" and "Cozy Fit x Arch Fit" are both Walker priorities called out explicitly — hands-free and arch support in one email.
- "Skechers Plus Members" at the very top confirms this is addressed to me as a known customer, not a cold blast.
- The layout renders cleanly — no broken images, no overlapping text, nothing jumbled.

## What's weak

- The hero model is a woman; the first CTA button says "SHOP WOMEN'S COZY FIT®" — I have to get past her to find men's.
- "Cozy All Year Long" in early July, days after a summer Final Call sale just ended, fights the season instead of working with it.
- No expiration on the BOGO anywhere I can see — without a deadline, I have no reason to act today versus next week.
- The JSON schema preview is now at least eight consecutive emails. This is a template defect that Skechers has had time to fix and hasn't.
- Eight emails in eight days is too many. I'm starting to skim past the subject line without reading it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a man in the hero** — or at minimum use a split hero showing both. If the first image I see is a woman in a chair with pink shoes, I'm already filtering. Show me the Slip-ins on a foot that looks like mine.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline** — "BOGO ends Sunday July 13" transforms a browsing email into an action email. Right now the offer floats with no stakes.
- 3. **Fix the preview template** — this is send number eight or nine with raw schema.org JSON leaking into the preview slot. That's a broken liquid/ESP tag, not a one-off glitch. Ship the fix.
- 4. **Reframe "Cozy" for summer** — the Cozy Fit technology is year-round but "Cozy All Year Long" in July sounds like fall clearance. "Slip into Summer" or "Arch Support, No Laces — All Day" sells the same shoes without fighting the calendar.
- 5. **Throttle the cadence** — two or three sends a week would keep me engaged; one a day or more is burning goodwill fast.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical: All images served over HTTP**, not HTTPS. Gmail, Outlook.com, Apple Mail, and Yahoo all proxy or block non-HTTPS image sources. Affected assets include the Skechers logo, all footer icons (App Store, Google Play, Curbside Pickup, AfterPay, Find A Store, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube), and at least one content image:

```
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/1/dde00662-169f-447d-b0e2-fc65f6c2290c.png  ← logo
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/49468f73-...png  ← missing alt
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/0ec56c9f-...png  ← App Store
...9 total on this domain
```

**`image.emails.skechers.com` does not appear to have HTTPS enabled or the template is pinned to HTTP.** This is the single highest-priority fix.

**Two images are missing `alt` text:**
- `o.gif` (Movable Ink pixel) — acceptable for a tracking pixel but should have `alt=""`
- `49468f73-...png` (content image, unknown subject) — must have descriptive alt text for screen reader compliance

**`<meta http-equiv>` tag is malformed** — the charset meta is missing the `http-equiv` attribute:
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Minor impact since UTF-8 is typically the default, but it's a spec violation.

**Duplicate responsive breakpoints:** `@media (max-width: 480px)` is defined twice (once in `<head>` `<style>` block and once in a second `<style>` tag), and `@media (max-width: 375px)` and `@media (max-width: 640px)` share identical rule bodies. No functional breakage, but increases parse weight unnecessarily.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`** suppresses user font-size preferences globally. Should be `100%`, not `none`, to preserve accessibility for low-vision users who rely on browser zoom.
