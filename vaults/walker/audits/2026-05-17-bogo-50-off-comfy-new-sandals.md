- BOGO 50% OFF is front and center before I even have to scroll; can't miss it
- SHOP MEN'S button actually exists in this send — first time I've seen that this week
- New Balance shoes in the "New & Trending" section are a brand I genuinely buy

## What's weak

- The entire hero is women's sandals — thong slides, wedge platforms, none of it for me
- SHOP WOMEN'S is the bold green primary button; SHOP MEN'S is the gray secondary — I'm an afterthought in my own inbox
- "Find the perfect styles for summer" preview text is the most generic eight words in retail marketing
- This is the seventh BOGO sandals email from DSW I've opened this week; the drumbeat has gone from familiar to numbing
- No deadline on the offer — no reason to click today instead of next Tuesday, or never

- 1. **Segment by gender and flip the button hierarchy.** If DSW's CRM knows I'm a 62-year-old guy, SHOP MEN'S should be the green button. This single change would lift clicks for men without touching anything else.
- 2. **Put one men's comfort sandal in the hero.** A New Balance slide or a Skechers arch-support sandal with the BOGO badge on it beats a wall of women's wedges for getting me to scroll.
- 3. **Add a deadline.** "Ends Sunday" or "3 days left" gives me a reason to act now. Right now this has no expiration and no urgency whatsoever.
- 4. **Give the New Balance section more real estate.** If that's what pulls male subscribers further into the email, surface it above the fold or call it out in the preview.
- 5. **Change the creative hook.** Seven near-identical BOGO sandal emails in a week trains me to ignore them. Rotate the subject framing — lead with the brand, the fit story, the specific product, anything other than BOGO sandals again.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Missing `http-equiv` on charset meta (medium)**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, Outlook and some legacy clients may not apply the charset declaration, risking mojibake on non-ASCII characters (e.g., the emoji in the subject line `👏`).

**Issue 2 — Capitalized `Src:` in all @font-face declarations (low)**
```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/appboy/...") format('woff');
```
CSS property names are case-insensitive per spec, but stricter email rendering engines (some older WebKit builds in Apple Mail) have been documented rejecting capitalized property names in embedded `<style>` blocks. All 10 `@font-face` blocks are affected. System font fallbacks are defined, so failure is graceful, but fonts may silently not load on affected clients.

**Issue 3 — Stale cache-busting timestamps on HelveticaNow font files (low)**

Unix timestamp `1755182939` = August 2025 (~9 months ago). If Braze rotates or re-uploads these assets, the stale query string may serve a cached 404. Not currently broken but represents a latent risk.
