## Executive summary

- There's that pink floral D'Lites sneaker again. I don't know whose cart this is supposedly reminding me about, but it sure isn't mine — I don't recall ever putting a women's floral sneaker in a shopping cart, and Skechers has now sent me that exact same shoe under about five different subject lines this week alone. "We noticed you noticing us" is trying to be cute, but what I actually notice is that this email has nothing to do with me.
- Below the fold there's a little more effort — a men's Arch Fit and a couple of men's slip-ins mixed into the "more options" grid — but it's buried under a wall of kids' and boys' basketball shoes that I have zero use for at 62. ## What's working

- The 20% off Skechers Plus member banner with a clear code (PREVIEW) and a deadline (9/20) is the one honest, useful piece of this email.
- There's a men's Arch Fit Solano-style shoe and a couple of men's slip-ins mixed into the "more options" row, so somebody's algorithm knows I exist.
- Big, legible text and buttons — I can read this fine on my phone without zooming.
- Curbside pickup and "find a store" icons are handy if I actually wanted to go look at shoes in person.

## What's weak

- That pink floral D'Lites sneaker is not my cart, not my style, not my size range — this is the same shoe chasing me from "Your cart expires soon!" and "Did you forget something?" and now this one too.
- The "more options" grid is stuffed with boys' Garage Sneakers and kids' basketball shoes — I'm not shopping for grandkids here, this feels untargeted.
- The 20% off offer is buried below a giant "SHOP ALL BEST SELLERS" banner instead of leading the email.
- "We noticed you noticing us" is a cute line wasted on a product I never looked at, which just makes the whole personalization angle feel fake.

- 1. Fix the cart-abandonment targeting — if I never viewed or added that pink floral D'Lites, stop sending it to me. Lead with an actual men's comfort shoe I've browsed, like the Arch Fit or Slip-ins shown further down.
- 2. Move the 20% off Skechers Plus banner up near the top — that's the actual reason I'd open this email, not a hero image of a shoe I don't want.
- 3. Strip the kids'/boys' items out of my "more options" row entirely — segment by gender/age properly.
- 4. Drop the cutesy "we noticed you noticing us" copy when the product shown proves you weren't actually noticing me.
