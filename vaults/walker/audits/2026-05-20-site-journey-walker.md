# Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Date:** May 20, 2026 | **Yesterday's Score:** 4/10

---

## 1. Executive Summary

I made it from the homepage all the way to the product I wanted — the Max Cushioning Arch Fit 2.0 Slip-ins, $135, solid deal with BOGO stacked on top. The Men's menu was actually clean and easy to navigate, and finding Hands-Free Slip-ins took maybe three taps. That's the good news. The bad news: the cart page was a complete blank white screen. Nothing. I couldn't see what I put in, couldn't proceed to checkout. That's a broken purchase. On top of that, logging in showed zero effect — no "Hi Walker," no saved size, no personalization anywhere. And when I searched "comfort shoes," the first results were Women's shoes. A 62-year-old man with sore feet doesn't need to hunt past Women's results to find his shoe.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

No change from yesterday. The cart failure alone keeps this from improving. A shopper who can't see their cart doesn't buy.

## 3. What's Working

**BOGO deal is impossible to miss.** The blue top banner — "BOGO 50% OFF Applied at Cart" — is front and center on every single screen. I noticed it immediately and it stayed with me. That's good merchandising. At $135 a pair, knowing I'm getting 50% off a second pair is a real motivator.

**Men's navigation menu is excellent.** When I opened the menu and tapped Men, I got a clean, full-screen drawer with large text and generous touch targets. "Shoes," "Comfort Technologies," "Hands Free Slip-ins" — all laid out with proper spacing. Even "Wide Fit" and "Extra Wide Fit" were quick links right there without digging. This is exactly how a mobile menu should work for someone my age.

**Getting to Hands-Free Slip-ins was fast.** Three taps: Menu → Men → Hands Free Slip-ins. That's it. No maze, no confusion. The category page loaded with 283 results and width filters (Wide Fit, Extra Wide Fit, Medium Fit) right up top. Smart placement.

**Product page communicates the key stuff.** "Max Cushioning Arch Fit 2.0" — that name alone tells me what I need to know. The Slip-Ins badge is visible. Price is clear. 17 reviews with 4 stars is enough social proof. The BOGO callout appears again on the product page itself. Good.

**Add to Cart button is large and blue.** Full-width, pinned to the bottom of the screen. I can't miss it and I can tap it easily without squinting.

---

## 4. What's Weak

**The cart page is a blank white screen. Nothing.** This is a broken journey. After going through the work of finding the right shoe, selecting size and width, and tapping Add to Cart — I land on a white void. No items, no total, no checkout button. This is the single most damaging problem on the site. If this happens to a real customer, they leave. Full stop.

**Login showed no effect whatsoever.** Step 3 was supposed to be logging in, but the screenshot looks identical to the homepage before login. The Members banner still says "Login." The account icon is generic. No name, no points balance displayed, no "Welcome back" — nothing that says the site knows who I am. If I logged in and nothing changed, that's either a broken login or a completely wasted personalization opportunity.

**Search for "comfort shoes" leads with Women's products.** I typed exactly what I want — "comfort shoes" — and the top results showed two Women's shoes. 1,442 results with no pre-filtering by Men's. That's a usability failure. A logged-in male customer searching comfort shoes should see men's results first, full stop. Instead I'm scrolling past Women's Arch Fit Arcade and Women's Cozy Fit.

**Homepage hero is not for me.** Young man in jeans, young woman in a dress. Red, White & Blue Collection. Memorial Day is fine, but the models are 30 years younger than I am. I'm not seeing myself in this. Hoka and Brooks put active older adults front and center. Skechers keeps skewing young on the hero.

**The "Login to earn rewards" banner competes with BOGO.** There are two promotional banners stacked — the BOGO and the Members reward — and then a horizontal scroll of categories. Before I even get to the hero, I've been hit with three informational layers. It's a lot to parse on a small phone screen.

**Size/width selector requires two choices before Add to Cart, with no guidance on which to pick first.** The Add to Cart screen shows two red "Please select" warnings simultaneously. A less experienced shopper might not know whether to pick width or size first. The UX should guide me step by step, not yell at me twice at once.

---

## 5. Recommendations

**Fix the cart page immediately.** This is P0. It doesn't matter how good the rest of the experience is if the cart doesn't render. Test the cart flow on mobile, fix whatever is breaking the render, and verify before next review.

**Apply gender filter when a logged-in male user searches.** If I'm logged in as a male account, "comfort shoes" should default-filter to Men's. If I'm not logged in, surface a "Shopping for:" prompt at the top of search results. 1,442 unfiltered results with women's products leading is a frustrating experience.

**Show the logged-in state clearly.** When I sign in, show my name in the header, display my points balance in the Members banner, and pre-fill my saved size on product pages. Even a small "Hi Walker — 340 pts" in the account icon area would make me feel like the site knows me.

**Add a "Men's Comfort Shoes" featured collection to the homepage hero or a dedicated banner.** "Slip-Ins + Arch Fit + Max Cushioning" as a single message targeted at men 55+ would resonate more than a generic patriotic lifestyle shot with 30-year-old models.

**Step the size/width selector.** Lead with width first (since it's listed first), disable the size grid until width is chosen, then reveal sizes. One decision at a time, no double-warning state.

Until the cart works and personalization is real, I'm not buying here — I'll go check Brooks or hit the DSW app instead. Score stays at **4/10**. No regression, no improvement. Fix the cart and this jumps immediately.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Salesforce Commerce Cloud (Demandware) stack. TTFB is consistently fast (25–37ms) across most steps via CDN, with one significant outlier at View Cart (122ms, likely caused by the 429 rate-limit on `/cart/`). LCP and CLS data were not captured by the audit tooling — those are the highest-signal Web Vitals and should be instrumented. Two critical infrastructure issues dominate: Kasada bot-protection is throttling automated sessions into real 429s on the cart page, and the View Cart step renders a document missing both `<title>` and `lang`, indicating a broken document shell at checkout entry.

---

## Accessibility

**15 violations across 3 pages.**

**Homepage (8 violations):**
- `aria-required-parent` [critical] — ARIA role used on element outside required parent context. Likely a custom carousel or tab widget with incorrect role nesting.
- `landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-unique` [moderate] — Multiple `<main>` elements or `<main>` nested inside another landmark. Indicates injected content (personalization layer, Monetate, or GTM) adding structural elements without coordination.
- `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` [moderate] — `<aside>` nested inside `<main>` or another sectioning element.
- `heading-order` [moderate] — Heading hierarchy skips levels (e.g., `<h1>` → `<h3>`). Common in component-driven builds where each component owns its own heading without global coordination.
- `region` [moderate] — Page content outside any landmark region.
- `aria-allowed-role` [minor] — Role value inappropriate for the element type.

**View Cart (4 violations):**
- `document-title` [serious] + `html-has-lang` [serious] — The cart page is missing both `<title>` and `<html lang>`. This is a document-shell failure, not a component issue. The cart likely renders into a stripped document frame (modal, iframe, or SPA shell that bypasses the full HTML template). Breaks screen readers at the most conversion-critical step.
- `landmark-one-main` [moderate] + `page-has-heading-one` [moderate] — No `<main>` and no `<h1>` on the cart page. Confirms the document is structurally incomplete.

**Search (3 violations):**
- `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique`, `region` [moderate] — Same landmark-nesting pattern as homepage; consistent with a shared layout component bug.

## SEO

- The View Cart page missing `<title>` would cause it to be indexed with no title if crawled (unlikely for `/cart/` specifically, but the root cause — broken document shell — could affect other pages rendered via the same mechanism).
- `G-1234567890` placeholder GA4 measurement ID present in production HTML source (`gtag/destination?id=G-1234567890`). This is a dummy ID that will fire no-op pings to Google Analytics and pollutes GTM containers with unconfigured tags. Should be removed or replaced.
- GTM container is loading with explicit `gtm_auth` and `gtm_preview=env-1` parameters — these are production GTM environment credentials hardcoded in the snippet. Acceptable but confirms there is no staging/preview GTM separation in this deployment.

**TTFB by step:**

| Log In | 37ms |
| Men category | 37ms |
| Men > Shoes | 25ms |
| Product detail | 28ms |

View Cart TTFB is ~4–5x higher than all other steps. The `/cart/` 429 in the network log is the direct cause — the client is being rate-limited on the cart endpoint, forcing a retry cycle before the document is served.

**Third-party script load (homepage `<head>`):**
20+ third-party scripts loaded, including: TikTok Pixel (×2 files), Pinterest, Google Ads (DC + AW + GA4), Bing, Yahoo (×2), Reddit, Quantum Metric, Monetate (×2 — both `se.monetate.net/js/2/` and `se.monetate.net/js/3/`), Impact, CreativeCDN, Bloomreach, GTM, Snapchat, Facebook, NextDoor, Taboola, Stape, PulseInsights, Go2SDK, WebFont Loader.

- All are marked `async` or deferred via `vice-managed-src` (consent management gate), which limits main-thread blocking. However, the volume drives DNS lookups, TCP connections, and parse time that compound on mobile.
- `tags.creativecdn.com/dhGobFICKSkk6J9nvQPw.js` is loaded **twice** on the Men category page — a duplicate script inclusion.
- Monetate is loaded via two separate entry points (`js/2/` and `js/3/`) simultaneously. Redundant if these serve the same purpose.
- WebFont Loader (cdnjs v1.6.28) loads 6 Mulish weights (`n3`–`n8`). The `async` attribute delays FOUT detection but the font loading itself adds render-blocking risk for above-the-fold text. `font-display: swap` status is not verifiable from the source sample alone.

**LCP and CLS are unrecorded.** These are the primary ranking signals and conversion predictors. The audit tooling should instrument `PerformanceObserver` for `largest-contentful-paint` and `layout-shift` entries.

## Mobile Optimization

- `--vh` CSS custom property set inline via JavaScript (`style="--vh: 6.640000000000001px"`) — this is a standard workaround for the iOS Safari `100vh` bug. Correct pattern, no issue.
- No `viewport` meta tag visible in the truncated source, but its presence can't be confirmed or denied from the snippet. Should be verified independently.
- The Kasada fingerprinting endpoint (`/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.416`) fires multiple times per session. On mobile networks, repeated 429 retries on this endpoint increase time-to-interactive for bot-protection clearance, directly degrading mobile UX.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 Rate Limiting (6 occurrences):**

All originate from the Kasada bot-protection fingerprinting endpoint:
```
/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.416
```
Plus one on `/cart/` itself.

This is Kasada's client-side challenge (`kpsdk`). The 429s indicate the bot-detection challenge is not resolving successfully — the client fails the fingerprint challenge and the server rejects subsequent requests. In an automated audit context this is expected, but the `/cart/` 429 means a real checkout attempt was blocked, not just a telemetry endpoint. If this occurs for real users (e.g., aggressive bot-scoring on headless browsers used by accessibility tools, or edge cases in the challenge flow), it represents a direct conversion blocker.

**CORS Error (1 occurrence):**
```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?...' blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```
Stylitics (outfit/lookbook widget) CSS is being loaded as a cross-origin stylesheet without the required CORS header on Stylitics' CDN response. This breaks the widget's styling entirely for affected users. The `ERR_FAILED` immediately following is the cascade failure from this block. This is a Stylitics-side misconfiguration but Skechers engineering owns the vendor relationship and should file a ticket.

## Recommendations

**P0 — Fix immediately:**

1. **View Cart document shell** — Investigate why `/cart/` renders without `<title>` and `<html lang>`. If the cart is a SPA overlay, ensure the base document template includes these before the JS hydrates. Check whether a Demandware template variant is missing the standard `<head>` include.

2. **Kasada + cart 429** — A 429 on `/cart/` itself is a checkout blocker. Audit Kasada challenge configuration to confirm the failure scenario only affects non-human agents, not real mobile users encountering the challenge during high-traffic events (flash sales, bot waves). Add alerting on `/cart/` 429 rate.

**P1 — Fix this sprint:**

3. **Stylitics CORS** — Open a vendor ticket with Stylitics to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` to their stylesheet CDN responses.

4. **Remove placeholder GA4 ID** — `G-1234567890` in production is dead telemetry and should be cleaned from the GTM container.

5. **Deduplicate scripts** — Remove the duplicate `creativecdn` inclusion on Men category. Audit Monetate dual-entry-point loading (`js/2/` vs `js/3/`) to confirm both are necessary.

**P2 — Instrument and measure:**

6. **Capture LCP and CLS** — The audit has zero coverage on the two most impactful Web Vitals. Add `PerformanceObserver` instrumentation for `largest-contentful-paint` and `layout-shift` to the audit runner.

7. **Resolve Homepage ARIA landmark violations** — The `aria-required-parent` [critical] and duplicate `<main>` violations suggest a personalization injection (Monetate or GTM) is inserting structural HTML into the DOM without coordinating with the page template. Audit what GTM tags or Monetate actions modify the DOM on homepage load.
