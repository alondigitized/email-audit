# Plush cushioning, no compromise
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-12**
## Executive summary

- Right off the bat, HOKA speaks my language. "Plush Without Compromise" — that's the kind of headline that makes me slow my thumb when I'm scrolling through the inbox after dinner. I've been eyeing the Bondi line since a guy in my walking group made the switch last year, and seeing the Bondi 9 and Gaviota 6 laid out side-by-side with a plain-English explanation of the difference — neutral versus stable — that's actually useful information I'd otherwise spend 20 minutes hunting down on a website.
- But there's no price anywhere in this email. Not even a ballpark. I'm a guy who does the math: what does this shoe cost me today versus at DSW next week? The free shipping offer at the bottom caught my eye for about two seconds until I realized I'd have to sign up for texts to get it. Hard pass. I've got enough pings on my phone as it is.
- I'd click the Bondi 9 link to browse — maybe. But I wouldn't forward this to a friend, and I wouldn't feel any urgency to act today. Solid browse email. Not a buy email.

## What's working

- "Plush Without Compromise" lands where I live — cushioning and comfort are exactly why a HOKA email would get opened by me in the first place.
- Showing both Women's and Men's versions of each shoe clears up any confusion immediately; I know there's something in here for me.
- The "Plush and Neutral vs. Plush and Stable" split is genuinely helpful editorial — I've never seen a brand explain that distinction this simply.
- Text is large enough that I didn't have to pinch-zoom on my phone; layout stays clean and readable.
- Single-focus email — they're selling two shoes, full stop. No sidebar clutter pulling me somewhere else.

## What's weak

- No price anywhere. I'm not brand-loyal enough to click blind — put the number on the page.
- The free shipping hook requires signing up for texts. That's a transaction I didn't come here to make, and it sours the whole bottom third of the email.
- "Shop Best Sellers" is a lazy CTA — take me directly to the Bondi 9 Men's page, not a generic shelf.
- Hero image models look like they're in their 30s jogging through a college campus. Nothing visual says "this shoe is built for a guy like me."
- No reviews, no star ratings, no "4,200 walkers gave this 4.8 stars" badge. I trust other people's feet more than I trust a photo.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a price on the hero** — "Bondi 9, now $139 — limited time" would stop my scroll cold. No price means no urgency.
- 2. **Change "Shop Best Sellers" to "Shop Bondi 9 Men's"** — give me a direct door to the shoe the email is actually about.
- 3. **Ditch the text-signup shipping offer; replace with a flat deal** — "Free shipping on any order this week" with no strings. Simple and trusted.
- 4. **Add social proof under each shoe** — even just a star rating and a review count is enough. One line, big impact.
- 5. **Swap the hero lifestyle photo** — a man visibly in his 50s or 60s walking a neighborhood or trail would make me feel seen rather than marketed at.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Malformed charset meta tag**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Without it, legacy Outlook and some webmail renderers do not parse this as a charset declaration. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```

**[HIGH] Conflicting duplicate viewport meta tags**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
<meta content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=yes" name="viewport" />
```
First tag disables user scaling; second re-enables it (`user-scalable=yes`). Duplicate viewport declarations produce undefined behavior across iOS Mail, Samsung Mail, and Android Gmail. Keep one, decide on `user-scalable` policy.

**[MEDIUM] CSS syntax errors in `@media (max-width:480px)` block**
```css
.nav_padding { padding-top:px !important; padding-bottom:px !important; }
.nav_border  { border-top:px solid ; }
```
Both rules have missing numeric values and `.nav_border` is missing a color value. These declarations are silently dropped by all parsers, meaning `.nav_padding` and `.nav_border` elements receive no mobile override. Likely a template variable (`{{nav_padding_px}}`) that was not substituted.

**[LOW] Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some webmail clients (Yahoo, AOL) surface the `<title>` value as a secondary preview. An empty title is a missed rendering opportunity but not a breakage.

**[LOW] `font-display: swap` inside `@font-face`**
No email client honors `font-display`; the property is ignored. Harmless but adds payload bytes across five `@font-face` blocks.

**[INFO] `a { color: #000001; }`**
Near-black hack to defeat Gmail's link-color override — intentional and correct technique, not an issue.
