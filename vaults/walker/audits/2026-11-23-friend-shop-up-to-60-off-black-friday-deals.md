# Friend, shop up to 60% off Black Friday Deals
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-23**
## Executive summary

- Shoe Carnival again. This is the same Black Friday email I've been getting since the 13th, with a new "Monday, Unboxed" banner on top. The big white "60% OFF" on the red banner is easy to read on my phone without my glasses. The subject says "up to," though, so I don't know what I'd actually pay.
- After that it turns into a gift catalog. "From: You, To: You" and "Cozy Everything" are mostly women's and kids' boots and clogs. The Nike section has a couple of men's sneakers. I didn't see one cushioned, arch-supported walking shoe, and there's not a Skechers, Hoka or Brooks in sight. This is the same $10 / $25 coupon I got on the 20th, 21st and 22nd, so I'm tired of it.
- Would I click? Probably not. I'd skim it and mark it read. I wouldn't forward it. The one thing that might make me look is the Nike men's section, and it isn't what I shop for.

## What's working

- The "60% OFF" banner is huge and readable on a phone without squinting.
- The coupon code HOLIDAY is clear, and the barcodes near the bottom are handy for paying in the store.
- Prices "under $30" are labeled on many shoes, which fits the way I do price-versus-quality math.
- The Shoe Carnival logo at the top and the clean layout feel familiar and trustworthy.
- The Afterpay and Shoe Perks $10 reward blocks are easy to find if I care about those.

## What's weak

- Almost none of the shoes are for me. I see women's Mountain Whites, girls' Bearpaws, and kids' Nikes, with very little for a 62-year-old man.
- Nothing mentions comfort, cushioning, arch support or slip-on. It's all gift-and-fashion talk.
- The "up to 60%" claim sits next to a coupon of only $10 or $25 off, and the fine print is tiny. I can't tell what's really on sale.
- It's the same $10 / $25 coupon and Black Friday message I've seen all week, so it feels like the fifth near-identical send.
- The email is very long, and there are about eight different calls to action: Shop Sale, Get Store Coupon, Top Gifts, Cozy, Nike, Holiday Gift Shop, SMS, and Word Stride. I don't know where to tap.
- The "Friend" greeting in the subject doesn't feel like they know me. It reads like a mail merge.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the repeat. Send me one clear offer and one category, such as men's walking and comfort shoes, instead of a catalog of everything.
- 2. Lead with men's comfort brands like Skechers, with a plain label such as "Walking shoes, 30% off."
- 3. Put the coupon thresholds in plain text near the top, such as "$10 off $75, $25 off $150, code HOLIDAY."
- 4. Cut the number of sections and use bigger, clearer buttons.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Men's walking shoes, up to 60% off today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$25 off $150 — comfort shoes for Black Friday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Skechers, New Balance and Nike. Code HOLIDAY saves $10 or $25.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Cushioned walkers for men, deals end Cyber Monday.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's markup is mostly standard ESP boilerplate with mobile and Outlook/Apple/Samsung fixes. Two problems are confirmed: an unresolved `[UNIQUE]` placeholder in a tracking-pixel URL, and a malformed UTM parameter in another pixel URL. The HTML source was truncated, so the footer, unsubscribe and most links could not be inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unresolved token in a pixel URL:** `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]`. The literal `[UNIQUE]` was not substituted before send. The pixel will most likely fire with a non-unique or invalid ID, which corrupts open attribution.
- **Malformed query string in a pixel URL:** `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20261123_mwk_start_your_week_AM​`
  - A UTM key/value is nested inside the `t=` parameter (`t=utm_campaign=…`).
  - The URL ends in an invisible character (zero-width space, U+200B), which the QA output shows after `_AM`. This can break parsing or campaign matching downstream.
- **Unverified links:** 68 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped as tracking/click-redirect domains, so none of their destinations or HTTP statuses were checked.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on content images (QA-confirmed):**
  - `…/m/1/23a03225-7c93-49fa-988c-8821675cac1b.jpg`
  - `…/m/1/fe4e293c-bccf-488a-bbf7-db06bf5745de.jpg`

  With images blocked or read by a screen reader, these render as nothing or as the filenames.
- **Tracking pixels with no `alt`:** the two `emltrk.com` images above have no alt attribute. They should carry `alt=""` so screen readers skip them.
- **Link underlines removed:** the global rule `a, a:link, … { text-decoration:none !important; color:inherit !important; }` strips underline and color from every link. Unless buttons or other cues replace them, links are harder to identify, which is a WCAG 1.4.1 concern.
- **Malformed meta tag:** `<meta content="IE=edge" />` has no `http-equiv`, so it does nothing.
- **Small mobile text:** `.responsive-text` sets `font-size:12px !important` at max-width 600px.
- **Plain-text part:** QA flagged it as more than 70% URLs (13,339 of 16,617 characters). This hurts the plain-text experience and is a mild spam-filter signal.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The subject begins "Friend,", which is probably a default fallback for a missing first name. No broken first-name token is visible in the subject or the visible HTML. If this inbox was never given a name, that is expected behavior.
- **Unresolved `[UNIQUE]`** in the pixel URL (see section 2) is the only confirmed token failure.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Authentication:** the `Authentication-Results` header was not found. The QA note says to expect it via the AgentMail relay, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown and this is not a confirmed failure. The sender domain is `email.shoecarnival.com`.
- **Unsubscribe and physical address:** the truncated HTML doesn't show the footer, so the unsubscribe link and postal address can't be assessed. The `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers weren't provided either.
- No confirmed compliance defects.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- The only visible campaign value is `utm_campaign=SCA_20261123_mwk_start_your_week_AM`. The name reads like a generic "start your week" template, while the subject is a Black Friday promotion. This may mean a reused campaign ID or template, which would put the sends in the wrong reporting bucket.
- UTM parameters on the 68 click links, and where they land, could not be verified.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Fix the `[UNIQUE]` substitution in the `0di86k1v.emltrk.com` pixel. Add a pre-send check that fails on any `[A-Z_]+` or `%%…%%` token left in the rendered HTML.
2. Rebuild the `ieezzy5m.emltrk.com` pixel URL with a correctly encoded `t=` value. Strip the trailing U+200B from the campaign string.
3. Add descriptive `alt` text to the two product/content `.jpg` images and `alt=""` to both tracking pixels.
4. Restore a visible link cue, either underline or color, on text links. Alternatively, scope the `text-decoration:none !important` rule to buttons only.
5. Trim the plain-text part so it isn't mostly raw tracking URLs.
6. Add `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"` to the IE meta tag, or remove it.
7. Re-run the audit on the full HTML and raw headers. That would confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC, `List-Unsubscribe`, the footer address, and the final destinations and UTMs for the 68 redirect links.
