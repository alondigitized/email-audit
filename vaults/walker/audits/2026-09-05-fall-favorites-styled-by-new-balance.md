# Fall favorites, styled by New Balance
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-05**
## Executive summary

- Called it — "The Lookbook" is right there in bold caps two seconds after I saw the picture: young women in oversized sweats and cargo pants, styled like a photo shoot, not a shoe ad. This is the same beat as the Jack Harlow email from yesterday, just the women's version. There's a 30% off banner up top that caught my eye for half a second, but everything under it is apparel — jackets, shorts, cargo pants — before it even gets to shoes, and then the shoes it does show (9060, 530, 204L, 327) are the chunky retro lifestyle sneakers, not anything I'd call a walking shoe.
- I'm not clicking this one. There's nothing here about comfort, arch support, or slip-on ease — it's a fashion lookbook aimed at twenty-somethings, and I'm 62. Not forwarding it either, unless it's to my daughter who might actually wear the woven cargo pants.

## What's working

- The 30% off select styles banner at the very top is clear and I saw it immediately.
- Photography is clean, no glitches, no overlapping text — professional-looking email.
- Product tiles are laid out simply with name and price position obvious, easy to scan even for me.
- "Log in or create an account" and social links at the bottom are legible, not tiny mouse-type.

## What's weak

- Models are all young women in loungewear — nothing about this says "comfort shoe for a 62-year-old guy."
- Zero mention of cushioning, arch support, or slip-on convenience anywhere in the email.
- The shoes featured (9060, 530, 204L, 327) are retro-lifestyle sneakers, not the walking/comfort category I actually shop.
- This is apparel-first, shoes-second — I had to scroll past five clothing tiles before any footwear showed up.
- Same brand voice as the last few sends (kids shoes, runner's gear, now women's lookbook) — I still haven't seen anything for me this month.

## Recommendations

- 1. Send me a men's comfort-shoe email — a guy my age in a 990 or Fresh Foam walking shoe, arch support and cushioning called out in plain text, not buried in a lifestyle shoot.
- 2. Lead with the shoe, not the clothes — if there's a footwear discount, put a shoe front and center in the hero image, not sweatpants.
- 3. If New Balance is going to segment by gender/age at all, use it — I keep getting kids' emails, runner's emails, and now women's lookbooks. None of them are me.
- 4. Make the 30% off concrete — tell me which styles qualify instead of "select styles."

- **Subject Alt B:** `New arrivals built for all-day wearing`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cushioned comfort, walking-ready, on sale now`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `See which men's styles are included`
