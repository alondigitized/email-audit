# The US94 Official Licensed Product Collection
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-03**
## Executive summary

- Wrong brand, wrong collection, wrong guy. I'm a Skechers man — I'm on their loyalty program, I buy their Slip-ins, I think about cushioning and comfort. This email lands in my inbox from adidas, pitching something called the "US94 Official Licensed Product Collection" with photos of young people in streetwear poses next to a lowrider. Nothing in here speaks to me — no comfort technology, no men's casual shoes in my style, no discount, no reason to click. I'd read the subject in my inbox, think "that's not for me," and swipe it away in about three seconds.

## What's working

- The email is clean and renders well. The hero photo is big and bold — I can see it clearly without squinting, which I appreciate on a phone. The "SHOP NOW" button up top is obvious enough. Adidas is a name I recognize, so I didn't think it was spam. The headline font is large and legible. Production quality is solid — no obvious technical problems.

## What's weak

- Almost everything relevant to me is missing. There's no price. No sale. No "members save X%." No comfort story. No mention of men's shoes I'd actually consider buying. The models in the hero look like they're in their 20s, posed with a vintage car in what feels like a streetwear campaign aimed at sneakerheads born in 1994. I was born in 1963. "The fashionableness that has been passed down through generations" — that's not how I talk, and it's not what I'm looking for when I shop for shoes.
- The word "fashionableness" in the body copy actually stopped me — it sounds awkward, like it was translated. That hurt my trust a little.
- There's also no offer. No "15% off." No "free shipping." No membership perk. It's just "here's a collection, go shop." That's asking me to do all the work with no incentive.

## Recommendations

- 1. **If adidas wants to reach me, show comfort.** I know adidas has cushioned walkers and casual shoes. Lead with that, not a streetwear heritage drop aimed at Gen Z.
- 2. **Give me a reason to click.** Even "free shipping on orders over $75" would help. Right now there's nothing pulling me in.
- 3. **Show models my age.** One photo of a guy in his 50s or 60s in a comfortable pair of sneakers would go a long way.
- 4. **Fix that copy.** "Fashionableness" needs to be rewritten — it reads as a translation artifact, not a brand voice.
- 5. **Segment better.** If I'm on adidas' list as someone who bought or browsed casual comfort shoes, send me those. Don't blast the same streetwear drop to everyone.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — adidas "US94 Official Licensed Product Collection"

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard failures confirmed: a malformed internal link using a relative-style URL scheme that will 404 in most clients, and a missing plain-text MIME part that risks deliverability filtering. Eight images are served over HTTP, creating mixed-content blocks in security-enforcing clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Malformed link URL — broken experience**

The QA checker caught an unresolved internal redirect path:
```
unknown url type: '/g/245b98f3-626a-40ee-8d62-ef80196aed1c?txn=5c795600-4744-11f1-9b7c-76b4f6b00c3b'
```
The click-redirect wrapper at `https://dv.adidas.com/o/245b98f3...` resolves to a relative `/g/...` path rather than an absolute HTTPS URL. This is a broken destination — clicking the link will fail or 404. The `txn=` param suggests this is a product or collection deep-link that was not finalized before send.

**[WARN] 29 tracking links use a click-redirect domain**

All CTAs route through `dv.adidas.com` or `click.comms.adidas.com`. These were skipped by the HTTP probe but should be spot-checked manually post-send for redirect chain integrity.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] 8 HTTP image sources — may be blocked**

The same arrow icon (`b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg`) is referenced 6 times over HTTP:
```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
An open/pixel-tracking image is also served over HTTP:
```
http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019df0142f19-...
```
Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook on secure networks proxy or block HTTP image sources. Repeated broken arrows in a product collection email will visually degrade the layout.

**[WARN] Two images missing `alt` text**

- The hero/tracking pixel at `dv.adidas.com/o/245b98f3...` — no `alt` attribute detected
- The open-tracking image at `click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...` — no `alt` attribute detected

Missing `alt` on content images breaks screen readers; missing `alt` on tracking pixels is acceptable but should be `alt=""` explicitly to suppress reader announcement.

**Font declarations duplicated across two `<style>` blocks**

`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` `@font-face` rules are declared twice (in blocks 2 and 3). While harmless in most clients, it adds ~1 KB of dead CSS and can cause parse inconsistencies in older Outlook renderers using the Word engine.

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text MIME part absent**

```
Text version is 0 chars
```
CAN-SPAM does not explicitly mandate a text part, but its absence triggers spam scoring increases in SpamAssassin (rule `MIME_HTML_ONLY`) and Proofpoint. Several corporate mail gateways reject HTML-only messages outright. A minimal text fallback with the unsubscribe URL is required.

**Unsubscribe / physical address** — not verifiable from the truncated source, but footer structure with `mobile-footer-padding-block` classes is present, suggesting standard footer exists. Should be confirmed against full source.

**`referrer: no-referrer` meta tag present** — this suppresses HTTP Referer headers on link clicks, which is intentional for privacy but will cause referrer=`(none)` in any analytics that rely on the HTTP header rather than UTM params.

All other CTAs should be verified to confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` survive the `dv.adidas.com` redirect chain.
