# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 2/10 (prev 2/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-09-08**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

Well, I made it as far as clicking around, but I never actually got a shoe in my cart, and I never actually got logged in either — even though I tapped the button for both. The browsing part is fine: the menu's clean, the type is big enough, and I found a pair of Arch Fit clogs that looked promising. But the two things that actually matter for a purchase — logging into my account and getting my item into the cart — both just... didn't happen. No error, no confirmation, nothing. And then the cart page itself came up completely blank, like the page never loaded at all. If I were on my porch with my phone, I'd have put it down right about there and called it quits.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**2/10** — same as yesterday, no real improvement where it counts. Browsing and category navigation are perfectly serviceable, but the purchase funnel is broken in three places: login does nothing visible, add-to-cart is blocked without a clear next step, and the cart page renders blank white. A shopper who can't log in, can't add to cart, and lands on a blank cart screen isn't going to buy anything.

## 3. What's Working

- The hamburger "MEN" flyout menu (step 4) is genuinely nice — big rows, clear chevrons, easy to tap with a thumb, no tiny text.
- "Hands Free Slip-Ins" as its own category tile on the Men's Shoes page is exactly the kind of thing that gets my attention — that's the whole reason I'm here.
- The Arch Fit product name and the size grid (M4/W5.5 through M14/W15.5) are large, clearly labeled tap targets — no squinting needed.
- Search for "comfort shoes" pulled back genuinely relevant results — Slip-ins and a "Max Cushioning" model right up top. That part worked as advertised.
- The promo banner ("25% off Apparel & Bags") is legible and not obnoxious.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Login is a dead end.** I tapped "Log In" and the screen looks identical to before — no login form, no modal, no account name showing up anywhere. Either it silently failed or nothing happened at all.
- **Add to Cart never completed.** The size grid shows a red "Please select a size" warning, and the cart badge stayed at 0. If I don't pick a size, fine — but the flow should have made that unmissable before I hit the button, or scrolled me back up to fix it.
- **The cart page is a blank white screen.** Nothing — no header, no "your cart is empty," no products, nothing. That looks like the site froze or broke, not like an intentional empty state. For someone my age, a blank screen reads as "I broke it" and that's when I close the app.
- **"Unisex" label on a men's product** is a little confusing when I got there by tapping through Men > Shoes. I want to know it's built for a man's foot, not lumped into "anyone's."
- Only 1 review on the clog I picked — thin proof for an $80 purchase.
- No visible sign that logging in (had it worked) would have shown me anything personalized — no name greeting, no "welcome back," no saved size or past orders surfaced anywhere in this journey.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix or re-test the login flow** — a tap on "Log In" needs to visibly do something: open a form, show an error, or reflect a logged-in state. Silent no-ops are the worst outcome.
2. **Fix the blank cart page** — even an empty cart needs a header, nav, and a "your cart is empty, here's what you looked at" message. A pure white screen looks broken.
3. **Guide the add-to-cart failure better** — if size isn't selected, scroll/highlight the size selector clearly instead of just a small red line of text below it, and don't let the Add to Cart button sit there looking "pressable" if it's not going to do anything without a size.
4. **Clarify men's vs. unisex product labeling** on category pages so a shopper doesn't second-guess whether they're in the right section.
5. **Surface personalization after login** — if I log in, show me my name, my last order, or my saved size. Give me a reason to have bothered.

## 6. Bottom Line

Skechers knows how to show me a comfortable shoe — the browsing and search parts prove that. But right now the site can't get me from "I like this shoe" to "I bought this shoe." Broken login, a cart that won't take my item, and a blank page where my cart should be — that's not a rough edge, that's the whole front door being stuck. Score stays flat at 2/10 until the actual purchase path works.

## 7. Evidence

**Step 1 — Homepage:** Clean hero image of a couple walking, a top promo bar for 20% off sale styles, and a nav row with "Slip-ins / Wide Fit / Arch Fit / Max Cush[ion]" — all comfort-relevant terms right up front, which I liked. Cart badge at 0, account icon plain (not logged in). Easy to read, nothing crowded. No sign of a popup to dismiss.

**Step 2 — Dismiss Popups:** No popup actually appeared to dismiss in what I could see — instead a second promo line appeared ("Limited Time Only! 25% off Apparel & Bags — Applied at Cart"). Otherwise identical to the homepage. Not confusing, just not what the step name implied happened.

**Step 3 — Log In:** Identical to the previous screen. No login form opened, no modal, account icon unchanged, cart still 0. This is a dead end — tapping "Log In" appears to have done nothing visible. Not personalized in the slightest, because it seems login never actually occurred.

**Step 4 — Men category:** A slide-out panel with "MEN" and rows for Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections, Collaborations, Clothing & Accessories, plus product thumbnails peeking at the bottom. Big, well-spaced, easy to tap. This was the best screen in the whole run for someone my age.

**Step 5 — Men > Shoes:** "Men's Shoes" header, three category tiles (Hands Free Slip-Ins, Walking Shoes, Athletic Sneakers), a "Free pickup" toggle, Filter/Sort controls, and "741 results." Relevant and readable — the Hands Free Slip-Ins tile is exactly my speed.

**Step 6 — Product detail:** "GO 3D Arch Fit - 3D AirNex Clog," listed as "Unisex," 5 stars but only 1 review, priced $80.00, a large clean product photo with arrows to browse other angles. Arch Fit branding is front and center, which matters to me, but the "Unisex" tag was a little jarring after drilling into Men's Shoes specifically.

**Step 7 — Add to Cart:** Size grid (M4/W5.5 through M14/W15.5), "Size Chart" and "Don't See Your Size?" links, a red "Please select a size" warning, a QTY dropdown, and a highlighted "Add to Cart" button. Cart badge still reads 0 — the add never went through because no size was picked, and the page doesn't make that failure obvious enough before you tap the button.

**Step 8 — View Cart:** Completely blank white screen. No header, no items, no empty-cart message, nothing. Looks like the page failed to load rather than showing an intentional empty state — a real red flag.

**Step 9 — Search "comfort shoes":** 1,517 results, topped by "Skechers Slip-ins: GO WALK Joy — Cozy Fit Easy" ($85) and "Max Cushioning Elite 3" ($78.99–$105), with color swatches and filter/sort visible. Genuinely relevant results for the phrase I typed — this part of the site did its job.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Mobile journey across 8 steps (Homepage → Login → Men category → Men>Shoes → PDP → Add to Cart → Cart → Search) surfaces three classes of code-level defects: 17 axe-core accessibility violations (2 critical, 3 serious), repeated 429/403 network failures from third-party scripts, and incomplete Core Web Vitals capture (LCP/CLS not recorded on any step). The page is built on Salesforce Commerce Cloud (demandware.static asset paths) with a heavy third-party tag stack (GTM, Monetate, TikTok, Snapchat, Pinterest, Quantum Metric, PulseInsights, Bing, Reddit, Taboola, CreativeCDN, Impact, Bazaarvoice/brsrvr).

## Accessibility

17 violations total, 2 critical, 3 serious:
- **[critical] button-name** (Homepage): buttons without discernible text — screen reader users cannot identify their function.
- **[critical] aria-required-parent** (Homepage): ARIA roles used without required parent role context, breaking assistive-tech semantics.
- **[serious] link-name** (Homepage): links with no accessible name.
- **[serious] document-title** (View Cart): empty/missing `<title>` on the cart page.
- **[serious] html-has-lang** (View Cart): `<html>` missing `lang` attribute on this step (present on other steps, so likely lost during an async cart re-render or SPA-style transition).
- **[moderate]** landmark issues recurring across Homepage and Search: `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-unique`, `landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-one-main`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `region`, `heading-order`, `page-has-heading-one` — indicates duplicated/nested landmark regions (likely from injected third-party widgets such as PulseInsights survey markup) and non-top-level `<main>`/`<aside>` elements.
- **[minor] aria-allowed-role**: invalid role values on Homepage and Search.

Pattern: the View Cart failures (missing title/lang/h1/main) suggest that step renders a distinct DOM state (possibly a partial/AJAX cart fragment) that lacks the document-level metadata present elsewhere.

## SEO

- `<html lang="en">` present on all steps except View Cart, where `html-has-lang` fails — a crawlable/indexable page missing a core signal.
- View Cart also fails `document-title` — an empty title tag on an indexable URL is an SEO defect, not just an a11y one.
- Heading structure issues (`heading-order`, `page-has-heading-one`) on Homepage/Search reduce content hierarchy clarity for crawlers.
- Large volume of render-blocking/parser-blocking third-party `<script>` tags in `<head>` (20+ async/vice-managed scripts before app content) — does not block indexing directly but inflates DOM parse time ahead of primary content.

## Performance

TTFB by step: Homepage 491ms, Login 569ms, Men category 569ms, Men>Shoes 58ms, PDP 58ms, Add to Cart 58ms, View Cart 127ms, Search 67ms.

- Homepage/Login/Men-category TTFB (491–569ms) is elevated for a server-rendered commerce page; the drop to 58ms on subsequent steps (Men>Shoes, PDP, Add to Cart) indicates those responses are served from cache (CDN/edge or client-side route) while the first three are hitting origin/dynamic rendering.
- LCP and CLS were not captured for any step — instrumentation gap. Given the third-party script volume (~20+ tags per page including multiple font/webfont loaders, GTM, ad pixels), LCP is at meaningful risk of delay and CLS risk from late-loading widgets (survey banners, personalization content from Monetate). Recommend re-running with a tool that captures these (Lighthouse/PageSpeed Insights or CrUX field data) before drawing conclusions.
- Repeated 429 (Too Many Requests) responses from the same fingerprinting endpoint (`/fp?x-kpsdk-v=...`, a Kasada bot-detection script) fired 5 times across the session — the client is retrying a rate-limited call rather than backing off, adding wasted network/CPU overhead on every navigation.

## Mobile Optimization

- Viewport unit workaround detected (`style="--vh: 6.64px"` custom property on `<html>`) — indicates a JS-based `100vh` mobile-viewport fix, a common pattern to compensate for mobile browser chrome but adds a layout-dependent JS execution on load that can contribute to CLS if applied post-render.
- No mobile-specific meta/viewport issues surfaced in the provided HTML samples beyond the above.
- 3 of 8 steps (Men>Shoes, PDP, Add to Cart) show 58ms TTFB suggesting effective caching for deeper funnel steps on mobile, which is favorable.

## Console & Network Errors

18 console errors / 9 distinct network failures:
- **429 (rate-limited), 5 occurrences**: `www.skechers.com/.../fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.779` — Kasada bot-mitigation fingerprinting endpoint, including one against `/cart/` itself. Repeated rate-limiting on a first-party path during a single session suggests either overly aggressive bot-protection thresholds or a client-side retry loop re-firing the same request.
- **403 (Forbidden), 3 occurrences**: `survey.pulseinsights.com/serve` — third-party survey widget blocked on Men category, PDP, and Search steps. Non-fatal to core function but generates recurring console noise and wasted requests.
- **Summarizer API errors, 3 occurrences**: "Unsupported Summarizer API languages" — a Chrome built-in AI API (`window.Summarizer`) is being invoked with an unsupported locale, likely from a third-party script (personalization or AI-assist widget) probing browser capabilities without a language guard.

## Recommendations

1. Fix `document-title`, `html-has-lang`, and missing `<main>`/`h1` on the View Cart view specifically — this step diverges from the rest of the site's document structure and is both an accessibility and SEO defect.
2. Add discernible text (`aria-label` or visually-hidden text) to icon-only buttons and links flagged by `button-name`/`link-name` on Homepage.
3. Audit landmark nesting — resolve duplicate `<main>` and non-top-level landmark roles, likely introduced by injected third-party widget markup (PulseInsights, personalization slots).
4. Investigate the Kasada `/fp` 429s: confirm whether the client is retrying without backoff, and whether rate-limit thresholds need adjustment — repeated fingerprint-endpoint throttling on `/cart/` risks false-positive bot flags on real mobile users.
5. Guard or defer the Summarizer API capability probe so it doesn't fire with unsupported language parameters, or remove it if unused.
6. Instrument LCP/CLS capture (Lighthouse CI, CrUX, or RUM) — current data has no visibility into these Core Web Vitals despite TTFB being tracked; with ~20+ third-party scripts loading per page, both metrics are plausible risk areas that need real measurement, not conjecture.
7. Reduce/defer non-critical third-party tags (ad pixels, multiple webfont loaders) that load synchronously ahead of primary content on early funnel steps where TTFB is already elevated (491–569ms).
