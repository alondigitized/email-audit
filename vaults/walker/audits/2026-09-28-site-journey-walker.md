# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 2/10 (prev 1/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-09-28**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I set out to do one thing on my phone: find a comfortable, slip-on walking shoe, put it in my cart, and see what it'd cost me with my Member discount. I got two out of three. The site showed me the right shoe, described it well, and even found more good options through search — but when I actually tried to buy the thing, it fell apart. I could not get logged in, I could not get the shoe into my cart properly, and the cart page itself showed me nothing but a blank white screen. For a fella my age who already gets a little nervous about typing card numbers into a phone, hitting a dead blank page at checkout is enough to make me close the tab and drive to the mall instead.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**2/10** — up from yesterday's 1/10, but barely. Browsing, product pages, and search all work and even look nice. But the purchase path — log in, add to cart, view cart — is broken at every single step. A site that shows me the shoe but won't let me buy it is worse than useless; it's a promise it doesn't keep.

## 3. What's Working

- The **hamburger MEN menu** (Step 4) is clean and readable — big text, clear categories (Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections). I didn't need my readers for that one.
- The **product detail page** (Step 6) for the Slip-ins Contour Foam shoe is genuinely good: big photo, clear price ($88.00), star rating with review count, and a "Members Get an Extra 10% Off" callout that's hard to miss. The "Hands Free Slip-ins" badge is right there above the fold — that's exactly what I'm shopping for.
- **Search for "comfort shoes"** (Step 9) actually understood me. It returned 1,574 results and the first two products shown were slip-ins and a relaxed-fit comfort mule, with prices and color swatches visible without scrolling. That's a rare win — most sites just throw everything at me.
- The **top banner discount** ("Extra 10% Off Everything") stayed visible and consistent across every page, which built a little trust that the price I'd see later matches what I saw scrolling.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Log In did nothing.** Step 3's screenshot is identical, pixel for pixel, to the homepage in Step 1. No login form, no modal, nothing. Either the login button doesn't respond or it silently failed. I never got a personalized experience — no name, no order history, no saved size.
- **Dismiss Popups also did nothing visible** — same image as the homepage. Maybe there was no popup, which is fine, but I have no way to tell the difference between "nothing to dismiss" and "the dismiss button is broken," and that's a little unsettling on a shopping site.
- **Add to Cart failed.** I never picked a size, and the page threw up "Please select a size" in red — but the Add to Cart button was highlighted like it had already been tapped. The cart icon stayed at "0" the whole way through. So near as I can tell, nothing actually got added.
- **View Cart is a blank white page.** Step 8 is completely empty — no header, no items, no "your cart is empty" message, nothing. This is the single worst thing on the whole tour. If I'd made it this far in real life, I would assume the site crashed and I'd bail.
- Because of the above, I never saw a real price breakdown with my 10% Member discount applied, which is the whole reason I'd bother signing in.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix Login first.** If the button isn't opening a modal or navigating anywhere on mobile, that's a hard blocker — nobody gets a personalized cart or saved size without it.
2. **Require size selection before the Add to Cart button lights up**, not after I tap it. Right now the button looks clickable and "done" even when it silently fails.
3. **The blank cart page needs to be treated as a fire alarm**, not a cosmetic bug. A shopper who successfully adds an item and then sees a white screen will not try again — they'll leave.
4. Once cart works, show the Member discount math right there on the cart page — I want to see $88 minus 10% before I put in a card number.
5. Keep doing what Search and the PDP are doing — don't touch those, they're the best parts of this whole walk.

## 6. Bottom Line

You've got the storefront window dressed nicely — good product photos, honest pricing, search that actually listens. But the register is broken. I can window-shop just fine; I cannot buy a shoe. Until log in and cart work, none of the nice stuff upstream matters, because nobody gets to the finish line.

## 7. Evidence

**Step 1 — Homepage:** Bold green "Outrun the Night" hero banner for a running shoe, with a persistent "Extra 10% Off Everything" bar up top and a 20% Boots & Outdoor callout below it. Nav row shows Slip-ins, Wide Fit, Arch Fit, Max Cushioning as tappable pills — comfort-forward categories right up front, which I appreciate. Icons (search, account, cart, menu) are small but tappable. No personalization visible — this is a generic homepage for anyone.

**Step 2 — Dismiss Popups:** Identical screenshot to Step 1. No popup appeared, or if one did, it's already gone with no visible confirmation. Neutral either way — didn't slow me down, but I can't confirm the mechanism works.

**Step 3 — Log In:** Also identical to Step 1 — no login form, no account panel, nothing changed. This is a hard failure. I never got logged in, so nothing after this point could be personalized to me, and Skechers Plus member pricing stayed theoretical rather than applied.

**Step 4 — Men category:** A slide-out menu with large, well-spaced tap targets: Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections, Collaborations, Clothing & Accessories. Easy to read without zooming, easy to tap with a thumb. This is the best navigation moment in the whole walk.

**Step 5 — Men > Shoes:** Landed on Men's Shoes with 750 results and three clear subcategory tiles up top — Hands Free Slip-Ins, Walking Shoes, Boots — plus a free in-store pickup toggle and filter/sort bar. Comfort categories are front and center, which matches what I'm actually shopping for. No friction here.

**Step 6 — Product detail:** Skechers Slip-ins: Contour Foam - Cozy Fit Ohtro, $88.00, 66 reviews, "Hands Free Slip-ins" badge, "Members Get an Extra 10% Off" note. Clean layout, readable text, a product photo big enough to actually judge the shoe. This page alone would've sold me.

**Step 7 — Add to Cart:** Size grid (7.5–9.5) with no size selected and a red "Please select a size" warning showing. The Add to Cart button is highlighted as if tapped, but the cart badge stayed at 0. Also notable: a "Sign In" prompt appears again here, reinforcing that I was never actually logged in. This step is where the purchase flow quietly breaks.

**Step 8 — View Cart:** Totally blank white screen — no header, no nav, no items, no error message. Whatever happened in Step 7 (or didn't), the cart page gave me nothing to look at. On a real phone, I'd think the site died and give up.

**Step 9 — Search "comfort shoes":** 1,574 results, led by the same Slip-ins Contour Foam shoe ($59.99–$88.00, multiple colors) and a Relaxed Fit Glide-Step Comfort mule ($48.99–$65.00), both tagged with the Members 10% off note. Prices, swatches, and product names all legible without zooming. This is a genuinely strong result for a search that could've gone generic.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary
Mobile journey through skechers.com (homepage → login → men's category → PLP → PDP → cart → search) surfaced repeated bot-mitigation rate-limiting on a third-party fingerprinting endpoint, one CORS-blocked stylesheet, and a cluster of accessibility/landmark violations concentrated on the homepage and cart page. Several performance fields were not captured (LCP, CLS unavailable for all steps).

## Accessibility
17 axe violations across 3 of 8 steps (Homepage, View Cart, Search):
- **Critical:** `button-name` and `aria-required-parent` on Homepage — buttons without discernible text and ARIA roles missing required parent containers.
- **Serious:** `link-name` (Homepage) — links lacking discernible text; `document-title` and `html-has-lang` (View Cart) — cart page missing `<title>` content and/or `lang` attribute at evaluation time.
- **Moderate:** Duplicate/non-top-level landmark issues (`landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-unique`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`) on Homepage and Search; `landmark-one-main`/`page-has-heading-one` on View Cart (no main landmark, no H1); `region` violations (content not contained in landmarks) on Homepage and Search.
- **Minor:** `aria-allowed-role` on Homepage and Search.

Men category, Men > Shoes, Product detail, Add to Cart steps: No issues found.

## SEO
- View Cart page fails `document-title` and `html-has-lang` axe checks — an empty/missing `<title>` and absent `lang` attribute on a cart page directly harms indexability and accessibility-linked SEO signals for that URL.
- Homepage/Log In/Men category HTML samples show `<html lang="en" ...>` present, so the `html-has-lang` failure appears specific to the cart page render, not site-wide.
- No other on-page SEO issues (meta/canonical/structured data) observed in the available evidence.

## Performance
- TTFB was healthy across all steps (23–428ms), with the initial Homepage/Log In/Men category loads (~420-428ms) notably higher than subsequent PLP/PDP/Cart navigations (23-109ms), consistent with client-side routing after first load.
- LCP and CLS were not captured for any step — instrumentation gap, not a site issue. Cannot assess visual load completeness or layout shift from this data.
- Homepage HTML head loads a large number of synchronous/async third-party tags before first render content (TikTok, Snapchat, Reddit, Taboola, Pinterest, Bing, Quantum Metric, Monetate, GTM, Impact, Creativecdn, Brightspot tracker, Yahoo, Pulse Insights, WebFont loader, go2sdk) — this volume of render-blocking/parsing-competing third-party scripts is a likely contributor to degraded LCP even though it wasn't measured here.

## Mobile Optimization
- No mobile-specific layout defects observed in the truncated HTML samples (viewport/meta not visible in truncated output — not verifiable from provided evidence).
- Repeated 429 responses from the device-fingerprinting endpoint (`/…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=...`, likely Kasada bot-mitigation) fired 5 times during the session, including once against `/cart/` itself (429 on the cart page network request) — this indicates the anti-bot layer is throttling legitimate session traffic, which could degrade cart functionality for real mobile users under normal browsing patterns.

## Console & Network Errors
- **429 rate-limiting (5x):** Kasada-style fingerprinting endpoint (`/…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.797`) returned 429 four times; `/cart/` itself returned 429 once — a rate limit hitting the cart route is a functional risk, not just noise.
- **CORS failure:** `web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css` blocked by CORS policy (missing `Access-Control-Allow-Origin`) — this stylesheet fails to load, likely affecting Stylitics outfit/styling widget rendering.
- **ERR_FAILED (1x):** Associated with the CORS-blocked resource load.
- **Summarizer API warnings (4x):** "Unsupported Summarizer API languages" — benign browser-API console noise (Chrome's built-in AI Summarizer feature), not a site defect.

## Recommendations
1. Investigate why the Kasada/bot-mitigation fingerprinting endpoint is 429-throttling legitimate sessions, especially the direct 429 on `/cart/` — this risks blocking real users from viewing their cart.
2. Fix the CORS configuration on `web-assets.stylitics.com` (add proper `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` header) so the Stylitics stylesheet loads.
3. Add `<title>` content and an `lang` attribute to the View Cart page render; add a single `<main>` landmark and one `<h1>` heading to the cart page.
4. Fix homepage button/link elements missing discernible text (`button-name`, `link-name`) — critical for screen-reader users completing checkout-adjacent flows.
5. Resolve duplicate/nested landmark structure (`main`, `complementary`, `aside`) on Homepage and Search results page, and wrap orphaned content in landmarks to fix `region` violations.
6. Capture LCP/CLS metrics in the monitoring pipeline — current instrumentation only returns TTFB, leaving Core Web Vitals unmeasured.
7. Audit and defer/async-optimize the large third-party tag payload on initial page load (14+ scripts) to reduce main-thread contention ahead of LCP.
