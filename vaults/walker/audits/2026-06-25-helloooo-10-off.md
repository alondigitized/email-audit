# Helloooo, $10 off!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- My thumb opened this one on pure DSW reflex — same muscle memory I've built over the last two weeks of near-daily opens. Subject said "$10 off" and I thought, fine, let's see what we're working with. Then the email loaded and the first thing I noticed was the tiered structure: spend $49, save $10; spend $99, save $20; spend $199, save $60. That's actually a fair deal for a shoe store, and the coupon code was right there in plain sight. Good start.
- But then I scrolled and hit "So New, So You" and it was heels. Strappy dress shoes. Same pattern I've been seeing in this inbox since mid-June — DSW shows me the women's new arrivals section like it's got something to prove. I'm 62, I'm in the market for cushioned walking shoes and maybe a pair of slip-on New Balances for weekend errands. Heels aren't on my list. The email recovered a bit with the "Dad's Day Savings" block — New Balance, Crocs, Converse all in one row, brands I actually cross-shop — but Father's Day was ten days ago. That section feels like leftovers.
- I'd probably click the Dad's Day brands block since those are my people, and "Shop Wide Width" at the bottom caught my eye in a good way. But I wouldn't forward this to a friend. I'd mark it as read and move on.

## What's working

- **Tiered discount at the top** — $10/$20/$60 off depending on spend is a real offer, not vague "up to X%" language. I knew exactly what I needed to spend.
- **New Balance and Crocs in the Dad's Day block** — those two logos are the ones that made me pause. Those are my brands. That section spoke to me even if it's running late.
- **"Shop Wide Width" button** — nobody my age needs another reminder that comfort fit matters, but seeing it called out explicitly in a size-selector block made me feel like DSW actually knows some of their customers have wider feet.
- **Clean layout, no broken images** — email loaded fast, everything I could see was where it was supposed to be. No mystery boxes, no missing photos.
- **Promo code visible in the hero** — I didn't have to hunt for it. It was right under the discount tiers.

## What's weak

- **"Helloooo" in the subject** — that's how my granddaughter texts me, not how a shoe retailer earns my click. Feels like someone on the copy team aimed this at a 34-year-old woman and forgot to adjust.
- **"So New, So You" = women's heels again** — I've now seen women's new arrivals featured prominently in at least four DSW sends this month. I get it. DSW has a women's shoe section. But it keeps landing as the featured content in emails going to a guy who's looking for cushioned walking shoes.
- **Dad's Day is over** — June 25, Father's Day was June 15. That section should have been the hero two weeks ago. Now it reads like clearance framing.
- **Preview text junk** — "Buy more, save more starting…NOW" is decent copy. What follows it in my inbox is a parade of invisible spacer characters that eat up preview real estate and look like garbage on some clients.
- **Size bubbles are small sizes** — the "Shop by Size" row shows 6, 6.5, 8, 8.5. Those are women's sizes. Missed opportunity to show 10, 10.5, 11 and signal you know men are reading this too.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Dad's Day block, not new arrivals** — if the purpose of this email is to push a buy-more-save-more deal, anchor it in content that speaks to the buyer. Swap the women's new arrivals to a secondary position; move New Balance/Crocs/wide-fit up to the first scroll.
- 2. **Kill "Helloooo" — go concrete in the subject** — the playful voice isn't earning anything here. Just tell me the structure: $10, $20, or $60 depending on how much I spend. That's the story.
- 3. **Fix the preview text** — strip the spacer characters or replace them with real secondary copy, something like: "New Balance + Crocs included. Use code at checkout."
- 4. **Update the size row to show men's sizes** — or show men's and women's side by side. Showing only 6–8.5 tells me the rest of the email is also probably not for me.
- 5. **Either retire Dad's Day or rename it** — "Men's Savings" or "Comfort Brands on Sale" would have longer shelf life and wouldn't feel like a stale Father's Day afterthought.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`Src:` capitalization in all 10 `@font-face` blocks (critical)**
CSS property names are case-sensitive. `Src:` is unrecognized; `src:` is required. All declared web fonts (Futura PT, HelveticaNow variants) will fail to load silently in every WebKit/Blink client (Gmail web, Apple Mail, iOS Mail, Outlook Mac). The email falls back to system fonts, which will shift spacing and layout.

```css
/* broken — all 10 @font-face blocks */
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');

/* fix */
src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');
```

**`Margin: 0` in heading reset rules**
`Margin` (capital M) is an invalid CSS property. The intended `margin: 0` reset on h1–h6 won't apply in any standards-compliant client.

**Contradictory `-ms-text-size-adjust` in the `*` selector**
The universal selector declares the property twice in the same rule — `100%` first, then `none`. The `none` value wins and disables text scaling on Windows Phone/IE Mobile.

```css
/* as written — none overrides 100% */
*{ -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%; ... -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
Prevents users from zooming on mobile. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA) violation. Remove or replace with `user-scalable=yes`.

**`supported-color-schemes` in `:root` CSS block**
This is a non-standard/deprecated CSS property. The correct CSS property is `color-scheme` only. The `<meta name="supported-color-schemes">` tag in `<head>` is the correct mechanism and is already present.
