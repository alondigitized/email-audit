# Gifts they'll wear beyond the holidays
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-05**
## Executive summary

- Well, here's another adidas email, and I had to look twice to find a shoe. The photo is two young folks on a bus bench in fleece and wide-leg pants. The sneakers are small and far from the camera, so I can't tell what I'd be buying. The subject says "gifts," and the body says "gear up early with fleece apparel, comfortable sneakers, and accessories." That's a lot of words for no product, no price and no deal.
- Then the email switches to a football rookie giveaway for adiClub members. It's got three young players holding footballs and a list of names I don't know. I'm 62 and I don't follow rookie wide receivers. It felt like two emails stapled together. Compared to the Samba email on the 2nd, which at least showed me a real shoe, this is thinner. It's slightly better than the Pikachu one, since it at least mentions gifts and comfortable sneakers.
- Would I click? Probably not. Would I forward it? No. I'd mark it read and move on. If it had shown me one cushioned slip-on with a price, I might have tapped.

## What's working

- The three "Shop Men / Shop Women / Shop Kids" buttons are big, black and easy to tap with my thumb. They're stacked, not squeezed side by side.
- The text is a plain size, and I could read it without zooming.
- The gifting angle fits the season, and the layout isn't broken. Nothing overlapped and no pictures were missing.
- The adiClub mention tells me members get something, even though I'm not sure what I'd win.

## What's weak

- There isn't a single shoe I can look at. The sneakers are tiny feet in a bus-stop photo, and "comfortable sneakers" is just a phrase.
- No price, no percent off and no free shipping. "Gear up early" gives me no reason to act today.
- The football giveaway is irrelevant to me. It takes up half the email and makes the whole thing feel like it wasn't written for a guy like me.
- The "Mix and Match" headline doesn't tell me what's being mixed or matched. Nothing here mentions cushioning, arch support or slip-on ease.
- It's the umpteenth adidas email in a week. The Care Bears, the cleats, the Samba and Pikachu emails have all come since the 29th, and this one adds nothing new.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put one real, comfort-first shoe in the hero with a price and a star rating, like the 9/29 "We saved you a search" email did. Make the headline say what it is, such as "Cushioned slip-ons, ready to gift."
- 2. Pick one email and one job. Move the football giveaway into a members-only send, and keep this one about gifts.
- 3. Add a real reason to click now, such as free shipping by a date or a holiday price. Right now there's nothing time-bound.
- 4. Replace the three identical Shop buttons with one button that names the product, such as "Shop men's comfort sneakers."
- 5. Show who the gifts are for with some older faces too, not only twenty-somethings and rookies.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt:** the image at `https://dv.adidas.com/o/d87f480c-c1e8-4119-92e6-086c36e6be36?...` has no alt attribute. If it is a content image, it is invisible with images off or in a screen reader.
- **Missing alt on tracking image:** the tracking image above also has no alt. A decorative or tracking image should carry `alt=""`.
- **Ineffective charset declaration:** `<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` has no `http-equiv="Content-Type"`, so it does not declare a charset. The charset then depends on the MIME header.
- **Web fonts don't load:** every `@font-face` for `AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` uses only `src: local('Arial'), local('Helvetica')…`. No web font is ever loaded, so these families render as local fallbacks, which looks intentional. Check that the `font-family` stacks end in a generic family (not visible in the truncated source).
- **Duplicate CSS:** the same `@font-face` and `@media` rules appear in multiple `<style>` blocks, which adds weight with no benefit.
- **Dropped `<style>` block:** `.ReadMsgBody`, `.ExternalClass` and `@media` rules sit in `<style>` blocks. Gmail with non-Google accounts and some other clients drop these, so responsive behaviour should be confirmed with inline fallbacks.
