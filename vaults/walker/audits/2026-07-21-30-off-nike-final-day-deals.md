# 30% off Nike + Final Day Deals
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-one days in a row. At this point opening a Shoe Carnival email is just muscle memory. The subject caught me — "Final Day Deals" is the kind of words that make a 62-year-old shift in his chair — but then I peeked at the preview text and it was a raw tracking URL. A long ugly web address where the hook copy should be. That's not an auspicious start for a "final day" of anything.
- Inside, the email is stacked: Back to School hero with Nike at 30% off, a loyalty points bump, something called Final Bell Deals at $59.98, Skechers Fashion Sneakers at $29.98, Women's Flats at $24.98, and a Shoe Perks coupon block at the bottom. That's six distinct asks in one scroll. I'm not buying back-to-school gear — my kids are in their thirties. And women's flats I don't need on any day. But the Skechers at $29.98 made me pause. That's my name on the label.
- Here's the thing I can't shake: Shoe Carnival sent me a 30% off Nike email on the 20th. And again on the 18th. Today's "Final Day" feels like the same play dressed in slightly different clothes. If this is genuinely the last day, I'd click on that Skechers section. But I'm not convinced. Mark as read — but eyeing that $29.98.

## What's working

- **Skechers at $29.98 is a real hook for me** — that brand is on my rotation and that price is low enough to make me actually check the styles
- **"Final Day Deals" in the subject creates real urgency** — vague enough to make me open, specific enough to feel like a deadline
- **Multiple concrete price points are visible** ($59.98, $29.98, $24.98, 30% off) — I can compare without squinting for hidden asterisks
- **Shoe Perks coupon block with a real code (SAVEWORE)** — I can see the discount math without hunting; that's how I like it
- **Subject line is clean and short** — no emoji alarm bells, no all-caps screaming, just the deal in plain English

## What's weak

- **Preview text is a raw tracking URL** — inexcusable; that's the one line inbox clients show before I open, and they burned it on a link
- **Back to School hero is the wrong flag to fly at me** — I'm 62, my kids graduated years ago; the entire frame of the main hero is aimed at someone else
- **Women's Flat Sale section is dead space for me** — not a complaint about the product, just wasted real estate when there's no men's comfort counterpart
- **This is the third Nike 30% off push in four days** — I noticed the 20th, I noticed the 18th; today's "Final Day" version feels like a threat that keeps getting extended
- **Six competing sections with no single clear winner** — my eye bounced from Back to School to Skechers to Women's Flats without a through-line telling me what THIS email is really about

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the Back to School frame for this send** — or at minimum segment men 55+ out of it; lead with the Skechers and comfort-shoe deals instead of a Nike x Back to School hero aimed at parents of middle schoolers
- 2. **Fix the preheader immediately** — "Final day: 30% off Nike + Skechers from $29.98" is right there waiting to be written; the tracking URL is a technical failure that someone needs to catch before it goes out
- 3. **Consolidate the six sections** — pick three: Nike 30% off, Skechers $29.98, and the Shoe Perks coupon; the Women's Flat section should live in a segmented send
- 4. **If "Final Day" is real, make it feel real** — add a time stamp or end-of-day callout ("Offer ends midnight tonight"); if it's not real, retire the phrase before I stop believing it entirely
- 5. **Put the Shoe Perks coupon code in the subject or preheader** — SAVEWORE is an actual reason to click; bury it at the bottom and half the audience misses it
- **Subject Alt A:** `Skechers from $29.98 · Final day to save`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Final day: use SAVEWORE before midnight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `30% off Nike, Skechers from $29.98, plus your $20 reward code inside`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Final day deals — Skechers $29.98, Nike 30% off, and a coupon that stacks`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed bugs: an unresolved merge token in the open-tracking pixel and a campaign-name mismatch between the tracking pixel and the email's actual content. Four images are missing `alt` attributes, two of which are content images (accessibility and image-blocking failure).

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[BUG] Unresolved merge token in open pixel**
```
https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]
```
`[UNIQUE]` is a literal unsubstituted placeholder. Open-tracking pixels rely on a per-recipient unique token to attribute opens to individual contacts. With the token unresolved, every open from this send collapses to the same value — open analytics for this campaign are unreliable. Confirm whether the ESP reported substitution errors on send.

**[INFO] 27 click-redirect links not probed**
All click URLs route through an ESP redirect domain and were skipped by the HTTP prober. Destination URLs and UTM parameters on click links cannot be verified from this source alone.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

Both images are product/hero images (not decorative), so empty `alt=""` is insufficient — descriptive text is required for screen readers and image-blocking environments.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
