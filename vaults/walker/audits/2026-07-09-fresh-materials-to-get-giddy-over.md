# Fresh Materials to Get Giddy Over
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- Another Crocs email, another morning of me squinting at my phone wondering if I need to pay attention to this one. I've been in this brand's inbox for what feels like half the summer — clearance blowouts, Stitch collboards, a women's neutrals collection I had absolutely no use for, then a genuinely good men's comfort email last week that made me think they finally figured me out. And now this.
- "Fresh Materials to Get Giddy Over" landed and I thought — okay, maybe new cushioning technology? New arch support? Nope. It's new colorways for the Crafted Clog, with the featured product being something called the Pony Hair Espresso. Genuine leather. Pony hair texture. A pun section called "For the Neigh-Sayers." Crocs, I'm sixty-two years old. I'm not neighing over anything.
- The email is clean and renders fine. The product callout does mention an adjustable strap and heel pillow, which is the one thing that made me pause. But there's no price, no deal, no reason to click today versus any other day. I'll mark it read and move on.

## What's working

- **Clean visual hierarchy.** Logo up top, strong hero shot of the clog family, one featured product with labeled callouts — my eyes knew where to go.
- **Product detail callout on the Pony Hair.** "Adjustable strap with heel pillow for a plush feel" is the only comfort language in the whole email, and it's the one line that stopped my scroll.
- **Four CTA buttons at the bottom.** Having "Shop Sale" right there next to "Shop Clogs" means I don't have to dig for it if I'm in buy mode.
- **No broken images, no layout weirdness.** It rendered just fine on my phone, which isn't always a given with these multiblock emails.

## What's weak

- **"Giddy" is not my word.** That subject line was written for a 28-year-old who collects Jibbitz charms, not a 62-year-old looking for a walking shoe that doesn't wreck his knees.
- **No price anywhere.** Five colorways, a featured leather clog, and I have no idea what any of it costs. That's a friction point I shouldn't have to Google.
- **The featured product is a fashion piece.** Pony hair espresso leather is a collector's clog. I'm not that customer. The July 5th "Comfort That's Built for Men" email knew exactly what I wanted — this one forgot I exist.
- **Blank preview text.** My inbox showed nothing next to the subject line — just dead space. That's wasted real estate that might have gotten me to open with more intention.
- **Seven Crocs emails in nine days.** The fatigue is real. Even a good email is going to get a skeptical first look at this pace.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the comfort story, even on a color launch.** The heel pillow callout is buried in section three. If new colorways now also come with updated padding or improved fit, say that in the hero. "Same plush feel. Five new looks." — that's an email I open and read top to bottom.
- 2. **Show a price or a reason to act today.** Even "starting at $59.99" gives me an anchor. Without it, I have no idea if this is a $40 clog or a $120 leather collector's piece.
- 3. **Drop the pun section or save it for a younger list.** "For the Neigh-Sayers" / "Think you've seen every clog?" reads as clever to someone in their 30s. I just want to know if it fits wide feet and comes in my size.
- 4. **Use the preview text.** Anything is better than intentional blank padding.
- 5. **Slow the cadence.** Three to four emails a week from one brand trains me to tune them out. One strong, well-targeted send beats five scattered ones.
