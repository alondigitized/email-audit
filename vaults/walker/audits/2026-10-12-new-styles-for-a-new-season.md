# New styles for a new season
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-12**
## Executive summary

- Well, this one has shoes right at the top, and that's a good start. A navy suede sneaker with a cream sole fills the first screen, with "Just landed" and a Shop now button. Then comes a grid of eight shoes with only numbers under them: 750, 950, 574, 204L and so on. I can see them, but a number doesn't tell me which one is cushioned or easy on the arch.
- Then the email turns into the same thing New Balance sent me on the 10th. The "Autumn essentials" fleece set, the young fella in the puffer vest, is back word for word. The sale bags are back too, like the 7th. This is the fourth or fifth New Balance email this week, and it feels like leftovers. I'd open it on a slow afternoon and maybe tap through to a shoe. I wouldn't forward it. There's nothing to tell a friend.

## What's working

- The shoe is the hero at the top. After the clothing and jacket emails on the 9th and 10th, I was glad to see a shoe first.
- "Shop now" is a big, plain button that's easy to hit with my thumb.
- The "up to 30% off select styles" banner sits right under the menu where I can't miss it. The 30% is in red, so my eye finds it.
- The grid shows each shoe on a clean white square. I can see the shape and the sole, and the 9060 is in there, which I liked on the 5th.
- The eight shoes are laid out two across with plenty of space, so my thumb won't hit the wrong one.

## What's weak

- The shoes are labeled with numbers only. I'd have to know that a 574 is the cushy one. There's nothing about cushion, arch support or slip-on, which is what I shop for.
- The "Autumn essentials" and "Shop the sale" sections are the same as the 10th and the 7th. I've seen both.
- The text in the lower half is tiny, especially the fine print under the sale. I'd have to zoom to read the dates and exclusions. The "select styles" wording and the "exclusions apply" line make me wonder what's really on sale.
- There's no price on any shoe, so I can't tell what 30% off means in dollars.
- Everything looks young and fashion-minded. There's no walking or all-day-comfort feel, and the men's and women's shoes are mixed together.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put one comfort message on the shoe grid, with a short line under each pair. For example, "Cushioned all-day walker, $89.99" would let me decide from the email.
- 2. Cut the clothing and sale sections that repeat the last few sends. Spend that space on two or three shoes with prices and a note on cushion or fit.
- 3. Make the buttons more specific than "Shop now" and "Shop clothing". "Shop men's walking shoes" would tell me where I'm going.
- 4. Put the sale end date up top next to the 30%, in larger type. Right now it's buried in the fine print.
- 5. Send fewer, more different emails. This week they've all run together.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's markup has template leftovers (placeholder title, a dummy `http://` background URL, `@font-face` rules with no `src`). It also has three images with no alt text, and its plain-text part is mostly URLs. The automated pass rate is 88% with 0 hard failures and 1 warning. The HTML source was truncated, so findings are limited to what is visible.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Placeholder, non-HTTPS URL in CSS:** `.bgwhite {background-image: url('http://image.XXXXX.png');}`. It is a dead placeholder and would trigger a mixed-content or blocked request if the class is applied.
- **Plain-text part is URL-heavy:** QA flagged `12666/14430 chars are URLs` (about 88%). The text alternative is mostly tracking links, which is a spam-filter signal and gives poor readability for text-only clients.
- **Probe coverage was partial:** 12 links were probed and 27 were skipped. The QA output gives no result for the probed links, so their status is unconfirmed.
- **Click tracking:** Links and images go through `link.email.newbalance.com`. The `/mo/egR1f966kc_…gif` open-tracking pixel is served from the same domain, which is consistent.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (3 images, QA-confirmed):**
  - `ShippingIcons_06.png`
  - `ShippingBanner_DarkMode_03.png`
  - `egR1f966kc_818512772_12422573_945413_23411103.gif`, the tracking pixel, which should have `alt=""`.
- **`@font-face` rules have no `src`:** Every declaration (`proxima-nova`, `linotype-didot`, and others) lacks a `src` descriptor, so no web font can load. Rendering falls back to Arial or Times New Roman.
- **Malformed meta tag:** `<meta content="IE=edge" />` has no `http-equiv`, so it does nothing.
- **Doctype mismatch:** The HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype is paired with an `xmlns` attribute and XHTML-style self-closing tags. This can trigger quirks in some clients.
- **Conflicting CSS:** `.bg1` is defined twice with different values, `#ffffff` and later `#58546d`. The later rule wins, which is probably unintended. The dark-mode override sets it to `#151415`.
- **Dark-mode link contrast:** `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark) { a {color:#cf0a2c !important;} }` on `#151415` backgrounds gives roughly a 3.3:1 contrast ratio by my estimate. That is below the 4.5:1 WCAG AA minimum for body text.
- **Template title:** `<title>New Balance Template</title>` was not replaced with campaign text.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found in the visible source. No unresolved merge tokens such as `%%…%%` or `{{…}}` appear in the truncated HTML or QA output.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
No issues found. QA reports no compliance failures, and it says an unsubscribe link is always probed. The probe result and the SPF/DKIM/DMARC headers are not in the supplied data, so I can't confirm either. The sender is `newbalance@email.newbalance.com`, and the footer and physical address fall in the truncated portion.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
No issues found. Links are wrapped in ESP redirects, and the destination URLs and UTM parameters are not visible in the truncated source or the QA output. The image path `…/NBUS/myNB_Welcome/UpdatedImages/` comes from a welcome-series asset folder. That suggests reused template assets, but it is not a defect in itself.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add `alt` text to `ShippingIcons_06.png` and `ShippingBanner_DarkMode_03.png`, and `alt=""` to the tracking pixel.
2. Remove or replace `url('http://image.XXXXX.png')` in `.bgwhite`, and use HTTPS if a real image is intended.
3. Add `src` to the `@font-face` rules (or remove them) so font behavior is intentional.
4. Shorten the plain-text part. Add real copy and reduce raw tracking URLs.
5. Fix the dark-mode link color to reach at least 4.5:1 contrast. Remove the duplicate `.bg1` rule.
6. Replace the `<title>`, change the meta tag to `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />`, and align the doctype with the markup style.
7. Re-run QA on the full HTML. That would give results for the 27 skipped links, the unsubscribe link, the authentication headers, and the UTM parameters.
