# Ends Today: 30% OFF Nike & Jordan Sale ​
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-24**
## Executive summary

- Shoe Carnival again, and it's the same Black Friday email I've been getting since the 13th. The coupon is $10 off $74.98 and $25 off $149.98 this time, where last week it was $10, $20 and $30 tiers. The "FINAL DAY! 30% OFF" banner is big and I can read it on my phone without my glasses. But it's Nike and Jordan, and that isn't what I shop for. I want cushioned, arch-supported, slip-on comfort, and the hero is a pile of fashion sneakers.
- The one shoe that's mine is the men's Nike Air Max Excee, and it's tucked in the second row. Nothing says Skechers, Hoka, Brooks or New Balance, which is where my feet actually go. It's the 19th-or-so email from them, so "Final Day" doesn't feel credible when I've heard "last call" before. Last Saturday they yelled "LAST CALL: Up to 70% OFF" and now it's "Final Day" again.
- I'd skim it and mark it read. I wouldn't forward it. The only way I click is the MENS link at the bottom.

## What's working

- The "30% OFF" in huge white type on red is easy to read on my phone without zooming.
- "Final Day!" tells me when it ends, and the fine print gives 11/27 for the Black Friday sales.
- The $10 / $25 coupon has a clear "ADD $10 / ADD $25" wallet option with barcodes, so I could use it in store.
- The men's Air Max Excee and Jordan Trunner Flow are labeled by gender, so I can find the men's shoes quickly.
- The MENS / WOMENS / KIDS nav at the bottom is big, plain and easy to tap.

## What's weak

- The hero is Nike and Jordan fashion sneakers (pink and leopard, a girls' running shoe). That isn't my foot or my style.
- Two of the four featured products are women's and girls' shoes, so only half the grid is even for a man my age.
- There's no mention of cushioning, arch support or slip-on, and no Skechers, Hoka, Brooks or New Balance. The 25% Skechers email on the 19th spoke to me far more.
- There are three competing offers: 30% off Nike, up to 60% off, and the $10/$25 coupon. The fine print is tiny, and it lists lots of exclusions, including Skechers, Brooks and New Balance. That is hard to read on a phone and feels like a bait-and-switch.
- The Word Stride game, SMS signup and Afterpay boxes pile on at the bottom and have nothing to do with buying shoes.
- Seeing the same coupon and the same "last chance" for two weeks makes me trust the urgency less.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment by what I buy. If I've bought comfort or walking shoes, lead with a men's comfort hero (cushioned walking, slip-ons) instead of Nike fashion.
- 2. Show one offer, not three. Pick the 30% off or the coupon, not both plus "up to 60%".
- 3. Make all four products men's if you know I'm a man, and put "arch support" or "slip-on" in the captions.
- 4. Move the exclusions to a plain-English line near the offer, so I don't find out at checkout that my brand is left out.
- 5. Cut the game, SMS and Afterpay tiles, and cut the repeat sends. One final-day email would be believed.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit: Shoe Carnival, "Ends Today: 30% OFF Nike & Jordan Sale"

**1. Technical Summary**
The HTML is a table-based, responsive template with Outlook, Gmail and Samsung fixes. The visible problems are a malformed tracking-pixel URL, an unresolved `[UNIQUE]` token in a second pixel, and invalid `<meta>` tags. Most of the body, the headers and the 37 tracking links were truncated or skipped, so many checks could not be completed.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- **Malformed pixel URL:** `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20261124_nike_AM - 20261123_103635​`
  - The `t=` value contains literal spaces, an unencoded `=`, and a trailing zero-width space (U+200B).
  - Some clients and proxies may truncate or reject this URL, which would break open tracking.
- **Unresolved placeholder:** `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]`
  - `[UNIQUE]` was not substituted, so this pixel can't identify the recipient. The QA capture may have done this, so it needs checking against a live send.
- **Unverified links:** 37 tracking links were not probed because they are redirect domains. Their destination status is unconfirmed, and no broken links were confirmed.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- **Missing alt text:** two content images have no `alt`.
  - `.../lib/fe3311737164047d701077/m/1/23a03225-7c93-49fa-988c-8821675cac1b.jpg`
  - `.../lib/fe3311737164047d701077/m/1/fe4e293c-bccf-488a-bbf7-db06bf5745de.jpg`
  - Screen readers and image-blocked clients will get no text for them.
- **Tracking pixels:** both `emltrk.com` pixels also lack `alt`. This is minor, but `alt=""` plus `width="1" height="1"` is the usual practice.
- **Invalid meta tags:** `<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />` has no `http-equiv`. `<meta content="IE=edge" />` also has no `http-equiv` or `name`, so it does nothing. Both are harmless in practice but are validity errors.
- **Mixed DOCTYPE:** the document declares HTML 4.01 Transitional but adds `xmlns` and self-closing XHTML-style tags.
- **Small mobile text:** `.responsive-text` is forced to `font-size: 12px !important` at ≤600px. This is below the usual 14px mobile minimum for body copy, though I can't tell which elements use the class.
- **Link styling:** the global rule sets `a { text-decoration: none !important; color: inherit !important }`. Links may have no visual affordance unless they are styled inline, and the truncated body doesn't show whether they are.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
- `[UNIQUE]` in the pixel URL is the only token problem found (see section 2).
- The subject line ends with a zero-width space. It is probably deliberate preheader or padding, but it adds invisible characters to the subject.

**5. Compliance**
- **Authentication:** the `Authentication-Results` header was not found. QA attributes this to the AgentMail relay, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC are unverified. This is a limit of the test setup, not a confirmed defect.
- **Unsubscribe and address:** the unsubscribe link, `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers, and the physical postal address are not visible in the truncated source. They are unverified, not confirmed missing.
- **Text part:** 7,285 of 9,953 plain-text characters (about 73%) are URLs. This is a spam-filter risk, likely from long tracking URLs.

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity**
- The only UTM data visible is `utm_campaign=SCA_20261124_nike_AM - 20261123_103635`, and it is inside the pixel URL, not on a landing link.
- The `utm_campaign` value contains spaces, which is malformed.
- No `utm_source` or `utm_medium` is visible.
- Landing-page URLs are hidden behind the redirect domain and were skipped, so UTM propagation and landing-page alignment can't be assessed.

**7. Recommendations**
1. URL-encode the pixel `t=` value (`%3D`, `%20`) and remove the trailing U+200B.
2. Confirm that `d=[UNIQUE]` resolves in a live send, and fix the template if it doesn't.
3. Add meaningful `alt` text to the two content images, and `alt=""` to the pixels.
4. Fix the metas: `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">` and `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge">`.
5. Raise the mobile `.responsive-text` size to at least 14px.
6. Re-run the audit with the full HTML and raw headers to check `List-Unsubscribe`, SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment, the footer address, and the redirect chains' final UTMs.
7. Shorten tracking URLs or add a fuller text alternative to bring the plain-text URL ratio down.
