# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-11**
## Executive summary

- Well, here we are again — that same pink floral D'Lites sneaker with the flower embroidery, front and center under a big "READY TO CHECK OUT?" banner. I have never once clicked on that shoe. Not on Monday, not today. At this point I'm starting to wonder if Skechers' computer just picked one item and decided I was buying it whether I liked it or not.
- Buried further down, though, there's actually something I'd look at twice — a men's slip-in called "Altman - Murdock," which is exactly my kind of shoe, hands-free and simple. Problem is it's stuck in a grid of six thumbnails after I've already scrolled past the abandoned-cart guilt-trip and a kids' sneaker with a basketball shoe I don't need. The 20% off code for Skechers Plus members near the bottom is a decent offer, but by the time I get there I've mostly checked out — pun intended.
- Would I click? No. Would I forward it? No, this isn't something I'd pass to a buddy. I'd probably just archive it, same as the last two or three of these.

## What's working

- The "MEMBERS TAKE AN EXTRA 20% OFF SALE STYLES" banner with code EXTRA20 is a real, specific offer I can act on if I were already buying.
- There's a men's slip-in shoe in the grid (Altman - Murdock) that actually fits my comfort/hands-free preference, even if it's buried.
- Layout is clean and readable on my phone — big text, clear buttons, no overlapping graphics.
- Payment options (Afterpay, Klarna) and store finder are visible if I wanted to check a shoe out in person.

## What's weak

- That pink floral D'Lites sneaker again — same one as "Your Cart Expires Soon!" and "Your Items Are Waiting!" this week. I never looked at it, and I'm getting tired of being told I did.
- The "READY TO CHECK OUT?" hero implies I've got items in a cart, which isn't true for me — feels like a guilt trip aimed at someone else's shopping history.
- No name, no loyalty points balance shown up top like the "Slip into something you'll love" email did — feels like a form letter, not something addressed to me.
- A kid's Garage Sneaker and a limited-edition basketball shoe take up prime real estate that could've shown me more men's comfort styles instead.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the men's slip-in (Altman - Murdock) as the hero image, not the floral D'Lites — show me shoes that match what I actually browse, not a cart item I never touched.
- 2. Move the 20% off member code up near the top, right under the header — that's the one thing that'd get me to actually read further.
- 3. Bring back the "Hi, Walker" personalization and points balance from the Sept 9 email — that one worked, this one reads generic.
- 4. Drop the kids' and basketball sneakers from a "forgot something" nudge — if I never looked at them, don't show them to me.
