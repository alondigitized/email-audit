# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 4/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-10-22**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I'm Walker, 62, and I browsed on my phone. The first half of the trip is decent. The slip-in and comfort shoes are easy to find, and the "Hands Free Slip-ins" and "Walking Shoes" tiles on the Men's Shoes page are what I came for. The second half falls apart. I never got logged in. My Add to Cart tap did nothing I could see: the bag icon still said 0. The cart page was a blank white screen. A shopper who can't buy usually goes to Hoka or Brooks. Search for "comfort shoes" worked fine, which was the one good surprise late in the trip.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**4/10.** Yesterday was 3/10, so this is a small improvement. Browsing and search are solid, but the cart and login problems still stop the purchase.

## 3. What's Working

- **The comfort language is right at the top.** The homepage nav shows "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Arch Fit," and "Max Cus…" (cut off). That's my vocabulary.
- **The Men menu is easy to use.** The text is big and bold, the rows are tall, and there's a clear Back arrow and an X. It has a "Comfort Technologies" section. My thumb could hit all of it.
- **The Men's Shoes page has useful shortcuts.** "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" are picture tiles above the Filter, Sort by, Size and Width buttons. Width matters a lot for my feet.
- **The product page is easy to read.** It shows a 4.5-star rating with 1,942 reviews, a $88.00 price, a Klarna/Afterpay line, and a "Hands Free Slip-ins" badge.
- **The size picker has big buttons.** Sold-out sizes are crossed out with a little bell icon, so I know right away what's gone.
- **The Add to Cart button is big and bright blue.** It's the clearest button on the whole site.
- **Search gave me what I asked for.** "comfort shoes" returned 1,621 results, led by Slip-ins Contour Foam and Relaxed Fit styles. Prices are shown as a range, and the "20% OFF for Members" line is easy to read.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Login never happened.** Step 3 looks the same as the homepage. The banner still says "Members Earn a $5 Reward… Login | Details," so I was never recognized. Nothing felt personal to me.
- **The Add to Cart tap didn't work.** The screen shows a red "Please select a size" warning, and the bag icon stayed at 0. This is how it should behave, but it's easy to miss. On a phone, the size picker is scrolled below the price, so I'd never have seen it at the top.
- **The cart page was blank.** Step 8 is a completely white screen with no header, no items and no message. I'd think the site was broken and leave. This is the biggest problem.
- **The product page is crowded before I see the shoe.** The header, the Members banner, a breadcrumb, a title, stars, Klarna, Afterpay and a red promo line all come before the first picture. The shoe itself is cut off at the bottom of the screen.
- **Nothing helps me compare brands or do the price-versus-quality math.** I don't see any comparison to Hoka, Brooks or New Balance. I don't see any "cost per month of wear" talk. I don't see arch support or cushioning explained on the product page.
- **Small text in places.** The promo line, "Details" links, color swatches and the "Size Chart" link are small. I would have to zoom in.
- **Confusing offer banners.** The top says "20% OFF Sitewide… Applied at Cart." Below it, the message changed from the Members $5 reward to "25% OFF Work & Safety." The product page says "20% OFF for Members." I can't tell whether I need to be a member, or what I'd actually pay.
- **The wide "Max Cus…" nav is cut off.** I'd have to know to swipe sideways.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page first.** Make sure it never shows a blank screen. Show a loading state, an error message with a retry button, or an empty-cart message with links back to shoes.
2. **Make the size requirement obvious before the tap.** Move the size picker above the fold. Or scroll the user to it when they tap Add to Cart. Make "Please select a size" larger, and confirm a successful add with a banner and an updated bag count.
3. **Make login work on a phone.** Show what changes after I log in: my name, my saved sizes and widths, and my member price.
4. **Trim the top of the product page.** Move the payment-plan and promo lines below the shoe photo.
5. **Put comfort details up front.** Add a short "Cushioning / Arch support / Slip-in" summary near the price.
6. **Show the real price.** Replace "20% OFF Applied at Cart" with the actual after-discount price, for example "$70.40 with 20% off."
7. **Make text bigger.** Make promo text and links at least 14–16px, and give links bigger tap areas.
8. **Add a comfort comparison.** A simple "How this compares" box would earn my trust, even if it's modest.

## 6. Bottom Line

I like Skechers' comfort and slip-in focus, and the browsing and search got me to a good shoe. But I couldn't complete the purchase. The login didn't register, the cart add wasn't confirmed, and the cart was blank. Until the cart reliably shows what I picked, I'll go buy from another brand.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary
The audit data is incomplete, and the journey was probably disrupted by bot mitigation.
- **Anti-bot blocking:** Seven of the 10 network errors are 429s. Six are the Kasada fingerprint endpoint (`/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.919`) and one is `/cart/` itself. The View Cart step likely received a 429 error page.
- **Stalled navigation:** The HTML samples for Homepage, Log In and Men category all report URL `https://www.skechers.com/` and have identical source. These steps probably did not navigate.
- **Missing metrics:** LCP and CLS were not captured for any step.
- **Findings:** There are 17 axe violations (2 critical, 3 serious, 10 moderate, 2 minor). They fall into three groups:
  - landmark structure,
  - unnamed interactive controls,
  - a document-level failure on the cart response.

## Accessibility
- **Critical:** `aria-required-parent` and `button-name` on the Homepage. Elements with child-only ARIA roles sit outside their required parents, and buttons have no accessible name (probably icon-only controls with no `aria-label`).
- **Serious:**
  - `link-name` on the Homepage: links with no discernible text.
  - `document-title` and `html-has-lang` on View Cart: the response has no `<title>` and no `lang` attribute. The homepage does set `<html lang="en">`, so this is probably an error or interstitial page, not the real cart template (see Console & Network Errors).
- **Landmark structure (moderate):**
  - Homepage: `landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region`. This points to nested or duplicate `<main>` elements and `<aside>` elements inside other landmarks.
  - Search: `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region`. The same pattern recurs on Search.
  - View Cart: `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one`.
- **Minor:** `aria-allowed-role` on the Homepage and Search, where a `role` is applied to an element that doesn't allow it.

## SEO
- **Page identity:** View Cart has no `<title>`. That is low impact if cart is `noindex`, but it confirms an error page was served.
- **No H1 on View Cart:** `page-has-heading-one` fails there.
- **Possible bot-mitigation impact:** Googlebot or other crawlers may hit the same 429 behavior on Kasada-protected paths. The data here doesn't show this, so verify it.
- **Unverified:** The truncated HTML sources don't show canonical, meta description or structured data, so those can't be assessed.

## Performance
- **TTFB:**
  - Homepage, Log In and Men category are 422–439ms, which is acceptable.
  - Men > Shoes, Product detail, Add to Cart and Search are 27–32ms, and View Cart is 137ms.
  - The very low values suggest cache, bfcache or client-side transitions, or error responses, not full origin renders. They are not reliable evidence of fast page loads.
- **LCP and CLS:** No data (`?`) for any step, so no Core Web Vitals verdict is possible. The collector likely failed, possibly because the PerformanceObserver ran after navigation or the page was blocked.
- **Third-party load in `<head>`:** More than 20 third-party scripts load there, all async:
  - GTM and GA (several destination tags)
  - TikTok pixel (three scripts)
  - Pinterest, Bing UET, Yahoo (`ytc.js`)
  - Impact, RTB House (`creativecdn`)
  - Bloomreach, Quantum Metric
  - Monetate (two scripts)
  - Pulse Insights
  - Tune (`go2sdk`)
  - Web Font Loader from cdnjs
- **Duplicate script:** `https://s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` is included twice.
- **Font loading:** The font loader is a separate third-party request (cdnjs `webfontloader.js`). The `wf-*-active` classes show fonts load through JS, which adds a dependency before text renders and risks FOUT or layout shift.
- **Preconnect:** Only Monetate has `dns-prefetch`. There are no preconnect hints for the critical first-party or font origins.

## Mobile Optimization
- **Viewport:** `--vh` is set inline (6.64px per 1vh, so a ~664px viewport). The site uses a JS-computed viewport-height workaround instead of `dvh` units.
- **Device misdetection:** Pulse Insights requests carry `device_type=desktop` during a mobile-emulated journey, so their device detection may be UA- or width-agnostic.
- **Header tag-manager flag:** The GTM URL includes `gtm_auth` and `gtm_preview=env-1`, which means a GTM environment snippet is being served. Confirm that this is production and not a staging or preview container.

## Console & Network Errors
- **429 on Kasada `/fp` (6 occurrences):** The bot-fingerprinting endpoint is rate-limiting the test client. This is the likely cause of the stalled navigation and the blocked cart.
- **429 on `/cart/`:** The cart document itself was rate-limited. This explains the missing title, `lang` and `<h1>` on View Cart.
- **403 on `survey.pulseinsights.com/serve` (3 occurrences):** `visit_count=91` suggests the client has been flagged for repeat visits. It also reports `device_type=desktop`.
- **"Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document" (4 occurrences):** Some script registers an `unload` handler, which is blocked by Permissions-Policy. `unload` handlers are deprecated and break bfcache. They are likely third-party.
- **"Unsupported Summarizer API languages…" (3 occurrences):** A script calls Chrome's built-in Summarizer API without a supported language. This is likely a third-party or injected script. It is harmless to the user but should be gated or fixed.

## Recommendations
1. **Re-run the audit with a clean setup.** Use an allowlisted IP, a lower request rate and a fresh profile, since `visit_count=91` shows heavy reuse. Confirm each step actually navigates (the first three share one URL) and fix LCP/CLS capture, for example with a `web-vitals` init script registered before navigation.
2. **Verify Kasada behavior.** Check that `/fp` and `/cart/` don't return 429 to real mobile users or legitimate crawlers.
3. **Fix the critical accessibility failures.** Add accessible names to icon-only buttons and links (`aria-label` or visually hidden text), and place ARIA-role children under their required parents.
4. **Fix landmark structure.** Use exactly one top-level `<main>`, move `<aside>` elements to the top level, label duplicate landmarks (`aria-label`), and remove disallowed `role` attributes.
5. **Confirm the cart template** has `<title>`, `<html lang>`, an `<h1>` and a `<main>` once the rate limit is out of the way.
6. **Reduce third-party weight.**
   - Deduplicate `ytc.js`.
   - Defer non-essential pixels until after consent or idle.
   - Self-host the fonts and use `font-display: swap` in place of the cdnjs loader.
7. **Remove the `unload` listeners** (use `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`) to restore bfcache eligibility, and guard Summarizer API calls with a supported-language check.
8. **Check the GTM `gtm_preview=env-1` environment** is the intended production one.
