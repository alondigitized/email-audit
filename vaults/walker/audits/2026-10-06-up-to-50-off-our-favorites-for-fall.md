# Up to 50% off our favorites for fall
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-06**
## Executive summary

- Well, here's another adidas email. At least this one tells me what it's about: up to 50% off for fall, no code needed. The big photo is a dark plaid with white stripes and the word "ADIDAS FAVORITES" spaced way out. It looks nice, but it's a picture of fabric. I can't tell if it's a jacket or a shirt, and it certainly isn't a shoe.
- The offer is plain and the three buttons are easy to tap. Still, nothing here speaks to a 62-year-old guy who wants cushioning and arch support. I get "Shop Men," and then I'm on my own. No shoe, no price, no sale item I can look at. "Up to 50%" could mean one old sock.
- I'd open it for the discount, but I probably wouldn't click. I wouldn't forward it. After a month of Care Bears, Pokémon and German soccer jerseys, a plain sale is a small improvement. It still doesn't show me anything I'd put on my feet.

## What's working

- The headline "UP TO 50% OFF FOR FALL" is big, plain text I can read without squinting.
- "No code needed" is honest and saves me a hunt for a promo code.
- The three black buttons are large, with clear labels and room between them, so my thumb won't miss.
- The layout is clean and short. It's one message, with no Pikachu and no pinstripe fellas.
- The sale beats the last few "drops" I've seen from them, because I can tell what's being offered.

## What's weak

- There isn't a single shoe in it. The photo is plaid cloth, and I can't tell what the product is.
- "Up to 50%" with no example, no price, and no "was/now" gives me nothing to do the math on.
- The "ADIDAS FAVORITES" lettering is thin and spread out. It looks fancy but is harder to read.
- "Fan fave pieces" is vague. It doesn't say shoes, jackets or anything else, so I can't tell if it's for me.
- "Shop Men" goes to who knows where. Nothing mentions walking, comfort or slip-ons.
- The tiny gray preheader link at the top is hard to read.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace the plaid with a real shoe or two, and put a sale price on them, like "Was $100, now $59." Then I can compare with Hoka and New Balance.
- 2. Make the buttons specific. "Shop Men's Shoes on Sale" tells me where I'm going. "Shop Men" doesn't.
- 3. Add a row of three or four sale items with prices under the hero, including a comfortable walking or slip-on style.
- 4. Put an end date on the sale if there is one. "Up to 50%" with no deadline feels like it never ends.
- 5. Make the preheader a larger, darker, real sentence.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Men's shoes up to 50% off, no code`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Fall sale: cushioned walkers from $49`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Walking and everyday sneakers marked down. No code needed.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sale prices on men's shoes and jackets through Sunday.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email has one QA failure: no plain-text MIME part (0 chars). Smaller issues are non-HTTPS image and tracking-pixel URLs, two images with no alt text, and an `alt="arrow"` on what looks like a decorative image. The HTML source was truncated, so footer, header and UTM checks could not be completed.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Skipped probes:** 21 tracking links on `click.comms.adidas.com` were not HTTP-probed because they are click-redirect domains. Their destinations and redirect health are unverified.
- **Non-HTTPS tracking pixel:** `http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/010201a112a8ffba-8958f4ab-0871-4f47-a6af-876794c11988-000000/y47r5-pBdLP2YS8gwH_EeGABvarNA18-gFC52xQHOgE=452` loads over plain HTTP. It will be blocked or rewritten by some clients, and it undercounts opens. It also has no alt text.
- No broken links were reported.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **HTTP images:** the image `b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg` (`http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/…`) is used 6 times over `http://`. Clients may block it as mixed content, so the arrow icons could drop out.
- **Missing alt text:**
  - `https://dv.adidas.com/o/05720a1d-60b8-4475-93c2-e89be7147abf?cp_tp=…` has no alt attribute.
  - The tracking pixel above has no alt either.
- **Uninformative alt:** the 6 arrow images use `alt="arrow"`. If they are decorative chevrons beside a CTA, `alt=""` is better, because screen readers will otherwise announce "arrow" repeatedly.
- **Present and fine:** `lang="en"`, the viewport meta, and `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`.
- **Redundant CSS:** the `<style>` blocks repeat the same `@font-face` and media-query rules, once with Calibri and Sans Serif `local()` fallbacks and once without. This adds weight but breaks nothing.
- **Fonts:** `AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` resolve only to `local()` Arial or Helvetica. No webfont file is loaded, so the brand typefaces will never render. `mso-font-alt: Arial` is set, which is correct for Outlook.
- **Doctype:** HTML 4.01 Transitional, which is acceptable for email.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The visible source and QA data show no unresolved merge tokens. Footer and body content were truncated, so this only covers the visible portion.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **FAIL: no plain-text alternative.** The text version is 0 chars. A multipart/alternative message with a text part is expected. Its absence can raise spam scores and hurts accessibility, since text-only clients get nothing.
- **Not verifiable:** the unsubscribe link, `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers, the physical postal address, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC results are not in the provided data. I am not flagging them as missing.
- **Sender alignment:** the From domain is `us-news.comms.adidas.com`. The tracking domains `click.comms.adidas.com` and `image.link.adidas.com` are on the same organizational domain, `adidas.com`.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. The 21 click-tracking links were skipped, and the truncated HTML does not show final destinations or UTM parameters, so none could be checked.
