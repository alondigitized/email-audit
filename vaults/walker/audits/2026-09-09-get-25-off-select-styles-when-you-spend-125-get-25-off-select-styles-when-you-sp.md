# Get 25% off select styles when you spend $125+, get 25% off select styles when you spend $125+
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-09**
## Executive summary

- Shoe boxes and shopping bags — that's a step up from Jack Harlow on a park bench, at least it's about the product. "Get 25% off select styles when you spend $125" is a real offer with a real deadline, which I respect, but $125 to trigger the discount is a stretch when I'm usually just replacing one pair. The subject line repeating itself twice ("get 25% off select styles when you spend $125+, get 25% off select styles when you spend $125+") is flat-out broken and made me think something glitched before I even opened it.
- Scrolling down, most of the "more to explore" tiles are exactly what's been bugging me all week — a young guy in a t-shirt and shorts, a woman in an oversized sweatshirt, pastel running shoes that look built for someone half my age. Only two of the six tiles are actual shoes I'd consider (the 471 and the Fresh Foam Arishi v4), and neither one calls out cushioning or arch support. I'd open it because "25% off" catches my eye, but I wouldn't click past the "Shop now" button, and I definitely wouldn't forward it — nothing here is worth passing along to a buddy.

## What's working

- Real percentage-off offer with a specific spend threshold and a hard end date (September 16th) — I know exactly what I'm getting and when it ends.
- "Free shipping on orders $75+" banner up top is a nice added incentive since I'm already close to that just buying one pair.
- The price-tier buttons (Under $50, $50-$100, Over $100) are a smart shortcut for someone like me who shops by budget, not by style name.
- Two actual shoe products shown at the bottom (471, Fresh Foam Arishi v4) instead of all lifestyle apparel.

## What's weak

- The subject line is duplicated word-for-word — looks like a mail-merge error, and it makes the brand look sloppy before I even get in the door.
- $125 minimum spend to unlock 25% off is a lot when I'm typically buying one pair around $70-$90 — feels like it's designed for someone stocking up, not me.
- Half the "more to explore" grid is t-shirts, socks, and an oversized sweatshirt on a young model — none of that is why I opened a shoe email.
- Not one word about cushioning, arch support, or comfort anywhere in this email — for a brand that could easily lean into that, they didn't.
- No loyalty/member pricing mentioned even though I've bought from them before — just a blanket public discount.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the duplicated subject line immediately — that's a basic QA miss that undercuts trust before the email is even opened.
- 2. Lower or tier the spend threshold (e.g., "20% off $75+, 25% off $125+") so people buying a single pair still feel like they're getting a deal.
- 3. Swap at least two of the apparel tiles for comfort-focused men's walking shoes with a callout like "All-day cushioning" or "Arch support built in."
- 4. Add a line about loyalty/member perks if I'm a known customer — right now this reads like it was sent to a stranger.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Save on comfort styles for men, women, and kids.`
