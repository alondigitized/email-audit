## Executive summary

- Another adidas email, and I've lost count since the 14th. This one is the plainest of the bunch. The "UP TO 60% OFF" in big white letters is easy to read without squinting, and I'll give them that. Then it's a woman in a red tracksuit leaning on a brick wall. Nothing there for a 62-year-old guy who wants a cushioned shoe he can slip on.
- The copy is short and the three black buttons are easy to tap. But "classic sneakers, jerseys, cozy fleece" is a catalog, not a pitch. No shoe, no price, no walking or comfort angle, and no deadline. I know the 60% already from the Nov 14 and Nov 18 emails, so this one adds nothing new.

## What's working

- The "UP TO 60% OFF" headline is huge and legible on my phone. I didn't have to zoom.
- The email is short, with one clear message and no clutter.
- The three buttons are big, dark and well spaced, so I can tap them with a thumb.
- It looks like adidas and matches the earlier sends, so I trust it's the real thing.
- The body text is a readable size.

## What's weak

- The hero is a woman in a tracksuit. Nothing in it speaks to a man my age or to comfort footwear.
- There's no product shown, no shoe, and no price. "Up to 60%" doesn't tell me what's actually cheap.
- No deadline or countdown. The Nov 18 email had a clock, so this one feels weaker than that one.
- It's the same Black Friday message I've gotten for a week. The Nov 20 "$80 and under" email at least gave me a price.
- Nothing on cushioning, arch support, walking, or slip-on styles.
- "Shop Men" goes to everything. I'd be digging through the whole men's section.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real product in the hero, like a cushioned walking shoe or slip-on with a "now $__" price. Show me one shoe I'd actually wear.
- 2. Add a deadline, such as "ends Monday," so the urgency feels real.
- 3. Change "Shop Men" to something specific like "Shop men's walking shoes" and link it to that category.
- 4. Say what the 60% applies to, and what the typical savings are.
- 5. Space the sends out. A recap of the week's deals would beat a seventh near-identical email.

# Technical Audit: adidas "Save up to 60% during Black Friday"

## 1. Technical Summary
The email has structurally sound responsive HTML (`lang="en"`, viewport meta, media queries). It has no plain-text alternative (0 chars) and loads several images and the tracking pixel over insecure `http://`. The HTML source was truncated, so footer, unsubscribe, merge-token and UTM checks could not be fully verified.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **21 tracking links were not probed.** The QA run skipped them as click-redirect domains, so destination status, redirect chains and landing-page validity are unverified.
- **Insecure image and pixel URLs (7 total):**
  - The "arrow" image is loaded 6 times over HTTP: `http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg`.
  - The tracking pixel is also HTTP: `http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/010201a1fc4ceed0-ca79024f-eb1f-4c3d-8aad-2a7385c44ffb-000000/GSVaTSKF65ERpTatnt3Gvgmk3…`.
  - Mixed-content or proxy blocking can suppress the arrow image. Open tracking from the pixel will also be under-counted where the client blocks HTTP.
- The mix of `image.link.adidas.com` (HTTP) and `dv.adidas.com` (HTTPS) shows inconsistent protocol handling in the template or ESP.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Plain-text part is missing (QA FAIL, 0 chars).** This hurts text-only clients, some spam filters and accessibility tooling.
- **Missing alt text on 2 images:**
  - `dv.adidas.com/o/2307719c-090e-494b-8ac1-8cd62673ecaa?cp_tp=…`. This is a content image, so the omission matters. Without alt text, a screen reader or an image-blocked client gets no information at all.
  - The tracking pixel `…GSVaTSKF65ERpTatnt3Gvgmk3lfTUGOby3p4TG8QwOE=452`. It should carry `alt=""`, `width="1"` and `height="1"` so it is treated as decorative.
- **Ineffective charset declaration.** The source has `<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` with no `http-equiv="Content-Type"`, so it does nothing. Encoding then relies on the MIME header alone.
- **Legacy doctype.** `HTML 4.01 Transitional` is common in email but offers no benefit over the XHTML or HTML5 doctypes.
- The CSS declares `AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` with `local()` sources only, so nothing is downloaded and they fall back to Arial or Helvetica. This is not a bug, but the web fonts never actually load.
- The stylesheet is duplicated: the `@font-face` and media-query blocks appear twice. This adds weight and risks the later block overriding the first.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found in the visible source. No unresolved tokens (`{{…}}`, `%%…%%`, `[FIRST_NAME]`) appear in the truncated portion. The rest was not available to check.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Authentication-Results header not found (QA WARN).** The report says SPF/DKIM/DMARC status is unknown. It attributes this to the AgentMail relay, so this is probably a test-harness limitation rather than a sender defect. It is unverified, not confirmed passing.
- The From address `adidas@us-news.comms.adidas.com` is a dedicated sending subdomain, which is good practice.
- The unsubscribe link, the `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers, and the physical postal address are not in the truncated source or QA data. They are unverified.
- `<meta name="ROBOTS" content="NOINDEX, NOFOLLOW">` and `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` are harmless for email.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Unable to assess. All 21 tracking links were skipped, and the click-redirect URLs hide the destination and any UTM parameters. Tags could be checked by resolving a redirect chain and inspecting the final URLs.

1. **High:** Add a plain-text MIME part (currently 0 chars). Mirror the main CTAs and include the unsubscribe URL.
2. **High:** Serve all assets over HTTPS. This means `image.link.adidas.com` (arrow image, 6 instances) and the `click.comms.adidas.com` open pixel.
3. **Medium:** Add a descriptive `alt` to the `dv.adidas.com/o/2307719c-…` image and `alt=""` to the tracking pixel.
4. **Low:** Use `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8">` so the charset declaration takes effect.
5. **Low:** Deduplicate the repeated `<style>` blocks.
