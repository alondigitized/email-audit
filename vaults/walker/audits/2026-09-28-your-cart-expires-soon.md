# Your cart expires soon!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-28**
## Executive summary

- There she is again — that pink floral D'Lites "Spring Bloom" sneaker, front and center under "YOU LIKED THESE." Same shoe I flagged on 9/17 (twice), 9/27 (twice), and again earlier today. It's not even subtle anymore — the subject line "Your cart expires soon" is trying to manufacture urgency around a women's shoe with flowers embroidered on it that has never once been in a cart of mine. At this point I'm not annoyed so much as bored.
- The one thing that pulled my eye was that 20% off boots & outdoor styles banner near the bottom — that's actually in my lane heading into fall, cushioned boots I'd wear around the yard. But it's buried under a wall of "you liked these" cross-sell tiles (kids' garage sneakers, a girl's glam racer shoe) that have nothing to do with me.
- I'm marking this one read and moving on. Wouldn't click, wouldn't forward — maybe I'd screenshot it for my wife as a "look what Skechers thinks I want" joke.

## What's working

- The 20% off boots & outdoor styles banner is genuinely relevant to a guy like me shopping into cooler weather.
- Clean typography, big readable "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" button — no squinting needed.
- Recognizable Skechers branding up top, no confusion about who sent it.
- No render bugs — everything loaded clean, no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- The hero product is, again, a pink floral women's sneaker — the same one I've now seen five-plus times across two weeks.
- "Your cart expires soon" is a lie — I've never put this shoe in a cart, so the urgency feels manufactured and a little insulting to my intelligence.
- The "you liked these" grid mixes in a boy's sneaker and a girl's glam racer shoe — none of that is me, and it just adds clutter.
- Zero mention of comfort, cushioning, arch support, or anything that'd actually get a 62-year-old guy to click.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the personalization engine — if I've never added this shoe to a cart, don't tell me it's "expiring." Pull real browse/cart history or drop the fake urgency entirely.
- 2. Lead with the boots & outdoor 20% off deal instead of burying it — that's the one relevant hook in the whole email for someone my age and gender.
- 3. Swap the "you liked these" grid for actual men's comfort styles — slip-ins, arch fit, walking shoes — if this is meant to be personalized.
- 4. Drop a repeated cart-reminder cadence for the same item across multiple sends; it reads as broken tracking, not personalization.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Arch Fit favorites, 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cushioned comfort, ready for cooler weather.`
