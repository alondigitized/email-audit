# Ready for Whatever Your Day Throws at You
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- Well, here we go again. Before I even tap the thing open, my inbox shows me that same wall of raw computer gobbledygook in the preview slot. I've counted it now across the July 12 "Summer Favorites," the July 11 BOGO, the July 10 "Keep it Cozy" — at this point it's been nine Skechers emails in a row with the same broken preview. That's not a glitch, that's nobody watching the store. The subject line "Ready for Whatever Your Day Throws at You" tells me absolutely nothing — no offer, no product — so I'm opening this one purely out of habit as a Skechers Plus member.
- Inside, the story improves fast. The 40% off number hits me the moment the email loads — good. Scrolling down, the WALKING section has men's shoes right there. Then "Sporty Slip-Ins" with "Just Step In" underneath — three words that tell me exactly what the shoe does for a guy who doesn't want to wrestle with laces. And then the Arch Fit® Athletics block with "Shop Supportive Styles." Two sections that speak directly to what I care about. That's real. The hero up top, though? Young runners charging at me under "Move It" — that's not my world. I'm walking the dog and hitting the hardware store, not training for a half-marathon.
- I'd click, specifically on the Slip-Ins or the Men's Walking shoes. I would not forward this to a friend — it's a solid loyalty play once you're inside, but the outside of the envelope is a mess.

## What's working

- **"Sporty Slip-Ins / Just Step In"** — three-word headline that tells me exactly what the shoe does. Hands-free is the whole point for me; that section sells itself.
- **Arch Fit® Athletics with "Shop Supportive Styles"** — "Supportive" is functional language that makes me stop scrolling. Arch support is my #1 purchase criterion.
- **40% off lands at the very top** — discount number is the first real thing I see, before any product image. Right call on placement.
- **Walking gets its own named section** — not lumped into "Active" or "Sport." Men's Walking as a standalone category tells me Skechers actually knows how I shop.
- **20% off for Skechers Plus members** — clearly labeled further down. As a member, that's directly relevant and I noticed it.

## What's weak

- **Broken JSON preview. Nine emails in a row now.** Raw schema code in the preview slot before every single Skechers send I've opened this month. At this point it's become their unofficial brand identity.
- **Hero models are half my age** — "Move It" with lean young sprinters is aimed at a different customer. If Skechers knows my purchase history, they know I'm not training for a 5K.
- **No expiration date on the 40% off** — the July 12 "Summer Favorites" email showed "Valid thru 7/13." This one has nothing I can find. A sale with no end date doesn't feel urgent; it feels lazy.
- **Running section comes before Walking** — I have to scroll past a category I'm not buying before I reach the one I am. Flip that order.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the broken preview text.** Everything else is secondary. Nine sends with raw JSON as my first impression is nine chances to lose the open on a recognizable sender.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline to the 40% off.** "Ends Sunday" or "Sale thru 7/17" is all it takes. No deadline = no urgency = I'll look later, which usually means never.
- 3. **Reorder the sections: Walking and Slip-Ins before Running.** Put the comfort categories where a comfort shopper lands first.
- 4. **Swap the hero creative for this list segment** — or add a secondary walking/casual model image. "Move It" with marathon runners is wrong for the Walker Miles customer.
- 5. **Rewrite the subject to carry the offer and the category.**
- **Subject Alt A:** `40% Off Walking & Slip-Ins — Just Step In`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Arch Fit Deal: 40% Off Select Styles`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sale on walking shoes, Slip-Ins, and Arch Fit styles — see what's on.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus members get 20% off regular price on top of sale pricing.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Skechers "Ready for Whatever Your Day Throws at You" (2026-07-14)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

All content images are served over plaintext HTTP from `image.emails.skechers.com`, and a Return Path monitoring pixel contains a protocol-less `src` that will silently fail in all clients. These are the two highest-priority fixes; everything else is lower severity.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Return Path pixel — broken `src` (no protocol)**
```html
<img src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1dbf0dcef79cbad82c12fe25342701e4499" />
```
Missing `https://` prefix. Email clients treat this as a relative URL; the pixel never fires. Inbox placement monitoring data for this send is lost.

**26 click-redirect links skipped by QA**
All CTAs route through `click.emails.skechers.com`. The automated probe could not resolve destinations, so UTM presence on landing pages is unverified (see §6).

**Krux/Salesforce DMP user-match beacons expose raw subscriber identifiers**
```
partner_uid=121173391   (beacon.krxd.net subid pixel)
partner_uid=9153f33f... (beacon.krxd.net sha25 pixel)
mi_u=8107766165         (ink1000.com open pixel)
```
Three distinct subscriber IDs are sent to two third-party domains (`beacon.krxd.net`, `ink1000.com`) with no obfuscation. This is an architecture-level data-sharing decision, but should be confirmed as intentional and covered by the privacy policy.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Systemic HTTP image serving — `image.emails.skechers.com`**
All 10+ content images (logo, product shots, app badges, social icons) are served over `http://`, not `https://`. Affected examples:
```
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/1/dde00662-169f-447d-b0e2-fc65f6c2290c.png  (logo)
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/0ec56c9f-eec2-471a-898d-0ca1cea7e115.png  (App Store badge)
```
Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all proxy images through HTTPS gateways, so rendering may survive — but corporate and government Outlook desktop clients that block mixed content will show broken image icons for the entire email.

**`ink1000.com` open pixel also uses HTTP**
```
http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif?mi_u=8107766165&mi_ecmp=...
```
Open-rate data from this secondary tracker will be undercounted in clients that enforce HTTPS-only image loading.

**Missing `alt` text — 2 images**
- `o.gif` (`ink1000.com`) — tracking pixel, `alt=""` is acceptable here but `alt` attribute itself is absent
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` — content image with no `alt` attribute; screen readers will read the filename

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied globally**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Disables iOS automatic font scaling for all elements. Can make small text unreadable on older iPhones without manual zoom. Should be scoped to specific elements or use `100%` instead of `none`.

**Duplicate responsive breakpoint blocks**
The `<style>` block defines `@media (max-width: 375px)` and `@media (max-width: 640px)` with identical rule sets, and a second `<style>` tag redefines `@media (max-width: 480px)`. This adds ~1KB of redundant CSS with no functional difference. Low priority but worth cleanup.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No broken or unresolved merge tokens visible in the source (no `{{token}}`, `%%token%%`, or `[token]` literals rendering in plain text).

Preheader text is static: `"Members take 20% off today"` — no first-name or segment personalization. Not a bug, but noted in case dynamic preheader is expected for this segment.

Campaign ID is date-stamped correctly for today: `MKG_US_L30D_U_CATEGORY_ENGAGEDOPEN_EN_07142026` — consistent across all four tracking pixels, which is correct.

---

### 5. Compliance

HTML is truncated before the footer; the following could not be confirmed from source:
- Physical mailing address (CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5))
- Unsubscribe link and its functionality

The QA summary line reads "100% pass rate, 0 issue(s), 0 warning(s)" but 14 warnings are listed in the same output — the summary counter appears to be a QA tool bug that is not aggregating the warning list. Do not treat the summary line as authoritative.

The Krux DMP pixels transmit subscriber-level identifiers to `beacon.krxd.net`, a Salesforce data cloud endpoint. Confirm this is disclosed in the privacy policy and that data-sharing consent is captured for applicable jurisdictions (CCPA, VCDPA).

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 26 CTAs redirect through `click.emails.skechers.com` — the probe was skipped for all of them, so UTM parameters on landing pages cannot be confirmed. Manually spot-check 2–3 destination URLs to verify:
- `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` are present
- `utm_campaign` value matches `MKG_US_L30D_U_CATEGORY_ENGAGEDOPEN_EN_07142026` (or an equivalent slug) for attribution consistency

If UTMs are missing from destination URLs, click revenue will not be attributable to this campaign in GA4/analytics.
