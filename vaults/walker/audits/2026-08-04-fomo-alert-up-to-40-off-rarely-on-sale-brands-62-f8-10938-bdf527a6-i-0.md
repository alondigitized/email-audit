# FOMO alert: up to 40% off rarely-on-sale brands
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-04**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one's different from the birthday parade DSW's been running on me — no "iconic" nonsense, no fake candles. Straight up: "40% off big-deal brands." Nike, New Balance, adidas, Crocs, Birkenstock — that's a real list, and a couple of those (New Balance, Crocs) are brands I actually rotate through. So my thumb paused for a second.
- Then the pictures loaded. A young woman in cutoff shorts and striped tube socks, "heat of the moment," pastel Puma sneakers with a bow on them, "Shop All Women," dress shoes and sandals under "Also Hot for Summer." Not one shoe on this page has a arch support call-out, not one model looks like they've ever heard of plantar fasciitis. This is a "start the school year right" email built for somebody's daughter, not for me.
- I wouldn't click. I might skim past the "Nike under $60" banner out of habit — that's a real number — but nothing here is telling me it comes in a men's 11 with any cushioning to speak of. Not forwarding this to anybody, and not marking it read with any enthusiasm either.

## What's working

- The subject and preview both name real brands and a real price point ("Nike under $60") instead of another birthday gimmick — that's the first DSW send in a while that reads like an actual sale.
- Big, legible "UP TO 40% OFF" up top — no squinting required.
- I recognize the DSW logo and layout instantly, so trust isn't the issue here.
- The VIP loyalty block at the bottom (5% back, free shipping, birthday gift) is at least visible if I were a member.

## What's weak

- Every single model and shoe pictured is women's — sandals, wedges, a Puma with a bow — there's nothing here for a guy my age looking for a slip-on walking shoe.
- "Start the school year right" — I don't have a kid starting school, and even if I did, that's not why I open a shoe email.
- Zero mention of comfort, cushioning, or arch support anywhere, which is the only thing that actually gets me to click on shoes.
- "Shop by Size" only goes up to 11 with no men's callout — I can't tell if my size is even in stock without leaving the email.
- Fine print buried at the very bottom ("select styles," multiple disclaimer blocks) undercuts the big "40% off" headline a little.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this — if DSW has my past browsing or purchase history (which after eight birthday emails, they should), swap the hero to men's comfort brands and drop the "Nike under $60" module up top where I'd actually see it fast.
- 2. Add one men's casual/walking shoe with cushioning language ("all-day comfort," "arch support") into the hero block instead of burying men's shoes off-screen entirely.
- 3. Drop or shrink the "back to school" framing for segments without kids — it reads as noise to me.
- 4. Put a real deadline on the 40% off — "FOMO" without an end date isn't FOMO, it's just a banner.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Nike under $60, New Balance up to 40% off — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Comfort brands rarely go on sale. This week they did.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Men's New Balance, Skechers & more marked down — while sizes last`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your size, your comfort brands, real markdowns inside`
