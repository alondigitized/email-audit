But then that "Step Up & Save" $15/$30 coupon block at the bottom is store-wide, so that part I could actually use if I were already shopping there for something in my size.
- Honestly, this whole email reads like it's aimed at my daughter, not me. Every model shown is a younger woman. ## What's working

- The $15/$30 off coupon codes are clear and usable store-wide, not tied to a specific gender or category.
- No cluttered logo wall this time — simpler, cleaner layout than the last several sends from this brand.
- Barcodes printed right in the email means I could use the coupon in-store without hunting for a code.
- "Fall Prep for Less" is at least a seasonally relevant headline, even if the products under it aren't for me.

## What's weak

- Every product and model shown — clogs, Crocs, HEYDUDE — is squarely women's fashion, nothing for a 62-year-old man's closet.
- No mention anywhere of arch support, cushioning, or men's comfort walking shoes, which is the whole reason I'd open a shoe email.
- SHOE PERKS banner up top but no points balance or personalized "you have X points" — feels like a form letter, not a "we know you" email.
- I have to scroll past three separate product blocks before hitting anything (the coupon) that applies to me at all.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this send by gender/interest — if Shoe Carnival knows I've never clicked a women's clog or Crocs email, don't lead with three straight blocks of them. Lead with the coupon and a men's comfort shoe up top instead.
- 2. Add a men's or unisex comfort category into the mix — Skechers, New Balance, Brooks — even a small block would give me a reason to keep scrolling.
- 3. Surface my SHOE PERKS point balance instead of just a generic "Sign Up" banner — I might already be a member.
- 4. Move the store-wide $15/$30 coupon up top since it's the one thing here that's actually relevant to any shopper, not just clog buyers.
