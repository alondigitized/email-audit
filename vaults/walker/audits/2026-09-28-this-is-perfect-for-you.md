# This is perfect for you!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-28**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one actually starts decent — that black Skechers Slip-in up top with the chunky cushioned sole looks like something I'd actually put on my feet, and "cozy fit" is the kind of language that gets my attention over "sporty" or whatever the kids are wearing. But then it falls apart the second I scroll to "here's some more options" — a boy's garage sneaker, a girl's glam racer in pink, kids' stuff mixed right in with the men's picks. I don't have grandkids in that age bracket shopping through my email, so that's just noise.
- The 20% off boots & outdoor banner catches my eye, but the deadline says "valid thru 9/28" — that's today. If I open this at 4pm I've got a few hours, if I open it tomorrow it's dead on arrival. Feels like I'm getting the tail end of somebody else's campaign.
- Would I click? Maybe on that hero shoe, if I felt like browsing. Forward to a friend? No. Mark as read and move on — it's a "some other time" email, not a "right now" one.

## What's working

- The hero shoe is a men's slip-in with visible cushioned sole — that's genuinely in my lane, hands-free and comfort-first is exactly what I look for.
- "Contour Foam - Cozy Fit" copy speaks to comfort, which matters more to me than style.
- Clean layout, no overlapping text or broken images — nothing to complain about visually.
- The 20% off banner is a real, specific offer, not vague "sale" language.

## What's weak

- The "more options" grid throws kids' and women's shoes at me right after a men's hero — feels like a generic template, not a list built for me.
- "Still thinking about it? You've got great taste!" — I don't know what this is referring to, I don't recall browsing this specific shoe, so it reads like flattery filler rather than something earned.
- The 20% off deadline is today, which means this either arrived too late to matter or is rushing me into a decision I didn't ask to make.
- No price shown anywhere on the hero shoe — if you want me to click, tell me what it costs.

## Recommendations

- 1. Filter that "more options" grid to men's styles only — showing me kids' garage sneakers wastes the real estate that should be reinforcing my interest.
- 2. Put an actual price (or "from $X") under the hero shoe — I'm not clicking through blind to find out if it's in my budget.
- 3. Extend or restate the boots/outdoor deadline with more lead time, or drop it from a "browse abandonment" email entirely — mixing an urgent sale with a soft "still thinking about it" nudge is a mismatched pairing.
- 4. Drop the "you've got great taste" line unless it's tied to something I actually looked at — feels hollow otherwise.
