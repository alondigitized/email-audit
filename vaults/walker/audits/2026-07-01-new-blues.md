# New blues
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- The subject line "New blues" got me to open out of curiosity — but the moment I landed in the body, I realized this was a color drop, not a comfort story. The whole email is about a "Legacy Pack" in frosty blue tones. Big hero image, six shoes laid out in a grid, a "Keep it chill" header. It's a clean, well-rendered send. But HOKA just had me excited with "Now walk it out" — they finally pivoted away from trail and talked to someone like me. This one pivots back toward aesthetics and color palettes, and I'm not shopping by color. I'm shopping by what holds up my arch for eight hours.
- I wouldn't click through to buy. I might poke around out of habit because I do trust HOKA, and I'd recognize the Bondi 9 and Gaviota 6 in that grid as shoes that actually fit my needs. But the email never told me why those shoes matter — only that they come in blue now. That's not a good enough reason to drop $250.
- I'd mark this as read. Wouldn't forward it. Wouldn't regret opening it, but I wouldn't act on it either.

## What's working

- **Clean layout, no render issues** — six shoes in a tidy grid, hero image loaded fine, nothing overlapping. Easy to scan on a phone without zooming.
- **"Shop Legacy Pack" CTA is clear** — one button, obvious copy, shows up twice. At least I know where the click goes.
- **Men's options are present** — Gaviota 6, Skyward X 2, Bondi 9, Speedgoat 7 are labeled Men's. I can see shoes that belong to my gender without guessing.
- **Free shipping offer is stated upfront** — the top banner calls out $250+ expedited shipping clearly. I appreciate when they lead with the deal rather than bury it.
- **Seasonal tie-in lands** — "Hot days call for cool colors" makes sense for July. It's not stale copy.

## What's weak

- **No comfort language anywhere** — not one mention of cushioning, arch support, stability, or any of the things that actually make me choose a shoe. The Bondi 9 is there but I'd never know it has max cushioning from this email.
- **"New blues" tells me nothing** — I opened thinking maybe there was a new comfort technology or a new silhouette. It's a color capsule. That bait-and-switch on the subject kills my trust a little.
- **Women's shoes mixed in without separation** — the Clifton 11 and Challenger 8 are women's, but they share the same grid as the men's. I have to read the tiny "Women" label under each. That's friction on a phone screen.
- **$250 free shipping threshold is high** — most of these shoes are $130–$180. I'd have to buy two pairs to qualify. That's not a meaningful offer for most browsing sessions.
- **The SMS signup block feels intrusive** — there's a full dark-background section asking me to sign up for texts. At 62, I don't want more texts. That block breaks the shopping momentum right when I'm scanning the grid.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the comfort story, not the color.** The Bondi 9 has max cushioning. The Gaviota has stability. One line under each shoe in that grid ("Max cushion. Men's.") would do more work than "frosty tones" ever could.
- 2. **Gender-split the grid.** Put the men's shoes in one block, women's in another, so I don't have to hunt. Better yet, serve me a men's-only version.
- 3. **Drop the $250 threshold or make it per-shoe.** Free shipping on a single pair gets people to the cart. $250 minimum gets people to close the tab.
- 4. **Move the SMS block to the footer** — or remove it entirely from the main flow. Interrupt the grid with a sign-up ask and you lose the scroll.
- 5. **Tie back to the "Now walk it out" thread** — that send built real goodwill. A line like "The everyday walkers from last week — now in new colors" would connect the two campaigns and reward subscribers who've been paying attention.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Cool colors. Max cushion. Built for hot days.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free expedited shipping on $250+ — shop the Legacy Pack`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The comfort shoes you already know, in frosty new tones`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate `<meta name="viewport">` tags (lines 5–6):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
<meta content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=yes" name="viewport" />
```
The two declarations conflict — `maximum-scale=1` (first) disables zoom; `user-scalable=yes` (second) re-enables it. Browser/client behavior on duplicate viewport metas is undefined. The second tag also uses reversed attribute order (`content` before `name`), which is valid but inconsistent.

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers and some email clients (notably Samsung Mail) surface the `<title>` as the document label. Empty title degrades accessibility.

**Malformed CSS — missing values in `@media (max-width: 480px)`:**
```css
.nav_padding { padding-top:px !important; padding-bottom:px !important; }
.nav_border  { border-top:px solid ; }
```
Both rules have zero-length numeric tokens — the `px` unit appears without a preceding value, and `border-top` is also missing its color value. These declarations are parse errors and will be silently dropped by all CSS engines. If `.nav_padding` and `.nav_border` are applied to nav elements, those elements will not receive mobile padding/border adjustments.

**Missing `http-equiv` on charset meta:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Should be `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />`. Without `http-equiv`, Outlook 2007–2019 (Word rendering engine) may ignore the charset declaration.

**Web font support scope:**
`@font-face` for `TheFutureHOKA` loads from `hoka.com/on/demandware.static/...`. Web fonts render only in Apple Mail, iOS Mail, and Samsung Mail — Gmail, Outlook (Windows), and most webmail clients will fall back to the system font stack. No explicit fallback `font-family` is declared in the `@font-face` blocks themselves (fallbacks must be set at the usage site in `font-family` stack declarations). Cannot verify those usage-site stacks from truncated source.

**`a { color: #000001; }` / `.footer_text a { color: #fffffe; }`:**
Near-black and near-white values are a known workaround to suppress Gmail's auto-link colorization. Intentional — no issue.
