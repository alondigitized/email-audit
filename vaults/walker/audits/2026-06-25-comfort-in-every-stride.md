# Comfort In Every Stride
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-08**
## Executive summary

- Opening a Skechers email is muscle memory for me at this point — I recognize the sender and I tap. But I'll say what I've been saying since early June: that preview text is broken JSON garbage again. That's seven-plus emails in a row now with the same technical failure sitting in my inbox before I even open the thing. At some point it stops being a glitch and starts being a statement about how much they care about the details.
- Once I'm inside, the BOGO 50% off banner stops me cold — that's the kind of math I like. Second pair half price, or 20% off if I'm buying one. That's real money on decent footwear. And "Comfort In Every Stride" set the right expectation in the subject line. Then the hero loads and I see a guy half my age in a track jacket jumping three feet off the ground. I'm 62. That's not my stride. I kept scrolling to find the walking section — and it's there, sitting in the middle of the email between the running shoes and the casual sneakers — but it's a thumbnail with no copy about what makes those shoes worth wearing.
- I'd click the BOGO offer because the deal is solid, but I wouldn't forward this to anyone. It's a promotional mass-blast that happened to include a walking section, not an email written for someone like me.

## What's working

- The BOGO 50% off offer is the first thing I see — no hunting required, deal is front and center with a "SHOP NOW" button big enough to tap.
- Subject line is short, 23 characters, and the word "Comfort" is the right hook for a guy who shops by feel, not by looks.
- The WALKING section exists with a dedicated "SHOP MEN'S" CTA — at least someone remembered that not every Skechers buyer is training for a 10K.
- The email body renders cleanly — no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout weirdness inside the scroll.
- BOGO terms are honest and clear up front: buy one, get one 50%, OR 20% off a single pair. No buried asterisk surprise.

## What's weak

- Broken preview text — again. Raw JSON code in my inbox preview for the seventh consecutive Skechers email in June. I've stopped being surprised and started being embarrassed on their behalf.
- The hero image is a young athletic guy mid-jump in a hoodie. "Comfort In Every Stride" is the subject; the hero should show someone striding comfortably, not somebody doing plyometrics.
- "MAKE YOUR MOVE" as the hero headline is a gym tagline. The word "comfort" disappears from the email the moment you open it — it's in a small gray subhead below the hero and nowhere else.
- The WALKING section is photo-only. No cushion callout, no arch support mention, no fit detail. Just shoes and a button. Doesn't give me a reason to tap that button over any other.
- 25% off apparel section — I don't care about Skechers T-shirts. That space could've been another walking or slip-on product.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text.** Whatever is injecting JSON schema into the preheader slot has been broken for a month. Every other improvement is secondary to this. Ship a fix before the next send.
- 2. **Replace the hero model.** Put a 55–65 year old man in comfortable walking shoes on a trail or sidewalk — not an athlete in compression gear on a gym floor. One photo change and this email speaks to a completely different (and loyal) segment.
- 3. **Move comfort language into the hero headline.** "Comfort In Every Stride" is in the subject — put it in the email too. Something like "All-Day Comfort, Built to Last" above the BOGO offer. Right now the body hero says "MAKE YOUR MOVE" and that comfort promise vanishes the second I open it.
- 4. **Add a deadline to the BOGO.** "Offer ends Sunday" turns a "maybe later" into a tap right now. No urgency visible anywhere in this email.
- 5. **Feature a specific walking shoe in the men's section.** Name it, cite a comfort feature (memory foam insole, arch support, cushioned sole), and link directly to it. Generic "SHOP MEN'S" is easy to scroll past.
- **Subject Alt A:** `BOGO 50% Off — Men's Walking Styles Inside`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Comfort Built for Walking — Buy One, Get One 50% Off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Men's walking shoes with cushion and arch support — offer ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Buy one, get one 50% off — new walking styles just added for men.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**27 click-redirect links** route through `click.emails.skechers.com` — standard SFMC click-wrapping. Not individually probeable, but the domain pattern is consistent and expected.

**Open-pixel (ink1000.com) served over HTTP:**
```
http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif?mi_u=8107766165&mi_ecmp=...
```
Mixed-content in HTTPS email clients (Gmail, Outlook 365) will silently block this pixel, undercounting opens attributed to this third-party tracker.

**Kruxd audience-sync beacons** present (`beacon.krxd.net`). These fire identity-matching calls with a hashed subscriber UID and a raw subscriber ID (`partner_uid=121173391`). The raw numeric ID is exposed in HTML source — low severity but worth noting if PII policy is strict.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources — systemic issue.** All 10+ images hosted on `image.emails.skechers.com` use `http://` not `https://`. Modern email clients with image proxy/caching (Gmail Image Proxy, Apple MPP) will either block or re-proxy these. Affected assets include the Skechers logo, app store badges, social icons, and all product imagery:

```
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/1/dde00662-169f-447d-b0e2-fc65f6c2290c.png  ← logo
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/49468f73-...png  ← unlabeled image (no alt)
```

**Two images missing `alt` text:**
- `o.gif` (ink1000 tracking pixel) — acceptable for a pixel, but technically non-conformant
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` — a content image with no alt; degrades screen-reader and images-off rendering

**CSS defensive patterns are present and correct:** `x-apple-data-detectors`, `.ExternalClass` normalization, `-webkit-text-size-adjust`, and dual breakpoints at 375px/640px/680px. No issues.

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta** prevents user zoom on mobile — an accessibility regression (WCAG 1.4.4). This is a common but real issue.
