# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-10-02**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I got as far as a shoe I liked, then the journey fell apart. The homepage leads with a racing shoe, the Skechers Aero Elite, which isn't what I shop for. The men's shoe pages do have the right ideas ("Hands Free Slip-ins", "Walking Shoes"), and the search for "comfort shoes" gave me real, relevant results. But my Add to Cart attempt ended on a "Please select a size" error. The cart page then came up as a completely blank white screen, so I never saw a cart. I'd have gone to Hoka or New Balance at that point. I also saw no sign that I was logged in.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** Yesterday was also 3/10, so I score it flat. I only have yesterday's score, not its screenshots, so I can't say which problems carried over. Today's wins are the comfort-oriented menus and search that works. The blank cart outweighs them, because a shopper who can't see a cart can't buy.

## 3. What's Working

- **The top nav speaks my language.** The scrolling strip reads "Slip-ins", "Wide Fit", "Arch Fit" and "Max Cus…" (cut off, probably Max Cushioning). Those are the things I shop for.
- **Men's Shoes page.** The first tiles are "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes". The photos are big and the labels are readable.
- **The menu drawer is easy to use on a phone.** It has big rows with arrows, plain words, and a Back button. "Comfort Technologies" is a category.
- **Product page.** The page shows the price ($88.00), a 4.5-star rating with 66 reviews, and a Hands Free Slip-ins badge. Those reviews make me trust it more.
- **Size buttons are large and easy to tap.** Sold-out sizes are struck through, and the page says what to do about it.
- **Search "comfort shoes" returned 1,578 results.** The first two were Contour Foam Cozy Fit Luxe Comfort and Glide-Step Comfort, which are the right shoes. Price ranges and color swatches show up in the results.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart page was blank white.** This is the worst problem, and it would end my visit.
- **Add to Cart didn't work.** I saw "Please select a size" and the cart badge still said 0. Many sizes are struck out. Only 7.5, 8.0, 8.5 and 9.0 look available. I may well have clicked Add before choosing a size, but the page didn't scroll me to the sizes, so I couldn't tell what to do.
- **No login evidence.** After "Log In" the screen looks identical to the homepage, with the same promos and a cart of 0. I saw no name, greeting, or "Welcome back", so nothing felt personalized.
- **The homepage doesn't speak to me.** It's a full-screen racing-shoe ad ("Built to…"). Nothing on the first screen is about comfort, slip-ins or arch support.
- **The promo text is small and gray.** "Limited Time Only! 25% off Apparel & Bags - Applied at Cart" is hard to read without zooming. The "applied at cart" wording is also confusing when the cart is empty or broken.
- **The men's shoes page spends a lot of space before any shoe.** There are 744 results and two screens of header, tiles, a pickup toggle and a filter bar.
- **No comparison or price-versus-quality content.** I saw nothing comparing comfort levels, or Skechers against Hoka or Brooks, and no "what you get for $88" math.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the blank cart first.** Find out why `/cart/` renders empty (a script error or a blocked request) and add a visible error fallback.
2. **Make the size error obvious.** Scroll to the size row and highlight it. Better, pre-select a size or show "Only 4 sizes left in stock".
3. **Show I'm logged in.** Put my name or a "Hi, Walker" in the header and use my past browsing to lead with slip-ins and wide fit.
4. **Add a comfort hero to the homepage.** Put Slip-ins, Arch Fit and Max Cushioning above or next to the running ad.
5. **Raise the promo text size.** Use darker text and make the BOGO terms clear.
6. **Add a short "Why it's comfortable" block on product pages.** Cover cushioning, arch support, weight, and a per-wear cost line.

## 6. Bottom Line

The ingredients for a comfort-first shopper are there: slip-ins, arch fit, good search, and trustworthy reviews. A shopper like me never gets to use them, because the cart is a blank page and the add-to-cart step doesn't guide you. I'd close the page and go look at New Balance. If they fix the cart, this site moves from a 3 to somewhere near a 6 or 7.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary
The audit covers 8 steps. Capture quality is limited: LCP and CLS were not recorded for any step (`?`), and the 429 responses suggest bot-mitigation interference. The Log In and Men category HTML snapshots report `https://www.skechers.com/` as their URL, so those steps were probably overlays or client-side transitions rather than real navigations.

- Axe found 17 violations across 3 pages: 1 critical, 3 serious, 11 moderate and 2 minor.
- The View Cart violations come from a 429 response (`https://www.skechers.com/cart/`), so they describe an error page, not the real cart.
- 6 network errors, all HTTP 429, five of them on the Kasada (`x-kpsdk`) fingerprint endpoint.
- The homepage injects a large number of third-party tags.

## Accessibility
- **Critical, `button-name` (Homepage):** at least one button has no accessible name.
- **Serious, `link-name` (Homepage):** at least one link has no discernible text.
- **Landmarks (Homepage):**
  - `landmark-main-is-top-level` and `landmark-no-duplicate-main` indicate multiple or nested `<main>` elements.
  - `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` and `landmark-unique` also fire.
  - `region` fires, so content sits outside landmarks.
  - The same landmark pattern recurs on Search (`landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique`, `region`), so it is likely template-level.
- **Moderate, `heading-order` (Homepage):** heading levels skip.
- **Minor, `aria-allowed-role` (Homepage, Search):** an invalid ARIA role is applied to an element.
- **View Cart:** `document-title`, `html-has-lang`, `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one` all fail. This is consistent with a bare 429 response page. Re-test once the cart loads.
- The homepage `<html>` tag does have `lang="en"`.

## SEO
- **View Cart:** the 429 page has no `<title>`, no `lang` and no `<h1>`. This is harmless if the cart is `noindex`, but it shows that rate-limited responses are not served as proper error pages.
- **Homepage:** `heading-order` and the multiple `<main>` elements weaken the document outline.
- **Not assessed:** canonical tags, meta descriptions and structured data fall outside the truncated HTML samples.

## Performance
- **Missing metrics:** LCP and CLS are not available for any step. The Web Vitals observers probably did not fire, or were cleared on navigation.
- **TTFB:** the full navigations measured 421–460ms on Homepage, Log In and Men category, and 305ms on View Cart. Men > Shoes, Product detail, Add to Cart and Search show 25–28ms, which is consistent with client-side routing or cache hits.
- **Third-party load:** the homepage `<head>` injects a large number of third-party tags:
  - Ad and marketing pixels: TikTok (3 scripts), Pinterest, Snapchat, Reddit, Facebook, Nextdoor, Taboola, Yahoo (`ytc.js`) and Impact.
  - Analytics and tag managers: Quantum Metric, Monetate (3 scripts), Bing, GTM, gtag (3 destinations), Bloomreach, Pulse Insights and Go2.
  - `s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` is injected twice and `tags.creativecdn.com` twice on the Men category page. This is duplicate tag firing.
- **GTM environment:** the GTM container loads with `gtm_preview=env-1&gtm_auth=…&gtm_cookies_win=x`. This is a non-live GTM environment (preview/staging snapshot) being served on the production domain.
- **Web fonts:** `webfontloader.js` is loaded from the cdnjs CDN. This adds a third-party dependency on the font-rendering path.

## Mobile Optimization
- The page uses a JS-set `--vh` custom property (`6.64px` in the sample). A value that small suggests the capture ran in a very short viewport, so viewport-dependent layout may not have been exercised at a realistic size.
- No viewport meta, tap-target or responsive-image data was captured, so those areas were not assessed.
- Mobile-specific results are limited by the missing LCP and CLS.

## Console & Network Errors
- **429 on Kasada (`/149e9513-…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.825`):** 5 occurrences. This is bot-mitigation fingerprinting rate-limiting the automated session. It is likely a test-harness artifact rather than a user-facing defect.
- **429 on `https://www.skechers.com/cart/`:** the cart page was rate-limited. This invalidates the View Cart step results.
- **Permissions policy violation (`unload is not allowed in this document`):** 6 occurrences. A script registers an `unload` handler, which is deprecated and blocks bfcache. It is probably from a third-party tag.
- **`Unsupported Summarizer API languages…`:** 2 occurrences. This comes from Chrome's built-in AI Summarizer API being called with unsupported languages. It is likely an injected script or extension.
- The console list shows 10 entries against a stated count of 26, so the remainder is not visible.

## Recommendations
1. Fix the critical `button-name` and serious `link-name` failures on the homepage by adding `aria-label` or visible text.
2. Consolidate the homepage to a single top-level `<main>`, make `<aside>` landmarks top-level, give duplicate landmarks distinct `aria-label`s, and fix the heading order.
3. Verify that the production GTM snippet does not load a preview or `env-1` environment.
4. Deduplicate the double-injected tags (`ytc.js`, `creativecdn`) and audit tag load against a performance budget.
5. Replace `unload` handlers with `pagehide` or `visibilitychange` to restore bfcache eligibility.
6. Serve rate-limited responses with a proper document (`<title>`, `lang`, `<main>`, `<h1>`) or a JSON body.
7. Fix the audit harness:
   - Allowlist the monitor in Kasada or add backoff and retry on 429.
   - Capture LCP and CLS with `PerformanceObserver` using `buffered: true`.
   - Run at a realistic mobile viewport.
   - Record the true URL for each step.
   - Re-run View Cart after a successful load.
