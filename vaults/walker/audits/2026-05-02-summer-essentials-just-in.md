# Summer Essentials, Just In
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-02**
## Executive summary

- Opened it, scanned it, kind of shrugged. The BOGO headline is good — that's the kind of deal that gets my attention — but then the email goes off in a bunch of directions that have nothing to do with me. I don't need women's styles, kids' stuff, or running shoes. I need comfortable slip-ons I can wear walking around town. The word "Slip-ins" doesn't appear once in this whole thing. It's like they forgot what I buy. And whatever happened in my inbox preview line was a mess — looked like computer code, not an offer.
- The BOGO offer would've driven me to shop if they'd pointed me straight at men's comfort shoes. Instead they scattered my attention across categories I'll never touch. Half the email was dead weight for me.

## What's working

- **The BOGO offer is loud and clear.** Buy One, Get One 50% off — that's right at the top, hard to miss, and it's exactly the kind of deal that would make me put down my coffee and pay attention. I'm no impulse buyer, but 50% off a second pair? That's real money.
- **Walking and Sandals categories showed up.** Those three little tiles — Walking, Sandals, Running — at least Walking and Sandals are in my wheelhouse. Nice to know those pages exist.
- **Clean, simple layout.** The sections stack cleanly and the text is readable enough. On my phone I didn't have to squint too hard.
- **Casuals shoe looks good.** That white and navy sneaker in the Casuals section is exactly what I'd wear on a weekend. Good photo, clean product shot.

## What's weak

- **Zero mention of Slip-ins or comfort technology.** I'm a Skechers customer because of Slip-ins and Air-Cooled memory foam. Neither appears anywhere in this email. Feels like they sent me the same thing they sent everybody else.
- **My membership got ignored.** I'm a Skechers Plus member. Not a single word about points, exclusive access, early dibs — nothing. That's a miss. A loyal customer deserves to feel recognized.
- **"Just Dropped" reads young.** That headline with the swooshy font is trying to feel trendy. I don't need trendy. I need comfort. The lifestyle photo underneath shows younger-looking folks, not someone my age.
- **Women, Kids, Clothing sections are clutter to me.** I scrolled past "WOMEN," "KIDS," "CLOTHING" and thought — why are you showing me this? I shop Men's. Always have.
- **25% off apparel is irrelevant.** I don't buy Skechers sweatshirts. A full banner for apparel discount is wasted on me.
- **Running section doesn't apply.** I walk. I don't run. That shoe looks like it belongs on somebody half my age doing 5Ks.
- **The preview text in my inbox was gobbledygook.** Before I even opened this thing, the preview showed something that looked like programming code. That's not a good first impression.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with Men's Slip-ins and comfort tech** — that's what I came for, that's what keeps me loyal.
- 2. **Put the BOGO deal front and center with a direct Men's CTA** — "Shop Men's BOGO" beats a generic "Shop Now."
- 3. **Acknowledge my membership** — even a small "Your Plus points are waiting" line would've made me feel like a real customer, not a cold lead.
- 4. **Cut the women's and kids' modules from my version** — or at least push them to the bottom. Email personalization 101.
- 5. **Fix that preview text** — whatever leaked that code gibberish should be the first thing the tech team looks at Monday morning.
- 6. **Swap "Just Dropped" language** — something like "New Comfort Picks for Summer" would speak to me a lot better.

## Technical Audit — Skechers "Summer Essentials, Just In" (2026-05-02)

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has a systemic HTTP/HTTPS mixed-content problem affecting 11+ images, which will cause silent image blocking in Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook 365. A secondary issue is a QA pipeline aggregation bug that reports "0 warnings" despite emitting 14.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**HTTP tracking pixel — ink1000.com**
```
src: http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif
     ?mi_u=8107766165&mi_ecmp=MKG_US_NONPURCLICK_U_NEW_NONPURCH_EN_05022026
```
Served over plain HTTP. Modern clients either block or strip it, destroying open-rate accuracy for this send.

**ReturnPath pixel — missing protocol**
```
src: pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1dbf0dcef79cbad82c12fe25342701e4499
```
No `http://` or `https://` prefix. Relative URL resolution fails inside email clients — pixel will never fire.

**Krux DMP beacons exposing hashed PII in URL**
```
https://beacon.krxd.net/1x1_usermatch.gif
  ?partner=LprH63a7_sfmc_100007688_sha25&partner_uid=9153f33fe5cdf59baf088bb835d...
  ?partner=LprH63a7_sfmc_100007688_subid&partner_uid=121173391
```
SHA-256 hashed email and raw subscriber ID transmitted to a third-party DMP (Salesforce/Krux). Confirm this is intentional and covered in the privacy policy; some jurisdictions treat hashed identifiers as personal data.

**26 click links through redirect domain**
All CTAs route through `click.emails.skechers.com`. Not itself an issue, but means UTM parameters (Section 6) cannot be verified without unwrapping redirects.

Gmail (since 2014), Apple Mail (since iOS 15), and Outlook 365 actively block or proxy HTTP image sources. The logo and all footer content images will be invisible in these clients.

**Missing `alt` text — two images**
- `o.gif` (ink1000 open pixel) — low priority (tracking pixel), but a non-empty `alt=""` is still required for valid XHTML
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` — appears to be content, not decoration; missing `alt` breaks screen reader fallback

**Viewport `maximum-scale=1`**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Low enforcement risk for email specifically, but a known accessibility barrier for low-vision users.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applied globally via the universal selector. Disables OS-level font scaling on iOS. Should be `100%` not `none`.

**Malformed `<meta>` charset tag**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Missing the required `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute. Some older clients (Outlook 2007–2013) will not recognize the charset declaration and may misrender special characters (e.g., the `®` in `<title>Skechers®</title>`).
