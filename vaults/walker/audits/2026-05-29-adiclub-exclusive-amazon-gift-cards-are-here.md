# adiClub exclusive: Amazon gift cards are here
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- Another adidas email — I've genuinely stopped counting at this point, but it's well over a dozen from them this spring alone. This one comes in on a completely different angle than I expected: not shoes, not sportswear, but Amazon gift cards. The idea of cashing in my adiClub points for an Amazon gift card is actually mildly interesting — Amazon is where I buy everything from — but the email itself is a mess. There's a massive blank white void below the two-sentence body copy, which is clearly where something important was supposed to render and didn't. No button, no link, nothing to tap. Even if I wanted to act on this, there's nowhere to go.
- On top of that, I spotted the fine print at the bottom: the eligible purchase window runs "April 8, 2025 – November 24, 2025." Today's May 29, 2026. If that's accurate, this offer already expired more than six months ago. That's not a typo — that's someone sending me a dead promo. I'd mark this read and move on.
- Would I click? No — there's nothing to click. Would I forward it? Absolutely not. Would I unsubscribe? Getting closer every send.

## What's working

- **The preheader is one of adidas's better ones** — "Members can redeem points for Amazon gift cards for more of what you love" actually explains the mechanism before I open, which is more than most of their sends do.
- **The concept is genuinely novel** — every prior adidas email has pushed product; a points-for-Amazon-gift-card redemption is a different beat and it got me to open.
- **adidas and Amazon logos are clean and immediately recognizable** — zero confusion about what brands are involved.
- **The offer amounts ($10 and $25) are stated clearly** in the body copy — no hunting for the value.

## What's weak

- **The email is visually broken** — there's a large, obvious blank section between the body copy and the footer where a CTA button and/or additional content clearly failed to render. No button = no click = wasted send.
- **The fine print dates say 2025** — "April 8, 2025 – November 24, 2025" is a dead giveaway this promo either expired or was assembled with stale copy. That's a trust hit, and I noticed it.
- **Zero shoe content** — I subscribed to adidas because they make shoes. This has no product, no comfort cues, nothing I came for.
- **Requires Prime membership AND adiClub** — two loyalty programs need to overlap for this to work; that's complexity I shouldn't have to read fine print to discover.
- **The cadence problem is real** — this is deep into a long streak of adidas emails this spring; the brand is burning goodwill fast.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the broken render** — whatever module was supposed to contain the CTA button didn't load. Ship nothing until that's verified. One working button beats a polished email with a dead middle.
- 2. **Update or kill the fine print dates** — if the offer is genuinely valid, the copy needs current dates. If it's expired, pull the email entirely. Sending a promo with 2025 dates in 2026 is a credibility problem.
- 3. **Show me what $10 or $25 buys at Amazon in the context of shoes** — a quick bridge like "use your gift card to shop footwear on Amazon" would at least tie it back to why I'm on this list.
- 4. **Add a visible deadline** — "offer ends X date" creates urgency and makes the offer feel alive, not archival.
- 5. **Surface my actual point balance** — "You have 850 points — enough for a $10 card" is infinitely more motivating than a generic description of the program.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your adiClub points unlock Amazon gift cards`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Trade your points for $10 or $25 at Amazon`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Redeem by [date] — $10 and $25 cards available now`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Members only: turn adiClub points into Amazon credit`

## Technical Audit — adidas adiClub: Amazon Gift Cards

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound with competent responsive CSS, but has two confirmed broken-experience failures: a malformed click-tracking URL and a missing plain-text fallback. Four HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources also risk silent blocking in modern clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Malformed click-wrapper URL**
The QA probe received a redirect to a relative-style path rather than an absolute URL:

```
unknown url type: '/g/749f19bc-2292-450b-8392-4bddbaf0ef67?txn=d5c16880-5b8b-11f1-82cb-8e0ec7ba23ea'
```

The source wrapper is:
```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/749f19bc-2292-450b-8392-4bddbaf0ef67?cp_tp=v3.eJz...
```

The redirect destination resolves to a path-only value (`/g/...`), meaning the click-tracking server returned a relative URL that most HTTP clients cannot follow. Any recipient clicking this link gets a dead end. The `txn` ID `d5c16880-5b8b-11f1-82cb-8e0ec7ba23ea` is unique per-send and appears correct; the fault is in the redirect handler, not the token.

**[WARN] 22 tracking links skipped** — all routed through click-redirect infrastructure (standard). No direct probe possible, but the one fully-resolved link above demonstrates the redirect handler has a defect worth escalating to the ESP.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] HTTP image sources (4 instances, same asset)**
The arrow graphic is loaded over plain HTTP from the same CDN four times:

```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```

Apple Mail (iOS 15+), Gmail, and Outlook 365 can block mixed-content HTTP images. The CDN (`image.link.adidas.com`) almost certainly supports HTTPS — this is a template authoring error. All four instances should be `https://`.

**[WARN] Open-pixel tracking beacon over HTTP**
```
http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019e74f7eb87-b4030f12-e158-4a05-8e36-3f02e96f05f6-000000/...
```
Same issue — open-tracking pixel served over HTTP. If blocked, open-rate reporting for this send will be undercounted.

**[WARN] Two images missing `alt` text**
- `749f19bc-2292-450b-8392-4bddbaf0ef67` (hero/dynamic image via `dv.adidas.com`)
- `bckwmv4ULdWiVQh7FYIKvKUmA6O_4IwTQjK88ebS4Nc=452` (tracking beacon)

The beacon intentionally has no alt (acceptable), but the hero image missing alt breaks screen-reader accessibility and degrades image-off rendering in Outlook/Gmail.

**`@font-face` declared twice** — `AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are each defined in two separate `<style>` blocks with identical `src` fallbacks (both resolve to system fonts anyway). Redundant but not harmful; suggests copy-paste in template assembly.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens visible in the truncated source (no `{{`, `%%`, `[%`, or similar unfilled placeholders). No issues found.

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text fallback absent**
QA confirmed: `Text version is 0 chars`. CAN-SPAM does not mandate a text part, but its complete absence:
- Increases spam-filter score on SpamAssassin-based filters (`MIME_HTML_ONLY`)
- Blocks rendering entirely in text-only clients and some corporate security gateways that strip HTML

The `<meta name="ROBOTS" content="NOINDEX, NOFOLLOW">` and `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` headers are present and correct for suppressing web crawlers and referrer leakage.

No issues with unsubscribe footer or physical address were observable in the truncated source, but the full HTML was not available for confirmation.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The broken redirect (`/g/749f19bc...`) means UTM attribution for at least one tracked link is unverifiable — the landing page is never reached, so no UTM parameters are passed. The remaining 22 click-wrapped links could not be probed. No direct UTM parameter inspection was possible from the truncated source.
