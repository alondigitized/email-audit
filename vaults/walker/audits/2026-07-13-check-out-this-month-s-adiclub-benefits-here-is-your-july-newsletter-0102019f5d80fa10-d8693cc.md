# Check out this month's adiClub benefits, here is your July Newsletter
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- Well, adidas finally figured out I'm an adiClub member, and they decided the best way to use that information was to send me a soccer sweepstakes newsletter. I'll give them one point for not putting "ADIZERO DROPSET PRO IS READY TO RACE" in the subject line like they did yesterday. But when I opened this thing, the entire email was about winning FIFA World Cup match balls and some signed Adizero item from a player I don't recognize. I'm 62. The last sporting event I followed closely was a Phillies game on the radio.
- The "Stay in the Know" section at the bottom tells me about events I already missed and asks me to download the app. For what? So I can get more alerts about soccer giveaways? The whole layout is clean and nothing broke, I'll say that. But there's not a single shoe in this email I can look at, try to size up, or think about buying. For a loyalty newsletter from a brand I've actually spent money with, that's a missed opportunity the size of a stadium.
- Mark as read and move on. No click. Definitely not forwarding it.

## What's working

- **adiClub branding is front and center** — I know immediately this is a members-only send, not a cold blast. That framing has value.
- **Email rendered cleanly** — Readable text, no broken images, decent structure on a phone screen.
- **"ENTER NOW" button is hard to miss** — Big, green, well-placed. Even on a small phone, my thumbs could find it.
- **Timely hook is real** — "With just 4 matches left" ties to an actual live event, not recycled promo language like half the other adidas sends this month.
- **Clean visual hierarchy** — Match ball giveaway lands at the top, secondary Adizero giveaway below it, info section at the bottom. The eye flows naturally.

## What's weak

- **Zero shoes, zero comfort content** — I'm in the adiClub because I buy adidas shoes. Not one shoe in the whole email. No Cloudfoam, no Ultraboost, no walking anything.
- **Adizero again** — I just reviewed the Adizero Dropset Pro email from yesterday and said clearly it's not for me. Now it's back inside the newsletter as a giveaway prize. The brand keeps serving me that line like I asked for it.
- **Both offers are sweepstakes, not savings** — I can't earn points, get a discount, or act on a purchase. The entire value prop is "maybe you'll win something." That's lottery logic, not loyalty.
- **"Stay in the Know" looks backward** — It recaps events I already missed. I don't need a highlight reel of things I couldn't attend. Tell me what's coming.
- **No acknowledgment of my actual purchase history** — I'm a member. They know what I buy. A smarter newsletter would swap in a "your category picks this month" module. This email treats me like a soccer stranger.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one product module targeted to what I actually buy** — Pull whatever's in my purchase history, or default to their top comfort/walking shoe, and give adiClub members a points multiplier on it. One block. That's the difference between a loyalty newsletter and a giveaway blast.
- 2. **Lead "Stay in the Know" with what's coming, not what I missed** — "Here's what's up next month for adiClub members" would get me to open the app. A recap of things I already missed is just salt.
- 3. **Segment out the soccer content for soccer buyers** — I understand World Cup tie-ins. They make sense for the right subscriber. But I'm not that guy, and a loyalty platform should know that.
- 4. **Give the preheader a real job** — Whatever leaked at the very top of this email isn't doing anything useful. Use that space to name one specific benefit: "Double points on your next purchase + match ball giveaway inside."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your July adiClub perks — enter, earn, and save`
- **Subject Alt B:** `adiClub July: member benefits + a giveaway inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Match ball sweepstakes + double-point offers for members this month`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `July exclusives: earn more, win more — just for adiClub`

## Technical Audit — Adidas adiClub July Newsletter

**From:** adidas@us-news.comms.adidas.com
**Reviewed:** 2026-07-13

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed failures: a broken relative URL on a linked image (likely a template rendering defect) and a completely absent plain-text MIME part. Four HTTP-sourced images are a secondary deliverability risk. All other issues are warnings.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Relative URL in link href — broken in email**

The QA scanner caught an unprefixed path used as a link destination:

```
unknown url type: '/g/f0732c31-784c-42ac-80a3-e92ae2da76bf?txn=28ba6980-7f07-11f1-80af-9295eb1db778'
```

Email clients do not resolve relative URLs. This link will 404 or do nothing for all recipients. The associated tracked click URL resolves to:

```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/f0732c31-784c-42ac-80a3-e92ae2da76bf?cp_tp=v3.eJzj...
```

The `/g/` prefix pattern is consistent with a dynamic content renderer (e.g., Movable Ink) that failed to prepend its base domain during template rendering. The `txn=` parameter suggests a transaction/session token that also did not get fully hydrated.

**[WARN] 26 tracked links use click-redirect domains** — not probeable, but expected for an ESP (Salesforce Marketing Cloud pattern on `click.comms.adidas.com`). Not flagged as an error.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] 4× HTTP image sources — same asset, loaded repeatedly**

```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```

This is the "arrow" image, referenced four times via `http://`. Gmail proxies images to HTTPS automatically; Outlook 2016+ blocks mixed-content images by default. iOS Mail will show a broken image icon. The asset should be served from `https://`.

**[WARN] Tracking pixel served over HTTP**

```
http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019f5d80fa10-d8693ccd-4f24-4551-aee9-84ba598edaa5-000000/...
```

Open-tracking pixels over HTTP are blocked by several clients. This undercounts opens.

**[WARN] 2 images missing `alt` text**

- Dynamic content image: `f0732c31-784c-42ac-80a3-e92ae2da76bf` (the broken-link image above)
- Tracking pixel: `eP-n5xsc15cbrLOWfMJ6ABqmBTOKCfl_R8D8aXXC004=452`

The tracking pixel missing `alt=""` is minor (decorative, should be empty string). The content image missing alt is a screen-reader failure.

**[INFO] `<meta http-equiv>` malformed**

```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```

Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Most clients infer charset from the MIME envelope, but this is technically non-conformant for HTML 4.01.

**[INFO] Duplicate `@font-face` declarations**

`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are declared twice across two `<style>` blocks with slightly different fallback stacks (`local('Calibri')` present in block 1, absent in block 2). No functional breakage, but bloats the `<head>` by ~1KB and creates maintenance confusion. Both declarations resolve to system fonts only — no web font URLs are present, so custom typefaces never load in any client.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens visible in the truncated source (no `{{`, `%%`, or `[%` patterns). The `txn=` parameter in the broken link is the closest candidate — it resolves to a static UUID (`28ba6980-7f07-11f1-80af-9295eb1db778`), suggesting it was substituted but the surrounding URL template was not, which is a template engine partial-render failure rather than a missing variable.

---

### 5. Compliance

**[PASS] ROBOTS meta tag present**

```html
<meta name="ROBOTS" content="NOINDEX, NOFOLLOW" />
```

Prevents web crawlers from indexing the email archive page.

**[WARN] `referrer` policy set to `no-referrer`**

```html
<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />
```

This suppresses the HTTP `Referer` header on all outbound clicks. Any analytics platform relying on referrer attribution for email traffic will see direct/dark traffic instead of email source. Intentional suppression is fine if UTM params are the sole attribution mechanism, but worth confirming.

**[FAIL] Plain-text MIME part absent**

```
Plain-text fallback missing or too short: Text version is 0 chars
```

CAN-SPAM does not require a text/plain part, but its absence:
- Raises spam scores in SpamAssassin and Gmail's filters
- Prevents unsubscribe link from being readable in text-only contexts
- Violates best-practice guidance from most ESPs

The unsubscribe mechanism and physical mailing address (required by CAN-SPAM §5) cannot be confirmed from the truncated source alone, but they must exist somewhere in the full HTML.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Cannot fully audit UTM coverage without seeing all 26 link targets, but the broken `/g/` URL means at minimum one CTA delivers no traffic to any destination — UTM or otherwise. The `cp_tp=v3.eJzj...` parameter on the `dv.adidas.com` destination is a Salesforce Marketing Cloud content-block token, not a UTM parameter. Confirm that standard UTM params (`utm_source=email&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=...`) are appended to the final landing page URLs after redirect chain resolution.
