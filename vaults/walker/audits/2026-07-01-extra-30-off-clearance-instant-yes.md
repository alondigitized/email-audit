# Extra 30% off Clearance = instant yes
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- Alright, Crocs again. I've lost count at this point — they've been in my inbox nearly every other day since mid-June. But I'll give them this: "Extra 30% Off Clearance" is at least a different number than the "25% off" parade I sat through for two weeks straight. The clearance angle caught my eye because I'm not paying full price for a Crocs sandal when the same shoe shows up on clearance a month later anyway.
- I opened the email and the layout is clean enough — big headline, I see the offer right away, no image loading issues. The shoe in the hero is a basic flip-flop style, nothing with the arch support or strap stability I actually care about. The "FINISH THE LOOK — 40% Off Accessories!" section below it lost me completely. I'm not shopping for Jibbitz charms at 62. That's not my finish line.
- I wouldn't click. I might poke around on the clearance page on my own later if I think about it, but this email doesn't give me a specific shoe to look at or a reason to move right now. I'd probably mark it read and move on — same as most of their sends this month.

## What's working

- The headline is the first thing I see and it's plain English — "Extra 30% Off Clearance" doesn't make me decode anything.
- Crocs is a brand I know, so sender trust was already there before I even opened.
- The patriotic red-white-blue imagery matches the July 4th week, so it at least feels timely instead of stale.
- "Savings on Savings" tells me this is stacked on top of an already-marked-down price, which is a real deal signal.
- No broken images, no weird layout gaps — it rendered clean on my phone.

## What's weak

- The preheader is absolute garbage — whatever showed up next to the subject line in my inbox was a wall of invisible characters. That first impression before I even opened? Wasted.
- No CTA button anywhere visible. Where am I supposed to tap? I'm not hunting for a clickable image on my phone.
- The "40% Off Accessories" block is irrelevant to me — I don't buy charms or lanyards. It dilutes the email for a comfort-shoe shopper.
- No specific shoe shown with a name, a fit callout, or a size note. Just a generic flip-flop photo with "sale sale sale" plastered over it like a newspaper ad from 1994.
- "Exclusions Apply" appears twice in tiny text. After this many Crocs emails, I've learned that means the one shoe I want probably isn't included.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a real tap target in there.** A big, clear button that says "Shop Clearance Men's" would double my click probability. I shouldn't be tapping a photo and hoping.
- 2. **Fix the preheader.** That HTML-entity soup is embarrassing. One line of real copy — "Clearance prices just got lower — today only" — does the job.
- 3. **Drop the accessories block** or move it to the very bottom. Walker doesn't buy charms. Lead with the comfort shoes, all the way down.
- 4. **Show me a specific shoe.** Even one featured clearance pick — "Men's Bayaband Slide, now $24" — gives me something to react to instead of a blurry collage.
- 5. **Add a deadline.** "Through July 4th only" costs nothing to write and turns this into a reason to act today instead of maybe-later.
