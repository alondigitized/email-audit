- The countdown timer with a live clock gives the offer real teeth — I don't like watching Rewards points sit on the table while a deal ticks out.
- "Rewards Member Exclusive" is front and center, which treats me like a known customer, not a cold email blast.
- The Crocs 25% off section calls out a brand by name that I actually cross-shop — that's concrete and clickable.
- The 20% figure is legible and unambiguous; I didn't have to decode the offer.
- Email renders cleanly with no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **The tracking URL preview text is back, same as June 15.** If it hasn't been fixed after the last send, someone needs to make this a priority — it reads as spam and almost cost Famous Footwear my open.
- Women's sandals and heels are the first thing I see after the offer banner — men's products are buried in the middle of a long scroll.
- Not a single word about cushioning, arch support, comfort technology, or anything that explains *why* a 62-year-old guy should care about these shoes beyond the price.
- The product grid thumbnails are tiny at phone scale — I'm squinting to tell if that's a New Balance or a Brooks, and I'm not reaching for my reading glasses over a promo email.
- This is the fourth "20% Rewards Member" email in about twelve days (June 13, 13, 14, 15, now June 25). The offer is starting to feel like ambient noise, not urgency.

- 1. **Fix the preheader before anything else.** The tracking URL in preview is killing opens. Drop in real copy that sells the deal on first glance in the inbox.
- 2. **Lead with men's shoes in the product grid.** Famous Footwear knows I'm a male Rewards Member — use that. Women's sandals shouldn't be my first scroll.
- 3. **Name one comfort-first hero product** above the grid — "New Balance wide-width walkers" or "Skechers slip-ons" with a direct link, not a wall of equal-sized thumbnails.
- 4. **Move the Crocs deal higher.** It's brand-specific, price-specific, and named — that's far more clickable than a generic product grid and it's buried below where many subscribers will stop scrolling.
- 5. **Vary the offer framing.** Even "your last 20% of the season" or "members-only midnight event" would feel less routine than the same headline that's appeared four times this month.

## Technical Audit — Famous Footwear "Extra 20% Ends Tonight"

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound for a legacy HTML4 ESP template, but carries a systemic alt-text deficit across 26 images, a broken `<meta>` charset declaration, a CSS property typo, and an empty preheader — all confirmed by source inspection and QA output.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**No broken links detected.** All 34 CTAs route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com` — expected for this ESP.

- QA skipped HTTP probing on all 34 links (tracking redirect domains — correct behavior).
- Open pixel present: `https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/open.aspx?HNXEV6FWO6QUBAUL7QDRYSZJAA.70254&d=70254&bmt=0`
- Destination URLs are fully obfuscated inside base64-encoded `qs=` params — downstream UTM verification is not possible from source alone (see Section 6).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<meta>` charset declaration is broken.**

```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```

Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Without it, the charset is not declared to the HTML parser. Should be:

```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```

**CSS property typo — `lineapacing`.**

```html
style="...lineapacing:20px..."
```

`lineapacing` is not a valid CSS property (likely intended as `letter-spacing` or `line-height`). The declaration is silently ignored by all clients.

**`max-height:auto` is invalid CSS.**

In the mobile `<img>` rule: `max-height:auto !important` — `auto` is not a valid value for `max-height`. Should be `max-height: none` or removed entirely.

**26 images missing `alt` text** — confirmed by QA. This includes the logo (`3b6a7dc1-6955-49f8-868a-bf62a15122ee.png`), which renders as an empty link in image-blocking clients and is read as nothing by screen readers. Decorative divider images (`4ccff4ad...`, repeated 4×) warrant `alt=""` but product/hero images need descriptive text.

**Preheader is empty.**

```html
<div class="preheader" style="font-size:1px;display:none !important"></div>
```

No text content. Inbox preview will fall through to the first visible text node, likely a nav element or body copy rather than the intended teaser.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All destination URLs are wrapped inside encoded click-redirect parameters (`?qs=ABB7...`). UTM parameters on landing page URLs are not inspectable from source.

**Risk:** If the ESP does not inject UTMs on redirect, sessions will arrive at famousfootwear.com attributed to direct/none rather than email. Verify by manually resolving one click URL and inspecting the final destination for `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` params.

**Deliverability signal:** QA flagged plain-text version at 80% URLs (12,648/15,765 chars). This suggests the plain-text alternative was auto-generated from HTML without stripping click-wrapped URLs — a pattern some spam filters penalize.
