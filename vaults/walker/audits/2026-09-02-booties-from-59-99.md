# Booties from $59.99 🍂
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-02**
## Executive summary

- Same story, same week — that green booties banner up top is the exact one DSW's been running since Sunday, just now with a leaf emoji bolted onto the subject. I scrolled past "your mid week mood boost" and a whole parade of sneakers with sparkles, bows, and slingback heels, and I never once landed on a single shoe I'd put on my own feet. There's a "Men" tab in the nav bar, sure, but that's the only concession to guys like me in this entire email — everything below the fold is booties, ballet flats, and rhinestone sandals.
- I'll give credit where it's due: it's a clean, well-built email, no broken images, easy to scroll on my phone. But it's clean and well-built for somebody else's closet. I'm marking this read and moving on — not clicking, not forwarding, unless it's to razz my daughter about her shoe habit.

## What's working

- The layout's tidy — nothing overlapping, images load fine, easy to thumb-scroll.
- "Booties from $59.99" up top is at least a concrete price, so I know what I'm getting into before I scroll.
- "Shop by Size" and "Shop Wide Width" near the bottom is a smart touch for anyone with real foot needs — wish it applied to something I'd actually buy.
- Free shipping and Afterpay mentioned at the bottom — fine print I'd actually read if there were a product up top worth buying.

## What's weak

- Every hero and category block — booties, "just dropped," "everyone loves these names," "top picks under $50" — is 100% women's footwear. Not one slip-on, walking shoe, or men's casual style anywhere.
- The "Men" nav link is the single nod to my existence in the whole email — no featured products, no comfort callout, nothing.
- No mention of cushioning, arch support, or hands-free comfort anywhere — the stuff that actually gets me to click.
- This is the same green bootie banner I've now seen four or five times this week from DSW — "Chocolate Kisses" one day, "For the Love of Loafers" the next, now "mid week mood boost." Different wrapper, same pitch, still not for me.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment the send — if DSW has my purchase history or browse data showing I look at men's comfort shoes, put men's product in the hero, not buried behind a nav tab.
- 2. Add a dedicated "Men's Comfort" or "Slip-On & Walking Shoes" module with real product — Skechers, Hoka, New Balance-style options DSW actually carries.
- 3. Retire the bootie hero for a few sends — I've seen it enough times this week that it's starting to read as background noise, not an offer.
- 4. Lead with a comfort/fit hook for guys like me instead of "mood boost" — arch support and easy on/off sell me faster than trend language ever will.
