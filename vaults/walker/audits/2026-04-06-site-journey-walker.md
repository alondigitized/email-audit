## Walker Miles — Skechers.com Site Journey Review
**Date:** April 6, 2026 | **Yesterday's Score:** 4/10

## 1. Executive Summary

Good news and bad news today. The good news: Skechers led with exactly what I came for — Hands Free Slip-ins front and center, easy menu navigation, clean product page with the BOGO deal prominently shown. The bad news: the cart page was completely blank. A white nothing. After I went through all the trouble of finding a shoe, picking my size, hitting "Add to Cart" — I ended up staring at an empty white screen. That's the whole reason I came here. If I can't get to my cart, I can't buy anything. And search dumped me into Women's shoes. Not a great trip overall.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 5/10

**Up 1 point from yesterday's 4/10.** The front half of the journey improved — the homepage messaging, the navigation, the product page all felt tighter. But the cart failure is a conversion-killer, and that drags everything down hard. You can't score well when the checkout door is bricked shut.

## 3. What's Working

**Homepage hero — immediately relevant.** The very first thing I saw was "SKECHERS HANDS FREE Slip-ins" in big bold letters below a photo of a casually-dressed man around my age sitting relaxed. That man looked like me. That product is what I want. That's a strong first impression. Zero confusion about what this brand sells.

**BOGO deal is visible everywhere.** The blue banner at the top — "BOGO 50% OFF Applied at Cart" — followed me across every page. As a price-conscious shopper, I appreciated the constant reminder. I didn't have to wonder if the deal was still valid.

**Men's navigation menu is clean and logical.** When I opened the menu and went to Men's, the layout was a simple vertical list: Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections, and then at the very bottom — "Hands Free Slip-ins" as its own direct link. That's exactly where I was headed. No hunting, no confusion. Tap targets were large, text was readable without squinting.

**Product page did its job.** The "Skechers Slip-ins: Max Cushioning Arch Fit 2.0 - Azusah" page showed me what I needed: big product photo, star rating, price ($135), and the BOGO deal restated in red. The Hands Free Slip-ins badge was right there. The size grid had large, tappable boxes. The "ADD TO CART" button was a big bold blue — you couldn't miss it.

**Width filter on the category page is a nice touch.** Wide Fit, Extra Wide Fit, Medium — right there at the top of the Slip-ins results. My feet aren't as narrow as they used to be. That filter is useful and I saw it immediately.

---

## 4. What's Weak

**The cart page is completely broken.** Step 8 was a blank white screen. Nothing. No items, no totals, no checkout button — just white. This is a catastrophic failure. A 62-year-old who doesn't troubleshoot websites just sees a broken page and gives up. I would have given up. This alone would have cost Skechers my purchase.

**Login step was invisible.** Step 3 was supposed to be me logging in, but the screenshot just shows the homepage again — no login form, no confirmation that I'm signed in, no "Welcome back, Walker." If I'm a Skechers Plus member, I want to feel recognized. I didn't.

**No Skechers Plus member personalization anywhere.** After what should have been a login, nothing changed. No loyalty points shown, no personalized greeting, no member-exclusive pricing callout. The BOGO banner is the same thing everyone sees. I'm a loyal member — treat me like one.

**Search for "slip in" served me Women's shoes first.** I searched "slip in" and got 1,027 results — a massive pile — but the first two visible products were both Women's. "Arch Fit Arcade - See Ya There" (Women's) and "Contour Foam - Cozy Fit" (Women's). If I'm logged in as a male member with a history of browsing Men's, there's zero excuse for this. It should know to show me Men's first.

**The popup dismissal step was unclear.** Step 2 just showed me the homepage with the rotating banner shifted to "Free Shipping with Skechers Plus." I couldn't tell if a popup appeared and was dismissed, or if nothing happened at all. Ambiguity like this is frustrating on a phone.

**263 results with no smart default sorting.** Hands Free Slip-ins for Men returned 263 options. That's overwhelming. With no logged-in personalization to help surface my likely size range or past purchase preferences, I'm left scrolling forever.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** This is Priority Zero. A blank cart means zero revenue from any shopper who reaches it. Test across mobile devices and browsers.

2. **Show logged-in status clearly.** After login, put "Hi, Walker" or a points balance in the header. Even just the person icon changing to filled/active would help. I need to know my login worked.

3. **Apply gender filter to search results automatically.** If I'm logged in as a male member, "slip in" search should default to Men's results. Showing Women's shoes first to a Men's customer wastes my time and erodes trust.

4. **Highlight Skechers Plus member benefits on the product page.** Add a line near the price: "You earn [X] points on this purchase." That's the loyalty hook that keeps me coming back.

5. **Reduce the result count burden.** 263 Slip-ins is too many choices. Surface a "Most Popular" or "Best for Comfort" curated shortlist at the top for shoppers like me who don't want to sort through everything.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Site runs on Salesforce Commerce Cloud (Demandware). Bot protection via Kasada SDK. 20+ third-party async scripts loaded on every page. Kasada fingerprint endpoint rate-limiting during the journey indicates SDK misconfiguration or overly aggressive polling. A Stylitics widget integration has a CORS misconfiguration that breaks the component entirely. LCP and CLS were not captured, leaving Core Web Vitals coverage incomplete. 32 axe violations across 4 pages, including 4 critical-severity ARIA issues.

## Accessibility

**32 violations total. 4 critical, 5 serious, 15 moderate, 3+ minor.**

**Critical**
- `aria-required-parent` (Homepage): ARIA role children exist outside their required parent container — likely a carousel or list component with broken markup.
- `aria-required-children` (View Product): Parent ARIA role is missing required child roles — common in custom select/listbox implementations.
- `aria-valid-attr-value` (View Product): One or more ARIA attributes have invalid values (e.g., `aria-labelledby` pointing to a nonexistent ID).
- `button-name` (View Product): Buttons with no accessible name — likely icon-only buttons (close, swipe) with no `aria-label`.

**Serious**
- `link-name` (Homepage): Anchor tags with no discernible text — likely image links missing `alt` text or `aria-label`.
- `link-in-text-block` (Men's Shoes): Links in body text differentiated only by color, no underline or other non-color indicator — fails WCAG 1.4.1.
- `aria-dialog-name` (View Product): Modal/dialog has no accessible name via `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`.
- `aria-input-field-name` (View Product): ARIA input role element (custom size/color selector) missing accessible label.
- `color-contrast` (View Product): Foreground/background contrast below WCAG AA 4.5:1 threshold.

**Moderate**
- `landmark-no-duplicate-main` + `landmark-main-is-top-level` (Homepage): Multiple `<main>` elements present; at least one is nested inside another landmark. Likely caused by a Monetate/GTM injected content block.
- `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` (Homepage, Men's Shoes): `<aside>` elements nested inside `<main>` or other landmarks instead of being top-level.
- `landmark-unique` (Homepage, Men's Shoes): Multiple landmarks of the same role without unique labels (e.g., two unlabeled `<nav>` elements).
- `heading-order` (Homepage): Heading hierarchy skips levels (e.g., `<h1>` → `<h3>`), likely in a promotional banner injected by personalization.
- `region` (Homepage, Men's Shoes): Content outside any landmark region — orphaned DOM nodes not wrapped in `<main>`, `<nav>`, etc.

## Mobile Optimization

- `style="--vh: 6.640000000000001px;"` set inline on `<html>` via JavaScript — this is a runtime JS fix for the iOS Safari 100vh bug. `dvh` CSS unit is supported in iOS Safari 16+ (released 2022) and would eliminate this JS dependency and its associated layout jank risk.
- The `--vh` value (`6.64px`) equals `1% of innerHeight`, confirmed by `window.innerHeight * 0.01`. This recalculates on resize but is JS-dependent and produces floating-point precision noise in computed values.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 Rate Limiting — Kasada Bot Protection (5 errors)**
```
429 https://www.skechers.com/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.308
```
The Kasada SDK (`x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.308`) is hitting its own fingerprint collection endpoint and being rate-limited. This indicates either: (a) the SDK is polling the `/fp` endpoint more frequently than the server allows, or (b) the bot protection rules are incorrectly triggering on the SDK's own requests. This can degrade bot-detection accuracy and cause false positives on real users.

**429 on `/cart/` (1 error)**
```
429 https://www.skechers.com/cart/
```
The cart page itself returned 429 during the audit. Consistent with elevated TTFB (156ms). Suggests the cart endpoint is rate-limited or under resource pressure — real users could be affected.

**CORS Block — Stylitics Widget (4 errors)**
```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?base=hotspots&version=320dd7a42c' 
from origin 'https://www.skechers.com' has been blocked by CORS policy: 
No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```
The Stylitics CDN (`web-assets.stylitics.com`) is not returning `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` for `skechers.com`. Both the direct CSS fetch and the XHR fallback fail. The Stylitics hotspot/outfit widget is **non-functional** on the page where it appears.
