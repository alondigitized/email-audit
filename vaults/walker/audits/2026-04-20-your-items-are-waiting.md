# Your Items Are Waiting!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-20**
## Executive summary

- This is a classic abandoned-cart nudge, and Skechers actually did the most important thing right: they showed me the exact shoe I was looking at — a Slip-ins Max Cushioning Arch Fit 2.0. That's me to a T. But then the email fumbles. The "recommended for you" section is packed with what looks like women's shoes, the 20% off offer is buried below the fold in small print, and the lower half of the email is a cluttered mess of app downloads, texting sign-ups, and buy-now-pay-later logos that have nothing to do with how I shop. The personalization hits in the hero and then disappears entirely.
- Solid recovery attempt for an abandoned cart, held back by a gender-mismatched recommendation block and a busy footer that dilutes the one thing that might have gotten me to click.

## What's working

- **The hero product is right.** That Slip-ins Max Cushioning Arch Fit 2.0 is exactly the kind of shoe I'd have in my cart. White, comfort-forward, slip-on — it's me. Seeing it front and center is a good reminder.
- **"Complete Your Order" is a clear, direct CTA.** No nonsense. I know exactly what to do. Boomer-friendly button copy.
- **The 20% off offer is a real reason to come back.** Even if it's buried, a deal on a single pair catches my eye when I scroll down. I'm a Plus member — discounts matter to me.
- **The headline is readable.** Big bold type at the top. I didn't have to squint.

## What's weak

- **Those "recommended" shoes look like women's styles.** I glanced at that row and saw slim, colorful shoes that didn't look like anything I'd wear. That's a trust-breaker — it tells me the system doesn't actually know me. If you're going to say "we think you'd love," show me Men's Relaxed Fit or another Slip-ins, not what looks like a Women's Air-Cooled section.
- **The 20% off lives below the fold.** I had to scroll past the product and the recommendation block to find the actual incentive. Lead with the offer, or at least put it near the hero.
- **"We Noticed You Noticing Us" is too clever.** I get it, but it feels like something written for a 28-year-old. Just tell me my cart is waiting. I don't need the wink.
- **The bottom half is noise.** Download the app, text this number, use Afterpay, use Klarna, find a store, follow us on Instagram. I'm not doing any of that. I'm 62 and I came here about shoes. All this clutter makes me feel like I'm being sold to rather than helped.
- **Afterpay and Klarna mean nothing to me.** I pay cash or card. These logos read as "for young people with debt."
- **No mention of my Skechers Plus membership.** If I'm already a loyalty member, tell me how my purchase connects to points or rewards. That would have moved the needle more than a buy-now-pay-later badge.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the recommendation block to men's styles** — specifically other Slip-ins or Arch Fit options. If the cart had a men's shoe, the "you'd love" row should be 100% men's.
- 2. **Move the 20% off above the fold** or integrate it directly into the hero — something like "Your cart is waiting — and here's 20% off to seal the deal."
- 3. **Strip the lower utility clutter.** The texting sign-up, app download, and BNPL blocks should be cut or moved to a slim one-line footer. Keep the focus on the abandoned item.
- 4. **Add a loyalty hook.** "As a Skechers Plus member, you're X points away from your next reward — this order gets you there." That would land for someone like me.
- 5. **Simplify the headline.** Something like "Still thinking it over, Walker? Your cart's waiting." is warmer and clearer than the current teaser copy.

### 1. Technical Summary
Email is sent via Attentive (ESP: `msgs.skechers.com` / redirect domain: `skechers.attentivemail.com`). All click-tracking links use HTTP rather than HTTPS, which is the primary infrastructure defect; otherwise the template structure is standard Attentive-generated HTML.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**HTTP click-tracking redirects (all links)**
Every href in the visible source routes through `http://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=...` — plain HTTP, not HTTPS. Modern email clients and corporate security gateways flag or strip non-HTTPS redirects. Example:
```
http://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=u001.LNc6VorXOZu5...
```
Expected: `https://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?...`

**Image source protocol**
The logo `<img>` src is truncated in the source but begins `http://imag…` — consistent with the HTTP pattern above. If images are served over HTTP, mixed-content rules in HTTPS-rendering clients (e.g., Apple Mail on iOS 17+) will block them.

**Unverifiable final destination URLs**
Because all links pass through Attentive's opaque redirect, UTM coverage and landing-page alignment cannot be confirmed from the source alone (see §6).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` element**
`<title></title>` — blank. Screen readers announce this as the document title in preview panes; should be set to the subject line or a descriptive fallback.

**Layout tables lack `role="presentation"`**
None of the `<table>` elements carry `role="presentation"`. Screen readers (NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver) may traverse them as data tables and announce row/column counts. Standard fix: add `role="presentation"` to every layout table.

**Image alt text — unverifiable**
The `<img>` tag is truncated in the provided source (`<img src="http://imag…`); alt attribute presence cannot be confirmed. If alt is absent or empty on the logo and product images, image-blocking clients (Outlook desktop default) will render blank space with no context.

**Preheader padding technique**
The preheader uses a mix of Unicode non-breaking spaces (U+00A0) and soft hyphens (U+00AD) as filler characters. This is a functional but fragile pattern — some spam filters penalize high ratios of non-printing characters. Confirm deliverability score is not impacted; zero-width spacers (`&#8203;`) are a lower-risk alternative.

**`#MessageViewBody a` CSS override**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This Gmail-targeted rule strips underlines and inherits color for all links in the body. Intended, but it removes the visual affordance that a text node is a link — an accessibility regression for low-vision users who rely on underlines.
