# Introducing Grey Days 2026.
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-01**
## Executive summary

- **From:** New Balance | **Subject:** Introducing Grey Days 2026.
- I'm a Skechers guy, so getting an email from New Balance is already a bit of a cold call. That said, New Balance isn't a foreign name to me — I wore their walking shoes years ago. This "Grey Days 2026" email opened with a big, clean hero image, and I'll give them credit for that. But the moment I registered the hero model — young woman, fashion-forward pose — I already started mentally checking out. Nothing about this email screams "this is for you, Walker." No comfort messaging, no slip-on ease, no price deal front and center. It's an aesthetic campaign built for sneakerheads and younger shoppers. I'm not that guy.

## What's working

- **"Grey Days" concept is simple enough.** I understood in about five seconds that this is a color-themed promotion. I didn't have to work for it.
- **Shop by Gender section is helpful.** Seeing a clear "Men" tile gave me a fast path to the stuff that might actually apply to me.
- **Product grid shows prices.** I can see numbers like "574 Core" with a price underneath. I appreciate not having to click through just to see what something costs.
- **Clean layout.** The email isn't cluttered. Big image, some text, shoes, done. On a phone that matters — I don't want to scroll through a wall of stuff.

## What's weak

- **The hero model is a young woman.** I'm a 62-year-old man. She's probably 28. I don't see myself in that image at all. It's not offensive, it's just irrelevant.
- **No comfort or walking technology mentioned.** New Balance has a real legacy in walking shoes. Nothing about cushioning, arch support, wide widths — the things that would actually get my attention. It's all about "the color grey."
- **"Grey Days" as a concept feels abstract.** A color event? I get it intellectually, but I don't feel any pull. There's no deal, no urgency, no "here's why grey is good for you." It's mood-board marketing.
- **Body copy is tiny on mobile.** The paragraph under the "Grey Days 2026" headline looks like it's 12-13px on a phone. I'd have to pinch-zoom, and I probably won't bother.
- **No price deal or promo code.** I'm price-conscious. If there's no discount, at least give me something — free shipping, a member perk. There's a "free shipping on orders $75+" banner at the top, but that's barely visible and feels like fine print, not a lead offer.
- **Not a Skechers Plus benefit.** I'm loyal to Skechers because the points program makes me feel appreciated. New Balance gives me nothing that makes me feel like a known customer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the hero for a male model, ideally 50+.** Men's walking/lifestyle is a real NB category. If this email hits my age demographic, show me someone who looks like me wearing grey shoes comfortably.
- 2. **Lead with a comfort hook, not just a color.** Something like "Grey Days Comfort Picks for Men" would stop me. Color alone doesn't motivate a purchase.
- 3. **Make the free shipping threshold a real CTA**, not a tiny top banner. "Free shipping today only on your Grey Days order" would work harder.
- 4. **Increase body copy size.** At least 15-16px for anyone over 55 reading on a phone.
- 5. **Add one clear men's featured pick** with a price and a direct "Shop Men's" CTA before the gender grid. Don't make me hunt.

## Technical Audit — New Balance "Grey Days 2026"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is built on an HTML 4.01 Transitional DOCTYPE with inline CSS classes and partial dark mode support. Several structural CSS issues (duplicate class definitions, empty `@font-face` declarations) and a mixed-content image reference are confirmed in the source.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **HTTP asset reference**: `.bgwhite` class references `url('http://image.XXXXX.png')` — a non-HTTPS background image URL. Even if the `XXXXX` is an ESP redaction artifact, the bare `http://` scheme will trigger mixed-content blocking in Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail when the page context is HTTPS. Verify the actual resolved URL is `https://`.
- Full CTA link targets and UTM parameters are not visible in the truncated source; see Section 6.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **Duplicate `.bg1` class definition**: Class `.bg1` is declared twice with conflicting values — first as `background-color: #ffffff` (white), then later overridden to `background: #58546d` (slate purple). The second declaration wins in cascade order, likely producing unintended background color on affected elements.
- **Empty `@font-face` blocks**: All nine `@font-face` declarations (proxima-nova, linotype-didot, ITC Garamond) are missing `src:` attributes entirely. These declarations load nothing. Fonts are presumably loaded externally (Adobe Fonts / TypeKit JavaScript), making the email non-functional if that external script is blocked by the client. If the intent is Adobe Fonts, there should be no `@font-face` in the stylesheet — the declarations as written are no-ops that add noise.
- **Malformed `<meta>` tag**: `<meta content="IE=edge" />` is missing the required `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"` attribute. This tag has no effect as written.
- **Single responsive breakpoint**: Only one media query (`max-width: 414px`) is present, targeting iPhone-size screens only. No intermediate breakpoint for larger mobile or tablet viewports (~600px is the standard email breakpoint).
- **Dark mode link override**: The truncated dark mode block applies `a { color: #cf0a2c !important; }` globally — forcing all links to NB red. Any white or light-colored text links on dark backgrounds will lose contrast, and the `!important` flag prevents per-component overrides.
