# Final hours for an extra 25% off
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-13**
## Executive summary

- Same neon-green-on-black tennis court, same "Back to School Sale" banner I've now seen from Nike on July 19th, July 22nd, August 5th, and August 8th — that's five sends in under a month with the identical creative. At this point I could draw that court from memory. The one thing that changed is the countdown pressure: "The Final Stretch," dates stamped right on the email (7.19.26 - 8.15.26), so at least I know this is winding down instead of dragging on forever.
- None of this is for me anyway — Air Force 1s, Air Max 270s, joggers, hoodies modeled by guys a third my age. There's no arch support, no cushioned walking shoe, nothing that speaks to comfort or all-day wear. This is a sneakerhead/athletic email wearing a "final hours" urgency costume.
- I'd skim it in about four seconds and archive it. Not forwarding this to anyone — my grandkids maybe, but not a fellow retiree.

## What's working

- The DAYONE code is spelled out plainly in the body along with the 25% and "Exclusions apply" caveat — no hunting for it.
- Date range printed right under the hero ("7.19.26 - 8.15.26") gives real urgency instead of vague "hurry now" language.
- "Free Shipping on Orders $50+" for Members sits right at the top, so loyalty perk is visible without scrolling.
- Layout is clean and easy to tap through even with the repeated banner — big buttons, readable type.

## What's weak

- Fifth appearance of this exact black/neon court graphic since mid-July — I called this out on the 8/8 send too, and it's still the same asset.
- Every product shown is athletic/streetwear on young models — nothing walkable, cushioned, or arch-supported for someone my age.
- "The Final Stretch" gets buried under a giant static banner instead of leading with the urgency — took real scrolling to find the actual offer copy.
- Second "25% Off Shoes" banner near the bottom just repeats the same message and same graphic again — feels like padding, not new info.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop reusing the same neon tennis-court asset for a fifth straight send — swap in different creative so regulars like me don't tune it out on sight.
- 2. Lead with "Final Stretch" urgency copy at the very top, above the fold, not after a giant static banner.
- 3. If Nike wants my click, show me a comfort-forward shoe (their walking/lifestyle line) instead of exclusively basketball and streetwear silhouettes.
- 4. Cut the redundant second "25% Off Shoes" banner — one clear CTA block is enough.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last day: extra 25% off ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `25% off shoes — final hours to shop`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Code DAYONE expires 8/15 at 9:30pm PT`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free shipping on $50+ for Members — today only`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email suffers from a systemic double-encoding bug in its click-tracking redirect layer — every tracked link wraps an already-URL-encoded destination a second time — combined with a missing plain-text MIME part, both of which will materially degrade click-through and deliverability.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Doubled-domain / double-encoding bug (Critical, 12 links affected):** All `click.em.nike.com/CL0/...` tracking links contain a percent-encoded destination URL (`https:%2F%2Fwww.nike.com%2F...`) nested inside what appears to be an already-wrapped redirect. QA flagged both `[FAIL] Doubled-domain bug in URL` and `[FAIL] Doubled-domain merge error in URL` on the same 12 URLs (e.g., `Nike.com`, `Men`, `Women`, `Kids`, `Get Help`, `Learn More.`, and all 4 product deep links to `nike.sng.link`). This is consistent with a merge/template error where the destination URL was concatenated into the tracking wrapper twice, or a redirect service re-wrapped an already-wrapped link. Evidence: `https://click.em.nike.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fwww.nike.com%2Fmembership%3Fcp=...` — the `https:%2F%2Fwww.nike.com` segment appears as an encoded fragment where a single clean destination path is expected.
- **Impact:** Depending on how the redirect service parses this, links may 404, redirect to an unintended URL, or silently double-decode and work by luck — this must be verified by a live click test before send, not assumed safe.
- **Deep links (`nike.sng.link`) share the same bug:** product links using mobile deferred-deep-linking (`_dl=mynike://...`, `_ios_redirect=`, `_android_redirect=`, `_fallback_redirect=`) are wrapped in the same doubled-domain pattern, so the mobile app deep-link fallback chain is also at risk.
- 24 tracking/redirect-domain links were skipped from HTTP probing (expected behavior for `click.em.nike.com` links, not itself an issue).

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** one image (`fup6niNjAAabBlThmX66b_CDqKsnUGW1W7WeBMJajes=452`) has no `alt` attribute — src: `http://click.em.nike.com/CI0/0100019ffb0b54cb-2faeb896-a17e-49a9-8f0d-3dd172038d6a-000000/fup6niNjAAabBlThmX66b_CDqKsnUG`. Screen readers will announce nothing or fall back to the filename.
- **Non-HTTPS image source:** the same image is served over `http://`, not `https://`. Mixed-content image sources are commonly blocked or flagged by mail clients (Outlook, Gmail proxy) and browsers, risking a broken/placeholder image for recipients.
- Responsive/media-query CSS (Foundation-for-Emails-style small-breakpoint rules) is present and appears structurally standard; no other rendering defects surfaced in the QA pass on the truncated markup.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No merge-token placeholders (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `%%FIELD%%`) or unresolved template syntax were observed in the truncated HTML or flagged by QA. No issues found in this category based on available evidence — note that the HTML was truncated, so tokens later in the document could not be inspected.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Plain-text MIME part missing:** QA reports `[FAIL] Plain-text fallback missing or too short: Text version is 0 chars`. CAN-SPAM doesn't strictly mandate a text part, but its absence hurts deliverability/spam-filter scoring and violates common multipart-MIME best practice; it can also cause failures for text-only or accessibility-focused mail clients.
- **Unsubscribe / physical address / authentication headers (SPF/DKIM/DMARC):** not present in the truncated HTML/QA payload provided — cannot confirm or deny compliance. The footer markup (`table.menu.footer__links--primary`, `footer__info`) is present structurally but is cut off before unsubscribe or postal-address text is visible. Flag as **unverified, not failed** — recommend re-running the audit against the untruncated source/footer and raw headers before sign-off.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- All primary nav/CTA links carry a consistent `cp=` campaign parameter (`nans_em_camp_nike_OTHER_20260810_c105589_...backtoschoolsale-finalhou_AEP-NA-US-MW-Email-BackToSchoolSale-Large-0813...`) rather than standard `utm_source/utm_medium/utm_campaign` — this is Nike's internal Adobe Campaign token scheme, which is consistent across links (`Men`, `Women`, `Kids`, `Nike.com`, `Get Help`, `Learn More.`), so campaign attribution tagging is at least internally consistent.
- **Cannot verify landing-page alignment** because every destination is wrapped in the doubled-domain tracking bug described in Section 2 — until that redirect issue is resolved and links are click-tested, email-to-site continuity (does `Men` land on `/men`, does the product link land on the correct PDP) cannot be confirmed as functioning.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Fix the double-encoding/doubled-domain bug** in the click-tracking wrapper before send — trace where the destination URL is being concatenated twice into the `CL0/` redirect path (likely a template/merge issue in the ESP's link-wrapping step). Affects 12 distinct links including all product PDPs and primary nav.
2. **Live-click-test every wrapped link** (desktop + `nike.sng.link` mobile deep links) post-fix to confirm correct final destination and that iOS/Android/fallback redirect params resolve properly.
3. **Add a non-empty plain-text MIME alternative** — current text part is 0 chars.
4. **Add `alt` text** to the flagged image and **serve it over `https://`** instead of `http://`.
5. **Confirm unsubscribe link, physical mailing address, and authentication headers (SPF/DKIM/DMARC)** against the full (untruncated) source and raw email headers — not verifiable from the payload provided.
