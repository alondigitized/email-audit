# Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Date:** June 9, 2026 | **Yesterday's Score:** 4/10

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Skechers is dangling the right bait — Slip-ins, Arch Fit, BOGO — but the line snaps at the finish line. The Men's navigation menu is clean, the size picker is solid, and they get the comfort vocabulary right. But two critical steps flat-out broke on me today: the cart page came up completely blank (a white screen, nothing rendered), and my search for "comfort shoes" dumped me back on the homepage with zero results. I also couldn't tell if I was ever actually logged in. For a 62-year-old guy on his phone trying to spend $125, a blank cart is not a confidence builder. That's a lost sale.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

**No change from yesterday.** The navigation improvements and size selector quality are real positives, but a blank cart page and a broken search are hard regressions that cancel them out. Any shopper who hits a white screen at checkout is gone — to DSW, to New Balance, to wherever the site actually works.

---

## 3. What's Working

- **Men's nav drawer (Step 4)** — This was genuinely good. Large, bold text. Full-width tap targets. "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Extra Wide Fit" are right there without digging. A 62-year-old can read and tap this without glasses on.
- **Hands Free Slip-ins category label (Step 5)** — The page headline is big and clear. "298 Results" tells me there's real selection. The width filter chips (Wide Fit, Extra Wide Fit) up top are smart — that's exactly what I'm looking for.
- **Size selector (Step 7)** — The grid layout with big square tap targets for each half-size is excellent. The sticky "ADD TO CART" blue button across the full bottom of the screen is perfect for a phone user. The inline red "Please select a size" reminder is clear and not annoying.
- **BOGO banner** — Persistent, visible. I noticed it every screen. That's good deal communication.
- **Product photography (Step 6)** — Clean, large image of the shoe. Left/right arrows for additional photos are easy to find.

---

## 4. What's Weak

- **Cart page is blank (Step 8)** — Complete white screen. Nothing. Zero. If I just spent 5 minutes picking a shoe and selecting a size and I tap "Add to Cart" and get a white page, I'm not waiting. I'm going to DSW.com.
- **Search is broken (Step 9)** — I typed "comfort shoes" and landed back on the homepage. No results, no acknowledgment, no redirect to a relevant category. That's not a search — that's a reload.
- **Login never confirmed (Steps 1/2/3)** — All three screenshots look identical. The banner still says "Login | details" after the login step. There's zero indication I was ever signed in. No "Welcome back, Walker." No points balance. Nothing. The personalization promise of Member Month is invisible.
- **"Excluded from promotions" on a $125 shoe (Step 6)** — I'm staring at a BOGO 50% OFF banner at the top of every page, and then the product I'm looking at tells me it's excluded. That's a gut punch. I'm not a lawyer — I don't want to parse promotion fine print on a 4-inch screen. This breeds mistrust.
- **No reviews on the product (Step 6)** — "No Reviews" on a $125 comfort shoe is a red flag for me. I check reviews. Hoka and New Balance have hundreds. This makes me hesitant.
- **Homepage hero (Steps 1/2/3)** — The hero banner skews young and female. I see a young woman in a skirt and a young guy jumping around. I'm 62. I want to see someone my age walking the dog or standing at a farmers market. Nothing here speaks to me.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page.** This is the highest-priority bug on the site. A blank cart = zero revenue from any shopper who hits it.
2. **Fix search.** "Comfort shoes" returning the homepage is not a search result. At minimum, map that query to the Arch Fit or Slip-ins category.
3. **Show login confirmation.** After login, display a name, a point balance, something. "Earn 2X Points" means nothing if I can't tell whether I'm logged in.
4. **Flag BOGO exclusions at the category level, not just the PDP.** If I browse into a shoe, spend time on it, and only learn it's excluded when I'm about to buy, that's a friction landmine.
5. **Surface user reviews or social proof on the PDP.** If a shoe has no reviews, show a "Be the first to review" prompt and link to verified reviews of similar models. Don't just show zero stars and move on.
6. **Age-inclusive hero imagery.** One image of a man 55–65 in casual walking shoes would do more for my trust than the summer fashion shoot.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

Skechers knows what I want — Slip-ins, Arch Fit, wide fit, BOGO deals. The infrastructure to reach me is there. But two core e-commerce functions (cart and search) broke during this visit, and I was never clearly logged in. At 4/10, this is a site that talks a good comfort game and then trips over its own shoelaces at the register. Until the cart works reliably, I'm buying at DSW where I know the checkout doesn't disappear on me.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

8-step mobile purchase journey on skechers.com (Salesforce Commerce Cloud / Demandware platform). Critical issues: cart page returning HTTP 429 to real users, View Cart rendered without `<title>`/`lang` attributes indicating a broken document shell, and KodeSafe bot-detection SDK rate-limiting itself against the origin. 20 AXE violations across 3 page types, concentrated in landmark structure and one critical ARIA parent/child violation.

---

## Accessibility

**Critical (2 instances — Homepage, Search)**
- `aria-required-parent`: Elements with ARIA roles requiring a parent role (e.g., `role="option"` without `role="listbox"`, or `role="cell"` without `role="row"`) exist on both Homepage and Search. Screen reader behavior is undefined for these nodes.

**Serious (2 instances — View Cart)**
- `document-title`: Cart page has an empty or missing `<title>`. Assistive technologies announce page context from the title on navigation; a missing title breaks orientation for screen reader users entering the cart.
- `html-has-lang`: Cart page `<html>` lacks a `lang` attribute. The Homepage HTML source confirms `lang="en"` exists there, so the cart is rendering from a different document shell (likely a modal/SPA route that bootstraps its own `document.write` or iframe). Language detection falls back to browser heuristics.

**Moderate (16 instances across all 3 pages)**
- `landmark-no-duplicate-main` + `landmark-main-is-top-level`: Multiple `<main>` elements detected, at least one nested inside another landmark. Likely caused by Monetate/GTM injecting content wrappers around existing `<main>`.
- `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`: `<aside>` elements nested inside `<main>` or other landmarks — likely ad/recommendation widgets.
- `landmark-unique`: Same landmark role used more than once without distinct labels.
- `region`: Content exists outside all landmark regions.
- `heading-order`: Non-sequential heading levels (e.g., `<h1>` → `<h3>`, skipping `<h2>`). Consistent across Homepage and Search, indicating a template-level issue.
- **View Cart also missing `<main>` and any `<h1>`** — the cart document shell is functionally headless.

---

## SEO

- **View Cart missing `<title>`**: If Googlebot ever crawls a cart URL (e.g., via a shared link or sitemap error), it will be indexed as an untitled page. Low risk since cart URLs are typically `noindex`, but confirms the document shell bug.
- **Heading order violations** on Homepage and Search affect crawler comprehension of content hierarchy.
- **Duplicate GTM containers**: Source shows at least 5 distinct GA4/Ads tag IDs (`G-XZTV9LQ9DQ`, `DC-9630990`, `AW-1066305778`, `G-1234567890`, `GTM-TDRBQZ`). Multiple containers firing duplicate conversion events will corrupt attribution data, not a direct SEO issue but impacts measurement fidelity.
- **Duplicate `s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js`** loaded twice in Men category source — double-fires Yahoo/Oath pixel.

## Mobile Optimization

- **Viewport height hack present**: `--vh: 6.640000000000001px` is set inline on `<html>`. This is a JavaScript-computed fix for iOS Safari's dynamic toolbar collapsing the `100vh` unit. The floating-point value (`6.640000000000001` vs a clean `6.64`) suggests it's computed at runtime without rounding — minor, but indicates the fix is applied unconditionally rather than only when needed.
- **No LCP/CLS data**: Cannot confirm image lazy-loading behavior, above-the-fold image sizing, or layout shift from late-loaded banners. These are the highest-impact mobile metrics.
- **Script payload on mobile**: 22+ third-party scripts on a mobile connection add significant parse/execute overhead even when `async`. No evidence of script bundling or consolidation through a server-side tag manager to reduce connection count.

## Console & Network Errors

**HTTP 429 — Rate limiting (6 network errors, 6 console errors)**

The most operationally significant finding. Two distinct endpoints are 429ing:

1. **`/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/.../fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.470`** (5× 429): This is the KodeSafe (PerimeterX) browser fingerprinting SDK calling its own challenge endpoint on `www.skechers.com`. The SDK is rate-limited against the origin it's supposed to protect — the bot protection system is throttling itself. This causes failed challenges and may silently degrade bot-score accuracy, potentially allowing bots through or blocking legitimate users.

2. **`/cart/` (1× 429)**: The cart page itself returned HTTP 429. Real users hitting checkout are being rate-limited and receiving an error page instead of their cart. This is a conversion-path blocker. Root cause is likely the fingerprinting SDK's repeated requests triggering the rate limiter, which then applies to subsequent same-session requests including `/cart/`.

**CORS error — Stylitics stylesheet**
```
Access to CSS at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?base=hotspots&theme=skechers&version=457243c89c'
blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```
Stylitics is a third-party outfit/hotspot widget. Their CDN is not returning CORS headers for cross-origin stylesheet requests. The widget will render unstyled or fail silently. This is a Stylitics configuration issue (they need to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` on their CDN), but Skechers should file a ticket with them.

**`net::ERR_FAILED`** (1×): Likely the downstream failure caused by the CORS-blocked Stylitics request.

## Recommendations

**P0 — Cart 429 is a conversion blocker**
The `/cart/` 429 must be root-caused immediately. Instrument whether it's the KodeSafe SDK's high-frequency fingerprint polling triggering the WAF/rate-limiter, or a separate issue. If KodeSafe is the cause: increase the rate limit exemption for the SDK's own challenge path, or configure the SDK to back off on repeated fingerprint failures.

**P0 — KodeSafe fingerprinting self-rate-limiting**
5 consecutive 429s on the `/fp` endpoint within a single session means the bot protection infrastructure is broken for this session. Audit the KodeSafe SDK retry behavior and the WAF rule that's throttling its own challenge endpoint. Coordinate with KodeSafe/PerimeterX support.

**P1 — View Cart document shell**
The cart page is missing `<title>` and `lang` — it's rendering from a broken document template. Locate the SFCC template or SPA route that renders the cart state and ensure it sets both attributes. This is a two-line fix with disproportionate impact on accessibility and correctness.

**P1 — `aria-required-parent` (critical AXE)**
Audit the Homepage and Search results components for orphaned ARIA role elements. Likely candidates: custom dropdown/combobox implementations or product grid components using `role="option"` or `role="gridcell"` without the required parent wrapper.

**P2 — Duplicate `s.yimg.com` script tag**
The Men category page loads `s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` twice. Remove the duplicate from the GTM configuration or template.

**P2 — Landmark and heading structure**
The `<main>` duplication strongly suggests a third-party tag (Monetate is the most likely candidate given it appears on all pages) is injecting a wrapping element that creates a second main landmark. Inspect the Monetate campaign configurations for DOM wrapper injection.

**P3 — Stylitics CORS**
File with Stylitics to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` for `www.skechers.com` on their CDN-served stylesheets.

**P3 — Third-party script hygiene**
Consolidate the 22+ third-party origins through a single server-side tagging container (Stape CDN is already present — use it more aggressively) to reduce DNS + TLS overhead on mobile. Prioritize removing the duplicate GTM container `G-1234567890` which appears to be a test/placeholder ID.
