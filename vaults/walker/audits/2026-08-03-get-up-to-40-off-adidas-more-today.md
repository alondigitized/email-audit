# Get up to 40% off adidas & more TODAY!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-03**
## Executive summary

- Well, DSW finally gave the birthday-email carousel a rest — this one's an actual sale pitch, which is a nice change of pace after the week I've had in my inbox. But then I actually looked at the pictures. Sandals. Wedge sandals, strappy sandals, cork platform sandals, on women half my age in flowy sleeves and crop tops. The subject line hooked me with "adidas" and I like adidas fine, but not one adidas shoe shows up anywhere below that logo strip — it's all women's sandals from Steve Madden and Lucky Brand.
- The brand logo row up top — Nike, New Balance, Crocs, adidas, Birkenstock — that's a good lineup, I'll cross-shop three of those five without blinking. But the second I hit "SANDALS UP TO 40% OFF" as a giant hero, I knew this wasn't built with a guy like me in mind. There's a "SHOP ALL WOMEN" button but no men's equivalent anywhere near it, even though there's a MEN tab in the nav.
- I wouldn't click, and I definitely wouldn't forward it — nothing here says "your feet, your size, your style." Straight to read-and-archive.

## What's working

- Recognizable logo row up top (Nike, New Balance, Crocs, adidas, Birkenstock) — brands I actually cross-shop, so my eye stopped there for a second.
- "UP TO 40% OFF" is a real, specific number, not some vague "big savings" line.
- Comparison pricing tags ("40% OFF COMP.") give me a sense they're not just marking things up to mark them down — I appreciate that kind of honesty, even if it's for shoes I'm not buying.
- The "Shop by Size" grid near the bottom is a smart, low-friction idea in theory — I just wish it applied to something I'd wear.

## What's weak

- Every single product shot is a women's sandal — wedges, cork platforms, strappy flats — on women's feet. Nothing here says "62-year-old guy needs slip-on comfort."
- No mention of cushioning, arch support, or all-day comfort anywhere — the stuff that actually gets me to click.
- "SHOP ALL WOMEN" button sits right there with no men's counterpart, so I'm clearly not who this email was built for today.
- The "MORE GREAT STYLES" strip finally shows a court sneaker that could pass for men's, but it's buried after four screens of sandals I'll never wear.
- Subject promised adidas front and center; I had to hunt for the one sneaker photo to find it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Send me a segmented version — if DSW knows I've browsed men's comfort shoes before, lead with men's New Balance or adidas walking shoes, not women's sandals. That's the single biggest miss here.
- 2. Put men's product photography in the hero, or at minimum split the hero into his-and-hers so I don't have to scroll past four sandal shots to find anything relevant.
- 3. Add a line about cushioning or arch support in the body copy — even one sentence would make this feel like it knows who I am.
- 4. Move that court sneaker product tile up near the top instead of burying it under "more great styles."
