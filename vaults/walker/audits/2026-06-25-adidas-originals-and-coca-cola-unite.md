# adidas Originals and Coca-Cola unite

## Executive summary

- Oh, come on, adidas. "It's the Real Thing." I'll give 'em the headline — that's a cute nod to Coca-Cola's old slogan and I recognized it immediately. But cute doesn't pay for itself. I'm opening this expecting shoes — maybe something on sale — and what I get is a fashion collab lookbook aimed at somebody about 40 years younger than me, built entirely around FIFA World Cup buzz that I did not ask for.
- The hero image is a young guy in a loud red-and-white patterned outfit. The body copy tells me this is about "bold graphics," "color," and "everyday silhouettes across apparel, footwear and accessories." Not a price. Not a fit callout. Not even a real button. The entire CTA is "Shop online or at your local adidas store." That's not a call to action, that's a shrug.

## What's working

- **"IT'S THE REAL THING" headline** — instantly recognizable callback, genuinely clever copywriting, one of the few things that made me pause even briefly.
- **No render bugs** — email loaded clean, the layout is simple, and nothing is broken or overlapping.
- **Current timing** — the FIFA World Cup 2026 hook is at least timely; adidas isn't sending me stale language.

## What's weak

- **Wrong audience, full stop** — the model is a young guy in streetwear. I'm 62. I wear New Balance 990s to the grocery store. Nothing in this email speaks to me.
- **No offer whatsoever** — no percentage off, no dollar amount, no free shipping. The one email from June 19th that actually got me to pay attention was "$30 off $100." This is the opposite of that.
- **Zero footwear specificity** — I came to adidas looking at their Ultraboost. There are shoes visible in a second image lower down, but they're flashy red fashion sneakers, not comfort walkers. Not my foot.
- **CTA is essentially non-existent** — "Shop online or at your local adidas store" buried in the body copy is not a button, not an action, not a reason to click.
- **Another FIFA email** — this makes at least the third or fourth World Cup-adjacent send in the past two weeks. I'm not a soccer fan. I've never given them a signal that I'm a soccer fan. This is spray-and-pray.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a real offer** — a dollar-off or percentage-off hook in the hero would have salvaged this entirely. Even "members get early access + 20% off" would have changed the math.
- 2. **Show a comfort shoe from the collab** — if there's a walking shoe or casual sneaker in this collection, lead with it. Don't bury it.
- 3. **Swap the hero model** — or at minimum show a broader age range. A 60-something guy wearing these casually would have made me look twice.
- 4. **Real CTA button** — "Shop the Collection" as a tap target, not a sentence in the body.
- 5. **Segment me out of pure fashion/collab sends** — I opened their app looking at Ultraboost. They have that signal. Use it.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — adidas Originals x Coca-Cola Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has two confirmed failures: a malformed internal redirect URL and a missing plain-text alternative. Several HTTP image sources also risk blocking in security-conscious clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken click-redirect URL**
One link contains an unresolvable relative path in the redirect chain:

```
unknown url type: '/g/cad39371-378a-40e6-ba18-6e779ef10f8c?txn=78291a80-706a-11f1-ad5e-aea38baf8e0c'
```

The outer URL (`https://dv.adidas.com/o/cad39371-...`) resolves to a relative `/g/` path that has no registered handler. This link is broken — clicks will result in an error page.

**[WARN] 29 tracking links skipped**
All click-redirect links route through `dv.adidas.com` and `click.comms.adidas.com`. The QA probe could not validate destinations. The broken link above is the only confirmed failure, but destination fidelity for the remaining 28 is unverified.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Six instances of HTTP image source (same asset, repeated)**
```
src: http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
Non-HTTPS sources are blocked by Gmail (and others) when images are proxied through Google's secure image cache. This arrow asset will fail to render in Gmail. Switch to `https://`.

**[WARN] HTTP pixel/beacon image**
```
src: http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019ee9d95a64-.../SV_Y9aZPtIc5eG1OKaMA_Piy7...
```
Also HTTP, also missing alt text. Likely an open-pixel tracker — same blocking risk applies.

**[WARN] Two images missing `alt` text**
- `cad39371-378a-40e6-ba18-6e779ef10f8c` (content image)
- `SV_Y9aZPtIc5eG1OKaMA_Piy7...` (tracking pixel)

Both lack `alt=""` attributes. Screen readers will announce the raw filename. The tracking pixel should have `alt=""` (empty) explicitly set; the content image should have descriptive alt text.

**Minor — `@font-face` declared twice**
`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` `@font-face` blocks appear in two separate `<style>` tags with identical `src` fallbacks (local Arial/Helvetica). Both degrade gracefully but the duplication adds unnecessary parse overhead.

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text alternative missing**
```

```
CAN-SPAM and most ESP best practices require a plain-text `text/plain` MIME part. A zero-length alternative means recipients on text-only clients receive nothing, and some spam filters penalize HTML-only messages.

No issues found with unsubscribe presence (not visible in truncated source, assumed present based on footer CSS classes `.mobile-footer-padding-block` being defined). Verify the physical mailing address and one-click unsubscribe are present in the live footer.
