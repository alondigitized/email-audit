# From '94 to '26, making soccer history
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- Another adidas email — I've genuinely lost count at this point, and I'm pretty sure this is well past a dozen from them this spring alone. The subject line pulled a faint flicker of nostalgia out of me: I was thirty years old in the summer of '94, and I remember there being a buzz about the World Cup coming to the States. That small recognition is about the most this email did for me.
- Here's the honest truth: I open adidas emails looking for something that'll help my feet. Cushioning, support, a deal on something comfortable and easy to slip on. What I got was a sweepstakes for World Cup tickets and a line of US94-branded soccer gear. No shoe. No price. No reason for me specifically to care. Just a brand-history lesson and a LEARN MORE button.
- I would not click. I would not forward this to a friend. I'd mark it read, think "adidas, again," and move on.

## What's working

- The "FROM '94 TO '26" hero graphic is bold and visually striking — retro-styled, immediately eye-catching, it earns a second look even from someone who wasn't a soccer fan.
- The World Cup sweepstakes banner at the top is at least clear about what's being offered, and the July 10, 2026 date gives it a concrete, real-world anchor.
- The email renders cleanly — no broken images, no layout breakage, nothing falling apart on my screen.
- adiClub is specifically called out in the sweepstakes copy, which at least signals they know I'm a member.

## What's weak

- Zero footwear relevance — no shoe is shown, no comfort story is told, nothing for a 62-year-old who came here looking for walking shoes.
- "LEARN MORE" is the vaguest CTA adidas could have picked. Learn more about what? A button should tell me what I'm walking into.
- "Rosters come and go, but the attitude lasts forever" reads like brand-speak. Inspiring to nobody, actionable to nobody.
- After the run of adidas emails I've gotten this spring — golf shorts, Amazon gift cards, an app-only 25% deal, a Chinese New Year jacket in late May — this one doesn't earn its spot in my inbox with anything new.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show a shoe, any shoe.** If the US94 collection includes footwear, lead with it. One hero shot with a price turns this from a brand ad into a shopping email. That's the whole job.
- 2. **Combine the sweepstakes with the shop.** "Enter to win tickets + shop the collection" in one CTA beats a standalone contest banner. Give me both reasons to click in one tap.
- 3. **Replace "LEARN MORE" with something specific** — "Enter Now," "Shop US94," "Claim Your Entry" — anything that tells me what happens when I press it.
- 4. **Anchor the email with an offer.** Even 20% off, even free shipping on the US94 line. Nostalgia alone doesn't move my wallet.
- 5. **Slow down the send cadence.** I've had more adidas emails this spring than I've had hot breakfasts. Each additional one erodes what little urgency the previous one created.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Enter now: 2 tickets to the '26 World Cup in LA`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop the official US94 collection + enter before July 10`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `adiClub members: enter to win + explore the '94 legacy gear`

## Technical Audit — adidas "From '94 to '26, making soccer history"

**Sender:** adidas@us-news.comms.adidas.com | **Reviewed:** 2026-05-30

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard failures: a broken click-tracking link with an unrecognized URL scheme, and a missing plain-text fallback. Several images use HTTP sources and lack alt attributes, creating mixed-content and accessibility exposure.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken tracking redirect — unknown URL type `/g/`**

The click-tracking wrapper resolves to a `/g/` path that the redirect handler does not recognize:

```
unknown url type: '/g/85f80ae0-5ca4-454d-bb93-d9eeae4da410?txn=4bfab500-5bf6-11f1-8f03-26d090a92706'
```

The outer redirect URL is:
```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/85f80ae0-5ca4-454d-bb93-d9eeae4da410?cp_tp=v3.eJzj...&mi_mid=019e799d-1c00-7000-a66c-ceae54fd9c81
```

The `/o/` → `/g/` transition inside the redirect chain is failing. Affected CTA(s) linked to tracking ID `85f80ae0-5ca4-454d-bb93-d9eeae4da410` are dead clicks.

**[WARN] 29 tracking links not probed**

All 29 remaining click-redirect URLs through `dv.adidas.com` and `click.comms.adidas.com` were skipped — no confirmation they resolve correctly beyond the one confirmed failure above.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Mixed-content: HTTP image sources (6 instances)**

The same arrow image is embedded six times via HTTP, not HTTPS:
```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all block or warn on HTTP image loads when the email is opened over HTTPS. The arrow asset will silently fail to render in most modern clients.

**[WARN] HTTP pixel tracker**

The open-tracking pixel is also served over HTTP:
```
http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019e77b38fed-70887710-4447-4618-9e6b-9f2b9699ec98-000000/P6H-9FgySrLTdsLFPMPWZ...
```
This means open-rate data will be undercounted wherever the client enforces HTTPS-only image loading.

**[WARN] Missing alt text — 3 confirmed images**

| Asset | URL fragment |
|---|---|
| `ec93a4e0-ec25-4fb9-9aaf-abcc6b5afec5.png` | `image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe5c15707c63...` |
| `85f80ae0-5ca4-454d-bb93-d9eeae4da410` (tracking pixel) | `dv.adidas.com/o/85f80ae0...` |
| Open-pixel (`P6H-9FgySrLTdsLFPMPWZliPr...`) | `click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...` |

Inline `alt=""` is present on decorative images but missing entirely on the above, which will surface as accessibility failures in email linting tools and reduce screen-reader compatibility.

**[WARN] Duplicate `@font-face` declarations**

The `AdihausDIN` (400, 700) and `AdineuePRO` (400, 600) rules are declared identically in two separate `<style>` blocks. Both resolve to local system fonts (`Arial`, `Helvetica`) — no external font load — so this is dead CSS weight, not a render failure. Still increases HTML payload unnecessarily.

**[WARN] `<meta charset>` missing `http-equiv`**

```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, some legacy clients (older Outlook, Lotus Notes) may fall back to default encoding and corrupt special characters.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**[FAIL] Plain-text fallback absent**

QA reports `Text version is 0 chars`. CAN-SPAM does not strictly require a text part, but its absence:
- Raises spam scores in SpamAssassin and similar filters (missing `text/plain` MIME part is a signal)
- Breaks display in text-only mail agents and some corporate security gateways that strip HTML

A minimal plain-text alternative with the unsubscribe URL and physical address should be present.

Authentication headers (`DKIM`, `SPF`, `DMARC`) are not visible in the provided source; they are envelope-level and would require raw message headers to audit. Sending domain `us-news.comms.adidas.com` is a subdomain of a known ESP — assumed properly configured but not confirmed here.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM Params, Landing Page Alignment)

The click-tracking URLs use adidas's own redirect service (`dv.adidas.com/o/`) with encoded payloads (`cp_tp`, `cp_cid`, `mi_cid`, `mi_mid`). UTM attribution is embedded in the redirect chain rather than appended as visible query params. This is a common ESP pattern — no naked untracked links detected.

However, because the `/g/` redirect failure (section 2) breaks at least one CTA, any UTM data attached to that link is also lost for affected clicks.
