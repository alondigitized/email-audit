# Your cart expires soon!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-29**
## Executive summary

- Same pink floral D'Lites, same "you liked these" line, same "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" button — I'm not going to keep pretending this one's different from the last five that hit my inbox this week. I never put that shoe in a cart, and no amount of confident copy about how I "weren't wrong" is going to convince me otherwise. It's like a salesman who keeps chasing you across the parking lot with the same jacket you already said no to.
- The one thing that saved this send from being a total waste of a tap: scroll down and there's actually a men's shoe in the "best sellers" grid, and a real 20% off code — UPGRADE — on cozy fit and glide step styles, which is exactly my lane. But it's buried under a wrong-gender hero image and a fake urgency headline, so I'd have to scroll past something irrelevant to get to something useful.
- Verdict: I'm marking this read and moving on. Not clicking, not forwarding — though if I did forward it to anybody it'd be my daughter with a note like "these people really think I ordered pink flowered sneakers."

## What's working

- The 20% off "Cozy Fit & Glide Step" banner near the bottom is a real, specific offer with a code I could actually use.
- There's a men's slip-in ("Arch Fit Arcade") and a men's relaxed fit ("Altman - Murdock") in the best-sellers strip — that's product I'd actually consider.
- Shop Now Pay Later and Curbside Pickup icons are clean and easy to spot, decent trust cues for someone comparing price and convenience.

## What's weak

- The hero is, again, a shoe I never added to a cart — floral D'Lites, wrong gender, wrong style, wrong everything for me.
- "You liked these and you weren't wrong" is presumptuous when I never looked at it in the first place.
- "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" button pointing at a shoe that isn't mine is the kind of thing that makes me trust the brand less, not more.
- The real offer (20% off) is buried two-thirds down the page under a wall of unrelated product tiles, including kids' sneakers and basketball shoes I have zero interest in.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the 20% off Cozy Fit & Glide Step offer at the very top — that's the actual reason I'd open this email, not a shoe I never touched.
- 2. Kill the "abandoned cart" framing entirely unless there's genuinely something in my cart — I've now seen this same floral shoe seven times and it's actively burning trust.
- 3. If this must be a cart-recovery email, at least show a product I've browsed before, not a static default image.
- 4. Move the men's best-sellers up front, since that's the only part of the body that's actually for me.
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% off Cozy Fit — comfort you can wear all day`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Arch Fit favorites, 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cozy Fit & Glide Step, 20% off with code UPGRADE`
