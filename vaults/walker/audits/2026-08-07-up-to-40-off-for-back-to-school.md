**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- Well, at least there's a real number in the subject line this time — "up to 40% off" beats another jersey drop. But this is back to school again, third time in two weeks the way I count it, and it's still got nothing to do with what I'm actually shopping for. There's not a comfort shoe, a walking sneaker, or an arch-support anything in this whole email — it's a locker photo, a backpack, and three teenagers doing bike tricks in a parking lot.
- The 40% off is real and clearly stated, I'll give them that. But the "Apple TV free month with adiClub" bit near the bottom tells me exactly who this is for — someone with school-age kids and a streaming subscription, not a 62-year-old buying his fourth pair of the same walking shoe. I'm not clicking, I'm not forwarding it, and I'm filing it in the same drawer as the jerseys.

## What's working

- The 40% off number is stated plainly, twice, no hunting for it.
- No render bugs — images loaded, text is clean, nothing overlapping.
- "SHOP NOW" button up top is easy to spot and tap.
- The offer terms are spelled out down at the bottom if you actually want to read them, dates included.

## What's weak

- Not one shoe in this email is a comfort or walking shoe — it's a Samba-style sneaker on a teenager, not the cushioned slip-on I'm after.
- The whole theme is back-to-school, which has nothing to do with me — no kids in that house anymore.
- Models are all clearly college-age or younger, nothing that looks like it's meant for someone my age.
- Apple TV freebie for adiClub members over $130 — a nice hook if you're twenty-five, but I don't see the connection to shoes at all.
- Big blank gaps between sections make me think something didn't load, even though it's probably just spacing.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this list — send the back-to-school stuff to people with kids in the household and send me something with actual comfort or walking shoes in it. That's the single biggest miss here.
- 2. Swap the hero model shot for something that isn't three teens on bikes — if I'm getting this email at all, show me a shoe I'd actually wear.
- 3. Drop the Apple TV incentive from a "shoes" email or explain it better — right now it reads like a random unrelated perk bolted on.
- 4. Tighten up the layout — those big empty gaps make it feel like the email broke.

**1. Technical Summary**
Standard multi-provider ESP send (Salesforce Marketing Cloud–style tracking via `click.comms.adidas.com` + `image.link.adidas.com`) with legacy HTML4.01 Transitional markup. Automated QA shows an 88% pass rate driven by a missing plain-text part and several HTTP (non-HTTPS) image references.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- 26 links route through `click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...` redirect infrastructure; the QA tool skipped HTTP probing on these by design (standard for click-tracking redirects, not a defect).
- One of these tracking redirects (`http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019fddd584cd-19ad156a-5537-47ac-b31c-cad570144322-000000/OuPQAFFRUoZzmYwKbKx6YKwl3...`) is being used as an **image `src`**, not an anchor — consistent with an open/beacon tracking pixel. It is served over plain HTTP.

- **[FAIL-adjacent, WARN]** HTTP (non-HTTPS) image source: `http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg` (alt="arrow") — flagged **7 times**, indicating this same asset is referenced 7 separate times in the template. Many mail clients (Outlook/Gmail in strict-HTTPS contexts) will block or warn on mixed-content HTTP images.
- Same HTTP issue on the tracking-pixel image above (`OuPQAFFRUoZzmYwKbKx6YKwl3...`).
- **Missing alt text** on two images: the beacon/tracking image noted above, and `https://dv.adidas.com/o/8defc74f-a762-4e26-96fd-a2277937af59?cp_tp=...`. Screen readers/blocked-image fallback will show nothing for either.
