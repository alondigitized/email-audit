# Members Only: Buy One, Get One 50% Off
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- I recognized the sender right away — Skechers Plus in my inbox, another day, same broken JSON garbage in the preview before I even tap. I've been watching that happen since the first week of June, and here we are again on the 25th. At this point it's practically their brand identity.
- Once I did open it, the BOGO deal is front and center, and "Members Only" is a phrase that makes me feel like I'm in the club rather than just another address on a blast list. That counts for something. But the hero image is a young woman in a sundress by a pool — not a 62-year-old guy who needs something wide enough for his foot and cushioned enough to walk the mall without wincing. I scrolled down to the Men's section eventually, and at least the shoes shown there looked like actual walking shoes, not fashion sneakers. Still, the Women's section comes first, which tells me where Skechers thinks the money is.
- Would I click? Probably yes, on the BOGO alone — that's a real deal on a brand I already own. Would I forward to a friend? No. The email doesn't tell me anything about what makes the shoe comfortable, and that's what my buddy Dave asks about before he buys anything.

## What's working

- **BOGO offer is impossible to miss** — big, high-contrast text right at the top, "50% Off" and "20% Off 1 Pair" are both spelled out clearly.
- **"Members Only" framing lands** — I feel like I earned this offer, which is different from the random blast emails I usually ignore.
- **Men's section exists and shows real shoes** — athletic/walking style shoes, not fashion sneakers, which is closer to what I'm looking for.
- **Clean layout, no broken images** — body renders fine; at least the email itself didn't break, even if the preview text was already a disaster before I opened it.
- **Seasonal timing feels right** — summer shoes, poolside look, makes sense for late June.

## What's weak

- **Broken JSON preview text — again** — this is the seventh or eighth time in June I'm seeing this. It was already embarrassing on June 6th, inexcusable by June 11th, and now it's just depressing. Someone at Skechers needs to actually fix this.
- **Hero model is a young woman** — I'm a 62-year-old man. That poolside image isn't for me and I feel it immediately.
- **No comfort language anywhere** — zero mention of cushioning, arch support, or anything that tells me whether these shoes will actually feel good on my feet. The deal is there; the reason-to-buy is not.
- **"SHOP TODAY" CTA is vague** — I want to shop men's comfort styles, not the whole catalog. Give me a button that takes me somewhere specific.
- **No urgency or deadline** — BOGO is compelling, but if it doesn't expire until July, I'll get to it later and probably forget.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text bug — this is priority one, full stop.** It has been broken in every Skechers email since at least June 6th. Every send with broken JSON is burning whatever goodwill the BOGO offer builds.
- 2. **Lead with a 55+ male model or at least a mixed hero** — a guy who looks like he's walking a park trail or doing light errands would make me feel seen rather than tolerated.
- 3. **Add one comfort signal to the hero copy** — something like "Extra Wide Available" or "Memory Foam Cushioning Included" next to the offer. The deal gets me in; comfort closes it.
- 4. **Replace "SHOP TODAY" with "Shop Men's BOGO"** — specific CTA, specific destination.
- 5. **Add a deadline** — "Offer ends Sunday" or "72 hours only" gives me a reason to act now instead of saving it for later.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has no broken primary links, but carries a protocol-missing tracking pixel that silently breaks inbox placement monitoring, a full sweep of HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources that will be blocked or downgraded by modern clients, and a stale campaign identifier mismatch across pixel tags. ### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Critical — Return Path pixel has no protocol:**
```html
<img src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1dbf0...">
```
Missing `https://` means email clients treat this as a relative URL. The pixel fires against the current document's base, not the intended domain. Inbox placement/deliverability monitoring (Validity/Return Path) receives no signal from this send.

**Stale campaign ID across pixels:**
Krux impression pixel encodes `campaignid=PRO_US_NA_U_SITE_FULL_EN_06142026` (June 14). Today is June 24. If this email was resent or recycled, audience attribution in the DMP will backdate 10 days. Confirm this matches the intended flight date.

**28 click-redirect links skipped:** All route through `click.emails.skechers.com` — standard SFMC pattern. UTM pass-through could not be verified without following redirects; see §6.

**Krux audience-sync pixels embed subscriber identifiers:**
```
partner_uid=9153f33fe5...  (SHA-256 hashed email)
partner_uid=121173391       (clear-text subscriber ID)
```
Both appear together in the same pixel call. Confirm this dual-ID pattern is intentional per your data-sharing agreement with Salesforce/Krux.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources — will be blocked:**
All images under `image.emails.skechers.com` use `http://`, not `https://`. Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook (2016+) proxy or block non-HTTPS image sources. Affects at minimum:
- Logo: `http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/1/dde00662-169f-447d-b0e2-fc65f6c2290c.png`
- App Store badge, Google Play badge, social icons (Instagram, Facebook, YouTube), Curbside Pickup, AfterPay, Find A Store icons — all HTTP.
- Movable Ink pixel: `http://www.ink1000.com/...o.gif`

**Missing alt text on two images:**
- `o.gif` (Movable Ink pixel) — alt="" would be acceptable here, but the attribute is absent entirely.
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` — likely a product/hero image with no alt. Screen readers skip it; images-off clients show a broken placeholder.

**`<meta charset>` missing `http-equiv`:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, older Outlook/Lotus clients may not enforce UTF-8, risking character encoding fallback.

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Disables user zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). No functional reason to cap it at 1.

**Duplicate responsive CSS blocks:**
Two `<style>` blocks in `<head>` contain overlapping breakpoints (375px, 640px in block 1; 480px in block 2). The `mobile-content`/`desktop-content` toggle defaults `desktop-content` to `display:none` at the document level — Outlook and other non-media-query clients will render only the mobile layout regardless of viewport.
