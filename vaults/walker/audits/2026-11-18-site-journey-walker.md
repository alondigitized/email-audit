# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-11-18**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

The browsing half of skechers.com on my phone is good. The menu, the category page and search all work. The buying half failed. On the shoe page I tapped Add to Cart without picking a size and got "Please select a size". Then the cart page came up as a blank white screen. I never saw a shoe in a cart or a total, and I couldn't have bought anything.

The site is also easy to leave unconvinced. The top banner says "Members Take 30% OFF Sitewide," but the shoe I picked says "Excluded from promotions." I never saw a login form or anything with my name on it, so nothing felt personal.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** That matches yesterday's 3/10, so I see no overall change.

- Browsing and menus are solid, at about 7/10.
- The path to buying is broken or unclear, at about 1/10.
- I can't name specific regressions or improvements against yesterday, because I only have today's screenshots.
- The cart is the same score-capping failure either way.

## 3. What's Working

- **Menu on the phone.** Tapping MENU, then Men, gives big, well-spaced rows: Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections. Each row has an arrow, a Back button and an X to close. That's easy for a 62-year-old.
- **The right shortcuts for me.** The strip under the header lists "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Arch Fit" and "Max Cushioning." That is what I shop for. On the Men's Shoes page, "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" are the first two tiles.
- **Clear filters.** Filter, Sort by, Size and Width are visible buttons, and "820 results" is stated.
- **Search worked.** "comfort shoes" returned 1,656 results with Filter and Sort. Prices and the member-discount line are readable without zooming.
- **Shoe page basics.** The price is large, and the star rating and "3 Reviews" are easy to see. Klarna and Afterpay payment options are shown. The size buttons are big enough to tap, and sold-out sizes are crossed out with a bell icon.
- **Add to Cart button.** It is full-width, dark blue and easy to hit.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Cart was blank.** Step 8 was an empty white page with no header, no message and no way back. That is the worst thing I saw. The cart badge also stayed at 0 the whole trip.
- **Size error.** "Please select a size" appears, but the size grid sits well below the photo. On a phone I had to scroll to find it, and the page didn't take me there after the failed tap.
- **Mixed messages on price.** The banner says 30% off sitewide, applied at cart. The shoe page says "Excluded from promotions." A shopper who counts dollars, as I do, sees that as bait-and-switch. The shoe cost $100, and I saw no sign of a discount.
- **No login or personalization.** Step 3 showed no sign-in screen. The page just gained a "Doorbusters" strip. I never saw a name, saved size, past orders or "welcome back." The banner talks about "Members," but nothing showed me I was one.
- **Early screens did little.** Steps 1 and 2 look the same, with a gray empty bar under the header on the first one. The hero photo is a holiday couple in formal clothes. Nothing in the first screen mentions comfort, arch support or slip-ins beyond the cut-off strip.
- **Search results aren't relevant enough.** The first result is a Women's black suede loafer. A man typing "comfort shoes" gets a women's shoe first.
- **Shoe choice.** The first shoes in Men's Shoes were loud, graffiti-style sneakers, not the casual walking shoes I'd want. The one I opened was a Day of the Dead design.
- **No comparison or value help.** I saw nothing like "vs. Hoka or Brooks" or cost-per-wear, and no cushioning or arch-support ratings on the cards.
- **Small text.** The "Excluded from promotions" note, the product subtitles ("Women's," "Men's") and the "Also in Wide" tag are small and gray. I'd have trouble reading them without zooming.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page now.** Show the items, a total and a Checkout button. If it's empty, say so and offer a link back to shopping. Never show a blank screen.
2. **Make the size step obvious.** When someone taps Add to Cart without a size, scroll to the sizes and highlight them. Ideally show sizes above the fold, or preselect the shopper's saved size.
3. **Make promo messaging match.** Don't say "sitewide" if shoes are excluded. Show the exclusion in the banner, or label the shoe where the shopper sees the price.
4. **Show that the shopper is logged in.** Put a name or "Hi, Walker" in the header, show saved sizes and widths, and show "Your member price."
5. **Lead with comfort on the homepage.** Put the Slip-ins, Arch Fit and Max Cushioning shortcuts on the first screen as big tiles, ahead of the lifestyle photo.
6. **Tune search to the shopper.** If I browsed Men's, rank Men's results first for "comfort shoes."
7. **Add comfort and value cues to the cards.** Show cushioning level, arch support and a plain price-versus-quality line.
8. **Make small text bigger.** Raise the size on the gray notes and tags.

## 6. Bottom Line

I like how Skechers organizes comfort: the Slip-ins and Arch Fit links and the clear menu are what I look for. But I couldn't finish a purchase. I didn't get a cart, I wasn't recognized as a member, and the discount message and the shoe page contradicted each other. A competitor that sells me a cushioned shoe without confusion wins me, whatever the logo. Today's 3/10 is the same as yesterday's.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

- Axe found 17 violations across 3 pages: 2 critical, 3 serious, 10 moderate, 2 minor. The Homepage has 9 of them, including both critical ones.
- `GET /cart/` returned **429 Too Many Requests**. The View Cart axe results (no `<title>`, no `lang`, no `<main>`, no `<h1>`) match an error or challenge page, not the real cart. Treat the cart a11y results as unverified.
- LCP and CLS were not captured for any step (`?`), so Core Web Vitals can't be assessed. Only TTFB is available.
- Some captured data looks unreliable. The Log In and Men category steps both show the homepage URL and the same TTFB (458ms). Product detail and Add to Cart both show 26ms. This suggests either a shared navigation entry or same-document transitions, not fresh page loads.
- The page loads a large number of third-party scripts (listed under Performance).

## Accessibility

**Homepage**
- **critical** `aria-required-parent`: an element with an ARIA role that needs a specific parent (for example `listitem` or `option`) is not inside it. This can break the accessibility tree.
- **critical** `button-name`: one or more buttons have no accessible name, such as icon-only controls with no `aria-label`.
- **serious** `link-name`: one or more links have no discernible text.
- **moderate** `landmark-no-duplicate-main` and `landmark-main-is-top-level`: there are multiple `<main>` landmarks, and at least one is nested.
- **moderate** `landmark-unique`: two or more landmarks share the same role and label.
- **moderate** `page-has-heading-one`: there is no `<h1>`.
- **moderate** `region`: content sits outside any landmark.
- **minor** `aria-allowed-role`: a role is set that isn't valid for its element.

**View Cart** (likely the 429 response, see Console & Network Errors)
- **serious** `document-title` and `html-has-lang`: the document has no title and no `lang`. The homepage has `lang="en"`, so this is not the normal template.
- **moderate** `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one`: there is no `<main>` and no `<h1>`.

**Search "comfort shoes"**
- **moderate** `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`: an `<aside>` or complementary landmark is nested inside another landmark.
- **moderate** `landmark-unique` and `region`: landmarks are not unique, and some content sits outside landmarks.
- **minor** `aria-allowed-role`: same class of issue as on the Homepage.

The `landmark-unique`, `region`, and `aria-allowed-role` violations repeat on Homepage and Search. That points to shared header, footer, or filter templates and one fix should clear both pages.

## SEO

- The Homepage has `<html lang="en">` and a title: "SKECHERS | The Comfort Technology Company".
- The Homepage has no `<h1>`.
- The Homepage has duplicate `<main>` landmarks.
- A 429 on `/cart/` returns a document with no `<title>` or `lang`. Cart pages are usually `noindex`, so the direct impact is low. Sustained 429s on crawlable URLs would cause crawlers to throttle.
- The truncated HTML didn't include canonical, meta description, Open Graph, or JSON-LD. These were not assessed.

## Performance

- **TTFB:** the Homepage is 530ms and Log In and Men are 458ms. All are within the 800ms "good" threshold. The other steps show 24–108ms, which is likely cache hits or same-document navigation and is not comparable to a cold load.
- **LCP and CLS:** not measured. Check whether the Web Vitals observer is injected before navigation and whether it survives page transitions.
- **Third-party load:** the source shows many async third-party tags:
  - Tag management and testing: GTM, Monetate (A/B), Cloudflare JSD
  - Analytics and session replay: Quantum Metric, Pulse Insights, Bloomreach (`brsrvr`)
  - Advertising pixels: Taboola, Snapchat, Reddit, Facebook, Pinterest, TikTok, Bing, Yahoo, Nextdoor, Criteo (`creativecdn`), Impact, Tune (`go2sdk`)
  - Other: Stape
- Monetate (client-side testing) can delay or shift rendering if it isn't anti-flicker gated.
- Fonts load through WebFont Loader from cdnjs (`wf-*-active` classes). This adds a JS-dependent font path and a third-party dependency on the critical rendering path.
- The page uses `dns-prefetch` for Monetate hosts. `preconnect` would save more time if those hosts are needed early.

## Mobile Optimization

- `--vh: 6.64px` is set inline by JS, which implies a viewport height of 664px. This is a JS workaround for mobile viewport height. CSS `dvh` or `svh` units would remove the JS dependency.
- No viewport meta, touch-target, or responsive-image data was in the truncated source, so those were not assessed.

## Console & Network Errors

- **429 on `https://www.skechers.com/cart/`.** This is the only network error. The Cloudflare challenge-platform script (`/cdn-cgi/challenge-platform/.../jsd`) is on the page, so rate limiting or bot mitigation likely triggered. Automated traffic may be treated differently from real users, so the cart's real behavior is unverified.
- **Console errors:** 19 are reported but only 10 are listed (7 and 3 below). Treat the rest as unseen.
  - 7 × `Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document`. A script is registering an `unload` handler, probably a third-party tag. Chrome is deprecating `unload`, and it also blocks back/forward cache.
  - 3 × `Unsupported Summarizer API languages were specified...`. This is the Chrome built-in AI Summarizer API called with unsupported language options. The source is not identified. It could be a page script, an injected tag, or the test browser.

## Recommendations

1. **Re-test the cart.** Rerun View Cart with a throttled cadence or allowlisted test traffic. Check the 429's headers (`Retry-After`, `cf-*`) and confirm real users aren't affected. Rerun the cart's axe scan afterward.
2. **Fix the critical Homepage a11y issues.** Add accessible names to icon-only buttons and links, and correct the `aria-required-parent` structure.
3. **Fix landmarks.** Use a single top-level `<main>`, label repeated landmarks (`aria-label`) so they're unique, and move stray content into landmarks.
4. **Add an `<h1>`** to the Homepage and cart templates.
5. **Fix the measurement harness.** Capture LCP and CLS reliably, and make sure each step produces a real navigation with its own URL and TTFB. Fresh Log In and Men category data is needed before drawing conclusions.
6. **Audit third-party tags.** Find which tag registers `unload` and which calls the Summarizer API. Move `unload` handlers to `pagehide`, and defer non-essential ad pixels until after load or consent.
7. **Optimize font and test loading.** Replace WebFont Loader with self-hosted `@font-face` and `font-display: swap` plus preloads. Confirm that Monetate has anti-flicker handling.
