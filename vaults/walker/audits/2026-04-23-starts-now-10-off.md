# STARTS NOW: $10 OFF!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-23**
## Executive summary

- DSW sent me a tiered "buy more, save more" discount email with a side of Earth Week shoe donation messaging. The discount is clear enough — $10 off $49 is the entry point — but most of this email wasn't built for me. The big visual section is all women's fashion heels and strappy sandals, which I'm going to scroll right past. There's a "SHOP MEN" link buried in the hero CTA row and a Shop by Size section toward the bottom, but neither feels like DSW actually knew they were talking to a 62-year-old guy who wants comfortable, practical shoes. The offer is fine. The targeting is lazy.
- The discount mechanics are real and readable. But DSW left a lot on the table by leading with women's new arrivals and not surfacing men's comfort or wide-width options until I've already half-tuned out.

## What's working

- **The tiered offer is crystal clear.** "$10 OFF $49 / $20 OFF $99 / $60 OFF $199" stacks vertically in big bold text — I can read that without my glasses. No guesswork.
- **Code NEWLOOKS is visible** right under the headline. I won't have to hunt for it.
- **Shop Wide Width button** near the bottom is the most relevant thing in this whole email for me. I have wider feet and I'm always looking for that filter. Good that it exists.
- **The Soles4Souls Earth Week tie-in** is a decent feel-good angle. Donating old shoes for $5 off — I'd consider that if I'm heading in-store anyway.
- **Mobile layout stacks cleanly.** No horizontal scrolling, text is legible, nothing looks broken.

## What's weak

- **The hero product imagery is women's shoes.** Heels, slingbacks, strappy sandals — nothing aimed at me. I know DSW sells men's shoes but you wouldn't know it from this email until you're three scrolls down.
- **"SHOP MEN" is tiny text in a row of five CTA buttons.** After the discount block it reads: SHOP WOMEN · SHOP MEN · SHOP KIDS · SHOP KIDS · SHOP EVERYTHING. Men's is one of five equally-weighted options. I'm not feeling special here.
- **The "So New, So You" section** is entirely fashion-forward women's new arrivals. Irrelevant to me. Just visual noise to scroll through.
- **The Soles4Souls $5 off is in-store only** (same-day in-store purchase). I'm a phone browser — that's a friction wall. Fine print buries this.
- **DSW Shoe Obsessed mention at the bottom** is vague. I'm a Skechers Plus member, not a DSW loyalty member that I know of. Was this offer personalized to me as a member? Can't tell. The loyalty section doesn't tell me my status or points balance — it just promotes the program generically, like they're not sure if I'm in it or not.
- **Size range shown (8–10.5)** skews smaller. A wider range or a "Shop Men's Size" shortcut would serve me better.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with men's content for male subscribers.** If they know I'm male — and they should from my purchase history — the hero imagery and first product module should reflect that. One clean men's lifestyle shot outperforms five women's heel photos for me.
- 2. **Surface comfort or wide-width options higher.** The Wide Width button is buried below the fold. Put it in the first scroll for male subscribers who browse comfort categories.
- 3. **Make the loyalty angle personal.** Tell me my current points, my tier, how close I am to the next reward. "You're 120 points from your next $5 reward" would hook me far better than a generic "join/learn more" pitch.
- 4. **Clarify the in-store vs. online split upfront.** The Soles4Souls $5 off being in-store only is a buried asterisk. If it's in-store, say "head in-store this week" in the headline so I don't click through expecting an online code.
- 5. **Consolidate CTAs.** Five shop links under the hero is too many choices at once. For a phone browser my age, fewer, bigger buttons work better.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`Src:` capitalized in all `@font-face` declarations (10 occurrences)**
CSS property names are case-sensitive in the spec. `Src:` should be `src:`. Most email clients are lenient, but strict parsers (some versions of Outlook Web Access, Samsung Mail) may silently skip the font load.

```css
/* Current (invalid) */
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...original.woff") format('woff');

/* Correct */
src: url("https://braze-images.com/...original.woff") format('woff');
```

**`<meta>` tags missing `http-equiv` attribute**

```html
<!-- Broken — no http-equiv, content alone is ignored -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta content="IE=Edge" />

<!-- Correct -->
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=Edge" />
```

Without `http-equiv="Content-Type"`, the charset declaration is inert. Outlook desktop renders from the DOCTYPE charset fallback, but webmail clients (Yahoo, AOL) may misinterpret encoding on non-ASCII characters.

**HelveticaNow fonts served as TTF only, with cache-busting query strings**

```
original.ttf?1755182939
```

TTF is the heaviest font format (~40–60% larger than WOFF2). Query strings on font URLs break HTTP caching in several email clients. Braze-hosted fonts should be served in WOFF format at minimum; WOFF2 is preferred.

**Futura PT fonts served as WOFF only — no format fallback chain**

Single-format `@font-face` rules with no `format()` fallback mean clients that don't support WOFF silently drop to the stack's system font. This is acceptable if the fallback stack is correct, but should be confirmed.
