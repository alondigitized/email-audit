# We Got Crafty!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- Another Crocs email. I've lost count at this point — looking back at my inbox, I've had these things stacking up since May 22nd, and most of them have been a mixed bag at best. So when I see "We Got Crafty!" in the subject line with absolutely nothing in the preview text — just blank space where copy should be — my thumb is already hovering over delete before the email even loads.
- Once I'm in, I'll give Crocs this: the product itself is actually interesting. There's a canvas version of the Classic Clog with some material callouts — washable canvas, some room for those Jibbitz charm things — and the colorway is a nice earthy neutral. That's more my speed than the red clogs or the studded platforms I've been seeing all week. The neutral color story makes sense for a guy my age who doesn't want to look like he's wearing a highlighter on his feet.
- But then I scroll down and the "Recommended for You" section has a Kids' Classic Clog in it. Kids'. I'm 62. I'm not buying my grandkids' shoes here. And then there's a Miami Toe Loop Sandal that looks like something from a beach resort boutique, not from someone who knows what I buy. No pricing anywhere, no discount, and the word "crafty" means nothing to me — I'm not an art class. I'll mark this as read and move on.

## What's working

- The canvas material callout — "100% soft washable canvas" — is the kind of product detail I actually appreciate; tells me something about the shoe
- Neutral/earthy color palette is a strong signal that this is a wearable, everyday shoe, not a fashion statement
- "Shop Clogs" button is clean and direct — verb plus noun, I know exactly where it goes
- The Classic Crafted Clog gets its own dedicated hero section with a clear product shot; no ambiguity about what's being sold
- Men's sizing is included in the Shop By Size grid, so at least someone thought about gender split

## What's weak

- "Recommended for You" includes a Kids' Classic Clog — I don't have small children, and this tells me the algorithm is guessing at best
- The Miami Toe Loop Sandal recommendation looks like a women's summer sandal; completely wrong for me
- No price, no offer, no "save X%" anywhere in sight — this is a brand email, not a buying email, and I'm not in the mood for brand emails after this many this week
- The "Shop Jibbitz Charms" button presumes I'm shopping for clog accessories; I'm shopping for shoes that don't wreck my feet by 3pm
- The cadence problem is real — this is what, the seventh or eighth email since May 22nd? The goodwill from a decent send on May 22nd is long spent

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with comfort language, not "crafty."** The canvas material is actually a selling point — washable, breathable, lightweight. That's what should be in the headline, not a pun about arts and crafts. "Lightweight Canvas. Zero Fuss." would speak directly to me.
- 2. **Fix the "Recommended for You" section.** Pull the Kids' Clog and the toe-loop sandal for a 62-year-old male subscriber. Show me the Baya Clog, the Classic Clog, maybe the men's flip flop if you must. The algorithm is hurting trust, not building it.
- 3. **Put a number on it.** Even a "starting at $XX" would give me a reason to click. Right now I have no idea what this shoe costs relative to the standard Classic Clog.
- 4. **Preview text is broken.** It's showing invisible characters — just blank in my inbox. Any real copy at all would be an improvement.
- 5. **Slow down the sends.** I've had eight emails in eight days. This "Crafted" story is solid enough to deserve breathing room; don't bury it in the pile.

## Technical Audit — Crocs "We Got Crafty!" (2026-05-30)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Solid structural foundation with proper responsive scaffolding and multi-weight custom font loading, but the footer asset pipeline has a blanket HTTP downgrade affecting 7 images, and 5 dynamically-injected recommendation images carry no alt attributes.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text body is URL-heavy (70.5% URLs)**
- QA measured 15,875 of 22,523 plain-text characters as URLs.
- Spam filters (SpamAssassin `URI_COUNT`, Gmail heuristics) penalize plain-text parts where URL content dominates prose. Risk: inbox placement degradation over time.

**34 click-redirect links not probed**
- All CTA links route through a click-tracking redirect domain; destination resolution was skipped. No broken-link verdict is possible from this audit. Recommend a separate redirect-chain check against the live send.

**Open-pixel missing alt**
- `<img>` at `https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?...` has no `alt=""`. Should be `alt=""` (empty string, not absent) per accessibility standards; absence causes some screen readers to announce the raw URL.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**5 dynamically-rendered recommendation images have no alt text**
```
https://10745993.recs.igodigital.com/rr/v2/.../i/1/walker@agentmail.to?category=Recommended for You
https://10745993.recs.igodigital.com/rr/v2/.../i/2/walker@agentmail.to?...
https://10745993.recs.igodigital.com/rr/v2/.../i/3/walker@agentmail.to?...
https://10745993.recs.igodigital.com/rr/v2/.../i/4/walker@agentmail.to?...
```
These are product recs injected at send time. With images disabled (common in Outlook/enterprise), recipients see nothing — no product name, no price, no fallback label. Alt text like `alt="Recommended product"` is the minimum; ideally the rec engine should populate product name dynamically.

**`<meta charset>` attribute missing `charset=`**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
The `charset` attribute is absent — this should be `<meta charset="utf-8">` or `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">`. Most clients infer UTF-8, but the malformed tag is technically incorrect and could cause rendering issues in strict parsers.

**Custom font load over CDN only**
`TT Crocs` is loaded from `media.crocs.com` with no system-font fallback declared in the `@font-face` stack. The CSS fallback chain in body styles should specify a web-safe fallback (e.g., `font-family: 'TT Crocs', Arial, sans-serif`) to prevent invisible or mis-sized text when the CDN is slow or blocked.

**No issues found:** Column stacking logic, viewport meta, and Apple/Gmail hack overrides are all correctly implemented.
