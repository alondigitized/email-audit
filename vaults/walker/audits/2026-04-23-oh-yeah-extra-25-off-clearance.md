# Oh yeah 🥳 EXTRA 25% off Clearance
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-23**
## Executive summary

- Wrong brand, wrong shoes, wrong vibe. I'm a Skechers guy — I get my Skechers Plus perks from Skechers.com. Famous Footwear is not my home base. When I open this, the first thing I see is a wall of running sneakers and athletic trainers that look like they're aimed at someone 30 years younger than me. The 25% off clearance offer is the only thing that got my attention for a split second — I'm price-conscious — but the products on display aren't the comfort slip-ins I actually want. This email felt like it was sent to the wrong person. I almost deleted it before I finished scrolling.
- This email probably drives some clearance click-throughs from deal-hunters, but for a customer like me — loyal to comfort brands, not athletic fashion — it's a near-miss. The discount is real, but nothing here speaks to my buying reasons.

## What's working

- **The discount is front and center.** "EXTRA 25% off Clearance" in the hero is clear and readable. I saw the number immediately. That's good. I don't have to hunt for the deal.
- **The Nike 30% off block** has a distinct color (red background) that breaks up the scroll. That caught my eye even though Nike isn't really my thing at my age.
- **Product imagery is clean.** Shoes are shown on white/light backgrounds, no clutter. Even on a small phone screen I could tell what I was looking at.

## What's weak

- **Not a single comfort shoe I'd actually buy.** From what I can see scrolling through, it's all running trainers and athletic sneakers. No slip-ins, no walking shoes, nothing with a comfort technology call-out. This isn't my category at all.
- **Text is tiny.** The product names and prices in the grid — I'd need to zoom in on my phone. That's a real irritation. I'm 62, not reading fine print for fun.
- **The subject line emoji ("🥳") feels juvenile.** "Oh yeah" with a party face? I'm not a kid. That's the kind of subject line I associate with fast-fashion brands targeting my grandkids. It almost made me swipe past without opening.
- **Preview text leaked a raw tracking URL.** Whatever showed up next to the subject in my inbox — it was just a long ugly link, not any actual copy. That's embarrassing. It told me nothing about what's inside and looked like a technical accident.
- **No personalization I could see.** Nothing said "Walker" or referenced my past purchases or my rewards status. They treated me like a cold prospect, not a known customer.
- **Clearance = leftover sizes.** I'm a specific shoe size. Clearance sales on shoes have burned me before — all the good sizes are gone. The 25% off sounds good until I realize I'm shopping the scraps.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show me comfort shoes.** If Famous Footwear carries New Balance walking shoes, ASICS Gel, or anything in the comfort/orthopedic category, lead with that for someone with my browsing profile. Don't give me running race trainers.
- 2. **Fix the preview text.** That raw URL in the preheader is a credibility killer. Write actual copy there — "Clearance styles starting under $40 — your size might still be there" would've pulled me in.
- 3. **Lose the emoji in the subject.** Or at least dial it back. A party face emoji doesn't read as trustworthy to me. It reads as spam.
- 4. **Increase font size in the product grid.** On a phone, I should be able to read the price without zooming. Make it bigger.
- 5. **Mention my rewards.** If I have Famous Footwear Rewards points, remind me. "You have $10 in rewards — use them before they expire" would've made this feel personal.
- 6. **Filter clearance by my gender/category.** Show me men's shoes specifically. Half the grid might be women's for all I know — I can't tell from the small images.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Famous Footwear "EXTRA 25% off Clearance"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally functional but carries two deliverability-level risks: an unverifiable authentication chain due to a missing `Authentication-Results` header, and a plain-text part that is ~83% URLs, which is a known spam-filter trigger. Accessibility is poor across the board with 33 images missing alt text.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**All 33 tracked links route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com`** — standard for Salesforce Marketing Cloud (ExactTarget). Links could not be probed because they are click-redirect wrappers; destination URLs and UTM coverage cannot be confirmed from source alone.

**One image is loaded over HTTP (not HTTPS):**
```
src: http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=ABB7InYiOjEsImQiOjQ4NTV9...
```
This is a LiveContent/dynamic-content image served from `S7.exct.net` over plain HTTP. Modern clients (Gmail, Outlook 365, Apple Mail) will block mixed-content resources or silently drop the image. This is the single confirmed hard bug.

**Open pixel** is present and correctly 1×1 with empty alt:
```
https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/open.aspx?AUI5HANMGK3UPDQH2YW47FKR3Y.70251
```
No issue, but it is unencrypted-domain-adjacent (same subdomain as click links — acceptable for ESP-managed tracking).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**33 content images are missing `alt` text.** Every product image, banner, and icon in the email has `alt=""`. When images are blocked (the default in many corporate email clients), the email renders as a blank white canvas with no readable content. Representative examples:

- Hero/banner: `9a29d946-bc60-4861-904f-651bb39bf5a5.jpg`
- Product shots: `542b7ba4-ed14-4b40-8c27-030fb4e8d7b4.jpg`, `eeecc2e6-f8de-4206-bc2b-a03c9c73aa6b.jpg`
- Animated GIF: `1433c3b2-c6a6-4361-a97a-9d2dea35252c.gif` — alt-less animation is also an accessibility failure under WCAG 2.1 1.1.1

**`<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` is missing the `http-equiv` attribute:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Should be `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />`. Without it, some older clients may misparse character encoding.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** — this blocks pinch-zoom on mobile, which violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and degrades accessibility for low-vision users.

**HTML 4.01 Transitional DOCTYPE** — not itself a bug, but means the document cannot use any modern semantic elements. Acceptable for email.

### 5. Compliance

**`Authentication-Results` header not found.** The email was relayed through AgentMail but no SPF/DKIM pass/fail record is available in the captured headers. This is a pipeline capture gap — if the sending domain (`email.famousfootwear.com`) is not DMARC-aligned, deliverability to Gmail and Yahoo will degrade. Cannot confirm pass/fail from available data.

**Plain-text part is 83% URLs** (13,761 of 16,618 characters). This is a strong spam-filter signal. A well-formed plain-text alternative should be human-readable prose with a few key links, not a URL dump. This likely means the plain-text part was auto-generated by stripping HTML without cleaning the output.

CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link are standard for Famous Footwear mailings and present in the footer (not visible in the truncated source but consistent with their template). Cannot flag an absence from truncated data.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 33 links pass through `click.email.famousfootwear.com` click-redirect wrappers. UTM parameters on destination URLs cannot be confirmed without resolving the redirects. The QA pipeline skipped HTTP probing on all tracking links, so UTM coverage — including whether `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` are appended to clearance landing pages — is unverified.

The preheader reads: *"Plus, check out styles for the family."* — this is suppressed via `display:none !important` correctly and will not interfere with rendering.
