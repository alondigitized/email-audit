# Your Items Are Waiting!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-10**
## Executive summary

- It's the pink flower sneaker again. That's the D'Lites "Spring Bloom," a women's shoe I never put in a cart. It sits front and center under "We noticed you noticing us…", which is a little creepy. I've now seen it in "Your cart expires soon!" and "Did you forget something?" and this same "Your Items Are Waiting!" subject on the 9th. Same shoe, same nag.
- The one new thing is the plaid banner way down the page: members stack an extra 10% off, valid through 10/11. That's a real offer, but I had to scroll past the shoe, the button and a grid of kids' sneakers to find it. The grid does have two men's shoes I'd look at, the Altman slip-in and the Summits Contour Foam. They're thumbnail-sized, though, and the Altman is a hands-free shoe, so it should have been the hero.
- Would I click? Probably not. If I did, it'd be the 10% banner or the Altman, not "Complete Your Order." I wouldn't forward it. I'd mark it read.

## What's working

- The Skechers logo and navy headline are easy to read, and I know who it's from.
- "Complete Your Order" is a big, plain button with an obvious tap target.
- The slip-on Altman and the Summits Contour Foam are in the grid. That's closer to what I actually wear.
- The "Members stack an extra 10% off sitewide" banner is large and clear once I get to it. The "Valid thru 10/11" date gives me a deadline I can believe.
- The Women / Men / Kids / Sale list near the bottom is simple. The large type is easy to tap on my phone.

## What's weak

- The women's pink floral sneaker is the hero again, and I never picked it. A 62-year-old man gets nothing from it.
- Four of the six "great options" are kids' shoes or waterproofer spray. That's not "more options we think you'll love" for me.
- The 10% off is buried below the fold, and the fine print is tiny and hard to read without zooming.
- The same subject line ran on the 9th and the 7th, on top of a week of other cart reminders. It feels like being chased.
- There's nothing about cushioning, arch support, or the slip-in fit I care about. The product names are all I get.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop showing a women's shoe to a man. Lead with a men's slip-in or walking shoe, or a shoe I actually browsed. Never lead with the most-viewed women's style.
- 2. Put the member 10% banner right under the hero, so the offer comes before the product grid.
- 3. Limit the grid to three men's comfort shoes, each with a short note like "Hands-free slip-in, Arch Fit, cushioned." Drop the kids' shoes and the spray.
- 4. Cut the cart reminders to one or two, not one a day, and vary the subject line.
- 5. Make the fine print bigger or shorter, and state the end date in the banner.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 10% member discount ends tomorrow`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Walker, your slip-ins are still in your cart`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Members take an extra 10% off through 10/11. Easy returns.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Hands-free comfort, still available in your size.`

  - The probe may have been rejected because it was a non-browser request, or because the token was single-use or bound to a session (`attn_sid`). Confirm manually with a real browser before treating it as a hard failure.
  - If the 400 also occurs for a real recipient, this is a compliance defect.

  - "Complete you Order"
  - "We noticed you noticing us... Your cart is still waiting..."
  - These are probably Attentive click-tracker throttling, so their destinations are unverified.

- **All links are Attentive-wrapped redirects.** Examples are the "web version" link and the logo link, which both use `skechers.e.attentivemail.com/e/c/...`. The final destinations can't be checked from the source.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- **`http://` images (about 14).** All are on `image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/{1,5,11}/...`:
  - Logo `dde00662-...png`
