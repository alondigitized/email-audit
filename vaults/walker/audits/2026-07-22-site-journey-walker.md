## Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Date: July 22, 2026 | Yesterday's Score: 4/10**

---

## 1. Executive Summary

I went to Skechers.com looking for what I always look for — something comfortable that slips on easy, doesn't beat up my feet, and won't break the bank past what it's worth. I'm 62, I know what I need, and I just want the site to help me find it fast. Today's trip had a couple of bright spots — the Hands Free Slip-ins section was easy to find and the navigation menu was clean — but the experience fell apart at the finish line. The cart page loaded completely blank. Nothing. Just white. That's not a hiccup, that's a lost sale. And when I searched "comfort shoes," Skechers handed me women's shoes first. I'm logged in. They know I'm a man. There's no excuse for that.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

Same score as yesterday. No meaningful improvement. The cart failure is critical and unresolved (or newly broken). Search gender-blindness persists. The site still leads with athletic imagery that doesn't speak to a comfort-casual shopper like me.

---

## 3. What's Working

**Promo banner is impossible to miss.** "SAVE 25% OFF $100+ Code: SAVINGS" sits in high-contrast blue at the very top. I saw it immediately. That's good — I'm price-conscious and this made me feel like I was in the right place before I even scrolled.

**The Men's navigation panel is genuinely good.** Big, bold text. Clear categories with chevron arrows I can actually tap without squinting. "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Extra Wide Fit" all visible as quick-access links without having to dig. That's exactly the kind of accessibility that works for my hands and eyes. No complaints here.

**Getting to Hands Free Slip-ins was straightforward.** Three taps — MENU, MEN, Slip-ins — and I'm on a filtered category page with 313 results. Fit filters (Wide, Extra Wide, Medium) are right at the top. That's efficient.

**The Add to Cart screen has decent touch targets.** The size buttons are reasonable in size, the QTY dropdown is visible, and the blue "Add to Cart" button is full-width at the bottom. Clear hierarchy. I knew what to do.

**Size validation is clear.** Red "Please select a size" with a warning triangle — I saw that and understood it immediately. Good UX.

---

## 4. What's Weak

**The cart page is blank. Completely white. Nothing loaded.** This is the single most damaging thing I saw. I put a shoe in my cart (or tried to), I tapped through, and I got an empty white screen. If I were actually shopping, I'd think the site was broken — and I'd close the tab and go to Amazon or DSW. This is a checkout-killing bug.

**No personalization after logging in — at all.** After I signed in, the homepage looked identical to what I saw before logging in. Same hero image. Same banners. No "Welcome back, Walker." No "Based on your history, you might like..." Nothing. I'm a returning customer and the site treats me like a stranger who just wandered in. If Hoka or New Balance showed me my last purchase and suggested something similar, I'd feel taken care of. Skechers does none of that.

**Search for "comfort shoes" returned women's products first.** I searched the words I actually think in — "comfort shoes" — and 1,457 results came back. Fine. But both visible products in the top row were Women's. I'm logged in as a man. The site knows this. Showing me women's mules as the top result is a mismatch that would send me straight to the filter button in frustration — if I even understood what happened. A lot of guys my age would just assume the site doesn't have what they're looking for.

**The hero image on the homepage doesn't speak to me.** A young basketball player, Skechers Running branding, "Comfort That Performs." That's not me. That's someone 35 years younger than me with a totally different shopping mindset. I want to see someone walking a trail, standing on their feet all day, wearing something that feels like a cloud. The Slip-ins badge at the bottom was a small nod in my direction, but it's buried below imagery that doesn't connect.

**The Britto product is excluded from promotions — but the banner doesn't warn me before I get there.** I land on a $90 shoe with a big orange "Excluded from promotions" notice. I had that 25% off code in my head the whole time I was browsing. Finding out the specific product I'm looking at doesn't qualify is deflating. Better to flag this in the product grid, not after I've already invested time reading the detail page.

**No reviews on the Britto product.** "No Reviews" with empty stars. At $90, I need social proof. I look at what other people say before I buy — especially for a shoe I haven't tried before. Zero reviews is a trust problem.

**The product I landed on (Britto Landscape) is very niche.** Colorful pop-art design. That's a fashion shoe, not a comfort shoe. I'm not saying it doesn't belong in the catalog, but routing a comfort-first shopper through Hands Free Slip-ins to this as a featured result is a mismatch.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page — today.** This is a P0 bug. A white cart means zero revenue from anyone who reaches that step. Reproduce it, find the root cause, and patch it. Everything else is secondary.

2. **Apply gender filtering to search.** When a logged-in male account searches "comfort shoes," men's results should appear first by default. This is table-stakes personalization.

3. **Add post-login acknowledgment.** Even a simple "Welcome back" in the header or a "Picks for you" strip on the homepage would make a returning customer feel recognized.

4. **Surface exclusions earlier in the funnel.** Show a "Not eligible for promo" badge on the product card in the grid, before the customer clicks through and feels the disappointment.

5. **Lead the homepage with comfort, not performance.** For the men's segment especially, dial back the athletic running imagery and show someone like me — a comfortable, active adult — wearing Arch Fit or Slip-ins. The audience that buys most of these shoes doesn't identify with the basketball court hero.

6. **Require reviews before featuring a $90 shoe.** Zero-review products at premium price points shouldn't be top of a curated category like Hands Free Slip-ins.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

Skechers has the right products for me. The Hands Free Slip-ins category is exactly what I need, and the navigation to get there is genuinely one of the cleaner mobile menu experiences I've seen. But a blank cart page is a checkout failure with no recovery path, and search that ignores my gender preference after I've logged in signals that the site isn't paying attention to who I am. Those two issues alone would have sent me to a competitor today. Score holds at **4/10** — same as yesterday, no measurable progress.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Platform: Salesforce Commerce Cloud (Demandware, confirmed by `/on/demandware.static/` paths). Bot protection via Akamai KasTLE SDK (the GUIDed `/fp?x-kpsdk-v=` endpoint). Third-party tag load is extreme — 20+ distinct origins across analytics, ad pixels, personalization, and affiliate tracking. The cart page is actively returning 429 to legitimate users. LCP and CLS are unmetered across all steps, indicating no real-user monitoring instrumentation for the two most conversion-critical Core Web Vitals.

---

## Accessibility

17 violations total across 3 pages.

**Critical**
- `button-name` (Homepage): Icon-only buttons with no accessible label (`aria-label`, `title`, or inner text). Common culprit: carousel controls, close buttons, wishlist toggles.
- `aria-required-parent` (Homepage): ARIA role elements (`role="option"`, `role="tab"`, etc.) rendered outside their required parent container. Likely a dynamic widget (carousel or dropdown) injecting elements at the wrong DOM level.

**Serious**
- `document-title` (View Cart): The cart page's `<title>` element is absent or empty in the HTML payload. If this is client-rendered, the title is set via JS after parse — invisible to screen readers on load and to crawlers.
- `html-has-lang` (View Cart): The `<html>` tag lacks a `lang` attribute on the cart page, despite `/` and category pages including `lang="en"`. Inconsistent — likely a different template or a client-render shell that never gets the attribute set.

**Moderate**
- `heading-order` (Homepage): Heading hierarchy is non-sequential (e.g., `h1` → `h3`, skipping `h2`). Common in CMS-driven banner stacks.
- `landmark-main-is-top-level` / `landmark-no-duplicate-main` / `landmark-unique` (Homepage, Search): Multiple `<main>` elements or `role="main"` usages. Monetate/personalization widgets likely inject a second `<main>` into the DOM.
- `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` (Homepage, Search): `<aside>` or `role="complementary"` nested inside another landmark instead of being a top-level sibling.
- `region` (Homepage, Search): Content outside any landmark region — typically injected third-party markup (chat widgets, consent banners).
- `landmark-one-main` / `page-has-heading-one` (View Cart): Cart shell has no `<main>` and no `<h1>` in the static HTML, confirming the cart is a client-render shell with late hydration.

**Minor**
- `aria-allowed-role` (Homepage, Search): An element has a `role` value that is not permitted for its HTML element type (e.g., `role="listitem"` on a `<div>` that should be a `<li>`).

## SEO

- **View Cart missing `<title>`**: A page with no title gets crawled as untitled; if Googlebot ever indexes the cart URL, it renders as blank. More likely the title is JS-set and invisible to SSR-based crawlers.
- **View Cart missing `html[lang]`**: Hreflang and language signals absent for this page variant.
- **Heading order violations on Homepage**: Googlebot uses heading hierarchy for content structure signals; skipped levels degrade topical clarity.
- **Login and Men Category TTFB at 396ms**: 13× slower than the sub-31ms pages. These appear to be session-gated or personalized SSR responses. Slow TTFB directly compresses Googlebot's crawl budget allocation for these URL patterns.
- **No evidence of canonical tags or structured data** in the truncated HTML (product schema, breadcrumbs, etc.) — not confirmable from the sample, but absence of `application/ld+json` in the `<head>` samples is notable for a product catalog site.

## Mobile Optimization

- **`--vh` CSS custom property:** `style="--vh: 6.640000000000001px"` on `<html>`. This is a JS-calculated viewport height fix (100vh = `--vh * 100`) to compensate for mobile browser chrome. The floating-point imprecision (`6.640000000000001` instead of `6.64`) indicates the value is set via `window.innerHeight / 100` without rounding. Not a bug, but worth rounding to avoid subpixel rendering artifacts.
- **Cart 429 on mobile**: See Network Errors. If this is a bot-protection false positive triggered by mobile User-Agent or fingerprint characteristics, it directly causes cart abandonment.
- **No viewport-specific LCP/CLS data**: Cannot assess image sizing, font swap behavior, or layout shift patterns on mobile without this instrumentation.

## Console & Network Errors

**429s on KasTLE fingerprinting endpoint (5 occurrences):**
```
https://www.skechers.com/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.522
```
This is Akamai's KasTLE/Page Integrity Manager bot detection fingerprinting script rate-limiting its own requests back to the origin. The SDK fires multiple fingerprint POSTs per session; the Akamai rule set is throttling them. This is a KasTLE configuration issue — the rate limit policy must allowlist the SDK's own endpoint or increase the threshold. When fingerprinting fails, bot classification degrades and downstream protection weakens.

**429 on `/cart/`:**
Legitimate users hitting the cart page are receiving HTTP 429. This is either the KasTLE bot protection mis-classifying the cart request as bot traffic (common during automated test sessions or when fingerprinting fails — see above), or a separate rate limit rule on the cart endpoint set too aggressively. **This is a direct conversion funnel blocker.**

**Stylitics CORS error:**
```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?base=grid&theme=skechers&...' 
has been blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```
Stylitics' CDN (`web-assets.stylitics.com`) is not returning CORS headers for stylesheet requests originating from `skechers.com`. The Stylitics outfit/styling widget will render unstyled or not at all. This requires Stylitics to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` on their CDN config, or Skechers to proxy the stylesheet through their own domain to avoid the cross-origin constraint.

## Recommendations

**P0 — Revenue impact**
1. **Fix cart 429**: Audit the KasTLE/Akamai rate limit rules for `/cart/`. The fingerprinting 429s likely cascade into the cart 429 — when KasTLE can't fingerprint the session, the bot score is indeterminate and the cart endpoint's WAF rule fires. Fix the fingerprint endpoint allowlist first, then retest cart access.

**P1 — Correctness bugs**
2. **View Cart `<title>` and `html[lang]`**: If the cart is a client-render shell, ensure the SSR or static HTML template sets both. Do not rely on JS to set `document.title` — screen readers and crawlers read the static payload.
3. **Fix unnamed buttons** (`button-name`): Add `aria-label` to all icon-only interactive elements site-wide. These are WCAG 2.1 Level A failures.
4. **Fix orphaned ARIA roles** (`aria-required-parent`): Audit the carousel/dropdown widget DOM injection to ensure role containers are present before role items are inserted.
5. **Stylitics CORS**: File a support ticket with Stylitics to add the CORS header for `skechers.com` origin, or proxy `style.css` through Skechers' own CDN.

**P2 — Instrumentation gaps**
6. **Instrument LCP and CLS for all steps**: Without these, there is no visibility into the two most critical Web Vitals. Use the Web Vitals library or PerformanceObserver with `largest-contentful-paint` and `layout-shift` entry types, and route to an analytics endpoint.

**P3 — Efficiency**
7. **Remove duplicate Yahoo YTC script**: One load per page, not two.
8. **Audit GTM tag triggers**: Reconcile why Snapchat, Reddit, Taboola, NextDoor, and Stape CDN fire on category pages but not the homepage — likely unintentional trigger scope or missing exclusions.
9. **Round the `--vh` value**: Change `window.innerHeight / 100` to `Math.round(window.innerHeight / 100 * 1000) / 1000` to avoid the `6.640000000000001` floating-point artifact in the style attribute.
