# Samba, reimagined just for you
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- Well, they finally got a shoe onto the screen that isn't a soccer jersey, so that's something — this is different from the last few adidas sends. The "Samba goes metallic" bit is fine, if that's your style, though the shoe in the photo just looks like a blue suede sneaker to me, nothing shiny or metallic about it — so if I hadn't read the subject line I'd have no idea what "metallic" meant. Then it takes a hard turn into "The Back to School Sale" with three teenagers and a bike, which has nothing to do with a retired guy like me buying his third pair of walk-around shoes.
- There's no price on the Samba, no mention of cushioning or arch support, nothing about sizing — just a mood shot and a slogan. The 40% off and the Apple TV freebie are aimed at parents outfitting kids for school, not me. I wouldn't click, and I sure wouldn't forward it — my grandkids' parents already get plenty of back-to-school noise from other places. This one goes in the "read halfway, then archived" pile.

## What's working

- The top banner "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" with a clean black "SHOP NOW" button is at least a clear, tappable call to action.
- The Samba product photo is sharp and well-lit, no rendering issues that I can see.
- Layout is simple, one column, easy to scroll on my phone without pinching to zoom.
- No spelling errors or garbled text anywhere in what I can see.

## What's weak

- The "metallic" claim doesn't match what's in the photo — the shoe just looks like regular blue suede to me, so the headline confused me rather than intrigued me.
- No price shown on the Samba at all — I always want to see the number before I decide whether it's worth clicking through.
- The Back to School section is three young guys with a bike and a basketball — nothing about that says "comfort shoe for a 62-year-old."
- Nothing here mentions cushioning, arch support, or slip-on comfort — the stuff that actually gets me to click.
- The Apple TV promo requires spending $130, which is a lot to drop on a shoe I haven't even seen a price for yet.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put an actual price or "starting at $X" on the Samba shot — I'm not clicking blind.
- 2. Either show the metallic finish clearly in better light, or drop that word from the headline — right now it's false advertising to my eye.
- 3. Split the Back to School creative out to a segment that's actually parents — send me something with a guy my age, maybe walking the dog or out for coffee, wearing a comfort shoe.
- 4. Add one line about comfort features (cushioned insole, arch support) since that's what actually moves me to click, not "bold new era of expression."
- 5. Drop the Apple TV tie-in for my segment — replace with a straightforward "free shipping over $75" or similar, something a value-shopper like me actually cares about.
