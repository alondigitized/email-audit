That's a shoe sale, plain as day, and I can read it without squinting.
- Where it loses me is the size grid. I've got to scroll past six rows of women's, men's, and kids' size buttons before I even see a shoe. And when the shoes finally show up, half of them are women's slip-ins and kids' sandals — fine for background noise, but not what I'm buying. - I'd open this one — the subject and the points balance pulled me in. I wouldn't forward it to a friend, but I might click through to men's if I'm already thinking about new walking shoes before the sale ends.

## What's working

- "Hi, Walker" and my 1000-point balance right up top — feels like they know I'm a member, not a stranger.
- 20% off full-priced styles, then 40% off select styles further down — two concrete, real offers, no guessing games.
- One men's hands-free slip-in style (Knowleton) is right there in the product grid — that's the shoe I actually want to see.
- Clean layout, big readable text, no overlapping graphics or broken images.
- "Labor Day Sale" is timely and matches what's actually going on this weekend, unlike some of the recycled cart-nag emails I've been getting.

## What's weak

- The size chart eats the whole middle of the email before I get to a single shoe — that's a lot of scrolling for a guy on his phone.
- Five of the six featured shoes are women's or kids' — I have to hunt for the one pair in my size range.
- No arch support or cushioning language anywhere — for a "comfort" brand pitch to a guy like me, that's a missed hook.
- "SHOP THE SALE" and "SHOP NOW" buttons dump me to a general sale page, not a men's or hands-free landing page.

- 1. Put a men's comfort/hands-free hero product above the size charts — lead with the shoe, not the size grid.
- 2. Swap at least half the product grid to men's styles since I'm clearly tagged as a member with purchase history.
- 3. Add one line about cushioning or arch support near the offer — give me the fit reason, not just the price reason.
- 4. Make the "SHOP THE SALE" button deep-link to men's or hands-free, not a generic catalog.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
Email uses standard SFMC-style responsive markup with multiple tracking pixels/beacons, but has a broken personalization token in the greeting and several image assets served over HTTP.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- Tracking infrastructure present and consistent: open-tracking pixel (`click.emails.skechers.com/open.aspx?...`), two Kantar/Krux (`beacon.krxd.net`) user-match pixels, one Krux ad-impression pixel (`campaignid=PRO_US_NA_U_SITESELECT_LC_EN_09072026`), an ink1000 pixel, and a Return Path deliverability pixel (note: `pixel.app.returnpath.net/...` is missing its `http(s)://` scheme, which will cause it to fail to load as a relative path in most clients).
- 59 click/tracking-redirect links were skipped by the automated HTTP probe (expected for SFMC click-redirect domains) — destination resolution for CTAs is **not verified** in this pass.
- No issues found in pixel/beacon presence or campaign-ID tagging consistency.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 2 images missing `alt` text: `o.gif` (ink1000 tracking pixel — low impact, non-content) and `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` (a content image — should have descriptive alt text for screen readers and for when images are blocked).
- 9 images served over `http://` rather than `https://`, which many mail clients (Outlook, Gmail proxy) will flag as mixed/insecure content and may block or fail to render:
  - `http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif`
  - `http://image.emails.skechers.com/.../dde00662-169f-447d-b0e2-fc65f6c2290c.png` (Skechers logo)

  - App Store, Google Play, Curbside Pickup, AfterPay, Find A Store, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube icons (all same `image.emails.skechers.com` host, all `http://`)
