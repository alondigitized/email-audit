# New Harry Potter™ collection
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-05**
## Executive summary

- Crocs again. After a week of 31% and 50% off, this one has no price in it anywhere. It's a red Harry Potter clog covered in little charms, and I'm not the buyer. I might buy it for a grandkid. I'd never wear it.
- It's clean and the text is big enough to read on my phone. The only offer is "free shipping on qualified orders" in the top bar, and the "qualified" part bothers me. I'd skim it and move on. I wouldn't click, and I wouldn't forward it, unless one of my grandkids is a Potter fan.

## What's working

- The hero shoe is big and sharp, and the Platform 9¾ ticket and charms are fun to look at.
- "All Aboard" on the gold block is easy to read, and the black "Shop Harry Potter™" button is large enough to tap.
- The four rounded buttons (Clogs, Jibbitz™ Charms, Sale, Sandals) are big, and "Shop Sale" gets me to the discounts.
- Black on gold has good contrast, so I didn't have to zoom.

## What's weak

- There's no price or percent off in the body. After last week's 50% and 31% emails, this feels like a step backward.
- It's aimed at fans and collectors, not at someone who wants cushioning and arch support.
- "Free shipping on qualified orders" is vague, and the fine print at the bottom doesn't explain it.
- The "Refer a Friend: Give 25%, Get 25% Off!" offer is the best deal in the email, and it's buried down in the black footer.
- Nothing about comfort, fit or support. It's all theme and no substance.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real offer next to the hero, such as the Sale link with a percent off, or the referral deal moved up.
- 2. Say what "qualified" means, for example "Free shipping over $50".
- 3. Add a line about the comfort of the clog for people like me. Something like "Same all-day cushioned comfort, now with Hogwarts charms."
- 4. Add a gift angle for grandparents, such as "Gifts for the Potter fan in your life".

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is table-based with a responsive `@media (max-width: 639px)` block and a hidden preheader. The automated QA run found 0 failures and 1 warning. The real defects are 7 `http://` image sources, one tracking pixel with no `alt`, and two malformed `<meta>` tags. The HTML source is truncated, so the footer, unsubscribe block and most links could not be inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** 24 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are on tracking domains. Their destinations and redirect health are unverified.
- **Open pixel:** `https://click.crocs-email.com/open.aspx?qs=…&d=10206&bmt=0` is 1×1 with `alt=""`, which is correct for a decorative image.
- **Third-party pixel without `alt`:** `https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&c_emailopened=Yes&d_dpuid=9153f33f…` has no `alt` attribute. This is an Adobe Audience Manager (demdex) beacon.
  - It sends an identifier (`d_dpuid`) to a third-party domain on open.
  - The identifier can't be confirmed as hashed from the truncated value.
  - It is a second open-tracking call, in addition to the ESP pixel.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Insecure image sources:** 7 images load over `http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/…`. They are the App Store, Google Play, Find Your Store, Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and YouTube images. Clients or proxies that block mixed content can drop them. The App Store and Google Play images carry the download CTAs, so losing them removes those CTAs. Their `alt` text is present.
- **Ineffective `<meta>` declarations:**
  - `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` has no `http-equiv="Content-Type"`.
  - `<meta content="IE=edge" />` has no `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`.
  - Neither tag does anything as written. Encoding then depends on the MIME `Content-Type` header, which matters because the markup uses zero-width and soft-hyphen characters.
- **Generic title:** `<title>Crocs</title>` doesn't match the subject line. This is minor.
- **Missing `alt` on the demdex image:** see section 2.
- **No other rendering issues found.** The following look correct:
  - `lang="en"`
  - viewport meta
  - Apple data-detector reset
  - Gmail hack (`u + .email-body`)
  - `font-display: swap` with the web-font fallback

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`, `[FNAME]`) appear in the visible HTML, but the source is truncated.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Authentication:** the QA warning `Authentication-Results header not found` is a limitation of the AgentMail relay. SPF, DKIM and DMARC status are unknown. It is not evidence of a failure.
- **Footer not inspected:** the truncated source ends before the footer. The unsubscribe link, physical postal address and `List-Unsubscribe`/`List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers could not be checked.
- **Sender:** `email@crocs-email.com` is a dedicated sending subdomain and matches the tracking domains (`click.`, `image.`).

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not verifiable. All 24 links are ESP click-redirects that were not resolved, so the final URLs, UTM parameters and landing pages are unknown.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Change all 7 `http://image.crocs-email.com/…` image sources to `https://`.
2. Add `alt=""` to the demdex pixel (`demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event…`). Confirm that `d_dpuid` is a hashed or opaque ID and not a raw email address.
3. Fix the meta tags:
   - `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">`
   - `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge">`
4. Re-run the audit on the full HTML. Resolve the 24 redirects to check UTM parameters and landing pages, and confirm the unsubscribe link, postal address and `List-Unsubscribe` headers.
5. Capture the raw headers (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) from a direct inbox-placement test. The relay strips `Authentication-Results`.
6. Set the `<title>` to the subject line or campaign name. This is optional.
