# BOGO 50% OFF SANDALS FOR YOU
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- Alright, here we go. DSW lands in my inbox again — this is, what, the eighth or ninth BOGO sandal email in two weeks? I open it because DSW is a name I know and shop, plain muscle memory at this point. But the second the email loads, I'm staring at red strappy heels, espadrilles, and a mosaic of women's sandals. Same song they've been singing since May 13. The hero literally says "GET YOUR SAND-HAUL" and the fine print clarifies it's "any pair of women's sandals." Cool. I'm a 62-year-old man looking for something slip-on and cushioned.
- There IS a "SHOP MEN" link tucked under the hero in tiny type, which is the only nod to me in the whole layout. That counts for something, but I shouldn't have to hunt for it. Everything below the fold — the grid, the size chart with women's sizing (5 through 11), the wide-width buttons — is pointed at someone else's foot.
- Would I click? Maybe the small "SHOP MEN" link, grudgingly. Forward? No. Mark as read and move on? That's what's actually going to happen.

## What's working

- DSW name in the From line — I trust the brand and I do shop there, so I open.
- They at least bothered to include a "SHOP MEN" text link under the hero — small gesture, but it's there.
- Offer is clear: buy one women's sandal, get second 50% off. No mystery math.
- "SHOP WIDE WIDTH" / "SHOP WIDE CALF" buttons tell me they think about fit — good signal for an older shopper even if these are still women's.
- VIP rewards callout near the bottom reminds me I have points sitting somewhere.

## What's weak

- The whole email is women's sandals. Strappy heels, wedges, espadrilles — not one men's shoe in the grid.
- Size chart runs 5 to 11 — women's sizing, full stop. I wear an 11 men's; this isn't for me.
- "Sand-haul" is cute marketing speak but it means nothing to me — and the pun gets lost on a 62-year-old guy who just wants to know if his sandal is in here.
- This is the same BOGO drumbeat I've gotten almost daily for two weeks. Cadence fatigue is real.
- No men's slide, no Crocs-style slip-on, no Skechers Arch Fit sandal anywhere — the comfort-brand audience is invisible.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment by gender** — if DSW knows I'm a male customer (and they should after years of purchases), send me the men's version of this BOGO with men's sandals in the hero. Right now they're shotgunning the women's creative at everybody.
- 2. **Lead the hero with a slip-on or sport sandal** for the men's send — Teva, Reef, Skechers, Crocs. That's the comfort crowd.
- 3. **Cut the cadence** — this is the 8th-ish BOGO sandal email in two weeks. Skip a day. Let the offer breathe.
- 4. **Make the size chart match the audience** — women's sizing in a unisex email is a tell that I'm not the target.
- 5. **Bigger "SHOP MEN" CTA** — if you're not going to segment, at least put a real button on it, not a tiny text link.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Men's BOGO 50% — slip-ons, sport sandals, walkers`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Walker, your BOGO men's sandal pick is waiting`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Skechers, Teva, Reef — buy one, second half off through tomorrow.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Cushioned slides and sport sandals. Ends tomorrow.`
