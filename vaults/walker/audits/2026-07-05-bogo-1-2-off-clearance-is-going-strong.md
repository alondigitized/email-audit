# BOGO 1/2 off Clearance is going strong 💪
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- I already opened the July 4th version of this exact deal — "BOGO 1/2 off Clearance" was the whole pitch then, and here it is again the very next morning, just with "going strong" tacked on. That's not a new email. That's a nudge. And I don't hate it, because the deal itself is genuinely good — clearance BOGO is where I actually find the shoes I buy. The 10% military discount tucked in the middle is a nice touch of character I wasn't expecting. The free shipping banner gives me one less excuse to put it off.
- But then I flip to my inbox preview slot and there it is again — the same raw tracking URL they've been shipping as preheader text since at least the June 25 send. I've flagged it in three prior audits. At this point it's not a mistake, it's a feature, and it makes me feel like I'm opening a bulk broadcast rather than something a brand put thought into for me specifically.
- I'd scroll this one. I wouldn't forward it. If the clearance section has men's casual walkers in my size, I'd click — the offer earns that. But the email itself didn't do much to get me there.

## What's working

- **BOGO clearance math is unambiguous** — "buy one, get one half off" on clearance is a deal I can feel in my wallet immediately. No conversion required.
- **"Happy 4th from Famous!" hero lands right** — timely, warm, not overwrought. It fits the calendar and doesn't feel like a templated blast.
- **Military 10% off is a genuine trust signal** — I don't know if Famous does this every send, but seeing it in there made the brand feel a little more like it has values. That matters to a 62-year-old.
- **Free shipping banner is prominent and clear** — no minimum buried in fine print from what I can see. That removes friction on a borderline purchase.
- **Email renders clean on mobile** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no obvious layout disasters. For a clearance blast with a big product grid, that's not guaranteed.

## What's weak

- **Preheader is a raw tracking URL. Again.** I've called this out in the June 25, June 26, July 2, and July 4 sends. Five emails. Still broken. At this point my inbox is showing me gibberish before I even open, and that's not how you treat a subscriber you want to keep.
- **"Going strong" is a vague non-deadline.** When does this end? Tonight? Sunday? "Going strong" is not urgency — it's a shrug. The July 4 email had the holiday as a natural cutoff. This one has nothing.
- **Product grid is wide but not deep on comfort.** I'm scanning for cushioned, easy-on walkers — Skechers comfort, Hoka, New Balance. What I see in the grid is a mix of athletic runners, fashion boots, and casual lace-ups. Nothing that says "this was picked for you, Walker."
- **Back-to-back sends dilute the impact.** I already got this deal yesterday. A follow-up 18 hours later with the same creative tells me the brand is working the list, not working for me.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader — this is the single highest-ROI fix available.** Any real copy at all beats a tracking URL. "BOGO ends Sunday — grab your size before it's gone." Done. No code sprint required.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline.** "Going strong" is marketing filler. "Ends Sunday at midnight" is a reason to click now. Pick a date, print it big.
- 3. **Surface one men's comfort pick in the hero.** A single callout — "Skechers Arch Fit walkers, now BOGO" — would have pulled me past the scroll. The deal is great; the hero doesn't tell me it applies to anything I'd actually wear.
- 4. **Slow the cadence or differentiate the follow-up.** If you're going to send back-to-back on the same deal, give the second email a reason to exist — a new product drop, a "sizes selling out fast" signal, a different hook. This one just repeats day one.
- 5. **Military discount deserves more real estate.** That's a genuinely differentiating move. I almost missed it. Put it higher or make it part of the hero for 4th of July week specifically.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Clearance BOGO ends Sunday — your size won't last`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Still 50% off your second pair — but not for long`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cushioned walkers, slip-ons, everyday casuals — half off your second pair through Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free shipping + BOGO clearance ends Sunday. Don't wait — sizes go fast.`

## Technical Audit — Famous Footwear "BOGO 1/2 off Clearance" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a legacy HTML4 table-based layout with standard click-tracking via `click.email.famousfootwear.com`. The primary technical issues are a pervasive missing-alt-text problem across all images and a plain-text part that is almost entirely URLs, creating deliverability risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **32 tracking links unverifiable** — all CTAs route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com` click-redirect. QA prober correctly skipped these; destination URL health (broken product pages, 404s) cannot be confirmed without manual spot-checks.
- **Open pixel present** — `open.aspx?B3OB33L4MHYEDCAPNPVIUIVZF4.70248` loads correctly as a 1×1 beacon. No issue, expected.
- No raw untracked `https://famousfootwear.com` hrefs detected in the visible HTML excerpt — all links appear wrapped.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**High severity:**
- **35 images missing `alt` text** — every content image in the email has `alt=""` (empty string), including the logo (`3b6a7dc1-...png`), hero GIF (`c51648d3-...gif`), all product images, and all footer/nav images. Screen readers will skip these entirely. In image-blocking clients (Outlook default, corporate environments), the email renders as blank.
- **One file with uppercase extension** — `528d8a06-...pnG` has a mixed-case `.pnG` extension. While the CDN likely serves it correctly, this is a hygiene defect that could fail on case-sensitive resolvers.

**Low severity:**
- `<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` is missing the `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute — the charset declaration is technically malformed (`<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />`). Most clients tolerate this.
- `user-scalable=0` in the viewport meta blocks pinch-zoom on mobile — accessibility anti-pattern (WCAG 1.4.4).
- `lineapacing:20px` (typo, should be `line-height` or `letter-spacing`) on `.darkmode.padl10m td` — dead CSS rule.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens or placeholder syntax (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `[FIRST NAME]`, `%%first%%`) visible in the HTML excerpt. No issues found.

---

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

- **Authentication headers not provided** — DKIM, SPF, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be assessed from HTML alone. The sending domain `email.famousfootwear.com` should be verified to have a valid DKIM signature and aligned DMARC policy (`p=quarantine` or `p=reject`). This is a gap in the QA data, not a confirmed failure.
- **Physical address and unsubscribe** — truncated HTML prevents confirming footer presence. CAN-SPAM requires a postal address and functional one-click unsubscribe in every commercial email; these should be verified in the full source.
- **Preheader mismatch** — Subject line promotes "BOGO 1/2 off Clearance" but the preheader reads "Member exclusive🚛 Free shipping on EVERYTHING." These are different offers. Not a compliance issue, but the preheader is suppressed via `display:none !important` on `.preheader` — confirm this renders correctly as hidden preview text in Gmail/Apple Mail rather than as visible body content in some clients.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM Params, Landing Page Alignment)

- **UTM parameters unverifiable** — all destination URLs are encoded inside `click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=` redirect strings. The ESP (Cheetah/Salesforce Marketing Cloud based on the `camarker-inner` class pattern) likely appends UTM params at redirect time, but this cannot be confirmed from the source. Manual click-through on a test send should confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` are present on the final URL.
- **No hardcoded UTM params** in the raw href attributes — either fully delegated to the ESP redirect layer or missing. Confirm with the ESP configuration.
