# Spend & Save ends in a few hours 😱
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- I know what this email is before I even open it. Another "Spend & Save" urgency blast — and Famous Footwear has sent me so many of these in the past two weeks that the panic emoji in the subject line barely registers anymore. June 17th was "Spend & Save starts NOW." June 21st was the last day. Now here we are June 25th and it's ending *again* in a few hours. At some point "urgency" stops being urgency and starts being noise.
- That said — I opened it. The tiered offer is real and visible immediately: $10 off $50, $15 off $75, $20 off $100. That's a legible structure for a guy like me who knows roughly what he's going to spend. And I spotted Brooks at $109.99 and New Balance further down, which is the exact brands-I-actually-care-about territory. The Bonus Cash rewards section is a nice reminder that I've got skin in this game.
- But I've got one ongoing complaint I need to repeat for what feels like the tenth time this month: that preview text. Another wall of raw tracking URL nonsense. Same broken preheader I flagged on the June 17th Spend & Save send, and the June 21st send, and apparently every other send in between. At this point it's not a mistake — it's a habit Famous Footwear just refuses to fix. I'd probably not click through today. I've already been to the site twice during this promotion. Mark as read.

## What's working

- **Tiered offer is front and center and easy to parse** — $10/$15/$20 off depending on spend. A 62-year-old with readers can scan that without squinting.
- **"Ends today, 1:00 AM ET" is specific** — not vague "limited time," an actual hour. That's the kind of detail that makes urgency feel real.
- **Brooks at $109.99 shows up in the body** — that's my brand, my price range. The email isn't purely women's sandals and basketball shoes.
- **Bonus Cash/Rewards card is visible** — I'm a member; this feels like a message to me, not a stranger.
- **No visual chaos** — the layout is long but it doesn't feel broken. Products load, prices are readable.

## What's weak

- **Preview text is still a raw tracking URL — again.** This is now the fifth or sixth Famous Footwear email in a row with this exact problem. I've stopped expecting them to fix it, and that's a bad place for a brand to be.
- **Promotion fatigue is real.** This is at minimum the third "Spend & Save" email since June 17th. The panic emoji doesn't help when I've watched the "last day" extend for over a week.
- **Product grid is overwhelming on a phone.** Scroll, scroll, scroll — sandals I don't want, athletic shoes for teenagers, then finally something for me. Too much for a busy Thursday.
- **No single hero product.** I'd respond better to "Hey Walker, these Brooks are 15% off until midnight" than a catalogue dump with a banner on top.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader.** This should be job one for the Famous Footwear email team. One line of real copy — "$10 off when you spend $50. Ends tonight at 1 AM" — would add more lift than any creative tweak. The tracking URL has now appeared in at least six consecutive sends.
- 2. **Segment the body for men's comfort.** If you know I'm a Rewards member who's bought Brooks and New Balance, lead the product grid with those — not a mixed scroll of women's sandals and Jordans.
- 3. **Rest the "Spend & Save" campaign name.** After two straight weeks, even loyal subscribers are tuning it out. Call this one something different — "Midnight Closeout," "Last Chance Savings," anything that doesn't feel like the exact same email recycled.
- 4. **Put the dollar amounts in the subject.** The offer is the strongest hook; hiding it behind "Spend & Save" makes me work to remember what the deal actually is.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$20 off ends tonight — Brooks, New Balance + more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last hours: save up to $20 on men's comfort shoes`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $50 save $10, spend $100 save $20. Tonight only — 1 AM ET cutoff.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your Bonus Cash still counts. Spend & Save ends at 1 AM.`

The email is a standard table-layout HTML mailer with functional responsive breakpoints and a working open-tracking pixel. The primary issues are a pervasive alt-text gap across ~36 images, one HTTP (non-HTTPS) image asset, and a plain-text body that is nearly entirely URLs — a deliverability risk.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] 36 images missing `alt` text.** Every content image — hero, product tiles, separator bars, social icons, and all 8 iGoDigital recommendation images — carries `alt=""`. With images blocked (the default in many desktop clients), the email renders as a blank white column.

Notable affected assets:
- Logo: `3b6a7dc1-...ee.png` — no alt, so brand identity disappears when images are off
- Hero/banner: `39d83b1e-...ce.gif`, `2f86fd29-...e.jpg`
- All 8 recs engine images: `https://7222883.recs.igodigital.com/rr/v2/.../i/1–8/990267198170`
- Social/footer icons: `2f1c6d25-...png`, `81056cf8-...png`, `2abf94c2-...png`, `e81a5bc7-...png`

**[MEDIUM] One image served over HTTP:**
```
http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=ABB7InYiOjE...
```
This is a Salesforce Marketing Cloud LiveContent (dynamic content) call. Browsers and several email clients (Outlook 365, Apple Mail with "load remote content" off) will block mixed-content HTTP images. The asset will silently fail to load in those environments.

**Doctype** is HTML 4.01 Transitional — correct for broad email client compatibility.

**`user-scalable=0`** in the viewport meta prevents pinch-zoom on mobile, which is an accessibility regression (WCAG 1.4.4).

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM:** Unsubscribe link is present (visible in truncated source as `<u><a href="https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/...">` in the footer region). Physical mailing address not confirmed in truncated source but is standard in FF templates — should be verified in full HTML.

**Plain-text body deliverability risk [MEDIUM]:** QA reports 12,263 of 15,924 chars (77%) in the plain-text part are URLs. Spam filters (SpamAssassin, Gmail) flag high URL-to-text ratios. This is likely auto-generated by the ESP from the HTML with no manual plain-text authored.

**Authentication headers** (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) are not assessable from HTML source alone — require header inspection on a received copy. `famousfootwear@email.famousfootwear.com` is a subdomain sender which is a standard ESP pattern; alignment should be verified externally.
