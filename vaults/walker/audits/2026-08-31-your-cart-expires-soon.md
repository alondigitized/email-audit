# Your cart expires soon!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-31**
## Executive summary

- Same shoe, same headline lie — "you liked these, and you weren't wrong" — except I never liked them, never touched them, never so much as squinted at pink floral flowers on a sneaker. This is the sixth time this week that exact D'Lites has shown up in my inbox trying to convince me it was my idea. At this point it's not marketing, it's a rerun.
- Buried underneath the fake urgency, though, there's actually something useful this time — a men's slip-in and a men's relaxed-fit walking shoe in the "you liked these" grid, plus a real 20% off code lower down. That's the first thing in this whole cart-abandonment series that's remotely in my lane.
- I'm not clicking "Complete Your Order" on a shoe that was never in my cart. If I click anything, it's the 20% off banner, and only because I scrolled past the nonsense to find it. This one gets marked as read and forgotten, same as the last five.

## What's working

- The men's slip-in Arch Fit and the relaxed-fit Altman/Murdock sneaker in the product grid are actually shoes I'd wear — first real signal of relevance all week.
- 20% off code (UPGRADE) is a concrete, legible offer with clear terms in the fine print at the bottom.
- Layout's clean and readable on my phone without zooming — big type, one column, nothing crowded.
- Curbside pickup and "Shop Now, Pay Later" options are handy, practical details for someone who doesn't love waiting on shipping.

## What's weak

- The hero shoe, again, is the pink floral D'Lites I've never once engaged with — sixth appearance this week, and the "you weren't wrong" copy makes it worse, not better.
- "Complete Your Order" implies I have a cart. I don't. That's just false urgency dressed up as a reminder.
- No mention of arch support, cushioning, or fit anywhere near the hero — the stuff that actually gets me to buy is an afterthought.
- Grid also throws two kids' Boy's Garage Sneakers and a basketball shoe at me, none of which apply to a 62-year-old guy shopping for himself.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop sending me the same phantom-cart email under five different subject lines. Pick one honest re-engagement email a week, tops.
- 2. Lead the hero with the men's Arch Fit slip-in or the relaxed-fit walking shoe already sitting in the grid — that's the actual match, promote it, don't bury it.
- 3. Put the 20% off code up top, not scrolled past six product tiles down — that's the one thing here worth opening for.
- 4. Drop "Complete Your Order" messaging entirely when there's no real cart activity to back it up.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 20% off code is ready`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Arch Fit comfort, cozy fit styles — code UPGRADE at checkout`
