# Extra 20% off Skechers starts NOW ➡️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- Famous Footwear knows what they're doing with me — they put Skechers right in the subject line and followed it up with a clean "Take an extra 20% off" hero the second I opened. That's my brand, that's my discount, and I saw it immediately without having to hunt. No complaints about clarity there.
- That said, once I got past the hero block, this email turned into a catalog dump. Women's sandals. Kids' shoes. A Brooks pair buried down in the "Shop Skechers" section that isn't even Skechers. For a 62-year-old guy who came here for walking shoes, all that extra scrolling felt like they forgot who they sent this to. The Men's section exists, but it's squeezed between Women's and Kids like an afterthought.
- I'd mark this as read and probably come back to it — the 20% deal is real and Skechers is exactly what I'd buy — but I wouldn't forward it to a friend and I wouldn't feel any urgency to click right now. "Starts NOW" without an end date doesn't make me scramble.

## What's working

- The hero offer is impossible to miss — red border, big type, "20% off Skechers" front and center. I didn't have to decode it.
- Famous Footwear is a brand I recognize and have bought from; seeing their name in the sender field didn't make me suspicious.
- "STARTING NOW" banner at the top signals this is timely, not a recycled promo blast.
- Rewards Members are called out, which tells me they know I'm in their loyalty program.
- The layout is clean — no broken images, no overlapping text, nothing embarrassing.

## What's weak

- The preview text is a raw tracking URL. Before I even opened this, my inbox showed me a wall of gibberish instead of a reason to click. That's a bad first impression.
- Women's and Kids sections take up more real estate than Men's. I had to scroll past a lot of sandals to find anything relevant to me.
- "Starts NOW" with no visible end date — I feel zero urgency. I'll probably forget about it.
- No comfort-specific language anywhere: no cushioning, no arch support, no slip-on callout. Skechers makes all of that and none of it showed up.
- Small product thumbnails make it hard to tell what I'm looking at on a phone screen. Bigger images with a clear model name would help me decide faster.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader immediately.** Replace the URL leak with something like "Men's Skechers walkers + slip-ons included — no code needed." That one change lifts open rate by itself.
- 2. **Lead with Men's.** Put the men's walking/slip-on section directly under the hero, before Women's and Kids. I shouldn't have to scroll to find my category.
- 3. **Add comfort copy to the hero or sub-headline.** Something like "Arch support. Slip-on ease. 20% off." That's the reason I buy Skechers — say it.
- 4. **Give me a deadline.** "Offer ends Sunday midnight" would get me off the fence.
- 5. **Feature one standout men's shoe with a name and price** — not a 3×3 grid of thumbnails. I want to see the Go Walk or the Arch Fit and know what it costs after the discount.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical: 28 images missing `alt` text.**

Every content image in the email has `alt=""` (empty string), including:
- Logo: `3b6a7dc1-6955-49f8-868a-bf62a15122ee.png`
- Hero/product images: `2de4ab3e-44a2-49bf-adfd-9f129374559a.jpg`, `183c1f4a-52f4-4303-b651-0aa220d8f3d2.jpg`, and 25 others
- Animated GIF: `7a2cff4a-e501-4115-9896-9ca05608a230.gif`

Impact: the email is entirely non-functional with images disabled (common in Outlook, corporate environments) and fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.1.1. Screen readers render silence.

**HTTP mixed-content image.**

```
src="http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=ABB7InYiOjEsImQiOjQ4NzZ9..."
```

This LiveContent dynamic block is served over HTTP. Modern browsers and several email clients (Apple Mail, Outlook 2019+) will block insecure resources when the parent document is considered secure. The asset is a personalization/live-content call — if blocked, the block renders broken or blank. The domain `S7.exct.net` is Salesforce Marketing Cloud's legacy CDN; HTTPS is available and should be used.

**Duplicate image references.** `4ccff4ad-f402-43de-a027-5eb147b6f238.jpg` (a 2px-tall divider) appears 4× with `alt=""` each time. Not a breakage risk, but wasteful and reinforces the alt-text gap.

**Viewport meta missing `http-equiv` on charset.** The charset meta is:
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
It's missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Some legacy Outlook/Windows Mail parsers may not apply the charset declaration, risking mojibake on special characters (the subject line contains `➡️`).

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM Params, Landing Page Alignment)

All 31 links route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com` redirects — UTM parameters are expected to be appended at redirect resolution time. They cannot be verified without following the redirect chain, which the QA probe skipped.

**Risk:** If UTM injection at the redirect layer fails (misconfigured ESP rule, A/B test variant, expired campaign config), attribution will be lost entirely on all clicks since there are no fallback UTMs embedded in the source URLs themselves. This is an architecture-level dependency on the ESP's redirect infrastructure being correctly configured for this specific send.
