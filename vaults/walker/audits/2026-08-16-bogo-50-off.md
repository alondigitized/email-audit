# BOGO 50% OFF 🚨
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-16**
## Executive summary

- Same DSW BOGO 50% off email, just wearing "School Supply Stock-Up" as a hat this time — I've seen this exact number every single day this week now, and it's stopped meaning anything. "They're Just Soooo You" over a row of stiletto heels and open-toe sandals is the part that tells me this brand isn't actually looking at who's opening the email, they're just blasting the whole list. I did spot two pairs of New Balance sneakers way down in "Everyone Loves These," which is the first thing all week that's remotely in my lane, but it's buried past six other sections of women's dress and sandal shoes.
- The offer itself — BOGO 50%, buy a pair get the second half off — is at least clearly stated up top, no games there. But between the heels, the strappy sandals, the "shop by size" running only up to what looks like women's sizing, and the general tone, this reads like a catalog for my wife, not me. I'm not clicking, and I'm definitely not forwarding it — there's nothing here for a guy my age who wants a slip-on walking shoe with some cushion in it.

## What's working

- BOGO 50% off is stated plainly right at the top, no hunting for the catch.
- The New Balance sneakers in "Everyone Loves These" are the one section that's actually gender-neutral and in my comfort-shoe wheelhouse.
- Store locator / in-store pickup / shoe donation blurb near the bottom is a nice practical touch for someone who still likes to try shoes on.
- Layout is clean and easy to scroll on my phone, no overlapping text or broken images that I can see.

## What's weak

- "They're Just Soooo You" over stilettos and ankle-strap heels — nothing about that is soooo me.
- Every category section — heels, sandals, "something fresh" dress shoes — skews women's, with men's casual/comfort shoes nowhere near the top.
- No mention of arch support, cushioning, or hands-free comfort anywhere, which is the whole reason I'd open a shoe email.
- "Shop by Size" grid tops out where it looks like it's built for women's sizing, not mine.
- Same subject-line trick ("BOGO 50% OFF") I've now gotten close to daily this week — the cadence is wearing me out.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split the send by gender/category — lead men on comfort/walking shoes (New Balance, Skechers, that kind of thing) instead of burying them seven sections down behind heels and sandals.
- 2. Swap "They're Just Soooo You" for language that actually fits a comfort-first shopper — something about cushioning or all-day comfort, not a fashion tagline.
- 3. Move the New Balance sneaker block up near the top for anyone whose browsing history says "men's athletic/casual."
- 4. Cut the send frequency — this is the same 50% number I've seen daily; space it out or I'll start tuning it all out.
- 5. Add a line about arch support or fit for comfort-category shoes specifically.
