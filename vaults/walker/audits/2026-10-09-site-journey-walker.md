# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 4/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-10-09**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I walked through Skechers.com on my phone, and it didn't go well. The homepage and the men's shoe pages look clean, and "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Arch Fit" are right at the top where I'd want them. But I never got a shoe into a cart. Step 7 stopped on a "Please select a size" error. At step 8 the cart page was a blank white screen. The login step looked the same as the homepage, with nothing to show I was signed in. A search for "comfort shoes" gave me 1,601 results, and the first two were women's styles.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** Yesterday was 4/10, so this is a small regression. The browsing is fine, but the purchase path is broken. A blank cart page loses a sale, and I'd have gone to Hoka or Brooks.

## 3. What's Working

- **Comfort labels up top.** "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Arch Fit," and "Max Cushioning" sit right under the header. That is what I shop for.
- **Men's menu is simple.** The drawer has big, plain rows: Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections, and so on. The text is large and the rows are easy to tap.
- **"Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" tiles** are the first things on the Men's Shoes page. That matches how I shop.
- **Product page basics.** It shows the price ($80.00), a star rating, and a clearly labeled Add to Cart button.
- **Size grid.** The size buttons are big, and the sold-out size 15 is crossed out. I could tap these without trouble.
- **Offer messaging is steady.** "Extra 10% off, applied at cart" shows on every page, so what I saw was consistent.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart showed nothing.** Step 8 was a completely blank white page. I couldn't tell whether the cart was empty or still loading, and either way I'd leave. The cart badge also stayed at 0 throughout.
- **Add to Cart didn't add anything.** I pressed it without choosing a size and got a red "Please select a size" message. The size grid sits far below the photo, so I'd have to scroll to find it, and nothing guided me there. The warning only appeared after I tapped the button.
- **No sign I was logged in.** Step 3 looked just like the homepage. There was no login form and no greeting. The product page still said "Sign in for free shipping & returns!" The site didn't feel personal to me at all.
- **The popup step showed nothing.** The "Dismiss Popups" screenshot is identical to the homepage, so nothing visible happened.
- **Search results didn't match me.** "Comfort shoes" showed a women's pink sneaker and a women's wedge loafer first. I'm a 62-year-old man, and the site did nothing with that.
- **No comparison or price-vs-quality help.** I saw no "why this shoe" or "vs. Hoka/Brooks" content. I did a quick mental comparison of $80 against the other brands, and the site gave me no help with that.
- **The hero is a lifestyle photo with no message.** I saw no headline or button explaining why this shoe is for me. Image-heavy pages like this take a lot of scrolling on a phone.
- **A $80 white mesh clog** was my sample product, with only 1 review. That is thin proof for someone who cares about arch support.
- **Small things.** "Free pickup at" has a tiny toggle and a small blue "Set Location" link. The page-count text is small too.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page first.** A blank screen after Add to Cart should never happen. Show the cart contents, or at least an "Your cart is empty" message with a link back to shopping.
2. **Make size selection harder to miss.** Scroll to the size grid or highlight it when I tap Add to Cart without a size. Better yet, put a "Select size" prompt near the top of the page.
3. **Show I'm signed in.** Add a "Hi, [name]" greeting and my saved size, and bring back what I looked at last.
4. **Use men's context in search.** If I browsed Men, show men's results first.
5. **Put comfort proof on product pages.** Add arch-support and cushioning ratings, "good for walking all day," and a short note on how this compares to Hoka and Brooks.
6. **Give the hero a clear message.** Add something like "Slip into all-day comfort" with a large "Shop Slip-ins" button.
7. **Make the small text bigger.** That covers the pickup toggle, result counts, and the "details" links.

## 6. Bottom Line

I like what Skechers makes, and the menu points me the right way. But if I can't get a shoe into a cart and see it there, the rest doesn't matter. The score drops from 4 to 3 because the cart failed and I saw no sign of personalization. I'd shop elsewhere today.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The journey data is unreliable for several steps.

- **Bot mitigation interfered with the run.** The Kasada endpoint (`/…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.864`) returned HTTP 429 five times, and `/cart/` returned 429. The View Cart step is therefore probably an error or challenge page, not the real cart.
- **Homepage, Log In and Men category captured identical HTML.** All three samples are the same document at `https://www.skechers.com/`, with identical head content and `--vh`. Those steps likely never navigated, or the page was served from a challenge or SPA shell.
- **TTFB values suggest the same.** Log In and Men both read 379ms, and four later steps read 24–30ms. Those figures look like same-document or cached measurements, not real server responses.
- **LCP and CLS were not captured for any step**, so there are no real Web Vitals.
- **Console errors are only partly shown.** The report counts 24, but 10 are listed.
- **Treat the findings below as indicative.** Any fix should be re-verified once the run avoids the 429s.

## Accessibility

17 axe violations were reported: 10 on Homepage, 4 on View Cart, 3 on Search.

**Homepage**
- **Critical:** `aria-required-parent` (ARIA roles used without their required parent roles).
- **Critical:** `button-name` (buttons with no accessible name).
- **Serious:** `link-name` (links with no accessible name).
- **Moderate:** the landmark structure is broken. `landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` and `landmark-unique` suggest nested or multiple `<main>` elements. `region` means content sits outside any landmark. `heading-order` means headings skip levels.
- **Minor:** `aria-allowed-role`.

**Search "comfort shoes"**
- **Moderate:** `landmark-unique` and `region`.
- **Minor:** `aria-allowed-role`.
- The same landmark problems appear on both pages, so the cause is probably a shared template or component.

**View Cart**
- **Serious:** `document-title` (no `<title>`) and `html-has-lang` (no `lang` attribute).
- **Moderate:** `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one`.
- The homepage HTML does have `<html lang="en">`, so these four are most likely from the 429 error response, not the real cart. They need re-testing on a real cart page.

## SEO

- **Title, canonical, meta description, structured data and heading hierarchy:** Not assessable. The HTML samples are truncated before `<title>` and the meta tags.
- **View Cart:** it has no `<title>` and no `<h1>`, but this is probably the 429 page.
- **Heading order:** the homepage `heading-order` violation can also affect the document outline.

## Performance

- **Web Vitals:** LCP and CLS are unavailable for every step.
- **TTFB:** Homepage is 396ms, Log In and Men category are 379ms, View Cart is 132ms, and the other four steps are 24–30ms. The first three are acceptable (under 800ms). The 24–30ms readings are not credible as real server TTFB (see Technical Summary).
- **Third-party load:** the homepage head injects a large number of third-party scripts. They include:
  - GTM and gtag (three destinations)
  - TikTok, Pinterest, Bing, Yahoo/Yimg, Impact, Tune and RTB House pixels
  - Monetate (two scripts)
  - Quantum Metric, Pulse Insights and Bloomreach (`brsrvr`)
- **Duplicate script:** `s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` is included twice.
- **Font loader:** web fonts depend on `webfontloader.js` from the cdnjs third-party CDN. This adds a render-affecting dependency and an extra connection. The `wf-*-active` classes confirm it ran.
- **GTM preview environment in production:** `gtm.js?id=GTM-TDRBQZ&gtm_auth=…&gtm_preview=env-1&gtm_cookies_win=x` loads a non-live GTM environment. That can ship unpublished tags and bypass the normal publish workflow. Confirm whether this is intentional.
- **Inline scripts:** large inline scripts, such as the `js-ignore-click` handler, and the `truste_*` style blocks add to head weight.

## Mobile Optimization

- **Viewport:** the inline `--vh: 6.64px` implies a viewport of about 664px high, so the mobile emulation applied and the site uses a JS-computed viewport-height variable.
- **Meta viewport, tap targets and responsive images:** not assessable from the truncated HTML.
- **Accessible names:** the unnamed buttons and links (`button-name`, `link-name`) are likely to affect mobile screen-reader users.
- **Script weight:** the 15+ third-party scripts add main-thread cost on mid-range mobile CPUs. This is likely but unmeasured, since there is no LCP or INP data.

## Console & Network Errors

**Network (6 errors)**
- **Kasada:** five 429 responses on `/…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.864`. The automation run is being rate-limited or challenged by Kasada, so this is probably a test-environment artifact, not a user-facing defect.
- **Cart:** a 429 on `https://www.skechers.com/cart/`. This blocked a valid cart render and invalidates the View Cart results.

**Console (10 listed of 24)**
- **429 resource failures:** three entries that match the network errors above.
- **`Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document`:** four entries. A script uses `unload`, which is blocked by Permissions-Policy and deprecated. It is most likely from a third-party tag. Moving it to `pagehide` would also keep back/forward cache eligibility.
- **`Unsupported Summarizer API languages…`:** two entries. This is the browser's built-in Summarizer AI API called with unsupported languages. It is probably from a third-party script or browser extension, and the source needs identifying.

## Recommendations

1. **Re-run the audit without bot mitigation interference.** Use an allow-listed IP or header, or a slower cadence. Verify that each step actually navigates, with distinct HTML per step, and capture LCP, CLS and INP.
2. **Fix the landmark structure.** Use a single top-level `<main>`, give duplicate landmarks unique `aria-label`s, and put page content inside landmarks.
3. **Add accessible names** to icon-only buttons and links. Fix the ARIA parent/child roles and the heading order.
4. **Re-test the cart page** for `<title>`, `lang`, `<main>` and `<h1>` once it loads without a 429.
5. **Check the GTM `gtm_preview=env-1` / `gtm_auth` embed** and remove it from production if it is unintended.
6. **Remove the duplicate `ytc.js` load.** Audit the third-party tag set and defer non-essential pixels until after load or consent.
7. **Replace the `unload` listeners** (identify which script registers them) with `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`.
8. **Self-host or preload the web fonts** to remove the cdnjs `webfontloader` dependency.
