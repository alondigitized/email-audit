# New Deals for Dad: 30% off Skechers, Nike & Jordan
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- Father's Day timing, Skechers right there in the subject line — I opened this one faster than most. The "Best DAD ever!" hero is corny, but I'm the target and I know it. I'm not above a little corniness if the deal is real, and 30% off Skechers qualifies as real. That's the brand I'm actively watching, so this landed squarely in my wheelhouse.
- The Skechers section is where my eyes went — three or four pairs of white and gray walkers, sale badge, clean layout. That part works. Then the Jordan block shows up and I mentally check out. Jordan shoes are for my son, not for me. I'm a comfort walker, not a sneakerhead. Half this email is talking to somebody else.
- What nearly derailed the whole thing before I even opened it: the preview text is a raw tracking URL. Just a big ugly link sitting in my inbox. Not a good look. I still opened it because the subject named Skechers, but that preheader killed any goodwill before I tapped.
- I'd probably click through to the Skechers section. I wouldn't forward this — the Jordan half would confuse anyone I sent it to.

## What's working

- **Skechers is named in the subject** — that's the one brand that gets me to open without overthinking it.
- **30% off is concrete** — not "up to" weasel language, just a clean round number in the hero and the section badge.
- **Tiered dollar-off stacking** — the $10 off $49, $15 off $99 banner with code SAVEMORE adds real value on top of the 30%, and the code is visible without hunting.
- **No Inflation Relief banner** — after seeing that red-and-yellow block for what felt like six weeks straight, its absence is a relief. Email reads cleaner.
- **Shoe Perks reward section** — the FREE $10 reward with a barcode at the bottom is a solid loyalty closer. I noticed it and I'm a member.

## What's weak

- **Preview text is a broken tracking URL** — "Shoe Carnival https://click.email.shoecarnival.com/..." sitting right there in my inbox is embarrassing. That's the first thing I see before I even decide to open.
- **Jordan section is dead weight for me** — 30% off Jordan is a different shopper. I'm not buying $130 Jordans at 62. That block pushes the Skechers content down and dilutes the message.
- **No comfort cues in the Skechers block** — I see the shoes but I get no copy about arch support, cushioning, or walking comfort. Those are the words that make me click. "Sale" alone isn't enough.
- **"Best DAD ever!" hero skews young** — the phrasing and the image feel like they're aimed at a 35-year-old buying for his dad, not at me, the dad doing his own shopping.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader immediately** — this is a broken deploy, not a creative problem. A leaked tracking URL costs opens. Any real copy — even "30% off Skechers + $10 off $49 with SAVEMORE" — beats what's there.
- 2. **Split the audience or section-title for me** — if Jordan has to stay, call the Skechers block something like "For the Walking Dad" and lead with comfort language. Give me a reason to keep scrolling past the hero.
- 3. **Add one comfort word per Skechers shoe** — "Memory Foam," "Slip-Resistant," "Wide Width Available" — anything that speaks to my actual decision criteria. Right now it's just image + price.
- 4. **Father's Day hero copy tweak** — reframe it from buying for dad to dad buying for himself. "Treat yourself, Dad" or "You've earned comfortable feet" lands better with me as the buyer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dad Deal: 30% off Skechers ends July 1`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Walker Miles, 30% off Skechers this Father's Day`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `30% off Skechers + $10 off $49 with code SAVEMORE — good through 7/1`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your Shoe Perks $10 reward is waiting — plus 30% off Skechers today`
