# It's live: save $30 on orders $100+
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- Alright — finally, adidas sent me something I can actually do the math on. "$30 off a $100 order." I understand that. I can hold that number in my head while I'm scrolling. I've been wading through World Cup jerseys, soccer cleats, and a pet accessories email for the better part of two weeks, so a straight savings offer is honestly a relief. "SPEND LESS GET MORE" is blunt — a little corporate — but at least nobody's asking me to pick sides between a midfielder and a striker.
- That said, once I get past the headline, I'm staring at a dark hero image with no product in it, a discount code, and a "SHOP NOW" button that dumps me into the full adidas catalog. For a guy who's been half-looking for a comfortable walking shoe, that's like handing me a $30 coupon and pointing at a warehouse. The savings are real. The direction is not.
- I'd probably click — $30 is $30 and I've got a shoe on my mental shortlist — but I'm not forwarding this to anyone and I'm not excited about it. It goes in the "deal, maybe later" pile, which usually means I miss the window.

## What's working

- **The offer is legible at arm's length.** "$30 off orders $100+" with code DEAL — no asterisk-hunting, no fine-print decoder needed. Two seconds on a phone screen.
- **Visual hierarchy does its job.** Big dark hero with oversized white type, offer copy below it. Eye knew exactly where to land.
- **The discount code is right in the body.** "Use code DEAL at checkout" — I don't have to dig or copy-paste from somewhere else. That's how it should always work.
- **Clean render, no broken pieces.** Every element loaded. That sounds basic but after some of the pet-merch sends earlier this month, I notice when an email just... works.
- **"SPEND LESS GET MORE" lands for my generation.** I'm not here for the lifestyle narrative. I want value. This says it plainly.

## What's weak

- **Zero product imagery.** The hero is a dark background and white letters. I have no idea what I'm shopping for. A $30 discount on a FIFA jersey I'll never wear is still a waste of a click.
- **"SHOP NOW" sends me nowhere useful.** A full catalog is a maze. At my age, wading through 400 items isn't browsing — it's a reason to give up and close the app.
- **No comfort or walking shoe angle.** This was a golden moment to say "here's a deal, here's what fits guys like you." They said nothing. Just like the Adizero send on June 7 — a shoe that was almost right but with no hook for me specifically.
- **No expiration date in the body.** "It's live" in the subject sounds urgent, but the email doesn't tell me when it dies. That turns "act now" energy into "I'll come back to this," which I never do.
- **Fine print is a wall.** There's a dense block of tiny legalese above the footer that makes me wonder what the exclusions actually are. One line and a link would do the same job.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put one shoe in the hero.** A cushioned walking sneaker or a slip-on — something a man my age might actually buy. The discount is the hook; a product is the reason to click with intent.
- 2. **Change the CTA to a category.** "SHOP MEN'S WALKING SHOES" or even "SHOP MEN'S COMFORT" is something I can act on. "SHOP NOW" is noise.
- 3. **Put the offer deadline in the body.** Even "Offer ends Sunday night" would move me. I've lost deals because the email didn't remind me the clock was running.
- 4. **Collapse the fine print.** "Exclusions apply — see site for details." One line, hyperlinked. The paragraph of 8-point type above the footer kills the momentum.
- 5. **Segment toward comfort footwear.** After weeks of soccer content, adidas clearly isn't sending me targeted emails. This discount could have said "browse men's walking and training shoes" and cost them nothing extra to set up.

### 1. Technical Summary

Two QA-confirmed failures: a broken click-tracking URL resolving to a relative path, and a completely absent plain-text alternative. Several HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources will be blocked silently by most modern email clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken click-redirect URL**
The QA probe reports `unknown url type: '/g/1c9d9603-5323-4bf8-b044-a0629eb7ddc9?txn=b6ff3200-...'` — the redirect target is a relative path, not an absolute URL. The wrapper click-tracking URL is:

```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/1c9d9603-5323-4bf8-b044-a0629eb7ddc9?cp_tp=v3.eJz...
```

The ESP's click-redirect service is returning a relative `/g/` path instead of the final destination URL. Any subscriber clicking this link lands on a broken or empty page.

**[WARN] 29 tracking links unverifiable**
All 29 click-redirect links route through `click.comms.adidas.com`. HTTP probing was skipped; destination URLs and UTM parameters cannot be confirmed without manual inspection or a click-redirect unfurl pass.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[FAIL] HTTP image sources — blocked by most clients**
The same arrow icon is referenced 6× over HTTP:

```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```

Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail proxy or block mixed-content HTTP images. This asset will render as a broken image for the majority of recipients.

**[FAIL] HTTP open/read tracking pixel**
```
http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019edd9c72f1-4b7a8943-e0a1-416e-9b26-d413d642eae8-000000/...
```
The open-tracking pixel is served over HTTP. Clients that block HTTP content will suppress this pixel, inflating non-open metrics and skewing deliverability scoring.

**[WARN] Two images missing `alt` text**
- `1c9d9603-5323-4bf8-b044-a0629eb7ddc9` (hero or primary image via `dv.adidas.com`)
- `yDUa0XB4JG7UMaqjv1WogMaNJ5tB1aavvvAxvj2RVd0=452` (the tracking pixel via `click.comms.adidas.com`)

Absent `alt` text on content images fails WCAG 2.1 AA and degrades image-blocked rendering in Outlook.

**[WARN] Malformed charset `<meta>` tag**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. The correct form is:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, this tag is a no-op; charset declaration falls back to the `Content-Type` header only.

**[WARN] Duplicate `@font-face` declarations**
`AdihausDIN` (400, 700) and `AdineuePRO` (400, 600) are declared identically across two separate `<style>` blocks. The second block is redundant; it adds parse overhead without adding any new variant. Low severity but indicates a template assembly issue.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM / Deliverability)
