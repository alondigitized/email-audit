# Train hard. Play hard in Nike.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- I've been getting Famous Footwear emails almost every single day this week — clearance BOGO, back-to-school, "last call" on the same deal I've seen four times already. So when a Nike performance email drops with "Train hard. Play hard" as the hero line, my first thought is: who is this for? I'm 62. My knees would laugh at "train hard." I'm looking for a shoe that doesn't make me think about my feet by noon, not a court sneaker for somebody's pickup game.
- That said, I noticed the 30% off Nike down the scroll and the "25% off Clearance" block near the bottom with a code. That clearance call-to-action is the only part of this email that actually speaks to how I shop. Clearance + real discount = where I find my everyday walkers. But Famous Footwear buried it at the bottom of an athletic hype email aimed at people half my age.
- I'd mark this read and move on. I wouldn't click the Nike performance stuff. If I was bored and in a bargain mood, I might tap the clearance section — but honestly the preview text alone nearly killed it before I got here.

## What's working

- **30% off Nike is a real number.** I don't have to do math or hunt for fine print — a concrete discount is visible.
- **25% off Clearance with code W36** at the bottom is exactly how I shop. Price comes first, brand second.
- **Clean layout.** No overlapping text, no broken images. Easy to scroll on my phone without accidentally tapping the wrong thing.
- **"SHOP MEN" button is right there.** I don't have to dig through women's and kids' tabs to find my section.
- **3X points visible.** I'm a rewards member and I like knowing when I'm earning more. That's a nudge.

## What's weak

- **"Train hard. Play hard." is not my life.** The hero line is written for a 28-year-old who cross-trains. I want a shoe that makes my afternoon walk easier on my arches, not one that's endorsed for the court and playground.
- **The preview text is a raw tracking URL.** In my inbox it looked like a phishing link — that almost made me hit delete before I even opened it.
- **Every image I can see skews younger and female.** The models and shoe silhouettes in that grid lean athletic and feminine. I didn't see a single shot that looked like my feet or my life.
- **The clearance deal is buried.** The best offer for someone like me is stacked below a full Nike athletic showcase. A lot of Boomer shoppers won't scroll that far.
- **Seventh or eighth Famous Footwear email this week.** I'm starting to go numb. When every send has a deal and a promo code, none of them feel special anymore.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the clearance offer in the hero, not Nike performance.** If the 25% off clearance matters, put it up top where I see it without scrolling. The Nike hype can be a secondary module.
- 2. **Fix the preview text — immediately.** A raw tracking URL in the inbox preview is a trust-killer. It looks like spam or a phishing attempt. Even "Shop Nike + 25% off clearance starting now" is infinitely better.
- 3. **Swap the hero copy for something that talks to comfort walkers.** "Nike cushioning you can feel all day" lands better for me than "Train hard. Play hard."
- 4. **Rotate in at least one men's lifestyle shoe image.** Air Max 90, a Pegasus, a walking-friendly silhouette. Give me visual proof this email is for me, not just a kid at a gym.
- 5. **Add a deadline to the clearance offer.** "Starting now" tells me it's open-ended. I'll think about it later and never come back. Give me a day — "through Sunday" — and I might tap tonight.

- **Subject Alt B:** `30% off Nike. Plus, clearance is down another 25%.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop men's Nike styles and take an extra 25% off 3,000+ clearance items with code W36.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Extra 25% off clearance styles — use code W36 at checkout. Comfort picks from Nike, too.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally functional but carries a high density of accessibility failures (21 images without alt text) and a deliverability-risk plain-text body. One invalid CSS property was detected inline, and destination URLs behind the click-redirect domain could not be validated by automated probes.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text URL density — HIGH RISK**
QA reports 6,888 of 9,267 plain-text characters are URLs (74%). This exceeds the 70% spam-filter threshold and may trigger Bayesian filters on volume senders.

**28 tracked links unvalidated**
All outbound links wrap through `click.email.famousfootwear.com`. QA skipped HTTP probes on all 28, so broken destinations cannot be ruled out. Manual spot-checking is required before send.

**Open pixel present**
`https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/open.aspx?IY32NCWDOZEUREZ5SOYMJ5ZCMY.70247&d=70247&bmt=0` — standard 1×1 pixel, correctly placed after `<body>`.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**21 images missing alt text (QA confirmed)**
Every product/content image has `alt=""` or no `alt` attribute. Decorative spacers (e.g. `4ccff4ad-f402-43de-a027-5eb147b6f238.jpg`, a 2px-high divider) can legitimately use `alt=""`, but linked images and product shots must have descriptive alt text for screen readers and image-blocking environments. The logo image (`3b6a7dc1-6955-49f8-868a-bf62a15122ee.png`) is wrapped in a link with `alt=""` — this leaves the link with no accessible label.

**Invalid CSS property — typo**
```html
style="...lineapacing:20px..."
```
`lineapacing` is not a valid CSS property (likely intended `line-height` or `letter-spacing`). The rule silently drops in all clients.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
Blocks user zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). iOS 10+ overrides this for accessibility, but it remains a compliance flag.

**`<meta>` charset tag malformed**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Without it, the charset declaration is ignored. Clients that don't inherit charset from the MIME header may misrender special characters.

**HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype**
```html
<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN" ...>
```
Triggers quirks mode in some clients. Modern email best practice is to omit the doctype entirely or use HTML5 (`<!DOCTYPE html>`), though this is low-risk in practice for most major clients.
