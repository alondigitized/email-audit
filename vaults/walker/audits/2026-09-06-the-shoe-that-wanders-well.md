# The Shoe That Wanders Well
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-06**
## Executive summary

- Well, would you look at that — Crocs actually made a shoe I'd wear. The Echo Element looks like a real hiking or trail sneaker, not a foam clog with a strap. Toggle bungee, rubber outsole, cushioned mesh upper — that's the language I understand, that's what I look for when I'm comparing against my Hoka or New Balance walkers. Problem is, there's no price anywhere on this thing. No "% off," no dollar figure, nothing. It's a nice-looking product launch, but it doesn't ask me to do anything except "Shop."
- I'd actually click through on this one, mostly out of curiosity about what it costs, but I wouldn't forward it to a buddy — there's nothing urgent or "hey look at this deal" to pass along, just a moodier version of a shoe ad. Compare that to the "Get Him a New Favorite Shoe" send from 2026-09-02 with the camo boat shoe — similar idea, actual shoe for a guy like me, but at least that one felt like it was pitching a gift. This one's just brand image-building.

## What's working

- The hero shoe is a legit outdoor-style slip-on with real tread and coverage — this is the first Crocs email in a while I'd consider for my own feet, not my grandkids'.
- The diagram callouts (cushioned mesh upper, toggle bungee, rubber outsole) speak directly to fit and traction, which matters more to me than any charm or logo.
- Clean layout, big readable type, no clutter — easy to read on my phone without zooming.
- The model's outfit and setting (rocky trail, boots) at least suggests an adult, outdoorsy buyer rather than a teenager.

## What's weak

- Not one price or percentage anywhere in this email — I have no idea if this is a $60 shoe or a $150 shoe, and that's the first thing I want to know.
- No clear single CTA — four shop buttons (Clogs, Jibbitz, Sale, Sandals) at the bottom, and none of them say "Shop Echo Element," so I'd have to go hunting.
- "Built to Roam" and the squiggly line graphic feel like ad-agency flourish; I just want to know if it fits my arch and what it costs.
- Nothing here acknowledges I'm a repeat shopper — no loyalty mention, no "you bought clogs before" personalization.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price or "starting at $X" right under the hero headline — that's the single biggest gap keeping me from clicking with intent.
- 2. Make the primary button say "Shop Echo Element" and put it right after the diagram, not buried in a four-button grid at the bottom.
- 3. Add a line about comfort specs that matter to guys my age — arch support, cushioning level, true-to-size fit — since that's the actual competitive battleground against Hoka and Brooks.
- 4. Drop or shrink the Jibbitz/Sale/Sandals buttons on this send; they dilute the one product you're trying to sell me.
