# Want $10 off? Tap for deets.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-25**
## Executive summary

- Eight — or nine, I've genuinely lost count — DSW birthday emails in a week, and here comes Thursday with "Tap for deets." I had to read that twice. "Deets." That's not my word, that's not my kid's word, that's a word I hear on TV and immediately change the channel. The $10 off landed fine; the slang wrapped around it made me feel like I accidentally wandered into a different store.
- Once I got inside the email, the tiered offer is actually solid — $10 off $49, $20 off $99, $60 off $199 — easy math, clear stacking. But then I scroll down and hit the size chart: 5, 5.5, 6, 6.5… and a button that says "SHOP WIDE CALF." Wide calf. That's a women's boot measurement. At that point the email stopped talking to me entirely. I'm a size 10.5 wide men's, and nobody in this send asked me that.
- I'd mark it read. I wouldn't click. I definitely wouldn't forward it — a buddy my age would have the same "who is this for?" reaction.

## What's working

- **Tiered offer is immediately readable.** Three tiers, stacked vertically, dollar amounts in big type — even squinting at a phone I can do that math in two seconds.
- **DSW is a brand I recognize and trust** — the logo at top left is clear, so I didn't think twice about opening.
- **The party invite is a real differentiator.** Saturday July 27, 11AM–6PM, free tote with donation — that's a concrete, human reason to show up in-store. More memorable than another coupon code.
- **No broken images or layout carnage.** Everything rendered cleanly; the hierarchy reads top to bottom without fighting itself.
- **VIP badge and $20 off for members is visible** — if I were already VIP, that would land.

## What's weak

- **"Tap for deets" is not how a 62-year-old reads his email.** That subject line belongs in a Snapchat, not my inbox.
- **The size grid and "SHOP WIDE CALF" button locked me out.** Sizes 5–11 with a wide-calf filter is a women's shoe section. I'm a man shopping for comfort sneakers; this section has nothing for me.
- **The product grid "So New, So You" shows shoes I can't identify at thumbnail size** — and whatever they are, none of them say cushioning, arch support, or men's casual to me.
- **Ninth birthday email in a week.** Cadence fatigue is real. Even a good offer loses impact when I've already deleted eight identical ones. The July 22 "FIRST DIBS ENDS TONIGHT" email was the high-water mark; everything since has felt like an echo.
- **Asterisk on $60 off $199\*** — I can see it, I can't read what it says on mobile, and that makes me nervous about whether the deal actually applies to anything I want.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the slang, add a men's hook.** Rewrite the subject around the offer without "deets." If this email is even partially going to men, the subject should sound like it.
- 2. **Drop a men's comfort product thumbnail above the fold.** One Skechers Arch Fit, one New Balance, anything with a cushion visual — gives me a reason to keep scrolling.
- 3. **Retire the wide-calf size grid for this send or segment it out entirely.** Walker doesn't need to see a women's filter. It signals the email wasn't built for him.
- 4. **Put the party date in the subject or preview.** "July 27 in-store" is a real, time-bounded reason to care — burying it below the fold wastes it.
- 5. **Explain the asterisk** — or remove the $60 tier if it's too restricted to be useful. Leaving me guessing is worse than not offering it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$10 off men's shoes — birthday sale, today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `DSW birthday: $60 off when you spend $199`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stack the savings — more you spend, more you save. Sale ends soon.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Birthday tiers: $10, $20, or $60 off. No guessing, no gimmicks.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`Src:` (capital S) in all 10 `@font-face` declarations** — Invalid CSS property; the correct keyword is lowercase `src:`. Apple Mail (the primary client that honors web fonts in email) may silently skip loading all custom fonts (Futura PT and HelveticaNow families), causing full fallback to system fonts.
- Affected declarations: all 10 `@font-face` blocks in the first `<style>` block
- Fix: lowercase `src:` in every block

**Conflicting `text-size-adjust` values** — The reset CSS sets both `-webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%` and `text-size-adjust: none` on the same `*` selector. The `none` value disables all text scaling, which conflicts with the `100%` intent and fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) on platforms that honor the unprefixed property.

**Missing `http-equiv` on charset meta** — `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` is missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Without it, some older Outlook/Lotus clients may not recognize the encoding declaration.

**Missing `http-equiv` on IE compatibility meta** — `<meta content="IE=Edge" />` is missing `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`. Inert without the attribute.

**Alt text** — Cannot confirm from truncated source. Must verify all `<img>` tags carry non-empty `alt` attributes.
