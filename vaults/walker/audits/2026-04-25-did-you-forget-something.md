# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-25**
## Executive summary

- **From:** SKECHERS | **Subject:** Did you forget something?
- Well, they got my attention — I must've left something in my cart and they're nudging me to finish up. The hero image shows a pair of Slip-ins, which is exactly my style, and 20% off a single pair is the kind of deal that makes me actually think twice. But then I scrolled down and saw a grid full of Women's shoes — Women's this, Women's that. I'm a 62-year-old man. Why are you showing me women's sneakers? That sloppy recommendation block nearly killed all the goodwill the hero built up. This email had one job — remind me what I left behind and get me back to the cart — and it half-did it.

## What's working

- **The hero is on point.** That light blue Glide-Step Atlas Slip-in front and center is exactly the kind of shoe I left in that cart. It's clean, it's big enough to see on my phone, and the "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" button in orange is hard to miss.
- **Dual CTA placement.** The button shows up right under the hero image *and* again after the product name. Good — I sometimes scroll past the first one.
- **20% off is real money.** That banner jumps out. I'm a Plus member, I notice discounts. This one's clearly communicated, and the "SHOP NOW" link is right there.
- **"COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" messaging is direct.** No cutesy language. Tells me exactly what they want me to do. I appreciate that.

## What's weak

- **Women's shoes everywhere below the hero.** This is the biggest problem. Four out of five product tiles in the "Shop All Best Sellers" block are labeled Women's. I'm a man. This looks like they grabbed a generic product grid and dropped it in without checking whose cart they're following up on. It feels careless — like the email wasn't written for me at all.
- **The email is too long.** After the core recovery message, I'm seeing App download, curbside pickup, buy now pay later, Find a Store, follow us on social... I'm 62, not a digital native. All this noise pushes the point further down and makes me work harder than I should.
- **Fine print at the bottom.** There's a "Spring Fling Sale" block of dense legal text at the very bottom. I'm not reading that on my phone. But if there are restrictions on the 20% off, I'd want to know before I click — not discover them at checkout.
- **No mention of my Plus membership.** I'm a loyal Skechers Plus member. This email doesn't acknowledge that at all. A quick "As a Plus member, here's your 20% off" would've made this feel personal instead of mass-blast.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the product grid.** If my cart has a men's Slip-in, recommend more men's shoes below — not women's. A 62-year-old man browsing Men's > Slip-ins should never see four Women's tiles in a recovery email.
- 2. **Lead with the cart item more explicitly.** Show me *exactly* what I left behind — product name, color, my size if they have it — so it jogs my memory. Right now the hero shoe is generic-looking; I want to see "your" item.
- 3. **Trim the utility modules.** Cut the App download, Curbside, and Pay Later blocks from cart recovery emails. That's noise. Save them for welcome or browse emails.
- 4. **Acknowledge my membership.** Even one line — "Your Skechers Plus member discount is waiting" — would've made me feel like a known customer, not a cold lead.
- 5. **Mention the discount's expiration.** 20% off is great, but how long does it last? Adding a deadline ("Offer ends Sunday") would push me to act today instead of later.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Skechers "Did you forget something?" (Cart Abandonment)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard Attentive-powered cart abandonment email. Two confirmed infrastructure issues (HTTP redirect URLs, malformed charset declaration) and one compliance gap confirmed in the visible source; the truncated HTML prevents full compliance verification.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**HTTP redirect domain — all CTAs** (confirmed)
Every link uses `http://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=...` — plain HTTP, not HTTPS. The initial hop from the email client is unencrypted, exposing the tracking token and any appended parameters. Attentive should be configured to issue HTTPS redirect URLs.

Example: `href="http://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=u001.LNc6Vo..."`

**UTM parameters** — not confirmed present
The raw source links contain no `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, or `utm_campaign` values. Attentive may inject them post-redirect, but that cannot be verified from the HTML. If Attentive is not configured to append UTMs, GA4/attribution will log these as direct traffic.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` element**
`<title></title>` is blank. Screen readers announce the document title; some email clients (Samsung Mail, Apple Mail) surface it. Should be set to something like "Skechers — Complete Your Purchase."

**Malformed charset meta tag**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Without that attribute the tag is a no-op — no charset is declared. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Older Outlook and Lotus Notes builds will fall back to their default encoding, which can corrupt non-ASCII characters.

**Link styling suppressed globally**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This removes underlines from all body links, which fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 (use of color alone to distinguish links). Affects Gmail web rendering where this selector applies.

**Preheader inflation technique**
The preheader uses Unicode fill characters (`U+034F COMBINING GRAPHEME JOINER` × 150+) and soft-hyphens (`&shy;` × 200+) to prevent inbox preview bleed-through. This is an industry-standard technique but adds ~600 bytes of noise and can misfire on some Asian-language email clients that interpret combining marks differently.

**Image alt text** — cannot confirm from truncated source. All `<img>` tags in the visible rows should carry descriptive `alt` attributes; the logo block at row-2 should have `alt="Skechers"`.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unrendered merge token syntax (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `%%first_name%%`) is visible in the truncated HTML — positive signal. Cannot confirm cart product tokens (product name, image, price) are rendering correctly from the truncated portion; those blocks should be spot-checked against a seed address with a known abandoned cart.

---

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM / Unsubscribe)

**Cannot confirm from truncated HTML:**
- Physical mailing address (CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5)) — required, typically in the footer
- Unsubscribe link/mechanism (CAN-SPAM §5(a)(3)) — required to honor within 10 business days

Both must be present in the non-truncated footer. Flag for manual verification.

**Sender domain authentication** — not verifiable from HTML source alone; requires header inspection. `hello@msgs.skechers.com` uses the `msgs` subdomain — confirm SPF, DKIM (`d=msgs.skechers.com` or aligned), and DMARC policy (`p=quarantine` or `reject`) are in place for this subdomain specifically.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

UTM coverage is unconfirmed (see §2). If Attentive does append UTMs at redirect time, verify the parameter set includes at minimum:
- `utm_source=attentive`
- `utm_medium=email`
- `utm_campaign=cart_abandonment`

The "web version" link in row-1 also routes through the Attentive click tracker — confirm the hosted version renders without broken personalization tokens if the email is opened without prior session context.
