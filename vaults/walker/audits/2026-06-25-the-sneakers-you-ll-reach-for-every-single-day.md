# The sneakers you'll reach for every single day
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- Alright, I open this one and there it is again — that same wall of raw code garbage in the preview, same as it's been every single Skechers email I've gotten this month. I've lost count, but according to my own memory it's at least eleven straight. At this point I'm starting to wonder if anyone over there actually looks at what lands in my inbox before they hit send.
- But once I'm inside the email, it cleans right up. "EFFORTLESS CASUALS — HOURS OF COMFORT" — that got my attention immediately, because that's exactly what I'm shopping for. Throw in a BOGO 50% off right at the top and now you're speaking my language. The email flows well: big clear sections for Women and Men, a "Made to Move" stretch for the more active stuff, and a 25% off apparel kicker at the bottom. Decent structure, good offer visibility.
- My honest call: I'd click through to the Men's Casuals. The BOGO is real money and "hours of comfort" is exactly what I need for a shoe I'm going to put on and forget about. I wouldn't forward it — nothing in here is so good I'd bother my brother with it — but I'd click.

## What's working

- **BOGO 50% off is front and center.** That's the single best thing in the email — I see the deal before I even read the hero copy.
- **"Hours of comfort" is the right phrase for me.** That's what I'm buying a shoe for. Whoever wrote that line understands what comfort-first shoppers are actually weighing.
- **Men's section has its own real estate.** "SHOP MEN'S CASUALS" button is clear, unambiguous, and gets me right to what I want.
- **"Made to Move" section extends the browse.** If I'd already seen the casuals section before, this gives me another reason to scroll and consider something different.
- **No broken images or layout chaos.** The body renders cleanly — text is legible, sections are distinct, the layout holds together on my phone screen.

## What's weak

- **That preview text is still broken JSON. Eleven emails in a row now.** This one reads "[{ "@context": "http://schema.org/"…" before it cuts off. I've watched this go unfixed since early June and at this point it doesn't feel like an accident — it feels like nobody's watching the store.
- **Women's section comes first and is bigger.** I'm a 62-year-old man. I don't need to scroll past a women's fashion spread to get to the section that's for me. Flip the order for a list segment that skews male.
- **Subject line has no offer in it.** "The sneakers you'll reach for every single day" is fine sentiment, but I don't know there's a BOGO waiting until I open. The best thing about this email is buried in the preview slot that's already ruined by broken code.
- **No loyalty acknowledgment.** I'm a Skechers Plus member. Other recent sends — like the one on June 18th with "it's your month" — at least made a nod to that relationship. This one treats me like a cold contact off the street.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text. This is the only thing that matters right now.** Eleven consecutive broken previews is not a glitch — it's a process failure. Patch it before the next send. Everything else in this list is secondary.
- 2. **Put the BOGO in the subject line.** The deal is the strongest hook in the email and it's invisible until you open. Move it forward.
- 3. **Flip the section order for men's segments.** Women → Men makes sense for a general send, but on a male-skewing list, lead with Men's Casuals.
- 4. **Add a Skechers Plus acknowledgment.** One line — "As a Plus member, your BOGO applies today" — turns a broadcast into a member moment.

- **Preheader Alt B:** `Comfort that kicks in and stays all day. BOGO 50% off ends soon.`

Two `<meta>` tags are also malformed due to missing `http-equiv` attributes.

```html

```
No `http://` or `https://` prefix. Email clients treat this as a relative URL path — the pixel never loads. Inbox placement monitoring data is absent for this entire send.
