# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-10-17**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I'm Walker. I want something slip-on, cushioned and arch-supported, and I want to find it without squinting. Skechers' mobile site is closer to that than it looks. The top nav says "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Arch Fit" and "Max Cushioning," and the Men's Shoes page opens with "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" tiles. That is how I shop.

Then the journey fell apart where it counts. My Add to Cart tap did nothing visible, because I hadn't picked a size and the page only said so in small red text. The cart page came up as a blank white screen. The cart badge read 0 at every step. Search for "comfort shoes" led with women's shoes. The login step showed me the homepage with no sign-in form and nothing personalized. I'd have stopped at the cart and gone to Brooks or Hoka.

**Compared to yesterday (3/10):** The score holds at about 3/10. I only have yesterday's number, not its screenshots, so I can't call out specific regressions or improvements. Nothing I saw today was a clear step up.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** The browse and product pages are decent, but the checkout path is broken in my view and the cart screen is empty. A shopper who gets this far and sees a white page leaves, and that is lost revenue.

## 3. What's Working

- **The comfort vocabulary matches my needs.** The tabs read "Slip-ins | Wide Fit | Arch Fit | Max Cus…", so I can see right away that this is a comfort brand.
- **The Men's menu is simple.** The slide-out drawer (step 4) has big, well-spaced rows: Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections. The text is bold and legible, and there is a clear Back button and X. This is easy on a 62-year-old thumb.
- **Men's Shoes lands me on my categories.** "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" are the first two image tiles. Filter, Sort by, Size and Width sit in one row. Width matters to me, so that's good.
- **The product page is clear.** The title is "Skechers Slip-ins: Contour Foam – Cozy Fit," with a 4.5-star rating and 1,933 reviews. The $88.00 price is large and easy to read. The Hands-Free Slip-ins badge is visible. The "From $15/month" payment option with Klarna and Afterpay is spelled out.
- **The size grid is easy to tap.** The size boxes are large, and sold-out sizes are struck through. The Add to Cart button is wide and high-contrast.
- **The deals are visible.** "20% OFF Sitewide" and "2 Pairs for $100" are in the top banner, but I don't let a discount make the decision.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart screen is blank (step 8).** I saw only white, with no items, no empty-cart message and no header. That's the worst thing on the journey. I can't tell if the page is broken or just slow.
- **Add to Cart didn't work, and the site didn't help (step 7).** I hadn't chosen a size. The only feedback was a small red "Please select a size" warning. The cart count stayed at 0. The size grid doesn't scroll into view or flash. On a phone the warning is easy to miss.
- **The size picker is far down the page.** At the top of the product page I see the price and title, then a long scroll before I reach size and Add to Cart. The size, quantity and Add to Cart area is a second screen's worth of scrolling.
- **The login step shows nothing (step 3).** The screenshot is the same homepage as steps 1 and 2, with no sign-in form. I can't say whether login worked. If it did, nothing showed it: no name, no greeting, no "welcome back." It didn't feel personalized.
- **The popup step is also identical to the homepage (step 2).** Either there was nothing to dismiss, or the screenshot didn't capture it.
- **Search "comfort shoes" led with women's shoes.** The first two results were labeled "Women's" (a pink sneaker and a black wedge loafer). I'm a man shopping for comfort, and it should know that, or at least offer a Men's/Women's choice up front. 1,612 results is also a lot with no obvious narrowing.
- **The homepage hero is lifestyle photography.** The couple on the couch looks nice, but it says nothing about comfort, arch support or price. The nav tabs are cut off at the screen edge ("Max Cus…"), so I wouldn't know to swipe sideways.
- **The text is small in places.** The "2 Pairs for $100" strip, the Klarna line, and the "Applied at Cart" fine print are all small. I'd need to zoom.
- **There's no comparison or price-vs-quality content.** I didn't see any "how does this stack up against Hoka, Brooks or New Balance" help, or any cost-per-wear math. I'm cross-shopping, and the site gives me nothing to help me stay.
- **"20% OFF… Applied at Cart" is misleading when the cart is blank.** I can't verify the promise if I can't reach a cart that shows it.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page first.** It must always render something: items and totals, or "Your cart is empty" with a link back to shopping. A blank white page should never ship.
2. **Make a failed Add to Cart obvious.** If no size is chosen, scroll to the size grid, highlight it, and show a larger error. Better, let me add to cart and ask for the size on the next screen.
3. **Move size and Add to Cart higher on the product page,** or add a sticky "Add to Cart" bar at the bottom of the screen.
4. **Default search and category pages to my gender once I'm in the Men's section.** Add a clear Men/Women toggle at the top of results. Surface "Slip-ins" and "Arch Fit" as quick filters for "comfort shoes."
5. **Show login success.** Show a greeting or name in the header after signing in. Personalize the homepage with slip-ins and wide-fit styles if I've browsed them.
6. **Put comfort and arch support in the homepage hero.** Add a plain-language line such as "Arch support, cushioning and hands-free slip-ins," and make sure the nav tabs show they scroll.
7. **Add a simple "Skechers vs. Hoka / Brooks / New Balance" comfort guide and a cost-per-wear callout.** Even a short one would help someone like me decide.
8. **Increase the font size for promo and payment text,** and keep at least 44px touch targets everywhere.

## 6. Bottom Line

The shelves are well organized for a comfort shopper, and the product page earns some trust. But I never got to the checkout, the cart came up blank, and a search for comfort shoes started with women's styles. The shoe has to fit, and the site has to let me buy it. Today it doesn't. Score: **3/10, unchanged from yesterday.**

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The captured data supports only a limited audit.

- **Bot protection interfered with the run.** Five 429s hit the Kasada fingerprint endpoint (`/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.904`) and one hit `/cart/`. Rate limiting by the bot-defense layer probably affected the journey, so the Add to Cart and View Cart results are unreliable.
- **Several steps may not have navigated.** The Log In and Men category samples show the URL `https://www.skechers.com/`, the same as the homepage. Their TTFB (388ms, 388ms) is also close to the homepage's 393ms. Either the steps were overlay or SPA interactions, or the capture read the same document each time.
- **LCP and CLS were not captured on any step** (`?`), so there is no Core Web Vitals data.
- **The View Cart violations are probably artifacts.** The cart returned 429, so axe likely scanned an error page.
- **The homepage has real accessibility defects**, including 2 critical and 1 serious violation.
- **Third-party script load is heavy.** Over 20 tag and pixel vendors appear in the rendered DOM.

## Accessibility

18 violations across 3 pages.

**Homepage (10)**
- **Critical:** `aria-required-parent` (ARIA roles without their required parent) and `button-name` (buttons with no accessible name).
- **Serious:** `link-name` (links with no accessible name).
- **Moderate:** `heading-order`, `region`, and landmark structure problems. `landmark-main-is-top-level` and `landmark-no-duplicate-main` together suggest a `<main>` nested inside another landmark, with more than one on the page. `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` and `landmark-unique` are also flagged.
- **Minor:** `aria-allowed-role`.

**Search "comfort shoes" (4)**: `aria-allowed-role` (minor), `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region` (all moderate). These repeat the homepage landmark patterns, so the site template is the likely cause.

**View Cart (4)**
- `document-title` and `html-has-lang` (serious), `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one` (moderate).
- These four are what a bare error response looks like. The page also returned 429 (see Console & Network Errors). Re-test the cart without the bot block before treating these as real defects.

**Not covered:** the Men, Men > Shoes, Product detail and Add to Cart steps had no axe results. That could mean no violations or no scan.

## SEO

- Evidence is limited. The HTML samples are truncated before the `<head>` metadata and are serialized DOM, not raw source. Title, meta description, canonical, structured data and robots could not be checked.
- The homepage has `<html lang="en">`.
- The cart document has no `<title>` and no `lang` (see Accessibility). If this is the 429 response, bots could see the same empty page. Confirm that crawlers are allow-listed by the bot-defense layer.
- Three journey steps share the URL `https://www.skechers.com/`. Confirm that category and PDP pages have their own canonical URLs and are not delivered only through client-side state.

## Performance

- **Web Vitals:** LCP and CLS were not captured for any step, which is an instrumentation gap.
- **TTFB:**
  - Homepage (393ms), Log In (388ms), Men category (388ms) and View Cart (328ms) are reasonable.
  - Men > Shoes (26ms), Product detail (27ms), Add to Cart (27ms) and Search (27ms) are implausibly low for a document request. They suggest cache or bfcache reuse, an in-page transition, or a stale navigation-timing entry. Do not treat them as real server timings.
- **Third-party load:** the rendered head contains many vendor scripts. I counted Google Tag Manager (plus gtag destinations for G-XZTV9LQ9DQ, AW-1066305778 and DC-9630990), Monetate, Quantum Metric, TikTok, Taboola, Snapchat, Reddit, Facebook, Pinterest, Nextdoor, Bing, Yahoo, Impact, RTB House (creativecdn), Bloomreach (brsrvr), Pulse Insights, Tune (go2sdk) and Stape.
- **Duplicate loads:** `s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` appears twice on every sampled page, and `tags.creativecdn.com/dhGobFICKSkk6J9nvQPw.js` appears twice on the Men sample. Deduplicate these in GTM.
- **Script growth:** the Men sample has more vendor scripts than the homepage (Taboola, Snapchat, Reddit, Facebook, Nextdoor and others). This fits tags being injected cumulatively into one long-lived document.
- **Fonts:** WebFont Loader is pulled from cdnjs, and the `wf-*-active` classes show fonts loaded through JS. This adds a third-party dependency and a JS gate on text rendering. Native `@font-face` with `font-display` and preload would remove both.
- **Viewport height:** `--vh` is set inline by JS. CSS `dvh`/`svh` units remove the need for the script.
- **GTM environment:** the GTM URL carries `gtm_auth`, `gtm_preview=env-1` and `gtm_cookies_win=x`. Confirm that production is meant to load this GTM environment.

## Mobile Optimization

- The `--vh` of about 6.64px implies a viewport of roughly 664px high, so the mobile viewport was applied.
- The viewport meta tag, tap-target size, input types and responsive images could not be checked because the sample is truncated.
- The `Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed` console message (below) points to `unload` handlers. These block bfcache restores, which matters most on mobile back/forward navigation.

## Console & Network Errors

Only 10 of the 24 console errors were provided.

**Network (6 total)**
- 5 × 429 on `/149e9513-.../2d206a39-.../fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.904`. This is the Kasada bot-defense fingerprint endpoint, and it is being rate limited.
- 1 × 429 on `https://www.skechers.com/cart/`. The cart document itself was blocked, which probably explains the View Cart axe results.

**Console**
- 3 × `Failed to load resource: 429`, matching the network errors.
- 4 × `Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document.` A script registers an `unload` listener, which is deprecated and blocked by policy here.
- 2 × `Unsupported Summarizer API languages were specified...`. This is a Chrome built-in AI call made with unsupported languages. It may come from a third-party script or a browser extension, so confirm the source before filing it as a site bug.

## Recommendations

1. **Fix the test harness first.** Allow-list the monitor with the bot-defense vendor, or slow its request rate. Then re-run the journey and confirm that Log In, Men and Men > Shoes actually navigate. The cart data cannot be trusted until `/cart/` stops returning 429.
2. **Fix LCP/CLS capture.** Register `PerformanceObserver` for `largest-contentful-paint` and `layout-shift` (with `buffered: true`) before navigation, and read the values at the end of each step.
3. **Fix the homepage critical and serious accessibility issues:**
   - Add accessible names to icon-only buttons and links.
   - Correct the ARIA parent/child roles.
   - Use a single top-level `<main>` and give each repeated landmark a unique label.
4. **Re-test the cart for accessibility** once it loads. If `document-title` or `html-has-lang` still fail, fix the real response. Also check whether error and block pages should carry them.
5. **Remove the duplicate tag loads** (`ytc.js`, `creativecdn`) and audit the 20+ vendors for ones that can be consolidated, deferred or dropped.
6. **Remove `unload` listeners.** Use `pagehide` or `visibilitychange` instead so bfcache works.
7. **Replace the WebFont Loader and JS-set `--vh`** with native CSS.
8. **Re-run the SEO checks** against raw HTML source (title, canonical, meta description, structured data).
