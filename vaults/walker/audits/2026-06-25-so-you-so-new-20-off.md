# So you, so new, & 20% off
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- My thumb landed on DSW before my eyes registered it — pure reflex, same muscle memory I've built over the last two weeks of near-daily sends. The "20% OFF" was big and legible right at the top, and for one second I thought they finally sent me something I could use. Then the grid loaded and I'm looking at strappy heeled sandals, floral wedge platforms, and low-cut women's slip-ons top to bottom. Every single product in this email is women's footwear. The headline says "so you, so new" — and whoever "you" is, it isn't me.
- I scrolled all the way to the bottom hoping to hit a men's section. The closest I got was a "Shop by Size" block that doesn't tell me anything about men's versus women's. The "Win Up to $50 Off" rewards callout caught my eye because I do have a VIP balance sitting there — but I can't spend it on shoes that aren't built for my foot.
- I would not click. I would not forward it. I'd mark it read and move on, same as I've done with most of their June sends.

## What's working

- The 20% OFF is front and center, large enough to read without glasses — no digging for the offer
- "SHOP SANDALS" button is clearly tappable, reasonable size for phone thumbs
- The "Win Up to $50 Off" rewards callout was the one thing that made me pause — I actually have points and that felt like real money on the table
- Clean grid layout, no broken images, nothing visually weird going on
- The DSW brand has enough trust equity that I open on sight — that's earned, not accidental

## What's weak

- Every product in the grid is women's — strappy heels, wedge sandals, floral platforms, open-toe slides. There is nothing here I could buy for myself
- "So you, so new" — whoever copywriting thinks "so you" targets me is wrong. That phrasing reads like a 38-year-old woman's Instagram caption
- The preview text starts fine ("Start the weekend off right") and then it's a wall of tiny blank squares filling the rest of the line — junk spacer characters bleeding into my inbox
- This is the fifth or sixth "20% off" DSW email in a short stretch; the discount doesn't feel special anymore, it feels like the base price
- No men's category, no comfort-walking callout, no signal that DSW knows anything about who I actually am as a customer

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment by gender before anything else.** Send the men's arrivals to men. This single fix would make the other four issues minor. Nothing else in this list matters if the product grid is wrong.
- 2. **Fix the preview text.** The spacer junk after "Start the weekend off right" renders as visible artifacts in the inbox. Replace with an actual second sentence of copy.
- 3. **Rotate the offer mechanic.** Back-to-back 20% off sends desensitize me to the number. Try a dollar threshold ("$20 off $100+"), a loyalty-exclusive offer, or a category-specific hook that makes this week feel different from last week.
- 4. **Drop one row of men's comfort shoes** even in a women's-first email — walkers, slip-ons, cushioned sandals. Give me a single tap target and I stay engaged.

- **Preheader Alt B:** `20% off sitewide. New arrivals hit the floor today.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`Src:` capitalized in all `@font-face` blocks** (confirmed in source):
```css
@font-face {
  font-family: 'Futura_PT_Regular';
  Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');
}
```
The CSS `src` descriptor is case-sensitive in spec. Capitalized `Src:` is invalid and will cause the font declaration to be silently dropped in strict parsers. All 10 `@font-face` blocks are affected. Fallback fonts will render instead.

**`-ms-text-size-adjust` conflict in the `*` reset:**
```css
* {
  -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%;   /* set first */
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;   /* overwrites — disables resize in Outlook */
}
```
The `none` value wins, disabling user text-size scaling in Outlook/Windows Mail.

**Missing `http-equiv` on charset meta:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, some older clients won't apply the charset declaration.

**HelveticaNow fonts served as `.ttf` only** — no `.woff` or `.woff2` variants. `.ttf` has limited email client support (Gmail Android may skip it). Futura PT is `.woff`-only with no `.woff2` fallback (suboptimal load size).

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta** restricts pinch-zoom for low-vision users on mobile — accessibility concern.
