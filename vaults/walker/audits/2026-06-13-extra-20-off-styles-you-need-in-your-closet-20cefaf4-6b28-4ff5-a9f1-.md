# Extra 20% off styles you need in your closet 😍
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- Well, here we go again — Famous Footwear in my inbox. The good news is I saw "20% off" and "Rewards Member Exclusive" right at the top, which is the kind of thing that makes me slow down instead of swiping past. And this time they actually showed me some shoes I recognize — Brooks and New Balance in the body, with real prices attached. That got my attention.
- But then there's the preview text. Same problem I flagged back on June 8th in the "Final 72 hrs" send — it was a wall of random letters and a URL that looked like a phishing link. This one does the exact same thing. That URL is the first thing my phone shows me next to the subject line, and my first instinct was to assume it was spam. Famous Footwear is burning goodwill with this recurring mistake.
- Inside the email the offer shifts too — they've been pushing "$20 off $100" all week, and now it's "extra 20% off." Fine, a different angle, but it reads like a new promo on top of the pile I already half-ignored. I'd mark this as read, maybe click over to the Brooks section. I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- **Rewards Member Exclusive badge at top** — treating me like a known customer, not a cold prospect. That earns a second look.
- **Brooks and New Balance visible with actual dollar prices** — those are the brands I actually shop, and seeing $109.99 on a Brooks shoe is the kind of signal that makes me pull out my glasses.
- **20% off is legible and front-and-center** — no hunting for the offer, it's right there.
- **Clean layout on mobile** — no broken images, no overlapping text, easy to scroll through without squinting too hard.
- **New Balance getting its own "30% off" section** — I noticed that block, that's a legitimate comfort brand getting featured treatment.

## What's weak

- **Preview text is a raw tracking URL** — for the second time in a week. This is the biggest unforced error in the send. It looks like a phishing link on my lock screen.
- **Air Jordan at $99.99** — I'm 62, not 22. Jordan's are not in my rotation. That section makes the email feel like it wasn't really built for me.
- **"Styles you need in your closet 😍"** — that heart-eyes emoji isn't my language, and "styles you need in your closet" sounds like it was written for a younger woman. I own shoes, not a "closet curation."
- **No comfort or fit callouts** — arch support, cushioning, slip-on, wide width — none of that is visible in the copy. They showed me shoes I might like but gave me no reason tied to how my feet actually feel.
- **Offer fatigue starting to set in** — between the "$20 off $100" flood last week and now "extra 20% off," the cadence is feeling pushy. I trust Famous Footwear but they're testing it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text** — this is the single highest-ROI fix. Put real copy in the preheader slot. Something like "Brooks, New Balance, and more — yours for less today" is infinitely better than a URL.
- 2. **Lead with the comfort brands in the hero** — move Brooks and New Balance up, push Air Jordan to the bottom or cut it for this segment.
- 3. **Add one line of fit/comfort copy per featured shoe** — "Extra cushioning. Wide width available." That's all it takes for a guy like me to feel like this was written for my feet.
- 4. **Let the offer breathe** — if you're switching from "$20 off $100" to "20% off," acknowledge it. "New deal — better than last week's" goes a long way.
- 5. **Swap the emoji** — drop 😍, try something neutral or nothing at all.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Rewards members: Extra 20% off Brooks & New Balance`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 20% member discount is live — comfort styles inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Brooks, New Balance, and more — take an extra 20% off today`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Member deal: 20% off the walking and casual shoes you've been eyeing`

## Technical Audit — Famous Footwear "Extra 20% Off" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (ExactTarget) send with no critical delivery blockers, but carries two confirmed mixed-content vulnerabilities, a systemic missing-alt-text problem across 20+ images, and a deliverability risk from a URL-saturated plain-text part. No hard errors; multiple warnings require remediation.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**All 32 click links unverifiable.** Every CTA routes through `click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=<encrypted>`. The QA prober correctly skipped these (redirect-only domains), but this means zero destination URLs were validated — broken product pages, expired promos, or missing UTM params cannot be ruled out from this audit alone. Manual spot-checking of at least the primary CTA destinations is required before send.

**Open pixel:** HTTPS confirmed (`click.email.famousfootwear.com/open.aspx`). No issue.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Mixed-content HTTP images (high risk):** Two dynamic content images are served over plain HTTP:
```
http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=ABB7...  (920205576257)
http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=ABB7...  (920155576550)
```
Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all block or strip non-HTTPS image sources under their mixed-content policies. These are almost certainly dynamic/personalized content blocks (LiveContent = Salesforce Content Builder dynamic zones). If they render blank, it likely means a layout slot silently fails. Fix: serve via HTTPS or configure the S7/ExactTarget CDN endpoint with TLS.

**20+ images missing meaningful alt text.** Confirmed by QA, including:
- Logo image (`3b6a7dc1-...png`) — `alt=""` present but empty; acceptable only if purely decorative, not when it's the primary brand mark and the only content in the header link
- All 8 iGoDigital recommendation images (`7222883.recs.igodigital.com/rr/v2/.../i/1–8/`) — product recs with no alt text fail screen readers and render as broken links in image-off environments
- Multiple editorial/banner images (`672c30ec`, `845fdf22`, `9c5cd961`, etc.)

**Viewport blocks user zoom:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
`user-scalable=0` + `maximum-scale=1` violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA) and is increasingly flagged by accessibility auditors. Many iOS mail clients ignore this, but it's a compliance risk.

**CSS typo:** `lineapacing:20px` in the darkmode padding cell — likely intended as `line-height`. Has no effect as written; will not break layout but is dead code.

**Charset meta missing `http-equiv`:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"` makes this declaration non-functional in legacy clients that don't infer charset from HTTP headers. Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
