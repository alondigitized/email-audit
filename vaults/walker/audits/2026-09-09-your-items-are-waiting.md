# Your Items Are Waiting!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-09**
## Executive summary

- Well, here it is again — that same pink floral D'Lites shoe, now for a fourth time this week, this time wrapped in "WE NOTICED YOU NOTICING US..." Cute line, I guess, but no — I didn't notice it, I never clicked it, and I sure didn't put it in a cart. This isn't a comfort shoe for me anyway, it's a women's fashion sneaker with embroidered flowers on it, so the whole premise of the email is wrong before I even get to the "recommendations."
- Down below there's at least something real — men's slip-ins, arch fit, a name I recognize (Altman - Murdock) — buried under a wall of women's and kids' shoes I have zero use for. Then a genuine 20% off code for members, which is the one thing here that'd get my attention if it weren't stapled to the bottom of six inches of irrelevant sneakers.
- I'm marking this read and moving on. Compare this to yesterday's "End of Season, Start of Savings" email that opened with my name and my points balance — that one I'd have opened front to back. This one goes straight past me, and I definitely wouldn't forward it to anybody.

## What's working

- The 20% off member code (EXTRA20) is a real, specific offer with a clear deadline — 9/14.
- "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" button is easy to spot and unambiguous, even if I've got no cart to complete.
- Men's slip-ins Altman - Murdock shoe shows up in the grid — that's actually my category, arch support and all.
- Layout is clean, nothing broken or overlapping that I can see.

## What's weak

- The whole email is anchored on a pink floral women's D'Lites shoe I never touched — fourth time this week, and it's not even close to something I'd buy.
- "Here's some more great options we think you'll love" is mostly kids' and women's shoes — Boy's Garage, Girl's Glam Racer — none of that applies to me.
- Fake urgency ("your cart is still waiting") when I never had a cart — that's a trust killer.
- The one relevant item, the men's arch-fit shoe, is stuck in a small thumbnail in row two instead of leading the email.
- Feels like the same recycled cart-abandonment template I've now seen most of this week, just reskinned.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop leading with a shoe I never looked at — if there's no real cart or browse history, don't fake one. Lead with the 20% member code instead, that's the actual offer worth my time.
- 2. Swap the hero product for the men's Altman - Murdock slip-in or another arch-fit style — put my category first, not last.
- 3. Cut the kids' and women's fashion sneakers out of "options you'll love" for a recipient with no history in those categories — segment the grid by gender/category actually purchased.
- 4. Move the 20% off banner up near the top, it's the strongest thing in the whole email and it's buried past six product tiles.
