## What's working

- Chocolate zip boot and olive gum-sole sneaker are both styles I'd actually try on — real men's casual options, not another floral throwaway.
- "Hi, Walker · Member" up top with my point balance is a decent personal touch, even if brief.
- Clean layout — one color, one shoe, one button, repeat. Easy to scroll on my phone without hunting for anything.
- No overlapping text or broken images that I can see — it all rendered fine.

## What's weak

- The "2 FOR $100" banner doesn't say what's included until you scroll to the fine print — I'm not going to squint for asterisks on my phone.
- Mauve and navy are clearly women's/unisex fashion sneakers with no arch support or comfort callout — half this email isn't for me.
- My 1000 points and $5 reward get one line at the very top and then vanish — didn't fold that into the offer anywhere else.
- No mention of comfort, cushioning, or arch support anywhere — for a guy shopping for all-day wear, that's the whole reason I'd pick Skechers over Hoka or New Balance, and it's just not here.
- Couple photo up top skews younger than me — I don't picture myself in that scene.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the men's product that fits my profile (the olive sneaker or chocolate boot) and put comfort language right on the image — "memory foam insole," "arch support" — since that's what actually moves me to click, not the color name.
- 2. Put the "2 for $100" terms right under the banner, not buried in the footer — tell me up front which categories qualify.
- 3. Weave my $5 reward into the CTA itself — "Shop Olive — apply your $5 reward" — instead of a one-line mention that gets orphaned at the top.
- 4. Segment by gender/interest so a comfort-first guy like me doesn't get two fashion-sneaker colors he'll never buy.
