# 20% off ends tonight!!!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- I'll be honest — when DSW shows up in my inbox now, my hand moves toward the delete key before my eyes finish reading. I've had what, six or seven of these this week? All some variation of "20% off." I opened this one out of sheer muscle memory, and what I found inside was a shoe wall that skews heavily toward women's styles, a "party shoes ready" banner that has nothing to do with me, and — here's the part that genuinely annoyed me — a teaser saying something "big is coming tomorrow" in an email whose entire subject line just told me the sale ends *tonight*. So which is it? Do I rush to buy, or wait for tomorrow's mystery? That kind of push-pull isn't urgency, it's just confusion.
- The bright spots exist. "SHOP WIDE WIDTH" and the "SHOP BY SIZE" grid are the kind of practical detail I actually want from DSW. At 62, finding a wide-width walking shoe that's on sale isn't a small thing. But those tools are buried below a wall of heels and pumps I'll never touch.
- Would I click? Maybe on the wide-width button — that's a real hook. Forward it to a friend? No. I'd just tell Dave to go straight to the DSW website instead of chasing these emails.

## What's working

- The 20% off is front and center — no digging, no mystery, the number is right there where it should be.
- "SHOP WIDE WIDTH" is exactly the kind of practical, fit-first detail that earns my click; that button alone might drag me in.
- The "SHOP BY SIZE" grid is clean and tappable on a phone — exactly how I want to shop.
- Email renders completely clean — no broken images, no overlapping text, nothing jumping around on my screen.
- VIP perks show up at the bottom (free shipping, 2% back) — I'm already a member, so that's a quiet, trustworthy signal.

## What's weak

- Half that shoe grid is women's heels and dressy sandals — I'm scrolling past stilettos looking for something I'd actually put on my feet.
- "PARTY SHOES READY / Something big is coming TOMORROW" flatly contradicts the subject line's "ends tonight" urgency — I felt like I was being worked, not helped.
- The preview text reading "TOMORROW: Hope you like surprises" next to a "ends tonight" subject is cognitive whiplash; it made me less likely to trust the deadline.
- "BRAND NEW…AND SO YOU" is pure marketing fog — meaningless to me on a phone at arm's length.
- This is at least the seventh 20%-off DSW email I've seen this week. The urgency stopped being urgent around email number three.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Open with a men's comfort shoe, not a catalog grid.** One clear walking shoe — Skechers, HOKA, New Balance — with the 20% badge right on it. That tells me in half a second whether this email is for me.
- 2. **Kill the "something big tomorrow" tease.** It actively undermines the "ends tonight" close. If tonight is the deadline, own it — don't give me a reason to wait until morning.
- 3. **Fix the preview text.** "TOMORROW: Hope you like surprises" is working against the urgency message, not with it. The preheader should back up the subject, not confuse it.
- 4. **Drop the triple exclamation points.** That's shouting. It reads like spam and it reads like desperation — neither of which makes me trust the sale is real.
- 5. **Acknowledge the cadence.** A "last call — we mean it this time" line would actually land after a week of these. Pretending it's the first email feels dishonest.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue A — Two `<meta>` tags are missing their `http-equiv` attribute**, making them inert in most parsers:

```html
<!-- As sent (broken): -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta content="IE=Edge" />

<!-- Should be: -->
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=Edge" />
```

Without `http-equiv="Content-Type"`, character encoding fallback depends entirely on the server header. Without `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`, the IE compatibility directive is silently ignored.

**Issue B — CSS reset conflict in `*` selector:**

```css
*{
  -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%;    /* set */
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%;
  ...
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;    /* overrides the first declaration */
  text-size-adjust: none;        /* non-standard; disables user zoom */
}
```

`-ms-text-size-adjust` is declared twice — `100%` then overridden by `none` — so the `100%` value never applies in IE/Edge. `text-size-adjust: none` is also an accessibility concern: it prevents OS-level text scaling for low-vision users on some clients.

**Issue C — `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

Locks pinch-zoom on iOS, violating WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Recommend removing `maximum-scale=1` or setting it to `5`.

**Issue D — Non-standard CSS property casing in all `@font-face` blocks:**

```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');
```

`Src:` (capital S) is used consistently across all 10 `@font-face` declarations. CSS properties are technically case-insensitive, but this is non-standard and may fail in strict parsers or future spec revisions. Standard form is lowercase `src:`.

**Note:** Web fonts load from `braze-images.com` but will be ignored by Gmail, Outlook (Windows), and most mobile clients — custom fonts are broadly unsupported in email and the fallback stack should be verified.
