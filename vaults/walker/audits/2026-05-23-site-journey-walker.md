# Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Review
**Date:** May 23, 2026 | **vs. Yesterday:** 4/10

## 1. Executive Summary

The path from homepage to product was noticeably cleaner today — Slip-ins and Arch Fit are right where I'd expect them, the product page for the Max Cushioning Arch Fit 2.0 hit every comfort keyword I care about, and the deal (BOGO 50% off) was impossible to miss. But the experience collapsed at checkout: the cart page loaded as a completely blank white screen. Nothing. And when I searched "comfort shoes," every result I could see on screen was Women's. For a 62-year-old guy who just wants to put a pair of slip-ins in a bag and pay, that blank cart is a brick wall.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 5/10

Up from 4/10 yesterday. The product discovery path improved — the right shoe reached me faster. But the cart is broken. You can't convert a sale through a white screen. Search miscategorization keeps a second entry point broken too. One point up, ceiling capped hard by a checkout-blocking bug.

## 3. What's Working

**BOGO deal is front and center.** The blue "BOGO 50% OFF Applied at Cart" banner stayed persistent across every page. I saw it at the top of every step. At $135 for a pair I might buy two of, that's the kind of math I actually do. Good.

**Men's menu is clean and scannable.** The full-screen menu panel (Step 4) is the best screen in this whole journey. Big text, full-width tap targets, no clutter. "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Extra Wide Fit" are right there as quick-links at the bottom — no digging required. A 62-year-old with big thumbs can nail those targets without squinting.

**Product page hits my comfort checklist.** "Max Cushioning Arch Fit 2.0," the Hands-Free Slip-Ins badge, 4.5 stars, 17 reviews, BOGO callout — that's everything I'd want to see in the first scroll. The shoe image was large and clear. The product name is a mouthful but the keywords landed.

**No disruptive popup.** Step 2 shows the banner simply rotated to the Memorial Day apparel promo. No modal blocking my view, no email capture wall. That was a relief.

**Size grid is usable.** The size buttons (Step 7) are adequately sized — I could tap 10.5 without hitting a neighbor. The full-width ADD TO CART button pinned at the bottom is excellent. That's how you build a button for older hands.

---

## 4. What's Weak

**The cart is a blank white page. Full stop.** Step 8 loaded nothing. Not a spinner, not an error message, not a product. Just white. That's not a friction point — that's a wall. If I'd actually made it to checkout, I'd have closed the app and called the 800 number. Or gone to Amazon. This is the single most urgent issue on this site.

**Search "comfort shoes" surfaced Women's products first.** Step 9 shows 1,444 results — a huge number — but the first two cards I see are both labeled "Women's." I'm logged in as a man. Skechers knows who I am. There's no excuse for defaulting a gendered search to the wrong gender. I would have assumed the site thought I was shopping for my wife and backed out.

**Login state is invisible.** Steps 1 and 3 look identical — both show the "Login" prompt in the loyalty banner, cart at 0. I can't tell if I'm logged in or not. There's no "Hi Walker" greeting, no name in the account icon, no confirmation. If I'm a member trying to earn that $5 reward, I need to know the system recognized me.

**The horizontal nav bar on homepage cuts off.** The category strip shows "Slip-ins | Sandals | Arch Fit | BOBs | G—" and cuts off. I can't tell what's hiding to the right without swiping. That's a discoverability issue — whatever comes after "BOBs" is invisible.

**Hero image skews young.** The "Red, White & Blue Collection" hero features two models who look about 30. No comfort messaging, no age-appropriate lifestyle context. I'm 62 and my feet hurt — show me someone enjoying a walk, not a fashion shoot.

**Validation errors before I even tried to add.** Step 7 already shows "Please select a width" and "Please select a size" in red before I'd done anything wrong. Those errors should only appear after I tap ADD TO CART without making selections — not preemptively. It feels like the site is scolding me before I've made a mistake.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart immediately.** This is P0. A blank cart means zero revenue from anyone who reaches that step. Investigate what broke between Step 7 and Step 8 — whether it's a session, cookie, or JavaScript failure — and resolve before anything else.

2. **Gender-filter search results for logged-in users.** If I'm authenticated as a male account, "comfort shoes" should default to men's results. Add a gender pre-filter or at minimum surface men's products above the fold. Don't make me dig through women's shoes to find my own.

3. **Surface a logged-in greeting.** Once I authenticate, put my first name or "Member" somewhere visible — top of the account icon, or in the loyalty banner ("Hi Walker — earn $5 on $75+"). I need confirmation the system sees me.

4. **Replace the hero with comfort-forward lifestyle imagery.** Show a man my age on a walk, at a farmer's market, on a golf course — wearing slip-ins. The product story is strong; the photography isn't selling it to me.

5. **Show validation errors only on attempted action.** Pre-populated red error text ("Please select a width") on a fresh page is bad UX. Trigger that only after a submit attempt.

6. **Expand or scroll-hint the top nav.** Either surface the full category list or add a visual cue (a fade gradient or "more" arrow) so I know to swipe right. Hidden categories are lost revenue.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

8 steps audited across the skechers.com mobile purchase funnel. Primary concerns: a critical WCAG failure on the cart page (missing `<title>` and `html[lang]`), bot-protection 429s that block the cart endpoint directly, a Stylitics CORS failure breaking the outfit widget, and a 10x TTFB spike on PDP/Add-to-Cart. Third-party script count is high (20+ async scripts per page), contributing to untracked LCP/CLS gaps.

---

## Accessibility

**Critical (WCAG 2.1 blocker)**
- `aria-required-parent` violation on Homepage — ARIA roles used outside their required parent context. Likely a carousel or list component where `role="option"` or `role="listitem"` exists without the required `role="listbox"` / `role="list"` wrapper.

**Serious (View Cart page)**
- No `<title>` element and no `html[lang]` attribute on the cart page. This is a WCAG 2.4.2 / 3.1.1 failure. Suggests the cart is rendered as a client-side overlay or partial view that bypasses the standard page `<head>` template — the shell document is likely never replaced.
- Missing main landmark and H1 on cart page — consistent with the above: the cart overlay renders body content without document-level structure.

**Moderate (Homepage)**
- Duplicate `<main>` landmark — likely caused by a CMS component or A/B testing injection (Monetate is active) inserting a second `<main>`.
- `<aside>` not top-level and content outside any landmark — applies to sidebar/widget content not wrapped in a sectioning element.
- Heading order skip — an `<h3>` or lower appears before an `<h2>` is established; common when component libraries use heading levels relative to their local context rather than document position.

**Moderate (Search page)**
- Same `<aside>` top-level and landmark uniqueness issues as Homepage — filter sidebar shares the same structural pattern.

---

## SEO

- **View Cart** is missing `<title>` and `html[lang]` — if Googlebot ever crawls a canonicalized cart URL, it will be indexed as an untitled document in an unknown language.
- Homepage `heading-order` violation means the H1→H2→H3 hierarchy is broken, which affects crawlability of page structure.
- 20+ third-party `async` scripts in `<head>` on every page. None block parsing, but they do compete for network/CPU during initial render, which indirectly affects CWV scores Google uses for ranking.

- **PDP TTFB of 334ms** is the major outlier — 10x vs. category pages. Likely cause: server-side personalization (Monetate `custom.js` is loaded synchronously with `charset="utf-8"` without `defer`, and Monetate's server-side decisioning can block TTFB on product pages), uncached inventory/pricing calls, or a Salesforce Commerce Cloud (Demandware — confirmed by `/on/demandware.static/` path) origin miss.
- LCP and CLS are not captured for any step — the audit tooling did not collect these. These are the two CWV metrics Google uses for ranking; they need to be measured before the audit can assess Core Web Vitals compliance.
- **WebFontLoader** (`webfontloader.js` v1.6.28 from cdnjs, loaded `async`) injects `wf-*` classes on `<html>` after fonts are ready. If CSS is conditioned on these classes for layout (e.g., `font-display: swap` gated by JS class), it will cause CLS on slow connections.

---

## Mobile Optimization

- **`--vh` CSS custom property** set inline on `<html>` (`style="--vh: 6.640000000000001px"`) — JavaScript-based fix for the mobile Safari 100vh bug. The value `6.64px` (× 100 = 664px viewport) is correct for a typical mobile screen, but this requires JS execution before the custom property is set, meaning any layout using `calc(var(--vh) * 100)` will shift on paint. This is a known CLS source.
- No `viewport` meta tag visible in the truncated HTML head samples (not confirmed absent, but not observed in the samples provided).
- Font loading uses WebFontLoader v1.6.28 (2018, unmaintained) rather than `font-display: optional` or `font-display: swap` in CSS `@font-face`, which is the modern standard and avoids layout shifts from FOUT/FOIT.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 — Kasada bot protection rate-limiting itself (5 of 6 network errors)**

```
https://www.skechers.com/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.416
```

This UUID-path URL is the Kasada (`x-kpsdk-v`) client-side fingerprint collection endpoint. The SDK sends repeated POST/GET requests to establish a bot score; 429s here mean Kasada's own infrastructure is rate-limiting the fingerprint polling loop. The SDK version `j-1.2.416` may be outdated. Immediate effect: pages that depend on a valid Kasada token before proceeding (checkout, login, add-to-cart) may stall or fail silently.

**429 — Cart endpoint blocked**

```
429 https://www.skechers.com/cart/
```

The cart page itself returned 429. This means the bot-protection layer classified the cart request as automated traffic and blocked it. A real user on a mobile browser should not hit this, but it indicates Kasada is misconfigured or its token-refresh loop is broken enough that a legitimate session can get flagged.

**CORS — Stylitics outfit widget CSS blocked**

```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?...' blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```

The Stylitics CDN is not returning `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` for its stylesheet. This is a Stylitics-side misconfiguration; the CSS is being loaded via `<link rel="stylesheet" crossorigin>` which enforces CORS, but the CDN is not sending the required header. The outfit-styling widget will render without its styles. This is a vendor issue to escalate to Stylitics.

## Recommendations

**P0 — Fix before next release**
1. **View Cart `<title>` and `html[lang]`**: The cart view is missing both. If cart is a client-side modal, the page shell must still have a proper `<title>` and the `<html lang>` must be set on the server-rendered document — not omitted because "it's an overlay." Fix in the Salesforce Commerce Cloud cart template.
2. **Kasada 429 loop**: Audit the Kasada SDK version (`j-1.2.416`) against current. The repeated 429s on the `/fp` endpoint suggest either an expired client-side script or a server-side rate limit set too low for the SDK's polling frequency. Coordinate with Kasada support; a misconfigured bot protection layer blocking its own fingerprinting will cause intermittent cart/checkout failures for real users.

**P1 — High priority**
3. **PDP TTFB 334ms**: Profile the Demandware origin response for PDPs. Likely suspects: Monetate server-side decisioning latency, uncached personalization, or missing CDN cache headers on product templates. Add `Cache-Control: s-maxage` where personalization is not required.
4. **`aria-required-parent` critical violation on Homepage**: Find the component using orphaned ARIA roles (likely a carousel with `role="option"` without `role="listbox"`). This is a keyboard/screen-reader blocker.
5. **Capture LCP and CLS**: The audit has no LCP or CLS data for any step. These are the primary CWV ranking signals. Instrument with real-user monitoring or run Lighthouse against all 8 steps.

**P2 — Medium priority**
6. **Stylitics CORS**: File a ticket with Stylitics to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` to their CDN response for `web-assets.stylitics.com`. Remove `crossorigin` attribute from the `<link>` tag as a temporary workaround only if the widget still functions without it.
7. **Duplicate `<main>` landmark**: Audit Monetate injection rules — A/B test variants injected into the DOM are likely wrapping content in `<main>` tags without checking if one already exists. Monetate campaigns should inject into `<section>` or named regions, not landmark elements.
8. **WebFontLoader → CSS font-display**: Replace the WebFontLoader script with `font-display: swap` in CSS `@font-face` declarations. The JS-class-based approach is a CLS source and relies on a 2018 unmaintained library.
9. **`--vh` JS fix**: If `100dvh` (dynamic viewport height) browser support is acceptable (Chrome 108+, Safari 15.4+, Firefox 116+), replace the JS-based `--vh` workaround with the native `dvh` unit. Current global support is ~95%.
