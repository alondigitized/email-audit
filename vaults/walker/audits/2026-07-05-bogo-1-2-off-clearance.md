# BOGO 1/2 off Clearance 👟🎇
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- Same deal, same day, second email. I already opened one from Famous Footwear this morning — the "going strong" version of this exact BOGO clearance pitch — and here's another one in my inbox before lunch with a different subject line and the same broken preheader URL they've been shipping since June. At some point "we're reminding you" becomes "we're spamming you," and Famous Footwear is right on that edge for me this weekend.
- That said, the BOGO clearance deal itself is legitimate. Famous Footwear is where I actually walk in, feel the shoes, and check the clearance wall — so the offer lands. The adidas 25% off section was an unexpected second reason to look around, and the 3X loyalty points on top of a BOGO is real math. There's money on the table here.
- But I'm not clicking. I already saw this deal at 8 a.m. The products shown are athletic fashion sneakers — bright colorways, low-profile styles — not the cushioned walkers or slip-ons I'm looking for. I'd mark this read and maybe drift back to the website later on my own. Not forwarding to anyone.

## What's working

- **BOGO clearance is the hero and it's immediately clear** — big, bold, front and center. No puzzle to solve.
- **Multiple value layers** — clearance BOGO plus adidas 25% off plus 3X points gives me more than one reason to keep reading.
- **Loyalty points visible** — as a Rewards member, seeing 3X stacked on top of a clearance deal is genuinely compelling math.
- **Subject line is clean and short** — reads fine on the phone in one glance, no emoji pile-up.
- **Military 10% off callout** — nice gesture even if it doesn't apply to me; it signals the brand is paying attention to something beyond pure sales copy.

## What's weak

- **Broken preheader, again** — raw tracking URL, same as the June 21st email, the June 25th email, the June 26th, the July 2nd. Five weeks. Still broken. This is now a brand reliability problem, not just a tech glitch.
- **Second email of the day with the same deal** — I already got "BOGO going strong" this morning from Famous Footwear. This one doesn't add anything new. Repetition inside 24 hours feels like they lost track of their own send schedule.
- **"Hurry, don't wait too long" is empty urgency** — the July 4th email used the same vague push. Give me an actual end date and I'll act. "Ends Sunday" changes the calculation.
- **Products shown aren't comfort-first footwear** — I see athletic fashion sneakers and adidas streetwear styles. Where are the Skechers GOwalk, New Balance 928, Brooks Addiction Walker styles that clearance usually carries? Show me what I actually shop.
- **No personal touch** — I'm a Rewards member; this email treats me like a cold prospect.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader.** Every other optimization is secondary. This has been broken across at least six consecutive sends. Whatever template or system is stripping the preheader and substituting a click-tracking URL needs to be patched before the next deploy.
- 2. **Put an end date on the BOGO.** "Ends Sunday night" or "3 days left" in the hero copy converts vague urgency into a real deadline. Walker checks sales aisles — give him a reason to go this weekend, not "eventually."
- 3. **Stop double-tapping the same day.** One clearance BOGO email per day is plenty. The morning "going strong" version and this one serve the same audience with the same offer. Consolidate or sequence them 24 hours apart.
- 4. **Segment by comfort/walking footwear for subscribers like me.** Lead with Skechers, New Balance, Brooks, and wide-width clearance styles — not adidas Sambas. I'd have scrolled longer.
- 5. **Use my name and Rewards status.** "Walker, your 3X points weekend ends Sunday" in the subject does more work than a generic blast.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has no critical blockers but carries two moderate-risk issues: a deliverability flag from extreme plain-text URL density, and a blanket alt-text omission across ~40 product images. Several minor HTML/CSS hygiene problems are also present.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text URL density — WARN**
Plain-text part is 78% URLs (15,938 / 20,318 chars). Spam filters (SpamAssassin `URI_RATIO`, Gmail's heuristics) penalize ratios above ~60–70%. This likely inflates the spam score on text-only rendering paths.

**31 click links not probed**
All CTAs route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=<base64>`. QA could not resolve final destinations. Link-rot and redirect-chain depth cannot be confirmed from the truncated source alone.

**Open-pixel present and functional**
`https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/open.aspx?GUTSHODNSMPUDJZ7VCHGQDLECU.70251` — standard ESP open tracking pixel, no issue.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Outdated doctype**
`<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` — this triggers quirks mode in some clients. Email best practice is `<!DOCTYPE html>` (HTML5).

**Malformed charset meta tag**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Missing the required `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute. Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Without it, some older clients may not honor the charset declaration.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta — accessibility violation**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
Disabling pinch-to-zoom violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA) and is explicitly blocked in iOS 10+ (Apple overrides it), but it signals poor accessibility posture and can cause issues on Android WebView clients.

**CSS typo in inline style**
```html
style="...lineapacing:20px;..."
```
`lineapacing` is not a valid CSS property (likely intended as `line-height` or `letter-spacing`). Found on the `darkmode padl10m` TD wrapping the "Want Free Shipping?" block. Has no visual effect but is dead/broken CSS.

**40 images with empty or missing alt text**
QA flagged 40 `<img>` elements with no descriptive `alt`. This includes:
- Logo image `3b6a7dc1-6955-49f8-868a-bf62a15122ee.png` — `alt=""` is present (acceptable for decorative/logo use)
- All product/banner images (e.g., `e8a8f339`, `8d2a3a15`, `4ea7e4db`, and ~37 others) — no `alt` at all

In image-off environments (Outlook default, corporate filters), these render as broken image icons with no context. Screen readers announce them as empty.
