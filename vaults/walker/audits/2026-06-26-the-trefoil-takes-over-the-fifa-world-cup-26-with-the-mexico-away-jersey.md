# The trefoil takes over the FIFA World Cup 26™ with the Mexico Away Jersey
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- Here we go again. I open adidas expecting shoes — I'm always looking for something comfortable, something I can wear all day — and instead I get another World Cup jersey. Third one this month? Fourth? I lost count after the June 21st send, which I noted at the time I was "keeping a mental tally." Well, the tally just went up again.
- The email itself is clean. Nice photography, the Mexico Away Jersey is front and center, the white-and-green colors are crisp. I'll give them the production value. But there's a sweepstakes pitch at the bottom for a "signed Trionda" — I don't know what a Trionda is, and I don't care enough to find out. There's no sale. No percentage off. Just a jersey and a contest.
- I'm not clicking. I'm not forwarding this to a friend unless that friend watches soccer. I'm hitting Mark as Read and hoping the next adidas email is about shoes again like that $30 off deal on June 19th.

## What's working

- Production quality is solid — the hero image is clean, the jersey is well-lit, layout doesn't break on mobile.
- Visual hierarchy is obvious: big image, big headline, you know immediately what the email is about.
- Current and timely — World Cup 2026 is actually happening now, so at least it's not a stale promotion.
- The "WIN A SIGNED TRIONDA" section adds a second hook for people who actually follow the sport.

## What's weak

- This is the third or fourth FIFA/Mexico jersey email in a row — the cadence has ground me down and I'm tuning out anything with "Mexico," "trefoil," or "FIFA" in the subject.
- No offer. No discount. No sale. There's a "Shop Now" button but nothing to pull the trigger on — $0 off is not a reason to click.
- The model is young, styled fashion-forward, posed artistically. That's not me. I don't see myself in this email at all.
- "Trefoil" in the subject line — I know that's adidas's logo, but it reads like insider jargon. They could just say "adidas" and communicate more to someone my age.
- The sweepstakes for a signed soccer ball feels like filler. I'm 62. I don't want to enter a contest; I want a good price on shoes that don't hurt my feet.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Stop sending me jerseys.** Segment this list. I've been browsing Ultraboost and walking shoes — not once have I clicked on FIFA content. This email shouldn't have reached me.
- 2. **Bundle any jersey send with a reason to buy something I'd actually want.** "Shop the World Cup collection — plus save 20% on Ultraboost and walking styles this week." One email, two audiences.
- 3. **Add a discount.** The June 19th $30-off email worked on me because I could do the math. This email asks me to pay full price for a product I didn't ask for.
- 4. **Shorten the subject.** 74 characters is too long for my phone. The offer — such as it is — gets cut off.

- **Subject Alt B:** `New: adidas FIFA World Cup 26™ Mexico Jersey`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus save on walking styles while you shop the drop`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — adidas Mexico Away Jersey Email

**Sender:** adidas \<adidas@us-news.comms.adidas.com\>
**Subject:** The trefoil takes over the FIFA World Cup 26™ with the Mexico Away Jersey

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard-broken links are present — one doubled-domain URL and one malformed `/g/` path that resolves incorrectly — which will send a subset of clicks to 404s. The plain-text MIME part is completely absent, a common spam-filter trigger.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Doubled-domain URL — "local adidas store" link**

The store-locator CTA encodes a full `https://www.adidas.com/us/stores` URL inside an already-absolute `click.comms.adidas.com` redirect URL, producing a double-domain pattern. This is a merge/template assembly bug.

```
https://click.comms.adidas.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fwww.adidas.com%2Fus%2Fstores/1/
  0102019f017bade2-55ca9b14-5efd-426d-84ba-e7da611c117b-000000/LNGAMeixxF1...=452
```

The outer domain is the click-tracker; the inner `https%3A%2F%2F...` is the destination. Whether the click-tracker properly URL-decodes and forwards depends on ESP config — any misconfiguration sends the user to a broken URL. Same URL flagged twice by QA (link check + merge-error check), confirming it is a genuine template bug.

**[FAIL] Malformed `/g/` path on dynamic image link**

QA detected `unknown url type: '/g/...'` on a `dv.adidas.com` URL. The URL itself resolves to a `?` query-param variant but the internal path reference uses `/g/` which the checker could not classify. This appears to be a CMS or dynamic-content token that was partially substituted — the `txn=998e1300-710b-11f1-822c-aea38baf8e0c` parameter suggests a personalization transaction ID that may not have been injected correctly.

```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/61cce83e-8795-4376-bb14-1ad8f845ca50?cp_tp=v3.eJzj...
  (referenced internally as /g/61cce83e-... with txn param)
```

**[WARN] 37 tracking links unverifiable**

All 37 click-redirect links through `click.comms.adidas.com` were skipped by the HTTP probe. No broken-destination confirmation or UTM validation was possible for the bulk of CTAs.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] 8× repeated HTTP image — "arrow" asset**

The same arrow icon (`b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg`) is embedded 8 times via `http://` (not `https://`):

```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```

Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all block mixed-content images by default. These arrows will render as broken image icons for the majority of recipients.

**[WARN] Missing `alt` text on two images**

- Dynamic content image: `dv.adidas.com/o/61cce83e-8795-4376-bb14-1ad8f845ca50?cp_tp=v3...`
- Tracking pixel / open-pixel image: `click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019f...` (also served over HTTP)

Both lack `alt` attributes. The open-pixel missing alt is low-risk; the content image without alt degrades screen-reader experience and images-off rendering.

**Minor — duplicate `@font-face` declarations**

`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are each declared twice in separate `<style>` blocks (once with a three-font fallback stack, once with two). No rendering breakage, but it adds ~1 KB of dead CSS and indicates a template-composition issue.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**[FAIL] Partial token substitution suspected on `dv.adidas.com` dynamic link**

The `txn=998e1300-710b-11f1-822c-aea38baf8e0c` parameter on the dynamic image URL is consistent with a per-send transaction token. The `/g/` vs `/o/` path discrepancy flagged by QA suggests the token was injected into one path format while the link template expected another. If this image is personalized content (e.g., hero product), a broken token means all recipients see a fallback or broken image.

No un-substituted merge tags (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `[FNAME]`) were detected in the truncated HTML — however, the HTML is truncated so this cannot be confirmed across the full document.

---

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text MIME part absent**

QA confirmed: `Text version is 0 chars`. A missing plain-text part:

- Violates CAN-SPAM best practice (not a hard legal requirement, but flagged by major ESPs)
- Increases spam score on SpamAssassin and Postfix-based filters (notably `MIME_HTML_ONLY`)
- Prevents display in text-only clients and some accessibility tools

**No issues found** on unsubscribe link presence (not visible in truncated HTML, but no flag raised by QA), sender domain authentication headers (DKIM/SPF/DMARC not inspectable from HTML source alone), or physical address (CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5)).

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Cannot confirm** UTM parameter coverage on the 37 click-redirect links — all were skipped by the HTTP probe. The doubled-domain store-locator URL (`/us/stores`) does not show any UTM params in the encoded destination, meaning store-locator clicks would arrive at adidas.com with no campaign attribution.

No issues found with the primary product landing page links that were inspectable (destinations appear to be `adidas.com/us/` paths consistent with the email subject).
