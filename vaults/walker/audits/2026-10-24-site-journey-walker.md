# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 4/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-10-24**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I went through skechers.com on my phone, and I couldn't buy anything. The site looks fine and the text is big enough to read. But the journey broke at the two points that matter:

- **Add to Cart did nothing.** The product page said "Please select a size," and the cart badge stayed at 0.
- **The cart page was a blank white screen.** There was no "your cart is empty" message and no link back to shopping. I'd assume the site was broken and go buy from Hoka or New Balance.

The login step didn't take effect either, so nothing felt personalized. The search for "comfort shoes" put women's shoes first, and I'm a man looking for men's shoes.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** Yesterday was 4/10, so this is a small slide.

I only have yesterday's score, not its screenshots, so I can't say exactly what changed. This is what I saw today:

- **No improvement I could confirm.** Nothing today looks clearly better than before.
- **Cart is a possible regression.** A completely blank cart page is a worse failure than a plain empty-cart message. It may be a loading problem, but I saw nothing on the screen.
- **Add to Cart still fails.** It rejected me with no size chosen, and I had no way to know that without scrolling back up.

## 3. What's Working

- **The header is easy to use.** Search, account, cart and MENU icons are large and well spaced, and MENU is labeled in words. That helps a 62-year-old who doesn't trust a bare hamburger icon.
- **The comfort row is on target.** "Slip-ins, Wide Fit, Arch Fit, Max Cus…" sits right under the header. That's my vocabulary, and it speaks to my feet, not the logo.
- **The men's menu is clear.** It has big rows with chevrons: Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, and so on. Text is large, and Back and X are easy to find.
- **The Men's Shoes page leads with what I want.** "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" are the first tiles. The Filter, Sort, Size and Width buttons are large, and Width matters to me.
- **The product page is readable.** The title is large, there's a 4.5-star rating with 181 reviews, and the $165.00 price is bold. Klarna/Afterpay payment plans are shown.
- **The size buttons are big enough.** They're easy to tap with a thumb, and the sold-out sizes (12.5, 14.0) are crossed out with a "notify me" bell. The blue Add to Cart button is full-width and obvious.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart is blank.** I got no items, no empty message, and no way forward.
- **Add to Cart fails silently.** The red "Please select a size" message sits far below the product photo, so it's easy to miss. I'd tap Add to Cart, see nothing happen, and give up.
- **There's no personalization.** After the login step I still saw "Login | Details" and a generic banner, so I was never signed in. The $5 member reward and "20% OFF for Members" lines are aimed at people who aren't logged in. Nothing shows my size, a past order, or "welcome back."
- **The hero image sells lifestyle, not comfort.**
  - It's a group of young people in a back alley, tagged "AI-generated models."
  - It has no headline and no button to tap.
  - It doesn't say cushioning, arch support or slip-on, and it doesn't look like me.
- **The price math is confusing.**
  - The top banner says "20% OFF Sitewide Applied at Cart."
  - The $165 shoe I picked says "Excluded from promotions."
  - Search results say "20% OFF for Members! Applied at Cart."
  - That's three claims that contradict each other, and I can't tell what I'd pay. I don't trust a discount I can't see.
- **The product page buries the comfort details.** In the first screen I saw no arch support, cushioning or slip-in explanation, and nothing about how the shoe feels. The photo is pushed below the price and payment plans.
- **There's no comparison against Hoka, Brooks or New Balance.** I'm comparing brands, and nothing here helps me make that call or shows value for the money.
- **Search put women's styles first.** The first two results were a pink women's sneaker and a women's wedge slip-on. For a men's shopper that isn't relevant. 1,648 results is far too many for a short query.
- **Some text is cut off.** The promo bar scrolls sideways and cuts off ("Applied c…"), and the pill row clips ("Max Cus…", the "Wi…" filter). The pills have no visual cue that they scroll.
- **The popup step showed nothing to dismiss.** The only change was the banner rotating to "25% OFF Work & Safety."

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the blank cart first.** Show real contents, or at least a clear "Your cart is empty. Keep shopping" message with a button. This is the most urgent problem.
2. **Make a missed size obvious.**
   - Scroll to the size grid and highlight it, or show a pop-up message.
   - Better, keep the red message right next to the Add to Cart button.
3. **Confirm the add worked.** Show a "Added to cart" panel with the item and a big "View Cart" button, and make the badge count go up.
4. **Make login stick.** Show my name or "Hi, Walker" after signing in, and drop the member-reward banner once I'm signed in.
5. **Use one clear price message.** Show the real price on the product page and don't show "20% off sitewide" on shoes that are excluded.
6. **Put comfort first on the product page.** Add a short bullet list near the top:
   - Slip-in, hands-free
   - Arch support
   - Cushioning level
   - Wide widths available
7. **Rank search by what I'm shopping for.** When I've been browsing Men's, show men's results first, and offer a "Men's / Women's" choice at the top.
8. **Add a "why Skechers" comparison.** A small chart against Hoka, Brooks and New Balance on price, cushioning and width options would help.
9. **Give the hero a headline and a button.** Something like "Comfort that fits your day. Shop Slip-ins" would do, ideally with an older person who looks like me.

## 6. Bottom Line

I like the idea that Skechers is built around comfort, and the menu and filters are decent. But I didn't get a shoe in my cart. Add to Cart didn't clearly work, the cart page was blank, and the discounts didn't add up. I'd leave and shop somewhere that works. **3/10, down a point from yesterday.** If the cart works and the add-to-cart message is clear, I'd put this back above 5.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The journey completed all 8 steps, but several results are unreliable.

- **Bot defense blocked the run.** Six requests to the Kasada endpoint (`/…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.936`) returned 429, and so did `GET /cart/`. The View Cart audit therefore most likely ran against a rate-limit response, not the real cart page.
- **Step URLs didn't change.** Log In and Men category both record `https://www.skechers.com/`, the same as Homepage. These steps were either overlays or SPA transitions that didn't update the URL, or navigation didn't happen.
- **No Web Vitals were captured.** LCP and CLS are missing on all 8 steps, so the performance conclusions below rest on TTFB and the HTML sample only.
- **Real findings:** 17 axe violations (2 critical on the homepage), two third-party stylesheets blocked by CORS, and a heavy third-party tag load.

## Accessibility

17 violations across 3 steps (Homepage 9, View Cart 4, Search 4).

**Homepage**
- **Critical:** `aria-required-parent` and `button-name` (buttons with no accessible name).
- **Serious:** `link-name` (links with no discernible text).
- **Landmark structure:** `landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region`. A nested or duplicate `<main>` suggests template or third-party injected markup.
- **Minor:** `aria-allowed-role`.

**Search "comfort shoes"**
- The same landmark and role issues as the homepage: `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique`, `region` and `aria-allowed-role`.
- These recur across templates, so they are likely in shared layout components.

**View Cart**
- `document-title`, `html-has-lang`, `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one`. A page with no title, lang, main or h1 fits an error or challenge response, and `/cart/` returned 429. Treat these as a likely artifact and re-test the real cart.

**Coverage gap:** no axe results are reported for Log In, Men, Men > Shoes, PDP or Add to Cart. That could mean they are clean or that they weren't scanned. The data doesn't say which.

## SEO

- The homepage HTML has `lang="en"`, and axe raised no `document-title` or `page-has-heading-one` violation for it, so title and h1 are present there.
- Log In and Men category recorded the homepage URL. If category navigation doesn't produce distinct, crawlable URLs, those pages can't be indexed. Verify that real hrefs exist for the nav links.
- Canonical, meta, hreflang, structured data and robots could not be assessed because the HTML samples are truncated before reaching them.
- The cart's missing title, lang and h1 are low-risk for SEO if the page is `noindex`, but confirm that.

## Performance

- **TTFB** is good on most steps: 22–27ms for Homepage, Men > Shoes, PDP, Add to Cart and Search.
- **Elevated TTFB** on Log In and Men category (488ms each) and View Cart (112ms). The identical 488ms on two steps suggests both reported the same navigation entry, which fits the URL observation above.
- **LCP and CLS:** not captured on any step. Check that the PerformanceObserver is registered early with `buffered: true`.
- **Third-party load.** The `<head>` carries more than 20 third-party tags: GTM, Quantum Metric, TikTok, Pinterest, Snapchat, Reddit, Facebook, Nextdoor, Taboola, Bing, Yahoo, Impact, RTB House, Bloomreach, Monetate, Pulse Insights, Tune and Stape. They are `async`, so they don't block parsing, but they compete for network and main thread.
- **Webfont loader.** `webfontloader.js` is fetched from cdnjs. That adds a third-party dependency on the critical text path and a risk of FOUT/FOIT. Self-host it, or use `font-display` with preloaded fonts.
- **A/B testing.** Monetate `custom.js` and `entry.js` can cause layout shift or flicker if they mutate above-the-fold content. This can't be confirmed without CLS data.
- **GTM environment.** The container URL includes `gtm_preview=env-1&gtm_auth=…`, meaning a non-default GTM environment is served. Confirm that is intentional.

## Mobile Optimization

- **Viewport height:** `--vh` is set by JavaScript (6.64px per 1%, so about 664px of viewport). CSS `dvh`/`svh` units would avoid this and the layout shifts it can cause.
- **Touch targets, viewport meta and responsive images:** not assessable from the truncated HTML.
- The `button-name` and `link-name` violations also affect touch and screen-reader navigation on mobile.

## Console & Network Errors

**Network (7):**
- 6× 429 on the Kasada fingerprint endpoint `/…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.936`. Repeated 429s on the bot-defense fingerprint call mean the automated session was throttled or flagged.
- 1× 429 on `https://www.skechers.com/cart/`.

**Console:**
- 6× `Failed to load resource … 429`.
- **CORS blocks.** Two stylesheets were blocked because the response has no `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` header:
  - `x.klarnacdn.net/onsite-messaging/fonts/v1.2/fonts.css`
  - `web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?base=grid&theme=skechers&version=5344fc3831`
- 2× `net::ERR_FAILED` follow the CORS blocks. Klarna messaging fonts and the Stylitics "shop the look" grid styling are likely not applied, so those widgets may render degraded.
- The header says 11 console errors, but only 10 are listed.

## Recommendations

1. **Re-run the audit with the 429s resolved.** Use an allowlisted IP or UA, slower pacing, or a bot-defense bypass for the monitor. Until then, the cart and any step after the first 429 are unreliable.
2. **Fix journey instrumentation.**
   - Assert `location.href` after each step.
   - Capture LCP and CLS with buffered observers.
   - Run axe on every step.
3. **Fix the critical and serious accessibility issues.**
   - Add accessible names to buttons and links.
   - Correct the parent roles on the `aria-required-parent` elements.
   - Remove the duplicate or nested `<main>`.
   - Move `<aside>` elements to top level.
   - Give repeated landmarks unique labels.
4. **Fix the CORS failures.**
   - Remove the `crossorigin` attribute from those two stylesheet links, or ask Klarna and Stylitics to send `Access-Control-Allow-Origin`.
   - Add a fallback so the widgets degrade gracefully.
5. **Reduce third-party contention.**
   - Defer non-essential marketing tags until after load or user interaction.
   - Self-host the font loader.
   - Audit Monetate for layout shift.
6. **Investigate the 488ms TTFB** on Log In and Men category once distinct navigations are confirmed.
7. **Confirm the GTM `env-1` and preview parameters** are intended for production.
