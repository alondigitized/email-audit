# Quilted comfort in every step
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-11**
## Executive summary

- Now THIS is more like it after a week of that pink floral cart nonsense. Up top there's a man in the shot wearing what looks like a men's quarter-zip and clean sneakers, standing right next to the woman — so for once I'm not scrolling past a shoe I'd never touch. "Cozy Fit" and "Comfort Reimagined" — fine, I don't need the marketing-speak, but at least it's pointed at comfort, which is the whole reason I look at Skechers in the first place.
- The shoes themselves, though — that navy one and the brown one under "New to Cozy Fit" — look chunky and a little busy for my taste, more like a dad-sneaker trend piece than something I'd wear to walk the dog. I do like seeing "Arch Fit" and "Slip-Ins" called out lower down as their own tiles — that's the stuff that actually gets me to click, more than "Cozy Fit" branding.
- Bottom line: I'd stop scrolling for a second, glance at "Shop Men," and probably poke at the Arch Fit or Slip-Ins tile before I'd bother with the new "Cozy Fit" line. Wouldn't forward it to a buddy, but I wouldn't delete it on sight either — first one all week that didn't feel like it forgot I exist.

## What's working

- A man is actually in the hero image this time, dressed like someone my age might dress, not another kids' shoe or florals.
- "Up to 40% off select styles" is right at the top, big and easy to read without squinting.
- Separate "Shop Men" button sits right next to "Shop Women" — no digging required.
- "Arch Fit" and "Slip-Ins" tiles further down speak directly to what actually gets me to buy — comfort tech, not a trend name.
- Clean layout, one column, big buttons — easy enough to tap on my phone without missing.

## What's weak

- "Cozy Fit" is a new name I don't recognize and don't care about — I want comfort, not another sub-brand to learn.
- The featured "New to Cozy Fit" shoes look bulky and busier than my usual pick — not what pulls me in.
- Small note under the models says "AI-generated" — that's a little offputting, makes the whole thing feel less trustworthy.
- "25% off apparel" banner in the middle is a distraction — I'm here for shoes, not shirts.
- Text is small in spots (all the fine print, the bottom nav links) — I'd need to zoom to read the terms.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with Arch Fit or Slip-Ins in the hero spot, not "Cozy Fit" — that's the terminology that actually sells me, proven by my own reaction here.
- 2. Drop or shrink the "AI-generated models" disclosure, or use real photography — it undercuts trust the second I notice it.
- 3. Cut the apparel banner entirely from a shoes email, or move it to the very bottom — it's just clutter for someone like me.
- 4. Make the men's shoe example one I'd actually wear — slip-on, simpler profile — instead of the chunky trail-looking one currently up top.
