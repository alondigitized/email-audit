# Find the fit for your foot
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-17**
## Executive summary

- Well, now HOKA's actually talking to me — that's more like it. Ninth email in two-ish weeks and this is the first one that leads with "Wide and X-Wide options," which is the actual thing that keeps a guy my age loyal to a shoe brand. My feet have spread out over sixty-some years and half the walking shoes on the shelf just don't come in a wide enough last. That's the headline that gets my attention, not another neon colorway.
- That said, once I get past the "Go far. Go wide." grid, it's back to the usual HOKA drift — six shoe tiles, most of them labeled "Women," then a road-running guide and a UTMB Chamonix ad that's about as far from my life as it gets. I'm not running a mountain ultra in France, I'm walking the dog and standing at work all day.
- Would I click? Yeah, probably — that "Shop Wides" button, just to see if the Bondi or Clifton actually comes in a 4E. Forward to a friend? Maybe to my buddy Earl who's been griping about his bunions. Wouldn't read past the wide-shoe section though.

## What's working

- "Your fit? Dialed" plus the wide-shoe framing is the first HOKA subject line in this run that actually speaks to a comfort-first, arch-support shopper like me.
- Clear, singular CTA — "Shop Wides" — shows up twice, easy to find, easy to tap.
- The men's Clifton 11, Clifton PRO, and Gaviota 6 are shoes I'd actually consider — simple grid, clean product shots, no clutter.
- No render bugs I can see — everything lined up, images loaded fine, text is legible at a decent size.

## What's weak

- Half the shoe grid is women's styles (Clifton 11 Women, Bondi 9 Women, Mach 7 Women) mixed right in with the men's — feels like a generic template, not something built for me.
- The trail-running photo and the UTMB Chamonix mountain-running block eat up the whole bottom half of the email and have nothing to do with wide-fit comfort shoes — total detour.
- No price, no percent off, no member pricing anywhere — just "shop," which doesn't give me a reason to act today versus next week.
- No mention of arch support or cushioning specifics for the wide models — just "our most popular styles," which is vague.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the UTMB/Chamonix block entirely for this send — it's a different audience and it buries the wide-shoe message that's actually working.
- 2. Filter the product grid to men's wide-fit styles only, since that's who clicked in from a "your fit dialed" subject line — the mixed grid dilutes the whole pitch.
- 3. Add a real number — "Wide and X-Wide now in 12 styles" or a price/deal — something concrete to act on.
- 4. Lead the copy with what wide actually solves: bunions, swelling, all-day standing — not just "our signature cushioning works best when it fits."
