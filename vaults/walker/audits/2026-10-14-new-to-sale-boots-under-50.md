# NEW TO SALE: Boots under $50
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-14**
## Executive summary

- The subject said "Boots under $50," so I opened it looking for boots. What I got was the same orange "BUY MORE, SAVE MORE" coupon block I've seen all week, and the boots came second. That's a bait-and-switch of a sort. The only boots in here are women's comfort boots "starting at $49.98." That isn't under $50. It's $49.98 and up.
- There is one thing I like. The Skechers Hands Free Slip-ins block ("up to 20% off") is the closest this email gets to me. A 62-year-old man with bad feet wants slip-ins, and that's the shoe I'd look at. But it's the middle of a long scroll, and the picture is women's styles. The one Skechers men's shoe, the Max Cushion Endeavour, is buried in a grid near the bottom.
- Would I click? Maybe the Slip-ins link, if I had the time. I wouldn't forward it. It's the 8th or so Shoe Carnival email this week, and it feels like a flyer. Mark as read.

## What's working

- The "$10 off $74.98 / $25 off $149.98, code SAVEMORE" block is big and bold. I can read it without my glasses.
- The Skechers Hands Free Slip-ins block is clear about the brand and the feature. That's what I shop for.
- The women's boot photo with the fall leaves feels seasonal and nice to look at.
- The barcodes and "add coupon to your mobile wallet" are handy. I could use them in the store.
- The "Shop Skechers Slip-ins" and "Shop Comfort Boots" links are plain and tell me where I'm going.

## What's weak

- The subject promised "Boots under $50," but the email leads with a coupon block. The only boot price is $49.98 and up, for women.
- I see nothing for men's boots. Nothing here is aimed at a 62-year-old man.
- Nothing is said about arch support or cushioning. Slip-ins is the only comfort feature mentioned.
- The email is too long. There are ten sections, including Afterpay, SMS, a word game, and a rewards box. I'd have quit scrolling before the sneaker grid.
- The fine print at the bottom is tiny. I'd need to zoom in to read the exclusions on the coupon and the Skechers discount.
- The coupon block is the same one from 10/10, 10/11, 10/12 and 10/09. After this many days, it doesn't grab me.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the email match the subject. Put the boots up top, include a men's comfort boot, and show the actual price.
- 2. Move the coupon block down. Give the offer a single line near the top.
- 3. Put a men's Skechers slip-in in the hero, with "cushioned" and "no bending to tie" in the copy.
- 4. Cut the email by half. Drop the Afterpay, word game and SMS blocks, or move them to a footer.
- 5. Make the fine print larger and say what's excluded.

## Technical Audit

**1. Technical Summary**
The truncated source and QA data show one likely tracking error and one unresolved merge token. Both are in the open-pixel URLs. The 88% pass rate has 0 failures and 1 flagged warning, but the HTTP probe skipped all 37 tracked links, so link destinations are unverified.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- **Campaign tag mismatch.** The pixel `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20261014_clogs_birkenstock_AM` carries `utm_campaign=SCA_20261014_clogs_birkenstock_AM`. The subject is "NEW TO SALE: Boots under $50". The tag looks like a stale clogs/Birkenstock template, which would misattribute this send in analytics.
- **Malformed query string.** `t=utm_campaign=...` nests an unencoded `=` inside the `t` parameter value. Some parsers will split or drop it.
- **Trailing zero-width space.** The same URL ends with an invisible U+200B (`...AM​`). It can corrupt the campaign value or break the request.
- **Unverified links.** 37 tracking links were skipped by the probe (click-redirect domains), so none of the redirect chains or landing destinations were checked.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- Two tracking pixels have no `alt` attribute (QA warning):
  - `0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]`
  - `ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?...`

  Add `alt=""` so screen readers skip them.
- **Meta tags missing `http-equiv`.** `<meta content="IE=edge" />` and `<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />` both lack `http-equiv`. The IE=edge directive does nothing, and the content-type declaration relies on the `charset` attribute alone.
- **Doctype mismatch.** The doctype is HTML 4.01 Transitional, but `<html>` declares `xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml"` with self-closing `<meta ... />` tags. This is non-fatal in most clients.
- **Link styling removed.** The global rule `a { text-decoration:none !important; color:inherit !important }` strips default link styling. Links then have no non-colour cue unless the markup adds one. The truncated source doesn't show whether it does.
- **Plain-text part is mostly URLs.** 7337 of 10291 characters (~71%) are URLs, which is a deliverability and readability warning for the text part. It is likely caused by long tracked URLs.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
- **Unresolved placeholder.** The pixel `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]` still contains a literal `[UNIQUE]`. The per-recipient identifier was never substituted. The open event can't be tied to a recipient, and the unrendered token suggests an ESP merge failure.

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
Not verifiable. The HTML is truncated before the footer, and no `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, SPF, DKIM or DMARC header data was supplied. No issues are confirmed. The physical postal address and unsubscribe link need checking against the full source.

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
- The only visible UTM, `utm_campaign=SCA_20261014_clogs_birkenstock_AM`, doesn't match the boots subject line. If this value is also on the click links, landing-page sessions would be attributed to the wrong campaign.
- No `utm_source`, `utm_medium` or landing URLs were visible or probed, so landing-page alignment can't be assessed.

**7. Recommendations**
1. Confirm and correct the campaign ID, so it reflects this boots send rather than `clogs_birkenstock`, across the pixel and all 37 click links.
2. Fix the `[UNIQUE]` substitution in the `0di86k1v` pixel, and check the ESP template for other unrendered tokens.
3. URL-encode the `t=` parameter value and strip the trailing U+200B from the pixel URL.
4. Add `alt=""` to both tracking pixels.
5. Add `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"` and `http-equiv="Content-Type"` to the meta tags, and align the doctype with the markup style.
6. Shorten the tracked URLs or add a more descriptive text part to bring the URL share of the plain text below 70%.
