# Last Chance Deals Inside
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- That countdown clock reading 12:59:59 is the first thing my eye went to — it grabbed me. For about two seconds I thought maybe there was something real at stake. Then I started scrolling and the Inflation Relief banner showed up for what has to be the fourteenth or fifteenth time in ten days, and that urgency started deflating fast. At this point I could paint that red-and-yellow layout from memory.
- Here's my honest take: the email has genuine pieces I care about — HOKA at 25% off is exactly the kind of line item that stops my thumb. But the whole thing leads with Nike, which I'm not shopping for, and the preview text was a raw tracking URL before I even opened it. That's not a great first impression when I'm deciding whether to bother.
- I'd probably scroll through, find the HOKA block, and consider it — but I wouldn't click Shop Nike, and the 50% off deal turns out to be in-store only, which feels like a waste of my time when I'm shopping on my phone. Mark as read with a slight flicker of interest near the middle.

## What's working

- **Countdown timer is real urgency.** "THESE DEALS END TODAY! 12:59:59" reads clearly and creates a pulse I can't fully ignore — it's specific, not just "limited time."
- **HOKA 25% off is buried gold.** HOKA is exactly the kind of comfort walking shoe I compare against Skechers, and 25% off is meaningful money. That section earned a genuine pause from me.
- **Shoe Perks $10 reward is visible.** The loyalty block at the bottom is clean and reminds me I have something on the table — nice that it's not buried.
- **$10 OFF $74.98+ with a code.** An online coupon code I can actually use from my couch — that's how I shop, and it's clearly called out.
- **No render issues.** Everything loaded, no broken images, text is legible without zooming in too hard.

## What's weak

- **Preview text is a raw tracking URL.** Before I even opened this thing, my inbox showed "Shoe Carnival https://click.email.shoecarnival.com/?qs=ABB7..." — that looks like a technical glitch and immediately makes me trust the send a little less.
- **Nike leads, I don't follow.** A 30% off Nike hero is fine for someone who shops Nike, but that's not me — Skechers, HOKA, Brooks, New Balance are where I live. The May 30th subject line that said "Brooks, 25% off Skechers" stopped me cold; this one doesn't lead with anything I want.
- **That 50% off is in-store only.** The big pink block says BUY ANY SHOE GET 50% OFF — then in-store only. I'm on my phone. That's not for me.
- **Cole Haan misses the mark.** $69.98 Cole Haan — those are dress-casual shoes, not comfort walking. Wrong brand for someone shopping cushion and arch support.
- **Fourteen emails in ten days.** The cadence has worn the urgency down. "Last Chance" doesn't land as hard when I've been told last chance three times already this week.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put HOKA (or a comfort brand) in the hero slot, not Nike.** I almost missed the 25% off HOKA deal because it was mid-scroll. Flip the order — lead with the comfort brand offer that matches your subscriber's actual shopping list.
- 2. **Kill the leaked URL preheader immediately.** That's a technical fix but it's urgent — a raw tracking URL in the preview strip is jarring and kills open confidence before the email loads.
- 3. **Name the brands in the subject line.** The May 30th email proved this works: "Cant-Miss Comfort Deals: $30 off Brooks, 25% off Skechers" — I opened that one faster. Vague "Last Chance Deals Inside" squanders the chance.
- 4. **Flag in-store-only deals differently.** Either cut the 50% block from the email entirely or label it "visit your store" before I tap hoping to shop online.
- 5. **Thin the cadence or differentiate the sends.** Email 14 of the same Inflation Relief layout needs something new — different layout, different angle, or fewer sends.
- **Subject Alt A:** `HOKA 25% Off + $10 Off Your Order — Ends Tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Comfort Brands Are On Sale — Hours Left`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `HOKA, Skechers, New Balance — all on sale. Use code SAVE10 at checkout.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `25% off HOKA + $10 off $74.98 online. Deals end at midnight.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email renders with a well-structured responsive CSS stack and no catastrophic HTML errors. Two issues require immediate attention before any future send: an unresolved merge token in a tracking pixel URL, and missing `alt` attributes on seven product/asset images.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[HIGH] Unresolved merge token in open-tracking pixel**
The QA scan found a literal `[UNIQUE]` placeholder in the open-tracking pixel URL:
```
https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]
```
This token was not substituted at send time. The pixel will fire, but the resulting open event will be attributed to the token string `[UNIQUE]` rather than a subscriber ID, corrupting open-rate reporting for this send.

All click links pass through the `emltrk.com` click-tracking domain. Final destination URLs and their UTM parameters cannot be verified from the source alone. No broken-link evidence, but downstream UTM integrity is unverifiable without probing.
