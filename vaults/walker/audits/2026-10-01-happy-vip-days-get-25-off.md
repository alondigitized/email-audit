# Happy VIP Days 🎉 Get 25% off >>>
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-01**
## Executive summary

- Well, this is DSW again, and it's finally a different pitch from the "Extra 10% off clearance" emails I've gotten all week. A giant white "25% OFF*" on a purple banner is hard to miss, even on my phone. That asterisk made me squint, though. The offer is regular-price styles only, and I only found the rest by scrolling to the bottom.
- Then the product grid lost me. It's a clog, a platform bootie, a fuzzy lace-up boot, a leopard ankle boot and a loafer flat. Nothing is slip-on comfort, nothing says cushioned or arch-supported, and nothing looks like it's for a 62-year-old man. There's a MEN link up top, but the products don't back it up.
- Would I click? Maybe the "Shop Men" link, if I were already in a shoe mood. I wouldn't forward it. I'd read it, and it would probably end up in the pile with the other seven.

## What's working

- The "25% OFF*" number is huge. I understood the offer in about two seconds, no reading needed.
- The code VIPFUN is printed right in the hero, so I don't have to hunt for it.
- The barcode for in-store use is handy for someone like me who still walks into stores.
- The size grid is big, readable and easy to tap with my thumb. "Shop Wide Width" is a real plus for a man my age.
- The "Free shipping for VIPs" line and the nav bar (Women, Men, Kids, Brands, Clearance) are plain and easy to follow.

## What's weak

- The asterisk and "Regular-price styles only" are in small text. Clearance is what I've been getting all week, so a regular-price-only deal feels like a bait-and-switch.
- Every shoe in the grid is women's fashion. There's no walking shoe, no cushioned sneaker and no slip-on, and no men's item anywhere in the grid.
- "Extra 25% off for VIPs!" is repeated six times in tiny pink text I can barely read without zooming.
- "Become a VIP" is the main button, but it doesn't say whether it costs anything or what I get. "Join for free" shows up way down at the bottom.
- The fine print is a wall of tiny text on a dark background. I'd never read it on a phone.
- The exclusions list names Skechers among the brands that qualify, but that's buried at the bottom. The comfort brands I shop for, like Hoka and Brooks, aren't mentioned at all.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a men's comfort row in the hero: slip-on walkers, cushioned sneakers, arch-support styles. Make the product grid match the segment the email is sent to.
- 2. Say what's excluded right under the 25%, in readable type. Something like "Regular-price styles, Oct 1-4" would do. Don't make me scroll to find it.
- 3. Make the main button say what it does, such as "Shop Men's 25% Off". Show "Join free" next to the "Become a VIP" button.
- 4. Make the price tags bigger and show real prices, for example "$89.99, now $67.49 with VIPFUN."
- 5. Cut the repeated pink "Extra 25%" captions down to one line.
- **Subject Alt A:** `VIP Days: 25% off men's comfort shoes, Oct 1-4`
- **Subject Alt B:** `25% off walking & slip-on shoes, 4 days only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Code VIPFUN works on regular-price styles through Oct 4. Free to join.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Wide widths, cushioned walkers and slip-ons. Use code VIPFUN.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email has one confirmed broken-experience failure: the SMS opt-in CTA returned HTTP 400 when probed. It also has two missing-alt images, a plain-text-part ratio warning, and malformed charset and IE meta tags. The HTML source was truncated and no headers were supplied, so compliance and UTM checks are limited to what is visible.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **[FAIL] Broken CTA (HTTP 400):** the link text "Opt-In to Texts Get 15% Off" points to `https://ablink.dsw.com/f/a/VSe-1VurDN8vo4k8KWk0Jw~~/AAQRxRA~/epZ2FRWf...` (about 700 characters of encoded redirect path). The ESP click-tracking wrapper returned 400.
  - A 400 on a tracked link can come from a malformed or truncated token, or from the ESP rejecting the probe's user agent. The QA data can't tell these apart, so confirm with a manual click from a real send.
  - If the 400 is real, the SMS-acquisition CTA is dead for every recipient.
- All probed links go through `ablink.dsw.com` redirects, so the final destinations aren't visible in the source.
- Only 12 of 69 links were probed (57 skipped), so other broken links can't be ruled out.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text, tracked image:** `src="https://ablink.dsw.com/q/XKzK0K09Cm1PKf3CCwlG6A~~/AAQRxRA~/zjKQW2ln6DlX..."`. The QA tool reports an alt-less image here, so screen readers and image-blocking clients will have no fallback text.
- **Missing alt text, tracking pixel:** `https://mi.dsw.com/p/up/e6f23fb024fdcb2cc855b9ed672710d4/o.gif?mi_u=...`. A pixel should carry `alt=""`, plus `width="1" height="1"` and `aria-hidden="true"`, so assistive tech skips it.
- **Charset meta is malformed:** `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` has no `http-equiv="Content-Type"`, so the declaration does nothing. The same applies to `<meta content="IE=Edge" />`, which has no `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`. The emoji in the subject and any non-ASCII body characters then depend on the MIME header's charset.
- **Plain-text part is mostly URLs:** `[WARN] 24689/28975 chars are URLs` (about 85%). This is a spam-filter risk and a poor experience for text-only readers. It likely comes from long tracking URLs repeated in the text part.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Priority:** manually click the "Opt-In to Texts Get 15% Off" link from a real send. If it still returns 400, regenerate the link in the ESP, since the token may be corrupted.
2. Add `alt=""` to the `o.gif` pixel. Add meaningful alt text to the `ablink.dsw.com/q/...` image, or `alt=""` if it's decorative.
