# Best styles for walking?
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- "Walk Stars." That headline landed. I'm 62, I walk every morning, and I've been slowly migrating away from my old New Balance standbys toward something with more cushion — so when HOKA uses the words cushioning, support, and lightweight feel in the first breath of the email, they've got my attention. That's not marketing fluff, that's my checklist.
- What I appreciated most is that they didn't bury the walking angle. The past few HOKA emails I've gotten have been trail running drops, "Legacy Pack" color stories in icy blue, and performance stuff aimed at people decades younger than me. This one is built around walking shoes — the Bondi 9, the Transport 2, and importantly, a whole "Available in Wide" section. That wide-width callout feels like they finally remembered I exist. The July 3rd "Get the right fit" send was the last time they spoke my language like this.
- That said, I'd mark this as read, not click-through right away. There's no price anywhere. No "members save X%," no limited-time offer, nothing that gives me a reason to buy today versus thinking about it next week. And the hero shoe is yet another icy-blue tone — this is the third HOKA email in two weeks leaning on that same cool, frosty palette. I get it. But I'd click through eventually on the Bondi 9 men's link, not on the "Shop Walking" category button.

## What's working

- **"Walk Stars" + the sub-copy trio** — "Cushioning? Check. Support? Check. Lightweight feel?" That's exactly how I shop. Whoever wrote that understood me.
- **"Available in Wide" section** — This is the detail that earns trust. Not every brand does this, and seeing Arahi 8 Men listed there made me stop scrolling. Wide sizes are hard to find, especially with the right cushion.
- **Men's versions of every shoe are shown** — Bondi 9 Men, Transport 2 Men, Arahi 8 Men. They didn't just fire-hose me with women's styles and assume I'd sort it out.
- **Clean layout, readable text** — No zooming required on my phone. Big images, short headlines. HOKA knows how to lay out an email.
- **"Top Travel Pick" Bondi section** — I travel light and walk a lot at airports. That framing hit. "Easy to wear and made to be seen" is the right pitch for a guy who wants one shoe that does everything on a trip.

## What's weak

- **No price, no offer anywhere** — This is the biggest miss. I'm a comparison shopper. I've been on the DSW site this week. If you want me to click now and not "maybe later," give me a number.
- **Women's shoes clutter the grid** — In "Let's go," half the tiles are women's styles. Walker (Women), Bondi (Women). If HOKA knows I'm a male subscriber, filter the grid. That half-relevant layout makes me work harder.
- **The hero is the same icy-blue tone as the last two emails** — "New blues" on July 1, "Hot days. Cool tones." on July 5, and now another frosty blue hero shoe. I noticed. Feels like they found one product shot and are riding it.
- **"Bondi 7" in the Travel section vs. "Bondi 9" above** — They just showed me the Bondi 9 in the main grid, then the Travel Pick section features the Bondi 7. That inconsistency makes me pause and wonder if the 7 is a clearance item or if there's a reason for it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price anchor or offer** — Even "starting at $140" or "members get 15% off this week" would give me a reason to click today. Right now this is a catalog, not a pitch.
- 2. **Segment the product grid by gender** — If HOKA has my data (and they should, I've bought from them), show me men's only. Cut the women's tiles entirely. Four clean men's shoes beats a mixed grid of eight.
- 3. **Retire the icy-blue hero for one send** — Any walking-specific color story — Earth tones, slate, brown gum sole — would feel fresher after three consecutive cool-tone emails. The shoe is gorgeous; it just feels like the same shoot.
- 4. **Name the Bondi 9 in the hero** — "Walk Stars" is fun, but linking the model name ("Bondi 9 — HOKA's most cushioned walker") gives me something to search and compare later.
- 5. **Clarify Bondi 7 vs. Bondi 9** — Either explain why the 7 is the "travel pick" (clearance? lower profile? price point?) or just update it to the 9.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate `<meta name="viewport">` tags — confirmed bug**

Two conflicting viewport declarations are present in `<head>`:

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
<meta content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=yes" name="viewport" />
```

The second declaration adds `user-scalable=yes`, contradicting the `maximum-scale=1` cap in the first. Browser behavior on duplicate viewport tags is undefined; some clients ignore the second, others merge them unpredictably. Only one tag should be present.

**Missing `http-equiv` on charset meta — minor**

```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```

Correct form is `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />`. Without `http-equiv`, some older clients (Outlook 2007/2010) may not honor the charset declaration.

**Empty `<title>` tag**

```html
<title></title>
```

Several email clients (Apple Mail, Outlook for Mac) display the `<title>` value in the preview pane or window title bar. An empty tag wastes the slot; it should match the subject line or be omitted.
