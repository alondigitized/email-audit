# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-11-01**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I looked for a comfortable pair of men's slip-ons on my phone, and the trip broke at the cart. The homepage and the men's shoe pages are clean and the text is easy to read. The product page for the Slip-ins Contour Foam Cozy Fit Kiroh looks good. But my Add to Cart tap didn't add anything. The screen said "Please select a size" and the cart badge still read 0. The cart page then loaded as a blank white screen. The search for "comfort shoes" put women's shoes at the top. I didn't buy anything, and I couldn't have.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** That's the same as yesterday, so I see no improvement and no clear regression. I only have yesterday's number, not what it was based on. The browsing is a solid 6 or 7. A blank cart page drags the whole trip down, because I can't spend money there.

## 3. What's Working

- **Readable text and clear header.** The logo, search, account, cart and MENU are all easy to find. The labels are big enough for me without pinching.
- **The men's menu is good.** It opens as a simple list with big rows: Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections and so on. There's a Back arrow and a big X to close it. That's how I like navigation.
- **The right words are up front.** The row under the header says "Slip-ins, Wide Fit, Arch Fit, Max Cus…". Under Men's Shoes, the first tiles are "Hands Free Slip-ins" and "Walking Shoes". That's what I shop for.
- **Product page basics are clear.** The price is $88.00 with a 4.5-star rating and 19 reviews. It shows the monthly payment option, and the "Members Get 20% OFF" line is in red where I can see it.
- **The size grid is easy to tap.** The boxes are large and well spaced. Sold-out sizes are crossed out and have a little bell for notifications. The big blue Add to Cart button is hard to miss.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart is a blank white page (step 8).** There is no message, no header and no way back. A shopper would think the site is broken and leave.
- **Add to Cart did nothing (step 7).** The page said "Please select a size" and the badge stayed at 0. The error is small red text, and it sits above the button, not on it. A person who taps once and sees nothing change would be confused.
- **The size grid is thin where I'd need it.** Sizes 7 through 10.5 are crossed out, and 11.5 is too. The sizes left are 6.5, 11, 12, 13 and 14. That's rough for a typical man's foot, and I'd probably give up here.
- **Search put women's shoes first (step 9).** The first two results were a pink women's Slip-ins shoe and a women's wedge loafer. I'm a man on the men's side. There was no "Men's" filter showing before I scrolled.
- **Nothing visible changed for me after login (step 3).** The screen is identical to the homepage. I saw no "Welcome back", no points balance and no personal picks. I can't tell whether I was signed in.
- **The homepage hero is holiday clothing (step 1).** The first big image is a couple in sweatshirts by a Christmas tree. It says nothing about cushioning or arch support. The "Arch Fit" and "Slip-ins" labels are cut off at the right edge, so I'd have to know to swipe.
- **No comparison or value help.** I saw nothing about how these shoes stack up against Hoka, Brooks or New Balance. I saw nothing on price versus quality or how long a shoe lasts. That's how I decide.
- **The first screenful of the product page is mostly price and financing.** The shoe photo is pushed below the fold. The "Slip-ins" logo is there, but the picture is hardly visible.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the blank cart first.** Show a real cart page, or a clear "Your cart is empty" message with a button back to shopping. Then find out why it came up blank.
2. **Make Add to Cart respond.** If no size is picked, scroll to the size grid and highlight it. Or open a size picker. Don't leave the button silent.
3. **Put the size warning right next to the button**, in bigger type. Better, let me pick a size before the page asks.
4. **Show my size up front.** If my usual size is gone, tell me early and offer similar shoes in stock.
5. **Search should respect the men's section I came from.** Show the men's results first, or put a clear Men / Women switch at the top.
6. **Make login visible.** Show my first name or a "Hi, Walker" in the header, with my points or member price right there.
7. **Lead the homepage with comfort.** Use a slip-in or arch-support image in the hero, and let the Slip-ins, Wide Fit and Arch Fit links wrap so none are cut off.
8. **Add a plain "How it compares" block on product pages.** A short comparison to other comfort brands, a cost-per-wear line, and a note on how the arch support and cushioning work would help me decide.

## 6. Bottom Line

I like Skechers' comfort ideas and the browsing felt easy for an older shopper. But I got to the product, couldn't add it, and then landed on a blank page. A comfort shopper like me would head to Hoka, Brooks or the shoe aisle at DSW instead. The cart has to work, or none of the rest matters.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

This audit has weak evidence, and the journey was probably blocked partway through.

- **Step URLs:** The "Log In" and "Men category" HTML samples both report `https://www.skechers.com/`, and the markup is identical to the homepage. Those steps probably didn't navigate, or they were overlays or soft navigations on the homepage.
- **Bot protection:** Six of the seven network errors are HTTP 429 responses from the Kasada fingerprint endpoint (`/…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.940`). The seventh is a 429 on `/cart/` itself. The automated session was rate limited or challenged.
- **Cart page:** The View Cart axe results (no `<title>`, no `lang`, no `<main>`, no `<h1>`) match a bare 429 error response, not the real cart template. Treat them as an artifact of the block, not as cart defects.
- **Missing data:** LCP and CLS were not captured on any step, and the HTML samples are truncated before `<head>` metadata.

## Accessibility

Axe reported 18 violations across Homepage, View Cart and Search. The View Cart findings are likely artifacts of the 429 page (see above).

**Homepage (10 violations):**
- **Critical, `aria-required-parent`:** An element with a child-only ARIA role (such as `listitem` or `tab`) sits outside its required parent.
- **Critical, `button-name`:** At least one button has no accessible name, likely an icon-only control.
- **Serious, `link-name`:** At least one link has no accessible name.
- **Moderate, landmarks:** `landmark-main-is-top-level` and `landmark-no-duplicate-main` together mean there are multiple or nested `<main>` elements. `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` and `landmark-unique` point to nested or duplicate `aside` and landmark regions.
- **Moderate, `region` and `heading-order`:** Some content sits outside any landmark, and heading levels are skipped.
- **Minor, `aria-allowed-role`:** A role is set on an element that doesn't allow it.

**Search results (4 violations):**
- The same `aria-allowed-role`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region` violations as the homepage. This suggests a shared header, footer or template component.

**View Cart (4 violations, probably the 429 page):**
- `document-title` and `html-has-lang` (both serious), `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one`.
- The homepage markup does set `<html lang="en">`.

## SEO

No full assessment is possible. The `<head>` metadata (title, description, canonical, robots, structured data) was truncated from every sample.

- **Possible crawler impact:** If Kasada returns 429 to crawlers on `/cart/` or other routes, that could affect indexability. Googlebot is normally allowlisted, so verify this.
- **Cart page:** The View Cart response had no `<title>`. That is harmless for a noindex cart but should be confirmed.

## Performance

- **TTFB:** Homepage is 486ms, which is within the "good" range (under 800ms). Log In and Men category are 359ms.
- **Suspect TTFB values:** Men > Shoes, Product detail and Add to Cart are 23–27ms. That is too low for server-rendered document loads over a mobile network, so these were likely soft navigations or cache hits. View Cart is 113ms and Search is 24ms, and both are likely the same.
- **LCP and CLS:** Not captured on any step, so there is no data.
- **Third-party load:** The homepage head loads 25 or more third-party tags. Examples are GTM, Monetate, TikTok, Pinterest, Bing, Yahoo, Taboola, Snapchat, Reddit, Nextdoor, Quantum Metric, Impact, RTB House, BloomReach, Pulse Insights, Tune and Facebook. That is a likely main-thread and network cost.
- **GTM preview environment:** The GTM snippet includes `gtm_preview=env-1&gtm_auth=…`, so a non-production GTM environment appears to be loading on the live site. This is a configuration concern.
- **Font loading:** Fonts are loaded through the JS `webfontloader` from cdnjs (`wf-*-active` classes). This adds a third-party dependency and a font-swap risk (FOIT/FOUT, layout shift) that can't be measured without CLS data.
- **Deprecated `unload` handlers:** See the console findings below.

## Mobile Optimization

- No viewport meta evidence is available, because the head is truncated.
- The inline `--vh` custom property on `<html>` is the JS workaround for mobile viewport height. `dvh` or `svh` units would avoid the script.
- The heavy third-party tag load is the main mobile risk.
- Mobile-specific checks were not possible: tap target sizes, viewport meta and responsive image markup were not in the data.

## Console & Network Errors

**Network (7):** All are 429s.
- Six are `/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.940`, the Kasada bot-defense fingerprint call. It is repeatedly rate limited, which points to the automation being flagged (or to client retry loops).
- One is `GET /cart/`, so the cart was never rendered.

**Console (27 total, 10 listed):**
- **`Failed to load resource … 429`:** These correspond to the Kasada and cart failures above.
- **`Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed`:** A script registers an `unload` listener, which is deprecated and blocked by permissions policy. It is likely from a third-party tag. It also harms bfcache eligibility.
- **`Unsupported Summarizer API languages…`:** This comes from the browser's built-in Summarizer API, called from page or extension code with an unsupported language. It is probably not first-party site code, so identify the caller before treating it as a defect.

## Recommendations

1. **Re-run the audit with the bot-defense block resolved.** Use an allowlisted IP or header, or a slower pace. Confirm each step's URL actually changes, and re-capture the cart accessibility results.
2. **Capture LCP and CLS.** Use `PerformanceObserver` with `buffered: true`, and record hard-navigation TTFB separately from soft navigations.
3. **Fix homepage accessibility:**
   - Add accessible names to icon buttons and links.
   - Fix `aria-required-parent` on the offending element.
   - Remove the duplicate or nested `<main>` and `<aside>` landmarks, and give any repeated landmark a unique `aria-label`.
   - Correct the heading order and wrap stray content in landmarks.
4. **Fix shared-template violations** (`aria-allowed-role`, `landmark-unique`, `region`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`) once. They recur on both the homepage and Search.
5. **Remove the GTM preview environment parameters from production.** Audit the tag inventory and defer or consolidate non-essential pixels.
6. **Find and remove the `unload` listener.** Use `pagehide` or `visibilitychange` instead.
7. **Replace the JS webfont loader with CSS `@font-face`.** Use `font-display: swap` and preload the critical weights.
8. **Verify bot protection allows legitimate crawlers and monitoring.** A 429 on `/cart/` and on the Kasada fingerprint endpoint should not occur for normal users or search engines.
