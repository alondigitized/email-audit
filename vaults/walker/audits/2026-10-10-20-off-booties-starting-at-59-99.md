# 20% off booties starting at $59.99 👀
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-10**
## Executive summary

- DSW again, with the same HAYRIDE 20% code I've been getting since the 8th. This is at least the sixth send. This morning's "20% off retro kicks 👟" was at least about sneakers, which is closer to my feet. This one is booties, and the model is a young woman in a red coat and a fuzzy skirt. I'm a 62-year-old guy who wants cushion and arch support, and I'm not in this email anywhere.
- The green "20% OFF*" banner is huge and easy to read on my phone, and I'll give them that. The small "Shop Men" link under it is the only thing aimed at me, and it's tiny. Below the banner I scrolled through a long run of heeled women's boots at $59.99. Nothing was slip-on, cushioned or arch-supported.
- I wouldn't click and I wouldn't forward it. I'd mark it read and wait for the sale to end. When I want comfort shoes, I go to Skechers or Hoka, and I only come back to DSW if they send men's comfort.

## What's working

- The "20% OFF*" banner is big, white on green, and readable without my glasses.
- The code HAYRIDE is printed right in the banner, so I don't have to hunt for it.
- The black "CLAIM 20% OFF" button is easy to tap.
- The $59.99 price circles on the boots are clear. I know what I'd pay.
- Free shipping, in-store pickup and "Shop wide width" are listed near the bottom. Wide width matters to my feet.

## What's weak

- Every product is a women's bootie with a heel. Nothing here is for a man or for comfort.
- The "Shop Men" link is tiny and sits under a women's-first hero.
- The boots are scattered and cropped at the edges of the screen. It looks artsy, but I can't tell which one is the deal.
- The asterisk hides the limits. The fine print says "regular-price styles only," excludes lots of brands, and gives the real dates (Oct 8–11), none of which is up top.
- The shop-by-size numbers are small for my thumbs, and the fine print is tiny.
- It's the sixth near-identical 20% email this week, and the "so cute / soo good / sooo worth it" copy doesn't speak to me.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment by gender and category. Send me men's comfort and walking shoes, and show cushioned slip-ons in the hero instead of heeled booties.
- 2. Put the real deadline in the hero, such as "Ends Sunday," instead of burying Oct 8–11 in the fine print. Real urgency is better than a sixth reminder.
- 3. Cut the cadence. One strong email beats six copies of the same code.
- 4. Make "Shop Men" a real button, and make the size tiles bigger.
- 5. Put a comfort filter such as "Wide width" or "Arch support" near the top, not near the bottom.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email has a failing SMS opt-in link (HTTP 400) and an empty first-name merge slot ("Hi ,"). The head markup also has two malformed `<meta>` tags and a link-heavy plain-text part. The HTML is truncated, so body, footer and header checks are limited to the QA data.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Broken link (400):** The CTA "Opt-In to Texts Get 15% Off" points to `https://ablink.dsw.com/f/a/fWh12Igg4MX5bteobwazlA~~/AAQRxRA~/6AVFhJwJ…` and returns 400.
  - Caveat: SMS opt-in redirects sometimes reject non-mobile or bot user agents, so a human check on a phone is worth doing. As probed, though, this is a revenue-relevant CTA that fails.
- **Plain-text URL ratio:** 20,775 of 24,816 characters (about 84%) are URLs. This comes from long `ablink.dsw.com` click-tracking wrappers and raises the risk of spam-filter scoring and an unreadable text part.
- **Probe coverage:** Only 12 of 63 links were probed and 51 were skipped, so the other links are unverified.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Malformed `<meta>` tags:** `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` and `<meta content="IE=Edge" />` have no `http-equiv` attribute, so neither declaration takes effect. Charset then depends on the HTTP/MIME `Content-Type` header alone.
- **Missing alt text, tracked image:** The image with src `https://ablink.dsw.com/q/0_LBEpApcXzWG3d3Oe3CmA~~/AAQRxRA~/OKweKf0m…` has no alt text.
- **Missing alt text, tracking pixel:** `https://mi.dsw.com/p/up/e6f23fb0…/o.gif?mi_u=…` has no alt attribute. Add `alt=""` to make it decorative.
- The `<title>` is only "DSW".

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Empty greeting slot [FAIL]:** The greeting renders as "Hi ," because the name field is empty. The template has no fallback, such as a Liquid `| default:` value or a generic "Hi there,".
- The pixel URL carries `mi_u=225090073`, which suggests a recipient ID is resolving, so the empty name is probably a missing profile attribute rather than a broken ID.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Not verifiable:** No raw headers were supplied, so SPF, DKIM, DMARC and `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` can't be assessed.
- The QA note says an unsubscribe link is always probed, and no failure was reported for it.
- The sender is `dsw@e.dsw.com`, a subdomain, and the footer's physical address wasn't visible in the truncated source.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **Wrapped links:** All inspected links are wrapped in `ablink.dsw.com` redirects. The final destination URLs, and any UTM parameters on them, aren't visible in the provided data.
- The only campaign identifier visible is the pixel's `mi_ecmp=20261010_P261408_V1_W-_Offer_Value`.
- I can't confirm that the landing pages match the subject's "20% off booties starting at $59.99" claim.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Manually test the "Opt-In to Texts" link on a mobile device. If it really returns 400, fix the redirect or destination, since the 15% offer is tied to it.
2. Add a fallback for the first-name token, such as `{{first_name | default: 'there'}}`. Alternatively, suppress the greeting when the name is empty.
3. Change the two head tags to `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` and `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />`.
4. Add `alt` text to the tracked image, and `alt=""` plus explicit `width="1" height="1"` to the `o.gif` pixel.
5. Shorten or reduce the tracked links in the plain-text part, or add more real text, to lower the URL ratio.
6. Re-run the audit with full headers and an untruncated body. That would let it check authentication, `List-Unsubscribe`, UTM parameters on the resolved destinations and the other 51 links.
