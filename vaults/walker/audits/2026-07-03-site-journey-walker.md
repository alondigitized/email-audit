# Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Date:** July 3, 2026 | **Yesterday's Score:** 4/10

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Skechers has the right product — Hands Free Slip-ins with Arch Fit are exactly what I came looking for. But this site still keeps getting in its own way. The homepage opens on Women's. The search for "comfort shoes" surfaces Women's products first. The BOGO deal plastered everywhere doesn't apply to the shoe I clicked. And the cart — the one place where Skechers actually closes the sale — rendered as a completely blank white screen. That last one is a dealbreaker. I'm 62, shopping on my phone, and I don't have the patience to reload and wonder if my size was saved or lost. I'm heading to DSW.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

Same score as yesterday. The navigation got cleaner in the middle, but the cart failure and gender-wrong search results are unresolved critical problems. No material improvement where it counts.

---

## 3. What's Working

**The Men's navigation menu (Step 4)** is genuinely good. Clean slide-out, big bold text, plenty of tap space between rows. "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Extra Wide Fit" are all visible without digging — that's the right stuff for a guy like me.

**The Hands Free Slip-ins category page (Step 5)** does the right thing up front: fit selection buttons (Wide Fit / Extra Wide Fit / Medium Fit) are large, blue, easy to tap. I didn't have to hunt for width options. "315 results" tells me they've got inventory.

**The size selector on the add-to-cart screen (Step 7)** is excellent. Each size is its own large, square tap target — no squinting, no fat-finger risk. Full-width blue ADD TO CART button pinned at the bottom is exactly how it should be done.

**The "Excluded from promotions" warning (Step 6)** is honest. I don't love the news, but at least it told me before I got to checkout. That's better than a nasty surprise at the end.

---

## 4. What's Weak

**Cart page is blank (Step 8).** White screen. Nothing. After selecting a product and attempting to add to cart, the cart rendered as an empty white page. This is a complete conversion failure. I have no idea if my shoe is saved, if my size carried over, or if my session even worked. I'm not going to sit here and reload three times — I'm going elsewhere.

**Homepage defaults to Women's (Steps 1–2).** I'm logged in (ostensibly) and the first tab selected is "Womens." Every time. There's a "Mens" tab right there but I have to tap it myself. At 62, if the first thing I see feels like it's not for me, I'm already halfway out the door.

**Search returns Women's first (Step 9).** I typed "comfort shoes" and got 1,417 results — fine. But the two visible products above the fold are both labeled "Women's." No apparent filtering, no personalization, no men-first default. If the site knows I'm a man (I'm logged in), it should act like it.

**Login step left no trace (Steps 1–3).** Steps 1, 2, and 3 look identical. I can't tell if I successfully logged in — no "Hi, Walker" greeting, no name in the header, no points balance, nothing. If I'm a Skechers Plus member and the hero banner is literally advertising member exclusives, the least they can do is acknowledge that I'm one.

**Product is excluded from the deal I clicked through for (Step 6).** The whole homepage screams BOGO 50% OFF. I navigate to a $125 Slip-ins with Arch Fit. First thing below the price: "Excluded from promotions." That's a bait-and-switch feeling, even if it's technically disclosed. Only 5 reviews on a $125 shoe doesn't help either — I need more social proof before I spend that kind of money.

**No comfort technology copy above the fold on the PDP (Step 6).** I see the shoe, I see the price, I see "Excluded from promotions." I do NOT see "ultra-cushioned insole," "arch support pod," "memory foam collar" — none of the language that would make me pull the trigger. The Hands Free / Slip-ins badge is there but it's a logo, not copy that sells me on why my feet will feel better.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** This is a P0 bug. A blank cart is a dead conversion. Check for JavaScript errors, session state issues, or rendering failures on mobile. Every day this persists is lost revenue.

2. **Remember my gender.** If I'm logged in as a male Skechers Plus member, default every tab, filter, and search result to Men's. This is basic personalization that should have been solved two years ago.

3. **Surface comfort tech copy on the PDP above the fold.** One sentence: "Arch Fit insole with podiatrist-certified arch support. Memory foam cushioning. Zero-tie entry." That's what sells me, not the product name.

4. **Don't lead with a deal that doesn't apply.** If a product is excluded from the BOGO promotion, either don't feature it under a BOGO hero banner, or immediately swap the banner message to the applicable offer ("Get an Extra 15% OFF with SALE15"). The current experience feels like a bait-and-switch.

5. **Show me I'm logged in.** A logged-in header should say "Hi, Walker" or show my points. Right now, steps 1–3 look identical — there's no reward for having an account.

6. **Cap search results and auto-apply Men's filter when user is male.** 1,417 results for "comfort shoes" is paralyzing. Default to Men's, apply a comfort technology filter, sort by best-rated. Give me 30–50 options, not 1,400.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

Skechers knows their product lineup for guys like me — Slip-ins, Arch Fit, wide widths. The mid-funnel navigation is better than average for mobile. But they're hemorrhaging conversions at the beginning (wrong gender default), at the search (Women's results for a logged-in man), and at the end (blank cart). The "excluded from promotions" gotcha on a $125 shoe after a BOGO homepage is a trust hit I didn't need. The fundamentals aren't there yet. **Score: 4/10 — no change from yesterday.**

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Site runs on **Salesforce Commerce Cloud (SFCC/Demandware)** — `/on/demandware.static/Sites-USSkechers-Site/` confirms the platform. The audit surface spans 8 steps; critical issues cluster around the View Cart state (missing document metadata), a Kasada bot-protection feedback loop causing 429s that blocked the cart page itself, and excessive third-party script loading across all pages.

---

## Accessibility

**18 violations across 3 pages. 2 critical, 4 serious, 10 moderate, 2 minor.**

**Critical (Homepage):**
- `aria-required-parent`: ARIA role elements rendered outside their required container hierarchy. Typical cause: custom carousel or list components emitting orphaned `role="option"` / `role="row"` nodes.
- `button-name`: Buttons with no accessible label — almost certainly icon-only buttons (hamburger, close, wishlist) missing `aria-label` or visually-hidden text.

**Serious:**
- `link-name` (Homepage): Image links with no alt text or `aria-label`. Likely hero/banner anchor tags wrapping `<img>` with empty or missing `alt`.
- `document-title` + `html-has-lang` (View Cart): The cart page is missing `<title>` and the `<html lang>` attribute entirely. The homepage HTML confirms `lang="en"` is set there — the cart page appears to render as a distinct document state (SPA shell or lightweight page render) that drops these tags. This is a WCAG 2.1 Level A failure and also an SEO defect.

**Moderate:**
- Landmark violations (Homepage + Search): `<main>` appears multiple times, `<aside>` is nested inside other landmarks rather than top-level, and content regions exist outside any landmark. Indicates landmark structure is generated by multiple independently-developed template partials without a unified layout contract.
- `heading-order` (Homepage): Skipped heading levels — probable cause is H2/H3 used for styling rather than hierarchy.
- `landmark-one-main` + `page-has-heading-one` (View Cart): Cart page has neither a `<main>` landmark nor an `<h1>`, consistent with the document-level metadata gap above.

---

## SEO

- **View Cart missing `<title>`**: Cart pages are typically noindexed, but the missing title is a symptom of a broken document shell. If this state is ever crawled or shared as a URL, it surfaces with no title in SERPs.
- **Duplicate GA4 measurement IDs**: `G-BH2DL4JXJL` and `G-XZTV9LQ9DQ` are both loaded via separate `gtag/js` script tags. This double-fires pageview events to two properties, inflating reported traffic and complicating attribution. Verify whether this is intentional (e.g., brand vs. regional property) or a tag management error.
- **Two Monetate entry points**: `se.monetate.net/js/3/...custom.js` and `se.monetate.net/js/2/...entry.js` both load — distinct Monetate SDK versions loading in parallel suggests a migration in progress with the old tag not yet removed.

The 451ms TTFB on Login and Men category is consistent with SFCC's server-side session resolution — these pages likely bypass edge cache because the request carries a session cookie or the category page triggers personalization logic. The other pages hit CDN cache.

**LCP and CLS not captured** across all 8 steps — these are the two Core Web Vitals that most directly affect Google Search ranking signals. The audit tooling needs to capture these; TTFB alone is insufficient.

## Mobile Optimization

- **`--vh` CSS custom property**: `style="--vh: 6.640000000000001px;"` is set inline on `<html>`. This is a JS-computed viewport height workaround for mobile browsers where `100vh` doesn't account for the address bar. The fractional precision (`6.640000000000001`) is a floating-point artifact — functionally harmless but indicates no rounding is applied.
- No `viewport` meta tag visible in the truncated HTML samples, but this is likely present and just truncated.
- No issues identified beyond what's covered in the script weight section above.

## Console & Network Errors

**Kasada bot protection — 429 rate limiting (6 errors):**

```
429 /149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.543
```

This is the **Kasada KapSolve** fingerprinting endpoint. The GUID path is Kasada's browser challenge token. Getting 5 consecutive 429s on this endpoint means Kasada's rate-limit is triggering on its own fingerprint polling — likely because the automated browser session exceeded the polling frequency limit. In a real user session this would be silent, but it indicates Kasada is actively rate-limiting this session. Critically, **`/cart/` also returned 429** — meaning Kasada's bot signal may have caused the cart page request itself to be rate-limited, which would explain any cart loading failure observed during the test.

**Stylitics CORS block:**

```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?...' 
has been blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```

Stylitics (outfit recommendation widget) is serving a CSS file without CORS headers. Since the browser is loading it as a cross-origin stylesheet, the missing `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` header causes a hard block. The widget will render without its styles or fail to initialize entirely. This is a **vendor-side configuration issue** — Stylitics needs to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` (or `*`) to the `style.css` response headers.

**1× `ERR_FAILED`**: A resource request failed at the network level (connection error, not HTTP error). Without the URL this can't be pinpointed from the provided data.

## Recommendations

**P0 — Fix immediately:**

1. **View Cart document shell**: The cart page must emit `<title>`, `<html lang="en">`, `<main>`, and `<h1>`. If the cart is a client-rendered SPA state within the same document shell, the JS that mutates the DOM is not updating `document.title` or the lang attribute. Audit the SFCC cartridge or client-side router responsible for cart page transitions.

2. **Kasada → Cart 429**: The `/cart/` 429 during the test suggests bot protection is interfering with legitimate cart access. Verify Kasada policy rules aren't over-triggering on the cart route. If the test was run from an automated browser (Playwright/Puppeteer), this is expected — but if it reproduces from a real mobile device, escalate to Kasada support with the session ID.

3. **`button-name` and `link-name` (critical/serious a11y)**: Audit all icon buttons and image-only anchor tags. Add `aria-label` to icon buttons; ensure all `<img>` inside `<a>` tags have non-empty, descriptive `alt` text.

**P1 — Fix within sprint:**

4. **Stylitics CORS**: File a ticket with Stylitics to add CORS headers to `web-assets.stylitics.com`. Confirm in your vendor contract who owns this configuration.

5. **Deduplicate Yahoo pixel**: Remove the duplicate `ytc.js` script tag. One is firing double events.

6. **Resolve dual Monetate SDK versions**: Remove either the `js/2/` or `js/3/` Monetate script — running both risks race conditions and doubled experiment assignments.

7. **Clarify dual GA4 IDs**: Confirm whether `G-BH2DL4JXJL` and `G-XZTV9LQ9DQ` are intentionally separate properties. If not, remove the redundant tag from GTM.

**P2 — Address in roadmap:**

8. **Capture LCP and CLS**: Re-run the audit with Core Web Vitals instrumentation. TTFB alone doesn't represent user-perceived load performance; LCP is the primary Google ranking signal.

9. **Landmark and heading structure (Homepage/Search)**: Refactor template partials to emit a single `<main>`, keep `<aside>` at the top-level landmark scope, and enforce heading hierarchy via a component-level convention or linting rule.

10. **TTFB spike on session pages (451ms)**: Profile the SFCC pipeline for Login and Men category pages. If personalization is the cause, evaluate edge-caching the page shell and deferring personalization to a client-side XHR/fetch after initial paint.
