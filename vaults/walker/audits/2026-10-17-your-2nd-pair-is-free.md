# Your 2nd pair is FREE!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-17**
## Executive summary

- "Your 2nd pair is FREE!" got me to open it, then the email told me something different. Below the subject there's a big orange "BUY MORE, SAVE MORE" coupon block. I've now seen that same block in my inbox on 10/11, 10/12, 10/14 and 10/16. The free-pair deal is further down, and it's "BOGO Free Boots and Clogs," marked "select women's." Once again I'm 62 and the headline offer isn't for me.
- I can read the "$10 OFF" and "$25 OFF" without my glasses, and the SAVEMORE code is plain. But this is the same email I got yesterday with the hero image swapped. The only kid in the pictures is a girl in tall boots. There's nothing for a man who wants a cushioned, slip-on walking shoe.
- I wouldn't click, and I wouldn't forward it. I'd mark it read. The $10 reward and the "MENS" link in the bottom nav are the only parts that speak to me.

## What's working

- The "$10 OFF $74.98+" and "$25 OFF $149.98+" numbers are huge and high-contrast. I can read them on my phone.
- The code SAVEMORE is easy to spot, and there's a "Get Coupon" link right under it.
- The mobile wallet section has barcodes I could scan in the store. That's practical.
- The Shoe Carnival logo up top matches the store I know, so I trust it's real.
- The $10 Perks reward at the bottom is a nice touch for a member.

## What's weak

- The subject says "2nd pair FREE," but the body limits it to select women's boots and clogs. That feels like bait-and-switch.
- There's no men's product anywhere. I see women's boots and a little girl in tall boots. Nothing is cushioned, arch-supported or slip-on.
- It's the same orange coupon block I've seen all week. I know it by heart, and the email doesn't treat me like a known customer.
- The fine print is tiny and I can't read it on my phone. That's where the exclusions are, and Hoka, Brooks and New Balance are on the excluded list.
- It's a very long scroll. The Word Stride game, the Afterpay box and the SMS box pile on and distract from the shoes.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the subject and the hero match. If the free pair only covers women's boots, say so, or lead with a men's offer for men on the list.
- 2. Send me something men's. A slip-on or walking shoe with cushioning and arch support would earn a click.
- 3. Change the coupon block when you send daily. Seeing it five days running makes me tune out.
- 4. Make the fine print bigger and put the main exclusions up top.
- 5. Cut the games and extra boxes and link one hero product, not a general sale.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible markup is a conventional table-based email with responsive CSS. The QA run found no hard failures (86% pass, 0 issues, 1 warning). The HTML is truncated, so the body, footer and headers couldn't be checked, and the 25 tracked links were not probed. The confirmed defects are an unresolved `[UNIQUE]` token and a stray zero-width character in two tracking-pixel URLs.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Zero-width character in a URL:** The image `src` is `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20261017_bogof_tall_boots_AM​`. It ends with an invisible U+200B after `_AM`. This can corrupt the campaign value when the request is parsed or logged, so `AM` may not match your reporting.
- **Odd parameter structure:** The same URL nests a query string inside `t=` (`t=utm_campaign=...`) without encoding the `=`. Whether the ESP's tracker can parse this couldn't be verified.
- **Redirect destinations unverified:** 25 `emltrk.com` click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe, so their destinations, status codes and redirect chains are unconfirmed.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** QA flagged two `<img>` elements, `ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m…` and `0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v…`. Both are probably tracking pixels. If so, they should carry `alt=""` and a presentational role rather than no `alt` attribute. If either is a content image, it needs descriptive alt text.
- **Links lose their visual cue:** The global rule `a, a:link, a:visited, … { text-decoration:none !important; color:inherit !important; }` removes underline and colour from every link. Text links then rely entirely on surrounding styling to be recognisable.
- **Small text on mobile:** `@media (max-width: 600px) { .responsive-text { font-size: 12px !important; } }` sets 12px on small screens, and 14px on wider ones. That is smaller than desktop, the reverse of the usual pattern.
- **Malformed head markup:**
  - `<meta content="IE=edge" />` has no `http-equiv`, so it does nothing.
  - `<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />` is a malformed hybrid. The `charset` attribute still works.
  - The HTML 4.01 doctype is combined with `xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml"`, which is inconsistent. This is low severity.
- **No issues found** in the viewport meta, `lang="en"` or `color-scheme` declarations.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Unresolved token:** `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]` still contains the literal `[UNIQUE]` placeholder. It was not substituted at send time, or the relay stripped it. If this is the open-tracking pixel, per-recipient opens won't be attributed.
- No other merge tokens were visible in the truncated source.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Authentication unverified:** No `Authentication-Results` header was found. QA attributes this to the AgentMail relay, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. This is a gap in the test setup, not a confirmed failure. Verify using the original headers from a direct-delivery copy.
- **Not assessable:** The unsubscribe link, `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers, and the physical postal address are all outside the truncated HTML or header data. The sender is `shoecarnival@email.shoecarnival.com`, and nothing in the evidence shows a violation.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- The only UTM value visible is `utm_campaign=SCA_20261017_bogof_tall_boots_AM`. It does match the subject's BOGO/"2nd pair FREE" offer and the 2026-10-17 send date. However, it appears only in a pixel URL, and the corruption noted in section 2 affects it.
- `utm_source` and `utm_medium` were not visible. Click-link destinations and landing-page alignment couldn't be verified because the links were skipped and the HTML was truncated.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the U+200B from the `t=utm_campaign=…_AM` value, and URL-encode the nested parameter.
2. Fix the `[UNIQUE]` token in the `0di86k1v.emltrk.com` pixel so it resolves per recipient.
3. Add `alt=""` to both tracking pixels, or real alt text if either is a content image.
4. Drop the `!important` link resets, or restore an underline on in-copy links. Raise `.responsive-text` on mobile to at least 14px.
5. Fix the meta tags by changing the second to `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">` and the first to `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge">`. Align the doctype and `xmlns`.
6. Re-run the audit with the full, untruncated HTML. Also capture the original headers (`Authentication-Results`, `List-Unsubscribe`) and probe the final destinations of the `emltrk.com` links, so sections 5 and 6 can be assessed.
