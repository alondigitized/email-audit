# Join VIP -> Get access to 20% off
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-29**
## Executive summary

- DSW again. This is the ninth send or so in about four days. Yesterday it was "Become a VIP & get 20% off!" with the same FABULOUS code, and now the subject is "Join VIP -> Get access to 20% off." It's the same offer in a new outfit. The pink hero with the big "20% OFF*" is easy to read, but the asterisk and the "Regular-price styles only" line right under it tell me it's not much of a deal.
- I'm a 62-year-old man looking for cushioned slip-ons, and nearly everything below the hero is women's. I see mary janes, stilettos, a pointy slingback, a knee-high boot and a leopard heel. The only men's thing is a "SHOP MEN" text link in the hero. No walking shoe, no cushioning, no arch support. This was meant for somebody else.
- I'd mark it read and move on. I wouldn't click, and I'd never forward it. I'm getting tired of the daily DSW nudge.

## What's working

- The "20% OFF*" in the pink hero is huge and I can read it on my phone without zooming.
- The code FABULOUS is printed right in the hero, so I don't have to hunt for it.
- The nav bar has MEN and KIDS next to WOMEN, and the hero has a "SHOP MEN" link, so I can get to my section.
- The size grid is big and easy to tap, and "Shop Wide Width" is a nice touch for people with wider feet.
- The footer is clear: free shipping, in-store pickup and the app are laid out simply.

## What's weak

- The product grid is all women's dress shoes and heels. Nothing in it says comfort, cushioning or arch support.
- The "BECOME A VIP" button makes me sign up before I get a discount. It's a membership pitch more than a sale.
- The asterisk and the "Regular-price styles only" line take some of the shine off the 20%. The long fine print at the bottom also lists lots of exclusions, including Skechers, which is a brand I shop.
- Every shoe has the same small red "Extra 20% off for VIPs!" caption. It's tiny, it's repeated ten times, and there are no prices to compare.
- It's the same offer I've seen all week, so the urgency doesn't feel real. This is a rerun of the 20% VIP email from yesterday.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment by gender. Send me men's comfort and walking shoes from brands like Skechers, Hoka and Brooks instead of heels.
- 2. Put real prices and a "was/now" price on each shoe, so I can do the price-versus-quality math.
- 3. Drop the sign-up wall, or say plainly that VIP is free and takes a minute.
- 4. Cut the cadence. One strong email beats nine near-identical ones.
- 5. Add a "Slip-ons & easy-on comfort" tile in the "That's not all, folks" section, next to the women's categories.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **[FAIL] Broken CTA (HTTP 400):** The link text is "Opt-In to Texts Get 15% Off". The URL is `https://ablink.dsw.com/f/a/ll4d5mLUZBp8fU5opKCtpg~~/AAQRxRA~/b4D9Tpbm0o…` (a very long SendGrid click-tracking URL).
  - This is one of the 12 links probed, and the only one that failed.
  - A 400 on a tracking redirect can mean a malformed or expired token. It can also mean the redirector rejected the automated probe.
  - Re-test in a real client or with a browser User-Agent before treating it as a confirmed production break. If it does fail for real users, it breaks the SMS opt-in conversion path.
- **Link coverage:** 57 links were skipped, mostly footer, utility and social links. Their status is unverified.
- **Tracking pixel:** `https://mi.dsw.com/p/up/e6f23fb0…/o.gif?mi_u=225090073&mi_ecmp=20261029_P251285_V1_W-_Offer_MI_DY` carries a user identifier (`mi_u`) and a campaign ID (`mi_ecmp`). This is expected and not a defect.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (2 images):**
  - `https://mi.dsw.com/p/up/…/o.gif`: this is a tracking pixel. It should have `alt=""` (plus `role="presentation"`), which is a low-severity issue.
  - `https://ablink.dsw.com/q/Q8j1ewqRhfBXP-s0eCrlaA~~/AAQRxRA~/s01sbU1J…`: this looks like the SendGrid open-tracking image, since it uses the `/q/` path rather than `/f/`. If so, it is also a pixel. The source is truncated, so I couldn't confirm it.

  Without `http-equiv="Content-Type"` and `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`, they don't declare anything. The charset is typically still set by the HTTP header (`Content-Type`), so this is low-risk.
