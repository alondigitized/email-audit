# FINAL DAY: Extra 20% OFF Your Purchase + up to 60% off Black Friday Preview Sale
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-15**
## Executive summary

- It's Shoe Carnival again, and it's the same Nov 14 "2 Days Only" email in a new coat. The top is easy to read on my phone: a big red "20% OFF" with "FINAL DAY" and the code HOLIDAY20. That part I like. Then the 60% Black Friday gold banner crowds in right under it, so I'm not sure which deal is the main one.
- The rest is a holiday catalog. There's a Crocs tile, two people in pajamas and slippers, Birkenstock, women's boots BOGO, and women's clogs. I'm a 62-year-old man looking for cushioned, arch-supported, slip-on shoes. Nothing here is for me, apart from maybe the Birkenstock sandals. Crocs and Birkenstock are brands I'd cross-shop, but the tiles show no men's styles and nothing about comfort.
- I'd skim it, maybe screenshot the code, and move on. I wouldn't forward it. I've had close to ten of these in two weeks, and "FINAL DAY" has lost its meaning. The Nov 11 Veterans Day email said "Final Day" too, and so did the Nov 8 Nike and Jordan one.

## What's working

- The big red "20% OFF" with HOLIDAY20 is readable without my glasses.
- "Even on sale prices!" is a nice honest touch, because it tells me the code stacks.
- Birkenstock at "Starting at $44.98" is a concrete price I can judge. It's one of the few things here that fits my comfort-shoe habits.
- The Shoe Perks strip at the top and the "$10 reward" box near the bottom are visible, but I won't buy on points alone.
- The tap targets ("Shop Now", "Shop Birkenstock") are clearly marked.

## What's weak

- Two offers, 20% off and up to 60% off, fight for the top spot, so I can't tell which one is real for me.
- The imagery is mostly women, kids and pajamas. There are no men's walking, slip-on or cushioned shoes anywhere.
- The "FINAL DAY" claim feels stale, since this brand has used it several times in the past week.
- The fine print at the bottom is tiny, and the asterisks on 20% and 60% make me wonder what's excluded.
- It's a very long scroll. The Crocs tile is just a gold box with a logo and no product or price.
- The barcode, Word Stride game, SMS and Afterpay boxes at the bottom are clutter I'll never use.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one offer, the 20% code, and move the 60% banner below it. Right now they're both shouting.
- 2. Add a men's comfort row with slip-ons, walking shoes and cushioned sneakers (Skechers, Hoka, Brooks, New Balance), with prices and arch-support or cushioning callouts.
- 3. Retire "FINAL DAY" unless it really is the last day. Use the real end time instead.
- 4. Put prices on the Crocs tile and show an actual shoe.
- 5. Cut the game, barcode and Afterpay boxes, and make the fine print bigger.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **27 tracking links were not probed.** The QA tool skipped them as click-redirect domains, so none of the destinations or redirect chains are verified.
- **Malformed pixel URL:** `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20261115_crocs_birkenstock_clogs_AM​`
  - The `t=` value holds a nested `utm_campaign=` key/value pair, so the parameter is nested and ambiguous.
  - The URL appears to end in a zero-width character (U+200B) after `_AM`. That can break campaign matching on the tracking side.
- **Unresolved token in pixel URL:** `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]`. See section 4.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on 3 images (QA WARN):**
  - The content image `.../m/1/26dd6216-979c-44ac-9738-3cc0e8befe11.jpg` has no alt, so it is invisible to screen readers and shows nothing when images are blocked. This is the substantive issue.
  - The two `emltrk.com` tracking pixels also lack alt. This is low impact, though `alt=""` is the correct fix for decorative pixels.
- **Ineffective meta tag:** `<meta content="IE=edge" />` has no `http-equiv`, so it does nothing. `<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />` also lacks `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. The `charset` attribute still works in HTML5 parsers.
- **Mixed markup standards:** The HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype is combined with an XHTML `xmlns` attribute and self-closing `<meta ... />` tags. This is not harmful in email clients, but it is invalid markup.
- **Global link reset:** `a, a:link, ... { text-decoration:none !important; color:inherit !important; }` removes default link styling everywhere. Links not given explicit styling elsewhere have no non-colour affordance. The truncated source doesn't show whether they are styled.
