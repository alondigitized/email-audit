# Download the app, get 25% OFF!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- DSW lands in my inbox again — third time in two days pitching this same "download the app, get 25% off" deal. June 1 it was "Shop the app ➡ Get 25% off!" and earlier today it was "In the app: 25% OFF & best sellers." Now here's a third one dressed up with a music festival theme. I'll give them credit for trying new creative — "LIVE YOUR FEST LIFE" is a different angle than the straight discount pitch — but the angle is completely wrong for me. I'm 62 years old. I'm not going to Coachella. I'm not "living my fest life." That hero headline might as well be in a foreign language for all it applies to my situation.
- The models are all young women. The sandals and slides on display are festival fashion, not what I'd reach for on a long walk or a full day on my feet. The "SH😮EMERGENCY" pun is cute but it's clearly written for someone scrolling TikTok, not someone like me who just wants to find a comfortable slip-on that won't wreck my arches by noon. DSW knows I shop there. They've sent me enough emails. None of this says "hey Walker, we thought of you."
- I'd mark it as read and move on. Wouldn't click, wouldn't forward it to anybody I know.

## What's working

- **DSW name in the sender field** still gets the email opened — that brand has enough muscle memory from my actual purchases that I'll at least glance.
- **25% off is a real number** — buried in the subject line but at least it's concrete, not "save big" vagueness.
- **"SHOP BY SIZE" section** is the one piece of this email that speaks to how I actually shop. Give me my size, show me what's available. That's useful.
- **No obvious render bugs** — the layout held together, nothing overlapping or broken in the render.

## What's weak

- **"LIVE YOUR FEST LIFE" hero is aimed at someone half my age** — I have zero connection to this framing, and it puts me off before I've scrolled an inch.
- **Every model in this email is a young woman** — not a single demographic signal aimed at a 62-year-old man. DSW has my purchase history. They know who I am. This email doesn't.
- **The app-download condition is friction I don't want** — they already hit me with this same mechanic yesterday and the day before. Asking me to download an app three times in two days doesn't make me more likely to download it; it makes me more likely to tune out.
- **"SH😮EMERGENCY" and "fest life" language** — this is written for Gen Z. I'm not judging the audience it targets, but I'm not in that audience, and DSW's CRM should know that.
- **Preview text is leaking invisible padding characters** — "Be the best dressed at every fest 🎤🎶" followed by a wall of blank space in my inbox. Looks sloppy even if the real copy line is passable.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the list.** If DSW wants men 55+ to click on a summer footwear offer, show them men's shoes — walking sneakers, cushioned sandals, slip-ons. The "fest life" creative is fine for a younger segment, but it should not be landing in my inbox.
- 2. **Drop the app-download gate, or at least rotate the offer.** Three identical mechanic emails in 48 hours is fatigue-inducing. If the deal requires the app, fine — but show me something worth downloading the app for. Not the same pitch three times.
- 3. **Put comfort language in the hero.** "Walk all day, feel great all summer" — something that signals DSW knows I care about how my feet feel, not how I look at a festival.
- 4. **Give the 25% off a deadline.** "This weekend only" would actually create urgency. Right now there's no reason to act today versus next week.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your feet will thank you. 25% off all weekend.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cushioned sandals, arch-support sneakers — find your summer fit`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Download once, save 25% — our best men's styles are waiting`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<meta>` charset tag missing `http-equiv`:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv="Content-Type"`, this tag has no semantic effect in XHTML. Charset is inferred from the sending server's `Content-Type` header, which usually saves it — but this is a latent defect.

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom (WCAG 1.4.4):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Disabling pinch-to-zoom fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Standard practice is to omit `maximum-scale` entirely.

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`:**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">
```
No `lang="en"` declared. Screen readers default to OS language setting, which will mispronounce content on non-English systems.

**Non-standard CSS property capitalization in `@font-face`:**
All 10 `@font-face` blocks use `Src:` (capital S) instead of `src:`. CSS property names are case-insensitive in spec and most parsers handle this silently, but it is a malformed declaration that could fail in strict or legacy renderers.

**HelveticaNow fonts use TTF only:**
The four newer HelveticaNow variants (`Regular`, `Medium`, `Bold`, etc.) are served as `.ttf` with no `woff` or `woff2` fallback. The legacy Futura PT variants correctly use `.woff`. TTF works in Apple Mail and iOS Mail, but WOFF2 is ~30% smaller and preferred.

**Near-black `#000001` is intentional but undocumented:**
Used as "black" in dark mode classes and border declarations. This is a known technique to prevent Outlook from auto-hyperlinking dark text. No defect, but worth flagging for maintainers who may not recognize the convention.
