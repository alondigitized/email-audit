# Some Sequels Play It Safe…
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- I open this one and I'm staring at a shoe that looks like it was designed for a 26-year-old doing CrossFit. The Echo II is bold alright — chunky, wavy sole, loud colorways, big "EVEN BOLDER" energy. Crocs keeps whispering "comfort underneath it all" in the copy, but the whole email is a fashion/aesthetic pitch. Nothing in here actually *shows* me the comfort story. No cushioning language, no arch support, no "walks all day" callout. Just "BOLD. SCULPTED. NEW." stacked twice and called it a day.
- Then I scroll to the Recommended for You tiles and one of the four is the **Kids' Classic Clog**. I'm a 62-year-old man. That recommendation doesn't reflect anything about me as a customer — it feels like they grabbed the generic template and didn't bother to filter for adult male. Throw in a "Shop Jibbitz™ Charms" CTA button and I'm wondering if Crocs even looked at who's on this list.
- Clean photography, no broken images, readable on my phone. The product does look interesting to me — thick sole, looks cushioned. But with no price in the email and no comfort specifics, I can't do my usual cost-benefit math. I'd mark this read, maybe remember it if I end up on the site anyway, but I'm not forwarding it and I'm not clicking through with intent to buy.

## What's working

- Product photography is genuinely strong — the Echo II pops against those dark, organic-textured backgrounds
- Layout is clean and mobile-readable; no visual clutter, no broken images
- One line of body copy earns its keep: *"new everything except the comfort it's known for"* — that speaks directly to the reason I'd consider a Crocs shoe
- This is a fresh product story, not another clearance blast — after a few weeks of "Extra 30% Off" I at least feel like they're telling me something new
- Crocs brand identity is consistent — immediately recognizable from the logo to the photography style

## What's weak

- Kids' Classic Clog in the "Recommended for You" section — at my age that's just a segmentation miss that makes me distrust the whole recommendation block
- "Shop Jibbitz™ Charms" CTA is noise for a 62-year-old male; I don't decorate shoes
- No price anywhere in the email — I have no way to do even a rough value check before committing to a click
- The entire visual hierarchy shouts *fashion*, not *comfort* — the one audience I'd actually want to convert (guys like me who buy Crocs for feel, not looks) never gets the reassurance they need
- Preview text is completely suppressed — blank space in my inbox where a hook should be; zero pull from the inbox view

## Recommendations

- 1. **Flip the copy hierarchy.** Lead the hero with the comfort claim, not the aesthetic claim. "Same legendary comfort. Completely new look." converts someone like me; "EVEN BOLDER" does not.
- 2. **Add a price anchor.** Even "Starting at $X" gives me something. Without it, I'm clicking blind.
- 3. **Fix the Recommended block.** Remove Kids' products for an adult male segment. Replace with men's clogs, walking sandals, or the LiteRide line — products that actually match my purchase history signal.
- 4. **Kill or replace the Jibbitz™ CTA.** Swap it for "Shop Men's Clogs" or "Explore Echo II" — something that connects to the hero product I just looked at.
- 5. **Write a real preheader.** Right now my inbox shows the subject and then silence. One line of copy there would lift open rate meaningfully.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The Echo II: Bolder Look, Same All-Day Comfort`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Meet Crocs' Most Sculpted Clog Yet`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New design, same comfort you already trust — the Echo II just landed.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Everything's new except the feel. Shop the Echo II Clog.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**7 images served over HTTP** (mixed content) — will be blocked by most modern clients when the email is viewed over HTTPS, rendering those images as broken:

| Image | HTTP URL |
|---|---|
| App Store badge | `http://image.crocs-email.com/.../9c36c0ef-....png` |
| Google Play badge | `http://image.crocs-email.com/.../13246762-....png` |
| Find Your Store | `http://image.crocs-email.com/.../c4df71ec-....png` |
| Instagram icon | `http://image.crocs-email.com/.../e18949ff-....png` |
| Facebook icon | `http://image.crocs-email.com/.../80f68cd8-....png` |
| TikTok icon | `http://image.crocs-email.com/.../51d8144e-....png` |
| YouTube icon | `http://image.crocs-email.com/.../1187ef58-....png` |

All seven share the same base `http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/` — a single CDN path that should be upgraded to HTTPS.

**5 images missing alt text:**
- Open-pixel (`demdex.net/event?...`) — should carry `alt=""`
- 4 personalized recommendation images (`igodigital.com/rr/v2/.../walker@agentmail.to?category=Recommended for You`) — should carry descriptive or empty alt text

**`<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` is missing its `http-equiv` attribute** — should be `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />`. Most clients infer this but it is technically malformed.

Custom font (`TT Crocs`) loaded via `@font-face` — unsupported in most email clients (Gmail, Outlook). `font-display: swap` is a web CSS property ignored in email; it introduces no harm but adds dead weight.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

Recommendation images resolve at send time via iGo Digital:
```
https://10745993.recs.igodigital.com/rr/v2/685c6ddd06fd7c003f1cd785/i/1/walker@agentmail.to
  ?category=Recommended for You
```
The email address (`walker@agentmail.to`) appears literally in the image URL — this is expected for server-side personalization but means the URL contains an unencoded `@` character. The space in `Recommended for You` is also unencoded; while many servers tolerate this, it should be `Recommended+for+You` or `%20`-encoded for strictness.

No unresolved merge tokens (`{{`, `%%`, `[%`) observed in the truncated source.
