# Wides go hard
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-28**
## Executive summary

- Well, now — "Wide sizes that perform" — finally somebody at HOKA remembers there's a fella out here with a wider foot who's tired of shoes squeezing his pinky toe all day. That caught my eye right off, more than anything they've sent me lately. But the six shoes lined up under it are a mixed bag — some men's, some women's, and no wide-fit badge or width label I can actually see on any of them, so I'm left wondering if the Bondi 9 I liked even comes in wide.
- The rest of it is the same HOKA playbook — a guy's foot mid-stride, a "Find Your Best Fit" quiz, a text-signup shipping deal. None of that's for me. I'm not signing up for texts, and I don't need a quiz to tell me I need a wide shoe — I just told them that.
- I'd click through to look at the Bondi 9 or the Bondi SR since those look like something I'd actually wear, but I wouldn't forward this to anybody. It's a decent open for once, still a middling click.

## What's working

- "Wide sizes that perform" is the first HOKA subject line all month that's actually about something I need.
- The hero banner is clean and readable — big bold type, no clutter.
- Bondi 9 and Bondi SR look like real walking shoes for a man my age, not race gear.
- "Everyone needs the right fit" is a decent, non-condescending line — doesn't talk down to me.

## What's weak

- Not one shoe in the grid is labeled "Wide" or "X-Wide" — the whole email is about wide sizing and I can't tell which of these six actually come in my width.
- Three of the six shoes are women's models, which don't do me any good.
- The "Find Your Best Fit" section drags in a guy running outdoors again — same trail-runner imagery they keep leaning on.
- Text-signup-for-shipping gimmick again — I don't text with companies, never will.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a "Wide / X-Wide available" tag directly on each product tile — that's the whole point of the email and it's missing.
- 2. Cut the grid to men's styles only, or split it clearly by gender with a header, so I'm not scrolling past shoes that don't apply to me.
- 3. Swap the running-guy photo in "Find Your Best Fit" for someone walking, or just a shoe on its own — keep the whole email consistent with the "wide fit" theme instead of sneaking a trail runner back in.
- 4. Drop the text-signup CTA for a plain email offer — a percentage off would land better than a shipping bribe.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Men's Wide sizes now in stock`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `More room, same comfort — see which styles fit your foot`
