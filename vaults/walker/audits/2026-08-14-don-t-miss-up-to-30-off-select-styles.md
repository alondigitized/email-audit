# Don't miss up to 30% off select styles
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-14**
## Executive summary

- Two wooden folding chairs. That's the hero image. No shoe, no foot, no arch support in sight — just furniture. I had to scroll a full screen before I even saw a sneaker, and by then I'd already half-decided this was a mis-fire. Once I get to "Up to 30% off select styles," fine, that's a real number, but it's generic — I don't know what's actually on sale until I dig through a grid of six random items, half of which don't apply to me at all.
- And that grid is the real problem: baseball cleats, kids' shoes, toddler shoes, running socks. Out of six "more to explore" items, maybe two are things a 62-year-old walking-around guy would even glance at — the 740 Lace and the 9060 Lace, both in that washed-out grey/white colorway that at least looks comfortable. The 1080v15 Suede might be worth a look too since that's their cushioned line, but the copy tells me nothing about arch support or all-day comfort, just a product name.
- This is a mark-as-read, not a forward-to-a-friend. There's a real discount buried in here, and the price tiers ("Under $50 / $50-$100 / Over $100") are actually a decent, honest way to shop by budget — I'll give them that. But I'm not digging through cleats and toddler shoes to find my size in a 1080. New Balance keeps sending me these grab-bag emails — same story as the August 7th and 10th "Top styles on sale" ones — and I'm getting worn down by it.

## What's working

- The BTS26 code and "Up to 30% off" is a real, specific offer, not vague marketing fluff.
- Shopping by price tier (Under $50 / $50-$100 / Over $100) is a smart, honest way to browse without hype.
- Free shipping banner at $75+ is clear and right up top.
- The 1080v15 and 9060 Lace at least look like cushioned comfort shoes, which is in my wheelhouse.

## What's weak

- The hero image is two folding chairs. No shoes, no offer context — just baffling as an opener.
- The product grid is a scattershot: cleats, kids' shoes, socks, toddler sandals — none of that is me, and it drowns out the two or three items I'd actually consider.
- No mention of cushioning, arch support, or comfort features anywhere in the copy — just product names.
- This is the third "up to 30% off" style email in a week and a half; it's starting to blur together with the others.
- "Shop the sale" price tiers don't actually show me any product — just three empty tiles, so I still have to click through blind.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap the hero image for an actual comfort shoe on a real foot, or at minimum a shoe — not furniture. That's the single biggest miss here.
- 2. Curate the "More to explore" grid by what I've actually browsed or bought before, not a random mix of cleats and kids' shoes.
- 3. Add one line of comfort-specific copy under a featured shoe — "plush foam, arch support built in" — something that speaks to why a guy my age should care.
- 4. Space out the "up to 30% off" sends — three near-identical ones in eight days is fatigue, not persuasion.
