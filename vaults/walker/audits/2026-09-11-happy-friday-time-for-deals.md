# Happy Friday! Time For Deals!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-11**
## Executive summary

- Well, this one's a genuine grab bag, and for once that works in my favor a little — I don't have to scroll past a wall of Nike/adidas before finding my brand. Skechers shows up about halfway down: "up to 25% off," a women's arch fit shoe pictured, and "earn 2x pts on all Skechers purchases." That last bit's a nice touch since I am a Perks member, but the shoe they picked to illustrate it is a women's model, so it's not exactly speaking to me.
- The $15/$30 storewide coupon up top is honestly the most useful thing in the whole email — clear dollar amounts, a real code, no brand restriction. I'd probably use that before I'd click through on any one brand block, since it stacks with whatever I actually end up buying in store.
- Would I click? Maybe on the "Shop Skechers" link just to see what's on sale, but I'm not rushing. Wouldn't forward it to anybody — this is a "check it later at the store" email, not a "hey look at this" email.

## What's working

- The $15/$30 coupon at the top is dead simple — dollar figures, a code, a threshold. No math required.
- Skechers gets its own dedicated block instead of buried in a logo lineup like the last few emails I've gotten.
- "Earn 2x pts on all Skechers purchases" speaks to me as a Perks member, even if the visual doesn't.
- The barcodes at the bottom mean I could just print this and take it to the store, which is how I actually shop anyway.

## What's weak

- The Skechers product shown is a women's Arch Fit shoe — nothing here shows me a men's comfort shoe.
- It's a long scroll — Nike, Jordan/Converse/adidas, Puma, Skechers, Crocs/HEYDUDE, then a charity donation ask. By the time I get to my brand I've already scrolled past three others.
- No mention of arch support, cushioning, or slip-on comfort anywhere near the Skechers block — just a percentage and a shoe I wouldn't buy.
- The charity round-up ask feels tacked onto the bottom of a sale email; fine cause, wrong spot.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the Skechers block up near the top, right under the storewide coupon, and swap in a men's comfort shoe — that's the single biggest lift for someone like me.
- 2. Add one line of actual product language near the Skechers photo — "cushioned," "arch support," "slip-on" — instead of just a percentage off.
- 3. Trim the number of brand blocks or let me jump straight to "my brands" — I've seen this same logo parade in five emails this week.
- 4. Keep the coupon code visible near each brand block, not just once at the top, so I don't have to scroll back up to remember it.
