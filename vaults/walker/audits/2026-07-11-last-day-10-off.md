# LAST DAY: $10 OFF!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- Six emails in three days about the same ten-dollar deal, and here we are again with "LAST DAY." I've seen DSW say "final hours" and "last chance" so many times this week that I barely even registered the urgency. What I *didn't* expect was to open it and find out the deal actually got bigger — there's a full tiered structure in there, up to sixty bucks off on a $199 spend. So good news: the offer upgraded. Bad news: the subject line said "$10 off" and buried the better deal entirely, which feels like a bait-and-switch in reverse — I almost didn't bother opening.
- Once inside, the hierarchy is clean enough on my phone. Big numbers, readable type, I can follow the tiers without squinting. But then the "SO NEW, SO YOU" new arrivals section rolled in and I counted four product shots — all of them women's heels, Mary Janes, and tall boots. Not a single cushioned walking shoe, no men's casual, nothing I'd ever put on my foot. That's two days in a row DSW has done this to me. Yesterday's send was wall-to-wall women's styles too.
- The "Shop Wide Width" button was the only thing in this email that felt like it was written with me in mind. I'll mark it read. Wouldn't forward it. Might click the wide-width section out of stubbornness, but I'm not counting on finding anything good.

## What's working

- **Tiered discount is genuinely better than the flat $10 DSW has been hammering** — $20 off $99 or $60 off $199 is real money if I'm buying a couple pairs, and the numbers are big and easy to read.
- **"Shop Wide Width" button** — that one small button is the most targeted thing in this email for a guy like me who needs a comfortable, wider fit. Glad it was there.
- **Clean layout, readable text** — no rendering weirdness, no broken images, the size grid is big enough to tap on a phone without pulling out reading glasses.
- **VIP perks reminder** — the rewards section at the bottom at least acknowledges I'm a member, even if it doesn't tell me my current balance.

## What's weak

- **Subject line undersells the actual offer** — "LAST DAY: $10 OFF!" sounds like the same tired deal, but there's a $60-off tier inside that deserved the headline treatment.
- **"SO NEW, SO YOU" is entirely women's product** — four pairs of heels and tall boots on my screen. Two days in a row now. DSW clearly isn't segmenting men vs. women here.
- **"LAST DAY" has zero credibility left** — after BOGO's "final hours" and three days of $10-off countdowns, this urgency claim bounces right off me.
- **"Shop Wide Calf" button** — fine for a different customer, but it signals loud and clear that this email wasn't built with me in mind.
- **Preview text padding** — the inbox showed the "trending kicks" line followed by a wall of invisible filler characters. Looked like a loading glitch on my phone before I opened it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the subject with the top tier** — "LAST DAY: Up to $60 OFF" tells me there's more on the table. "$10 OFF" makes me assume I've already read this email.
- 2. **Swap the new arrivals grid for men's content** — if DSW knows I'm a male subscriber, show me men's walking shoes, comfort fits, or Skechers/New Balance crossovers, not heels and Mary Janes.
- 3. **Call out fit details in the hero** — one line under the tiered discount like "wide widths available" or "comfort & arch-support styles included" would do a lot of work for me.
- 4. **Rotate the urgency language** — "LAST DAY" after a week of final-hours warnings is white noise. Try something that resets the clock: "New tiers, final hours" or "Biggest discount of the sale."
- 5. **Show me the math on the top tier** — "Spend $199, save $60 — that's two pairs of Skechers" is the kind of simple calculation that gets Boomers moving.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA) violation. Remove `maximum-scale=1` or replace with `maximum-scale=5`.
  ```html
  <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
  ```

- **`text-size-adjust: none` in universal reset** — Applied via `*{}` selector, disabling OS-level text scaling across all elements. This compounds the zoom restriction above.
  ```css
  *{ -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%; -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; ... text-size-adjust: none }
  ```
  The vendor-prefixed properties correctly set `100%`, but the unprefixed `text-size-adjust: none` overrides them in supporting clients.

- **`Src:` (capital S) in all `@font-face` blocks** — CSS property names are case-insensitive per spec so this parses correctly, but it is non-standard and could fail in a strict XML parser (which XHTML technically requires). Low risk in practice; fix for hygiene.

- **Inconsistent font asset cache-busting** — TTF (HelveticaNow) files include a query-string version stamp (`?1755182939`); WOFF (Futura_PT) files do not. If Futura assets are updated at the same URL, recipients' clients may serve stale cached versions.
  - Stamped: `original.ttf?1755182939`
  - Unstamped: `original.woff` (no version)

- **Alt text on images** — Cannot audit; source truncated before `<img>` tags appear.

- **Dark mode** — Implementation present (`prefers-color-scheme: dark`, `color-scheme` meta). Cannot fully audit dark-mode image swaps or text contrast without complete source.
