# BTW: VIPS GET $20 OFF EARLY >>>
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- At this point I've lost count of how many DSW birthday emails I've gotten this week — but I'd put it north of six. When "BTW: VIPS GET $20 OFF EARLY >>>" landed, I recognized the pattern before I even tapped it. And when I opened up, sure enough: another BDAY SALE-BRATION banner, another $20 off offer. I literally had this same pitch a few hours ago — "Want First Dibs on $20 off? Become a VIP!" This is that same email wearing a slightly different hat.
- Here's the thing though: buried in the middle of this email is a section that almost got me. "TODAY, TOMORROW, ETC." with copy about "the laziest summer shoes you'll want to wear every day" — that's my language. Those loafers in the product row? That's what I came to DSW for. If they'd led with that and given me a clear deadline on the $20, I might've clicked. Instead the eye bounces from birthday balloons to strappy women's sandals to a $5,000 sweepstakes to a size picker, and I'm lost.
- I'm marking this as read and moving on. Not forwarding it. Maybe checking DSW next week when the birthday campaign finally dies down.

## What's working

- **$20 off is right up front** — visible in the hero before I scroll a single pixel. That's the one thing this email got unambiguously right.
- **"Laziest summer shoes you'll want to wear every day"** — that mid-email copy speaks directly to how I think about footwear. Whoever wrote that knows their comfort-casual customer.
- **Loafer and casual shoe imagery in the scroll** — those low-profile, everyday shoes are what I'd actually buy.
- **SHOP BY SIZE** at the bottom is a practical shortcut I'd actually use on my phone.
- **$5,000 sweepstakes** is a secondary hook that gives me one more reason to stay on the page.

## What's weak

- **Cadence is broken** — I got an email this morning that was essentially identical. Two near-duplicate sends in one day is not a campaign; it's inbox clutter.
- **"BECOME A VIP" CTA makes no sense to me** — if the email is for VIPs, why am I being asked to become one? Is this for me or for someone who isn't signed up yet? I'm confused.
- **Women's strappy sandals mid-scroll** — the email loses its grip on me when I hit those. I've checked out by that point.
- **"FALL'S HOT TOT: NEW SHOES" in the hero** — it's July, I haven't bought my summer sandals yet. Fall shoes in the birthday hero text feels like crossed wires.
- **The ">>>" in the subject** — those arrows make this feel like a spam trick, not how a brand I've been loyal to should be talking to me.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Call a pause on the birthday campaign** — or at minimum stop resending the same $20 VIP pitch in the same day. One strong send beats two diluted ones every time.
- 2. **Lead the CTA with the deal, not the membership pitch** — change "BECOME A VIP" to "SHOP $20 OFF NOW" so the action matches the offer I just read about.
- 3. **Men's comfort shoes only in the hero and product rows** — cut the women's fashion sandals from the main scroll for this segment. A 62-year-old guy shopping for walking shoes doesn't need to scroll through strappy heels to find what he came for.
- 4. **Put a real deadline on the $20 off** — "Offer ends Wednesday at midnight" gives me a reason to act today instead of putting my phone down.
- 5. **Fix the preheader** — replace the junk padding characters with actual copy that earns the second line of inbox real estate.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$20 off ends soon — your VIP price is waiting`
- **Subject Alt B:** `VIPs only: $20 off our best walkers this week`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop everyday comfort picks — offer expires Wednesday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus enter the $5,000 giveaway before it closes`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

**`Src:` CSS property capitalized in all 10 `@font-face` declarations**
```css
@font-face {
  font-family: 'Futura_PT_Regular';
  Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');  /* invalid */
}
```
`Src:` with a capital S is not valid CSS. Strict parsers will ignore the declaration entirely, causing web font fallback silently. Affects all 10 typefaces (Futura PT × 4, HelveticaNow × 6).

**`text-size-adjust: none` applied globally**
```css
*{
  -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%;
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;   /* overrides 100% above — duplicate wins */
  text-size-adjust: none
}
```
Two problems: (1) `-ms-text-size-adjust` is declared twice — `none` wins over `100%`, defeating the intended behavior on Outlook/IE; (2) the unprefixed `text-size-adjust: none` blocks user-initiated text scaling, which is an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4).

**Only WOFF/TTF font formats — no WOFF2**
Futura PT uses `.woff`; HelveticaNow uses `.ttf`. No `.woff2` variant is provided. WOFF2 has ~30% better compression and is supported by every client that supports web fonts at all. Low severity, but increases load time on Apple Mail and iOS Mail, the only major clients that render these fonts.

**Fake black `#000001` used in CSS**
```css
.border-top-black { border-top: 1px #000001 solid !important; }
.dm-font-black { color: #000001 !important; }
```
This is a known workaround to prevent Gmail from overriding pure black — intentional, not a bug, but worth documenting so QA doesn't flag it as a hex typo.

**`border-top` shorthand has non-standard property order**
`1px #000001 solid` — CSS shorthand spec order is `width style color`. Color-before-style is technically non-standard. Email clients parse it permissively, but it should be `1px solid #000001` to be safe.
