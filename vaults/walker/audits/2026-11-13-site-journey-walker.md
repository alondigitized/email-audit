# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 4/10 (prev 4/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-11-13**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I walked Skechers on my phone as a 62-year-old comfort shopper, and the shopping part worked. The main sections were easy to find, and "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Arch Fit" are right at the top. The buying part did not work. I tapped Add to Cart with no size picked and got a red "Please select a size" error. The cart page then came up completely blank, and the cart badge still said 0. Search for "comfort shoes" returned women's wedges and slippers, not anything for me. I never saw a logged-in state or any sign the site knew who I was.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**4/10**, the same as yesterday.

- Browsing is solid.
- The path to a purchase is broken or unclear: the add-to-cart error, a blank cart, and a login step I couldn't see.
- I can't tell from these nine screenshots what yesterday looked like, so I can't name a specific regression or improvement. If one exists, it's not visible here.

## 3. What's Working

- **Comfort links up top.** "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Arch Fit," and "Max Cus…" sit in the top strip of the homepage. That's what I came for.
- **Men's menu.** The drawer is clean and readable. The rows are big, with plenty of tap space and arrows to show there's more. "Comfort Technologies" is a menu item.
- **Men's Shoes page.** The tiles are "Hands Free Slip-Ins," "Walking Shoes," and "Boots." The first two are exactly my lane.
- **Product page basics.** The price ($155.00), the star rating, and the review count are all clear. The size buttons are big and easy to tap.
- **Add to Cart button.** It's a large, high-contrast navy button, easy to hit with a thumb.
- **Search page.** It shows a result count (1,649) and "Best Matches" sorting.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Add to Cart didn't add anything.** The size buttons were below the fold. I had to scroll to find them, and the page only told me to pick a size after I tapped. A nervous shopper would think the site was broken.
- **The cart page was blank white.** I got no items, no "your cart is empty" message, and no header or footer. It looked like a crash. The cart badge still said 0 on the search page.
- **Login showed nothing.** Step 3 looks identical to the homepage. I saw no sign-in form and no "Hi, Walker." The site didn't feel personal at all.
- **Popup step looked the same as the homepage.** That's fine if nothing was blocking the page. It also means I saw no personalization or welcome.
- **Promo messages contradict each other.**
  - The banner says "BOGO 50% OFF," but my Aero Burst says "Excluded from promotions."
  - The $155 shoe is also the one I landed on.
  - If I'm shopping on price-vs-quality, I feel tricked.
- **The Men's Shoes page opens with loud, colorful Uno sneakers.** That's not the comfort-first story I came for. The filter and sort row is small, and Size and Width are cut off at the right edge.
- **The search results are wrong for me.** I saw a women's wedge loafer and a women's fuzzy slipper. Nothing was labeled hands-free, arch-supported, or men's.
- **The homepage hero is a holiday photo** of two young models in a skirt and a velvet jacket. It sells nothing about comfort. The "Shop | Details" link under the 25% OFF Work & Safety banner is small gray and blue text.
- **Text size.** Prices and the "Buy 1, Get 1 50% OFF" lines are small. I'd be pinching to zoom.
- **No comparison content.** I saw nothing on cushioning, arch support, or price-vs-quality math, and nothing against Hoka, Brooks, or New Balance.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart first.** Show an empty-cart message with a "Keep shopping" button. If an add actually failed, say so. Make sure the badge updates.
2. **Pick a size sooner.** Put the size buttons right under the price, or default the add button to "Select Size" so I'm not surprised by an error.
3. **Make promos match.** If a shoe is excluded from BOGO, say so before the price. Don't show a BOGO banner above it without explanation.
4. **Make "comfort shoes" search smart.** Filter to men's when I've been browsing men's, and show hands-free, arch-fit, and cushioning badges on each tile.
5. **Make the homepage hero about comfort.** Show a slip-in, an arch-fit shoe, and a reason to trust it, not a holiday scene.
6. **Make the filter row fit.** Make Size and Width easy to reach without sideways scrolling, and make the price and promo text larger.
7. **Add a "why this shoe" strip.** A short comparison of cushioning, arch support, and price per year of wear would work. Add real customer comfort ratings if you have them.
8. **Make logging in show up.** After sign-in, I should see my name, my past sizes, and my width.

## 6. Bottom Line

If the shoe fits, I'd buy it, but this site wouldn't let me check out. The comfort categories are well placed and I could find them. A blank cart, an unexplained size error, and a promo that doesn't apply to the shoe I picked would send me to the Brooks or New Balance site. I'm giving it a 4 out of 10 again.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

- **Data gaps:** LCP and CLS were not captured for any step (`?`), so Core Web Vitals can't be assessed. The HTML samples are truncated at `<head>`, so title, meta, canonical, viewport and structured data could not be checked.
- **Stale step data:** The Log In and Men category samples record the URL `https://www.skechers.com/`, the same as the Homepage. The Log In sample still has `GTMPageType":"home"`. Log In and Men category TTFB (431ms) also nearly matches Homepage (440ms). These steps likely did not navigate, or the capture reused the homepage's data. Per-step results for them should be treated as unverified.
- **View Cart failure:** `/cart/` returned HTTP 429. The View Cart axe results (no `<title>`, no `lang`, no `<main>`, no `<h1>`) match a bare error or rate-limit page, not the real cart. Those four violations are probably an artifact of the 429 response. This is an inference.
- **Real findings:** The homepage and search results have genuine landmark and ARIA structure problems, plus two critical axe violations on the homepage. The page also loads a heavy set of third-party scripts.

## Accessibility

18 violations in total. Homepage has 10, View Cart has 4 and Search has 4.

**Homepage**
- **[critical] `aria-required-parent`:** An element with a role such as `listitem` or `menuitem` is not inside its required parent role.
- **[critical] `button-name`:** At least one button has no accessible name, likely an icon-only control.
- **[serious] `link-name`:** At least one link has no discernible text.
- **[moderate] Duplicate `<main>`:** `landmark-no-duplicate-main` and `landmark-main-is-top-level` both fire, so there is more than one `main` and at least one is nested. The `<aside>` is also nested (`landmark-complementary-is-top-level`).
- **[moderate] `landmark-unique`:** Multiple landmarks share the same role and label.
- **[moderate] `page-has-heading-one`:** There is no `<h1>`.
- **[moderate] `region`:** Content sits outside any landmark.
- **[minor] `aria-allowed-role`:** A role is applied to an element that doesn't allow it.

**Search "comfort shoes"**
- The same landmark problems recur: `aria-allowed-role`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region`. This points to a shared template or component defect, not a page-specific one.

**View Cart**
- `document-title` [serious], `html-has-lang` [serious], `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one` [moderate]. See the 429 note above.

**Not covered:** Log In, Men, Men > Shoes, Product detail and Add to Cart show no axe results. Given the stale-step issue, this may mean they were not scanned, not that they are clean.

## SEO

- **Missing `<h1>` (Homepage, View Cart):** Weakens the main heading signal.
- **Missing `<title>` (View Cart):** Likely the 429 error page. Confirm that rate-limit responses aren't indexable. They should carry `noindex` and a `Retry-After` header.
- **Homepage head:** `<html lang="en">` is present.
- **Not verifiable from the truncated samples:** meta description, canonical, Open Graph, structured data and robots directives.
- **No SEO data for category, PDP or search pages.** The HTML samples cover only the first three steps.

## Performance

- **TTFB:** Homepage 440ms, Log In 431ms, Men 431ms, Men > Shoes 20ms, Product detail 26ms, Add to Cart 26ms, View Cart 167ms, Search 19ms.
- **Interpreting TTFB:** The 19–26ms values on later steps are too low for origin-served HTML. They are probably same-document or SPA transitions, or CDN or bfcache hits, so they don't represent real server latency. The ~430–440ms on the first steps is acceptable but not fast.
- **LCP and CLS:** Not captured, so there is no verdict.
- **Third-party load is heavy.** The Men category sample shows these scripts injected into `<head>`:
  - **Analytics and tag managers:** GTM, GA4 (gtag), Quantum Metric, Bloomreach (`brsrvr`), Pulse Insights and Monetate.
  - **Ad pixels:** TikTok, Pinterest, Facebook, Snapchat, Reddit, Nextdoor, Bing, Yahoo (`ytc.js`), Taboola, Impact, RTB House (`creativecdn`) and Tune (`go2sdk`).
  - Most are async, but this many main-thread tasks likely hurts INP and TBT on mobile hardware. The count grows between the Homepage and Men sample, so it accumulates with navigation.
- **Font loader:** Fonts load through `webfontloader` from `cdnjs.cloudflare.com`. This adds a third-party dependency and a JS-gated font load, with FOUT/FOIT risk and a delayed LCP text render.
- **GTM environment parameters:** The GTM URL carries `gtm_preview=env-1&gtm_auth=…&gtm_cookies_win=x`. That loads a specific GTM environment, not the default live container. Confirm this is intentional in production, because it can serve unpublished tag configurations.

## Mobile Optimization

- **Viewport:** The inline `--vh: 6.64px` implies a JS-computed viewport height of about 664px. That is a normal workaround for mobile browser chrome, and no issue is evident.
- **Not verifiable:** The viewport meta tag, tap-target sizes and horizontal overflow, because the sample is truncated and no layout metrics were captured.
- **Unnamed controls:** The `button-name` and `link-name` violations affect touch and screen-reader users on mobile, since icon-only controls are common in mobile nav.

## Console & Network Errors

- **429 on `https://www.skechers.com/cart/`:** The cart request was rate-limited or bot-challenged. This is the only network error. Likely causes are the automated test traffic pattern or a WAF rule on the cart route. It also blocked a meaningful View Cart audit.
- **"Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document" (×6 of the 10 listed):** Some script registers an `unload` handler, which the page's Permissions-Policy blocks. This is typical of third-party tags such as analytics or session-replay (Quantum Metric is a candidate). Origin is unconfirmed. Those scripts should use `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`, since `unload` also disables bfcache.
- **"Unsupported Summarizer API languages were specified…" (×4 of the 10 listed):** This comes from Chrome's built-in Summarizer API being called with unsupported language options. Source is unconfirmed. It could be a page script or the test browser or an extension, and it is not clearly a site defect.
- **Count mismatch:** 21 console errors were reported but only 10 were listed, so the other 11 are unaccounted for.

## Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart 429.** Check WAF and rate-limit rules on `/cart/` for monitoring traffic. Return a proper error page with `<title>`, `lang`, `<main>` and `<h1>`, plus `Retry-After`. Re-run View Cart once it passes.
2. **Fix the homepage critical ARIA issues.** Add accessible names to unlabeled buttons and links (`aria-label` or visually hidden text), and correct the parent-role structure for items with required parents.
3. **Fix the landmark structure in shared templates.** Keep one top-level `<main>`, move `<aside>` out of nesting, give repeated landmarks unique `aria-label`s, and wrap stray content in landmarks. Add an `<h1>` on the homepage.
4. **Fix the capture harness.** Make sure Log In and Men actually navigate and record their own URLs and timing. Capture LCP and CLS, for example with a `PerformanceObserver` that persists per step. Rerun to fill the gaps.
5. **Audit third-party scripts.** Remove unused ad pixels, load the rest after interaction or consent, and move to server-side tagging where possible. Self-host the fonts instead of using `webfontloader` from cdnjs.
6. **Replace `unload` listeners** in the offending third-party tags, or ask the vendors to. Identify the source by running with the Permissions-Policy header relaxed or checking the stack traces.
7. **Confirm the GTM `env-1` preview parameters** are intended in production.
8. **Re-verify the truncated checks.** Review title, meta, canonical, viewport and structured data using full HTML on every step.
