# Now's your chance
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- I saw "New Balance" hit my inbox and thought — finally, maybe they're coming back around to men's shoes. Wrong again. The preview text says "Top kids' styles on sale now" and at that point I've already put my phone down. I'm a 62-year-old man who doesn't have young grandkids I'm shopping for. This is the second time this month New Balance has sent me a kids' email — the June 14th "Classic styles for kids" didn't land either, and I said so then.
- The email itself is clean and well-built. The product grid is clear, the "Now's your chance" headline is readable, and the 25% off block at the bottom with the JUNESALE code is straightforward. None of that matters because the entire email — every CTA, every product, every image — is aimed at parents buying sneakers for children. The model in the hero is young, legs in a dark skirt, wearing a chunky gray sneaker that comes in kids' sizing.
- I'd mark this as read without clicking. I wouldn't forward it. If my daughter needed sneakers for her kids I'd maybe text her a screenshot of the JUNESALE code, but that's about as far as this email takes me.

## What's working

- **The sale block lands clearly.** "Enjoy up to 25% off select shoes" with "Use JUNESALE at checkout. Now through Saturday" is simple, visible, and trustworthy — no buried asterisk tricks.
- **No render bugs.** Product images are clean, buttons are properly spaced, text is legible at phone size.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean.** Hero first, three CTAs below it, product grid, then the discount block. My eye moves through the email without confusion.
- **Short subject line.** 18 characters — renders perfectly on my iPhone without truncation.

## What's weak

- **Wrong audience, again.** Every CTA reads "Shop babies & toddlers," "Shop little kids," "Shop big kids." I am none of those things and I don't buy for any of those people.
- **The hero model signals kids, not me.** Young person's legs, chunky kid-coded sneaker, dark skirt. Nothing in the visual language speaks to a 62-year-old man.
- **Zero comfort language.** No arch support, no cushioning, no slip-on, no wide widths — none of the words that make me actually lean forward when reading a shoe email.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** New Balance has sent me six or more emails this month, and most of them have missed me. "Now's your chance" sounds urgent; the preview kills it in one line.
- **The 25% off code JUNESALE at the bottom might cover men's shoes** — but I'd never scroll that far because the first half of the email has already told me to leave.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment men's vs. kids' lists.** This is the single biggest lever. Send the kids email to parents who have clicked kids categories. Don't spray it at a 62-year-old man with no purchase history in kids' sizes.
- 2. **If you're sending me a sale, lead with what I can actually buy.** The 25% off block is buried below a kids-only product grid. If JUNESALE applies to men's shoes too, say that in the subject and put a men's hero image up top.
- 3. **Add comfort-oriented copy to any men's-relevant block.** "Arch support. Wide widths. All on sale." Four words that would stop my scroll.
- 4. **Reduce email frequency.** Six emails in a month from New Balance is fatigue territory. Fewer sends to the right segments would get more opens.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS class collision — `.bg1` defined twice with conflicting values**
```css
/* definition 1, line ~8 */
.bg1 { background-color: #ffffff; }

/* definition 2, later in <style> */
.bg1 { background: #58546d; }
```
The second rule wins (cascade order), silently overriding the first. Any element using `.bg1` expecting white will render with a dark purple/grey background instead.

**Malformed `@font-face` — duplicate `font-family` descriptor**
```css
@font-face {
  font-family: proxima-nova,sans-serif;
  font-weight: 700;
  font-style: italic;
  font-family: proxima-nova,sans-serif;  /* duplicate, invalid */
}
```
Duplicate descriptors within a single `@font-face` block are invalid per CSS spec. The rule may be ignored entirely in strict parsers.

**Empty `@font-face` declarations (no `src:`)**
All nine `@font-face` blocks lack a `src:` descriptor, making them syntactically invalid and non-functional. If proxima-nova and linotype-didot are loaded via an Adobe Fonts JS snippet (not present in the provided source), these declarations are redundant noise; if they're the sole loading mechanism, the fonts will never load and the fallback stack (`Arial`, `Times New Roman`) will render instead.

**Invalid IE compatibility meta tag**
```html
<meta content="IE=edge" />
```
Missing the required `http-equiv` attribute. Valid form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, Outlook 2007–2016 will ignore this directive and may fall back to an older rendering mode.
