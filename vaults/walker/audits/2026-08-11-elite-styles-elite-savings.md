# Elite styles 👟 Elite savings 💰
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-11**
## Executive summary

- Ninth or tenth time this summer Famous Footwear's landed in my inbox, and this one's actually got some things I use — running shoes with real cushioning, a couple men's models mixed in, and a straightforward BOGO plus points offer up top. It's not the "kids fit check" nonsense from a couple days ago; there's an Adidas Adizero Pacer and a Brooks Anthem 8 in men's sizing right there in the grid, which is the first time in a while I've seen my own foot represented without having to hunt for it.
- That said, the whole thing is still built around "performance running" — carbon-plate racing shoes, a woman doing yoga stretches for the Brooks Defyance Max ad, "run the year with athletics built to perform." I'm not training for a 5K, I'm walking the dog and standing at Home Depot. The men's shoes shown are decent, but the copy and hero images are aimed at somebody younger and more serious about pace times than I am.
- I'd open it — BOGO half off is real money — but I'd scroll past the hero and go straight to the product grid looking for something with arch support, not race-day specs. Wouldn't forward it to anyone; nothing here is a "hey, thought of you" moment.

## What's working

- BOGO half off plus 2X points is a clear, concrete offer right at the top — I don't have to dig for the deal.
- There's an actual men's shoe in the grid (Adizero Pacer, Anthem 8 Medium/Wide) — the "Medium/Wide" callout on the Brooks is the kind of detail I actually care about.
- Brooks Defyance Max price drop ($119.99 to $99.99) is specific and easy to read — a real number beats "up to X% off."
- Layout is clean, big product photos, no overlapping text or broken images that I can see.

## What's weak

- The hero section is all racing-shoe, athlete-focused copy — "athletics built to perform," a shoe balanced on a starting block. That's not how I shop.
- Model in the Brooks ad is a younger woman mid-stretch — doesn't match me at all, and it's front and center for the one "price drop" callout in the email.
- Text is small throughout, especially the fine print block at the bottom — I'd need to zoom to read any of the reward program details.
- No mention of arch support, cushioning, or "all-day comfort" anywhere — words that would actually get my attention are missing.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead the hero with a comfort/support angle, not race performance — something like "cushioned support for every mile you actually walk" — and swap the starting-block shoe photo for a lifestyle shot of an older guy walking, not sprinting.
- 2. Put a men's comfort shoe (not just running) front and center in the top third — I have to scroll past three racing shoes before I see anything I'd wear.
- 3. Bump body text size on the offer details and fine print — hard to read at a glance on my phone.
- 4. Add a plain "slip-on / easy on-off" or "arch support" filter callout near the top CTAs (Shop Women / Shop Men) so I know where to click without guessing.
