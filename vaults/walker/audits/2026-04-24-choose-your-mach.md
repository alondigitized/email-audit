# Choose your Mach
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-24**
## Executive summary

- Wrong brand, wrong shoes, wrong me. This is a HOKA email landing in my inbox, and from top to bottom it's talking about plated speedsters and tempo-training and race miles. I'm 62, I walk around the mall and the neighborhood — I'm not putting pace on anything. The whole email reads like it was built for a 30-something who runs 5Ks on weekends. The Mach Remastered at the bottom catches my eye a little — they call it a "lifestyle shoe" — but by the time I scroll that far, I've already mentally checked out. No price. No comfort callouts. No reason for me to trust a brand I don't know well enough to buy from.

## What's working

- **Clear product naming and structure.** Mach X 3, Mach 7, Mach Remastered — it's organized. I can tell there are three different shoes and that they're different things. That part isn't confusing.
- **Men's button is visible.** Every section has a "Shop Men's" CTA right next to Women's. I appreciate that. I don't have to hunt for what applies to me.
- **The Mach Remastered section.** "Lifestyle shoe" is the only language in this whole email that gestures at someone like me. If they had led with that, I might have paid more attention.

## What's weak

- **None of this is for me.** "A plated speedster for next-level race or training miles." I don't race. I don't train. I need shoes that don't kill my feet at hour two of a Costco run. HOKA makes me feel like I walked into a sporting goods store when I wanted a shoe store.
- **No prices anywhere.** I'm price-conscious. Show me the number. Without it, my brain immediately assumes "expensive."
- **Tiny text on a phone screen.** I'm reading this on my phone and the feature callouts under each shoe — "Propay Profly+ plate," "Speed-centric Meta-Rocker" — are so small I'm squinting. I shouldn't have to zoom in to read a marketing email.
- **Performance jargon I don't know.** "Super critical foam midsole," "Hypermodern materials" — I have no idea what any of that means or why I should care. Skechers says "memory foam" and I know exactly what I'm getting.
- **No lifestyle context.** Every photo is a clean product shot on a white/grey background. No one wearing them. No one walking anywhere. I can't picture myself in these shoes.
- **Mach Remastered feels like an afterthought.** The most relevant product for me is buried at the very bottom of the scroll.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with Mach Remastered for a non-runner audience.** If HOKA wants to grow beyond hardcore runners, the lifestyle shoe should headline for a segment like me.
- 2. **Add price.** Even a "starting at $X" line. Price-conscious shoppers bounce when there's no number.
- 3. **Increase font size on feature bullets.** On a phone, those three-column callouts are nearly unreadable at normal zoom.
- 4. **Translate the jargon.** "Super critical foam = lighter cushion that springs back" is something I'd understand. "Super critical foam midsole" is not.
- 5. **Put a human in the shot.** One lifestyle image of someone wearing these while walking or doing everyday stuff would do more for me than three product-on-white photos.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Missing `http-equiv` on charset meta tag:**
  ```html
  <meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
  ```
  Should be:
  ```html
  <meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
  ```
  Without `http-equiv`, Outlook and some legacy clients may not honor charset declaration.

- **Duplicate and contradictory `viewport` meta tags:**
  ```html
  <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
  <meta content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=yes" name="viewport" />
  ```
  First tag disables user zoom (`maximum-scale=1` without `user-scalable`); second explicitly allows it (`user-scalable=yes`). Second tag wins in most parsers, making the first tag a no-op. Pick one and remove the other.

- **Empty `<title>` tag:** `<title></title>` — some spam filters flag empty titles; also breaks screen-reader announce on open.

- **Aggressive universal selector CSS reset:**
  ```css
  * {margin-top:0px;margin-bottom:0px;padding:0px;border:none;outline:none;-webkit-text-size-adjust: none;}
  ```
  `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `*` prevents iOS from scaling text at all widths. Should be `100%` on `body` only, not `none` on every element.
