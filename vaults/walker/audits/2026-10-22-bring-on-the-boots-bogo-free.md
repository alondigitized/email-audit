# Bring On the Boots + BOGO Free
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-22**
## Executive summary

- Shoe Carnival is a store I know, and the orange "Bring on the Boots" banner is easy to read on my phone without my glasses. But this is the same orange coupon blockbuster I've seen all week. The "BUY MORE, SAVE MORE" block ran on 10/11, 10/12, 10/14, 10/16 and 10/17, and here it is again with $10 and $25 off. That's too much email for one shoe store.
- BOGO on boots isn't aimed at me. The hero is a young woman on a fence, and the BOGO fine print says select *women's* boots and clogs. The kids' sale comes next. The men's boot section is third, and it's one pair of brown Chelseas at $59.98. I wanted cushioning, arch support and slip-on ease, and nobody mentions comfort anywhere. Would I click? Maybe the men's boot link if I were already shopping. I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- The banner and "BOGO FREE" circle are huge and readable, so I knew the deal in two seconds.
- The men's boot sale, "Starting at $59.98", gives me a real price. At least one block was for a man.
- The Shoe Perks strip at the top and the "$10 reward" tile near the bottom tell me there's a member benefit.
- The barcodes and "Add to wallet" coupons are handy. I could show the clerk right off my phone.
- The layout is clean, with no broken images, and it fits one phone column.

## What's weak

- The "Shop BOGO Free" and "Shop Men's Boot Sale" links are small underlined text, not buttons. They're hard to tap with my thumb.
- The fine print is tiny and heavy: "select styles", "exclusions apply", and a long list of excluded brands. That makes the offer feel less honest.
- The hero model and the featured booties are women's. The kids' block gets as much room as the men's.
- Nothing mentions comfort, cushioning, arch support or easy slip-on wear. The shoes are shown as fashion.
- The offer stack is cluttered: BOGO, a kids' sale, a men's sale, $10 or $25 off, wallet coupons, SMS, Afterpay and a Word Stride game. I couldn't tell which deal was the real one.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the hero and links clearer for someone like me. Put a men's comfort or walking boot up top. Add a big tappable button like "Shop Men's Comfort Boots", and tell me which BOGO styles and sizes qualify.
- 2. Cut the clutter. Choose either BOGO or the dollars-off coupon, and drop the games and Afterpay tiles.
- 3. Show the end date, 10/31, near the top. Right now it's buried in the fine print.
- 4. Add a comfort line such as "cushioned insoles, easy on/off" to the men's section.
- 5. Vary the creative. I've seen that orange coupon block all week.

## Technical Audit: Shoe Carnival, "Bring On the Boots + BOGO Free"

**1. Technical Summary**
The email is a standard ESP-built table layout with Outlook, Gmail and Samsung client fixes. The one confirmed code defect is an unresolved `[UNIQUE]` placeholder in a tracking-pixel URL. The HTML source was truncated, so the body, footer and CTA links could not be checked.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- **Unresolved token in a tracking URL:** `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]` still contains the literal `[UNIQUE]`. The ESP did not substitute a per-recipient identifier, so this open or tracking hit can't be tied to a recipient. The brackets are also unencoded, which is invalid in a URL query.
- **Hidden character in a URL:** `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20261022_mens_kids_bogof_AM​` ends with an invisible zero-width character (U+200B) after `_AM`. This can corrupt the campaign value when the URL is parsed.
- **Nested query string:** the UTM string sits inside the `t=` parameter (`t=utm_campaign=...`). This is the tracker's own format, but it isn't a standard UTM parameter on a landing URL.
- **Two tracking pixels:** `ieezzy5m.emltrk.com` and `0di86k1v.emltrk.com` are two separate hosts. One may be a legacy or duplicate pixel.
- **Links not verified:** 30 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are click-redirect domains. Their final destinations and status codes are unconfirmed.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- **Missing alt:** both flagged images are the `emltrk.com` tracking pixels above. Add `alt=""` (plus `width="1" height="1"`) so screen readers skip them. This is low severity.
- **Malformed meta tag:** `<meta content="IE=edge" />` has no `http-equiv`, so it does nothing.
- **Mixed doctype:** the document uses an HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype with `xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml"` and self-closing tags. This is harmless in practice but inconsistent.
- **Link styling:** the global rule `a, a:link, a:visited ... { text-decoration:none !important; color:inherit !important; }` removes default link styling on every anchor. Whether links are still distinguishable depends on inline styling. I couldn't see that in the truncated source.
- **Small text:** `.responsive-text` is forced to `font-size:12px !important` at ≤600px. That is small for mobile body text.
- **Not verifiable:** alt text on content images and the preheader, because the source is truncated.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
