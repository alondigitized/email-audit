# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-03**
## Executive summary

- There it is again — the pink floral D'Lites - Spring Bloom, front and center under a "READY TO CHECK OUT?" banner, and I want to state for the record: that shoe has never once been in my cart. I don't own anything floral, I've never clicked on anything floral, and yet Skechers is convinced I'm one confirm-order tap away from buying a woman's sneaker with embroidered flowers on it. This is at least the fourth time this week that same shoe has shown up in my inbox in some flavor of "you forgot this."
- Below the fold there's a decent grid of men's stuff — the Slip-ins Arch Fit Arcade, the Relaxed Fit Altman-Murdock — and the 20% off member banner is legit useful, same one that's been running all week. But leading with a shoe I've never looked at, framed as if I abandoned it mid-checkout, just tells me their system doesn't know who I am.
- I'm not clicking this one. It's going straight to read, maybe archived. I wouldn't forward it — there's nothing here worth sharing, just another "did you forget" nag with the wrong shoe attached.

## What's working

- The men's shoe row further down is real product I'd actually consider — the Slip-ins Arch Fit Arcade and the Relaxed Fit Altman-Murdock both have that slip-on, cushioned look I go for.
- The 20% off member banner is clean and dated (thru 9/7), same offer as the rest of this week's emails, so at least it's consistent.
- "SHOP ALL BEST SELLERS" and the additional category links give me an easy way to skip past the cart nonsense if I want to browse on my own.

## What's weak

- The hero shoe — that pink floral D'Lites - Spring Bloom — is not a shoe I've ever looked at, let alone put in a cart. Fake urgency on a product I have zero interest in.
- "READY TO CHECK OUT?" implies I left something mid-purchase. I didn't. That's a trust problem, not just an annoyance.
- No arch support, cushioning, or comfort-fit language anywhere near the top — just checkout pressure.
- This is the same cart-abandonment gimmick I got a day ago with "Your cart expires soon!" — same floral shoe, different headline. Feels like the same broken automation firing twice.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix whatever's populating "abandoned cart" — if I never added an item, don't tell me I did. That's the single biggest trust-killer in this send.
- 2. Swap the hero product for something from my actual browsing history, or if there's no real cart data, lead with the 20% member offer instead — that one's legitimate and I already respond to it.
- 3. Move the men's product grid up higher, right under the hero, so there's something relevant before I've scrolled halfway down.
- 4. Add one line about arch support or all-day comfort near the top — that's what actually gets my attention, not a checkout banner.
