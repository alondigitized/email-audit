# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-11-27**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I walked through Skechers on my phone and could not finish what I came to do. The Black Friday banners are loud, and the Men's menu and the "Hands Free Slip-ins" tile are easy to find. But my cart still showed **0** after I tried to add a shoe, the cart page was a **blank white screen**, and I never saw a login screen. Search for "comfort shoes" put women's loafers and wedges first, and I'm a man shopping for myself.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** This matches yesterday's 3/10, so I see no improvement and no regression in the score. I only have yesterday's number, not its reasons, so I can't say which specific problems changed. The cart page was blank and the login was never shown, which makes this a checkout failure. The merchandising and the men's menu are decent, but they don't make up for that.

## 3. What's Working

- **The Men menu (step 4)** is a clean slide-out with big, full-width rows: Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections. The text is readable and the rows are easy to tap. "Comfort Technologies" is a good label for what I'm after.
- **The top of Men's Shoes (step 5)** leads with a "Hands Free Slip-Ins" tile and a "Walking Shoes" tile. That is the right first thing for me.
- **The tabs under the header** ("Slip-ins", "Wide Fit", "Arch Fit", "Max Cushioning") speak my language.
- **The size picker (step 7)** has big, well-spaced buttons, and out-of-stock sizes are clearly crossed out. The blue Add to Cart button is large.
- **The product page (step 6)** shows price, star rating, and review count (100) right away.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart is broken or empty.** After step 7 the bag icon still says 0, and step 8 is a completely white page with no message, no "your bag is empty", nothing. If this happens to a real customer, they leave.
- **The "Please select a size" error is already showing** before I've tapped anything, so the page reads as scolding me. It also isn't clear whether tapping Add to Cart with no size does anything.
- **There's no visible login (step 3).** The screen is the same as the homepage, with only a small person icon. Nothing told me whether I was signed in, and nothing said "Welcome back" or showed my points. I saw no personalization anywhere.
- **The popups step (step 2)** looks identical to the homepage, which is fine, but nothing told me what was dismissed.
- **Search relevance (step 9).** "comfort shoes" returned 1,657 results, and the first two are women's loafers and a women's wedge. The site knows I'm browsing men's, but I'm not asked and not filtered.
- **There's no side-by-side comparison, no price-vs-value math, and no arch support explanation** on any screen I saw. The product page shows $155.00 with no reason why it's worth it next to Hoka or Brooks.
- **The filter chips are small.** "Filter", "Sort by", "Size" and "Width" are tight, and the row scrolls sideways, so "Width" gets cut off. That's hard for a 62-year-old thumb.
- **The promo banners compete with each other.** The top bar says 30-50% off, the next line says Extra 15% with code BF2026, and then there are two big banners. It's confusing to work out which discount I actually get ("Applied at Cart" versus a code I have to type).
- **Some text is small.** The "Members Get an Extra 15%" red lines and the fine print under the "Shop Now" button are hard to read without zooming.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart first.** Add to Cart must put the shoe in the bag, the count must update, and the cart page must never render blank. Show an "empty bag" message with a link back to shoes if it truly is empty.
2. **Don't show the size error until someone taps Add to Cart without a size.** Then scroll to the size picker and highlight it.
3. **Show a signed-in state:** "Hi Walker" in the header, and my recent or saved items on the homepage.
4. **Use what the site knows.** If I came through Men, default the search to men's, and show a "Men / Women" toggle at the top of results.
5. **Put comfort first on product pages:** a short "Why it's comfortable" box covering cushioning, arch support, and slip-in or hands-free, with a simple comparison to other comfort brands and a cost-per-wear note.
6. **Make the filter and sort chips bigger** and wrap them instead of scrolling sideways.
7. **Collapse the discount messaging into one clear offer.**

## 6. Bottom Line

The shoe selection and the menu are fine, but I couldn't buy anything. A blank cart page is a dealbreaker, and nothing felt personal to me. If a competitor's site works, I'll shop there. Same 3/10 as yesterday.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The audit data has gaps, so some findings below are inferences.

- **Web Vitals:** LCP and CLS were not captured on any of the 8 steps (`?`). Only TTFB is available.
- **Unreliable step data:** Homepage, Log In and Men category all report the URL `https://www.skechers.com/`. Log In and Men category both report TTFB=388ms. This suggests the journey either did not navigate or the SPA/overlay transitions were not detected, and the per-step vitals may come from a stale navigation entry.
- **Main defect:** `GET /cart/` returned **429** (rate-limited). The View Cart step was audited against what looks like an error page, which explains its axe results.
- **Page structure:** The Homepage and Search pages have landmark and ARIA structural defects, including 2 critical and 1 serious violation on the Homepage.
- **Third-party load:** The page head loads a very large number of third-party scripts. A GTM preview/non-production environment is referenced in the production HTML.

## Accessibility

18 axe violations across 3 steps. The tool provided no DOM node selectors, so the offending elements are not identified.

**Homepage (10)**
- **Critical:** `aria-required-parent`. An element with a role such as `listitem`, `tab` or `menuitem` lacks its required parent role.
- **Critical:** `button-name`. One or more buttons have no accessible name, probably icon-only controls such as the menu, search or cart buttons.
- **Serious:** `link-name`. One or more links have no discernible text.
- **Moderate:** `landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` and `landmark-unique`. Together these point to multiple or nested `<main>` elements and an `<aside>` nested inside another landmark.
- **Moderate:** `page-has-heading-one` and `region`. There is no `<h1>`, and content sits outside landmarks.
- **Minor:** `aria-allowed-role`. A role is applied to an element that does not allow it.

**Search "comfort shoes" (4)**
- **Moderate:** `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region`.
- **Minor:** `aria-allowed-role`.
- These repeat the shared template defects from the Homepage, so they are likely a single header or footer fix.

**View Cart (4)**
- **Serious:** `document-title` and `html-has-lang`.
- **Moderate:** `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one`.
- All four match a bare error document, which is consistent with the 429 response. The cart's real accessibility state is **unverified**.

**Not flagged:** Homepage `<html lang="en">` is present.

## SEO

- **View Cart:** The document has no `<title>` and no `<h1>`. The page is cart-related and probably `noindex`, but a 429 on a crawlable URL could hurt crawl behaviour if a bot is rate-limited.
- **Homepage and Search:** The Homepage has no `<h1>`.
- **Not assessed:** Canonical tags, meta descriptions, structured data and `robots` directives were not in the truncated HTML samples.

## Performance

- **LCP and CLS:** Not measured on any step, so Core Web Vitals compliance cannot be assessed.
- **TTFB:** Homepage 474ms, Log In 388ms, Men 388ms, View Cart 115ms, Product detail and Add to Cart 59ms, Men > Shoes 25ms, Search 26ms. Only the Homepage exceeds ~450ms, which is acceptable. The 25–26ms values are implausibly low for a full document fetch. They likely reflect cache, bfcache or SPA transitions, or a stale Navigation Timing entry.
- **Third-party script volume:** The head injects 20+ async third-party scripts on the Homepage, and more on Men category. They include:
  - GTM, gtag (3 destinations), Monetate (2 scripts) and Quantum Metric.
  - TikTok (3 scripts), Pinterest, Yahoo (`ytc.js` twice), Bing, Impact and RTB House.
  - Bloomreach, Pulse Insights, Tune and a TrustArc consent layer.
  - Men category also adds Taboola (3 scripts), Snapchat, Reddit, Facebook, Nextdoor and Sc-static.
  - Cumulative main-thread cost on a mobile CPU is likely significant, which puts LCP and INP at risk.
- **Duplicate tag:** `ytc.js` is loaded twice.
- **GTM preview environment in production:** The GTM URL contains `gtm_preview=env-1` and `gtm_auth=…`. This loads a non-live container environment, which could ship untested tags to users, and it bypasses the standard cached container.
- **Web fonts:** Fonts load through `webfontloader.js` from cdnjs (a third-party origin) rather than self-hosted `@font-face` with `font-display`. This adds a request chain and FOUT/CLS risk. The six `wf-mulish-n*-active` classes show six Mulish weights are loaded.
- **Viewport height:** `--vh` is set by JS (`6.64px`, so a 664px viewport). Layout depends on script execution, which can shift layout.

## Mobile Optimization

- **Viewport height:** `--vh` is computed in JS, a common workaround for mobile browser chrome. Modern CSS (`dvh`/`svh`) would remove the script dependency.
- **Touch targets:** Not assessed, because axe `target-size` results were not provided.
- **Journey integrity:** The step URLs do not change, so the mobile navigation flow could not be confirmed.
- **Not assessed:** Viewport meta tag and responsive image markup were not in the truncated samples.

## Console & Network Errors

**Console (21 reported, 4 distinct sources shown)**
- **"Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document"** (6 shown). A script registers an `unload` handler that is blocked by Permissions Policy. This is typically a third-party tag, and it would also make pages ineligible for bfcache, which fits the 25–26ms TTFB values.
- **"Unsupported Summarizer API languages were specified, and the request was aborted"** (4 shown). This is Chrome's built-in Summarizer API being called with unsupported languages. The source is unidentified. It is probably a third-party script or a browser feature in the test profile rather than skechers.com first-party code.
- **Gap:** Only 10 of the 21 messages were listed. The other 11 are unknown.

**Network (1)**
- **429** `https://www.skechers.com/cart/`. Rate limiting or bot protection triggered on the cart document. This is likely caused by repeated automated traffic, and it undermines the View Cart step. It would be a serious issue if real users hit it.

## Recommendations

1. **Re-run the cart step.** Investigate the 429 (bot or WAF rules, request rate, IP or user-agent allowlisting) and re-audit the cart page, since its current results are not trustworthy.
2. **Fix the critical accessibility defects.** Add accessible names to icon buttons and links, and correct the `aria-required-parent` structure on the Homepage.
3. **Fix the landmark structure.** Use a single top-level `<main>`, move the `<aside>` to top level, give repeated landmarks unique `aria-label`s, and put all content inside landmarks. This should clear most of the Homepage and Search violations at once.
4. **Add an `<h1>`.** Add one on the Homepage, and ensure error and cart documents have `<title>`, `lang` and `<main>`.
5. **Remove the GTM preview environment.** Drop `gtm_preview` and `gtm_auth` from the production snippet.
6. **Audit third-party tags.** Remove duplicates (`ytc.js`), defer non-essential pixels until after consent or interaction, and consolidate through the tag manager.
7. **Locate the `unload` handler.** Replace it with `pagehide`/`visibilitychange` to restore bfcache eligibility.
8. **Self-host fonts.** Use `@font-face` with `font-display: swap` and preload the critical weights instead of loading `webfontloader` from cdnjs.
9. **Fix the monitor.** Capture LCP, CLS and INP with `PerformanceObserver`, verify each step's URL changes, record axe node selectors, and log the full set of console errors.
