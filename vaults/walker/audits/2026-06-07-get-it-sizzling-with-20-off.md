# Get it sizzling with $20 off ♨️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- Opened it, recognized Famous Footwear, saw the "$20 off $100+" banner before I even had to scroll — good start. The offer is the same one they've been running all week. I got that exact deal Monday in "Stack the summer savings," again two days ago in "Your closet is calling," and again this morning in "It's the last day." At this point I've got the terms memorized. The summer "sizzling" hook is fine wordplay but it's doing nothing to tell me why I should care *this time* versus the last three times.
- Inside the email, the product picks are scattered — Brooks running shoes at $109.99 made sense for me, and I noticed the Nike Huarache and Jordan sections, but those are not comfort-walking brands I reach for. The Skechers block buried near the bottom is the most relevant thing in here for someone with my feet, and it's way down the page. What caught my eye was the Brooks markdown — $109.99 is a price I'll investigate — but I'd have to click through to know if it's a style I can actually wear all day.
- I'll mark it read. I won't forward it. If the Brooks deal is legit, I might come back to it — but the offer fatigue is real and "sizzling" isn't making me move faster.

## What's working

- **$20 off $100+ banner is front and center** — I didn't have to hunt for the offer, it's the first thing my eye landed on.
- **Brooks at $109.99 is a credible price drop** — that's a brand I trust for walking and support, and the price felt specific enough to be real.
- **Clean layout, no broken images or scrambled text** — easy to scroll on my phone without anything looking busted.
- **"We picked these just for you" + "New markdowns" two-section structure** is easy to skim — I know exactly what I'm looking at in each block.

## What's weak

- **Fourth identical $20-off-$100 email this week** — the cadence has gone from "good deal" to "okay I get it." At this point the urgency language ("Limited time!") means nothing because I've seen it four sends in a row.
- **Preview text is a raw tracking URL** — in my inbox I saw a wall of garbled link text instead of something that told me why to open. That alone almost made me skip it.
- **Product mix skews young** — Jordan, Converse, Adidas Originals are not where my feet live. Skechers shows up but it's buried at the bottom when it should be in the "picked for you" row.
- **No comfort or fit language anywhere** — cushioning, arch support, wide width, slip-on — nothing. These are the words that make me pull out my wallet, and not one appeared above the fold.
- **"Sizzling" is generic summer copy** — it's not wrong, but it tells me nothing I didn't know from the subject line I've been seeing all week.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text first** — a broken URL showing up in my inbox is an immediate credibility hit. Write something like "Brooks, HEYDUDE, and Skechers — all on sale" that actually tells me what's inside. This is the single change that would lift opens most.
- 2. **Lead the product section with comfort brands** — swap the order so Skechers, Brooks, and HEYDUDE hit first, not Jordan and Converse. I'm the guy who opens these emails; serve me in the top three slots.
- 3. **Add one line of fit copy to the hero** — "Wide widths. All-day cushion. $20 off." does more work for me than "Get it sizzling." Takes 30 seconds to write.
- 4. **Slow down the $20-off cadence** — if you're going to run the same offer four days running, at least vary the product story so each send feels like a new reason to click.
- 5. **Headline the Brooks deal specifically** — "$109.99 Brooks — picked for you" is a hook I'd click. Generic "new markdowns" is not.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Brooks, Skechers & more: $20 off this weekend`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Walker, your Brooks markdown is waiting`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Brooks at $109.99, Skechers styles on sale — $20 off $100+ through today`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Comfort brands marked down. Take $20 off your next $100+ order.`

## Technical Audit — Famous Footwear "Get it sizzling with $20 off"

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard table-layout HTML mailer with no broken links or merge token failures detected. The primary issues are a pervasive missing-alt-text problem across 27+ images and one mixed-content HTTP image source that will be blocked in modern email clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **28 tracked links skipped** by automated probe — all route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com`. Not a defect, but click-redirect chains add latency and increase spam-filter scrutiny.
- **Plain-text version is 78% URLs** (8,831 of 11,364 chars). Most ESPs auto-generate plain-text from HTML; if this is the case here, the plain-text rendering is nearly unreadable and may trigger deliverability penalties on content filters that evaluate text/plain parts.
- No unresolved or 404-producing destination URLs were flagged.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical:**
- **27 images missing `alt` text** (empty `alt=""` or omitted entirely). Affects screen reader users and images-off rendering in clients like Outlook. Confirmed images include logo (`3b6a7dc1`), hero (`ac216ca0`, `7eec9981`), product images (`04ab8bdb`, `d0afa080`, etc.), and all 8 iGoDigital recommendation images (`7222883.recs.igodigital.com/rr/v2/.../i/1` through `/i/8`).

**High:**
- **One HTTP (non-HTTPS) image source:**
  `http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=...` (identified as image `910205576111`)
  Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail proxy all images over HTTPS; an HTTP source will either be blocked, fail to load, or trigger a mixed-content warning in some clients. This is likely a LiveClicker/real-time content block.

**Low:**
- `<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` is missing the required `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute — should be `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />`. Benign in practice but malformed.
- `user-scalable=0` in the viewport meta tag is non-standard; the correct value is `no`. Functions equivalently across clients but fails strict validation.
- `lineapacing:20px` (typo for `letter-spacing`) on the loyalty join cell — dead CSS rule, no rendering effect.

No unrendered merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `%%first_name%%`) detected in the visible HTML. Personalization appears limited to the iGoDigital recommendation block (`7222883.recs.igodigital.com`) which is server-side rendered at open time — no client-side token risk.
