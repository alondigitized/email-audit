# THE FINAL AWAITS
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- I'll be straight with you — I stared at this thing for about five seconds and the word that came to mind was "again." Another soccer email from adidas. Last week it was Club América, the week before that it was some sweepstakes newsletter, and now it's "La Final" — Spain versus Argentina, July 19th, New Jersey. I'm sure it's a big deal. I genuinely do not care.
- The email is clean and renders fine. The big red "LA FINAL" poster image looks sharp, the two buttons are easy to read, and there's no junk cluttering the layout. Whoever designed it did their job. The problem is their job had nothing to do with me. I don't follow soccer. I don't own a jersey. I'm not traveling to New Jersey to watch anything on July 19th.
- I would not click. I would not forward it. I'd mark it read and wonder for the fourth or fifth time this summer why adidas thinks I'm a soccer fan.

## What's working

- **Layout is genuinely clean.** Big hero, clear headline below it, two buttons — the hierarchy makes sense even on a small phone screen.
- **The CTAs are specific.** "SHOP SPAIN" and "SHOP ARGENTINA" are at least honest about what I'm clicking into. Not some vague "Discover More."
- **Timely.** The final is July 19th — that's two days from now. At least it's not a stale promo sitting in my inbox a week after the event.
- **No render bugs.** Everything loaded, nothing overlapped, text was legible without zooming.

## What's weak

- **Wrong guy, wrong sport.** I am 62 years old. I have never once gone looking for a Spain or Argentina jersey. This email might as well be addressed to someone else.
- **Zero offer.** No discount, no member pricing, no free shipping. Just "shop this team." That's not a reason to click, it's a catalog page.
- **No comfort or footwear anywhere in sight.** The one thing adidas actually sells that I'd consider buying — walking shoes, cushioned soles, anything I could put on my feet — is completely absent.
- **Fourth soccer email in two weeks.** At some point the cadence itself becomes the problem. Club América, the sweepstakes newsletter, and now La Final — I'm starting to feel like I accidentally subscribed to ESPN.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment me out of the soccer sends.** If I've never clicked a soccer email in my adiClub history — and I haven't — stop sending them. Ship me the comfort walking content instead.
- 2. **Add an offer.** Even "Free shipping on Final Day orders" would give me a reason to pause instead of archive.
- 3. **Put something I can actually use in the email.** A banner below the soccer hero with "New arrivals: Men's walking & comfort" would catch people like me who scrolled past the hero.
- 4. **Lower-case the subject.** "THE FINAL AWAITS" in all caps reads like a spam filter honeypot.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Final day jerseys — Spain vs Argentina, July 19`
- **Subject Alt B:** `2 days until La Final — shop your side`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Messi. Yamal. One game. Shop the kit before kickoff.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Spain or Argentina — gear up before July 19.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed failures: a broken redirect link that will 404 for recipients, and a missing plain-text alternative that harms deliverability. One HTTP image asset is also present, which modern clients will block or warn on.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect — relative path in redirect target**

The click-tracking wrapper at `dv.adidas.com` is resolving to a relative path instead of an absolute URL:

```
unknown url type: '/g/e11762a6-49f6-4c32-a6a8-c70e9eb1f2f3?txn=603a5600-...'
```

The decoded `cp_tp` payload contains a `/g/...` path without a hostname. When the redirect server attempts to forward the user, it has no base domain to construct a valid URL. Recipients clicking this link will receive an error page. The full tracking wrapper URL is:

```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/e11762a6-49f6-4c32-a6a8-c70e9eb1f2f3?cp_tp=v3.eJzj...
```

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] HTTP image asset**

One tracking pixel / image is served over plain HTTP:

```

```

Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail proxy or block mixed-content images. This asset will likely be suppressed or trigger a security warning.

**[WARN] Two images missing `alt` text**

- `e11762a6-49f6-4c32-a6a8-c70e9eb1f2f3` (hosted on dv.adidas.com)
- `uPldZcPr0YAVewsBWgfRdt-wg...=452` (the HTTP asset above)

Images-off rendering will show blank boxes with no context. WCAG 2.1 §1.1.1 requires meaningful alt text on informational images.

**Duplicate `@font-face` declarations**

`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are each declared twice across two `<style>` blocks with identical `src` fallback stacks (local Arial/Helvetica). No functional breakage, but adds ~600 bytes of dead CSS payload.

The charset meta tag is malformed — `http-equiv="Content-Type"` is absent. Should be:
```html

```
Without it, some legacy clients may misinterpret encoding.
