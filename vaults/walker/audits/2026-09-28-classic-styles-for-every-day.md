# Classic styles for every day
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-28**
## Executive summary

- Well, this is actually shoes I'd wear — the 327, the 530, the 574, the 9060 — real classic New Balance silhouettes, not baseball cleats or a store opening three time zones away. But "kids' styles and sizes" right under the headline "For every day" tells me pretty quick this one's not really about me — it's a family sizing push, babies and toddlers and little kids getting top billing before the grown men's shoe I'd actually buy.
- The shoes themselves look good — clean product shots, the ones I recognize from my own closet days. But there's no price on any of them until I scroll clear to the bottom, and by then it's a 25% off $125 threshold buried under a picture of shopping bags, same move Skechers pulled with their boxes-and-bags email I saw last night. No mention of cushioning, arch support, or which of these is the comfort walker versus the running shoe — I'd have to click through and guess.
- I'd probably open it since New Balance is a brand I trust, but I wouldn't click past the product grid. No real offer up front, no fit info, and half the email's about matching sneakers for the grandkids — I'll pass this one along to my daughter instead of clicking anything myself.

## What's working

- Real, recognizable New Balance shoes above the fold — 327, 530, 574 — not lifestyle fluff or a stranger in a windbreaker.
- Free shipping on $75+ banner right at the top, easy to spot, no squinting required.
- Product photos are clean and well-lit, easy to tell one shoe from another even on my phone.
- "Available in all sizes" section does at least nod at men's sizing, even if it's wrapped in a family-pack message.

## What's weak

- The whole email is framed around babies, toddlers, and kids — I had to scroll past three kid-focused shop buttons before I saw men's anything.
- No price, no % off, no member pricing anywhere near the shoes themselves — the only offer is at the very bottom, under a photo of paper shopping bags, same trick the Skechers box email pulled.
- Nothing about cushioning, arch support, or fit — for a guy comparing New Balance against Hoka and Brooks on comfort, that's the whole ballgame and it's missing.
- Two separate near-identical grids of the same six shoes (327, 530, 574, 9060) — repetitive, feels like padding rather than new information.
- "Get 25% off select styles" language, but no styles are actually called out as included — same vague fine-print feeling as the "25% off select styles when you spend $125+" email I got the other day.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the offer where the shoes are — slap "25% off $125+" right under the hero image, not buried six screens down under a bag photo.
- 2. Lead with men's, not kids' — a guy my age isn't shopping toddler sizes, and burying men's product under "Shop babies & toddlers" wastes my scroll.
- 3. Add one line of comfort copy per shoe — "cushioned sole," "extra arch support," whatever's true — since that's the actual reason I'd pick New Balance over Hoka or Brooks.
- 4. Cut the duplicate product grid — show the six shoes once, not twice.
- 5. Show a price or price range on the product cards themselves so I don't have to click to find out if it's in my budget.
