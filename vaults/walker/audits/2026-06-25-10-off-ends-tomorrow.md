# $10 off ends tomorrow!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- My thumb opened this one before my brain had a vote — same DSW muscle memory I've built over two weeks of near-daily opens. "Ends tomorrow" in the subject line added a little jolt, enough to make me feel like I'd miss something if I didn't look. Good open hook.
- Then the email loaded and I'm staring at a young woman in a bikini top holding a flip-flop. "Summer's chicest flip-flops." The word "chicest" alone tells me DSW is not talking to me right now. This is the same problem I flagged on the June 19th "summer's top 4 trends" send — the hero is aimed squarely at women, probably Gen Z, and I'm a 62-year-old guy who wanted to know if my New Balance were on sale.
- Here's the saving grace: I scrolled far enough to find "Up to 25% Off Big-Deal Brands" with Crocs and New Balance logos sitting right there. That section had my attention. If DSW had led with that, or at least pointed a man toward it from the subject line, this email would've been a genuine win. As-is, I'll probably click through to the brands section specifically, but only because I already know to scroll past the women's content. A new subscriber like me would've bailed at the flip-flop hero.
- Mark as read, maybe click to New Balance. Would not forward.

## What's working

- **"$10 off ends tomorrow" delivers urgency that held up** — a single concrete deadline in the subject is exactly why I opened it.
- **Tiered Buy More Save More offer is visible and legible at the top** — $10/$20/$60 tiers with a promo code, no mystery about what the deal is.
- **New Balance and Crocs both appear in the brands section** — two of my regular cross-shop brands showing 25% off is genuinely useful information.
- **Shop by Size section is practical for mobile** — as a phone browser, I appreciated being able to filter by size without hunting through a full catalog page.
- **No layout breaks, no broken images, no overlapping text** — the email rendered cleanly.

## What's weak

- **The hero is a young woman in a bikini top promoting women's flip-flops** — as a 62-year-old man, I felt like I opened the wrong email entirely.
- **"Chicest" in the preview text is a disqualifier** — that word tells me who DSW wrote this for, and it's not me.
- **The Crocs/New Balance deal is buried in the lower third** — the most relevant content for a guy like me required scrolling past the women's hero and the SHOE-MERGENCY section.
- **No mention of comfort, cushioning, or men's anything in the first scroll** — my whole buying criteria is invisible above the fold.
- **DSW cadence is starting to fatigue** — this is the eighth-ish email since June 17, and the $10 off offer has appeared at least three times now. The urgency is starting to feel like the boy who cried wolf.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the hero for the Big-Deal Brands section, or at least add a men's callout above the fold** — one sentence like "Men's New Balance & Crocs included" in the hero would've stopped my scroll immediately.
- 2. **Segment the send** — my purchase history should tell DSW I buy men's comfort shoes, not women's flip-flops. Send me the version with the men's hero.
- 3. **Pull "Up to 25% off New Balance + Crocs" into the subject line** — that's the click-driver for someone like me; the generic "$10 off" is table stakes at this point.
- 4. **Kill "chicest" from the preview** — replace with something gender-neutral or men's-facing.
- 5. **Rest the $10 offer** — this is at least the third "$10 off" email this month. Feels stale.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `New Balance, Crocs, and more on sale now`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — `Src:` capitalized in all 11 `@font-face` declarations (invalid CSS)**
Every `@font-face` block uses `Src:` (capital S) instead of the valid lowercase `src:`. While forgiving parsers may ignore the case, strict CSS parsers in some email clients will silently drop the entire font rule, causing fallback fonts to render instead.

```css
/* Example (repeated 11×): */
@font-face {
  font-family: 'Futura_PT_Regular';
  Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');  /* ← invalid */
}
```

**Issue 2 — Conflicting `-ms-text-size-adjust` declarations in the `*` reset**
The global `*` selector sets `-ms-text-size-adjust` twice with contradictory values:
```css
* {
  -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%;   /* line 1 */
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;   /* line 2 — overrides line 1 */
}
```
`none` disables automatic text sizing in IE/Outlook Windows. This can cause small text to remain unscaled and is an accessibility concern.

**Issue 3 — `Margin: 0` (capital M) in heading reset**
```css
h1, h2, h3, h4, h5, h6, p, ul, li {
  margin: 0 !important;  /* valid */
  Margin: 0;             /* not a valid CSS property */
}
```
Harmless in most parsers but should be corrected to prevent lint errors and unpredictable behavior in strict renderers.

**Issue 4 — `<meta charset>` missing `http-equiv` attribute**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />  <!-- missing http-equiv -->
```
Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, some legacy Outlook and Lotus Notes clients may not honor the charset declaration.

**Issue 5 — No `.woff2` font format**
Futura PT fonts are served as `.woff` only; HelveticaNow fonts as `.ttf` only. Neither variant includes `.woff2`, which is 26–34% smaller and is the preferred format for modern clients that support web fonts in email (Apple Mail, Samsung Mail).

**No issues found:** Viewport meta, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, `format-detection`, `color-scheme` meta tags, and dark mode media query structure are all correctly implemented.
