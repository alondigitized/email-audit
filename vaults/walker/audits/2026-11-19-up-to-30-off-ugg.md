# UP TO 30% OFF UGG!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-19**
## Executive summary

- DSW again, and UGG again. This is the fourth UGG send in four days, after "The ultimate UGG destination ➡" this morning, and I'm tired of it. The big coral "UP TO 30% OFF*" over the UGG logo is easy to read on my phone without my glasses. I'll give them that. But I'm a man who wants a slip-on that doesn't hurt, and a fuzzy women's-style boot doesn't speak to me.
- The rest is a scroll of brands I didn't ask about: Hunter, Converse, Lemon socks, a woman in New Balance 327s. At least New Balance is a name I cross-shop. Nothing here mentions cushioning, arch support or walking. The "Shop by Size" block with Wide Width is the only thing that sounds like my feet.
- I wouldn't click. I might forward it to my wife if she wants boots, but for me it's a mark-as-read. The banner at the top says the Black Friday sale "starts soon for VIPs," so I can't tell if 30% is on today or later.

## What's working

- The "30% OFF*" in huge coral type is readable on my phone without squinting.
- The black "SHOP UGG" button is big and obvious, and easy to tap.
- The "Shop by Size" block with Wide Width and Wide Calf buttons is the first thing that sounded like it was meant for my feet.
- New Balance showing up is a brand I actually compare against Skechers and Hoka.
- The VIP rewards box is clear: "Join for free" or "Log in."

## What's weak

- UGG boots in the hero don't match a 62-year-old man who wants comfort and walking shoes. It's a fourth send on the same theme this week.
- The top banner says the sale "starts soon for VIPs," which muddies the 30% offer. Is it on now or not?
- The "Brands on your list" gift block is aimed at someone shopping for presents, with a young woman in a bright print. None of it is about me.
- Hunter, Converse and socks are scattered in a pink grid. Nothing tells me about cushioning or arch support.
- The fine print is tiny and long. An asterisk on the headline offer with "details below" makes me wonder what's being hidden.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop sending the same UGG creative four days running. Segment by what I've browsed. If I've looked at walking or slip-on shoes, lead with a men's comfort brand like New Balance, Skechers or Hoka.
- 2. State the sale dates in the hero. Say "Today only" or "Starts Friday for VIPs" so I know when 30% applies.
- 3. Add a men's comfort block with slip-ons, cushioned walkers and arch support, with a real price.
- 4. Shorten the fine print or put the key exclusion next to the offer.
- 5. Make the New Balance block a hero with a specific shoe and price, rather than a mood shot.

# Technical Audit: DSW, "UP TO 30% OFF UGG!"

The HTML source was truncated, so findings are limited to the visible `<head>`, the QA output, and the two flagged image URLs.

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a Braze-built XHTML 1.0 Transitional template with dark-mode and web-font handling. Two accessibility gaps and two invalid `<meta>` tags are confirmed in the source. The plain-text part is heavily URL-weighted, and authentication, unsubscribe and UTM compliance could not be verified from the data provided.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Plain-text part is URL-heavy.** The QA check reports 22,395 of 26,075 characters (about 86%) are URLs. This is above the 70% threshold. It is likely caused by long click-tracking URLs (`ablink.dsw.com/q/...~~/AAQRxRA~/...`) in the text alternative, and it is a spam-filter risk.
- **Image `src` points at a click-tracker.** The image `PZKtQFCy3H_2Nl_...` loads from `https://ablink.dsw.com/q/Icvf6yCm-KUGiXoUvyQf4g~~/AAQRxRA~/...`. `ablink.dsw.com` is a click-tracking redirect domain. If the image is wrapped, it may count as a click or fail to load in clients that block redirects. I could not confirm from the truncated source whether this is a wrapped image or the open pixel.
- **Link coverage.** 12 links were probed and 51 skipped. The skipped links are footer, utility and social links, and the unsubscribe link is always included. The QA data gives no per-link status, so I can't confirm any broken links.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt attributes (2, from QA):**
  - `PZKtQFCy3H_...` (`ablink.dsw.com` image). If it is content, it needs descriptive alt text. If it is decorative, it needs `alt=""`.
  - `https://mi.dsw.com/p/up/e6f23fb0.../o.gif`. This is a tracking pixel and should carry `alt=""`. The missing attribute is low severity but still invalid for accessibility.
- **Viewport blocks zoom.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` prevents pinch-zoom, which conflicts with WCAG 1.4.4.
- **No `lang` attribute.** The source shows `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` with no `lang` or `xml:lang`. Screen readers can't determine the language.
- **Two `<meta>` tags have no `http-equiv`:**
  - `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />`
  - `<meta content="IE=Edge" />`
  
  Both are inert as written. Charset is then declared only by the MIME header, and the IE compatibility directive does nothing.
- **Web fonts.** The Futura and HelveticaNow `@font-face` rules load from `braze-images.com` as `.woff` and `.ttf`. Only Apple Mail and a few other clients support them, so most clients will use the fallback stack. I couldn't see the fallback stack in the truncated source, so I can't confirm it is adequate.
