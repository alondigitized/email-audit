---

## 1. Executive Summary

The front half of this journey — browsing, finding Slip-ins, landing on a product — is genuinely decent for a guy my age. Big text, clear categories, the BOGO deal front and center. But the back half collapses entirely. I never actually got logged in, "Add to Cart" threw a validation error before I could complete it, and the cart page loaded as a completely blank white screen. You can't buy a shoe from a blank page. Skechers is doing the hard work of getting me to the product and then fumbling the handoff at the moment that matters most.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

**Flat vs. yesterday (4/10).** Navigation is a slight improvement — "Hands Free Slip-ins" is one tap from the Men's menu, which I genuinely appreciated. But the blank cart is a conversion-killing bug that erases any gains. No regression, no recovery. Same broken floor.

---

## 3. What's Working

**BOGO deal is everywhere, impossible to miss.** That blue "BOGO 50% OFF Applied at Cart" bar rode along on every single page — homepage through search results. For a guy who watches his wallet, that kind of persistent reinforcement works. I never had to wonder if the deal still applied.

**Men's menu is well-organized.** The slide-out drawer (Step 4) had large, spaced-out text — I could read every item without squinting. "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Extra Wide Fit" were right there as flat quick-links at the bottom. Someone thought about my demographic.

**Slip-ins category page hits the right notes.** "Hands Free Slip-ins" as the page header, fit filters (Wide Fit, Extra Wide Fit, Medium Fit) as blue pill buttons, 301 results — that's a category page that respects what I came for. Clean breadcrumb too.

**Product page is readable and deal-forward.** The Contour Foam Kiroh page showed the name in big bold text, price at $88 clearly, and the BOGO callout in red right under it. No hunting required.

**Size grid has usable tap targets.** The size buttons in Step 7 are big enough that I'm not playing finger-precision games. That's more than I can say for a lot of sites.

**ADD TO CART button is excellent.** Full-width, dark blue, high contrast. Exactly how a mobile CTA should look.

**Search returns relevant results.** "Comfort shoes" pulled 1,485 results and led with Skechers Slip-ins: Arch Fit Arcade — Slip-ins and Arch Fit in the same product name is exactly what I searched for. The Men's version was right beside the Women's with clear labeling. Good relevance.

---

## 4. What's Weak

**Login never happened.** The "Login | details" prompt in the member banner persisted through every single step — Steps 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, and 9. The account icon never changed. Whatever the login step attempted, it didn't complete. I never got a logged-in state, which means zero personalization — no saved size, no loyalty points balance shown, no order history. I'm a stranger on my own account.

**The cart is a blank white page.** Step 8 — empty. Completely blank. Not a loading spinner, not an error message, not a partially-rendered cart. Just white. This is not a minor friction point; this is the entire reason the site exists, and it's broken. If this happened to me in real life, I'm closing the browser and heading to DSW.

**"Add to Cart" blocked by validation before the cart even loads.** Step 7 shows a red "⚠ Please select a size" warning, and the cart counter in the header still reads zero. The cart failure compounds this — even if I selected a size, the destination was broken.

**Only 1 review on the product page.** One. I trust brands more when other people have already worn them. One review tells me almost nothing. For $88, I want to know if these actually hold up over six months of walking, not just one person's first impression.

**No comfort content on the product detail.** The product is called "Contour Foam" but I saw no callout explaining what that means for my feet — no arch support mention, no cushion depth, nothing about how this compares to Air-Cooled Memory Foam or GOga Mat. I'm supposed to take the name on faith.

**Popup "dismissal" wasn't meaningful.** Step 2 just swapped the promo banner message from Member Month to Summer Apparel Sale. No modal was shown or dismissed. That's either a missed upsell (newsletter, loyalty signup) or the capture worked but isn't visible at this viewport.

**Still not logged in = no personalization anywhere.** Every page feels like I walked into a store where nobody knows me. "Member Month! Earn 2X Points" is in the banner but I can't access it because login is broken.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** Blank white cart = zero revenue. This is the single highest-priority fix on the site, full stop. A/B test it if you must, but a blank cart is not a viable state for any user.

2. **Confirm and surface the logged-in state.** After login, show my name or a "Hi, Walker" greeting somewhere visible. Change the account icon. Show my points balance on the homepage. I should feel recognized.

3. **Add comfort technology callouts on product pages.** Under the price, before the size grid, tell me what "Contour Foam" does for my arch and heel. One line: "Dual-layer foam with contoured arch support for all-day comfort." That's the reason I'm buying Skechers instead of Brooks.

4. **Surface social proof or address the review gap.** One review isn't enough to justify $88. Either surface aggregate ratings from similar styles, or add a "verified fit" badge, or pull in a brief comfort summary. Give me a reason to trust the shoe beyond the name.

5. **Pre-fill size on the product page if logged in.** I have a saved size. Use it. The "Please select a size" error wouldn't happen if the site already knew I wear a 10.5 wide.

6. **Add a "Compare with Arch Fit" or cross-brand comparison nudge.** I cross-shop Hoka and New Balance. Skechers won't stop me from comparison shopping, but they could anchor my decision with a side-by-side comfort spec table that makes the Slip-ins look best-in-class.

Skechers has a real product for my demographic — slip-ins, arch fit, wide fit, comfort tech — and the navigation on mobile is genuinely finding its footing. But broken login and a blank cart mean I literally cannot buy the shoe I found. You can't win a customer you lose at the register. Fix the cart, fix the login state, add comfort credibility to the product page. Until then, this journey ends in frustration, not a purchase.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The skechers.com mobile journey has three systemic issues driving most findings: (1) Kasada bot-detection (KPSDK) rate-limiting its own fingerprinting calls, cascading into a broken cart page with invalid HTML; (2) an extremely heavy third-party script payload with several duplicate injections; and (3) a cluster of landmark/ARIA violations on the homepage suggesting an improperly nested component tree. The Stylitics CORS block is a silent widget failure unrelated to the above.

---

## Accessibility

| Severity | Rule | Page | Root cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical | `aria-required-parent` | Homepage | ARIA role applied to element outside required parent container (likely a list/grid component) |
| Serious | `document-title` | View Cart | `<title>` absent — cart page returned by Kasada 429 fallback lacks full HTML scaffold |
| Serious | `html-has-lang` | View Cart | Same cause — error response HTML omits `lang` attribute |
| Moderate | `heading-order` | Homepage | H levels skip, likely H1→H3 in a promotional component |
| Moderate | `landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-unique` | Homepage | Multiple `<main>` or nested `<main>` elements — Monetate/personalization injection likely inserting a second document structure |
| Moderate | `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` | Homepage, Search | `<aside>` nested inside another sectioning element |
| Moderate | `page-has-heading-one`, `landmark-one-main` | View Cart | Again: stripped error-page HTML, not a real cart render |
| Moderate | `region` | Homepage, Search | Content outside any landmark — likely ad/tracking widgets rendered outside the main content flow |

**Priority fix:** The View Cart `document-title` + `html-has-lang` + missing `<main>` cluster is almost certainly a Kasada 429 returning a bare error page rather than the actual cart. Users on flagged sessions see an invalid, inaccessible document.

## Mobile Optimization

- `--vh: 6.640000000000001px` is set inline on `<html>`. The floating-point precision noise (`6.640000000000001` vs `6.64`) is a JavaScript `innerHeight / 100` calculation without rounding. Minor but spams the style attribute with garbage precision.
- WebFontLoader 1.6.28 via cdnjs loads the Mulish font family asynchronously — the `wf-*-active` classes on `<html>` confirm it's working, but this pattern is deprecated; native `@font-face` with `font-display: swap` in CSS is the current standard and avoids the extra JS parse/execute cost.
- No evidence of `<meta name="viewport">` misconfiguration from the truncated HTML, but not confirmed present.

## Console & Network Errors

**Kasada KPSDK 429s (5 network + reflected in 6 console errors):**

```
https://www.skechers.com/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/
  2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.470
```

The KPSDK fingerprinting endpoint is rate-limiting its own requests from the browser. This path pattern is Kasada's client-side bot-challenge script calling home to validate the session token. Five 429s in a single journey means either the session was flagged as non-human (likely due to automated audit tooling), or Kasada's backend is throttling. The downstream consequence: `/cart/` also returned 429, rendering the cart page as a stripped error document.

**Stylitics CORS block:**

```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?base=hotspots&theme=skechers&version=e1a1d2e966'
has been blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```

Stylitics's CDN is not sending `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` for this CSS file. The outfit/hotspot widget will fail silently. This is a Stylitics CDN configuration issue — the request origin (`skechers.com`) is not in their CORS allowlist for this asset. Needs to be escalated to Stylitics.

## Recommendations

**P0 — Fix or route around Kasada breaking the cart:**
The bot-detection layer returning a 429 on `/cart/` without a proper HTML fallback is a checkout blocker. Legitimate users on flagged sessions get an inaccessible page with no title, no lang, and no main landmark. At minimum the 429 response should return a full HTML document with a human-readable error and proper document structure.

**P1 — Remove duplicate script injections via GTM audit:**
`ytc.js` (Yahoo) and `dhGobFICKSkk6J9nvQPw.js` (CreativeCDN) fire twice. Audit GTM container for duplicate tags. This is pure waste — extra HTTP requests, extra JS parse time, potential double-counting in analytics.

**P1 — Fix ARIA `aria-required-parent` on homepage:**
A critical axe violation. Identify the component rendering an ARIA role (likely `role="option"` or `role="listitem"`) outside its required container and fix the DOM hierarchy.

**P2 — Fix Stylitics CORS:**
File a ticket with Stylitics to add `skechers.com` to the `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` allowlist for `web-assets.stylitics.com`. No code change on Skechers's side; this is a vendor configuration fix.

**P2 — Collect LCP and CLS:**
All LCP and CLS values were missing from this audit pass. These are the two Web Vitals most heavily weighted by Google's ranking signal. Run a Lighthouse CI or CrUX collection pass to fill the gap.

**P3 — Round the `--vh` CSS variable:**
Change the JS viewport-height calculation from `window.innerHeight / 100` to `Math.round(window.innerHeight / 100 * 10000) / 10000` or equivalent. Eliminate floating-point noise in the inline style attribute.

**P3 — Migrate off WebFontLoader:**
Replace `webfontloader.js` + GTM injection with native `@font-face { font-display: swap }` in the site CSS. Eliminates one async JS file and one third-party cdnjs dependency.
