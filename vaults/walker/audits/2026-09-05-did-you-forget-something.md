# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-05**
## Executive summary

- Well, would you look at that — it's the pink floral D'Lites again, parked under "READY TO CHECK OUT?" like I'm one tap away from buying a sneaker with flowers stitched on it. I have never clicked on that shoe, never put it in a cart, and this is at least the fourth time this week Skechers has sent me this exact routine. I scrolled past the fake cart nag today though and actually found something useful further down — a real grid of shoes including men's slip-ins and an Arch Fit pair, plus a 20% off member discount banner that's a genuine number I can use.
- The problem is the email leads with the wrong shoe and buries the good stuff. If I open this on my phone, the first thing I see is a floral sneaker that has nothing to do with me, and I'd have to scroll a good ways to hit anything relevant. I'm not deleting it in disgust like the earlier ones this week, but I'm also not clicking "Complete Your Order" — I never started one. I'd scroll to the men's section or the 20% off banner if I bothered to open it at all, but honestly the subject line gives me no reason to.
- Verdict: I wouldn't click the hero CTA, but I might scroll to the member discount banner if I'm already annoyed enough to open it. Not forwarding this — it's the same tired cart trick with a decent offer bolted on underneath.

## What's working

- The 20% off for Skechers Plus members banner is a real, specific number with a deadline ("Thru 9/7") — that's honest and useful.
- There's an actual product grid further down with men's shoes in it — Men's Slip-ins Relaxed Fit and Men's Arch Fit Arcade — so somebody at Skechers remembered I exist.
- Layout is clean, no broken images or overlapping text, buttons are big enough to tap without squinting.
- "Shop Now, Pay Later" and "Find a Store" footer options are handy for someone who likes to actually try shoes on.

## What's weak

- That pink floral D'Lites is back for what feels like the fourth or fifth time this week — I never touched it, and it's exhausting to keep seeing it framed as "my cart."
- The hero banner "READY TO CHECK OUT?" is 100% built around a purchase I never started — that's not personalization, that's a guess that missed.
- Nothing in the subject or hero mentions comfort, cushioning, or arch support — the stuff that actually gets me to click.
- The real offer (20% off) and the relevant men's shoes are buried two-thirds down the email, past the fake-cart theater.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the 20% off member offer and a men's Arch Fit or comfort shoe instead of the fabricated cart reminder — that's the strongest, most honest content in this email and it's currently the last thing I'd see.
- 2. Retire the pink floral D'Lites cart callback entirely — I've now seen it enough times this week that it reads as broken personalization, not a nudge.
- 3. Move the men's product grid up near the top so a guy like me sees something relevant before I decide to bail.
- 4. Put the 9/7 deadline for the 20% off deal in the subject line — that's a real urgency hook, better than a vague "did you forget" guilt trip.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Skechers Plus: 20% off ends 9/7`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your member discount is ready — 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Save 20% on regular-priced styles, including men's Arch Fit`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Members save 20% through Sunday — shop comfort styles now`
