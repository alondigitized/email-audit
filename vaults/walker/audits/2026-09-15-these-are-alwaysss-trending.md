## What's working

- The Skechers "up to 25% off" banner still catches my eye since that's a brand I actually buy.
- "Earn 2X Pts on all Skechers purchases" is a nice touch if I'm already a member — feels like they know I care about points, even if points don't drive my decision.
- The step-up coupon code ($15/$30 off) at the bottom is at least a real, usable discount regardless of brand.

## What's weak

- Hero image and headline are two young women in Converse sneakers — nothing in that visual says "comfort shoe for an older guy."
- No men's product shown anywhere in the entire email — not one photo, not one SKU.
- "Final Day Deals" on the Skechers 25% off feels recycled — I saw this same sale five days ago in the "Skechtember" send, so the deadline urgency doesn't feel credible.
- Way too much going on — Converse, workwear boots, Journee boots, mobile wallet, step-up coupon — I have to hunt for the one section that's relevant to me.
- No mention of cushioning, arch support, or slip-on comfort anywhere, which is the whole reason I open a shoe email.

- 1. Lead the email with a men's comfort shoe — Skechers Slip-ins or an Arch Fit style — instead of Converse on twentysomethings. That's the single biggest lift available here.
- 2. Don't recycle "Final Day" language on a sale that's been running all week — either make it genuinely urgent or drop the fake deadline.
- 3. Cut the email down — Converse, Journee boots, and workwear can go to their own targeted sends instead of one everything-email.
- 4. Show the men's Skechers styles specifically if you're going to segment by purchase history — I've bought comfort walkers before, so show me that, not leggings and a dog.
