# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 2/10 (prev 2/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-09-12**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

Well, I made it from the homepage all the way to the cart... and then the cart just wasn't there. Blank white screen. Nothing. I picked out a nice pair of the Slip-ins Contour Foam ones, hit "Add to Cart" without grabbing a size first (my bad, sorta — more on that below), and when I went to check my cart, the page just gave up on me. No basket icon, no "your cart is empty," no header, nothing but white. That's the kind of thing that makes a fella close the browser tab and go check Amazon instead. On top of that, the "Log In" step didn't show me a login screen at all — it's the same picture of that couple walking down the steps, three times in a row. Either the login button doesn't do anything on mobile, or whatever this thing captured, it wasn't a login form. The bright spot: browsing menus and searching "comfort shoes" actually worked well and showed me relevant shoes with wide-fit options called out.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**2/10** — Same as yesterday, no real change. A shopper cannot see what's in his cart. That's a hard stop on any purchase, and no amount of nice product photography upstream fixes a broken cart page downstream.

## 3. What's Working

- The homepage leads with comfort-technology callouts right in the top nav — Slip-ins, Wide Fit, Arch Fit, Max Cushioning — which is exactly the kind of shorthand I look for. Good instinct.
- The hamburger menu for Men is clean: big tappable rows (Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections), plus thumbnail photos so you know you're in the right place.
- The Men's Shoes category page surfaces "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" as its first two tiles — again, right where my eyes go.
- The product page for the Slip-ins Contour Foam is legible: name, star rating (4.5, 118 reviews), price ($88) all sit up top without me scrolling or squinting.
- Search for "comfort shoes" returned genuinely relevant results — a Slip-ins Go Walk Joy and a Max Cushioning Elite 3 — with a "Also in Wide" tag on one of them. That's useful, real information, not noise.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Cart page is completely blank.** After tapping Add to Cart, the View Cart screen rendered nothing — no header, no nav bar, no items, no empty-cart message. This is a full breakdown of the purchase path.
- **Login never appeared.** The "Log In" step screenshot is pixel-identical to the homepage. I never saw a sign-in form, so I have no idea if my account, my order history, or my saved size would even show up. No personalization was demonstrated anywhere in this run.
- **Add to Cart let me proceed without a size**, then scolded me with "Please select a size" only after I'd already tapped the button — and several sizes (6.5, 7.0, 11.5, 14.0) were greyed out/struck through with no explanation why, which is confusing when you're trying to figure out if your size even exists in this style.
- Cart icon in the header shows "0" through literally every single step of the journey, including after Add to Cart — so even setting aside the blank cart page, nothing confirms an item was ever added.
- Small print throughout (the top orange sale banner especially) is tiny — I'd be pinching to zoom on that if I weren't just scrolling past it.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page rendering — top priority, full stop.** A blank screen after checkout intent is a guaranteed lost sale. Even an empty-cart state needs a header, nav, and a "start shopping" prompt.
2. **Make the login flow actually reachable and testable on mobile** — either the tap target isn't triggering a modal, or the modal isn't rendering. Either way, a shopper needs to be able to sign in and see his account reflected somewhere (saved sizes, order history, loyalty points).
3. **Confirm cart count updates immediately after Add to Cart** so a customer isn't left wondering if the button actually did anything.
4. **Nudge size selection earlier or more visibly** — grey out the Add to Cart button until a size is picked, or auto-scroll to the size grid, instead of letting the tap fail silently and then flash a small red warning.
5. Keep doing what search and category browsing are doing — comfort-first framing, wide-fit callouts — just get the transaction plumbing working so people can actually buy the shoe they found.

## 6. Bottom Line

You can find a comfortable shoe here just fine. You cannot buy one. That's the whole ballgame for an ecommerce site, and until the cart page stops going blank, none of the nice comfort-tech merchandising upstream matters. No regression from yesterday's 2/10, but no real improvement on the thing that actually matters either — the same purchase-blocking wall is still standing.

Genuinely useful and on-target for what I typed in. Cart badge still shows "0" here too, consistent with Step 7/8's failure.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary
Mobile audit covered 8 steps from homepage through search. Findings: 18 axe-core accessibility violations (2 critical, 4 serious), missing `<title>`/`lang`/`<h1>` on the cart page, a blocked cross-origin stylesheet, and repeated 429s from a bot-detection/fingerprinting endpoint and the cart page itself. LCP/CLS were not captured for any step, so the performance picture below is TTFB-only.

## Accessibility
- **Critical:** `button-name` and `aria-required-parent` failures on Homepage — buttons without discernible text and ARIA roles used without required parent context will break screen-reader navigation.
- **Serious:** `link-name` (Homepage) and `document-title` + `html-has-lang` (View Cart) — cart page ships with no page title and no `lang` attribute, which also affects assistive-tech language switching.
- **Moderate:** Landmark structure is broken across Homepage, View Cart, and Search — `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique`, `landmark-one-main`, `region`, `heading-order`, `page-has-heading-one`. This pattern (duplicate/non-top-level `main`, missing `h1`) suggests a templating issue where personalization/CMS widgets inject nested landmark roles rather than semantic containers.
- **Minor:** `aria-allowed-role` on Homepage and Search (2 occurrences) — invalid role values on non-landmark elements.

## SEO
- View Cart is missing `<title>` and `lang` — both directly impact indexability/crawlability and are also axe a11y failures (see above), indicating the cart route uses a different render path than category/PDP pages.
- `heading-order` and `page-has-heading-one` violations mean multiple templates lack a single canonical `<h1>`, weakening topical signal to crawlers.
- Homepage/Log In/Men category HTML samples all report the same URL (`https://www.skechers.com/`) despite representing distinct user states — if this reflects actual navigation behavior (not just the capture tool), it indicates client-side route changes aren't updating the address bar/History API, which breaks deep-linking, canonical URLs, and analytics/attribution.
- Head is loaded with 20+ third-party async scripts (TikTok, Snapchat, Reddit, Taboola, Monetate, Quantum Metric, Pulse Insights, etc.) before/interleaved with core page scripts — this bloats crawl/render cost per page but doesn't block indexing directly since they're `async`.

## Performance
- TTFB is inconsistent across the funnel: Homepage (443ms), Log In (426ms), Men category (426ms) are notably higher than Men > Shoes (68ms), PDP (60ms), Add to Cart (60ms), Search (58ms), View Cart (125ms). The elevated values on the first three steps combined with unchanged URLs (see SEO note) suggests these were served from a heavier SSR/personalization path (Monetate decisioning, session bootstrap) rather than the lighter cached/PLP routes.
- **LCP and CLS were not captured for any step** — this is an instrumentation gap, not a measured "no issues found." Web Vitals data is required to assess real mobile load experience and should be re-run with a paint-timing observer attached before drawing performance conclusions.

## Mobile Optimization
- Homepage HTML sets `style="--vh: 6.64px"` on `<html>`, indicating a custom viewport-height calculation to work around mobile browser chrome (address bar) resizing — a reasonable mobile-specific mitigation, assuming it's kept in sync via a `resize`/`visualviewport` listener.
- No `<meta name="viewport">` tag was visible in the truncated HTML samples provided; this should be confirmed directly since its absence would be a high-severity mobile rendering issue.
- Full mobile-specific responsive markup (image `srcset`, lazy-loading attributes, breakpoint CSS) could not be assessed from the truncated HTML samples — recommend a follow-up pass with full DOM capture.

## Console & Network Errors
- **429 Too Many Requests** (5 occurrences) on a bot-detection/device-fingerprinting endpoint (`x-kpsdk-v` parameter indicates Kasada bot mitigation) and on `/cart/` itself — the cart page returning 429 is significant, as it can block legitimate mobile users from viewing their cart, likely triggered by the audit's automated/headless traffic pattern tripping rate limits.
- **CORS violation:** `style.css` from `web-assets.stylitics.com` blocked — "No Access-Control-Allow-Origin" — this stylesheet fails to load on every page that includes it, likely affecting the outfit/styling widget's appearance.
- **Unsupported Summarizer API language** warnings (4 occurrences) — a Chrome built-in AI API (`window.ai`/Summarizer) is being invoked with unsupported locale parameters; this is a first-party or third-party script calling an experimental browser API incorrectly, not a user-facing failure but noise indicating unhandled feature-detection.
- `net::ERR_FAILED` corresponds to the blocked Stylitics CSS request above.

## Recommendations
1. Fix critical accessibility issues first: add discernible text/`aria-label` to icon-only buttons and links (Homepage), and correct ARIA role/parent nesting.
2. Add `<title>`, `lang`, and a single `<h1>` to the View Cart template — confirm whether cart renders via a different framework/path than category/PDP pages, since it's missing basics present elsewhere.
3. Consolidate landmark structure (single `<main>`, top-level `<aside>`) across Homepage, Cart, and Search templates to resolve the 7 landmark-related violations.
4. Investigate the 429s on `/cart/` and the fingerprinting endpoint — determine if rate limits are triggered by legitimate mobile traffic patterns or only automated/audit traffic; a real user hitting a 429 on cart view is a checkout-blocking bug.
5. Fix CORS headers on `web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css` or self-host/proxy the asset.
6. Re-run the audit with LCP/CLS instrumentation attached — current performance data is TTFB-only and insufficient to certify mobile load performance.
7. Confirm whether Homepage/Log In/Men category are genuinely served at the same URL; if so, fix client-side routing to update the URL via the History API for correct deep-linking and analytics attribution.
