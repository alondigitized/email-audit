# NEW! to Sale: 30% OFF NIKE + JORDAN
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-18**
## Executive summary

- Shoe Carnival again, and this time they're pushing Nike and Jordan. That's a young man's shoe, not mine. The top of the email is easy to read on my phone: a big red "30% OFF" with "BIGGEST SALE OF THE YEAR ON NIKE & JORDAN." The photo shows a young couple and a kid holding up sneakers, and none of them look like me.
- Below that, it turns into the same catalog I've been getting all week. There's Skechers at 25% off, booties, cozy and clog sales, and the same HOLIDAY $10 / $25 coupon I got on the 13th and 16th. The Skechers block is the only part that speaks to me, and it's tucked second. It does say "Glide-Step," which is a real comfort line.
- Would I click? Probably the Skechers link, if I'm in the market. I wouldn't forward it. It's another "Black Friday" email in a week of them, and Black Friday is still more than a week away.

## What's working

- The 30% OFF hero is huge and readable without my glasses.
- The Skechers 25% off block is a brand I know and cross-shop. The Glide-Step name means something to me.
- The $10 / $25 coupon with the code HOLIDAY is clear, and it has barcodes I can scan at the register.
- The "Shop Skechers" and "Shop Nike & Jordan" links sit right under each block, so I know where each one goes.
- The footer has a clear Womens / Mens / Kids / Accessories bar, so I can get to Mens without hunting.

## What's weak

- The hero is Nike and Jordan with a young family. Nothing in it speaks to cushioning, arch support, or slip-ons.
- The "30% OFF*" asterisk is small, and the exclusions sit way down in tiny fine print. The Skechers 25% is "Entire Stock" in the header, but the fine print says "Extra 25% off Skechers" with exclusions.
- Most of the email is women's and kids' items (booties, cozy, clogs). There's very little for a man my age.
- The coupon, wallet add, SMS, Afterpay and game blocks stack up into a long scroll. That's a lot of clutter for one phone screen.
- The Shop Skechers link and the "Select styles" labels are small. They're hard to tap and read.
- "Black Friday Deals Are On" feels stale next to my inbox. It's the fourth or fifth sale email this week, and the coupon is a repeat of last Friday's.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with comfort. Put the Skechers Glide-Step block on top, with a men's walking or slip-on shoe and one clear price.
- 2. Show men my age in the photos, and add a men's section with cushioned walking shoes.
- 3. Cut the repeating coupon, wallet, SMS and Afterpay stack. Say once what's different about this send.
- 4. Make the exclusions legible, or at least put a "see details" link near each offer.
- 5. Make the links bigger and use real button shapes.
- **Subject Alt A:** `30% off Nike & Jordan, 25% off Skechers`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Men's walking shoes: 25% off Skechers`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cushioned walkers and slip-ons, plus code HOLIDAY for $10 or $25 off`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sale ends 11/22. Men's comfort picks inside.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's HTML is a conventional table-based layout with responsive CSS. It has two content images with no alt text and two tracking-pixel defects: an unresolved `[UNIQUE]` token and a campaign name that references the wrong brand. SPF/DKIM/DMARC, unsubscribe and link destinations could not be verified from the data supplied.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unresolved token in the open pixel:** `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]` still contains the literal `[UNIQUE]` placeholder. The ESP did not substitute it at send time, so this pixel cannot identify the recipient and open attribution is likely broken for it.
- **Malformed pixel URL:** `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20261118_skechers_nike_jordan_AM​`
  - The `t=` value is itself a `utm_campaign=...` string, so the parameters are nested rather than separate.
  - The URL ends with an invisible zero-width character (U+200B) after `_AM`. This can corrupt campaign-name matching in analytics.
- **Campaign name references the wrong brand:** the value `SCA_20261118_skechers_nike_jordan_AM` says "skechers" in a Shoe Carnival send (`shoecarnival@email.shoecarnival.com`). This looks like a template copy-over and will misattribute the campaign in reporting.
- **30 tracking links not probed:** the QA run skipped them as click-redirect domains. Destination status codes, redirect chains and UTM preservation are unverified.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt on content images (confirmed by QA):**
  - `https://image.email.shoecarnival.com/lib/fe3311737164047d701077/m/1/23a03225-7c93-49fa-988c-8821675cac1b.jpg`
  - `https://image.email.shoecarnival.com/lib/fe3311737164047d701077/m/1/fe4e293c-bccf-488a-bbf7-db06bf5745de.jpg`

  With images blocked or a screen reader, these show nothing or the raw filename. If either carries the Nike/Jordan offer, the message is lost.
- **Tracking pixels have no `alt`:** the two `emltrk.com` images lack it. They should carry `alt=""` so assistive tech skips them.
- **Link affordance removed globally:** the global rule `a, a:link, a:visited, a:hover, a:active ... { text-decoration:none !important; color:inherit !important; }` strips underline and colour from every link. Any text link not otherwise styled will be indistinguishable from body text. This is a minor accessibility risk.
- **Invalid meta tags:** `<meta content="IE=edge" />` has no `http-equiv`, so it does nothing. `<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />` has no `http-equiv` either, though the `charset` attribute still declares the encoding. Both are harmless but non-standard.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The one unresolved token found is `[UNIQUE]` in the `0di86k1v.emltrk.com` pixel URL (see section 2).
- No other raw tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`, `[FIRSTNAME]`) appear in the truncated HTML. The rest of the body was not available to check.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Authentication-Results not found:** this is a QA warning, expected through the AgentMail relay. SPF/DKIM/DMARC status is unknown, not shown to be failing.
- **Not verifiable:** the HTML supplied is truncated before the footer. The unsubscribe link, physical postal address and `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers could not be checked.
- The sender domain `email.shoecarnival.com` matches the image host `image.email.shoecarnival.com`. This is consistent, with no mismatch.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **UTM campaign value is unreliable:** it names the wrong brand and carries a trailing U+200B (section 2). Site analytics keyed on it would be misattributed or split.
- **Landing pages not verified:** all 30 click links were skipped, so landing-page alignment with "30% OFF NIKE + JORDAN" and UTM preservation across redirects are unconfirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Fix the `[UNIQUE]` substitution in the `0di86k1v.emltrk.com` pixel. Check the ESP's personalization syntax and the send-time merge.
2. Correct `utm_campaign` to the right brand, for example `SCA_20261118_shoecarnival_nike_jordan_AM`. Strip the U+200B and restructure the `t=` parameter so UTMs are separate query params.
3. Add descriptive `alt` text to the two `image.email.shoecarnival.com` JPEGs, and `alt=""` to both tracking pixels.
4. Restore a visible link style, such as underline or colour, for text links by overriding the global `color:inherit !important; text-decoration:none !important` where needed.
5. Re-run QA with the full HTML and raw headers, so footer, unsubscribe, address, `Authentication-Results` and `List-Unsubscribe` can be checked.
6. Resolve a sample of the 30 tracking links with a follow-redirects probe to confirm final URLs, status codes and UTM carry-through.
7. Fix the meta tags: `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge">` and `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">`.
