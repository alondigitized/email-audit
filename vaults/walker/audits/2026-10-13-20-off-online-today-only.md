# 20% Off Online TODAY ONLY!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-13**
## Executive summary

- Same-day follow-up to this morning's "Happening NOW! 20% Off w/ code FALL20," and the offer is identical. The yellow "20% OFF" and "CODE: FALL20" are big enough that I can read them without my glasses, and I like that. But "TODAY ONLY" on the same code I got hours ago makes me trust the deadline less. Last week it was "BUY MORE, SAVE MORE" every day, and now it's the same code with a new "final day" label.
- The rest of the email is the usual long scroll. The woman in clogs sits right under the offer, and there's no man in the picture. Sandals, boots, flats, bootie tiles, a word game, Afterpay and SMS signup all follow. The Skechers tile at $59.98 and the family athletics tile are the only parts that speak to me. They're small and buried halfway down.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I were already shopping for cushioned slip-ons. I wouldn't forward it. I'd skim it and move on.

## What's working

- The "20% OFF" and "CODE: FALL20" are huge black-on-yellow type. I can read them on my phone without zooming.
- The Skechers tile is there, with adult shoes starting at $59.98 and kids at $39.98. I know that brand and trust it.
- The "Family Athletics" tile lists New Balance and ASICS. Those are brands I already compare.
- The offer matches the earlier send, so the code is the same one I saw this morning.
- The "Add coupon to your mobile wallet" block with barcodes is handy if I walk into a store.

## What's weak

- "Today only" on the same FALL20 code from this morning feels fake. It's the second send today.
- The hero image is a young woman in clogs, and I don't see a single man or anyone near my age.
- Nothing mentions cushioning, arch support, slip-on or hands-free, and no men's walking shoes appear above the fold.
- The "Shop Now" link under the code is small and plain, not a real button. Several of the "Shop..." links further down are tiny too.
- The email is very long, with Birkenstock, Fall Shop, boots, flats, a word game, Afterpay and SMS. It buries the one offer I came for.
- The fine print is tiny and dense. It says "some exclusions apply" next to the offer, and I'd have to read it to know whether Skechers counts.
- It carries the orange "BUY MORE, SAVE MORE" coupon block again, with $10 off $74.98 and $25 off $149.98, in store only. That sits right beside an online-only 20% and muddies things.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to one offer and one real button, like "Shop Men's Comfort Shoes." Drop the games and the Afterpay and SMS blocks.
- 2. Put a man's cushioned slip-on or walking shoe in the hero, or let me choose a men's hero.
- 3. Don't send a second "today only" with the same code. Say "Last chance: ends at midnight ET" and show the actual time.
- 4. Move the Skechers and New Balance tiles up, and add words like "memory foam" and "arch support."
- 5. Make the "Shop Now" link a full-width button, and state plainly which brands are excluded.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Code FALL20 ends tonight. Comfort shoes included`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Skechers from $59.98, plus New Balance and ASICS. Online only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Use FALL20 at checkout before midnight ET. Exclusions apply.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The provided source and QA data show no hard failures. The QA summary reports 100% pass, but two image-tag warnings point to malformed or unresolved tracking-pixel URLs. The HTML is truncated and no headers were supplied, so compliance and landing-page checks can't be confirmed.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **30 tracking links were not probed.** The QA run skipped them because they are on a click-redirect domain (`*.emltrk.com`). Redirect destinations, status codes and chain length are unverified.
- **Unresolved placeholder in a pixel URL.** `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]` still contains the literal `[UNIQUE]` token. If this is the delivered markup, the ESP did not substitute the value. The pixel then can't identify the recipient and open tracking for it is likely broken.
- **Malformed pixel URL.** `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20261013_clogs_birkenstock_AM​` has two problems:
  - A zero-width space (U+200B) trails `..._AM`. Depending on the client and tracker, it can be percent-encoded into the parameter value or break the request.
  - The value `utm_campaign=...` is nested inside the `t=` parameter, so the string will be parsed as `t` = `utm_campaign=SCA_...` and not as a standalone UTM parameter.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Two images have no `alt` attribute:** the pixels at `ieezzy5m.emltrk.com` and `0di86k1v.emltrk.com`. If they are 1×1 tracking pixels, add `alt=""`, `width="1"`, `height="1"` and `style="display:none"` so screen readers skip them.
- **Link styling removes visual affordance.** The global rule `a, a:link, a:visited, a:hover, a:active { text-decoration:none !important; color:inherit !important; }` strips underlines and link colour from every anchor. Text links are then only distinguishable if they are styled inline or are buttons. I could not verify this in the truncated body.
- **Invalid meta tags.** `<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />` and `<meta content="IE=edge" />` lack `http-equiv`. This is harmless in practice, but invalid.
- **Doctype and syntax mismatch.** The HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype is combined with `xmlns` and self-closing `/>` tags. Clients tolerate this, so it is low severity.
- `lang="en"`, a viewport meta tag and a `color-scheme` meta tag are present, so no issues there.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The only unresolved token found is `[UNIQUE]` in the pixel URL above (see Section 2).
- No other merge tokens (`{{ }}`, `%%`, `*|`) appear in the visible source. The remaining body was truncated and can't be assessed.
