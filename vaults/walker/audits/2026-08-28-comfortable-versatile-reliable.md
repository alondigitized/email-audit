# Comfortable, versatile, reliable.
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-28**
## Executive summary

- Straight off, this is a runner's email — running shoes, running apparel, a couple in their twenties in performance gear posing for "The RC Collection." I'm 62, I walk for comfort, I'm not training for a 5K. New Balance has sent me kids' shoes, low-profile grids, new arrivals, and now this — not one of these has been aimed at a guy like me looking for a cushioned, slip-on, arch-support walking shoe.
- The shoes themselves — the 1080v15, the Fresh Foam X 880v15 — those are actually the kind of cushioned New Balance styles I'd wear, so there's something salvageable buried in this grid. But the framing is all wrong: "For every mile," "built for runners," young models in racing gear. That's not my mile.
- I'd scroll past this one. Not annoying enough to unsubscribe over, but nothing here makes me stop and tap. Wouldn't forward it — nothing to share with a friend, no deal, no story.

## What's working

- The 1080v15 and Fresh Foam X 880v15 look like genuinely comfortable, cushioned shoes I'd actually consider.
- Price-tier buttons ($75–99.99, $100–124.99, $125–149.99) are a nice honest way to shop by budget without hiding the numbers.
- Free shipping on $75+ banner up top is clear and easy to read.
- Clean grid layout, no rendering bugs, text is legible without zooming.

## What's weak

- Whole email is "For every mile" / runner-focused — nothing speaks to comfort-casual walking, which is what I actually shop for.
- The RC Collection section is all young, fit runners in racing tops and short-shorts — not my demographic, not my occasion.
- No offer beyond a vague price range — no percentage off, no promo code, nothing that feels like a deal.
- Same eight-tile product grid format New Balance keeps sending (like the 8/25 "New arrivals" email) — I'm getting numb to this layout.
- Nothing acknowledges me as a repeat subscriber or loyalty member — just a generic blast.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split the audience — send a "walking & everyday comfort" version of this email with lifestyle shots (someone walking a dog, standing all day at work) instead of a runner tying his laces mid-stride.
- 2. Lead with the cushioned models that cross over to comfort shoppers — 880v15, Fresh Foam Arishi — and drop the RC Collection racing gear entirely for guys like me.
- 3. Give me an actual offer — a percent off or a loyalty perk — not just a price range, which isn't a deal, it's just a price.
- 4. Vary the layout — three near-identical grid emails in a week is fatiguing; try a single hero product with a real "why this shoe" story instead.
