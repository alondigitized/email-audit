# 20% off for rewards members 🙌
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-03**
## Executive summary

- Famous Footwear caught my attention with a 20% off members offer, and I do have a rewards account there since they carry Skechers. But once I'm inside the email, it's mostly women's fashion content, Birkenstocks, and "Style of the Week" with a young lady modeling resort wear. As a 62-year-old guy looking for comfortable men's shoes — preferably something slip-on — this email felt like it was addressed to my daughter, not me. The discount is real, the SHOP MEN button is there, but I had to hunt past a lot of stuff that wasn't for me to get to it.

## What's working

- The 20% off member offer is front and center — I see it immediately. It's bold, easy to read, and the code (I can make it out) is clearly displayed. I like that they acknowledged me as a member right in the subject line; at least they know I have an account. The SHOP MEN button in the hero means I don't have to dig to find my section.
- The Birkenstock module is well-laid-out and the product photos are clean. Not my brand, but the section looks professional and easy to browse. Crocs and Brooks modules lower in the email also show specific products with visible pricing, which I appreciate — I hate vague "starting at" language.
- The email is clean — no broken images, no jumbled text, no overlapping blocks. On my phone that matters.

## What's weak

- This email is mostly not for me, and it shows. The "Style of the Week" hero — large photo, lots of real estate — features a young woman in a fashion-forward outfit. That's fine, but it's not speaking to a 62-year-old guy at all. Then comes a big "up to 40% off women's sandals" banner. I'm skimming past half this email because it literally isn't for me.
- The biggest problem I can see before I even open it: the preview text in my inbox is a raw tracking URL — a long string of letters and numbers that means nothing to me. On my phone, that's what I see next to the subject line. It looks broken, maybe even suspicious. That almost made me delete it without opening.
- The Birkenstock push is heavy. I know the brand but it's not what I shop. If they know I've been buying Skechers comfort shoes at their stores, why are they pushing Birkenstock cork sandals at me?
- The email is also quite long — lots of scrolling on mobile to get to anything that might actually apply to me. By the time I got to the Brooks section, I was tired.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix that preview text.** Whatever is causing a URL to leak into the inbox preheader needs to be fixed before the next send. I nearly deleted this unopened.
- 2. **Segment the hero.** If I'm a man with a purchase history in men's comfort/casual, the "Style of the Week" should show men's content — or skip it for my segment entirely.
- 3. **Lead with my products.** 20% off is a good hook. Follow it immediately with a "Top picks for you in Men's Comfort" block. The women's sandals section is wasted real estate for me.
- 4. **Show me Skechers.** If my purchase history is Skechers-heavy, surface those products. That's the product I came to Famous Footwear for.
- 5. **Cut the length.** Two or three focused modules for men's comfort would convert better than six-plus modules where half are irrelevant to me.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Famous Footwear "20% off for rewards members"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard table-layout HTML mailer with responsive media queries and Salesforce Marketing Cloud (ExactTarget) tracking infrastructure. Two mixed-content HTTP image sources and pervasive missing alt text are the primary technical defects; deliverability risk is elevated by a URL-heavy plain-text part.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**30 click-redirect links unverifiable** — all CTAs route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=...`. QA skipped HTTP probing on all 30; destination URLs and redirect chain health are unconfirmed.

**Open pixel uses HTTP indirection** — the 1×1 tracking pixel at `click.email.famousfootwear.com/open.aspx?GPNTEE7IDWNEJIG2CIPCC24IKE.70249` is wrapped in a zero-dimension `<div style="font-size:0;line-height:0">`, which is standard, but the pixel itself should be confirmed HTTPS end-to-end.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**23 images missing `alt` text** — every product/hero/divider image has `alt=""`. In image-blocked environments (Outlook default, Apple Mail Privacy Protection) the email renders as a blank column of empty boxes with no text fallback. Notable gaps:

- Logo: `3b6a7dc1-6955-49f8-868a-bf62a15122ee.png` — no brand name alt
- All product shot JPGs (`ac216ca0`, `52828420`, `ad0cb890`, etc.)
- Social/footer PNGs (`850f4f2d`, `252df0eb`, etc.)

**Two HTTP image sources will be blocked** by modern clients enforcing mixed-content rules:
```
src="http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=ABB7...DW"   (id="920205575250")
src="http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=ABB7...DY"   (id="920155575454")
```
These appear to be ExactTarget LiveContent (dynamic content blocks). HTTP-sourced images are blocked silently in Gmail, Outlook 365, and Apple Mail — the affected content zones will be invisible.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** — `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0">` blocks pinch-to-zoom on mobile, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is flagged as an accessibility violation by most auditors.

**`lineapacing` typo** — `style="...lineapacing:20px..."` on a `<td>` in the header block is a non-property and will be silently ignored by all clients.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (e.g., `%%FIRSTNAME%%`, `[FIRST_NAME]`) are visible in the truncated source. The subject line contains no personalization token — consistent with no in-body tokens found. No issues confirmed.

**Authentication headers**: Not present in the provided data — DKIM, SPF, and DMARC alignment for `email.famousfootwear.com` sending domain cannot be assessed from HTML alone. No issues found within scope of this review.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 30 CTAs route through the SFMC click-tracking wrapper (`click.email.famousfootwear.com`). UTM parameter presence on destination URLs cannot be confirmed because the click-redirect chain was not probed. If UTMs are injected at the redirect layer rather than hardcoded in the `href`, they will be stripped on any cached or direct-open scenario. This is an unverified risk, not a confirmed defect.

No landing page mismatch issues are detectable without resolving the redirect chains.
