# if you’re in the mood to scroll…
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- I've been getting Crocs emails for weeks now — I've lost count, but I reviewed a "Extra 25% off" email back on June 13th and now here's another one with the exact same offer dressed up in the laziest subject line I've ever seen from this brand. "If you're in the mood to scroll…" is not a reason to open an email. It's barely a sentence. I opened it anyway because I recognized Crocs in the sender line, not because anything compelled me.
- Inside, the offer is there — Extra 25% Off sale styles — and that's legible enough. But three things killed any momentum instantly: the shoes shown are a beige platform clog, a hot pink classic, and a lavender one — none of which are anything I'd put on my feet. Then I scrolled to the fine print and saw the offer ended Monday, June 22nd. Today is the 24th. I'm two days late to a sale I was never invited to in any meaningful way. And there's no actual Shop button anywhere — just app store badges. Not how I shop.
- This one's going to sit in my inbox unclicked. I wouldn't forward it to anyone. I'd mark it read and move on.

## What's working

- The "Extra 25% Off" hero copy is big and readable — I didn't need to squint to understand the offer.
- "Stack the Savings" + "already up to 50% off" stacks the value math clearly — that kind of layered savings framing works on me when the product is right.
- The Crocs logo renders cleanly at the top, so at least I know who sent it in half a second.
- No weird layout breaks or overlapping text — the email displays without visual chaos.

## What's weak

- The offer expired two days ago (fine print says June 22nd, 11:59pm MT). Sending me a sale email after the sale ends isn't a gentle reminder — it's a broken experience.
- No CTA button anywhere in the body. Just app store badges at the bottom. How am I supposed to shop?
- Three pairs of shoes — hot pink, lavender, and a platform style — none of which are men's, none of which say "comfort" or "walking." I'm invisible in this email.
- Jibbitz charms at $2 and $7 means nothing to me. I've never bought a Jibbitz charm and I'm not starting now.
- The preheader is pure invisible padding — "Crocs" followed by a wall of zero-width characters. Blank preview, missed chance.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the offer timing first.** Don't send a sale email two days after the sale closes. Either accelerate this send or extend the deadline — right now the fine print actively undermines the hero offer.
- 2. **Add a men's comfort shop CTA button.** Something like "Shop Men's Sale Styles" — one button, above the shoe triptych, links directly to men's sale footwear.
- 3. **Swap the imagery.** Show a men's casual or walking clog in neutral tones (navy, black, gray) — not platform heels and neon pink.
- 4. **Cut the Jibbitz section** or move it to a secondary module. It's noise for this audience.
- 5. **Rewrite the subject line** with the actual offer in it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 25% off sale styles — stacked savings today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Sale shoes already 50% off — take another 25%`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ends soon: men's and women's styles already marked down, now even lower.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Cushioned, casual, on sale — plus Jibbitz starting at $2.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Missing `alt` on tracking pixel:**
```html
<img style="visibility:hidden;display:none" height="1" width="1"
  src="https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?...">
```
No `alt` attribute present. Should be `alt=""` to satisfy WCAG 2.1 / screen reader pass-through. Low severity since the image is intentionally hidden, but technically non-conformant.

**Issue — 8 HTTP image sources in footer (mixed content):**

All app-store badges and social icons are served over plain HTTP from `image.crocs-email.com`:
```
http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/9c36c0ef-333c-4e6c-bec8-9e0ac799d475.png  (App Store)
http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/13246762-1111-4174-bca8-2f84a6bf62a5.png  (Google Play)
http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/c4df71ec-309f-44f4-b10a-27bb864eeb78.png  (Find Your Store)

http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/80f68cd8-...png  (Facebook)
http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/51d8144e-...png  (TikTok)
http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/1187ef58-...png  (YouTube)
```
Gmail and Apple Mail will block these or downgrade them to broken images when the email is viewed in a secure context. All primary product images use `https://media.crocs.com` — the footer images are inconsistently templated. **This is the highest-severity rendering bug in this send.**

**Issue — Malformed charset `<meta>` tag:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Missing the required `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute. The correct form for email is:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without it, some older Outlook and Lotus Notes clients may default to a different charset. Low-to-medium severity.
