# Bad Bunny Gazelle Solar Gold
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-22**
## Executive summary

- Another adidas email. I've lost count, and this makes about the eighth in a week and a half. This one is a pair of mustard-yellow suede Gazelles with a Bad Bunny tie-in. They're good-looking shoes, I'll give them that. But I'm 62, and a fashion sneaker named for a pop star isn't what I'm shopping for. I want cushioning and arch support, and ideally a slip-on.
- There's no price, no sale and no deadline. There's nothing about how the shoe feels on the foot either. The copy says "glow of the Caribbean at sunset," which is poetry, not information. Compare that to the Oct 15 email with "up to 50% off." At least that one gave me a reason to look.
- I wouldn't click, and I wouldn't forward it. I'd mark it read and move on. My grandson might like the shoes, but he doesn't need me to forward him an ad.

## What's working

- The photos are sharp and clean, and the suede color is easy to see. I know exactly what the shoe looks like.
- The layout is simple: one column, big headline, and two big black "SHOP NOW" buttons. Nothing hides, and the buttons are easy to tap on my phone.
- The body text is a decent size and I could read it without zooming.
- It mentions materials: "premium rich honey suede," a leather lining and a gum outsole. That's at least some substance.

## What's weak

- There's no offer at all. No price, no percentage off, no free shipping, nothing.
- Nothing about comfort. There's no word on cushioning, arch support or whether you can walk in them all day. A flat gum-sole Gazelle isn't known for that.
- This is a fashion-celebrity collab aimed at younger buyers. I felt like a stranger here, not a customer.
- The black banner at the top, "Explore the latest adidas best sellers," competes with the real message. It also doesn't match the shoe I came for.
- Both buttons say just "SHOP NOW." It doesn't say which shoe or what I'll find.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a real reason to buy. Give a price, a member perk or a shipping offer, along with an honest line on who the shoe is for.
- 2. Say what the shoe is like to wear, including cushioning, weight and fit.
- 3. Remove the generic best-sellers banner so the eye goes straight to the shoe.
- 4. Change the button text to name the shoe, such as "Shop the Solar Gold Gazelle."

## Technical Audit

**1. Technical Summary**
The template is mostly sound: it has `lang="en"`, a viewport meta tag and responsive media queries. It has three confirmed problems: no plain-text part, several `http://` image sources, and images with missing or non-decorative alt text. The HTML was truncated and the headers weren't supplied, so compliance and UTM checks can't be completed.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- 19 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are click-redirect domains. Their destinations, status codes and redirect chains are unverified.
- Mixed content: 4 instances of the `arrow` image use `http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg`. The same host is probably reachable over HTTPS.
- The `http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/010201a161647d75-73dbc9b6-…/cMgG-eoOxUdfiCs9cVZLuLAcM1-JBsMG0aum0JK0XUI=452` image looks like the open-tracking pixel. It is served over plain HTTP, so clients that block or rewrite non-HTTPS images will under-count opens.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- The image `dv.adidas.com/o/5970351a-53a1-42d3-ad0d-258b34e31a56` has no alt attribute. It may be the main product image, which would leave the key content invisible to screen readers and to image-blocked clients. Add descriptive alt text.
- The `arrow` images carry the filename-style alt "arrow". If they are decorative, use `alt=""` so screen readers skip them.
- The tracking pixel has no alt. Use `alt=""` with explicit `width="1" height="1"`.
- `<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` has no `http-equiv="Content-Type"`, so it doesn't declare a charset. Some clients may fall back to a default encoding and garble special characters.
- The document uses the HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype with XHTML-style self-closing tags. This is harmless in practice, but inconsistent.
- The `<style>` blocks repeat the same `@font-face` and media-query rules. The `@font-face` rules only use `local('Arial')` and similar sources, so they never load a web font. This adds weight and raises the risk of Gmail's ~102KB clipping, but the total size is unknown from the truncated source.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
No issues found in the visible source. No unresolved tokens such as `%%…%%`, `{{…}}` or `[FIRSTNAME]` appear. The truncation limits this check.

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
- **Plain-text part:** [FAIL] The QA check reports a 0-character text version. A multipart/alternative email without a text part can hurt spam scoring and accessibility. If AgentMail's relay strips this part, the finding is a capture artifact, so check the raw MIME.
- **Authentication:** [WARN] No `Authentication-Results` header was found. This comes from the relay, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC are unverified. It is not evidence of an adidas failure.
- **Unsubscribe link, physical address, List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post headers:** not visible in the truncated HTML or the supplied data, so they can't be assessed.
- The sender is `adidas@us-news.comms.adidas.com`, a subdomain of the click-tracking domain.

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
Can't be assessed. Links are wrapped in `click.comms.adidas.com` redirects, and the destination URLs and UTM parameters weren't resolved. The probe skipped all 19 links.

**7. Recommendations**
1. Add a meaningful plain-text alternative, or confirm the relay isn't dropping it.
2. Serve the `arrow` image and the tracking pixel over HTTPS.
3. Add alt text to the `dv.adidas.com` image, `alt=""` to decorative images (`arrow` and the pixel), and `width="1" height="1"` to the pixel.
4. Change the charset meta to `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8">`.
5. Remove the duplicate `<style>` and `@font-face` blocks.
6. To close the gaps in this audit, re-run with the full HTML and raw headers. Resolve the redirect chains to check UTMs and landing pages. Check `List-Unsubscribe`, the footer unsubscribe link and the physical address, and read SPF, DKIM and DMARC from the original message.
