# Great styles 👠 Greater savings 💰
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- I'm going to be blunt: I've opened more Famous Footwear emails this past week than I have doctor's appointments, and by now I could write the copy myself. BOGO half-off clearance. Same deal I got July 2nd on sandals, July 4th for the holiday blast, and then twice more on the 5th. Now here it is again dressed up with a new subject line — "Great styles, greater savings" — like slapping a fresh coat of paint on the same shed. The sale hasn't changed. The products haven't changed. Even that broken preheader tracking URL is still there, same as it's been for weeks.
- What really got under my skin this time is that little heel emoji 👠 sitting right in the subject line. A women's high heel. I'm a 62-year-old man shopping for walking shoes and slides, and the very first thing I see in my inbox is a stiletto. That's not a small thing — that one character told me right away this email wasn't built for me.
- I'd mark this as read and move on. Wouldn't click, wouldn't forward. I'm not saying the deal is bad, I'm saying I'm exhausted by the same deal arriving in a new costume every 24 hours.

## What's working

- **BOGO half-off clearance hero is immediately legible** — even on my phone at arm's length, I could read the offer without zooming.
- **"SHOP MEN" CTA is visible** — at least they remembered I exist somewhere below the fold.
- **3X points callout is there** — I do have a Rewards account, so that bonus landing in my eyeline is a genuine nudge.
- **Slides and casual walking shoes are in the product grid** — the Crocs-style slides and the cushioned sneakers are actually in my wheelhouse, so the merchandise itself isn't wrong.

## What's weak

- **That 👠 heel emoji in the subject killed the targeting** — a women's dress shoe is the exact opposite of what I shop for; it signals "this email is for someone else."
- **Broken preheader — again** — raw tracking URL as preview text, same bug Famous Footwear has been shipping since at least late June. This is not a new observation, and it should have been fixed three sends ago.
- **"Stars, stripes and savings" hero is two days stale** — the July 4th patriotic framing felt timely on the 4th; on July 6th it just looks like they forgot to update the template.
- **No comfort language anywhere in the visible copy** — cushion, arch support, wide width, slip-on — none of it. I'm shopping by feel, not by "style," and this email speaks entirely to style.
- **Fifth spin of the same promotion** — I got the same BOGO clearance pitch on July 2, July 4, and twice on July 5. Cadence this aggressive reads as desperation, not value.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the women's heel emoji from any send that includes "SHOP MEN."** Segment by gender or use a neutral shoe emoji. One mismatched character in the subject line erases any targeting credit the rest of the email earns.
- 2. **Fix the preheader.** This is the fifth email in a row with a raw tracking URL as the preview text. A single line of real copy — "BOGO half-off ends soon + earn 3X points this weekend" — would move the open needle more than any hero redesign.
- 3. **Retire the July 4th hero creative.** On July 6th, "stars, stripes and savings" reads as a leftover. A simple "Clearance is live — BOGO 1/2 off" keeps the deal front and center without the stale seasonal wrapper.
- 4. **Add one comfort signal in the hero or subhead** — "Cushioned walkers, slides, and everyday sneakers — all BOGO" would tell me this email has shoes for my feet, not just feet in general.
- 5. **Slow the cadence.** Five near-identical promotional emails in four days trains me to ignore Famous Footwear in my inbox. One well-crafted send with a deadline would outperform five identical blasts every time.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — 30 images missing alt text**
Every content image uses `alt=""`. QA flagged 30 distinct image assets. When images are blocked (common in Outlook, corporate mail), the email renders as a blank white block. Evidence — representative samples:
- `3b6a7dc1-6955-49f8-868a-bf62a15122ee.png` (logo image, linked, `alt=""`)
- `e8a8f339-b583-4a79-909e-54142232bd21.jpg`
- `8d2a3a15-f25a-4d2b-9f44-7b58df20c3b6.jpg`
- *(27 additional)*

**Medium — `user-scalable=0` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
Blocks pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Violates WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 and iOS accessibility guidelines.

**Low — CSS property typo**
In the "Want Free Shipping?" `<td>`:
```
style="...lineapacing:20px;..."
```
`lineapacing` is not a valid CSS property. The declaration is silently ignored; intended value (likely `line-height` or `letter-spacing`) is lost.

**Low — Malformed charset meta**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. The charset declaration is inert as written; browsers/clients fall back to their default encoding detection. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```

**Low — HTML 4.01 Transitional DOCTYPE**
Legacy but universally accepted in email clients. Not a defect, noted for completeness.
