# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-11-21**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I walked through skechers.com on my phone and got as far as picking a shoe. I never got it into a cart.

The front half is good for a guy like me. The top bar has "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Arch Fit." The Men's menu has a "Comfort Technologies" section. The Men's Shoes page has tiles for "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes." That is the shoe I'm shopping for, and they put it up front.

The back half fell apart:
- **Login:** nothing visibly happened.
- **Add to Cart:** I never picked a size, the page told me to, and the cart badge stayed at 0.
- **Cart page:** it came up completely blank.
- **Search:** "comfort shoes" put a women's loafer first.

**Compared with yesterday (3/10):** I can't see yesterday's screenshots, only the score. Today the browsing is fine and the buying is broken, so I see no real improvement.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10, flat against yesterday's 3.**

A shopper can find a comfortable shoe easily. A shopper cannot buy one in this run. The shopping steps work and the money steps don't, so the site can't score higher than a 3.

## 3. What's Working

- **Comfort language up front.** "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Arch Fit" sit in the top nav on the homepage. I didn't have to hunt for them.
- **The Men's menu is easy to use.** It has big, readable rows with plenty of space between them: Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections, Collaborations, Clothing & Accessories. It has a Back button and an obvious X to close. I could tap those without trouble.
- **Men's Shoes page.** The title and the "825 results" count are clear. The "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" tiles are what I came for.
- **Product page.**
  - The price is large at $135.00.
  - It shows 4.5 stars from 29 reviews.
  - It shows the Slip-ins logo.
  - It offers Klarna and Afterpay payment plans, which helps with the price-vs-quality math.
- **Size picker.** The size buttons are big, easy to hit with a thumb, and clearly marked when a size is sold out. The "Add to Cart" button is wide and impossible to miss.
- **Search results.** They show a count (1,648), a "Best Matches" sort, color swatches, and an "Also in Wide" tag. I liked the "Also in Wide" tag.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Login seemed to do nothing.** Steps 2 and 3 look identical to the homepage. There was no sign-in screen, no "Hi, Walker," and no points balance. The cart badge stayed at 0. I felt no personalization at all.
- **Add to Cart didn't work.**
  - The screen showed a red "Please select a size" warning. The shoe never went in.
  - It's good that the site tells me what's wrong. But for a 62-year-old, an error that sits next to the button, with no sizes pre-selected, is easy to miss or get annoyed by.
  - Part of this may be how the test ran: nobody tapped a size first. But the site also doesn't remember my size or suggest one.
- **The cart page was blank white.** There was no cart, no empty-cart message, no "continue shopping" link, and nothing to read. A real customer would assume the site was broken and leave.
- **The discount message contradicts itself.** The top banner says "Members Take 30% OFF Sitewide," but the shoe I picked says "Excluded from promotions." That is confusing, and it hurts trust. I trust a brand more when the email and the site tell the same story.
- **Search relevance is shaky for me.** The first result for "comfort shoes" was a women's loafer ("Cypress – Chic Surge, Women's"). I'm a man shopping for walking comfort. The site doesn't know that, even though I browsed Men's right before.
- **Homepage doesn't speak to me.** The first screen is a dressy holiday photo of a couple in party clothes. It says nothing about cushioning, arch support, or slip-ons, and the first deal is for "Doorbusters," not comfort. The comfort links are small tabs that run off the right edge ("Max Cus…").
- **Small text.** The nav tab labels, the "Early Access Doorbusters" strip, and the "Members Get 30% OFF!" fine print are small. I'd be pinching to zoom.
- **No comparison help.** There's nothing here that helps me weigh Skechers against Hoka, Brooks, or New Balance, and nothing that shows what $135 buys me in cushioning.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart first.** Show an empty-cart message with a "Continue Shopping" button. Find out why the page rendered blank.
2. **Make Add to Cart forgiving.** Pre-select a size for returning shoppers, or scroll to the size picker and highlight it when someone taps the button without one.
3. **Confirm login visibly.** Show "Hi, [name]" and the points balance in the header after sign-in. Confirm the cart badge updates after an add.
4. **Reconcile the promo messaging.** If a shoe is excluded, say so in the banner ("30% off most styles") or on the card, before the product page.
5. **Lead with comfort on mobile.** Make "Slip-ins / Arch Fit / Wide Fit" large tiles on the homepage. Use a bigger font for the nav tabs and make the row wrap or scroll with an arrow.
6. **Use browsing context in search.** Rank men's styles first for someone who just browsed Men's. Add quick filters for "Slip-ins," "Arch Fit," and "Wide."
7. **Add a plain-English comfort summary** to each product page (cushioning, arch support, who it's for). Add a "what you get for $135" line.

## 6. Bottom Line

Skechers has the shoe I want and makes it easy to find. Then the site loses me, because I can't get it into a cart and the cart page is blank. If I'd been a real customer, I'd have opened another brand's site, and I'd probably have bought from whoever made it easy. The comfort part of the site is fine. The buying part needs fixing. I'm scoring this **3/10, unchanged from yesterday**.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The journey ran through 8 steps with 18 axe violations (2 critical, 2 serious on the homepage, 2 serious on the cart), 21 console errors and 1 network error.

- **Cart step:** `GET /cart/` returned **429**. The cart axe findings (no `<title>`, no `lang`, no `<main>`, no `<h1>`) are what an unstyled error page would produce. I think the View Cart audit ran against the rate-limit response, not the real cart, but the sample doesn't confirm this.
- **Web Vitals:** LCP and CLS were not captured for any step (`?`), so only TTFB can be assessed.
- **Instrumentation:** The audit is limited by truncated HTML, which hides `<title>`, meta tags, canonical, structured data and the viewport meta tag. Of 21 console errors, only 10 were listed.
- **Step capture:** The "Log In" and "Men category" steps all report URL `https://www.skechers.com/`, and their `<head>` HTML matches the homepage. Either those steps are in-page overlays or the navigation didn't occur. Both are plausible, and I can't tell which from the data.

## Accessibility

Homepage:
- **[critical] `button-name`:** One or more buttons have no accessible name.
- **[critical] `aria-required-parent`:** Child ARIA roles sit outside their required parent roles, for example `listitem` or `tab` without `list` or `tablist`.
- **[serious] `link-name`:** Links have no discernible text, which is typical of icon-only links with no `aria-label`.
- **Landmarks:** `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region` together indicate more than one `<main>`, nested landmarks and content outside any landmark.
- **[moderate] `page-has-heading-one`:** There is no `<h1>`.
- **[minor] `aria-allowed-role`:** A role is used that isn't valid for its element.

Search results:
- `aria-allowed-role`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region` recur. They are the same template-level problems as on the homepage, so one fix in the shared layout should clear both pages.

View Cart (likely the 429 response):
- **[serious] `document-title`:** There is no `<title>`.
- **[serious] `html-has-lang`:** There is no `lang` attribute.
- **[moderate] `landmark-one-main`:** There is no `<main>`.
- **[moderate] `page-has-heading-one`:** There is no `<h1>`.

Not flagged: the homepage `<html>` has `lang="en"`.

## SEO

- The homepage and search pages have no `<h1>` (`page-has-heading-one`).
- The cart error response has no `<title>`. If bots or crawlers also get a 429 on `/cart/`, that URL is returned with no title or language metadata. Cart pages should be `noindex`, so the main concern is how the error is served, not indexing.
- Canonical tags, meta descriptions, Open Graph tags and JSON-LD could not be evaluated because the HTML was truncated before they appear.
- Steps 1–3 all report the same URL. If the category pages do not have distinct URLs, that is an SEO and shareability problem, but the capture alone doesn't prove it.

## Performance

| Men category | 430 ms |
| Men > Shoes | 22 ms |

- **Full-document TTFB:** The homepage, Log In and Men category pages are at 430–443 ms. That is acceptable but not fast for a CDN-fronted site.
- **Very low TTFB:** The 21–24 ms values on Men > Shoes, product detail, Add to Cart and search are implausible for a server-rendered document. They are probably soft navigations or stale navigation-timing entries, so these steps are likely not measured properly.
- **Third-party load:** A large number of third-party tags load on the homepage:
  - **Tag managers and testing:** GTM, Monetate (`custom.js` and `entry.js`) and Pulse Insights.
  - **Analytics and session replay:** Quantum Metric, Bloomreach (`brsrvr.com`) and `go2sdk`.
  - **Ad pixels:** TikTok, Pinterest, Snapchat, Reddit, Facebook, Nextdoor, Bing, Yahoo, Impact, RTB House (`creativecdn.com`) and Taboola.
  - **Impact:** Even though they are async, they compete for main-thread time on mobile. This is a likely cause of poor INP and LCP, but without metrics it is unconfirmed.
- **Fonts:** Mulish loads through the JS WebFont Loader from cdnjs (`wf-mulish-*-active` classes). That adds a third-party round trip and JS dependency before font swap. A self-hosted `@font-face` with `<link rel="preload">` would avoid both. The truncated head shows only `dns-prefetch` for Monetate and no `preconnect`.
- **Rate limiting:** The 429 on `/cart/` shows that Add to Cart → View Cart can be throttled in a normal single-user journey.

## Mobile Optimization

- **Viewport units:** `--vh` is set inline by JS (`6.64px`, so a 664 px viewport height). This is a legitimate workaround for mobile browser chrome, but it depends on JS and can cause layout shift. CSS `dvh`/`svh` units avoid that.
- **Viewport meta:** The viewport meta tag and tap-target sizing could not be verified from the truncated sample.
- **Unnamed controls:** The unlabeled buttons and links from axe are most likely icon controls in the mobile header, which also affects screen-reader users.
- Beyond those, no mobile-specific issues were visible in the captured data.

## Console & Network Errors

- **Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed (about 6 of the 10 listed errors):** This is a third-party script, or a page script, registering an `unload` handler in a context where Chrome's policy blocks it. `unload` is being deprecated and also blocks bfcache. The offending script should be identified and moved to `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`.
- **"Unsupported Summarizer API languages…" (4 of the 10 listed errors):** This is Chrome's built-in Summarizer API being called with unsupported language options. It looks like third-party or injected code, not first-party code. It's non-fatal but pollutes the console on every page.
- **429 `https://www.skechers.com/cart/`:** The cart request was rate-limited or bot-throttled. This could be WAF behaviour against the automated test client (headless Playwright), so it may not reflect real user traffic. If real users hit it, it is a conversion-critical failure.
- **Missing detail:** 11 of the 21 console errors were not provided, so the above may be incomplete.

## Recommendations

1. **Investigate the `/cart/` 429.** Check whether the WAF or rate limiter is hitting the automated client or real users. Re-run the audit with the cart actually loaded. Serve error pages with a proper `<title>`, `lang` and `<main>`.
2. **Fix critical axe issues first.** Add accessible names to icon buttons and links (`aria-label` or visually hidden text), and correct the ARIA parent/child role structure on the homepage.
3. **Fix the shared layout landmarks.** Use a single top-level `<main>`, don't nest `<aside>` or `<main>`, label repeated `<nav>` and `<aside>` elements uniquely, and put all content in landmarks. This clears the landmark and region findings on both the homepage and search.
4. **Add an `<h1>`** to the homepage and search templates.
5. **Replace `unload` listeners** with `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`, and find which script registers them.
6. **Review the third-party tag inventory.** Remove duplicates and defer non-essential pixels until after interaction or consent. The Yahoo tag appears to load twice.
7. **Check the GTM container URL.** It includes `gtm_preview=env-1` and `gtm_auth`, which points to a non-live GTM environment being served on production. Confirm that is intended.
8. **Self-host and preload fonts** instead of loading them through WebFont Loader from cdnjs.
9. **Fix the audit harness.** Capture LCP and CLS with `PerformanceObserver` (`buffered: true`), use real navigation timing so the 21–24 ms TTFB values are meaningful, check that "Log In" and "Men category" actually navigate, and export the full console log and untruncated `<head>`.
