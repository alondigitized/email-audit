# Intro to Style: Sneaker Trends
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-19**
## Executive summary

- "Style Syllabus: Sneaker Edition" — at least this one's not another kid with a backpack. It's teenagers and college-age kids skateboarding and running track, models decades younger than me, but the shoes themselves — that's what got my eye. Nike Classic Court, some Adidas throwback, a Nike V5 RNR, and an Asics Gel-Venture 11 called out as "Daily Comfort." Gel-Venture is actually a shoe I'd look at — I've heard good things about Asics cushioning from a buddy of mine.
- But here's the thing — nobody tells me a single word about comfort, arch support, or cushioning technology, except that one "Daily Comfort" label slapped on the Asics. No mention of memory foam, no arch support callout, nothing about all-day wear. It's styled like a school notebook — chalkboard backgrounds, "syllabus" gimmick — clearly built for teenagers shopping for the new semester, not a guy my age just wanting a shoe that doesn't hurt by 3pm.
- Would I click? Maybe on the Asics, since that's the one shoe that even gestures at comfort. Wouldn't forward this to anybody — it's not embarrassing, just not for me. Skim it, note the $20 off $99.98 coupon at the bottom, and move on.

## What's working

- The Asics Gel-Venture 11 "Daily Comfort" tag is the one line in the whole email that speaks my language.
- The $20 off $99.98+ / $10 off $74.98+ coupon codes are clear and I can actually read the dollar amounts without squinting.
- Four different brands shown side by side — Nike, Adidas, Nike again, Asics — which fits how I actually shop, comparing across brands rather than being loyal to one.
- No obvious broken images or overlapping text — it rendered clean.

## What's weak

- Every model is a teenager or young adult — skateboarding, sitting on bleachers, running track. Not one person looks like they could be my kid, let alone me.
- "Style Syllabus" and "Classic Court / Low Profile / Y2K" labels are pure Gen-Z shorthand — I don't know what "Low Profile" or "Y2K" means as a shoe category, and nothing explains it.
- Zero mention of comfort, cushioning, or arch support outside that one Asics tag — for a guy who buys shoes on how they feel, not how they look, that's a big miss.
- The 30% off badge only applies to "select styles" on Nike Family Sale — vague, and I've seen this same 30%-off Nike badge and "Family Sale" language before (flagged it on the August 15th "Fit Check" email too).
- Long, scroll-heavy layout — four separate hero shoe blocks before you even get to the coupon, more work than I want to do on my phone.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap in a photo or a section aimed at comfort-first customers — an over-40 guy in a Skechers or Asics walking shoe, cushioned sole visible — instead of an all-teen cast top to bottom.
- 2. Add one plain-English line under the Asics or any "comfort" shoe: "memory foam insole," "arch support," "all-day wear" — words that actually match how I shop, not style jargon.
- 3. Cut the "Syllabus / Classic Court / Low Profile / Y2K" school theme for anyone over 40 — it reads like it's speaking past me, not to me.
- 4. Move the $20/$10 off coupon higher — I had to scroll through four shoe blocks to find the one thing that would actually get me to buy.
- 5. Clarify "select styles" for the Nike 30% off — tell me which models qualify instead of making me click through to find out.
