## Executive summary

- There it is again — that pink floral D'Lites - Spring Bloom sneaker, third time this week now, still sitting in a "cart" I never put it in. I don't know who Skechers thinks is opening this inbox, but it isn't a 62-year-old man who wears a men's size 11. "We noticed you noticing us" is trying to be cute and it's just annoying at this point — I noticed nothing, I clicked nothing.
- The one saving grace is they finally scrolled down to some actual men's shoes in the "more options" grid — a Men's Slip-ins Relaxed Fit and a couple others mixed in with the women's stuff — plus a real 20% off member discount banner that's clear as day. But leading with the same wrong shoe for the fourth straight email is just sloppy, and I'm not wading through a floral sneaker and three women's styles to find the one pair that's actually mine to consider.
- I'm not clicking, and I'm not forwarding this to anybody — my buddies would just ask why Skechers thinks I want pink flowers on my feet. Marking as read and moving on.

- The 20% off member banner near the bottom is clean and easy to read — real number, real deadline (thru 9/7), no digging required.
- They did finally slip a couple of men's options into the "more options" grid, including a slip-in — that's the kind of hands-free shoe I actually want.
- Payment options (Afterpay, Klarna) and curbside pickup are visible if I were actually buying, though irrelevant to me today.

## What's weak

- The hero shoe is, again, the pink floral D'Lites I never put in a cart — same mistake as the last three emails this week.
- "We noticed you noticing us" is a cutesy, slightly stalker-ish subject-adjacent headline that doesn't build trust, it undercuts it.
- I have to scroll past the wrong hero image and a "Complete Your Order" button for a shoe I don't want before hitting anything relevant to me.
- The men's items in the grid are tiny thumbnails buried among women's and kids' styles — no dedicated men's section up top.
- Fourth near-identical cart-nag email this week is just fatigue at this point, not a nudge to buy.

- 3. Cut the "more options" grid down to my actual browsing history/gender, or at minimum put men's styles first.
