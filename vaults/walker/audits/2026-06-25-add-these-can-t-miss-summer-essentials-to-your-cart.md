# Add These Can't-Miss Summer Essentials to Your Cart
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- Another Skechers in my inbox, and before I even get to the subject line I'm already bracing for that broken preview text — and sure enough, raw JSON again. I've been seeing that same garbage since the first week of June. Seven or eight sends in a row now. At some point somebody has to notice.
- Once I actually get inside the email, though, it's a decent send. The BOGO 50% off banner is right at the top where I can't miss it, the summer categories — sandals, canvas sneakers, jellies — are the right stuff for this time of year, and the layout is clean enough that I don't have to hunt. The "Everyday Sandals" section with "Less coverage, more comfort" is the only line in the whole email that actually sounds like it was written for me. The rest of it — "New Heat," "Can't-Miss" — feels like it's aimed at someone thirty years younger.
- I'd probably click through to the sandals and canvas pages because of the BOGO. But I wouldn't forward this. There's nothing here I'd put my name on sending to a friend. Mark it read, click sandals, done.

## What's working

- **BOGO 50% off is right at the top.** Clear offer, visible immediately — I don't have to scroll to find the deal.
- **"Everyday Sandals / Less coverage, more comfort"** — that tagline actually speaks to how I shop. Comfort is the word that gets my attention.
- **Clean layout, no broken images.** After months of emails with missing heroes and garbled sections, this one rendered without drama.
- **Canvas sneakers look right for summer.** Breathable, light-colored, simple — those are shoes I'd try on.
- **Men's / Women's split CTA at the bottom** is helpful. I appreciate not wading through women's styles to find what's for me.

## What's weak

- **Broken preview text again — for the eighth time.** Raw JSON schema dumped where a sentence should be. I've said it in every review this month and nothing changes. This alone keeps me from recommending Skechers emails to anyone.
- **"New Heat" is not talking to me.** That headline is aimed at a 28-year-old. At 62, "heat" doesn't make me want shoes — it makes me want to sit inside with the A/C.
- **Jellies are confusing for a 62-year-old guy.** The red strappy ones in the photo look like women's resort wear. No copy explains whether there's a men's version or who this is for.
- **Zero comfort details where it counts.** No arch support, no cushioning callout, no width options — the things that actually drive my decision don't appear anywhere.
- **"LET'S GET TESTING" in the footer looks like QA copy that shipped.** Whatever that line is supposed to say, it reads like something that wasn't meant for my eyes.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text.** This is the single highest-leverage change and it costs nothing. One clean sentence — "BOGO 50% off summer sandals, jellies, and canvas — shop the new arrivals" — would have lifted open rate on seven consecutive sends. The broken JSON has been the lead story in every Skechers email since early June.
- 2. **Swap "New Heat" for a comfort-first headline.** Something like "Summer's Most Comfortable Pair Is Waiting" lands for my demographic. "Heat" is fashion speak; I don't want heat, I want to stay cool and comfortable.
- 3. **Add one comfort callout per product block.** Under Canvas Sneakers, say "Memory foam footbed." Under Everyday Sandals, say "Contoured arch support." Give me a reason to believe it's worth the click.
- 4. **Clarify who Jellies are for.** If there's a men's jelly sandal, show a male foot and say so. If it's women's only, pull it from the men's-facing send entirely.
- 5. **Put a deadline on the BOGO.** "Through Sunday" or "This week only" makes me act instead of bookmark.
- **Subject Alt A:** `BOGO 50% Off: Sandals, Canvas & More`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Summer Sandals & Canvas — Buy One, Get One 50% Off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cool canvas sneakers, everyday sandals, and the comfiest jellies of summer — all BOGO.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New summer styles with BOGO 50% off — shop canvas, sandals, and jellies now.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- `beacon.krxd.net/1x1_usermatch.gif` — fired twice with hashed PII (`partner_uid=9153f33f…` SHA-256 hash, `partner_uid=121173391` raw subscriber ID). These are Salesforce/Krux audience-match pixels; confirm they are disclosed in the privacy policy and that consent is obtained where required (GDPR/CPRA).
- `beacon.krxd.net/1x1_ad_impression.gif` — ad impression beacon embedded in a transactional/promotional email; verify this is intentional and covered by consent.

- `https://click.emails.skechers.com/open.aspx?…` — HTTPS, no issues.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**[CRITICAL FAIL]** The automated QA check confirms an empty greeting merge token:

> Greeting renders as **"Hi ,"** — the first-name token resolved to an empty string.

This indicates the ESP send did not apply a fallback value (e.g., `%%[IF EMPTY(FirstName) THEN "there" ELSE FirstName]%%` in SFMC syntax). Every contact missing a `FirstName` value received the broken greeting. Depending on list hygiene, this could affect a significant percentage of the send.

No other unresolved merge tokens were detected in the visible HTML, but the source is truncated — full template scan recommended.

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM / unsubscribe:** HTML source is truncated before the footer; unsubscribe link and physical mailing address presence cannot be confirmed from the provided excerpt. QA findings do not flag a missing unsubscribe, suggesting it is present further in the document.

**Data broker pixels (Krux/Salesforce DMP):**
- Two `beacon.krxd.net` usermatch pixels transmit a hashed email (`sha25` parameter) and a raw subscriber ID to a third-party data broker. Under CPRA (California) and similar state laws, this constitutes a data sale/share to a third party and requires disclosure + opt-out path. Confirm this is covered in the privacy policy and that the unsubscribe/opt-out flow covers DMP syncing, not just email sends.
