# Believe That 1
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- Adidas sends me "Believe That 1" and I stare at it for a solid ten seconds trying to figure out what the heck they're talking about. A young man is posed in front of something that looks like a giant blue bowling ball. Turns out — once I finally read the body copy — this is Anthony Edwards' basketball shoe. I know Anthony Edwards is an NBA player because my grandson mentions him, but that's the extent of my knowledge here.
- The copy talks about "explosive play" and "fearless style" and "reflecting Ant's" — Ant? I had to stop and parse that. The shoe itself, when I finally see it in the second panel, is a yellow-orange-black high-top sneaker. That's a young man's basketball court shoe. I wear New Balance walking shoes and I'm actively cross-shopping Skechers arch-fit right now. There is zero overlap.
- What makes this worse: I just got the Mercedes-AMG F1 collab email yesterday and the Adizero speed email a few days before that. This is the third straight adidas send aimed squarely away from me. I'll mark it read and move on. I'm not clicking, I'm not forwarding — the only person I'd forward this to is my grandson, and he doesn't need my help finding basketball shoes.

## What's working

- The email renders cleanly — no broken images, no overlapping text, everything laid out in a clean two-panel structure
- The "SHOP NOW" banner at the top is clear and visible right away, no guessing what the call to action is
- adidas is at least a brand I recognize; I didn't think it was spam
- The subject line is short and doesn't feel like a scammy blast (no exclamation points, no ALL CAPS pressure)

## What's weak

- "Believe That 1" means nothing to me without knowing it's Anthony Edwards' catchphrase — I had to read three paragraphs to even figure out what product this was
- Every visual signal — young model, basketball pose, high-top court shoe — tells me this email was built for a 24-year-old, not a 62-year-old man shopping for something comfortable to walk around in
- No price, no offer, no discount — just a brand moment with a SHOP NOW button that goes... somewhere. I have no idea what this shoe costs or why today is the day to buy it
- This is the third near-identical adidas blast in four days. At this point the brand is training me to skip their emails on sight

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show me a price or a reason to act today.** A launch price, a member discount, even "new arrival" with a specific date — something that makes NOW feel different from next week.
- 2. **Put the product upfront.** I scrolled past a moody portrait before I saw the shoe. Lead with the shoe, not the athlete.
- 3. **Add one line of comfort copy that I can hold onto.** "Responsive cushioning for all-day wear" — even that would give me a hook. Right now the copy reads as performance jargon for players, not everyday wearers.
- 4. **Segment or suppress.** I have never clicked a basketball or performance shoe email from adidas. I have been shopping the comfort/casual category. There is enough signal in my click history to stop sending me court shoes.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility
