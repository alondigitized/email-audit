# Don't Miss Out On Your 15% OFF Welcome Offer! 💸
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-12**
## Executive summary

- A Crocs welcome email landing right when I've been thinking about picking up a pair of their clogs for walking around the neighborhood — so the timing's decent. The 15% off is front and center, no hunting around for the deal, which I appreciate. I'm not a puzzle-solver when I'm checking email; I want to know what you're offering in about five seconds flat.
- That said, this thing sends me in six different directions. Sandals, platform clogs, work clogs, charms, a preference quiz, and an app download prompt — I'm 62, not a scavenger hunt kind of guy. There's no one shoe they're saying "Walker, this is the one for you." I almost felt like I accidentally opened a gift shop website instead of a targeted email.
- I'd mark it as read and maybe come back to it, but I'm not clicking immediately. Too much clutter. I wouldn't forward it — there's nothing in here that says "this is specifically for guys like me." One of my sons might wear this stuff, but this email didn't give me a reason to think of him either.

## What's working

- **The 15% off is impossible to miss.** Big, bold, sits at the top — exactly where a deal should live. I see it without reading a word of body copy.
- **Crocs is a brand I already know and cross-shop**, so opening from "Crocs" in my inbox didn't feel like a risk.
- **The "Shop Clogs At Work" category caught my eye** — that's my sweet spot, an easy-on shoe that can handle a full day on my feet.
- **The email renders clean.** No broken images, no scrambled text. That matters to me more than people think — nothing makes me bail faster than a busted-looking email.
- **Seasonal fit is right.** Spring, sandals, lighter colors — I'm already thinking about getting out of my heavy shoes.

## What's weak

- **Too many CTAs pulling me in different directions.** Sandals, classic sandals, clogs, platforms, charms — pick ONE for the welcome email and let me settle into it.
- **The lifestyle imagery skews young and female.** Nothing in the photos signals "this is for a guy my age who needs a comfortable, low-key shoe." I felt like a tourist browsing something that wasn't made for me.
- **"Pair Down Your Preferences" asks me to do work.** A quiz in a welcome email? I just gave you my email address. That's already a commitment. Don't ask me for more homework before I've even bought anything.
- **"Try Them on for Size" pushes an app download.** I do not want to download an app. I'm already on my phone reading your email. Just let me shop.
- **The preview text is completely useless.** All I saw in my inbox was "Crocs" followed by nothing. The subject line was doing all the heavy lifting alone.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero product for this welcome send.** Lead with your bestselling men's comfort clog — name it, show it on a real person, give me the before/after on comfort. Don't open with a product wall.
- 2. **Add one clear "CLAIM MY 15% OFF" button at the top**, before any category navigation. Right now the offer floats without a clear action attached to it.
- 3. **Fix the preview text immediately.** Even one sentence — "Your feet will thank you, starting with 15% off" — does more than whatever is happening now.
- 4. **Cut the app download section entirely from the welcome email.** Save it for a post-purchase flow. Right now it's just friction.
- 5. **Show a comfort signal.** One line of copy — "cushioned footbed," "lightweight," "easy slip-on" — gives a guy like me a reason to believe this brand knows what I need.
- 6. **Ditch the Jibbitz charms section from this send.** That's for a different audience. A welcome email should sell the shoe, not the accessories.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 15% off is waiting — claim it before it expires`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New to Crocs? Here's 15% off your first pair`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cushioned, lightweight, and slip-on easy — plus 15% off to get started.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your welcome discount is inside. Pick the comfort style that fits your day.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Crocs Welcome 15% Off Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has no blocking errors but carries several infrastructure risks: 7 images served over HTTP will be suppressed by security-conscious clients, the Adobe Fonts `@import` fails silently in Gmail, and click links expose no UTM parameters in the raw `href` — analytics continuity depends entirely on the redirect server appending them.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text URL density — spam filter risk**
QA reports 9,902 of 12,316 plain-text characters are URLs (80%). Most spam filters penalize plain-text alternates that are URL-dominated. The plain-text version should be a human-readable summary, not a dump of encoded redirect links.

**Opaque click links — UTM visibility unknown**
All 35 CTAs use `click.crocs-email.com/?qs=<base64>`. No UTM parameters are present in the raw `href` values. If the redirect server does not decode and append `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, etc., all click traffic will land as direct/organic in site analytics. Example:
```
https://click.crocs-email.com/?qs=ABB7InYiOjEsImQiOjQ4NzR9AAEAAAAAAFTZKXNE...
```
Verify the redirect chain appends UTMs before the final destination URL.

**35 links not probed**
QA skipped HTTP validation on all click links due to the redirect domain. Destination URL health (404s, redirect loops, landing page availability) is unconfirmed.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**7 images served over HTTP — will be blocked**
Email clients including Outlook, Apple Mail with Enhanced Privacy, and some corporate proxies block non-HTTPS image sources. The following images will render as broken placeholders for a material portion of recipients:

| Alt text | File | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| "Download on the App Store" | `7462caab-b5fb...png` | `http://image.crocs-email.com/...` |
| "Get it on Google Play" | `f744a0ca-a299...png` | `http://image.crocs-email.com/...` |
| *(missing alt)* | `3bf2b026-9650...png` | `http://image.crocs-email.com/...` |
| "Instagram" | `072197b5-fcef...png` | `http://image.crocs-email.com/...` |
| "TikTok" | `ddc4ffa6-81a7...png` | `http://image.crocs-email.com/...` |
| "YouTube" | `1ba1d5d1-3ccc...png` | `http://image.crocs-email.com/...` |
| "Facebook" | `92f76fa0-21d5...png` | `http://image.crocs-email.com/...` |

All `image.crocs-email.com` assets should be served over HTTPS.

**Open pixel missing `alt=""`**
The Adobe/Demdex open-tracking pixel at `demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?...` has no `alt` attribute at all. Screen readers will read out the raw URL. Fix: `alt=""`.

**`3bf2b026...png` missing alt text and served over HTTP**
Double failure — no alt and HTTP source. Content is unknown from truncated HTML; if decorative, use `alt=""`. If informational, add descriptive text.

**Adobe Fonts `@import` stripped by Gmail**
```css
@import url("https://use.typekit.net/vqb8ega.css");
```
Gmail's web client strips `<style>` blocks containing `@import`. Proxima Nova will not load; the email will fall back to the declared `sans-serif` stack. This is expected behavior but worth confirming the fallback renders acceptably.

**`<meta charset>` missing `http-equiv`**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, this tag has no effect. Charset declaration is currently relying on the sending server's `Content-Type` header alone.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Likely unfired merge token — empty `<span>` in headline**
```html
<a href="...">Take <span style="white-space:nowrap"></span></a>
```
The `white-space:nowrap` span between "Take" and the "15% Off" row is empty in the rendered source. This pattern is consistent with an unpopulated first-name token (e.g., `Take {{first_name}}`). If intentional, remove the dead span. If a merge token was intended, it silently dropped, producing "Take" with no following word — a broken reading experience.

**Subscriber ID and campaign ID appear hardcoded in open pixel**
```
d_dpuid=9153f33fe5cdf59baf088bb835d3b39ef90f188bd8540a4faba92899227ced00
c_subscriberID=400467828
campaignID=us_x_trigger_welcome-15peroff_lastcall
```
If this is a rendered test instance, expected. If any of these are static strings rather than ESP merge tokens in the template, open-event tracking will attribute all opens to a single subscriber ID.

---

### 5. Compliance

**Unsubscribe link and physical address** — not visible in the truncated HTML provided. The QA summary reports no hard failures in this area (86% pass, 0 issues). Assuming these are present in the email footer, which is outside the excerpt. Confirm the unsubscribe link is functional and the physical address matches the CAN-SPAM registrant.

**Adobe Audience Manager pixel (`demdex.net`) — consent alignment**
The open-tracking pixel fires to Adobe's DMP on open. For recipients in opt-in jurisdictions (GDPR, CASL), confirm consent covers cross-platform audience tracking, not just email delivery. This is an ESP/legal question, not purely a code issue, but the pixel is present unconditionally.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM attribution is opaque**
All destination URLs are hidden behind `click.crocs-email.com/?qs=<base64>`. The redirect server must decode and forward at minimum:
- `utm_source=email`
- `utm_medium=email`
- `utm_campaign=welcome-15peroff-lastcall` (matching `campaignID` in the pixel)

Without verification of what the redirect resolves to, there is no confirmation that landing pages receive attributable sessions. Decode one `?qs=` value in a staging environment and inspect the final URL to confirm.
