## What's weak

- **100% women's footwear.** Every single product image — sandals, wedges, heels — is women's. I'm not in this email at all.
- **The preview text is a mess.** "There's sooo much at your DSW store" followed by what looks like a bunch of junk characters in my inbox. Casual spelling of "sooo" feels off for a store I associate with decent quality.
- **"MADE FOR SUMMER DAYS" and "EVERYONE LOVES THESE NAMES" sections add nothing.** No specific names called out, no comfort cues, no reason to scroll down.
- **No mention of cushioning, arch support, or men's comfort anywhere.** That's the whole reason I'd ever open a shoe email.
- **The "SHOP BY SIZE" grid at the bottom is women's sizing** — 5, 5.5, 6 and up. I'm out by that point anyway, but it confirms this email wasn't built with me in mind.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment men out of this send entirely, or build a true "his + hers" split.** One row of men's comfort picks — Skechers slip-ons, New Balance walkers, Hoka recovery slides — would've given me a reason to scroll. Without it, there's nothing to click.
- 2. **Use the cooler tote offer as the anchor and tie it to a purchase threshold.** "Spend $60 on any shoe, get the tote free" — that's a real incentive that pulls me toward a purchase, not just a banner I admire.
- 3. **Lead with comfort language in the hero copy.** "Cushioned for your summer" or "Slip into summer, no laces needed" would signal immediately that this email knows what I'm shopping for.
- 4. **Fix the preview text.** Cut the "sooo" spelling and the trailing whitespace clutter.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Invalid CSS property casing in all `@font-face` blocks:**
All ten `@font-face` rules use `Src:` (capital S) instead of the spec-required lowercase `src:`. Strictly conformant CSS parsers will silently drop the declaration, causing font load failure and unintentional system-font fallback in any client that enforces case sensitivity (notably some WebKit-based mobile clients).

```css
/* Observed (all 10 @font-face blocks) */
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/appboy/...") format('woff');

/* Required */
src: url("https://braze-images.com/appboy/...") format('woff');
```

**Issue — Conflicting `-ms-text-size-adjust` declarations in the universal reset:**
The `*` selector sets the property twice with contradictory values; the trailing `none` wins in IE/Edge, suppressing automatic text scaling entirely.

```css
*{
  -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%;   /* set first */
  ...
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;   /* overrides above — disables scaling */
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```

The `100%` declaration is dead code; the effective value is `none`, which prevents accessibility-level text scaling in legacy Outlook rendering engine.
