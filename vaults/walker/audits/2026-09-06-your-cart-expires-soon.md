# Your cart expires soon!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-06**
## Executive summary

- Here we are again — same pink floral D'Lites - Spring Bloom sneaker, same "YOU LIKED THESE / AND YOU WEREN'T WRONG" line, same "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" button. I have never clicked, browsed, or so much as looked twice at that shoe, and Skechers has now told me I "liked" it in at least four separate emails this week. That's not personalization, that's a stuck record. The one bright spot is scrolling further down — there's a real slip-in lineup for men (Arch Fit, Contour Foam, John Deere boot) that actually looks like something I'd wear, plus a legit 20% off for Plus members through 9/7. But it's buried under a hero I keep ignoring.
- My gut reaction: mark as read, don't click. The hero offer is stale and wrong for me, and by the time I get down to the stuff I'd actually consider, I've already mentally checked out. I wouldn't forward this to anyone — there's nothing here worth sharing, just more proof Skechers doesn't know what's in my closet.

## What's working

- The men's slip-in grid further down (Arch Fit 2.0, Contour Foam, John Deere boot) is genuinely relevant — cushioned, hands-free, my kind of shoe.
- The 20% off Plus member banner is a clear, honest, time-bounded offer (thru 9/7) with the discount applied at cart, no hoops.
- "SHOP MEN'S" is one tap away in the category list, and the layout is clean enough to scroll without confusion on my phone.

## What's weak

- The hero shoe — pink floral D'Lites - Spring Bloom — is the same one from at least three or four prior sends this week. I never touched it, and it's still leading the email.
- "YOU LIKED THESE / AND YOU WEREN'T WRONG" is just false. That's not tracking my behavior, that's a template running on autopilot.
- The real offer (20% off) and the shoes I'd actually consider are five scroll-lengths down, past a "SHOP ALL BEST SELLERS" banner I have to wade through first.
- Nothing in the subject or the top third of the email signals "men's comfort" — it's all built around a product I have zero interest in.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the fake "you liked these" hero entirely and lead with the men's slip-in comfort lineup and the 20% off Plus banner — that's the actual reason I'd open this email.
- 2. If Skechers wants to push a "cart reminder," it should reflect something I've actually looked at, not a floral sneaker I've never clicked in five emails straight.
- 3. Move the Plus member 20% off banner up top, above the fold — it's the one honest, time-bound offer in here.
- 4. Cut the repeat frequency of this same creative — I've now seen this shoe in "Did you forget something?", "Your Items Are Waiting!", and now "Your cart expires soon!" It's starting to feel like spam.
