# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-09**
## Executive summary

- Another cart nag, and I still haven't put a thing in a cart. This is at least the seventh one in about two weeks. "Ready to Check Out?" with a "Complete Your Order" button is a strange thing to send a man with an empty cart. The hero shoe is a black runner with a green and gray swirl sole, called "UNO - Alma De Guerrero." That's a loud, young-looking shoe I've never looked at. It isn't the skull sneaker from the 11/05 and 11/08 sends, so they changed the picture but not the routine.
- The buried 20% off for Skechers Plus members is the one useful thing here. The six-shoe grid below it is also closer to my taste than the hero is. There are waterproof slip-in boots and Arch Fit models, and three of the six are men's. I would not click the cart button, since there's nothing in my cart. I might tap "Shop All Best Sellers" if I needed boots for winter. I wouldn't forward it. I'd mark it read.

## What's working

- The 20% off for Plus members banner is big and readable on my phone, with the dates (thru 11/10) right there.
- The men's slip-in waterproof boots (Respected, Orson) are my kind of shoe: hands-free and sturdy for cold weather.
- Arch Fit and slip-ins are named in the product titles, so I can tell the comfort features without guessing.
- The black "Shop All Best Sellers" bar is a wide tap target. The category list (Women, Men, Kids) is also easy to hit with a thumb.
- It's clean, and nothing looked broken.

## What's weak

- "Ready to check out?" assumes I have a cart, and I don't. It feels like a stranger's email, not a known customer's.
- The hero shoe is a flashy runner with a camo sole. It has no cushioning or arch story, and it isn't something I'd wear.
- Three of the six grid shoes are women's, and they're mixed in with the men's without any sorting.
- The 20% offer is buried below the shoes, and the fine print is tiny. It says "regular-priced styles" only, and that excludes a lot.
- Two orange and black "Complete Your Order" buttons point at nothing I picked. The grid captions are small text.

## Recommendations

- 1. Don't send cart emails to someone with no cart. Send a men's comfort and slip-in selection, with the 20% Plus offer as the hero.
- 2. Move the 20% off banner to the top, and put the real deadline (tonight, 11/10) in the subject.
- 3. Replace the grid with men's items only, and show a price or "Arch Fit" badge on each.
- 4. Make the captions bigger.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Plus members: 20% off ends tomorrow`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Slip-in boots, 20% off for Plus members`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Waterproof slip-ins and Arch Fit styles, through 11/10.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your Plus discount applies automatically at checkout.`

The decoded macro is `unsubscribeLink`. The token is per-recipient, so the 400 may be a probe artifact (HEAD or bot request, or a consumed or expired token) rather than a dead link. ## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Plain-text part is empty.** The QA check reports "Text version is 0 chars". This is a deliverability and accessibility defect for text-only clients and screen-reader fallbacks.
- **Missing alt text:**
  - `246216_BKGR_HERO_LG` (`images.skechers.com/image;width=auto,format=auto/246216_BKGR_HERO_LG`)
  - `de642685-…jpg`: this image is wrapped in a CTA link, so the link has no accessible name.
  - `a6ec5198-…jpg`
  - Tracking pixel `attentivemail.com/wf/open?upn=…`: this is a 1×1 pixel, so a missing alt is low impact (`alt=""` is correct).

- **Malformed charset meta.** `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` has no `http-equiv="Content-Type"` or `charset` attribute, so the declaration is ineffective. Clients that rely on it may mis-decode characters. This does not apply if the MIME headers declare UTF-8.
