# Your cart expires soon!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-08**
## Executive summary

- Well, there it is one more time — that same pink floral D'Lites sneaker, sixth time this week now, this time under "YOU LIKED THESE, AND YOU WEREN'T WRONG." I never liked it, I never clicked it, and telling me I "weren't wrong" about a shoe I never looked at twice doesn't land. It's not even trying the fake-urgency cart angle this time, just doubling down on the recommendation being right, except it isn't.
- Buried under that, though, is the one thing that actually got my attention: men's slip-ins, the Arch Fit and Relaxed Fit lines, right there in the second product row, plus a real "MEMBERS TAKE AN EXTRA 20% OFF" banner with a code. If they'd led with that block instead of the floral sneaker, I'd have looked twice.
- I'm not clicking this one, and I'm sure not forwarding it. Mark as read, same as the last five.

## What's working

- The men's Arch Fit and Relaxed Fit slip-ins showing up in the grid are actually in my lane — hands-free, arch support, casual.
- The 20% off member banner with a real code (EXTRA20) and a clear end date (9/14) is honest and specific, no fine print tricks.
- Payment options like Afterpay and Klarna and a "Find a Store" link are useful if I ever do want to try something on.

## What's weak

- That pink floral D'Lites is now front and center for the sixth straight email this week — I never engaged with it, and "you weren't wrong" rings hollow when I never said anything.
- The hero product photo eats a huge chunk of the screen before I get to anything relevant to me — a lot of scrolling past a shoe I have zero interest in.
- No greeting, no "Hi, Walker," no mention of my points — feels like a stranger's mass blast, not the personalized touch some of their other sends manage.
- Kids' Boy's Garage Sneakers mixed into "you liked these" makes no sense for a 62-year-old guy who's never bought kids' shoes from them.

## Recommendations

- 1. Drop the recycled pink floral D'Lites hero entirely — lead with the men's comfort styles that are actually relevant, since that's the product row that'll get a click from someone like me.
- 2. Put the 20% off member offer at the very top, above the fold, not buried two-thirds down the page.
- 3. Personalize it — my name, my points balance — the way the "Last Chance Labor Day Savings" email did on 9/7, which scored better with me.
- 4. Filter the "you liked these" grid by actual browsing history, not a blanket mix of women's, kids', and men's styles.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Members: extra 20% off comfort styles`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Arch Fit favorites, 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Save an extra 20% on slip-ins and walking shoes through 9/14`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Comfort styles you've actually looked at, now 20% off`
