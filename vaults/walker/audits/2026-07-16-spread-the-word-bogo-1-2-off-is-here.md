# Spread the word 📣 BOGO 1/2 off is here
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- BOGO half off — okay, that lands. I've been watching Famous Footwear pile into my inbox every single day this week, and I'll admit the offer itself is hard to ignore when it's printed that big. But then I scroll down and it's the same story I keep bumping into with these folks: the product grid is full of low-profile white sneakers, pastel athletic shoes, and silhouettes that look like they belong to my daughter-in-law. The Birkenstock section did slow me down — I just got their dedicated email yesterday and I'm already thinking about a new pair of Arizonas before fall — but even that section feels like it's buried under the rest.
- The preheader is a raw tracking URL. That's the second or third time Famous Footwear has done this to me in the last two weeks. I don't know what causes it and I don't care to find out, but every time I see gibberish in that preview line I hesitate before opening. Today the BOGO got me past that hesitation. Not every day.
- I wouldn't click through, and I wouldn't forward this. I'd mark it read and wait to see if there's a men's-specific version in tomorrow's inevitable follow-up.

## What's working

- **BOGO hero is unmissable.** Full-width, bold type, right at the top — I knew what the email was about before I finished loading it.
- **Birkenstock placement is a win for me.** After yesterday's dedicated Birkenstock send, seeing those sandals again in a BOGO frame made me stop and think about whether Arizonas qualify.
- **3X points callout.** I saw it near the bottom. I'm a Rewards member and that kind of stacking matters when I'm already close to a redemption threshold.
- **Layout is clean.** No broken images, text isn't overlapping, the grid renders evenly. Whatever was wrong with the tracking URL preheader didn't spill into the body.

## What's weak

- **The preheader is a raw URL — again.** This is at least the second time this month. It makes the email look like spam before I even open it.
- **Almost nothing in this product grid is for me.** White low-tops, pink athletic shoes, silhouettes aimed at women or teenagers. I counted maybe one or two pairs I'd even bother clicking.
- **No men's comfort anchor.** No wide-width callout, no cushioning mention, no arch support language — nothing that says "hey Walker, this BOGO is for your feet too."
- **No deadline visible.** A BOGO without an expiration date loses urgency fast. I don't know if this is good through the weekend or through next month.
- **Daily email fatigue is real.** This is Famous Footwear's sixth or seventh email in seven days. The offer has to work harder to cut through when I'm already tired of seeing the sender name.

- 1. **Fix the preheader — permanently.** Whatever QA step is missing, add it. A raw click-tracking URL destroys inbox trust faster than any discount can rebuild it.
- 2. **Add a men's comfort section above the fold.** Even a two-product callout — wide widths, cushioned sole, something with "arch support" in the copy — would make this feel like it was sent to me, not to the store's average customer.
- 3. **Put a deadline on the BOGO.** "Ends Sunday" or "Through July 20" turns browsing into urgency. Right now I have no reason to click today versus next week.
- 4. **Anchor the Birkenstock section higher.** After yesterday's Birkenstock send, I'm primed. Put it in the first scroll position and link directly to men's sandals.
- 5. **Thin the product grid.** Fewer products, bigger images, readable prices. On my phone this grid is small enough that I can't tell what I'm looking at without zooming.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Famous Footwear "BOGO 1/2 Off" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally functional but has a significant deliverability risk from an over-dense plain-text body and a pervasive accessibility failure across all 35 images. A minor CSS typo silently drops a style rule, and the viewport meta blocks user zoom.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text URL density — WARN**
The plain-text alternative is 79.2% URLs by character count (14,511 of 18,317 chars). This is above the 70% heuristic threshold flagged by the QA tool and is a known spam-filter signal on Gmail/Outlook bulk filters. The plain-text body likely consists almost entirely of raw click-tracking URLs with little readable prose.

**32 tracking links unverified**
All click links resolve through `click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=...` with base64-encoded payloads. The QA tool skipped HTTP probing on all 32. Destination URLs and UTM parameter presence are unconfirmed.

**Open pixel**
`https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/open.aspx?5FJ43GQ7Y7VEJKMFWSQH4NP4CI.70248&d=70248&bmt=0` — standard 1×1 tracking pixel, no issue with the pixel itself, but it uses no `alt` or `role="presentation"`, which is fine for this pattern.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — 35 images**
Every content image in the email has either no `alt` attribute or an empty `alt=""`. The QA tool flagged 35 warnings across 33 distinct images. Key instances:

- `3b6a7dc1-6955-49f8-868a-bf62a15122ee.png` — the brand logo, which is also the primary click link. A linked image with `alt=""` renders as an unannounced link for screen readers and shows a broken-image icon with no label when images are blocked. This one requires descriptive alt (e.g. `alt="Famous Footwear"`).
- `721856e1-1af4-4bc2-bac9-f51f882b5e40.gif` — animated GIF, purpose unknown from truncated HTML, missing alt.
- `4ccff4ad-f402-43de-a027-5eb147b6f238.jpg` — 2px-tall decorative divider, repeated 4×. `alt=""` is technically correct for decorative spacers per WCAG 1.1.1; however the QA tool is flagging these because the attribute appears to be absent, not just empty, in at least some instances.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
This disables pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is flagged by iOS/Android accessibility audits. Removing `user-scalable=0` and `maximum-scale=1` has no practical layout impact for most email designs.

**`http-equiv` missing on charset meta**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Without it, some legacy clients (Outlook 2007–2013) may not parse the charset declaration. Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```

**CSS typo — `lineapacing`**
In the inline style on a content `<td>`:
```html
style="...lineapacing:20px;..."
```
`lineapacing` is not a valid CSS property (likely intended as `line-height`). The rule is silently ignored in all clients. The affected cell's line spacing falls back to the browser/client default.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 32 destination URLs are wrapped in `click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=<encoded>` and were not probed. UTM parameter presence on the final landing pages is unverified. Without resolving at least a sample of these links, it is not possible to confirm whether `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, or `utm_content` are passed through to famousfootwear.com.

The preheader text reads "Save on the best styles for back-to-school" while the subject line advertises "BOGO 1/2 off" — this is a content-continuity mismatch, but per the scope of this audit, only flagged as a technical observation (preheader is rendered as `display:none` in the `<div class="preheader">` block with correct suppression for inbox display).
