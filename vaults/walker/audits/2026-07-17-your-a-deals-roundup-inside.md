# Your A+ Deals Roundup Inside🍎
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- Seventeen days in a row from Shoe Carnival. I counted. When this one landed in my inbox, the subject said "A+ Deals" with a little apple emoji — and I knew instantly it was a back-to-school roundup. That's not a bad thing on its own, but before I even opened it, the preview line was a long jumbled URL — the kind of thing that made me wonder for a split second if someone hacked my email or if Shoe Carnival just broke something. That's a rough first impression.
- Inside, though, it's actually a decent haul. Birkenstock at 25% off is the second time they've hit me with that deal this week — I noticed it on the 13th when they ran that "Easy As ABC" Birkenstock email, and again on the 15th with the "NOT a Drill" send. At this point I've had three Birkenstock nudges in five days, and I'm honestly starting to warm to the idea. Then I see Brooks Price Break, $40 off. Brooks is a legitimate walking/comfort brand. That one moved the needle for me.
- But the email is stacked — six separate sale modules crammed top to bottom, most of them aimed at "Family Athletics" and kids' back-to-school. The family stuff washes right off me. I'd mark this as read and maybe circle back to that Brooks section, but I wouldn't forward it — there's too much noise for my buddies to wade through.

## What's working

- **Birkenstock 25% off with a "LIMITED TIME" badge** — after three Birkenstock emails this week, I'm actually primed for this offer and it reads as credible.
- **Brooks Price Break at $40 off** — Brooks is a real walking shoe brand I trust; this is the first time they've led with Brooks in 17 emails, and it's the right call for me.
- **Shoe Perks $20 off coupon visible** — I can see the barcodes right in the email without hunting. Makes me feel like a member who got something.
- **Shoe Carnival brand I recognize** — even at email 17, I still know who this is and I don't hate them yet.

## What's weak

- **The preview text is a raw tracking URL** — it showed up in my inbox as a long "https://click.email..." string. That looks like an accident and it's not confidence-inspiring before I even open the email.
- **"Family Athletics" everywhere** — at least three of the six modules are aimed at kids or family athletics. I'm 62 with grown kids; those sections are wallpaper to me.
- **No men's comfort focus in the hero** — the top section has an athletic shoe with a young-feeling layout. Nothing in that hero tells me this is for a man my age who wants cushioning.
- **Six competing sections, no clear winner** — my eyes bounce around. If you've got Brooks and Birkenstock in here, lead with one of them, not the generic 30% family sale.
- **Seventeen straight days** — I'm not unsubscribing yet, but the cadence is wearing on me. This email could've combined two or three recent sends instead of being another daily drop.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Brooks $40-off as the hero** — put a comfort shoe front and center for the man who actually walks in them. Don't bury it in module five.
- 2. **Fix the preview text immediately** — a raw URL in the preheader damages trust before the email opens. Real copy only, even a single line: "Brooks, Birkenstock, and coupon codes — all in one place."
- 3. **Cut the family athletics modules by half** — two family modules, tops. Swap the rest for men's walking or comfort-focused content.
- 4. **Consolidate the back-to-school and Birkenstock beats into one clear theme** — don't try to do both in the same email; pick the one that moves more product for your audience.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Brooks $40 Off + Birkenstock Deal — Inside`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Your Shoe Perks Deals: Brooks, Birkenstock & More`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Brooks Price Break, Birkenstock 25% off, plus your $20 coupon — all live now.`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Three comfort brands, one email. Your Shoe Perks rewards are waiting.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Shoe Carnival "A+ Deals Roundup" (SCA_20260717_friday_deals_AM)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Four critical tracking link failures stem from a zero-width space (`U+200B`) appended to every UTM campaign string; a second open-tracking pixel contains a potentially unresolved `[UNIQUE]` merge token. Both issues affect measurement fidelity before any recipient clicks.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**CRITICAL — Zero-width space corrupts all tracked link types**

All four emltrk.com link verb types (`rd`, `rl`, `p`, `f`) fail with `'ascii' codec can't encode character '\u200b' in position 74–75`. The invisible character trails the campaign value:

```
...&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260717_friday_deals_AM​   ← U+200B here
```

This breaks click-redirect (`?rd`), unsubscribe/relink (`?rl`), open-pixel (`?p`), and forward (`?f`) tracking uniformly — 60% pass rate in QA reflects this. The zero-width space was likely introduced by a copy-paste from a rich-text editor into the template's UTM field.

**MEDIUM — `[UNIQUE]` token in secondary tracking pixel**

```
https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]
```

If this is a literal string rather than a substitution that resolves at send time, every open will be attributed to the same phantom subscriber ID. Verify against ESP preview substitution output before send.

**INFO — 25 redirect-domain links not probed**

Expected behavior for click-redirect domains; not actionable, but confirm all destinations resolve post-send with a test message.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**WARN — 2 content images missing `alt` text**

```
9521013e-7444-4804-9257-f83e6db7b8bc.jpg
a97db5d3-cfac-4b76-923a-ca38510bf3a6.jpg
```

Both are hosted on `image.email.shoecarnival.com`. Missing `alt` leaves image-blocked clients (common in corporate Outlook) with no context.

**WARN — Tracking pixel missing `alt=""`**

```
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&...
```

Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` to suppress screen-reader announcements. Currently carries no `alt` attribute at all.

**MINOR — Malformed IE-compat meta tag**

```html
<meta content="IE=edge" />
```

Missing required `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"` attribute. Correct form:

```html
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
```

**MINOR — No dark mode declarations**

`color-scheme` and `supported-color-schemes` are set to `light` only. No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block is present, meaning Apple Mail dark mode will invert colors automatically without any sender control.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM / Unsubscribe)

The `?rl` link type in the emltrk.com tracking stack is consistent with a tracked unsubscribe/relink. However, because all `?rl` URLs fail due to the `U+200B` corruption, **the unsubscribe redirect is currently non-functional** — a direct CAN-SPAM compliance risk. Physical mailing address and sender identity (`shoecarnival@email.shoecarnival.com`) appear correct from available source; full compliance verification requires the un-truncated footer.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM / Landing Page)

The `U+200B` corruption in every `?rd` click-redirect means UTM parameters will either fail to pass through or be dropped entirely by the tracker, resulting in all clicks landing as direct/unattributed traffic in analytics. No campaign-level data will be recoverable for this send without a post-hoc fix to the tracker's raw log.
