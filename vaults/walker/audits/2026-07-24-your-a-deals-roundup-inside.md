# Your A+ Deals Roundup Inside🍎
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-four mornings in a row. And today they came back with the exact same subject line they used a week ago — "Your A+ Deals Roundup Inside" with that little apple emoji. I remember that one. I reviewed it on July 17th. They ran it again like I wouldn't notice, or maybe they're counting on the fact that I've got 23 other Shoe Carnival emails stacked up and my memory isn't what it used to be. Well, it is.
- I opened it anyway — force of habit at this point — and what I found is a loud, busy back-to-school email packed with kids, teens, Jordan Brand blocks, and classroom puns. I'm 62. My kids are grown. My grandkids aren't my shopping problem. There's a Crocs section at the bottom for $39.98 and a Brooks "$40 Off" block buried in the middle that almost made me sit up, but I had to scroll past three panels of teenagers to get there. The preview text in my inbox was a raw tracking URL. Not a word of copy. Just a link that looks like spam.
- I won't click. I won't forward this. I'll mark it read and move on, same as yesterday and the day before.

## What's working

- **The hero discount is clear** — "$20 OFF $60+" with the code `SAVE20HC` is right at the top, easy to read, no squinting required.
- **Brooks shows up** — "$40 OFF" on Brooks is the kind of number that'll stop me mid-scroll. That's a brand I trust and they actually named it.
- **Crocs at $39.98** — that's in my price range and a brand I cross-shop. Points for including it.
- **Shoe Perks section** — the free $10 reward reminder at the bottom is a nice loyalty nudge, and I'm already a member, so I notice it.
- **No layout disasters** — the email rendered cleanly. Sections are readable, images loaded, nothing is broken or overlapping.

## What's weak

- **Recycled subject line, word for word, from July 17th** — that's seven days ago. I noticed. If your own subscriber notices you're running the same creative twice in a week, you've run out of ideas.
- **This is a kids email, full stop** — every model I can see is under 25. Back-to-school puns. Jordan Brand. The entire visual language says "parents shopping for their children," not "man shopping for his own feet."
- **Preview text is a raw tracking URL** — that's what I saw in my inbox before I opened it. "Shoe Carnival https://click.email.shoecarnival.com/?qs=ABB7..." That looks like a phishing attempt on a bad day and careless tech on a good one.
- **No expiration date I can see** — the $20 off code is there but I have no idea if it expires tonight or next month. That kills any urgency.
- **The Brooks and Crocs deals are buried** — the two things that actually matter to me are in the bottom third of a scroll-heavy email I almost skipped.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the Brooks and Crocs blocks up** — if you're going to send this to a 60+ male subscriber, lead with the comfort brands. Pull one of those panels above the fold.
- 2. **Fix the preheader** — a tracking URL in the inbox preview is a trust signal in the wrong direction. Even "Code SAVE20HC — save $20 on your next pair" would be better than what's showing now.
- 3. **Don't recycle the subject line within the same week** — if the creative is this similar, at least change the subject so it doesn't feel like a copy-paste.
- 4. **Add a deadline to the code** — "Expires Sunday" or "This weekend only" would give me a reason to act today instead of letting it sit.
- 5. **Segment this one away from me** — a grandparent might send this to their kid. But a 62-year-old male buying for himself should get a version where the hero model isn't 16.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Shoe Carnival "A+ Deals Roundup" (SCA_20260724_friday_deals_AM)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Two click-tracking URLs contain an embedded Unicode zero-width space (`U+200B`) that will silently break link resolution for any subscriber clicking those links. All other infrastructure (ESP routing, responsive CSS scaffold) is functional, though four images lack alt text and one open-tracking pixel contains what appears to be an unfilled merge token.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**CRITICAL — Zero-width space corrupts two tracked URLs**

Both failing URLs share the same malformed `t=` parameter:

```
utm_campaign=SCA_20260724_friday_deals_AM​
                                          ^
                                          U+200B ZERO WIDTH SPACE (invisible)
```

Affected:
- `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?rd&i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260724_friday_deals_AM​`
- `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?rl&i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260724_friday_deals_AM​`

The `rd` and `rl` suffixes suggest these are the redirect and/or re-engagement tracking variants. The zero-width space was almost certainly introduced by copy-pasting the campaign name from a rich-text source (Google Docs, Slack, etc.). URL-encoded it becomes `%E2%80%8B`, which will either corrupt the `t=` parameter value or cause the tracker to reject the request entirely — either way, click attribution for those link slots will be lost or misreported.

**INFO — 26 tracking links skipped (expected)**

All 26 emltrk.com click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe. This is normal behavior; no action needed.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

| Content image | `9521013e-7444-4804-9257-f83e6db7b8bc.jpg` |

The two content images should have descriptive `alt` text for screen readers and for image-blocked clients (Gmail image proxy-off, corporate firewalls). The tracking pixels should have `alt=""` explicitly set to suppress screen-reader announcements.

**No dark mode declared**

`<meta name="color-scheme" content="light" />` and `<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light" />` explicitly opt out of dark mode. This is a deliberate choice (not a bug), but means Apple Mail and iOS dark-mode users will see the email in a light-mode forced view rather than an auto-adapted one.

**HTML 4.01 Transitional DOCTYPE**

Standard for ESP-generated email; no action needed.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**WARN — Possible unfilled token in open-tracking pixel**

```
https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]
```

`[UNIQUE]` is a literal string visible in the HTML source. If this is the ESP's macro syntax (e.g., Salesforce Marketing Cloud uses `%%_messageContext%%`), it must be replaced at send time by the platform. Verify in the sent copy that `[UNIQUE]` was substituted with a per-recipient hash. If it arrived unsubstituted in the delivered message, the open-tracking pixel will fail to record unique opens and all subscribers will share a single open event.

No other merge tokens or personalization fields were visible in the truncated source.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The `t=` parameter in the emltrk tracking URLs appears to carry the UTM campaign value inline (`utm_campaign=SCA_20260724_friday_deals_AM`), which the tracker presumably appends to the destination URL on redirect. The zero-width space corruption (see §2) will cause the campaign value to arrive malformed at GA4/analytics on the landing page — expect a spike in `(not set)` or a garbled campaign name in Friday's session data.

All other UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_content`) cannot be confirmed from the truncated source, but should be verified in the full redirect chain.
