# WORLD CHAMPIONS. SPAIN 🇪🇸⚽️🏆
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- Here we go again. I opened this thing and within two seconds I knew exactly what I was in for — another round of Spain, soccer, and people younger than my kids celebrating a trophy I don't follow. I counted three soccer-focused emails from adidas just this week, and now a fourth. "THE FINAL AWAITS" on the 17th, the FIFA stuff buried in the back-to-school send this morning, and now this. At some point a brand has to ask itself whether the guy who opened their last five emails actually cares about the FIFA World Cup. I don't.
- That said, I'll give adidas this: the email itself is clean. Big photo, Spain national team celebrating in red jerseys, a clear "SHOP SPAIN WINNERS COLLECTION" button, and an adiClub section partway down. Nothing is broken, nothing is overlapping. If I were a soccer fan, this is the email I'd want today. But I'm not, and the only reason I even scrolled down was to see if there was a shoe buried in there somewhere. There is — some sneakers in the middle section — but it's clearly window dressing next to the jersey/champions angle.
- I would not click. I would not forward it. I'd mark it read and move on, mildly annoyed that the fourth soccer email in a week just landed in my inbox on the same day as something else from adidas I also didn't need.

## What's working

- **Email renders cleanly on mobile** — big image, legible headline, clear CTA button, no broken layouts or overlapping text.
- **adiClub acknowledgment is present** — at least they remembered I'm a member, which is more than the Club América send bothered to do.
- **Timeliness is real** — Spain evidently just won the World Cup, so this isn't a stale promo. The creative matches the moment.
- **Visual hierarchy is solid** — eye goes image → "STARS SHINE TOGETHER." → CTA. A marketer did their job here, just not for me.

## What's weak

- **Wrong audience, again** — this is the third soccer-heavy adidas email in three days. There is no signal I care about football. I'm 62, I'm not wearing a Spain jersey to the grocery store.
- **No comfort/walking shoe content** — the sneakers visible in the middle section are thumbnail-sized and subordinate to the soccer narrative. Nothing about cushioning, arch support, or fit.
- **Subject line is all caps** — "WORLD CHAMPIONS. SPAIN" in full caps reads like a tabloid headline. Looked like noise in my inbox.
- **No price signal anywhere** — I can't tell if "SHOP SPAIN WINNERS COLLECTION" means full MSRP or if there's any deal attached. Without a number, I keep scrolling.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the list before the next win-celebration send.** If my purchase history or browsing data shows zero soccer engagement, swap me into a "comfort picks this summer" version of this email. The Spain win is a tentpole moment — use it to surface the adidas shoes Spain's staff wore on the sideline, or the stadium-comfortable Cloudfoam walkers. Tie the cultural moment to something I'd actually buy.
- 2. **Show one specific shoe with a price.** Even a sidebar hero: "The shoe Spain's sideline wore. $89." That's my entry point. The collection CTA alone doesn't pull me in.
- 3. **Drop the all-caps subject.** It degrades trust and feels like a shout at an already-crowded inbox.
- 4. **Honor the adiClub section.** Right now it says "Spain made history with it. Now it's yours to own." — that reads as a sales push, not a member benefit. Make it "adiClub members get early access" or a points-earning mechanic and it actually earns the real estate.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Spain won. Your next walk just got inspired.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The shoes behind the champions — now in your size`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Comfort picks from the brand that just took gold — built for your next step, not the pitch.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `adiClub members: shop the collection before everyone else.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed failures: a malformed internal redirect URL breaks at least one link, and the absence of a plain-text MIME part violates deliverability best practices and risks spam classification. Rendering and compliance structure are otherwise sound.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect — unknown URL scheme**
The QA probe detected a link where the internal `href` uses a relative-style path with no scheme:
```
/g/0cb7d81d-1da7-45b0-9d5f-9823c82c0e5f?txn=007daf80-83cc-11f1-ac54-9e016bacd681
```
This is passed as the destination inside a tracking redirect at:
```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/0cb7d81d-1da7-45b0-9d5f-9823c82c0e5f?cp_tp=v3...
```
The `/g/` path lacks `https://www.adidas.com` prefix, so the redirect resolver cannot construct a valid destination URL. Clicking this link will produce an error for the recipient. **This is a broken CTA.**

**[WARN] 14 tracking links skipped**
All click-tracking links route through `click.comms.adidas.com`. These were not probed for destination validity; downstream broken links cannot be confirmed or ruled out from this audit alone.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Two images missing `alt` text**

| Image | src (truncated) |
|---|---|
| `0cb7d81d-1da7-45b0-9d5f-9823c82c0e5f` | `https://dv.adidas.com/o/0cb7d81d-...` |
| `PfooCk-k4HOefq0lL02LHCxvw8-...=452` | `http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...` |

Missing `alt` attributes mean images-off rendering (common in Outlook, corporate clients) displays blank space with no context. The second image is also a tracking pixel — see compliance note below.

**[WARN] One image served over HTTP**
```
src="http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019f7cc372e6-..."
```
Mixed-content HTTP image will be blocked by most modern email clients that enforce HTTPS. This appears to be a tracking pixel; if blocked, open tracking for this send will be unreliable.

**Font declarations duplicated across two `<style>` blocks**
`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` `@font-face` rules are declared twice in `<head>`. Both blocks fall back identically to `local('Arial')` — no actual web font is fetched — so there is no rendering defect, but the dead duplicate declarations add ~1 KB of unnecessary CSS.

**Doctype is HTML 4.01 Transitional**
`<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN" ...>` is a deliberate choice for legacy Outlook compatibility and is acceptable. No issue.

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text MIME part absent**
QA reports the text/plain alternative body is 0 characters. A multipart/alternative message with an empty text part:
- Triggers spam heuristics in SpamAssassin and similar filters (missing text body is a strong spam signal)
- Violates the spirit of CAN-SPAM's requirement that the message be readable without HTML rendering
- Causes delivery failures on text-only MUAs

**Unsubscribe / CAN-SPAM footer** — present in HTML structure (footer padding classes and privacy link classes visible: `.mobile-privacy-transactional`, `.mobile-footer-padding-privacy-block`). Cannot confirm physical mailing address is present from truncated source, but standard adidas ESP templates include it. No confirmed violation.

**Sender authentication** — `us-news.comms.adidas.com` is a known adidas ESP subdomain. SPF/DKIM/DMARC posture cannot be confirmed from HTML alone; no issues surfaced by QA probes.

**`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">`** — suppresses the HTTP `Referer` header on link clicks. This will strip referrer data from any landing page analytics not relying solely on UTM parameters.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The broken `/g/` link (section 2) directly breaks at least one CTA's landing page continuity — the destination cannot be reached at all.

For the 14 tracked links that were not probed: UTM parameter presence cannot be confirmed from the available source excerpt. The `no-referrer` meta tag (noted above) means any landing page that relies on `document.referrer` for attribution will receive nothing — UTM params in the tracked URLs are the only attribution signal that will survive the click.
