# Oooh! BOGO 50% off!
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-01**
## Executive summary

- Well, I opened it because BOGO 50% off is always worth a look — that's just common sense. But the moment I scrolled past the header, I knew this one wasn't for me. Every single shoe in this email is a wedge sandal or some strappy women's thing. "Natural Beauties" with a woman in a floral dress — that's not my shopping trip. I'm a DSW customer too, but I'm here for sneakers and casual shoes for myself, not sandals for somebody's brunch outfit. The deal is real, the products are just wrong for me entirely.
- The offer is strong but the targeting is zero. Sending a women's-only spring sandal email to a 62-year-old male customer is a wasted send. I'm not converting on this.

## What's working

- **The offer is crystal clear.** BOGO 50% off is right there at the top, big and readable. I didn't have to hunt for it.
- **The hero image loads.** Images aren't broken, the email renders cleanly. That part I can't complain about.
- **"Shop by Size" module at the bottom** is actually a smart idea — size-first shopping is how I think about buying shoes.

## What's weak

- **Every product is women's.** Wedges, strappy sandals, floral platforms — not a single men's shoe in sight. DSW has a whole men's section; none of it showed up here.
- **"Naturally, women's offer"** — they even put it right in the email. So why is it in my inbox? That's a targeting miss, plain and simple.
- **The text is small and stacked on busy images.** On my phone I'd be squinting at the script-style overlays on those product photos. I shouldn't have to pinch and zoom to read a sale email.
- **No personalization.** No "Hey Walker" or anything that tells me they know I've bought men's shoes here before. I'm just a random address to them.
- **No visible price points.** I can see the BOGO offer but I can't read any actual prices on the products. I want to know what I'm working with before I click anything.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Don't send women's-only emails to men.** Basic segmentation — if I've only ever browsed or bought men's shoes, keep me out of the women's offer list unless you're specifically pitching gifting.
- 2. **Add a "For Him" secondary section.** Even a small module — "Shop Men's BOGO too" — would have kept me interested instead of scrolling past.
- 3. **Increase the text size on product overlays.** That script font on the photo is stylish but I genuinely cannot read it without zooming on mobile.
- 4. **Surface a price.** Show me one anchor price — "from $49.99" — so I know whether this deal is worth my time before I click.
- 5. **If you're going to pitch gifting, say so.** "Pick something up for her" would at least give me a reason to stay.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Duplicate conflicting `text-size-adjust` declarations (confirmed)**
In the universal `*` reset rule, `-ms-text-size-adjust` is declared twice with conflicting values:
```css
*{
  -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%;   /* line 1 — sets to 100% */
  ...
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;   /* line 2 — overrides to none */
  text-size-adjust: none
}
```
The `none` value wins in cascade, disabling OS-level text scaling for all elements globally. This can cause accessibility problems on mobile (WCAG 1.4.4 — Resize Text) and is unintentional given the first declaration.

**Issue 2 — `<meta charset>` missing `http-equiv` attribute (confirmed)**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
The `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute is absent. Without it, Outlook desktop and some legacy clients do not interpret this as a charset declaration. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```

**Issue 3 — HelveticaNow fonts served as `.ttf`, not `.woff`/`.woff2`**
The six HelveticaNow @font-face rules load `.ttf` files (~2–3× larger than `.woff2`). Futura PT correctly uses `.woff`. Not a rendering blocker, but increases load payload for Gmail/Apple Mail users where web fonts render.
