# Buy more = SAVE more 😍
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-27**
## Executive summary

- DSW again. This is the same "buy more, save more" ladder with code SOBRIGHT that I saw yesterday in "Have you used your $15 off yet?" Last week it was 20% off with code JINGLE, all week long. The pink banner at the top is big and easy on my eyes: $15 off $49, $20 off $99, $60 off $199. I can do that math in my head.
- Then the email turns into a boot show. "Bootie Reboot," "cool-girl booties," snakeskin heels, a fuzzy skirt, a model in a red coat. All of it is women's. I'm a 62-year-old man looking for a cushioned slip-on, and nothing below the banner is for me. The only men's link is a small "Shop Men" under the banner.
- I'd skim it and leave it. I wouldn't click, and I wouldn't forward it. The savings ladder is fine, but it doesn't make me want a pair of shoes.

## What's working

- The three-tier savings ladder is clear. I can see right away what I save at each spend level.
- The big pink banner is easy to read on my phone, with large numbers and no squinting.
- The "Claim $15 Off" button is black on pink, so it stands out.
- The barcode and the dates (October 26-28) in the fine print tell me the offer is real and has an end date.
- The size grid, including "Shop Wide Width," is something I'd use. Wide width matters to my feet.

## What's weak

- Everything below the banner is women's: booties, heels, flats, slippers. Nothing speaks to a man my age.
- There's no comfort, cushioning or arch support anywhere. I don't see Skechers, Hoka, Brooks or New Balance, or a slip-on.
- "Shop Men" is a small underlined link that's easy to miss and hard to tap on a phone.
- The "$49.99" circle on the tan boot looks like a price, but the fine print says it's "from" $49.99. That's slightly confusing.
- This is the same offer I keep getting, now with a new code. The cadence is wearing thin, and the boot pitch feels like a different email stuck under the banner.

## Recommendations

- 1. Send men a men's version. Lead with comfort and slip-on walking shoes from brands I know, and put a "Shop Men's Comfort" button right under the banner.
- 2. Make the tap targets bigger. The "Shop Women / Men / Kids" links are tiny underlined text. Make them buttons.
- 3. Tie the offer to a product. Say something like "$20 off $99 gets you a pair of walkers plus socks," so I can see how to reach the threshold.
- 4. Fold in the sale cadence. One clear offer per week, not a new code every other day.

  - A 400 from a tracking redirect usually means the wrapped destination is malformed or the redirect is rejecting the request. It could also be an artifact of the probe if the destination is an `sms:` or deep-link target. The data doesn't distinguish these, so verify manually from a mobile client.
