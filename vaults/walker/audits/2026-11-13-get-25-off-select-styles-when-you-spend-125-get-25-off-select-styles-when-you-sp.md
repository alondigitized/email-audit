# Get 25% off select styles when you spend $125+, get 25% off select styles when you spend $125+
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-13**
## Executive summary

- Well, this is the third New Balance email in a week that doesn't lead with a walking shoe. Last time it was shopping bags, and now it's a box of red Christmas ornaments filling the top third of my phone. They're cute, but I can't buy a Christmas ball with a "B" on it to walk in. The offer is clear enough, though: 25% off select styles if I spend $125 or more, through the 18th.
- The shoe grid is down below the fold and mixes in kids' Hook & Loop styles and a sock. The 608v5 and the 574 are the only ones that look like something I'd wear. There's no cushioning or arch talk anywhere, and "select styles" doesn't tell me whether my shoe is included. I'd probably skim it and move on. I might tap the 608v5 if I were already shopping. I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- "Get 25% off select styles" is big, plain and easy to read on my phone, with no squinting.
- The black "Shop now" button is wide and easy to tap with my thumb.
- The dollar tiers (Under $50, $50–$100, Over $100) fit how I shop. I like to see the price first.
- "Walking" is in the footer nav, so a walker does have somewhere to go.
- The 608v5 and 574 look like the dependable everyday shoes I'd consider.

## What's weak

- The hero is a box of ornaments, with no shoe and nothing about comfort. It's the same shopping-bag trick as the last few sends.
- The $125 minimum is a catch, and "select styles" is vague. The fine print repeats itself twice, in small type that's hard to read.
- Half the grid is kids' shoes (Hook & Loop, Rave Run) and a sock. It doesn't feel aimed at a 62-year-old man.
- There's no mention of cushioning, arch support or slip-on ease, and no reviews or prices on the shoes.
- The grid items have no prices or sale tags, so I can't tell what 25% off gets me.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with a men's walking shoe, such as the 608v5, in the hero. Add a line like "Cushioned all-day comfort" and a price.
- 2. Fill the grid with men's walking and comfort styles, with sale prices shown. Drop the kids' shoes and the sock.
- 3. Say which shoes qualify. Move the $125 rule up next to the headline and cut the duplicate fine print.
- 4. Add a men's walking button, such as "Shop men's walking shoes."
- 5. Say when the sale ends, for example "Ends Nov 18."

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML has template leftovers (placeholder background URL, default `<title>`, no-`src` `@font-face` rules) and 3 images without alt text. The source was truncated and the QA run has no header or link-probe failures, so the unsubscribe, UTM and merge-token checks below are limited to what was visible.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Placeholder asset in CSS:** `.bgwhite {background-image: url('http://image.XXXXX.png')}`. This is an unreplaced template URL over insecure `http://`. It will 404 or trigger mixed-content blocking if the class is applied.
- **Link probing was partial:** 12 links were probed and 25 skipped (footer, social and utility links were deprioritized). No broken links were reported among the 12. The 25 skipped links are unverified.
- **Plain-text part is mostly URLs:** 12,096 of 13,860 characters (about 87%) are URLs. That points to long redirect-wrapped tracking links (`link.email.newbalance.com/...`) and little readable text. It can hurt spam scoring and plain-text readability.
- **Open-tracking pixel:** `https://link.email.newbalance.com/mo/egR1f966kc_818512772_12647898_125195_23891567.gif` is a tracking pixel with no alt attribute (see §3).

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (QA-confirmed, 3 images):**
  - `ShippingIcons_06.png`
  - `ShippingBanner_DarkMode_03.png`
  - `egR1f966kc_..._23891567.gif`, the tracking pixel, which should carry `alt=""` and hidden dimensions.
- **Reused assets:** the first two images are served from `/custloads/818512772/NBUS/myNB_Welcome/UpdatedImages/`. That path is a welcome-program folder, so the assets look reused from another template.
- **Dark-mode contrast:** `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark) { a {color:#cf0a2c !important;} body {background:#151415 !important;} }`. #cf0a2c on #151415 is about 3.3:1, below WCAG AA's 4.5:1 for normal text. The rule applies to every `<a>`, including text links.
- **Fonts never load:** the `@font-face` blocks (`proxima-nova`, `linotype-didot`, and others) have no `src`. Every client falls back to Arial or Times New Roman.
- **Conflicting CSS:** `.bg1` is declared twice with different values (`background-color:#ffffff`, then `background:#58546d`). The later one wins, which is probably unintended.
- **Template hygiene:**
  - `<title>New Balance Template</title>` was never replaced.
  - `<meta content="IE=edge" />` has no `http-equiv`, so it does nothing.
  - An HTML 4.01 doctype is combined with an `xmlns` XHTML attribute and self-closed tags.
  - The only `.bgwhite` background-image rule is the placeholder URL above.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Remove or replace the `url('http://image.XXXXX.png')` placeholder, or delete the unused `.bgwhite` rule. Serve all assets over HTTPS.
2. Add `alt` text to `ShippingIcons_06.png` and `ShippingBanner_DarkMode_03.png`, and `alt=""` plus `width="1" height="1"` to the tracking pixel.
3. Change the dark-mode link colour to something lighter than `#cf0a2c` to reach 4.5:1 or better on `#151415`.
4. Add `src` to the `@font-face` rules or delete them, and replace the default `<title>`.
5. Remove the duplicate `.bg1` declaration and fix `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge">`.
6. Fix the duplicated subject and preheader string in the ESP.
7. Shorten the redirect-wrapped URLs, or give the plain-text part more readable copy.
8. Re-run the audit with the full HTML and the original headers (SPF, DKIM, DMARC, `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`). Probe the 25 skipped links and the final redirect UTM parameters.
