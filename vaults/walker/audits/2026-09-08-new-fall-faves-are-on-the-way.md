# New fall faves are on the way
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-08**
## Executive summary

- Well, at least this one's a change of pace — no more BOGO 50% off, no more animal print, no more "GO GO GO" screaming at me. This is "From The Brown Up," a whole email built around boots, heels, and strappy sandals in autumn brown tones. Problem is, I scrolled through nearly the entire thing and didn't see a single men's shoe until two models down, where there's a sneaker and an ankle boot sitting on a table — and even those aren't clearly labeled men's or women's. Everything else is women's heels, strappy sandals, and a woman modeling the clothes.
- The "Sneakers from $59.99" banner up top caught my eye for half a second — that's a real number I can act on — but then the whole email pivots into fashion-trend territory that has nothing to do with hands-free, cushioned, arch-supported anything. This reads like it was written for someone building a fall wardrobe around ankle boots, not someone looking for a shoe that won't hurt his feet on a walk.
- I wouldn't click, and I definitely wouldn't forward this to a friend — there's nothing here for a guy like me. I'd scroll past and mark it read.

## What's working

- The $59.99 sneaker banner at the top is a real, specific price — that's the one thing that could've pulled me in.
- Layout is clean, no broken images or overlapping text that I could see.
- "Shop All Women," "Shop Wide Width," "Shop Wide Calf" sections are at least clearly labeled, so I know quickly this isn't for me.
- Free shipping and Afterpay mentions at the bottom are legible, standard trust cues.

## What's weak

- No men's category or men's product shown anywhere until way down the page, and even then it's not called out as men's.
- Entire creative — "From The Brown Up," "Grounded In Brown" — is a women's fashion trend push, nothing about comfort, cushioning, or arch support.
- No offer code or percentage off tied to the brown trend items — just "try the trend," which isn't a call to action I can act on.
- Feels like the same templated DSW blast regardless of who's opening it — no sign they know I've been buying men's comfort shoes.
- Six or seven emails this week were BOGO 50% off on repeat; now it's a hard pivot to a fashion trend with zero relevance to me. Neither cadence feels like they're paying attention to who I am.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment by gender/purchase history — if I've never clicked a women's heel, don't lead the whole email with them. Swap in a men's comfort/casual hero for subscribers like me.
- 2. Put a real offer next to that $59.99 sneaker banner — a specific % off or a deadline would make it worth clicking through.
- 3. Add a men's row up top, not buried after three women's-only sections.
- 4. Tie back to comfort features (arch support, slip-on, cushioning) somewhere in the copy — right now it's 100% style, 0% function.
