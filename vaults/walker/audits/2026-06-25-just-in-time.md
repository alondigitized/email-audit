# Just in time
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- I opened this expecting HOKA to have finally moved on — ten days ago they sent me "Move fast for Father's Day" when Father's Day was already gone, and I gave them a 5. Now it's June 25th, Father's Day was June 15th, and they're running "Just in time" over a gift card push for a holiday that's been dead for nearly two weeks. There is nothing "just in time" about this. The subject pulled me in with false urgency, and the hero slapped me with an offer built for someone shopping FOR me, not for me myself. I'm the dad. I'm not buying myself an eGift card.
- The one thing keeping this from being a total waste of my time is the Bondi 9 section, halfway down. "The ultra-cushioned game changer for everyday run and walking" — that's exactly what I want to hear from HOKA. Comfort, every day, flat ground. If they'd led with that instead of a Father's Day ghost story, I'd have had my wallet open. Instead I scrolled past it on my way to the delete button.
- I will not click. I will not forward this. I'm starting to wonder if anyone at HOKA checks a calendar before they hit send.

## What's working

- **Bondi 9 copy hits the mark.** "Ultra-cushioned game changer for everyday run and walking" — that's my exact frame when I'm shoe shopping. One line that actually spoke to me.
- **Email renders clean on mobile.** Text is big enough to read without pinching, images load, layout holds together. At least HOKA's production team knows what they're doing.
- **"Shop eGift Cards" CTA is clear.** Wrong offer, but the button doesn't make me guess. Verb plus noun, easy to find.
- **Visual hierarchy guides the eye.** Gift card hero leads, three shoe rows follow, free shipping closes. Logical structure even if the lead offer is wrong for me.

## What's weak

- **Second Father's Day push, second time it's stale.** June 15th's "Move fast for Father's Day" was already a miss — Father's Day was the same day. Now it's ten days later and we're still here. At some point this becomes brand-damaging, not just inefficient.
- **Gift-giver framing for the dad.** "Haven't snagged the perfect gift for Father's Day? Let him choose." I'm the him. I'm not snagging gifts for myself via a gift card loop.
- **"Just in time" sets up a promise the email can't keep.** That subject line implies urgency and relevance. What I got was neither.
- **The socks promo requires a $200+ spend.** Socks aren't what I'm buying a $200 shoe for. Low-value incentive, high threshold. Doesn't move me.
- **Free shipping requires a text signup.** Frame it as a benefit, not an exchange. "Give us your phone number and we'll give you free shipping" is the least exciting sentence in retail.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pull the Father's Day creative now.** Today. Doesn't matter that it was already built — running stale holiday messaging erodes the trust the June 17th "Stability without the bulk" and June 16th "Early access: Torrent 5" emails worked hard to build.
- 2. **Lead with the Bondi 9.** Make it the hero. Full-width image, the cushion copy, a price, and a direct "Shop Bondi 9" link — not a homepage. That's an email I actually click.
- 3. **Reframe the free shipping offer as a perk, not a transaction.** "HOKA members get free expedited shipping" lands better than "sign up for texts and we'll ship faster." Make me feel like a known customer.
- 4. **Drop the socks threshold or raise the prize.** If I'm spending $200 on running shoes, socks are table stakes. Offer a discount, a second pair, or early access — something that moves the needle.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Broken mobile CSS rules** — `@media only screen and (max-width:480px)` contains two invalid declarations with missing values:

```css
.nav_padding { padding-top:px !important; padding-bottom:px !important; }
.nav_border  { border-top:px solid ; }
```

Both are silently dropped by all parsers. The numeric values and border color were never populated. Mobile nav spacing and border will render incorrectly.

**[MEDIUM] Duplicate conflicting `<meta name="viewport">` tags:**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
<meta content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=yes" name="viewport" />
```

The first omits `user-scalable`; the second explicitly sets `user-scalable=yes`. Parsers generally use the last declaration, but behavior is undefined. Collapse to one tag.

**[LOW] Missing `http-equiv` on charset meta:**

```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```

Correct email form is `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />`. Modern clients infer charset from the MIME envelope, so this is non-critical, but malformed.

**[LOW] Empty `<title>` tag** — `<title></title>`. Samsung Mail and some threading UIs surface the title; populate it (e.g., the subject line).

**[LOW] Custom web fonts unsupported in major clients** — `TheFutureHOKA` and `TheFutureHOKAMono` are loaded via `@font-face` from `hoka.com/on/demandware.static/...`. Gmail (web + Android), all Outlook versions, and Yahoo ignore `@font-face`. Fallback `font-family` stack is not visible in the truncated source — must verify a system font (e.g., `Arial, sans-serif`) is declared on all `font-family` properties.

`font-display: swap` on `@font-face` declarations is valid CSS but has no effect in any email client. Harmless, not a defect.

**[INFO] Off-black link color `#000001`** — `a { color:#000001; }` is an intentional iOS auto-linker workaround (prevents iOS Mail from overriding near-black text with blue links). This is a known technique, not a defect, but should be documented in the template's maintenance notes so future editors don't "fix" it to `#000000`.
