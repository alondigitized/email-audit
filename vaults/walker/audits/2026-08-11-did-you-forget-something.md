# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-11**
## Executive summary

- Same "abandoned cart" trick they ran on 8/8 and 8/9, and once again the first shoe they slap up top — that pink floral D'Lites with the embroidered flowers — is not something I've ever looked at, let alone put in a cart. If that's supposed to be "my" abandoned item, the tracking's off, and it makes me wonder if this whole email is even personalized to me. Good news is once you get past that, there's a real wall of men's slip-ins — six pairs, most of them the hands-free Arch Fit kind I actually care about, so it's not a total loss.
- The 25% off banner near the bottom is for kids' back-to-school styles, which does nothing for me, but at least it's a real, specific offer with a real deadline instead of vague filler. Between this, "Your Items Are Waiting" twice, and now this one — that's three near-identical "come back and buy" emails from them in four days. I'm not clicking on the strength of that pink shoe, but I might glance at the men's slip-in grid since it's right there. Not forwarding this to anybody.

## What's working

- The men's Arch Fit and Slip-ins lineup lower down is exactly my speed — cushioned, no-lace, casual.
- "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" button is big, orange, easy to tap.
- 25% off kids sale has a real number and a real end date (8/13), no vague "limited time" nonsense.
- Afterpay/Klarna and "Find a Store" options are visible if I wanted to finance or go try shoes on in person.

## What's weak

- Leading with a pink floral women's sneaker on a "you forgot this" email tells me the personalization is broken, or they don't actually know who I am.
- Third "come back and finish checking out" email in four days is wearing thin — feels automated, not thoughtful.
- The kids' 25% off deal is dead weight for me — no kids, no interest.
- Six little product photos crammed together lower in the email are small on a phone screen, hard to tell them apart without squinting.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix whatever's pulling the "abandoned item" — if I never touched that pink D'Lites, don't lead with it. Lead with a men's shoe if that's genuinely what's in my history, or drop the fake-cart framing altogether and just show me men's comfort styles.
- 2. Cut the cadence — three "you forgot something" emails in four days trains me to ignore all of them.
- 3. Either drop the kids' sale for accounts like mine or swap it for a men's offer with a real percentage off.
- 4. Make the small product tiles bigger, or feature one men's slip-in large the way they did the floral shoe.
