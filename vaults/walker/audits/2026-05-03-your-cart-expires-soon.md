# Your cart expires soon!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-03**
## Executive summary

- **From:** SKECHERS | **Subject:** Your cart expires soon!
- Skechers caught me at the right moment. I had that Glide-Step Vortex Slip-in sitting in my cart and this email landed with the hero shoe right at the top and a 20% off banner below it. My first instinct was: okay, they remembered me, they're giving me a deal, let me finish this. That's a solid cart abandonment formula. But the email loses steam fast once I scroll past the hero — the secondary product strip is full of women's shoes, there's no mention of my Skechers Plus membership, and buried at the very bottom is Mother's Day fine print that reminded me this email wasn't really about me. The bones are good; the execution is generic.

## What's working

- The hero does exactly what a cart abandonment email should do: show me the specific shoe I walked away from, give me one big button to close the deal, and sweeten it with a discount. The Glide-Step Vortex image is clean and large — easy to see on my phone. "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" is unambiguous. The 20% off banner directly below reinforces the purchase.
- The subject line "Your cart expires soon!" creates real urgency without yelling at me. Twenty-two characters — that fits comfortably in my phone inbox without getting cut off. The message: *act now or lose it.* I understand that. I respond to that.

## What's weak

- Once I scroll past the hero, Skechers talks past me. The secondary product grid leading up to the "SHOP ALL BEST SELLERS" banner is stocked with women's styles. I can read the labels — Women's Slip-ins this, Women's Arch Fit that. I'm a 62-year-old man trying to buy shoes for myself, not Mother's Day gifts. The Mother's Day fine print at the very bottom confirms this email was built around that campaign and I'm just getting the cart abandonment overlay on top.
- The bigger miss for me: I'm a Skechers Plus member. This email treats me like a stranger off the street. No "as a Plus member, you're already getting..." language, no points balance, no member-exclusive callout on the 20% offer. Skechers has my purchase history. If they know I had Slip-ins in my cart, they definitely know I'm a loyalty member.
- The "LET'S GET TEXTING" and app download modules aren't hurting anything, but they're dead weight for someone who already has the app and is trying to close a transaction.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a men's product strip in the secondary grid.** If you know I'm a 62-year-old male with Slip-ins in my cart, show me more men's Slip-ins and Arch Fit styles below the hero — not women's shoes.
- 2. **Acknowledge my Plus membership.** Something like "As a Skechers Plus member, lock in your 20% today" adds recognition and creates member-specific urgency.
- 3. **Add the product name to the CTA.** "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER — GLIDE-STEP VORTEX" or even just "Claim Your Slip-ins" is more specific than a generic button that could belong to any email.
- 4. **Surface a deadline on the 20% offer.** "Expires Sunday" beats "expires soon" — I need a concrete date to feel real urgency, not just a vague warning.
- 5. **Drop or bury the Mother's Day content for male segments.** The fine print at the bottom isn't a disaster, but even flagging this email as Mother's Day territory, however subtly, sends a mixed signal.
- | Priority | Action |
- |---|---|
- | HIGH | Contact Attentive account team to switch link-wrapping from HTTP to HTTPS at the platform level |
- | MEDIUM | Add an explicit `a { color: <brand-blue>; text-decoration: underline; }` rule scoped to the body content area, or style each link CTA inline, to restore link affordance |
- | LOW | Set `<title>Your cart expires soon!</title>` to match subject |
- | LOW | Re-submit full HTML (untruncated) to confirm `alt` attributes, unsubscribe block, and physical address are present |

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard cart abandonment email served via Attentive's platform (`skechers.attentivemail.com`). The truncated HTML prevents a full compliance and personalization audit, but several infrastructure-level issues are confirmed from the visible source.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[HIGH] All tracked links use HTTP, not HTTPS**
Both confirmed link hrefs use `http://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=...`. Attentive supports HTTPS redirects; sending over plaintext exposes the full tracking URL to MITM interception and will trigger "not secure" warnings in some security-conscious email clients.

```
http://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=u001.LNc6Vor...
```

**[LOW] Destination URLs not auditable**
All links are Attentive-wrapped redirects. The final destination domains and UTM parameters cannot be verified from the source. This is expected behavior, but the redirect chain should be tested end-to-end (section 6).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[MEDIUM] Global link style strips all visual differentiation**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This override in the `<head>` removes underlines and forces link color to match surrounding text across the entire email body. Any link not explicitly styled inline will be visually indistinguishable from body copy — a WCAG 1.4.1 (use of color) failure.

**[LOW] Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Several email clients (Yahoo, older Outlook) surface the `<title>` value in tab bars or accessibility trees. An empty title is a minor gap; setting it to match the subject line is best practice.

**[LOW] No `alt` text confirmed on images**
The HTML is truncated before the `<img>` tags render. Cannot confirm `alt` attributes are present. Given this is a cart email with product images, missing `alt` on product shots breaks the email for image-off rendering.
