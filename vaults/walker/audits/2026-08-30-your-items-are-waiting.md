# Your Items Are Waiting!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-30**
## Executive summary

- Same pink floral D'Lites, front and center, again. That's now the seventh time this week that shoe has landed in my inbox, and I'll say the same thing I said the last six times — it's not, has never been, and will never be my cart. If Skechers actually looked at what I click on they'd know a men's sneaker like the Slip-ins Arch Fit or that Murdock casual style is what gets my attention, not a floral pink sneaker with embroidered flowers on it.
- The one thing that did catch my eye scrolling down was the "20% off" banner and the row of men's options underneath the cart shoe — the Arch Fit Arcade and the Relaxed Fit Altman both look like shoes I'd actually wear. But by the time I got there I'd already mentally checked out because the headline offer up top is for a shoe that isn't mine.
- I'm marking this one as read and moving on. Not forwarding it, not clicking "Complete Your Order" — there's nothing in that cart worth completing.

## What's working

- The 20% off banner with the actual code (UPGRADE) is clear and easy to spot — that's a real, usable offer.
- Further down there are men's shoes in the "more great options" row — the Arch Fit Arcade and the Murdock casual sneaker are both things I'd consider.
- Layout is clean, big readable text, one clear CTA button up top — easy to navigate on my phone.
- "Shop Now, Pay Later" and "Find a Store" options are visible for anyone who wants to check a shoe in person before buying, which I appreciate.

## What's weak

- The cart reminder is, again, a shoe I never put in a cart — pink floral D'Lites, embroidered flowers, doesn't belong anywhere near a 62-year-old guy's inbox.
- This is at least the seventh nearly-identical "your cart" email this week with the same product, same layout, same button copy — it's starting to feel like spam even though it's a brand I know.
- Nothing in the hero section speaks to comfort, arch support, or slip-on ease — the stuff that actually gets me to buy.
- The "Boy's Garage Sneaker" listings in the grid are irrelevant to me — I don't have kids that age shopping through my account.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop sending me a cart reminder for a shoe I never added — if your system can show me the Arch Fit Arcade and Murdock styles further down the email, put one of those at the top instead. That's the single biggest fix.
- 2. Cut the repeat cadence — six nearly-identical emails this week for the same shoe is going to get this whole sender marked as noise, not just this one email.
- 3. Lead with the 20% off "Cozy Fit & Glide Step" offer since that's a real, specific deal — bury the maybe-abandoned-cart shoe below it instead of leading with it.
- 4. Drop kids' sneakers from my recommendation grid — I've never bought children's shoes from them.

## Technical Audit

1. Technical Summary
Email renders on the Attentive (Skechers) email platform but ships with two broken tracked links, no plain-text alternative, and 15 image assets loaded over plain HTTP.

2. Link & Tracking Issues
- Broken link (401): image link for "Image of Men's OG Anunoby Limited Edition: SKX Nexus NYC Blue (Pre-Order) Basket" returns 401 Unauthorized.
- Broken link (400): the "Unsubscribe" link returns 400 Bad Request — this is a compliance-relevant failure, see Section 5.
- Two additional links ("Complete you Order" CTA and "We noticed you noticing us..." link) returned 429 (rate-limited) during probing; status could not be confirmed and should be manually re-verified.
- All links route through Attentive's `skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click` redirect wrapper with per-recipient `upn`/hash tokens — standard click-tracking, no anomalies in structure.
- QA probing coverage: 12 of 38 material links probed (26 skipped per click-likelihood ranking); footer/utility/social links deprioritized.

3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 15 image assets reference `http://image.emails.skechers.com/...` instead of `https://` (header logo, product images, app-store badges, payment/social icons). Non-HTTPS sources are commonly blocked or flagged by mail clients on HTTPS-only pages, causing broken/missing images for recipients.
- Two images missing `alt` text: `021bd784-...gif` and `a6ec5198-...jpg` (the latter also affected by the HTTP issue above). Missing alt text degrades accessibility (screen readers) and fallback rendering when images are blocked.
- The open-tracking pixel (`/wf/open?upn=...`) also has no `alt` attribute, though this is typically inconsequential for a 1x1 tracking pixel.
- Responsive CSS (`@media max-width:620px`) and MSO/Outlook conditional handling (`x-apple-data-detectors`, `mso-hide`) are present and appear standard for this ESP's template generator; no structural issues found in the truncated source.

4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No merge-token or personalization-syntax issues found in the visible HTML (no unresolved `{{ }}`/`*|...|*` placeholders in the truncated source). Full personalization block (product recommendations) is beyond the truncation point and not verifiable.

5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- Unsubscribe link returns HTTP 400 — this is a CAN-SPAM concern: the one-click unsubscribe mechanism is non-functional as tested. Recommend immediate verification, since a broken unsubscribe link is a legal exposure, not just a UX bug.
- Physical mailing address and footer compliance text are beyond the truncated HTML and could not be verified.
- No SPF/DKIM/DMARC header data was provided in this payload — sender authentication cannot be assessed from the given evidence.

6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
