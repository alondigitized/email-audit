- I recognize the Gazelle — it's a real, established sneaker, not some jersey I don't care about.
- The black "SHOP NOW" bar up top is at least a clear, unmissable button.
- Layout is clean, no overlapping text or broken images that I can see.
- "Legendary sneaker" copy at least tries to lean on heritage, which is the one thing that could hook an older shopper.

## What's weak

- Whole email is themed around back-to-school — locker room, backpacks stuffed with school supplies — none of that applies to me.
- Not one word about comfort, fit, arch support, or cushioning — the stuff that actually gets me to buy a shoe.
- No price, no discount, no sale — nothing that tells me why today's the day to act.
- Model is a young guy in shorts and gym socks; doesn't look anything like me or how I dress.
- Second half of the email drops the shoe altogether and just sells backpacks and lunch bags — dead space for me.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split the audience — send the school-supplies stuff to parents and a comfort-and-fit-focused Gazelle email to older, non-parent shoppers like me.
- 2. Put an actual offer in there — a %/$ off or even "as low as $XX" — right now there's no reason to act now versus next month.
- 3. Add one line about the shoe's comfort or all-day wearability — even a heritage sneaker can be pitched on cushioning for guys my age.
- 4. Cut the backpack section entirely from any send that also includes the shoe — it splits focus and wastes the back half of the email on people who aren't shopping backpacks.
- 5. Swap the hero image for a shot that doesn't scream teenager — or at minimum include a pair of legs that isn't locker-room shorts and soccer ball.
