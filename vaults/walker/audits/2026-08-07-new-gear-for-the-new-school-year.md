# New gear for the new school year >>>
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- Here we go again — DSW's second back-to-school blast of the day, and just like yesterday's version, this one's built around backpacks and PTA meetings, not a 62-year-old guy looking for a cushioned walking shoe. Big blue "BACK-TO-SCHOOL KICKS" headline, a kid in a red hoodie striking a pose, a girl in a plaid skirt lacing up sneakers — cute for somebody's mom, not for me.
- That said, DSW at least buried something useful in there for me this time: a "SHOP MEN'S SNEAKERS" button and, further down, a "SHOP BY SIZE" grid with wide-width and wide-calf options. That's the first genuinely relevant thing I've seen from this brand all week — I've got wide feet and that "wide width" callout actually caught my eye scrolling past.
- Would I click? Maybe the men's sneakers button, just to see what's on sale — but I'm not opening this back-to-school theme twice in one day. Wouldn't forward it, wouldn't linger on it. Mark as read and move on.

## What's working

- The "Up to 40% off Big-Deal Brands" banner up top is bold and easy to spot before I even scroll.
- "SHOP MEN'S SNEAKERS" is an actual button in my category, which is more than most of DSW's emails this week have bothered with.
- The "SHOP BY SIZE" grid plus "SHOP WIDE WIDTH" and "SHOP WIDE CALF" buttons speak directly to a fit problem I actually have — that's the kind of detail that gets my attention.
- Clean layout, no broken images or overlapping text this time — a relief after that gap-in-the-header email from yesterday.

## What's weak

- The whole hero section — kids, backpacks, "PTA meetings," "first-day approved" — has nothing to do with me. I scrolled past three separate kid/teen photos before hitting anything relevant.
- This is the second "back-to-school" DSW email today after "Back-to-school-ready kicks" — that's a lot of repetition for one inbox in one day.
- Subject line doesn't tell me anything concrete — "New gear for the new school year" could be about anything, and it's definitely not about me.
- That "Shoe-it-Forward" donation section is nice in theory but it's competing for space with the actual sale, and I had to hunt for the men's stuff underneath it all.
- Wall of fine print at the bottom — offer details, exclusions, barcode — feels like the usual retail small print dump rather than a clean, honest offer up front.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split the send list — put men's/comfort shoppers in a separate email that leads with the men's sneaker sale and the wide-width options, not backpacks. That size/width info is genuinely good, don't bury it below three screens of kids.
- 2. Cut the back-to-school theme entirely for a segment like me — I don't have kids in school, and DSW's been hammering this angle two days running.
- 3. Lead with the wide-width/wide-calf callout higher up for anyone who's clicked "men's" before — that's a real differentiator DSW has and it's hidden near the bottom.
- 4. Put an actual percentage or dollar figure in the subject line instead of vague "new gear" language.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Men's comfort sneakers, up to 40% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Wide width now in stock — 25% off today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: wide width and wide calf sizes, in stock now`
