# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 4/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-10-07**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I got through the front door of Skechers.com on my phone and found my way to a men's shoe, but I never got a pair into a cart. The home page, menu, Men's Shoes page and search all load. They're readable and mostly easy to tap. The cart step ended on a completely blank white screen, and the cart badge read 0 the whole way through. As a shopper I'd have given up there, or gone to Hoka or Famous Footwear.

The shoes I'm looking for are on the site. "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit" and "Arch Fit" are the first things I see, and "Hands Free Slip-Ins" is the first tile on the men's shoe page. But the checkout path isn't dependable, and the login step showed me nothing at all.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**4/10**, up from 3/10 yesterday.

I only have yesterday's score, not its screenshots. I can't name specific regressions or fixes. This score has to be read against today's evidence: the front half of the journey is solid and the back half doesn't work.

## 3. What's Working

- **Header and navigation.** The logo, search, account, cart and MENU are all big enough to tap, and the "MENU" label helps. The Men menu has large rows with arrows and plenty of space between them: Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections, and so on. It's clear and easy on thumbs.
- **My kind of shoe is up front.** The top strip has "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Arch Fit" and "Max Cushioning." The Men's Shoes page opens with "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes." That's the comfort, hands-free and walking content I came for.
- **Product page basics.** The name is clear ("GO 3D Arch Fit – 3D AirNex Clog"), the $80.00 price is large, and the size grid is big and easy to hit. I like the strikethrough on the sold-out size.
- **Search works.** "Comfort shoes" returned 1,579 results, and the first ones are things like "Arch Comfort" and "Cozy Fit Luxe Comfort." Prices are clear and the BOGO 50% note is repeated.
- **Text size.** I could read it without pinching to zoom.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart is broken, or at least looks broken.** After "Add to Cart" the cart page was totally blank, and the badge said 0. No message, no spinner, no "your cart is empty." Even if this is a slow load, a blank page loses the sale.
- **The add-to-cart step showed an error.** The screen said "Please select a size" in red and the cart still showed 0. That's reasonable behaviour, but nothing in the flow chose a size. The step shows no confirmation of an item being added.
- **Login showed nothing.** The "Log In" screenshot is the same home page as before, with no sign-in form or account screen. I never saw a logged-in state, so I can't say anything felt personalized to me. The "Sign in for free shipping & returns!" line near Add to Cart pushes login on me, which isn't a good look for a first-time shopper.
- **The pop-up step shows nothing.** It looks identical to the home page, so there was nothing to dismiss. That's fine, but it adds no value.
- **No comparison or price-vs-quality help.** I cross-shop Hoka, Brooks and New Balance, and I saw no comparison, no cushioning or arch ratings, and no "which one is right for me" guide. The only quality signal I saw was "1 Review" on the clog.
- **The product I landed on wasn't my kind of shoe.** It's a white, open-mesh, unisex clog with a single review. That's not a confident choice for a 62-year-old man buying casual walking shoes.
- **Search results lean women's.** The first two results are both labeled "Women's." I'm a man searching "comfort shoes," so I'd expect men's at least mixed in.
- **Too many promos.** There are three at once in the header ("BOGO 50% off," "25% off Apparel & Bags," and "Buy 1, Get 1 50% OFF! Applied at Cart"). They push the shoes down the screen. "Applied at Cart" also means I can't see my real price until a cart that doesn't work.
- **Clutter on the shoe list.** 754 results with a Filter and a Sort but little guidance.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart.** It should show items, or at least an "empty cart" message with a way back. Never serve a blank white page.
2. **Show clear add-to-cart feedback.** Confirm "Added to cart" and update the badge. Disable or flag Add to Cart until a size is chosen, with the error placed right next to the size grid.
3. **Make sure login works on mobile.** The account icon should lead somewhere visible, and a logged-in state should greet me by name and show my size and past orders.
4. **Show the price I'll actually pay.** Put the BOGO price in the listing, not just "Applied at Cart."
5. **Add comfort proof.** Arch support and cushioning ratings, a "walking all day" tag, and a plain comparison against other comfort brands.
6. **Filter search by men's by default** when I've been browsing the Men's section.
7. **Trim the promo stack** to one message so the shoes come first.
8. **Feature a better default product.** A proven slip-in or arch-support style with plenty of reviews would build more trust than a single-review clog.

## 6. Bottom Line

The shop window is good, and the shoes I want are easy to find. But the till doesn't work. I'd browse here, and I'd probably buy the same shoe somewhere I could check out. Until the cart works, this is a 4: a slight improvement on yesterday, but still not a site I can buy from.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The journey completed, but the data is limited in three ways:
- **Missing metrics:** LCP and CLS were not captured on any step (all `?`), so there is no Core Web Vitals evidence.
- **Bot-protection blocks:** Kasada (`x-kpsdk`) bot-protection requests were rate limited with 429s, and `/cart/` itself returned 429. The View Cart step was probably an error or interstitial page, not the real cart.
- **Repeated page captures:** The Homepage, Log In and Men category samples show the same URL (`/`) and identical truncated HTML. Those steps may not have navigated, or the capture was stale. Treat their per-step results with caution.

Axe reported 18 violations: 2 critical, 3 serious, 11 moderate and 2 minor. Most are concentrated on the Homepage and View Cart.

## Accessibility

**Homepage**
- `button-name` [critical]: at least one button has no accessible name.
- `aria-required-parent` [critical]: an element with a role that needs a specific parent (e.g. `listitem`, `menuitem`, `tab`) is not inside one.
- `link-name` [serious]: at least one link has no discernible text.
- `aria-allowed-role` [minor]: a role is applied to an element that does not allow it.
- `heading-order` [moderate]: headings skip levels.
- Landmark structure [moderate]: `landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` and `landmark-unique` all failed. A nested or duplicate `<main>` is likely, along with unlabeled repeated landmarks.
- `region` [moderate]: some content sits outside any landmark.

**View Cart**
- `document-title` and `html-has-lang` [serious]: the document has no `<title>` and no `lang`. The homepage has `<html lang="en">`, so this document is not the normal site template. It is consistent with the 429 response to `/cart/`.
- `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one` [moderate]: there is no `<main>` and no `<h1>`.

**Search "comfort shoes"**
- Same `aria-allowed-role`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region` failures as the Homepage. This points to the shared header/footer template.

Axe results are not present for the Log In, Men category, Men > Shoes, Product detail or Add to Cart steps. These are either clean or were not scanned.

## SEO

- **View Cart:** the missing `<title>` and `<h1>` come from the 429 response. If bots or crawlers get this response, it is an indexability risk. Cart pages should normally be `noindex` anyway.
- **GTM preview environment:** the homepage loads GTM with `gtm_preview=env-1&gtm_auth=…&gtm_cookies_win=x`. That is a non-live GTM environment snippet running on the public production site. It can fire unpublished tags and skew analytics.
- **Structured data and canonical tags:** the truncated HTML samples do not show canonical tags, meta descriptions or JSON-LD, so these could not be evaluated.

## Performance

- **Web Vitals:** no LCP or CLS data was captured on any step.
- **TTFB:**
  - Homepage 490ms, Log In 430ms and Men category 430ms are acceptable but not fast. They are within the "good" threshold (under 800ms).
  - Men > Shoes, Product detail, Add to Cart and Search show 26–28ms, which is implausibly low for a document load. This suggests client-side route changes, cache hits or a service worker, not full navigations.
  - View Cart at 111ms is the 429 response, not real cart performance.
- **Third-party load:** the homepage `<head>` injects more than 20 third-party scripts. These include GTM, Monetate (two scripts), TikTok (three), Pinterest, Bing UET, Yahoo (`ytc.js`), Quantum Metric, Bloomreach (`brsrvr`), Impact, RTB House (`creativecdn`), go2sdk/Tune, Pulse Insights, Google gtag (three destinations) and WebFont Loader from cdnjs.
  - They are marked `async`, but they add main-thread, network and CPU contention.
  - `s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` is injected twice, so the Yahoo tag is duplicated.
- **Font loading:** web fonts are loaded through the JS WebFont Loader from a third-party CDN (cdnjs). This adds a dependency and delays font activation until the loader runs. The `wf-mulish-*-active` classes confirm the fonts did load.
- **Back/forward cache:** the repeated `Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed` errors mean third-party code still registers `unload` handlers. These handlers are deprecated and block bfcache eligibility, which hurts back-navigation speed.

## Mobile Optimization

- **Viewport:** the root element has an inline `--vh: 6.64px`. The value is implausibly small, consistent with a very short viewport or a `window.innerHeight` of about 664px measured during an automated run. The JS `--vh` workaround for mobile browser chrome can cause layout shifts on resize or scroll. CLS was not captured, so this cannot be confirmed.
- **Viewport meta tag and touch targets:** these are not visible in the truncated samples, so they could not be assessed.
- **Run caveat:** the 26–28ms TTFBs and the 429s suggest the run was affected by automation, so mobile behavior may not match a real device.

## Console & Network Errors

The report lists 30 console errors but shows only 10 samples. All of them fall into four types:
- **429 on Kasada fingerprint endpoint** (5 of 6 network errors): `…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.864`. The bot-defense client is being rate limited. It also has `net::ERR_FAILED` console entries, which look like the paired failed requests. If this happens to real users, it can block the Kasada token and cause downstream failures such as the cart 429.
- **429 on `https://www.skechers.com/cart/`:** the cart document itself was rate limited or blocked, so the View Cart step did not reach the real cart.
- **`Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document`** (4 samples): a third-party script is registering `unload` handlers. These are blocked by the document's Permissions-Policy.
- **`Unsupported Summarizer API languages…`** (2 samples): a script calls Chrome's built-in Summarizer API with unsupported language options. The request is aborted and the error is harmless to users, but it is noise and may come from a third-party or experimental script.

## Recommendations

1. **Re-run the audit** with LCP/CLS capture fixed, and confirm each step actually navigates. Steps 1–3 appear to share a URL and the same HTML.
2. **Investigate the Kasada 429s.** Check whether the `/fp` rate limit and the `/cart/` 429 affect real users or only automated traffic, and allowlist the test runner if it is only the latter.
3. **Fix the Homepage critical issues:**
   - Add accessible names to unnamed buttons and links.
   - Give role-dependent elements their required parent roles.
4. **Fix the landmark structure.** Use one top-level `<main>`, move any `<aside>` to the top level, and label repeated landmarks (e.g. `aria-label` on each `<nav>`).
5. **Fix heading order** on the Homepage.
6. **Make sure the cart error and interstitial responses** include `<title>`, `lang`, `<main>` and an `<h1>`.
7. **Remove the GTM preview environment parameters** (`gtm_preview`, `gtm_auth`, `gtm_cookies_win`) from the production snippet.
8. **Deduplicate the Yahoo `ytc.js` tag** and audit the third-party tags. Consider consolidating through GTM, deferring non-essential ones and removing unused ones.
9. **Remove `unload` listeners** (or have vendors update their tags) to restore bfcache eligibility. Use `pagehide` instead.
10. **Self-host or preload the web fonts** instead of loading them through the cdnjs WebFont Loader, or switch to CSS `@font-face` with `font-display`.
11. **Replace the JS-set `--vh` with the CSS `dvh`/`svh` units** if it is only used for viewport height.
