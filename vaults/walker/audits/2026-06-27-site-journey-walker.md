# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 4/10 (prev 4/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-27**
## Full review
---

## 1. Executive Summary

I'm Walker Miles, 62, and I just tried to buy a pair of Skechers Slip-ins on my phone. The site got me to the right product — Max Cushioning, Arch Fit, Hands-Free Slip-ins, exactly what I came for. But then it fell apart. The cart page loaded completely blank. Nothing there. I'd already picked my size, I'd already decided this was my shoe, and I hit a wall. On top of that, logging in didn't seem to do anything — no "Welcome back, Walker," no saved preferences, nothing. And when I searched "comfort shoes," the first results I saw were Women's. I'm a 62-year-old man who just logged in. That's not a small miss.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

Same score as yesterday. Navigation into the right shoe category improved slightly, but the blank cart is a complete conversion killer — and it's a regression from what a working checkout should do. The search gender mismatch and invisible logged-in state drag it down further. Nothing improved enough to move the needle.

---

## 3. What's Working

**BOGO deal is impossible to miss.** The "BOGO 50% OFF Applied at Cart" banner is pinned at the very top, every single screen. As someone who watches price, that's reassuring. I know the deal is there when I'm ready to pull the trigger.

**"Hands Free Slip-ins" is right in the menu.** When I opened Men, I could see "Hands Free Slip-ins" as a direct link without drilling through subcategories. That's exactly the phrase I think in. It felt like the menu was written for me.

**The product page headline is clear and readable.** "Skechers Slip-ins: Max Cushioning Arch Fit 2.0 - Azusah" — big text, hits all three of my keywords in the title. Four stars, 51 reviews, price upfront. The "Members Get Buy 1, Get 1 50% OFF in Cart" loyalty call-out right below the price is a smart placement.

**Width and size buttons are large enough to tap.** The MEDIUM / EXTRA WIDE buttons and the size grid are well-spaced. At 62, I'm not squinting or fat-fingering those. That's better than most sites.

**Comfort Technologies is its own menu section.** I noticed it in the Men menu. If I'd tapped that, I might have found arch support content. Good that it exists even if I didn't go deep on it.

---

## 4. What's Weak

**Cart page loaded blank. White screen. Nothing.** This is the worst thing on the site. I picked size 10.5, I was ready to buy, and the cart was empty — visually and functionally. The bag icon still showed "0." I don't know if my item is in there or not. I'm not going to sit there refreshing. I'm going to go to DSW.

**Login showed no sign it worked.** Steps 1 and 3 look identical. Same homepage, same hero image, no "Hi Walker," no "Your Rewards," no browsing history, no recommendations. If I'm logged in, I can't tell. That kills any loyalty value — I signed up, I gave them my info, and they're treating me like a stranger.

**The "Please select a size" error showed even after 10.5 was highlighted.** I can see in the screenshot that 10.5 has a visible selection border, but the red warning is still there saying I haven't selected a size. That's confusing. Did I tap it or not? Am I going to be charged for the wrong size? I'd second-guess myself.

**Search returned Women's shoes first.** I searched "comfort shoes." I'm a logged-in man. The first two visible results — both Women's. No men's product in the first viewport. That's a personalization failure at the most basic level.

**Homepage hero is a young woman in colorful art shoes.** The Britto collaboration is clearly aimed at a different customer. I'm looking for max cushioning and arch support. Nothing in that hero image speaks to me. The horizontal nav bar (Slip-ins, Sandals, Arch Fit, BOBs) is useful but small and secondary to an image that isn't for me.

**318 results with no comfort pre-filter.** The Hands Free Slip-ins category landing page dumps me into 318 results with only fit-width filters (Wide, Extra Wide, Medium). No way to quickly sort by arch support level or cushioning rating. That's a lot of scrolling on a phone.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart.** This is priority one. A blank cart page is a conversion black hole. Whatever broke between "Add to Cart" and the cart render needs to be fixed before anything else matters.

2. **Show logged-in state visibly.** Put "Hi Walker" or even just a name initial in the account icon area. Surface points balance and last order. Right now login feels broken.

3. **Gender-filter search results by account profile.** I'm logged in as a male customer. "Comfort shoes" should default to Men's in the first visible results.

4. **Fix the size validation error.** If 10.5 is highlighted, clear the warning. The conflict between visual selection state and validation message is a UX bug that creates doubt at the worst possible moment.

5. **Add a comfort/arch-support filter to the category page.** 318 results need narrowing. A "Arch Fit," "Max Cushioning," or "All-Day Comfort" filter chip at the top would get me to the right 30 shoes fast.

6. **Lead the homepage with something that speaks to 50+ comfort shoppers.** Even a rotating hero or a secondary module — "Engineered for all-day comfort" — would signal I'm in the right place before I open the menu.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

Skechers has the right product for me. The Max Cushioning Arch Fit Slip-ins are exactly what I'd buy. The path to find it is workable. But the execution on the back half — login, cart, search — is broken in ways that would lose me as a buyer today. A blank cart after I've committed to a $135 shoe is not a hiccup, that's a lost sale. Score: **4/10**, unchanged from yesterday. The navigation side is marginally better; the cart failure cancels it out.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Platform: Salesforce Commerce Cloud (Demandware). Heavy third-party tag stack (20+ async scripts per page: GTM, TikTok pixel, Pinterest, Reddit, Bing, Snapchat, Facebook, Yahoo, Taboola, Nextdoor, Monetate, Quantum Metric, Bloomreach, Pulse Insights, Tune/Go2SDK, Stylitics, Impact). Bot-detection layer is HUMAN Security (formerly PerimeterX) via UUID-keyed fingerprint endpoint. LCP and CLS were not captured, leaving the Web Vitals picture materially incomplete.

---

## Accessibility

**17 violations across 3 pages.**

**Homepage (9 violations):**
- `aria-required-parent` [critical] — ARIA role children exist outside required parent context. Violates WCAG 2.1 SC 1.3.1.
- `button-name` [critical] — One or more buttons have no accessible text. Fails WCAG 2.1 SC 4.1.2; keyboard and screen reader users cannot operate these controls.
- `landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-no-duplicate-main` [moderate] — Multiple `<main>` elements or `<main>` nested inside another landmark. Indicates templating error or injected widget wrapping page content.
- `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique`, `region`, `heading-order`, `aria-allowed-role` [minor/moderate] — Structural landmark and heading violations consistent with ad-tech or personalization widget injection overriding page structure.

**View Cart (4 violations — highest severity):**
- `document-title` [serious] — `<title>` is empty or absent. This strongly suggests the cart is rendered as a client-side modal/overlay without a proper HTML document shell, or SPA navigation is not updating the document head.
- `html-has-lang` [serious] — `lang` attribute absent. Contradicts the homepage `<html lang="en">`, confirming the cart view is a different document context or a broken SPA route.
- `landmark-one-main`, `page-has-heading-one` [moderate] — No `<main>` or `<h1>`, consistent with the modal/frameless rendering hypothesis.

**Search results (4 violations):**
- `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique`, `region` [moderate] — Same widget injection pattern as homepage.
- `aria-allowed-role` [minor]

## SEO

- Missing `<title>` on the cart page means Googlebot sees a titleless document if it crawls that URL. Low direct impact (cart is typically noindexed) but signals a structural defect in head management.
- Missing `lang` on cart same issue.
- HUMAN Security fingerprint URL pattern (`/149e9513-01fa.../fp`) generates 429s; if Googlebot hits rate limits on JS execution it will soft-render the page incorrectly.
- Duplicate `tags.creativecdn.com` script injection observed in Men category HTML — two identical `<script>` tags for the same resource. Could produce double-fired conversion events affecting attribution data quality.

Login and Men category are 10–13× slower than other pages. Likely causes: server-side session/auth check on Login, and a CDN cache miss on the Men category (possibly personalization or A/B test preventing caching). These TTFB values are the floor for LCP, so uncaptured LCP on these steps is expected to be meaningfully worse.

**Script load:**
- 20+ third-party `<script async>` tags injected into `<head>` on every page load. Each represents a separate DNS lookup, connection, and parse cycle on a mobile connection.
- Monetate loads two scripts (`se.monetate.net/js/3/...custom.js` and `se.monetate.net/js/2/...entry.js`) — the `custom.js` variant has a `charset` attribute but no explicit `async` on the `se.` subdomain version, risking parser block.
- WebFontLoader (`webfontloader.js`) loads 6 Mulish weight variants (n3–n8). Font variants are applied via JS class injection (`wf-mulish-n5-active` etc.), meaning text remains in fallback font until JS executes.
- LCP and CLS not captured — audit cannot confirm Web Vitals compliance without these values. This is a gap in the instrumentation, not the site.

## Mobile Optimization

- `--vh: 6.640000000000001px` CSS custom property set inline on `<html>` — this is a JavaScript-computed viewport height fix for the iOS Safari address bar shrink bug. The floating-point imprecision (`6.640000000000001` vs `6.64`) is harmless but indicates the value is set without `toFixed()` rounding. If this variable is used in layout calculations it may cause subpixel rendering artifacts on some devices.
- No mobile-specific issues visible from HTML source beyond the third-party script weight common to both desktop and mobile.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 rate limits on HUMAN Security fingerprint endpoint (5 occurrences):**
```
/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39.../fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.513
```
This is the PerimeterX/HUMAN Security client-side challenge token endpoint. Repeated 429s mean the bot-detection service is rate-limiting the client session. On a legitimate mobile browser this should not happen — the volume of retries suggests either the SDK is in a retry loop, or the service is miscalibrated and treating the automated audit session as a bot (expected for synthetic tests). **However, the cart page itself returned 429:**

```
429 https://www.skechers.com/cart/
```
If this occurs for real users, it means the cart page is blocked by the bot-detection layer — a direct conversion funnel break. This is the highest-priority network finding.

**CORS error on Stylitics:**
```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?...' 
blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```
The Stylitics CDN is not sending `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` (or wildcard) on this stylesheet. The subsequent `ERR_FAILED` is the browser aborting the resource load. Stylitics-powered outfit/hotspot widgets will render without their styles. This is a vendor CDN misconfiguration — fix requires Stylitics to add the CORS header on their asset CDN.

## Recommendations

**P0 — Conversion impact:**
1. Investigate cart 429: determine whether `skechers.com/cart/` rate-limits real users or only synthetic/bot sessions. If real users are affected, adjust HUMAN Security allow-listing for authenticated sessions.

**P1 — Accessibility (WCAG legal exposure):**
2. Fix `button-name` violations on homepage — add `aria-label` or visible text to all icon-only buttons.
3. Fix `aria-required-parent` — audit the component(s) rendering orphaned ARIA role children.
4. Fix View Cart document shell — ensure SPA navigation updates `<title>`, `<html lang>`, a `<main>` landmark, and an `<h1>` when the cart view is activated.

**P2 — Third-party/infrastructure:**
5. Remove duplicate `tags.creativecdn.com` script injection from Men category (templating bug in the tag manager or server-side include).
6. File ticket with Stylitics to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` header on `web-assets.stylitics.com`.
7. Investigate Login and Men category TTFB spike (385ms) — check whether Akamai/Fastly caching is disabled on these routes due to personalization headers, and whether session validation can be deferred.

**P3 — Instrumentation:**
8. LCP and CLS were not captured in this audit. Instrument Real User Monitoring (Quantum Metric is already loaded) or a synthetic runner that records Core Web Vitals per step before reporting compliance.
9. WebFontLoader adds JS-dependent font rendering — evaluate switching to `<link rel="preload">` + `@font-face` CSS to eliminate the FOUT dependency on JS execution.
