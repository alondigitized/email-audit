# Enjoy 20% off select styles
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-28**
## Executive summary

- **From:** New Balance Offers
- **Subject:** Enjoy 20% off select styles
- Well, I must've signed up for their emails at some point — maybe when I was poking around for walking shoes. The "Thanks for signing up" banner at the top at least explains why I got this. And 20% off sounds good on paper. But when I scroll down to see what they're recommending for me? Skate shoes. Skate shoes and jogger pants. I'm 62 years old. I don't skate. The algorithm or whatever they're using picked the absolute wrong stuff for me, and that killed whatever excitement the discount built up. I'll be honest — I almost didn't make it past the product grid.
- The offer is real, the design is clean enough, but the product recommendations are so far off from who I am that the conversion potential just evaporates. A 20% welcome discount is a good hook — New Balance threw it away by pointing me at skate shoes.

## What's working

- **The 20% off is front and center.** Big text, easy to read, right under the hero. I saw it immediately and it got my attention. That's the right call — lead with the deal.
- **The design is clean and loads fast.** Simple layout, nothing cluttered. On my phone it didn't feel jumbled. Big enough images to see the products clearly.
- **Welcome context is clear.** "Thanks for signing up" tells me right away this is a welcome email and the discount is for being a new subscriber. I appreciate when emails explain why they're showing up in my inbox.
- **"Shop now" button is easy to tap.** Nice and big. My thumb found it without squinting.

## What's weak

- **The product recommendations are completely wrong for me.** NB Numeric 272, Franky Villani 417, Tiago Lemos 808 Lite — those are skate shoes named after skateboarders I've never heard of. I'm a 62-year-old man looking for comfortable everyday shoes with good support. Nobody at New Balance seems to know anything about me as a customer.
- **No comfort, no walking, no support messaging anywhere.** New Balance makes excellent walking shoes — that's literally why older guys like me consider them. None of that is in this email. Not a word about cushioning, arch support, or anything I actually care about.
- **The lifestyle photo feels like it's aimed at people half my age.** The couple in the bottom image look like they're in their 30s, wearing trendy streetwear. I don't see myself in that picture at all.
- **The survey at the bottom feels tacked on.** I just got here. I haven't even bought anything yet and they're already asking me to take a survey? That feels backwards. Win my business first.
- **Fine print under the CTA is tiny.** I had to lean in to read "Must click through email to qualify for offer." On my phone that would've been nearly impossible without pinching to zoom. If there are strings attached to the deal, I need to be able to read those strings.

- 3. **Move the survey to a post-purchase flow.** Don't lead with a feedback request before I've even clicked anything. It makes the brand seem scattered.
- 4. **Increase the fine print size.** Legal-speak about offer conditions needs to be legible. If I can't read it without zooming, I'm going to distrust the whole offer.
- 5. **Use an image that matches my demographic.** At minimum, feature a male model over 50 somewhere in the email. I'd feel a lot more seen.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`.bg1` class defined twice with conflicting values:**

```css
/* first declaration — line ~9 */
.bg1 { background-color: #ffffff; }

/* second declaration — line ~66 */
.bg1 { background: #58546d; }
```

The second rule wins in all clients. Any element using `.bg1` expecting white (`#ffffff`) will render as muted purple (`#58546d`). This is a copy-paste regression from a previous template variant.

**`@font-face` rules have no `src:` property:**

All nine `@font-face` blocks declare `font-family`, `font-weight`, and `font-style` but omit `src:`. Web fonts will silently fall back to the stack fallbacks (`Arial`, `Times New Roman`, `serif`). This is functional but means custom typography never loads — even in clients that support it (Apple Mail, iOS Mail).

One block has a duplicate `font-family` property:
```css
@font-face {
  font-family: proxima-nova,sans-serif;
  font-weight: 700;
  font-style: italic;
  font-family: proxima-nova,sans-serif;  /* duplicate — parse error */
}
```

**`<meta>` tag missing `http-equiv` attribute:**

```html
<meta content="IE=edge" />
```

Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
```

Without `http-equiv`, this tag is inert. Minor — IE/Edge rendering engine targeting is legacy, but the intent is nullified.

**`<title>` contains template boilerplate:**

```html
<title>New Balance Template</title>
```

Clients that surface `<title>` as the tab label or in accessibility trees (e.g., Outlook desktop, some screen readers) will display "New Balance Template" rather than a meaningful subject or brand string.
