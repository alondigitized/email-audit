# Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Date:** July 2, 2026 | **Yesterday's Score:** 4/10

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Skechers.com holds together on the browse side — the Men's nav drawer is clean, the Slip-ins category page delivers the right product type, and the product detail page communicates price and the BOGO deal clearly. But two showstopper failures drag the experience back down: the cart page rendered as a completely blank white screen (a critical checkout blocker), and a "comfort shoes" search served me Women's products first despite being a logged-in male shopper. I never felt like the site knew who I was. For a 62-year-old who just wants to find a comfortable slip-on and buy it with minimal friction, this is still not a reliable shopping experience.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**4/10** — No change from yesterday. Navigation improvements are real but the blank cart is an existential conversion failure, and the search personalization miss compounds it.

---

## 3. What's Working

- **BOGO banner is impossible to miss.** The "BOGO 50% OFF Applied at Cart" sticky bar at the very top is prominent, readable, and follows me through every page. Good for a deal-oriented shopper like me.
- **Homepage quick-links are well chosen.** Slip-ins, Sandals, Arch Fit, BOBs — those four shortcuts above the hero image hit exactly the categories I browse. "Slip-ins" especially stands out; I can tap it without hunting through menus.
- **Men's nav drawer is excellent.** Clean full-screen slide-out with large text, well-spaced rows, and a "Hands Free Slip-ins" shortcut visible right in the quick-links area below the main categories. Touch targets feel generous. A 62-year-old thumb can navigate this without squinting or missing.
- **Slip-ins category page communicates fit options immediately.** Three big blue buttons — Wide Fit, Extra Wide Fit, Medium Fit — appear right at the top. That matters to me. Fit is the whole game.
- **Product detail page text is readable.** The product name, $120.00 price, star rating, and Members BOGO offer are all large enough to read without zooming. The patriotic shoe imagery tied to the USA soccer campaign is visually engaging.
- **Size selection has a clear error state.** The red "Please select a size" warning with a caution icon is unambiguous. I know exactly what I need to do before I can proceed.
- **ADD TO CART button is massive and blue.** Full-width, high-contrast, hard to miss. That's right.

---

## 4. What's Weak

- **Cart page is completely blank.** Step 8 is a white void. Nothing. No cart items, no totals, no checkout button — just white screen. This is catastrophic. If I added a shoe to cart and landed on a blank page, I would assume the site broke and leave. This is a direct revenue loss event.
- **Login produced no visible change.** Steps 1, 2, and 3 all show the exact same homepage screenshot. There's no indication I'm logged in — no "Welcome, Walker" greeting, no account name in the nav, no personalized offer. The account icon looks the same before and after. I can't tell if login even worked.
- **Search for "comfort shoes" surfaced Women's products.** 1,414 results, and the first two cards I see are both labeled "Women's." I'm a male shopper who went through the Men's nav. This is a basic personalization failure. I'd quickly lose confidence the site understood my needs.
- **Hero image doesn't speak to my priorities.** The "USA TAKES THE MATCH" soccer-themed hero is fine for a campaign, but it's sporty and young-skewing. Nothing above the fold speaks to comfort, cushioning, arch support, or the everyday walking use case that actually drives my purchase intent.
- **Only 3 reviews on the featured product.** The Glide-Step Altus - Earned Stripes shows 5 stars but only 3 reviews. That's not enough social proof for a $120 shoe. I cross-shop; I want to see 50+ reviews before I trust a product at that price.
- **No comfort specs on the product listing above the fold.** I see the name, price, and BOGO deal on Step 6 — but no mention of cushioning technology, arch support, or what makes this shoe comfortable. That information may exist further down, but I'm not told to look for it.
- **Skechers Plus prompt during checkout feels intrusive.** Between the size selector and the Add to Cart button sits a "Join for Free Shipping, Free Returns" Skechers Plus upsell. I already have items in cart. Don't make me think about a loyalty program right before I checkout.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Investigate and fix the blank cart immediately.** This is priority one. A blank cart means zero conversions from this session. Test across devices; if it's a JavaScript error, fix before anything else.

2. **Surface gender preference in search results.** A logged-in male shopper searching "comfort shoes" should see Men's products first. Apply gender filter automatically based on account profile or browsing session. If this requires a fallback for guests, show a "Showing results for: All / Men / Women" toggle at the top of results.

3. **Add a logged-in state indicator.** After login, show a name or greeting in the nav ("Hi, Walker" or a filled account icon). Right now the site feels anonymous even after I sign in.

4. **Add comfort feature callouts to the product card tile.** Below the product name in the listing grid, add 1–2 comfort technology badges (Air-Cooled Memory Foam, Arch Fit, etc.). This is a purchase driver for my demographic and it's missing from the browse experience.

5. **Swap or supplement the hero for comfort messaging.** A USA sports tie-in is fine as a secondary banner, but lead with something that speaks to the core buyer: walking comfort, all-day wear, slip-in convenience. "No-Bend Entry. All-Day Comfort." over a lifestyle image of someone my age doing daily errands would resonate more.

6. **Increase the review threshold before featuring a product.** Don't promote a $120 shoe with 3 reviews. Either pull in more review content or feature products with deeper social proof.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

The bones are decent — Skechers has clearly put work into mobile navigation, and the Men's slip-ins funnel is logical. But the blank cart is a critical failure that renders the purchase path broken, and the search personalization miss means even a well-targeted search query can't close the sale. I'm no more confident today than yesterday that Skechers.com can reliably convert me. Same score: **4/10**. Fix the cart, and this moves to a 6 overnight.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Site runs on Salesforce Commerce Cloud (SFCC), identifiable from `/on/demandware.static/Sites-USSkechers-Site/` paths. The mobile journey has three compounding issues: a bot-protection layer (Kasada) that is rate-limiting its own fingerprinting calls, a View Cart page that fails to update core document metadata (title, lang), and an extremely heavy third-party script payload on every page with several duplicate registrations.

---

## Accessibility

**18 violations across 3 pages.**

**Homepage (10 violations):**
- `button-name` [critical] — one or more buttons have no text or `aria-label`. Likely icon-only buttons (hamburger menu, search, wishlist).
- `aria-required-parent` [critical] — ARIA role used outside its required parent container. Likely a listbox or grid item rendered outside its parent role.
- `link-name` [serious] — icon-only anchor tags with no accessible text and no `aria-label`.
- `landmark-no-duplicate-main` / `landmark-main-is-top-level` [moderate] — multiple `<main>` elements exist simultaneously, likely from a modal or overlay injected without removing the existing main.
- `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` [moderate] — `<aside>` nested inside another landmark (`<main>` or `<section>`).
- `heading-order` [moderate] — heading hierarchy is non-sequential (e.g., h1 → h3).
- `landmark-unique` / `region` [moderate] — duplicate landmark roles, and content outside any landmark.
- `aria-allowed-role` [minor] — role value invalid for the element type.

**View Cart (4 violations — most severe):**
- `document-title` [serious] — `<title>` is empty or missing. The cart is either a full-page SPA transition that doesn't update `document.title`, or a modal rendered in an iframe without a title.
- `html-has-lang` [serious] — `<html>` element has no `lang` attribute. Confirms cart is rendered in a separate frame or document context that doesn't inherit the parent page's `lang="en"`.
- `landmark-one-main` / `page-has-heading-one` [moderate] — no `<main>` and no `<h1>`, consistent with a bare-bones embedded cart view.

**Search page (4 violations):** Subset of homepage issues — duplicate landmarks, content outside regions, misapplied `aria-allowed-role`.

---

## SEO

- **View Cart missing `<title>` and `lang`:** If `/cart/` is a distinct URL (not a modal), it is unindexable as-is and will not pass Core Web Vitals assessment in Search Console. The missing `lang` also breaks hreflang association.
- **No structural SEO issues visible** on homepage or category pages from the HTML sample — `lang="en"` and document structure appear correct there.
- **Heading order violations on homepage** affect crawlability of content hierarchy but are not blocking.

**Third-party script bloat (visible in HTML head):**
- 20+ third-party scripts loaded per page, including: GTM (×3 destinations), TikTok Pixel (×2 scripts), Monetate (×2 — both `/js/2/` and `/js/3/` endpoints loaded simultaneously), Yahoo pixel (`ytc.js` registered **twice** on all pages), Snapchat, Reddit, Stapecdn, Facebook, Nextdoor, Taboola, QuantumMetric, Bloomreach, PulseInsights, Impact, go2sdk/Tune, Stylitics, WebFontLoader.
- All are `async` but they still compete for network bandwidth, parse CPU, and contribute to TBT.
- **Monetate dual-load** (`/js/2/` and `/js/3/`) is a clear misconfiguration — one of these is redundant and doubles Monetate's network cost.
- **Yahoo pixel double-load** (`vice-managed-src="https://s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js"` appears twice in the same document) means the pixel fires twice per page.
- **6 Mulish font weights** loaded via WebFontLoader — `wf-mulish-n3` through `wf-mulish-n8`. Each is a separate request. FOUT is being managed via JS class toggling (`wf-active`) but the load is not preloaded.

---

## Mobile Optimization

- **`--vh` CSS variable** (`style="--vh: 6.640000000000001px"`) is the standard iOS Safari 100vh workaround. Value of 6.64px × 100 = 664px visible viewport height — correct pattern, properly applied via JS on load/resize.
- **`vice-managed-src` attribute** is used on multiple scripts (Pinterest, Impact, Facebook, Nextdoor, Taboola, Yahoo) — this is a custom consent-management lazy-load pattern. Scripts with this attribute are held until consent is granted. Works as intended but means the consent layer controls load order, not just presence.
- No viewport meta tag visible in the truncated HTML samples, but its absence from the snippet is not conclusive — it is almost certainly present in the non-truncated `<head>`.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 Rate Limiting — Kasada bot protection (5 network errors, 6 console errors):**

All 429s hit the same endpoint pattern:
```
/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.522
```
This is the **Kasada fingerprint collection endpoint** (bot protection). The client SDK is polling this endpoint and being rate-limited by its own server. This indicates either: (a) the session triggered Kasada's suspicious-traffic threshold and was soft-blocked, or (b) the SDK is retrying too aggressively on error. In a real user session, this manifests as failed bot-protection handshakes which can cascade to block legitimate API calls — most likely the proximate cause of the **429 on `/cart/`** as well.

**CORS failure — Stylitics:**
```
Access to CSS at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?base=grid&theme=skechers...' blocked by CORS policy
```
The Stylitics "shop the look" / outfit widget stylesheet is blocked. The `style.css` is being requested cross-origin without the appropriate `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` header from stylitics.com's asset CDN. This means the Stylitics widget on any affected page will render unstyled or broken. This is a configuration issue on the Stylitics side (or their CDN) — Skechers would need to raise it with Stylitics to update their CORS headers.

**`net::ERR_FAILED`:** One generic network failure, likely a dependent resource of the blocked Stylitics request or a cancelled Kasada retry.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Fix Kasada 429 cascade** — Coordinate with Kasada to investigate why the fingerprint endpoint is rate-limiting its own client in normal browse sessions. Temporarily instrument the SDK error callback to measure how frequently real users hit this state. The `/cart/` 429 is a checkout-blocking severity issue.

2. **Fix View Cart document metadata** — Whether `/cart/` is a full page or an embedded frame, it needs `<title>Cart | Skechers</title>` and `<html lang="en">`. If it's a SPA transition (no full page load), update `document.title` programmatically on cart open. This is a serious accessibility and SEO defect.

3. **Remove Monetate dual-load** — `/js/2/` and `/js/3/` are both in the `<head>` on all tested pages. Remove one. This is a straightforward SFCC template fix.

4. **Remove duplicate Yahoo pixel registration** — `ytc.js` is registered twice via `vice-managed-src`. Deduplicate in the tag manager or consent script.

5. **Fix Stylitics CORS** — File a ticket with Stylitics to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` to their CDN's response headers for `web-assets.stylitics.com`.

6. **Fix homepage accessibility criticals** — `button-name` and `aria-required-parent` are WCAG 2.1 Level A failures. Add `aria-label` to all icon-only buttons; fix the misparented ARIA grid/listbox role.

7. **Instrument LCP and CLS** — All 8 steps are missing LCP and CLS values. Without these, there is no Web Vitals baseline. Add RUM instrumentation (QuantumMetric is already loaded and supports this) or use the `web-vitals` library with a reporting endpoint.

8. **Audit third-party script inventory** — 20+ scripts per page is excessive. With GTM already present, most pixels (TikTok, Reddit, Facebook, Nextdoor, Pinterest, Taboola, Snapchat) should fire through GTM tags rather than as hard-coded `<script>` tags in the SFCC template. Consolidating through GTM restores centralized consent enforcement and removes the vendor-specific `vice-managed-src` workaround.
