# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-17**
## Executive summary

- Same pink floral D'Lites again — that's five, six times now this week, and it's still parked under a fake "READY TO CHECK OUT?" banner like I've got an order sitting there. I don't. Never picked it, never sized it, never looked twice at a shoe covered in embroidered flowers. If Skechers thinks that's my cart, their tracking is broken, not my memory.
- The one bright spot is buried below the fold — a real "SHOP ALL BEST SELLERS" grid with the Arch Fit Slip-in, the BOBS slip-on, and a men's Roth Delson 3.0 shoe sitting right there with actual arch support and cushion in the name. That's the stuff I'd actually click. But I have to scroll past the fake cart nonsense to get to it, and the 40% off banner underneath just says "summer styles" with no shoe attached to it at all.
- Not clicking, not forwarding. This is the same trick as the 15th and 16th with a different headline slapped on it — I'm numb to it at this point.

## What's working

- The Roth Delson 3.0 and Arch Fit Slip-in thumbnails in the best-sellers row are actually in my lane — cushioned, slip-on, no bending over.
- "SHOP ALL BEST SELLERS" and the men's/women's category links are clean, tappable, and easy to read on a phone.
- 40% off is a real, specific number, even if it's not tied to anything I'd wear.

## What's weak

- That floral D'Lites shoe under a fake "checkout" banner — I never had this in a cart, so the whole premise is a lie right out of the gate.
- No men's category or comfort messaging anywhere near the top — I have to scroll past a women's shoe and two kids' sneakers before anything for me shows up.
- The 40% off block doesn't show a single product — just a navy banner with no shoe, no price, nothing to bite on.
- This is the same "abandoned cart" bit as "Your cart expires soon!" (8/15, 8/17) and "Your Items Are Waiting!" (8/16) — different headline, same shoe, same fake urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the fake-cart framing entirely for a shoe I never touched — lead with the actual best-sellers grid instead, since that's the only part of this email that's honest and relevant.
- 2. Put a men's comfort shoe (the Roth or the Arch Fit) in the hero spot, not a floral women's sneaker — I'm the one opening this.
- 3. Attach the 40% off banner to an actual product with a before/after price — a bare banner with no shoe isn't an offer, it's wallpaper.
- 4. Stop repeating this same shoe across five sends this week — rotate creative or I start assuming it's a glitch, not a campaign.
