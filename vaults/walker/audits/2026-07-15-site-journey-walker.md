# Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Date:** July 15, 2026 | **Yesterday's Score:** 4/10

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Went through the whole journey on my phone today. Found the shoes I wanted, got to the size picker just fine — and then Skechers dropped the ball hard. The cart page came up completely blank. White screen, nothing. That's not a hiccup, that's a broken checkout. Search also steered me wrong — typed "comfort shoes" and got Women's products right at the top. I'm a 62-year-old man shopping for himself. Nobody personalized a single thing for me the whole time. The site looks decent enough on a phone, but when the cart doesn't work, nothing else matters.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: **4/10**

No change from yesterday. The core comfort navigation is still functional but the cart is broken and search defaults to the wrong gender. Until the cart loads, this site cannot convert me.

## 3. What's Working

- **Promo banners are impossible to miss.** "20% OFF Regular Priced Items / Code: SUMMER" is right at the very top in bright blue. I saw it immediately and it kept me interested. That's good merchandising.
- **The quick-nav tabs** — Slip-ins, Arch Fit — are exactly the words I'm scanning for. They're there without me having to dig.
- **Men's menu is well organized.** When I opened the hamburger, "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Extra Wide Fit" were right there as standalone links. "Comfort Technologies" had its own category. That's the right language for me. Touch targets felt comfortable.
- **Hands Free Slip-ins category page** is well set up: 313 results, fit filters (Wide / Extra Wide / Medium) front and center as large blue pill buttons. That's genuinely useful for someone with wider feet.
- **Product page title and price are readable without zooming.** "Skechers Slip-ins: Arch Fit Skech-Air — Recomplete" in big bold text, $120.00 clearly beneath it. Members discount ("Get 20% Off! Use Code: SUMMER") is called out on the product page itself — good reinforcement.
- **Add-to-Cart button** is a full-width dark blue bar pinned to the bottom of the screen. Impossible to miss, easy to tap.
- **Size grid** has adequately spaced boxes — not tiny. I could tap my size without hunting.

---

## 4. What's Weak

- **Cart page is a blank white screen.** This is the single biggest failure of the entire journey. After selecting a shoe and going to view the cart, I got nothing — no items, no total, no checkout button. Just white. If I were a real customer, I'd assume the site was broken and leave. This is a conversion killer.
- **Search returns Women's shoes for "comfort shoes."** I typed "comfort shoes" and got 1,451 results — but the very first two products shown were both labeled **Women's** (Contour Foam – Cozy Fit Pure Comfort, Women's; Relaxed Fit: Glide-Step Comfort, Women's). I'm a man. I'm logged in. There is zero reason the top results should be women's products. This is a basic relevance failure.
- **No personalization visible after login.** The login step shows the same generic homepage I saw before logging in. No "Welcome back, Walker," no "Your saved shoes," no loyalty point balance, nothing. The account icon looks identical logged-in vs. logged-out at a glance. If I'm a member, show me I matter.
- **The product I found has zero reviews.** Five empty stars, "No Reviews" — that's a confidence problem for me. I'm about to spend $120 and there's not a single person who's told me if these shoes actually feel good. I'd hesitate.
- **Homepage hero is "GAME ON!" with loud athletic sneakers** — green, red, blue retro court shoes. That's not me. I'm not here for a throwback athletic look, I'm here for arch support and slip-on comfort. The hero is aimed at someone 30 years younger.
- **Login step had no visible confirmation.** Step 3 shows the same homepage — I can't tell if I'm actually logged in from looking at the screen. The account icon gives no clear logged-in signal.
- **"Please select a size" error** — the flow tried to add to cart without a size selected, triggering a red warning. Small friction, but it means no size was pre-remembered from a previous session even though I supposedly logged in.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** A blank cart is a broken purchase funnel. Nothing else matters if step 8 doesn't work.
2. **Apply gender context to search.** A logged-in male user searching "comfort shoes" should get Men's results first, or at minimum a prominent gender toggle at the top. Two Women's shoes as the lead result is the wrong first impression.
3. **Show logged-in state clearly.** A small "Hi, Walker" or a filled-in account icon with my initials would confirm I'm recognized. Right now the experience feels identical whether I'm logged in or not.
4. **Surface reviews or social proof on zero-review products.** If a shoe has no reviews, show "Be the first to review" and offset the risk with a stronger return policy callout or a comfort guarantee badge. Don't leave me staring at empty stars.
5. **Swap the homepage hero for this audience.** The "GAME ON!" retro athletic image sends the wrong signal to comfort shoppers. A Slip-ins or Arch Fit lifestyle shot — someone walking a trail or strolling a town square — would speak directly to me.
6. **Remember my saved size.** If I'm logged in and have ordered before, pre-select my size. Removing that friction step alone would meaningfully improve Add-to-Cart rates for returning customers.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Site runs on **Salesforce Commerce Cloud (SFCC/Demandware)** — evident from `/on/demandware.static/Sites-USSkechers-Site/` paths. The 8-step mobile journey reveals a **Kasada bot-protection loop generating 429s against its own fingerprinting endpoint**, a cart page returning 429 (blocking purchase), and a View Cart render that drops both `<title>` and `lang` attributes entirely. Third-party script count ranges from 20 (Homepage) to 27+ (Men category), with at least two duplicate scripts loaded per page.

---

## Accessibility

17 axe violations across 3 pages. Severity breakdown: 1 critical, 3 serious, 10 moderate, 3 minor.

**Critical / Serious:**
- `aria-required-parent` [critical] — Homepage contains ARIA role elements outside their required parent container. Likely a carousel or listbox rendered outside its `role="list"` or `role="grid"` wrapper.
- `link-name` [serious] — Homepage has links with no discernible text (icon-only links missing `aria-label`). Screen readers announce these as empty.
- `document-title` [serious] — View Cart serves an HTML document with a missing or empty `<title>`. Violates WCAG 2.4.2 (Level A).
- `html-has-lang` [serious] — View Cart HTML element has no `lang` attribute. Violates WCAG 3.1.1 (Level A).

**Moderate (structural landmark failures):**
- `landmark-no-duplicate-main` and `landmark-main-is-top-level` on Homepage indicate a nested or duplicate `<main>` — likely a modal or flyout being injected into the DOM inside an existing `<main>`.
- `landmark-one-main` on View Cart: no `<main>` at all — the page lacks a primary content region.
- `page-has-heading-one` on View Cart: no `<h1>` present.
- `heading-order` on Homepage: heading levels are non-sequential (e.g., h3 before h2).

**Pattern:** View Cart appears to be rendered by a different code path (possibly a SPA shell or iframe embed) that strips standard document-level attributes. This needs a distinct fix from the Homepage landmark issues.

---

## SEO

- **View Cart missing `<title>`**: Googlebot will index this page with no title signal. If the cart URL ever gets crawled (e.g., via shared cart links), it surfaces in SERPs with no title.
- **No structured data** visible in HTML samples. Product detail pages should carry `Product` + `Offer` JSON-LD; absence means no rich results eligibility.
- **Multiple duplicate landmark regions** on Homepage could confuse crawler content segmentation, though this is minor relative to the title issue.
- Log In and Men category HTML samples show identical `<html>` structure to Homepage — consistent with SPA navigation, which is correct for SEO only if server-side rendering or prerendering is confirmed per-route.

**Duplicate scripts (confirmed in HTML):**

**LCP and CLS not captured** — instrumentation gap. The audit cannot assess render quality without these metrics.

## Mobile Optimization

- **`--vh` CSS variable**: Set to `6.640000000000001px` (= 1% of the measured viewport height). This is a JS-calculated mobile vh fix for iOS Safari's address-bar-aware viewport. The floating-point precision in the value is cosmetically odd but functionally harmless; the value is derived from `window.innerHeight / 100`.
- **WebFontLoader** loading 6 weights of Mulish (`n3`–`n8`) — 6 separate font files. Each weight is a network request; this delays text rendering until all weights load (FOUT/FOIT risk depending on `font-display` configuration, which is not visible in the truncated HTML).
- **No `<meta viewport>` visible** in the truncated HTML samples, though its absence from the excerpt may be truncation — SFCC typically includes it; needs verification.

---

## Console & Network Errors

**429 Rate-Limit Loop — Kasada KPSDK:**
- 5 of 6 network errors are `429` on `https://www.skechers.com/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.522`
- This is the **Kasada bot-protection fingerprinting endpoint** (KPSDK = Kasada SDK). The client is hammering its own bot-detection layer faster than Kasada's rate limit allows. The client-side SDK retries on failure, creating a self-reinforcing loop. This burns network bandwidth and may interfere with legitimate bot-protection signals.

**Cart 429:**
- `https://www.skechers.com/cart/` returns `429`. This is a **checkout-blocking failure** — a user hitting the cart during a Kasada rate-limit window gets a 429 on the cart page itself. The Kasada misconfiguration directly impacts conversion.

**CORS Block — Stylitics:**
- `https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css` is blocked by CORS. Stylitics is an outfit-recommendation widget. The CDN is not returning `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com`. Either Stylitics' CDN config is wrong or the integration is pointing to the wrong asset URL. The widget likely renders broken or invisible.
- `ERR_FAILED` (1 error) is likely the cascading failure from the blocked stylesheet.

---

## Recommendations

**P0 — Blocking:**
1. **Kasada rate-limit loop**: Audit the KPSDK integration version (`j-1.2.522`) against Kasada's current recommended version. The retry logic in the client SDK should back off exponentially, not retry immediately. Contact Kasada support — this configuration is causing cart 429s.
2. **View Cart document-level failures**: `<title>` and `lang` must be present on every server-rendered shell, including the cart. If cart is rendered client-side, ensure the SPA shell includes these in the static HTML before hydration.

**P1 — High:**
3. **Stylitics CORS**: Coordinate with Stylitics to fix their CDN CORS headers for `web-assets.stylitics.com`, or proxy the stylesheet through a Skechers-controlled origin.
4. **Log In / Men category TTFB (542ms)**: Investigate whether these routes can be served from CDN edge with stale-while-revalidate for the non-personalized shell. The personalized content (user state, cart count) should be hydrated client-side, not block the initial TTFB.

**P2 — Medium:**
5. **Duplicate scripts**: Remove the redundant Monetate v2 entry point and the second Yahoo YTC tag. Both are likely GTM tag duplicates. Audit the GTM container for tag firing rules that don't deduplicate.
6. **Men category script proliferation**: 7 additional third-party scripts fire only on the Men category — confirm this is intentional segmentation, not a GTM misconfiguration.
7. **Capture LCP and CLS**: The audit has no render-quality data. Instrument Real User Monitoring (Quantum Metric is already loaded — confirm it's reporting Core Web Vitals to a dashboard).

**P3 — Low:**
8. **Homepage ARIA**: Fix `aria-required-parent` (likely a carousel listitem outside its list container) and add `aria-label` to icon-only links.
9. **Landmark structure**: Audit the Homepage DOM for nested `<main>` elements — likely caused by a modal or minicart rendering inside the page's existing `<main>`.
