# Check out this month's adiClub benefits, here is your August Newsletter
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-19**
## Executive summary

- Well, this is a first — a whole newsletter and not one shoe in sight until you get to the fine print about "Samba" and "running shoes" buried in a paragraph. This is soccer jerseys, sweepstakes for a signed World Cup ball, a Penn State mascot, and a Strava tie-in. None of that puts anything on my feet, and none of it tells me what a pair of shoes costs.
- I get that this is a "newsletter" and not a straight sales pitch, but adidas seems to have forgotten I signed up for shoes, not sweepstakes and fitness app integrations. My wife runs marathons and even she wouldn't know what to do with half this content.
- I'm not clicking, I'm not forwarding, and honestly I'm getting close to unsubscribing at this point — this is at least the sixth adidas email in a row with nothing relevant to how I shop.

## What's working

- The "adiClub" branding is at least consistent and I recognize it as a loyalty program I'm supposedly part of.
- Layout is clean, big buttons, easy to read on my phone without squinting.
- The "REDEEM NOW" button at the bottom at least gestures at using points for savings, which is the kind of thing I'd want to know about.

## What's weak

- Not a single shoe photographed anywhere in this email — it's a soccer jersey, a track suit, a mascot, and lifestyle shots.
- The hero offer is a sweepstakes for a signed match ball, which does nothing for a guy who just wants comfortable shoes.
- The Penn State Nittany Lion bear costume section is completely baffling to me — I have no connection to that school or its mascot.
- The Strava cross-promotion assumes I'm tracking runs and workouts, which isn't how I shop for shoes.
- No price, no percentage off, no specific product — just "unlock savings" with no number attached.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with an actual shoe and an actual number — a specific comfort/walking shoe with a real discount, not a sweepstakes for sports memorabilia.
- 2. Cut the soccer jersey hero image entirely for a newsletter going to a general customer list — save it for soccer-specific segments.
- 3. Drop or resegment the Penn State mascot content — that's college-town regional marketing, not general audience material.
- 4. Put a real dollar or percent figure on the "Unlock Savings" section instead of vague language.
- 5. If this is meant to be a loyalty benefits roundup, show me my actual points balance up top — that's what would make me feel like a known member.

- **Subject Alt B:** `New comfort styles + your August member savings`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `See what your points can save you on right now`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Comfort shoes, real discounts, no sweepstakes required`
