## Executive summary

- DSW again, and it's the same HAYRIDE 20% code I've now seen several times this week, after "20% OFF?! You better be-leaf it 🍁" and "FYI: we've got 20% off right now" yesterday. The green banner with the big white "20% OFF*" is easy to read on my phone, and I like that. But then the email turns into a women's tall-boot parade. The "boot math" line, pay once and wear always, is the one idea that spoke to me, because I do price-versus-quality math. It was attached to women's riding boots, though.

## What's working

- The big white "20% OFF*" on green is readable without my readers.
- The code HAYRIDE and the "CLAIM 20% OFF" button are easy to find at the top.
- "Boot math: pay for them once + wear them always = basically free" is my kind of reasoning.
- The shop-by-size grid with wide width options is thoughtful. Wide is a real need for me.
- The barcode and the offer details at the bottom are honest and show the dates, October 9–11.

## What's weak

- Every model and every product is women's. Nothing here speaks to a man my age.
- "Shop Men" is a tiny underlined link, and I had to hunt for it.
- There's no comfort, cushioning, arch support, or slip-on content anywhere. Those are what win me over.
- It's the same 20% HAYRIDE offer I've already had this week, so it feels like the fourth copy of one promo.
- The asterisk and the long fine print make me wonder what's excluded. Skechers is on the list, and I buy Skechers.
- The CTA buttons are pale orange with white text, and they're harder to read than the green banner.

- 1. Segment by gender. Send men like me a men's comfort hero, such as walking shoes, slip-ons, and wide widths, instead of tall boots.
- 2. Make "Shop Men" a real button, the same size as the women's one.
- 3. Cut the cadence. One 20% email per week is plenty.
- 4. Darken the orange buttons so the text is readable.
- 5. Move the fine print about brand exclusions up near the offer, so I know what's in before I click.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Broken CTA (400):** "Opt-In to Texts Get 15% Off" points to `https://ablink.dsw.com/f/a/XIq6TDuiFxkQu5n--tC4bw~~/AAQRxRA~/w6cCWZ3C…` and returned 400.
  - This is a Braze click-tracking redirect. A 400 from the redirector usually means a malformed or expired tracking token, or that the probe was rejected.
  - A bot-detection rejection is possible, so confirm manually with a real client.
  - If it is real, the SMS-acquisition CTA is dead for every recipient.
- **Probe coverage:** only 12 of 65 links were probed (53 skipped, footer and social deprioritized). The other links are unverified.
- **Tracking domains:** `ablink.dsw.com` for click and open tracking, and `mi.dsw.com` for the `o.gif` pixel. Both are first-party subdomains, which is good for deliverability. No issues beyond the 400.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (2 images):**
  - `https://ablink.dsw.com/q/WHxg5QYiMltI95Sgh7CHIQ~~/AAQRxRA~/pgwrWxJ56rXA-48u…`
  - `https://mi.dsw.com/p/up/e6f23fb024fdcb2cc855b9ed672710d4/o.gif?mi_u=225090073&mi_ecmp=20261009_P261405_V3_W-_Offer_Value`
  - Both look like tracking pixels (the `/q/` open-tracking path and `o.gif`). If so, they should carry `alt=""` so screen readers skip them. Low severity.
- **Malformed `<meta>` tags:** the head has `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` and `<meta content="IE=Edge" />`, both without `http-equiv`. As written, neither is a valid declaration.
