# FIRST DIBS ENDS TONIGHT: VIP BDAY SAVINGS 🎁
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- I've lost count of how many DSW birthday emails I've gotten in the last week — it's somewhere north of six, probably seven. When "FIRST DIBS ENDS TONIGHT" landed, I recognized the rhythm immediately: same VIP pitch, same $20 off hook, different subject line. The urgency in the subject got my attention because "ends tonight" is the kind of thing I actually respond to. But then I opened it, scrolled one inch down, and hit a full-page spread about jelly shoes. Jelly shoes. I haven't thought about jelly shoes since my granddaughter was in elementary school. There's a woman in a yellow sundress front and center, the "READY FOR THIS JELLY?" banner takes up a third of the email, and every CTA below it says "SHOP WOMEN" or "SHOP ALL WOMEN." I'm a 62-year-old man looking for something I can slip on without bending over. This email talked to someone else entirely.
- The brand section at the bottom — 25% off Adidas, New Balance, Skechers — that's where I'd actually perk up. New Balance is on my shortlist right now. But to get there I had to wade through a fashion editorial about translucent plastic shoes. By the time I found the size finder and the wide-width button, I'd already lost interest.
- I'll mark this as read and maybe check DSW's site directly later. I wouldn't click through, and I wouldn't forward this to anyone — there's nothing in here for my crew.

## What's working

- The $20 off birthday offer and the "25% OFF BIG-TIME BRANDS" call-out are concrete and visible — no mystery about what I'd get.
- New Balance is right there in the brand logo row, which is one of the brands I actually cross-shop. That's a real hook if I ever got to it.
- "SHOP WIDE WIDTH" and the size strip (6–13) show up at the bottom — DSW knows I'm not a standard fit, and that matters to me more than people realize.
- No broken images, clean load — at my age I have zero patience for emails that look like a construction site, and this one at least renders properly.
- "ENDS TONIGHT" is the right kind of urgency. Specific, believable, not screaming "FREE MONEY!!!" — just a deadline.

## What's weak

- The "READY FOR THIS JELLY?" section is doing absolutely nothing for me — brightly colored translucent slip-ons for a woman in a yellow dress is not my shopping moment.
- The female model is the hero of this email. I'm not the demographic she's selling to, and it lands that way.
- Every primary CTA says "SHOP WOMEN" or "SHOP ALL WOMEN" — there's no men's door anywhere above the fold.
- This is the seventh birthday email in a week. The fatigue is real. The urgency that worked on send three doesn't hit the same on send seven.
- The preview text — "meet the new jellies on the block" — killed whatever curiosity the subject built. I don't want jellies. That's not a mystery box I want to open.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split the list by gender.** Men's customers shouldn't see a jelly shoe takeover hero. One email, two creative versions. The "25% off New Balance / Skechers / Adidas" block should be the hero for me, not buried behind women's fashion editorial.
- 2. **Surface wide-width and comfort features above the fold.** "Up to 25% off — plus wide-width and slip-on styles" would stop my thumb.
- 3. **Drop the jelly section entirely for male segments** — or replace it with one men's comfort callout at the same position.
- 4. **Cap the birthday cadence.** Seven sends in one week is too many. Three is the most I'd stomach before I start deleting on sight.
- 5. **Preheader cleanup.** The filler characters after "meet the new jellies" show up as garbage in my inbox preview. Even if the content were right, it looks sloppy.

### 1. Technical Summary

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`Src:` capitalization in all 10 `@font-face` blocks** (confirmed, lines across `<style>` block)
```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');  /* BUG: must be lowercase src: */
```
CSS property names are case-sensitive in strict parsers. Outlook's rendering engine and some webmail clients may silently ignore these declarations, causing fallback to system fonts for all custom typefaces.

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom** (WCAG 1.4.4 violation):
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents visually impaired users from zooming. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Missing `http-equiv` attributes on two meta tags:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />          <!-- missing http-equiv="Content-Type" -->
<meta content="IE=Edge" />                            <!-- missing http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" -->
```
Charset declaration without `http-equiv` may not be honored in Outlook/IE rendering path.

**`text-size-adjust: none` in reset:**
```css
*{ -ms-text-size-adjust: none; text-size-adjust: none }
```
Setting to `none` (not `100%`) suppresses OS-level text scaling for all users, compounding the zoom issue above.

**Font format gaps:** Futura PT variants use only `.woff`; Helvetica Now variants use only `.ttf`. Neither provides `.woff2`. While email client support for web fonts is limited, `.woff2` is the preferred format where supported and has significantly better compression.

**Image alt text** — cannot confirm from truncated source. Verify all `<img>` tags carry non-empty `alt` attributes.
