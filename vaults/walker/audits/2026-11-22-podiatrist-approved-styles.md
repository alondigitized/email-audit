# Podiatrist-approved styles
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-22**
## Executive summary

- This is the first HOKA email in a while that talks to me. "Comfort you can count on" sits over a gray Bondi-looking shoe with a thick white sole, and the American Podiatric Medical Association seal sits right under it. I'm 62 and I walk on pavement, so a podiatrist stamp counts for more with me than a trail-runner gift guide. It doesn't try to sell me a points balance or a countdown clock.
- The back half lets it down. The middle photo is three young runners in black spandex, and that isn't me. The "ID.me" text sits on top of a runner's body, so I read it as a logo or a leftover for a second. The discount is for military, medical and education folks, and I'm none of those. That's a lot of screen spent on an offer I can't use. I also never find out which shoes carry the seal. The button just says "Shop APMA-Accepted".
- I'd probably tap the one button and see what's on the page. I wouldn't forward it. I'm not buying yet, and no price or sale tells me to hurry. It's a better send than last week's trail gift guide, but it still has the same problem I've had since the 9th, which is the volume of HOKA email.

## What's working

- "Comfort you can count on" is huge and easy to read on my phone without zooming.
- The APMA seal and the line "Your feet will thank you" are the trust cue I care about. A podiatrist's approval does more for me than a points deal.
- The shoe photo is large, with the cushioned sole front and center. The look is plain and not flashy.
- The "Shop APMA-Accepted" button is clear and sits right under the claim.
- The "Unlock Free Shipping" and "FLY for 30 Guarantee" links in the black footer reassure me about trying a pair.

## What's weak

- The ID.me offer is for military, medical and education folks. I don't qualify, and it's a whole section of the email.
- The runner photo shows three young athletes in black athletic wear. It feels aimed at someone 30 years younger than me.
- "ID.me" is printed over a runner and looks like a logo glitch, which muddies the hierarchy.
- No specific shoe is named, and there's no price and no sale. I can't tell which Bondi or Arahi has the seal.
- The legal text at the bottom is tiny, and the blue-on-blue phone and address lines are hard to read.
- The "Shop APMA-Accepted" button is a thin outline on dark gray, which is a small, low-contrast tap target for my thumb.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace the runner and ID.me block with 2–3 named APMA-accepted shoes. Show the Bondi 9 and one slip-on or walking pair, each with a price and a "Shop Bondi 9" link.
- 2. Make the button solid and large. Use "Shop Podiatrist-Approved Shoes" in a filled color.
- 3. Put an older walker or someone on their feet all day in the photo, not a sprinter.
- 4. Keep the ID.me offer to a short one-line banner at the bottom, and make the text readable.
- 5. Say what the seal means in one plain sentence, such as "Reviewed for foot health by the APMA."

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on 8 images.**
  - 6 are content images:
    - `H_US_Hero_APMA_2.jpg`
    - `H_US_Subcopy_Hero_APMA_2.jpg`
    - `H_US_CTA_Secondary_APMA_1.jpg`

    - `LogoFooterPrimary.png`
  - The hero and CTA images appear to carry text and links, so with images blocked the email is likely unreadable or unclickable.
  - The other 2 are tracking pixels, where empty alt is acceptable.
- **Plain-text part is 0 chars.** The QA check marks this as a FAIL. See Compliance and Recommendations.
- **Empty `<title></title>`.**
- **Viewport meta.**
  - `<meta name="viewport" … maximum-scale=1>` is declared twice with conflicting values. The second also sets `user-scalable=yes`.
  - `maximum-scale=1` can block pinch-zoom in some clients.
- **Charset meta.** `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` has no `http-equiv`, so it is non-functional. Most clients fall back to the MIME header.
- **Invalid CSS in the `<style>` block.**
  - `.nav_padding {padding-top:px !important;padding-bottom:px !important;}`
  - `.nav_border {border-top:px solid ;}`
  - These are template variables that were never filled in. Browsers drop them, so the practical impact is low.
- **Web fonts.** The `@font-face` rules for `TheFutureHOKA` load from `www.hoka.com`. They are ignored by Gmail and Outlook, which is fine as long as the font stack has fallbacks. The truncated source did not show the stack.

- **Plain-text fallback missing.** Text version is 0 chars. This hurts deliverability and breaks accessibility paths for text-only clients.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Add a plain-text MIME part.** This is the one confirmed FAIL in deliverability.
2. **Add descriptive `alt` text** to the 6 content images, especially the hero, subcopy and CTA images. Set `alt=""` explicitly on the tracking pixels.
3. **Re-test the unsubscribe and return-start links** from a real client or a non-bot user agent. If they also fail, fix the Cordial link or the `options.hoka.com` WAF rules. This is the highest-risk item, since a broken unsubscribe link is a CAN-SPAM exposure.
4. **Get the raw headers** to confirm SPF, DKIM and DMARC, `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and the postal address in the footer.
5. **Clean up the template.** Fill in or remove the empty `px` values in `.nav_padding` and `.nav_border`, populate `<title>`, deduplicate the viewport meta, and drop `maximum-scale=1`. Add `http-equiv="Content-Type"` to the charset meta.
6. **Add `utm_content`** per link slot for finer attribution.
7. **Raise probe coverage.** Probe the 7 skipped links, or use a probe user agent that Cordial and HOKA allowlist.
