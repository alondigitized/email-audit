# Almost over! 20% off 😮
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- My thumb hit this before I even processed "Almost over" — pure DSW muscle memory, same reflex I've been logging every send since early June. That trained habit is doing a lot of heavy lifting here, because once I'm inside the email, it stops working for me pretty fast. The hero is clean enough: "20% OFF" in big type, deadline urgency baked in. Good start.
- But then I scroll half an inch and hit a wall of women's shoes. Heels, wedges, block-heeled ankle boots, strappy sandals — the whole "BRAND NEW... AND SO YOU" grid is female-coded fashion footwear. I am a 62-year-old man shopping for cushioned walking shoes, and there is not one product in this email that speaks to me. The size selector below it runs 5 through 8.5 — those are women's sizes. Even the "Shop Shoe Calf" button is for boot shafts. I felt like I accidentally opened my wife's email.
- Then there's the SHOERISE block — a bright red banner screaming "TODAY! WIN UP TO $50 OFF, JUNE 23 & 24." It's June 25th. That event ended yesterday. "TODAY!" is factually wrong, and that kind of stale copy makes me wonder what else isn't quite right. I'd mark this read and move on. Wouldn't forward it to anyone — nothing here that a guy like me would send a buddy.

## What's working

- **"20% OFF" hero** — big, legible, lands above the fold. No squinting required.
- **"Almost over" + "You have until TOMORROW"** — deadline urgency across subject and preview is a clean one-two; it's the main reason I clicked in.
- **DSW brand recognition** — I open these on reflex, and the sender name in my inbox still earns the tap without me having to think.
- **Visual hierarchy** — the eye goes to the discount number first, no fighting for attention against a noisy layout.
- **DSW Rewards visible in footer** — loyalty member framing at the bottom is a nice trust signal even if I had to scroll to find it.

## What's weak

- **Zero men's product** — every shoe in the grid reads as women's fashion footwear. There is not one walking shoe, sneaker, or men's casual comfort pick to be found.
- **SHOERISE block is stale** — "TODAY! JUNE 23 & 24" when it's June 25th. "TODAY!" is a trust-breaker; feels like I missed a party I wasn't even invited to.
- **Size grid is women's** — 5 through 8.5 with a "Shop Shoe Calf" button. Wrong gender, wrong shopping signal for me.
- **"SHOP MORE" CTA is a waste** — generic verb, no product category, no destination promise. Doesn't pull me anywhere specific.
- **Preview junk padding** — "You have until TOMORROW" and then a long train of invisible spacer characters bleeding into the preview. Looks truncated and sloppy on my phone.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment by gender or at minimum swap hero product grid** — the single biggest lift. If I'm a male subscriber, I should see men's walking shoes, not block heels. Even one men's comfort product in the hero would change the read.
- 2. **Kill or update the SHOERISE block** — if the in-store event is over, either pull the block or replace it with the current online offer. "TODAY!" on an expired event date is the kind of thing that erodes trust one send at a time.
- 3. **Replace "SHOP MORE" with a specific CTA** — "Shop Men's 20% Off" or "Shop Comfort Styles" — anything with a destination noun.
- 4. **Fix the size grid for men's** — or conditionally hide it. Showing women's sizes to a male subscriber is a demographic slip.
- 5. **Preheader cleanup** — strip the spacer characters; let the preview breathe or add a second line of real copy.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Walker, 20% off ends tomorrow — comfort picks inside`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last day: 20% off men's walking & casual`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Grab your size before it's gone — offer ends midnight tonight`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` is missing the required `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute. Correct form: `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />`. Without it, some legacy clients (Outlook, older Android mail) may fail to declare charset correctly.

- `<meta content="IE=Edge" />` is missing `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`. Correct form: `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=Edge" />`. Functionally ignored in modern clients but technically malformed.

- All `@font-face` blocks use `Src:` (capital S) rather than `src:` (lowercase). CSS property names are case-insensitive per spec, but this is non-standard and some strict parsers (older WebKit-based clients) may skip the declaration entirely, causing fallback font rendering.

- Futura PT fonts are provided in `.woff` only; HelveticaNow in `.ttf` only. No `.woff2` variant is provided for either family. `.woff2` is universally supported in modern webmail clients and significantly smaller (~30% vs `.woff`). Not a rendering blocker, but increases font load latency.
