# New Nike Air Max 💥
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- I know Nike. I've had Nike walking shoes off and on for thirty years. So when "New Nike Air Max" shows up from Famous Footwear, I don't delete it — I give it a second. But I'll be straight with you: the second I open this thing, I'm looking at a young guy jogging around in a lifestyle sneaker with "Catch some air" as the hook. That's not me. That's my grandson.
- The Spend & Save tiers at the very top are what kept me from closing it immediately — $10, $15, $20 off depending on how much I spend. I've been watching Famous Footwear run that promotion all week. They ran it June 17, they ran it June 16, and here we go again. The deal's fine, but at this point it's furniture. I barely see it anymore.
- What I'm really not seeing is anything about fit, comfort, or arch support in any of the Air Max styles on display. It's a wall of sneakers in colorways I'd never pick off a shelf — bright pinks, neon accents, the kind of shoe my feet would argue with by noon. I'll mark this read and keep going. I won't forward it.

## What's working

- **Spend & Save is front and center.** The tiered discount sits in the top banner where I can't miss it — $10/$15/$20 off. I still see value in that, even if the creative underneath doesn't speak to me.
- **Subject line is clean and honest.** "New Nike Air Max" — 18 characters, tells me exactly what I'm getting, no fake urgency screaming at me. A refreshing change from the emoji-bomb sends earlier this month.
- **Nike is a brand I recognize.** Cross-shopping Famous Footwear means I do consider Nike, so this isn't irrelevant brand noise.
- **The slides section near the bottom.** "30% off Nike" with a $74.99 sandal — that's actually closer to my wheelhouse than the Air Max stuff. Slides matter to me, especially in summer. That section at least gave me a reason to scroll.
- **Renders clean.** No broken images, no overlapping text, nothing that made me think something went wrong on their end.

## What's weak

- **Same broken preheader. Again.** I flagged this on June 15. I flagged it on June 16. I flagged it again on June 17. That tracking URL garbage — `https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=ABB7…` followed by the word "Want" — is still sitting right below my subject line in the inbox preview. At some point this stops being a technical glitch and starts being a "nobody checked" problem.
- **Model is half my age.** The hero shot is a young, athletic guy jogging. Famous Footwear knows I'm a loyal customer. They could at least occasionally remind me they have shoes for people who walk, not sprint.
- **Air Max is a lifestyle sneaker, not a comfort shoe.** No cushioning callouts, no arch support language, no wide-width mention. Just colorways. If you want my money, tell me how it fits, not how it looks on someone twenty-five years younger.
- **Too many emails this week.** This is at least the fourth Famous Footwear email in the last ten days running some form of a spend-and-save offer. It's starting to blur. I'm not deleting the brand, but my finger's getting closer to "unsubscribe."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader. This week. Not next send.** Write a real two-sentence hook — something like "New colorways, plus save up to $20 when you spend $100." That alone would move the Open Likelihood materially. This is the single highest-leverage change available and it costs nothing.
- 2. **Add a men's comfort callout in the Air Max section.** Even a one-liner — "Memory foam footbed" or "Lightweight cushioning for all-day wear" — would give a guy like me a reason to look twice at a lifestyle sneaker.
- 3. **Feature the sandal/slide earlier.** If the 30% off Nike slide section is in the email, don't bury it below the fold. That's a summer impulse buy for a 62-year-old. Move it up.
- 4. **Rotate the demographic.** One send with a model who looks like he's been on his feet all day, not training for a 5K, would go a long way toward making me feel seen.
- 5. **Slow the cadence.** Four to five sends per week on the same promotion is too much. Combine campaigns where possible — one strong email beats three diluted ones.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — HTTP image asset (mixed content):**
```
src="http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=ABB7InYiOjEsImQiOjQ5MTN9..."
```
This is a Salesforce Marketing Cloud LiveContent block served over HTTP. Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail proxy or block HTTP image sources; this element will fail to render for a significant share of recipients. Fix: ensure the LiveContent block is configured to serve over HTTPS, or replace with a static HTTPS asset.

**37 images with empty or absent `alt` text** — includes what appears to be the logo (`3b6a7dc1`), a horizontal rule GIF (`13751a56`), and all product/hero images. Screen readers will either skip these or read the filename. For a product email where images carry the primary CTA, this is a functional accessibility failure, not just a compliance note.

**Malformed charset meta tag:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Most clients tolerate this, but it can cause charset detection fallback in older Outlook and some webmail clients, producing garbled characters in non-ASCII content.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** — prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 violation; also affects users with low vision.
