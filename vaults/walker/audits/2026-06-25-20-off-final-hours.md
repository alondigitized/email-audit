# 20% OFF: final hours!

## Executive summary

- Pure muscle memory got me here — my thumb hit DSW before my brain even caught up, same as it has every single day this week. The "20% OFF: final hours!" subject barely registered; at this point I could receive a DSW email in my sleep and open it. So they got the open, I'll give them that.
- But then I'm inside and it's like I accidentally walked into the women's section of the store. "THEY'RE JUST SOOO YOU." "GET INSPIRED." Wedges. Strappy sandals. Chunky heels. Page after page of shoes that aren't remotely for me. The whole email is dressed up for someone forty years younger and two X chromosomes over. There's not a single men's walking shoe, not one cushioned slip-on, nothing that looks like it belongs on my feet. Even the "WIN UP TO $50 OFF" sweepstakes banner feels like it belongs on a scratch-off card at a nail salon.

## What's working

- **20% OFF lands first** — big, bold, readable without any zoom. That's all I need to know the offer exists.
- **DSW muscle memory is real** — six or seven sends this week have trained me to open on reflex; the brand earned that even if this particular email didn't.
- **"Final hours" sets genuine urgency** — I believe it because DSW actually does rotate these offers; they're not crying wolf.
- **No broken images or layout chaos** — the email renders cleanly, nothing overlapping, no empty gray boxes.
- **"SHOP BY SIZE" at the bottom** — the one genuinely useful thing; I always check size availability and at least that section exists.

## What's weak

- **Every single product is women's shoes** — wedges, strappy sandals, kitten heels. I don't see one men's shoe, not one comfort walker, not one slip-on with arch support. I might as well have opened the wrong person's email.
- **"SOOO YOU" and "SLAY" copy** — I'm 62. That language lands somewhere between cringe and invisible. Didn't make me trust the brand more.
- **Zero cushioning or comfort language** — the one thing that would hook me: mention of memory foam, arch support, wide width. Nothing.
- **Seventh DSW email this week** — I've been noting this fatigue all June. The "final hours" urgency barely lands when I've already had "last chance" twice this week.
- **"WIN UP TO $50 OFF" sweepstakes block** — felt like a non sequitur dropped into the middle. No context, no obvious relevance to the 20% offer.

- 1. **Show me men's shoes** — even one row of men's comfort walkers, cushioned casuals, or wide-width options in this email would have put me in the "add to cart" headspace. The entire product selection is wrong for half the subscriber base.
- 2. **Lead with something comfort-specific** — "Cushioned. Slip-on. 20% off. Final hours." That headline would have my full attention.
- 3. **Cut one send from this cadence** — the fatigue is real. A "final hours" email has more urgency when I haven't already gotten four "almost over!" emails.

## Technical Audit — DSW "20% OFF: final hours!"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is built on Braze, uses a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional layout with custom web fonts and dark mode support. The truncated source exposes several low-severity but real technical defects; full compliance and link audit is constrained by the truncation.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully assess** — HTML is truncated before body links appear. Based on what is visible:

- Sending domain is `e.dsw.com` (ESP subdomain). Confirm DKIM signing aligns to this subdomain, not `dsw.com`, to avoid SPF/DKIM mismatch flags.
- Font assets served from `braze-images.com` — third-party CDN, not DSW-owned. If Braze's CDN has an outage, custom fonts silently fail. Fallback stacks are present, so rendering degrades gracefully — acceptable risk.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`http-equiv` missing on charset meta tag (confirmed bug):**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, older Outlook and some webmail clients may not register the charset declaration, risking mojibake on special characters.

**`Src:` (capitalized) in all `@font-face` blocks (confirmed bug):**
```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');
```
CSS property names are technically case-insensitive per spec, but several stricter email client parsers (notably some Samsung Mail versions) will reject the rule. Should be lowercase `src:`. All 10 `@font-face` declarations are affected.

**Intentional near-black `#000001` / `#00000F`** — not a bug. This is a known technique to prevent Gmail dark-mode from auto-inverting true black (`#000000`). Confirmed intentional by the dark mode class names (`.dm-bg-black`, `.dm-font-black`).

**`sup` font-size set to `48%`** — results in ~8px type at base 16px. Screen readers will read it correctly, but visually it may be illegible for footnote/asterisk disclosures. Flag for accessibility if any legal disclosures use `<sup>`.

**Cannot assess:** `alt` text coverage, `role` attributes, heading hierarchy, image-to-text ratio — all in the truncated section.
