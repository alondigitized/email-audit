## What's working

- The shoes lead. Four of them sit at the top and I can see them right away.
- The eight-shoe grid is clean, and the names are easy to read. Fresh Foam is a cushioning name I know.
- The product names are plain, with no model-number puzzles. I can tell a 680v9 from an Arishi.
- The top menu has Men, Women, Kids and Sale in simple words, and it's easy to read on a phone.
- The footer has a "Walking" link beside Running and Lifestyle. That's the first thing in the email that speaks to me.

## What's weak

- I don't run, and the whole email is about running. Nothing says walking, slip-on or arch support.
- The three price-range buttons confuse me. They don't say what I get or what I should do.
- The offer looks expired. The fine print says it ended October 6, which is "yesterday" for me, and that kills my trust.
- "Up to 25% off select styles" with a $125 minimum is a lot of strings. I don't know which shoes qualify.
- The jackets, shorts and leggings take up half the email. The RC Collection is clothes for young runners, not for me.
- The fine print is tiny and I'd need to zoom in to read it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put one clear offer at the top with a real deadline, and don't send it after the deadline has passed.
- 2. Replace the three price buttons with one button that says "Shop shoes under $100".
- 3. Add a walking row: a Fresh Foam walker or a slip-on, with "cushioned for all-day wear."
- 4. Cut the apparel to one block, or drop it for a send aimed at men who walk.
- 5. Make the fine print bigger and plainer.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's QA run passed with 0 failures and 1 warning (88% pass rate). The source has two real accessibility gaps (missing alt text) and some template leftovers in the CSS (placeholder URL, `@font-face` rules without `src`). Compliance, personalization and UTM checks can't be confirmed because the HTML is truncated.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Plain-text part is mostly URLs (WARN).** 12,788 of 14,848 characters (about 86%) are URLs. That points to long redirect-wrapped tracking links. A text part that is almost all URLs can raise spam-filter scores and is hard to read for text-only clients.
- **Link probing was partial.** 12 material links were probed and 29 were skipped, and no broken links were reported among the probed set. The skipped footer, utility and social links are unverified.
- **Placeholder URL in the stylesheet.** `.bgwhite {background-image: url('http://image.XXXXX.png');}` is a non-HTTPS placeholder. If `.bgwhite` is applied anywhere, it requests a dead, insecure resource and may trigger mixed-content warnings. I couldn't see whether any element uses the class.
- **Tracking pixel.** `https://link.email.newbalance.com/mo/egR1f966kc_818512772_12382219_121902_23323046.gif` is the open-tracking pixel. It is served over HTTPS from the same tracking domain as the content images.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on two content images.**
  - `ShippingIcons_06.png` (`https://link.email.newbalance.com/custloads/818512772/NBUS/myNB_Welcome/UpdatedImages/ShippingIcons_06.png`)
  - `ShippingBanner_DarkMode_03.png` (same path)

  These carry shipping information, so with images blocked or a screen reader that information is lost. The third flagged image, the `/mo/…gif` pixel, is decorative, so it should have `alt=""` rather than descriptive text.
- **Broken `@font-face` declarations.** Every `@font-face` block (`proxima-nova`, `linotype-didot`, and others) has no `src`. They are also written as `font-family: proxima-nova,sans-serif;`, which is invalid inside `@font-face`. The custom fonts can't load, so clients always use the fallback (`Arial,sans-serif`, or `Times New Roman, serif` for `.fst10` and `.fst12`).
- **Malformed meta tag.** `<meta content="IE=edge" />` has no `http-equiv`, so it does nothing.
- **Generic title.** `<title>New Balance Template</title>` is a template placeholder, not the subject line. Some clients and web-view pages show it.
- **Conflicting class definitions.** `.bg1` is defined twice, first as `#ffffff` and then as `#58546d`. The later rule wins, and the dark-mode override uses `!important`. This is a likely maintenance hazard, but the elements that use `.bg1` aren't visible in the truncated source.
- **Doctype mismatch.** The HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype is paired with an `xmlns` attribute and self-closed tags. Clients usually tolerate it.
- **Dark mode.** The `color-scheme` meta tags and `prefers-color-scheme` rules are present. The dark-mode banner asset name (`ShippingBanner_DarkMode_03.png`) suggests an image swap, but I can't confirm how it is wired in the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens appear in the visible source or QA output. The truncated body can't be checked.

No issues found in the data provided. The QA notes say an unsubscribe link is always probed, and none was flagged. SPF, DKIM and DMARC results, the `List-Unsubscribe` headers and the physical address in the footer weren't in the input, so I can't confirm them.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
No issues found. No UTM parameters or landing-page URLs were visible in the truncated source or QA output, so there is nothing to verify. One observation: the image paths contain `myNB_Welcome`, which suggests the shipping assets were reused from a welcome-series template. That is a naming observation, not a confirmed defect.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to `ShippingIcons_06.png` and `ShippingBanner_DarkMode_03.png`. Set `alt=""` on the tracking pixel.
2. Fix or remove the `@font-face` blocks. Add real `src` URLs, or drop them and rely on web-safe stacks. Use a single family name in each.
3. Remove the `http://image.XXXXX.png` placeholder from `.bgwhite`, or replace it with a real HTTPS asset.
4. Reduce the plain-text URL density. Use shorter tracking URLs, or put more readable copy in the text part.
5. Set a real `<title>`, and change the meta tag to `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge">` or remove it.
6. Remove the duplicate `.bg1` rule so there is one source of truth.
7. Re-run the audit on the untruncated HTML and raw headers. That would let the unsubscribe and `List-Unsubscribe` headers, SPF/DKIM/DMARC, the physical address and the UTM parameters be checked.
