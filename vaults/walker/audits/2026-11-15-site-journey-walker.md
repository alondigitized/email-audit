# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-11-15**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I tried to buy a pair of slip-ins on my phone and couldn't get as far as a working cart. The homepage and the Men's menu are easy to use. The Men's Shoes page has a "Hands Free Slip-Ins" tile near the top, which is what I want. On the product page I tapped **Add to Cart** without picking a size, got a red "Please select a size" warning, and my cart stayed at 0. Then the cart page came up as a completely blank white screen. Search for "comfort shoes" showed women's loafers and wedges first, though I'm a man shopping in the Men's section.

Compared with yesterday I can't point to specific changes, because I only have yesterday's score (3/10) and not what it was based on. Today's journey doesn't give me a reason to move that number.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10** (yesterday: 3/10, so no change).

The browsing is decent, but the cart is blank and I never completed anything. A shopper who can't reach checkout is a lost sale.

## 3. What's Working

- **Menu drawer (step 4).** The text is big and readable, and each row has plenty of space to tap. There's a clear Back arrow and an X. "Comfort Technologies" is a category, which is what I look for.
- **The top strip on the homepage.** "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Arch Fit" and "Max Cus…" are the things I shop on. The strip scrolls sideways, and "Max Cus…" is cut off, so it isn't obvious there's more.
- **Men's Shoes page.** The "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" tiles are exactly my categories. The Filter, Sort, Size and Width buttons are easy to find.
- **Product page.** Price ($88.00), the 4.5-star rating with 1,977 reviews, and the Klarna/Afterpay options are all easy to read. The Slip-ins "Hands Free" badge is prominent.
- **Size grid.** The size buttons are big and the sold-out sizes (6.5, 15, 16) are crossed out with a bell icon, so I know right away what's not available.
- **Add to Cart button.** It's large, dark blue and hard to miss.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Add to Cart didn't work.** I hit the button with no size selected. The "Please select a size" message was easy to miss, and it sits above the quantity box, not next to the button. If I'd missed it, I'd have thought the button was broken. The cart badge stayed at **0**.
- **The cart page was blank (step 8).** All I got was a white screen, with no header, no message and no "your cart is empty." I'd assume the site was broken and go to another store.
- **No login or personalization (step 3).** The "Log In" step shows the same homepage as step 1. I never saw a sign-in form, and nothing greeted me by name. The homepage shows a young couple in holiday outfits. There's nothing for a 62-year-old comfort shopper.
- **Popups (step 2).** It looks identical to step 1, so either nothing popped up or the screenshot didn't capture it.
- **Search for "comfort shoes" (step 9).** The first two results are women's shoes (Cypress, Relaxed Fit Parallel Lite). It doesn't know I came from the Men's section. 1,655 results is a lot with no guidance.
- **Promo text is tiny and confusing.** "BOGO 50% OFF," "25% OFF Work & Safety" and "Buy 1, Get 1 50% OFF! Applied at Cart" are small. "Applied at Cart" is a promise I couldn't check, because the cart never loaded.
- **No comparison content.** Nothing on screen compares Skechers to Hoka, Brooks or New Balance, and there's no price-vs-quality information beyond the star rating.
- **Men's Shoes grid (step 5).** The first two products I see are colorful graffiti-print sneakers, not the cushioned walking shoes I want. There's also no sign of arch support or cushioning on the product cards.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the blank cart page.** This is the most important one. It should always show a header, the items I added, or a clear "your cart is empty" message.
2. **Make the size error obvious.** Scroll to it, highlight the size grid, and put the message right above the Add to Cart button. Or preselect a size.
3. **Show a login screen and greet me by name when I'm signed in.** Show recently viewed shoes and my size.
4. **Make search follow the section I came from.** If I'm browsing Men's, show men's shoes first. Make "comfort" results lead with cushioned and arch-supported shoes.
5. **Put comfort first on the homepage.** Slip-ins, Arch Fit and cushioning should come before holiday-outfit photos. Use a photo that feels like my life, not a fashion shoot.
6. **Show comfort details on shoe cards.** Add a small line such as "Arch support," "Hands-free" or "Extra cushioning," and larger text for deals.
7. **Show the discount math.** Show the actual price after "Buy 1, Get 1 50% Off," so I can see it's a good deal.

## 6. Bottom Line

I like the shoes Skechers sells and the menu is easy to use. But I couldn't finish a purchase. A blank cart is the kind of thing that sends me to Famous Footwear or Brooks. If the cart worked and the search knew I was a man looking for comfort, this would be a 6 or 7. Right now it's a 3, same as yesterday.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

- Axe found 18 violations across 3 of the 8 steps (Homepage 10, View Cart 4, Search 4). Two are critical (`aria-required-parent`, `button-name`), both on the Homepage.
- `GET /cart/` returned **429**. The View Cart violations (no `<title>`, no `lang`, no `<main>`, no `<h1>`) are probably from the rate-limit error page, not the real cart template. Treat the View Cart accessibility results as unreliable.
- LCP and CLS were not captured for any step (`?`), so Core Web Vitals cannot be assessed.
- The Log In and Men category steps both report URL `https://www.skechers.com/`, and their HTML samples are identical to the Homepage's. The journey probably didn't navigate, or these are overlays/in-page state. The audit also has no data on those two pages' own markup.
- Of the 21 console errors, only 10 were provided (4 Summarizer API, 6 `unload` permissions-policy violations).

## Accessibility

- **Critical: `aria-required-parent` (Homepage).** An element with a child-only ARIA role (e.g. `listitem`, `tab`, `menuitem`) sits outside its required parent.
- **Critical: `button-name` (Homepage).** One or more buttons have no accessible name. Icon-only controls (menu, search, cart) are the usual cause.
- **Serious: `link-name` (Homepage).** Links have no discernible text, probably icon or image links without `aria-label`/alt text.
- **Landmark structure (Homepage, Search):**
  - `landmark-main-is-top-level` and `landmark-no-duplicate-main` (Homepage) mean there are multiple or nested `<main>` landmarks.
  - `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region` appear on both Homepage and Search. This points to a shared template or layout problem, not one page.
- **Headings:** `page-has-heading-one` fails on Homepage and View Cart.
- **Minor: `aria-allowed-role` (Homepage, Search).** An ARIA role is set on an element that doesn't permit it.
- **View Cart (likely the 429 error page):** `document-title`, `html-has-lang`, `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one` all fail. The Homepage has `lang="en"`, so the missing `lang` here points to a non-app response.
- **Not reported:** Men category, Men > Shoes, Product detail and Add to Cart have no violations listed. I can't tell whether they were scanned and clean, or not scanned.

## SEO

- The Homepage has no `<h1>` (per axe).
- `<html lang="en">` is present on the Homepage.
- The truncated HTML doesn't show `<title>`, meta description, canonical, Open Graph, hreflang or JSON-LD. I can't assess any of them.
- Log In and Men category report the Homepage URL, so there is no evidence of distinct URLs, titles or canonicals for those pages.
- A 429 on `/cart/` could affect crawlers if a bot hits it, though the cart should be `noindex` regardless.

## Performance

- **TTFB:** the Homepage is 485 ms. Log In and Men category are 452 ms. Men > Shoes through Search are 19–24 ms, with View Cart at 105 ms.
  - 19–24 ms is implausibly low for origin responses. These are probably cache hits, bfcache or prerender restores, or stale navigation entries. Confirm the metric collection before relying on them.
  - The 452 ms on Log In and Men, close to the Homepage's 485 ms, fits the "no real navigation" theory above.
- **LCP/CLS:** missing for every step. This is a measurement gap in the harness. For example, the `PerformanceObserver` may not be registered early enough, or `buffered: true` may be missing.
- **Third-party load:** the Homepage `<head>` pulls in over 20 third-party scripts. These include GTM, GA4, Google Ads/DoubleClick, TikTok, Pinterest, Bing, Yahoo, Taboola, Snapchat, Reddit, Facebook, Nextdoor, Criteo, Quantum Metric, Monetate, Pulse Insights, Impact, Bloomreach, Tune and StackAdapt. All are `async`, so they don't block parsing, but they compete for main thread and bandwidth on mobile.
- **Monetate (`async`):** client-side personalization applied after load is a known CLS and flicker risk. It is worth checking once CLS is captured.
- **Fonts:** Web fonts load through `webfontloader.js` from cdnjs, which adds a third-party origin and a JS-dependent font path. A first-party `@font-face` with `font-display` and preload would remove that dependency.
- **Link handling:** a click handler calls `preventDefault` on `.js-ignore-click` links until `app:JsLoaded`. Those links do nothing until the main JS has loaded, so the delay is visible on slow mobile connections.

## Mobile Optimization

- The `--vh` CSS variable is set inline by JS (`6.64px`, implying a ~664px viewport). That is a standard workaround for mobile browser chrome, but it depends on JS running before layout.
- The viewport meta tag and touch target sizes can't be verified from the truncated HTML, and axe didn't report `target-size` or `meta-viewport`.
- No other issues found in the provided data.

## Console & Network Errors

- **429 `https://www.skechers.com/cart/`:** the server rate-limited the request, probably through bot or WAF protection reacting to automated traffic. It invalidates the View Cart step and may mean real cart access is flaky under load.
- **`Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document` (6 of 10 shown):** a script registers an `unload` handler, which is deprecated and blocked by policy. It also prevents bfcache restores. The offending script isn't identified. The usual sources are third-party tags, so check the stack traces.
- **`Unsupported Summarizer API languages…` (4 of 10 shown):** this comes from Chrome's built-in Summarizer API being called with unsupported languages. It may be an injected script or browser extension in the test profile, not site code. Check the source URL before attributing it to Skechers.
- **Missing data:** 11 of the 21 errors were not provided, so there may be other error types.

## Recommendations

1. **Fix the harness first.**
   - Capture LCP and CLS with buffered observers.
   - Check that Log In and Men actually navigate to distinct URLs.
   - Re-run View Cart with a delay or retry on 429.
   - Log the full console error list with source URLs.
2. **Site, critical/serious accessibility:**
   - Give icon-only buttons and links accessible names (`aria-label`, visible text or alt text).
   - Fix the ARIA parent/child structure.
   - Add an `<h1>` to the Homepage.
3. **Site, landmarks:**
   - Use a single top-level `<main>`.
   - Give `<aside>` and `<nav>` regions unique labels.
   - Move stray content into landmarks.
4. **Site, cart and error pages:** make rate-limit and error responses a full HTML document with `<title>`, `lang` and `<main>`. Review the WAF rate limits on `/cart/`.
5. **Site, `unload` handlers:** find the script that registers them and replace with `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`.
6. **Site, third-party tags:** audit the 20+ tags and defer or remove low-value ones. Check the Monetate CLS impact once CLS is measured.
7. **Site, fonts:** self-host fonts with `font-display` and preload instead of loading `webfontloader.js` from cdnjs.
