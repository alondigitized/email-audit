# [Preview] What we’re wearing this fall
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- This one caught me off guard after a solid week of birthday sale hammering. The subject shift away from "IT'S OUR BDAY" to a fall preview felt like DSW finally turning the page, and I'll give them credit for that — the cadence reset was welcome. But when I actually got into the email, the birthday sale was still right there at the top. Twenty dollars off, Become a VIP. So what is this, exactly — a fall preview or another bday push? Feels like two emails got stitched together and nobody made a call on which one was running.
- The Fall Trend Watch section left me cold. "Setting difference moves for fall," Mary Janes, young women posing with shoes — that's not my world. I'm a wide-width guy looking for cushioned walkers, not a fashion editor. I had to scroll past a whole lifestyle section to find "Shop Wide Width" and "Shop Wide Calf," which is genuinely the most useful thing in this entire email for someone with my feet. DSW buried the lead.
- Net result: I'd skim past the trend content without blinking, and I might click the $20 VIP offer if I haven't already burned it this week — but honestly I can't even remember at this point, that's how many DSW emails I've gotten. Not forwarding this to anyone.

## What's working

- **$20 off birthday offer is visible without scrolling** — the sale banner at the top of the email lands immediately on mobile, easy to register
- **"Shop Wide Width" and "Shop Wide Calf" links** — this is the single most relevant thing in the email for my feet, and the fact that DSW surfaced it at all tells me they at least have the data somewhere
- **"Shop By Size" grid** — practical, tappable, skips me past the catalog hunt; I appreciate that
- **No render bugs** — loads clean, images are all there, nothing overlapping or broken
- **The $5,000 contest** — I'm not going to win, but that banner got a second glance out of me; it's a decent scroll-stopper

## What's weak

- **"Fall Trend Watch" reads like a women's fashion magazine, not a men's shoe email** — Mary Janes is literally labeled in the photo grid; whoever built the segment list did not have me in mind
- **The subject line feels like an accidental QA miss** — "[Preview]" in brackets at the front looks like a template tag that escaped, not an intentional creative choice; erodes trust before I even open
- **Two campaigns in one email, and neither wins** — birthday sale up top, fall editorial in the middle, $5,000 contest below; I don't know what DSW wants me to do first, and that confusion costs clicks
- **No deadline on the $20 off** — I've seen this offer at least three times this week; without an end date I have zero urgency to act today versus next Wednesday
- **Preheader leaks spacer junk** — the meaningful copy ("+ Join VIP for $20 off shoes") is good, but my inbox showed a long string of blank character placeholders after it; it's the kind of sloppy detail that makes a brand look rushed

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment men out of this send and lead with men's fall comfort shoes** — or at absolute minimum, swap the Mary Janes section for something that doesn't actively remind me this email wasn't built for me
- 2. **Pick one campaign** — fall preview or birthday sale; both running together at full volume dilutes each; pick the one with the stronger hook and give it the whole email
- 3. **Move "Shop Wide Width" to the hero zone** — for customers with fit concerns, that's the reason to click, not "Setting difference moves for fall"
- 4. **Put a hard expiration on the $20 off** — even "offer ends Sunday" would push me to decide today
- 5. **Drop the "[Preview]" from the subject** — if it's intentional branding, it's not landing; if it's accidental, this is a QA fix

- **Preheader Alt A:** `New arrivals built for walkers. VIP offer ends soon.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully audit** — body links not included in the truncated source. From what is visible:

- All web font assets are served from `braze-images.com` (Braze CDN). If that CDN has an outage, fallback fonts trigger silently — not a deliverability risk but worth noting.
- WOFF font URLs lack cache-busting query strings (e.g., `original.woff` with no `?timestamp`), while TTF variants include `?1755182939`. Inconsistency means stale WOFF files may be served after an asset update.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Two malformed `<meta>` tags** — missing required `http-equiv` attribute:

```html
<!-- ACTUAL (broken) -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta content="IE=Edge" />

<!-- CORRECT -->
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=Edge" />
```

Without `http-equiv`, the charset declaration is inert in Outlook and older clients, and the IE compatibility hint is ignored entirely.

**Invalid CSS property casing in all `@font-face` blocks:**

```css
Src: url("...woff") format('woff');  /* capital S — invalid */
src: url("...woff") format('woff');  /* correct */
```

CSS property names are case-sensitive in some parsers. Major clients (Gmail Android, older Samsung Mail) may silently drop the `@font-face` rule, causing fallback fonts to render instead of Futura PT / HelveticaNow. This affects all 10 declared font faces.

**Redundant `Margin` + `margin` in reset:**

```css
h1, h2, h3, h4, h5, h6, p, ul, li {
    margin: 0 !important;
    Margin: 0;   /* also invalid casing, redundant */
}
```

Minor, but indicates a pattern of copy-paste from an older template without linting.

**`<title>` is bare "DSW"** — some clients surface the `<title>` as a preview or tab label. Not a blocking issue, but a more descriptive value (matching subject line) is preferred.

**`alt` text on images** — cannot audit; image tags not included in truncated source.
