# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-16**
## Executive summary

- There she is. Again. The pink floral D'Lites sneaker, big and centered right under the "READY TO CHECK OUT?" banner, same as every other cart-reminder Skechers has fired at me this week. I don't have a cart with a women's floral sneaker in it — never have — so this whole email opens on the wrong foot before I even get to the good stuff further down.
- And there IS good stuff further down, which is the frustrating part. The "shop additional styles" grid down below actually has men's items mixed in — Arch Fit Arcade slip-ins, a Murdock slip-in in gray, even some basketball shoes — but they're buried as tiny thumbnails after the hero has already lost me. If they'd led with any one of those instead of the floral shoe, I'd have kept scrolling.
- Would I click? No. I'd glance at it, recognize the same recycled cart nag, and archive it. Not forwarding this to anybody.

## What's working

- The "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" banner is bold and the CTA is unmistakable — I know exactly what button to hit if that were actually my cart.
- Buried in the grid there's a men's slip-in Arch Fit and a gray Murdock slip-in — those are actually in my lane, cushioned, hands-free styles I'd look twice at.
- The 20% off for Skechers Plus members with a clear code (PREVIEW) and expiration date (9/20) is a real, honest offer with fine print that isn't hiding anything shady.
- Shop Now Pay Later and Curbside Pickup icons are clean and easy to tap on a phone.

## What's weak

- The hero product is, once again, the pink floral D'Lites — not my cart, not my style, and I've now seen this exact shoe chase me through five or six emails this week.
- Nothing in the top two-thirds of the email signals "menswear" or "comfort shoe guy" at all — it's all rendered like I'm a 30-something woman abandoning a floral sneaker.
- The genuinely relevant men's items are shrunk down to thumbnail size in a grid, competing with kids' basketball shoes and women's styles, instead of getting their own spotlight.
- "Ready to check out?" implies urgency about a real cart, but since it's the wrong shoe, it just reads as a broken personalization, which makes me trust the "membership" and "offer" parts of the email less too.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the cart-recall logic first — if I never put that pink floral shoe in a cart, stop leading every email with it. That's the single biggest lift available here.
- 2. If Skechers can't confirm what's actually in my cart, don't run a "complete your order" hero at all — swap to a general men's comfort/sale hero like the sweater-vest guy from the "Last call for extra 20% off" send, which scored well with me.
- 3. Promote the men's Arch Fit and Murdock slip-ins out of the small grid and into a second hero block — those are the shoes that'd actually get me to tap.
- 4. Trim the send frequency — this is at least the sixth near-identical cart email this week; space these out or I'll start tuning out the subject line entirely.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Arch Fit slip-ins are still here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Skechers Plus members save 20% through 9/20`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Comfort you browsed, now with a code`
