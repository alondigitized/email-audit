# Extra in your bag = extra savings!
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- Well, at least I knew the sender before I even looked — "Crocs" is a name I recognize. But the subject line threw me. "Extra in your bag = extra savings" — okay, is that a BOGO deal? A tiered discount? I had to open just to figure out what they were selling, and that's not a compliment. And when I did open it, the first thing I noticed was a wall of blank white space staring back at me — big empty rectangles where something was supposed to be. Then finally a flip-flop on a red-white-blue background and a headline that said "25% Off Select Work Styles." Work styles? I retired. I'm not shopping for work shoes. The subject teased a volume deal and the body delivered a discount on something I don't need. That mismatch killed whatever momentum the brand name gave them at the start.
- I won't be clicking this one. I've gotten too many of these from Crocs lately — back on June 23rd they actually ran a smart multi-buy deal (Buy 2, save 20%; Buy 3, save 30%) that at least had real math in it. This email feels like a step backward from that. I'd mark it read and move on.

## What's working

- Crocs is a recognizable sender — I didn't have to wonder who this was from.
- The red, white, and blue hero image has a seasonal July Fourth feel that at least signals they're paying attention to the calendar.
- "25% OFF" is clear — one number, easy to parse, no arithmetic required.
- The fine print at the bottom confirms an end date (July 2nd), so there's a real deadline buried in there.

## What's weak

- Massive blank white sections top to bottom — whatever images were supposed to fill those blocks didn't render, and it looks broken and amateurish on my phone.
- The subject says "extra in your bag = extra savings" but the email body just says 25% off select styles — I was expecting a tiered or BOGO deal and got a plain discount instead. Bait-and-switch feeling.
- "Work styles" — I'm 62 and retired. The category targeting missed me completely. I want walking shoes, slip-ons, comfort sandals. Not work clogs.
- No visible CTA button anywhere in the body — how am I supposed to click if there's nowhere obvious to tap?
- Preview text was blank garbage — invisible spacer characters that showed nothing useful in my inbox. After the subject, that was my last chance to understand what the email was about before opening.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the render.** Those blank white sections need to be tested on actual mobile clients before sending. Nothing tanks trust faster than a broken layout on my iPhone.
- 2. **Align the subject to the body offer.** If it's 25% off, say 25% off in the subject. The clever "extra in your bag" wordplay landed nowhere because the body didn't follow through with a multi-item mechanic.
- 3. **Retarget the category.** "Work styles" is the wrong hook for a comfort-casual customer. Lead with "walking sandals," "all-day comfort," or "cushioned slip-ons" to speak to someone like me.
- 4. **Add a visible CTA button** with specific copy — "Shop Sandals Now" or "See Men's Comfort Styles" — something I can actually tap.
- 5. **Surface the deadline in the hero.** "Ends July 2nd" in tiny fine print does nothing; put it under the headline where I can see it.
- 6. **Write a real preheader.** The invisible spacer-character trick just wastes the one line of preview copy I'd see before deciding to open.
- **Subject Alt A:** `25% off comfort sandals — ends July 2`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Walk into summer: 25% off select styles`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free economy shipping on qualifying orders. Shop men's sandals and slip-ons now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Offer ends July 2nd at 11:59pm MT. Select styles, discount applied in cart.`

**Malformed `<meta>` tags (missing `http-equiv`):**

```html
<!-- as-sent (broken) -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta content="IE=edge" />

<!-- correct form -->
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
```

Without `http-equiv`, these tags are inert. The charset declaration won't be honored by clients that parse it strictly. Low-severity in practice (UTF-8 is widely assumed), but worth correcting.

**Missing `alt` attribute:**
