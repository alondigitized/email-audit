# This is perfect for you!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-18**
## Executive summary

- Another Skechers email, and another one that thinks it knows me when it doesn't. The subject says "perfect for you." The hero is a black Twinkle Toes light-up kid's sneaker with rhinestones on the toe. I'm 62. I have never looked at that shoe. "STILL THINKING ABOUT IT?" assumes I left something behind. I didn't. This is the same cart-nag family as "Your cart expires soon!" and "Did you forget something?" from the last few days, only with a different shoe.
- The grid underneath is a grab bag: women's sneakers, two boys' Garage Sneakers, a women's leather slip-in. One shoe is for me, the Men's Slip-ins John Deere waterproof relaxed fit. It's small and sits in the top right corner of the grid. It's the only thing in the whole email that speaks to a hands-free, comfortable shoe for a man my age.
- The 20% off for members is the only part worth anything. It's decent, it's clear, and the Oct 21 deadline is real. But it's buried under kids' shoes. I wouldn't forward this. I might tap through to the men's section for the 20%, but I'd probably just mark it read.

## What's working

- The "MEMBERS TAKE 20% OFF" block is big and easy to read, with a clear "Valid thru 10/21" deadline.
- The Men's Slip-ins John Deere waterproof shoe is exactly my kind of shoe, hands-free and relaxed fit.
- The category list (Women, Men, Kids, Clothing, Sale) is large type with big tap targets. My thumbs and eyes can handle that.
- The Skechers logo is familiar, and the type is big and readable on my phone.

## What's weak

- The hero is a kid's light-up sneaker. It's the wrong shoe for me by a mile, and it takes up the best real estate.
- "STILL THINKING ABOUT IT?" and "You've got great taste!" imply I showed interest. I didn't. After several of these cart nags this week, it feels like a stranger pretending to know me.
- Only 1 of the 6 grid shoes is a men's shoe. Two are boys' shoes and three are women's, so the "options we think you'll love" are way off.
- The fine print is tiny, and the discount excludes sale items and more. Nothing says what a member price actually saves me on a men's slip-in.
- The subject line promises something personal and the email doesn't deliver it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Don't send an "abandoned browse" hero to someone who hasn't browsed that item. Lead with a men's Slip-ins shoe, and put the 20% right next to it.
- 2. Fill the grid with men's walking and slip-in styles, and add arch-support and cushioning callouts.
- 3. Move the member 20% banner above the grid.
- 4. Make the CTA say "Shop Men's Slip-ins" instead of "Take Another Look."
- 5. Cut back on the frequency of these cart-style sends.
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% off Slip-ins for members, ends 10/21`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Men's Slip-ins: easy on, all-day comfort`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Members save 20% on full-price men's walking shoes through Oct 21.`
