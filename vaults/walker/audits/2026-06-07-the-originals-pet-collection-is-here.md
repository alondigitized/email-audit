# The Originals Pet Collection is here 🐶🐱
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- I opened this one fully expecting some kind of shoe sale follow-up, maybe something like that Adistar XLG 2.0 from earlier today, and instead I got four photos of dogs and cats wearing adidas collars. I sat there for a second wondering if I'd accidentally opened the wrong email. The Originals Pet Collection. For my dog, I guess — except I don't have one.
- Look, I'm not mad at adidas for selling pet gear. Fine. But I have opened something like six or seven of their emails this week and the batting average of "things relevant to Walker Miles" is not great. World Cup jerseys, James Harden shoes, and now a cat in a tiny adidas shirt. The copy down below says "treat your companion to iconic tees and collars for a style statement that matches your own" — and I thought, my companion is my La-Z-Boy recliner, and it doesn't need a collar.
- I marked it as read and moved on. Would not forward it. Would not click it. Would actively wonder why adidas is spending email budget on me for this.

## What's working

- The 2×2 pet photo grid is clean and the images render well — no broken pictures, no garbled layout.
- The "ORIGINALS" brand overlay is consistent and the typography is legible — big enough for me to read without pinching.
- adidas is a brand I know and trust, so the sender name at least got me to open rather than trash.
- The body copy is short — one paragraph — which I appreciate. They didn't bury me in text.

## What's weak

- This email has nothing to do with me. I'm a 62-year-old guy shopping for comfortable walking shoes. A pet apparel collection isn't my aisle.
- There is no offer. No percent off, no dollar amount, no free shipping. Nothing to give me a reason to click even if I had a dog.
- The only CTA I can see is a small "Shop Now" tucked in the navigation banner at the top — that is not a hero CTA, that's furniture.
- "Browser-based viewing only" appears to be bleeding into the preview text slot, which is wasted real estate and looks sloppy.
- The demographic signals are four-legged animals. Not a single image of an older man, not a shoe, not a comfort product anywhere.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Don't send this segment to me at all.** If adidas has any behavioral data on my opens — I've been clicking on walking shoes and sale footwear — this email should have been suppressed for my segment. That's the single biggest fix.
- 2. **Add an actual offer.** "The collection is here" is a press release, not a promotion. Even "free shipping on pet orders over $35" gives me a reason to click.
- 3. **Put a real hero CTA button in the email body.** "Shop the Pet Collection" as a visible button, not just a nav link.
- 4. **If you must send this to me, cross-sell.** A footer block saying "Not shopping for a pet? Check out our comfort walkers →" would at least rescue the send for people like me.

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard failures confirmed by QA: a broken deep-link URL scheme and a missing plain-text fallback. Multiple non-HTTPS image assets and missing alt attributes represent deliverability and accessibility risk at scale.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Malformed deep-link URL**
The click-tracker resolves to a relative-style path that is not a valid URL type:
```
unknown url type: '/g/2d243c6f-d035-4342-95b3-f1d537ced76f?txn=54718080-62c5-11f1-8cc3-0690aac23d16'
```
The outer redirect URL (`dv.adidas.com/o/...`) likely decodes to an internal deep-link path rather than an absolute `https://` URL, causing link resolution failure in some clients. This is a broken CTA — the destination is unreachable via that path.

**[WARN] 17 tracking links skipped**
All click links route through `click.comms.adidas.com` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud click-redirect). Normal for ESP infrastructure, but the broken deep-link above lives inside this redirect chain, meaning the failure is silent until after the redirect resolves.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Two `<img>` tags served over HTTP (not HTTPS)**

| Asset | HTTP Source |
|---|---|
| "arrow" (`b0c011a2-...jpg`) — appears twice | `http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg` |
| Tracking pixel (`8Ghe0OgFoF2mxQH...`) | `http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019ea454785a-...` |

Gmail and Outlook.com proxy all images through HTTPS; mixed-content HTTP sources are blocked or silently broken in many clients. The arrow asset appears to be a UI element, not just a pixel — its failure will produce a visible broken image.

**[WARN] Missing `alt` text on two images**
- Hero/dynamic image: `dv.adidas.com/o/2d243c6f-...` — no `alt` attribute
- Tracking pixel: `click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...` — no `alt` attribute (acceptable for pixels if `alt=""` is set explicitly; currently absent)

**`<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` — missing `http-equiv`**
The charset meta tag is malformed: `<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` is missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. This is harmless in modern clients but incorrect per HTML 4.01 (the declared DOCTYPE).

**Duplicate `@font-face` blocks**
`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are declared twice across two separate `<style>` blocks with identical fallback stacks. No functional breakage, but adds ~800 bytes of dead CSS weight.

### 5. Compliance

QA reports text version as 0 characters. CAN-SPAM and most ESPs require a plain-text MIME part. Its absence also triggers spam filters (SpamAssassin `MIME_HTML_ONLY` rule adds scoring). This is a sending infrastructure issue — the SFMC template is not generating a multipart/alternative envelope.

**Unsubscribe:** Not verifiable from truncated source, but the footer CSS classes (`mobile-footer-padding-block`, `mobile-privacy-transactional`) indicate a standard SFMC footer block is present. Assumed compliant pending full source review.

**Authentication headers:** Sending domain is `us-news.comms.adidas.com` (SFMC subdomain). DKIM, SPF, and DMARC alignment cannot be confirmed from HTML source alone — no issues flagged, but should be verified at the MTA level.
