# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-10-28**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I walked the site on my phone and never got a shoe into my cart. At step 7 I tapped Add to Cart without picking a size. The page said "Please select a size", and the cart badge stayed at 0. At step 8 the cart page was a blank white screen with nothing on it. I also never got logged in, so nothing was personalized.

The parts that suit me are good. The "Slip-ins", "Wide Fit", "Arch Fit" and "Max Cushion" tabs, the "Hands Free Slip-Ins" tile, and the men's menu are all easy to find. The checkout path is what lets it down.

Compared with yesterday's 3/10, I see no real improvement. I can't tell whether anything got worse, because I only have today's screenshots.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** That matches yesterday. The browsing is decent, but I couldn't buy anything. A blank cart page and a login that did nothing would send me to Hoka or Brooks.

## 3. What's Working

- **Comfort tabs under the header.** Slip-ins, Wide Fit, Arch Fit and Max Cushion are what I shop for. I could see them without hunting.
- **Men's menu.** The drawer has large rows and clear labels: Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections. The text is big and the rows are easy to tap. "Comfort Technologies" speaks to me.
- **Men's Shoes page.** The "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" tiles are right at the top. Filter, Sort, Size and Width buttons are close by. The page says "801 results", so I know how big the list is.
- **Size picker.** The size buttons are big, roomy boxes that are easy to tap. Sold-out 6.5 is crossed out, which is clear.
- **Add to Cart button.** It is wide, dark blue and easy to hit.
- **Price and payment info.** The product page shows $100.00 and "From $17/month" with Klarna and Afterpay. The price is easy to read, and it shows "Excluded from promotions" up front.
- **Search.** "comfort shoes" returned 1,647 results, mostly cushioned sneakers and slip-ons ("Contour Fit", "Relaxed Fit"), so it's relevant.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart was blank.** Step 8 is an empty white screen. I can't tell whether it failed to load or the cart was just empty. Either way I have nowhere to go next.
- **Add to Cart didn't work as I'd expect.** I got a red "Please select a size" message with no item added. The error is fair, but the size grid sat well below the product photo. I had to scroll past a lot to find it. The cart badge stayed at 0.
- **Login didn't do anything.** Steps 1, 2 and 3 look identical. The "Login or Join to Reveal" banner and the "Login to Unlock Mystery Discount" lines in search results were still there, so I was never logged in. I saw no sign it knew who I was.
- **The "Mystery Deal" and "trick or treat" gimmick.** I don't want to log in to find out what a discount is. It isn't clear what I'd get, and the red "Login to Unlock Mystery Discount" text repeats on every product.
- **The homepage is a soccer ad.** The first screen is a big Harry Kane hero image. Nothing on it tells me about cushioning, arch support or slip-ons.
- **The product I landed on doesn't fit me.** The "UNO – Alma De Guerrero" is a $100 skull-print fashion sneaker. In the first screen I see nothing about cushioning or arch support, and no reviews.
- **No comparison against other brands.** I saw nothing that helps me weigh Skechers against Hoka, Brooks or New Balance, and nothing on price versus quality.
- **Small text.** "Excluded from promotions", the Klarna lines, the size chart link and the "Women's" labels are small. I'd have to zoom to read them comfortably. The top tab row also runs off the edge ("Max Cus…"), and I had to swipe to see the rest.
- **Search returned women's shoes first.** Women's items ("Women's" under the name) showed up in my first results, though I'm shopping men's.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page first.** It should always show something: the items, or a clear "your cart is empty" message with a button back to shoes. Never a blank screen.
2. **Make Add to Cart say what happened.** If a size is missing, scroll to the sizes and highlight them. After a successful add, show a confirmation and update the badge.
3. **Make login work, and make it count.** After I log in, the banners should change, for example "Welcome back, Walker", and the mystery-discount text should go away or show the real offer.
4. **Show the discount without making me log in.** Or at least say what it is ("up to X% off") so I know whether it's worth the effort.
5. **Put comfort first on the homepage.** Lead with Slip-ins, Arch Fit and Max Cushion, ahead of the soccer hero.
6. **Add comfort details to product pages.** Show things like cushioning level, arch support, slip-in or hands-free, and a "walking comfort" rating high on the page. Add reviews from people my age.
7. **Add "compare" content.** A simple "Skechers vs. Hoka / Brooks / New Balance" page, or price-per-comfort-feature info, would give me a reason to stay.
8. **Make the text bigger.** Make the small labels bigger, and let the tab row wrap or add an arrow so I can tell it scrolls.
9. **Filter search results by men's when I'm browsing men's.**

## 6. Bottom Line

I like what Skechers makes. The menus, the Slip-ins tabs and the Hands Free tile are the kind of thing I'm after. But I couldn't finish a purchase: no shoe in the cart, a blank cart page, and a login that didn't register. Until the cart works, I'm shopping at Hoka or Brooks. Same score as yesterday, 3/10.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The journey was partly invalidated by bot mitigation. The Kasada fingerprint endpoint (`/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.940`) returned 429 six times, and `/cart/` itself returned 429. The "View Cart" page the audit evaluated is therefore a block or error response, not the real cart.

Three other limits on the data:
- **Web Vitals:** LCP and CLS were not captured for any step.
- **URLs:** every step reports `https://www.skechers.com/`, so the URL never changed.
- **HTML samples:** the Log In and Men category samples are identical to the homepage's. Either navigation did not occur, or the samples were captured from the same document.

Treat everything except the homepage and, partly, search as unverified.

## Accessibility

18 axe violations were reported across three pages.

**Homepage (10 violations):**
- **Critical:**
  - `aria-required-parent`: ARIA roles that require a parent role are not inside one.
  - `button-name`: one or more buttons have no accessible name.
- **Serious:** `link-name`, links with no discernible text.
- **Landmark structure (moderate):**
  - `landmark-main-is-top-level` and `landmark-no-duplicate-main` together indicate nested or multiple `<main>` elements.
  - `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` and `landmark-unique` also fail.
  - `region` shows content sitting outside any landmark.
- **Headings (moderate):** `page-has-heading-one` fails, so there is no `<h1>`.
- **Minor:** `aria-allowed-role` fails.

**Search results:** `aria-allowed-role`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region` repeat the homepage's template-level landmark problems.

**View Cart:** `document-title`, `html-has-lang`, `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one` all fail. The lack of title, `lang`, main landmark and h1 matches a bare 429 response, not the cart template. These four are probably artifacts of the block page.

The homepage does set `<html lang="en">`, and the repeated landmark failures point to shared header/footer markup.

## SEO

- **Headings:** The homepage and search results have no level-one heading (`page-has-heading-one`). This is the main confirmed SEO finding.
- **Rate limiting:** `/cart/` returned 429. Cart pages are normally non-indexable, so that is low risk. If the 429s also apply to crawlers on indexable URLs, that would hurt crawl budget.
- **Head tags:** The HTML samples are truncated before `<title>`, meta description, canonical, Open Graph and structured data. These could not be verified.
- **Step URLs:** All steps report the same URL. If that reflects real behavior, such as client-side state changes with no URL change, those views are not independently crawlable.

## Performance

- **TTFB:** The homepage was 346 ms. Every other step was 23–31 ms except View Cart at 121 ms. Those low values likely reflect cached responses or non-document navigations, so they are not representative of cold loads.
- **LCP and CLS:** Not captured (`?`) for any step, so there are no Core Web Vitals to assess.
- **Third-party load:** The homepage head injects about 20 third-party scripts. All are `async`, but they compete for main-thread time and bandwidth on mobile. They include:
  - GTM, gtag, Bing UET and Quantum Metric
  - TikTok, Pinterest, Snap, Reddit, Nextdoor, Taboola and Yahoo pixels
  - Monetate, Pulse Insights, Impact, RTB House, Bloomreach and Tune
- **Duplicate loads:**
  - `s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` appears twice on the homepage and Log In samples.
  - `tags.creativecdn.com/...js` appears twice in the Men category sample.
- **Fonts:** Mulish loads through `webfontloader.js` from cdnjs, with `wf-*-active` classes toggled by JS. This adds a third-party dependency and a risk of FOUT and layout shift. It can't be confirmed without a CLS measurement.

## Mobile Optimization

- The `--vh` custom property is set inline on `<html>` by JS (`6.64px`). This is the legacy workaround for mobile viewport height, and `dvh`/`svh` units can replace it.
- The viewport meta tag and touch-target sizes could not be evaluated because the head is truncated and no target-size rule was reported.
- Nothing else mobile-specific was observed.

## Console & Network Errors

27 console errors were reported, but only 10 are listed, in three recurring types:
1. **`429` resource failures:** These match the Kasada `/fp` requests. Kasada (`x-kpsdk`) is likely rate-limiting or flagging the automated client, so the cause is probably the test harness and not a defect that affects real users.
2. **`Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed`:** A script, probably third-party, registers an `unload` handler. Chrome blocks it, and it would also prevent bfcache eligibility.
3. **`Unsupported Summarizer API languages…`:** This comes from a script calling the Chrome built-in Summarizer API with unsupported language options. It is non-fatal, but the call is failing.

The 7 network errors are 6 × 429 on the Kasada `/fp` endpoint and 1 × 429 on `https://www.skechers.com/cart/`. The cart 429 is why the View Cart axe results are unreliable.

## Recommendations

1. **Re-run the audit** with a bot-protection allowlist or a slower pace, and with real per-step navigation. Verify each step's URL and DOM before treating its results as valid. Capture LCP, CLS and INP.
2. **Fix homepage and search accessibility, in priority order:**
   - Give every button and link an accessible name (`button-name`, `link-name`).
   - Ensure ARIA roles sit inside their required parents.
   - Use a single top-level `<main>`.
   - Make landmark labels unique.
   - Add one `<h1>`.
3. **Audit the third-party tag stack in GTM or the consent layer:**
   - Remove the duplicate `ytc.js` and `creativecdn` loads.
   - Defer non-essential pixels until after interaction or consent.
4. **Find the script registering the `unload` handler** and switch it to `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`.
5. **Fix or remove the Summarizer API call** that passes unsupported languages.
6. **Replace the JS-driven `--vh` workaround** with `dvh`/`svh`, and self-host or preload the fonts to reduce the dependency on cdnjs.
7. **Confirm `<title>`, meta description, canonical and structured data** on the full HTML, since the samples were truncated.
