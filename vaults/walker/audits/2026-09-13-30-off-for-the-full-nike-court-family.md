## Executive summary

- Well, this is a new brand knocking on my inbox — Shoe Carnival, not one I've bought from, and it's a Nike/Jordan "Full Court Family" sale top to bottom. Cute photo of a dad and son jogging in the park, but that's about the extent of the appeal to me. There's not a single walking shoe, slip-on, or comfort-brand name in the whole thing — it's basketball-court sneakers, kids' sizing, and a "Family Athletics Sale" pushing Jordan and Converse at $59.98. None of that is what gets my attention.
- The one thing that did catch my eye was the coupon block near the bottom — "$15 Off $99.98+ or $30 Off $149.98+" with an actual code, STEPUP. That's a real, usable discount structure, and if it applied to a Hoka or Skechers walking shoe I'd take it seriously. But everything above it is Nike/Jordan/Converse/adidas family athletic gear, so the coupon feels bolted onto an email that was never built for me.
- I'd mark this one as read and move on. Wouldn't click, wouldn't forward — maybe I'd mention the "Word Stride" game thing to my grandkids since it's clearly aimed at that crowd, not me.

- The "$15/$30 off" coupon block is legible with a clear code and dollar thresholds — no fine-print hunting required for that part.
- Layout is clean, no overlapping text or broken images that I could see.
- Shoe Perks mention at the top at least signals there's a loyalty angle, even though it's buried under sign-up copy.

## What's weak

- Every product shown — Jordans, Nike court shoes, Converse, adidas family athletics — is basketball/kids/family sport gear, nothing for a 62-year-old looking for arch support and cushioning.
- No comfort language anywhere: no "cushioned," "arch support," "all-day comfort," nothing that signals this brand even carries what I look for.
- The models are a father and young son — fine for a family sale, but it tells me clearly this email isn't built with me in mind.
- "Family Athletics Sale" pricing starts at $59.98 for adults — not framed as a value or comfort buy, just a team-sport basics sale.
- The coupon code at the bottom is generic store-wide, which almost makes the Nike/Jordan wall above it feel like wasted real estate for someone like me.

- 2. Add a men's comfort/walking shoe module below the coupon block — even one row of Skechers Hands Free Slip-ins or Hoka walkers would give me a reason to scroll instead of bail after the hero.
- 3. Make the $15/$30 coupon the headline, not an afterthought — that's the one universally useful piece of this email; put it up top where everyone sees it regardless of what's on sale.
