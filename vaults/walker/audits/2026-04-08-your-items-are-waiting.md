# Your Items Are Waiting!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-08**
## Executive summary

- A competent but underpowered cart abandonment email. The creative leads with a clever, self-aware headline ("WE NOTICED YOU NOTICING US...") that earns attention, but the momentum fades quickly. The hero product gets too little emphasis, the 20% off banner arrives late and without urgency framing, and a bloated lower half dilutes the single-action focus this email needs. The bones are right; the execution needs sharper prioritization.
- Functional recovery email that will convert some abandoners, but leaves measurable revenue on the table by not committing to one job.

## What's working

- **Headline voice.** "WE NOTICED YOU NOTICING US… YOUR CART IS STILL WAITING…" is self-aware and distinct — it avoids the generic "You left something behind" template language and creates a moment of personality.
- **Abandoned item is shown.** The specific product (Max Cushioning Arch Fit 2.0 - Aemon) is surfaced with its name and a clear "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" CTA — the primary conversion action is unambiguous.
- **Cross-sell module.** "HERE'S SOME MORE GREAT OPTIONS WE THINK YOU'LL LOVE" with a product row is a smart hedge for abandoners who weren't fully sold on the cart item.
- **20% off incentive.** A discount offer exists, which gives hesitant abandoners a financial nudge.

## What's weak

- **Discount is buried.** The 20% off banner appears below the cross-sell grid — well past the fold for most readers. The single biggest conversion lever in the email is an afterthought in layout hierarchy.
- **No urgency anywhere.** No "limited time," no stock scarcity, no expiration on the offer. Cart abandonment emails live and die by FOMO; this one has none.
- **Hero product gets minimal visual real estate.** The cart item image is small. For a shoe brand competing visually, a larger hero shot of the specific abandoned product would do more work than the headline.
- **Lower third is a department-store menu.** WOMEN / MEN / KIDS / CLOTHING / NEW ARRIVALS / SALE category links, app download, curbside pickup, buy-now-pay-later, and a store locator all crowd the bottom half. This is a cart recovery email, not a homepage. The noise dilutes intent.
- **"SHOP ALL BEST SELLERS" CTA is a distraction.** Placing this dark, prominent button right below the cross-sell grid routes users away from completing the original cart rather than toward it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the 20% off offer to the hero.** Place it immediately below the headline and above the abandoned product — make the incentive visible before the user has to scroll.
- 2. **Add an expiration to the discount.** "Offer ends tonight" or "48 hours only" will lift click-through without any copy change to the discount itself.
- 3. **Enlarge the abandoned product image.** Give the Arch Fit 2.0 a proper hero treatment — a lifestyle shot or at minimum a larger product image. The shoe is the reason they're here.
- 4. **Cut the category nav and app links.** This is a transactional email; save the editorial navigation for newsletters. Every secondary link is a conversion leak.
- 5. **Rename or remove "SHOP ALL BEST SELLERS."** If cross-sells are included, link those directly to the specific products shown — don't offer a generic detour.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Skechers "Your Items Are Waiting!" Abandoned Cart Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Table-based abandoned cart email deployed via Attentive (ESP). All outbound links and image assets are served over HTTP rather than HTTPS, creating a systemic security/deliverability risk across the entire send.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**HTTP redirect domain (high severity)**
All tracked links route through `http://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=...` — plaintext HTTP, not HTTPS. Example:
```
http://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=u001.LNc6VorXOZu5avyAvDTfGTW...
```
This affects every link in the email including the "web version" and logo CTA. Consequences: browser security warnings on redirect, potential stripping/blocking by secure mail proxies, and HSTS preload failures.

**Image assets served over HTTP**
Image `src` begins with `http://image.emails.skechers.co…` (truncated in source). HTTP image delivery triggers mixed-content warnings in webmail clients that load the email over HTTPS (Gmail, Outlook.com).

**UTM parameters unverifiable**
Destination URLs are fully encoded inside Attentive's redirect payload. Cannot confirm UTM attribution params are appended post-redirect without live resolution. This is an audit gap, not a confirmed issue — recommend spot-checking a decoded URL.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` is empty**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some email clients (notably Outlook desktop) surface the `<title>` value in the window title bar or task switcher. An empty title is a minor but unnecessary omission.

**`<meta>` charset declaration is nonstandard for email**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. The canonical email-safe form is:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
HTML5 shorthand (`<meta charset="utf-8">`) is also acceptable. The current hybrid form may be misinterpreted by older Outlook/Notes clients.

**`nl-container` table missing layout attributes**
The outermost `<table class="nl-container">` has no `width`, `border`, `cellpadding`, or `cellspacing` attributes. Width is controlled only via CSS, which Outlook (Word rendering engine) ignores on table elements. Inner content sets `width:600px` via CSS on `.row-content`, but without a table-level `width="100%"` attribute the container may render unconstrained in Outlook 2016+.

**Global link style override removes underlines**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This strips underline from all links in the message body. Combined with inheriting text color, linked text may be visually indistinguishable from body copy for users who rely on underline as a link indicator (accessibility/WCAG 1.4.1 risk).

**Alt text unverifiable**
Image tags are truncated in the provided source. Cannot confirm `alt` attributes are present. Given the hero-image-heavy layout, missing `alt` text would be a rendering and accessibility failure for image-blocked clients.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unrendered merge tokens are visible in the provided HTML — no `{{first_name}}`, `%%EMAIL%%`, or similar placeholders left exposed. The subject line ("Your Items Are Waiting!") and preheader ("You left something in your cart...") contain no personalization, which is expected but worth noting as a missed opportunity for cart-specific tokens (item name, price).

No broken/unpopulated merge token artifacts detected in visible HTML.

---

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM / CASL**
The provided HTML truncates before the footer. A compliant footer must include: physical mailing address, one-click unsubscribe link, and sender identification. These cannot be confirmed absent from the full source — flag for footer review.

**Sending domain**
`From: hello@msgs.skechers.com` — uses a subdomain (`msgs`) separate from the root domain (`skechers.com`). DKIM/SPF/DMARC alignment cannot be verified from HTML; authentication header values would need to be pulled from raw message headers. The subdomain pattern is standard for ESPs but alignment (strict vs. relaxed) should be confirmed in DNS records for `msgs.skechers.com`.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All destination URLs are opaque inside Attentive's encoded redirect strings. Direct UTM parameter inspection is not possible from this HTML. No UTM params are visible in the pre-encoding layer. Attentive typically appends UTM params configured in the platform — this should be verified in a decoded redirect or via Attentive campaign settings to confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` are set for abandoned cart flows.
