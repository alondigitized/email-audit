# Buy 2 = 20% off, Buy 3 = 30% off!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- I've seen this deal before — two days ago, in fact. The June 23rd email said "Buy 2, Take 20% Off! Buy 3, Take 30% Off!" and here it is again, dressed up with a "Star-Spangled Style" patriotic bow tied on top. I get it, it's almost the 4th of July, but the offer itself is word-for-word identical. That said, the math at the top of this email is still the clearest thing Crocs has sent me in weeks — two tiers, two numbers, no riddle. I gave them credit for that on the 23rd and I'll give it to them again here.
- The problem is what happens below the red clog. A work styles section — I'm 62 and not shopping for work shoes. An accessories section — I don't buy charms. And then there's a whole TikTok "Super Brand Day" section with what looks like a buckled cream sandal that belongs on someone half my age. That bottom third of the email is not for me, and it muddies what was a clean message up top.
- I'd click "Select Styles" on the hero deal if I hadn't already seen it Tuesday and passed. At this point I'm marking it read and moving on.

## What's working

- **The hero math is still solid.** "20% Off 2 Pairs, 30% Off 3 Pairs" lands immediately — no mental decoding required. My kind of offer.
- **The timing gets a small bump.** "Star-Spangled Style" ties it to the 4th of July weekend. Late June is exactly when I might be stocking up on slides for the holiday.
- **The red clog is iconic and I recognize it.** Classic Crocs. No confusion about what brand I'm dealing with.
- **The email renders clean.** No broken images, no overlapping text. Easy to scroll on my phone.

## What's weak

- **I literally got this offer two days ago.** The June 23rd send was the same tiered deal. Sending it again this quickly without upping the ante or adding a real deadline makes me trust the urgency less, not more.
- **The preview text is garbage.** Whatever my inbox showed me before I opened was invisible characters and code — not a single word. Zero reason to open from the preview line alone.
- **Work styles and accessories are filler for me.** I don't need Crocs for work, and I'm not buying Jibbitz charms. That's two full content blocks wasted.
- **The TikTok collab block is for someone else entirely.** A "Super Brand Day" with a fashion sandal and app download CTAs — that's aimed at a 24-year-old, not me. It undercuts the otherwise simple message.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a real deadline to the hero.** "Offer ends July 4th at midnight" would do more work than any copy tweak. Right now it feels open-ended, so there's no reason to act today vs. next week.
- 2. **Lead with a comfort angle, not just a style angle.** "Star-Spangled Style" is the hook, but I stay for the comfort. "Break in summer without breaking your feet" or any cushioning callout would pull me in harder.
- 3. **Cut or move the TikTok block.** Either segment it to a younger audience or push it below the footer. It visually signals "this email isn't for you" mid-scroll.
- 4. **Fix the preheader.** The invisible character trick leaves the preview blank in my inbox. Use real copy there — one line that either restates the deadline or teases the specific deal.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Star-Spangled Savings: Buy 2 pairs, save 20%`
- **Subject Alt B:** `4th of July deal — 20% off 2 pairs, ends Friday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Offer ends July 4th. Grab 2 pairs or 3 and stack the savings.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Classic Crocs, summer prices — 20% or 30% off depending how many you grab.`
