- Well, finally a shoe at the very top of my screen. A cream-and-tan 530 on a foot, black pant leg above it, and it fills the first screen. That's a good start. But this is the same "The 530, in new colors" subject as yesterday's 10/19 send, and it looks like nearly the same creative. I didn't need a second copy.
- Below the shoe, four more 530s sit in a grid: brown, black and two silvery tones. Then the email turns into a fleece sweatsuit ad with a young woman in a puffy jacket, and ends with the red shopping bags I've seen on 10/7, 10/13 and 10/14. I'm a foot man. The shoe part is the only part for me.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I were already thinking of a 530. Forward it to a friend? No. Nothing in here says cushioning, arch support or all-day walking. It's a fashion sneaker with a heritage line, and the only comfort word is "cozy," which is about colors.

## What's working

- The hero is a single shoe on a foot, big and clear. I knew what was being sold right away.
- The black "Shop now" button next to "Visit a store" is easy to see, and it's big enough for my thumb.
- The four-shoe grid gives me real color choices without much scrolling.
- The footer has "Running," "Walking" and "Lifestyle" links, so I can find the walking shoes if I go looking.
- The text is a decent size, and the layout didn't break anywhere I could see.

## What's weak

- The 25% off line is a tiny red strip above the hero. I almost missed it, and the deadline is only in the fine print at the bottom.
- "An expression of our running heritage" tells me nothing about how the shoe feels on my foot. There's no cushioning, no arch support and no price.
- The middle third is women's and men's fleece sets on young models. That isn't me, and it pushes the shoe content far down.
- The red bag and box picture at the bottom is the same trick as 10/7, 10/13 and 10/14.
- The grid shoes have no names or prices, so I can't compare them.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the 25% off and the "ends tonight" deadline in the hero, as a real headline above or beside the button. The deal is buried now, and it's the only reason to act today.
- 2. Add a comfort line under the 530: "Cushioned, all-day walking comfort" or something like it, plus a price or "from $X." A walker needs to know how it feels, not what it stands for.
- 3. Drop the apparel block and the bag picture on a shoe send. Use that space for names and prices under the four shoes.
- 4. Don't re-send the same subject on back-to-back days. Change the creative or the angle.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible markup is a reused New Balance template with leftover placeholder CSS, no-op `@font-face` rules, and three images without `alt`. The HTML source is truncated and the QA run found no hard failures (0 issues, 2 warnings), so body-level checks on UTMs, merge tokens and the unsubscribe link can't be confirmed.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Probe coverage:** 12 of 40 links were probed and 28 were skipped. The QA output shows no broken links among the 12 probed, and the 28 skipped are unverified.
- **Plain-text part:** 12,848 of 14,802 characters (about 87%) are URLs, so the text alternative is mostly tracking-wrapped links. It's hard to read and may add spam-filter weight.
- **Open pixel:** `https://link.email.newbalance.com/mo/egR1f966kc_818512772_12469090_810378_23513526.gif` is an ESP open-tracking pixel. All images are served from the ESP tracking domain `link.email.newbalance.com`. No issue beyond the alt warning in section 3.
- **Placeholder CSS:** `.bgwhite {… background-image: url('http://image.XXXXX.png');}` is a leftover placeholder over insecure `http://`. If any element uses `.bgwhite`, clients will request a dead, non-HTTPS URL.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (3 images):**
  - `ShippingIcons_06.png`
  - `ShippingBanner_DarkMode_03.png`
  - `egR1f966kc_…gif` (the tracking pixel, which should carry `alt=""`)
- **Reused assets:** The two shipping images sit under `/custloads/818512772/NBUS/myNB_Welcome/UpdatedImages/`. That path suggests they were reused from a welcome-email template. The promo's shipping content should be checked against what the welcome path currently serves.
- **Web fonts won't load:** Every `@font-face` block (`proxima-nova`, `linotype-didot`, and others) has no `src`. They do nothing, so text falls back to Arial or Times New Roman.
- **Malformed head:**
  - `<meta content="IE=edge" />` has no `http-equiv`, so it has no effect.
  - The HTML 4.01 doctype is combined with an `xmlns` attribute on `<html>`.
  - `<title>New Balance Template</title>` is a generic template title instead of the subject.
- **Dark-mode contrast:** In `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)`, `a {color:#cf0a2c !important}` sits on a `#151415` background. That works out to about 3.3:1, below the 4.5:1 WCAG AA minimum for normal text. The rule also forces every link, including any white-on-dark CTA text, to red. The CSS is truncated, so I couldn't check whether it's scoped further.
- **Duplicate selector:** `.bg1` is defined twice (`#ffffff`, then `#58546d`). The second silently wins.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found in the visible source. The truncated HTML shows no unresolved `%%…%%`, `{{…}}` or `[FIRSTNAME]` tokens. The remainder wasn't available, so this is not a full check.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Authentication:** The `Authentication-Results` header wasn't found. QA attributes this to the AgentMail relay, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC are unknown. This is a capture limitation, not evidence of a sender failure. The From domain is `email.newbalance.com`, which should be verified from raw headers.
- **Unsubscribe and address:** QA says an unsubscribe link is always included in probing, and it raised no failure. The unsubscribe URL, `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers, and the physical postal address were not in the visible excerpt, so they are unconfirmed.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable from the data provided. The excerpt contains no CTA `href`s, and no probe destinations or UTM values were reported. Because links are ESP click-wrapped, UTMs can only be checked on the final landing URLs after the redirect.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add `alt` text to `ShippingIcons_06.png` and `ShippingBanner_DarkMode_03.png`, and `alt=""` to the tracking pixel.
2. Remove the `.bgwhite` placeholder `background-image: url('http://image.XXXXX.png')`.
3. Delete or fix the `@font-face` blocks so they have a real `src`. Otherwise drop them and rely on the Arial fallback.
4. Fix the head: set `<title>` to the subject, correct the IE meta to `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`, and align the doctype and `xmlns`.
5. Scope the dark-mode `a {color:#cf0a2c !important}` rule so it doesn't override button text, and use a lighter red that reaches 4.5:1 on `#151415`.
6. Shorten the plain-text part by using fewer or shorter tracked URLs, with real copy around them.
7. Rerun QA with the full HTML. Also capture raw headers (`Authentication-Results`, `List-Unsubscribe`) and probe the 28 skipped links, including a UTM check on final landing URLs.
