# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-11-25**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

The Black Friday sale banners are easy to see and the Slip-ins product page is good. But I couldn't finish buying. The size buttons showed "Please select a size" and the Add to Cart step ended with the cart badge still at 0. The cart page then came up as a **blank white screen**. Search for "comfort shoes" gave me women's wedge loafers first. I never saw a signed-in state, so nothing felt personal. I would have given up and gone to Hoka or Brooks.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** That is the same as yesterday. The browsing pages work, but the checkout path fails. A broken cart on Black Friday is the most costly place to fail.

## 3. What's Working

- **Hands Free Slip-ins is easy to find.** It sits right at the top of Men's Shoes, with Walking Shoes next to it. That is what I shop for.
- **The top menu helps.** "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Arch Fit" are listed right under the logo. The Men menu also has "Comfort Technologies."
- **The product page is clear.** It has a big $110.00 price, 4.5 stars with 444 reviews, and Klarna/Afterpay payment plans. A Slip-ins logo on the photo tells me it's hands-free.
- **The size grid has big buttons.** Sold-out sizes are crossed out, and the blue Add to Cart button is wide and easy to tap.
- **The Men's drawer is readable.** The text is large, the rows are well spaced, and it has a clear Back button.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart failed.** Step 7 still showed "Please select a size" and a cart count of 0. Step 8 was an empty white page. I can't tell whether the cart is broken or just slow. Either way I would have walked away.
- **No width or arch choice at the size step.** I'm looking for comfort, and the page doesn't ask me about it where I choose a size.
- **The real price is hidden.** The page says "30% OFF! Applied at Cart" in small red type, and the homepage adds a separate 15% code. I'd have to work out what this shoe costs myself. The fine print is tiny without zooming.
- **Search ignored who I am.** "comfort shoes" gave 1,659 results, and the first two were women's. There was no Men's filter or comfort category at the top. The Filter, Best Matches, and Size buttons are also cut off at the edge.
- **The homepage hides the comfort message.** It is almost all sale banners, and the "Slip-ins / Arch Fit" row scrolls off the right edge ("Max Cus…").
- **No comparison against other brands.** I saw nothing that shows why this shoe beats a Hoka, Brooks, or New Balance, and nothing on price versus quality.
- **No personalization.** The login step landed on the same homepage as before, with no greeting or "welcome back."
- **The popups step looked the same as the homepage.** The only visible change was a different strip of text under the header.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart first.** Add a loading state and an error message, and never show a blank page.
2. **Show the final price.** Put something like "$110 → about $77 with 30% off" on the product page, and show the code savings at checkout.
3. **Make the size error obvious.** Keep the message near the button, and add a one-tap "Wide" choice.
4. **Show comfort first in search.** Use the shopper's gender and "comfort" intent, and put Slip-ins and Arch Fit near the top of the results.
5. **Put the comfort categories on the homepage.** Show Slip-ins, Arch Fit, and Wide Fit as large, tappable tiles.
6. **Make the logged-in state visible.** Show a name or recently viewed shoes, and keep the cart count correct.
7. **Make the filter bar and promo text bigger.** Fit the buttons on the screen without side-scrolling.

## 6. Bottom Line

I like the shoe and the Slip-ins idea. But the site didn't let me buy it, and search showed me women's loafers. The best deal in the world doesn't help if the cart is a blank page. I'd go back to my other stores until this is fixed.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

- The journey has real gaps. LCP and CLS were not captured for any of the 8 steps, so Core Web Vitals can't be assessed.
- The "Log In" and "Men category" steps both report URL `https://www.skechers.com/`, and their HTML is identical to the homepage (same `--vh` value, same script order). These steps probably did not navigate, or were captured before the route changed. Their metrics and axe results may describe the homepage again.
- `GET /cart/` returned **429**. The "View Cart" axe results (no `<title>`, no `lang`, no `<main>`, no `<h1>`) match a bare error or rate-limit response, not a rendered cart page. Treat those four violations as a symptom of the 429, not as a cart template defect.
- The homepage has the most substantive accessibility problems: 2 critical, 1 serious and several landmark-structure violations.
- The page loads a very large number of third-party tags, and a GTM preview/environment snippet is present in the served HTML.

## Accessibility

18 axe violations were reported (2 critical, 3 serious, 11 moderate, 2 minor).

**Homepage (10)**
- **critical `aria-required-parent`**: an element with a role that requires a parent role (e.g. `listitem`, `tab`, `option`) is not inside that parent.
- **critical `button-name`**: one or more buttons have no accessible name, such as icon-only controls without `aria-label`.
- **serious `link-name`**: one or more links have no discernible text, such as image or icon links without alt text or `aria-label`.
- **moderate `landmark-main-is-top-level` and `landmark-no-duplicate-main`**: `<main>` is nested inside another landmark and there is more than one. This likely comes from a template or SPA shell rendering `<main>` twice.
- **moderate `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`**: an `<aside>` or `role=complementary` is nested inside another landmark.
- **moderate `landmark-unique`**: duplicate landmarks, such as multiple `<nav>` or `<aside>` elements without distinct `aria-label`s.
- **moderate `region`**: content sits outside any landmark.
- **moderate `page-has-heading-one`**: no `<h1>`.
- **minor `aria-allowed-role`**: a `role` is set that is not valid for its element.

**View Cart (4)**
- `document-title` (serious): no `<title>`.
- `html-has-lang` (serious): no `lang` attribute. The homepage does set `lang="en"`.
- `landmark-one-main` (moderate): no `<main>`.
- `page-has-heading-one` (moderate): no `<h1>`.
- All four are consistent with the 429 response body (see Console & Network Errors).

**Search "comfort shoes" (4)**
- `aria-allowed-role` (minor), `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` (moderate), `landmark-unique` (moderate) and `region` (moderate).
- These repeat the homepage landmark and role problems, so they are probably in shared template components.

**No axe results were recorded for** Log In, Men category, Men > Shoes, Product detail and Add to Cart. Either those pages had no violations, or the steps were not scanned or did not navigate.

## SEO

- Homepage and search results have no `<h1>` (`page-has-heading-one`). This weakens the primary heading signal.
- The cart response has no `<title>` and no `<main>`. This is probably the 429 body and is not SEO-relevant if `/cart/` is `noindex`, but confirm that is so.
- Duplicate `<main>` landmarks on the homepage indicate a malformed document outline.
- `<html lang="en">` is present on the homepage.
- The truncated HTML samples don't show `<meta name="description">`, canonical, Open Graph or JSON-LD, so those could not be checked.
- Three steps report the same URL, `https://www.skechers.com/`. This is a data-capture problem, but if it reflects real behavior (client-side navigation without URL updates), it would be an SEO and analytics issue.

## Performance

Only TTFB was captured:

| Step | TTFB |
|---|---|
| Homepage | 422 ms |
| Log In | 491 ms |
| Men category | 491 ms |
| Men > Shoes | 58 ms |
| Product detail | 62 ms |
| Add to Cart | 62 ms |
| View Cart | 124 ms |
| Search | 56 ms |

- The first three steps are at 422–491 ms, which is acceptable but near the 800 ms "good" TTFB threshold. Log In and Men category have identical values, which supports the view that they did not make a new document request.
- The steps from Men > Shoes onward are 56–124 ms. That is too fast for uncached full-document loads, so they are likely cache hits or in-page transitions. They are not comparable to a cold load.
- **LCP and CLS are unavailable for every step.** Possible causes are the observers being registered after load or the metric reads failing. This is a gap in the audit tooling.
- **Third-party script load is heavy.** The homepage head injects these tags:
  - ads and analytics: TikTok (3 scripts), Pinterest, Snapchat, Reddit, Taboola (3), Nextdoor, Yahoo (`ytc.js` twice) and Microsoft/Bing UET
  - tag managers and testing: GTM plus 3 gtag destinations (DC-9630990, AW-1066305778, G-XZTV9LQ9DQ) and Monetate (3 scripts)
  - session replay, survey and other: Quantum Metric, Pulse Insights, Bloomreach (`brsrvr`), Impact, RTB House (`creativecdn`), Tune (`go2sdk`) and Facebook
- The scripts are mostly `async`, but their combined main-thread and network cost is large and is a likely cause of poor INP and LCP.
- **Web fonts load through webfontloader from cdnjs** (`wf-mulish-*-active` classes). This adds a third-party round trip, and the page is exposed to FOIT/FOUT and layout shift unless fallback metrics are matched.
- **A GTM preview/environment snippet is in the served HTML.** The URL carries `gtm_auth=…&gtm_preview=env-1&gtm_cookies_win=x`. A non-live GTM environment is being served, which may load different tags than intended and adds a cache-unfriendly parameterized URL.

## Mobile Optimization

- `--vh: 6.64px` is set inline on `<html>`, which implies a viewport height of about 664 px. This is a JS-based viewport-height workaround. It is functional, but newer CSS units (`dvh`/`svh`) would avoid the script dependency and the layout recalculation.
- The viewport meta tag and tap-target sizes were not visible in the truncated HTML and were not tested.
- The unlabeled buttons and links (`button-name`, `link-name`) matter most on mobile, where icon-only controls are common.
- Heavy third-party JS (see Performance) has a larger impact on mobile CPUs.

## Console & Network Errors

**Network (1)**
- `429 https://www.skechers.com/cart/`: the cart request was rate-limited. A likely cause is bot or WAF protection reacting to automated traffic, or request bursts from tags firing during the Add to Cart > View Cart sequence. Because the response replaced the cart page, the View Cart audit data is unreliable. The 429 should be treated as a test-environment finding unless it reproduces for normal users.

**Console (21 reported, 9 listed)**
- **"Unsupported Summarizer API languages were specified, and the request was aborted."** (4 listed)
  - A script calls Chrome's built-in Summarizer API without a supported `outputLanguage` or `expectedInputLanguages`. It repeats on several pages.
  - The source is unconfirmed. It is probably a third-party widget, but it could be a browser extension or the test harness. Check the stack trace.
  - Impact: the summarization feature silently fails.
- **"Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document."** (5 listed)
  - Code registers `unload` handlers (a likely source is an analytics or session-replay script) and the browser's Permissions-Policy blocks them.
  - `unload` handlers are deprecated and make a page ineligible for bfcache. Replace them with `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`.
- The 12 unlisted errors could not be classified.

## Recommendations

1. **Re-run the capture.**
   - Verify that Log In, Men category and the later steps actually navigate, and record the real URL for each.
   - Capture LCP, CLS and INP with observers registered before navigation (`buffered: true`).
   - Retry or slow the cart request, or whitelist the test client, so View Cart renders without a 429.
2. **Fix the homepage ARIA and landmark errors:**
   - Give every icon button an accessible name (critical `button-name`).
   - Give every icon or image link an accessible name (serious `link-name`).
   - Add the missing required parent roles (critical `aria-required-parent`).
   - Keep a single top-level `<main>`.
   - Move `<aside>` elements out of other landmarks.
   - Label duplicate `<nav>` and `<aside>` elements.
   - Wrap remaining content in landmarks.
   - Remove invalid `role` values.
3. **Add one `<h1>`** to the homepage and the search results template.
4. **Make error responses valid documents** with `<html lang>`, `<title>` and a `<main>`, so rate-limit or error pages don't fail basic checks.
5. **Replace `unload` listeners with `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`**, and find the script registering them to restore bfcache eligibility.
6. **Trace the Summarizer API error to its source script** and pass supported language codes, or feature-detect before calling.
7. **Audit third-party tags:**
   - Remove duplicates (Yahoo `ytc.js` loaded twice, Pinterest and Taboola scripts loaded more than once).
   - Defer non-essential pixels until after consent or idle.
   - Confirm GTM is serving the live environment, not `env-1` preview.
8. **Self-host the web fonts** with `font-display: swap` and size-adjusted fallbacks instead of loading webfontloader from cdnjs.
9. **Replace the JS `--vh` workaround** with `dvh`/`svh` CSS units where possible.
