# PSA: sandals are 40% OFF!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- Third DSW email today with "sandals 40% off" in the subject line. Third. I counted. First one this morning was "Starts today: 40% off sandals" — I clicked that one. Then "Ahhh, yay! 40% OFF sandals" hit around midday. Now this one. My thumb moved out of pure DSW muscle memory at this point, but the moment it loaded I read "Regular-price **women's** styles only, in stores & online with code TRENDY" right under that giant 40% banner. So those nine product images I was hoping were men's slides or walking sandals? Wedges. Strappy heels. Open-toe flats. Nothing in that grid belongs on a 62-year-old man's foot.
- There's a "SHOP MEN" link above the product grid and a clearance section lower down, but by the time I found those, I'd already decided this one was noise. The email is well-executed for someone — just not me. Clean layout, no broken images, the semi-annual clearance section at the bottom is actually interesting if I take 30 seconds to scroll — but I'm not going to scroll. I already clicked the morning sandals email and got nothing worth buying. I'm not doing it a third time today.
- Mark as read. No click, no forward. Maybe revisit the clearance section in a calmer moment, but not today.

## What's working

- The 40% hero is massive and lands immediately — I read the number before anything else, and that reflex still works even on my phone.
- "SUMMER (SAN)DEALS" is a clever enough headline that it registered even at a glance; DSW's copywriting is consistently snappy.
- The semi-annual clearance callout lower down ("40% OFF CLEARANCE — twice a year, tons of deals, going fast") is genuinely useful framing; that urgency is real and I believe it.
- Shop by Size grid is a nice touch — if the sizes had been men's, I would've clicked straight through.
- The VIP/loyalty section with Afterpay and rewards reminder is a legitimate trust signal for a member like me.

## What's weak

- The asterisk doing a lot of heavy lifting: "SANDALS*" in 80-point type, then in mouse-print below: "Regular-price women's styles only." That's a bait and a switch. Subject line says nothing about women's — I walked in expecting a men's deal.
- Nine product photos, all women's styles. Zero men's product shown anywhere above the fold or in the main grid. I am invisible to this email.
- This is the third identical-premise email from DSW in a single calendar day. I know they want to drive urgency but this is cadence fatigue in real time.
- The size grid runs 5 through 11 — a dead giveaway these are women's sizes. I wear a 10.5 wide; none of this grid maps to me.
- "SHOP MEN" link exists but sends me to a generic page, not a men's sandals deal. The offer doesn't follow me there.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the list by gender** before sending — or at minimum, send Walker a version with men's sandals in the hero grid and the TRENDY code scoped to men's. The infrastructure clearly exists; the segmentation just wasn't applied here.
- 2. **Pull one men's sandal or slide into the grid** even if the headline stays women's-focused. One Skechers Go Walk Arch Fit sandal in that 3x3 gives me a reason to stick around.
- 3. **Throttle the same-day duplicate sends** — "Starts today," "Ahhh yay," and "PSA" are three subject lines for the same 40%-off-sandals story on June 25. Pick one.
- 4. **Give the asterisk a fighting chance** — put "Women's styles" in the subject or preheader so male subscribers don't feel tricked.
- 5. Move the clearance section above the product grid for browsers who aren't in the women's sandals lane — the Semi-Annual Sale is the stronger hook for a deal-seeker like me.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Men's sandals up to 40% off — shop now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `40% off sandals — including men's styles`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Comfort sandals, walking shoes, and more — use code TRENDY`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Men's picks included — plus 40% off clearance in stores`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<meta charset>` missing `http-equiv`**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, older Outlook and some webmail clients may not correctly apply the charset declaration.

---

**`Src:` (capital S) in all `@font-face` blocks**
```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');
```
CSS property names are technically case-insensitive per spec, but this is non-standard and some stricter parsers in email clients may skip the declaration. All 10 `@font-face` rules are affected. Should be lowercase `src:`.

---

**`text-size-adjust` conflict in reset**
```css
*{-ms-text-size-adjust: 100%; -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; ... text-size-adjust: none}
```
Vendor-prefixed properties set to `100%` (permit scaling) while the unprefixed `text-size-adjust: none` disables scaling in conforming browsers. This is contradictory and will produce inconsistent behavior across clients.

---

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents user zoom — an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4). Low risk for email but worth flagging if accessibility compliance is a requirement.

---

**Font format coverage gap**
Futura PT fonts are served as `.woff` only — no WOFF2. HelveticaNow fonts are served as `.ttf` (with cache-busting params `?1755182939`). Email clients that do support web fonts (Apple Mail, iOS Mail) prefer WOFF2 for size/speed. Neither family provides a WOFF2 variant, meaning fallback system fonts will render in most clients.

---

**Redundant/non-standard CSS in `:root`**
```css
supported-color-schemes: light dark;
```
This is not a valid CSS property — `color-scheme` is the correct property (already set correctly via the meta tag and the `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block). The `:root` declaration adds no effect and should be removed.
