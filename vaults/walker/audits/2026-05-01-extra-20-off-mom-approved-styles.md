# Extra 20% off + mom-approved styles 🎁
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-01**
## Executive summary

- This is a Mother's Day gifting email from Famous Footwear, and right out of the gate it's not talking to me. The subject says "mom-approved styles," the hero image is a young woman holding gift bags, and pretty much every product push is aimed at whoever Mom is — not at me browsing for myself. I spotted Skechers in there, which caught my eye for a split second, but they buried it mid-email between Brooks and Birkenstock. The 20% off is real and I noticed it, but this email is asking me to shop *for* someone else, and that's a different mental mode than "hey Walker, here's something comfy for you." I'm not annoyed, but I'm not energized either.
- The offer is decent and the discounts are real, but the targeting is completely off for a 62-year-old male shopper. Almost nothing here speaks to my comfort-first, shop-for-myself habits. If I were buying a gift for my wife, maybe — but there's no men's hook to pull me in.

## What's working

- **The 20% off is front and center.** I saw it immediately. Clear, bold, readable even on a small phone screen. That number does its job.
- **Skechers is in there.** Even just seeing the logo mid-scroll made me pause. That brand name has pull for me.
- **Price-point gift guide section.** The circular price bubbles (looks like different tiers) are a nice shorthand. Easy to scan on a phone without squinting.
- **Birkenstock sandals are visible.** Not my brand, but recognizable and clear product shots.

## What's weak

- **It's a women's gifting email wearing a thin unisex disguise.** The hero is a young woman with gift bags. The "mom-approved" framing in the subject line sets the expectation, and the body delivers exactly that — women's styles, gifting angles, nothing aimed at me. I'm not "mom." I'm the guy who might buy for mom, or more often, I'm buying for myself.
- **The preview text is a raw tracking URL.** That's not a typo or a minor flaw — that's embarrassing. My inbox showed a garbage link instead of a reason to open this email. First impression: something went wrong.
- **Skechers is buried.** If they've got Skechers — my brand — why is it sandwiched in the middle? Lead with the thing that'll hook me.
- **No men's angle whatsoever.** Not even a small callout like "Something for Dad too?" Nothing. This email doesn't know I exist.
- **The barcode/app section at the bottom.** I'm on my phone already — why are you showing me a barcode to scan? That whole section felt like filler I had to scroll past.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this better.** Send the "mom-approved" version to customers who have purchased women's styles or have browsing history that suggests gifting. For male-skewing customers like me, flip the framing: "Gift her something she'll love — or treat yourself while you're at it."
- 2. **Lead with the brand that matters to the recipient.** If I'm a Skechers buyer, Skechers should be the hero, not the third brand down.
- 3. **Fix the preview text.** That raw URL has to go. One sentence — any sentence — is better than a tracking link in my inbox.
- 4. **Add a men's module.** Even one row of men's comfort shoes, slip-ins, anything. Acknowledge that half the database isn't buying for Mom, they ARE Mom's husband.

## Technical Audit — Famous Footwear "Extra 20% off + mom-approved styles"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound (table-based layout, standard ESP click-wrapping via `click.email.famousfootwear.com`) but carries two deliverability risks: a plain-text body that is 71% raw URLs, and two dynamic content images served over HTTP. Accessibility coverage is poor across the board — essentially no content images have alt text.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text URL density — WARN**
Plain-text part is 6,808 of 9,618 chars (71%) raw URLs. This exceeds the ~70% threshold that Barracuda, SpamAssassin, and some Gmail filters treat as spam signals.

**25 click-wrapped links not probed**
All outbound links route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud). The QA skip is expected, but no UTM parameter passthrough could be verified (see §6).

**Open pixel**
Pixel fires to `https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/open.aspx?QUO6SLLJ3H2UJAABVCBMIS6YVU.70253` — standard SMC open tracking, no issue.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources — WARN (high priority)**
Two LiveContent (dynamic/personalized) images are served over plain HTTP:
```
http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=ABB7...  (id: 920205575250)
http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=ABB7...  (id: 920155575454)
```
Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail proxy/block non-HTTPS image sources. These images will silently fail for a significant portion of recipients. The `cl.S7.exct.net` hostname suggests a legacy SMC LiveContent domain; the HTTPS equivalent should be available.

**Missing alt text — WARN (22 images)**
Every content image — including the logo (`3b6a7dc1-6955-49f8-868a-bf62a15122ee.png`) and all product images — has `alt=""`. Images-off rendering (common in Outlook) will produce a blank email with no text fallback whatsoever.

**Viewport blocks zoom — accessibility issue**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
`user-scalable=0` / `maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is flagged by accessibility validators.

**Malformed charset meta tag**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Missing the required `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute. The correct form is:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Most clients will still infer UTF-8, but this is a spec violation.

**Doctype**
HTML 4.01 Transitional is standard for email and not an issue.

### 5. Compliance

**Authentication headers**: Not evaluable from HTML source alone; requires DMARC/DKIM/SPF header inspection from a received copy. The sending domain `email.famousfootwear.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain — alignment depends on SMC configuration.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 25 outbound links are wrapped through `click.email.famousfootwear.com` and the raw destination URLs are not visible in the source. UTM parameter presence (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) cannot be confirmed without resolving the redirects. The QA tool skipped probing all 25 click links. This is a gap — destination URL UTM coverage should be verified by resolving a sample of click links manually or via a redirect-following probe.
