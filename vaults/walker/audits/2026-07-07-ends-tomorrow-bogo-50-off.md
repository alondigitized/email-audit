# Ends tomorrow: BOGO 50% OFF
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- I've been tracking this BOGO deal since DSW launched it two days ago with the "Say whaaaat" email — which, credit where it's due, actually felt like news after two weeks of 25%-off reruns. So when this showed up in my inbox this morning saying it ends tomorrow, I opened it. That's fair. That's how deadline reminders are supposed to work.
- But then I look inside and I'm staring at sixteen pairs of women's heels, kitten-heel flats, and strappy sandals. Every single shoe in that "Brand New...And So You" section is something my wife might buy. Not one sneaker, not one walking shoe, not one slip-on anything. DSW has my purchase history. They know I'm not shopping for T-strap pumps. That "And So You" tagline reads like they built an AI to speak to a 35-year-old woman and forgot they had men on the list.
- The offer itself is real and I'd still click SHOP MEN — but that button is wedged between SHOP WOMEN and SHOP KIDS like an afterthought. Mark as read, maybe click through on the bus. I'm not forwarding this to anyone.

## What's working

- **BOGO 50% OFF is front and center** — top of the email, large text, no ambiguity. On my phone I don't even have to squint.
- **"Ends tomorrow" does its job** — after watching DSW stretch "FINAL HOURS" across a week and a half, this one-day deadline actually feels real.
- **Three-button CTA row with SHOP MEN** — it's small but it's there. At least they didn't pretend men don't exist.
- **DSW Rewards is visible** — I can see there's a loyalty angle at the bottom, which matters to me even if it didn't drive the open.
- **No broken images or layout weirdness** — the email renders clean on mobile.

## What's weak

- **The entire feature section is women's fashion shoes** — every shoe in that grid is a heel or flat. For a male subscriber, this is like getting a steak coupon and finding a salad inside.
- **Preview text is a sentence fragment followed by garbage** — "Same with our Semi-Annual Sale..." and then a column of invisible spacer characters. It reads like someone pasted filler copy and never checked it on a real inbox.
- **Shop by Size goes 5 through 11 — that's women's sizing** — men's sizes start at 7 and go up. I wear a 10.5 wide. This section isn't built for me.
- **The clearance section is in-store only** — "40% OFF CLEARANCE*" sounds great until the asterisk says stores only. I'm not driving somewhere for clearance shoes. That's a bait I'm tired of chasing.
- **Two DSW emails in one day is too many** — "5 hot trends" already came through this morning. This one showing up a few hours later makes both feel cheaper.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the feature grid for a men's section** — or at minimum split it 50/50. If DSW has my purchase data, they know which half of the store I shop in. One men's comfort shoe in the hero would have me leaning forward instead of scrolling past.
- 2. **Fix the preview text** — "Same with our Semi-Annual Sale..." is a sentence fragment from nowhere. That slot should read something like: *"One more day on BOGO — grab a second pair at half off before midnight."*
- 3. **Make the in-store clearance opt-in, not the default** — either show online clearance here and save the in-store pitch for geo-targeted sends, or at least lead with the BOGO since that actually applies to me online.
- 4. **The Shop by Size should toggle Men/Women** — showing women's sizes to a male subscriber is a small thing that communicates you didn't really personalize anything.
- 5. **Consolidate sends** — the "trends" email and the deadline reminder don't need to be separate fires today. One tighter email that says "trends + BOGO ends tomorrow" would have felt less like inbox clutter.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`http-equiv` missing on two `<meta>` tags:**
```html
<!-- As sent (invalid) -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta content="IE=Edge" />

<!-- Correct -->
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=Edge" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, both tags are inert. The charset declaration in particular may cause Outlook and older clients to mis-detect encoding.

**`Src:` (capital S) in all 10 `@font-face` blocks:**
```css
/* As sent — invalid property name */
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');
```
CSS property names are case-sensitive in some parsers. All 10 font declarations use `Src:` (capital S), which is technically invalid. Most modern email clients tolerate this, but it will fail in any strict CSS parser and may silently skip font loading. Should be lowercase `src:`.

**Near-black color values (`#000001`, `#00000F`) in dark mode classes:**
```css
.dm-font-black { color: #000001 !important; }
.dm-bg-black { background: #00000F !important; }
```
These are deliberate workarounds to prevent Apple Mail from auto-linking black text (Apple targets exact `#000000`). The technique is valid and widely used, but the `#00000F` background value is non-standard and may render as visually imperceptible dark blue on some OLED displays. Confirm this is intentional.

**`<title>DSW</title>` — bare brand name only.** Some email clients display the `<title>` as a tooltip or in accessibility trees. Not a blocking issue, but a missed opportunity.
