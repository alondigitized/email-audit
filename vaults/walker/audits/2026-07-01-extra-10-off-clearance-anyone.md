# Extra 10% off clearance, anyone?
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- Another DSW email, another Tuesday — my thumb's basically on autopilot by now. Six, seven sends in the past week, and I've lost count of the sandals. To be fair, "Extra 10% off clearance" is the first subject from these folks in a while that actually made me pause before swiping. Clearance math I understand. Stack an extra 10% on already-marked-down stock and my brain starts doing arithmetic.

But the minute I started scrolling I felt like I'd walked into the wrong section of the store. Strappy sandals, wedge heels, kitten-toe pumps, women's espadrilles — wall to wall. Nothing in this email looks like it was built for a 62-year-old man who needs cushioning and a wide toe box.
- I will not be clicking any product CTA here. I *might* thumb over to the clearance section on my own if I'm bored tonight, because the 10% off is legitimate value. But I'm not forwarding this to anyone, and I'm not clicking anything in the scroll.

- **Every product shown is women's footwear** — Wedges, strappy heels, kitten-toe mules. As a man looking for a wide-width walking shoe, I scrolled the entire email and felt invisible.
- **No comfort or fit language anywhere** — No cushioning, no arch support, no wide widths, no "great for walking." Nothing that tells me why I'd want to buy shoes here versus anywhere else.
- **"Mid Week Mood Boost" section is filler** — "Upcoming summer picks" with a generic CTA helps nobody. That slot could've been men's clearance picks or a "comfort picks on clearance" hook.
- **The preheader spacers** — My inbox shows "Better get it while you can" and then a grey void. Not broken exactly, but it looks lazy at a glance.
- **Seventh email in a row from this sender** — The cadence is wearing me down. The clearance subject bought them one more open from me; I'm not sure next time will go the same way.

- 1. **Surface men's clearance product picks in the body** — Even two or three New Balance or Skechers men's walkers in the clearance grid would've made me scroll with purpose instead of obligation. The offer is there; the product proof isn't.
- 2. **Replace "Mid Week Mood Boost" with a clearance callout by category** — Men's shoes, women's shoes, sandals — let me navigate to my corner. This is the email equivalent of a store with no signage.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 failure)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents mobile users from zooming. Remove `maximum-scale=1` — email clients that need zoom restriction handle this natively.

**`http-equiv` missing on two meta tags**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta content="IE=Edge" />
```
Both require the `http-equiv` attribute to be actionable. Correct forms:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=Edge" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, the charset declaration in particular may be ignored by older Outlook versions.

**`Src:` capitalized in all @font-face blocks**
```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/appboy/communication/assets/font_assets/files/67dd7a3bf1de1d0066403c87/original.woff") format('woff');
```
CSS property names are case-insensitive per spec, but `Src:` is non-standard and will fail in any strict parser. Should be lowercase `src:` across all 10 font declarations.

**`-ms-text-size-adjust` declared twice with conflicting values**
```css
*{-ms-text-size-adjust: 100%; ... -ms-text-size-adjust: none; text-size-adjust: none}
```
The second declaration (`none`) overrides the first (`100%`). Additionally, `-webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%` and the unprefixed `text-size-adjust: none` are contradictory — in browsers supporting the unprefixed property, adjustment is disabled while the webkit-prefixed line signals normal adjustment. Resolve by settling on a single intent: `100%` (allow normal adjustment, prevents font boosting) or `none` (fully disable).

**`Margin: 0` capitalized in heading reset**
```css
h1, h2, h3, h4, h5, h6, p, ul, li{...Margin: 0}
```
Not a valid CSS property (see `Src:` note above). This is a known Outlook MSO hack but should be documented as intentional, not left ambiguous.

**`.woff` only for Futura PT; `.ttf` only for HelveticaNow**
No `.woff2` sources declared for any font family. Moot in most email clients (web fonts are blocked), but if fallback rendering matters, `.woff2` should be the primary format with `.woff` as fallback.
