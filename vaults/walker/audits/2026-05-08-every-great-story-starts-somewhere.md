# Every great story starts somewhere
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-08**
## Executive summary

- Adidas sent me a brand poetry email about backyard soccer. I'm a 62-year-old guy who shops Skechers for easy slip-ons and a decent deal. These two things have nothing to do with each other. The email is clean, no broken images, nice photography — but it has zero offer, zero product specifics, and zero connection to anything I'd actually buy. It reads like something written for a 22-year-old who plays pickup soccer on weekends. I don't play soccer. I barely want to tie my shoes.

## What's working

- The photography is sharp. Three guys in what looks like athletic streetwear, big open composition — it loaded instantly and didn't have any ugly gaps or broken elements. If I were actually an adidas fan looking for style inspiration, the visual presentation would hold up. The headline "BACKYARD LEGENDS" is punchy and easy to read even on a small phone screen. The type is large enough that I don't have to squint. The layout is clean and simple — one column, top to bottom, nothing confusing.

## What's weak

- Everything below the surface. This email has no offer. Not even a soft one. No "20% off," no "new arrivals," no "free shipping this week." Just inspirational copy about pitches and legends and writing your own story. I'm 62. I'm not writing my legend on a backyard pitch — I'm trying to find comfortable shoes that don't wreck my knees. This email reads like an ad campaign, not an email to a customer.
- The content is soccer-coded. "The ball at your feet," "the pitch is waiting," "the beautiful game" — I had to read that twice to even understand what they were selling. Shoes? Jerseys? No idea. There's no product shown. Not a single item. After all that dramatic copy, I'm supposed to just hit "Shop Now" and go find something myself? At my age, I want to be pointed somewhere specific. I don't want a treasure hunt.
- The preheader text at the very top looks garbled — it reads like broken copy or a test string that slipped through. That's the first thing I'd see in my inbox preview, and it wouldn't make any sense.
- No mention of my account, no loyalty recognition, nothing that says "hey Walker, you're a customer, here's something for you." I'm a stranger to this email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show me a product.** If this is about footwear — show the shoe. One hero product, one price, one button. That's all I need.
- 2. **Give me a reason to click today.** A deadline ("48 hours only"), a discount ("members save 20%"), or free shipping. Something tangible.
- 3. **Match the creative to the audience.** If I'm in the men's casual or comfort segment, show me men's casual shoes — not soccer imagery.
- 4. **Fix the preheader.** Whatever that top text is, it reads broken. That's the first impression in the inbox.
- 5. **Acknowledge I'm a customer.** Even "New arrivals for you" beats generic storytelling.

## Technical Audit — adidas "Every great story starts somewhere"

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard failures: a malformed destination URL inside a click-tracking redirect (broken CTA) and a zero-length plain-text MIME part. Six additional warnings around HTTP image assets, missing alt text, and a malformed charset meta tag.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken CTA destination**
The click-redirect for image/CTA `cd7b1907-ed90-4e45-b152-f41b3dbc78b0` unwraps to a relative path:
```
/g/cd7b1907-ed90-4e45-b152-f41b3dbc78b0?txn=9d45d780-...
```
Relative URLs are invalid as ESP redirect destinations. The outer tracking wrapper (`https://dv.adidas.com/o/cd7b1907-...`) will resolve, but the inner destination will 404 or error. Template likely passed a relative URL where an absolute was required.

**[WARN] 23 click-tracking links** use redirect domains (`dv.adidas.com`) — expected for ESP infrastructure, but each layer of redirect adds latency and a failure point.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] HTTP image assets — mixed-content risk**
The "arrow" image (`b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg`) is referenced 4× over HTTP:
```
src: http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-...jpg
```
Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail enforce HTTPS for remote images — these will render as broken image icons in those clients.

**[WARN] HTTP tracking pixel**
```
src: http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019e055621ea-.../gYNUzOgqiePTNKF7...
```
Same mixed-content issue; open-tracking pixel will be blocked.

**[WARN] Missing alt text**
Two images lack `alt` attributes:
- `cd7b1907-ed90-4e45-b152-f41b3dbc78b0` (appears to be a hero/CTA image)
- `gYNUzOgqiePTNKF7udHXhm06Fy7SVCWG8rKfY-VLLmk=452` (tracking pixel)

**[BUG] Malformed charset meta tag**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Missing required `http-equiv` attribute. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Older Outlook versions may fail to detect the charset and render garbled characters.

**[MINOR] Duplicate `@font-face` declarations**
`AdihausDIN` (400, 700) and `AdineuePRO` (400, 600) are declared in two separate `<style>` blocks. Adds ~800 bytes of redundant CSS.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unfired merge tokens visible in the HTML source. Subject line is broadcast copy with no personalization — no issues.

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text MIME part is 0 characters**
QA confirmed `text/plain` body is empty. CAN-SPAM best practices and many ISP spam filters require a readable plain-text alternative. HTML-only messages are penalized by several spam scoring systems (SpamAssassin `MIME_HTML_ONLY` rule).

**[WARN] `no-referrer` meta suppresses HTTP Referer header**
```html
<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />
```
All click-throughs will omit the `Referer` header at the destination server. UTM query-string parameters survive, but server-side referrer-based attribution (e.g., direct-traffic fallback logic) will be impacted.

Unsubscribe link and physical address could not be confirmed — footer was outside the truncated source window.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**[FAIL] Broken CTA will 404** — the relative-URL issue in §2 means at least one primary CTA click leads to an error page, not the intended product or landing page.

**[WARN] Non-standard attribution parameters**
Tracking URLs use `cp_tp`, `cp_cid`, `mi_cid`, `mi_mid` (Movable Ink / adidas ESP params) but no standard GA4 UTM params (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are visible in the decoded destination. If GA4 is the web analytics platform, session attribution will be untagged and bucketed as direct/none.
