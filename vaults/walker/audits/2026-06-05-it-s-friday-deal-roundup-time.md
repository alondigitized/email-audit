# It's Friday Deal Roundup Time 🤩
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- I'll be straight with you — the second I saw that familiar red-and-yellow Inflation Relief banner, I exhaled. Here we go again. That banner has been sitting at the top of nearly every Shoe Carnival email I've opened for the past two weeks, and by now it's like wallpaper. I register it without seeing it.
- What actually pulled me in was scrolling down and finding Brooks. Two sections of it — a $40 off deal on the Ghost 1 and a $89.98 price on the Ghost 8. That's a real number for a shoe I've been circling. Back on May 30th they sent me a dedicated comfort email — Brooks and Skechers named right in the subject — and that one hit differently. This one buries the good stuff under Women's Sandal Blowout sections and Kids' deals that have nothing to do with me.
- The killer blow, before I even opened it? The preview text was a raw tracking URL. On my phone, where I'm doing most of my inbox browsing, that is straight-up ugly and it made me wonder for a half-second if it was legit. I would open it — I usually do with Shoe Carnival — but I'd open it skeptical, not excited. I probably wouldn't forward this to anybody, and I'd scroll straight past the women's sections to find whatever's in it for me.

## What's working

- **Brooks showing up by name** — two dedicated blocks with actual prices ($40 off, $89.98) is the kind of content I open Shoe Carnival for. That's my shoe.
- **$10 off $74.98+ with an online code** is a simple, usable offer — no jumping through hoops, no in-store-only catch on that one.
- **Loyalty reminder ("Check My Points | Join Our Team")** at the bottom is a nice nudge; I do have a SHOE PERKS balance and I like being reminded it's there.
- **"Friday" framing** gives the roundup a slight pulse — feels like today-only even if it doesn't say so explicitly.

## What's weak

- **Preview text is a raw URL.** On my phone, before I even open the thing, I'm staring at a tracking link. That's sloppy and it erodes trust right at the inbox level.
- **Women's Sandal Blowout leads the scroll and dominates prime real estate** — two sections for women's shoes before I get to something in my size range.
- **No comfort details on the Brooks blocks** — no mention of cushioning, arch support, or width. Just a price and a product name. For a guy who's choosing between the Ghost 1 and the Ghost 8, I need a reason, not just a number.
- **Inflation Relief banner again.** This is at minimum the seventh or eighth time I've seen it. It has zero meaning to me anymore — it's just a red rectangle now.
- **Too many lanes at once.** Kids' sandals, Nike Court, women's, athletics, in-store-only — it's a grab bag, not a curated send. The May 30th comfort roundup was better because it put my brands in the subject line and led with them.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the women's-first scroll and lead with Brooks.** If you know I've been opening emails with Brooks in the subject, put Brooks at the top. Don't make me scroll past three women's sections.
- 2. **Fix the preview text.** Whatever broke the preheader, fix it before the next send. A raw URL in the inbox preview is embarrassing and costs opens.
- 3. **Add one comfort detail to the Brooks block** — "superior cushioning," "arch support," anything that connects the price to why I should care about that specific shoe over the other.
- 4. **Retire the Inflation Relief banner** or swap it for something fresh. It's been in nearly every email for two weeks. It's invisible now.
- 5. **Segment the roundup.** If I've been clicking on men's comfort and Brooks, surface that first. The Women's Sandal Blowout can exist — just not above the fold for me.

## Technical Audit — Shoe Carnival "Friday Deal Roundup" (2026-06-05)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Five tracking redirect URLs contain a zero-width space (`U+200B`) embedded in the UTM parameter string, causing ASCII encoding failures that will break click attribution for those links. A second tracking domain has an unresolved `[UNIQUE]` merge token, resulting in a timeout on what is likely the unsubscribe redirect.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**CRITICAL — Zero-width space in UTM parameter (5 links)**

All five failures share the same root cause: a `U+200B` zero-width space character appended immediately after `SCA_20260605_friday_deal_roundup_AM` in the `t=` parameter value.

```
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?rd&i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260605_friday_deal_roundup_AM​
                                                                                                        ^
                                                        U+200B at position 82 (invisible in most editors)
```

This character is not ASCII-encodable and will cause redirect failures or silently corrupt the downstream UTM value in analytics. Likely introduced by a copy-paste from a rich-text source (Notion, Google Docs, Word) into the campaign build tool. All five affected parameter types: `?rd`, `?rl`, `?p`, `?f`, and the open-pixel `?i=...&amp;t=`.

**HIGH — HTML-encoded ampersand leaking into raw URL (1 link)**

The open-pixel URL uses `&amp;` instead of `&` in the raw href:

```
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&amp;t=utm_campaign=...
```

When this URL is fetched by an email client, `&amp;t=` is treated as a literal query parameter name, not a separator. The tracking server will mis-parse the parameter string. This is an HTML-entity escaping error in the template: `&amp;` is correct inside HTML attribute values, but the QA checker is reading the decoded URL and confirming the entity was not re-encoded after being passed through the redirect builder.

**HIGH — Unresolved merge token causing timeout (1 link)**

```
https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?rd&d=[UNIQUE]
```

`[UNIQUE]` was not substituted before send. Given the `?rd` parameter pattern (redirect) and the `d=` token (likely a subscriber or device identifier), this is plausibly the unsubscribe or preference-center redirect. If so, it constitutes a functional CAN-SPAM compliance failure — the unsubscribe mechanism is broken for recipients who received this literal URL. Confirm against the template source which anchor element carries this href.

Skipped due to click-redirect domain detection. No finding, but coverage is partial.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**WARN — Two tracking images missing `alt` attributes**

- Open pixel: `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=...`
- Second pixel: `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]`

Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` to suppress broken-image indicators when images are blocked. Screen readers will also announce an unlabeled image if `alt` is absent.

**LOW — Dark mode explicitly suppressed**

```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light" />
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light" />
```

This opts out of OS-level dark mode rendering in Apple Mail and Samsung Mail. Acceptable as an intentional choice if the design relies on fixed background colors, but should be documented as deliberate — not a default.

**INFO — Legacy DOCTYPE**

```html
<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN" ...>
```

Standard for commercial email; no action required.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**HIGH — UTM corruption propagates to analytics**

The zero-width space in `utm_campaign=SCA_20260605_friday_deal_roundup_AM​` will cause one of two failure modes depending on the ESP redirect server's URL encoding behavior:

1. **Percent-encoded forward**: campaign value arrives in GA4/Adobe as `SCA_20260605_friday_deal_roundup_AM%E2%80%8B` — a new, unmatched campaign dimension.
2. **Drop on encoding error**: redirect fails entirely, click is not attributed.

Either way, click-through data for this send will be split or lost in campaign reports.
