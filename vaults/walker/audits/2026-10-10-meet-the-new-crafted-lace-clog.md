# Meet the New Crafted Lace Clog
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-10**
## Executive summary

- Well, here's Crocs again, and this time there's a new shoe. The Crafted Lace Clog is the first thing in two weeks that isn't the 30% off and free shipping I got on 10/2, 10/3, 10/4 and 10/6. The headline reads "styles that really sleigh." It's a pun on sleigh bells, and a red clog on a little girl's ankle in a ruffle sock. The copy underneath says "She's Perfectly Festive." I'm a 62-year-old man, and nothing in this email was meant for me.
- The first thing I noticed was how little it told me. There's no price, no comfort or arch-support talk, and no offer beyond "free shipping on qualified orders" in the tiny black bar at the top. Below the hero there's an Afterpay block that takes up a lot of space. I don't buy shoes in four payments. The "Recommended for You" row is the same black Classic Clog and Baya Clog I've seen before. There's also a kids' clog and a Disney Pixar Lightning McQueen clog, and none of that is for a man my age.
- I wouldn't click, and I wouldn't forward it. I'd mark it read and move on. If I wanted a comfortable clog for walking around, nothing here tells me why this one is better than the Classic. It looks good, but it doesn't speak to me.

## What's working

- The hero is big and bold, and I could read "styles that really sleigh" on my phone without squinting.
- "New" in the white bubble tells me right away this isn't another rerun of the sale.
- The "Shop Crafted Lace" button is black, easy to see and easy to tap.
- The layout is simple and uncluttered, with plenty of space around everything. It reads fine with big text.
- It's a change from the 30% off email I've gotten four times this week.

## What's weak

- The hero is a girl's ankle in a ruffle sock, and the copy says "She's." Nothing speaks to a man my age.
- There's no price, no discount, and no reason to buy it today. "Free shipping on qualified orders" is tiny, and "qualified" makes me suspicious.
- The Afterpay block is bigger than the product copy. I don't want to pay for shoes in four payments, and the fine print about being over 18 and late fees is off-putting.
- "Recommended for You" doesn't feel personal. It has a kids' clog and a Disney character clog, and nothing about cushioning or arch support.
- The bottom buttons (Shop Clogs, Shop Jibbitz Charms, Shop Sale, Shop Sandals) are generic. Nothing says walking, comfort or men's. The Kids' Classic Clog and the Lightning McQueen clog sit awkwardly spaced in the grid.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the comfort story. Show the clog on an adult, with a line like "Cushioned, lightweight, easy to slip on." Tell me what the lace detail adds, and give me a price.
- 2. Shrink the Afterpay block to a single line, or move it to the footer. The product should have more room than the payment plan.
- 3. Make "Recommended for You" actually fit the subscriber. Show men's walking and slip-on styles, and drop the kids' and Disney clogs for someone who has never bought them.
- 4. Put the shipping or any offer in a visible banner with the terms. Say what "qualified" means, for example "Free shipping over $50."
- 5. Make the CTA more specific: "Shop the Crafted Lace Clog" or "See the Crafted Lace Clog," with the price.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The source is well-structured (responsive CSS, dark-mode-safe resets, `lang="en"`, hidden preheader). Confirmed issues are limited to missing `alt` on product images, the recipient's address exposed in image URLs, and two ineffective `<meta>` tags. The supplied HTML is truncated and the QA run included no header data, so footer, unsubscribe and authentication checks can't be confirmed.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** 26 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe (`click.crocs-email.com`). Destinations and redirect health are unconfirmed.
- **Three tracking pixels:**
  - `https://click.crocs-email.com/open.aspx?...&d=10199&bmt=0`, a 1x1 open pixel with `alt=""`.
  - `https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&c_emailopened=Yes&d_dpuid=...`, an Adobe Audience Manager pixel. It is hidden with `visibility:hidden;display:none` and has no `alt`.
  - The `recs.igodigital.com` image requests (see section 4).
- **Pixel loading:** `display:none` pixels may not fire in some clients. Open data from `demdex` and `open.aspx` can therefore diverge.
- **Unencoded space:** the recommendation image URLs contain `category=Recommended for You`, with raw spaces in `src`. Strict parsers may truncate or break the URL. It should be `Recommended%20for%20You`.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `alt` on product images (real issue):** the four `10745993.recs.igodigital.com/rr/v2/.../i/1` through `/i/4` images have no `alt`. They are content images ("Recommended for You"), not decorative. Screen readers will announce the URL or nothing, and with images blocked the slots are empty.
- **Missing `alt` on the `demdex` pixel:** this one is hidden and non-content. Add `alt=""` for consistency, which is low severity.
- **Ineffective meta tags:** `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` and `<meta content="IE=edge" />` have no `http-equiv` attribute, so the browser ignores both. The charset declaration is not actually set in the HTML. It is normally covered by the MIME `Content-Type` header, which I can't see here.
- **Generic title:** `<title>Crocs</title>` is the same for every send. This is minor, since email clients rarely display it.
- **Not confirmable from the truncated source:** `role="presentation"` on layout tables, `mso` fallbacks and VML buttons.
- **Passing:** `lang="en"`, viewport meta, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, `format-detection`, and a hidden preheader with a zero-width-character spacer.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **No unresolved tokens** (`%%...%%`, `{{...}}`, `[[...]]`) appear in the visible source.
- **Recipient email in image URLs:** the recommendation URLs are `https://10745993.recs.igodigital.com/rr/v2/685c6ddd06fd7c003f1cd785/i/N/walker@agentmail.to?category=Recommended for You`. The subscriber's plaintext email address is a path segment in every request.
  - It leaks to the recs provider and to any proxy or log along the path (including image proxies).
  - It is not URL-encoded (`@` appears raw).
  - A hashed or opaque subscriber ID would be safer.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Not verifiable:** the HTML was truncated before the footer, and no headers were supplied.
  - There is no evidence for the physical postal address, the visible unsubscribe link, or `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058).
  - There is no evidence for SPF, DKIM or DMARC results either.
- The sender (`email@crocs-email.com`) uses a dedicated sending subdomain-style domain, and tracking is on the matching `click.crocs-email.com`. That alignment is consistent, but not proof of authentication.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **No issues found** that can be confirmed. All 26 click links are wrapped by `click.crocs-email.com`, so UTM parameters and landing URLs can't be read from the source or probed. Resolving the redirects would be needed to check UTM naming, the campaign matching "Crafted Lace Clog", and landing-page status.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to the four `igodigital` recommendation images, such as the product name. Add `alt=""` to the `demdex` pixel.
2. Stop putting the raw recipient email in `igodigital` image URLs. Use a hashed or opaque subscriber key, and URL-encode `category=Recommended%20for%20You`.
3. Fix the meta tags: `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">` and `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge">`.
4. Resolve the 26 skipped tracking links to check final destinations, status codes and UTM params. Capture the full source (footer) and the raw headers (`List-Unsubscribe`, `Authentication-Results`) so the compliance section can be audited.
