# Starting today: Buy 2, get 30% off select styles
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-06**
## Executive summary

- Crocs again. After the 10/23 "Extra 50% Off" and the 31% sales at the end of October, this is another new offer. This time it's Buy 2, 30% off. The headline is huge and I could read it on my phone without squinting. The big black "Buy 2 / Take 30% Off" on pink, with one button below it, is easy to take in.
- The offer isn't as simple as it first looks. I have to buy two pairs to get the 30%, and "Exclusions Apply" is printed twice. I'm one man with two feet. I'd consider a second pair only if the first one fit well. The photo shows the Baya clog, which is a perfectly good garden shoe. But there's no word about cushioning, arch support or walking, and those are what I shop for.
- Would I click? Maybe, out of curiosity about which styles are included. I wouldn't forward it. I'd mark it read and carry on.

## What's working

- The "Buy 2 Take 30% Off" headline is big, bold and readable on a phone.
- There's one clear black "Shop The Sale" button, and it's easy to tap.
- The three clogs in neutral colors (stone, olive, grey) suit me better than the pink-and-charms stuff they sent on 11/5.
- The fine print gives a real deadline, Sunday 11/8 at 11:59pm MT. That's honest and specific.
- It's a short, simple email with no clutter up top.

## What's weak

- The offer needs a second pair, which doesn't fit how I shop. I buy one pair that fits.
- "Exclusions Apply" appears twice and the styles aren't named. I don't know whether the shoes I'd want are included.
- Nothing about comfort, cushioning, arch support or walking. It's just a picture of a clog.
- The deadline is only in the fine print at the bottom. The top of the email doesn't create any urgency.
- The 40% off accessories is aimed at the Jibbitz crowd, not at me.
- The refer-a-friend block and app download links add noise.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the deadline up top ("Ends Sunday") and name the styles that are included. I shouldn't have to hunt through the fine print for either.
- 2. Add one line about comfort, like "all-day cushioning, easy slip-on", next to the clogs. That's the reason I'd buy.
- 3. Offer a single-pair discount to loyal subscribers, or say plainly that the second pair is a gift or spare.
- 4. Show a men's walking or slip-on style for people like me, not only the clog.

## Technical Audit

**Technical audit: Crocs, "Starting today: Buy 2, get 30% off select styles"**

**1. Technical Summary**
The visible markup is structurally sound: it has `lang="en"`, a viewport meta tag, a mobile media query at 639px, and a preheader. Confirmed defects are 7 `http://` image sources, one tracking pixel with no `alt`, and `<meta>` tags that lack their `http-equiv` or `charset` attributes. The HTML was truncated, so footer, unsubscribe and destination-URL checks could not be done.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- 24 links were skipped because they are on tracking or click-redirect domains (likely `click.crocs-email.com`). Their final destinations and HTTP status are unverified.
- The open pixel is `https://click.crocs-email.com/open.aspx?qs=…&d=10213&bmt=0`. It is 1x1 with `alt=""`, which is correct.
- A second third-party pixel is `https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&c_emailopened=Yes&d_dpuid=…`. This is Adobe Audience Manager (demdex). It is a separate tracking beacon and has no `alt` attribute (see section 3).
- No broken links are confirmed.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- **Non-HTTPS images (7).** All are served from `http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/`:
  - App Store badge (`9c36c0ef-…png`)
  - Google Play badge (`13246762-…png`)
  - Find Your Store (`c4df71ec-…png`)
  - Instagram (`e18949ff-…png`)
  - Facebook (`80f68cd8-…png`)
  - TikTok (`51d8144e-…png`)
  - YouTube (`1187ef58-…png`)

  Clients that don't proxy images may block or warn on these, and the footer would then render without its icons. The same host likely serves HTTPS elsewhere in the email.
- **Missing `alt` (1).** The demdex `event?d_dpid=196107…` image has no `alt` attribute. The attribute should be `alt=""` because the image is decorative. Screen readers may announce the URL otherwise.
- **Malformed meta tags.** Three tags are incomplete:
  - `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` has no `http-equiv="Content-Type"` or `charset=`.
  - `<meta content="IE=edge" />` has no `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`.
  - `<meta name="x-apple-disable-message-reformatting" />` is fine.

  Without the `http-equiv`, the first two do nothing. If an ESP or sanitizer stripped the attributes, this is an artifact of the pipeline and not a Crocs defect. The charset is still normally set through the MIME `Content-Type` header.
- **Generic title.** `<title>Crocs</title>` is generic. This is minor.
- No other issues are visible in the truncated source.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
No issues found. No unrendered tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`, `[FIRSTNAME]`) appear in the visible source.

**5. Compliance**
- **Authentication.** The QA check found no `Authentication-Results` header. The QA note attributes this to the AgentMail relay, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC are unverified and not confirmed failures. The sender `email@crocs-email.com` is a dedicated sending domain, so verify alignment with the raw headers.
- **Unsubscribe and address.** The unsubscribe link, physical postal address and `List-Unsubscribe` header are not in the truncated source or QA data. They are unverified, not confirmed missing.

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity**
- UTM parameters and landing-page alignment can't be assessed. Every click goes through the redirect domain, and the probe was skipped.
- The promo copy ("Buy 2, get 30% off select styles"; preheader "accessories for 40% off") can't be matched to a destination from the available data.

**7. Recommendations**
1. Switch all 7 `image.crocs-email.com` assets from `http://` to `https://`.
2. Add `alt=""` to the demdex pixel `<img>`.
3. Confirm the `<meta>` tags carry `http-equiv="Content-Type"` and `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"` in the sent message, or drop them if the pipeline strips them.
4. Re-run the audit on the full HTML and raw headers. This would let you check the unsubscribe link, `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, the postal address, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment.
5. Resolve the 24 redirect links with a follow-redirects probe. Check that final URLs return 200 and carry the expected UTMs.
