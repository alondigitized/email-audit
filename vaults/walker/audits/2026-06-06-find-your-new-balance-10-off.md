# Find Your (NEW!) Balance + $10 Off
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- Well, the red-and-yellow Inflation Relief banner is back. That's thirteen — maybe fourteen — in a row now. I've stopped counting. At this point I see that banner the moment I scroll down and my thumb is already moving toward the delete button. But then I hit that New Balance section and I actually stopped. "Weekends Call for Comfort." Someone over there finally figured out what I want to hear. Brooks showing up below that made me feel like at least half this email was written for somebody like me.
- The subject line is clever — I caught the New Balance pun on the second read and I appreciated it. The $10 off code was right there, no digging. And the Shoe Perks reward at the bottom reminded me I've got points stacking up.
- I'd click the New Balance button, I'll be honest. I wouldn't forward this to a friend — it's too much like the seventeen other Shoe Carnival emails they've already gotten this month. But I wouldn't mark it read and move on either. That New Balance section saved this one.

## What's working

- "Weekends Call for Comfort" for New Balance is copy written for me — a 62-year-old who just wants a shoe he can put on Saturday morning without thinking about it.
- Brooks showing up with a $40-off sale is a double hit — that's my exact cross-shop brand, right there in the same email.
- The $10 off code (SAVE10) is spelled out clearly, minimum spend stated, no mystery. That's the kind of transparency I trust.
- The Shoe Perks reward reminder at the bottom works — I do have points, and seeing that nudges me.
- Subject line is short, doesn't scream, and the New Balance pun earned a second look from me.

## What's weak

- The broken preview text is an embarrassment — what landed in my inbox was a raw tracking URL, not a single word of copy. That alone almost cost them the open.
- The Nike Court / Air Jordan section at the top is completely useless to me, and it's the first thing I see after the banner — wrong audience, wrong placement.
- That Inflation Relief banner has been in every single email for two-plus weeks. It's invisible to me now. If the brand has a real offer, they need a new skin for it — this one has turned into wallpaper.
- No specific product details anywhere — no mention of arch support, cushioning, or whether these come in wide widths. I need that before I click "buy."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the New Balance section.** Move "Weekends Call for Comfort" to the top of the body, above the Nike content. That's the offer that earns a click from me; it shouldn't be buried below a brand I don't shop.
- 2. **Retire the Inflation Relief banner or redesign it completely.** After 14 emails the creative is invisible. Same offer, fresh look — or it's wasted real estate.
- 3. **Add one product-level detail in the NB/Brooks sections.** "Extra cushion. Wide widths available." Two words of copy and I'm much closer to clicking.
- 4. **Fix the preview text immediately.** A tracking URL in the preview pane is technically broken and makes the send look like spam.
- 5. **Give the offer a visible end date.** Even "this weekend only" would give me a reason to act now instead of later.

### 1. Technical Summary

Three tracking links are broken due to a zero-width space character (`\u200b`) embedded in the URL string, causing ASCII encoding failures. Four product/beacon images are missing alt attributes, creating accessibility gaps.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Critical — Zero-width space corrupts 3 tracking URLs**

The QA scanner hit an `'ascii' codec can't encode character '\u200b'` error on all three variants of the `ieezzy5m.emltrk.com` link:

```
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?rd&i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260606_nike_brooks_athletics_AM​
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?rl&i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260606_nike_brooks_athletics_AM​
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?p&i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260606_nike_brooks_athletics_AM​
```

The stray `\u200b` appears at position 83–84 — immediately after the trailing `AM` in the campaign token. This is almost certainly a copy-paste artifact introduced during template editing. Depending on the ESP's URL handling, these links may silently redirect to a 400/error page or drop tracking entirely rather than surfacing a visible broken link to the recipient.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text on 3 product images:**

These are Demandware/Salesforce Commerce Cloud product images. Screen readers will announce the raw filename. Should carry descriptive alt text (e.g., `alt="New Balance 574 — Men's Sneaker"`).

**Missing alt on tracking beacon:**

`https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&...` and `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]` are both missing `alt=""`. Tracking pixels should carry an explicit empty alt (`alt=""`), not omit the attribute — some screen readers will attempt to read the src URL otherwise.

**Dark mode:** The `color-scheme` and `supported-color-schemes` meta tags are set to `light` only. No dark-mode media query or forced-colors handling is present. Not a breakage, but images and white-background sections may invert poorly in dark-mode clients (Apple Mail, Outlook on Windows 11).
