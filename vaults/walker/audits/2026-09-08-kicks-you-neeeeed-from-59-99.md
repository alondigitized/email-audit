# Kicks you neeeeed from $59.99
**Score:** 2/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-08**
## Executive summary

- Well, this one's a total swing and a miss — every single shoe in this email is a women's shoe. Nike running shoe with a pink swoosh, an Adidas with gold stripes, some kind of platform sneaker, and then boots and slingback heels down in the "more for your rotation" section. Not a single men's casual or walking shoe in sight. I don't wear heels, I don't wear boots with heels on them, and "shop all women" is not a button I'm ever clicking.
- The "$59.99" price point almost got me for half a second, and I'll admit the sneaker style itself doesn't look bad, but then I actually look at the picture and realize none of this is sized or styled for me. This is a flat "mark as read" — actually, this is closer to a "why is DSW sending me this" moment. Not forwarding it to anyone, and it does nothing to make me trust the brand more.

## What's working

- The $59.99 price is called out clearly in a big orange price bubble on the hero image — that's a real, concrete number, not vague "sale" language.
- Layout is clean and uncluttered, easy to scan on my phone without squinting.
- "Shop Wide Width" and "Shop Wide Calf" buttons show DSW at least thinks about fit variety, even if it's misapplied here.

## What's weak

- Every shoe shown — sneakers, boots, heels — is a women's style. Nothing here says "men's" anywhere on the page.
- "Shop All Women" as the main navigation CTA tells me flat out this email wasn't built with me in mind.
- No arch support, cushioning, or comfort-tech callouts anywhere — just "iconic kicks" and vague rotation talk.
- The "Can You Keep a Secret??" VIP app teaser is just noise — doesn't tell me anything or offer me anything concrete.
- I'm not a first-time DSW browser; if they know my past clicks were on men's comfort shoes, this doesn't show it at all.

## Recommendations

- 1. Send men's inventory to male subscribers — swap in a comfort walking shoe or slip-on with visible cushioning, not a women's Nike/Adidas lineup. This alone would fix the email for someone like me.
- 2. Lead with a comfort/fit callout in the hero copy — "all-day cushioning" or "arch support built in" — instead of just "iconic kicks."
- 3. Cut the "Can You Keep a Secret??" filler block — it wastes scroll space with zero payoff.
- 4. Add a size row that includes men's sizing if this is going to a mixed list, or gate the whole send by gender history.
