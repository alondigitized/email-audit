# Friday Deals + 15% Off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- They said "FINAL DAY" yesterday. Today I've got another email in my inbox. That cadence is starting to wear on me — this is somewhere around email number eleven from Shoe Carnival in the past ten days, and every single one of them opens with that same red-and-yellow Inflation Relief banner. I know what this email looks like before I even open it.
- That said, "15% Off" in the subject is a number, and numbers slow me down. Inside, I found the usual stacked-tile structure — six offer blocks, three of which are women's sandals or kids' athletics, none of which are for a 62-year-old man with aching feet. But then I got to the Skechers Slip-Ins block, 25% off, with a direct "Shop Skechers Slip-ins" link, and that's the one tile in this whole email that felt like it was actually meant for me. Stack the 15% code on top of that and I'm paying attention.

- The Skechers Slip-Ins tile with "UP TO 25% OFF" and a direct shop link is exactly my category — no laces, cushioned, comfort-first — and it's there
- Online code MAY15 is printed in plain text right in the hero block; no digging through fine print, no "check the app"
- The Shoe Perks $10 reward barcode at the bottom is screenshot-ready — I can walk into a store with that on my phone
- "Get Coupon!" is a big, tappable button — easy to hit on a phone with average-sized fingers
- Afterpay mention is a genuinely useful nudge for someone buying a pair or two at once

## What's weak

- Two out of six offer tiles are Women's — BOGO sandals and a Sandal Blowout — both labeled as such; I scrolled right past
- "Kids' Starting at $29.98" in the Athletic Sale block is a dead end for me; empty nest here
- That raw tracking URL leaked into the preview text — it's the first thing I saw in my inbox and it looked broken
- The Inflation Relief banner is on day eleven; at this point it reads like a screen-saver, not a campaign
- "FINAL DAY" was yesterday's subject line — today's email with a different offer and no acknowledgment of that undercuts trust in the urgency claims

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the send** — suppress women's and kids' tiles for male subscribers, or at minimum lead with the men's comfort tile instead of BOGO women's sandals
- 2. **Fix the preheader immediately** — a raw tracking URL in the preview slot is the single highest-leverage fix available right now; it costs a click before the email even opens
- 3. **Retire the Inflation Relief banner** — eleven days is two weeks; the creative is invisible now; a fresh visual hook would do more work
- 4. **Put a real expiry date on the hero** — "LIMITED TIME" means nothing; "Today through midnight" or "Ends Sunday 5/31" creates actual urgency
- 5. **First-name the subject line** — "Walker, your 15% off is here" pulls harder than "Friday Deals + 15% Off" for someone who's received this template ten times already

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed `<meta>` — IE compatibility directive will not fire:**
```html

```
Missing required `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`. Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
```

**Malformed charset meta — incomplete `content` value:**
```html
<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />
```
`content` is truncated (should be `"text/html; charset=utf-8"`). The correct form is:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Most clients will still detect UTF-8 via BOM/sniffing, but this is a spec violation.

**Dark mode — incomplete declaration:**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light" />
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light" />
```
Only `light` is declared. In Apple Mail and Outlook dark mode, images with white/transparent backgrounds and white-on-dark text will invert unexpectedly. Either add `dark` support or add explicit `background-color` overrides for known problem areas.

**Conflicting responsive breakpoints — 600px vs 660px gap:**
The stylesheet defines rules at `max-width: 600px` (block 1) and `max-width: 660px` (block 2) separately. Between 601–660px, some rules apply and others don't, creating a dead zone where layout can break unpredictably. Consolidate to a single breakpoint.
