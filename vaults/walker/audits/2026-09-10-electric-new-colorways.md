# Electric new colorways
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-10**
## Executive summary

- Well, "Electric new colorways" and that's exactly what I get — a shoe that looks like it belongs at a rave, all neon green and black. My grandkids might get a kick out of it, but that's not something I'd be caught wearing to church or the grocery store. It's a step up from last week's cliff-diver and canyon-jumper nonsense, at least — I can see actual shoes here, and they're front and center where they belong.
- The "Available in Wide" section is the one bright spot for me — my feet aren't what they used to be, and knowing a shoe comes in wide without me having to dig for it saves me a trip to the store just to find out it doesn't fit. But between the punk-rock color scheme up top and that free hat gimmick with a $200 spend threshold, this doesn't feel like it's talking to me.
- I'd skim past this one. Not a click, not a forward — maybe I'd glance at the "Walk On" section since that's more my speed, but the neon stuff up top already lost me before I got there.

## What's working

- The Clifton PRO and Bondi 9 shoes are laid out clean, two-by-two, with men's and women's clearly labeled — no hunting required.
- "Available in Wide" called out by name is genuinely useful to a guy my age with wide feet.
- The "Walk On" section with the older-looking fella out for a stroll in cushioned shoes is closer to who I actually am than most of what HOKA sends me.
- Real product shots, no broken images, nothing overlapping — technically it renders fine.

## What's weak

- That hero shoe is neon green and black — looks like something a teenager would wear to a laser tag birthday party, not "electric" in a way that appeals to me.
- The free hat offer requires a $200+ purchase, which is a lot of shoe money just to get a free hat I probably wouldn't wear anyway.
- The Strava marathon challenge at the bottom is a hard miss — I'm not running a 26.2, at your own pace or otherwise, at 62.
- Nothing here mentions arch support or all-day cushioning specifically, which is the whole reason I'd even open a shoe email.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the "Walk On" cushioned walking shoe instead of the neon racer — that's the shoe that actually fits my life, put it up top where I'll see it first.
- 2. Drop or shrink the marathon/Strava pitch, or at least segment it away from folks who've already shown they browse comfort/walking styles.
- 3. Call out arch support and all-day cushioning in the hero copy, not just "focused on the run."
- 4. Lower that free-hat threshold or make the free gift more obviously tied to a shoe I'd actually buy.
