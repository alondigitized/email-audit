# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-11-20**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I'm Walker, 62, on my phone, and I did not get to buy shoes today. The homepage loads and the menu is easy to follow. But the size picker had no size selected, so the Add to Cart tap didn't add anything, and the cart page came up blank white. The cart badge still said 0 on the last screen. I did find a men's slip-in shoe, but I couldn't buy it.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** That's the same as yesterday.

The score is flat because the cart is still broken. Browsing is decent, but a customer who can't check out is worth nothing to the store.

## 3. What's Working

- **The menu drawer is good for my eyes and thumbs.** Men's has big, plain rows: Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections. I can read it without squinting.
- **Men's Shoes has a "Hands Free Slip-Ins" tile and a "Walking Shoes" tile at the top.** Those are the two things I shop for.
- **The top chips on the homepage ("Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Arch Fit," "Max Cushion") match what I want.** They're easy to find.
- **The product page is clear.** It shows the name, $135.00, 4.5 stars with 29 reviews, and Klarna/Afterpay payment options.
- **The size buttons are large, and sold-out sizes are crossed out.** I could tap them comfortably.
- **The search results show prices, color swatches and an "Also in Wide" tag.** The wide tag is useful to me.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Add to Cart failed.** The page said "Please select a size," and no size had been picked. The cart badge never moved off 0. The size error sits well below the price, so I'd miss it. Nothing scrolled me to it.
- **The cart page was blank white.** There was no items list, no empty-cart message and no error. I would assume the site is broken and leave.
- **The "Dismiss Popups" and "Log In" steps look identical to the homepage.**
  - I couldn't tell whether I was logged in.
  - Nothing said "Welcome back, Walker."
  - Nothing felt personalized.
- **The banner says "Members Take 30% OFF Sitewide," but the product page says "Excluded from promotions."** The same shoe hit me with both messages. That kind of mismatch costs a brand trust.
- **The homepage photo is a dressed-up couple under holiday lights.** It has no comfort message, and the shoes are barely visible. It doesn't speak to me.
- **The "comfort shoes" search put a women's loafer (Cypress - Chic Surge) first.** I'm a man shopping men's casual, and the results mix genders.
- **I saw no comparison content.** There was no Hoka, Brooks or New Balance comparison, and nothing on price versus quality or arch support. The "Arch Fit" chip is the only nod to arch support.
- **Some text is small.** The "Members Get 30% OFF! Applied at Cart" line and the product subtext are on the small side.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart first.** Make the cart page always show something: items, or "Your cart is empty," or an error with a retry button.
2. **Fix the missing-size flow.** When someone taps Add to Cart without a size, scroll to the size grid and highlight it. Better, move the "Please select a size" warning right next to the button.
3. **Make the banner and product page agree.** Either exclude the product from the banner offer in the banner's wording, or stop excluding it.
4. **Show that I'm logged in.** Put my name or a "Hi, Walker" in the header, with recently viewed shoes and my last size.
5. **Filter "comfort shoes" by the gender I've been browsing.** If I came from Men's, show men's results first.
6. **Put comfort content up top.** A few lines on cushioning, arch support and slip-in ease would help, along with an honest price-per-wear comparison against Hoka, Brooks and New Balance.
7. **Bump the small promo and subtext sizes for older eyes.**

## 6. Bottom Line

The store has what I want, but I couldn't get it into a cart. A blank cart page and a silent size error would send me to Brooks or Famous Footwear. Fix the cart and the size prompt, and this journey gets a lot better.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The journey's data is only partly reliable. Two things limit the audit:

- **Web Vitals are incomplete.** LCP and CLS were not captured on any of the 8 steps, so only TTFB is available.
- **The View Cart step probably audited an error page, not the cart.** The one network error is `429 https://www.skechers.com/cart/`, and the View Cart axe results (no `<title>`, no `lang`, no `<main>`, no `<h1>`) are what a bare rate-limit response looks like.

The HTML samples for "Log In" and "Men category" both report `https://www.skechers.com/` and match the homepage markup. Either those steps opened overlays or client-side views without a URL change, or the step URL was recorded before navigation finished. Axe also reported nothing for the Log In, Men, Men > Shoes, Product detail and Add to Cart steps. I can't tell from this data whether those pages are clean or were never scanned.

Confirmed findings:
- 3 critical and 4 serious axe violations on the homepage, plus a View Cart page that failed on document basics.
- Heavy third-party tag load.
- Repeated Permissions-Policy `unload` violations.

## Accessibility

18 violations were reported, on 3 pages.

**Homepage (10)**
- **Critical:** `button-name`. Buttons have no discernible text, probably icon-only controls such as search, menu or carousel.
- **Critical:** `aria-required-parent`. An ARIA role is used outside its required parent, e.g. `listitem`, `tab` or `menuitem` without `list`, `tablist` or `menu`.
- **Serious:** `link-name`. Links have no accessible name, probably image or icon links.
- **Moderate (landmarks):**
  - `landmark-main-is-top-level`
  - `landmark-no-duplicate-main`
  - `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`
  - `landmark-unique`
  - `region`

  Together these suggest more than one `<main>`, with `<main>` and `<aside>` nested inside other landmarks.
- **Moderate:** `page-has-heading-one`. There is no `<h1>`.
- **Minor:** `aria-allowed-role`. A role is set that the element doesn't allow.

**View Cart (4)**
- **Serious:** `document-title`. No `<title>`.
- **Serious:** `html-has-lang`. No `lang` attribute. The homepage does set `lang="en"`, which supports the error-page theory.
- **Moderate:** `landmark-one-main`.
- **Moderate:** `page-has-heading-one`.

**Search "comfort shoes" (4)**
- `aria-allowed-role` (minor).
- `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region` (moderate).

The landmark and `aria-allowed-role` issues repeat on the homepage and search pages, so they likely come from shared header, footer or template code.

- **No `<h1>`** on the homepage, View Cart and (per the axe results) possibly other pages. This is a weaker on-page signal and a structure problem.

## Performance

- **LCP and CLS are not available for any step.** They show as `?` on all 8, which points to a collection problem in the harness (observers registered too late, or navigations not completing). No LCP or CLS conclusions can be drawn.
- **TTFB:**

  | Step | TTFB |
  |---|---|
  | Homepage | 502ms |
  | Log In | 450ms |
  | Men category | 450ms |
  | Product detail | 357ms |
  | Add to Cart | 357ms |
  | View Cart | 125ms |
  | Men > Shoes | 26ms |
  | Search "comfort shoes" | 19ms |

  - The Homepage, Log In and Men figures are acceptable, though the homepage is near the 500–800ms "needs improvement" boundary on a throttled link.
  - The Log In and Men figures (450ms) look reused from the previous document rather than measured on new navigations.
  - The 26ms and 19ms figures are not credible as origin TTFB. They are most likely SPA or soft navigations, bfcache or cache hits, or stale navigation entries.
  - Product detail and Add to Cart share 357ms, which fits Add to Cart being an XHR with no new document.
- **Third-party script load is heavy.** The homepage `<head>` has about 20 or more async third-party tags:
  - GTM, GA4, Google Ads, DoubleClick, Bing UET
  - TikTok, Pinterest, Snapchat, Reddit, Facebook, Nextdoor
  - Taboola, Yahoo, RTB House (`creativecdn`), Impact, Tune
  - Monetate (two scripts), Quantum Metric, Bloomreach, Pulse Insights, TrustArc
  
  The Men category sample shows still more (Taboola, Snapchat, Reddit, Facebook, Nextdoor), so tags accumulate as the session goes on. This puts main-thread, network and INP pressure on a mobile CPU.
- **Fonts:** `webfontloader.js` is loaded from cdnjs (`cdnjs.cloudflare.com`). This adds a third-party dependency and a JS-driven font load (`wf-*-active` classes) that can cause FOUT or layout shift.
- **GTM preview tags in production.** The GTM URL has `gtm_preview=env-1` and `gtm_auth=…`, so it is loading a non-default GTM environment. Confirm this is intentional.
- **Pulse Insights** is loaded with an SRI `integrity` attribute on a vendor bootstrapper URL. If the vendor updates the file, the hash fails and the script is blocked.

## Mobile Optimization

- **Viewport height variable:** the `<html>` element carries `style="--vh: 6.64px"`. This looks wrong for a mobile viewport. It may be a JS bug computing `innerHeight * 0.01` before layout, or an artifact of the test viewport. Any layout that relies on `--vh` (`calc(var(--vh) * 100)`) could collapse.
- **Not verifiable:** the viewport meta tag, tap-target sizes and responsive images. The truncated source doesn't show them, and axe didn't report `meta-viewport` or `target-size` issues.
- **Main-thread cost from tags:** the third-party load above is the main mobile risk (see Performance).

## Console & Network Errors

21 console errors were reported, but only 10 were listed. The two patterns below are the only ones I can assess.

- **`Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document` (6 of the 10 listed).**
  - A script registers an `unload` event handler, and the page's Permissions-Policy blocks it.
  - Chrome is deprecating `unload`, and it also disables bfcache.
  - The likely sources are third-party tags (analytics or session replay such as Quantum Metric). The source isn't identified in the data.
  - Replace the handlers with `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`.
- **`Unsupported Summarizer API languages were specified…` (4 of the 10 listed).**
  - This is the Chrome built-in AI Summarizer API called without supported language options, and it recurs on multiple pages.
  - It is probably an injected script, extension or tag rather than first-party code. Identify the caller.
  - It is non-fatal but noisy.
- **Network: 1 error.** `429 https://www.skechers.com/cart/` is rate limiting or bot protection, probably triggered by automated traffic. It is the likely cause of the failed View Cart accessibility result.
  - If real shoppers can hit 429 on `/cart/`, it is a serious conversion risk. Review rate-limit thresholds and the bot-management rules.
  - If only the test client is affected, treat the View Cart data as invalid.

## Recommendations

1. **Fix the harness.**
   - Register the LCP and CLS `PerformanceObserver`s with `buffered: true` before navigation.
   - Wait for `load` or `networkidle` on each step.
   - Record the final URL per step, and re-run axe on each distinct page.
2. **Treat the View Cart data as invalid** until it is re-run with the 429 handled (back-off, session warm-up, or allow-listing the monitor). Then confirm whether `/cart/` itself is returning 429 for real users.
3. **Fix the critical and serious accessibility issues on the homepage:**
   - Add accessible names (`aria-label` or visible text) to icon-only buttons and links.
   - Correct the ARIA role parent and child structure.
4. **Clean up the landmark structure.** Use exactly one top-level `<main>`, move `<aside>` outside other landmarks, give repeated `<nav>` landmarks unique `aria-label`s, and put page content inside landmarks.
5. **Add a single `<h1>`** per page.
6. **Remove or replace `unload` handlers.** Use `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`, and find which tag registers them.
7. **Audit the tag stack.** Remove duplicates and unused pixels, load the rest through GTM with consent gating, and defer non-essential tags until after interaction.
8. **Investigate the `--vh: 6.64px` value** and the GTM `gtm_preview` environment parameter on production.
9. **Self-host the web fonts** and use `font-display: swap` plus `preload`, in place of `webfontloader` from a CDN.
10. **Identify the Summarizer API caller** and fix or remove its language options.
