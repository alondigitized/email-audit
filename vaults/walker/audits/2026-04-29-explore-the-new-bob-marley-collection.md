# Explore the new Bob Marley collection
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-29**
## Executive summary

- I'll be honest — I had to look twice at the sender to make sure this wasn't spam. Adidas. Okay. I wear Skechers, but I'm on a few mailing lists. The Bob Marley hook got my attention for about three seconds — I grew up listening to the guy — but once I scrolled past that big photo, I realized pretty fast this email wasn't built for someone like me. It's dark, fashion-heavy, no comfort technology mentioned anywhere, and it's pitching *football* — meaning soccer, not the kind I watched growing up. The Mix and Match discount buried in the second half is the most useful thing in here, and even that requires buying two items. I'd scroll right past this on my phone.
- Wrong brand for me to begin with. Nothing targeted at my age, my comfort needs, or how I actually shop. The Bob Marley brand equity is real, but it's not doing heavy lifting for a 62-year-old guy who just wants a good deal on shoes.

## What's working

- **Bob Marley name recognition.** Even I know who he is. The big hero image stops the scroll momentarily — that's something.
- **"Mix, Match, Save" is a clear value proposition.** 30% off with a code on two or more items — I understand that. Simple enough.
- **Clean layout.** Not cluttered. I can read the main sections without pinching and zooming, which I appreciate.

## What's weak

- **Completely off-target for me.** I'm a Skechers Plus member browsing Men's comfort. Nothing here speaks to comfort, fit, or practicality. It reads like it was built for a 22-year-old streetwear fan.
- **The football framing means nothing to me.** "Bob Marley loved football" — they mean soccer. That's a hard connection to make with an American Boomer who grew up with the NFL.
- **Dark, moody imagery.** "The Mix and Match Event" image is all black — visually striking, maybe, but it doesn't show me products I'd want to buy. I need to see what I'm getting.
- **No product shots.** I never see actual shoes or gear I could imagine wearing. Just big artistic photos.
- **No personalization whatsoever.** This reads like a mass blast. Nobody greeted me by name, nobody referenced my purchase history or the fact that I buy men's casual. I'm a stranger here.
- **"Football is Freedom" tagline.** That means nothing to me. Sounds like marketing-speak.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show the products.** A row of actual shoes with prices and a "Shop Men's" button would do more for me than a moody black photo.
- 2. **Lead with the discount.** Don't bury the 30% off code below two hero images — put it where I can see it in the first scroll.
- 3. **Explain the Bob Marley connection in plain English.** One sentence. "Bob Marley loved football. We made shoes to celebrate that." Done. Right now the copy assumes I already know the story.
- 4. **Use my name.** If adidas knows who I am, act like it. "Walker, mix and match and save 30%" is infinitely better than a generic blast.
- 5. **For a guy like me, swap in comfort messaging.** Even within the Bob Marley line — if there's a slip-on or a comfortable trainer — say so. That's what would get me to click.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

Any recipient clicking the linked asset (image `60041370-7c7d-4263-98a9-edd910448b29`) will hit a dead destination. ### 3. Rendering & Accessibility


Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all proxy images over HTTPS; a plain-HTTP `src` will either be blocked or the proxy will fail the mixed-content request, rendering the image broken. Change both domains to `https://`.

This suppresses the `Referer` header on all outbound navigations from the email. This is intentional for some senders but means landing-page analytics will not receive email-origin referrer signals. If referrer data feeds any downstream analytics (separate from UTM params), this suppresses it.
