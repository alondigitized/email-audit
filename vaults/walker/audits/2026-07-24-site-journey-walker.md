# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 4/10 (prev 4/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-24**
## Full review
---

## 1. Executive Summary

Mixed bag — Skechers gets some things right for a guy like me (the Men's menu has "Hands Free Slip-ins" right there, and Arch Fit is called out by name on the product page), but the back half of this journey fell apart. The cart page loaded completely blank — I would've thought my phone froze and walked away. Search returned Women's shoes first when I searched "comfort shoes." And I never got a clear signal that I was actually logged in. Yesterday was a 4/10, and today is holding right there.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

**No change from yesterday.** The blank cart is a conversion killer. Search gender-mismatch pushes me toward a competitor. The homepage is still pitching basketball and running to what should be a comfort-casual audience.

---

## 3. What's Working

- **"Hands Free Slip-ins" in the Men's menu.** Right at the bottom of the nav panel — easy to spot, good tap target. That's my language.
- **"Comfort Technologies" as a menu category.** I noticed it immediately. Someone at Skechers understands that I shop by how it feels, not by style name.
- **Wide Fit / Extra Wide Fit filter pills front and center** on the category page. Huge win — I've spent years hunting for width options buried three filters deep on other sites.
- **320 results for Hands Free Slip-ins** — good selection signal, even if I can't see them all at once.
- **Product name is clear:** "Skechers Slip-ins: Arch Fit D'Lites 330" — I know exactly what I'm looking at. Price ($110) and the member discount call-out are readable.
- **"Slip-ins" badge on the product image** — I can see at a glance it's hands-free. That matters.
- **Add to Cart button** is full-width, blue, hard to miss. Good thumb target.
- **Size grid is clean** — numbers are big enough to tap without squinting.

---

## 4. What's Weak

- **Cart page is completely blank.** Step 8 was a white screen. Nothing. No cart, no item, no error message, no spinner. I'd assume the site broke. This is the single most damaging issue — it kills the purchase.
- **No login confirmation visible.** Steps 1, 2, and 3 look identical. The account icon doesn't change, no name appears, no "Welcome back, Walker" moment. I can't tell if I'm logged in or just browsing as a guest.
- **Homepage hero doesn't speak to me.** Young basketball player, Skechers Running, "Comfort That Performs." That's a 25-year-old's pitch. I want to see a guy walking a trail or standing at a barbecue without his feet aching.
- **Search for "comfort shoes" returns Women's first.** If I'm logged in as a male member, my search results should default to Men's. Seeing two Women's shoes at the top — Cozy Fit and Glide-Step — makes me feel like the site forgot who I am.
- **Only 7 reviews on the product.** That's thin. At $110, I want to see what 50 other people said about the cushioning and whether it held up after six months. Low review count makes me nervous.
- **Product images are small** with tiny left/right arrows. I want to see the side, the sole, the interior. The zoom icon is there, but on a phone it's fiddly.
- **"Please select a size" error** (Step 7) was shown after attempting to add — which means the journey tried to add without selecting first. That's a clunky flow; it should prompt size selection before reaching that point.
- **Banner cycling through collaborations** (Care Bears, then Britto) doesn't land for me. Those are brand collab promotions aimed at a younger audience. Real estate I'd want used for "Arch Fit — engineered for all-day comfort."

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** A blank cart is a dead sale. This needs to be the top engineering priority — full stop.
2. **Log-in state needs a visual confirmation.** After logging in, show my name or a "Hi, Walker" in the header. Right now I can't tell if authentication worked.
3. **Filter search results by account gender.** If I've told Skechers I'm a man, "comfort shoes" search should surface Men's results first, or at least a gender toggle above the grid.
4. **Homepage hero rotation** should include a comfort-casual story — a Men's walking shoe, someone over 50, copy like "All-day comfort starts here." Not every frame needs to be performance athletics.
5. **Boost social proof on the product page.** Show a summary of what people say about arch support and cushioning above the fold. Even a two-sentence highlight ("Reviewers love the cushioning for long walks") would move me closer to buying.
6. **Size pre-selection prompt.** When I tap "Add to Cart" without a size, scroll me up to the size grid with a gentle highlight — don't just show an error and leave me to figure it out.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

Skechers knows I'm a Slip-ins / Arch Fit / Wide Fit shopper — the navigation and the product page prove that. But the cart broke, the search didn't know I'm male, and I never felt like a recognized customer. At $110 a pair I'm already on the fence; a blank cart page sends me straight to Brooks or New Balance. Fix the cart first, fix the search gender default second, and this could easily climb to a 6 or 7.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Salesforce Commerce Cloud (Demandware) stack with heavy third-party tag orchestration via GTM. Critical functional failures on the cart page (HTTP 429, missing document metadata). Bot-detection fingerprinting (Kasada) is rate-limiting itself during normal browsing sessions. LCP and CLS are unmeasured across all steps — a significant monitoring gap.

---

## Accessibility

**17 violations across 3 pages.**

**Critical (Homepage)**
- `aria-required-parent`: ARIA role elements are orphaned from their required parent context — likely carousel or listbox components with incorrect DOM nesting.
- `button-name`: Icon-only buttons with no accessible label (`aria-label`, `aria-labelledby`, or visible text). Common culprit: close/menu/carousel buttons.

**Serious (View Cart)**
- `document-title`: `<title>` element is empty or absent. Confirms the cart page renders an incomplete HTML shell — either the title is injected client-side after axe scans, or SSR is failing for this route.
- `html-has-lang`: `lang` attribute missing on the cart `<html>` element. Homepage has `lang="en"` correctly set, so this is a cart-specific template regression.

**Moderate (all pages)**
- Multiple `<main>` landmarks on Homepage (`landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-main-is-top-level`) — component or slot injection creating duplicate mains.
- View Cart has no `<main>` at all (`landmark-one-main`, `page-has-heading-one`) — consistent with the incomplete shell hypothesis.
- `heading-order` violation on Homepage: heading levels skip (e.g. H1 → H3), likely from independently developed components each assuming they own the top level.

## SEO

- **View Cart missing `<title>`**: Googlebot will index this as an untitled page if it crawls it.
- **View Cart missing `lang`**: Hreflang and localization signals break for this URL.

## Mobile Optimization

- `--vh: 6.640000000000001px` is set as a CSS custom property on `<html>` — this is a JS-driven 1vh substitute (common 100vh mobile Safari workaround). The floating-point value (`6.64000000000001`) indicates no rounding, which could cause sub-pixel rendering differences. Should be `Math.round` or `parseFloat(...toFixed(2))`.
- No LCP or CLS data captured — without these, mobile rendering quality (image sizing, font loading, layout stability) cannot be assessed.
- The Men category `<head>` includes additional pixels (Snapchat, Reddit, Stape, Facebook, Nextdoor, Taboola) not present on Homepage — page-type-specific tag injection is working, but it increases per-page cold network cost on mobile.

## Console & Network Errors

**Kasada fingerprint endpoint — 429 (5 requests):**
```
https://www.skechers.com/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/
  2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.522
```
This is Kasada's bot-detection SDK calling its own fingerprint collection endpoint. Five 429 responses during a single browsing session means the site's own bot-protection layer is rate-limiting its own fingerprint collector — either the per-session request budget is too low, the SDK is being initialized multiple times (consistent with duplicate Monetate/GTM tags), or Kasada is mis-classifying the session as a bot and throttling it. This is a **functional defect**: if Kasada can't complete fingerprinting, bot-challenge flows may fire on legitimate users.

**Cart endpoint — 429:**
```
https://www.skechers.com/cart/
```
The cart page itself is returning 429. This is a **checkout-blocking error** — users hitting rate limits on the cart route cannot proceed to purchase. May be the same Kasada challenge loop or an independent cart API rate limit.

**Stylitics CORS block:**
```
https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?base=grid&theme=skechers&version=10353fa6f8
```
Blocked by CORS policy — `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` header missing or misconfigured on the Stylitics CDN for `skechers.com`. The outfit/shop-the-look widget CSS will not load, breaking that UI component silently.

## Recommendations

1. **Kasada rate limit** (P0): Investigate why the `fp` endpoint returns 429 during normal sessions. Audit whether duplicate tag initialization (2× Monetate, 2× Yahoo YTC) is triggering multiple SDK inits per page, exhausting the per-session fingerprint budget. File a ticket with Kasada support with HAR traces.

2. **Cart 429** (P0): Determine if `/cart/` is behind the same Kasada wall or a separate rate limiter. A 429 on the cart endpoint is a revenue-blocking failure.

3. **View Cart HTML shell** (P1): The cart page is missing `<title>`, `lang`, `<main>`, and H1. Either the SSR template is incomplete for this route or client-side hydration is not running before axe/crawlers scan it. Confirm server-rendered HTML for `/cart/` directly via `curl` — if the shell is bare, fix the SFCC cart template.

4. **Stylitics CORS** (P1): Coordinate with Stylitics to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` to their CDN responses, or self-host the stylesheet.

5. **Deduplicate Monetate and Yahoo YTC** (P2): Two Monetate initialization paths and two identical Yahoo YTC tags are loaded. Remove duplicates — they add unnecessary network requests and may be contributing to the Kasada over-firing.

6. **Capture LCP and CLS** (P2): All 8 journey steps are missing LCP and CLS. Without these, Core Web Vitals cannot be assessed. Add Real User Monitoring (Quantum Metric is already integrated — enable Web Vitals reporting) or capture them in the test harness via `PerformanceObserver`.

7. **Button accessible names** (P2): Audit all icon-only buttons on the Homepage and add `aria-label` attributes. These are critical violations under WCAG 2.1 SC 4.1.2.

8. **`--vh` precision** (P3): Round the viewport height calculation: `document.documentElement.style.setProperty('--vh', \`${(window.innerHeight * 0.01).toFixed(2)}px\`)`.
