# Step 1: Pick (2) pairs of women’s sandals
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- So DSW knows I exist — I get that. But they sent me to the wrong shoe aisle. The second I read "women's sandals" in the subject I knew this wasn't for me, yet I opened it anyway because it's DSW and I do shop there. What I found was a clean, well-built email that somebody clearly put real work into — it just had nothing to do with me. BOGO 50% off is a genuine offer, the layout loads fast on my phone, and the size-selector section is actually a smart idea. Except the sizes top out around 11 in a women's range and "Shop Wide Calf" is decidedly not my problem.
- The real frustration isn't just getting a women's email — it's that DSW has my purchase history. They know I buy men's wide-width comfort shoes. This send says their segmentation doesn't talk to their email list, and that makes me trust them a little less as a brand. A company that sends me the wrong gender promo doesn't feel like it knows me.
- I'd mark it read and move on. If I'm forwarding this, it goes to my wife — not a buddy who needs walking shoes like I do.

## What's working

- **BOGO 50% OFF is instantly readable.** Big, bold type at the top — I know the deal in two seconds, no squinting required.
- **The Step 1 / Step 2 subject-to-preview structure is clever.** That two-part hook actually teaches you how the deal works before you even open it. Smart writing.
- **DSW is a trusted sender name.** I don't trash their emails on sight — I cross-shop them regularly.
- **Shop By Size section is a solid feature.** Clicking directly to my size from the email is exactly how I'd want to browse on a phone with my big thumbs.
- **Clean render, no broken images.** Everything loaded, nothing looked sloppy.

## What's weak

- **Wrong gender, full stop.** I'm a 62-year-old man buying men's comfort walkers. Every product image, every headline, every CTA in this email is women's sandals. The "SHOP MEN" link exists but it's tiny text buried below the hero.
- **Not a single men's product in sight.** No Skechers, no Hoka, no wide-width walking shoe — nothing I'd recognize as being for me.
- **The free cooler tote GWP is buried.** It's squeezed into a cramped green banner below the main promo. A real gift-with-purchase hook deserves its own moment.
- **The fine-print wall at the bottom is brutal on a phone.** Multiple paragraphs of dense legal text stacked at the foot — that's not readable on a 6-inch screen for anyone, let alone someone my age.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the list by gender before sending** — this is the only fix that actually matters. Men should not receive a women's sandals BOGO. Use purchase history; DSW has it.
- 2. **Lead a men's version with comfort + fit language** — "BOGO 50% Off Men's Comfort Shoes" with a cushioning or arch-support callout and Wide Width front and center.
- 3. **Make the size selector gender-appropriate** — men's version runs 8–14 Wide Width, not 5–11 Wide Calf.
- 4. **Give the cooler tote GWP its own row** — bigger image, one punchy line: "Spend $75, take home a FREE cooler tote." It earns more real estate than it's getting.
- 5. **Replace the fine-print block with a "full details" link** — one sentence and a tap is all the footer needs.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Men's Shoe BOGO — buy 2, get the 2nd 50% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your size is in: BOGO 50% off comfort walkers`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Wide widths, arch support, Skechers & more — shop the deal`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `No code needed — discount applies automatically at checkout`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed `<meta>` tags (2 instances):**
- `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` is missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Some older Outlook versions will ignore the charset declaration.
- `<meta content="IE=Edge" />` is missing `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`. The IE compatibility directive has no effect without the `http-equiv` attribute.

**`@font-face` uses `Src:` (capital S):** All 10 `@font-face` declarations use `Src:` instead of the spec-compliant lowercase `src:`. Most modern clients are case-insensitive for CSS properties, but this is technically invalid and could fail in strict parsers.

**Font asset cache-buster inconsistency:** HelveticaNow font URLs include a timestamp query string (e.g., `?1755182939`) while Futura_PT URLs do not. If Futura_PT assets are ever re-uploaded and cache-busted, the URL pattern will diverge further from HelveticaNow, complicating future updates.

**Font CDN is third-party (`braze-images.com`):** All 10 web fonts are hosted on Braze's CDN. Clients that block remote font loading (e.g., Gmail, many corporate proxies) will silently fall back to system fonts. The declared fallback chain is not visible in the truncated source — confirm fallback stacks are set.

**`<title>` is non-descriptive:** `<title>DSW</title>` provides no subject context. Accessibility best practice is to match the `<title>` to the email subject so screen reader users hear a meaningful document title.
