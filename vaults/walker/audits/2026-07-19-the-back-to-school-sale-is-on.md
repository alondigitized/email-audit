# The Back to School sale is on
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- Nike's back in my inbox, and at least the subject line told me exactly what I was getting into. "Back to School sale" — I opened it, and the first thing that hit me was that basketball court hero in black and neon yellow with "Back to School Sale" screaming at me. Bold, loud, unmissable. I'll give them that.
- But then I started scrolling, and I felt the familiar Nike problem settle in: this email isn't for me. The "School Essential Savings" block shows Nike Air Max 270 Women's Shoes, Nike React Revanche Women's Slides, a Women's Oversized Pullover Hoodie, and a Women's Crewneck Sweatshirt. I'm 62 years old. I haven't been back to school in four decades, and every product in the featured section is women's. By the time I got to the in-store 25% off apparel block, I was done. I'd have to drive somewhere to use that deal.
- Contrast this with that May 27th "Extra 20% off select styles ends soon" email — that one was generic, but at least it was honest about being a broad discount email. This one is wrapped in a theme that has nothing to do with me. I'd mark it as read and move on. I wouldn't click, and I definitely wouldn't forward it to anyone.

## What's working

- **The offer is real and stacked** — 50% off select styles plus an extra 25% with code OPTONE is a serious discount if something in there fits you.
- **Free shipping for members is right at the top** — "Members: Free Shipping on Orders $80+" in the preheader is the kind of thing I actually care about; it doesn't hide that benefit.
- **Dates are visible** — 7.15.26–8.15.26 tells me exactly how long I have; no guessing.
- **Layout is clean** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum. Renders fine on my phone.
- **Member acknowledgment** — the "Members Apply" note in the offer block tells me I'm logged in as someone, even if Nike doesn't really know who I am.

## What's weak

- **Every featured product is women's** — Air Max 270 Women's, React Revanche Women's Slides, Women's hoodie, Women's sweatshirt. I can't find a men's comfort shoe anywhere in the first scroll.
- **"Back to School" is not my season** — I haven't had a school-age reason to buy shoes since the Clinton administration. This campaign hook does zero for me.
- **"Win is Back in Session" lands wrong for my age** — that copy sounds like it belongs in a high school hallway, not my inbox.
- **The in-store 25% off apparel block requires me to find a Nike Store** — that's friction I won't bother with. I buy online because it's easier on my feet.
- **No comfort, cushioning, or walking-shoe language anywhere** — if there's a men's walking shoe in this sale, I'd never know from this email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Feature at least one men's comfort or walking style in the hero product block** — even one Air Max or something with visible cushioning messaging would give me a reason to scroll further.
- 2. **Drop the "Back to School" theme for the men's segment entirely** — or at least headline it differently. "Summer Sale" or "End of Season Savings" works for a 62-year-old without making him feel like he received the wrong email.
- 3. **Replace "Win is Back in Session" with offer-first copy** — lead with the savings number. Boomers trust concrete math over clever wordplay.
- 4. **Remove the in-store-only block for anyone who hasn't visited a store in 12+ months** — it's clutter if I'm an online-only buyer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 50% off — plus an extra 25% with your code`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Big savings, no backpack required`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ends Aug 15 · Members get free shipping on $80+`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack your savings: 50% off + extra 25% with OPTONE`

## Technical Audit — Nike "Back to School Sale" Email

**From:** nike@official.nike.com | **Campaign:** `AEP-NA-US-MW-Email-BackToSchoolSale-Large-0719`

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has two confirmed infrastructure defects: a missing plain-text body (0 chars) that will hurt deliverability scoring, and an open-tracking pixel served over HTTP that most modern clients will silently block. The QA tool's 19 "doubled-domain" link failures are false positives from the standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud click-redirect URL structure and do not represent broken links.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**QA "doubled-domain" findings — false positives, no action required.**
All 10 flagged URLs follow the standard SFMC click-tracking format:
```
https://click.em.nike.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fwww.nike.com%2F...
```
The QA checker misidentified `nike.com` appearing in both the tracking domain and the URL-encoded destination as a merge error. These URLs resolve correctly. The `/CL0/` path is the SFMC redirect handler; the URL-encoded payload is the intended destination.

**Product link redirect chain depth — 3 hops.**
Product CTAs (e.g., Air Max 270, ReactX Rejuven8, Phoenix Fleece items) route through:
1. `click.em.nike.com` (SFMC click tracking)
2. `nike.sng.link` (Branch.io mobile deep link)
3. `www.nike.com` (final landing page)

Nav/footer links skip Branch.io (2 hops). The Branch.io layer adds latency on web and carries `_fallback_redirect`, `_ios_redirect`, and `_android_redirect` parameters — correct structure, but each hop is a potential failure point.

**28 tracking links skipped** by QA HTTP probe — expected behavior for click-redirect domains; not a defect.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[FAIL] Tracking pixel served over HTTP.**
```
src: http://click.em.nike.com/CI0/0100019f7aed589b-...
```
Gmail, Outlook 365, and Apple Mail proxy/block mixed-content images. This pixel will not fire in most clients, causing open-rate undercounting. Should be `https://`.

**[WARN] Tracking pixel has no alt text.**
The `<img>` for the open-tracking beacon omits `alt`. Correct fix is `alt=""` (empty string signals decorative/invisible image to screen readers).

**Custom font fallback is correct.** `Nike Futura` is declared via `@font-face` but is unsupported in Gmail and Outlook. The inline CSS elsewhere falls back to `Helvetica Neue, Arial, sans-serif` — acceptable degradation.

**XHTML 1.0 Strict doctype** is legacy but broadly compatible with major email clients.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

HTML provided is truncated — cannot audit merge token rendering end-to-end. No unrendered token syntax (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `%%first_name%%`) is visible in the supplied markup. Cannot confirm whether first-name personalization is present or conditionally guarded against null values.

---

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text fallback is 0 characters.**
CAN-SPAM does not mandate a `text/plain` MIME part, but a missing or empty fallback is a material deliverability signal. SpamAssassin and many ISP filters penalize HTML-only messages. This also fails WCAG guidance for email accessibility.

Physical address and unsubscribe link cannot be confirmed — the HTML source is truncated before the footer. Authentication headers (SPF, DKIM, DMARC pass/fail) are not derivable from HTML and were not included in QA data.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**No standard UTM parameters.** All destination URLs use Nike's proprietary `cp=` attribution parameter:
```
cp=nans_em_camp_nike_OTHER_20260714_c104517_..._backtoschoolsale-startwk_...
```
Standard `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` parameters are absent. Any third-party analytics platform (Google Analytics, etc.) receiving this traffic will classify it as direct/unattributed unless the `cp=` value is mapped in a custom integration.

**Product variant targeting is correct.** Style-color codes in the Branch.io deep links (`HJ3222-602`, `HV4484-801`, `DQ5858-897`, `FZ5996-894`) match the product names in link text and are passed through to the fallback/redirect URLs — no product-variant mismatch detected.
