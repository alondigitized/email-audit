# Psst...you've got 2 days to claim your $10!
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-21**
## Executive summary

- Same $10/$20/$60 tiered deal I've now seen from DSW three times this week — fine, at least I don't need a calculator. But the whole top half is "Hey, What's New?" with a wall of women's heels, strappy sandals, and ballet flats. Not one men's shoe until you scroll way down to a small New Balance sneaker buried in row three. That's the third or fourth DSW email in a row that treats me like I'm shopping for someone else.
- The "Shoeprise Savings Hunt" banner with a scavenger hunt for $50 off in-store is a genuinely different idea, I'll give them that — but it's the kind of thing my grandkids might do, not me. I'm not driving to a mall to hunt for a red arrow.
- This one's getting marked as read and moved on. Nothing here says "comfort shoe for a 62-year-old guy," and I've already seen the discount tiers enough times this week that they don't feel special anymore.

## What's working

- The $10/$20/$60 tiered offer is clear and doesn't make me do math — consistent with what they've run all week.
- I spot one New Balance sneaker in the grid, which is at least in the right neighborhood for me.
- "Shop by Size" and "Shop Wide Width" near the bottom is a nice touch — wide width matters to guys my age with wider feet.
- Free shipping and in-store pickup icons are easy to scan.

## What's weak

- Almost the entire product grid is women's heels, sandals, and flats — I had to scroll past a dozen images before anything remotely men's showed up.
- "That's Not All, Folks" section doubles down with more heels and a "Women's Shoes" button — no equivalent "Men's Shoes" button anywhere near it.
- The Shoeprise in-store scavenger hunt is a distraction that has nothing to do with buying shoes online, and it's not a format that appeals to me.
- No mention of arch support, cushioning, or slip-on comfort anywhere — the stuff I actually care about.
- This is the same $10/$20/$60 offer I've seen from DSW at least twice this week already, so it's starting to blur together.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split the hero grid by gender or let me segment — lead with men's comfort/walking shoes for guys like me instead of assuming everyone wants heels.
- 2. Cut or shrink the Shoeprise in-store hunt section — it's clutter for an online shopper and doesn't serve the email's core purpose.
- 3. Add a men's comfort callout — Skechers, Hoka, New Balance walking shoes with arch support — since that's the category I actually browse.
- 4. Vary the subject line language — "psst" and "waaay too good" is getting old after this many sends this week.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your $10 is waiting — 2 days left`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Men's comfort sneakers, up to $60 off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New Balance, Skechers, and more — all with arch support`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `2 days left to save on your next pair`
