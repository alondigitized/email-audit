# A reimagined running experience
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- Adidas sent me a shoe email — an actual shoe, not a soccer jersey — so right off the bat I was in slightly better spirits than I've been with this brand lately. The Hyperboost Edge is front and center, the layout is clean, and the hero image is big and easy to see. I'll give them that.
- But the longer I looked, the more I felt like this was sent to someone about twenty years younger and thirty percent faster than me. "Reimagined running experience." "High mileage training." That's not my life. My life is a long walk around the lake, a round of golf, maybe a trip to Costco. The shoe looks like something a guy in compression socks wears to a 10K. And there is no offer — no price, no discount, no dollar amount, nothing. Just "shop online or at your local adidas store," which is barely a sentence, let alone a call to action.
- I'd mark this read and move on. Wouldn't click, wouldn't forward. The June 19 email — the one with a concrete $30 off $100 — that was the kind of math I can act on. This one's just a catalog page in my inbox.

## What's working

- The shoe is right there at the top — no clutter, no competing hero images, just the product front and center
- Email rendered cleanly on my screen; no broken images, no layout weirdness
- adidas is a name I recognize, so I gave it a second look instead of swiping past
- The "SHOP NOW" banner up top at least shows me there's a place to buy something — even if it points to best sellers, not this shoe

## What's weak

- Zero offer — no price, no discount, nothing. After the $30 off email six days ago, this feels like a step backward
- "High mileage training" tells me this shoe is not for someone like me; they might as well have said "not for you"
- No CTA button for the actual product — "Shop online or at your local adidas store" is just text, not something I'd know to tap
- No cushioning or comfort language anywhere — performance tech means nothing to me without knowing how it feels on a 90-minute walk
- No adiClub hook despite me being a member; the June 16 FIFA email at least tried to tie the rewards angle

- 2. **Lead with comfort, not performance.** Swap "high mileage training" for something like "all-day cushioning" or "ready for the long haul" — that's the language that actually applies to my walking life.
- 3. **Give the main product a real CTA button.** "Shop online or at your local adidas store" is barely a nudge. Put a tappable "Shop Hyperboost Edge" button under that hero image.

## Technical Audit — Adidas "A reimagined running experience"

**Sender:** adidas@us-news.comms.adidas.com | **Reviewed:** 2026-06-24

---

### 1. Technical Summary

One broken CTA link will deliver a dead-end experience to any recipient who clicks it. The email also has no plain-text alternative, which harms deliverability and violates common ESP best-practice policies.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Malformed inner URL in click-redirect**

The QA probe resolved this tracking wrapper:
```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/c1b9600a-9a8c-435e-a38c-94682fe5f6b4?cp_tp=v3...
```
…to an inner destination of:
```
/g/c1b9600a-9a8c-435e-a38c-94682fe5f6b4?txn=e1536280-7057-11f1-a17a-aea38baf8e0c
```
The inner URL is a relative path (`/g/...`), not an absolute URL. The click-redirect system cannot resolve a relative path, so this link breaks. Root cause: the destination URL was likely not fully templated before the tracking wrapper was applied. Any CTA pointing to this destination delivers a broken experience.

**[WARN] 23 tracking links not probed**

All click.comms.adidas.com redirect links were skipped by the HTTP prober. The broken-link check above is the only confirmed redirect failure, but the remaining 23 cannot be cleared without probing.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] HTTP image sources (not HTTPS) — same asset repeated 4×**

```
src: http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
This arrow/icon asset is referenced four times over HTTP. Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail enforce mixed-content blocking — the image will be suppressed or replaced with a broken-image placeholder in those clients.

**[WARN] Tracking pixel served over HTTP**

```
src: http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019ed12d799a-66203eff-4975-4397-ad9c-3e46a450ff2f-000000/...
```
Open-tracking pixel is HTTP. Open-rate data will be undercounted in clients that block mixed content.

**[WARN] Two images missing `alt` text**

- `c1b9600a-9a8c-435e-a38c-94682fe5f6b4` (the image linked to the broken URL above)
- `izeT8u65O9ErFOTMJPsg5x0xCi1IKcH_nQdnU3L_H-g=452` (the tracking pixel)

The first is a content image and needs descriptive alt text for screen readers and image-off rendering. The pixel can use `alt=""`.

**[WARN] Malformed charset `<meta>` tag**

```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Missing required `http-equiv` attribute. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Without it, the charset declaration is ignored by HTML4 parsers — the doctype declared is HTML 4.01 Transitional.

**[WARN] Duplicate `@font-face` declarations**

`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are each declared twice in separate `<style>` blocks with slightly different fallback stacks (first block includes `Calibri` and `Sans Serif`; second does not). The second declaration wins, silently dropping those fallbacks. Consolidate into one block.

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text alternative is 0 characters**

The QA check confirms the `text/plain` MIME part is empty. This is a significant deliverability risk: Spamhaus, SpamAssassin, and major ISP filters penalize HTML-only messages. Some ESP terms of service require a plain-text part. Minimum viable fix: a stripped-text version of body copy plus the unsubscribe URL.

**[WARN] `no-referrer` meta tag suppresses referrer signal**

```html
<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />
```
This prevents the landing-page server from seeing the email referrer. Any server-side analytics that branch on `document.referrer` or HTTP `Referer` will see null. This is presumably intentional (adidas relies on UTMs instead), but it is worth confirming that all CTAs carry UTM params — see §6.

**Authentication headers** (DKIM, SPF, DMARC) are not inspectable from HTML source alone; audit requires raw message headers. The sending subdomain `us-news.comms.adidas.com` suggests a dedicated ESP subdomain, which is standard practice.

**CAN-SPAM**: Physical mailing address and unsubscribe mechanism were not visible in the truncated HTML. Verify both are present in the footer.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM coverage unknown** — all 23 click-redirect links were skipped by the prober, so UTM decoration on destination URLs cannot be confirmed. The `no-referrer` meta (§5) means UTMs are the only attributable signal from this email; if any CTAs omit UTM params, those clicks are unattributable.

**Broken CTA breaks the funnel** — the `/g/c1b9600a...` link failure (§2) means at least one product/collection CTA delivers a dead end. If this is a primary hero CTA, conversion impact is high.
