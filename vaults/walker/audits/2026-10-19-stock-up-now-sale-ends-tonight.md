# Stock up now, sale ends tonight!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-19**
## Executive summary

- Well, here's Crocs again, one day after the "Crocs at Work" email, and it's the same Buy 2, 3, 4 ladder (20/30/40% off) I saw on 10/18. This time the headline is bigger and friendlier: "Grab 4 pairs and score 40% off." I got the point in two seconds on my phone. The "sale ends tonight" subject is honest too. The fine print says 11:59pm MT on the 19th.
- But I'm 62. I don't need four pairs of clogs. Four pairs for 40% off is a stretch for a guy who wants one comfortable pair with good arch support. Nothing here talks about cushioning, support, or walking. It's two Baya clogs on purple and a lot of "buy more." I'd glance at it, think "that's the same deal as yesterday," and move on. I wouldn't forward it. If I wanted a Crocs clog I'd already know about the deal, since it's been in my inbox two days running.

## What's working

- The headline is huge and I can read it without zooming. "Buy 2, 20% off, 3 for 30%, 4 for 40%" is spelled out right under it.
- The black "Shop The Sale" button is big and easy to tap with my thumb.
- The deadline is real. The fine print says Monday, October 19th at 11:59pm MT, and that matches the subject line.
- The shoe photo is a plain clog, nothing cute like the Halloween charm email on 10/5. The green and bone colors are the kind I'd actually wear.
- Free shipping is in the black bar at the very top.

## What's weak

- The 4-pair math doesn't fit me. I'd have to buy three more pairs I don't need to hit 40%.
- Nothing about comfort, cushioning, or arch support. Those are the reasons I shop this category.
- "Select styles" and "Exclusions Apply" make me wonder if the clogs in the picture are even on sale. I can't tell.
- It's the same ladder as 10/18, just with new wording. That's the second day in a row, and it's the same deal I've seen all month.
- The refer-a-friend block, app download and social icons take up half the email. For me they're clutter.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a "one pair" price in the email, such as "Just need one? Take 20% off the Baya Clog," so a single-pair buyer like me isn't ignored.
- 2. Add one line on comfort, such as "Cushioned, lightweight, easy to slip on." That would be a reason to click.
- 3. Name which styles are included, or put the sale price under the clogs in the photo.
- 4. Cut the footer down. Drop the app download and refer-a-friend blocks, or move them below the legal text.
- 5. Skip the sale email the day after a nearly identical one. Space them out or change the creative.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Tonight only: Baya Clogs up to 40% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ends 11:59pm MT. Free shipping on qualified orders.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Light, cushioned, easy on. Pick your colors before midnight.`

## Technical Audit

1. **Technical Summary**
The email's HTML is well structured, with a preheader, a viewport meta tag, responsive media queries and a language attribute. The QA data shows 7 images served over plain `http://`, one tracking pixel with no alt text, and no authentication headers to verify. The source was truncated, so footer and link findings are limited to what the QA checks reported.

2. **Link & Tracking Issues**
- 20 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are click-redirect domains (likely `click.crocs-email.com`). Their destinations are unverified, so this is an audit gap rather than a confirmed defect.
- The open pixel `https://click.crocs-email.com/open.aspx?VP6V4XGOZS3E5IJG6UZ6ODS64U.10198&d=10198&bmt=0` is correctly sized (1x1, `alt=""`).
- A second pixel, `https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&c_emailopened=Yes&d_dpuid=...`, is an Adobe Audience Manager (demdex) beacon. It has `height="1" width="1"` and `style="visibility:hidden;display:none"`, but no `alt` attribute (QA WARN). Because it is `display:none`, some clients won't fire it, so open counts from this pixel will undercount.

3. **Rendering & Accessibility**
- Seven images use `http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/...`: App Store, Google Play, Find Your Store, Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and YouTube. Mixed-content blocking or proxy rewriting can break or warn on these in some clients. The same host is probably available over HTTPS.
- The demdex pixel has no `alt`. Add `alt=""` so screen readers skip it.
- `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` and `<meta content="IE=edge" />` have no `http-equiv` attribute, so they have no effect. The charset and compatibility directives are not applied. Add `http-equiv="Content-Type"` and `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`, or use `<meta charset="utf-8">`.
- The `<title>` is the generic "Crocs".
- The `TT Crocs` web font falls back to system fonts in clients that don't support `@font-face`, which is expected. I can't check the fallback stack in the truncated source.
- The `lang="en"` attribute, viewport meta, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` and the Gmail and Outlook fixes are all present.

4. **Personalization & Merge Tokens**
No issues found. The visible source has no unresolved merge tokens such as `%%...%%`, `{{...}}` or `[FirstName]`. The tracking IDs are rendered values. The truncated source limits this check.

5. **Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
- `Authentication-Results` was not found (QA WARN). SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown because the message came through the AgentMail relay. This is an audit-environment limitation, not evidence that Crocs fails authentication. Check the original headers to confirm.
- I couldn't verify the unsubscribe link, the physical postal address or the `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers, because the footer and headers were not in the truncated data. This is an unverified item, not a finding.
- The sender `email@crocs-email.com` is on a dedicated sending domain, which is consistent with the tracking domains.

6. **Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
- All 20 tracked links are wrapped in click redirects, so I can't see the final URLs or whether UTM parameters survive. Cannot confirm.
- The subject says "sale ends tonight" and the preheader says "Last call for up to 40% off." Landing-page continuity for the offer can't be tested without resolving the redirects.

7. **Recommendations**
1. Change all 7 `http://image.crocs-email.com/...` image sources to `https://`.
2. Add `alt=""` to the demdex pixel `<img>`.
3. Fix the two meta tags by adding `http-equiv="Content-Type"` and `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"` (or use `<meta charset>`).
4. Resolve the 20 skipped redirects in a follow-up pass to check final URLs, UTM params and the sale landing page.
5. Re-run the audit against the raw message to check `Authentication-Results`, `List-Unsubscribe` and the footer compliance content.
6. Replace the generic `<title>` with a descriptive one (minor).
