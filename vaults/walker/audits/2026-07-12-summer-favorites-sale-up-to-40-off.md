# Summer Favorites Sale Up to 40% Off
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, well-structured sale email from Skechers — a brand I actually buy from — and the 40% off deal caught my eye the moment I opened it. The hero is crisp, the deadline ("Valid thru 7/13") is right there so I know I'm not looking at a stale promo, and the Skechers Plus member bonus — 20% off regular-priced styles with code SUMMER — is a real incentive stacked on top of the clearance. That's two reasons to reach for my card.
- But here we go again. Same wall of JSON schema nonsense in the preview slot that I've now seen in nine straight Skechers emails. I noted it back on the July 3rd "Customer Approved Sneakers" send, I mentioned it on July 7th, July 10th — and Skechers still hasn't fixed it. At this point it's not a bug, it's a brand statement, and not a flattering one.
- The bigger miss is that this email treats me like a generic shopper, not a 62-year-old guy who buys Skechers specifically for the cushioning and arch support. No mention of comfort features anywhere. The hero models look like they jog to a farmer's market on Saturdays — that's not me. I'd still tap "SHOP MEN'S" to see what's actually on sale. I would not forward this to a friend.

## What's working

- **40% off is front and center in the hero** — I see the number before I scroll at all, which is exactly how a sale email should open.
- **Skechers Plus member exclusive is prominently placed** — the 20% off code SUMMER for regular-priced styles sits right below the hero as its own block. As a Plus member, that's a real second offer, not a footnote.
- **Deadline is visible** — "Valid thru 7/13" tells me this is time-sensitive without burying me in fine print.
- **Clean body render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no empty placeholder blocks. The email itself looks professional on my phone.
- **Men's section gets its own category block** — "SHOP MEN'S" is there with what looks like a cushioned athletic shoe pictured, which is at least in the right neighborhood for me.

## What's weak

- **That JSON preview garbage — for the ninth time.** Raw schema code in my inbox before I even open it. It was aggravating by email three; by email nine it's just Skechers telling me they don't proofread their own marketing.
- **No comfort language anywhere in the copy** — if I've bought Skechers for the arch support and Max Cushioning, this email doesn't reflect that at all. "Summer Favorites" could be flip-flops or fashion sneakers for all I know.
- **Women's leads the category buttons** — "SHOP WOMEN'S" comes before "SHOP MEN'S." I'm a guy. That ordering makes me feel like an afterthought in my own inbox.
- **Hero models skew twenty years too young** — the couple in the banner looks like they're in their mid-30s at best. Skechers knows my purchase history; this creative doesn't reflect who's actually buying.
- **No specific product called out** — I'm clicking blind into a sale page. Show me an Arch Fit Walker marked down to $79 and I'm pulling my card out immediately. "Up to 40% off select styles" makes me skeptical of what actually qualifies.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text.** Nine consecutive sends, same broken JSON. Write the ticket, ship the fix. One line of real copy — "Sale ends tomorrow — cushioned walkers included" — would meaningfully lift opens.
- 2. **Lead with comfort language in the hero** — swap "Summer Favorites Sale" for something like "Sale on Arch Fit, Max Cushioning & More." Costs the designer three words; converts better for buyers like me.
- 3. **Reorder the category blocks for male subscribers** — segment by purchase history or account gender, put "SHOP MEN'S" first for men. Dead simple personalization.
- 4. **Feature one specific men's comfort shoe with a real marked-down price** — a concrete number ("$79, was $110") gives me something to react to instead of a vague percentage promise.
- 5. **Age up the hero creative** — one image of a 60-something man in a comfortable walking shoe goes further with this subscriber base than a jogging couple half my age.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Skechers "Summer Favorites Sale" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) table-based email. The dominant issue is that `image.emails.skechers.com` and two third-party tracking domains serve assets over plain HTTP, which modern email clients block or proxy-rewrite. One tracking pixel has a malformed (protocol-less) URL that will 404 in all clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**ReturnPath seed pixel — missing protocol (confirmed bug)**
```html
<img src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1dbf0dcef79cbad82c12fe25342701e4499" ...>
```
No `https://` or `http://` prefix. Email clients treat this as a relative path, resolving to something like `https://emails.skechers.com/pixel.app.returnpath.net/...`, which will 404. ReturnPath inbox-placement data for this send is lost.

**ink1000.com open pixel — HTTP**
```
src: http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif?...
```
Plain HTTP. Gmail and Outlook.com proxy or strip non-HTTPS tracking pixels.

**26 click-redirect links skipped by QA** — UTM parameter consistency cannot be confirmed from available data. Downstream landing page audit is blocked.

**Krux DMP pixels expose raw subscriber identifiers**
```
partner_uid=9153f33fe5cdf59baf088bb835d3b39ef90f188bd8540a4faba92899227ced00  (SHA-256 hashed email)
partner_uid=121173391  (raw subscriber ID)
```
Sending hashed PII and internal subscriber IDs to a third-party DMP (`beacon.krxd.net`) in pixel query parameters is technically functional but surfaces as a data-handling risk depending on privacy policy scope.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**All `image.emails.skechers.com` assets served over HTTP** (11 images confirmed by QA). Affected assets include the Skechers logo, social icons (Instagram, Facebook, YouTube), app store badges, and Curbside Pickup / AfterPay icons. Gmail auto-proxies these; Outlook desktop may block them outright.

**Two images missing `alt` text:**
- `o.gif` — `src: http://www.ink1000.com/...` (tracking pixel, alt="" acceptable but not present)
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` — `src: http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/...` — content image with no alt; renders as empty space in image-blocked clients

**`<meta>` charset tag missing `http-equiv` attribute:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, the charset declaration is ignored by some legacy clients (Outlook 2007–2016).

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied globally:**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Disables user font-size scaling on iOS for all elements. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) requires text to be resizable up to 200% without loss of content. Should be scoped to specific elements where zoom-breaking is genuinely a layout concern.

**Duplicate responsive breakpoints** — `max-width: 640px` and `max-width: 375px` blocks contain identical rule sets. The 375px block is fully redundant; it adds parse cost and maintenance surface with no distinct behavior.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens visible in the available HTML (no `%%variable%%` or `{{variable}}` fragments). Preheader text ("100s of new markdowns just added") is hardcoded. No issues detected in the truncated source.

### 5. Compliance

CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe mechanism are not present in the truncated HTML — standard footer placement means these are likely present further in the document but cannot be confirmed from available source.

Authentication headers (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) are not derivable from HTML alone. Sending domain is `emails.skechers.com`; alignment with `skechers.com` root domain should be verified in mail headers.

**QA summary line inconsistency:** The automated QA report ends with `100% pass rate, 0 issue(s), 0 warning(s)` despite logging 14 `[WARN]` entries above it. The QA tool is not counting warnings toward its summary totals — the output is misleading and the tool's pass/fail logic needs a fix.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Campaign ID `PRO_US_NA_U_SITESELECT_FULL_EN_07122026_A` is consistent across the Krux DMP pixel and the ink1000.com pixel — internal campaign tagging is coherent.

UTM parameters on the 26 click-redirect links cannot be inspected (QA skipped all `click.emails.skechers.com` URLs). It is unknown whether UTM params are present, consistent with the campaign ID above, or missing entirely.
