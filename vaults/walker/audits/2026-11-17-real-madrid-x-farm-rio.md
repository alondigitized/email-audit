# Real Madrid x FARM Rio
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-17**
## Executive summary

- Another adidas email, and I've lost count since the 15th. The black banner got me first. "Up to 60% Off for Members During Black Friday First Access" with a ticking clock is a real offer, and it's the only thing here for a guy like me. Then it turns into a soccer-fashion story. The scarf has big pink flowers on it and the model has a ball balanced on his head. I'm a 62-year-old shopping for cushion and arch support, so I felt like I'd wandered into the wrong store.
- The "Shop Now" button doesn't tell me what I'd be shopping for. It's a Real Madrid collection, not walking shoes. The second half is a contest to win a "style kit" with baggy sweats and a pink cap. That's not my closet. I'd skim it, notice the 60% banner, and close it. I wouldn't click and I wouldn't forward it. It's a mark-as-read for me.

## What's working

- The black banner with the 60% member offer and countdown clock stands out, and I could read it without squinting.
- The offer is clear: the percentage is up top and it's tied to member pricing.
- The "Shop Now" and "Enter Now" buttons are big black blocks, easy to tap on my phone.
- The body text is a readable size and the layout is a simple single column.

## What's weak

- There isn't one shoe in the whole email. The hero is a scarf, a soccer jersey and a ball.
- The 60% banner isn't connected to the collection below it. I can't tell if the Real Madrid stuff is even part of the sale.
- The copy is brand-speak, like "rooted in creativity, connection and a shared love of the game." It has no price, no fit and no comfort detail.
- The contest for a style kit is a distraction from any buying decision, and the models in the baggy streetwear look decades younger than me.
- "Up to 60%" is vague. It doesn't say which items or which sizes.
- After six or seven adidas emails in a week and a half, none about walking comfort, it feels like I'm a stranger on a list.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put an actual shoe in the hero with a real price, like "Cloudfoam walkers, now $54 for members." Tie the countdown banner to specific products, not a fashion collection.
- 2. Make the button say what's behind it, like "Shop Member Deals" or "Shop Men's Walking Shoes."
- 3. Cut the contest or move it to the bottom. Give me one short line on comfort or fit instead of two paragraphs of poetry.
- 4. Segment by interest. A man who looks at cushioned shoes shouldn't get the soccer-fashion version.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cushioned comfort picks for members, up to 60% off through tomorrow.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Early access is open. See the best deals on everyday shoes.`
