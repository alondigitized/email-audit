# Skechtember is HERE
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-10**
## Executive summary

- Now this is more like it — a whole email dedicated to Skechers, no wading through the Nike/adidas wall I've been slogging through all week. "Skechtember" is a cute enough name, and right off the bat there's a Hands Free Slip-ins category tile, which is exactly the kind of shoe I actually buy. The family sale banner (up to 25% off) and the stacked $15/$30 off coupon at the bottom give me something concrete to act on too.
- That said, the runner model up top doing sprints on a boardwalk isn't really "me" — I'm not out there in short-shorts logging miles, I just want something cushioned to walk the dog in. And it's a long scroll to get to anything useful; there's a lot of category-tile real estate (Cozy Fit, Glide-Step, Work Footwear, Kids) before I hit an actual dollar-off deal.
- I'd open this one and probably click through to the Hands Free Slip-ins tile specifically. Wouldn't forward it to anyone, but I'd stick around long enough to check if that $30 off code applies to what I want.

## What's working

- All-Skechers, no competitor logo wall — first time all week I haven't had to hunt for my brand.
- "Skechers Hands-Free Slip-ins" tile is dead-on for how I actually shop — no bending down, no laces.
- The $15/$30 off coupon at the bottom with a real code (STEPUP) and clear thresholds is an offer I can act on.
- Shoe Perks 2X points and the reward-reveal graphic give me a reason to feel like a known member, not just a mass blast.

## What's weak

- The lead visual is a woman sprinting outdoors — doesn't match a 62-year-old guy looking for comfort shoes, so the hero doesn't pull me in the way it should.
- Way too much scrolling before the real offer — six category tiles, a game promo, a rewards graphic, all before the $15/$30 coupon.
- "Up to 25% off" on the family sale banner is vague — no specific style or price called out, so I can't tell if it applies to anything I'd wear.
- The Word Stride game tile feels like filler — I'm not signing up to play a word game to maybe win a prize.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the $15/$30 off coupon code up near the top, right under the hero — that's the actual reason to keep reading, don't bury it after six tiles of clutter.
- 2. Swap the hero image for a men's comfort/slip-in shoe on a real foot, not a sprinting runner — match the model to the product being pushed.
- 3. Cut or shrink the Word Stride and Kids tiles for a subscriber like me who's never engaged with either — trim the email down so the walk to the offer is shorter.
- 4. Give "Family Sale up to 25% off" a specific anchor product or percentage so it doesn't read as vague filler.
