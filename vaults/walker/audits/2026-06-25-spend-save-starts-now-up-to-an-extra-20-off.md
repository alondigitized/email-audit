# Spend & Save starts NOW! Up to an extra $20 off 🤑
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- Famous Footwear shows up in my inbox, and before I even tap it open, I'm staring at that same wall of tracking URL garbage in the preview text — the exact same broken preheader I called out on the June 15th send, then again on June 16th, and now here it is a third time. "https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=ABB7..." followed by a random "Want." At some point that stops looking like a tech glitch and starts looking like nobody over there ever tests on an actual phone.
- Inside the email, the story is genuinely better. The Spend & Save tiers sit right at the top — $10 off $50, $15 off $75, $20 off $100 — simple math I can do in my head while I'm still standing at the kitchen counter. Skechers shows up in the "picked for you" section, Brooks gets its own dedicated block at $109.99, and there's even a Bonus Cash incentive for store pickup. Those are my brands. That's the offer that works for me. Then I keep scrolling and there are Air Jordans for $75 and Jordan shoes for $99. I am 62 years old. I am not the Air Jordan customer, and that whole section is just noise I have to wade through to get back to the stuff I care about.
- Would I click? Probably yes, specifically the Brooks section. Would I forward this to a friend? No — a friend would see that tracking URL in their inbox preview before they even open it, and wonder why I'm sending spam.

## What's working

- **Spend & Save tiers are front and center**: $10/$15/$20 off the moment I open it — no hunting, no fine print to decode before the offer reveals itself
- **Skechers appears in the "picked for you" row**: My brand, first product visible — that's how you earn the scroll
- **Brooks gets a dedicated branded section**: Logo, price, clean "SHOP BROOKS" button — exactly how you sell a comfort brand to someone who cares about the name
- **Bonus Cash with Store Pickup**: Nice incentive if I'm running errands anyway; makes the decision feel smarter, not just cheaper
- **"BUY NOW" buttons are plain and readable**: No "Discover the Journey," no "Explore the Collection" — just a button that does what it says

## What's weak

- **Third straight email with a raw tracking URL as the preview text**: I flagged this June 15th, I flagged it again June 16th, and here it is again on June 25th — "https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=ABB7..." is not a preheader, it's a process failure that's been left unfixed for ten days
- **Air Jordan / Jordan brand sections are the wrong aisle for me**: Two separate Jordan features take up real estate that could have been HOKA or New Balance walking shoes — I'm not the customer they're selling to there
- **"Starts NOW" with no end date visible**: Urgency without a deadline is just noise; I've got no reason to act today instead of next week
- **Zero comfort or fit language anywhere in the email**: A comfort-brand shopper like me needs to see "cushioned," "arch support," or "slip-on" somewhere — "Spend & Save" could be advertising anything

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text — this is the single most important fix**: Three consecutive sends with a broken tracking URL leaking into the preheader is a template or tagging problem that needs to be fixed before the next campaign goes out, full stop
- 2. **Add an end date to the Spend & Save hero**: "Ends Sunday" or "This weekend only" turns "starts NOW" into a real reason to act instead of a vague nudge
- 3. **Pull one of the Jordan sections and swap in a comfort/walking product block**: For any segment that includes a Walker-type subscriber, that real estate should be HOKA, New Balance, or a Skechers Arch Fit feature
- 4. **Add one comfort line to the hero copy**: Something as simple as "Cushioned. Supportive. Starting at $50." tells a comfort shopper immediately that this offer is aimed at them

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Spend & Save weekend starts now — up to $20 off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `$10 off $50 · $15 off $75 · $20 off $100 — on comfort brands you trust`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Skechers, Brooks, New Balance — pick your pair, pick your savings tier`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**FAIL — Charset meta tag is malformed.**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute. This tag is inert as written; without it, some clients fall back to their default charset. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```

**FAIL — Zoom disabled (WCAG 1.4.4 violation).**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
`user-scalable=0` and `maximum-scale=1` together prevent users from zooming — a hard accessibility failure for low-vision readers. Remove both constraints or set `maximum-scale=5`.

**WARN — 22 content images have empty or absent `alt` text.** This includes all product images, all 8 iGoDigital recommendation images (`recs.igodigital.com/rr/v2/.../i/[1–8]/990267198170`), and decorative divider images. When images are blocked (Outlook, corporate mail), the email renders as a blank column with no textual fallback.

The logo image has `alt=""` — acceptable for a decorative logo, but providing `alt="Famous Footwear"` would be better for screen readers.

**WARN — HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype.**
```html
<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN" ...>
```
Not a rendering bug in email clients (which largely ignore doctypes), but this is 25-year-old boilerplate. Benign.
