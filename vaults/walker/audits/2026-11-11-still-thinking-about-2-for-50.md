# Still Thinking About 2 for $50? 👀
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-11**
## Executive summary

- Crocs again. This is my seventh in about two weeks, and the third "2 for $50" in two days. Yesterday it was "Skip the Line: 2 Pairs for $50," and earlier today it was "2 for $50. Need We Say More?" Now it's "Still Thinking About 2 for $50?" I'm not thinking about it. I'm being nagged about it.
- The email itself is clean. The headline is big and white on olive green, and I could read it on my phone without squinting. There's one round white button and one clog. But I've seen this deal three times now. "Good deals deserve a second look" reads like a salesman following me around the store. The "Shop Early Access" button also confuses me, since the deal has been out for days. What's early?
- Would I click? Probably not. Would I forward it? No. A clog is fine for the yard, but I want cushioning and arch support. Nothing here tells me about either. I'd mark it read and move on.

## What's working

- The "2 FOR $50" headline is huge and easy to read without zooming.
- The deal is simple, and the price is right there in the hero.
- There's one white button with plenty of space around it, easy to tap with my thumb.
- The deadline in the fine print, November 14th at 11:59pm MT, is a real end date.
- The olive clog on a matching green background looks good, and the colors fit the season.

## What's weak

- It's the third "2 for $50" email in two days. It feels like pestering, not a reminder.
- "Shop Early Access" doesn't fit. The sale has been running all week, so early access to what?
- The email says nothing about comfort, cushioning, arch support or all-day wear. Only a slip-on look.
- The fine print is tiny and sits at the bottom. The real deadline (Nov 14) isn't in the headline area, where it could create urgency.
- There's no price per pair and no reviews. I can't tell if two clogs for $50 is a good deal or a markdown on a $35 shoe.
- The "Select Styles & Colors" and "Exclusions Apply" lines leave me guessing whether the olive clog in the photo is even included.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop sending the same offer three times in two days. Send one reminder, and make it say something new, like the deadline or a product that's selling fast.
- 2. Put the end date in the hero: "Ends Saturday, Nov. 14."
- 3. Replace "Shop Early Access" with something plain, like "Shop 2 for $50."
- 4. Show a few styles with prices and star ratings, and add a comfort line for people like me.
- 5. Say which styles are included, so the clog in the photo isn't a surprise exclusion.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML is a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud-style send (`click.crocs-email.com/open.aspx?qs=...`) with mostly sound structure. ## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified tracking links:** QA skipped 12 links on tracking/click-redirect domains (`click.crocs-email.com`) and did not probe them over HTTP. Their destination status is unknown, so this is a coverage gap and not a confirmed defect.
- **Third-party pixel:** QA flagged `https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&c_emailopened=Yes&d_dpuid=...`, an Adobe Audience Manager open-event beacon. It appears in the QA data but not in the truncated source I was given.
- **Open pixel:** `click.crocs-email.com/open.aspx?...` is a 1x1 image with `alt=""` inside a `height:1px; overflow:hidden` div. This is correctly implemented.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt on the demdex pixel:** QA flagged this image. It is a tracking pixel, so the impact is minor. It should have `alt=""` so screen readers don't announce the URL.
- **Charset and compatibility metas have no `http-equiv`:** `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` and `<meta content="IE=edge" />` have no `http-equiv` attribute, so as written the browser ignores them. This may be an artifact of how the source was captured, so treat it as moderate confidence. Clients usually rely on the HTTP `Content-Type` header, but the intended charset declaration is not in effect.
- **Generic `<title>`:** `<title>Crocs</title>` is not descriptive. This is low severity.
- **Correct practices seen:**
  - `lang="en"` is set.
  - The viewport meta is present.
  - The `-webkit-text-size-adjust` and `-ms-text-size-adjust` rules are present.
  - The Apple data-detector reset (`a[x-apple-data-detectors]`) is present.
  - The Gmail fix (`u + .email-body #new-gmail-hack`) is present.
  - The Outlook `mso-hide` override is present.
  - The `@font-face` rules use `font-display: swap`.
- **Not verifiable:** the font fallback stack, `role="presentation"` on layout tables, and alt text on content images all fall outside the truncated source.
