# 🚨Early Black Friday Deals🚨
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-09**
## Executive summary

- This is Shoe Carnival again, and I've lost count. Since about Oct 27 I've had the Halloween 25% off blast, the "Yule Always Trend In Black" email on Nov 5, and the "FINAL DAY" Nike and Jordan one on Nov 8. Now it's "Early Black Friday Deals" the very next day. The headline is big, red and easy to read on my phone without my glasses. But "early" and "preview" only tell me the real sale is coming later, so nothing here feels urgent.
- I scrolled for something that fit me. The top is almost all women's boots and winter slippers, "Santa's BOGO Free Faves." That isn't my foot. Further down I found men's Nike Revolution 8 running shoes and a Timberland boot. Nothing says slip-on, cushioned or arch support. The $10 off $74.98 or $25 off $149.98 coupon is clear, but I've seen that same coupon all month.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I were already looking for shoes. I wouldn't forward it, and I'd mark it read and move on. Too much, too often, and too aimed at women.

## What's working

- The "Black Friday Preview Sale" banner is huge and readable on a phone without squinting.
- The coupon block is easy to understand: $10 off $74.98 or $25 off $149.98, with the code HOLIDAY in plain sight.
- The "Add to your mobile wallet" coupons with barcodes are handy if I'm standing in the store.
- The bottom nav has Men's right there, and the bold section headers keep the long scroll organized.
- I know the brand, and the Nike, Timberland and New Balance names catch my eye.

## What's weak

- The first screen of products is all women's boots and slippers, and I'm a 62-year-old man.
- "BOGO free" only applies to "select women's boots and gear," and I only learn that from the tiny fine print at the bottom.
- There is nothing about comfort, cushioning, arch support or slip-ons. The shoe photos have no prices, so I can't do my own price-versus-quality math.
- It's the sixth or seventh email in two weeks with the same red coupon look, and "Preview" means the real deal isn't here yet.
- There are too many things competing for a tap: Word Stride game, SMS signup, Perks reward, Afterpay. It's a lot of scrolling for a small payoff.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment by gender. If a man has bought men's shoes, lead with men's walking and casual shoes, then New Balance and Hoka-style comfort picks, before women's boots.
- 2. Show prices and sale prices on every shoe photo. That's what makes me click.
- 3. Put the coupon near the top and cut the game and Afterpay tiles. Spell out the BOGO terms next to the hero, not in the fine print.
- 4. Slow the cadence, or save the coupon for the actual Black Friday send so it feels special.
- 5. Add a "Comfort Picks" row with slip-on, cushioned, arch-support styles.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The markup is mostly standard ESP-built (Responsys/Oracle-style `emltrk.com` tracking) with Outlook, Gmail and Samsung fixes. The confirmed defects are an unresolved `[UNIQUE]` token in a tracking pixel, a malformed UTM query on the other pixel, and missing `alt` attributes on 4 images. Link destinations, authentication and the footer could not be verified from the data provided.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Malformed tracking-pixel URL.** `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20261109_mwk_black_friday_versions_AM​`
  - The `t=` value contains a nested `utm_campaign=...`, so the parameter is `t=utm_campaign=…` rather than a clean key/value pair.
  - The URL ends with a zero-width space (U+200B, visible as `AM​`), which can corrupt the campaign value on parse or be percent-encoded as `%E2%80%8B`.
- **Unresolved placeholder.** `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]` still contains the literal `[UNIQUE]`. See section 4.
- **Unverified links.** 54 click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe, so destination status, redirect chains and final URLs are unverified. That is a coverage gap, not a confirmed failure.
- **Plain-text part is mostly URLs.** URLs make up 10,434 of 13,904 characters (75%, above the 70% threshold). This is a spam-filter risk and a poor plain-text experience, and it is consistent with long redirect URLs.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `alt` on content images** (not just pixels):
  - `…/m/1/23a03225-7c93-49fa-988c-8821675cac1b.jpg`
  - `…/m/1/fe4e293c-bccf-488a-bbf7-db06bf5745de.jpg`

  Screen readers will announce the filename or nothing, and image-blocked clients show no fallback text.
- **Pixels without `alt`.** The two `emltrk.com` pixels also lack `alt`. Use `alt=""` plus `width="1" height="1"`.
- **Malformed meta tags.** `<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />` and `<meta content="IE=edge" />` have no `http-equiv`, so the second is a no-op.
- **Doctype mismatch.** The HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype is paired with `xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml"` and self-closing `<meta … />` tags. This is not harmful in practice, but it is inconsistent.
- **Link styling reset.** The global `a { text-decoration:none !important; color:inherit !important }` removes the default link affordance. Any text link that lacks its own inline styling would be indistinguishable from body text. I can't confirm this occurs because the source is truncated.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Unrendered token:** `?d=[UNIQUE]` in the `0di86k1v.emltrk.com` pixel. If the ESP expected `[UNIQUE]` to be replaced per recipient and it wasn't, the open pixel can't attribute opens to a recipient. It may be a test-send artifact, since the message passed through the AgentMail relay.
- No other unresolved tokens are visible in the truncated source.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Authentication-Results header not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The QA tool attributes this to the AgentMail relay, so it's a verification gap rather than a confirmed failure.
- Unsubscribe link, physical postal address and `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers could not be assessed. The source is truncated before the footer and no headers were supplied.
- The From domain `email.shoecarnival.com` matches the image host `image.email.shoecarnival.com`, which is consistent for alignment. This is not a defect.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- The only UTM evidence is `utm_campaign=SCA_20261109_mwk_black_friday_versions_AM`, and it appears inside the malformed pixel URL (see section 2). The campaign name is well-formed, dated 2026-11-09 and encodes the AM version.
- `utm_source` and `utm_medium` could not be verified, and landing-page alignment could not be checked, because the 54 tracking links weren't resolved.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Fix the pixel URL: remove the nested `utm_campaign=` from `t=`, strip the trailing U+200B, and URL-encode the value.
2. Make sure `[UNIQUE]` is substituted at send time. If it is a test-send artifact, confirm that the production send resolves it.
3. Add descriptive `alt` text to `23a03225-…jpg` and `fe4e293c-…jpg`, and `alt=""` to both tracking pixels.
4. Fix or remove the two malformed `<meta>` tags (`http-equiv="Content-Type"`, `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`), and align the doctype with the markup style.
5. Shorten redirect URLs or add more human-readable text to the plain-text part, to bring URL share below 70%.
6. Re-run the audit with a full-length HTML source and the raw headers, so the footer and unsubscribe link, `List-Unsubscribe`, SPF/DKIM/DMARC and the resolved destination URLs (UTM propagation) can be checked.
