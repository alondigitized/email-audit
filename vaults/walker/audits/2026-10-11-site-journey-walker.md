# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-10-11**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I got through the front door and found shoes, but I never got a pair into a cart. On the product page I tapped Add to Cart without picking a size and got a red "Please select a size" error. Then the cart page was a completely blank white screen. The cart badge showed 0 on every screen. I also never appeared to be logged in: the "Log In" step looked identical to the homepage, with nothing personal anywhere.

The first few steps were decent. The tabs across the top say "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Arch Fit," which are the things I shop for. The Men's menu had large, readable rows. When I searched "comfort shoes," the first two results were women's styles. I'm a man shopping the men's side, and no comparison to other brands showed up anywhere.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10** (yesterday: 3/10, so no change)

I can't see yesterday's screenshots, so I can't point to a specific regression or improvement. Judging only by today's screens, the checkout path is broken. A blank cart page means no sale, no matter how good the shoes are.

## 3. What's Working

- **Top tabs that match what I want.** "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Arch Fit" are right under the logo. That is hands-free and arch support, and I didn't have to hunt for them.
- **Men's menu.** The rows are big and spaced out, with plain labels like "Shoes," "Comfort Technologies," and "Shop by Activity." My thumb can hit them.
- **Men's Shoes page.** The first thing is a row of photo tiles: "Hands Free Slip-Ins," "Walking Shoes," and "Boots." The tile photos are clear. There are 773 results, and Filter and Sort sit in an easy spot.
- **Size buttons.** They're big boxes with room between them, easy to tap with my thumb.
- **Add to Cart button.** It's wide, dark blue, and unmistakable.
- **Search.** It returned 1,602 results with prices and color swatches, and it understood "comfort" well enough to show cushioned shoes.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Blank cart.** The cart page was pure white with no items, no message, and no way back. I would have gone to Hoka or Brooks at that point.
- **No size warning until I tapped.** The page didn't tell me a size was required until I tried to add. The error did show up in red, but nothing told me what happened next.
- **Cart count stuck at 0.** It was 0 on every screen, including after I tried to add.
- **No sign of being logged in.** Step 3 looked identical to the homepage. There was no greeting, no name, and no points balance.
- **Popup step looked unchanged.** I can't tell whether anything was dismissed, so I can't say whether it was in my way.
- **The first product I landed on had no reviews and an extra caution.**
  - It shows "No Reviews" with five empty stars.
  - It's $100 and says "Excluded from promotions," which contradicts the "Extra 10% OFF Everything" banner above it.
  - It's a graphic-print sneaker, not a cushioned walker.
- **Search results.** The first two were women's, and the second was a wedge heel. I never saw anything on the page that let me filter to men's.
- **Small print.** The "details" links and the fine print under the price are small for my eyes. The tab bar is also cut off ("Max Cus…"), so I wouldn't know there's more unless I swipe.
- **No comparison with other brands, and no value math.** Nothing helped me weigh price against quality.
- **Free shipping.** It's tucked behind "Sign in for free shipping & returns," which feels like a gate.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page first.** Show the items, or show a "Your cart is empty" message with a button back to shopping. A blank page should never ship.
2. **Make the size requirement obvious.** Highlight the size row and scroll to it when someone taps Add to Cart without picking one. Keep the error message, but pair it with a visible cue.
3. **Update the cart count** the moment something is added, and show a small confirmation.
4. **Show I'm signed in.** After login, show my name or a greeting in the header, plus anything personal like recent views.
5. **Respect men's shopping in search.** Default or offer a "Men's" filter at the top, so a man doesn't have to scroll past women's wedges.
6. **Reconcile the promo messaging.** Don't show "Extra 10% off everything" and "Excluded from promotions" on the same screen without explaining it.
7. **Put comfort details up front on each product:** cushioning, arch support, and whether it's slip-in. A short "Comfort Features" row with those facts would do it.
8. **Increase small text sizes** and let me tap a bigger area around links like "details."
9. **Think about the competition.** A simple "Compared with Hoka and Brooks" panel that makes the value case (price, warranty, comfort tech) would earn some trust.

## 6. Bottom Line

I like the idea of what Skechers sells: slip-ins, arch fit, and wide sizes. But I couldn't buy anything. The size error was my own miss, and the site could have guided me, but a blank cart page is the site's fault. Until the cart works and shows me what I put in it, the nice menus don't matter. I'd go back to another brand or the sales aisle at DSW. Score stays at **3/10**.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

- The journey was heavily affected by bot-mitigation rate limiting. Five of the six network errors are HTTP 429 responses from a Kasada endpoint (`/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.864`), and the sixth is a 429 on `/cart/`.
- The View Cart axe results (no `<title>`, no `lang`, no `<main>`, no `<h1>`) are consistent with a bare 429 error document, not the real cart template. Treat them as a likely artifact of the block, not a cart defect.
- LCP and CLS were not captured for any step (`?`), so there are no Core Web Vitals to assess. Only TTFB is available.
- Log In, Men category and the other sampled steps report the URL `https://www.skechers.com/`. The sampled DOM for those steps is the same homepage document, so the navigation may not have completed or may be SPA/overlay-based. Per-step results should be read with that caveat.
- The homepage has 9 axe violations, including 1 critical for `button-name` and 1 critical for `aria-required-parent`. The search page has 4 violations, and View Cart has 4 (probably the 429 artifact).
- The homepage loads a GTM container with `gtm_preview=env-1&gtm_auth=…&gtm_cookies_win=x`. This is a GTM preview/staging environment parameter on a production page.

## Accessibility

17 violations in total, from axe runs on 3 of the 8 steps (Homepage, View Cart, Search). The other steps have no axe results, so coverage is incomplete.

**Homepage (9)**
- Critical: `button-name`. There are buttons with no discernible text.
- Critical: `aria-required-parent`. An ARIA role is missing its required parent, for example a `listitem` or `tab` outside its list or tablist.
- Serious: `link-name`. There are links with no accessible text.
- Moderate, landmark structure:
  - `landmark-no-duplicate-main`: more than one `<main>`.
  - `landmark-main-is-top-level`: a `<main>` is nested inside another landmark.
  - `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`: an `aside` is nested in another landmark.
  - `landmark-unique`: landmarks share the same role and name.
  - `region`: content sits outside any landmark.
- Minor: `aria-allowed-role`.

**Search "comfort shoes" (4)**
- Moderate: `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique`, `region`.
- Minor: `aria-allowed-role`.
- The `aria-allowed-role`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region` violations repeat from the homepage, which points to shared header/footer/template markup. The duplicate/nested `<main>` problem appeared only on the homepage.

**View Cart (4), likely a 429 artifact**
- Serious: `document-title`, `html-has-lang`.
- Moderate: `landmark-one-main`, `page-has-heading-one`.
- The homepage HTML does have `<html lang="en">`, so the missing `lang` here supports the error-document explanation.

## SEO

- The homepage has `<html lang="en">`, and no homepage heading or title violation was reported.
- The HTML samples were truncated before `<title>`, meta description, canonical, Open Graph tags and JSON-LD, so these could not be assessed.
- The View Cart document had no `<title>` or `<h1>`. This is probably the 429 response, and the cart should be `noindex` anyway.
- Several steps return identical `<html>` and head content for the same URL. This is a harness issue, not necessarily a site issue.
- The Kasada 429s mean crawler-like clients can be challenged or blocked. Confirm that verified search bots are allowlisted.

- The head injects a large number of third-party scripts, all `async`:
  - Tag management and analytics: GTM, gtag (G-, AW-, DC-), Quantum Metric, Pulse Insights, Monetate (two scripts), Bing UET.
  - Ad pixels: TikTok (three scripts), Snapchat, Reddit, Facebook, Nextdoor, Taboola, Pinterest, Yahoo (`ytc.js`, loaded twice), Impact, RTB House (`creativecdn`), Tune (`go2sdk`).
  - Others: Bloomreach (`brsrvr`), Stape (`stapecdn`).
- This creates main-thread, network and INP risk on mobile, which cannot be quantified without LCP, CLS and INP data.
- `webfontloader.js` is fetched from cdnjs and loaded `async`, so Mulish rendering depends on JavaScript. This risks FOUT/FOIT and a layout shift or late LCP. Check for `font-display` and `<link rel="preload">` on the font files.
- `ytc.js` is requested twice, which is a duplicate load.

## Mobile Optimization

- The viewport was 664px high (`--vh: 6.64px`), and the `--vh` custom property is set in JS. This is a common workaround for mobile browser chrome, and no issue was observed.
- The viewport meta tag, tap target sizes and responsive images could not be assessed because the head was truncated and no Lighthouse or tap-target data was provided.
- The unaudited third-party load and the late-set JS-dependent font and layout variables are the main risks on mid-range mobile CPUs and networks.

## Console & Network Errors

**Network (6)**
- Five 429s on `https://www.skechers.com/149e9513-…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.864`. This is the Kasada bot-defense fingerprint endpoint. The audit client is being rate-limited or challenged.
- One 429 on `https://www.skechers.com/cart/`. The cart page was blocked or throttled.

**Console (24 recorded; 9 shown)**
- `Failed to load resource: 429`, three times. These correspond to the network 429s.
- `Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document`, four times. Some script is registering an `unload` handler, and the `unload` Permissions-Policy blocks it. Chrome is deprecating `unload`. The source is unattributed, but it is likely a third-party tag. It should be replaced with `pagehide` / `visibilitychange`.
- `Unsupported Summarizer API languages were specified…`, three times. This is the Chrome built-in Summarizer API being called with unsupported language options. Its origin is unattributed, probably a third-party or experimental script. It is harmless to users but is console noise, and it indicates calls to an on-device AI API that was probably not intended.
- 15 of the 24 console errors were not provided, so this list is incomplete.

## Recommendations

1. **Re-run the audit after resolving the rate limiting.**
   - Use a lower request rate, a stable IP, or an allowlist for the audit client.
   - Verify that each step actually navigates, by logging the real URL per step.
   - Treat the View Cart results as invalid until the cart loads without a 429.
2. **Capture LCP, CLS and INP.** The `?` values suggest the observers were not attached early enough. Add the `web-vitals` library, or `PerformanceObserver` with `buffered: true`, before navigation.
3. **Fix the critical accessibility issues first.** Give an accessible name to every unlabeled button and link, and correct the ARIA parent/child structure (`aria-required-parent`).
4. **Fix the landmark structure.**
   - Use one top-level `<main>`.
   - Move `aside` elements out of other landmarks.
   - Give duplicate landmarks distinct `aria-label`s.
   - Wrap remaining content in landmarks.
5. **Remove the GTM preview parameters** (`gtm_preview=env-1`, `gtm_auth`) from the production container snippet, if they are not intended. Confirm which GTM environment is serving.
6. **Audit the tag load.**
   - Remove duplicates (for example `ytc.js` twice).
   - Defer non-critical pixels until after consent or interaction.
   - Consider server-side tagging to cut client work.
7. **Replace `unload` listeners** with `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`, and find which script registers them.
8. **Review font loading.** Self-host or preload Mulish with `font-display: swap`, instead of relying on webfontloader from a public CDN.
9. **Verify SEO basics with a full-HTML capture:** title, meta description, canonical, structured data, and `noindex` on cart and account pages.
