# ENDS TONIGHT: $10 OFF 🚨
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- Eleven emails in four days. At this point my phone practically opens DSW on its own. The subject — "ENDS TONIGHT: $10 OFF" with a red siren — got my thumb moving before I even thought about it, especially because yesterday's send literally said "ends tomorrow." They followed through on the arc, and I respect that consistency.
- But then I opened the thing and it said "COMING IN HOT!" with row after row of women's sandals and wedges in nude and tan. That $10 off is pinned in a skinny yellow banner at the very top — easy to miss — and after that it's a discovery scroll that has nothing to do with why I clicked. I'm a 62-year-old man looking for cushioned, comfortable men's shoes. What I got was a Nordstrom Rack catalog for my daughter.
- I won't click any of the "SHOP NOW" buttons buried under those product rows. I might tap that yellow banner if I can find it again. Mostly I'll mark it read and wonder how DSW keeps making the same mistake — building urgency in the subject, then blowing the landing with irrelevant creative.

## What's working

- **The subject earned its open.** "ENDS TONIGHT" is the natural payoff after yesterday's "ends tomorrow" — it's the only DSW send this week that felt like the logical next chapter instead of a restart.
- **Yellow banner at the top keeps the $10 promise alive.** At least it's above the fold. I spotted it before the scroll started.
- **"SHOP BY SIZE" block near the bottom** is actually useful — lets me cut straight to my size without wading through categories.
- **Clean render, no broken images.** Everything loaded, nothing overlapped.

## What's weak

- **"COMING IN HOT!" is a bait-and-switch in spirit.** The subject said urgent deal; the hero said new arrivals. Those are two different emails.
- **Women's shoes wall-to-wall.** Every product image I can see — wedges, strappy sandals, heeled mules — reads as women's. I'm not the guy they're selling to in the body of this email.
- **$10 off is in a banner, not the hero.** For a send with that much urgency in the subject, the deal should be the headline, not a thin stripe I could easily scroll past.
- **Six-plus "SHOP NOW" buttons with no differentiation.** I don't know which one to tap. They all say the same thing and they're stacked on totally different product sections.
- **"The countdown starts now" preheader, then garbage.** Whatever tracking spacer characters are after that line show up as blank boxes in my inbox — looks like a broken email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the $10 off the hero, not a banner.** Big bold number, expiration time, men's shoes behind it. The subject earned that treatment; the body has to deliver it.
- 2. **Lead with men's comfort or walking shoes** — even one row of Skechers, HOKA, or New Balance before the women's sections. Show me something, then show her everything.
- 3. **Cut the CTAs to two.** One for the deal ("Grab $10 Off Now") and one for browsing ("Shop New Arrivals"). Multiple identical "SHOP NOW" buttons cancel each other out.
- 4. **Fix the preheader.** Strip those spacer characters. Even "Offer expires at midnight" is better than trailing junk.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Duplicate conflicting `-ms-text-size-adjust` in `*` selector:**
```css
*{
  -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%;   /* declared first */
  ...
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;   /* overrides — wins */
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
The `none` value wins due to cascade order. This disables automatic text scaling in IE/Edge. If intentional, remove the dead `100%` declaration. If not, revert `none` → `100%` for both properties.

**Issue 2 — `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks user pinch-zoom on mobile. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1` — modern responsive email does not require it.

**Issue 3 — Malformed charset meta tag:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Without it, Outlook and older WebKit clients won't process this as a content-type declaration. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```

**Issue 4 — No `woff2` font format declared:**
All `@font-face` rules serve either `.woff` (Futura PT) or `.ttf` (HelveticaNow). No `.woff2` sources are present. `woff2` is supported by all modern email clients that support web fonts (Apple Mail, Samsung Mail) and compresses ~30% smaller than `woff`. Impact: slower font load, higher bandwidth on cellular.

**No issues found:** Dark mode CSS structure (`prefers-color-scheme: dark` block) is well-formed for the visible portion. XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype is legacy but appropriate for broad ESP compatibility.
