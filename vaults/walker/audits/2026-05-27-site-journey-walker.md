# Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Date:** May 27, 2026 | **Yesterday's Score:** 4/10

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Skechers has a genuinely solid foundation for a guy like me — Hands Free Slip-ins are right there in the Men's menu, the product pages are clean, and that BOGO deal is hard to miss. But two failures kill the trip: the cart page loaded completely blank (I'd think my order evaporated), and when I searched "comfort shoes," every result was Women's. I walked the Men's section for twenty minutes and the site still doesn't know I exist. Same core problems as yesterday. No meaningful progress.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

**No change from yesterday.** The navigation and product experience improved slightly in clarity, but a blank cart and a gender-blind search engine are the same brick walls they were 24 hours ago. Skechers is losing the sale at the finish line, twice.

---

## 3. What's Working

- **BOGO 50% OFF deal is impossible to miss.** That blue banner across the very top is the first thing I see. For a guy who does price-vs-quality math, that's a good opening handshake.
- **Men's menu is excellent for older hands.** The drawer menu (Step 4) has large, well-spaced rows — Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies — with generous tap targets. No hunting around. "Hands Free Slip-ins" is right there as a quick link below the fold.
- **"Comfort Technologies" lives in the Men's nav.** That's exactly the kind of category label that earns my trust. It signals Skechers knows why I'm here.
- **Product page (Step 6) reads clearly.** Big bold product name, 4.5 stars, $120 price, and the BOGO callout in red right under it — I'd take all that in without zooming.
- **ADD TO CART button (Step 7) is a home run.** Full-width, blue, sticky at the bottom of the screen. I can tap that with my thumb without thinking about it.
- **Size grid is finger-friendly.** Twelve size boxes with generous whitespace between them. No fat-finger disasters waiting to happen.
- **287 Hands Free Slip-ins results.** That's real depth. Filtering by Wide Fit and Extra Wide Fit right at the top of the listing page is a thoughtful touch.

---

## 4. What's Weak

- **Cart page loaded blank white (Step 8).** Complete. Failure. Nothing. Not a spinner, not an error message — just a white void. I would have no idea if my item was saved, lost, or if something broke. At 62, my first instinct isn't "the JavaScript is still loading" — it's "I did something wrong" or "this site is broken." I close the tab and go to DSW.

- **Search "comfort shoes" returned Women's shoes (Step 9).** I just spent the last ten minutes in the Men's section. I browsed Men's Slip-ins. I looked at a Men's product. I tried to add it to cart. Then I searched "comfort shoes" and the first two results — both of them — are Women's. The Arch Fit Arcade and Cozy Fit both say "Women's" right under the name. If Skechers can't connect those dots for a logged-in male shopper, the personalization isn't working at all. 1,439 results is overwhelming noise without a gender filter applied.

- **Login step captured nothing (Step 3).** The screenshot looks identical to the homepage. No "Welcome back" greeting, no name in the nav, no loyalty points balance surfaced anywhere. If I logged in, the site doesn't seem to care. I have no idea what my Skechers Plus points are or whether I'm close to a reward.

- **Homepage hero skews young (Steps 1–2).** The lifestyle photo shows two people who look 25 and 30. The "Win a Soccer Fan Prize Package" banner is complete noise to me. Neither image speaks to a 62-year-old who wants cushioning, arch support, and easy entry. Hoka and New Balance are running ads with older adults right now. Skechers is leaving that message on the table.

- **No cushioning/arch support language on the listing page (Step 5).** The category is called "Hands Free Slip-ins" which is great, but the product cards visible below give me nothing about comfort specs — no "Max Cushioning" badge, no "Memory Foam" callout, no arch support note. I'd have to click every product to find out if it's actually comfortable.

- **"Please select a size" error shown before the ADD TO CART tap (Step 7).** This tells me the automation triggered ADD TO CART without choosing a size — which is what any confused user might do if they scroll past the size grid. The error message is clear and the icon is visible, so this one is fine as a recovery moment, but the cart being blank immediately after is still catastrophic.

- **Secondary promo banner rotates mid-session (Steps 1 vs. 2).** First visit shows "Spring into Summer Sale 25% OFF Apparel." Second pass shows "Get Dad a Gift He'll Love!" Those are two different messages in the same slot within the same session. That inconsistency makes me feel like I might be missing something or the site is shuffling deals on me.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page. Today.** A blank cart is a conversion-destroying bug. At minimum, surface an error state or a loading indicator. Ideally, audit why it goes white — likely a JS failure on cart data fetch.

2. **Default search to the shopper's browsing gender.** I was in Men's the entire session. "Comfort shoes" should default to Men's results with a clear "Also showing Women's / Switch" toggle. This is a single personalization rule — not a large engineering lift.

3. **Surface the logged-in state visibly.** Put "Hi, Walker" or "Your Points: 240" somewhere in the header after login. Even just the account icon turning to my initials makes me feel known.

4. **Add comfort-spec callouts to listing cards.** One line under each product thumbnail: "Memory Foam Insole" or "Max Cushioning" or "Arch Fit." I shouldn't have to tap every card to find out if my feet will thank me.

5. **Refresh the homepage hero for the core customer.** One lifestyle image featuring a man 55–65 walking casually would do more for my trust than any soccer giveaway banner.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

Skechers built a clean, navigable mobile experience right up until the moment I tried to buy something. The Men's menu is thoughtful, the product page is readable, and the BOGO deal is well-positioned. But a blank cart page and search results that ignore my browsing history are the same two failures that cost this site yesterday's sale. **Score: 4/10 — no change.** The architecture is right; the execution breaks at checkout and search. Fix those two and this jumps to a 7 quickly.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Stack: Salesforce Commerce Cloud (SFCC/Demandware), identified via `/on/demandware.static/Sites-USSkechers-Site/` paths. 20+ third-party scripts injected on every page. Bot-detection is Kasada (`x-kpsdk-v`). Three distinct failure modes observed: Kasada rate-limiting legitimate sessions, a broken View Cart HTML shell, and CORS misconfigurations on two third-party vendors.

---

## Accessibility

**15 violations across 3 pages. Two are serious; one is critical.**

**Homepage (8):**
| Severity | Rule | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | `aria-required-parent` | ARIA role used on element without required parent context — likely a listbox/option or grid/row orphan |
| Moderate | `landmark-no-duplicate-main` | Multiple `<main>` elements rendered simultaneously, suggesting partial-page injection by Monetate/GTM |
| Moderate | `landmark-main-is-top-level` | `<main>` is nested inside another landmark |
| Moderate | `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` | `<aside>` nested inside non-body landmark |
| Moderate | `landmark-unique` | Duplicate `role="navigation"` or `role="complementary"` without distinguishing labels |
| Moderate | `heading-order` | Heading levels are non-sequential (e.g., h1 → h3, skipping h2) |
| Moderate | `region` | Content outside any landmark — likely injected ad/promo content |
| Minor | `aria-allowed-role` | ARIA role applied to element that doesn't permit it (e.g., `role="button"` on `<div>` that should be a `<button>`) |

**View Cart (4) — most severe:**
| Severity | Rule | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| Serious | `document-title` | `<title>` is missing or empty — the cart page has a broken HTML shell |
| Serious | `html-has-lang` | No `lang` attribute on `<html>` — contradicts all other pages which have `lang="en"`. Cart may be rendered in a separate iframe or headless shell |
| Moderate | `landmark-one-main` | No `<main>` element present |
| Moderate | `page-has-heading-one` | No `<h1>` present |

The View Cart page is structurally incomplete. The HTML shell used for `/cart/` is different from the rest of the site and missing foundational elements.

**Search (3):**
Same `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique`, and `region` violations as Homepage — indicates these are systemic to the layout template, not page-specific.

## SEO

- **View Cart missing `<title>`**: Googlebot will see a blank title if it crawls this URL. Low risk since carts are typically `noindex`, but confirms the page shell is broken.
- **Duplicate `<main>` on Homepage**: Crawlers parsing semantic structure will encounter conflicting document outlines. Indicates third-party tag injection (Monetate) is inserting structural elements into the DOM.
- **Non-sequential headings**: Heading hierarchy issues affect crawler-parsed content hierarchy.
- **No structural issues with canonical, hreflang, or robots** visible in the provided HTML fragments.

## Mobile Optimization

- **`--vh` CSS variable**: Set inline as `--vh: 6.640000000000001px` — this is a JS-based fix for the mobile browser 100vh bug. The floating point imprecision (`6.640000000000001` instead of `6.64`) means no rounding is applied before writing to the DOM. Low visual impact but indicates the script doing the calculation doesn't sanitize output.
- **No `<meta name="viewport">` visible** in the truncated HTML samples — cannot confirm it's present, but the `--vh` fix implies mobile viewport handling is JS-driven.
- **WebFont via JS**: Font loading depends on JavaScript execution. On slow mobile connections or if a script earlier in the chain errors, fonts may FOUT or fail to load entirely.

## Console & Network Errors

**Kasada 429s (5 network errors, 3 console errors):**

```
429 https://www.skechers.com/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/.../fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.416
```

This is the Kasada bot protection fingerprint endpoint. Five 429s from a single browsing session means Kasada's rate limit is being hit by legitimate user traffic — the fingerprint script is polling too aggressively, or Kasada's session token isn't being reused correctly between requests. This will silently degrade bot-protection coverage and may cause downstream features gated behind Kasada challenge completion (checkout, account) to fail.

**Cart 429:**
```
429 https://www.skechers.com/cart/
```
The cart page itself returned 429. Combined with the 127ms TTFB, this is the direct cause of the View Cart performance spike. Users who hit this will see either an error or an empty cart state.

**CORS — `reporting.cdndex.io` (2 errors):**
```
Access to XMLHttpRequest at 'https://reporting.cdndex.io/error' blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```
cdndex.io is a CDN edge reporting/RUM endpoint. Their error reporting endpoint does not send `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com`. The XHR silently fails — error telemetry is being lost.

**CORS — Stylitics stylesheet (1 error):**
```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?...' blocked by CORS policy
```
Stylitics (outfit inspiration widget) is loading a stylesheet cross-origin without correct CORS headers. The widget will render unstyled or not at all. This is a vendor-side misconfiguration.

## Recommendations

**P0 — Cart 429 / broken HTML shell:**
- Investigate why `/cart/` returns 429 in normal user sessions. Check if Kasada fingerprint failures are causing downstream rate-limit propagation to the cart endpoint.
- Audit the cart page HTML template — it is missing `<title>`, `lang`, `<main>`, and `<h1>`. This is a separate template from the rest of the site.

**P1 — Kasada over-polling:**
- Work with Kasada support to review the `fp` endpoint call frequency. Five 429s in a single session is abnormal. Ensure the SDK version (`j-1.2.416`) is current and that session tokens are being persisted correctly.

**P1 — Duplicate `<main>` / landmark violations:**
- Audit Monetate and GTM tag configurations — third-party injection is creating duplicate structural landmarks. Add structural element injection to tag QA checklist.

**P2 — CORS vendors:**
- File tickets with Stylitics and cdndex.io to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` headers for `www.skechers.com`. Until resolved, Stylitics widget styling is broken and RUM error data is being dropped.

**P2 — `aria-required-parent` (critical):**
- Locate the component triggering orphaned ARIA roles and add the required parent wrapper. This is the only critical-severity accessibility violation and the most likely to trigger automated compliance flags.

**P3 — Script consolidation:**
- 20+ analytics/pixel scripts fire on every page load. Consolidate under a single GTM trigger strategy with proper load sequencing. Prioritize deferring non-essential pixels (Reddit, Yahoo, Taboola, Nextdoor) until after LCP.

**P3 — WebFont loading:**
- Replace `webfontloader.js` with `<link rel="preload" as="font">` + `font-display: swap` for the Mulish font family. The JS-based approach introduces render dependency on script execution.
