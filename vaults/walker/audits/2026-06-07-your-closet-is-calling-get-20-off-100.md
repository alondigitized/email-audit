# Your closet is calling ☎️ Get $20 off $100
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- Look, Famous Footwear is in my regular rotation and $20 off a hundred bucks gets my attention — they ran that exact same deal two days ago in "Stack the summer savings," so I already know what it is and how it works. Opening this felt like checking a store's updated weekly circular, not getting something new. The creative says "trendy styles just for you," and that's where they lost me — I don't shop for trendy. I shop for something that won't kill my feet by 3pm.
- What's inside is a grab-bag: a dark sneaker section, a $74.99 markdown shoe I can barely read the name on, what looks like a Jordan at $99.99 (same one from the June 5 send), and then Brooks at $109.99 with a young woman in athletic gear doing the modeling. The Brooks caught my eye because I do cross-shop them — but nothing in this email tells me whether that $109.99 pair has any cushioning worth talking about, whether it comes in a wide, or whether it runs true to size.
- I would not click today. I'd mark it as read, remind myself the $20 code probably has a few more days on it, and maybe go looking on my own terms. Would not forward this to anyone.

## What's working

- **$20 off $100+ is front and center in the hero** — pink banner, hard to miss, I spotted the number before I even scrolled. That's the right call.
- **Brooks showing up in the product mix** is a brand I actually cross-shop, so the curation isn't totally off base.
- **Markdown pricing is visible** — seeing $74.99 and $99.99 with "these markdowns won't last long" gives me a reason to act, not just browse.
- **No visual clutter or layout breakage** — email renders clean, nothing's overlapping or broken, text is legible on a phone.

## What's weak

- **Preview text is a raw tracking URL** — in my inbox that's what I see next to the subject, and it looks like a mistake or spam. Almost made me delete before opening.
- **"Trendy styles just for you" is not my language** — I'm 62, I need comfort, not trend. That header is aimed at someone twenty years younger.
- **Same $20/100 offer as June 4's "Stack the summer savings"** — Famous Footwear has now sent me this same basic deal three times in a row. It's starting to feel like a form letter, not a promotion.
- **The Jordan at $99.99 was also in the June 5 email** — I know because I was tempted then and didn't pull the trigger; seeing the exact same shoe two days later with no new angle doesn't make me more likely to buy it.
- **No comfort or fit copy anywhere** — no mention of cushioning, arch support, wide widths, or anything that would help me decide whether any of these shoes would actually work for my feet.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill that preheader URL and write real copy** — something like "Same $20 off, but now with new markdowns added" actually gives me a reason to open when I already saw this deal earlier this week.
- 2. **Drop "Trendy styles just for you" — replace with something comfort-adjacent** — "Great fits for all-day comfort" or "Cushioned picks on sale now" would make me feel like they know why I buy shoes.
- 3. **If you're going to repeat the same offer, add a freshness signal** — show me what's new since the last send. "12 new markdowns since Tuesday" would be enough.
- 4. **Give the Brooks section some functional copy** — even one line like "extra cushioned, wide widths available" is the difference between a scroll-past and a tap.
- 5. **Vary the cadence or vary the deal** — three $20/100 emails in four days erodes trust in the offer. Rotate the mechanic or I start ignoring the whole sender.

## Technical Audit — Famous Footwear: "Your closet is calling / Get $20 off $100"

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound and uses a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud/ExactTarget stack. Two issues require fixing before send: a mixed-content HTTP image that will be blocked in modern clients, and pervasive missing alt text across 27 images.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text body URL density — WARN**
QA reports 8,919 of 11,468 plain-text characters are URLs (78%). Spam filters (SpamAssassin, Postfix content filters) flag ratios above ~70%. The plain-text part should be a human-readable summary with a small number of bare URLs; strip redirect wrappers from the plain-text version or rewrite it as prose.

**28 click-redirect links — INFO**
All CTAs route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=…` with base64-encoded payloads. QA correctly skipped HTTP probes on these. No broken destination URLs can be confirmed or denied without resolving the redirects; UTM continuity is unverifiable (see §6).

**Open pixel — OK**
`click.email.famousfootwear.com/open.aspx?GHHODTIYMOEEBHNN52FGLMWIEU.70246` — HTTPS, standard 1×1, no issues.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image — BUG (will break in production)**
```
src: http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=ABB7InYiOjEsImQiOjQ5MDB9…
```
This ExactTarget LiveContent dynamic-content image is served over plain HTTP. Gmail, Outlook 365, and Apple Mail all proxy or block non-HTTPS image sources. The image will fail to load or trigger a security warning for a significant share of recipients. Fix: ensure the LiveContent endpoint is served at `https://cl.S7.exct.net/…` (Salesforce supports HTTPS on this domain).

**Missing alt text — 27 images**
Every content image — logo, hero, product tiles, recommendation-engine images from `7222883.recs.igodigital.com` (8 items) — has `alt=""`. The logo link (`3b6a7dc1…png`) is the most critical: it is the only content visible when images are blocked in Outlook/corporate environments, and a blank alt leaves the link unidentifiable. Minimum fix: `alt="Famous Footwear"` on the logo; descriptive alt on product images; `alt=""` is acceptable only for the hairline divider (`4ccff4ad…jpg`, `height="2"`).

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
Disabling pinch-zoom fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is blocked outright by iOS Safari 10+. Remove `maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0`.

**`<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` — malformed**
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, the charset declaration has no effect in HTML4 Transitional; character encoding falls back to client default, which can corrupt emoji and special characters.

**Duplicate image reference**
`4ccff4ad-f402-43de-a027-5eb147b6f238.jpg` appears 4× in QA output. If this is the same hairline divider reused as a separator, that is fine; if it is a production CMS deduplication error, verify intent.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 28 CTAs are wrapped in opaque `click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=<base64>` redirects. The encoded payloads likely carry UTM parameters, but this cannot be confirmed without resolving the redirects. QA skipped resolution. **Action required:** spot-check 2–3 decoded click URLs to verify `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` are appended before the final destination and that the offer ($20 off $100) is surfaced on the landing pages. If the redirect layer strips query strings, UTM attribution will be lost.
