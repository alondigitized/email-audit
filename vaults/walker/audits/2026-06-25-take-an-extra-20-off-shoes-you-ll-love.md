# Take an extra 20% off shoes you'll love.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-percent off, Rewards Member exclusive — that's the headline, and Famous Footwear gets credit for putting it right up front where I don't have to hunt. I recognized the sender in half a second. But before I even opened this thing, my inbox showed me a long string of gobbledygook instead of a real preview — looked like a tracking link someone forgot to swap out. I almost swiped it away just like I nearly did with the "Final 72 hrs" send back on June 8th that had the same problem. These folks keep stepping on their own shoelaces.
- Inside, there's a decent catalog spread. Brooks shows up — and at $74.99 that's a price point that makes me lean forward. But then right below it there's a big "DUDE" brand banner and what looks like a Jordan or Nike lifestyle shoe at $99.99. That's not my aisle. I'm not a 35-year-old trying to look fresh at the gym. I'm 62 and I need something that doesn't make my feet ache by noon.
- I'll scroll past the Dude sneakers and maybe click into the Brooks section, but I'm not forwarding this to anyone. My buddy Frank doesn't need to know Famous Footwear is selling skate shoes.

## What's working

- **Rewards Member Exclusive + 20% off leads the email** — that banner is right at the top, big enough to read without squinting, and I knew immediately this was a member perk, not a random sale.
- **Brooks is in here** — seeing a brand I actually wear, priced at $74.99, is exactly the kind of signal that slows my thumb down.
- **Clean layout, no busted images** — the shoe grid loaded, nothing overlapping, easy enough to scan on my phone.
- **Seasonal relevance** — it's summer, these are warm-weather shoes, no confusion there.
- **Familiar sender** — Famous Footwear has enough history with me that I gave it the benefit of the doubt even after that garbage preview text.

## What's weak

- **Preview text is a raw tracking URL** — this is the second time in two weeks they've done this to me. It looks like spam or a phishing attempt. Fixed it would cost them nothing; broken it costs them opens.
- **Dude shoes get a featured banner — I have no idea what Dude is and it doesn't sound like it's for me.** That big "UP TO 25% off DUDE" section is prime email real estate wasted on a brand I've never heard of and probably can't pronounce correctly.
- **"Shoes you'll love" is a nothing phrase** — I've been getting 20% off Rewards Member emails for weeks now. Same offer, different subject line wrapper. Nothing here tells me why *this* 20% off is different from the last three.
- **No comfort or fit cues anywhere in the visible grid** — no "cushioned," no "arch support," no "wide width available." For someone who shops on those words specifically, this reads like a generic catalog.
- **No deadline or urgency** — I've got no reason to click today versus Thursday. The June 8th send at least had "Final 72 hrs." This one just floats.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text immediately** — this is the highest-impact change, costs nothing, and they've already blown it twice this month. The preview should reinforce the 20% offer or hint at what's inside.
- 2. **Lead the hero grid with comfort brands** — put Brooks, New Balance, or Skechers first. Save the Dude section for a separate younger-demographic send. Segment exists for a reason.
- 3. **Add a deadline** — even "This weekend only" or "Ends Sunday" gives me a reason to act now instead of closing the tab and forgetting.
- 4. **Swap "shoes you'll love" for something concrete** — call out cushion, comfort, walking, or a specific brand in the subject. That's what slows me down.
- 5. **Surface one product detail** — even a small "wide widths available" or "memory foam insole" tag on one or two items would speak directly to me.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Extra 20% off comfort shoes — this weekend only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your top brands are on sale. Tap to see what's waiting for you.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Brooks at $74.99. New Balance on sale. Your 20% off inside.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Famous Footwear "Extra 20% Off" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound and renders correctly at a baseline level, but carries two active deliverability/rendering risks: HTTP-sourced dynamic content images and a plain-text version composed almost entirely of raw URLs. Accessibility failures are widespread across image assets.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**31 tracking links skipped by QA** — all routed through `click.email.famousfootwear.com` (ESP click-redirect). This is expected behavior; destinations could not be probed.

**Open pixel confirmed** — `https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/open.aspx?NM2HGPPDFSOEDOCXTO3CBLKK7U.70250&d=70250&bmt=0` — 1×1 `<img>`, standard.

**No issues found** with link structure itself; all `<a href>` values point to the click-redirect domain consistently.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] 2 mixed-content (HTTP) image sources** — these are SFMC LiveContent dynamic blocks loaded over plain HTTP:
- `http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=ABB7...` (×2)

Gmail, Outlook.com, and most modern clients block non-HTTPS image sources or flag the message with a security warning. These images will fail to render for a significant portion of recipients.

**[HIGH] 26 images missing alt text** — includes the logo (`3b6a7dc1-...png`, `alt=""`), the divider reused 4× (`4ccff4ad-...jpg`), all 8 igodigital product recommendation images (`990267198170`, indices 1–8), and all primary editorial images. Recipients with images blocked see blank space with no context.

**[MEDIUM] Zoom disabled via viewport meta** — `maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0` prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. This violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and degrades the experience for low-vision users.

**[LOW] Invalid inline CSS property** — `lineapacing:20px` at the nav/reward block (typo; no valid CSS property by that name). Style is silently dropped; line spacing intent is lost.

**[LOW] Malformed charset meta** — `<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` is missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Without it, some legacy Outlook and Lotus Notes clients may not apply the charset declaration. Correct form: `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />`.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens visible in the truncated source (no `%%TOKEN%%`, `[[TOKEN]]`, or `{{TOKEN}}` patterns). Product recommendation slots are populated via igodigital (`7222883.recs.igodigital.com/rr/v2/64595da29c77916e41c5a42c/i/{1–8}/990267198170`), indicating server-side rendering at send time — not client-side tokens. No issues found.

---

### 5. Compliance

**[MEDIUM] Plain-text version is 78% URLs** — QA reports 10,729 of 13,763 characters in the plain-text version are raw URLs. Spam filters (SpamAssassin, Google Postmaster) penalize plain-text versions that read as link dumps rather than human-readable content. This is a deliverability risk, not a CAN-SPAM violation per se.

**CAN-SPAM** — physical mailing address and unsubscribe mechanism are not visible in the truncated HTML, but the footer is cut off. Assuming they exist (standard practice for Famous Footwear); cannot confirm from available source.

**Authentication headers** — not inspectable from HTML source alone; DKIM/SPF/DMARC status requires header inspection from a received message. The sending domain `email.famousfootwear.com` is a standard ESP subdomain alignment pattern.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All click links use the ESP's opaque `qs=` parameter encoding (base64-encoded JSON containing `d`, `NM`, etc.). Standard UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are **not visible** in the pre-redirect `href` values — they may be appended by the ESP at redirect time, or they may be absent.

If UTM parameters are not being appended server-side during redirect, Google Analytics and site analytics will attribute all traffic from this email to `(direct)` rather than the campaign. This cannot be confirmed without following a redirect chain.

---

### 7. Recommendations

| Priority | Action |
|---|---|
| **HIGH** | Replace `http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx` sources with HTTPS equivalents. Contact SFMC/ExactTarget to confirm if LiveContent supports HTTPS serving; if not, serve the dynamic blocks from a HTTPS CDN. |
| **HIGH** | Add descriptive `alt` text to all 26 flagged images. Logo → `alt="Famous Footwear"`. Product images → product name + price. Dividers → `alt=""` (explicit decorative empty string is acceptable). |
| **MEDIUM** | Rewrite the plain-text version to contain human-readable prose (offer summary, key CTAs as plain text URLs). Reduces spam filter risk from the current 78% URL ratio. |
| **MEDIUM** | Verify that UTM parameters (`utm_source=email`, `utm_medium=promotional`, `utm_campaign=...`) are being appended at click-redirect time. Pull a sample GA session to confirm traffic is attributed to the email campaign, not `(direct)`. |
| **LOW** | Remove `user-scalable=0` and `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta, or replace with `maximum-scale=5` to re-enable zoom. |
| **LOW** | Fix `lineapacing:20px` → `letter-spacing:20px` or remove, depending on intent. |
| **LOW** | Add `http-equiv="Content-Type"` to the charset meta tag. |
