# See the summer's top styles
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-05**
## Executive summary

- "Best foot forward," and it's back-to-school season — well, I don't have a kid heading to campus and I sure ain't a teacher, so this one's DOA for me before I even get past the headline. Subject line said "top styles," which got me to open it, but the whole email is dressed up as a back-to-school sale, with a runner pack sprinting through a crosswalk like they're late for a flight. That's not my life. The Clifton 11 tile at least caught my eye for half a second, but it's buried under "Voted most popular" grid clutter, and the ID.me student/teacher discount pitch at the bottom seals it — this offer just isn't built for me.
- Meanwhile, this is the third HOKA email I've gotten this week that isn't actually a sale — it's a seasonal repackaging of the same shoes I've already seen (Clifton 11 again, after the July run and the "Meet the Cliftons" send yesterday). At some point I start tuning these out entirely.
- Would I click? No. Forward to a friend? No — unless that friend has a kid in high school. This gets a glance and a mark-as-read.

## What's working

- The Rincon 5 hero shot is clean and the gradient sole actually looks nice — good product photography.
- "Voted most popular" is a smart trust cue — social proof without me having to dig for reviews.
- Big, bold typography is easy to read on my phone without squinting.
- Men's and Women's are labeled right under each shoe, so I can tell fast which ones apply to me (Clifton 11, Transport 2).

## What's weak

- The entire framing is back-to-school — I've got no kids in school and no classroom to shop for, so the offer doesn't apply to me at all.
- The runner pack photo is young, urban, sprinting — nothing about it says "62-year-old guy who wants cushioned support for walking," it says marathon training.
- No price, no percentage off, no dollar amount anywhere — just "Shop Best Sellers" and "Shop Back to School." I've got nothing concrete to react to.
- Fourth Clifton 11 appearance in my inbox this month (June 16, July 22, and now this) — I've seen this shoe enough.
- The student/teacher ID.me discount at the bottom is the only real "savings" in the whole email, and it's explicitly not for me.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this one — send back-to-school messaging to people with school-age kids or teacher accounts, and give guys like me a "comfort for your daily walk" version instead. That single change would fix most of what's wrong here.
- 2. Put an actual price or percentage on the hero offer. "Shop Best Sellers" with no number gives me nothing to act on.
- 3. Swap the crosswalk running photo for a lifestyle shot that isn't tied to marathon training — someone walking the dog, running errands, standing comfortably all day.
- 4. Lead with the Clifton 11 or Transport 2 for men up top instead of burying them in a four-tile grid under a women's shoe I won't buy.
- 5. Stop resending the Clifton 11 every couple weeks — if there's nothing new to say about it, leave it out of the next send.
