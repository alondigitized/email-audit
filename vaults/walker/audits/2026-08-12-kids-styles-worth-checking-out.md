# Kids' styles worth checking out
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-12**
## Executive summary

- Well, at least this one's honest about it — "Kids' styles worth checking out," no pretending it's for me. And it's a hard pass right out of the gate. Every model in here is a kid, every shoe is a kid's shoe, and the whole layout is "shop by age" for toddlers through big kids. I don't have anyone in that bracket anymore. My kids are grown and out of the house, same complaint I had about the "Back to school bestsellers" email a couple weeks back — this brand really likes to lean on that back-to-school calendar regardless of who's actually reading.
- There's one line buried near the bottom — "A pair for them, and one for you" — that's the only nod to somebody my age, and it's a throwaway link, not an actual product for me to look at. Everything else, from the hero shot to the six shoe tiles, is kids' sneakers I have zero use for.
- I'm marking this read and moving on. Not clicking, not forwarding — unless I know somebody with grandkids starting school, in which case maybe I'd forward it to them instead of using it myself.

## What's working

- Clean layout, easy to scan on my phone — big photos, clear section headers like "Shop by age" and "Shop by style."
- The "Up to 30% off select styles" offer at the bottom is a real, specific number with a code (BTS26) and dates spelled out.
- No render bugs — images loaded, text lined up, nothing broken.
- "A pair for them, and one for you" at least acknowledges an adult might be reading this.

## What's weak

- Wrong audience entirely — I don't have school-age kids, and nothing here is a shoe I'd wear.
- No comfort, cushioning, or arch support language anywhere — not even in the "for you" teaser.
- Six shoe tiles, all kids' models, none of which apply to me.
- Sender treated me like a generic New Balance subscriber, not someone whose past opens/clicks would show I'm a 60-something guy, not a parent of little kids.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this list — if a subscriber's history shows no interest in kids' categories, don't send the kids' email at all, or at minimum swap the hero for the men's comfort angle.
- 2. Turn "A pair for them, and one for you" into an actual featured product for adults — a men's walking or comfort shoe — instead of a one-line afterthought link.
- 3. If you're going to target me anyway, lead with a men's-comfort subject, not kids' back-to-school.
- 4. Tone down the back-to-school cadence for non-parent segments — this is at least the second one in a few weeks hitting the same beat.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Comfort picks while the kids shop`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `A pair for the family, one for you too`
