# Extra 30% off clearance 👀
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- I opened this one out of habit more than excitement, and the minute I saw it, I thought — didn't I get this exact email yesterday? Because I did. Same offer, same "Extra 30% Off Clearance" headline, slightly different subject emoji. At this point Crocs has sent me roughly the same clearance deal four or five times in the past two weeks, and my tolerance for it is wearing thin.
- The email itself is clean and loads fast — I'll give them that. Big text, easy to read on my phone, the product image is sharp and clear. But what they're showing me is flip-flops. I'm a guy who just turned 62 with feet that need arch support, and the hero product is a flat flip sandal. That's not a shoe I'm going to wear to the farmer's market or on a walk around the neighborhood. Crocs makes closed-toe clogs with cushioning that I'd actually consider — but that's not what they're pushing here.
- I'd mark this as read and move on. I wouldn't click, wouldn't forward. If I were shopping Crocs clearance I'd go directly to the site because at least there I can filter for men's comfort styles — this email doesn't help me find what I actually want.

## What's working

- **Offer is front and center.** "Extra 30% Off Clearance" is the first thing I see in big bold text. No riddle to decode, no click required to find the deal. That I appreciate.
- **Clean mobile layout.** Large readable text, no clutter, renders without breaking. Easy on older eyes on a phone screen.
- **Crocs logo is immediately visible.** I know exactly who sent this without squinting at a sender address.
- **"Sale-on-Sale" framing.** The small "SALE ON SALE" label above the headline actually communicates that this is clearance-on-clearance, which means deeper discounts. That's worth noting.

## What's weak

- **Product mismatch.** The hero image is a flat flip-flop. For someone like me — older guy, needs cushioning and arch support — that's not the Crocs product I care about. The closed clog lineup is why I'm on their list.
- **Déjà vu fatigue.** I got an almost identical email yesterday ("Croc yeah—extra 30% off clearance"). The day before that was the same deal with a different subject line. This is the third nearly identical clearance email in three days. I'm starting to tune it out.
- **No visible CTA button.** I see the hero image and the headline, but there's no "Shop Clearance" button I can tap. The whole section presumably links somewhere, but a 62-year-old with modest digital confidence wants to see a clear button, not guess that the image is clickable.
- **"40% Off Accessories" section is irrelevant to me.** Jibbitz charms and laces? That's not why I'm in this email. It feels like padding for a younger shopper, not something I'd ever buy.
- **Preview text is blank.** The inbox showed nothing useful next to the subject — just invisible characters. Wasted space that could have told me "men's clearance styles under $40" or something that made me more likely to open.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show me a product I'd actually wear.** Swap the flip-flop hero for a men's Classic Clog or LiteRide — something with the cushioning story front and center. Crocs has the comfort angle, they're just not using it here.
- 2. **Add a "Shop Men's Clearance" button with real tap target.** Don't make me guess the image is clickable. A solid green or black button with clear text would double my confidence that I know where I'm going.
- 3. **Rest this offer.** Three nearly identical clearance emails in three days is too many. Rotate in a different message — maybe a comfort/fit story — before coming back to clearance.
- 4. **Give the deadline.** "Ends Sunday" or "Today Only" turns a shrug into a reason to act now. No deadline means I'll do it later, which means never.
- 5. **Fix the preheader.** Use those 80 characters to tell me something concrete: "Men's clogs and sandals on clearance — extra 30% off today" beats blank space every time.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Men's Crocs clearance — extra 30% off ends soon`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your size is in clearance: extra 30% off today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Clogs, sandals, and slides — men's clearance styles, extra 30% off`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack the savings: clearance prices just got 30% better`

**Missing alt on open-tracking pixel** — `<img ... src="https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?...">` has no `alt=""` attribute. Screen readers will attempt to announce the full URL string.

**Custom font** (`TT Crocs`, 3 weights via `@font-face`) — implementation is correct (WOFF2 primary, WOFF fallback, `font-display: swap`). Gmail and all Outlook versions will silently fall back to system fonts; no action required unless fallback rendering has not been QA'd.
