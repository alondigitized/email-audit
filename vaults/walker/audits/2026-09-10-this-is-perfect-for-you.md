- Well, this one's a head-scratcher right out of the gate. "Still thinking about it?" — about a kid's light-up Skechers Garage sneaker? No, I was not thinking about that, because I never looked at that. It's got wheels and lights and cartoon graphics, and I'm 62. Somebody's cart-abandonment robot grabbed the wrong shopper.
- After that swing and a miss, the "more options" grid is a total mixed bag — a couple of women's and men's slip-ins in there that I'd actually glance at, sandwiched between more kids' racer sneakers in pink and orange. It reads like they dumped the whole recently-viewed bucket into one email without checking who was doing the viewing.
- The one thing that stopped my thumb was the "MEMBERS TAKE AN EXTRA 20% OFF" banner — that's a real, concrete deal and I am a member. But it's buried two-thirds down under a wall of irrelevant kids' shoes. I'm not clicking through a mess like this, and I'm sure not forwarding it — unless it's to my daughter, as a "did you mean to send me your kid's wish list?" joke.

## What's working

- The 20% off members banner is a legit, specific, time-bounded offer (thru 9/14) — that's the kind of deal that gets my attention.
- The Men's Slip-ins Relaxed Fit and two Women's Arch Fit options in the grid are actually in my comfort-shoe lane.
- Clean, simple layout — big readable text, one column, easy to scroll on my phone without squinting.
- Skechers logo up top, so I know who it's from right away.

## What's weak

- The hero product is a kid's light-up "Skechers Garage" sneaker — nothing about that says "recently viewed by Walker."
- Half the "more options you'll love" grid is boys' and girls' novelty sneakers, not my size or my style.
- "Still thinking about it? You've got great taste!" flattery feels canned when the product itself is completely wrong for me.
- The real offer — 20% off — is buried below four rows of irrelevant shoes instead of leading with it.
- No arch support or slip-on comfort callout anywhere near the top, which is the only thing that'd get me to tap.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the targeting first — if I never browsed kids' shoes, don't build the whole email around one. That's the single biggest lift here.
- 2. Move the 20% off members banner up top, right under the header — that's the actual hook for someone like me.
- 3. Swap the "more options" grid to only show men's/unisex comfort styles when the hero product doesn't match the recipient's browsing history.
- 4. Lead with a comfort callout — "slip-on, arch support, all-day cushion" — not "great taste" flattery.
- 5. Drop the kids' Garage sneaker from this send entirely for adult subscribers with no kids' purchase history.
