**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-09**

- Well, this one's actually not bad, at least compared to the soccer cleats and K-pop nonsense they've been sending me all week. Three low-profile leather sneakers on a set of concrete steps, a real price point right in the headline — $80 and under — and no rotary phones or dragon faces in sight. That's progress for adidas.
- But "back to school fit for the season" tells me right away this isn't for me — I haven't bought school shoes in decades and I'm not chasing a "new look" for the fall semester. These are slim retro leather sneakers, not the cushioned, arch-supported, slip-on comfort I actually shop for. Nice enough looking shoes if I had a grandkid heading back to campus, but there's nothing here about comfort, cushioning, or all-day wear despite the little "shop sneakers with all-day comfort and style" line up top — which is the one thing that would've caught my eye, and then the email itself doesn't back it up at all.
- I wouldn't click, wouldn't forward it — I'd just close it out. Mildly relevant price-conscious offer, wrong shoe, wrong audience.

## What's working

- The price point is right there in the headline — $80 and under is concrete, and I do care about price-vs-quality math.
- Clean, uncluttered layout — one hero image, one headline, one CTA. Easy to read without zooming.
- No render bugs — image loaded fine, text is crisp, nothing overlapping.
- That teaser line about "all-day comfort" at the very top is the kind of hook that would've gotten me to open, even if the email itself doesn't deliver on it.

## What's weak

- "Back to school" framing is a hard miss for a 62-year-old — I'm not shopping for a "new look for the season" like a teenager.
- These are slim retro leather trainers, not the cushioned, hands-free, arch-support shoes I actually buy — nothing about comfort tech, insoles, or slip-on ease.
- No sizing, fit, or comfort details at all — just "grab the perfect pair," which tells me nothing useful.
- Big dead white space in the middle of the email with nothing in it — feels unfinished.
- No loyalty or member pricing shown even though I've got a Creators Club account with them.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this list by use case — show me a "comfort dad shoe" or slip-on option instead of lumping everyone into "back to school." A 62-year-old and a 16-year-old don't want the same shoe.
- 2. Actually deliver on the "all-day comfort" promise from the preheader — mention cushioning, arch support, or all-day wear in the body copy, not just the teaser line.
- 3. Fill that blank space in the middle with a second product row or a size/fit callout instead of leaving it empty.
- 4. Add a loyalty callout for Creators Club members if there's extra value there — I do notice when a brand treats me like a known customer.
