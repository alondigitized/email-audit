# WOOHOO—up to 25% off!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- My thumb was already moving toward this before my brain caught up — twelve-plus days of DSW training will do that. The 25% off number at the top registered instantly, same as the June 15 send and every one before it. But then the email loaded fully and I saw "SNEAKERS SIZZLE," a young woman in a teal windbreaker, and the words "OR GO FULL-ON Y2K" in giant type. I genuinely had to read that twice. Y2K. Like the year 2000. I was *forty* then.
- The clearance block halfway down — "UP TO 50% OFF, NEW SUMMER STYLES ADDED" — is the one spot where I slowed down and actually read. That's money. But getting there required scrolling past an energy-drink ad's worth of Gen Z copy that had nothing to do with me or my feet. The "SHOP BY SIZE" row is smart and usable. The rest feels like it was written for my son's college roommate.
- I'll mark this read. I won't click, I won't forward. If the clearance number had been in the subject I might have gone straight to it — but "WOOHOO" earned a half-second of attention at most.

## What's working

- **25% off is front and center** — brand row plus big headline, visible the second the email opens, no hunting required.
- **Crocs in the brand logo strip** — I actually noticed that. Crocs is on my short list; seeing the logo in the brand row told me the sale extends somewhere I care about.
- **Clearance section with a real number** — 50% off clearance is a stronger hook than the hero 25%, and adding "NEW SUMMER STYLES ADDED" made it feel timely rather than stale inventory.
- **SHOP BY SIZE row** — practical, easy to tap on a phone, respects my time. That's the kind of shortcut I actually use.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text. At least the thing loads right.

## What's weak

- **"WOOHOO" and "like whoa" and "Y2K"** — that's three pieces of language that belong on a college freshman's TikTok, not in my inbox. Every word of that hero copy pushed me further from the buy button.
- **Young woman model, fashion-forward sneakers** — the demographic signal is completely off. I'm 62 and shopping for something that won't wreck my arches; this looks like it's selling me on what to wear to a music festival.
- **No comfort or walking signals anywhere in the hero** — cushioning, support, slip-on, wide width — none of it. The word "comfort" doesn't appear once above the fold as far as I can tell.
- **Same offer, again** — I got "up to 25% off" on June 15. And June 14. And June 13. At this point the number isn't a deal, it's background noise.
- **Preview copy is trying too hard** — "Aaaaand...is like whoa" reads like a text from someone who wants me to know they're very online. It told me nothing about what's actually in the email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the hero with comfort or walking language, not sneaker trend copy.** "Your feet called. 25% off the brands that listen" beats "SNEAKERS SIZZLE" for someone my age every time.
- 2. **Move the 50% clearance callout up** — either into the subject or as a secondary banner below the main offer. That's the stronger number and it's buried.
- 3. **Swap the model or at least show the shoes without the lifestyle pose** — a clean product shot of a cushioned sneaker on a white background communicates more to me than a windbreaker editorial.
- 4. **Kill "WOOHOO" from the subject** — it reads like spam and it reads young. The offer is strong enough to stand alone.
- 5. **Give the preview something useful** — name the clearance percentage, name a brand, give me one concrete reason to open.
- **Subject Alt A:** `25% off New Balance, Hoka & more — summer sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 50% off clearance — new summer styles just added`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Clearance just got bigger — comfort brands, your sizes, real savings`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New Balance, Hoka, Crocs — up to half off on fresh summer stock`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Invalid CSS property casing — `Src:` in all `@font-face` blocks**

Every `@font-face` declaration uses a capital `S`:
```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');
```
The CSS spec requires lowercase `src:`. Strict CSS parsers (including some versions of Apple Mail and WebKit) will silently drop the declaration, causing fallback font rendering for all custom typefaces. This affects all 10 font declarations.

**Font format suboptimality**
- Futura PT: `.woff` only — no `.woff2`, no fallback for clients that only support TTF (older iOS Mail).
- HelveticaNow: `.ttf` only — larger file payload than `.woff`/`.woff2`; `.ttf` support in email clients is limited to Apple Mail and iOS Mail.

**`supported-color-schemes` in `:root` CSS block**
```css
:root {
  supported-color-schemes: light dark;  /* invalid — this is a meta value, not a CSS property */
}
```
`supported-color-schemes` is only valid as a `<meta>` tag value (already correctly declared in `<head>`). As a CSS property it is silently ignored. Harmless but incorrect.

**`-ms-text-size-adjust: none` on `*` selector**
Applied globally alongside `100%` on the same selector — redundant but not harmful.

**`#000001` near-black**
Used in `.border-top-black` for mobile. This is a deliberate dark-mode-prevention technique (prevents some clients from treating the value as "true black" and inverting it). Intentional — no fix needed.
