# [Happening now] Get $10 off
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- I opened this because DSW dropped "$10 off" and "happening now" in the subject — same deal they've been running all week, but the urgency hook still pulls me in. Two seconds into the body and I knew I'd been had. The whole email is about embellished women's sandals. Strappy, gold-hardware, jewelry-on-the-strap sandals. The hero model is a young woman in a red dress. The two buttons say "SHOP ALL SANDALS" and "SHOP ALL WOMEN'S." That's it for above the fold.
- I had to scroll down past all of that to find anything that might apply to me: a Father's Day gift card section and a "Shop Wide Width" button buried near the bottom. Neither of those are why I opened the email. The $10 off from the subject never showed up as a headline in the body — so the message I got walking in was "we lured you here with a discount and now we're showing you women's shoes."
- I won't click anything here. I won't forward it. I'll mark it read and move on, mildly annoyed that DSW has now sent me the third or fourth variation of the "$10 off" pitch since Tuesday morning, and this one doesn't even put the offer front and center.

## What's working

- **DSW is a brand I know** — I've got the VIP card, and "DSW" in the sender field gets an automatic open from me.
- **"Shop Wide Width" button exists** — that one button at the bottom shows they know some of their customers have fit needs. That's the right instinct, wrong placement.
- **Father's Day gift card block** — at least they acknowledged that men might receive something from this email, even if it's just pointing us at gift cards.
- **Email renders cleanly** — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds together on mobile.

## What's weak

- **Hero is entirely women's content** — "WE EMBELLISHED A LITTLE" with a young woman in strappy jeweled sandals is zero percent aimed at a 62-year-old man looking for cushioned walking shoes.
- **The $10 off from the subject disappears in the body** — subject says "get $10 off," body opens with fashion copy about hardware embellishments. That's a bait-and-switch feeling I don't like.
- **CTAs say "Shop All Women's"** — putting that in the button copy tells me I was an accidental recipient, not an intended one.
- **Preview text is polluted** — "+ Inside: when more is more" is already vague, and then it's followed by a wall of invisible spacer characters that my inbox preview shows as blank space. It tells me nothing useful.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is roughly the third "$10 off" email this week from DSW. The one on June 3rd ICYMI was the same deal. I'm starting to tune them out.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment men out of this send** — if my account history shows I buy men's comfort shoes, this embellished-sandal email should never land in my inbox. That's the number-one fix.
- 2. **Put the $10 off in the hero copy** — if the subject promises $10 off, the first thing I see when I open should be "$10 OFF TODAY" not fashion editorial language.
- 3. **Move "Shop Wide Width" above the fold** — that button belongs in the top third of the email for comfort-focused shoppers, not buried below gift cards.
- 4. **Give the $10 off a minimum purchase context** — I want to know if it's $10 off $50 or $10 off anything. The vagueness makes me distrust the offer.
- 5. **Retire "happening now" for a few sends** — it's lost meaning after back-to-back urgency emails this week.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`http-equiv` attributes missing on two meta tags:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />           <!-- missing http-equiv="Content-Type" -->
<meta content="IE=Edge" />                             <!-- missing http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" -->
```
Without `http-equiv`, these directives are no-ops in Outlook/IE rendering engines.

**Capitalized `Src:` in all `@font-face` blocks:**
```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/.../original.woff") format('woff');
```
CSS property names are case-insensitive in browsers but some email client CSS parsers (notably older Samsung Mail) will reject capitalized property names, causing all 10 custom font declarations to silently fail.

**`-ms-text-size-adjust` declared twice with conflicting values:**
```css
* { -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%; ... -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
The second declaration wins (`none`), disabling text scaling on Windows Phone/IE. The `100%` value is immediately overwritten — the first declaration is dead code.

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents user-initiated zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). iOS 10+ ignores this, but Android WebView and some email clients enforce it.

**Font format coverage gap:**
- Futura PT: only `.woff` loaded — no `woff2` fallback.
- HelveticaNow: only `.ttf` loaded — no `woff` or `woff2`.
Clients that prefer `woff2` (most modern) will fall back to system fonts silently.
