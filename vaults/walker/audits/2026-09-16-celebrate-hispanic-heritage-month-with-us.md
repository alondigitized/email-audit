## Executive summary

- Well, this one's different, I'll give them that — no "60% off clearance" banner screaming at me for once. It's a Hispanic Heritage Month piece about the DSW Foundation donating to the Milagro Foundation, and there's a genuinely interesting nugget in there — Carlos Santana started that foundation, and DSW sells a whole line of his shoes. I didn't know that, and if I'm honest, a loafer or two in that lineup looked sharp enough that I scrolled twice.
- But here's my problem: there's no offer. No percent off, no dollar amount, nothing I can act on today. It's a feel-good brand story with a "Carlos by Carlos Santana Shoes" product plug bolted on, and then right back into the same size grid — 5 through 11 in half sizes — that I've flagged on every single DSW email this week. That's women's sizing again, in an email that otherwise has nothing to do with gender at all.
- I wouldn't click, and I definitely wouldn't forward it — not because it's offensive, just because there's nothing here that moves me to act. I'd read the Santana bit out of curiosity, maybe respect the brand a little more for it, then mark it read and move on. No harm, no real gain either.

## What's working

- The Santana/Milagro Foundation story is genuinely interesting — it's the first thing all week that made me pause instead of scroll past.
- No fake urgency, no ALL CAPS, no "FINAL HOURS" nonsense — a nice break from the carousel of clearance banners.
- The Carlos Santana shoe collection has some sharp-looking men's dress/casual pieces — loafers and a chukka boot — that are at least in my wheelhouse style-wise.
- Clean layout, one story at a time, easy to follow without much zooming.

## What's weak

- Zero offer, zero percent-off, zero reason to act today — this reads like a press release, not a marketing email.
- The size grid is 5 through 11 in halves again — same women's sizing block I've called out on four straight DSW sends this week. Nobody proofread that against a men's-focused product feature.
- Nothing about comfort, cushioning, or arch support anywhere — those Santana shoes look stylish but I have no idea if my feet would survive a full day in them.
- "LEARN MORE" and "FIND YOUR STORE" are both soft CTAs — neither one is asking me to buy anything specific.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pair the story with an actual incentive — even "15% of Carlos Santana shoe sales support Milagro this month" gives me a reason to buy, not just read.
- 2. Fix the size grid — pull the men's range (7-13, some half sizes) since the featured product is a men's shoe line.
- 3. Add one line of comfort/fit info on the Santana shoes — cushioned insole, break-in time, true-to-size — that's what actually gets a guy like me to click "shop."
- 4. Swap "LEARN MORE" for something with a verb tied to the product, like "Shop the Collection."
