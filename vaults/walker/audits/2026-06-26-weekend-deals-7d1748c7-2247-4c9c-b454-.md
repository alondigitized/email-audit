# Weekend 🤝 Deals
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- Another Famous Footwear weekend blast, and once again that preview text in my inbox is a raw tracking URL. I didn't even need to check — I knew before I opened it. At this point that broken preheader is so predictable it's almost become their brand signature, and not in a good way.
- Once I got inside, it's a classic grab-bag: multiple brands, multiple percentage-off banners, stacked one after the other. Nike at 30% off, adidas at 25%, Brooks priced out at $109.99, then a big 50% off women's sandals section sitting right in the middle like it accidentally landed in my inbox. That women's sandal block is a recurring theme with Famous Footwear — they've done this to me before, most recently on the June 24 Naturalizer send. I'm a 62-year-old guy shopping for walking shoes, not kitten-heel slides.
- The Brooks section is the one thing that caught my attention. Brooks makes a serious walking shoe and seeing a specific price point — $109.99 — gives me something concrete to act on. But it's buried below the women's sandals section, which means I had to scroll past content that had nothing to do with me before I found what I'd actually buy. I'd mark this as read and maybe circle back to the Brooks link, but I wouldn't forward it to a friend.

## What's working

- **Multiple concrete discounts are immediately visible** — 20% off, 30% off Nike, 25% off adidas, and a specific dollar price on Brooks. No vague "up to" language on the headline offers.
- **Brooks at a hard price point ($109.99) is exactly the kind of signal I respond to** — a real comfort brand with a real number.
- **No render bugs I could see** — layout held together cleanly across all sections, images loaded, no overlapping text.
- **"Weekend" urgency is implicit and credible** — doesn't feel like a manufactured countdown, just a natural reason to browse now.
- **Famous Footwear is a brand I recognize and trust**, so I opened without hesitation despite the ugly preheader.

## What's weak

- **The raw tracking URL preheader is back. Again.** I flagged this on the June 8 send, the June 21 Samba email, and every week since. It looks like spam and it's embarrassing for a brand this size.
- **The 50% off women's sandals block is prominent and aimed at someone who isn't me** — scrolling past a section of women's slides to find men's comfort shoes is friction I shouldn't have to deal with.
- **"Weekend Deals" is the laziest possible subject line** — no specificity, no hook, no reason for me to prioritize it over the other four Famous Footwear emails I've gotten this week.
- **The Jordan section ($99.99) signals a younger buyer** — retro sneakers and athletic silhouettes aimed at a 25-year-old don't move the needle for me.
- **No arch support, cushioning, or comfort language anywhere in what I can see** — that's my purchase trigger and it's completely absent.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader. Full stop.** This is the highest-impact change and it costs nothing to fix. A broken tracking URL as preview text is the first thing I see in my inbox and it reads as spam. Replace it with actual copy tied to the strongest offer.
- 2. **Lead with the Brooks/comfort section for male subscribers** — gender and category segmentation would let me see shoes I'd actually buy before I hit the women's sandals block.
- 3. **Put comfort language in the subject** — "arch support," "wide fit," or "walking shoes" would make me open this faster than a generic "Deals" flag.
- 4. **Condense the multi-brand grid** — five different percentage-off banners create visual noise. Pick two, go bigger on each.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Brooks + Nike: Weekend prices on walking shoes`
- **Subject Alt B:** `30% off Nike, Brooks from $109 — this weekend only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Brooks, adidas, Nike — weekend markdowns on comfort shoes worth clicking.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Up to 30% off + Brooks from $109. Real weekend deals, no gimmicks.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound with no broken links or fatal rendering failures, but carries two systemic issues: a pervasive missing-alt-text problem across 30+ images (full accessibility failure in image-off environments) and a plain-text body that is 78% URLs (deliverability spam-signal risk).

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **31 click-redirect links** routed through `click.email.famousfootwear.com` — all wrapped in the ESP's click-tracking redirect. HTTP probing was skipped on all 31; destination URL validity is unverified by QA.
- **Open pixel** present: `https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/open.aspx?GOI27EXAJL7UDLBWLMZMLCKMVA.70255` — standard, no issue.
- **Recommendation image domain** (`7222883.recs.igodigital.com`) hosts dynamic product recommendation images, consistent with a Salesforce Marketing Cloud / iGo Digital integration — no issue with the pattern, but alt text is missing on both rendered recommendation slots (see section 3).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — 30 images missing alt text.** Every content image in the email has `alt=""`. This covers the logo, hero banner, all product images, divider rules, and both personalized recommendation images. In image-blocked environments (Outlook default, many corporate clients) the email renders as a blank sequence of broken image placeholders with no text fallback.

Affected images include:
- Logo: `3b6a7dc1-6955-49f8-868a-bf62a15122ee.png`
- Hero/divider: `4ccff4ad-f402-43de-a027-5eb147b6f238.jpg` (appears 4×)
- All product tiles: `ac216ca0`, `9530cc1f`, `7f2e3d9d`, `22adaed9`, `aad4a2f7`, `e2ea3d8c`, and 15+ more
- Recommendation slots: `recs.igodigital.com/rr/v2/.../i/1/990267198170`, `.../i/2/990267198170`

Decorative spacers/rules may legitimately carry `alt=""`, but the logo and all product/CTA images must have descriptive alt text.

**Doctype:** `HTML 4.01 Transitional` — legacy but standard practice for email; not a defect.

**Viewport:** `user-scalable=0` suppresses pinch-zoom on mobile — this is a minor accessibility regression but common in ESP templates.

**CSS typo:** `lineapacing:20px` (line 1 of inline styles on the loyalty-reward text cell) — should be `line-height`. This property is silently ignored by all clients.
