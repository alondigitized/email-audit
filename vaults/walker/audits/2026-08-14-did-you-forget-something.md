# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-14**
## Executive summary

- Third day in a row now — that pink floral D'Lites - Spring Bloom sneaker is right back at the top of my inbox, same "complete your order" pitch I saw on the 11th, 12th, and now the 14th. I never put that shoe in a cart. I've never even clicked on it. At this point it's not marketing, it's a broken record, and it's telling me their targeting has no idea who I actually am.
- Below the fold there's an actual mix — some men's slip-ins, the Delson 3.0, a 40% off summer sale banner — but I have to scroll past a women's floral shoe and a boy's Garage Sneaker to get there. If I'm skimming on my phone during coffee, I'm not getting that far.
- Not clicking, not forwarding. Marking as read and moving on, same as the last two times this exact stunt showed up.

## What's working

- The Men's Delson 3.0 - Roth Shoes tile is actually in my lane — slip-in comfort shoe, decent size in the grid.
- 40% off summer styles banner is bold, easy to read, clear number.
- "Shop Now, Pay Later" and "Find a Store" footer options are fine, low-friction extras even if I won't use them.

## What's weak

- That pink floral D'Lites sneaker is the hero image again — third straight day of the same shoe I've never touched, still telling me to "complete my order."
- Grid below it is mostly women's and kids' shoes (Boy's Garage Sneaker twice, BOBS Skip Cute) before I hit anything men's.
- "Ready to Check Out?" headline assumes I abandoned a cart — I didn't, so the whole premise feels off from the jump.
- No cushioning, arch support, or comfort-tech language anywhere — just shoe names and prices as marked.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the retargeting logic — stop hero-featuring a product I never viewed or carted. If there's no real cart data on me, don't fake urgency with "complete your order."
- 2. Lead the grid with men's product, not women's/kids', if you're going to keep blasting this list to me.
- 3. Add one line of comfort language near the men's shoes — arch support, cushioned insole, slip-on ease — that's what actually gets me to tap.
- 4. Cut the repeat cadence — three near-identical "you forgot something" emails in four days reads as desperate, not helpful.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Men's slip-ins, up to 40% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your size is still in stock — 40% off summer`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Delson 3.0 and more comfort slip-ins, no cart required`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `40% off summer styles, online only through 8/19`
