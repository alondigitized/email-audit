# Happening now: BOGO 50% off!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-06**
## Executive summary

- DSW again, and this is the fourth BOGO-flavored email I've had since "BOGO 50% off starts…NOW!" this morning, followed by "Gifting season starts… NOW!" and "ICYMI: BOGO 50% off shoes." The orange banner at the top is clear enough: "Long weekend, big deal, BOGO 50% off." A second pair at half price is a deal I understand. But I've now seen it three times today, and "Happening now" is a funny thing to call something I was already told about.
- Below the banner it turns into a fashion magazine. A "Your Fall Cheat Sheet" section pushes tall boots, animal print, and "reimagined loafers." The models are young and stylish, and none of it is a man looking for a cushioned slip-on. The only men's link I could find is the small "Shop Men" under the banner. I'd glance at it and move on, and I wouldn't forward it to anyone.
- Verdict: I'd open it out of habit, tap "Shop Men" if I wanted shoes, and otherwise mark it read. Nothing in it speaks to comfort or arch support.

## What's working

- The big orange "BOGO 50% OFF*" banner is easy to read, and the offer is plain: buy one pair, get half off a second pair.
- "Shop Women / Shop Men / Shop Kids" sits right under the offer, so I can get to the men's section quickly.
- The "Shop by Size" block has large numbers and "Shop Wide Width," which is useful for my feet.
- "Free shipping for VIPs," "in-store pickup," and the app download are laid out clearly near the bottom.
- The green buttons are big enough to tap with my thumb.

## What's weak

- Almost the whole email is women's fashion: tall boots, animal print, and loafers on a woman in a skirt. That's not me.
- Nothing mentions comfort, cushioning, arch support, or slip-ons. The retro sneakers photo is the closest thing to my shoe.
- It repeats the BOGO offer I got this morning, and the "Happening now" subject adds no urgency to a deal I'd already seen.
- The fine print and BOGO exclusions run on for pages in tiny gray type on a dark background. I can't read it without zooming, and the asterisk worries me.
- The "Your Fall Cheat Sheet" heading looks cut off. The orange letters run into the banner behind them and are hard to read.
- The VIP Shoebox Shuffle game and the Montana trip are filler. I don't want to play a game for points.

## Recommendations

- 1. Send me a men's version. Put a men's comfort shoe in the hero, and lead with walking and slip-on styles.
- 2. Say once in the subject line when the BOGO ends, and don't send three reminders in one day.
- 3. Move "Shop Wide Width" and the men's sizes higher, and cut the fashion trend section for men.
- 4. Make the fine print bigger and lighter, with the key exclusions summarized in one line near the offer.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Buy one, get one half off: comfort sneakers & slip-ons`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cushioned, easy-on styles for him. Wide widths in stock.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible head markup is mostly sound: it has a dark-mode meta tag, `color-scheme` support and a standard XHTML doctype. The confirmed issues are plain-text bloat (81% URLs), three images with no `alt`, a viewport tag that blocks zoom, and no `lang` attribute. The HTML was truncated and the QA run only probed 12 of 69 links, so the body, footer and headers couldn't be checked.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Plain-text part is URL-heavy.** The QA check warns that 21,809 of 26,817 characters (~81%) are URLs. This is typically wrapped `ablink.dsw.com/q/...` tracking links. It is a spam-filter risk and makes the text version hard to read.
- **Link coverage is partial.** The QA run probed 12 material links and skipped 57. No failures were reported for the probed set, so broken links are not confirmed. The skipped footer and social links are unverified.
- **An image `src` goes through the click-tracking domain.** The image `src` is `https://ablink.dsw.com/q/ir75Wjo7koRIWYbTHLYTug~~/AAQRxRA~/uxyIO53y...`. Image sources normally point at a CDN, not a redirect/tracker host. This adds a redirect hop and could fail in clients that don't follow redirects for images. It could also be the open-tracking pixel, which I can't tell from the excerpt. Confirm what it serves.
- **Pixel naming may be incomplete.** The open pixel is `https://mi.dsw.com/p/up/e6f23fb0.../o.gif?mi_u=225090073&mi_ecmp=20261006_P261400_V3_W-_Offer_Trend`. The `W-_Offer` segment of the campaign ID looks like it may have an empty field. This is an inference from the string, not confirmed.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text, 3 images (QA-confirmed):**
  - the `ablink.dsw.com/q/ir75Wjo7...` image
  - `mi.dsw.com/.../o.gif`
  - `braze-images.com/.../6ab5af451fe339008a438e25/original.jpg`
  
  The tracking pixel needs `alt=""` plus `width="1" height="1"`. The other two need descriptive alt text, or `alt=""` if they are decorative. If either is a content image, its content is lost when images are blocked.
- **Zoom is disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` blocks pinch-zoom, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 and 1.4.10-style resize needs.
- **No document language.** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` or `xml:lang`. Screen readers fall back to the default language.
- **Both meta tags are invalid.** `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` and `<meta content="IE=Edge" />` have no `http-equiv`, so they do nothing. The charset declaration is ineffective, which matters if the Content-Type header doesn't set the charset.
- **Web fonts are limited.** `@font-face` loads Futura PT and Helvetica Now from `braze-images.com` (`.woff`, `.ttf`). Gmail and several Outlook builds ignore custom fonts. I couldn't see the fallback stacks in the truncated source.
- **Minor CSS.** `-ms-text-size-adjust` is declared twice, as `100%` and then `none`, so the second wins. It is harmless but conflicting.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found in the visible source. I saw no unresolved Liquid or Handlebars tokens (`{{ }}`, `{% %}`), but the body was truncated, so the greeting, CTAs and footer weren't checked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Unverified, not cleared.** The unsubscribe link, physical postal address and `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are not in the provided data. The QA note says an unsubscribe link was always included in the probe set, but no result was reported for it.
- **Authentication unverified.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC results were not provided. The `From` is `dsw@e.dsw.com` and the tracking hosts are `ablink.dsw.com` and `mi.dsw.com`, all on dsw.com, so alignment is plausible. That is an inference, not a confirmed pass.
- The image warning above matters here too. A tracking pixel without `alt` or dimensions is a small risk, not a compliance failure.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Visible links are wrapped in `ablink.dsw.com/q/...` redirects, so the final destination URLs and any UTM parameters can't be seen in the excerpt. The only campaign identifier visible is `mi_ecmp=20261006_P261400_V3_W-_Offer_Trend` on the pixel.
- Landing-page alignment with "BOGO 50% off" is unverified, because the redirect targets weren't resolved in the data provided.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add `alt=""` (with `width="1" height="1"`) to the `o.gif` pixel, and meaningful alt text to the other two flagged images.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Add `lang="en"` and `xml:lang="en"` to `<html>`.
4. Fix the meta tags: `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">` and `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge">`.
5. Check why an image `src` uses the `ablink.dsw.com/q/` click-tracking redirect. Serve it from the image CDN if it is a content image.
6. Slim down the plain-text version: shorter or unwrapped URLs, and fewer links.
7. Re-run the audit on the full HTML and raw headers (unsubscribe, List-Unsubscribe, Authentication-Results). Resolve the redirect chains to confirm final URLs, UTMs and the 57 skipped links.
