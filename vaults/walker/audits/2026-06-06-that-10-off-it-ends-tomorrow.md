# That $10 off? It ends tomorrow.
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- Nine DSW emails in four days. At this point opening one of these is basically a reflex — my thumb moves before my brain does. But I'll give them this: "That $10 off? It ends tomorrow" is the best subject line they've sent all week. No screaming caps, no stacked exclamation points. Conversational. Almost sounds like a friend reminding me. I tapped it.
- Then I got inside and my stomach dropped a little. The headline says "BRAND NEW...AND SO YOU" — fine — but every shoe on the screen is a heel, a wedge, a mule, or a pump. There's maybe one or two darker flats near the bottom of the grid that could pass as unisex, but the whole email is clearly aimed at women. The sizes start at 5. There's a "SHOP WIDE CALF" button. I don't have calves to shop for. The $10 off urgency from the subject? Buried in the fine print at the very bottom next to a tiny coupon barcode. I had to squint.
- Mark as read. I'm not forwarding this, I'm not clicking, and I'm starting to wonder if DSW has my account mixed up with someone else's.

## What's working

- **Subject line is genuinely strong** — "That $10 off? It ends tomorrow." is calm, direct, and creates a real deadline without sounding desperate. Best copy they've sent in this whole run.
- **DSW brand is instantly recognizable** — logo at the top, clean nav bar, I know where I am immediately.
- **VIP section at the bottom** is a nice reminder that I could be getting free shipping; it doesn't feel pushy.
- **Layout is clean** — no broken images, no overlapping text, nothing weird going on technically.

## What's weak

- **Every product in the hero grid is women's shoes** — heels, mules, pumps, wedges. There is nothing here for a 62-year-old man looking for a cushioned walking shoe.
- **The $10 off urgency is bait-and-switch** — the subject made me think I'd land on a clear offer. Instead I got a new arrivals showcase with the discount buried in fine print at the bottom with a barcode.
- **"SHOP WIDE CALF" is not a button for me** — that is a signal DSW sent the wrong segment this email entirely.
- **Sizes starting at 5 confirm this is women's content** — men's sizing starts around 7–8; this tells me the moment I look at the size selector.
- **This is the ninth email in four days** — even when the subject is good, the cadence is exhausting and erodes trust fast.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface the $10 off in the hero, not just the subject.** A banner or a bold offer block right below the header — "Your $10 off expires tomorrow. Shop before midnight." — would keep the promise the subject made and give me a reason to scroll.
- 2. **Segment men's content to men.** I have no idea why DSW keeps sending me women's new arrivals. Show me men's comfort walkers, wide-width options, slip-on casuals. That's the email I'd forward to a buddy.
- 3. **Replace "BRAND NEW...AND SO YOU" with something that earns the "you."** If the "you" in that headline is a 62-year-old guy looking for arch support, the photo grid needs to back it up.
- 4. **Cut the email cadence.** Nine in four days is too many. Even when I open, I'm increasingly suspicious of what I'll find inside.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Four confirmed issues:**

**a) Malformed charset `<meta>` tag**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Missing the required `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute. Without it, the charset declaration is silently ignored by legacy email clients and some webmail parsers. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```

**b) Conflicting `-ms-text-size-adjust` values in the `*` selector**
```css
*{
  -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%;   /* set first */
  ...
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;   /* overrides — effective value */
  text-size-adjust: none
}
```
The effective value is `none`, which disables automatic text-size adjustment in IE and legacy Outlook. This can cause unreadable small text on high-DPI Windows devices. The `100%` declaration is dead code.

**c) Uppercase `Src:` in all `@font-face` blocks**
All ten `@font-face` declarations use `Src:` (capital S) instead of the standard `src:`:
```css
@font-face {
  font-family: 'Futura_PT_Regular';
  ...
  Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');
}
```
CSS property names are technically case-insensitive per spec, but strict email client CSS parsers (notably Gmail's inliner and some Outlook versions) may reject non-lowercase property declarations, causing the font rules to be dropped silently.

**d) `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and violates Apple App Store accessibility guidelines for content rendered in WKWebView (iOS Mail). Remove `maximum-scale=1` or replace with `maximum-scale=5`.
