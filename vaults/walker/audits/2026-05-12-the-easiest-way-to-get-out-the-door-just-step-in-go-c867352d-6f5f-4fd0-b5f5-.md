# The Easiest Way to Get Out the Door, Just Step In & Go
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-12**
## Executive summary

- Alright, I'll say this — when I opened it, the big bold "Hands-Free Hassle-Free" stopped me cold. That's my language. That's exactly what I'm after when I'm grabbing shoes to head out for a walk or run an errand. Skechers knows their audience here, at least in the body of the email.
- But before I even got there, my inbox already showed me something weird next to the subject line. Instead of a teaser like "try our most comfortable slip-in yet," I see what looks like a wall of computer code — some kind of bracket-and-quotation-mark soup. On my phone, that stuff takes up my entire preview pane. I almost didn't open it because it looked like a broken message or a scam.
- Once inside, the email actually delivers. The offer is visible up top, the slip-in shoes look genuinely comfortable, and there's a Men's section so I know they're at least aware I exist. But that preview text failure almost cost them the open, and there's nothing telling me this deal expires — so I'd bookmark it and probably forget about it. I'd click through on the slip-ins, but I wouldn't forward this to a friend with that garbled preview still attached.

## What's working

- **"Hands-Free Hassle-Free" hero copy** lands exactly on what I care about — no bending, no fumbling, just step in and go.
- **Dual CTAs on slip-ins** — "Shop New Slip-Ins" and "Shop Best-Selling Slip-Ins" give me two entry points, and that category distinction (new vs. proven) actually maps to how I think about buying.
- **20% off a single pair is up top and visible immediately** — I don't have to scroll to find the deal, which matters on a phone screen.
- **Skechers+ section is present** — reminds me there's a free loyalty program, and joining looks painless.
- **Clean, uncluttered layout** — no broken images, nothing overlapping, the shoe photography is clear and the light colorway looks lightweight and comfortable.

## What's weak

- **That preview text is a disaster.** I'm looking at raw code in my inbox. That's the first thing I see. My gut reaction was "this looks like a phishing email" and I nearly deleted it unread.
- **Dual offer creates decision paralysis.** Is it 20% off one pair, or is it the BOGO 50%? The smarter deal depends on how many pairs I'm buying, and the email doesn't help me figure that out.
- **No deadline anywhere.** "20% off" with no expiration means I'll say "I'll come back to this" — and I never will. I need a little push.
- **The Men's section button is buried** behind a Women's/Men's split block that's deeper in the scroll. For a guy like me who's getting this email, Men's should come first or be louder in the hierarchy.
- **No sizing or width callout.** I run wide and I care about arch support. Nothing in here tells me these shoes come in my size or that there's extra cushioning on a specific model.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader — immediately, before anything else.** That JSON leak is killing open rates. Replace it with something like: *"Slip on, step out — no bending required."*
- 2. **Pick one offer and lead with it.** The 20% off single pair is the cleaner, easier sell for someone buying one pair. Save the BOGO message for a separate send or a secondary block.
- 3. **Add a hard deadline.** Even "Offer ends Sunday" would do it. That's the nudge I need to not close the tab.
- 4. **Flip the Women's/Men's order or make a Men's-only version.** If they know I'm a male subscriber, Men's comes first. Period.
- 5. **Drop one line of product detail under the hero shoe.** Something like "Available in wide widths • Extra cushioned insole" would stop me mid-scroll and make me feel like this shoe was picked for me.

### 1. Technical Summary

One confirmed rendering break (empty first-name merge tag) and a systemic HTTP-image problem affecting 12+ assets. All other infrastructure is standard but carries known deliverability and blocking risks detailed below.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**27 click-redirect links** are wrapped through `click.emails.skechers.com` — standard ESP redirect, not individually probeable but structurally sound.

| `1x1_usermatch.gif?partner=LprH63a7_sfmc_100007688_sha25&partner_uid=9153f33...` | `beacon.krxd.net` | Salesforce DMP SHA-256 user sync |
| `1x1_usermatch.gif?partner=LprH63a7_sfmc_100007688_subid&partner_uid=121173391` | `beacon.krxd.net` | Subscriber ID sync |

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — HTTP image sources (12 assets):**

All product/content images and every footer icon (social, App Store, Google Play, AfterPay, Curbside Pickup, Find A Store) are served over `http://image.emails.skechers.com`. Gmail, Apple Mail, and most modern clients block or proxy HTTP images, which means:
- Social and app-store icons in the footer will silently fail to load on clients that enforce HTTPS
- The `ink1000.com` open-tracking pixel (`http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif`) will be blocked the same way, undercounting opens

**Malformed `<meta>` tags:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />          <!-- missing http-equiv="Content-Type" -->
<meta content="IE=Edge" />                            <!-- missing http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" -->
```
Both tags are missing the required `http-equiv` attribute. Without it, charset and IE compatibility hints are ignored.

**Duplicate media query blocks:** The CSS contains two identical rule sets for `.show`, `.mobile-hidden`, and related classes — one at `max-width: 375px` and one at `max-width: 640px`, then a third block in a separate `<style>` tag at `max-width: 480px`. This is redundant but not breaking.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**FAIL — Empty first-name merge token (confirmed by QA):**

The automated check caught a greeting rendering as `"Hi ,"` — the subscriber first-name field resolved to blank. This is a broken experience for any subscriber whose profile lacks a first name and no fallback default (e.g., `"Hi there,"`) was configured in the ESP template.

No other merge token failures were detected in the visible HTML excerpt.

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM / unsubscribe:** The HTML is truncated before the footer, so the unsubscribe link and physical mailing address cannot be confirmed present. These must be verified in the full source before send.

**Sender domain alignment:** From header is `no-reply@emails.skechers.com`. For DMARC alignment, SPF and DKIM must both authenticate against `emails.skechers.com` or pass relaxed alignment to `skechers.com`. Email authentication headers (DKIM-Signature, SPF result, DMARC result) are not included in the provided source — cannot confirm pass/fail from available data.

**Third-party DMP pixel disclosure:** The Krux/Salesforce DMP user-match pixel (`beacon.krxd.net`) syncs a SHA-256 hashed subscriber identifier to a third-party ad platform. Depending on applicable privacy regulations (CCPA, GDPR for any EU recipients), this behavioral/ad-targeting pixel may require explicit disclosure or opt-out support beyond standard unsubscribe. Verify this is covered by Skechers' existing consent framework.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The campaign identifier `MKG_US_NONPURCLICK_U_SLIPIN_NONPURCH_EN_05122026` appears in two tracking pixels (krxd.net impression and ink1000.com), confirming campaign tagging exists at the pixel layer.

Click-through URLs are wrapped in `click.emails.skechers.com` redirects — UTM parameter presence on destination URLs cannot be confirmed from the provided source. This must be spot-checked by following one redirect to verify `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` are appended to landing page URLs.
