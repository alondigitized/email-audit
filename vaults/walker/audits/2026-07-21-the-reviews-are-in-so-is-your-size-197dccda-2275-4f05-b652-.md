# The Reviews Are In & So Is Your Size
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- "Most Talked About" — okay, Skechers, I'm listening. After yesterday's "Final Hours" sale push, I half expected another discount bomb. Instead they went the social-proof route: real customer quotes, star ratings on each shoe, and an Arch Fit product front and center. For a guy like me who reads reviews the way some people read the sports page, that framing works. I opened it, I scrolled it, I read the quotes.
- But here's my beef: the subject line promises "So Is Your Size" — and then the email delivers exactly zero follow-through on that. No size availability. No "your size is in stock." Nothing. That's a bait the email doesn't bother catching, and it's the kind of thing that chips away at trust with me. I'm not a guy who forgets when a headline doesn't match the body.
- And then there's the BOBS B Cute 2.0. Right there in the "Most Talked About" roundup — a shoe that is clearly a women's slip-on. Three products total and one of them isn't for me? That's a miss. The Arch Fit Arcata and the Cozy Fit slip-on have my attention, but I'm not clicking through a women's shoe to get there. Mark it read, move on.

## What's working

- Arch Fit® front and center — that's my category and Skechers knows I've been clicking on it.
- Real customer quotes under each shoe, not marketing copy — short, specific, credible. "Love them. Nice colors." That's how a real person talks.
- Star ratings visible at a glance on mobile — I don't have to squint or hunt for them.
- The Cozy Fit slip-on is a natural fit for me (hands-free adjacent), and showing it right after Arch Fit is smart sequencing.
- No crowded hero — one image, one headline, one button. Easy to read on my phone without zooming.

## What's weak

- BOBS B Cute 2.0 is a women's shoe — putting it in a "top picks" roundup for my inbox is sloppy targeting.
- "So Is Your Size" in the subject goes nowhere — there's no size personalization, no inventory callout, no payoff. Feels like a headline they didn't bother to back up.
- The 20% off offer is behind an SMS opt-in wall — it's not a straightforward discount, it's a trade. I know a conditional offer when I see one.
- The preheader is broken code again. Same JSON garbage I've been seeing since at least July 12th. At this point it's just part of the Skechers "brand experience" I guess.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pull the women's shoe.** Swap BOBS B Cute 2.0 for a men's Hands Free slip-on or another Arch Fit variant — two of three is not a batting average I'd brag about.
- 2. **Make "So Is Your Size" real.** If you're going to promise size availability, at least show "Sizes 8-15 available" or a size selector. Otherwise cut that half of the subject.
- 3. **Lead with the 20% off more honestly.** Either make it a headline offer or drop it to the footer — burying a conditional discount mid-scroll feels like fine print.
- 4. **Fix the preheader.** This is the seventh or eighth email in a row with JSON code bleeding into the preview slot. It's an embarrassment at this cadence.

## Technical Audit — Skechers "The Reviews Are In & So Is Your Size"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard table-based HTML email using Salesforce Marketing Cloud. The primary technical risks are pervasive HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources that will be stripped by major clients, and a broken Return Path inbox-monitoring pixel caused by a missing URL protocol.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Broken Return Path pixel — missing protocol**
```html
<img src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1dbf0dcef79cbad82c12fe25342701e4499" />
```
No `https://` prefix. Email clients treat this as a relative path, so the pixel never fires. Inbox monitoring data for this send is lost.

**Third-party tracking pixel inventory (all hidden `display:none`):**
| Pixel | Domain | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Open pixel | `click.emails.skechers.com` | SFMC native open |
| User-match | `beacon.krxd.net` (×3) | Salesforce DMP / Krux audience sync |
| Open pixel | `ink1000.com` | Third-party open tracker (HTTP — see §3) |
| Inbox | `pixel.app.returnpath.net` | Return Path — broken (above) |

26 click links all route through `click.emails.skechers.com` redirects — expected for SFMC, but destination UTM consistency cannot be verified without following them (see §6).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — HTTP image sources blocked by Gmail, Apple Mail, and others**

All `image.emails.skechers.com` assets and the `ink1000.com` open pixel are served over plain HTTP. Modern clients either block or proxy-rewrite these, breaking image load and open tracking:

```
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/1/dde00662-...png  (Skechers logo)
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/49468f73-...png  (missing alt)
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/0ec56c9f-...png  (App Store badge)
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/9bb508d0-...png  (Google Play badge)
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/07cb60b5-...png  (AfterPay)
http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif?...                             (open pixel)
```
14 HTTP image sources total per QA findings.

**Missing alt text**
- `o.gif` (ink1000.com open pixel) — no `alt=""`
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` — no alt text; if this is a content image it's invisible in image-off environments

**Accessibility — viewport and font scaling**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-zoom. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).

```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applied globally — suppresses user-agent font scaling across all elements, a further accessibility regression on iOS.

**Duplicate media query blocks**
`@media (max-width: 375px)` and `@media (max-width: 640px)` contain identical rule sets (`.hideme`, `.widthFull`, `.width50percent`, etc.). This is dead-weight CSS that adds ~800 bytes with no differentiated behavior between the two breakpoints.

A third `<style>` block appears at the bottom of `<head>` duplicating `.show` and `.mobile-hidden` rules already defined in the first `<style>` block.
