# Always classic. Always Spezial.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- I opened this one because adidas landed in my inbox and I figured maybe they had something on sale — a walking shoe, some cushioned trainers, anything my feet would actually thank me for. Instead I got a nostalgia trip to the '70s and an ad for a flat-soled fashion sneaker that looks like something my grandson would wear to a concert. Not what I was hoping for.
- The FIFA World Cup giveaway section didn't help. I'm not a sweepstakes guy, and I'm not particularly plugged into soccer culture. The AdiClub angle was at least in the right neighborhood — loyalty program, member perks — but a prize box full of gift cards doesn't get me any closer to finding a shoe that works for a long day on my feet. I'm not clicking through to enter a contest when I could be checking what Brooks or Hoka has on clearance.
- Mark as read, move on. I wouldn't forward this to anyone I know at my age. Maybe my grandson.

## What's working

- **Adidas brand recognition is instant** — the three-stripes logo and clean layout meant I didn't think twice about whether this was spam.
- **Product photography is clear and handsome** — three colorways displayed cleanly, I could tell right away what shoe they were selling even at phone size.
- **FIFA tie-in creates a timely hook** — at least the email has a reason to exist right now, not just "hey here's a shoe."
- **No render bugs** — images loaded, text didn't overlap, footer was readable. Clean send.

## What's weak

- **This shoe is not built for my feet.** Flat gum sole, low profile, canvas upper — no arch support, no cushioning story, nothing. The copy talks about "versatile" and "fresh colorways" but says nothing about how it'll feel after two hours of walking the mall.
- **The '70s throwback angle works against me.** Yes, I was alive in the '70s. No, I don't want to dress like I'm trying to relive them. This reads more like it's targeting a 28-year-old who wasn't.
- **The FIFA giveaway is noise, not signal.** I didn't open this email to enter a sweepstakes. If there's a purchase offer buried somewhere, I missed it — no discount, no promo code, no "members save X%" anywhere I could find.
- **No size, fit, or comfort detail anywhere.** I want to know if it runs wide, if there's any cushioning, if it's easy to get on. Zero of that made it into this email.
- **"Learn More" is as generic as it gets.** That button tells me nothing about where I'm going or why I should bother.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with a comfort story or swap the product entirely for Walker's audience.** If adidas wants my attention, show me the Ultraboost, the Grand Court Comfort, anything with cushioning copy. The Spezial is a great shoe for someone — just not me.
- 2. **Replace the FIFA giveaway section with a member offer.** "AdiClub members: 20% off this week" would've made me stop scrolling. A $2,000 prize box I'll never win doesn't.
- 3. **Add a product callout that speaks to fit and feel** — even one line: "Padded collar, flexible sole, all-day wear" beats "versatile enough for any look" for someone shopping with their feet in mind.
- 4. **Make the CTA earn the click.** "Shop the Spezial" or "Explore the Collection" beats "Learn More" every time.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — adidas "Always classic. Always Spezial."

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed broken experiences: a malformed destination URL surfaced by the click-tracker, and a completely absent plain-text fallback. Eight instances of the same `arrow` image served over HTTP will be blocked by modern clients that enforce mixed-content policies.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Relative URL leaked into click-tracker destination**
The QA probe caught `unknown url type: '/g/461a04c3-88d8-4106-9fcb-26bf47a4c849?txn=34aeab80-4fea-11f1-b83d-82575556174b'` — a bare `/g/` path that was never absolutized before being wrapped by the `dv.adidas.com/o/` redirect. The click-tracker cannot resolve a root-relative URL, so this link will error at click time for any recipient who hits it. The wrapper URL itself (`dv.adidas.com/o/461a04c3…`) is structurally valid; the problem is the payload inside `cp_tp`.

**[WARN] 39 tracking links skipped**
All click URLs route through `click.comms.adidas.com` or `dv.adidas.com` redirect chains. QA probing was blocked at the redirect layer; destination URL validity was not verified for those 39 links. Manual spot-check of final destinations recommended before send.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] HTTP image served 8× — will be blocked**
`b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg` (the "arrow" icon) is referenced via `http://` on every instance:
```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-...jpg
```
Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all proxy or block non-HTTPS image sources. All 8 instances will silently break; replace with the `https://` equivalent.

**[WARN] Tracking pixel served over HTTP and missing alt**
```
http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019e28c00c0e-f82f7e60-228f-4267-bf01-e5539b616b29-000000/ibd7EAfbPWD03BKRu65JeUqXX...
```
Open-tracking pixels should always be HTTPS; HTTP pixels are blocked by the same mixed-content rules and will silently kill open-rate reporting.

**[WARN] Two content images missing `alt` text**
- `14986549-7195-4dbb-b2f8-4d5e4b2903b6.png` — no `alt` attribute
- Tracking pixel `461a04c3-88d8-4106-9fcb-26bf47a4c849` — no `alt=""` (should be empty string for decorative/tracking images)

Screen readers will announce the filename; decorative/tracking images must carry `alt=""`.

**[INFO] `<meta>` charset tag missing `http-equiv`**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, some legacy Outlook versions will not honor the charset declaration.

**[INFO] Duplicate `@font-face` declarations**
`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are declared twice across two separate `<style>` blocks with identical rules. No functional breakage, but adds ~1 KB of dead CSS to every send.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (`{{`, `%%`, `[[`) are visible in the provided HTML. The `mi_cid`, `mi_mid`, and `cp_cid` parameters in click URLs appear to be populated (no placeholder strings). The `txn=34aeab80-4fea-11f1-b83d-82575556174b` in the broken link is a literal UUID rather than an unreplaced token, so the personalization pipeline fired — the issue is URL construction, not merge rendering.

---

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text MIME part absent**
QA reports 0 characters in the text version. CAN-SPAM does not explicitly mandate a plain-text part, but its absence is a strong spam-filter signal (SpamAssassin `MIME_HTML_ONLY` rule), and RFC 2045 best practice requires a `multipart/alternative` structure. Most enterprise ESPs will auto-generate a stripped plain-text version; confirm whether the SFMC template suppressed it.

**[INFO] `referrer: no-referrer` meta tag**
```html
<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />
```
This strips the HTTP `Referer` header on any navigation that bypasses the click-tracker. For the 39 tracked links this is redundant (attribution flows through the tracker, not the header). For any untracked direct `href`s, it will zero out referrer-based analytics attribution on the landing page.

**[INFO] Physical address and unsubscribe footer** — not verifiable from the truncated source. Confirm both are present in the rendered footer (required by CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) and §5(a)(3)).

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The click-tracker encodes destination URLs inside the opaque `cp_tp` parameter (base64 + compressed). UTM parameters, if present, are baked into that payload and will be passed through on final redirect. The broken `/g/` link means at least one destination never resolves, so conversion attribution for that CTA is lost entirely regardless of UTM setup.

The `no-referrer` meta (noted above) means any non-tracked href that reaches adidas.com will arrive with `document.referrer === ""`, defeating session-stitching on the site side.
