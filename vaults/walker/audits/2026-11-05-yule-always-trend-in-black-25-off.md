# Yule Always Trend In Black🖤+$25 Off
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-05**
## Executive summary

- Shoe Carnival again. This is the fifth or sixth red, orange or gold coupon email I've gotten from them in two weeks. The "Black Friday Preview Sale" banner and the "$10 off $74.98 or $25 off $149.98" coupon block are easy to read on my phone without my glasses. Then the email turns into a fashion magazine. The subject said "Yule Always Trend In Black," and the black-shoe section is mostly women's flats, strappy sandals and tall heeled boots. I'm a 62-year-old man who wants cushioned, slip-on shoes.
- The one Nike sneaker is the only thing near me, and it's a dark running-style shoe with no price and no mention of cushioning. The $29.98 boot and clog sale is a better offer than most of the email. It's labeled "Women's" with kids' at $24.98, so I'd skip it. The "Mens" link in the bottom nav is too small and too far down for me to bother with.
- I'd open it out of habit, scroll about half of it, and not click. I wouldn't forward it. If a coupon is the only reason I'd go, I'd save the barcode and wait for a Skechers or New Balance deal.

## What's working

- The "$10 off / $25 off" coupon block is big and clear, with the code HOLIDAY and the price thresholds right on it. I can do that math without squinting.
- The barcodes near the bottom are handy. I could just show my phone at the register.
- It's a brand I know, and the logo at the top makes it clear who it's from.
- The Nike sneaker photo is the only thing aimed at a man, and the shoe itself looks solid.
- The "Shop the Sale" and "Get Store Coupon" links tell me what happens next.

## What's weak

- Nearly all the "Black" products are women's: Blue Aura flats, Badgley Mischka sandals, Christina Royale heeled boots. I'm not who this was written for.
- Nothing mentions comfort, cushioning, arch support or slip-on convenience. I came for those.
- Product captions are tiny, and the "Shop Nike" and "Shop Blue Aura" links are small tap targets on a phone.
- The boot and clog sale is labeled "Women's" and "Kids'," so the best price in the email isn't for me.
- It's the same coupon blast I've seen all week, with a new holiday banner on it. The fine print runs long and is small and hard to read, and there are "some exclusions apply" notes, so the offer doesn't feel fully honest.
- The "Mens" link at the bottom is the only way to a men's page, and it's tiny.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment by who's reading. Show me a men's black comfort sneaker or slip-on at a price, with a "Shop Men's Black" button.
- 2. Put the coupon first and make the black-shoe theme about one product, not four brands.
- 3. Add comfort details to the product tiles: "cushioned," "slip-on," "wide widths."
- 4. Make the captions and links larger, and make the buttons real buttons.
- 5. Shorten the fine print and put the exclusions up front.
- 6. Space out the sends. A new angle each week beats the same coupon in a new color.

- **Malformed pixel URL:** `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20261105_black_color_story_AM​` has two problems:

- **Unprobed links:** 32 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe, so their destinations and status codes are unverified. This is a coverage gap, not a confirmed defect.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `alt` (4 images):**
  - `23a03225-7c93-49fa-988c-8821675cac1b.jpg` (`image.email.shoecarnival.com/lib/fe3311737164047d701077/m/1/`)
  - `fe4e293c-bccf-488a-bbf7-db06bf5745de.jpg` (same path)
  - The two `emltrk.com` pixels. These are tracking pixels, so this is minor, but `alt=""` is still expected.
  - The two content images are the real accessibility concern, because screen readers and image-blocked clients get no text equivalent. Whether they are linked or purely decorative can't be determined from the truncated source.
- **Ineffective `<meta>` tags:**
  - `<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />` has no `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. The standalone `charset` attribute still works in HTML5 parsers, but the `content` value is malformed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Fix the `[UNIQUE]` pixel.** Confirm whether the placeholder is substituted at send time or only missing in the captured copy. If it is missing at send time, fix the ESP template.
2. **Repair the `t=utm_campaign=...` pixel parameter.** Remove the trailing zero-width character and URL-encode the value.
3. **Add `alt` text** to the two content images and `alt=""` to both tracking pixels.
4. **Correct the meta tags** to `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">` and `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge">`.
5. **Lower the plain-text URL ratio** by adding more descriptive text, or by using shorter links in the text part.
6. **Close the audit gaps.**
   - Capture the full HTML and raw headers (`List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, `Authentication-Results`) from a direct inbox, not the relay.
   - Resolve the 32 redirect links to check final URLs, status codes and UTM carry-through.
