# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-10-18**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I got to a shoe I liked, but I never got it into a cart. The product page showed "Please select a size" in red, and the cart count in the header stayed at **0**. The cart screenshot is a completely blank white page. If I were standing in my kitchen with my phone, I'd assume the site was broken and go back to Hoka or Brooks.

The first half of the trip works. The menu is readable, the Men's Shoes page is organized, and the product page has price, reviews and payment options. Nothing in the logged-in step looked different, so I can't say I was recognized. The search results are relevant to comfort, but the first two shoes are women's styles.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10** (yesterday: 3/10). That's no change.

- **Improvement:** The menu and the Men's Shoes page are clear, and the product page has good information.
- **Regression:** I can't point to one. I only have yesterday's number, not its details.
- **Still broken:** Checkout is still blocked. The blank cart page is a hard failure that no amount of good browsing makes up for.

## 3. What's Working

- **Menu drawer (step 4):** Big bold labels with arrows, a clear Back button and a large X. "Shoes" and "Comfort Technologies" are right where I'd look.
- **Men's Shoes page (step 5):** It opens with "Hands Free Slip-ins" and "Walking Shoes" tiles. That is how I shop.
- **"Slip-ins / Wide Fit / Arch Fit / Max Cushioning" strip (step 1):** It speaks my language the moment the page loads.
- **Product page (step 6):** The price ($88.00) and the 1,933 reviews are easy to see. The monthly payment line helps too.
- **Size grid (step 7):** The sizes are laid out in a clear grid. Sold-out sizes are struck through, so I don't waste a tap on them.
- **Add to Cart button (step 7):** It's wide, dark blue, and easy to hit with a thumb.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Blank cart (step 8):** A white screen with no header, no message and no "your cart is empty." This is the worst moment of the trip.
- **Add to Cart didn't register (step 7):** The error says "Please select a size," but the cart badge stayed at 0. Either the site failed without telling me, or the test never picked a size. For a real customer, a default or a prompt at the top of the page would have helped.
- **Size selection is far from the shoe (step 6 to 7):** The size grid is a long scroll below the photo. Some of the most common men's sizes (8, 8.5, 9, 11.5) are crossed out. That's discouraging, and there's no "notify me" explanation next to them.
- **Login step isn't visible (steps 2 and 3):** Both look identical to the homepage. I saw no popup being dismissed and no login form. The header still shows the empty cart, and the product page later says "Sign in for free shipping & returns!" So I was clearly not logged in, and I got no personalization.
- **Search shows women's shoes first (step 9):** "Women's" labels are on both top results. As a 62-year-old man, I'd feel the site isn't paying attention to me.
- **Small text:** The "Applied at Cart" lines and the gray promo strip are small and low-contrast. I'd have to pinch to read them comfortably.
- **No comparison or price-vs-quality content:** Nothing helps me weigh Skechers against Hoka, Brooks or New Balance, and nothing says whether $88 is worth it. The "2 Pairs for $100" deal is the only value message, and the "applied at cart" part is confusing when the cart never worked.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page first.** It must never render blank. Show the header, the item list or an "empty" message, and a way back to shopping.
2. **Make Add to Cart reliable.** If a size is missing, scroll to the size grid and highlight it. Keep the error message right by the button.
3. **Prove the login worked.** Show "Hi, [name]" in the header and a reason to sign in, such as saved sizes, width or a "my comfort" preference.
4. **Respect the shopper's category.** When I came in through Men's, search should rank men's results first, or at least offer a "Men's" toggle at the top.
5. **Move size and width up the product page**, closer to the price, with a clear "we'll notify you" message on sold-out sizes.
6. **Increase the font size and contrast** on promo lines. Make the "Applied at Cart" text bigger and darker.
7. **Add simple comfort content:** a short "Why Contour Foam / Arch Fit" explainer and a quick comparison with other comfort brands, plus a cost-per-wear idea.

## 6. Bottom Line

I like the idea of Skechers. Slip-ins, arch fit and walking shoes are what I'm looking for, and the site says so up front. But I couldn't finish the purchase: the cart showed 0, then a blank page. Today is no better than yesterday, so it's still a 3/10. Fix the cart and the add-to-cart flow, and this becomes a real contender.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

- **Several findings likely come from the test harness, not the site.** Five of the six network errors are 429s on Kasada's bot-defense fingerprint endpoint (`/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.904`). The sixth is a 429 on `/cart/`. The automated session was rate-limited or challenged, so the View Cart step probably audited an error or interstitial page, not the real cart.
- **The captured journey is not trustworthy.** The HTML samples for Homepage, Log In and Men category all report `https://www.skechers.com/`. The later TTFBs (28–46ms) are far below the first loads (410–437ms). Those steps may be SPA transitions, cached responses or stale timing entries, not real navigations.
- **Web Vitals are incomplete.** LCP and CLS were not captured for any step, so nothing can be concluded about them.
- **The remaining code-level findings are real.** They are the Homepage and Search accessibility violations, the landmark structure, the third-party script load, and the console warnings.

## Accessibility

18 axe violations across three steps.

**Homepage (10)**
- **critical `aria-required-parent`:** ARIA roles are used outside their required parent (for example `tab`, `option` or `listitem` without `tablist`, `listbox` or `list`).
- **critical `button-name`:** buttons have no accessible name, which is typical of icon-only controls such as carousel arrows, close buttons or the cart/search icon.
- **serious `link-name`:** links have no discernible text, typical of image-only or icon links.
- **moderate `landmark-main-is-top-level` and `landmark-no-duplicate-main`:** there is more than one `<main>`/`role="main"`, and at least one is nested. This points to templated fragments or an injected container with its own main.
- **moderate `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique`, `region`:** the landmark structure is inconsistent, and some content sits outside any landmark.
- **moderate `heading-order`** and **minor `aria-allowed-role`:** headings skip levels, and a role is set on an element that doesn't allow it.

**View Cart (4)**
- **serious `document-title` and `html-has-lang`:** the document has no `<title>` and no `lang`.
- **moderate `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one`:** there is no main landmark and no `<h1>`.
- **Likely cause:** all four are consistent with a bare 429 response page, given the `/cart/` 429. The homepage has `lang="en"`, so the real cart template probably isn't affected. Re-test before filing these as cart defects.

**Search "comfort shoes" (4)**
- **moderate `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique`, `region`** and **minor `aria-allowed-role`:** the same landmark and role issues as the Homepage, so they likely come from shared templates, probably the header, footer or filter sidebar.

The repeated landmark and role violations on Homepage and Search suggest a shared layout component.

## SEO

## Mobile Optimization

- **Viewport:** `--vh: 6.64px` implies a viewport of about 664px high, so the emulation is working. The `<meta name="viewport">` is not visible in the truncated source.
- **Tap targets:** the unnamed buttons and links (see Accessibility) are probably icon controls. Check that they meet a 44×44px target size.
- **Third-party JS on mobile:** the volume of tags above is a mobile CPU and bandwidth concern. Data to measure it (TBT/INP) was not collected.
- **Navigation:** if the category and login steps were client-side only, check that mobile menu links are real anchors with distinct URLs.

## Console & Network Errors

- **429 on Kasada `/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.904` (5 occurrences).** The bot-defense fingerprint request is being rate-limited, which is likely why the session degraded. This is likely caused by the headless or automated browser. A real user on a clean browser may not see it. Run this journey with a warmed profile, slower pacing or an allowlisted IP before treating it as a site bug.
- **429 on `/cart/`.** The cart page itself was blocked or throttled, so the View Cart step is invalid (see Accessibility). If real users see this, it is a revenue-critical issue.
- **`Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document` (5 occurrences).**
  - A script is registering `unload` handlers, which are deprecated and block bfcache.
  - Chrome is now disallowing them.
  - The source is probably a third-party tag. Find it in DevTools, the "Issues" panel, or by searching for `addEventListener('unload'` / `onunload`.
- **`Unsupported Summarizer API languages…` (2 occurrences).** This is Chrome's built-in AI Summarizer API being called with unsupported languages. It is probably an extension or injected script in the test browser, not skechers.com code. Verify it by loading the page in a clean profile.
- **Count discrepancy:** 24 console errors are reported, but only 10 are listed (3× 429, 2× Summarizer, 5× unload). The rest are unknown.

## Recommendations

1. **Fix the test harness first.**
   - Handle Kasada: use a persistent, warmed profile with realistic pacing, or an allowlist.
   - Assert that each step's URL and title changed.
   - Re-run View Cart and discard its axe results if the page was a 429.
   - Capture LCP and CLS with buffered observers.
2. **Fix the Homepage ARIA defects.**
   - Give every icon button and link an accessible name via `aria-label` or visually hidden text.
   - Correct the `role` and required-parent structure.
   - Fix the heading order.
3. **Fix the landmark structure across templates.**
   - Use one top-level `<main>` per page.
   - Label repeated `nav` and `aside` landmarks distinctly.
   - Put page content inside landmarks.
4. **Find the script registering `unload` handlers.** Replace it with `pagehide` or `visibilitychange` to restore bfcache eligibility.
5. **Audit third-party tags.**
   - Remove the duplicate Yahoo script.
   - Load tags only when needed, for example after consent or on specific pages. The Taboola, Snapchat, Reddit and Facebook tags only appear on the category page.
   - Self-host the fonts.
   - Confirm the GTM environment parameters are intended in production.
6. **Re-verify the cart template** for `<title>`, `lang`, `<main>` and `<h1>`, once a valid cart render is captured.
