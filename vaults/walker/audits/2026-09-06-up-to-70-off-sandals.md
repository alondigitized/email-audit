# UP TO 70% OFF SANDALS!​
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-06**
## Executive summary

- Sandals up top, up to 70% off — but let's be honest, it's still Labor Day weekend and I'm not exactly rushing into open-toe shoes for my feet, arch support and all. Then it's the same lineup I've been seeing all week from Shoe Carnival: 40% off athletic brands with Nike, adidas, Jordan front and center, and Skechers stuck at the bottom of the logo row again, right next to "and more" like an afterthought. Crocs at $39.98 is at least a name I recognize, though those aren't cushioned enough for a full day on my feet either.
- There's a lot going on here — sandals, athletics, Crocs, a "buy any shoe, get 50% off a pink sticker shoe" thing I don't even understand, coupon codes, mobile wallet barcodes. It's a flyer, not a personal offer. I'd scroll through once out of habit, not click anything, and definitely not forward it — nothing here says "this is for you specifically," it says "here's everything in the store, good luck."

## What's working

- The 70% off sandal banner up top is bold and easy to read without squinting.
- Lowest Price Guaranteed and Labor Day Sale framing feels timely for the actual weekend.
- Crocs Sale section is a clean, simple call-out — one product, one price, no clutter.
- No broken images or overlapping text that I can see — it all rendered fine.

## What's weak

- Skechers is buried at the bottom of the athletic brand row again, same complaint as the last several Shoe Carnival emails I've gotten this week.
- Nothing here speaks to comfort, arch support, or walking shoes — it's sandals, basketball-brand sneakers, and Crocs, none of which are my go-to.
- Way too many offers stacked in one email — sandals, athletics, Crocs, a pink sticker shoe deal, coupon codes, a mobile wallet section. My eye doesn't know where to land after the first two banners.
- The "50% off a pink sticker shoe" banner is confusing — I have no idea what that even means without walking into a store.
- This is basically the same "40% off athletic brands, Nike/Jordan/adidas first" block I've already seen three times this week from this brand.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email down to one clear offer instead of six stacked promos — pick sandals or athletics or Crocs, not all three plus a mystery in-store deal.
- 2. If comfort brands like Skechers, Hoka, or New Balance are in stock, lead with those instead of burying them under Nike and Jordan every single send.
- 3. Drop or explain the "pink sticker shoe" promo — as written it reads like a printing error, not an offer.
- 4. Since I've gotten near-identical athletic-brand blocks daily this week, vary the creative or slow the cadence so it doesn't feel like the same email on repeat.
