# For warm weather running
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-12**
## Executive summary

- Another New Balance running email, and I'm not the audience again. "Running, uninterrupted" — lightweight, breathable performance shoes built for hot-weather runs — then a whole "RC Collection" section with two twentysomethings in racing kits, running bras, 3-inch shorts. That's marathon-training gear, not comfort-casual shoes for a guy who wants his feet to stop hurting by lunchtime. I don't run, I walk, and this brand still hasn't figured that out after — what, six or seven emails now.
- Buried at the very bottom is "Up to 30% off select styles" with a code, which is the only thing that would've gotten my attention, and they put it dead last after a wall of performance-running product I'll never buy. Not clicking, not forwarding — straight to archive.

## What's working

- The hero shoe shot (lavender/sage 1080v15) is clean and well-lit, easy to look at even if it's not my speed.
- "Up to 30% off select styles" with code BTS26 is a concrete, specific offer — I just wish it led the email instead of closing it.
- "Visit a store" as a secondary CTA is good — I like the option to go try shoes on in person rather than guess my size online.

## What's weak

- Every model shown is young and dressed for a race, not a walk — nothing here signals "built for a 62-year-old on his feet all day."
- No mention of cushioning, arch support, or all-day comfort anywhere — it's all "lightweight," "breathable," "built for runners."
- The 30% off deal, which is the one thing I'd act on, is buried at the bottom after three separate product carousels I have to scroll past.
- Apparel section (bras, short-shorts, racing tees) is completely irrelevant to me and just adds scroll fatigue.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the discount, not the running pitch — "Up to 30% off" should be the first thing I see, not the last.
- 2. Swap in a walking/comfort shoe as the hero product instead of a race-day trainer, especially since they clearly have my email segmented as "warm weather" — segment it for style too.
- 3. Cut the RC Collection apparel block entirely for recipients who've never clicked a running-apparel link — that's dead weight for someone like me.
- 4. Add one line about cushioning or all-day support to the copy — something like "comfort that keeps up, mile after mile" instead of purely performance language.
