# Join VIP, get $20 off early. Don’t miss out!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- I've been getting DSW birthday emails for what feels like two solid weeks now, and when this one hit my inbox I recognized the dance immediately — "Join VIP, get $20 off early" is practically the same subject line as yesterday's send and the one before that. The $20 hook still works on me, I'll admit, but when I actually opened it I got a wall of strappy wedge sandals surrounded by tulips. That's not my aisle. I'm a 62-year-old man who wants cushioned sneakers or a solid slip-on — not a pink flower arrangement with heel straps.
- To DSW's credit, the layout is clean and nothing's broken. The hierarchy is easy to read even on my phone. The 40% off banner is big and obvious. But 100% of the visible product is women's fashion sandals. The only thing that could pull me toward a click is that secondary "up to 25% off big-deal brands" strip — Nike and Adidas logos in small print near the middle — because *maybe* that's where men's walking shoes are hiding. But they buried it and I'd have to go looking.
- Mark this one as read and move on. Wouldn't click. Definitely wouldn't forward to a friend. My buddy Phil doesn't want to see wedge sandals either.

## What's working

- **$20 VIP offer is still in the subject line** — concrete number, I know exactly what I'm getting if I join.
- **40% off is displayed in a big, legible badge** — readable at arm's length on my phone without zooming.
- **No broken images or layout glitches** — the email renders clean top to bottom, which I appreciate because I've gotten plenty that don't.
- **"Shop by Size" grid near the bottom** — when I do shop DSW, finding my width is always the first thing I need, so that section is a practical touch.
- **Big-deal brand logos (Nike, Adidas)** — hints that there might be men's athletic shoes in the mix somewhere, even if they don't front it.

## What's weak

- **Every product image is women's wedge sandals** — I'm a man who wants walking shoes with arch support; this might as well be a catalog from a different store.
- **The birthday campaign has been running so long I've gone numb** — I counted at least six DSW birthday emails this week before this one arrived; the $20 VIP hook was new and sharp on Monday, but it's lost its edge by Tuesday.
- **"It's a sale-ebration" is a pun I didn't need** — the playful voice reads young-female, not 62-year-old-male; nothing wrong with fun copy, but it signals this isn't aimed at me.
- **The 25% off big-deal brands is buried below the fold** — that's the one section that might apply to my shopping, and I'd scroll right past the sandal parade before I reached it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the hero by gender** — show me men's walking shoes or even a gender-neutral comfort slip-on in the hero; leave the wedge sandals in the women's version. One email shouldn't do both.
- 2. **Lead with the big-deal brands block for male subscribers** — the Nike/Adidas/Converse row belongs at the top for a 62-year-old cross-shopping athletic brands; don't make me dig.
- 3. **Retire the birthday campaign or change the wrapper** — the bday creative is exhausted; even when the underlying offer is good, I'm tuning it out because the packaging feels like the fourth identical ad this week.
- 4. **Make the VIP $20 offer expiration visible** — "Don't miss out" is vague; if it expires tonight or this weekend, say so; I'll respond to that more than to a generic nudge.
- 5. **Swap "sale-ebration" for copy that speaks my language** — something around fit, comfort, or durability lands better with Boomer males than puns.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Early VIP access: up to 40% off, today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Men's walking & comfort shoes included — shop by size`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Missing `http-equiv` on charset meta:**
```html
<!-- Current (invalid for XHTML) -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<!-- Required -->
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
XHTML DOCTYPE requires the `http-equiv` attribute. Without it, some older Outlook/Lotus clients may misinterpret encoding.

**Issue — Invalid CSS property casing in all 10 `@font-face` blocks:**
```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');
```
`Src` (capital S) is not valid CSS. Some parsers will silently ignore it, causing font fallback to system fonts. Affects all 10 declarations. Should be lowercase `src:`.

**Issue — `text-size-adjust: none` in global reset:**
```css
-ms-text-size-adjust: none;
text-size-adjust: none;
```
`none` disables all automatic text scaling, which is more aggressive than the standard `100%` value. Combined with `maximum-scale=1` on the viewport meta, this effectively locks out pinch-to-zoom and OS-level font scaling — an accessibility regression for low-vision users.

**Issue — `maximum-scale=1` on viewport:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks user-initiated zoom on iOS Safari. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA) requires content to be resizable up to 200% without loss of functionality.
