# Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review — Walker Miles
**Date:** April 11, 2026 | **Vs. Yesterday's Score: 4/10**

---

## 1. Executive Summary

I fired up skechers.com on my phone today hoping to grab a pair of Slip-ins. The Men's navigation was actually pretty solid — I found the Hands Free Slip-ins category without much trouble, landed on a good-looking shoe at a fair price with the BOGO deal. But the experience fell apart at the worst possible moment: the cart page came up completely blank. Nothing. White screen. I had no idea if my shoe was in there or not. On top of that, the homepage greeted me with Mother's Day content wall-to-wall, no sign Skechers knows I'm a guy who's been buying their shoes for years, and when I searched "slip in" the top results were all Women's. Today felt like shopping as a stranger, not as a loyal Plus member.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

Same score as yesterday. The cart failure is a critical regression that stops a sale dead in its tracks. No improvement in homepage personalization or search relevance.

## 4. What's Weak

**Cart page is completely blank.** This is the single worst thing I saw today. Step 8 — the cart — rendered as a pure white screen with nothing on it. I tapped ADD TO CART (after the "please select a size" reminder), and then went to view my cart and saw absolutely nothing. Did my shoe make it in? Do I have a BOGO discount applied? Is there a checkout button? I have no idea. At my age I'm not going to dig around troubleshooting a website — I'm going to close the tab.

**Homepage doesn't know I exist.** The entire above-the-fold screen is Mother's Day: pink flowers, women in pink shoes, "Get Mom a Gift She'll Love." I'm a 62-year-old guy logged into a Skechers Plus account. Not one piece of men's content, not a single Slip-in, not a "Welcome back, Walker" — nothing. Feels like I landed on the wrong website.

**Login captured nothing visible.** Steps 2 and 3 looked identical to the homepage. No confirmation I was logged in, no personalized greeting, no "your Plus benefits are active" acknowledgment. The BOGO deal shows at the top but that's available to everyone.

**"Please select a size" is a wall, not a guide.** Step 7 showed an error triangle and the message, but no size was pre-highlighted or suggested. Older shoppers benefit from a little more hand-holding here.

**Search returned Women's first.** I searched "slip in" and got 1,026 results — but the two products shown above the fold are both labeled Women's. Zero filtering by my gender, zero personalization from my login. I had to scroll past women's shoes to find what I came for.

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** A blank cart is an abandoned purchase. This is P0.
2. **Personalize the homepage for logged-in men.** I should see Men's Slip-ins or a "Welcome back" banner, not Mother's Day flowers.
3. **Apply gender context to search.** A logged-in male user searching "slip in" should see Men's results by default, with a clear toggle to switch.
4. **Show login confirmation.** After sign-in, give me a brief "Welcome back, Walker — your Plus benefits are active" message so I know the site knows me.
5. **Pre-select or highlight the most popular size** on the product page, or at minimum animate the size grid when the error fires so my eye goes directly to it.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Audit covers 8 steps of the mobile purchase funnel on skechers.com (Salesforce Commerce Cloud / Demandware stack). The site carries an unusually heavy third-party script burden, has a functional cart blocker (HTTP 429), a broken third-party widget due to CORS misconfiguration, and 4 critical ARIA violations on the product detail page. LCP and CLS were not captured, leaving core Web Vitals incomplete.

---

## Accessibility

**30 axe violations detected; 4 are critical severity.**

| Severity | Count | Pages Affected |
|----------|-------|----------------|
| Critical | 4 | View Product |
| Serious | 5+ | Homepage, Men's Shoes, View Product |
| Moderate | 15+ | Homepage, Men's Shoes, View Product |
| Minor | 1 | Men's Shoes |

**Critical violations — require immediate remediation:**

- **`image-alt`** (View Product): `<img>` elements missing `alt` attributes. Product images without alt text are both an accessibility failure (WCAG 1.1.1) and a functional loss for screen reader users navigating the PDP.
- **`button-name`** (View Product): Buttons with no discernible text. Likely icon-only buttons (size selectors, image carousels) with no `aria-label`.
- **`aria-required-children`** (View Product): An element with a role that mandates specific child roles (e.g., `role="listbox"` without `role="option"` children) — indicates broken ARIA widget implementation.
- **`aria-valid-attr-value`** (View Product): Invalid ARIA attribute values (e.g., `aria-labelledby` pointing to a non-existent ID). Will silently break assistive technology.

**Serious violations:**

- **`aria-dialog-name`** (View Product): Modal/dialog overlay missing `aria-labelledby` or `aria-label`. Focus management inside modals is likely also broken.
- **`aria-input-field-name`** (View Product): Form inputs (size, quantity?) without accessible labels.
- **`link-in-text-block`** (Men's Shoes): Links distinguished only by color — fails WCAG 1.4.1 for users with color-vision deficiency.
- **`link-name`** (Homepage): Links with no text content — likely icon links in navigation or social links.
- **`color-contrast`** (View Product): Foreground/background contrast below 4.5:1 WCAG AA threshold.

**Structural landmark issues (Homepage, Men's Shoes):**
- `landmark-no-duplicate-main` + `landmark-main-is-top-level`: Multiple nested `<main>` elements. Indicates a component is rendering its own `<main>` inside an outer `<main>` — common SFCC template bug.
- `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`: `<aside>` elements nested inside `<main>` rather than being siblings.
- `heading-order`: Heading levels skip ranks (e.g., `<h1>` → `<h3>`).

## Performance

| Step | TTFB | Status |
|------|------|--------|
| Homepage | 46ms | Good |
| Log In | 34ms | Good |

- **Men's Shoes at 177ms TTFB**: The PLP likely isn't edge-cached (faceted URLs or session-dependent state bypassing CDN). Compare with category pages on a warm CDN hit.
- **View Cart at 136ms**: Plausible for session-dependent cart rendering, but the 429 errors below mean this page was also partially blocked during the audit run.

**Third-party script overload** — the `<head>` contains 15+ async ad/analytics scripts loading before first paint:

> TikTok pixel (×2 scripts), Pinterest, Google Ads, DoubleClick, Bing, Reddit, Yahoo (×2 tags), Snapchat, Quantum Metric, Monetate (×2 scripts), Impact, Bloomreach, Facebook, NextDoor, Taboola, Stape server-side tag, CreativeCDN, Go2SDK/Tune, WebFontLoader

Each is `async` so they don't block parsing, but they do compete for network bandwidth, main-thread time, and can trigger layout shifts when they inject content. On a mobile connection this payload is significant.

- **WebFontLoader (`webfontloader.js` from cdnjs)**: Loads 5 weights of Mulish (n3–n7). The `wf-*-active` classes on `<html>` confirm FOUT is being managed, but loading a synchronous font-swap orchestration library from a CDN adds a network round-trip. Native `font-display: swap` in CSS would be lighter.

- **LCP not captured**: Without LCP values, it's impossible to assess render performance on the largest content element (likely a hero image or product image). This should be instrumented.

## Mobile Optimization

- **Viewport height JS hack**: `--vh: 6.640000000000001px` is set as an inline style on `<html>` via JavaScript. This is the standard workaround for iOS Safari's dynamic toolbar affecting `100vh`. Acceptable, but the floating-point precision suggests the calculation runs without rounding (`window.innerHeight * 0.01` without `Math.round`/`toFixed`).
- **No `preconnect` hints observed** for high-priority origins (Monetate, Quantum Metric, Bloomreach) — only `dns-prefetch`. On mobile, upgrading to `<link rel="preconnect">` for above-the-fold resource origins would reduce connection overhead.
- **LCP image `loading` attribute**: Cannot confirm `loading="eager"` and `fetchpriority="high"` on the hero/LCP image — these are required for good mobile LCP. Without LCP measurement this is unverifiable.

## Console & Network Errors

**Functional blocker: Cart page returning HTTP 429**

```
429  https://www.skechers.com/cart/
```

The cart endpoint itself is rate-limited. This is a direct conversion-path failure — users attempting to view their cart may receive an error page or blank state. This needs investigation against Kasada (bot protection) configuration and any WAF rate-limit thresholds applied to `/cart/`.

**Kasada fingerprint endpoint 429 storm (5 occurrences):**

```
429  /149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.308
```

This is the Kasada bot-protection client fingerprinting endpoint. Five 429s in a single session indicates the Kasada SDK is retrying aggressively after an initial rejection. This can happen when:
- The session token is stale and the SDK re-fingerprints repeatedly
- A WAF rule is misconfigured to rate-limit the fingerprint path itself
- The SDK version (`j-1.2.308`) has a retry bug

The side effect is that Kasada-gated resources (likely including `/cart/`) are also blocked.

**Stylitics CORS failure (2 occurrences):**

```
CORS: https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?base=hotspots&theme=skechers&version=50a631f787
```

The Stylitics "shop the look" / hotspot widget CSS is blocked by a missing `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` header on Stylitics' CDN response. This appears to be a vendor-side CORS misconfiguration or a missing origin allowlist entry for `www.skechers.com`. The widget will fail silently — inline hotspot styling will be broken or unstyled. Skechers should file a support ticket with Stylitics and verify the origin is whitelisted in their Stylitics account configuration.

## Recommendations

**P0 — Functional blockers:**

1. **Investigate Kasada 429 on `/cart/`** — determine whether the WAF rate-limit threshold is too low for legitimate mobile sessions or whether stale Kasada tokens are triggering retries that exhaust the limit. Consider exempting the `/cart/` route from aggressive rate-limiting or tuning the Kasada retry backoff.
2. **Fix Kasada SDK retry storm** — the 5× repeat 429s on the fingerprint endpoint suggest unbounded retries. Confirm the deployed SDK version (`j-1.2.308`) is current; Kasada releases often include retry-behavior fixes.

**P1 — Critical accessibility:**

3. **Add `alt` text to all PDP product images** — this is both WCAG 2.1 Level A and impacts SEO image indexing.
4. **Add `aria-label` to all icon-only buttons** on PDP (add-to-cart, image gallery nav, quantity controls).
5. **Fix ARIA widget structure** — audit `aria-required-children` failures; likely a listbox or tab widget with incorrect role hierarchy.
6. **Fix `aria-labelledby` references** pointing to missing IDs — run `document.querySelectorAll('[aria-labelledby]')` and verify every referenced ID exists in the DOM.

**P2 — Third-party / integration:**

7. **Resolve Stylitics CORS** — contact Stylitics support to add `https://www.skechers.com` to their CORS allowlist for `web-assets.stylitics.com`.
8. **Remove `G-1234567890` from GTM** — audit the GTM container for stale/test tags and remove or disable them.
9. **Deduplicate Yahoo pixel** — `s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` fires twice on Men's Category; remove one instance from the GTM container.

**P3 — Performance:**

10. **Capture LCP and CLS** — instrument Real User Monitoring (or expand synthetic audit) to collect these metrics. TTFB alone is insufficient for Web Vitals compliance.
11. **Upgrade `dns-prefetch` to `preconnect`** for above-the-fold resource origins (Monetate, Quantum Metric, image CDN).
12. **Replace WebFontLoader with `font-display: swap`** — remove the cdnjs WebFontLoader dependency and declare font-face with `font-display: swap` directly in CSS.
13. **Audit GTM container** for low-priority tags that can be deferred to `DOMContentLoaded` or user-interaction triggers rather than firing on page load.
