# Your Items Are Waiting!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-21**
## Executive summary

- This is a cart abandonment email, and I'll give Skechers credit — they got the hero right. There's a men's Slip-in right up front, the Arch Fit Glide-Step Pro, which is exactly the kind of shoe I'd have left sitting in a cart. The "Complete Your Order" button is big and obvious, which I appreciate. But then they undercut themselves: the "we think you'll love" recommendation row is loaded with **women's shoes**. I'm a 62-year-old guy. I don't need women's Arch Fit sneakers recommended to me. It makes me feel like they're just blasting the same email to everyone. The 20% off offer buried lower down is actually a strong hook for me — I just had to scroll past stuff that wasn't mine to find it.
- Hero is on target, discount is real, CTA is clear. But the gender mismatch in recommendations, missing personalization cues, and buried discount all leave money on the table for someone like me.

## What's working

- **The hero product is right for me.** Skechers Slip-ins: Arch Fit Glide-Step Pro — men's, comfort-focused, slip-on. That's my lane. I can see it clearly, the photo is clean, and "Complete Your Order" is a no-nonsense button.
- **20% off is real.** That banner cuts through. I'm a Plus member and I pay attention to discounts. That's the kind of thing that gets me back to the cart.
- **Text size is acceptable.** I didn't have to squint at the main headline. "WE NOTICED YOU NOTICING US" is big enough to read without reaching for my reading glasses.
- **The cart nudge framing is honest.** "Your cart is still waiting" doesn't feel like a trick. It just reminds me I left something behind, which is true.

## What's weak

- **Recommended products are women's shoes.** I can clearly see "Women's Slip-ins" labels on the products in the "we think you'll love" row. That's a straight-up targeting miss. Why are you showing me women's shoes? That row should be men's comfort styles — more Slip-ins, more Arch Fit, maybe walking shoes. Instead it feels like a copy-paste job.
- **The 20% off offer is too far down.** If I'm on my phone and I'm not scrolling much, I might never see it. That's the strongest hook in the whole email and it's buried after a section of products I don't want.
- **"We noticed you noticing us" is a little cutesy.** It's clever-ish, but not quite my language. I'd rather just be told straight: "Hey, you left something in your cart. Here it is." Keep it simple.
- **No mention of my Plus membership.** I'm a loyal customer. The email doesn't acknowledge that at all. No point balance, no "member exclusive," nothing. I might as well be a stranger off the street.
- **The navigation list (Women, Men, Kids, Clothing…) is small.** On my phone those are tight little text links. If I'm trying to tap "Men" I might hit "Women" by mistake.
- **That spray bottle product** in the recommendations row looks like it's a shoe deodorizer or something. It's oddly placed and makes the row feel unfocused.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the recommendation row for men.** Show me men's Slip-ins, men's Arch Fit walkers, men's wide-width options. If I left a men's shoe in my cart, the cross-sell should be men's shoes — full stop.
- 2. **Move the 20% off block above the recommendations.** Put the discount right after "Complete Your Order." That's the second-best reason to buy; don't hide it.
- 3. **Acknowledge my Plus membership.** Something like "As a Skechers Plus member, here's your 20% off" turns a generic promo into a loyalty reward. That matters to me.
- 4. **Simplify the "We Noticed You Noticing Us" headline.** Try something more direct: "You left something behind — still want it?" That's plain, honest, and works for my age group.
- 5. **Larger tap targets on the navigation links.** On mobile at my age, those text-only nav links are too small. Either make them buttons or drop them from this email entirely — I don't need a full site map in a cart reminder.

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is sent via Attentive's infrastructure (`msgs.skechers.com` / `attentivemail.com`). All links route through HTTP click-tracking redirects, image assets appear to also use HTTP, and the truncated source prevents full inspection of the unsubscribe block and any personalization tokens.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**HTTP click-tracking (confirmed):** Every anchor in the visible source uses `http://` not `https://`:
```
http://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=u001.LNc6...
```
Modern email clients (Gmail, Apple Mail) will flag or strip HTTP links. Attentive supports HTTPS redirects — this should be `https://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?...`

**HTTP image source (likely):** The only visible `<img>` begins with `http://imag…` (truncated). If confirmed, mixed-content rules in some clients will block the image silently.

**UTM parameters:** Not visible in the HTML. They may be appended at the Attentive redirect destination, but that cannot be confirmed from the source. If they're absent on the final landing URL, click attribution will be unresolved.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Triggers lint warnings in some deliverability tools; low severity but trivially fixable.

**`#MessageViewBody a` override:**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none; }
```
This nullifies link contrast for Outlook.com users who rely on link underlines or high-contrast themes. Links become visually indistinguishable from body text.

**No `alt` text confirmable:** The `<img>` tag is truncated before the `alt` attribute is visible. Must verify all product images have descriptive `alt` text — especially critical here because product images are the primary content of a cart abandonment email and render as broken boxes if images are blocked.

**Preheader spacer technique:** Uses a mix of `͏` (U+034F combining grapheme joiner) and `­` (soft hyphen) for inbox preview padding. Both techniques are valid, but mixing them in the same preheader string is unusual and may behave inconsistently in certain clients (e.g., Samsung Mail). A single character type is preferable.
