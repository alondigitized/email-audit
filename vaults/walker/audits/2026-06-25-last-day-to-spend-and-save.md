# Last day ‼️ to spend and save 🤑
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- Famous Footwear again. At this point I recognize their sends before I even look at the sender name — and not always in a good way. The "Last day to spend and save" subject got my attention because I actually care about spend-and-save tiers; that's my kind of math. But the second I tapped it open and saw that same tracking URL wall-of-garbage in my preview text — the exact same broken preheader I've been staring at since at least June 15th — I felt that familiar mix of mild frustration and resignation. Ten sends. Still not fixed.
- The email body itself is a better story. The Spend & Save tiers are front and center, the tiered discount structure is legible, and the brand grid that follows actually has names I recognize — New Balance 30% off, Crocs 25% off, Brooks called out with a specific price point. For a guy like me who comparison-shops those brands, that's relevant real estate. The Brooks at $109.99 is the kind of anchor that makes me stop scrolling.
- I'd click — probably on the Brooks deal or the New Balance section. I would not forward this to a friend. And I'd feel a lot better about this email if Famous Footwear spent ten minutes fixing the preheader that's been broken for two solid weeks.

## What's working

- **Spend & Save tiers are the hero and they're legible** — the tiered structure reads fast, which is how I need to consume offers on my phone.
- **Brooks at a named price point ($109.99)** is the strongest signal in the email; that's a brand I actively cross-shop and a real number I can evaluate.
- **New Balance and Crocs sections** — both comfort-brand staples that mean something to me; they didn't bury these in the footnotes.
- **"Last day" urgency is credible** — Spend & Save promos do expire, and I know that from experience with this brand.
- **Clean layout, no broken images or overlapping text** — the email renders fine once you're past that preview text disaster.

## What's weak

- **The tracking URL preheader is still broken** — June 15, June 16, June 17, June 21, now June 25. I've stopped being surprised and started being annoyed. This is a QA failure that's now become a brand impression problem.
- **50% off women's sandals section** — I scrolled right past it, but it took up real estate that could've spotlighted another men's comfort option.
- **No comfort language anywhere** — cushioning, arch support, wide width — none of it. The email sells price, not fit, and for a guy like me, fit is the deciding factor.
- **Jordan at $99.99 near the bottom** — not my demographic, not my buying occasion. Feels like they mixed audience segments into one batch send.
- **Another Spend & Save, same creative cadence** — this is the third or fourth variation on this same promo in ten days. Starting to feel like wallpaper.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader — this week, not next quarter.** One line of real copy ("Your tiers: $10 off $50 · $15 off $75 · $20 off $100. Ends tonight.") does more work than a 120-character URL string. This is the single highest-impact fix and it costs nothing.
- 2. **Lead the brand grid with men's comfort first** — New Balance and Brooks above the fold, Crocs right behind. Don't make me scroll past women's sandals to find my category.
- 3. **Add one fit/comfort detail per featured shoe** — even three words: "wide widths available" or "cushioned insole" next to the Brooks callout would close the gap between browsing and buying for me.
- 4. **Rotate the creative** — Spend & Save three times in ten days reads as desperation. Even reframing it as "Summer Comfort Event" gives it a fresh hook without changing the underlying mechanic.
- 5. **Segment out Jordan/performance athletic** — that section belongs in a different email to a different list.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last day: $20 off when you spend $100 — Brooks, New Balance, Crocs`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Spend & Save ends tonight — your $20 off is waiting`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Tiers: $10 off $50 · $15 off $75 · $20 off $100. Ends at midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New Balance, Brooks, Crocs — all tiered pricing ends tonight.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Mixed-content HTTP image (high priority):**
```
src: http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=ABB7InYiOjEsImQiOjQ5MTR9...
```
Modern clients (Gmail, Outlook 365, Apple Mail) block non-HTTPS image sources. This asset — likely a LiveContent/dynamic personalization block from Salesforce Marketing Cloud — will silently fail to render in most clients.

**34 images missing `alt` text:**
All product and hero images have `alt=""`. Screen readers will skip them entirely; images-off rendering (common in corporate Outlook environments) will show blank gaps with no fallback text. Notable examples:
- Logo image `3b6a7dc1...png` — no alt, so brand identity disappears when images are blocked
- Separator/spacer `4ccff4ad...jpg` appears 4 times with no alt — acceptable for decorative use, but the pattern is applied to all images indiscriminately

**`<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` is malformed:**
Missing `http-equiv` attribute. Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Without it, charset declaration is ignored; emoji in subject line (`‼️ 🤑`) could corrupt in some legacy clients.

**`user-scalable=0`** in viewport meta is non-standard; correct value is `no`. Minor, but worth normalizing.
