# Happy Crocs Day: Earn 4X Pts for 3 Days Only👏
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-23**
## Executive summary

- My first reaction was that it's Crocs Day and I'm supposed to be excited about points. I've worn Crocs around the house for years, so the clog sale price of $34.98 got my attention. But the top of the email isn't Crocs. It's the same orange "BUY MORE, SAVE MORE" coupon block I've been seeing all week, like in the 10/17 and 10/22 sends. I had to scroll past it to get to the Crocs banner.
- Once I got there, the offer is clear. Members earn 4X points on Crocs for 3 days, and spending $50 gets a $10 reward. I don't let points make the call, though. The shoe has to fit first, and nothing here tells me about fit, cushion or arch. After the Crocs section it turns into a "Friday Deal Roundup" of boots, Nike, Madden Girl and kids' sales. That's a lot of different messages in one email.
- Would I click? Maybe the $34.98 clog if I were already thinking of a pair. I wouldn't forward it. I'd probably read it and move on.

## What's working

- The "CROCS DAY 10.23" banner and "3 DAYS ONLY! 4X POINTS" are big and easy to read on my phone without glasses.
- The Metallic Buckle Clog at $34.98 is a real price on a real shoe, so I can do the price math.
- The product photo is a pile of actual Crocs. I know what I'm getting, and there's no young model pushing me away.
- The $10 reward for spending $50 is spelled out in a yellow badge.
- The coupon codes and barcodes at the bottom are handy if I'm standing in the store.

## What's weak

- The orange coupon block leads the email, so the Crocs story I was told about gets pushed down. It's also the same block as all week.
- The "Shop Crocs" and "Shop Metallic Buckle Clog" links under the images are tiny, and they're hard to tap with my thumb.
- Nothing mentions comfort, cushioning, arch support or slip-on ease, which is what I shop for.
- The Friday roundup (Madden Girl, kids' boots, Brooks, Nike, Family Athletics) is too much and doesn't match the Crocs subject.
- The fine print is long and in tiny type. "Select styles" and "exclusions" are all over it, so I don't fully trust the 4X claim.

- 3. Cut the Friday roundup to two or three items, and include a men's walking or casual shoe from Brooks or New Balance.
- 4. Make the preheader real copy. Right now it's a tracking link.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit: Shoe Carnival, "Happy Crocs Day: Earn 4X Pts for 3 Days Only👏"

### 1. Technical Summary
The visible markup is a standard table-based template with client-specific fixes. Two real defects show up in the tracking pixels: an unresolved `[UNIQUE]` placeholder and a malformed `t=` parameter. The source is truncated and 32 links were not probed, so body links, the footer and the unsubscribe mechanism could not be verified.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unresolved token in pixel URL:** `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]` still contains the literal `[UNIQUE]`. The ESP did not substitute it. The open pixel will likely record no recipient or a generic one, so open attribution is broken or skewed.
- **Malformed pixel query string:** `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20261023_friday_deals_AM​`
  - The `t=` value contains an unencoded `=` (`t=utm_campaign=...`). That is a nested key/value inside a single parameter.
  - The URL ends in an invisible zero-width character (U+200B), visible in the QA output after `_AM`. It can break exact-match campaign reporting or cause a 404 on the pixel.
- **Unverified links:** 32 tracking/click-redirect links on `emltrk.com` were skipped, so destination status, redirect chains and final URLs are unconfirmed.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** the two `emltrk.com` images above lack `alt`. These are tracking pixels, so the impact is low. Add `alt=""` and `width="1" height="1"` so screen readers skip them.
- **Meta tags:** `<meta content="IE=edge" />` has no `http-equiv`, so it does nothing. `<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />` is also non-standard, with no `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. The `charset` attribute still sets the encoding in most clients.
- **Doctype mismatch:** the HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype is paired with an `xmlns` attribute and self-closing `<meta ... />` tags, which are XHTML idioms. This is generally tolerated by email clients.
- **Link styling:** the global rule `a, a:link, ... { text-decoration:none !important; color:inherit !important; }` removes default link affordances. Any link without explicit inline styling will be indistinguishable from body text.
- **Inconsistent breakpoints:** the CSS uses 600px (`.responsive-text`), 660px and 450px. `.responsive-text` sets 12px font size on viewports of 600px and below, which is small for legibility.
- Cannot assess the rest because the source is truncated.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Unresolved token:** `[UNIQUE]` in the pixel URL, described above. This is the only token failure confirmed in the visible source.
- The truncated source shows no other tokens, such as a first-name greeting.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Authentication:** the QA reports "Authentication-Results header not found". This is expected with the AgentMail relay, so SPF/DKIM/DMARC status is unknown rather than failing. The sender domain `email.shoecarnival.com` is a dedicated subdomain.
- **Unsubscribe link, physical address and `List-Unsubscribe`/`List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers:** not verifiable. The HTML is truncated and the headers were not provided. No issue is asserted.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- The only campaign identifier visible is `SCA_20261023_friday_deals_AM`, and it sits inside the malformed pixel `t=` value. It does not appear as a proper `utm_campaign` query parameter.
- That identifier says "friday_deals" while the subject is a Crocs Day / 4X points promotion. This may be a generic send label, but it could muddy campaign reporting.
- UTM params on the click links and landing-page alignment can't be assessed because the 32 click links were not probed.

### 7. Recommendations
1. **Fix the `[UNIQUE]` substitution** in the open-pixel template (`?d=[UNIQUE]`). Send a seed test and confirm the rendered value is populated.
2. **Correct the first pixel's query string.** Encode or separate the `t=` value, for example `t=utm_campaign%3D...`, or use a dedicated parameter. Strip the trailing U+200B.
3. Add `alt=""` and explicit 1×1 dimensions to both tracking pixels.
4. Replace `<meta content="IE=edge" />` with `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />`, or remove it. Use `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />`.
5. Raise the mobile `.responsive-text` size above 12px, and give in-body links an explicit visible treatment.
6. Re-run the audit on the full HTML and raw headers to confirm the unsubscribe link, `List-Unsubscribe` headers, SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment, click-link UTMs and landing pages.
